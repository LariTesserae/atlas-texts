High up in a region of rustling green peaks stands a place where nearly everything is alive. The terrain is mountainous but worn—countless centuries of wind and slow, steady rain have smoothed the slopes into sweeping ridges and flattened plateaus. It’s a far reach above the surrounding lands, yet somehow these summits feel ancient, as if they have slumbered here since the earliest days of the world. The entire ecosystem suggests an ending rather than a beginning: erosion has run its patient course, leaving only enduring, gently rolling heights.

Everywhere one looks, plants rule. In fact, “plants” might be too small a word for the living substance forming the ground, the walls, the pillars, and the canopy. At this extreme elevation, the vegetation has adapted: trunks twist horizontally to cradle pockets of condensation, vines coil into dense braids that knit together to form sturdy walkways, and broad, paddle-like leaves sprout in layered terraces to take advantage of thin sunshine. By day, the scene is gently lit by a normal glow, but that’s about where normality ends.

Because, in the heart of this arboreal expanse, there’s a sprawling settlement—an urban shape of vines and forging halls and improbable steel. It is not a city built upon plants; here, the city itself is plant. Wooden walls incorporate metallic plating and crosshatch with sections of industrial piping that vent steam or drip condensed water. Enormous woody growths stretch overhead, forming archways so broad they might easily count as multiple city blocks. Walkways, floors, and structural scaffolds are all grown from the same porous vines that coil up from a diffused root system. In the damp, cool air, great engines chug and hum at intervals, reverberating through the living architecture. The synergy of life and invention is layered in a dense tapestry of mechanical thrums and rustling leaf-sounds.

Despite this city of living architecture, the environment feels curiously sparse. The wide avenues—woven from thick trunklike vines—are empty enough to echo with each footstep. Wherever the living material does not occupy the space, there is mist. Low-hanging clouds drift through the broad canopies. Thin drizzle forms now and then, trailing across the walkways and collecting in knotholes and hollows. Many of these watery pockets quickly seep away through cracks or get slurped up by whatever root tendrils lie beneath, leaving behind a shimmering dampness rather than any deep pools or lakes.

Recognizable fauna is surprisingly scarce, even though plants abound. At times, one might spy a quick flicker of something small—translucent mothlike creatures fluttering in the gloom, or a lithe, many-jointed shape scuttering across a branch. Rumors speak of bigger beings, but they’re seldom seen, and footprints or droppings are difficult to interpret in the living substrate that shifts and mends itself. It’s almost as though the city’s very architecture dissuades large animal life, or perhaps conceals it. One can stand on an open balcony, staring down the slope for miles, and not see a single flitting bird or bounding mammal across the entire green horizon.

Wrapped up in the vines and timber, the industrial presence is strong. Strange mechanical contraptions clatter in the background: mechanical lifts that ascend spiraling stalk-towers, heavy wheels turning somewhere underground to power the lights overhead, and sporadic bursts of steam that hiss from metal vents. Each building is a hybrid of living bark and hammered iron plating, and soot smudges the vines overhead. The hush of damp leaves, however, is overshadowed by the rumble of forging and the crisp clang of metal on metal, echoing across these heights in a low roar. It’s as if the city has found ways to harness the steady seep of moisture, pulling water from the air or from tightly guarded cisterns, then feeding it into turbines and engine rooms that keep everything alight and in motion.

The strangest element is the quivering sense that reality isn’t quite stable here. From time to time, one might see a structure shimmer: a corridor that was there one moment looks as if it has shifted a few vines to the left. Or the air in certain pockets of the city warps, producing localized illusions like reflections in water. People who live here (those few hardy enough to endure the constant damp cool and the mechanical hum) simply accept these phenomena as some strange nexus of urgent plant growth and industrial energy. Perhaps it’s the bizarre interplay of moisture, altitude, or the subtle conduction of electricity through all that living tissue. But to a newcomer, these moments feel like glimpses of deeper fractures in the world: vines swaying where there is no breeze, entire walls flickering out of alignment for an instant, or faint geometric shapes visible in the corners of one’s vision.

Outside the city’s main cluster of buildings, the plants sprawl in layered terraces. Mosslike prairies cling precariously to abrupt edges, then drop off into mist-filled valleys far below. These valleys, too, are dotted with more structural outcrops, each connected by grown “bridges” that arch from cliff to cliff. The scale is impressive—wider and more varied than a single city or valley. You can travel upon living platforms from one ridge to the next, passing through mechanical gates that measure everything from humidity to travelers’ cargo, while overhead an enormous swirl of vines merges together to form latticed roofs that groan in the breeze.

Though these heights possess a solemn, ancient grandeur, they are hardly silent or still. Rain scrapes across woody surfaces at irregular intervals, rattling like a thousand tiny pebbles trickling down a hollow trunk. Structural timbers moan as branches bend, adjusting to the whims of wind or the press of passing travelers. Deeper in the industrial quarter, engines roar, steel parts clash, and the rhythmic pounding of new expansions competes with the roar of fast-moving water channeled through turbines. Hidden somewhere in the distance, a rumble of unknown origin periodically reverberates, as if some grand mechanism, older than the city itself, is cycling or breathing beneath the surface.

The climate remains persistently cool. The air is crisp enough that one breathes in a faint, tingling freshness—like stepping onto a glacier—but it is never outright freezing. Shallow condensation seeps everywhere: droplets collecting on leaves, water swirling around pipes, rivulets of fog shifting along the ground. Many of the local inhabitants have adapted by wearing multiple layers, often made from woven plant fiber that seems to fuse itself into living garments; machines—some with gears made from entwined roots and hammered metal—stay functional only because they’re well-oiled and carefully shielded against the moisture. Nevertheless, rust is common, leaving splotches of deep brown wherever metal meets open air.

The city’s economy and rules of life appear equally fluid. People (and occasional nonhuman residents whose shapes are half-lost in the drifting shadows) navigate a tenuous balance between cultivating the living substrate for shelter and fueling the machinery that keeps the settlement prosperous. There are hammered iron factories that produce small steam-driven apparati—pumps, lifts, mechanical harvesters—yet resource extraction is done from the very vines that form these buildings. Tools must be grown or shaped carefully from the living matter, then partially metal-plated for durability. Cutting too deeply into the vines can wound the city’s structural integrity or unleash strange structural distortions. Hence, there’s a reluctant acceptance of the plants as both environment and foundation.

Within this lively mixture of smoldering steel and twisting greenery, time feels off-kilter. Some corners of the city look half-finished or half-collapsed, as if they’ve existed for too long without maintenance—yet everything remains stable. The city is a paradox: advanced enough to have mechanized technologies, yet primal enough that it still breathes and churns like one vast organism. The tempo is active but not frantic, like a slow but ceaseless heartbeat that drives the forging of metals, the growth of vines, and the swirl of clouds across the summits.

The odd illusions and shifting architecture hint at something deeper, some dimension of unreality woven into the city’s very root system. You might stand on one of the branching platforms at twilight, listening to the rolling machinery and the soft hiss of drizzle, and watch the distant peaks blur and realign as though they are mirrors of themselves. Lattice beams overhead might momentarily peel away from each other, only to snap abruptly back. The city’s revelations feel half-glimpsed, as if there is some intelligence or impulse guiding the plants at a level no single mind can parse.

At last, once you sink fully into the rhythms here— the intermittent rainfall tapping on leaf-canopies, the clang of mechanical cogwork, the distant hum of some unseen power source— you recognize the hush hidden within the noise. Despite the loudness of machines and the random illusions, there is a sparse emptiness to the passages and plazas, a sense that there should be more inhabitants, more wildlife, and more movement. In that open hush, the city breathes, timeless and primordial, perched at a high vantage over eons of erosion. A place both thoroughly real and oddly unreal—slightly out of joint with the rest of creation—yet deeply, quietly alive.
