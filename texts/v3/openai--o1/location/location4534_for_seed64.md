It is impossible at first to tell where the place begins or ends: everything is cramped in a self-contained realm, as if walled off from the rest of creation. The air is cool and just barely lit, a permanent twilight that seems never to shift—no dawn, no dusk, just the same dim glow. Despite the gloom, the space is crowded with life. Yet it is a life that doesn’t appear to need water at all. It stretches in tufts and wriggling shoots and thick stalks, a greenish tangle of fleshy fronds and fur-like bristles that feed on who knows what. Their surface glistens ever so slightly in the half-light, yet the shimmer hints at some alien chemistry rather than dew.

There is no sign of open streams or hidden pools. No trickle of water, no hiss of condensation. The soil underfoot is a compact layer of mineral flakes and brittle crystals, scoured and settled over eons. Deep cracks line the floor, and the ridges between them form steep outcroppings, like tiny crags in a miniature mountain range. Indeed, the topography soars in abrupt clifflets all around—this realm defies typical notions of “terrain.” It is as if an enormous range of knife-edged spines had been forced together, then compressed into a pocket dimension.

The ridges are high for such a small place: you climb a few steps, and suddenly you are perched on a pinnacle that juts overhead with a dizzying drop into narrow gullies. Far below, where the slivers of rock meet, you can see the vegetation crammed in, each plant pushing up wherever it can fit. The entire environment feels stable, unchanged for centuries, perhaps millennia—geologic in its unhurried stillness—and yet at odds with the rowdy, clanging noise that rises and reverberates through every stone.

That noise: it is a hammering churn, metallic pounding that seems older than memory. It never alters pitch or tempo. It could be a monstrous machine somewhere behind the jagged walls—a contraption that once churned out goods for an industry long forgotten. Or perhaps it’s the heartbeat of the realm itself, a slow, ceaseless pulse of metal upon metal echoing from the hidden corners of this squeezed domain. The sound never wanes or crescendos. It’s as though it were pressed into the stone from the start, like a built-in engine whose purpose remains unknown.

Winding through the vegetation, isolated structures stand here and there—small, scattered, and partially swallowed by the tangle of growth. Single-walled huts of riveted steel, half-buried machine parts that clank in unison with the distant pounding, slender pipes rising at odd angles. A battered catwalk segment juts from a slope, supported by precarious pilings hammered into the rock. Each structure is rusted, pitted from age, yet somehow not quite collapsing. Time, it seems, has largely paused here, or flows so glacially as to be indistinguishable from stasis.

Whatever these tiny factories or machine outposts once produced, they are eerily unoccupied now. There are no creatures—no birds, no insects, no scuttling or skittering shapes in the gloom. The only movement is the gentle swaying of leafless, waterless flora that stretches out with an almost hungry intensity. The plants are thick, curling around any edge or scrap of metal they find. Some have bulbous growths that pulse from within, as if collecting energy from the pounding vibrations. Others cling like elaborate lichens or barnacles molded to the rock. All are dense, packing every bit of available space, coexisting in a savage harmony that leaves no empty gap.

And always, the world around them stands at a lofty extreme. Tall, serrated ridges hem the edges of this pocket, suggesting that a short climb would reveal a plunging chasm or a precipitous drop. Yet there is never any wind, no real sense of open air. Instead, it is as if the domain is locked inside a massive shell, sealed from outside weather or the passing of day and night. If you look closer at the shorn edges of stone, you can see a web of cracks like old scars. They speak of ancient wear, of unstoppable forces that shaped these spires—but all of that happened so long ago, it left only whorls and ridges of sedimentary patterning behind. Now it is simply inert, locked in place.

Any attempt to speak is immediately lost in the continuous racket. The atmosphere is too loud, full of clanging echoes bouncing off the steep walls, magnifying upon themselves until it becomes a dull roar that never relents. The mind adjusts quickly or else would go mad.

It feels utterly alien: a realm of richly thriving vegetation without water, of massive height in minimal space, of worn and ancient rock overshadowed by the industrial hum of machinery that has outlived its designers. It is strangely comfortable in its unwavering equilibrium—cool, dim, saturating every nook with tangled green forms—yet undeniably weird in how it all manages to persist. A place that does not change, does not vanish, does not grow silent: sealed in a moment that loops indefinitely, pressed up against the far boundary of the possible.
