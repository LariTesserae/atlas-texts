High elevation means the air is thin and chill—even colder than one would expect of a place so wet. Yet here, dampness clings to every structure with the tenacity of fresh dew on metal. It is all metal, in fact; there is no natural rock or soil anywhere, only oddly angled panels, grids of walkways, and labyrinthine piping, all riddled with rivets and plated seams. This constructed world perches atop something unknown—concealed beyond thick, artificially rusting bulkheads at the periphery, itself perched on a lofty horizon. From moment to moment, one can’t quite tell whether the infrastructure is half-built or entirely complete; everything shows signs of assembly yet seems frozen in time.

A constant wash of condensation beads on overhead piping and drips into shallow channels below. These twisting canals form watery corridors. Steam wafts and lingers in the cold air, collecting around corners and inside narrow chambers. It’s dim here: a faint grayish glow emanates from occasional industrial lamps that flicker as if caught in a perpetual half-power state. The gloom reveals little, but what shapes you do glimpse in the corners or overhead appear thick with mechanical detail—armatures, tethered cables, silhouettes of crane-like limbs, quivering vents.  

The sound is overwhelming—an unending roar of industrial churn, as if hundreds of engines and giant fans whir in unison behind every wall. It never ceases or even shifts in register, stuck in a fixed cycle, a stable equilibrium of motion. This is the hushless heartbeat of the place. Metal plates rattle underfoot from the rumble, and your ears fill with a metallic roar that blurs the senses. Each drip of condensation falling into water troughs is amplified to a deafening ping when it meets the steel. They’re like metallic rain droplets landing in time with an unseen, unchanging clock.

Out from narrow vents and hidden alcoves, fauna congregates in improbable densities—only they’re not typical animals. Some appear to be living creatures forged of smoking steel or cunningly sculpted alloys, all manner of segmented limbs jointed as if grown from pipelines. Many nest within spherical pods that hum with faint electricity. Others hang from girder frames, wings folded like hinged plates of armor. Flesh-and-blood life-forms also scuttle about, glossy with moisture, their bodies either newly formed or strangely modified by the glimmer of technology. They are so numerous they almost jostle one another in the tight recesses, layered upon layered, crowding across the corridors in constant, if cyclical, activity. Yet “movement” feels strange here—like a complicated clockwork dance that never truly progresses.

Vegetation is nearly absent: just a handful of stringy, bluish vines (also artificially grown?) that spiral from valve casings or clutch at the base of humming turbines. They add only a ghostly presence, scraggly tangles in a place otherwise dominated by metal.  

Though the place never changes, the sensation is still of something being continually assembled—a paradox of perpetual construction without any visible progress. Walls and floors glisten as if newly forged, corners appear half-sealed with riveted plating, and scaffolding towers overhead, giving the impression of an unfinished cathedral. And yet no worker arrives to weld the joints; no new beam is ever laid. The place has always been like this, an industrial chrysalis locked in a single moment of pre-completion.

You realize the entire setting is contained, somehow pocketed—a hidden microcosm at great altitude. Perhaps it’s inside a hollow peak, or straddling a cliff’s rim in such a way that no outsider can see it. The cold is biting, but the loudness of the machines and fauna makes it feel stifling too, as if there’s no space for air. Water continually seeps through valves in the walls, sometimes sputtering in mechanical coughs, sometimes condensing in cloistered pockets. Everything is wet to the touch, metallic surfaces slick beneath your fingertips.  

What emerges is a place of utter strangeness: a built realm with no natural base, a kingdom of rivets and pipeworks, a deafening menagerie of mechanical and living creatures. The damp, the cold, the gloom, all remain unshifted in their intensity—locked in equilibrium. Time here is a standstill forging, a perpetual embryonic state where the wet metal world hums, drips, crawls, and roars, yet never truly advances. And so it remains: a secret industrial womb perched in the heights of a constructed mountain, forever suspended in the moment of its own becoming.
