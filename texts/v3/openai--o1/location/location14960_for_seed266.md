The ground here is a fathomless swath of dust and pulverized mineral, its surface cracked into labyrinthine plateaus and precipitous drops. At first, the land might be mistaken for a parched desert—yet the dust is not sand as we know it. Instead, it has a crystalline texture that shimmers in what appears to be perfectly ordinary daylight. A pale, whitish glow bounces off the high, jagged ridges, some of them thousands of meters above the rumpled valleys below. It is warm, dry, and utterly devoid of any plant—yet life is undeniably present.

When you first arrive, the silence can feel absolute. Only a low hum of distant machinery hints at deeper activity. For a long time, your ears register no footsteps across the dust—until you notice movement in the corners of your vision. Creatures slink through the canyons, shapes half-seen against the high walls. They move ponderously among the cracks and ledges, navigating the jagged terrain with an uncanny ease. Up close, their forms are strange: heavy, chitinous or stone-like plating—some appear to be arthropod-like, others spool out tendrils of shimmering filaments that radiate gentle pulses of light. It’s never clear what they eat or how they breathe, but they flourish in the dryness, scuttling along the rock as if this realm were specially crafted for them.

Traces of watery erosion are absent; the canyon walls show no sign of water-sculpted curves, no smooth gullies. Instead, the ridges and spires seem worn through the slow ablation of time itself. Small fractures have grown massive over countless ages; entire cliffs look as if peeled back like layers of paper. Earthquakes or some other internal rumblings of the planet may have played a part long ago, but whatever happened, it happened so slowly and so steadily as to leave the place feeling… inevitable.

Woven into this grand emptiness is a curious patchwork of industrial infrastructure, labyrinths of dark metal gantries and towers that cling to the edges of ragged mesas. If you climb closer, you spot vents stacked in improbable angles, thick pipes descending into the valleys far below. Periodically, they exhale invisible fumes or faint heat shimmers, releasing ghostly waves that warp the light. Ramps and cargo lifts groan quietly, built from an alloy that ages slowly yet is still weathered by the relentless dryness.

From a distance, you might imagine the structures as an abandoned city, but that’s only partly true. There are glimpses of activity—swarms of slow-moving drones, which might be pilotless or perhaps guided by unknown inhabitants behind reflective glass watchtowers. They shimmer with internal energy, lights flickering along their undersides in a measured pulse. Their sounds are soft mechanical whirs and the occasional metal clank echoing across the canyon floor. Yet the space between these machines is vast; it’s as if the builders intended the city to encompass an entire horizon, leaving huge swaths of open, empty rock between each cluster of buildings.

A subdued sunlight bathes everything in warm yellow, unbroken by clouds or any greenery that might soften it. The sky is a hazy amber, giving the daytime a strangely timeless glow. Occasionally, something dark with many wings crosses the sky, coasting on rising thermals off the canyon walls. Whether those flying forms are organic creatures or mechanical constructs is not entirely clear. They seldom land.

At the heart of the largest plateau, you find evidence of large-scale industry: massive pistons thrust in and out of the ground, possibly harvesting energy from the planet’s tectonic resonance. Endless rows of cylindrical chimneys stand silent except for the occasional rumble and hiss from below. A few spires are topped with rotating arms, slowly turning at a pace so languid you wonder if they’ve been turning for centuries. A dried, whitish dust crusts these machines, the color so consistent with the surrounding rock that the mechanisms can fade into the background when viewed from afar.

All of this is oddly calm. The hush of the place is striking—a hush broken only by the faint hiss of compressed gases, the dull clank of metal plates meeting rock, and, once in a while, the resonant calls of animals that sound more mineral than mammal, echoing across the canyons. At times, you hear what could be described as a gong, a metal note reverberating through the tunnels of stone, so muffled and slow that it barely registers as a “sound,” more a subtle pressure wave in the chest.

No water flows here. No greenery offers shade. Whatever moisture or nourishment exists is locked inside crystals or present in vapor form deep below the crust. The animals—even the largest, towering quadrupeds with segmented shells—appear to have evolved (or been engineered) to survive without conventional hydration. The city and its outposts have apparently tapped into deep resources: lines of pipes lead far below the planet’s surface, where perhaps subterranean pockets of non-aqueous fluids or geothermal reactions support both life and industry.

Time itself stretches. Nothing changes in an instant here, not even a shift in the coloring of the sky. The daily cycle is a slow drift from a golden glow to a slightly deeper bronze. When night comes, the moon—if there is one—might cast an eerie, colorless reflection on the metal scaffolds. Even then, the endless drones continue their patient tasks. You sense that the machines, the creatures, the rocky towers—everything, in fact—recognize that transformations occur over millennia here, not days or years. 

Stand on the edge of one of the taller plateaus, and you can gaze out across a landscape so enormous that the horizon feels unthinkably far. The emptiness between the industrial nodes underscores how deliberately placed they are and how little else intrudes on that design. In the silence, you notice that the planet seems to hum with a soft resonance—a testament to the slow processes shaping this environment from within. This is a place of endless dryness, uncanny life forms, and methodical machines—where time moves at a crawl, yet the presence of industry and strange fauna is constant, woven into the rock and dust in a vast, ancient dance.
