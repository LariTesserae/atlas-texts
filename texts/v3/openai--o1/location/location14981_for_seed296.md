High ridges swell into jagged shapes all around, shattered as if from eons of relentless wind and slow, methodical erosion. This region is ancient, the angles and slopes tamed over millennia yet still dramatic enough to conjure awe: steeps that rise abruptly into thin, misty air, then drop sharply into hidden depths. Major spines of mountainous rock run through the area, cracked by time, splaying out in colossal arcs. The stone surfaces are worn nearly smooth in places, but every now and then, there is a cleft or chasm that echoes with the sound of trickling water.

Despite the altitude, moisture lingers in small pockets. In fleeting moments, rain gusts in from gathering clouds, spattering on worn streets and dripping down thick vines. The sunlight—when it breaks through—arrives at a slant, not strong enough to chase away the dimness that perpetually hangs in the gullies. More often, the sky remains leaden. Silvery condensation beads along the city’s rust-stained scaffolding, collecting in narrow gullies where mosses and lichens have established a surprising niche, painting rock and metal with lush, deep greens. Within these sheltered rivulets, thin waterfalls sometimes tumble for only a few hours or days before they vanish again, leaving behind glistening trails of algae and moss.

A dense, layered urban settlement clings to the slopes—its buildings rising and falling along the grand ridges, stacked one atop another. It feels as if the city’s weight has pressed the underlying rock flat in certain spots. Over many centuries, countless blocks, walls, catwalks, and bridges have fused themselves to the ancient stone—rusted girders hammered into the rock face, narrow steel staircases weaving over lumps of cliff, and parapets shaped to conform to each unique crag. Much of the construction arches overhead, forming galleries of passageways and platforms. Buildings crowd one another, sometimes seemingly balanced on spindly supports over deep drops. You can see glimpses of tall chimneys and squat industrial halls hidden behind older facades, each exhaling a gentle plume of steam or delicate smoke that mingles with the high-altitude air.

In the gloom, dozens of electric lights string themselves in haphazard lines across open plazas or blink from high towers. Their filaments give off only a weak, yellowish glow, half-choked by the swirling mountain mists. Below them, factory-like structures remain active at all hours. Machines the size of old carriages whir low and steady, their motors thrumming beneath the hush of the wind. A few pumping stations, fed by channels from the ephemeral streams, hiss softly. Occasionally, you hear a release valve snapping open and then closing, adding short bursts of metal clatter to an otherwise quiet environment.

Despite the heavy presence of steel and scaffolding, the vegetation here remains unexpectedly vibrant—an odd contradiction in the cool, thin air. Lush ferns drape from balcony edges, trailing broad fronds toward the gloom below. Some buildings have rooftop gardens, brimming with thick, twisting creepers dotted by pale flowers. Dampness fosters tangles of root systems that slip under cracked pavement. Many of the smaller streets yield to clumps of creeping plants, forcing residents to actively cut back the vines so they don’t block entire pathways.

Local animals are rare, only occasionally glimpsed in passing. A faint silhouette might dart behind a crate, too quick to identify. Sometimes you’ll see little amphibious creatures—pale lizardlike shapes with wide webbed feet—lurking around the condensation near a dripping pipe. Or a startled winged creature flapping from an overhang of vines, quickly disappearing into the shrouded heights. Their presence is fleeting, as though the world here has demanded they remain few, hidden, and wary.

Yet for all the quiet, the place is far from stagnant. In the distance, thunderheads boil over mountain crests, sending moaning winds that gust through the valleys. Rains come and go, in half-hour bursts or lasting through entire afternoons, teasing new waterfalls from the crannies of the summits. The vegetation, likewise, pulses with slow energy, creeping further each season to blanket the structures. The city’s machinery hums day and night, grinding, cutting, and refining—there are workshops for forging metal, industrial lines packaging resources hauled up from the deeper mines. Rails perched on precarious ledges carry freight between sectors of this multi-level settlement. You can sometimes spot the small locomotives chugging along, their lights swirling through the persistent haze.

The weirdness here is subtle yet ever-present. Occasional patterns in the rock striations look eerily like script, or the vines swirl in fractal-like designs that no one has clearly deciphered. Some claim faint glowing motes slip across the ravines after dark, only to vanish if approached too closely. In certain older courtyards, monolithic, time-gnarled statues stand in permanent half-shadow, worn nearly featureless, as if they belong to a civilization lost centuries before the first tower was even built. It’s in these corners you catch a flicker of deep age—this land was primordial long before human hands draped it with girders and steel.

Over everything rolls an eerie hush, broken here and there by mechanical murmurs and the patter of water. It lends the city an air of soft tension, as though nature and industry both thrive in the same breath, each quietly shaping the other. It is vast enough to be called a region, yet intimately woven—densely packed with alleyways, bridges, rivulets, and outcroppings of stone, all layered in a collage of life and metal. Endlessly ancient yet undeniably active, this lofty, damp realm stands perched on the brink of its own history, continuing to shift and reshape itself with each passing year.
