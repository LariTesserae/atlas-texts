At first there is only brightness. A stark, almost dazzling fluorescence saturates the entire sky. You sense this dazzling wash long before you see anything else, because for a time there is no horizon line to break it. It’s just that white-yellow radiance, filling every corner. Slowly, as your vision adjusts, the shape of the world unravels in smaller pieces.

You stand on what feels like a plateau—elevated, but not a single monolithic cliff. Rather, it’s more akin to a continent-sized span of ragged highlands. There is an air of cool drifting across your skin, despite the fierce clarity of the light. The movement of wind is constant here. At times it howls, at others it roars. Its presence is so pervasive that silence is almost unimaginable. Every now and then, a series of reverberating booms rolls through like far-off thunder. You can’t tell at first whether they come from fissures in the ground, from the sky, or from some living presence. 

In such dryness, you might have expected to see dusty plains or rocky peaks—but beneath your feet, instead of stone or soil, there is living matter. Vast, stringy tangles reminiscent of roots or vines sprawl across the plateau. Pale, fibrous rods weave and press into each other, forming a tough, spongy substrate. There’s practically no visible water. Whatever nutrient or fluid these plants require must remain locked away inside them or drawn from hidden sources—mineral-laden breezes, perhaps, or some kind of gaseous nourishment that saturates the planet’s atmosphere. Thicker trunks—stitched from the same fibrous mass—rise in the distance, distorted by swirling haze. These trunks might be the beginnings of unimaginable towers of plant-growth, or perhaps giant root-pillars bridging segments of the plateau. They are weathered smooth, dull as old ivory, each surface etched by ages of storms.

Everywhere you look—if you pause long enough to focus against the relentless wind—there is motion. The plants themselves constantly flex in slow pulses, as though they are expanding and contracting in random bursts. A faint hum arises from the vegetation, reminiscent of mechanical drones. In some places, loud clacking or churning noises erupt, sounding like industrial machinery under strain. A large trunk will periodically shift, snapping fiber upon fiber in a drawn-out shriek, then settle again. It’s as though the entire environment is engaged in a continuous, centuries-old engineering project—except no one built it. It was grown.

Despite the dryness, life is abundant. Strange, leggy creatures scamper in and out of the fibrous substrate. Some are swift and gracile, long-limbed, with exoskeletons that blend seamlessly with the surrounding vegetation. Others are low-slung, broad-shelled animals that grunt and hiss with surprising volume, rummaging through pockets of decaying plant matter. A swirling flight of what might be winged pollinators passes overhead in roiling funnels, their collective thrumming so loud you can feel it in your bones. There’s an almost industrial roar to it—imagine thousands of mechanical fans spinning in unison. The entire plateau pulses with this raw, clattering life.

The temperature remains on the cool side. The wind that tears across the plateau whips away any trace of warmth. Sometimes the gusts become so strong that whole sections of fibrous ground shift and squeal. You can see glimpses of a lower level of interlaced vines beneath cracks—layer upon layer of living substrate, a labyrinth that extends downward. How far does it go?

On a broad horizon—sprawling far beyond any single vantage—you sometimes glimpse blasts of color. Long ribbons of vine-like leaves, perhaps, or spore clouds drifting in luminous arcs. When these correspond with the wind patterns, they form swirling storms or tidal flows of dust-like pollen. It’s as if weather systems are made of living flakes—glittering spores that reflect the sharp, bright light. In those moments, a deafening clamor arises—imagine rustling leaves magnified a thousandfold, combined with the hiss of sandstorms, the swirl of giant mechanical blowers, and the crash of thunder. 

If you look closer, you’ll see that the thickest pillars of plant mass are pockmarked with rows of holes, and from these holes, geysers of pale spores gush forth. Spore-laden gusts spin overhead, and in their wake you might see bizarre, twisting shapes momentarily coalesce—a swirl of glimmering cloud that almost seems to take on a monstrous silhouette before breaking apart again. Some watchers, hidden in the vines, utter piercing calls at these shapes; perhaps these calls are hunts or warnings or something else entirely.

No obvious structures, no roads or craft, no sign of conventional industry—yet the environment feels tireless, as if it is always in the throes of some colossal, ongoing production. The trunk-like plants churn, the vines flex, the fauna scuttles. The plateau’s scale is so immense it defies any easy map. Over the contorted horizon—if “horizon” is the correct term for a place of living ridges and braided superstructures—there are storms of motion, an incessant interplay of light and shadow. And always, always, that pounding, roaring, throbbing symphony of sound.

In the midst of this, there’s a strangeness—something in the way the vegetation pulses, as though possessed by a singular awareness or an engineering intention. Certain wide ridges occasionally buckle out of sync with the rest, almost as if they’re responding to your presence, or processing signals from some far-flung region of this living domain. A new wave of spore-spray might erupt, leaving behind a faint metallic tang in the gusting air. The dryness does nothing to still this world; it’s as if the usual need for water has been replaced by the hum of chemical and mineral exchange.

You can stand in one place for hours, and the landscape never settles. The stormy atmosphere of swirling spores, the roaring noise from trunk displacements, the ceaseless scuttling of creatures—none of it calms or diminishes. This is a place of restless life, forging and reforging itself in real time. It feels ancient yet feverishly active, all at once. And though there is no single blueprint for this environment—no city plan, no deliberate architecture—some hidden logic holds everything together. It is an industrial complex without factories, a planet-scale mechanism built not of steel and screws but of fibrous growth and tempestuous wind.

You can almost smell the planet roaring its own heartbeat, vibrating in your chest. In each swirl of bright spore clouds, you see its age and vitality. All is a tapestry of living matter, weathered and self-propagating, covered by a perpetual wind. High up here, in the dryness of a world with no true “sea” and no single, stable ground, life still flourishes in ways both bizarre and grand. The environment’s brilliance sears the eyes, the cacophony pounds at your ears, and the constant motion makes you doubt the solidity of your footing. Yet it endures, colossal and alive, in an endless, dynamic surge of creation.
