High in a realm of thin, dry air where clouds rarely form—this is a plateau seemingly carved from ages upon ages of planetary shifts. The temperature is brisk to the skin, as if the sun (a weak and distant star) can’t quite break through the atmosphere’s haze. It is a place of paradox: though water is nearly nonexistent here, vegetation reigns supreme.

These plants do not rely on water in the sense we know. Their stalks glisten in a subdued, crystalline way, suggesting they pull nourishment from mineral veins deep beneath the ground. They bristle and shift, grown in layered tangles of fibrous substrates that web across bare stone. Living architecture abounds: entire “walls” of woven plants form natural ramparts at the plateau’s edges. Along ridges and gullies, thick mats of moss-like life form broad, whorled designs that might seem fractal in shape, as though mathematics itself weaves through every leaf.

Across this plateau, the ground has been worn down to its ancient essence. Cliffs are smoothed to gentle curves from eons of subtle, near-lifeless erosion, giving the entire region an air of age and finality. All that remains is wind-sculpted bedrock and the unstoppable tide of these alien plants that do not thirst for water. Despite the abundance of foliage, there is a feeling of space, of breath between each structure. It’s as though the vegetation prefers to cluster in clumps and arcs, leaving large swaths of emptiness under the dim, cool skies.

Here, too, is an urban presence—though “urban” feels like a faint descriptor for the monumental, near-spiritual constructs scattered across the region. Buildings, or perhaps shells of hyper-advanced technology, emerge straight from the braided vegetation. Sheets of living bark and swirling, bio-crystal paneling swirl upward, organically shaped yet precise. Towers might stretch dozens of meters tall and arc in improbable curves that standard physics would protest, but the technology in play is well beyond modern. Strange humming energies run beneath the bark, drawing from the planet’s core perhaps. Sometimes, with a groan or shudder, entire sections shift or realign, as if in constant re-tuning to an invisible code.

Though the light is dim, an ambient glow crawls along the vine-like networks, pooling in nodes of pale radiance. At night—or whatever passes for night in this realm—these nodes illuminate pathways that spiderweb across the plateau, revealing how extensive the settlement truly is. Yet no single vantage point offers a clear overview; the city merges with the living ground, with vantage points teased in glimpses.

The fauna is surprisingly abundant, though rarely glimpsed at eye level for long. You hear them first. There is tremendous noise echoing through the gullies and corridors: whirring, chirping, resonance that swells and contracts in complicated rhythms. Some creatures are carried by spore-clouds, flitting from one fibrous tower to another. Others scuttle across the plant-ridges, made of chitinous plates that blend with the bark. Now and again, a large, quadruped form—tall as a small tree—ambles through, its steps accompanied by a thunderous squeal. Each beast has adapted to the dryness and to the strange metabolic processes of the plants, possibly extracting nutrients from crystals or capturing intangible energies from the atmosphere.

Nothing here is entirely quiet. The wind has a loud undercurrent, as if you stand atop a cosmic vent. It roars and then settles into long, sonorous howls, ricocheting off the smooth canyon walls. The vegetation seems to join in, rustling in a kind of collective hiss that arises from millions of tiny stiff leaves. When one of the beyond-modern devices activates—be it a gate or a tower or something deeper underground—a cascade of sonic pulses sweeps outward, rattling the landscape like a chorus of low-throated horns. In response, certain beasts bellow back, and the living plant-walls quiver. There is an endless interplay of echoes, calls, and signals, resulting in a constant symphony of life and machine.

Over the epochs, the plateau has settled into a final, timeless shape. The rock, sculpted by an ancient dryness, stands as an enduring skeleton for this extraordinary ecosystem to climb upon. And the ecosystem itself seems locked in cyclical processes: spore-dispersal storms swirl across canyons, new vines coil into place, and animals (some winged, some creeping) harvest the fruits—glowing pods and gem-like bulbs—that appear unpredictably along the towering stems. It’s a living place, perpetually in flux, even as it feels ancient enough to have existed since before recorded memory.

This is a landscape of proud emptiness contrasted with impossible abundance—vast spans of wind-gnawed rock hosting a total dominance of bizarre, waterless flora. The air is always cool, the light subdued, the seemingly boundless plateau ringed by precipitous drops into unseen depths. Tucked amidst the layered vegetation are advanced constructs—city-like, but grown rather than built, humming with energies that push at the limits of known physics. At regular intervals, the deep pulses of otherworldly machinery remind both beings and plant life that an order, or at least a presence, underlies this place.

If you stand where one of the living walls meets open sky, the vantage reveals a horizon that arcs away in high, flat plains. The gnashing wind is loud in your ears, and in the distance, you see shapes moving. Steady herds, odd drifting masses of spores, maybe giant translucent creatures supported by unknown forces. The plateau is a place never fully silent or still. You can’t help but feel the tug of time’s immensity as you witness an ecosystem that has adapted in ways entirely unknown. A realm grown from dryness, yet so thoroughly alive, it overflows in spectacle—loud, active, and forever strange.
