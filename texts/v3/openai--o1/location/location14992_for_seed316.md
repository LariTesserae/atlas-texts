Across an expanse so immense it defies easy comprehension, the ground lies worn to a nearly featureless plain—ancient and settled under the press of long eons. Broad sheets of shallow water pool across this land, meeting in marshy basins and trickling through fissures in the stone. The terrain has been shaved down by time, battered by countless ages of storms, until only a subdued undulation remains. In the distance, the horizon mutates in a haze that wavers with the planet’s own weather systems and perhaps something stranger, as if spacetime itself hiccups on occasion.

Although the land is low, it feels inexhaustibly wide. There is no single spot that dominates the view; everything is equally flattened and stretched. The wetness of the place clings to every surface, creeping up rocks, forming rivulets in hairline cracks, gathering in warm, humid film on any structure that still stands. The atmosphere is thick, almost tactile, hinting at a warm temperature that never fully subsides.

Vegetation exists—but lightly, as if nature hasn’t wholly committed to rooting here. Pale reeds cluster in pockets of water-logged ground, trembling in the near-constant wind. Curls of colorless moss cling to water-smoothed boulders, which are themselves half-sunken into the black mud. You could wander for miles without encountering anything else that grows—yet from time to time, a single twisted trunk, shriveled and ancient, breaks the horizon like a sentinel.

Then there is the sound. It is a roar that never relents. Heavy gusts hurtle across the plain, droning at all pitches, sometimes bursting into shrieking crescendos that shutter your eardrums. The water contributes a gargling, slapping undercurrent—waves lurch and slap against half-submerged ridges. Farther out, thunderheads tower and rumble, rolling flashes of light that strobe menacingly against a dim sky. Though the light is weak, the storms are furious, and each thunderclap becomes an unending roar echoed by what feels like the planet’s own deep voice far below the crust.

In the margins of this turbulent stage are scattered leftovers of unknown origin. Low stone walls, battered spires, isolated chunks of worked rock—each half-foundation or toppled pillar stands alone, battered by wind and water. No cohesive settlements remain, nothing you could call a city or even a village. Some structures jut from the standing water at canted angles, scummed over with algae, as though the land has swallowed most of whatever once existed here. Clambering over them reveals no machinery, no recognizable technology—just the faint presence of shaped stone in an environment otherwise claimed fully by the wild tumult.

All around, the sense of reality is stretched thin. The air ripples in ways you can’t always attribute to heat. Sometimes the towering storm clouds above appear to coil downward, twisting into impossible shapes before dispersing again in some cosmic glitch. The wind carries sounds that might be voices—low, groaning murmurs or high-pitched hissings—though it could just be the turbulence crashing into the stones. Strange illusions flicker at the edge of your vision: watery reflections that show vantage points you haven’t stood in, or shadows that race across the ground even when the sun is completely hidden behind roiling clouds. There’s an unsettling sense that time and perspective might diverge at any moment.

Though life is scarce, it isn’t absent. Flashes of movement sometimes skim across the water—a sleek shape gliding just under the surface before vanishing into the gloom. Occasionally, overhead, silhouettes dart through vaporous cloud banks, wingbeats lost in the monstrous howling all around. You may catch a glimpse of glistening chitin or leathery hide in a momentary break of stormlight. But these sightings are few and fleeting, like hints that something else is present, only half-tethered to the physical plane.

It never truly quiets here; everything churns and grinds incessantly. The land’s final, ancient shape—smooth as a worn seabed—looks paradoxically both stable and constantly re-dreamed. The sky tears itself apart in rolling battles of thunder, jets of lightning sometimes flickering sideways before doubling back upon themselves. The shallow-water expanse hisses, steams, and boils under sudden onslaughts of warm deluge, as if the humidity builds until it can condense in a single violent burst. And through it all, the half-buried ruins remain, patient reminders that something once stood here—yet the place seems determined to reclaim every single remnant of an orderly past.

Even standing motionless, you feel buffeted by everything that’s happening: the thick air, the oppressive noise, the intangible sense that each moment might shatter into new forms. There is no lull, no true stillness in this vast domain. It’s a world-sized threshold where water, wind, and reality itself converge into a roaring, boundless upheaval—dense with presence, yet strangely bereft of easy explanation, as if the land itself is caught mid-transformation, forever.
