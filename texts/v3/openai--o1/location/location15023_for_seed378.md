Heat haze shimmers over a broad highland plateau, where each breath of air is laden with moisture that never quite condenses into a true fog but leaves every surface slick to the touch. Beads of water cling to the fractured rock faces; the stones themselves appear to weep from hidden channels deep within their cores. Though adjacent to the seepages, one might expect a flush of green, there are no true plants here—only a diffuse film of pale, bluish fungus clinging in occasional patches where the rock is dampest. The fungi seem half-mineral themselves, forming thin sheets with a translucent sheen, and in the brimming sunlight they reflect a soft glow back into the scorched air.

The sun looms bright and merciless above, brutally hot. The land is high enough that gigantic cumulus clouds sometimes flicker in from beyond the horizon, but they rarely bring relief; if they choose to burst, their storms are momentary, more like a soft hiss of drizzle than true rainfall. Even so, each hidden spring trickling through the plateau’s flanks ensures mud-slick gullies and shallow water-filled hollows appear here and there, scoured by gentle yet persistent flows that have shaped the terrain over untold generations.

Atop the plateau rises a rambling settlement of industrial origin. Its architecture is sparse, the buildings set far apart to dissipate heat and built upon broad, elevated foundations to avoid partial flooding whenever the groundwater surges. Crude metal catwalks connect one building to another, bridging over rivulets and mineral-stained channels. Stout chimneys of corrugated steel bristle with piping and low hums of activity—but strangely, the machinery they power makes only occasional clicks and muted whirs, as if muffled by the thick, wet air. One can tell by the arrangement of overhead cables and the orderly ranks of cylindrical storage vats that manufacturing or large-scale processing once flourished here. Now it seems reduced or somehow quieted, an ebb in an ongoing cycle rather than outright abandonment.

Despite the silence of human endeavor, the settlement is alive with creatures adapted to the pervasive heat and lingering dampness. Small scaled quadrupeds emerge from hidden burrows in the rock; they have wide, flat feet to pad across slick surfaces with minimal slippage. Scurrying across catwalk beams, creatures with leathery wings harvest the faint fungi from cracks in the walls—or perhaps they drink ephemeral rivulets that appear after the midday scald. Many of these animals bear luminous markings: subtle bands of phosphorescence that pulse in the gloom when dusk falls, which helps them locate mates or navigate the labyrinthine scaffolding.

Larger predators stalk around the edges of the settlement, attracted to the water collecting in runoff basins. Sleek, reptilian forms watch from behind stone outcrops or from beneath abandoned steel platforms, waiting to pounce on unwary smaller fauna that gather in watery gullies. Their presence hints at a complex food chain—one that does not rely on the usual flora but on the ephemeral fungal mats and the minerals that leach from the weeping cliffs.  

Strangest of all, beyond the racks of industry, are wide, circular vents set into the stone, some surrounded by faintly glowing runic etchings—remnants of an earlier, perhaps more mystic engineering. At intervals, they exhale blasts of humid, superheated wind, sending swirling vortices of steam into the bright sky. These vents feed into a labyrinth of subterranean channels that maintain the plateau’s surprising moisture, as though an entire island of water flows far beneath.  

Aside from a gentle hiss from the vents, the odd drip of water, and the occasional rasping cry of the winged creatures, a hush pervades the plateau. Footsteps on metal land like distant echoes. Each building, with its rust-streaked surfaces and dull paint, seems to regard the overshadowing emptiness stoically, resting within this vast open realm as if waiting for a next era of expansion or a final reclamation by the creeping fungal veneer.  

Here, time flows slowly but not without process: lightning storms flicker in the distance on hot nights, sending flickering reflections across the shallow effusions of water. Creatures wander forth at dawn for precious moisture, then retreat during the white-hot apex of day. Nothing sprouts from the ground the way we expect on green worlds, yet life abounds in cunning forms, coexisting under a merciless sun. The half-industrial skeleton of the settlement endures, battered by eons of erosion and shaped by the steady seep of mineral-rich water, forging its own odd equilibrium that is silent, brilliant, and always a little otherworldly.
