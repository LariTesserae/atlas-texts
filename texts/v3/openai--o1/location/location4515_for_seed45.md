Vast formations thrust upward in jagged silhouettes against a sky starved of light. By some strange alchemy, this place is both engulfed in darkness and suffused with a heavy, simmering warmth. There is no sign of water anywhere—no ponds, no dampness, no slender veins of moisture in the rock. Instead, the ground cracks underfoot, parched plates of mineral that seem newly birthed from the planet’s crust. Here, everything is bone-dry, as though the last drop of liquid evaporated ages ago or never existed at all.

Although the terrain rears up to impossible heights, its shape is in flux, splintering and shifting as if still forming. Spires of crystal and basalt appear half-finished—some teeter precariously atop the dark foundation, others arch overhead like embryonic bridges. Far above, swirling clouds of mineral dust and shimmering particles zip past, roused by unseen forces. You can sense constant motion underneath the stillness, an undercurrent of chaotic energy that makes the ground gently quake from time to time. All is raw here: the throbbing geology has yet to settle on a final arrangement.

In this near-darkness, only the faintest gleams reflect across the surfaces—tiny flickers from luminous veins of rock, faint pulses in stray crystal growths or swirling sparks mingling with drifting dust. Even these glints feel tentative, like they are being tested or discovered for the first time, an embryonic illumination. The warm air itself appears thick with unspoken potential, stirring up swirling eddies of hot wind whenever fresh rock burgeons from beneath the crust.

No plants break the barren rock; no lichen, no moss, nothing soft with color or life. Equally absent are animals or figures of motion, leaving a hush hanging over the land. Yet it is not silent. Deep in the canyons and pockets between towering cliffs, there’s a low murmur—echoes of unsettled stone, the gentle hiss of mineral dust in the swirling updrafts, as if the world itself breathes sporadically. Warm updrafts move from chasm to chasm with a pensive sigh, carrying flecks of glowing debris that vanish as they rise under the dim gloom.

Broken, half-built structures outline the horizon, though it is unclear whether they are natural forms shaped by the planet’s youth or the earliest traces of some other process. They stand scattered at different elevations: some perched high on outcroppings, others forming arches near the base of massive stone pillars. Fragmented pathways or intervals of symmetrical angles hint at a once-organized pattern—but time, or the chaotic birth throes of the land, has warped them into partial relics, their purpose long drowned in the swirling darkness. No tools or technology appear anywhere in sight: the rock alone sculpts itself here, guided by the bare bones of primeval forces.

At the edges of your awareness, the land reveals itself to be both infant and ancient. It is unsettled, trembling with the raw impulses of creation. Spires crumble only to re-form moments later in new shapes, and the flow of the dust-laden wind draws shifting patterns through the gloom. Occasional bursts of heat bubble up from beneath fractures, as if molten minerals are testing their boundaries. Everything is in a state of hyperactive forging—yet stillness lingers in the spaces between.

No water washes over these surfaces, no living creature roams through them, and no bright sun pierces the darkness. Yet the whole domain is alive with possibility—the hush of a place on the cusp, its geography in mid-gesture, vibrant in the act of becoming. The dryness, the newness, the warmth, and the whisper of collapsing and reshaping stone define this landscape—a churning, embryonic world of spiked peaks and half-known structures, holding out its jagged hands in the quiet dark, waiting for whatever comes next.
