In this place, the air carries a faint mineral tang, as if the surrounding earth and stone are still raw and freshly formed—except there is no visible water anywhere to temper or smooth those minerals. The environment is dry, nearly devoid of any fluid humidity, yet it’s cool to the touch. You feel it on your skin immediately upon stepping into these subterranean expanses. The space stretches beyond what you can see, receding into shadowy corridors and improbable caverns. It’s simultaneously a vast and bounded world: you know you’re beneath the surface, but looking around, you can’t always tell where the rock ends and the vegetation begins.

The plants here—if “plants” is even the right term—crowd every surface, every gap, every jagged ledge. They twist gracefully upward through vents in the ceiling rock, forming living pillars that support the entire subterrestrial environment. These growths have no apparent need for water as we know it. Their stems are fibrous, almost metallic, with a faint shimmer under the steady, cool light. Their leaves (or maybe fronds) fan out into broad, translucent membranes, capturing the subtle, phosphorescent glow from the stone overhead. Some of these membranes blink with internal pulses of energy, as if quietly photosynthesizing in a realm of minimal light. The effect is mesmerizing—covering every visible surface in a layer of pulsating green-gold life.

Despite the abundant vegetation, the space feels uncrowded, even sparse; these growths leave wide open areas between their clusters, forming avenues and plazas. Within these corridors, you occasionally glimpse large mechanical constructs—industry made of metals and odd alloys. These machines are anchored into the living pillars, weaving in and out of the fibrous plant structures. It seems the architecture thrives symbiotically with mechanical scaffolding, forming a massive industrial settlement built from both living matter and manufactured steel. There are entire rows of scaffolding that function as catwalks or thoroughfares, busy with quiet, half-hidden life forms.

On some levels, huge furnace-like devices hum softly, suggesting an industrial purpose somewhere in the gloom. Every now and then, you catch the sharp clang of metal upon metal, echoing once and then swallowed by the silence. The air is still—mostly quiet—yet alive with small hints of motion: a rustle in the brush, a faint hiss from conduits buried in the plant stems, a distant grinding from the raw geology as it is slowly carved or grown. Everything is under constant transformation, as if the city is still “becoming” itself. Mechanical pumps push oxygen (or some analogous gas) through biotic root networks, powering both the metallic infrastructure and the plants’ continued expansion.

Though dryness seems to dominate, abundant fauna roams in the spaces between the vines and the towering pillars. Some creatures have spindly limbs and semitranslucent skin, adapted to the limited light. You can see little eyes blinking from crevices, or catch the flicker of a tail vanishing behind a cluster of fibrous stems. From time to time, you spot predatory shapes slipping through the shadows, as their silhouettes ripple with the rhythmic glow of luminescent leaves. Even though they keep mostly out of sight, there’s enough movement that you can tell the ecosystem is healthy, teeming with specialized life forms you’d never see in a surface world with conventional water and soil.

And it’s so strange. There are small, rust-colored pods suspended from overhead vines, steaming slightly in the cool air; a fleeting hint of pungent spice accompanies their venting. Glowing spores loft in swirling patterns, drifting in slow, circular arcs as if guided by unseen currents. Perhaps these spores feed some of the odd habitats or help new vegetation anchor in the rocky substrate. Large cogs or turbines built into plant trunks rotate lazily, feeding or storing energy from the friction of the shifting geology. No one seems perturbed by the coalescence of the mechanical and organic—it’s all part of a singular ecosystem, forging a new type of subterranean city. Each element, from the humming generators to the neon-laced vines, appears to play a role in the delicate web of life and industry here.

The settlement itself spans multiple caverns and hollows, each with different strata of plant-cities. The upper levels, closer to the surface, are lined with thick, hearty trunks that anchor everything below. Moving down deeper, you find newly formed growths—tender, embryonic fronds sprouting from pure, crystalline rock. Some of these slender plants are clearly still settling in, weaving themselves into carefully placed mechanical frameworks. Construction is constant: you see scaffolding being rearranged; new segments of tunnel are reinforced; glowing vines are spliced into metallic piping. This place is simultaneously a construction zone and a living forest—an ambitious fusion of life, slow geology, and bustling machinery.

Rules of the world here are unlike a typical surface ecosystem. Fluids, if any, move in capillary systems you can barely see, possibly carrying exotic chemicals that nourish the plants. Temperatures remain cool, stabilized by rock insulation and the unknown processes of the industrial–biological infrastructure. Strange crystals recur in pockets along the cavern walls, faintly buzzing with stored energy. Some holes in the stone exhale breezes that carry whiffs of machine oil. Others inhale, drawing air and spores deeper into the formation. Perhaps there’s an enormous breath forever cycling in these underground expanses.

Yet, for all the bustling and the distant churning of gears, the overall mood remains hushed. Footsteps sound loud as you tread across fibrous walkways or ring with metallic clicks on the industrial catwalks. In the open plazas, the throbbing vegetative growth invites contemplation; you could stand and watch the leaves’ soft, rhythmic luminescence for ages. Flutters of motion catch the eye: a small creature scurrying up a pipe, something else rustling deeper in the undergrowth. Occasionally, you will hear an echoing hiss or faint squeak as predator meets prey—life continuing in the quiet, unending cycles of this place.

Strangeness hangs in the atmosphere, yet it is also purposeful. Everything here belongs, is used, or is being developed. Vegetation forms the walls, the architecture, the roads; the mechanical supports harness energy, provide structure, and refine the raw resources. Together, they sustain a realm that feels perpetual, self-contained, caught in an evolving cycle of growth and industry. And you, standing among the softly pulsing vines and quietly churning machines, sense that this city is no mere coincidence, but a precarious harmony of dryness, technology, and living light—an entire subterranean civilization, quietly but actively becoming all it can be.
