It comes on you gradually: a place at the upper edges of some world, not a wide expanse, but a steeply peaked basin that feels both intimate and towering at once. The air is cool, pressed thin by elevation so high that the clouds snag and cling to the sharp, freshly thrust pinnacles. Those rock walls look newly born from the planet’s crust, their ridges crisp and unsoftened by time. Fissures and cracks still show raw edges, dark with lingering damp, as if the stone itself sweats from stone-deep pressure.

No sunlight pierces here for more than brief moments. Most hours, a dim hush falls, though the hush is broken by an undercurrent of bustling life hidden in pockets of shadow. The moisture that does exist comes in brief sighs: thin rivulets trickle along slick walls, a mist congeals in dark hollows, and occasional droplets slide across lichen-crusted overhangs. The surrounding ground is wet to the touch, muddy in places—just enough to feed the small outcrops of colorless grasses and pale ferns that cling to the edges of stones, or to the bulges in the walls.

But for all the meager flora, the place is seething with animal life. You notice movement everywhere. Many of the creatures are small—too many species to name or even fully distinguish, scuttling in every gap or recess, pressed so close together that they nearly crawl across one another. A faint chittering and clacking resonates at all times, occasionally rising to a communal outcry, as if they sense or feed off the same pulses in the earth. In the submerged corners you see slick bodies slip under water pockets, sending ripples outward in the faint glow of unknown, luminescent algae.

Every noise sounds magnified in this confined cathedral of stone. There is a continuous hum of wingbeats—thick-bodied insects droning low, or smaller creatures with soft fur and membranous flaps gliding from ledge to ledge. The environment is layered with echoes; pounding footsteps of heavier animals, their forms mostly hidden behind jagged columns, come and go like a slow heartbeat. Amid these living clamorings, you notice that nothing here appears shaped by human hands: no laid stones, no carved wood, no sign of metal workings. Perhaps, somewhere in the dark, there’s a tribe huddling behind boulders using only sharpened bones or chipped flints, but no real sign betrays their presence.

Despite the seemingly chaotic press of life, the transformations in the landscape itself are slow and methodical. Water seeps in and sculpts the rock drop by drop. The ferns and mosses expand at an unhurried pace, almost creeping as the eras pass. There are no sudden storms, no violent upheavals—just a quiet, ongoing rearrangement as rifts shift fractionally and new cracks open in the brittle spires.

Here, in this narrow and towering place, something utterly strange has taken root. Though the vegetation is sparse, though the light is an almost perpetual twilight, the teeming creatures have an uncanny intensity: eyes that reflect faint grey glints, limbs that contort just a degree too far, spines that spiral in patterns that feel vaguely alien. Some have calls that rattle the ears, reverberating off walls adorned with ropey fungal threads that flicker with ghostly phosphorescence. And so, in the darkness, a sense of immensity confronts you—an intense density of life and an eerie hush that isn’t truly silent. You stand at the threshold, dwarfed by the looming fresh-cut mountains, surrounded by a near-overflowing animal abundance, wondering what deeper mysteries dwell in the next echoing alcove of this high, damp, and primitive domain.
