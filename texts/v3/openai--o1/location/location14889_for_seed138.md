A hot dampness saturates the air, making every breath feel just a little heavier than it ought to. The horizon stays low—there aren’t imposing mountain ranges or high plateaus here—just broad stretches of flatland broken by wetlands and shallow pools. Despite the water’s presence, green life is surprisingly light on the ground. In places, one can see thick spongy moss clinging to the bases of rocks or the corners of large cement barriers, and there are stunted reeds clustered at the water’s edge, but they don’t dominate the landscape. Occasional shriveled shrubs poke through the mud, roots finding tenuous purchase in the newly formed soil. The vegetation feels transitional, as if it only just began to take hold.

And yet, in the midst of this humid, soft terrain stands a tightly knit cluster of industrial structures. They loom in the near distance, not tall like skyscrapers, but busy and well-built: corrugated metal siding glowing dull in the dim light, scaffolding and catwalks bridging narrow alleyways, large fans spinning slowly in the thick air. Smoke stacks—shed of bright paint save for rust streaks near the top—belch faint plumes that blend with the haze overhead. The gloom never lifts. It’s a perpetual twilight, heavy with clouds or an ever-present smog that filters out the brightness, leaving everything cast in half-shadows.

Though it’s a relatively small settlement in overall footprint—more like a cramped industrial district than a sprawling metropolis—it feels impressively concentrated. Pipes run in lines across the ground and overhead, insulated cables loop from one building to another, and the enclosing presence of machinery hums with purpose. Great mechanical pumps draw water up from somewhere beneath the rollers of muddy soil, feeding it into cisterns and vats. Even from far off, one can hear the churn of engines that never cease, their rhythmic clank and hiss making the place a cacophony of moving metal.

The ground feels fresh-formed, as though this land was only recently uncovered or artificially built. Anywhere the earth has been cut to make a ditch or a footing, you see clean edges and sharp angles that haven’t had time to soften under weathering. The edges of concrete foundations show few cracks or signs of wear. Over by the water’s edge, portions of the ground appear newly dredged, as if the engineers keep adjusting the terrain to manage the wetlands. And yet, there is an air of timelessness—never changing, as if the thick humidity and the constant drumming of machinery have locked the moment in place. The same haze, the same steady throbbing of engines, the same standing pools of water remain day after day, as if all is part of a carefully maintained equilibrium.

Amid these factories, one can sometimes glimpse shapes flitting between the pipes and towers—the local fauna, which is surprisingly plentiful. Insects swirl in thick clouds near any light source, their wings catching the occasional flash from the halogen lamps perched on the highest scaffolds. Birds with slick, dark plumage roost in half-hidden niches among beams and vents, occasionally swooping low to pick off a wayward creature from the pools below. Amphibious reptiles glide through the wetlands: broad-backed salamanders or long-bodied lizards with gills flattened against their sides, at ease in both muddy water and the machines’ underbelly. The warm climate suits them. Even from a distance, their mating calls or occasional splashes join the factory clamor.

Everywhere, there is noise: a constant mechanical rumble, punctuated by shrill whistles from pressurized valves, the clang of metal doors, the bubbling of water in channels, and the cry of some watery creature off in the reeds. It can feel overwhelming, but there’s a sense of balance to it; the sound never crescendos or falls silent. It has found its own stable pitch and volume, echoing across the lowland. A faint slosh of water seeps under many of the walkways, and you might catch the drip-drip sound from overhead pipes or drainage spouts.

The day never truly brightens and never truly darkens. The sky remains in a permanent murk, making shadows diffuse. It keeps the heat smothering but never scorching. Nothing here rushes forward in time; it feels like the settlement has always been in this state of half-lit industry, operating with measured steadiness. The machines keep running, the creatures adapt, the water seeps and pools, and the world around remains unimpressed by time. It is an equilibrium of steam and shadow—small in extent yet heavy with presence, a humid urban labyrinth in the middle of quiet, murky marshes.
