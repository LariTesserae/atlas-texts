From a distance, it appears as a colossal tiered plateau rising from the haze—an immense structure perched on high, with edges that vanish into a bright sky. At such extreme elevation, one might expect brutal cold or thin air, but the atmosphere here is warm, gently so, as if wrapped in a slow-moving thermal current. There’s a stiff breeze at times, rustling leaves and rattling the engine housings that dot the landscape, but the air has a subdued gentleness—like an ancient caretaker drifting from level to level.

Nearly everything in sight has been constructed. There is no naked rock or untouched soil—beneath your feet lies an engineered ground of interlocking plates, thick, stony segments mottled with the mud-green patina of time. Above and wrapped around these surfaces are myriad tiers of carefully cultivated greenery, so dense and overflowing that, at a glance, it looks like nature has reclaimed the city. Yet closer inspection reveals every trunk, every vine, every bloom to be planted with precision. Terraced planters, hanging canopies, and labyrinthine twisting walkways support the roots of colossal tropical trees. Trailing ferns and creepers cling to the edges of stone, drawing moisture from pockets of condensation that gather along ridges and corners. Even the smaller shrubs have discreet water tubes feeding them—a hidden network of irrigation that nudges droplets onto leaves and petals, a perpetual cycle of carefully maintained dampness.

In the air, a persistent industrial hum resonates, punctuated by the rhythmic clang of far-off machinery. Engine stacks protrude from workshop roofs and drop exhaust in curling plumes. There’s a convergence of mechanical activity and flourishing life in every direction, as if the whirring machines and the thick, waxy foliage have found an odd equilibrium. Occasionally, a series of massive vent hatches clank open, releasing clouds of warm, moisture-laden steam that envelop entire stairways and ramps before drifting away. The light here is bright, seeming to come not merely from the sun overhead but also from an array of powerful lamps and luminous rods embedded into the substrate. Each major terrace has its own glow, so that no corner remains in shadow for long.

Fauna abounds, vibrant and varied. Creatures scuttle across pipes and under the leaves of crowded planters. Flocks of small, mechanical-boned birds circle overhead before teal-tinted wings shimmer and they dart into an array of air ducts. Perhaps they’re living, perhaps not—on closer look, some have glimmering feathers of actual plumage, others are slender and metallic. Sinuous amphibian-like beings dwell in clusters of artificial freshwater pools. Now and then, you catch glimpses of large creatures prowling across distant terraces—four-legged shapes, some with thick fur, others with exposed mechanical joints, equally at home among steel husks and greenhouse glades. Life, in all its forms here, is layered and intermingled, orchestrated by engineers and nurtured by the environment’s subtle wetness and warmth.

The entire plateau complex seems ancient yet stable—like it has stood for centuries, battered by intermittent rains, buffeted by bright sunshine, but never quite losing its sense of deliberate design. Each beam and column has a worn, softened edge, smoothed by long cycles of weather. Despite the constant hum, the place changes slowly, almost imperceptibly: pipes are replaced or new planters built over decades, so that old sections melt into new expansions without any jarring shift. The slow pace gives the impression of inevitability; this environment doesn’t hurry. It breathes and grows at its own patient rhythm.

And through it all, a subtle mystery persists. Though the place doesn’t reek of the bizarre, you sense slight disturbances, a gentle “otherness.” Everyday shapes may have unusual angles; a walkway might turn out to be a living limb of some half-grown, half-forged entity. It’s less a place that openly shocks you than one that makes you pause, noticing a valve that appears to be built of living root or a cluster of leaves that reflect the glow of machinery too precisely. There’s a hush beneath the clangor—an underlying unity that merges the harsh lines of industry with the unbounded fecundity of lush vegetation.

Beyond this plateau, the environment opens to more terraces and connecting causeways, each sprawling outward. Far in the distance, you can spot silhouettes of similar pillars bridging across chasms of bright sky. It’s a vast, constructed domain whose heights and hollows continue well past the horizon, an open realm shaped by industrious hands but softened by time and relentless growth. You can sense that people live and work here: scattered workshop enclaves, enclaves brimming with mechanical parts, aquaponic gardens humming with insect life. And though it never ceases to clank and hum, you get the feeling that everything here operates on a grand, slow-moving cycle, with broad arcs of existence measured in epochs rather than moments. The plateau stands tall over it all, bright in the sun, half city, half superstructure, crowned by a persistent tapestry of greenery and the voices of countless creatures.
