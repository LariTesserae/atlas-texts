At first glance, there seems an impossible contradiction here: the place sits at a high, cool altitude with no water in sight, yet the vegetation is somehow lush and thriving. There is no hint of rain or pooling lakes—nothing so much as a trickle—but still, green life radiates from every crack and ledge in the stone. Perhaps what looks like flora is an entirely different sort of organism, grown from anchored minerals and crystalline deposits rather than sugars and chlorophyll. Yet “lush” is the only word that feels right—everything grows here as if the mountain itself were feeding it.

This plateau is not large—barely the size of a small valley or a modest courtyard if it were fenced in—but it feels vast, because it is so empty. Each plant stands alone as though it needs a wide berth to breathe, leaving stretches of bare, porous ground between one tuft of growth and the next. The terrain is composed of striated stone that’s been weathered over innumerable centuries. You can see layers and banding, each a different shade of soft silver or charcoal. It is a place that has found its balance over countless eons. Time-worn crevices are smoothed or sharpened here and there, but the mountain’s foundation has long been set in its ways, neither crumbling nor shifting in any hurry.

The sky is that perfect mountain-blue—thin, crisp air that bites at your lungs when you inhale too quickly. Despite the dryness and the altitude, the temperature is not hostile. It’s cool, but it doesn’t burn the skin or numb the fingers; it’s the gentler kind of chill that reminds you you’re high above the restless plains. Even from the edge of the plateau, it drops off into a vast nothingness, spanned by swirling curtains of dust-laden wind. Those droning winds are only the beginning of the noise, for the sounds here are deafening.

It’s unclear at first where the roar originates—until you notice that the flora is alive with more than mere botanical presence. A profusion of creatures scurries across the rocky surfaces and within the plants’ coils. They vary in form: some are reminiscent of standard insects—glinting metallic shells and segmented legs—while others are larger, reminiscent of small reptiles or birds, yet none look exactly familiar. They seem to subsist either on the mineral-laced vegetation or upon each other. And they are loud. Chittering, shrieking, drumming, clattering—there are so many overlapping calls that each rock face seems to ring with it. Below it all, a deep subsonic hum rises from the base of the plateau. It’s as if the mountain has its own pulse, thrumming up through your feet when you stand still.

Between patches of rocky ground and thick tangles of strange, shimmering fronds, you catch sight of scattered constructs. They might be ruins, or perhaps outposts of technology beyond anything recognizable as human. Some appear to be slender towers of black alloy that reflect the normal daylight with a subtle rainbow sheen, while others are no more than collapsed arches of unknown composition. The few structures still intact seem to be engaged in inscrutable energies—flickers of light arc between them at intervals, or shimmering pulses drift out across the plateau and disperse into the air like invisible waves. The fauna nearby flock to these arcs and pulses, feeding on them as though they were fruit. Occasionally, one of the structures emits a powerful crack or boom, sending shockwaves of distortion through the atmosphere—deep chords that echo for miles and add to the deafening tapestry of sound.

Even more unsettling is how everything seems in constant motion. The vegetation sways and pulses, not in gentle breezes, but in orchestrated convulsions. It’s almost as though each piece of growth has a will of its own, quivering in time with the arcs of light from the technology. The ground occasionally quakes, but only subtly, as if large creatures are tunneling below, or some cosmic engine inside the mountain is cycling through its phases. In the distance, you can see momentary bursts of energy—a swirl of dust or debris that suggests miniature storms that flare up and then vanish. The atmosphere is forever churning, never quite at rest.

Initially, the dryness invites comparison to deserts, but there is no sense of withering under a scorching sun. There’s no sun’s sizzle here—only a cool dryness, as if all moisture has been replaced by faint, vital currents of energy that keep these life-forms alive. You get the impression that rainfall has never touched this place. Instead, perhaps the swirling ionic storms overhead or pulses from the black alloy towers nourish life via means as yet unknown to physics textbooks.

The rules of the world here seem different—time, matter, and biology forging an uneasy union on the heights. Everything is at once ancient and newly born, stable yet in feverish flux. You might watch a saddle of rock rearrange its shape over the course of an hour, slowly warping under some internal force. You might see clusters of those metallic creatures busily piecing together some new outgrowth of the towers, only to abandon it days later, leaving it to fade or dissolve.

And through it all, the noise is relentless. It isn’t “wind” in any normal sense; it’s a braiding of roars, rattles, and vibrations that build to a near-constant thunder in your ear. Anyone venturing across the plateau would feel enveloped in percussion, as though stepping inside a colossal drum. Even the life-forms here must have adapted to the cacophony—some add their own strange calls, others slip through the cracks in near-silence, letting the roar camouflage them.

In this cathedral-sized region of stone and swirling, coruscating energies, you sense that the dynamic chaos is by design—a natural (or unnatural) equilibrium. Everything lives at high frequency. Where a typical living world might require water, here, some intangible phenomenon takes that crucial place. This plateau belongs to itself, sealed off by altitude, dryness, and the intangible complexities of technology far beyond everyday comprehension. The more you look, the more you suspect that the plateau is neither a mere mountain nor an isolated outcrop of advanced experimentation, but a singular entity—a living biosphere running on pure turbulence and brilliant, churning life. It is a place that roars, a place that thrives, a place whose strangeness you can feel pounding in your chest with every thrum that resonates up through the living stone.
