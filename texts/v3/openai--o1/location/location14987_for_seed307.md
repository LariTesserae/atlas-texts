Between parallel ridges of newly formed rock, a narrow canyon steams with heat. The air presses in—hot, almost blistering, yet made humid by countless trickles of water that bead along the stone walls. You might at first think it a desert oasis, but there is no green here. No leaves, no vines, only water ghosting over the stones in rivulets that shimmer with otherworldly glints. The canyon is dizzyingly tall, almost as if it were the inside of some immense chimney, but it’s scarcely wider than a corridor. The sky above is merely a sliver of pale light. Everything feels contained, compressed, as though we have stumbled into a secret pocket of the world.

Some of the towering rock faces look newly torn from the earth, still sharp-edged and unsettled, their minerals not yet worn by time. Metallic flecks in the stone catch the daylight, throwing pinpoints of brightness across the haze. Now and then, a thin sheet of hot water sluices down the rock, vanishing into cracks in the canyon floor. With each splash, the air grows denser, the vapor swirling in silent eddies, as though the wind doesn’t quite know how to behave here.

At the base of these cliffs, the ground is slick and pebbled, scattered with unusual growths—stubby, spongy lumps that might be the beginnings of fungal life, but their composition seems more mineral than organic. They pulse faintly, in time with the surging of hidden currents beneath the rocky surface, or with the slow breath of some presence deeper still. One might see these lumps shift and subtly change shape. Then you blink and they look exactly as they did before. Hard to say if it was your eyes playing tricks in the humid half-light, or something more uncanny.

Though plants are all but absent, the place is startlingly alive. Creatures glide across the stony walls—chitinous, many-legged, some as small as the nail of your thumb, others large enough to stretch between one foothold and the next in a single spidery stride. They move in tight clusters, so dense that if you look for too long, you begin to see them as a single writhing tapestry. There are also faint glimpses of larger forms lurking in the steam: silhouettes with wide, ridged backs, perhaps amphibian or reptilian, that shift between vantage points on the ledges. They drag themselves along the rock, leaving wet trails, blinking heat-fogged eyes. Oddly, one or two appear to hold small rocks in their front limbs—maybe just a coincidence, or some rudimentary attempt at tool use. Occasionally you’ll see them tap these stones against the canyon wall before disappearing in a sliding shuffle of limb and tail.

In the hush, the only things that break the silence are plinks of dripping water, the scuttling of hundreds of jointed legs, and the gentle hiss of steam vents. Now and then, far overhead, the faintest rumble occurs—maybe a landslide in a distant part of these mountains, or something else shifting in the planet’s crust. Though the light is typical daylight, the thickening air transmutes it into a hazy glow, making the entire canyon feel like a half-submerged dream, or a moment stolen from waking reality.

Yet that sense of dreamlike distortion aligns with an even deeper strangeness. At the periphery of your vision, the rocks sometimes distort: bulge and recede, as if the canyon walls breathe in and out. Turn your head, and they snap back to sharp, fresh edges. The water that trickles down occasional ridges might change its direction for no apparent reason, or trace shapes that look like impossibly straight, geometric lines before vanishing into a hidden crack. Now and again, you might catch a flicker of color—neon orange, or crystalline lavender—only for it to fade into the hot bright gray of the mineral surfaces. It’s as if the laws of nature have begun to warp inside this confined gorge, making you wonder if the place itself straddles more than one reality.

Despite the overwhelming number of creatures, they do not crowd you. They remain intent on their hidden errands, brushing by silently, climbing in quick surges and drifting rest. Some open their mouths, lined with rows of blackish teeth or chitinous ridges, but no recognizable cry escapes—only the faintest hiss, sublimated by the saturating heat. On rare occasion, you may see two creatures confront each other, jaws parted, as a smaller one tries to scramble away. Predator-prey drama flickers through the gloom like half-glimpsed nightmares. Then the moment resolves into a visceral scuffle or a swift, graceful kill—which might go silent enough that the only clue is the sudden rustle of bodies.

This place is newly torn from the depths, shaped by forces we can only guess at. No human hands have built or molded here; there is no path, no sign of structure, only the raw world. Yet it bears a strange, ancient memory in its bones. One mossy-lipped crack might vent a gust of steam reminiscent of an almost volcanic birth. Another might yield a watery trickle so pure it seems alive. Are these newborn rivulets of molten mineral or are they an echo of environmental processes we can’t fully parse?

However you try to name it—cavern, canyon, fissure, or some improbable pocket dimension—this realm feels as though it’s still deciding the shape it wants to take. It seethes with potential, fluid as a dream, equally capable of cradling life and swallowing it. An intensifying heat roils through, enough that the moisture in the air condenses into motionless droplets, dancing in midair a moment before rejoining the flow. Sharp ridges of rock angle overhead, flaring with color when steam hits them just right. Shadows loom behind the walls of vapor. Then the wind shifts, the dreamlike distortions recede, and you see only glistening stone and countless creatures navigating a brand-new labyrinth of mineral and steam.

It is a world still in its infancy, but teeming with life despite the absence of greenery. A domain of silent hunts, of sudden, half-seen shapes, and of strange surges of energy that bend reality’s edges. You stand at the border of wonder and something else—something that rubs at the corners of the mind, too new or too timeless to define. The silence never quite leaves; it thrums in harmony with the scuttle of claws and the gentle patter of droplets, a hush that promises that this hidden gorge, carved in a blaze of molten birth, is only the first step down a road where the usual laws do not apply. And the path deeper is open, if you dare follow.
