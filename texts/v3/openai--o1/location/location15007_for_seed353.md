It is so dark here that at first you might believe your eyes are closed. Except the heat on your skin says otherwise: a dry, oppressive warmth that seeps through everything. There is no water—a fact as self-evident as the hush in the air—and yet, paradoxically, the world you stand in is thoroughly alive with vegetation. Not just “alive”—it is made of plants. Every surface, every wall, every towering form: some expression of leafy or fibrous growth, fused seamlessly with industrial girders and riveted plates. It is as though a once-thriving jungle, too stubborn to die, swallowed a factory from a more mechanical age and now grows on, ablaze with metabolic energy, without a drop of water to spare.

Each step carries you deeper into this labyrinth of twisted vines and cables. The vines, thick as a person’s arm, coil around enormous beams of black iron. Leaves like draping sheets of leather hang overhead, forming half-arches that block what little light filters in. The darkness is almost absolute—little more than the flicker of distant, guttering lamps fed by uncertain power lines that vanish into tangles of photosynthetic roots. A hush pervades, broken now and then by the low groan of straining metal or the whispery rasp of bark sliding across bark.

Everywhere you look, plants have grown into shapes reminiscent of scaffolds and catwalks—like the ruin of some impossibly ancient foundry. Only, it isn’t truly ruinous. The structure is fully occupied: there is no empty space, not even a hint of open ground. Thick, fibrous mats of root and trunk fuse with metal foundations, forming floors that sag like living membranes underfoot. The temperature is near-scorching, kept that way by hidden currents of chemical heat: perhaps the vegetation’s own metabolism, or some subterranean engine left humming centuries ago, pumping hot air through the under-layers.

There are no animals here—no insects buzzing, no rodents scurrying. Yet the place refuses to stay still. At the corners of your vision, you sense subtle motions as leaves sway in a breeze you cannot feel. Sometimes it’s the roots themselves adjusting, coiling across the metallic surfaces as if responding to an unseen command. This entire landscape is unsettled, as if each part is trying to expand and contract simultaneously. Somewhere in the distance, you hear another metallic groan, like a gate forced open by relentless vines that are neither entirely mechanical nor purely organic.

The mood is deeply strange: an industrial forest with no splashes of water, where dryness reigns yet life persists in an impossible lushness of cellulose and steel. The old surfaces are pitted with age and time-worn indentations. You can sense, in every crack and seam, how old this structure is—ancient beyond measure. Indeed, the shapes look half-finished and half-collapsed at once: the final iteration of an ecosystem that has, over millennia, found its stable equilibrium in chaos. Rust lines the iron stanchions; the bark envelops these corroded edges. The entire expanse feels as though all change is so gradual, so constant, that nothing ever truly rests in a final form.

Moving forward, you pass what might have once been a corridor of thick piping. Now it is a snarl of roots grown through rusted tubes, forming archways overhead. The darkness grows deeper still—though the heat and the dryness intensify. It feels like an eternal dusk. Occasionally, you will pass a weird industrial outcropping—perhaps a large gear or a cylindrical compressor—utterly entangled in green-black vines as if slowly being digested. There is no water to be found; the plants, alien in their evolution, must draw sustenance from the mineral-laden dryness, from chemical baths, from the faint trickle of energy that hums through old power lines.

In the deepest sections, the “floor” gives way to layered platforms of—what appear to be—knotted trunks, spiraling out like giant braided cables. This gives the place a sense of being high above some unseen chasm, though it could be illusions of scale: everything is both vast and oppressive in the near darkness. Here and there, you glimpse flickers of activity—glowing filaments peeking between plates of bark, mechanical arms that shift overhead with an oil-slick hiss, perhaps some vestige of a long-lost production line still functioning in a hidden vault. The air resonates with muffled mechanical sighs and nearly inaudible clicks, while leaves and branches creak against each other in fitful movement.

Sometimes you spot entire walls of massive, overlapping leaves, each leaf an arm’s length thick, studded with twisting cables that pulse faintly as if carrying current. No space is left uncovered: the environment is packed to the brim. Over centuries, it must have built itself outward from some central core, forging a single continuous structure that can never be neatly separated into “machine” and “plant.” The bark is etched with patterns reminiscent of circuit boards, and the riveted plating looks as if it has grown scales. A thick dryness hangs in the atmosphere, stinging your nose and throat. Hot gusts swirl from hidden vents—occasionally coughing out clouds of fibrous dust that glimmer in the gloom before settling again.

Despite the near silence, the place does not feel tranquil. It feels turbulently restless, as though these fused, archaic forms are always in a slow war, each wanting more space or more resources. It has been this way for ages, beyond counting. No single vantage point reveals the whole. Instead, you sense that this environment continues on and on in every direction: a labyrinthine tangle of metal scaffolding and carnivorous, heat-loving vine. It must stretch for miles, forming a self-contained region of dryness and heat and unstoppable growth.

The thought of water or animal life is inconceivable here. With no water, with no creatures to move about, these plants have become the entire ecology, the entire architecture, the entire being of the place. Like a machine incapable of rest, it churns and shifts in slow, unstoppable cycles, generating dryness and heat in endless repetition. Though you can scarcely see, a part of you knows the structure extends far beyond your horizon, continuing to claim more territory in its labyrinth of living, industrial architecture—an unrestrained, ancient, and impossibly engineered ecosystem, always hot, always dark, always restlessly alive in its own strange way.
