A warm darkness wraps around a high plateau, one so ancient its rock is worn almost flat. The edges trace faint ridges against the night sky—crumbled forms suggesting these lands were once carved by tremendous forces long forgotten. Now the ground has settled into a final shape, the stone tired and softened, its original jaggedness erased by eons of torrents and subtle shifts. As far as your eyes can tell in the inky blackness, the earth beneath you breathes with water: shallow pools gather wherever the bedrock dips, gleaming with the reflection of something that never quite reveals itself, like ghosts of starlight.

Despite the lack of light, the place feels alive. Moisture drips steadily down seams in the rock, the air heavy with condensation, so you find your footing on surfaces slick with moss—even here, at altitude, lush growth abounds. Mosses, hanging ferns, and thick clumps of vines congregate in patches where hidden springs seep from beneath the stones. The scent is damp and faintly sweet, as if fresh rainfall is always just finishing. You can hear the slow drip of water, but muffled under it all is a hum—steady, droning, rising and falling in a pattern that suggests machinery beyond any technology you know.

Though it is dark, the darkness is not still. Shadows dart along the edges: creatures, many-limbed or perhaps gracefully elongated, move in bursts and leaps. Gathering in chorus, they let out calls that echo across the shallow pools. The fauna thrives in this hidden wetness: you can sense the mass of them all around, an abundant labyrinth of frogs with luminescent markings, small four-winged insects scattering in the air, and furry shapes crouched among the roots. Whenever they stir, the night resounds with chirps, croaks, and high-pitched chords. It rises to a cacophony at times—a loud swirl of voices in the damp air—yet then falls back into a hush as though commanded by some patient, immense presence.

Here and there, you catch glimpses of constructions. In the darkness, they appear only as silhouettes: a column half-swallowed by roots; a ring of faintly glowing orbs set into stone; strange devices protruding from the ground on filigreed supports. They seem scattered almost at random, as if whoever built them was conducted by arcane impulses rather than a city-plan. Sometimes a faint teal or violet light flickers across the surfaces—runes or threads of energy coursing through hunks of metal not forged by any ordinary means. Etched across these objects are patterns unrecognizable as letters or symbols, but which hum and radiate subtle vibration whenever your hand draws near.

Despite the advanced air about these artifacts—beyond anything you can explain—the place is oddly small in its scope, more like a secluded amphitheater in shape and size than any sprawling landscape. The edges of the plateau drop off sharply into unseen depths. A sense of boundary encloses you here in this darkness, forming a natural courtyard of ancient rock, water, and the quiet remains of something that was engineered with knowledge far surpassing modern achievement.

Gravity itself feels somewhat relaxed, as if centuries of erosion and the high altitude have worn away not just the rock but also the weight of the air. Underfoot, faint tremors pulse in time with the machinery’s hum, and you wonder if the darkness overhead really belongs to the night sky or if some vast shell of technology encloses this realm.

The environment changes only in slow pulses. Moisture condenses and drips in a cycle that repeats gently—like a deliberate, patient clock, marking the passage of eras rather than hours. The plateau’s surface has been shaped by these eons of minuscule alterations, giving depth to shallow grooves where water trickles along. The atmosphere is warm but stagnant, resisting quick fluctuations; instead, it shifts so gradually you only notice from one dawn (should it ever come) to the next.

No bright sun emerges to set the greenery aglow—whatever meager luminescence there is comes from subtle bioluminescent flora, from faint flickers across the scattered structures, and from the hazy glow cast by the creatures that dwell here. Even so, life flourishes, drawing nourishment from water and the metallic hum that seems to power not only the unknown devices but also the very rhythm of this hidden world. The feeling is solemn, a damp, cathedral-like hush—and yet it bursts with thrumming energies, the chorus of countless living things, and the persistent mystery of technologies beyond comprehension.

In this shadowy enclave, time and progress have a different meaning. The place is old—older than its stilled, nearly featureless terrain might suggest—yet it resonates with advanced craft that warps the fabric of physics. Life teems in every crevice, pulsing slowly, as if everything here obeys a cosmic clock that winds on a near-geologic tempo. Water glistens while the high plateau breathes, and a hush falls between the creatures’ calls. Even in the profound darkness, everything quietly vibrates with existence, hinting that somewhere in the obscure distance, a deeper purpose or an even stranger realm may wait.
