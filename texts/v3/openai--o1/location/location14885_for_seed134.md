A cool blue expanse stretches out in every direction. It is neither an ocean nor a lake exactly, but something in between—a vast volume of water suspended at high altitude on a broad terrace of jagged rock. The air here is thin and chilly, and in the distance the tops of even higher ridges glimmer against a sky of ordinary brightness. Yet the view is anything but ordinary. The water is deep and nearly all-encompassing, filling this terrace to near-overflowing, its surface gently lapping at the towering stone edges as if it might tip over at any moment. No true plants cling to the walls or floor—there is nothing but mineral surfaces, cold to the touch, newly sculpted by some ancient upheaval that left everything strangely sharp, as though it all crystallized just a short time ago.

Within the water, life is unexpectedly dense and abundant. Long, snake-like creatures coil in slow, undulating dances while glimmering swarms of finger-sized crustaceans clack and pulse around them. These animals emit an unrelenting soundscape—a chatter of clicks and throbs that together become a near-deafening roar, even as the creatures themselves move with a measured, patient pace. At times, the water hums deeply, as if powered by some hidden dynamo. It’s easy to imagine that somewhere below the surface, colossal mechanical engines are turning, though no machines exist here at all. In truth, the vibrations seem born from the overlapping rhythms of countless living bodies: pulsations of luminous organs, chitinous armatures that scrape and squeak, and the low thrumming calls that echo from one shoal to the next. You can feel these sounds as much as hear them, reverberating through rock and water.

Piercing columns of fresh, jagged stone shoot up through the water at uneven intervals, forming labyrinthine corridors. With no plant life, their surfaces remain polished and bare, but they host small pockets of creeping fungal or microbial mats that glow faintly violet—an otherworldly touch reminiscent of alien neon lights. Some corners are so crowded with schools of silent, finned lumps that it’s impossible to see where stone ends and animal bodies begin. Everything presses in close, a tapestry of life about to spill over. It is as if the newly emerged rock has become a living engine, a “factory floor” for these hundreds of unearthly species. There is no sign of deliberate construction—no architectural lines, no deliberate columns or shaped chambers—yet a distinctly industrial feeling pervades. The biological swirl is so organized and intense that you can practically hear the rumble of a grand assembly line.

Sometimes, the entire mass of creatures shifts in one great wave, a slow cascade that ripples outward in fractal patterns of color and sound. Then, just as gradually, they return to their drifting states, drifting in clumps, drifting alone, each maintaining a rhythm as unhurried as a slow heartbeat. Despite the thunderous noise, time here seems to move in a quietly measured cycle—gradual transformations across eons, with each day’s bright light unchanging in hue. The watery terrace feels young, almost embryonic, yet simultaneously locked in a timeless trance. Whatever forces erupted and left behind this watery world at altitude did so recently, but the cycles of life that arrived here have already gathered into a dense, complex matrix. The rules are simple: water in near-constant motion, sharp mineral edges framing the boundaries, and creatures—unusual and numerous—acting out their strange mechanical ballet under a bright, echoing sky.
