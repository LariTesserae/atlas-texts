A long, lowland stretch of greenery extends in every direction, formed almost entirely of interwoven plants. The ground itself isn’t even really “earth” in the usual sense—rather it is a mesh of twisted, bark-like vines that have grown thick and pressed down firmly over centuries. Through the net-like surface, there are pockets and hollows where cool beads of moisture collect. Some are small pools hidden under layered leaves, and all around them faint mists gather—like breath on a chilly morning—before dispersing into the open air. The dampness is obvious: droplets pearl along every exposed surface, giving off an almost metallic sheen under the subdued daylight.

The vegetation here spans from slender creepers that dangle overhead to colossal trunk-like stems knitting together in slow, patient arcs. Everywhere you look, the plant life pushes up or out, as though continuously reasserting its dominance: short shrubs that disguise themselves as bulges in the vines, and thick, columnar roots that melt seamlessly into the landscape. In the distance, these living forms rise in a few carefully planned towers—the settlement. It is not huge, more like a small cluster of sculpted growth. Wood-and-living-stem scaffolds are arranged with geometrical precision around lumps of hammered metal. Metal plates and piping almost gleam against the subdued greens and soft browns, evidence of an industrial craft weaving itself into a realm of the purely organic. Whether the buildings were grown or built, or some mixture, is difficult to say. Each structure seems part architecture, part plant: the city is the vines, and the vines are the city.

The air carries only quiet sounds. Now and then, a metallic clank echoes from deeper within the settlement, from one of the small foundries or perhaps a half-buried engine. Creaky scaffolding might shift when the wind picks up, and vines often groan low like distant thunder as they slowly grow or bend. But overall, a hush prevails—this place feels restful, like a hollow inside a massive garden. The subdued buzzing of the occasional motor or generator hints at industrial processes going on just out of sight. Slow plumes of steam escape from cylindrical vents built into the living walls, condensing into swirling wisps before fading into the still air. Now and again, you’ll hear the subtle trickle or dripping of water as it slides through the foliage and pools in hidden recesses.

Although plants dominate, there are few creatures to be seen. Now and then, a flicker of movement in the undergrowth catches your eye—a slight scuttle that might be an insect or a small amphibian. The vegetation’s fullness stands in contrast to the scarcity of animal life, giving the impression that any wildlife here moves as quietly and inconspicuously as possible. Indeed, the deep networks of vine and root likely harbor entire labyrinths for tiny, seldom-seen creatures, but sightings are rare.

The settlement seems ancient: everything speaks of long use and a steady process of weathering. Mosses and lichen cling to the edges of polished metal plates. Parts of the vine architecture wear the marks of wind and time, having thickened and hardened in layers upon layers. Yet, despite the calm surface, there’s a definite energy underfoot—an ongoing cycle of growth, mechanical hum, dampness, and condensation. Occasional rains seem to pass through, refreshing the drooping leaves, giving everything a renewed vigor. You get the sense that if the settlement’s inhabitants were to leave this place for even a short time, the plant realm would whoosh back in, covering up the metal structures entirely.

Beyond the towers stand a few wide gates of living wood, shaped to admit the small cargo wagons that rumble in from time to time. Their engines chug gently—industrial, but not massive or overwhelming. A faint smell of peat or organic fuel lingers. Traces of technology appear in smaller details too: riveted joints reinforcing living buttresses, pulley systems hoisting crates of raw materials, lights powered by cables threaded into the vines. The effect is unusual yet strangely harmonious, as if some truce was reached between the organic and the machine.

Light in this realm is moderate—neither dull nor overly bright—filtered through the layered leaves that form canopies overhead. At ground level, the interplay of shadow and translucence creates soft patterns on every surface. In the city’s upper levels, where the vines arc and weave to be used as walkways, the daylight is clearer, illuminating the sprawl of plant architecture all around. From these heights, you can observe the settlement’s boundaries: a relatively contained area hemmed in by denser growth on all sides, beyond which the land continues, but the city does not. There might be more out there, but here is an enclosed domain, small and intimate.

Despite the settling hush, this place is alive with movement on a grand timescale: every leaf trembles in the damp breeze, and the slow expansions of stems and branches suggest an ongoing dance between cultivated growth and the gentle grind of industry. Perhaps that is this realm’s rule: a delicate balance, a living forging-place where cultivated technology and rampant plant life learn to coexist. Even if the human presence is subdued, the city hums with purposeful activity, continuing its subtle transformations in the quiet of its damp, cool cradle. And so the world here endures—softly lit, strangely calm, yet restlessly alive under the tightly knit arches of vines and the steady pulse of small engines.
