It’s a cold place, though not in the usual sense of chill and empty spaces. There’s dampness on every surface and in every crevice, a kind of condensation that beads perpetually along metal railings and rivets, a crisp veil of moisture clinging to leaf and building alike. The land is low and somewhat flat, yet it feels vast—broad wetlands broken only by clusters of imposed structures that loom everywhere. These are not scattered houses or huts: wide boulevards and interlaced pipes feed into blocky industrial complexes. All of it feels half finished, or perhaps half emerged from the earth, like the land itself is still figuring out what shape it wants to take.

From a distance, the city (and it is very much a city, or at least a patchwork mosaic of towns grown together) seems to stretch out across the plain in dense, huddled blocks. Iron and steel beams jut upward, layered with walkways and catwalks, overlapping in ways that don’t entirely make sense. Pockets of vegetation push defiantly through the cracks. Creeping vines find their way up the sides of warehouse walls and snake along electrical conduits. Pale mushrooms sprout in dark corners, their edges glinting with condensation. There is green here—a lushness that thrives on the constant damp, forming thick clusters of moss and glossy broad-leaf shrubs, their leaves tinted with faint, otherworldly purples under the normal daylight.

That light slips in under a perpetually rolling sky. Though it’s daytime, clouds keep the land in a softly shifting glow. Occasionally, the patter of brief rain rings against metal roofs, or condensation drips from the underside of steel walkways in a gentle, trickling lament. Between rains, an unusual hush settles—a quiet that feels charged, as if waiting for the next wave of commotion.

That tension is echoed in the constant hum of machinery. The city’s industrial heart is not boisterously loud but ever-present, running behind thick walls and underground channels. You can sense the pulse of turbines, the faint rattle of conveyer belts, and the murmur of pressurized steam lines threading through labyrinthine corridors. Now and then, a piercing whistle cuts the air—a release valve somewhere, or perhaps a distant train. But mostly there’s a hush that blankets everything, a quietness that feels almost eerie in how it saturates the entire region.

Still, the streets and alleyways are alive—jammed together so much that there is scarcely a gap. In the centers of streets, rickety cargo vehicles trundle by on rails, hauling raw materials from unknown quarries (or are they from deep underground?). The roads often wind around strange lumps of half-formed geology—great mounds of fresh earth that may be newly pressed upward by stirring energies beneath. As though the land is in flux, you’ll see raw sections of bedrock exposed, as if the planet is just now shaping its surface. Mud and clay swirl in shallow pools by the roadside; the newly revealed ground often steams in the cold air.

It’s cold enough that breath fogs in front of faces—human or otherwise. People and creatures bustle about in protective garments, heavy coats or improvised leathers. Creatures adapted to the chill damp nest in the walls: small amphibian-like things that leap out from cinderblock cracks, bright-eyed rodents with oddly elongated limbs, flocks of black-plumed waterbirds perched on iron rafters. There’s more fauna than you might expect in a place so packed with machines and metal. If you peer into the waterways snaking through the alleys—narrow canals or artificial channels for runoff—you’ll spot quicksilver fish darting between tangles of waterweed, or streams of insect larvae swirling below the surface. Life is layered here, even in the shadowy undercity.

In certain sections, the architecture climbs higher. Glistening pipes ascend to the rooftops, carrying steam or water, or perhaps stranger substances. Walkways connect building to building above the streets, forming twisting multi-level thoroughfares. An occasional surge of energy crackles through overhead cables, accompanied by flickers in yellow lamps that cast normal but somehow disquieting light on the damp streets.

Lost among the reverberations of factory hum, you can sometimes hear the clang of a piece of newly assembled metal scaffolding or the abrupt slam of a heavy door in a wind gust. But the real storminess here isn’t loud—rather, it’s a tension in the air, a sense of everything forever on the brink of some upheaval or transformation. The city’s expansions feel organic, unstoppable, as though every day more catwalks, more cables, more living structures sprout forward without a coherent plan. New machinery is constantly forged and installed. Old gear, half-broken, sinks into the watery ground—a mechanical archaeology forming in real time.

The weirdness lurks in the corners and back-alleys. Strange metallic growths appear where equipment was abandoned, twisting themselves like vines in unnatural shapes. Some believe these shapes are guided by methodical engineers in hidden labs; others claim the city itself is growing, alive, both technological and organic. Time here can feel stretched, as if something in the damp darkness warps perception—walking down one corridor might place you unexpectedly far from your origin. There are rumors of “soft spots” in reality where entire alleyways vanish or reappear within days, leading to bizarre anomalies: shops that change location overnight, entire blocks that fold into each other.

And through it all, the place is saturated with motion. Turbulent energies ripple through the atmosphere: swirling steam vents, shifting plating under foot, half-buried devices rumbling with new life, to say nothing of the unexplainable flickers at the edges of one’s vision. This city is a chrysalis caught mid-transformation. The geometry of the streets, the topography of the ground, even the composition of the underlying rock—none are fully settled. It’s raw and embryonic, yet teeming with life and mechanism at once. 

Yes, it’s cold. Yes, it’s wet, with only intermittent rains rolling through. Yes, it’s somehow both quiet and unnervingly busy, a hush layered over an oppressive density of steel, vegetation, and living creatures. But through the drifting steam and beads of condensation, an emerging vitality throbs in every block, in every pipe and vine. The land has not yet chosen its final shape; the city surges on around it, heedless, forging both wonders and uncanny distortions as it goes. Something about this place feels both alien and inevitable, a thick tapestry of industry and raw creation that hums in a secret key, prompting you to wander deeper still—despite the strangeness you sense with each step.
