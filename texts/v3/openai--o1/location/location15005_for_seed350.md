At first it’s difficult to see anything at all, because the place is dim—almost claustrophobically so. Everything is steeped in watery half-light, as though the sun barely filters through churning, sediment-thick water overhead. You’re halfway submerged in this warm, fluid environment; sometimes you can make out a dark horizon in the distance, sometimes it blurs, and sometimes you swear it flickers in and out of existence altogether.

The water around you is dense yet surprisingly alive with vegetation that looms and tangles. Enormous fronds—part seaweed, part fern, part something entirely alien—curl up from the soft silt floor. Their leaves twist around one another like serpents in slow motion, continually sprouting new shoots as if in frantic growth spurts. Colors are muted here: shades of deep green, gray, and occasional phosphorescent glows that flicker in patterns like Morse code. In some pockets, rifts in the swirling sediment let you see spires of rock or twisted steel protruding from the floor. You realize the ground beneath must be freshly formed—sharp edges, newly cracked surfaces, no sign of the steady smoothing that eons of erosion would bring.

Structures dot the landscape—if you can still call it that—like abandoned cairns or half-buried factories. Rusted scaffolds jut out at sharp angles. Machines squat in the darkness, their gears exposed to the swirling water. At unpredictable intervals, they shudder to life—massive pistons grinding, enormous turbines spinning, each motion accompanied by a deafening roar that sets the water around you humming with shockwaves. There’s an entire industrial labyrinth down here: conveyor belts that glow with eldritch energy; elongated pipes that snake off into deeper regions, disappearing among the thick swirling kelp-like vegetation. It becomes clear this industrial sprawl is scattered widely, a half-sunken testament to a civilization that once tried—and perhaps failed—to tame these waters.

No animals inhabit these grooves and hollows. Not a single fish darts by, not even an ambling crustacean. Instead, the water feels thick with something intangible, as though the very laws of reality are fragmenting. You catch glimpses of shapes that cannot exist: fractal loops of piping that bend in on themselves, spooling off into corners of spacetime you cannot fully see. Vegetation pulses with a heartbeat-like throbbing, though there is no pulse down here—no living creatures, no hearts to beat, just the ceaseless mechanical churn. Sometimes, for a split second, a wall of water seems to peel away, revealing an impossible corridor leading nowhere. Then it collapses, replaced by swirling particulates of silt and swirling leaves, as though it never existed.

The sound is near-unbearable. It’s an onslaught: grinding steel, roaring currents, the clanging of unknown mechanisms striking metal upon metal. Occasionally, a thunder-like crash booms out of nowhere—as if the ceiling of this watery realm tears open and slams shut, leaving behind a moment of lesser turbulence before the cacophony ramps up again. The entire place is in a state of permanent upheaval. Currents shift with no warning, flinging broken shards of metal, clumps of vegetation, and swirling clouds of debris in a vortex of unstoppable force. Towering columns of water buckle under the changes. In some spots, you see them twisting into funnels like watery tornadoes, then dissipating, only to re-form somewhere else minutes later.

Strangest of all is how the world refuses to hold still. Shapes blossom, billow, and fade. The faint glow that serves as your only light source pulses in irregular cycles, throwing tall, wavering shadows across half-submerged structures. Even those silhouettes refuse to behave—some flicker out of sight, others stretch into impossible angles, sliding along surfaces at speeds that have no relation to the water’s current. It’s as though the boundary between physical space and pure nightmare has grown perilously thin. One moment you could swear you’re standing (or floating) in a massive flooded factory, the next you might find yourself in a corridor enclosed by flora that moves as if it has purpose.

And through it all, that oppressive sense of density persists—there is so much happening in every cubic foot that at times it feels like you could reach out and grasp handfuls of raw, churning chaos. The warm water never rests; the environment is never calm. Wherever you look, something roils or glimmers or uncoils or collapses. Nothing is stable. Nothing is quiet. Yet in this tumult, the vegetation thrives, the steely remnants persist, and the place hums with a kind of heavy, electric tension, as though it is alive with an energy that surpasses mere biology. A submerged, storm-wracked frontier—freshly formed, perpetually shifting, and filled with the sound of an entire reality grinding against the edges of its own improbable existence.
