It is a place that should not, by common rules, exist: a planet-wide cityscape rising organically from a land without water, where vegetation flourishes in lush profusion despite not a drop of liquid to be found. The air is warm but holds not even the faintest scent of moisture. It feels more mineral than atmospheric, as though composed of fine-floating salts and dust. One might expect dryness to imply barrenness, but here it does not. Plants of a wholly different biology cling to one another in tight, clambering tangles whose leaves and stalks glitter under the ordinary daylight. It’s as if they feed on something else—some trace chemical drifting through the dryness or perhaps the barest hints of minerals in the land itself.

The terrain is low, a nearly flat expanse that stretches on and on until it rounds the planet’s horizon. The scale is so great it has its own sense of weather—though this ‘weather’ is more a swirling of faint dust and pale light, with swirling gusts that rustle leaves and swirl together pockets of grit. This swirling dust is gentle yet ceaseless, hinting at geological processes that are only just beginning. The land itself feels as though it is still taking shape. Certain areas appear raw, newly cracked, seeping faint vapors or glimmering crystals—embryonic geology in action, not yet weathered by time or worn by water, as no water exists here.

But the city—if one can truly call it that—seems to have grown rather than been built. No metal scaffolding, no mechanical hum. Masses of thick, trunk-like growth form archways and columns, an urban maze of living structures. You can wander through entire “districts” that look almost like giant blossoming spires and interlocked canopies. The result is dense: one can hardly see open space between these living constructions. It’s as though every inch of ground holds something—a root, a woven collage of bark-like material, or thick masses of fungal plating—everything pressed tight up against everything else.

Despite the absence of technology, the settlement’s organization is unmistakable. Soaring corridors, wide courtyards grown from massive flowering structures, and layered plateaus of spongy, bark-like walkways form a cohesive system of paths. Maze-like canopies spread overhead, shading open areas from the dry, unclouded sky. Quiet pockets of activity flicker among the leaves—birds, insects, or some manner of tiny life-forms scuttling in and out of the winding citylike growth. This fauna is prolific, each creature specialized to this waterless world, each adapted to glean whatever vital sustenance these living structures produce.

And oh, yes, the life here is not static. It is vibrant and in motion, teeming with quiet urgencies. Multiple processes swirl in the background: new stems rising, new corridors forming, the scuttling and chirping of hidden birds, silently shifting insect wings, the hush of breezes that coax micro-fragments of dust through the air. Even with so many organisms, the sounds are subdued, mostly faint rustles, delicate scurries, the flutter of membranes gliding one branch to another. If ever there was a hush that felt alive, it is here.

This is a land that, though almost without liquid water, still brims with living energy. The raw geology of this world is not yet fully formed, the landmass itself in a young, malleable state—yet it blankets the horizon in every direction, impossible in scope. One can sense that it is ancient in origin, but it has only recently begun to reveal its permanent shape. Urban-like, but grown from nature’s blueprint rather than human design, the entire planet is a testament to how life, resourcefulness, and community structures can emerge under the most alien conditions. And so it remains: vast, warm, dense with presence, a quiet realm of slow but active processes—an unlikely miracle of a world still crafting itself out of emptiness.
