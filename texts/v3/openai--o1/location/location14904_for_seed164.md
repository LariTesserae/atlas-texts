It is a plateau of staggering breadth, its edges falling away into deep, fog-choked valleys that appear only as ragged outlines in the half-light. The elevation is so great that the horizon bends subtly, a reminder that this lone plateau is but a fraction of a planet-scale rise. Everything here feels oversized and strangely empty, as though air and land alike stretch far beyond what any sense of familiarity can hold. And yet, it teems with green growth, a lush vegetation that covers every boulder and crevice in mossy blankets and faintly glimmering fronds—but you’ll see no birds or other animals among these plants. At first glance, the life here seems to feed on itself, locked in its own equilibrium, unbothered by the near absence of roaming creatures.

An endless cold seeps in from all sides. The air is neither biting nor violent, but permanently chilled, carrying a wetness that condenses on metallic surfaces and drips from countless stone outcrops. In some patches, shallow pools and marshy pockets gather between old rock folds, as if the land, newly emerged, still holds the memory of primordial waters. Rivulets appear from nowhere, seeping from natural cracks, hugging the edges of great rocky spurs. The water’s presence is never in doubt: it runs and spills in slow trickles and is wicked up by hungry green carpets of vegetation. Breath condenses in the thin, cold air, forming clouds that linger longer than they should, contributing to a muffled sense of everything being wrapped in its own layering of mist.

Even in the hush of high-altitude gloom, the sound is clamorous. Ahead, a vast industrial city—perhaps the only structure for thousands of miles—rises in faceted tiers from a central valley floor. From a distance, one can see serpentine conduits and squat factories forging outward, ring after ring. They are built from black steel and shining alloys that reflect an otherwise normal daylight, looking perpetually slick under the constant condensation. Gargantuan, rust-brown pipes coil high overhead, pumping something unseen: perhaps water, or electricity, or some sustenance for the machines deep within. Somewhere below, in the labyrinth of subterranean works, massive engines roar in an unwavering thrum. It is a thunder that vibrates up through the rock and into the soles of your feet—a singular, mechanical heartbeat that never changes.

For all its industrial presence, the city seems uncannily still in its operations. Workers are rare, and you see no bustle of bodies or vehicles crossing the platforms. There are rail lines that vanish into tunnels, conveyor belts that run day and night, and cooling towers belching steam into the thin sky. Yet there is no clang of hammer on steel, no calls of laborers. One might wonder who—if anyone—monitors these continuous processes. The sprawl of machinery trudges on in solemn, unchanging ritual, as though the technology itself crossed from function into a kind of geological layer. It simply is, and it always has been.

No animals call these highlands home. No birds, no small scuttling rodents—nothing breaks the landscape except for the rustling of plant fronds in the damp wind and the ceaseless industrial noise. And somehow, that near-void presence of animals feels more unnatural than the looming factories. The vegetation alone thrives, an unchallenged monarchy of spore and leaf, blanketing every available surface in thick patches of emerald, lavender, and obsidian-black foliage. Strange vines cling to the factories’ exteriors, weaving among the giant pipe networks, laced with bright droplets of condensation. These droplets glisten as if they carry trapped light, forming a thousand tiny mirrored worlds.

It feels as though time has faltered here, locked in a stable equilibrium. The plateau does not seem to erode further; its jagged, youthful rock forms and knife-edge crags remain sharply defined. Winds come and go, but they do not topple old structures or wear them down. The damp cold persists evenly, day after day. Even the machinery’s hum never wavers in pitch or pattern. As if by some uncanny design, all processes are self-sustaining, all forces in constant balance, each detail adhering to a silent instruction that forbids change.

Yet a distinct oddness pervades. Perhaps it is the geometry of the roads that spiral inward like a labyrinth, or the way some pipes appear to shimmer at the edges, or that the vegetation sometimes glows faintly at night. It could be how the clouds’ behavior around the plateau defies normal meteorology, or how you can stand on a rusted catwalk, cup your hands to your ears, and detect a layered echo—mechanical roar upon mechanical roar, folded and fed back on itself, with no singular source. The entire city, and the landscape around it, feels like a grand machine, or part of a still-unfolding cosmic apparatus: impossibly grand, discreetly alive, and entirely unchanging. Perhaps that is why no fauna roam these heights. Life here is something else entirely—a marriage of endless green, dripping water, and tireless metal joined in a noise-laden hush that never breaks.
