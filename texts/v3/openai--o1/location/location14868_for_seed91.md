The first thing you notice is the sound. It fills every corner of the space—an immensely loud, echoing thrum that shudders through the stiff, cold air. It’s impossible to pinpoint a single source. It might be the heavy machinery scattered throughout or the constant churn of life within the dense plant growth. The noise resonates through the soil and the spidery roots of that vegetation, winding like cords of sinew throughout this subterranean realm.

You’re below the surface here; not far enough beneath the earth to feel entirely sealed off, but just deep enough that sunlight barely filters through. Dim, filtered streaks slip through cracks or occasionally funnel down from above, landing in glowing pools. The pockets of moisture are intermittent—spidery drips from overhead, shallow rivulets running between the roots, and patches of condensation collecting on ragged metal surfaces. There is a definite chill to the air—each breath you take emerges like a wisp of white, dispersing before you can study it.

The landscape unfolds on a broad scale, like an immense underground valley. Everything appears newly formed, as if some ancient shift in the earth wrenched open a chamber that once lay hidden. Sharp-edged stone ledges rim the perimeter, slick with dampness. In many sections, the rock face gives way to massive expanses of living green—towering fronds, thick vines, moss-carpeted slopes. The vegetation has overtaken nearly every surface. It’s as if the plants themselves have become the bedrock, the scaffolding; huge trunks and fibrous growths form the walls of tunnel-like corridors. Far above, clusters of wide leaves and broad canopies create natural ceilings, weaving a dense mesh of life. Yet even though plant growth is everywhere, there is a sense of emptiness between each cluster; the stems and trunks space themselves widely, never crowding out the gloom entirely. 

Scattered among the roots and vines are remnants of an industrial effort. Large metal pylons, half-buried pipes, and silent conveyor belts appear and disappear in the vegetation, as though someone had once tried to build a factory in the depths of this cavern and then abandoned it. Thick cables stretch overhead, glimmering with condensation; occasionally you catch flickers of old lights or hear the wheeze of ancient turbines. Some of the machinery is still active—quietly rotating gears, deep droning pumps, or clanking pistons that create the deafening soundtrack. Others seem to have become part of the ecology itself, with vines coiling around rusted cogs and leaves sprouting between steel plates.

Despite the hum of hidden turbines and the rumble of heavy motors echoing across the tunnels, living creatures are rarely seen. Once in a while, you might catch a glimpse of some hardy, cold-blooded creature slipping between the roots, or the flash of pale wings flitting in the dimness. These sightings are fleeting, reinforcing a sense that any larger fauna prefer the corners, far from the roar of machinery. 

Light, though minimal, does exist here—faint artificial glows from old industrial lamps and the occasional ghostly clarity of some bioluminescent moss. The plants seem well-adapted to what little illumination reaches them: a perpetual twilight that keeps their growth slow and deliberate. Dripping water sets a cold, rhythmic beat in the background, though it’s easily lost beneath the industrial racket. 

Time moves sluggishly in this place. You can sense that the variations—like the slow creep of a vine across a girder or the gentle trickle of water wearing grooves into the fresh rock—happen over months or years rather than hours. Everything feels newly sculpted yet old in its stillness, as though the world here has only just emerged and is now quietly content to keep expanding and knitting itself together, day by cold, echoing day.

Yet it is not entirely static. The machinery hums in the distance, faintly vibrating the soil and root networks, a reminder that human or at least technological influences linger. The engines pulse, feeding on some unseen power source. You can almost imagine they are part of the environment’s slow heartbeat.

When you pause for breath, the cold gnaws at your fingers and cheeks. There is no sign things will warm up soon. The entire domain stays locked in this rarely disturbed hush, punctuated and dominated by its own rolling roar. You stand there, listening to the deafening tapestry of machines and water droplets and the faint stirring of growth creeping toward the future. A sprawling, subterranean forest of industry and flora, biding its time, alive in its quiet, unhurried interplay of cold earth and unstoppable green.
