A humidity clings to every surface here, like the air itself has condensed into fine droplets that bead on sprawling leaves and thick, fibrous trunks. It’s warm—almost stifling—yet not oppressive. The landscape stretches out low and wide, gently dipping into basin-like valleys where broad sheets of greenery shimmer with moisture. At first glance, one might think of it as jungle, but there’s very little that’s familiar; every living thing has some strange, alien twist. 

It seems as if plants have entirely taken over. Enormous root networks tangle aboveground, forming buttresses that hold the ground in place. Entire structures—pillars, arches, and winding stairways—are molded from living stems and vines, braided together in bizarre, symmetrical patterns. Bridges of living creepers span gullies, weaving overhead in dense canopies. The place moves with a curious pulse as mossy floors contract or expand underfoot, spongy surfaces flexing like muscle, responding to unseen impulses. There is a sense of fresh creation here, as if these plants only recently burst out of the soil and shot upward, never encountering the passage of time that might round off their edges.

The environment has the feel of an organic city: living “walls” of bark create honeycomb-like corridors; overlapping leaves form a patchwork “roof” that filters brilliant, normal sunlight into a greenish haze. It’s urban in scale: you can walk for miles through interconnected, leaf-shrouded halls and pass countless clusters of towering floral citadels. But there’s no hint of tools or machinery, not a single metal beam or piece of glass. This place grew itself into a dense web of architecture. And there are no animals here—at least, none that you’d recognize as such. No bird calls, no skitter of insects across the leaves. Only plants upon plants upon plants, forming an impossibly vivid labyrinth.

Yet the silence is nowhere to be found. In fact, it’s deafening. Somehow, the swirling pulses of life produce a continuous roar, a crescendo of rustling leaves and grinding cellulose. Booming gusts tear through the corridors of tangled trunks, sending them whipping and clattering against one another. There’s a ceaseless storm of sound, as if all these plants are humming at once—and in moments of heightened turbulence, that hum crescendos, building and building, threatening to shatter the eardrums. You might see bright splays of color shimmer across the surfaces of enormous petals as the sonic waves ripple through them, causing instantaneous color-shifts in the blinking of an eye. 

Everything looks newly formed. There are ragged edges where bark meets vine, still raw and thick with sticky, sap-like fluids that drip into shallow puddles. Runnels of water snake between the plant columns, forming narrow streams that carry away bits of organic debris. Now and again, a sudden spasm will shake entire treetop complexes—branch-bridges splitting off with shuddering cracks and quickly re-twining themselves somewhere else, as though the architecture is in a perpetual act of self-reinvention. There is no stable order here, only growth, rearrangement, and metamorphosis. 

Reality blinks at the edges. Surfaces sometimes waver, or entire sets of winding corridors fold in on themselves, flickering out of sight with a rippling distortion, then appearing again with a new configuration. As you watch a vine arch uncoil, it can spontaneously sprout new offshoots that climb the trunks to knit themselves into windows or balconies. There’s an enormous sense that the boundaries of space are softer than they should be, a living geometry running rampant and ignoring conventional rules. 

In the broad squares—if they can be called squares—whole neighborhoods of woody towers might burst into a swirl of leaves that vanish for a moment, only to reemerge across the way in a slightly different shape. Walking through it is like stepping into a living storm of constant flowerings, collapses, and re-formations. In some places, tight knots of vegetation have built up so high that entire canopies bend low enough to brush the ground. Paths are forced to twist away, contorting around these giant, breathing tangles. 

And through it all, that continuous roar of growth, wind, and friction rattles your chest. No animals, no footfall of creatures, just a loud, thrumming presence. Sometimes it changes pitch, like the entire city is tuning itself; sometimes it reaches a state of near-silence, only for the next wave of motion to explode even louder than before. 

If there are rules here, they aren’t obvious. You sense that the place is more event than location—an ongoing, volatile phenomenon of plant-life that churns and swells and reconfigures itself in real time, heedless of any logic but its own. Perhaps it lurched into being quite recently, erupting from beneath the soil in a single fractal gesture, and now it restlessly expands, adjusting every moment. Who knows if it will keep growing forever or just collapse into nothing? 

One lingering impression remains: although it’s enveloped in perpetual warmth, thick with living canopy, and fiercely bright in the filtered sunlight, there is something fundamentally impossible about it. The shapes, the way they multiply, the near-constant rearrangement of structure—all of it feels like stepping into some half-formed dream that forgot to obey the usual limits of reality. Yet for now, it exists, shimmering with condensed water droplets, roaring with the flutter of living greens, and suspended in a vibrant, state-shattering turbulence of never-ending change.
