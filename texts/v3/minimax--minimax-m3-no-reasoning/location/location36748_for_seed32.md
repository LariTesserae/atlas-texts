# The Hall of Quiet Furnaces

---

You find it by following a sound you cannot place.

Not a sound you *hear* — a sound that arrives in the bones of your teeth, in the soles of your feet, in the hinge of your jaw. A hum so low and so total that it has become architecture. It is the loudest silence you have ever stood inside.

---

The chamber is small. Cathedral-small, ruin-small — a pocket of space the size of a modest ballroom, and there is no inch of it left unoccupied.

Every surface has been built, layered, packed, *filled*. Not a single square remains bare, and yet the eye is not crowded. It is something stranger than crowded: it is *answered*. For every thing, a response. For every form, its neighbor placed against it as in a sentence. The walls do not need decoration because the walls are made of the decoration. The floor is the same as the ceiling. The ceiling is the same as the floor. You cannot, after a moment, say which way you came in.

---

The material is mineral. Pale. Bone-colored, chalk-colored, the color of the inside of a shell. It has been worked — not with tools you would recognize, not with hammers or chisels, but grown, deposited, laid down in long patient accretions by something without hands. The whole place is the size of a beehive carved from a single block of compressed seasons. Nothing here was assembled. Everything *crystallized into its place* over a span of time you cannot feel.

The texture underfoot is not smooth. It is not rough. It is the texture of ten thousand small decisions, each one made in the same direction: a faint stippling, a skin of tiny hemispheres, a field of pinhead domes you could press your cheek against and feel the patient grammar of them.

---

There is no water. There has perhaps never been water.

What there is, instead, is a dry bright air so clear it seems to vibrate. You can see all the way to the opposite wall and beyond, except there is no beyond, there is only more chamber, more packed order, more small dense forms crowding every plane. The light is everywhere and from nowhere — not falling from a source, not leaking through a window, but *resident*, as if the air itself remembers how to shine. It is the light of high plateaus, of salt pans at noon, of the interior of a tooth. It does not cast shadows, or it casts so many of them in so many small recesses that they average to gray.

You cannot tell, after some minutes, whether your eyes are open or shut. The bright is the same in both.

---

Vegetation. You will look for it, at first, because your mind supplies it without asking.

There are no plants. There are no plants. There are no plants.

But there are things that answer to the same question.

On the walls, growing from the mineral in slow arrested gestures, are forms that suggest stems, fronds, husks, husks of husks, the dried skeletons of flowers you have never seen. They are not green. They are the same bone-white as their substrate, only fractionally more yellow, fractionally more brittle. They are not alive. They may have been alive once, or they may only be the *idea* of alive, the way a fossil is not the animal but is the animal's insistence. They reach. They curl. Some of them, near the corners where two packed walls meet, have intertwined into lattices no thicker than a fingernail and the size of a dinner table, and the lattices are as hard and as quiet as the rest.

You will not be able to stop running your fingers along them.

---

No animal has ever been here.

You will know this without being told. The place is too *attended*. There is a quality of having been watched over by something that had nowhere else to look. Every element is in its position with a devotion that no animal's passing could have produced. This is a place kept by something that does not move, or that moves only in the way a stalactite moves, or a glacier, or a year.

The hum is the only fauna. The hum is the only fauna. The hum is the only fauna. You will start to think of it as a creature. You will be wrong, but the thought will not leave you.

---

The sound is the room's heartbeat, or its breath, or its thought.

It is not one sound. It is a *chord*, the way a chord is several sounds that have agreed to be one. A low note — felt, not heard — holds the floor, and against it, slightly higher, slightly brighter, a constellation of overtones that you can almost pick apart with attention. They will not be picked apart. They resettle the moment you reach for one. It is the sound of a tuning fork struck in a room made of itself. It is the sound of a hive of bees, but with the bees removed, and the honeycomb left to ring.

It does not get louder. It does not get softer. It does not stop. It is the equilibrium of the place, its resting state, the note a struck bell returns to between strikes. You will, in time, feel that it is the only sound in the world, and that all other sounds you have ever heard were the small deviations from it.

---

Nothing here will change while you are here. Nothing here has changed in a long time. The dust — if there is dust — is dust that has always been here. The small mineral garden on the walls has been opening its unspeaking flowers for longer than you have a unit for. The hum is the hum it was before you entered and will be after.

You are the only variable in the room. You, with your warm moving body, your wet soft mouth, your loud heart. The room, if it knows you, is being polite about it.

---

It is a small place.

It is a complete place.

It is the size of a held breath and the age of a thing that has been held for a very long time. It is a chapel built by no one for no one, and it is full, it is packed, it is *saturated* with itself, every inch accounted for, every inch in conversation with the next, and the conversation has not paused in a thousand years and will not pause when you leave.

You will not be able to leave immediately. The bright will hold you. The hum will hold you. The mineral flowers, dead and patient and asking nothing, will hold you in their small white hands.

When you do go, you will find, on the back of your hand, a faint dusting of bone-pale powder. You will not be able to brush it off. It will be there tomorrow.

---

*The Hall of Quiet Furnaces does not have a door. It has the memory of one.*
