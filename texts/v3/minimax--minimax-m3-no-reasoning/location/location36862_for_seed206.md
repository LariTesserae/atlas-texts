# The Boiler Thicket

You come to it through a stand of bamboo that has grown so dense you must turn your shoulders to pass. The path is not a path — it is a creek, shin-deep, the water the color of weak tea and warm to the touch. By the time you squeeze through the last curtain of culms and step into the clearing, the sound has been building in your ears for several minutes, and now it is all around you: a deep, shuddering hiss, felt in the sternum before it is heard.

The boiler is what they would have called a boiler. Now it is a hill.

Something happened to the machinery. Something happened, and the machinery forgot to stop, and the plants forgot to ask why it was still running. Generations of growth have sealed the boilers into the earth like bones in clay, and the earth has worn the boilers into the shape of itself, so that what remains is a long, low mound of dark iron — corrugated, riveted, sweating — from whose every seam and broken valve a thicket of green erupts.

The mound is maybe forty meters long. Maybe less. The scale is hard to read because everything is overgrown. The boilers lie in a row, five or six of them fused by rust and root, and from each one a column of vapor climbs straight up into the still air because the air is so still and so warm it has nowhere else to go. The columns do not drift. They rise like pulled glass until they hit a certain altitude and then they flatten into a lid, and the lid is the ceiling of the place, a low overcast that is not quite cloud and not quite steam but something in between that has been here long enough to have weather of its own.

The ground between you and the mound is a marsh. The water is everywhere. It stands in shallow panes between the hummocks of root, so still it has forgotten it is water and become a kind of dark glass, and from the glass the plants file up — ferns, things with leaves like hands, a kind of cane-stalk with joints that click softly when the steam-shudder moves the air. Between the plants, the silt is orange. The water going past your ankles is the color of rust, and it is the color of rust because the water is rust, because the boilers have been weeping their iron into this basin for longer than the cane has been cane.

The sound is not one sound. It is a chord of it. There is the high continuous note of the venting steam, thin and pure and a little bit wrong in the ear, like a kettle that has boiled for decades and forgotten to become silence. There is a lower rumble, deeper, felt in the feet, the boilers themselves still breathing somewhere down inside the green, the fire still coming up with whatever it has been fed. And under that, the small sounds of the place adjusting to itself: the click of the cane, the slow release of pressure somewhere with a sigh, the small splash of something moving in the water that you do not look at directly. Birdsong clatter at the rim, all the green answering.

There are birds. There are many birds. They are the size of your thumb, iridescent, the color of bottle glass, and they live in the rust-colored cane and the ferns with the hands, and they are loud. They are loud in the way that small birds are loud when there are many of them and they are happy, and they are happy here because the warmth from the boilers runs up through the roots and the steam keeps the air soft and the insects are endless. The birds do not know what the boilers are. The boilers are a hill that is warm.

The vegetation is total. The vegetation is the substrate. Where there is not iron there is green, and where there is not green there is the water that bears the green, and where there is not water there is the rust that the water becomes. There are no paths. There is no floor. You are standing in the system. The system has no outside. The system is a clearing in the bamboo the size of a small room, and the small room is a furnace that has forgotten it is a furnace, and the forgetting has made it a forest.

You notice, late, that nothing here is moving. Not really. The steam does not rise. It is held there, the columns standing as if cast in some invisible glass, and the water does not flow, it only stands and sweats, and the birds do not fly away, they only turn their heads. The light does not move either. It is the light of late afternoon in a place where late afternoon has been happening for a very long time. The clearing is warm and the light is gold and the gold does not move, does not travel, does not change, and the only thing that changes is the pressure of the sound against your chest and the slow, slow drip of condensation from the nearest boiler seam onto the leaf of the nearest cane-plant, and the cane-plant drinking it.

Nothing changes in this place. The boilers vent. The mice stand. The mice — there are mice, small and rust-colored, living in the iron — they stand on the rivet heads and they wash their faces and they do not run from you even when you come close, because you are not being unwatched, you are being watched, but the watching is not alarmed. You are a new thing. New things come sometimes. The system does not change, but new things come into it, and the system absorbs them, and they become the system, and the system goes on being the system.

You stand in the rust-colored water in the clearing of the bamboo in the low warm gold and the sound still thrums. The smallest valve in the system is the valve of your own attention. You stand there. You sweat. The mast. The thing that holds the sail up. You hold the sail up. The system holds the sail up. The thing that holds the sail up is the same thing that has been holding the sail up since the sail went up. The system is a clearing. The boats are leaning. The boots are creaking. The boots you are wearing. You are wearing boots. You are standing in the water in the boots.

The sound is loud. The sound has been loud. It will go on being loud. The gold will not move. The gold will not move. The gold is the gold of a place that has been gold for a very long time, and the rust-colored water will go on being rust-colored, and the cane will go on clicking, and the birds will go on being the size of your thumb and the color of bottle glass, and somewhere inside the iron, deep in the iron, in the place where the iron is still iron and has not yet become the hill, the fire will go on being fed whatever it is fed, and the fire will not go out, because the fire has not been told that it is allowed to go out, and nothing here is allowed to go out, and nothing here is allowed to change.

You are allowed to leave. The bamboo will let you pass. You turn your shoulders. The water goes back to being glass behind you. The sound follows you for a few steps and then it does not. You are out. You are out of the clearing. You are out of the system. You are standing in the bamboo in the green-wood in the world where things change, and the light moves here, and the wind moves here, and the things in the trees move.

You do not look back. You look back.

The clearing of the boilers is there. The steam stands there. The gold is still the gold. The birds are still the birds. The marsh is still the marsh.

Nothing has changed.
