# The Foundry in the Throat

The place is smaller than you expect. You come to it along a fissure in a basalt cliff, and the fissure narrows, narrows, and then opens — not into a canyon, not into a cave, but into *itself*, a space the size of a great cathedral compressed into a chamber no larger than a wardrobe. It is hidden the way a held breath is hidden, the way a name is hidden in a mouth. The walls around you are wet. Everything is wet. The water is not falling, not flowing — it is *exhaled*. The stone weeps from every seam. Condensation rolls in slow rivulets down surfaces that were not, an hour ago, surfaces at all.

## The Heat

The heat is the first thing, before the eye adjusts. It presses inward from every direction, not with the dry insistence of a desert but with the damp, fleshy insistence of a closed mouth. The air is almost water. Breathing is drinking. The heat comes from below — from the throat itself — and it smells of iron and rot and something older than either, a mineral musk, the smell of stone remembering what it was before it was stone. Sweat does not run here. It *appears*, blooming across the skin in sudden full sheets, as if the body has finally given up pretending it is separate from the air.

## The Geometry

The chamber has no shape. That is the first wrong thing, the wrong thing that lets the rest in. It is roughly the size of a small room, but the geometry is negotiable. Look at a wall: it is a wall. Look again: it is the inside of a throat. Look again: it is the underside of a leaf the size of a continent. The stone — if it is stone — shifts at the corner of vision. Always something else when seen directly. Always *this* when seen sidelong, in peripheral collapse. The floor is soft. Not sand-soft. Tongue-soft. It gives a little when stepped on, and where you have stepped, the impressions fill slowly with warm water that tastes of copper.

There is no sky. The ceiling, if there is a ceiling, is a low dome of dark wet rock from which the water descends in threads so fine they are visible only when the light catches them — and the light is a problem, because the light has no source.

## The Light

It is dim. The dimness is not darkness; it is *interior*. The place glows the way a body glows under a bruise, a deep dull red, and the red shifts, and the shifts are slow enough that you can feel them in your pulse. There are veins of something brighter running through the walls — not lava, not quite, but something that has the *idea* of lava: a slow orange pulse, a remembered heat, a thing that has been heat so long it cannot stop being it. The light does not illuminate. It *suffuses*. It comes from inside the stone, from inside the water, from inside the air. Looking at any surface for more than a few seconds produces a faint afterimage that does not fade, so the air is thick with the ghosts of every surface you have already looked at.

## The Construction

Everything here is *made*. This is the second wrong thing, the one that takes longer to understand.

The walls are not walls. They are bricks. You can see the seams if you look — millions upon millions of tiny bricks, fitted together with a precision that no human craft has ever achieved, each one the size of a tooth, each one slightly different in color, ranging from oxblood to bone to a black that is not a color but an absence. The bricks are warm. The bricks are wet. The bricks are *breathing* — a slow collective expansion and contraction, almost imperceptible, the rhythm of sleep, the rhythm of a thing digesting. Between the bricks, in the mortar lines, grow things. The mortar is not mortar. The mortar is a living tissue, a pinkish-gray substance that pulses faintly and from which, in places, small pale structures emerge — curls, horns, fronds, shapes that are almost recognizable and almost not. They do not move. You watch them. They do not move. You look away. When you look back they have grown a little.

The floor is paved in the same bricks, but smaller, and the pattern in the floor is a pattern. It takes time to read. It is a diagram. It is the diagram of this place, drawn at a scale of one-to-one, and it includes you.

## The Vegetation

It is not vegetation. It is the idea of vegetation, performed in materials that have never heard of chlorophyll.

From every horizontal seam in the walls, from the mortar-lines and from the mouths of the small pale structures, things grow. They are dense — there is no space between them, no air, no light left unoccupied. They crowd and layer and overlap with a packed urgency, a lushness that has nothing to do with joy. Ferns of folded metal, a foot tall, rusting as they grow, the rust a kind of bloom. Mosses of gray-green fur, soft as a rabbit's belly, growing on the bricks in patches that shift their borders. Flowers — and these are the worst — flowers the size of fists, in colors that should not exist in this light, a deep blue, a luminous yellow, petals of something between skin and paper. They smell of milk and ink. The smell is so strong it has texture. The smell is almost a sound.

Vines of a substance like wet rope, twisted, knotted, hanging from the ceiling in curtains, some of them still moving, growing, an inch at a time, the way a fingernail grows. They brush your shoulders as you turn. Where they have brushed the bricks, the bricks are stained darker. You do not know if this is damage.

The vegetation does not photosynthesize. It eats the light. It eats the heat. It eats the water. It eats the air. It is a digestion in the shape of a garden, and it is *hungry*, but it is not hungry for you. You are not the food. You are the weather.

## The Sounds

The place is loud. The loudness does not come from any single source. It is the cumulative voice of everything happening at once.

A constant low rumble, below hearing, that you feel in your teeth and in the small of your back. Above it, the sound of water, but not water in any form you know — not rain, not a stream, not a drip. A thick sound. A *digestive* sound. A swallowing. Above *that*, the sound of growth: tiny fractures, tiny whispers, a sound like rice being poured, like paper tearing, like many small things being born at once. Above *that*, at the edge of audibility, a singing. Not voices. Not music. A resonance in the brick itself, in the stone, in the air. A note, held forever, slightly flat, slightly *wrong*, the note that would be played if you struck the inside of a living thing.

And underneath everything, from the throat, a breath. A long, slow, wet inhalation. Then, after a time that is not quite the same length each time, an exhalation. The walls flex with it. The water streams faster during the exhalation. The flowers close, then open, then close.

You are inside a breath.

## The Fauna

You almost do not notice them. They are rare. They are so well-camouflaged against the wet brick that for a long while you think you are alone.

The first one you see is the size of a thumb, clinging to a vine, and you take it for a bud. It is not a bud. It has a face — not a face, a *suggestion* of a face: two darker patches, a seam that might be a mouth. It watches you with the unblinking patience of a thing that has no eyelids. When you move, it does not. When you hold very still, it moves — one leg, then another, the locomotion of a thing that is also a thought.

There are others. Small pale shapes in the mortar-lines, shapes in the flowers, shapes pressed flat against the ceiling, translucent, their internal organs visible as darker threads, their hearts beating in a slow rhythm that is not the rhythm of the breath. They are out of phase. They are a second clock, running slightly faster or slower than the place itself. They do not interact. They do not eat each other. They do not eat the vegetation. You do not see them eat at all. Perhaps they eat the sound. Perhaps they eat the dimness. Perhaps they are ornaments.

In the deeper parts, where the chamber narrows again into the throat, you glimpse something larger. Only a glimpse. A shape in the dark red glow that is bigger than you by an order of magnitude. It is still. It has been still for a long time. It is not asleep. It is *being thought about*, and the thinking is slow, and the thought has not yet finished.

## The Erosion, or the Lack

There is no erosion here. This place has not been worn. It has not yet been *finished*. It is under construction. The bricks are still being laid — you can hear it, a faint clink-clink-clink from somewhere deeper in, a sound of tiny hammers, tiny trowels, tiny hands. The vines are still being hung. The flowers are still being opened. The whole place is assembling itself around you in real time, the way a dream assembles itself around a dreamer, and you are inside the construction, and you are also part of the diagram on the floor, and the diagram includes you, and the construction knows this.

The water is not yet fully water. The stone is not yet fully stone. The air is not yet fully air. Everything is a little too new, a little too warm, a little too close to whatever it was before it was what it is.

## The Weirdness

The weirdness is not a feature. The weirdness is the substrate. Everything here obeys a physics that is recognizably *a* physics but not the one you were born under. Cause does not always precede effect. Distance is sometimes a flavor. The bricks, when you finally touch one — and you have to, you have to, because the place is unbearable if you do not touch it — the brick is warm and soft and slightly yielding, and the wall behind the brick is *further away than it should be*, and the brick remembers the wall, and you remember the brick, and for one vertiginous instant you remember being the brick and you remember being the wall and you remember being the small creature on the vine and you remember being the diagram on the floor and the floor remembers being you.

The flowers are not flowers. The water is not water. The breath is not a breath. The throat is not a throat. The throat *contains* a throat, and that throat contains another, and so on, and you are somewhere in the middle of a recursion that has been going on for a very long time and will go on for a very long time and has, at its center, not an answer but a *sound*: the note, the held note, slightly flat, slightly wrong, the note that the place is trying to remember how to sing.

## The Activity

It is active. It is not active in any way you can intervene in. The breath continues. The bricks are laid. The vines grow their slow inch. The small creatures shift. The flowers open and close. The water weeps. The heat presses. The diagram on the floor updates itself, faintly, in lines too thin to see unless you crouch and look sideways. The diagram is a self-portrait. The diagram is *becoming* a self-portrait. The diagram is not yet finished and will not be finished while you are here, and possibly not when you leave.

You are inside a thing that is becoming itself, and the becoming is slow, and the becoming is loud, and the becoming is wet, and the becoming is hungry in a way that is not about you, and the light is dim, and the heat is total, and the place is the size of a wardrobe and the size of a cathedral and the size of a throat inside a throat inside a throat, and the small creatures watch you with the patience of things that are also thoughts, and the singing continues, and the breath continues, and the breath *is* the singing, and you are breathing it now too.

You have been breathing it for some time. You are not sure you remember how to stop.
