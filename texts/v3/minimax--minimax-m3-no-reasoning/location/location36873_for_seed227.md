The water comes first, because the water is everything here. It falls in threads from a fissure in the rock above, six meters up, where the ceiling of this place cracks just enough to admit the sky — though you cannot see the sky for the dark. The water has been falling for so long that the stone beneath it has learned the shape of the impact. A bowl. A small stone bowl, almost perfectly round, almost perfectly silent in the way that a thing becomes silent when it has been making the same sound for ten thousand years. The water hits it and the bowl rings. The bowl rings and the ringing is the place.

The ringing is everywhere. Not just at the bowl. There are resonant stones throughout — a low shelf of pale rock along one wall, a leaning slab near the entrance, a scatter of smaller stones arranged in no pattern you can discern, that hum when the water strikes them, that hum in sympathy with each other when struck. The place is a single instrument that has been tuning itself for longer than memory. The sound is not loud in the way thunder is loud, or in the way a voice raised to be heard over a crowd is loud. The sound is loud the way a held note is loud when you cannot stop hearing it. It is loud the way pressure is loud. It goes through the body. It lives in the teeth.

The cold is the second thing. The water is cold, the air is cold, the stone is cold, the moss that clings to the stone in the deep places where the falling spray never quite reaches is cold. The cold does not bite. It is not a working cold, a winter cold, a cold that wants something from you. It is the cold of a cellar, of a well, of the inside of something that has not been warmed since it was made. You learn quickly that you will not be warmed here. You learn to stop expecting it. The cold becomes the background against which all other sensations are drawn.

The light is not light. It is the suggestion of light. It comes in from the same fissure the water comes in from, and the hours of the day can be read in the way the suggestion shifts. Sometimes there is almost a color — a grey, a green-grey, the color the world has when the sun is behind a great thickness of cloud and the cloud is the only sky. Sometimes the suggestion of light is so faint that you must reach out and touch the stone beside you to know which direction the fissure is. Sometimes, at the deepest part of whatever passes for night in this place, the suggestion disappears entirely and you are inside a sound that has no edges, and the cold has no edges, and the smell of wet stone and growing things has no edges, and you are not lost because there is nothing to be lost inside of, you are simply present, somewhere, in a body, in a place.

The place is small. You can cross it in a hundred paces, if the hundred paces were not interrupted by the need to crouch, to duck, to follow the only path that is not a path but a worn smoothness in the rock that the water has been smoothing since before any human hand touched this place. The ceiling is low where the ceiling is lowest, high where a long-ago geological event left a vaulted chamber that the water still falls into and out of by various channels. The walls are not walls. They are suggestions of walls, curtained in places by hanging moss, in places by trailing roots that have found the fissure above and grown down into the dark because roots do not care about light, roots only care about water, and there is water here.

There is green here. The green is strange because the green should not be, because the dark is so close to total, because the cold is so settled, because the place is so small. But the green is here. Moss in deep emerald drifts along the lower stones. Ferns uncurling in impossible slowness from cracks that hold a little soil, a little accumulated dust of centuries, a little patience. A small tree, almost a tree, more a determined shrub, growing from a ledge near the ceiling, its roots wrapped around the stone, its leaves a darker green than the moss, almost black, hanging down like a question. The green grows because the water falls and the water brings with it whatever the water brings, and the cold is not so cold as to be a refusal, and the dark is not so dark as to be a refusal, only a delay. The green is in no hurry. The green has had a long time.

There are no animals. There is the sound of small things moving in the moss, perhaps, if you are very still, and the patience to wait. A beetle, polished black, the size of a thumbnail, that walks the rim of the stone bowl without falling in. A pale creature with too many legs that lives in the crack between two stones and does not come out, that you learn of only because sometimes the crack is a different shape than it was the day before. Something that flies, briefly, near the ceiling, in the suggestion of light, that you glimpse only as a movement, a shadow, a thing that is and then is not. The place is not empty of life. The place is full of a life that has learned to ask for very little, and to wait for what it is given.

There are marks of hands here. Not many. A cairn, near the entrance, built of small flat stones balanced on each other in a way that suggests care, suggests someone who had time, suggests someone who came back. A groove worn into one of the walls, at shoulder height, that could be a tool mark, could be a water mark, could be a mark made by someone leaning there often enough to leave a polish on the stone. A shape carved, low, near the floor, of something that might be a bird, might be a fish, might be a leaf — the erosion has had its work on it, has been having its work on it for a long time, and will continue. A small fire ring, of gathered stones, in a hollow where the smoke would have found its way up and out through some vent you cannot see. No ash in it now. No memory of a fire. Only the stones, in their patient circle, waiting, as everything here waits, for the next thing to happen, and the next, and the next.

The place is old in a way that is not ruined. Ruin is a thing that happens to things that were once other things. This place was always this place. The fissure was always the fissure. The bowl was always the bowl. The moss was always, or was almost always, the moss. The cairn is the newest thing here, and even the cairn is old. Nothing in this place is in a hurry to become something else. The water falls. The bowl rings. The cold settles deeper into the stone. The green grows another fraction of a fraction of a fraction of a millimeter in the direction of its growing. The sound goes on, and on, and on, and is, in the end, the place itself, the only true inhabitant, the only thing that has been here longer than the green, longer than the stone, longer than the cold, the sound that the place makes to itself in the dark, the sound that you, if you stay, will become a part of, the way the cairn is a part of it, the way the fire ring is a part of it, the way the polished groove on the wall is a part of it, the way the beetle on the rim of the bowl is a part of it, a small, patient note in a song that has no end and needs no end and will not have an end, will only have a continuation, will only have you, in it, for as long as you are here, listening.
