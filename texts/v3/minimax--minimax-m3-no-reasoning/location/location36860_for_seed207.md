# The Cistern

You come down through stone.

Not stairs, exactly — there is no chiseled geometry here. The passage is a widening throat, and the walls move away from you by degrees, and the cold of them meets the cold of the air with no line between. Damp. The kind of damp that isn't weather, that doesn't belong to any day, that has lived here so long it has forgotten it ever lived anywhere else. Condensation threads itself along the stone in slow capillary rivers, and where the threads meet they form drops, and the drops gather on your sleeve if you stand long enough in one place, and the cold lets them stay there.

The ceiling is not far above you. It is not far below you either — the chamber has a feeling of being *nested*, of resting inside something else, the way a seed rests inside a fruit, or a fist inside a glove. The stone of it is rough in places and polished in others, and the polished places are where hands have touched, or where water has touched, or where both have agreed to leave their marks together. There is no telling, after enough centuries, which was which.

---

What lives here is green, and it is everywhere, and it does not move.

Mosses. Ferns folded tight. Things without names that have no Latin and need none, that have spent so long without being named by anyone that the not-being-named has become a kind of fluency. They cling to the ceiling and climb the walls and fill the floor in a low, dense, breathing carpet, and the green of them has too many greens in it — chartreuse and olive and the almost-black of deep shade and a strange bluish sage where the light is wrong, where the light is *whatever it is*.

There is very little light. What there is comes from the objects. There is no other word. The objects.

---

Scattered through the chamber, set into the moss and the stone as if they grew there, are the things that were brought down or left here or made here, and they have been here a long time, and they have made the peace with the green that the green has made with the stone. Some are the size of a thumb. Some are the size of a child curled sleeping. They are not smooth. They are not ornamented. They are *machined* — in a way the word machining was never meant to describe. The surfaces are continuous in a way surfaces should not be continuous. The edges do not catch the light because the light does not behave at the edges; it bends, gently, like water around a stone in a stream, and is gathered again on the other side, and the moss at the base of each object has grown up the sides an inch or two before refusing, before remembering it cannot.

They hum. Not loudly. Not in any register you would call a hum if you heard it once. But the chamber is mostly quiet, and quietness has a way of revealing things that noise hides, and so after you have been here for some minutes you realize that the air is not silent. There is a sound below the threshold of attention, a felt presence, a vibration that lives in the teeth and in the small bones of the wrist and not quite in the ears. It is not unpleasant. It is not pleasant. It is the sound of something working that does not need you to listen to it.

You do not know what they are doing. You are not meant to know. They have been doing it longer than you have been a thought, and they will be doing it after.

---

The water is the other inhabitant.

It does not flow so much as *persist*. It moves down the walls in sheets so thin they are almost a thought of wetness. It gathers in shallow bowls carved into the floor — not carved, exactly, *worn*, by patient hands or by patient time or by something patient that is not quite either. In the bowls it is still, and black, and reflects the low glow of the objects in a way that is not quite a reflection because the objects do not quite emit light in the ordinary sense. The reflections sit on the water's surface and do not break. Nothing falls into them. Nothing is permitted to.

The cold is everywhere. It is the temperature of stone that has never known sun. It is the temperature of water that has been in no hurry. Your breath does not quite mist — it is too damp here for that — but if you exhale slowly against a dark stone face, a ghost of warmth remains on the stone for a moment before the damp takes it back.

---

There is no wind. There is no animal. There is no insect. The green does not rustle because there is nothing to rustle it; the ferns hold their curled shapes like fists remembering what they once held. There is no rot because there is no death — the moss does not die here, it simply slows, and what slows eventually stops, and what stops becomes part of the carpet in a way that does not smell like anything.

The quiet is not empty. The hum is in it. The slow drip is in it — somewhere, at the edge of hearing, water falls into water at intervals that may or may not be intervals. There is the small sound of your own breathing, which you begin to hear differently here, because the chamber is large enough to contain it without crowding it and small enough to give it back to you changed.

---

The stone, the green, the water, the cold, the hum, the slow dark. They are not separate things. They are a single arrangement that has been negotiating itself for a very long time, and the negotiation is over, and what remains is the result, which is this place.

You could come back in a hundred years. It would be the same. Not unchanged — nothing is unchanged — but *equivalent*. The moss would have crept another inch along this wall. The bowl would have widened by a fraction. The hum would have shifted in a key you would not notice. The objects would be where they are. They would be doing what they are doing.

You are a visitor. The visitor is permitted. That is part of the arrangement.
