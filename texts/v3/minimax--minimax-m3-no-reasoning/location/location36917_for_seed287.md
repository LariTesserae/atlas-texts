# The Boiler Cathedral

---

You arrive through a passage you will not be able to find again.

The heat finds you before the architecture does. It arrives as a pressure on the skin, a weight, a suggestion that the air itself has texture. The walls are sweating. They have been sweating longer than anyone left to remember, and the minerals in that sweat have built themselves into a crust the color of an old bruise — yellowed cream, faint rust at the edges, the powdery green of verdigris. The crust is thick. It has stratified. You can see, where a piece of it has fallen away and revealed the dark stone beneath, that it has been built up in slow patient layers, the way a stalagmite is built, the way a pearl is built, the way a memory is built by a liar who is telling the truth. The crust weeps. The crust is the room's skin.

The room is not a room.

It is the inside of something.

You do not have the right word for it. There is no right word. It was a furnace once, perhaps, or a digestive organ, or the housing of an engine the size of which the people who built it could not easily have imagined. The geometry of it is wrong for any of those — too irregular, too *curious*, with a ceiling that climbs and then dives and then climbs again, and pillars — those are pillars, surely, they must be pillars — that bend slightly inward at the top as though listening. There are seven of them. You did not count them on purpose. You counted them because the eye counts things, in a place like this. There are seven. They are not load-bearing. They do not seem to be load-bearing of anything.

The sound arrives.

You were not prepared for the sound.

It is not a sound so much as a *presence of sound* — a hum so low it lives in the bones of you, in the molars, in the small bones of the inner ear, a frequency that does not so much enter the body as remind the body it has a resonance. Beneath it, layered, there are higher things: a hiss, a tick, a faint rhythmic clangor from somewhere far above and to the left, as of a metal thing striking a metal thing in a metal place, in a metal way, slowly, patiently, forever. The whole chamber breathes. The pillars breathe with it. The crust on the pillars thickens imperceptibly while you stand there. The air shimmers. A drop of condensation forms on a strut near your shoulder, swells, hangs, and falls, and the sound of its fall is louder than it should be, because the whole place is *listening*, the way a sea cave is listening, the way a great bronze bell is listening even after it has been struck.

The light is the light of a forge seen from a long way off, in fog, in memory. It comes from nowhere you can identify. It is amber. It is amber the color of weak tea, of a cloudy sunset, of an eye that is tired. It does not cast shadows so much as *suggest* their absence — the pillars have a darker side, but you could not say the light is what makes them so. The light is what *agrees* with them. Somewhere in the upper reaches of the chamber, beyond the haze, there is the suggestion of a brighter thing, the way there is the suggestion of a sun behind a cloud, and this brighter thing pulses. You do not notice the pulsing for a long time. When you do, you cannot tell if you have been watching it for a second or an hour.

The heat, the sound, the light — they are the same thing, you will eventually understand. They are what the place *is*. They are not a byproduct. They are a process.

---

The vegetation is not vegetation.

The vegetation is what the place has grown in the absence of any plants. The pillars are furred with it — a thick pelt of something that is not moss, not lichen, not fungus, not any of the categories you brought with you. It is the color of an old penny in places, and of tarnished silver in others, and of a sickly luminous green at the deepest folds. It grows in curls. It grows in spirals. Where a draft moves through the chamber — and there is always a draft, somewhere — it trembles like the fur of a sleeping animal. It is warm to the touch. It is, in places, hot. It is dry, except where it is wet, except where the crust beneath it weeps through.

The pillars are not the only things it has colonized. It has spread across the lower walls, climbed them partway, and there it has stopped, as if it has reached a height it does not care to exceed. It has also spread across the floor — or across what is functioning as the floor. The floor is the same dark stone as the walls where the crust has not claimed it, and where the stone is bare it is *polished*. Not by feet. Not by anything you can imagine. Polished as a river-polished stone is polished, by long and patient contact with something that was not quite water and not quite air. The patches of growth on the floor are dense and intertangled, the color of a bruise in a half-healed state, and they form — you did not notice at first — patterns. Spirals. Nested spirals. You did not notice because they are the same pattern as the chamber itself, the pattern of the thing's *breathing*, and your eye had already given up trying to see them as separate.

The floor is uneven. There is a low place in the center, a kind of shallow bowl, and the bowl is filled with water. The water is not still. The water is *working*. It moves in slow lazy convection — a thumbprint of darker warmth rising, a chill creeping in from the side, a tiny tremor at the surface that is not a tremor but a release of some dissolved gas. The water is the color of tea. The water is hot. The water is the source of the steam that hangs, low, across the floor, thickening as it cools, veiling the lower third of the chamber in a moving milk. The water is not clean. There are things in it — you can see them when the light catches them, flat slow shapes, dark, the color of the stone — but they are not animals. They are not plants. They are *precipitates*. They are the place's way of thinking about itself. They drift on the thermal currents and they never reach an edge.

---

There is a structure.

It is in the northwest quadrant of the chamber, if the chamber has quadrants, if the chamber has a northwest. It is a small thing — a single building, or the remains of one, or the memory of one. It is not made of stone. It is not made of the growth. It is made of metal.

The metal is copper. The copper has gone the way of all copper in such places — it has oxidized, it has greened, it has wept, it has been wept upon. It is the color of a statue in a sunken garden. It is shaped — you walk toward it through the warm mist, and the sound of your footsteps is wrong, too soft, as if the floor is forgiving you something — it is shaped like a *machine*. The machine has a base, a wide low drum of copper darkened almost to black. Out of the base rise pipes. The pipes go up. The pipes go sideways. The pipes go *into* the wall, and come out of the wall, and go *into* the floor, and there is one pipe that has broken free of wherever it was going and now hangs in the air, curving downward, weeping a slow drop at its tip every several seconds. *Plip. Plip. Plip.* The plip is the loudest sound in the chamber, after the hum.

The machine is not running.

The machine is not *not* running.

There is a wheel on the side of the drum, and the wheel is moving, very slowly, in the way that a windmill blade moves in a wind that has not made up its mind. There is a small lever, and the lever is vibrating, in sympathy with something — the hum, perhaps, or the breathing of the chamber, or a current in the hot water below. There is a glass face set into the drum — *glass*, you think, surprised, you had not expected glass — and behind the glass, in the amber half-light, a needle quivers against a dial. The numbers on the dial are not numbers you recognize. The needle is between two of them. It has been between two of them, you suspect, for a very long time.

The machine is not abandoned. The machine is *between* states. It is waiting for something that is not going to happen, and it has been waiting with such patience that the waiting has become its function, and the patience has become its engine. The growth has not claimed it. The growth respects it, the way a forest respects a fence post it has decided to grow around.

---

You sit.

There is a stone here, near the edge of the bowl of hot water, that is the right height. You did not bring anything to sit on. The stone offered itself. The stone has been sat upon before. You can feel the polish of it, the human-shaped absence in the crust.

You sit, and the chamber settles around you like an animal settling around a hand. The hum moves through you. The mist curls against your ankles. The needle on the dial of the copper machine moves — not perceptibly, not in any way you could measure, but it *moves*, and you trust this, the way you trust that the sun is moving even when you cannot see it. The place is in balance. The place is in a balance that took a long time to achieve, that will take a long time to leave, that you — a single human weight, a single small warmth — will not disturb. The crust on the pillars grows. The drop at the end of the broken pipe falls. The convection in the water continues. The growth trembles in the draft. Somewhere, far above, the brighter thing pulses. You watch it pulse for what you think is a long time.

When you look away, you cannot remember if it was the same brightness as before.

You will not find the passage out.

The passage out will not be here when you look for it.

The passage out is somewhere else in the place, and the place is bigger than the chamber, and the chamber is bigger on the inside than it was when you came in, and the chamber is patient, and the chamber is warm, and the chamber is listening.

The plip of the pipe counts the seconds for you. The hum counts the years.

You are not afraid. The chamber did not intend for you to be afraid. The chamber intends very little. The chamber is a process, and you are a small warm interruption in the process, and the process is already accommodating you, the way it has accommodated the copper machine, the way it has accommodated the growth, the way it has accommodated its own long slow forgetting of what it was for.

You will sit for a long time.

The chamber will not notice.

---

*The place is called, by no one, the Boiler Cathedral. The name is wrong. The place does not care. The place is older than the name and will outlive the name and is already, in its patient way, forgetting the name. There are other chambers. You will not see them. They are deeper. They are louder. They are more strange. They are all the same chamber, in the way that all the rooms of a great bell are the same room, in the way that all the chambers of a heart are the same chamber. They breathe together. They hum together. They wait, together, for a thing they have forgotten the name of.*

*You sit.*

*The plip falls.*

*The needle, between two numbers, moves.*
