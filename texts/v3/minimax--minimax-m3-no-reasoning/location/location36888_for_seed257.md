# The Sunken Sanctuary

You arrive on a long current, and the current knows where it is going, because it has been making the same journey for longer than anything remembers.

The water is warm. Not the warmth of a sunlit shallow — there is no sun here — but the warmth of the deep thermocline, the slow heat that seeps from below. It presses against you like a held hand. It is the temperature of breath, of skin, of something that has always been warm and will always be warm. The water has weight, but it is the weight of patience.

The first thing you notice, after the warmth, is that there is nothing soft. There is no sand. There is no mud. There is no silt to be felt rising beneath a fin or a palm. The floor, when you find it, is shaped. It was shaped so long ago that the shaping has become the shape — every edge rounded into a kind of slow statement, every tool-mark eaten away until only the intention remains, like a word worn smooth on a tongue. But the intention remains. It is unmistakable. Nothing here is given by geology. Every surface is a decision, an old one, made by hand with nothing sharper than another hand's labor and a stone.

It rises. That is the second thing. The floor becomes a step, and the step becomes another, and then you are climbing — drifting up the flank of something that was meant to be climbed, or descended, or inhabited in some manner that we no longer have words for. A seamount, perhaps, but a seamount that was quarried into architecture before it was drowned, or a mountain that was built, stone by fitted stone, into something that resembled a mountain so closely that the water could not tell the difference, and so came in anyway, and has been here ever since.

The scale is wrong, in the way that cathedrals are wrong. You could circle it in a slow afternoon, if an afternoon meant anything here. You could take it in with one long look, if your eyes could reach the far wall. But the far wall is dark, and the dark is part of it. The dark lives inside the architecture. The architecture is built with dark in mind.

It is a small place. A large room, repeated. A grove of pillars, or what were once pillars, or what were always going to become pillars — the stone has settled into verticality the way water settles into level. Each column is fitted from many stones, and the seams between the stones have been sealed with a paste that has outlasted everything else, and is now the brightest thing in the dark, a pale mineral line that catches what little light there is and offers it back, dimly, the way a moon does. The light here is not from above. The light here is from the seams.

You do not see the light. You see the seams. The light is the absence of dark. The dark is the substance, and the light is where the seams remember to give it back.

The fauna. There is so much fauna. The fauna is in the seams, in the niches, in the chambers the architecture never closed off, in the slots and the ledges and the hand-cut gutters and the basins and the places where two fitted stones met and were meant to meet and now, after all this time, are where the small bright lives have chosen to be. They are packed in. They lie in the grooves. They pour over the rounded steps in curtains of slow motion. They fill the columns with their presence, the way smoke fills a chimney — not by being there, but by being everywhere at once, in the breathing, in the way the water moves around them. There is no empty space in this place that has not been taken. The architecture is not, in the end, the thing you came to see. The architecture is the structure the life has built its life inside.

But the architecture is still there, and the architecture is still made. The architecture has not surrendered to the life. It holds. The stones still know they were stones. The pillars still understand that they are vertical. The basins still hold the silt that drifts in, because they were carved to hold silt, and the silt comes, and the silt is held. The grooves still direct the current, because the grooves were cut to direct the current, and the current obeys, though it has forgotten why.

The water is slow. The water is the slowest thing here, which is to say, the water is patient. The water does not want anything. The water is warm and it moves, and the moving is the only thing it does, and the moving is not even a thing it does on purpose. The water moves because the heat moves, and the heat moves because of the planet, and the planet does not know this place exists. The water has worn the stone, and the stone has accepted the wearing, and the wearing is now part of the design. The tool-marks are gone. The chisel-strokes are gone. What remains is the underlying idea, repeated so many times in the stone that the stone remembers it for the maker.

And the quiet. The quiet is not empty. The quiet is full. The quiet has weight. There is a sound, or there are sounds, but they are not sounds a person would call sounds — they are the feeling of a fin brushing a column, the almost-not-there pop of a small mouth opening and closing on a small thing, the long slow groan of water passing through a long slow channel, the almost-rumble of something large moving somewhere you cannot see, very far away, very far below, in a place where pressure becomes a kind of slow music. The quiet is so full that you can hear yourself in it, your own small heart, your own small heat, your own small movement through the warm dark, and you understand that you are one more presence in a place that is already more than full, and that the place is not hostile to you, and that the place is not friendly either. The place is only itself. It was itself before you. It will be itself after. You are, for the length of your visit, one more thing in the seams.

You climb. The architecture goes up. It is higher than it is wide, this place — a tower, or a spire, or what would have been a spire in air, and is now a spire in water, a slow finger pointing up into the warm dark at nothing in particular. At the top, or near the top, there is a platform, and the platform is bare, and the platform was meant to be bare, and the bareness is part of it. The platform is the only place where the architecture is fully visible, where the stone is not crowded with the fauna, where the seams are just seams, where the hand-worked surfaces show their grain. You can stand here. You can float here. You can see, in a slow circle, the whole of the place you have come to, and the whole of the place you have come to is a small dense dark warm ancient built thing, holding, in the water, on a mountain that was a mountain and is a mountain, on the back of a world that does not know it is here.

The water is warm.

The water is patient.

The water is the place.
