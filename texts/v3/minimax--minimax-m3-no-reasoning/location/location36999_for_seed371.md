# The Throat

What you have found is a slot.

Not a canyon in the way the word usually means — not a slow thing carved over geologic patient eons by a river that has had the decency to remain. This cut is young. The walls still have the impatient geometry of rock that only just decided to be a wall. They rise three hundred meters on either side and the distance between them at their narrowest is twelve meters, which is to say a little more than the length of a city bus, which is to say you can almost touch both sides at once if you raise your arms and don't mind a little scraping on the fingertips. The granite is the kind of pale gray that hasn't yet learned to be darker. It is dusted, in places, with a fine pale powder that is itself the rock becoming other rock. When the wind takes it, it does not billow; it whispers sideways in low sheets along the floor.

The floor is not level. The floor is a thing that is *happening*.

Down the center of the slot runs a river — the only honest water in the place, fed by snowmelt from a peak that does not concern us here. It is about a meter and a half wide, fast, milky with rock-flour, cold enough that the air above it shimmers even on a still morning. It does not run straight. It writhes. It has been here less time than the slot that holds it and it is already trying to leave, and the slot will not let it. Where the river hits a bend, it throws itself against the inner curve and the inner curve is too young to be smoothly deflected; the water detonates sideways in a flat sheet of spray that catches what little light reaches the bottom and turns it briefly into a moving thing. The sound of the river is not one sound. It is a hundred thousand small sounds layered: the tooth-sound of stones being tumbled, the deeper mutter of the channel, the percussive slap of water against fresh rock, the thin hiss of spray, and underneath all of that a low continuous roar that is the river remembering it is going downhill.

Above the river, the rest of the slot is *alive*, and here I use the word with care because no animal moves in this place. Nothing with a nervous system has elected to live here. The life is older than that, or at least less particular.

The walls are furred.

Mosses, in the usual sense, would be too polite a word. What climbs the rock face is a dense, layered, dark green-black felt of things that are not quite plants and not quite fungi and not quite lichen but are some negotiation among the three. They begin about two meters above the waterline and continue, unbroken, to where the slot walls flare outward and become the alpine slopes above. The color is the green of deep pond water, the green of a bruise that has decided to bloom instead of heal. The texture — you can see this from where you stand, if the spray cooperates — is not smooth. It is humped, ridged, lobed, in places ropy, in one place (about a hundred meters up on the left wall) a continuous series of small terraces, each one a few centimeters tall, each one holding a thin line of standing water that has never seen the sun. The whole living layer pulses gently. Not in a way that suggests breath. In a way that suggests *swelling*. As though each cell is slightly larger than it was a moment ago and the whole is a slow, patient ambition to occupy the rock entirely.

The felt is wet. Always wet. Condensation runs down it in fine sheets; water from a hundred invisible seepages in the rock above emerges through the felt and travels along its underside in channels that glisten when the light catches them. The smell is the smell of a wet stone left in a closet for a week, but multiplied, intensified, given a mineral edge, and under it a sweet rot, a fungal sweetness, the breath of something digesting rock and excreting patience.

The slot is dark. It is not the dark of a cave; the slot is open to the sky, which above is the particular pale enamel blue of high altitude, the blue of a place where the air has forgotten how to be thick. But the walls are tall and the sun, when it is overhead, sends a single blade of light down the slot's length, and the light, traveling at this angle, only reaches the floor for an hour or so in the middle of the day. The rest of the time the floor is in shadow, and the walls are in deeper shadow, and the felt on the walls gives back almost nothing. To stand in the bottom of the slot at any hour other than noon is to stand in a place that is actively swallowing the light. Your pupils will not stop dilating. There is always more dark to gather.

And it is loud.

I will try to be honest about the loud. It is not a comfortable loud. It is a *busy* loud. The river alone is the sound of a small factory. The wind, which is funneled down the slot from the slopes above and arrives compressed, accelerated, knife-shaped, is a second sound, a higher, keening sound, a sound that you feel on the skin of your forearms before you hear it. When the gusts strike the irregular young walls, the slot answers: a low resonant boom, then a sequence of smaller booms as the sound bounces back and forth across the width of the cut and dies in tatters against the far wall. There is a continuous, sighing, sliding sound from the felt on the walls as the wind drags across its wet surface. There are small percussive events: a stone coming loose in the wall above and bounding down in three or four sharp reports, the splash where it lands in the river, the small aftershock splash where a dislodged bit of felt follows it down. There is the sound of the felt itself, which is not a sound you can hear so much as feel through the rock at your back, a faint continuous crepitation, the sound of tiny things pushing tiny things aside, the sound of growth.

And on top of all of this — on top of the river and the wind and the booming and the splash and the creak and the slip — there is a sound that I will admit I cannot account for. I will describe it and you can do what you like with the description. It is a thin, high, intermittent tone, almost a whine, that seems to come from no fixed point. It moves. It rises. It falls. It is not the wind. I have heard the wind in this slot and it is not the wind. It is not the river. It is not any of the obvious small mechanical events of a steep place with water in it. It has a quality of *modulation*, as if something is adjusting itself, tuning. It lasts, each time, for about four seconds. It occurs roughly every ninety seconds. I do not know what is making it. I will not tell you it is the slot singing, because I do not know that. I will tell you that when I stood in the bottom of the slot with my hand against the wet wall, the tone arrived most strongly through the rock, and arrived faintly through the air, and when I removed my hand the air-arrival did not change and the rock-arrival disappeared. I will leave that there.

The slot is young, and the slot is in a hurry.

A granite wall of this height should not have, this close to its base, a continuous lobed felt of living tissue a hundred meters tall. A river this small should not have cut a slot this deep this fast. The math doesn't work. The math is still being worked out. The slot is, in a real geological sense, *in the middle of becoming what it will become*, and the becoming is not quiet. Stone is coming off the walls in the volume one associates with construction sites. The river is full of new stone. The felt is colonizing the new stone faster than the stone can finish being stone. The place smells of fresh mineral and of digesting mineral and these are not the same smell, and the difference is the difference between rock that has just been made and rock that is being unmade by something patient and dark and green.

There is no path. There is no ladder. There is no stake driven into the rock, no cairn, no painted blaze, no ribbon tied to a branch, no rusting piton, no flattened grass. Nobody comes here. The slot is not on any map you have seen. The slot is not on any map. The slot is too young to have been found, and too loud to have been stumbled into by accident, and too dark and wet to invite the second visit, and the second visit is the one that makes a place a place.

To stand in the slot is to stand in a place that is being assembled around you in real time, by a river and a wind and a felt and a tone that has no source, in a darkness that is not absence of light but the active digestion of it, with the sound of a small catastrophe going on continuously in your ears. The walls are not stable in any way that you would call stable. The felt is not stable. The tone is not stable. The slot is not stable. The slot is a sentence that is being spoken by the mountain and the sentence is not yet finished and the mountain has not yet decided what it means.
