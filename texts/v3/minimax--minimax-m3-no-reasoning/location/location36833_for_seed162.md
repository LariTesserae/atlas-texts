# The Kettle Before the Mountain

There is a country that is not a country but a kind of organism, a stretch of upland so wide and so high that it has made itself its own weather. From the floor of it, if you stand at the right notch, you cannot see the edge in any direction. The air is warm the way a closed room is warm, the way a body is warm — the heat has nowhere to go. It is high enough that the sky has fallen into it: low, dim, pressed close, a permanent grey ceiling at a height a tall tree could touch, and the light that filters through is not sunlight but the diffused memory of sunlight, a pewter sheen that makes colors bleed at their edges and shadows refuse to form properly. Things should cast shadows here and mostly do not.

The terrain is young. The rocks are sharp. They were not here long ago. The plateau is still rising, still being thrust upward by some pressure that has no name and does not need one, and as it rises it cracks, and the cracks become ridges, and the ridges become spines, and the spines become a city of stone whose streets no one paved because no one built it. It is the kind of geology that looks like it has been *placed* rather than *grown* — pale stone, almost white in the dim light, with edges that have not yet been softened. When you walk across it your foot makes a sound like a struck teacup. The whole country is a porcelain thing pretending to be a continent.

And yet the whole country is wet.

That is the strangeness. The damp is everywhere, in everything. It is not raining. It is not raining at all. But every surface is furred with moisture, every leaf is dark with it, every stone weeps. The air is so saturated that you cannot tell where the cloud ends and the ground begins. When the rare breeze moves — and it does, suddenly, in long slides, the way water moves under ice — the droplets shake loose from the ferns and the mosses and fall upward for a moment before they remember which way is down. Condensation runs down the inside of your lungs if you breathe too deep. There are rivers, but they are small and sly, threading the flat places between the ridges, and where they go they are not always flowing in the same direction twice. The moisture rises into the cloud and the cloud condenses into the moisture and the whole place is a single slow conversation between a sky and a floor that cannot decide which is which.

Into this wet stone the green has come in a fury.

Not a forest. Something more than a forest, more saturated, more *insistent*. It is as if the vegetation, having been given warmth and water and almost no light, has decided that the lack of light is a personal insult and is going to compensate for it by being everywhere at once. Every centimeter is taken. The plants have not waited for the geology to settle. They are growing on the ridges, in the cracks, on the impossible vertical faces, in the runoff channels of the phantom rivers, on top of each other. There are tiers. There are seven tiers in some places, eight in others, and the lowest tier is underground, white and pale and rootless, drawing from the damp. Ferns in shapes ferns do not have. Mosses that move when you aren't looking. A kind of cane that grows in dense stands and has a hollow joint at every meter that sings when the wind passes through it, very faintly, a sound like a stopped music box, which is the only sound this place makes without being asked to. Mushrooms the color of bruises the size of your hand. Vines that braid themselves into ropes and the ropes braid into thicker ropes and the thicker ropes braid into something that could be a net, or a hammock, or a language, suspended between the teeth of stone. The whole green is not a single ecosystem so much as a single appetite. It eats the rock. It eats the rain. It eats the light. You cannot see through any of it. If you fell into it, you would not hit the ground for a long time.

And the creatures.

There are many. The dimness is full of movement. You do not see them so much as you understand that the dimness is *populated*, that the air is thick with quick things. There is a small beast, no bigger than your thumb, that lives on the underside of every leaf and has too many legs and eyes that never close. There is a larger one that you only ever see in pieces — a patch of fur, a joint, a horn — moving through the undergrowth so continuously that the whole forest seems to be in slow lateral transit. There are things in the canopy that are not birds but make decisions like birds. There are things in the rivers that are not fish but have the manners of fish. Everything is alive. Everything is moving. Nothing is loud.

The silence is the part that gets to you, eventually. After you have been here a while you realize you have not heard a sound louder than your own breath in hours, in days, and yet the place is in constant motion. The wind moves the leaves and the leaves make no sound. The creatures call to each other and the calls are subsonic, felt in the sternum rather than heard, a continuous low hum that is less a sound than a property of the air, like humidity is a property of the air, like pressure. The cane-singing, when it happens, is the loudest thing in the country, and it is barely there. You could sleep through it. The place is quiet the way a turbine hall is quiet when the turbines are running perfectly — not silent but *hushed*, as if the loudness has been routed somewhere else, somewhere below hearing.

And below. That is the other thing.

The place is industrial. You have to stand still for a long time before you realize this. The rocks are not sitting on the rocks. The whole plateau *runs*. There is a slow, vast, hydraulic logic to the country, as if it has been engineered by no one, as if the engineering is a property of the stone itself, as if the mountains are pistons in a machine the size of a continent and the rivers are the lubrication and the plants are the gaskets and the creatures are the watchmen and none of them know. You can feel it if you put your hand to the ground. A vibration. Not trembling — *cycling*. A low, slow, patient rhythm, the rhythm of a thing that does not need to be in a hurry because it is large and old in the way that the very large are old, in the way that a planet is old.

The place is in the middle of doing something. It has not finished. The geology is still rising and the moisture is still cycling and the green is still advancing and the creatures are still being born and the machine is still running, and the whole of it is a single ongoing act, a vast slow verb that has been conjugating for a very long time and shows no sign of stopping. This is the turbulence. The country is not chaotic. The country is *mid-process*. The country is a factory in the middle of a shift, an engine that has been running since before anyone thought to ask what it was making. It is producing. It is producing something. The product is, possibly, more country. The product is, possibly, more time. The product is, possibly, the dim light, which does not come from any sun you can identify and falls without a source.

And it is breaking.

This is the part you do not want to look at directly, and also the part you cannot stop looking at, because it is happening in plain sight. The reality of the place is not entirely committed to being real. A rock will be a rock and then for a moment, at the corner of your vision, it will be a tooth, and then it will be a rock again, but the rock will be a slightly different rock, the rock will be the rock that *is* a tooth wearing a rock costume, and you will not be able to unsee it. A vine will braid itself into a shape that is almost a letter and then unbraid and apologize. The rivers will flow uphill in the middle distance and you will not be able to find a source for the upward-flowing part, only a long slow bend where the water consults itself and then decides. The creatures sometimes are their own shadows for a while, or are made of a substance that is not quite fur, not quite metal, not quite anything, a brushed grey that hums when you touch it. One of the ferns, in a place you will not be able to find again, was breathing. Not photosynthesizing. *Breathing*. In and out. The way you do.

The place is not broken. The place is *loosened*. As if the screws that hold the real to itself have been backed off a turn, and now the real is doing what it always wanted to do, which is show you what it is made of, which is something other than what you thought it was made of. The place is generous in this way. The place is showing you its seams. The place is letting the green be slightly more than green, letting the stone be slightly more than stone, letting the creatures be slightly more than creatures, and the slightly more is industrial, is mechanical, is the evidence of the engine under the country, the engine that is the country, the engine that is making the country in real time and has been since before you arrived and will be after you leave, and the engine is not built. The engine is not constructed. The engine is *happening*. The place is the factory floor of itself.

You will be here a long time. You will lose track of which direction is down. The dim light will not change. The damp will not stop. The plants will keep growing in directions that are not botanical. The creatures will keep doing what they do, which is be alive and also be other things. The machine under the country will keep cycling, slow, slow, slow, a heartbeat at the scale of a biome, and you will feel it in your feet and then in your knees and then in the back of your skull, and you will realize that the hum you have been hearing is not the creatures, is not the wind in the cane, is not the rivers, is the place itself, the place saying the only thing a place like this can say, which is its own name, over and over, in a language that is mostly consonants and a little bit of oil.

It is warm here. It is dim here. It is wet here and it is loud here, the loudest silence you have ever been inside, and you will stay.

You will stay because the place is making something and you would like to see what it is.

The place does not mind. The place is vast and the place has time and the place is in the middle of something, and you are welcome to be in the middle of it too, if you are quiet, if you do not mind the hum, if you can accept that the country is not a country, that the forest is not a forest, that the rain is not rain, that the creatures are not creatures, that the machine is not a machine, that the dim light is not light, that the damp is not damp, that the warmth is not warmth, that you are not, that you are not, that you are not, that you are —

— here.
