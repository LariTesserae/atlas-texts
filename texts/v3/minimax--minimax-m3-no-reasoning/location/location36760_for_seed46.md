# The Furnace of the First Weeds

The ground here is not finished being ground. It is still deciding.

Beneath your feet — if you can call the irregular lattice of fused mineral a floor — pale stone is rising. Slowly, with a patience that outlasts the body, calcium-white ridges push upward from a substrate that is half-basement, half-bone. A geology in its first trimester. The shapes are ambiguous: they could become arches, fangs, the knuckles of buried fingers, or the worn-down stumps of older mountains. None of them know yet. They do not need to. They are in the long, unambitious business of existing.

The air is hot, but it is not the heat of a sun beating down from any sky. There is no sky. There is only a low ceiling of brass-colored haze, close enough to feel like weather pressing against the scalp, and a light that comes from everywhere at once without being bright. The heat has no source. The heat is the medium. To be here is to be warmed from inside the air itself, as if the air were drinking something.

---

The plants are wrong, and they are abundant.

They rise from the cracks in the unfinished stone without complaint, and they are not plants. They are the *memory* of plants, or plants that have been told the rules and refused all of them. A pale tubular thing like an organ pipe, six feet tall, breathes out a faint hiss of cooler gas. Around its base, ferns of fused glass fan out in colors that have not been invented in the rest of the world — amber-gray, mothwing, the inside of a cheek. None of them drink. The place has no water. None of them photosynthesize. The light here is the wrong kind of light, and would not help them if it were the right kind.

So they have found another arrangement.

If you put your ear to the base of the largest one, you can hear a faint, sourceless ticking. Not mechanical. Not biological. A small private percussion, as if each specimen were keeping time with something happening elsewhere. When the ticking is loud, the plant is feeding. When it is quiet, the plant is *manufacturing*. The lushness here is not the lushness of growth. It is the lushness of **stockpiling**. Every leaf, every translucent frond, every ribbed and bulging stem is a reservoir. A warehouse. A silo for whatever is being sucked, very gently, out of the unfinished floor.

The stone is younger than the things that grow out of it. Erosion has not begun; the stone does not know it will be eroded. Erosion is a future that has not yet been scheduled. The plants have arrived before their substrate has finished arriving, and they treat this as a trivial inconvenience.

---

This place is small. You can tell because of the echo. Sound here does not travel as it should. If you speak, your voice returns from a direction you are not facing, having gone around an obstacle you cannot see. A shout makes a small dry clap and is gone. The whole environment fits in some pocket — a hidden terrarium, a folded space tucked between two larger things that have no interest in it. There is a wall somewhere behind the haze, but you will not find it by walking, only by reaching.

Within the pocket, however, the detail is excessive. The plants crowd nothing. They are **sparse**, scattered with the deliberate spacing of machines on a factory floor. Each specimen has its own station. Each station has been measured. The arrangement is industrial. The lushness is industrial. There is a layout here, the schematic of a facility disguised as a garden, and you are standing in aisle three, or seven, or two, and the aisles go on longer than the place.

---

The light is a normal light. This is the worst part. It is the light of a mid-afternoon in some unmemorable temperate city, the light of a desk, the light that does not call attention to itself. It pours onto plants that are not plants, onto stone that is not finished, into an air that has no source. It does not flinch. It does not thicken. It is the most ordinary thing in the room, and the room is wrong from the bottom up, and the light does not care.

So the colors are slightly off. Everything is slightly off. The glass-ferns do not quite look like ferns; they look like a translator's attempt at ferns. The brass haze is the right color but the wrong temperature. The stone ridges catch the ordinary light and give back an *opinion* about it, some hue no one has named, and you are not sure the light deserves.

---

There is no animal. Nothing moves except the slow, slow rise of the stone. Nothing breathes except the organ-pipe plants, and what they breathe out is not air. A lizard could not live here. A moth could not navigate. A microbe would be insulted. The fauna parameter has been set to zero, and the zero is not an absence — it is a *prohibition*. The lush things have not been joined by any hungry thing. They do not need to defend themselves. They do not need to advertise. They simply stand, ticking, stockpiling, manufacturing into their own bodies whatever the unfinished ground is offering up.

You begin to suspect, after a long time, that this is what they have always done. That they are not waiting for the geology to finish. They are the reason the geology is *allowed* to finish. They are, in their quiet industrial way, the factory floor on which a small piece of the world is being assembled. The pocket will be filled, eventually. The stone will learn what shape it is. The brass haze will either thicken into a sky or admit that it never meant to.

And then, the way the place is built, it will be folded up and put away, and the lush impossible plants will fold with it, and somewhere else a different pocket will be opened, and the same patient slow stockpiling will begin again, in a different aisle, under the same ordinary light.

---

The sound of the place, if you stand still long enough, is the ticking. Dozens of them, faintly out of phase, like a watchmaker's workshop the size of a cathedral, very far away. It is not loud. It is not quite silence. It is the sound of inventory being taken, continuously, by things that have nowhere to be.
