The first thing is not a thing at all. It is the absence of sound, the way it sits on the eardrum like a hand.

Then light, which is the color of a smudge. Gray that has forgotten blue. It comes from everywhere and nowhere, a thin scrim of it hanging in the air three meters off the ground and never quite resolving into a sky. Above the scrim is the suggestion of a colder, paler gray, the inverted image of the one below it. The horizon is where they meet, and the horizon is very far away.

You are standing on what was once high and is now settled. The elevation reads in the lungs first, a small tight feeling at the back of the throat, and in the mind as a suspicion that things are too big. The plain extends in every direction, a soft dark plate of weathered stone, basalt or something older, polished by a long time until every edge has been folded under. It is not flat in the way of floors. It is flat in the way of water that has stopped moving. There are rises where the stone has bunched, shallow swells like knuckles under a sheet, and between them are the pockets. Pools. Dark, still, rimmed with a thin white crust of mineral or salt or something colder. The moisture is mostly in the air, but it falls here, condenses, sits. The wind is not a wind. It is a slow turning of the whole atmosphere, a vast and patient migration of cold air, and where it passes over the pools it lifts a low ghost of vapor that creeps along the ground and joins the scrim of light above.

Nothing has been built. There is no wall, no road, no sign that anything was ever placed here by hands. The stone is undisturbed except by time, and time has done its work so completely that the disturbance has become the texture. This is a place that has forgotten being changed.

But the silence has weight, and the weight has a source.

They are the fauna, and they are not sparse so much as sparse in the way that mountains are sparse. There are not many. The plain is a thousand kilometers to the next one. But what is here, is here. You can see one from where you stand, although the distance is deceptive. It rises from the stone as if it had been lying down and only now, after centuries, had decided to stand. It is the color of the stone, or the stone is the color of it — the same dark gray, the same long weathering, the same folded edges. It is impossible to tell where one ends and the other begins, except that the creature is breathing. Not audibly. The breath is visible. A faint heat-shimmer, or what would be a heat-shimmer in warmer air — a slow distortion of the gray light at the rim of its flanks, a steady visible pulse that means the air is being moved out and pulled in by a structure the size of a small hill. It is not a mammal. It is not anything in the old categories. Its outline is too geometric, in places — a long even ridge where the back should be, a faceted shoulder, a flank that flattens into a plane and then curves under. It is not made. It is grown. But the growing has a logic to it that does not feel like accident. The proportions are too even. The right angles are too right.

There is no sound from it. The breathing makes no sound. The slow shifts of its weight, when it makes them once or twice in an hour, do not make a sound. The only sound is the absence of sound, and the way the absence has texture. There is a subsonic pressure, perhaps. A feeling in the inner ear, a small unmooring, as if the air is not quite still.

It is working. That is the only word that fits, although the working is not the working of any industry you would recognize. There is a seam in its flank, or what might be a seam — a long line where the surface changes texture, becomes faintly ridged, slightly warmer in the visible spectrum. From this seam, very slowly, a dark liquid is being extruded. It runs down the flank in a thin sheet, finds a groove, follows the groove to a vent or aperture lower on the body, and from there drops in a long heavy thread to the stone. The thread falls, hits, spreads, and a tiny channel is cut, and the channel is the length of a man and the width of a finger and it took perhaps an hour to be made. The creature is processing the stone, or the air, or the moisture. The material it extrudes is not waste, or if it is, it is also a fertilizer, a binder, a feed. Where the dark liquid has run, the stone looks different — darker, smoother, faintly metallic. After many years, a small apron of new material extends from the creature's flank and meets the plain. After a long time, a new surface has been made, and it is the surface the plain is now made of. The old stone is being slowly consumed, slowly replaced. The creature is a foundry. It is a refinery. It is, on the timescale of the plain, an industrial process, and it is alive, and it is silent.

There are others, at distances that defeat understanding. One to the north, a darker shape against the gray. One far to the south, a smudge that might be two. None closer than a morning's walk, none farther than a day. They are evenly spaced. The spacing is not accident.

The cold is not felt as cold because the air is so still. The temperature is in the lungs, in the slow formation of frost on anything that breathes out. The water in the pockets is dark and is not frozen, which is strange at this temperature, and is not explained. The mineral rims around the pockets are not ice. The vapor that rises from them does not rise. It sinks. It falls back, and is replaced, and the cycle is so slow it has to be inferred.

The vegetation is a rumor. There is a thing that might be lichen on a few of the higher stones, a thin orange film that does not seem to be alive so much as to be a property of the stone. There is a single upright stalk in the distance, very far, a black thin line against the gray, and it does not move. It might be a stem. It might be a piece of the old stone that has survived differently. The fauna do not eat it. The fauna do not seem to eat at all, in any way visible, in any way that produces motion. Whatever they take in, they take in through the stone or the air or the light, and whatever they put out, they put out in the long slow seams.

The light does not change. There is no day. There is no night. There is the gray, and the gray varies in opacity but not in color, and the opacity changes on a cycle longer than a lifetime. The eye adjusts. The pupils dilate into the dim and find a world of small differences, of fine shadows, of the glint of moisture in a groove, of the faint heat-haze at the flank of the distant hill-that-breathes. The world is not dark. It is unlit. It is what light does when there is not enough of it to be a thing.

The weirdness is in the seams. The weirdness is in the right angles on the flanks. The weirdness is that the spacing of the creatures is too even. The weirdness is that the moisture collects in the same pockets, the same low places, year after year, and the new dark material from the flanks extends in the same directions, and the channels cut by the extruded liquid always curve in the same arcs. The weirdness is not enough to make the place a dream. It is only enough to make the place feel watched, or held, or tended, by something that does not show itself and does not need to.

This is a place that has been here a long time. It will be here longer. The creatures do not hurry. The stone does not hurry. The light does not hurry. The silence is the only sound, and the silence is very, very patient.
