# The Sodium Mire

The ground here does not behave as ground should.

It heaves, for one. Slow undulations, like the breathing of something buried so deep it has forgotten it is buried. Underfoot: a crust the color of old bone, chalky and warm to the touch despite everything, perforated with breathing holes from which a fine mist exhales on a cycle you can almost — but never quite — predict. Through the crust, when you press your weight against it, the dark beneath shifts with the sluggish patience of something thick. It is not water. It is not oil. It has the refractive index of both and the mercy of neither.

I should not have come here. I should not be writing this. But I am writing this, so I am here, so I will describe what I see and you must do with it what you will.

---

**Terrain.** The land has the topography of a rumor. Once, perhaps, there were hills. Once, perhaps, there were rivers. Whatever cataclysm attended the unraveling of this place scoured it into a broad, shallow depression — miles across, ringed at its far horizons by the crumbling stumps of mountains that have been weathered down to their philosophical essence. What remains between is flat the way a body of water is flat: a surface tension over enormous depth. The crust floats on that depth, or adheres to it, or is slowly being digested by it — none of the locals (and I will come to them) can agree.

The crust itself is treacherous in a particular way. It is thick where you do not step and thin where you do. Walking here is a series of small surrenders. Each footfall either holds or doesn't, and when it doesn't, you do not fall — you *subside*, slowly, your legs swallowed to the calf or the knee in the warm dark below, and the dark closes around your boots with a sound like a mouth remembering how to speak. You learn to move quickly. You learn to trust the lighter patches. You learn, eventually, to step on the things that look most like they shouldn't hold your weight, because the things that look sturdy have usually been hollowed out from below by the burrowers, and a false crust is worse than a thin one. A thin one you can feel give. A false one waits until the third step.

---

**Materials.** Everything is sodium.

I do not mean this as a metaphor. I mean the crust is salt — but not the salt of the sea, not the salt of the kitchen, not even the salt of the deep mines. It is a salt that has been *refined*, somewhere upstream of the visible landscape, by processes I cannot begin to trace. It is sodium the way steel is iron: deliberately, industrially, with intention. The crust is sodium chloride in some places, gleaming pink with lithified algae that never existed. Sodium hydroxide in others, where the surface weeps a thin clear film that is not water and should not be touched with bare hands. Sodium carbonate in the chalky uplands at the margins, where the crust has dried into dunes of white powder that move when the wind passes, which is often. The dunes sing. Not metaphorically. The crystals shear against each other and the sound is a low chorus, almost subsonic, that you feel in the back of your teeth before you hear it.

In the deepest depressions, where the crust has failed entirely, there are pools. They glow. A faint, intermittent luminescence, the color of a match struck and forgotten. Whatever is dissolved in those pools is giving off that light, and what is dissolved in those pools is not, by any assay I trust, supposed to exist.

---

**Atmosphere.** Wet. Perpetually, oppressively wet, but not with water. The air is saturated with brine. You do not sweat here; you *crystallize*. Exposed skin acquires a fine white dusting within minutes. Eyelashes frost. Every breath tastes of the sea at its most metallic, plus something underneath — a dry mineral tang, like licking a battery, like the aftertaste of licking a battery in a dream where the battery was alive.

Above: a sky. I have to call it a sky. There is something up there, something that occupies the space a sky would occupy, but it is not the sky I was born under. It is the color of weak tea. It is the color of weak tea held up to a light you cannot see. It moves, slowly, in patterns that do not correspond to any weather I have ever witnessed. The light that filters down through it is diffuse, sourceless, directionless — the kind of light that makes shadows impossible and depth uncertain. Things ten feet away look like things a hundred feet away. Things a hundred feet away look like things painted on the back of your eyelids.

It is never dark here. It is also never light. It is always this dim, sourceless, sour wash, as if the sodium itself were faintly radioactive and the entire landscape were glowing with its own exhausted light.

---

**Sound.** Loud.

I keep coming back to this because it surprised me most. I had expected silence. I had expected the hush of dead places, of places stripped of biology, of mineral worlds where nothing moves and nothing calls and the only sound is the wind. That is not what this is.

There is a sound here, and it never stops. It is the sound of *industry*. Massive industry. The sound of foundries running at capacity, of refineries in full production, of stamping presses and rolling mills and the great deep-throated roar of furnaces that never bank. The sound is everywhere. It comes from below the crust, from within the dunes, from inside the glowing pools. It comes from the sky itself, sometimes, in great slow rolls like distant thunder that has been *domesticated*, broken to harness, put to work. The sound is rhythmic in places and arrhythmic in others, and where the rhythms intersect they produce a beat, a phase, a kind of music that is not for you.

It is loudest at the center, where the crust is thinnest, where the pools are densest, where the ground moves fastest. I did not go to the center. I do not think anyone goes to the center.

---

**Fauna.** They are here. They are abundant. They are not alive.

I have to be careful here. I do not mean they are dead. I mean they are not alive in the way the word *alive* usually means. They are active. They are present. They have purposes, and they pursue those purposes, and the purposes are *industrial* in character.

They are small, mostly. The size of crabs, mostly. The size of rats, some of them. A few as large as dogs. Their bodies are sodium — chitinous plates of translucent salt, jointed with the same dark substance that pools beneath the crust. They move with a clicking, purposeful industry. They mine. They refine. They carry.

I watched a procession of them for an hour. They emerged from a hole in the crust, each clutching a fragment of — something, some darker mineral, some denser salt — and they carried it in a column across the crust to another hole, where they disappeared. Each one carried the same load. Each one moved at the same pace. Each one clicked its plates in the same rhythm, which contributed, I realized, to the great ambient roar — they were part of it. They were a *fraction* of it.

There are larger ones. I did not see them well. I saw shapes moving under the crust, in the pools, in the deeper brine. Shapes that turned, slowly, and regarded me with the patient attention of machines that had been asked to consider whether I was relevant to their operations. I was not relevant. They turned away. I was grateful.

There are things in the sky, too. Whether they are fauna or atmosphere or something else, I cannot say. They are dark. They move against the dim light like holes in the air. They make no sound. Once, one of them passed directly overhead, and the ground beneath it *brightened*, briefly, as if something had been inspected.

---

**Vegetation.** None.

I should say: none that I recognize as vegetation. There are structures in the dunes that look, from a distance, like coral. Branching, calcified, growing in patterns that suggest response to light and current. But they do not grow. They are deposited. They are extruded. They are the residue of processes that have nothing to do with biology. If you break a piece off — and I broke a piece off, because I am a fool — the inside is layered. Concentric. Like the growth rings of a tree, except the rings are not years. They are shifts in the brine chemistry. They are the *shifts in the brine chemistry recorded in mineral*. The whole landscape is a record of itself. It is a record that is still being written.

I have not eaten since I arrived. There is nothing here to eat. There is also nothing here that is *not* something, if you follow me. The crust is sodium. The pools are sodium. The fauna are sodium. The dunes are sodium. The sky is sodium. I am being slowly salted. I can feel it on my skin, in my lungs. In another day I will be part of the record.

---

**Tech.** Yes.

I want to be precise about this. I do not see machines. I do not see factories, engines, smokestacks, power lines. I see none of the apparatus of industry. And yet: this place *is* industry. This place is industry the way a reef is a city, the way a forest is a factory. The entire landscape is a single integrated operation, and the operation has been running for — I do not know how long. The erosion here is *ancient*. The oldest surfaces have been weathered down to their philosophical essence. But the operation is ongoing. It has not stopped. It may never have started; it may simply *be*, the way a river simply is.

The fauna are part of it. The brine is part of it. The crust, the pools, the dunes, the sky — all part of it. Whatever produced this place is still producing this place, and what it is producing is more of itself, refined and re-refined, a closed loop of mineral becoming mineral becoming mineral, with the fauna as its catalysts and the pools as its crucibles and the great roar as its breath.

I think the sky is a byproduct. I think the dim sourceless light is slag, is exhaust, is the faint radioactive glimmer of a process that has been running so long its emissions have replaced the atmosphere. I think the atmosphere itself, the weak-tea color, the brine-saturated air, the perpetual dim — all byproduct. All waste. All the slow exhausted breath of a machine that has forgotten it is a machine.

---

**Weirdness.** I have been circling it. I will name it now.

The place is wrong. Not metaphorically wrong. *Topologically* wrong. I noticed it first in the dunes. The dunes cast shadows that do not correspond to the angle of the light. I checked. I walked around them. I traced their outlines. The shadows are correct in length but incorrect in *direction*, as if they are being cast by a sun that is not where the sky suggests the sun is, or by a sun that has been *edited* out of the sky and only its shadow-producing function remains.

Then the pools. I approached a pool. I saw my reflection. My reflection was correct in color and approximate in shape. But it was facing the wrong direction. It was facing *me*, which is correct, but I was facing *away from it*, which is not. I turned around. The reflection turned around. It was correct again. I did not approach the pool again.

The distances are wrong. I have been walking for three hours and I have not reached the ring of mountains at the horizon. The mountains have not grown. They remain at the same approximate distance, the same approximate size. The crust between me and them has remained the same approximate crust. I think I am walking in place. I think the place is *eating* the distance, metabolizing it, using it as fuel for whatever process the great roar is sustaining.

The sounds are wrong. The great roar has a *structure* that I keep almost recognizing. It is organized. It is hierarchically organized. There are rhythms within rhythms, processes within processes. I think, sometimes, that I can hear *phrases*. I think, sometimes, that I can hear *sentences*. I do not listen. I do not want to hear the sentences.

The fauna click. The fauna click in patterns that are not random. The fauna click in patterns that *answer* something, deep below, that I cannot hear. There is a conversation here. There has been a conversation here for a very long time. I am interrupting it only in the sense that my presence has required a small adjustment to the conversation, the way a particle in a cloud chamber requires a small adjustment to the cloud.

---

**What I am doing here.**

I came looking for something. I do not now remember what it was. I think the place has *edited* that memory, the way it has edited the shadows, the way it has edited the distance. I think I will stay here for a while. I think the place will have me. I think the place will *record* me — I think the concentric rings inside the broken coral-dune will, in some future that is also some past, include a faint trace of me, a layer slightly darker than the layers above and below, the signature of an interruption, a particle in a cloud, a small irrelevance in a vast and patient industry.

I am going to put down my pen. I am going to walk toward the mountains. I do not think I will arrive. I do not think it matters.

The crust is firm under my feet. The brine is on my lips. The roar is in my chest. Somewhere, far below, something is being refined.

I will be part of it soon.
