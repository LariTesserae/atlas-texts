# The Condensate Flats

You stand on the lip of what was, perhaps, a caldera. Perhaps. The word *caldera* implies fire, and fire implies something that burned, and burning implies a chemistry this place has no memory of. But the geometry — that broad concavity, that ring of high ground leaning inward like cupped palms — suggests something emptied, something that withdrew. The stone here is the color of a bruise going yellow. It is cold in a way that has nothing to do with the air, which is barely air at all.

The elevation is real. You can feel it in the thinness, in the way each breath feels like sipping through a straw. The sky above is not so much dark as *absent of effort to be lit*. A black so flat it has no gradient, no constellations, no memory of any sun that may once have crossed it. Whatever photons exist here are not coming from up there. They are coming from *down here*, from the city.

---

The city.

It does not glitter. It does not glow in any way that the eye was designed to receive. It *persists*. You become aware of it gradually, as your pupils widen and your retina begins to register wavelengths the visible spectrum politely declines to acknowledge. The structures — if structures is the right word, if *right* is a category that survives this place — rise in dense-packed columns from the floor of the basin. Not skyscrapers. Not spires. Something between a stalagmite and a stalactite, grown rather than built, though the mathematics of their growth obey rules no geologist would recognize. They cluster. They touch. They form lattices that, from a distance, read as a single undulating surface, and from close up, as canyons of dark metallic ribbon, as branching capillaries, as the inside of a lung that has forgotten how to exhale.

There are thousands of them. Tens of thousands. The basin extends for miles in every direction you can pan, and the density does not relent. It is a city in the way a nerve ganglion is a city: a knot of activity so concentrated that the concept of *building* dissolves into *tissue*.

---

The fauna is the part that requires you to sit down, if you can find a place to sit that isn't also fauna.

They are not large. Most are the size of a hand, of a fist, of a spread-fingered palm. Their silhouettes are angular in ways that hurt to look at — not because they are sharp, but because they describe a geometry you cannot place. Think of a paper crane folded from a rectangle that was never flat. Think of a starfish that learned to walk on five ankles. Think of something with the cross-section of an open wound and the patience of a cathedral. Their surfaces are matte black, the same black as the sky, but they carry that faint not-light the city gives off, as if they are listening to a frequency and re-emitting it as a kind of low-grade bioluminescence that is not bioluminescence.

They are *everywhere*.

On every horizontal surface. Inside every gap between the city's vertical growths. Pouring along the ground in slow rivers, parting around your boots, resuming behind you with the indifference of water around a pier. They are stacked. They are nested. They make sounds against each other — chitter, scrape, a low subsonic vibration you feel in the molars rather than hear — and the aggregate of these sounds is the *loudness* of this place. It is not a roar. It is a hum. It is the sound of a forest if every leaf were an insect. It does not stop. It does not modulate. It is the resting respiration of a place that is, in some fundamental sense, *breathing them*.

They do not appear to notice you. Or rather — and this is worse, somehow — they notice you the way a person notices a new piece of furniture. There is a brief local reorganization. A few of them rotate to present a different facet. Then they resume.

---

The water is the part you keep forgetting until you step.

It is in the air. Not as mist, not as fog — there is no visible moisture, no cloud, no condensation you can wipe from a surface. But it is *in* the air, the way salt is in seawater, and you can taste it on the back of your tongue at all times. It is in the stone. The rock underfoot, which from a distance reads as solid as old concrete, is *saturated*. It is weeping, though no film is visible. When you press a palm to it, the chill transfers with a wetness that seems to come from inside the rock, from somewhere below the surface, as if the geology itself is a sponge that was wrung out long ago and is now slowly, slowly, reabsorbing. There are no puddles. There are no streams. There is only the *possibility* of water, the imminent water, the water-that-is-about-to-arrive, and you walk through it without ever walking through it.

This is why everything is glistening. Not wet-glistening, but *cold*-glistening. The faint not-light from the city catches on the surfaces of ten thousand angular things and on the surfaces of ten thousand matte-black creatures and on the surface of the rock and on the surface of you, and every surface says: *I am here. I am at the dew point. I am about to become water. I am waiting.*

---

The dynamic of this place is glacial. You will not see a thing change. You could sit here for an hour — a subjective hour, a unit of time that the thin air makes elastic — and the configuration of creatures on the nearest vertical surface will be, as near as you can determine, *the same configuration*. They are patient. They are not slow; *slow* implies a thing that wants to go faster. They are *patient*. The time signature of this place is geological. Whatever they are doing, they are doing it on a scale where your presence is a single frame in a film reel a thousand kilometers long.

And yet — the erosion parameter. *Emerging.* This place is *being made*. The lowest tier of the city's growth, the part that meets the rock, is rough. It is not finished. It is the work of a thing that is still deciding what shape to have. The creatures, in their slow patience, are *constructing*. They carry fragments — no larger than a grain of sand to your eye, but a fragment is a fragment — and they deposit them, and the deposit is the city, and the city is not done. There are unfinished edges everywhere, places where the lattice opens into raw mineral, places where the next layer has not yet been laid. You are in a workshop. You are in a *womb*, in some sense, but a womb the size of a valley and a hundred thousand workers deep.

---

The tech is the part you should not examine closely.

You don't, at first. The eye slides off the city's surface. It is the visual equivalent of a word on the tip of your tongue. The vertical growths are not made of material; they are made of *arrangement*. The lattice is not architecture; it is a *standing wave* that has decided to take solid form for the convenience of the creatures. Look closely — and the creatures do not stop you from looking closely; they simply make room, the way a crowd makes room for someone crouching to tie a shoe — and the things that appeared to be dark metallic ribbon resolve into nothing, into a disturbance, into a fold in the local rules. The physics here is not lying to you. It is simply not speaking your language. The creatures are *engineering* it. They are the workforce of a project that uses the vacuum and the dark and the wet-cold as raw materials and produces, as output, *structure*. Whether that structure is for them, of them, or *is* them, you cannot tell, and you suspect the question is malformed.

There is no infrastructure in the human sense. There is no road, no wire, no pipe. But there is *conveyance*. Information, energy, matter — these flow through the lattice in ways you can almost track, the way you can almost track a thought through the convolutions of a brain you are looking at for the first time. The creatures are at every junction. They are, perhaps, the junctions.

---

The cold is not a temperature. It is a *condition*. It is the default state of a place where the last warm thing happened so long ago that the concept of warm has been retired. The rock is cold. The air is cold. The creatures are cold. Your breath, if you could see it, would crystallize instantly and fall to the ground and become part of the construction, but you cannot see it, so it simply *happens*, somewhere down there, in the unseen maintenance of the place.

You are the warmest thing here. You are a small bright transient in a field of patient cold. The creatures do not mind. They simply continue, and the hum continues, and the not-light continues, and the city continues, unfinished, emerging, patient as a glacier and dense as a reef and dark as the inside of a closed eye.

---

You stay longer than you intended. You are not sure why. Perhaps because the hum is doing something to the rhythm of your heartbeat, syncing it, slowing it, until you feel you are almost one of them. Perhaps because the place is so *complete* in its otherness that it has the gravitational pull of a well-built sentence. Perhaps because the water in the air is about to become water on your skin and you want, in some way you cannot fully justify, to be there when it does.

The hum does not change.

The dark does not lift.

The city builds itself, one fragment at a time, and the creatures carry the fragments, and the rock weeps its slow invisible weeping, and the cold continues, and the cold continues, and the cold continues.
