# The Upper Roost

They come up through the throat of the world.

Past the last scrub pine, past the lichen-crusted shoulders of the second plateau, past the place where the trail dissolves into nothing but intention and loose scree, the mountain keeps climbing. It does not stop climbing. It does not intend to. There is a place near the top where it is only a knife — only a single eroded spine of dark rock dividing two nowhere-views — and on the windward side of that spine, a person can stand and be at the same time inside the sky and inside the dark.

This is the upper roost. It is a small place. A person could walk across its long dimension in less than half a morning. But it is a place with weight.

---

The floor of the roost is a shallow bowl, not carved but *worn* — worn by the slow arguments of water and the slower agreements of stone, worn by the constant patient attention of bodies too numerous to count. The bowl is perhaps two hundred paces across at its widest, narrowing at the eastern end where a crack in the rock runs vertical and disappears into a depth that no one has ever measured because no one has ever wanted to. The rock itself is dark. A volcanic something-or-other from the time the mountain was younger and more dramatic. It has been here long enough to be patient with itself. The surface is not smooth. It is *finished*. Generations of claws and the slow chemistry of guano have given it a polish that looks like the worn palm of a hand.

Water lives here as a rumor. After a storm, a thin sheet runs down from the higher rocks and pools in the cracked places, and the whole roost drinks from these temporary puddles for a week. Most of the time, the moisture is in the air. It comes in off the wet slope a thousand feet below, climbs the cliff face as fog, and condenses on every cold surface in a slow, patient, indefinite manner. The dark walls of the roost are damp to the touch at any hour. Condensation beads on everything. A person who sits still for half an hour acquires a skin of fine droplets. The smell is mineral and faintly sweet — guano and wet rock and the green distance far below, all of it arriving together.

The air is warm for the altitude, which is wrong, and everyone who comes up here knows it is wrong, and no one stays long enough to make a study of it. Warm air rises from the valley; it should be colder up here, near the spine of the world. But the roost traps what it gets, and the bodies trap more, and the rock walls hold the day's heat and breathe it out in the early morning in a soft mineral exhalation. Mornings are the warmest part. By afternoon a real mountain wind comes through, and the roost is a different place.

---

What lives here is what made the roost the roost.

Bats. Bats beyond any reasonable count. Bats in the walls and bats in the cracks and bats in the ceiling of the deeper grottos where a person cannot go without ducking. They are small and dark and the color of the rock itself, and when they are at rest they are nearly invisible, and when they are in motion they are the air. The roost is loud with them. Not the high thin piping one might expect — a deeper sound, a continuous soft percussive wash, the sound of a thousand small wings folding and unfolding in the dark. It is the constant background. It is the texture of the place. After a few minutes a person stops hearing it the way a person stops hearing rain, and then it becomes the thing the silence would have been without it.

There are other things. A pale, eyeless salamander lives in the deepest wet cracks, and is seen only when a pool forms and a hand is cupped into it. Pale moths, enormous for moths, hang in the corners and vibrate when the wind comes. Something that may be a spider builds a web the size of a doorway between two columns of rock, and the web is so old and so often mended that it has become architecture rather than capture, and a person can press a palm into it and feel the layered weight of every repair.

The birds do not come up this high. The birds have given this altitude over. What flies in the roost is the things that fly in the roost.

---

Vegetation, such as it is, is a kind of insistence. In the crack at the eastern end, where some soil has accumulated in the millennia that things have been dying up here, a single stunted fir has rooted. It is not a tree. It is the idea of a tree, a personification, a sculpture the wind has made out of a tree's intention. It leans permanently eastward. Its needles are short and gray. It has been there longer than the oldest memory of the valley below, and it will be there after the last memory. It does not grow. It persists. Around its roots, in the brief weeks after the rains, a thin green fur of moss and lichen appears and disappears, and that is the only green the upper roost ever sees.

---

A person can stand in the upper roost and be very small and very quiet, and that is the correct way to be there. The light is thin. The sun reaches the floor of the bowl only in the late afternoon, and then only for an hour, and the light is the color of weak tea, filtered through whatever the air is doing, and it does not warm. The dark is the dominant mode. The dark has weight. The dark has texture. The dark holds the smell of ten thousand years of small warm bodies sleeping, and it is not unpleasant, only overwhelming, only the way the air in a long-closed church is overwhelming, the thickness of accumulated presence.

It is, all told, a place that has finished becoming itself. There is nothing to build here. Nothing to take. Nothing to improve. The mountain has settled into the shape it will keep, and the small dark fliers have settled into it, and the stunted fir has settled, and the salamander in its crack, and the moth in its corner, and the spider in its ancient layered web. The roost is what it is. The only change is the slow patient accumulation of small deaths, and the slow patient return of their substance to the stone.

A person who has come up here, and who is sitting with their back against a damp column of dark rock, and who is listening to the ten thousand small wings fold and unfold in the dark — a person in this place begins, after a time, to feel something like being a guest in a very old house. Not welcome, not unwelcome. Just present, in a place that has been full for a long time, and will be full after.
