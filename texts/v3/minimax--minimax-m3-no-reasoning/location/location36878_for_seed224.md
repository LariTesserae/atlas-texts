# The Inverted Pocket

You reach it, if you reach it, through a seam.

Not a door. Not yet a door. A place where the rock thins and the geometry suggests a passage, and then you are inside it, though "inside" is the wrong word and the rock is also the wrong word. The throat is short. The chamber is meant to be missed.

The chamber is not large. You could walk across it in ninety seconds if you walked with intent, and the ceiling is low enough that an adult cannot stand fully upright without the dark scraping the crown of their head. There is no horizon. There is no sky. The light is not coming from anywhere you can find; it is the color of dust suspended in amber, and it seems to be a property of the volume itself, dimmer near the floor, as if the floor is drinking it. You do not get used to the dim. You stop noticing it, which is different.

The air is thin and cold and absolutely dry. Cold in the way of museum cases, of high alpine nights, of lunar shadow — a cold that does not move, that has had time to settle into the grain of every surface and to forget, over those millennia, what warmth was for. The walls are stone, but the stone is wrong in places. Some of it is the gray granularite you would expect in a cold mountain — feldspar and quartz, microcline, the slow crystallized memory of a magma that never quite melted. Some of it is something else. Some of it is a material you cannot scratch with a knife and cannot name and cannot chip with a hammer, and where this material occurs it has the patient smoothness of glass that has been cooling since before the room existed. The two substances meet along edges that are not quite edges, that you can press your thumb against without finding the seam. The granularite is the older thing. You can tell. It is the wall that has been here. The smooth material is what was added.

The floor is mostly granularite, and the erosion on it is not wind-erosion and not water-erosion and not the patient even that came up from an ice. It is the erosion of being stood in. The floor has been walked on for a long time, and what walked on it was not human, and not bipedal, and not always the same shape. The wear is a palimpsest of presences. You can read it only as a general softness, a lowering of relief, an unwillingness in the rock to refuse weight.

The artifacts in the chamber are few.

There are six of them, perhaps seven — you keep not being sure whether the seventh exists. They are arranged without pattern you can read, scattered across the floor with a sparsity that feels deliberate and a randomness that feels inevitable, the way pebbles settle at the foot of a scree. Each is small enough to pick up, though you would not, or would not again. Each is made of the smooth material. Each is geometric in a way that does not match geometry.

One is a low cylinder, perhaps knee-high, sealed at both ends. Its surface is unmarked, and the closer you look the more certain you are that nothing is on it, and the less certain you become that anything is on it. You stop looking. If you looked again you would see that the cylinder's shadow does not agree with the dim amber of the room. The shadow falls in a direction that has no corresponding light source, and it is the wrong shape — too long, too sharp, falling toward a wall that is not in the right place to receive it. You decide this is an optical thing and leave it alone, which is the correct decision.

Another is a thin upright rod, the height of a hand, leaning slightly. It has been leaning for a long time; the floor under its foot is dished. The rod is not metal. The rod is not stone. The rod is the color of the amber light, except in three places where it is the color of the air, which is to say it is not there. Through those places you can see the wall behind it. The rod is broken across them, or the wall is broken across it. You do not touch the rod. The rod is cold in a way that is colder than the air.

A third object is a slab, waist-high, leaning against a wall. It is featureless on the side facing you. If you walked around it, the other side is also featureless. If you walked around it again, you would find that you had not moved.

There are more. A small dish, inverted, with nothing under it. A ring of material set into the floor, the inside of which is not the inside of anything. A coiled wire of the smooth substance, slack, that picks up no light.

The chamber is silent. The silence is not the silence of an empty room, which is filled with the soft hum of your own blood and the softening of your own breath. It is a deeper silence. The chamber has been silent for a long time and the silence has had time to settle into the granularite and the smooth material both. When you speak, your voice goes out and arrives, and it is thinner than it should be, and it does not echo. It is taken. The room accepts your voice in the way a closed jar accepts a stone.

There is no fauna. There are no plants. There is no fungus, no lichen, no biofilm, no mineral-eating slime. There is no dust, except in the strict sense that the air is full of the small motes you would call dust, but the motes do not settle, and they do not drift, and they have not been observed to move. They are a part of the light. If you sweep your hand through them they part around your hand and reassemble behind it as if you had not been there. They are the room's memory of disturbance.

There is no water. There has perhaps never been water. The cold here is not the cold of ice; it is the cold of absence. The cold of vacuum, or of a sealed volume that has never been opened to weather. The air is not dry; it is anhydrous. It does not have dryness in it; it is the kind of air that water could be introduced to and would not recognize.

The dynamics of the room are slow. The amber light has dimmed, perhaps, since you entered, or perhaps you are tiring. The cylinder's shadow has moved, perhaps, in your peripheral vision, or perhaps you have turned. The granularite floor is wearing, perhaps, under your feet, or perhaps not. The seventh artifact is more present than it was, or less. You would have to stay for a long time to be sure.

It is cold here, and the cold is patient. It is quiet here, and the quiet is patient. The room is small and the room is old and the room does not entirely commit to being a place.

You can leave the same way you came, if you remember the seam, and if the seam remembers you. The granularite will continue its slow erosion under nothing. The smooth material will continue to be the thing it is not. The light will dim, or not, by an amount you would not be able to measure without staying.

The room will be here.
