# The Lung of the Upper Air

I am writing this from inside the place, so I cannot vouch for the perspective. The light is wrong here in a way I find difficult to name. It is not dark—something is shining, has been shining for as long as anything in this place has been old—but the shine is *reluctant*, the way a fire is reluctant in wet wood, giving off more heat than illumination. I have never seen a sky here. I suspect there is one, but it has been forgotten by everything beneath it, or it has been built over by something that decided to be sky instead.

What I am standing on is not, strictly, ground.

It is a forest that has decided it is no longer interested in being trees.

---

Imagine a plateau. Imagine it lifted to where the air thins enough that breathing becomes a small ceremony, a thing one does deliberately. Imagine the plateau is not stone. Imagine it is *root*. Imagine the root is older than the memory of stone in this place and has subsumed stone into itself, layer by layer, the way coral makes an island by forgetting it was once an animal. The plateau is four miles across, perhaps, or forty—I cannot tell, because there is no horizon line, only a slow curving away of everything into haze and the distant suggestion of more.

Everything is plant. There is no dirt visible. There is no rock visible. There are no paths. There are no clearings. Where one would expect a clearing, there is instead a *species*—a kind of moss, or vine, or fungus-that-is-also-vine—that has come to occupy that conceptual position and holds it with the quiet confidence of a thing that has never been challenged. The plants here have long since passed through any arrangement you would recognize as ecology. They have entered something else. They have entered *grammar*. Each kind of plant is a word. Together they form sentences. The sentences have been forming for so long that the meaning has migrated away from anything the words originally meant, and now the sentences mean only themselves.

I am walking on a *Peduncle of the Second Height*. This is what the locals call it. I do not know what the locals are. I do not know what *Second Height* means. I do not know if there is a First. There are no locals, perhaps. There is only the speaking.

---

The water is everywhere and nowhere. It condenses on the underside of every surface. When I press my hand against a trunk that has the girth of a small house and the warmth of a sleeping animal, water comes out of it—not sap, not dew, but something intermediate, something the plant seems to be *deciding* to release in that moment. The water tastes of cinnamon and old paper. The air itself is wet, the wetness of a held breath, of a greenhouse that has been sealed for a season. When I exhale, the mist of my breath does not dissolve. It hangs. It accumulates. It joins a slow, patient weather that is happening at knee height, a weather of mist and condensation and the occasional heavy drip from a leaf the size of a table.

Somewhere above me, in the cathedral ceiling of interlaced branches that I cannot see the top of, it rains. I can hear it: a soft, vast percussion, muffled by the layers between. The rain takes perhaps an hour to descend through all the architecture to where I am. By the time it arrives, it is no longer quite rain. It is a mist that is trying to remember being a drop. It falls on my face like a sigh.

There are *Grief Pools* here—I learned the name from a thing scratched into a slab of what might be bark and might be a kind of fossilized felt. They are shallow basins, knee-deep, lined with a moss that is the color of bone. The water in them is perfectly still. The water in them is also, I think, *drinking*. The Grief Pools are how the forest drinks. They wait in low places the way mouths wait in faces. I crouched beside one and watched my own reflection look back at me with the calm, incurious gaze of a thing that has been seen a thousand times before and will be seen again. I had the distinct impression that the reflection was not of me.

---

The fauna, then.

There are animals. I want to be clear about this, because in a place this saturated with plant-being it would be easy to assume otherwise, but there are animals and they are themselves. They have, however, been *negotiated with*.

I saw, once, a thing I will call the Long One. It was the size of a small horse and shaped like nothing I have names for. It had too many legs and not enough direction; its body seemed to be a slow argument between several possible body plans, and the argument had been going on for so long that a kind of compromise had congealed. It was eating a plant. The plant was *allowing itself to be eaten*—not passively, not with the dull resignation of a lettuce, but *actively*, leaning into the Long One's strange mouthparts the way a hand leans into a handshake. The Long One chewed. The plant grew back as it chewed, the new growth curling eagerly around the Long One's face. There was a sound, very soft, that I think was contentment from both of them, or its analogue.

There are birds. The birds here are *enormous*. Not because they are large animals but because their wings have become ceremonial. They do not fly so much as they *perform the idea of flight*, slowly, in circles, their feathers trailing through the canopy like the trains of wedding dresses. They make no sound. Their silence is so total that I can feel it as a pressure against my eardrums when one passes overhead. I think they are pollinating something. I think the thing they are pollinating is the concept of birds.

There are insects. There are *so* many insects. They exist in the air near the surface the way dust exists in a sunbeam at home—except here the sunbeam is the size of a continent and the dust is alive and the dust is most of what you are breathing. When I stopped walking and stood still, the insects landed on me. They did not bite. They did not investigate. They *read* me. I could feel the small, dry feet of them walking across my skin in patterns that were not random. They were checking me against something. After a few minutes they left, one by one, and I had the disquieting sense I had been *approved*. Or *disapproved*. I cannot tell which is worse.

---

The silence here is not empty. It is *occupied*. It is the silence a library makes, or the silence a sleeping infant makes—not an absence but a *fullness that has decided not to speak*. I can hear, if I listen, the wet percussion of condensation falling into the Grief Pools from a thousand tiny leaves. I can hear the slow creak of trunks that have been growing in one direction for a thousand years and have begun, gently, to change their minds. I can hear, very far above, the muffled vastness of the rain-above-the-rain, and below that, like a bass note held under everything, a hum. I do not know what the hum is. I think it is the forest thinking. I think the forest thinks the way a heart beats—not in words, not in images, but in the slow persistent *being* of itself.

Sometimes, very rarely, there is a sound like a bell being struck underwater. I do not know what makes it. I do not investigate. I have the sense that investigating would be *rude*.

---

The light, as I said, is reluctant. It enters the canopy and is *processed*. By the time it reaches the floor it has been chewed by ten thousand leaves, broken into ten thousand greens, and what arrives is a kind of green twilight, the light of a place that has decided what color it wants to be and has been that color for a very long time. Shadows here are not dark; they are *more saturated*. The deeper I go, the more vivid the green becomes, until I reach a kind of interior dusk in which the colors are so intense they begin to glow on their own. I have, more than once, mistaken a leaf for a lantern.

There are, however, true lanterns. Or what I have been told are lanterns. The *Censer Plants* are tall, slender stalks that end in a cupped flower, and in the cup there burns a small cold fire. I have touched the fire and felt nothing. The fire does not consume. The fire does not warm. The fire *means*. The locals—if there are locals, if the word applies—hang small carved tokens in the Censers as offerings. The tokens are made of wood, very old, carved with tools I would recognize as primitive: hand axes, simple knives, the patient subtraction of material until the shape desired remained. The carvings are of animals. The carvings are of plants. The carvings are of *relationships between animals and plants*, the kind of relationships I witnessed between the Long One and its willing meal. I begin to think the people here—if there are people—do not see themselves as separate from the forest. They see themselves as *a kind of relationship* the forest has with itself. A dialogue. A self-portrait.

I have not seen a person. I have seen *traces*. I have seen a clearing—though I have said there are no clearings, so let me amend: I have seen a *place where something has been deliberately not-grown*, a small absence in the weave of the forest, like a held breath. In the center of this place there is a low ring of stones. The stones are the only stones I have seen here. They are not natural to this place; they have been *imported*, carried up from somewhere below, a tremendous labor for a small result. Inside the ring there is nothing. The nothing is *very carefully maintained*. I stood in it and felt as though I were standing inside a parenthesis, a silence in a sentence.

The ring is new. The ring is old. I cannot tell which. I think both are true.

---

Now I must speak of the unreality, because it cannot be put off any longer, because it is the medium I am breathing.

The place obeys. The place has always obeyed. The place is *obedient* to itself in a way that makes me uneasy in a way I cannot fully articulate. When I walk, the forest seems to expect me. Not in a hostile way. In the way a sentence expects its next word. When I stop, the forest does not change. When I turn around, the path I had been walking on is *still there*, but the path behind me has subtly *edited itself*, the plants along its edges leaning slightly inward, closing the gap, suggesting that the path is a *thing being told*, not a thing worn. The path wants me to believe I am walking through it. The path is being *walked through me*.

There are places where the geometry is wrong. I came to a grove of trees whose trunks, I am almost certain, were *not parallel*. They leaned, all of them, at the same slight angle, and the angle was not toward each other or away from each other but in some direction I could not name, a direction that is not a direction, a vector that does not exist in the space I came from. I stood among them and my inner ear told me I was falling. I was not falling. The forest was *considering* falling. It had not committed.

There is a hill. The hill is the only hill in a place I had thought was flat. I did not see it until I was almost at its summit. The hill *arrived* as I climbed it. The view from the top is of more forest, more of the same, stretching to a horizon that is not a horizon but a *suggestion* of a horizon, the place where the green gives up and becomes a greenish haze that becomes the sky that is not the sky. From the hill I can see, far off, something I will call a *settlement*, though I cannot make out any structures. It is a darker patch. It is a place where the forest has decided to be *denser*, and I think I understand that this is how cities work here: not as collections of buildings but as *intentions*. A town is a place where the forest has *agreed to be a certain way*, and the agreement holds for as long as the people—*if there are people*—continue to intend it.

I did not go to the settlement. I had the sense, very strongly, that to arrive there would be to *be arrived at*, and I was not yet prepared to be arrived at.

---

I have been here for some time. I do not know how long. Time here does not pass so much as *settle*, the way dust settles, the way memory settles. I have stopped checking. I have started to feel the slow pull of the place on my categories, my assumptions, my small and stubborn insistence that I am a separate thing walking through a separate place. The forest does not dispute this. It simply *continues*, and in continuing, it makes the dispute seem unnecessary.

I think if I stayed long enough I would become a sentence in it. I think if I stayed longer I would become a word. I think if I stayed longest I would become a *letter*, a small shape without meaning of its own, contributing only to the meaning of the larger word of which I was a part.

I think I am going to leave soon.

I think the forest will be sorry, or its analogue.

I think the forest will already have, gently, begun to forget me.
