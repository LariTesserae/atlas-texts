# The Long Bottom

---

The place has no ground.

There is no ground here. There has not been ground here for a very long time — and the things that built what came after did not, on the whole, miss it. *Built* is the wrong word. *Grew* is wrong. The word in the old trade tongue is *jalsuàth*, which translates to "that which was decided," and which no one living now bothers to translate anymore.

You arrive — if arrival is possible — at the Long Bottom.

---

## The Surface

What meets you first is black.

Not the black of shadow or night or pigment, but the black of a material that has refused all other colors for so long that the refusal has become its nature. It is warm to the touch — unpleasantly warm, the warmth of a fever or a hand held too long — and it is not stone. You walk on it. It flexes. A quarter-inch give, then the substrate catches, firm. Something beneath it is breathing in slow tectonic rhythm, and the whole surface shifts by millimeters beneath you as you move.

This is the skin.

The skin is the layer you see, walk on, that catches light. It is everywhere. It is the entire visible world from any vantage the City would let you find. It is six feet thick in the places it has been measured, which is not many, and it is composed of a substance the surveyors called *resilin* before they stopped surveying and the word fell out.

Resilin is organic. You can tell because it heals. Where boots have scuffed it, the scuff marks fade over hours. Where something has been dropped and chipped a fragment loose, the missing piece fills back in over days, edges creeping toward each other like two shores closing. It is not, however, alive in any way that the older taxonomies knew how to name. It does not reproduce. It does not die. It does not feed. It does not, so far as anyone has established, think.

It just is the bottom of things.

What lies beneath the skin is unknown, and this is one of the few honest statements that can be made about the place.

---

## The Edges

The Long Bottom has no edges.

This is not true. The Long Bottom has edges. It has been mapped, in the deep terramapping surveys of the second age, to a distance that took the site distant from the site to install it, and that distance was very large. It has edges in the way that oceans have edges, which is to say, in the way that has never once mattered to anyone inside them.

You can spend the rest of a life walking and never reach an edge. The City, such as it is, is not built near one. The City is built near the center of the surveyed expanse, and the surveyed expanse is a small portion of the whole, and the whole vast thing is large enough that weather happens inside it.

Not the weather you are used to.

---

## The Weather

What weather there is comes from below.

The skin sweats. This is the right word. In the warm hours — and they are almost all warm hours, the Long Bottom holding a temperature the body interprets as a held breath, a little above blood-temperature, a little below fever — moisture collects against the underside of the skin and gathers in beads and rolls down. Slowly, because the grade is so gentle as to be theoretical. The beads find each other. The beads become rivulets. The rivulets find low places, and the low places are not low by much, but they are lower than elsewhere, and so the water lies in them.

It does not pool. Nothing here pools. The water is taken back. The skin drinks, and the drinking is not the drinking of soil — it is the drinking of a thing which has decided it is thirsty, and which stops drinking when it has had what it needed.

You stand in a place where the water has just been.

The air is already wet.

You will not be dry again for some time.

---

## The City

The City is built.

Not on the skin. *Of* the skin. The buildings grow out of it the way bodies grow out of bodies, in the way of continuations. The walls are resilin if they were grown, and metal if they were made, and these are different categories that the architecture has not, over the long centuries, bothered to reconcile.

The City is dense. It is dense the way a sentence is dense, the way a held chord is dense, the way a small space that has been lived in by many bodies for a long time is dense. It is layered. The City has been built and built on, and the layers are not always contiguous; a roof can be a street, and the street can be a room, and the room can be a garden that was abandoned and a street that was roofed and a chapel that was demolished and a floor that was floored. The lower layers are warm and wet and dim. The upper layers are warm and wet and lit.

It is warm everywhere. It is wet everywhere. There is no season. There is no time of day that changes by a factor the body can name. The light is a constant.

It is bright, but it is not the brightness of a sun.

---

## The Light

The light comes from above.

No — the light comes from *within*. The skin has, distributed through its substance at irregular intervals, structures that the first surveyors called *lamps* and the later ones called *nodes* and which a school of philosophy now calls *the names*. They are not lamps. They do not burn. They are not nodes. They do not relay. They are not the names. They are luminescent. They have always been luminescent. They will, presumably, continue to be.

They emit a warm light, the light of low fire or held honey, and they do not flicker, and they do not dim, and they do not go out.

You can stand beneath one and shade your eyes with your hand and the hand will glow. The light is strong enough to read by at the bottom of a well, beneath a street, in a room no one has entered for years. The City, all of it, is lit.

There is no night.

The first inhabitants of the City had no word for *night*. They made one. The word did not catch on. There is no night.

---

## The Sound

It is mostly quiet.

This is the first strange thing, and you notice it the way you notice an absence — by the way everything else has more room.

There is no wind. There is no wind because there is no weather with wind in it. There is no rain. There is rain sometimes, a fine condensation that falls from the lamps and collects on the skin and is drunk back, and this is not weather, this is circulation. There are no storms. There are no storms because there is nothing to storm.

What there is, in sound:

- The breathing of the skin, which is a very low sound, felt more than heard, the way you feel a large animal breathing if you lay your head against its side.
- The patter of condensation, when it falls, which is the sound of very small water on a surface that is slightly yielding, and which is, on the whole, a kind, quiet sound.
- The hum of the lamps, which is below hearing, even when you try.
- The City.

The City is a place of many bodies and many small sounds. Footfalls. A low conversation through a wall. A door, the closing of which is soft because the hinges are resilin and the city wears them. Somewhere far off, an engine, which is the only industrial sound, and which has been running, on and off, for a long time.

Mostly quiet is not absent. Mostly quiet is a held thing, like a held note, like a held breath.

---

## The Animals

There are many animals.

This is the second strange thing, and you cannot, in the end, stop noticing it.

There is no plant life. There is no plant life because there has never been plant life here, because there is no soil for plant life, because there is no sun for plant life, because the things that built the place did not build it for plants. The light is for seeing. The skin is for walking on. The water is for plants.

There are no plants.

There are animals. There are many animals.

They are not, any of them, animals you would recognize. Some of them are very large. Some of them are very small. Some of them have legs. Some of them do not. Some of them have been named. Most have not. The ones that have been named were named badly and the names have not been improved upon.

They live. They live the way you live, which is to say, they eat and they move and they reproduce and they die. They live on the skin. They live off the skin. They live in the water that the skin sweats. They eat each other. They are eaten by each other. They are many.

They are quiet.

This is the third strange thing. The animals are quiet. Not silent — they make sounds, small sounds, the sounds of bodies moving and bodies calling and bodies eating — but quiet in the way that a forest is quiet at the bottom of a forest, in the way that the deep sea is quiet, in the way that a place is quiet when it is full of life and the life has, over a long time, agreed upon a register.

There is a sound the larger ones make, low and brief, when they are in distress, and it carries.

There is a sound the small ones make, high and brief, when they are calling.

There is a sound, rarely, when an animal is dying, which is a low sound, and a long sustained sound, and which carries farther than the others.

---

## The People

There are people.

There are people because there have always been people, because the City is a place that was built for people, because people build things because they are people and this is a kind of tautology that the City has not, over the long centuries, bothered to break.

The people are warm-blooded. Their skin is not resilin. They wear clothes. The clothes are damp. The clothes are always damp. The clothes are made of many materials, and the materials are grown in vats on raised platforms in the upper layers, where the lamps are strongest, because the lamps are the only light and the only warmth, and the plants that make the clothes need both.

There are plants in the City. There are plants in vats in the City. There are no plants on the skin. The line is firm. The line has been firm for a long time.

The people work. The people work in the engines, which run, which have always run, which will, presumably, continue to run. The engines are not engines for making anything. The engines are engines for keeping the engines running. They are engines for the circulation of water, which must be moved, because the skin does not drink evenly, and the water, if left to settle, will pool, and pooling, here, is the only disaster the place knows.

The engines hum. The engines hum the way the lamps hum, low and constant and below hearing. The engines are many. The engines are layered, like the City, like the strata of a long inhabited place.

The engines are old.

The engines are very, very old.

---

## The Weather, Revised

There is weather.

You realize, after a time, that there is weather. There is weather the way there is weather inside a closed hand — not weather of the sky, but weather of the body, the weather of a place that is alive in some way that the older taxonomies did not name.

The skin breathes. The skin, when it breathes out, exhales a warmth that rises. The breath, when it rises, meets the cooler air above. The cooler air is cooler by a degree. The breath condenses. The breath falls back as the condensation you have been walking in, the fine rain, the constant almost-rain that never quite becomes rain.

There are drafts. The drafts come from below the skin, from the place the breath comes from, and they are warm and they move from places, and the places they move from are the low places, the places the engines have worn thin.

There are currents in the water. The water moves. The water moves slowly, in the low places, in the channels the City has carved through the skin, in the pipes the engines have laid. The water is warm. The water carries things. The things it carries are small.

There are fogs. Sometimes there are fogs, when the breath is held back, when the engines are working harder, when the place is full of a moment of more.

The fogs come up from below. They are warm. They are wet. They are not thick.

You learn them.

You learn that the fog is a kind of weather, the kind of weather the place knows how to make.

You learn that the place is making weather the way a body makes weather, by being a body, by being warm and wet and alive.

---

## The Things You Cannot See

What is below the skin is unknown, and this is one of the few honest statements.

The first surveyors sent sounds down. The sounds came back changed. The sounds traveled long and came back changed in ways that suggested distance and depth and not-emptiness.

The first surveyors sent lights down. The lights came back changed. The lights came back darker, in colors that were not the colors they had been sent down as.

The first surveyors sent instruments down on lines. The lines went slack. The lines came back, eventually, and what they brought back came back changed, and the changes were catalogued.

The most recent surveyors do not go over the edge. The most recent surveyors stay on the body, near the City, near the engines, near the lamps, in the warm and the wet and the lit.

What is below the place is unknown. What is below the place is presumed, by some, to be *of* the place, in the way the skin is *of* the place, in the way the lamps are *of* the place, in the way the animals are *of* the place.

What is below the place is presumed, by some, to be the place itself, in the way a body is the body, in the way a held thing is the hand.

---

## The Feeling of the Place

You walk, and the place gives a little, and the place is warm, and the place is wet, and the place is lit, and the place is full of life, and the place is mostly quiet, and the place has not changed in a long time.

The place is long. The place is long the way a held note is long, the way a body is long, the way a thing that has not stopped being itself is long.

The place is not empty. There is no emptiness here. There is no openness in the surface. The place is full of itself.

You walk, and the skin warms the soles of your feet through the damp leather. There is a sound, distant, of an animal low and calling. There is a sound, closer, of the water falling from a place the water has just been. There is a sound, beneath you, of the skin breathing.

You walk, and the lamps glow, and the lamps do not flicker, and the City stands on the skin, and the City is dense, and the City is built, and the City is inhabited, and the engines hum, and the animals are many, and the place is warm, and the place is wet, and the place is lit, and the place has not changed.

The place has not changed.

The place has not changed in a long time.

The place, you begin to suspect, has not changed in a very long time, and the place, you further suspect, is not going to change, and the place, you begin, at last, to feel, has not changed in a way that is the same as the way a body is not changing when it is breathing in the dark, when it is held in a hand, when it is warm, when it is alive, when it is alive and not going anywhere else, when it is alive and being itself the way a place is itself, the way a body is itself, the way a held thing is itself, and you walk, and the skin gives, and the water runs, and the lamps glow, and the animals breathe, and the engines hum, and the City stands, and the place is, and the place is, and the place is.

---

The Long Bottom is patient.

The Long Bottom has always been patient.

The Long Bottom will, presumably, continue to be patient.
