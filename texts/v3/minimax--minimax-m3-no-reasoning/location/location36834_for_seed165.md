What arrives first is the sound. A pressure that isn't pressure, a roar that is not produced by any single throat or falling mass. It comes from everywhere and from the precise center of the place, both at once, and it has been happening for so long that the stones themselves seem to be shaped by it, polished on their windward sides into long listening curves. You do not get used to it. You only learn to speak beneath it, lips moving against the constant.

The land is high. The kind of high where the air forgets what warmth tastes like before it reaches you, and what moisture it carried has already been wrung out by the climb. You stand on a saddle between two ridges and the entire visible world is the color of old iron: a rust-purple that shifts toward black where the light fails, and toward bone where the wind has scoured something alive. The elevation is not mountain. It is older than mountain. It is the underside of something, perhaps, or the part the rest of the world is built on top of.

The terrain is a shelf, then a falling-away, then another shelf, descending in long terraces toward a valley that is the color of a bruise held up to a candle. The terraces are not made of stone in the way stone is made anywhere I have ever been. They are made of compression. Layers of something that was once sediment and is now something else — a material that rings when struck, and where the wind has carved it, the cross-section shows the same shape repeated: a line, a bend, a line, a bend, as if the world had once been folded, and the folding had been recorded, and the recording had become the substance. The terraces are the unrolled pages of a very old gesture.

There is no grass. The vegetation is a rumor of vegetation: gray lichens the size of dinner plates, spaced yards apart, each one a different species, each one apparently alone. A small twisted shrub, three of them on the entire saddle, growing out of cracks that run in directions that have nothing to do with drainage or fault or any logic a surveyor's hand would recognize. They are not dead. They are not alive in any way that I would trust. They are present.

Water exists, but as a concept that has been split. There is a seep halfway down the second terrace, a dark stain on the stone that is wet to the touch and tastes of iron and something older than iron. A thin channel, perhaps an inch across, runs from the seep for forty feet and then stops, the water simply not continuing, as if it has been told to stop, and has. There is a second channel, higher up, that does the same thing, and a third, and a fourth, each one ending at the same kind of place: a place where the stone becomes faintly translucent and the water seems to pass through it instead of across it, and you can see, if you kneel, a dim suggestion of the water continuing on the other side of the stone, in a direction that is not the direction the water was going. There are many such places. I counted eleven along the saddle alone. The water is being routed. By what, I cannot say. The stone itself, perhaps. A negotiation long since concluded.

Light is the color of weak tea held up to a paper. It does not come from a single direction. It comes from a smear in the upper sky, low and wide, the same hue as the bruise-valley below, as if the world is lit from the inside of a wound. Nothing casts a clean shadow. Objects have a soft penumbra on one side and no penumbra on the other, and the un-shadowed side is slightly luminous, as if the stone had retained some of the light from an earlier time and is slowly spending it. You can see this most clearly on the ruins.

There are ruins. Three of them on the saddle, and others, smaller, on the terraces below. The largest is a wall, perhaps fifteen feet long and at its highest six feet, made of the same material as the terraces, worked into blocks. The blocks do not fit in the way blocks fit. They meet at angles that are not ninety, and where they meet, the seam is a soft line, as if the blocks had been grown together rather than laid. There is a door. The door is a rectangle in the wall, but the rectangle is not a closed shape; if you look at it long enough, the rectangle begins to suggest that one of its sides is also the side of a different rectangle elsewhere, and the wall behind the door is the wall of a different building, and you are looking through a door in one place into a door in another place, and the two places are the same place but offset by a distance the eye can almost but not quite compute. I did not step through. The sound was louder there, and the air had a taste, and the taste was the taste of a question that has been asked so many times it has become a flavor.

The other ruins are simpler. A low platform. A ring of stones, each one taller than a person, set in a circle that is not quite closed, the gap pointing toward the bruise-valley. A single block standing on end in the middle of the saddle, smooth, with a shallow cup carved into its top, the cup filled with water that does not freeze, though the air around it is cold enough to ache in the teeth. I drank from the cup. The water was not cold.

I do not know who made these. I do not think the question applies. The things here are not made in the way things are made. They have arrived, or they have always been, or they are the result of a process that is still running but at a tempo the eye cannot catch. I sat on the platform for what I judged to be an hour and the light did not change and the wind did not change and the sound did not change and I could not tell if I was watching or being watched, and at some point I stopped trying to tell, and the hour ended, and I stood up, and the cold came back into me all at once as if it had been politely waiting.

There is no animal. There is the suggestion of animal: a track in the seep area, a long narrow print with no clear analog in any bestiary I carry in my head. A second track, a different shape, near the ring of stones, crossing the first at an angle that suggests the two makers were not in the same place at the same time but had both passed through, and were perhaps aware of each other in a way that left a mark only in the pattern. There is a feather, gray, at the base of the cup-block. I do not know from what. The feather is not moving in the wind. The wind is moving around it.

The scale is open. From the saddle you can see for a long distance in the clearer light, and the distances are wrong. The bruise-valley below has features that I would judge to be a mile off, and after walking for what felt like an hour, those features are still the same size, and the time has been longer than an hour. The far ridge of the valley is a wall of the same compression-stone, and where the wall meets the sky, the sky is not the sky. It is a different material, or the same material in a different state, and the line between the two is a long horizontal seam like the seam in the ruin-wall, and the seam is faintly luminous, the color of the cup-water, and the sound comes from it, I think, or goes into it.

I am going to describe the sound, because the sound is the place.

The sound is not wind. It is not water. It is not a machine. It is the sound of a very large, very old throat attempting to pronounce a word it has not yet finished forming, and the word is being revised as it is being spoken, and the revisions are audible, and the revisions are the weather. There is a low component that you feel in the long bones, a hum that has been running for so long it has worn a groove in your attention and you begin to time your breath to it without meaning to. There is a higher component, intermittent, a kind of keening that rises and falls in a pattern I could not determine to be periodic or random; it may be both. And there is a third component, the one I have not been able to name, which is a kind of silence-shaped sound, an audible absence, as if part of the noise had been cut out and the cutout itself was making a noise to compensate. When I stood at the door in the ruin, the third component became louder than the others, and I could feel it in my teeth, and I understood, in the way one understands things at the back of one's head, that the door was not a door, and the place on the other side of the door was not a place, and the sound was the sound of the question being asked of me, and the question was, *are you the same as what I am*, and the answer, the one the place had been waiting for, was the answer I gave by not stepping through, and the answer was received, and the cup filled, and the feather was left, and the hour ended.

I came down off the saddle. I do not remember the descent clearly. The terraces had rearranged, or I had, or both, and the seep was in a different place, and the water in it tasted different, and the lichen I had passed on the way up was a different lichen, though of the same family, perhaps, or perhaps not. The compression-stone held its fold-pattern, but the pattern had changed: the bends were more frequent, the lines shorter, as if the page had been crumpled and smoothed again. The sound followed me down, and then below me, and then behind me, and then nowhere, all at once, as if a door had closed, though I had not reached the bottom of the valley yet, and the valley floor was a long way off, and the light there was a different light, and the air was a different air, and the place I had been on the saddle was already a thing I would have to describe to be believed, and the describing would be the place, in the end, and not the saddle, and I was bringing the saddle down inside me, in the cup of my memory, where the water does not freeze.
