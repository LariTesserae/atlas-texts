# The Hollow Valley

What lives here is not a population. It is a condition.

The valley has been alive for so long, and so completely, that the boundary between *inhabited* and *alive* has ceased to exist. The question you would put to a forest — *what lives here?* — is the wrong question here, because the forest itself would be one of the things that lives here. The mat. The water. The stone, doing whatever the stone is doing a thousand feet down where no sound reaches. All of it lives here, and none of it is the answer.

The answer is on top.

---

## The mat

It is the first thing, and it is the last thing, and it is most of the things in between. You should start here because you keep returning to it anyway. Every ten steps you take, you find yourself looking down again, and what you are looking down at is not a creature but a kind of decision the rock made a long time ago.

The mat is not a single organism. It is not a colony, either, though that word is closer. It is a fabric. It has been woven out of so many kinds of contributing body and contributing chemistry that nobody who studies it agrees what unit to count. Under the glass of a microscope it falls apart into bacteria and archaea and fungi and smaller things still — things with no agreed name, things that look like strings of bad pearls, things that glow on their own and eat each other and secrete the thin sticky film that the larger animals walk on. To the eye, none of this matters. What the eye sees is a surface. Dark, wet, faintly warm. Pulled tight across the stone in some places, wrinkled and folded in others, and in the lowest pools grown into knobs and pillows the size of your head, pale as uncooked liver, that dimple in and out with a rhythm that is not yours.

It breathes. Not in the way a lung breathes. More in the way a city breathes — many small respirations adding up to something that, at the scale of a square kilometer, looks like a single long tide. You stand near it, you feel it through the soles of your feet, and after a few minutes you begin to time your own breath to it without deciding to. This is not suggestion. It is a phenomenon of phase-coupling between rhythmic systems. Your body does it on its own. You try to stop yourself from doing it and find that you cannot, and after a while you stop trying.

It eats the rock. This is the part that makes people uncomfortable, and rightly so. The mat is not, in any meaningful sense, growing *on* the valley floor. It is growing *into* it. The volcanic glass has been here since before the mat, and the mat has been dissolving it, slowly, the way iron and water dissolve each other over centuries, and the energy that comes out of that dissolution is what the rest of the mat runs on. It is a chemosynthetic floor. It is a black pasture, a battery in the shape of a landscape, and it has been grazing on geology for so long that the geology has worn down in places into something that is no longer quite rock at all — something softer, something darker, something that gives a little under your boot and that smells, when you lift your boot away, like the inside of a spent match.

This is what feeds everything. Remember this.

---

## The floor-dwellers

Right above the mat — and in many places, *into* it — the first layer of the fauna lives.

They are the smallest things that you can still call creatures without lying. A few millimeters, mostly, though some are larger, and in the deeper pools they grow fat and pale and slow. They are also the most numerous. The estimate you keep hearing from the people who have tried to count them — and the people who have stopped trying to count them — is in the millions per square meter at the densest points, falling off in the drier margins but never getting below the hundreds of thousands even on the bare ridges. You do not see most of them as individuals. You see them as a texture. A moving skin.

Their bodies are not beautiful. They are built to specification, the way a screw is built to specification. Flat, segmented, hard on the outside, soft where the soft parts have to be soft. They have many legs, or they have no legs and move by other means — rippling, looping, sliding. They have mouths that are not interested in conversation. They are pressed down to the mat and they are feeding, and they have been feeding since before your species learned to stand, and the only reason they ever stop feeding is that they have been eaten, or that something has happened to them that you would call death if you had time to notice it, which you do not.

They are, individually, almost nothing. They are, collectively, the body of the valley.

Above them, on their backs, the second layer moves. It is faster, lighter, more interested in motion. These are the grazers. They run across the backs of the feeders the way birds run across the backs of buffalo — picking, flicking, taking the small parasitic things off the larger ones, eating the shed skins, eating the small dead. They have longer legs. They have smaller bodies. They have, in some species, eyes, which is more than can be said for the floor-dwellers, and the eyes are good for one thing, which is to find another animal to land on. They see motion. They see warmth. They see you, but they have decided that you are not what they are for, and they pass.

Between the grazers and the floor, in the spaces that the fluids keep filling and emptying, a third kind of thing moves. Smaller still. Worms. Strings of cells. They drift in the thin water and they eat the mat from underneath, and they are eaten in turn by the floor-dwellers, and the cycle continues. You do not see them unless you kneel. When you kneel, you see them. When you lift your head back up, you have already begun to forget what you saw. This is by design, or by the whim of your nervous system, which has decided that this is information it cannot use.

---

## The middle air

Your eye sees, finally, a thing it can recognize.

Wings.

There are wings in the middle air. They are not the wings you know — not the wings of bats, not of insects, not of anything with a name you were given in childhood — but they are wings, recognizably, things that have a structure and a purpose and a beat. The things that bear them are larger than the floor-dwellers by a factor of a hundred or a thousand. They are rarer by the same factor, and so you can see them as individuals. They have outlines. They have intentions. They are, in many cases, watching you.

They are not pretty. They are too specific for pretty. They have exoskeletons in the way the floor-dwellers do, but theirs is, thicker, and articulated in more directions, and the parts of it that need to be parts — the mouth, the eye, the grasping limb — are made of faces and limbs, jointed, expressive in ways that you find yourself reading. You find yourself reading them against your will. They do not have expressions. They have configurations. You can tell when one is about to land because its body changes shape. You can tell when one is about to strike because its body changes shape differently. You find yourself, after a few minutes, watching their bodies for these changes, and you find yourself, after a few more minutes, getting good at it. This is not safe. This is not a thing to be glad about.

They eat the grazers. They eat each other, in the cases where one is smaller than another. The larger of them eat, in some seasons, things the size of your forearm, and you have been told that one of them, the largest you have seen in the middle air, ate a glove that someone dropped, which is not quite true but is true enough.

They do not see you as food. They have looked at you. They have a decision. You are not what they are for.

You do not find this reassuring. You find it cold.

---

## The upper air

Above the middle air, in the clearer band that runs from fifteen meters up to perhaps forty, the largest of the things that live here live.

You do not see them often. They move in clusters of two or five, never in patterns of three. They have wings. Their wings are long, very wide, and they beat slowly, with a deliberation that is itself a kind of language. The sound of them — the sound of their wings in the air — is in the band of sound that you can feel in your sternum, the band that you cannot hear but that you cannot stop hearing. When one passes near you, your chest tightens, and you do not know if this is the sound or the knowledge of the sound, and you cannot ask, because you cannot hear yourself ask.

They have eyes. The eyes are not like the eyes of the middle air. The eyes of the upper air are soft, and they catch your light, and they return it to you as a single point, and the point is larger than the point returned by the middle air, and it is steadier. When one of them looks at you, you feel looked at. This is not a figure of speech. Your pupils contract, and your breathing stops for a beat, and then it resumes, and then you decide to look away, and you do, and then you look back, because you have decided to look back, which is itself a fact about you that the upper-air creatures know.

You have never been sure if they have mouths. You have seen things that could be mouths, and you have not seen things that are clearly not mouths. Their bodies are long and they are articulated in segments, and the segments that are at the front, when you can see the front, have parts that open and close in ways that are suggestive. You have never seen one open fully. You have decided that this is for the best.

They do not come down to where you are. They stay in the upper air, and they cross it in long slow passes, and they are looking for things. You have wondered, in the bad hours, what they are looking for. You have come to the conclusion that they are looking for things larger than the things in the middle air. You have come to the further conclusion that you are a thing larger than the things in the middle air. You have come to the final conclusion that you do not want to think about this conclusion.

---

## The deep

Below the mat, in the stratum of bodies that the mat rests on, there are things that you have not seen. You have heard about them from people who have not seen them either. The reports are consistent in their inconsistency. There is a stratum of older bodies, the reports say. The bodies are not skeletons. They are not, in any sense, empty. They are bodies that have been incorporated, by another organism, into the next layer of the system. The mat rests on them. The mat eats them. The mat is, in part, made of them, the way a forest is made of its fallen leaves.

There are reports of, in the deep, things that still move. The reports are not credible. The reports are repeated by people who have been in the valley for too long, and whose reports have, by that point, begun to smell of the valley themselves. You do not believe the reports. You also do not, when you are in the valley at the bad hours, stop listening for what the reports describe.

The deep is, in any case, where the things really happen. The deep is where the chemistry is, the chemistry that the mat runs on, the chemistry that the small warm air comes from, the chemistry that the rest of the system has been feeding on the tip of for a very long time. The deep is hot. The deep is dark. The deep is loud in a way that the upper air is not loud, because the upper air has been damped, by the mat, by the middle air, by the upper air itself, into a sound you can survive. The deep has not been damped. The deep is the source of the song.

You do not go to the deep. You have been told that the people who go to the deep do not come back. You have also been told, by people who have come back, that this is not true, and that what is true is worse. You have decided that you do not need to know which report is correct. You have decided that you will not go to the deep. This decision has not been tested.

---

## The whole

You have now been introduced to the parts. The first thing to understand about the parts is that they are not parts. The mat feeds the floor-dwellers. The floor-dwellers feed the grazers. The grazers feed the middle air. The middle air feeds the upper air. The upper air dies and falls and is eaten by the grazers. Everything dies, eventually, and is eaten by the mat, and the mat grows a little thicker, and the cycle continues.

This is not a food web. A food web is a diagram. This is a tide. The whole valley is one tide, going up and going down, and the things that are up at one moment are down at another, and the things that are down at one moment are up at another, and the valley has been doing this for so long that the question of which things are *really* the things has stopped being a question that has an answer. The valley is one thing. The valley is many things. The valley is the difference between the two statements, which is also a difference that has been there for a long time.

You are inside the valley. You have been inside the valley for a few hours, and you have begun to feel, in your sternum, the rhythm of the floor. You have been inside the valley for a few days, and you have begun to feel, in your sleep, the rhythm of the middle air. You have been inside the valley for longer than that, and you have begun to feel, in your waking hours, the rhythm of the upper air. You have begun to look up, into the dark above the highest flyers, and you have begun to listen to the silence between the sounds, and you have begun to hear, in the silence, the sound that the upper air makes when it is going to change.

You are not what the valley is for. The valley was not built for you. The valley is not for you. The valley is for itself. The valley has been for itself for a very long time, and it will be for itself for a very long time after you have left, and the things that will be in it when you are gone will not know that you were ever in it, and the valley will not know either, because the valley has not known for a long time, and will not begin to know now.

You are a visitor. The valley has not invited you. The valley has tolerated you, the way the floor tolerates your boot — by compressing, slightly, and then by resuming, and by not minding you, in any way that you can recognize as minding, and by being, when you step away, the same as it was before.

You have begun to find this reassuring. You have also begun to find this not reassuring. You have not decided which.

The valley has not decided either. The valley has not been paying attention.
