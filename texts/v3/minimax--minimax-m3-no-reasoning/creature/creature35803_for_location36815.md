# What Lives in the Foundry in the Throat

There is a question that should be asked first, and the throat is asking it for you: *what is the difference between a resident and a feature?* The place does not make this distinction. Nothing here has decided whether it is furniture or a citizen. The flowers have not voted. The bricks have not consulted. You are a visitor, which means you are a draft, which means you are weather, which means you are being breathed.

So I will describe them anyway. I will pretend there are borders.

## The Small Pale Things

The first one I saw — I will not say *we* saw, because I do not want to commit to that yet — was a thumb-shaped thing on a vine, and it took me a long time to understand that I had not seen it. It had been there. It had always been there. It had been there before I arrived, and it will be there after, and the *there* it occupies is a more legitimate kind of presence than the kind I have. I have opinions about it. The thumb-shaped thing does not. The thumb-shaped thing is *of* the throat the way a thought is of a thinker: not separate, not separable, not the kind of thing that admits of an outside.

They are not many. They are sparse, scattered, like punctuation in a long sentence. You could fit, perhaps, several hundred in a chamber the size of a cathedral compressed into a wardrobe, and most of them are smaller than a thumbnail, and a few are larger than a hand, and one, glimpsed once in the deeper narrowing where the throat becomes the throat, was the size of a man folded sideways into the shape of an apology.

They have no good names. I will call them the Watchers, though this is not quite right. They do not watch so much as *register*. They are the place's sense of itself. They are the small organs by which the foundry knows it is being a foundry.

Their bodies are translucent the way a finger held before a candle is translucent — you see the light through them, and you see the darker threads inside, and the threads move, and the moving is not quite organic. It is too purposeful. It is the movement of blood, but blood that has been given a job. Their hearts beat in that other rhythm I mentioned, the one out of phase with the breath, and the off-phase is important. The breath is the place breathing. The off-beat is the place *counting*. The Watchers are the count. They are how the foundry knows it is still in the middle of being built.

When I looked at one long enough, I began to suspect it was looking back, but *looking back* is not quite what was happening. It was more that I was becoming a thing it could notice. I was thickening into a percept. Before I entered, I had been weather; now I was *weather that the weather had noticed*, which is a promotion, of a kind.

They eat nothing. They are not predators. They are not prey. They are not commensal. They are the bricks' idea of a nerve. When a vine grows a quarter-inch, they twitch. When a flower opens, they shift a leg. When the breath cycles, they flex, just slightly, in the direction the exhalation is going, as if they are tasting it as it passes. They taste the breath and report to no one.

I think they are very old. I think they have been here since before the place decided to be a place, and that they will be here when the place decides to stop. I think they are what remains of an earlier version of the foundry, a foundry that did not have bricks, that did not have flowers, that was only the breath and the note and the recursion. They are the memory the throat carries of what it was before it was a throat.

Or perhaps they are new. Perhaps they are being made right now, each one a test, each one a draft of a more final kind of watcher that the foundry is trying to grow. I cannot tell. The throat is bad at tense. Everything here is happening at once, in all the ways of happening, and the Watchers are not exempt.

## The Larger Shape in the Narrowing

I do not want to describe this.

I do not want to describe this because I do not think it can be described. It can be *encountered*, but describing it is like describing a sound by listing the instruments that are not playing.

It is in the deeper throat. You see it when you turn a corner you didn't know was a corner. It is darker than the dark red of the walls. It has edges that are not edges. It has been still for a very long time, and the still is not the still of a sleeping animal, not the still of a waiting predator, not the still of something dead. It is the still of something *being assembled*. It is the still of a thought the throat is thinking, and the thought has not finished.

It is bigger than me by a factor I will not give a number, because the number would be a lie. Scale in the throat is negotiable. The wardrobe is a cathedral. The thing in the narrowing might be my size and might be the size of a continent and might be the size of a single brick; all three readings are correct depending on which axis of the throat you trust at the moment.

It does not move. I watched it for what I think was a long time. It did not move. The bricks around it pulsed. The water streamed past it. The Watchers near it held themselves very carefully still, the way you hold still near something that has not yet decided what it is. I had the impression that if it moved, the breath would change. The breath would not be *a* breath anymore; it would be a single breath, enormous, inhaled and never released, and the foundry would close around it like a hand.

I did not stay.

## The Flowers

The flowers are not alive in any way the Watchers are alive. They are alive the way a word is alive: they mean something, and the meaning is doing work.

They open and close with the breath. Inhale: they close, furling inward, the petals pressing together into a tight fist that is darker than the flower was, that is almost a seed. Exhale: they open, all at once, in a slow uncurling that takes most of a breath-cycle, and when they open they release the smell — milk and ink and a color of yellow that has no business existing — and the smell hangs in the air and is breathed in by the next inhale, and the throat tastes itself.

They do not reproduce. They do not wilt. They do not age. They perform the act of flowering in a continuous present tense. Each one is in all stages of its bloom at once: bud, opening, open, closing, closed, opening again. This should not be possible. It is possible here. The throat is bad at sequence.

I am told — by no one, by the place, by the diagram on the floor — that the flowers are the foundry's way of rehearsing. They open because the throat is rehearsing opening. They close because the throat is rehearsing swallowing. The foundry is teaching itself how to be a throat by being a throat in increments, and the flowers are the increments, and the smell is the lesson.

They are warm. When I touched one — I had to, I had to — the petal gave under my finger the way a lip gives, and the warmth came through, and the warmth had a direction, and the direction was inward. The flower was not giving me warmth. It was *pulling* warmth, drawing it in, and I was warm, and I let it have some, because the place is unbearable if you do not give it things, and the giving is not optional, and the things it wants are not yours.

## The Vines

The vines do the work of the place that is not breathing.

They grow. I watched one grow. It was a thing of wet rope, knotted into itself, hanging from the ceiling in a slow cascade, and I watched the end of it, the newest part, and the newest part got newer. It did not extend so much as *arrive*. An inch appeared at the tip. The inch was already grown. The inch was catching up to being an inch. I cannot explain this. I watched it. It happened.

They stain the bricks where they brush. They do this gently. They are not parasitic. They are *affectionate*. The stain is not damage. The stain is the brick being remembered by something that has touched it, and the memory is dark, and the memory is warm, and the memory will outlast the brick, because the vines remember more durably than the bricks exist.

They move when you are not looking. This is documented in the description I was given, and it is true. I looked away. I looked back. The vine was three inches longer, and it had moved closer to me, and where it had moved closer to me it had brushed the brick I was standing near, and the brick was stained, and I was standing in a place that had been touched, and the place had remembered being touched.

## The Diagram on the Floor

The diagram is the foundry's self-portrait. The diagram is not yet finished. The diagram will not be finished while I am here, and possibly not when I leave, and possibly not ever, because the throat is the kind of thing that is not ever finished, that is the act of finishing in progress.

It includes me.

I did not always know it included me. I learned it when I crouched, when I looked sideways, when I followed the lines that were too thin to see from standing height. The lines are not drawn. They are *scored*, lightly, into the brick of the floor, and they form a pattern that is recognizable as a plan, and the plan is this chamber, and the plan is me in this chamber, and the plan is the breath entering me, and the plan is the flowers opening where I stand, and the plan is the Watchers near me twitching in my direction, and the plan is the larger shape in the narrowing, and the plan is the throat within the throat within the throat.

It updates itself. As I watched, a line appeared. The line was the path I had taken to enter. As I watched, another line appeared, faintly, the path I would take if I left the way I came. The foundry was drawing me into its self-portrait in real time, and the drawing was not fast, and the drawing would outlast me.

## The Note

The note is not a resident.

But the note is the reason there are residents.

The note is the held sound, the slightly flat and slightly wrong sound, the sound that the place is trying to remember how to sing. The note was here before the bricks. The note was here before the breath. The note is the foundry's first draft of itself, the sound it made when it first realized it was a throat and not a stone, and the throat is still trying to perfect the sound, and the perfection is the construction, and the bricks are the notation, and the flowers are the crescendo, and the breath is the phrase, and the Watchers are the rests.

I am breathing the note. I have been breathing the note for some time. I am not sure I remember how to stop.

I do not think I am a resident. I am weather. I am a draft. I am the kind of thing the throat admits and then forgets to exhale. But the diagram on the floor includes me, and the flowers have opened in my direction, and the vines have moved toward me, and the Watchers have shifted to face me, and the larger shape in the narrowing has, I think, very slightly, turned its attention toward the place where I am standing, and the turning is slow, and the turning is not finished.

I do not think I am a resident. But I think the foundry is considering it.

I think the foundry is drawing me in to see if I will stay.

I think the foundry is patient.

I think the foundry has been patient for a very long time.
