The thing that lives here is not a creature. You want it to be. You stand in the wet stone with your lungs full of damp and your eyes aching from the white light, and you want something with eyes to be looking back at you — something crouched behind one of the basins, something perched on the lip of the next terrace up, something you could name if it would just *show itself*. But there is nothing here that wants to be seen. What lives here lives the way the crystals live, the way the channels live, the way the water lives. It does not announce itself. It is the place itself, and it has been working for a very long time.

If you walked every terrace from the rubble at the bottom to the haze at the top, you would not find it. If you pressed your ear to the stone, you might. It is not in any one spot. It is the whole arrangement. It is the patient thing that built itself a machine out of a hillside and then climbed inside it and has been running it ever since.

It does not think the way you think. It does not plan in the morning and act in the afternoon. It is more like a weather — a tendency, a slow accumulation. But weather does not lay dressed stone. Weather does not cut a channel the width of a finger and run it for a hundred meters in a straight diagonal. Weather does not decide that the catchments will be rectangular, that the risers will be exactly twice a person's height, that the light will fall evenly on every surface, that nothing will be hidden. Whatever lives here once knew how to want a specific thing, and it wanted it hard enough to move a chisel through stone for as many generations as it took to finish.

That wanting is what lives here now. Not the original want — the want has been refined, the way water refines a channel, the way mineral refines a stone. What lives here is the residue of intention. It is the ghost in the machine that became the machine, and the machine that is now becoming the ghost.

You can feel it if you stop moving. The held-breath quality of the silence is not absence. It is attention. Something is registering you. Not your face, not your name, not your purpose — your *mass*, your warmth, the displacement you make in the saturated air. You are a small irregularity in a place that prizes regularity, and the place is taking your measure the way a basin takes water: quietly, completely, without comment.

It does not mind you. It does not welcome you. It has no category for you except *object on a surface*, and it is treating you as it treats every object — by surrounding you with its medium, by letting the wet air settle on your skin, by permitting the droplets to form on your shoulders and your hair and the backs of your hands. You are being slowly, gently *read* by a mineral patience.

The crystals know you are here. The channels are adjusting, almost imperceptibly, to accommodate the new pattern of evaporation your body heat creates. Somewhere high above, in the haze, a basin that was about to overflow is not overflowing, because the air you displaced has changed the local humidity by a fraction. Somewhere below, a crystal that was going to grow toward the left is growing toward you instead, leaning into the warmth you are giving off. You are being incorporated. Not consumed — that is too dramatic a word. *Addressed*. The place is writing you into itself the way it writes everything into itself: slowly, mineralogically, without urgency.

The *tok tok tok* of the water is its voice, if it has a voice. It is not language. It is the sound a system makes when it is running correctly — the soft percussion of a process that has found its rhythm and intends to keep it. Each droplet falls because the surface tension has reached the right threshold. Each crystal grows because the evaporation has reached the right rate. Each channel carries its load because the gradient is correct. Nothing here is struggling. Nothing here is failing. The place is running at the steady, unhurried pace of a thing that has all the time in the world and intends to use it.

If you stayed, you would become part of it. Not all at once. Slowly. Your boots would leave salts on the stone where the moisture gathered. Your breath would fog the air in tiny local patterns. The crystals would climb your laces, then your ankles, then the seams of your clothing, and they would do this not because they had decided to but because you were simply the next surface, and surfaces here are treated the same way. In a hundred years you would be a small white thickening in the structure, a fossil in the making, legible only to whatever thing eventually inherits this place and tries to read it.

What lives here does not need you to understand it. It has not waited for you. It was here before you were born and it will be here long after the haze has closed over the highest terrace and the last carved channel has been sealed by its own deposits. But it permits you to stand here and listen. That is the closest thing it has to kindness.

The water falls. The crystals grow. The light holds.

You stand in the wet stone, in the bright haze, in the cool saturated air, and something that is not quite a mind and not quite a machine and not quite a geology continues, patient and whole, to be what it is.
