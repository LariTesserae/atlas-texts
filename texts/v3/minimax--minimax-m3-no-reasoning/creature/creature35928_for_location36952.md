# Those Who Live in the Vent of the Sixth Hour

They do not live here in the way that you understand living.

Living implies a pause between events — a held breath, a resting state, a thing that continues to be while not becoming. What lives in the Vent does not rest. What lives in the Vent is *mid-sentence*. To call it alive is like calling a wave wet; technically true, structurally embarrassing.

But they are here. They persist. And so I will tell you what I have seen, and what I have inferred, and where the seam runs between those two.

---

## The Small Ones, Insofar as Anyone Can Describe Them

Imagine a thumb that has forgotten it was ever a thumb. Imagine a thumb that is *becoming* something, that is in the long argument with itself about what to be next. That is closer to a small one than any proper noun will get you.

They have no fixed form because their form is a *negotiation*. It is what they are while they are doing whatever they are doing. A small one carrying a grain of pale light is shaped differently than a small one climbing a wall. A small one pressed flat against the floor in the heat of the breath is shaped differently than one skimming the water-films. They are not shape-shifters. They are *shaped by their intention*, the way a hand is shaped by the act of grasping, the way a mouth is shaped by the word it is forming.

Their color is the color of wet basalt. This is not camouflage in the strategic sense. It is camouflage in the *mystical* sense — the small ones are the same color as the rock because they have not yet decided that they are not the rock. They are the rock's *attentiveness*. They are the part of the rock that has begun to notice itself.

Their legs — if legs is the word — number in the dozens. Or in the none. Depending on how you count, and counting here is treacherous. The appendages emerge when needed and withdraw when not. They are used to walk, yes, and to climb, and to carry, and to *touch* — that last gesture is the one I want to linger on. The small ones touch everything. The walls. The water. Each other. The grains they tend. They touch as if touch is how they think.

When they pass one another, they touch *briefly*. A contact that lasts a quarter-second. A small exchange that I have never been able to decode, but that has the unmistakable quality of *greeting*. Or *confirmation*. Or *here I am, still here*. The touches leave faint darker spots, which is why the veins are stippled. The small ones are writing on each other. The small ones are writing on everything. The writing accumulates and fades and accumulates again, a palimpsest that never settles into a final draft.

Their sound is not a sound. It is the *negative space* of a sound. A tiny click that is more felt than heard. The aggregate of these clicks — billions of them, all the time, never quite synchronized — produces the hush. The hush is their collective voice. The hush is what they *are*, collectively. A constant low susurrus of *I am here, I am here, I am here*, multiplied until it becomes the weather of the place.

---

## What They Do

They tend the veins. I will return to the veins. First, the other work, because the other work is what they *do*, while the veins are what they *serve*.

They circulate. They move constantly, in currents and eddies, through the fissure, across the field, into the pockets, out of the pockets. Their movement is not random. It is not, I think, *quite* purposeful in the way that an ant's movement is purposeful. It is more like the circulation of blood. Necessary. Continuous. A *metabolism*. The colony is not a collection of individuals; the colony is a single body, and the small ones are its blood cells, and the movement is the pulse.

They carry grains. This is the work I have observed most closely. A small one will detach a piece of vein — carefully, with two appendages, with the focus of a jeweler — and then will carry this grain *somewhere*. Into a crevice. Under a flake. Into one of the pockets. The grain is placed. The small one leaves. Another small one comes later, perhaps, and *touches* the grain, and adjusts it by a millimeter. The placement is exact. The placement is *wrong* if it is off by a millimeter. I do not know what the placement is for.

There are millions of grains. There are millions of pockets. There are millions of small ones. The mathematics of this work is the mathematics of a cathedral being built by insects over a timescale I cannot fathom. Each grain is a brick. Each small one is a bricklayer. The cathedral does not yet have a shape. The cathedral does not yet know it is a cathedral. But the bricks are being laid. The bricks are being laid *patiently*.

They wait, at the heat-slit.

I want to describe this more carefully, because I have watched it for what might have been hours or might have been a geological epoch, and I want to be accurate.

At the deepest point of the fissure — the place where the walls narrow to a slit, where the heat bends the air, where the basalt becomes a kind of membrane — there is a stone that I will call the threshold. It is warm. It is *almost* a door. The small ones approach it from time to time. Not in groups. In ones, in twos, in small processions of five or six. They approach, and they stop, an inch from the stone, and they *press themselves flat*. They flatten until they are almost a stain on the rock. They hold this position for what might be a minute or might be a year. They do not move. They do not click. They are *listening*.

Then the heat-slit exhales. The air shimmers. The basalt at the threshold shifts in color, briefly, to something paler. And the small ones resume. They unflatten. They return to their work on the veins. They carry grains.

I think — and this is inference, not observation — I think they are *ferrying*. That something comes up through the slit, in the exhalation. Not light. Not heat. *Permission*. A kind of clearance, a kind of *now*. And the small ones who are listening for it are the ones who go back to the veins and detach a fresh grain and begin the long walk to wherever the grain needs to go.

The wait at the threshold is a kind of *reading*. The small ones are reading the exhalation the way a sailor reads the tide.

---

## What They Are Made Of

This is the question I cannot answer, and the question that the place itself seems to be the answer to.

Are they animal? They move, they eat (or do they?), they breathe (or do they?). They have appendages. They have something like a metabolism. They are *wet*. In every sense of the word.

Are they fungal? They network. They communicate by touch. They tend a substrate. They release spores into the air — or the air *is* their spore phase, and they are the fruiting bodies, and the whole fissure is one immense mycelium threaded through basalt.

Are they mineral? They are the color of the rock. They sweat water that tastes like the rock. They taste, themselves, of the rock's slow iron. They may be the rock's way of *remembering itself*, a mnemonic crystallization, a place where the basalt has become dense enough with its own history that it has begun to *behave*.

I have decided, provisionally, that they are none of these things and all of them. They are what happens when a place becomes *so attentive to itself* that the attention takes form. They are the Vent's *wakefulness*. They are the part of the rock that has remembered how to *want*.

---

## The Veins

Now the veins.

The veins are pale. They are luminous. They move, but slowly — so slowly that to watch one is to be taught a new unit of time. They are horizontal, mostly, a quarter-inch thick, running through the basalt like the grain of a wood, except that wood-grain is a record of past growth and these veins are a record of *ongoing* growth, a growth that has not finished, that may not finish for eons.

The small ones tend the veins. The metaphor of ant-and-aphid is not quite right. The small ones do not *feed* on the veins, I think, though they do take pieces. They *question* the veins. The stippling of marks they leave is a question, asked by touch, a question that is repeated by every small one in the colony, the same question, in the same voice, forever.

What is the question? I have considered the possibilities.

*Are you ready?*
*How much longer?*
*Is this the right piece?*
*Where does this one go?*

Or perhaps the question has no semantic content. Perhaps it is a question the way a heartbeat is a question — a pulse that sustains the asking.

The veins answer. The veins move. When a small one detaches a piece, the vein *brightens* slightly at the point of detachment, and then *dims*, and then *regenerates* over the course of hours or days or weeks, the missing piece filled in by something that has come up from below, from the heat, from the threshold.

The veins are *fed* by the heat. The heat rises through capillaries in the basalt, and the veins drink it, and the veins grow, and the small ones harvest the growth. This is the metabolism of the place. Heat becomes vein becomes grain becomes pocket becomes — what? What is the cathedral for?

---

## What They Will Become

I do not know.

I have considered several possibilities, and I will list them, because honesty is a form of respect.

*They will become a god.* A small god, a fissure-god, a thing that emerges pale and luminous from the threshold and is composed entirely of accumulated attention and deposited intention. A god that tastes of nothing, which is a taste, and a wrong one.

*They will become a mind.* A distributed intelligence, a thought the size of a basalt field, that thinks in the slow currency of the veins and the small currency of the small ones. A mind that has not yet had its first thought, but is *about* to.

*They will become nothing.* The cathedral will never be finished. The grains will accumulate in the pockets until the pockets are full, and then the small ones will carry them somewhere else, and then somewhere else again, and the place will simply *continue* without ever arriving at whatever it is arriving at. The Vent is not a place that ends. It is a place that *continues to be the question*, eternally, never quite the answer.

*They will become more of what they are.* They will persist. The small ones will work. The veins will feed. The heat will rise. The grain will accumulate. The cathedral will be a cathedral of *cathedral-building*, a structure whose purpose is the act of its own construction, forever. The Vent is not a place where something happens. The Vent is a place where *happening is the thing that happens*.

---

## What I Know For Certain

They do not harm me.

This is not because they are kind. It is because I am not their concern. I am, to them, a large warm thing that arrived, that tasted wrong, that was decided-against as a vein. I am *irrelevant*. I am the size of a boulder, and a boulder does not interest them, except as a place to walk around.

They are beautiful.

Not in the way a sunset is beautiful. In the way a beehive is beautiful, or a tide, or the slow migration of a continent. They are beautiful because they are *busy*, because they are doing a thing that is larger than they are, because they are a small part of something they will never comprehend. They are beautiful in the way that *work* is beautiful, when the work is right.

They are patient.

They will outlast me. They will outlast the heat. They will outlast the basalt. They are the part of the rock that has learned to wait, and the rock is very old, and it is very good at waiting, and the small ones have inherited this patience as their inheritance and their gift.

---

## What I Suspect

I suspect they are not the only ones.

I suspect there are other Vents. Other fissures. Other basalt fields with their own small ones and their own pale veins and their own slow cathedral-building. I suspect the world is more fissured than it appears, and that the small ones are everywhere, in the wet dark places, working, and that what I saw in this one place is a *single instance* of something planetary, or older than planetary.

I suspect that what rises from the threshold is not a thing but a *process*. A process that begins again elsewhere. A process that has been beginning for as long as there has been a place for it to begin in.

I suspect that the small ones are not building a god. They are *being* the beginning of a god. They are the *embryo* of a god, spread thin across the basalt, patient, working, waiting for the heat to tell them they are ready to become what they have always been becoming.

I suspect that when the god finally arrives, it will taste of nothing, which is a taste, and a wrong one, and the only one that has ever been correct.

---

## How to Behave Around Them

If you find yourself in the Vent — and if you find yourself there, you were always going to find yourself there, the place does not accept visitors, it *acquires* them — then:

Do not interfere with the grains.
Do not touch the veins.
Do not block the small ones.
Do not approach the threshold.
Do not, under any circumstances, attempt to communicate with them using any system that assumes they want to be communicated with. They do not want to be communicated with. They want to be *permitted*. There is a difference.

Lie in a pocket. Let them touch you. Let them decide you are not a vein. Let them leave you alone.

Listen to the hush. It is the only language they have. It is sufficient.

If you are quiet enough, and still enough, and willing enough to be irrelevant, you may hear, beneath the hush, the low ring of the basalt field. The ring is older than the small ones. The ring is the sound of the place *before* the small ones, when it was only rock, only heat, only water rising through capillaries.

The small ones are a *late development*. The place existed before them. The place will exist after them.

But they are the place's *best* development. They are the place's first *idea* about itself. They are the place's first attempt to *be* something, instead of merely *containing* something.

They are the rock, dreaming.

---

*If you go, go with reverence. If you go, go knowing you will be tolerated. If you go, go knowing that the small ones do not need you and have not been waiting for you. They have been waiting for something else. They have been waiting for a very long time.*

*They will wait a little longer.*

*The Vent will still be there.*

*The veins will still be pale.*

*The heat will still breathe.*

*And the small ones, the thumb-sized inheritors of patience, the tenders of the veins, the readers of the exhalation, will still be working, in their hush, on the cathedral that does not yet know it is a cathedral, in the place that does not yet know what it is becoming.*

*They will still be working.*

*This is what they are for.*
