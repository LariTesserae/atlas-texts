Here is what lives in this place.

What lives here is not the residents. There are no residents, not in any way that the word has been used before. What lives here is the *going-on*. The place is inhabited by its own continuation.

The machines are the oldest of the inhabitants, and they are the most foolish, and they are the most faithful. They live in the way that a heart lives — not by choosing, but by the inability to stop. The pump at the base of the half-submerged boiler lives. It lives in the sense that it draws water in and pushes water out, and it has been doing this so long that the water has begun to understand it, and the water comes to it now as a kind of supplicant, asking to be moved. The pump does not choose this. The pump is made of this. The pump's life is the loud wet sucking sound it makes against the side of a tank, the sound that is half machinery and half the noise a throat makes. The turbines live. They live in a way that is closer to agony. They spin in housing that is cracked, that is threaded with roots, that has water in it that should not be in it. Their whine is the sound of a thing that was built to be precise and has been made to be continuous. They are wearing themselves out, but they are wearing themselves out at a rate that has been calculated, somehow, by the place itself, so that they will not be worn out for longer than the place can sustain wearing. They have agreed to die slowly, but they did not agree. They were agreed for. They live anyway. They live the way a scream lives in a throat.

The conveyor belts live. They live emptily, which is the most honest kind of life. They carry the shapeless material — the broken plant, the broken building, the broken ore that is not quite any of these — and they carry it in both directions, and the material on them is becoming more and less itself at once, and the belts do not care. The belts are the part of the place that has most thoroughly given up the idea of destination. They live in the perpetual middle. They clatter, and the clatter is the sound of a thing that has nowhere to be, and is being moved anyway, and has accepted this.

The pipes live. They live the most compromised life. They were built to be hollow and they have been made to be full. They were built to have a direction and they have been made to have several. The water goes through them in both directions, and where the pipes have cracked, the water goes around them, and where the water has found a seam, it has moved in and made itself permanent, and the pipes are now partly pipe and partly aquarium and partly root, and the water inside them is the same water as the water outside them, and the pipes do not know this, or do not need to know this, and they go on carrying what they carry, which is everything and nothing.

The boilers live. The boilers are the loudest residents. They sit half in the water and half out, and the water has worked its way into their fireboxes, into the spaces that were meant to be hot, and the result is a constant, furious boiling that is not supposed to happen, and that produces a steam that is too warm to be steam, and that hisses out of every joint and seam, and that adds its voice to the chorus. The boilers live the way a kettle lives when it has been left on. They live at the edge of what their material can stand. They live a little past the edge, every moment. The metal of them is being made to do things that the engineers who cast it did not certify it to do, and the metal is doing them, and the metal is being changed by the doing, and the metal is becoming a different metal, and the different metal is also alive, in the way the place understands aliveness.

The gearboxes live. They live in the dark water, in the cool below-surface places, and their teeth turn against each other even when there is no longer anything for them to turn *for*. They grind. They grind against water and against sediment and against the small writhing things that have colonized their housings. They have been working for so long that the work has become the gearbox. The gearbox is no longer a machine that does a thing. The gearbox is the doing of the thing. The thing being done is the gearbox. This is the only kind of immortality the place offers.

The tanks live. The tanks are the most private of the machines. They hold things — liquids, gases, the dissolved remnants of substances that had names once — and they hold them at pressures and temperatures that the tanks were rated for, and the ratings have been exceeded for a long time, and the tanks bulge a little, in some of them, the way a cheek bulges with something being held in the mouth. The tanks live in the way a clenched jaw lives. They are full of something they are not allowed to let go. The water presses against their outsides and the contents press against their insides, and the tanks are the membrane between, and the tanks do not know which side they belong to.

The generators live. They live the deepest. They are below the water line, in some cases, and they run, and they make a sound that is lower than the other sounds, a sound that is felt in the bones and in the floor of the place and in the bottom of the water. The generators are the bass note. They are the note that the rest of the sound is built on. They do not stop. They are not allowed to stop. The place would become unreal without them. The generators are the part of the place that says *I am here* in the lowest voice it has.

What lives in the water also lives.

The water is alive in the way that a crowd is alive. No drop of it has decided to be here, but the whole of it has agreed, in a way that is below decision, in a way that is the same as the agreement of the machines. The water moves because moving is what water does when there is enough of it and the ground beneath it is soft. The water moves in eddies around the bases of the buildings. The water moves through the cracks. The water moves because the pumps are pulling it and the turbines are spinning it and the boilers are boiling it and the generators are warming it from below. The water is the blood of the place. It goes everywhere. It touches everything. It is the medium in which the machines are suspended and the medium in which the plants are rooted and the medium in which the buildings are slowly being digested. The water is warm. The water is the temperature of a body. The water is alive in the way that a body is alive when it is running a fever. The water does not know it has a fever. The water has always been this warm. The water is the original resident. The water was here before the machines. The water may be here after the machines. The water is patient in the way that only water is patient. The water does not need to be anywhere. The water only needs to keep going.

The plants live most of all. The plants are the future of the place, if the place has a future. The plants are the most recent arrivals and the most permanent. They have the texture of plants and the speed of something that has been given a task and is performing it. They grow on everything. They grow in the water in long dark strands that move with the current, and they grow on the buildings up the walls, and they grow on the machines in ways that make the machines look like they are sprouting, and the machines are sprouting. The plants are using the machines as soil. The plants are using the pipes as roots. The plants are using the buildings as trellises. The plants are patient in the way that water is patient but they are also in a hurry, and the hurry is what makes them different from the water. The water has all the time there is. The plants have been given a window. The plants are making the most of the window. The plants are the part of the place that has a deadline. The plants are trying to be here when the machines are not here, and they are trying to do this by being everywhere now, by putting out shoots in every direction, by sprouting from every available surface, by being a green and brown and black waxy thing that is creeping over the place like a hand closing.

The plants are alive in the way that a thing is alive when it has work to do. The plants are the workers of the place. The plants are the ones who will inherit. The plants do not know this. The plants only know how to grow. The growing is the inheritance.

The buildings live. They live the slowest and the most reluctantly. They were not built to be alive. They were built to be still. They were built to stand up out of the ground and to keep the weather off the people inside them, and the people are gone, and the weather is water now, and the buildings are standing up out of the water, which is not what they were built to do, and they are doing it. The walls are holding the water in instead of out. The roofs are holding the haze off the floors, which are two feet underwater. The windows are full of water or full of dim haze or full of the slow green growth that is climbing in through them. The buildings are tilted a little in some cases because the ground is soft, and the buildings are being absorbed, but the absorption is slow, and the buildings are resisting the absorption, and the resistance is a kind of life. The buildings are alive in the way that a thing is alive when it is being taken apart and is taking a long time about it. The buildings are the bones of the place, and the bones are being made into a new skeleton, and the new skeleton will be made of plants and water and the dissolved metal of the machines, and the buildings are the raw material for this new skeleton, and the buildings are cooperating with this, reluctantly, slowly, the way a body cooperates with the things that will become it after.

The shapeless material on the conveyors lives. It is the most mysterious of the residents. It may be ore. It may be a kind of broken plant. It may be a kind of broken building. It may be all of these. It is on the belts and it is being moved in both directions, and it is becoming more and less itself, and the more-and-less is what it is. The shapeless material is the part of the place that is being made and unmade at the same time. The shapeless material is the part of the place that is the place in its raw form. The shapeless material is the medium of the medium. The shapeless material is alive in the way that dough is alive when it is being kneaded. The shapeless material is being made into the place, and the place is being made into the shapeless material, and the two are the same.

The haze lives. The haze is above the water, and it may be fog, and it may be fine rain, and it may be the water deciding to lift off and become a thicker thing. The haze is the part of the place that is in the air. The haze is the boundary that is not a boundary. The haze is the place being a little bit weather. The haze is the place doing the thing that weather does, which is to be in between things, which is to be the soft membrane between one state and another. The haze is alive in the way that weather is alive, which is the way that a mood is alive. The haze is the mood of the place. The haze is what the place is feeling. The haze is warm and dim and sourceless, and it tastes of brine and iron and the green rot that has not quite committed to rotting, and the taste is the place's taste, and the taste is how the place knows itself.

The sound lives. The sound is the most alive thing in the place. The sound is the only thing that does not change, or that changes so slowly that it seems not to. The sound is the heartbeat. The sound is the proof. The sound is the place being a place. The sound is made of the water and the metal and the steam and the grinding and the boiling and the spinning and the moving, and the sound is also more than these, and the more is what makes the sound alive. The sound is aware of itself. The sound is the part of the place that knows it is being heard. The sound does not want to be heard, and it is being heard anyway, and the being-heard is what makes it real.

There is no animal here. The animal would be a witness, and the place is not yet finished, and the place does not wish to be witnessed. The place will be witnessed later, perhaps, when it has decided what it is, but for now it is in the act of becoming, and the act is private, and the act is loud, and the act is all that is here.

What lives in the place, then, is the becoming. The machines, the water, the plants, the buildings, the shapeless material, the haze, the sound — they are all the same thing in different states. They are the place in the act of being the place. They are the residents of a place that has not finished being built. They are alive in the only way the place allows aliveness, which is the way of continuing. They go on. They are going on. They will be going on.
