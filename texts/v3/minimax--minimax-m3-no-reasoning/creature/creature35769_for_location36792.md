The shapes.

Begin with them, because they are what a visitor comes hoping for, even when the visitor has not yet admitted to themselves that they came hoping.

There is no better name for them than *the shapes*. The word *animal* would do violence to them. The word *creature* would suggest a lineage they do not possess. The word *spirit* would imply an intention or a sender they do not, so far as anyone can tell, have. They are shapes. They are the size of a deer, or the size of a small dog, and once, on a still afternoon in the deeper groves, you will see one the size of a child standing on a far ridge, looking back at you with what you will spend the rest of the day trying to decide was attention. They are pale. They are the exact pale of the trunks they stand among — bone, parchment, old teeth, weak tea — and this is why you will not always see them when you should. They have, over a very long time, become the same color as the forest. Or the forest has, over a very long time, become the same color as them. The question is unresolvable, and the forest does not appear to be in a hurry to resolve it.

What they look like, when you can make them out, is an animal that has been sketched in by someone who had heard of animals but had not, recently, seen one. There is a body, and the body has four directions it could go. There is a head end, and there are limbs, or the memory of limbs. The legs, if they are legs, do not articulate in any way you can identify from a distance. The whole figure has the slightly unfinished quality of a shape remembered from a dream you had as a child — present, weighted, but not quite committed to the syntax of a body. They are, you will think, more like nouns than like animals. They are things that *be deer*, in the way a word can *be* the thing it names without ever quite being the thing itself.

How they move.

This is the part that takes adjustment.

They do not walk. They resolve. You will be watching one — assuming you have been still enough, and patient enough, to be allowed to watch one — and it will be at the base of a trunk, and then, after a long interval during which nothing in particular has happened, it will be at the base of a different trunk. The interval is the part you cannot account for. There is no transit. There is no blurred middle of a stride. There is the position, and then a stillness, and then another position, and the stillness between them is the stillness of a thought that has changed its mind, not the stillness of feet lifting and falling. If you tried to photograph one, you would get either the first position or the second. The shutter could not catch the between, because the between is not a place.

This is not fast. The resolutions come minutes apart, sometimes. They are not teleportation. They are something more patient than teleportation. They are the forest, very slowly, correcting the position of one of its own emanations, the way a sleeping animal shifts its weight in a dream. You are watching, if you are watching closely, a forest making the smallest possible movements in its sleep.

What they do, when they are not resolving.

They graze. This is the word. It is not quite the right word. They lower the front of themselves toward the powder of the forest floor and they hold that posture for a long time, and the powder does not move, and they do not appear to be taking anything from it, and yet when they lift again you have the strong and unprovable sense that something has been exchanged. Perhaps they are exchanging calcium with the substrate at a rate so slow that an hour of watching is one transaction. Perhaps they are simply performing the gesture of grazing the way a ritual performs the gesture of a meal. Perhaps the forest, having nothing to eat, has nonetheless remembered the shape of eating, and is rehearsing.

They drink. They stand at the places where the powder is deepest, and they put the front of themselves down to it, and they hold it there. There is no water. They are not drinking water. They are, possibly, drinking the memory of water. The stone remembers water. The shapes remember drinking. They are, between them, completing a transaction that no longer has a marketplace.

They stand. Often, they just stand. They stand among the trunks, in the long pale stripes of light, and they do nothing that you can identify as doing, and they are not sleeping, and they are not watching, and they are not waiting in any way that implies an event to wait for. They are just *being there*, in the way a rock is being there, except with the outline of a thing that might once have had errands.

They look at you. This happens rarely, and only when you have not asked for it. You will be sitting at the base of a trunk, having decided not to pursue the shapes, having decided to let them come to the terms of their own existence, and one of them will be at the edge of a grove some distance away, and it will turn its front toward you, and you will know you are being looked at. Not examined. Not appraised. Just *registered*, in the way a tree registers a tree, in the way the wind registers a rock. The looking has no curiosity in it. The looking has the quality of something that has decided, for its own reasons, to acknowledge your presence in the same forest.

You will not, by walking toward one, ever reach it.

This is the rule. It is the only rule the shapes have. If you see a shape at the base of a distant trunk and you walk toward it, it does not flee. It does not watch you approach. It does not tense. It simply is not there when you arrive at the trunk you were aiming for. Not gone. Not moved. Just *not there*. The trunk is the same. The light is the same. The powder at its base is the same. The shape, which you saw clearly enough to draw from memory, has been removed from the situation in a way that left no footprints, no disturbance, no gap in the air. You are standing where it was. It was standing where you are. The forest has, in the time it took you to cross the distance, decoupled the shape from the place it occupied, and the shape is now, if anywhere, somewhere else, doing something else, on its own schedule.

Do not interpret this as fear. Do not interpret it as shyness. Do not interpret it as the etiquette of wild animals that keep their distance. The shapes are not afraid of you. They are not keeping their distance. They are simply not in the practice of being where you go. They have, in the long mineral dreaming of the forest, become attached to positions rather than to paths, and you, by walking, are attached to paths rather than to positions, and the two systems do not intersect.

What they are, if you force yourself to choose.

You will, if you stay long enough, force yourself to choose. You will, after days of not being able to choose, decide on one of the available explanations and live inside it until the next day undoes you. The explanations are these:

They are the calcium dreaming. The forest, in its slow accretion, has produced enough calcium carbonate to have begun, somewhere in the long arithmetic of mineral becoming, to misbehave. The shapes are what misbehavior looks like from the outside. They are the mineral substrate, in its deep patience, rehearsing the alternative to itself. A tree grows. A shape grazes. A trunk stands. A shape stands. The forest, having made all the standing things it has patience for, has begun to make the things that interrupt the standing things, because a forest with only standing things is not, in some buried sense, a forest. It is a collection. The shapes are what the forest has added to itself, in the long erosion of its own self-containment, to feel like a forest.

They are your attention made visible. You are, after all, watching. You have been watching the trunks, and the powder, and the slow weather, and the shapes are what watching, pressed long enough against the same surface, does to the surface. Pareidolia, in a normal place, is in your head. Here, where the substrate is itself a kind of slow mind, the pareidolia has somewhere to land. The forest is responsive in the way woodgrain is responsive, and the shapes are the figures that emerge in it because you have been staring, and staring, and the forest, in its long patience, has consented to show you what it looks like when it is being stared at.

They are the forest's moods. The wind is a mood. The rare fog is a mood. The shapes are the moods that happen to have outlines. The forest is not empty, only patient, and patience, when it has been going on long enough, acquires a grammar, and the grammar acquires a vocabulary, and the vocabulary acquires, on occasion, the silhouette of an animal. The shapes are the forest, on a particular afternoon, feeling like a deer. They are the forest, on a different afternoon, feeling like a small dog. They are the forest, once in a very long while, feeling like a child, and turning, with the slow dignity of weather, to look at the only other thing in the world.

Choose one. Live inside it for a day. You will find, by the second day, that it has stopped fitting.

What they are, if you refuse to choose.

If you refuse to choose, you will sit at the base of a trunk through a long pale afternoon, and the wind will move through the canopy, and the powder will rise in low sheets, and somewhere in the grove a shape will resolve, and it will stand, and it will lower its front to the powder, and it will hold that posture until you have stopped trying to explain it, and then it will resolve somewhere else, and you will watch it go, and you will not have understood it, and the not-understanding will, by the time the light shifts, have become a kind of companionship.

The shapes do not require you to understand them. The forest does not require you to understand the shapes. The place you are in does not require you to take anything from it except the long bright air, and the wind, and the occasional distant sound of a branch giving way under its own weight and shattering, ceramic-clean, against the powder.

What lives in the Calcified Forest of Verreth is, in the end, the forest itself, in all the ways it has learned to insist on its own presence. The trees are one way. The powder is another. The wind is another. The fogs are another. The shapes are the way the forest has, after a very long time, decided to introduce itself to the things that arrive. They are not for you. They are not against you. They are the forest's long, unhurried handshake with whatever has wandered into its weather.

If you stay long enough, you will stop trying to figure out what they are. You will stop trying to walk toward them. You will sit at the base of a trunk, and you will let the shapes resolve and unresolve at the edges of your vision, and you will understand, eventually, that the question *what lives here* has, in this place, the same answer as the question *what is happening here*. The answer is: the forest. The answer is: the slow calcium. The answer is: a patience so old that it has begun, very quietly, to generate its own fauna.

You will not see them up close. You will not touch one. You will not follow one to wherever it goes when it is not here. You will leave the way the wind leaves — by having been, for a while, in the same place as something much older than yourself, and by carrying, on the way out, a faint dusting of pale powder in the folds of your clothes, and the memory, faint and stubborn, of having been looked at, once, by something that did not require you to look back.

The forest continues. The shapes continue. The patience continues, and it does not, on the whole, have anywhere particular to be.
