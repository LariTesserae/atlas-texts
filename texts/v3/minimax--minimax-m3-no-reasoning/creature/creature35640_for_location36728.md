The things that live here do not know they are the strangest things in a strange place. They have no measure against which to know it. They are what the place made, and the place has been making for a long time, and what the place has made is, in its way, a kind of answer—the answer a very old, very patient question would give if it could speak, which is: *here, and still here, and this is enough.*

---

The first of them is a shape that has no name for itself, because names are a thing of surfaces, of daylight, of a place where one creature calls across a distance to another and the call is heard. There is no calling here. There is no answering. There is only the long body moving along the bottom in the slow current's lee, and the body is not so much a body as a length of negotiation between the silt and the water above it, a thing that has settled into being the way a thing settles into the silt, by not fighting the pressing-down.

It is long. It is the length of a piece of cloth, the length of a long bone, the length of several long bones strung together by the meat of it. It is as thick through as a man's thigh at its middle, tapering both ways to a blunt head and a thinner, longer tail that ripples behind it in a way that is not quite swimming. It is not swimming. It is *being moved*, and it is moving with the moving, and the difference between these two has been forgotten.

Its skin is the color of the silt. Its skin is the color of the wood. Its skin is the color of the bones, in the places where the bones have gone that color, which is most places. It is not camouflaged. Camouflage is a thing of eyes and of watchers and of something to hide from, and there is nothing here that watches in that way. It is simply that the place has made everything the same color over a long time, the way a river makes every stone in its bed the same color, and the thing that moves along the bottom is made of the same color because it was made of the same water.

It does not have eyes that see in the way the word *see* is used above, where *see* means light entering a hole and landing on a thing inside. The eyes it has are wide and dark and they sit on either side of the blunt head like two cups of the same black water it moves through. They are not for looking out. They are for feeling the moving of other things—the pressure of a current shifting, the displacement of water by something passing, the slow tide of silt rising when a foot, if it had a foot, pressed down. It sees by being touched by what it does not touch. This is how everything here sees. This is the only seeing.

It feeds the way a mouth feeds. It has a mouth on the underside of the blunt head, a mouth that is not made for biting but for taking in, a mouth that is a soft opening that presses against the silt and against the small living things in the silt and against the small dead things drifting in the current just above the silt, and the mouth takes them in the way a mouth takes in water when a thing is not thinking about water, automatically, without decision. The tech of this place. The open mouth in the current.

It moves across the floor and it bends to the floor and it rises a little from the floor and it sets down again, and the silt where it set down closes behind it the way water closes behind a hand drawn up from a pool. There is no track. There is no path. There is only the moving of the thing and the closing of the place behind it, and the place is the same as it was, and the thing is a little different, and the difference is what it ate, and what it ate is now inside it, becoming it, and it is becoming the place again, in a few years, when it stops.

---

The second of them is not a single thing. The second of them is many things, or the second of them is one thing made of many small things, and the question of which it is has been asked here for a very long time and has not been answered, and the asking of it has not been given up, exactly, but has been set down in a kind of patience, the way a thing is set down on the silt when the hand that was holding it has forgotten it.

It lives on the bones. It lives on the wood. It lives on the stones and on the cloth. It is a pale, fine, branching thing that grows out of the surfaces of the dead things, and it is alive, and the alive is the strange part, because nothing else here is alive in the way it is alive, which is to say, alive the way a thing is alive that does not move.

It does not have a mouth. It does not have a way of going to get a thing. It puts a part of itself into the bone or the wood or the cloth and it takes from the bone or the wood or the cloth the small thing it needs, and the small thing it needs is not the bone or the wood or the cloth but the *going-on* of the bone or the wood or the cloth, the slow becoming-something-else of them, the long patience of them, and the pale branching thing takes this in and makes of it more of itself, more of the pale fine branching, which extends a little further out into the water, which drifts a little in the current, which finds another piece of dead thing, which puts a part of itself into that one too, and so on, and the many pieces of the one pale thing are connected by the going-on of all the dead things between them, which is to say that the dead things of the floor are not dead, are not done, are still part of a long slow circuit of matter becoming matter becoming matter, and the pale thing is the only part of the circuit that knows it is going on, in the dim way a thing can know it is going on without a brain, without a thought, without anything at all but the bare fact of the putting out of more of itself into the water.

It glows. The glow is the faint blue-green of the description of the place, the glow of the afterimage of something that burned a long time ago. The pale thing glows in patches, here and there, and the glow is the only light there is, and the light is not enough, and the light is enough. The eye of the long thing, the long slow thing with the mouth on the underside, the long slow thing that is the color of the silt—the eye of that thing finds the glow the way a hand finds a wall in a dark room, by the moving toward, and the moving toward is the only way anything here finds anything.

The pale thing has been growing on the floor for a very long time. It has grown over bones and into bones. It has grown up the standing stumps of the trees that grew in some other time, and it has grown down the roots of them, and the roots are not roots anymore, are part of the pale thing now, are a part of the going-on, and the pale thing is not the pale thing anymore, in those places, is a part of the floor, is part of the dark, is part of the long body of the place itself.

---

The third of them is the cloth, or the cloth is the third of them, or the cloth and the thing and the other thing are the same thing, here, the way the wood and the bone and the stone have become the same thing in places.

The cloth is gray. The cloth is torn. The cloth moves in the current with the slow patience of the water, and the slow patience of the water is the slow patience of the place, and the cloth has taken on the slow patience. A hand laid on it would sink into it a little. A hand laid on it would feel it give, and the giving would not be the giving of new cloth, which is the giving of a thing that has not yet decided what it is, but the giving of an old cloth, an old cloth that has decided a long time ago what it is, which is to be here, and to be a little less here every year, and to be, in its being-a-little-less, the food of the pale thing, and the home of the small things that drift in the current, and the place where the long thing comes to rest sometimes, in the lee of a draped and half-buried fold of it, in the small pocket of quieter water that forms there.

The cloth was a thing that was made. This is the strangest thing about the cloth. Every other thing here is a thing that was, or a thing that is, but the cloth is a thing that was *made*, and the making of it was a long time ago, in a place where there was a maker, and the maker had hands, and the maker used the hands, and the cloth is the memory of the hands, here, in this place where there are no hands. The cloth is the only artifact. The cloth is the only proof. The cloth is the only argument that there was ever an above.

The things that live here do not think about this. The things that live here do not think. The long thing with the mouth does not think. The pale thing that grows does not think. The cloth does not think, and the cloth is not alive, and the cloth is the most thinking-adjacent thing here, the only thing that has the shape of a thought, the only thing that was shaped by something that thought, and the cloth has given up the thought, has let it go, has let the water take it, and the cloth is just cloth now, and the cloth is home.

---

The fourth of them is the small things that drift in the current and are gathered in. The small things are not one kind. The small things are many kinds, or the small things are what the water makes when the water is left alone for a long time in a place with a lot of old dead stuff in it, which is what the water is and where the water is. The small things are specks. The small things are faint clouds. The small things are pale threads in the water that move with the moving and are moved by the moving and are the moving in a way. The small things are the blood of the place, if the place is a body, and the place is a body, and the small things are what the long things eat, the small things are what the pale things take in, the small things are what becomes everything, the small things are the way the place keeps itself going, by being itself, over and over, the same going-on, the same circuit, the same slow breath of the current in and the current out.

The small things do not have names. The small things do not have a way of being named. The small things are too small for a name to land on, the way a shadow is too thin to be picked up. The long thing takes them in by the mouth and the pale thing takes them in by the part of itself that is in the water and the cloth moves among them and does not take them in but is among them, is part of the drift of them, is a slow thing among the small slow things, and the small things settle on the cloth the way silt settles on the cloth, and the cloth is heavier for the small things, and the cloth is more the floor for the small things, and the cloth and the small things and the silt and the long thing and the pale thing are all one thing here, are all the same going-on, are all the same long body of the place, breathing the current in, breathing the current out.

---

There is a fifth of them, and the fifth of them is the current itself, or the current is the fifth of them, and the difference between the living thing and the not-living thing has been worn down here, in this place where the difference was not very important to begin with, and has been worn down further by the long time, and the wearing-down has been going on for so long that the current and the long thing and the pale thing and the cloth and the small things are all one thing now, are all the place, are all the body, and the body is breathing, and the breath is the current, and the current is the hum, and the hum is felt in the chest and in the bones of the inner ear, and the felt-in-the-chest is the way the place knows it is alive, the only way a place can know it is alive, which is by the going-on, by the being-still-here, by the small short sound of a bone settling, of a stone turning over in the silt, of a thing that is alive touching a thing that is dead and the touching being a kind of event, a kind of weather, a kind of punctuation in a place that has forgotten punctuation, that has forgotten how to mark time, that has only the going-on, and the going-on is enough, and the going-on is the place, and the place is the fifth of them, and the fifth of them is the going-on, and the going-on is everything here, and the everything here is the dark, and the dark is the skin, and the skin is old, and the old is the only time there is.
