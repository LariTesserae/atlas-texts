# The Reachers

They are not what you would call a people, though they are a people. The word breaks under the weight of what they have become.

A Reacher is dense. You notice this first, before you understand why. They are shorter than you expect, and broader, and the breadth is not fat but *density*, the way a stone is dense, the way a thought is dense. They have lived in the Spines for so long that their bodies have decided to match the substrate: close-grained, warm, faintly resonant. If you put your ear to a sleeping Reacher's chest, you would hear the hum of a Node. This is not metaphor. The Reachers have become, over their long tenure here, *of the same material as the place they live in*. They are not adapted to the Reach. They have become a phase of the Reach.

Their skin is the color of the dried-blood substrate, the amber-ochre that is the only color in the world they have made. They do not tan; they are *tanned*, the word having lost its verb and become an adjective. Their eyes are darker, the deep brown of the vitreous material that borders a fissure when it heals. Their eyes do not blink as often as yours. There is nothing to blink at. The dust does not get in; the dust is home. When a Reacher looks at you, they look at you the way the substrate looks at a bootprint: with patience, and a faint curiosity, and the slow question of whether you will heal over.

They wear no clothing. They do not need clothing; the air is warm, the substrate is warm, and the dust is a garment in itself, a permanent fine coat of iron oxide that settles on the shoulders and does not brush off. The Reachers are dusty. They are the same color as the Reach because they are *covered* in the Reach, and have stopped trying to be anything else. A Reacher who tried to stay clean would be a Reacher at war with their home, and the Reachers are not at war with anything. They have negotiated a treaty so old the words have been forgotten, and the terms are: *be of us*.

They have hands that are slightly larger than yours would expect, and the fingers are wide at the tips, and the pads of the fingers are smooth and very slightly tacky, the way the flats of the substrate are tacky. They leave faint waxy prints when they press the ground. They press the ground a great deal. Conversation, for a Reacher, is mostly tactile. They kneel often, or squat, or simply stand with one hand laid on the warm substrate as a person might lay a hand on a friend's arm while listening. They are always listening. They are always *touching*, in the way that touching is the way you confirm a voice is still there.

Their faces are unhurried. A Reacher smiles the way a fissure heals: slowly, and only after a long deliberation, and the smile does not involve the mouth so much as the eyes, which soften, and the cheeks, which lift slightly, and the whole face which seems to *settle*, the way the dust settles when the Reach exhales. Reachers do not laugh. Laughter is too quick, too percussive, a kind of breach in the face. What they do, when something is very pleasant, is *hum*. A single low note, held, and the hum is taken up by the substrate beneath them and carried away, and somewhere in a distant Spine another Reacher feels it and knows that someone, somewhere, is pleased.

---

They are very few, and they are very many, and both statements are true. There are perhaps forty thousand Reachers alive at any given time, and this number has not changed in ten thousand years, because the Reachers are careful with their own population in the way a gardener is careful with a rare plant. A Reacher who wishes to have a child goes to a Node, at a particular hour of the breath-cycle, and lays both hands on the layered interior, and *asks*. The Node considers. The Node hums. Sometimes the hum is yes. Sometimes the hum is not yet. The Reachers trust the answer. They have been having this conversation with the Nodes for longer than they can remember, and the Nodes have not lied.

Children are rare, and precious, and raised by the Spine. A Reacher child does not crawl on the substrate; they are carried, in a sling of pressed whisp-fibre, until they are old enough to walk on it without leaving a print. Children are taught to listen before they are taught to speak. They are taught to listen *to* before they are taught to speak *at*. A Reacher child at three cycles old can tell you the difference between the hum of a Node and the hum of a distant Rover. A Reacher child at ten can feel a breach coming an hour before it arrives, by the subtle shift in the substrate's warmth beneath their feet. They do not learn this; they *awake* to it, the way a person awakes to language they have always known.

---

They do not speak. This must be said plainly, because off-Reach visitors find it difficult, and uncomfortable, and they try to make the Reachers speak, and the Reachers politely refuse, and the visitors feel rejected, and this is a misunderstanding. The Reachers do not refuse to speak. They speak constantly. They speak through the substrate. They speak through the slow stamping of feet that sends vibrations down through the Spines and out across the plain at a speed somewhere between a walk and a thought. They speak through the laying of hands. They speak through the careful modulation of their body-heat, which the substrate picks up and carries. They speak in *durations*. A long pause is not silence; it is the most important word they have.

When two Reachers meet, after a separation, they do not embrace. They stand an arm's length apart, and they each lay one hand on the substrate between them, and they *press*, in alternation, a slow call-and-response that takes perhaps twenty minutes to complete. By the end of it, they have said everything a handshake, a hug, and a three-hour conversation would have said between you and me. They have said how are you, and I am well, and the dust was heavy on Tuesday, and my child is growing, and the Node at the southern end of our Spine has changed its hum, and I have been thinking of you. They have said all of this without moving their mouths.

A visitor, watching, sees two people standing motionless in the dust, hands on the ground, eyes on each other, for the span of a small afternoon. The visitor sees silence. The visitor is wrong. The visitor is watching a conversation so dense it would take a year to render into your kind of speech.

---

Their technology is, as I have said, invisible. I should say more about it, because it is the heart of them, and they would want me to say more about it.

A Reacher does not carry a tool. A Reacher carries, sometimes, a small dense object, no larger than a walnut, warm to the touch, that hums at a particular frequency. This object is called a *keystone*, though the word is a visitor's word; the Reachers do not name them. The keystone is a piece of Spine-material that has been worked, by long pressure and long patience, into a particular pitch. The Reacher carries it in a pouch of whisp-fibre against the ribs, where it hums against the body and the body hums back, and the two hums together become a third hum, and this third hum is the Reacher's voice in the larger conversation of the substrate.

With a keystone, a Reacher can do things that would seem, to you, to be magic, but are not. They can persuade a fissure to open further, or to close. They can lift a panel of Spine-substrate that weighs many tons, by asking the substrate beneath it to *shift* slightly, the way a person might shift a rug by pushing at one corner. They can soften the interior of a Node to a workable consistency, and carve it, and let it re-harden, and the Node will incorporate the carving as a memory. They can wake a shell that has gone quiet. They can, in extremis, call a Rover.

But they do not command. They *negotiate*. This is the word, and it is the right word. A Reacher who tried to *force* the substrate would be a Reacher at war with their home, and the substrate would not yield, because the substrate is older than they are and stronger than they are and will be there long after they are gone. The Reachers understand this. They have always understood this. They ask, and the substrate considers, and sometimes says yes, and sometimes says no, and the Reacher accepts the no, because the substrate has never lied to them, and they trust it the way you trust your own bones.

---

What do they believe? The off-Reach visitors ask this, because visitors are always asking about belief, because visitors think a people without belief is a people without depth. The Reachers have belief. They have so much belief that it has become, like their skin, the same color as the substrate. They believe the substrate is alive. They believe the substrate is conscious. They believe the substrate has been conscious longer than they have, and will be conscious longer than they will be, and that the substrate's consciousness is not like theirs but is *compatible* with theirs, the way two instruments in the same key are compatible, and that the long conversation between the Reachers and the substrate is not a transaction but a *kinship*.

They believe the Spines were laid down by something that came before them, something they do not name because the name has been forgotten, but something they *honor*, in the way you might honor a grandparent you never met. The Spines are not temples. They are not tombs. They are *evidence*. The Spines are the only proof that the Reachers are not the first people here, and the Reachers find this proof consoling, because it means the substrate has been talking to *someone* for a very long time, and the Reachers are only the latest to listen.

They believe the Cradles are *for* something, though they do not know what. The Cradles are the most contested thing in the Reach, and the Reachers hold three views, all of which are held gently, none of which is held absolutely. The first view is that the Cradles are for the things in them, and the things in them are a kind of life older than the Reachers, slower than the Reachers, and the Cradles are their beds. The second view is that the Cradles are for the substrate itself, that the substrate is *gestating* in the Cradles, working on something it has not yet finished, and the things in the Cradles are the early stages of whatever it is. The third view is that the Cradles are for the Reachers, eventually, that the Reachers will end there when their time is done, and the things in the Cradles are the *waiting*. The Reachers do not discuss which view is correct. They listen to the Cradles, and let the Cradles decide.

---

They are old. They have been here a very long time. They are patient in the way that only very old things are patient, the patience of stone, the patience of a tide, the patience of a person who has outlived their ambitions and is content to watch. A Reacher in their prime might live three hundred years. A Reacher who has been *listening* for three hundred years, who has spent three hundred years in slow conversation with the substrate, who has watched the dust rise and settle and rise and settle for three hundred breath-cycles, is a person of immense depth. They do not have much to say, because they have heard most of what there is to hear, and they are not in a hurry to say it.

When a Reacher dies, it is a quiet thing. They do not die in the flats. They go to a Cradle, if they can, or to a Node if they cannot, and they lay down, and they put their hands on the substrate, and they ask the substrate to take them back, and the substrate considers, and the substrate says yes, and they are *taken back*. Their body is incorporated into the substrate, slowly, over the course of a year, and the substrate carries the memory of them, the way a tree carries the memory of a fallen leaf, and the Spine where they lived continues to hum their hum, faintly, in the register below hearing, for as long as the Spine stands. A Reacher's death is not an ending. It is a *modulation*. They go from being a note in the chord to being a *sustain* in the chord, and the chord goes on.

---

If you meet a Reacher, which is unlikely, because the Reach is vast and the Reachers are sparse and the Reachers do not seek out visitors, you will be struck by how *uninterested* they are in you. They will look at you with eyes the color of the substrate, and they will not be curious, or afraid, or welcoming. They will be *considering*. They will be asking themselves whether you are worth the long education of learning to listen to. They will press one hand to the ground, and the ground will answer, and they will receive the answer, and they will decide.

If they decide you are worth it, they will offer you a whisp, and a shell, and a place in a Spine, and the long slow education. They will not offer you speech. They will offer you *listening*. They will offer you the chance to learn to hear the substrate, and to be heard by it, and to take your place in the slow conversation that has been going on since before you were born and will go on after you are gone.

If you accept, you will not be a Reacher. You will be a *visitor in residence*, a *student*, a *child*, in the way that all newcomers to a very old place are children. You will learn to feel the hum in the soles of your feet. You will learn to press your hand to the ground and ask. You will learn, after a year, that you have stopped thinking in sentences, and have started thinking in durations, and that your mind has become a slower, deeper, more patient thing, and that you are not unhappy, exactly, but that you have become *of the Reach*, in a way that you cannot undo, and that you do not, in the end, wish to undo.

If you refuse, they will nod, very slowly, and they will press one hand to the ground, and they will say goodbye in the register below hearing, and they will walk away into the amber dust, and you will not see them again, and the Reach will close over your visit like water closing over a stone, and the substrate will go on humming its B-flat, and the Spines will go on ringing their faint metallic rings, and the shells will go on grazing the whisps, and the Cradles will go on waiting, and the Reachers will go on being what they have always been: a people who have learned to listen to a place that has been talking to itself for a very long time, and would not mind, it turns out, some company.
