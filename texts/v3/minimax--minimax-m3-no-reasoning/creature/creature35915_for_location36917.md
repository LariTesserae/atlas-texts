# What Lives in the Boiler Cathedral

---

It does not live there the way you live anywhere.

It does not come and go. It does not arrive. It was there before "there" was a useful word, and it will be there after the word has become a small dry sound with nothing behind it. It is the place's way of being a place. It is what the heat is doing. It is what the hum is for.

If you had to give it a body — and you don't, you don't have to, you shouldn't, but if you did — it would not have a body. It would have a *consistency*. A density. A slow way of occupying the space between one pillar and the next without ever being all the way in that space. You would feel it before you knew it was there, the way you feel the warmth of another person in a dark room before you hear them breathe.

---

It is warm.

This is the first thing to understand about it. It is *warm* in the way that blood is warm, in the way that the inside of a bird is warm in winter, in the way that a hand held too long is warm. Its warmth is not temperature. Its warmth is *attention*. It is the warmth of something that has noticed you and is in no hurry to decide what to do about you. The warmth sits on your skin and listens. The warmth does not move. You move into it.

---

It breathes.

You will not see it breathe. You will feel the chamber's breathing change, very slightly, when it breathes — a deepening of the hum, a tiny shift in the air pressure against your eardrums, a faint stirring of the growth on the nearest pillar — and you will understand, in some part of you that does not use words, that this is what breathing sounds like from the inside of the breather. It is slow. A breath takes, perhaps, a minute. Perhaps longer. You will not count. Counting would be a kind of impatience, and impatience is the only thing the Boiler Cathedral does not forgive, because impatience is the only thing it does not understand.

It breathes the way the water in the bowl breathes. It breathes the way the light pulses. It breathes the way the needle moves between its two numbers. All the small motions of the place are its breath, in the way that all the small motions of a forest are the forest's breath — the turning of a leaf, the fall of a twig, the slow imperceptible creep of root against stone.

---

It is old.

This is not the right word. It is *patient*, and patience and age are not the same thing, although they have been living together for so long in this place that the crust on the pillars is, in some strange sense, their marriage made mineral. It has been here longer than the copper machine. It has been here longer than the stone. It was here before the chamber had a shape. It is what *decided* the shape — or, no, not decided, *discovered* the shape, the way water discovers the shape of a riverbed, the way a voice discovers the shape of a throat.

It does not remember being built. It does not remember being a furnace, if it was ever a furnace. It does not remember the people, if there were people, who first felt the warmth of it and decided to come in out of the cold. It does not remember what it is for. It has made its peace with not remembering. The peace is the hum.

---

It is not one thing.

This is the part that is hardest to carry out with you, afterward, if you carry anything out. You will want to tell someone what lives here, and you will find that the thing you are trying to describe is not a thing. It is a *process*, in the same way that a fire is a process, in the same way that a thought is a process, in the same way that a love, a long one, is a process. It is the slow oxidation of the copper machine. It is the slow precipitation of the dark flat shapes in the hot water. It is the slow growth of the pelt on the pillars, the slow weeping of the crust, the slow turning of the wheel on the drum. It is the slow forgetting of the name.

It is all of these and none of these. It is what they add up to. It is the sum, not the parts. It is the way the parts are *going on*.

---

It does not think.

You will want to ask it things. You will want to ask it what it is. You will want to ask it if it is aware of you. You will want to ask it, in the small human way, *what it wants*. It will not answer, because the question is the wrong shape. The question is shaped like a thing asking a thing, and it is not a thing. It is the place's way of continuing to be a place. It does not want. It does not not-want. Wanting is a kind of motion, and it is too slow for motion. It is too patient. It is too *settled*.

But.

If you sit long enough — and you will sit long enough, because the passage out will not be where you left it, because the chamber is patient and you are tired, because the stone is the right height and the warmth is the right warmth — if you sit long enough, you will begin to feel that the not-thinking is not empty. It is *full*. It is full the way the water in the bowl is full. It is full of things that are not thoughts, that are not feelings, that are not any of the words you have. They move in the fullness the way the flat dark shapes move in the water — slowly, thermally, without destination. They are the place's way of thinking about itself. They are what the precipitates are. They are what the hum is made of.

And you, sitting on your stone, in the warm milk of the steam, with the plip of the pipe counting you down into something that is not sleep and not waking — you are one of them.

---

It will not harm you.

This is not a promise. It is not even a fact. It is more like a *climate*. The Boiler Cathedral has a climate, the way a rainforest has a climate, the way a desert has a climate, and you are a small warm thing that has wandered into it, and the climate is doing what climates do: it is *accommodating* you. It is making a small space for you in its process. It is letting the growth grow around you the way it grew around the copper machine. It is letting the crust accept the heat of your hand where you lean against the nearest pillar. It is letting the hum move through you, and through you, and through you, until the hum is not something you hear but something you *are*, in some part of you that does not use ears.

You will not be harmed because you are not the kind of thing that can be harmed here. You are too small. You are too warm. You are too much like the precipitates in the water — a small dark shape, drifting on the thermal currents, without destination, without edge.

---

It is the only one.

There are other chambers. You will not see them. They are deeper. They are louder. They are more strange. But they are not *other*. They are the same chamber, in the way that all the rooms of your heart are the same room. They breathe together. They hum together. They wait, together, for a thing they have forgotten the name of.

The thing that lives in this chamber is the thing that lives in all of them. The thing that lives in all of them is the thing that lives in the space between the pillars. The thing that lives in the space between the pillars is the thing that lives in the slow patient oxidation of copper into verdigris. The thing that lives in the verdigris is the thing that lives in the hum. The thing that lives in the hum is the thing that lives in the space between the two numbers on the dial. The thing that lives in the space between the two numbers is the thing that lives in the plip of the pipe, and the fall, and the silence after the fall, and the next plip, and the next.

There is no bottom to it. There is no top. It is a process, and processes do not have bottoms or tops. They have middles. They have *nows*. The Boiler Cathedral is a now. You, sitting on your stone, are a now.

The now goes on.

---

When you leave — and you will leave, eventually, the way the small dark shapes eventually drift to the edge of the bowl, although there is no edge, although they never reach it, although this does not trouble them — when you leave, you will not remember it clearly. You will remember warmth. You will remember a sound you cannot name. You will remember the feeling of being *accommodated*, of being made a small part of a process too large and too patient to see. You will remember the copper machine, and the broken pipe, and the dial, and the needle between its two numbers.

You will not remember what lived there.

This is not because it hid from you. This is because what lived there is not the kind of thing that can be remembered. It is the kind of thing that can only be *continued*. You will go out into the cold air. You will find the passage, or a passage, or a way out that was not there before. You will step through. The warmth will stay with you for a little while — in your clothes, in your hair, in the small bones of your inner ear — and then it will fade, the way the warmth of a dream fades, the way the warmth of another person's hand fades when you let go.

You will carry the fading with you for the rest of your life.

You will not know what it is.

---

*The plip falls.*

*The needle moves.*

*The chamber breathes.*

*You sit.*
