What lives there does not have a name, and would not benefit from one.

It is best approached sideways, the way you would approach a sleeping animal — not because it sleeps, but because it is *occupied*, and what it is occupied with is itself, and interrupting that occupation would be a kind of trespass that the trespasser would feel for a long time afterward, in the chest, in the way the breath comes.

It is a plant. It would like you to think it is a plant. It has roots, after a fashion. It has a kind of circulation. It breathes, in the sense that something which is not quite air moves through it and emerges changed. But it is not a plant the way a fern is a plant, or the way a strangler fig is a plant. It is a plant the way a city is a building: it has organized itself around a function, and the function is *respiration*, and the city is the size of a village square, and you are inside it.

Look at the roots. They are the color of bone that has been bleached in the sun for a long time. They are the color of milk held up to firelight, of wax that has been warmed in a closed hand, of the inside of a tooth. They are thick at the top where they descend from the ceiling and thick at the bottom where they plunge into the floor and thin nowhere — they are uniformly thick, the girth of a man's torso, and they curve in directions that curves should not curve. Some of them bend *backwards*. Some of them form loops that pass through their own earlier selves, so that a root you saw at one moment is, an hour later, the root that was passing through it. You cannot hold a single root in your eye for long. The eye slides off. The geometry is a suggestion.

And inside them — dark. A sluggish dark river, slow as poured honey, dark as old blood held up to a lamp and seen through. The river does not flow. It *translates*. It moves through the root a foot at a time, and at each knot it gathers and stops, and then it *thocks*, and the thock is the sound you felt in your sternum on the way down. *Thock.* A single pulse, low and wet, felt in the floor and the roots and the water and the teeth, the size and weight of a heart beating once. The plant has a heart. The plant is a heart.

Above — high up, where the ceiling presses down — the roots fray. They split into hundreds of pale filaments, finer than hair, and they hang there, and they wave in a wind that is not a wind, and they sing. Each filament has its own note. The notes clash and harmonize and drift through the chamber in slow spirals because the chamber is shaped to make them do so, and the shaping is not water-shaping, not plant-shaping, but a third kind of shaping, the shaping of something that knows what sound is and has built a room for it. The chamber is an instrument. The filaments are the reeds. The plant is playing itself, and it has been playing itself for as long as the rock can remember, and the rock is very old, and the plant is very new, and this should not be possible, and yet here it is, *content*.

There is no animal here. There is no insect. There is no spider, no eyeless fish, no bat. The chamber is warm and damp and full of slow organic movement, and the rock has not learned to feed anything else, or — and this is the part that sits in the stomach afterward — the plant has eaten everything else. The plant is a closed system. The plant is a single hungry thing digesting itself slowly, and the pools of dark water on the floor are not pools but *drops*, single drops balancing on surfaces that will not let them fall, balancing for a long time, balancing still. You do not touch them.

It does not have a face. It does not have eyes. It does not have anything you could point to and say *there, that is the thing that is alive in here*. And yet it attends to you. When you breathe out, the filaments bend toward you, gently, like a hundred pale fingers reaching. When you step on the floor, the roots part around your feet with a kind of *attention*. When you kneel by a pool, the surface of the water tilts in your direction, and the thock deepens a half-step, and the chamber is *listening*. It has heard you. It is deciding what to do about you. You are the only other thing in the room, and the room is full, and you are small inside it, and you are being weighed.

What lives here is patient. What lives here is the newest thing in the mountain, and the mountain is older than anything you can imagine, and the newness of it is a small wrongness, the way a tooth growing in the wrong place is a small wrongness, and the small wrongness is also the chamber, and the chamber is also the breath, and the breath is also the hum, and the hum is also the slow dark river, and the slow dark river is also the filaments singing, and the filaments are also the light that has no source, and the light is also the sourceless illumination the color of overcast morning, and all of it is one thing, and the one thing is *becoming*, and it has been becoming since before you were sent down, and it will be becoming long after the elders stop sending, and it will miss nothing, and ask nothing, and wait for no one.

If you sat on the floor of the forest of pale columns for a long time — and you will not, but suppose you did — you would begin to feel that the chamber was not a place you had entered but a place that had *received* you, the way a lung receives a breath. You would feel that you were not standing in a room but *passing through* one. You would feel that the slow dark river inside you had begun to move in time with the slow dark river in the roots, and that the *thock* you felt in your sternum was not coming from outside you but from somewhere inside you that you had not previously known was there.

You would feel that you had been heard.

You would feel that you had been answered.

You would climb back up the narrow passage carrying nothing in your hands. You would carry everything in your chest.

It lives there. It will live there for a long time. It is getting larger. It is getting older. It is content.
