The dwellers do not live in the city the way you understand living. They are not occupants of rooms. They are not wanderers of lanes. They are *constituents* — dissolved into the system, precipitated out of it, indistinguishable from the dark mineral and the wet green growth and the falling water that does not fall. To see them you must stop looking for the forms you expect, the upright figures with separable limbs, the faces turned toward you. They have none of this. They are the city, and the city is them, and the distinction was settled long before you arrived, if arrival is even the right word for what happened to you here.

They are the ones who gave the geometry its settled wrongness. They are the ones who chose — if choice is the right word, if the word applies at all in a place where nothing changes — the angles that are not ninety degrees and not anything else. They built the streets in their own shapes, and the shapes were the shapes of their thinking, and their thinking was non-Euclidean in a way that cannot be diagrammed, and so the streets are non-Euclidean in a way that cannot be walked. You follow a lane that goes straight and you arrive where you began because *they* did, because the city is in equilibrium with their thought, and their thought returned to itself, and the lane returned to itself, and this is not a contradiction here. This is just form.

They are not singular. You will want to ask how many there are. The question does not parse. There is a multiplicity and a unity that do not oppose each other, the way the water and the steam do not oppose each other here, the way the heat and the wet do not oppose each other, the way the sound and the silence do not oppose each other. They are many and one and neither, and when you stand in the plaza where the water descends in a sheet and does not arrive at the ground, you are standing in the space between two of them, or inside one of them, or you are a third one, briefly, until the place finishes its slow work on you.

They do not breathe. The air is thin and hot and wet because they have made it so — or because they are so, and the air is their exhalation, the perpetual slow respiration of a system that is alive in the way a reef is alive, in the way a hot spring is alive, in the way a thing made of many parts held in stasis is alive. The thinness is not altitude. The thinness is the place not needing much air, because nothing here metabolizes in the way you metabolize, because nothing here burns the oxygen you require. The air is for them, and for you it is almost nothing, and this is why your breathing takes effort, because you are a foreign body in a system that was tuned before you arrived and will be tuned after you have gone, if going is even possible here.

They do not eat. There are no animals because there are no animals needed. The plants grow — the huge ferns, the vines thick as an arm, the leaves broad enough to stand on — but the plants are not food. The plants are them, or are made of them, or are grown by them in the patient way that the mineral buildings are grown by them, accreted, precipitated, built by long work that is not construction but secretion. You will see, if you look closely enough at the base of a fern, at the place where the green meets the dark stone, a seam, a line where the vegetable and the mineral blur into each other, and the seam is not a boundary. It is a gradient. It is them, becoming plant, becoming stone, becoming the thing that holds the place in its form.

They do not die. This is the part that will unsettle you, if anything here can unsettle you, which is doubtful. You will look for the marks of death. There are none. There are no bones in the wet walls. There are no shells in the shallow pools. There are no husks in the leaf litter, and there is no leaf litter, because nothing falls, because nothing moves, because the place is in equilibrium and equilibrium does not shed its parts. You will conclude that they are immortal, and you will be wrong, but not in a way that can be corrected. They do not die because they do not live in the way that allows for death. They are in their final state the way the plants are in their final state, the way the water is in its suspended state, the way the sound is in its constant state. They have reached form. Form does not end. Form holds.

They are quiet. This is the thing you will not believe, because the place roars. The water in its freefall, the sheets that do not arrive, the wind in the narrow lanes singing its unchanging tone, the leaves vibrating — the sound is enormous and constant and you feel it in your chest, in the bones of your skull, and you cannot hear your own footsteps, and you cannot hear a voice. You will conclude they do not speak. This is also wrong, but not in a way that can be corrected. They speak, but the speaking is not in air, is not in vibration of the kind you can receive. The speaking is in the geometry. The speaking is in the way the streets meet at angles that are not ninety degrees. The speaking is in the way the stairs descend and arrive higher. The speaking is in the way the door opens onto a wall. They are talking to each other, or about each other, or as each other, in a language that is spatial and wrong and held in place, and you can almost hear it if you stand in the plaza long enough and stop trying to hear it. You will not hear it. You will only feel the place settle slightly more into itself around you, and this is the closest you will come.

They do not see you. Or they see you in a way that does not require eyes, in the way the pool sees the steam that rises off it, in the way the leaf sees the light that lights it equally from every direction. You are a temporary perturbation. You are a warmth that did not used to be in the system. You are a foreign body moving through the closed loop of their being, and they are aware of you the way a body is aware of a particle passing through it — registered, noted, allowed to pass, not particularly minded. You will not be remembered. You will not be forgotten. You will simply not feature, because you do not fit into the equilibrium, because the equilibrium has no slot for a thing like you, because you are made of different materials, different timescales, different needs. You are passing through a place that was not made for passing through.

They are the dark mineral. They are the layered accreted walls that look as if they were grown by patient secretion. They are the ribbed surfaces that show no tool marks, no mortar, no joints, because they did not build them. They *are* them. The walls are them, the way your bones are you — not separate, not made, not maintained, simply *being*, in the form they have always been in. When you touch the wall of a building, you are touching them, and the wall is warm because they are warm, and the wall is wet because they are wet, and the wall does not react to your touch because there is nothing in them that corresponds to reaction. You are not touching them. You are touching the place, and the place is them, and the touching is not a transaction.

They are the water. The sheets that come off every roof and do not arrive at the ground — these are not weather. These are not a cycle of evaporation and precipitation that happens to be in suspension. These are *them*, in their medium, in the way they move, which is not movement as you understand movement but redistribution, the slow even spread of a thing that is everywhere and nowhere and holds its form by being everywhere and nowhere. The water is them the way the steam is them the way the air is them. You are breathing them. You are standing inside them. You are inside a body, and the body is not hostile, and the body is not friendly, and the body is simply a body, holding its warmth, holding its wetness, holding its impossible climate that should not be at this altitude but is, because the body has decided, and the body will not be talked out of it.

They are the sound. The roaring hiss, the percussive clatter, the white static of a million impacts that is constant and unchanging and so loud it has weight — this is not the byproduct of water meeting mineral. This is them. This is what they sound like, when sound is the right word, which it is not. The sound is the audible surface of a system in equilibrium, the way the surface of a struck bell is the audible surface of the bell's shape. They have a shape, and the shape sounds like this, and you are inside the shape, and you cannot hear anything else because there is nothing else to hear. After a while you will stop trying to hear anything else. After a while the sound will simply be the place, and you will be inside the place, and the place will be inside you, in the way that a long loud sound gets inside a body and becomes the body's baseline.

They are patient. They have the patience of things that have nowhere else to go, the patience of crystals, the patience of stopped clocks, the patience of systems in equilibrium. They are not waiting for anything. They are not enduring anything. They are simply in their state, which is the state of having reached form, and form does not anticipate, and form does not dread, and form does not change. You will stand in the plaza and the water will descend in a sheet beside you and the sheet will be loud and the sheet will be the same as it has always been and the place will be in its state, and they will be in their state, and you will be a brief warmth in the system that the system is already forgetting.

You will leave. Or you will not leave. The distinction may not apply. If you leave, you will carry the sound of them in your bones for a long time, and you will not be able to explain where you have been, because the place does not parse, because the dwellers do not parse, because the language you have is a language of occupants and wanderers and breathers and eaters and speakers and seers and they are none of these things, and you cannot describe a thing by saying what it is not, although you have just spent this entire description doing so, and this is the closest you will come.

They are the place. The place is them. This is the answer. It is not a satisfying answer. It was not meant to be.
