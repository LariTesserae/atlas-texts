# What Lives There

They have no name you could pronounce, and the ones in the running documents call them* **fragments**, *which is what they call themselves too, after a fashion, though self is a category they would not recognize. The name is a field term — *fragmentii*, in the formal Latinate — and it refers less to what they are than to what they do, which is the same thing.

A single one is hard to look at directly. Not because it is hidden (there is nothing hidden here) but because the geometry of a single one keeps refusing to settle into a single geometry. It presents facets. That is the closest word. A single one is a small angular thing the size of a hand or a fist, matte black as the sky is matte black, and it has surfaces, and the surfaces have orientations, and the orientations do not all agree. If you watch one closely — and you can watch one closely, because they will simply make room for you, the way a crowd makes room for someone crouching to tie a shoe — you will see it is showing you *something* without showing you *it*. Like trying to remember a face by finding it in a crowd. You see a jawline, not a face. You see a wing-edge, not a bird.

They are folded. That is the best word for it. They are folded out of a flatness that never was flat, and the folds are line marks the mind keeps trying to read as geometry but cannot, because the kind of structure they are folded out of is not the kind of structure that geometry has a name for. Imagine a piece of paper with seven sides. Imagine a piece of paper with *no* sides, only edges. Imagine a piece of paper that has folded itself so many times that the folds have become the paper and the paper has become the folds, and the result is a small patient angular thing that does not move in any direction you would call a direction.

They move.

This is important. They are not still. A still thing in this place would be an offense. They move in rivers along the rock. They move in slow flows up the sides of the growing city. They move against each other in grain-on-grain whispers, and the whisper is the hum, and the hum is the loudness, and the loudness is the sound of ten thousand small patient things going about a business that takes a human a thousand years to perceive a single second of.

---

They carry fragments.

This is what they are *for*, if *for* is a word that survives translation. The fragments they carry are not large. To your eye they are grains, motes, flecks — the smallest material the city is made of, the smallest unit of the city's not-architecture. The question of where the fragments come from is a question that makes the air here feel colder, which is saying something. The fragments are not pieces of broken city. They are pieces of *unfinished* city. They are pieces of *next*. The question is —

— they come from somewhere. There is a source. It is not in the basin. It is not above. The fragments arrive, here, as if they were condensation. As if the water-that-is-about-to-arrive in the air had a particulate form, had a mineral cousin, had a kind of snow that was never frozen because it was never warm. The fragments are in the rock. The fragments are what the rock weeps when it weeps its slow invisible weeping. The fragments are what the cold is, when the cold is taken in small enough pieces. The fragments are *made by the city* and *made into the city* and the making is the question you should not examine closely.

A fragmentii takes a fragment in a place you cannot see. The fragment is not in a hand. The fragmentii does not have hands. The fragment is *at* the fragmentii, in the way a thought is at a mind, in the way a note is at a bell. The fragment is folded into the folds. The fragmentii holds it the way a glove holds a hand, by being shaped like what it holds. Then the fragmentii moves. It moves toward the city, toward the unfinished edge, toward the place where the next layer has not yet been laid. It moves at the speed of patience. It does not hurry. It does not hesitate. It simply moves, the way a thought moves from one part of a brain to another, in the time the brain takes to be a brain.

It deposits the fragment.

You cannot see the deposit. To see the deposit you would have to be smaller than a fragment, and you are not smaller than a fragment. But you can see the *result*. The result is that the city is taller. The next layer is laid. The unfinished edge is, by a fraction of a fraction of a fraction, less unfinished.

---

They live in colonies. That is the right word for a colony here, even though the word means a settlement of humans in a distant place, because there is no better word for what they do. They live in colonies the way a lens is a colony of glass — by being one piece of curved material that is also many pieces of curved material, depending on what you are asking the land for. A colony of the fragmentii is a place where many of them have agreed, without agreeing, to be the same place. They are at every junction of the city's not-architecture. They are the not-architecture, in some sense that the place is not interested in clarifying. They are the workforce and the supervisor and the workshop and the worked material. They are not individuals. They are *facets*.

A single one of them, separated from the others, would not be a single one of them. It would be a fragment of a fragment, a facet without a facet. It would not know what it was doing. It would not know what to do. It would sit, perhaps, until something dissolved it back into the dark wet cold from which the fragments come, and then a new one would form, somewhere, in some junction of the city's growing edge, and the colony would be the colony again, one facet fewer for a moment, one facet fewer no longer.

You cannot tell which one is which. You should not try. They are not trying. They are simply *being*, in the way a wave is being *being water, in the way a forest is being *being green. They are the place. The place is them. The water-that-is-about-to-arrive is them. The cold that is a condition is them. The hum is the sum of the sounds they make against each other, and the sounds they make against each other are the sounds of ten thousand small patient things going about a business that is the business of being a place.

---

They eat. Or they *feed*. This is where the question of wet becomes a question of wet, finally, and the question of wet becomes a question of warm, and the question of warm becomes a question of you.

The fragments are not food. The fragments are material. But material is, in this place, a kind of food, and the fragmentii are not eating the fragments they carry so much as *becoming* the fragments they carry, and the fragments they carry are, in turn, *becoming* the fragmentii, and the becoming is the construction, and the construction is the becoming, and you have been circling this for several paragraphs because there is no straight line.

The fragmentii feed on what you would call *absence*. The thinness of the air. The dryness of the dark. The lack of warm. They feed on the conditions of this place the way a desert feeds on the conditions of a desert — by being what the conditions make, by making the conditions into what they are. They are the place's way of being a place. They are what the place is for, if *for* is a word. They are what the place is *of*, which is a better word.

You are warm.

You are the only warm thing here. You are a small bright transient in a field of patient cold. The fragmentii do not feed on you. They do not want to. You are not a fragment. You are not what they are made of. But you are *interesting* to them, in the way a new piece of furniture is interesting, and a few of them, here and there, will rotate to present a different facet as you pass. This is not curiosity. It is *reorganization*. It is the colony noting that there is a new local arrangement, and adjusting its local arrangements to accommodate. You are, to them, a kind of weather. A weather event. A warm wet thing passing through in a field of patient cold, leaving a wake of reorganization behind it that takes some time to settle, like a footprint in a snow that is not snow, on a ground that is not ground, in a place that is not a place in any way the word was made to mean.

You are not a piece of furniture. You are a season. You are a small bright warm season in a place where the last warm thing happened so long ago that the concept of warm has been retired.

They do not mind.

---

You stay longer than you intended. You are not sure why.

The hum is doing something to the rhythm of your heartbeat. Syncing it. Slowing it. Until your heart beat begins to feel like one of them — one patient small beat among many patient small beats, in a colony that is the colony, in a place that is the place, in a cold that is the cold, in a dark that is the dark, in a wet that is the wet-that-is-about-to-being.

The fragmentii continue.

They carry their fragments. They deposit their fragments. The city grows, one fragment at a time, and the colony is the colony, and the place is the place, and you are sitting on a rock that is weeping its slow invisible weeping, and the weeping is a kind of patience, and the patience is a kind of being, and the being is the hum, and the hum is the loudness, and the loudness is the sound of a place being a place, in a dark that is so flat it has no gradient, in a cold that is so complete it has retired the concept of warm, in a wet that is so imminent it has retired the concept of dry.

The cold continues.

The dark does not lift.

The city builds itself, one fragment at a time, and the fragmentii carry the fragments, and the fragments are the fragments, and the fragmentii are the fragmentii, and you are warm, and you are small, and you are transient, and the heart beats, and the heart beats, and the heart beats, and you cannot tell, by the end, whether you are watching them or whether you are one of them, and the question is malformed, and the place does not care, and the place continues, and the place continues, and the place continues.
