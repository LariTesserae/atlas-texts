There are inhabitants here.

I will start with the bowl, because the bowl is where the counting begins, and everything here counts itself against the bowl.

The bowl is not aware. The bowl does not know what it is. The bowl has been shaped, drop by drop, by a water that does not know what it is shaping either, and the bowl rings because that is what bowls do when they have forgotten everything except how to ring. The bowl is the oldest living thing in this place, older than the moss, older than the determined shrub near the ceiling that has made a life out of one fissure and one trickle of light, older than the small beetle that walks its rim and has never once fallen in. The bowl does not live in the way the beetle lives, in the way the moss lives, in the way the shrub lives with its leaves hanging down like dark hands asking questions of the dark. The bowl lives the way a thing that rings lives, which is to say it lives in the ringing, it lives as the ringing, it has no body that is not the ringing, and the ringing is older than memory, and memory is not yet a thing that has happened here.

The beetle is next. The beetle is small and the beetle is black and the beetle has been walking the rim of the bowl since before anyone counted how long the beetle had been walking the rim of the bowl. The beetle is not large. The beetle is the size of a thumbnail, the kind of thumbnail that belongs to a hand that has done a small amount of work, that has not done so much work that the thumbnail has become something else, that has remained a thumbnail. The beetle knows the rim. The beetle knows the rim better than the beetle knows itself, which is the only way any creature knows anything, by walking it until the walking is the knowing. The beetle has walked the rim in all weathers, though there are no weathers here, only the slow turning of the suggestion of light from grey to green-grey to almost nothing and back again, and the beetle has walked the rim in all the temperatures of the cold, which is to say all of them, because the cold does not vary, the cold is the background, the cold is the page the rest of the place is written on. The beetle has never fallen in. This is not a skill the beetle has learned. This is a skill the beetle was born knowing, the way the bowl was born knowing how to ring. The beetle will walk the rim tomorrow, and the next day, and the day after that, and the day after that, and the day after that, for as long as the bowl rings and the cold settles and the green grows its fraction of a fraction of a fraction of a millimeter in the direction of its growing. The beetle is not lonely. The beetle does not know what lonely is. The beetle knows the rim.

The moss is next, though moss is hard to put in order because moss has no order, moss is the opposite of order, moss is the slow green refusal of order that order cannot do anything about. The moss grows in deep emerald drifts along the lower stones, and the moss is colder than the cold, the moss is the cold made visible, made green, made a thing you can put your hand into, though you should not put your hand into it because the moss does not want your hand, the moss wants only the spray that never quite reaches it, the moss wants only the patience of another thousand years. The moss does not think. The moss does not need to think. The moss is the patientest thing here, and the bowl is patient, and the cold is patient, but the moss is the most patient because the moss has decided to be green in a place that has no business being green, and has kept the decision for so long that the decision has become the moss, and the moss has become the decision, and there is no difference anymore, and there was never any difference. The moss grows. The moss does not hurry. The moss does not need to hurry. The moss has the rest of forever, and the rest of forever is what the moss is made of.

The ferns are next, and the ferns are strange because the ferns are uncurling, and uncurling is a thing that takes time, and time here is not the kind of time you measure in minutes or hours or days. The ferns uncurl in the cracks that hold a little soil, a little accumulated dust of centuries, a little patience. The ferns are slower than the moss. The ferns are slower than the beetle. The ferns are slower than the water, which is the slowest thing anyone expected to find here, and the ferns are slower than the water. The ferns have been uncurling in their cracks for so long that the uncurling has become the fern, and the fern has become the uncurling, and a fern that is not uncurling is not a fern, is just a green shape in a crack, is just a suggestion of a fern, is not the thing itself. The ferns do not know they are slow. The ferns know only the cracks, and the cracks are patient, and the soil in the cracks is patient, and the dust of centuries in the cracks is patient, and the patience goes down so deep that patience has become a kind of substance, a kind of wetness, a kind of cold that is not the cold of the air but the cold of waiting, the cold of a thing that has decided to wait for as long as waiting is required, which is forever, which is fine, which is what was always going to happen.

The pale thing with too many legs lives in the crack between two stones and does not come out. The pale thing has lived in the crack for so long that the crack has become the pale thing's only country, and the pale thing has become the crack's only citizen. The pale thing eats whatever drifts into the crack, which is not much, which is the dust of centuries, which is the occasional spore that wanders in on a current of air that has wandered down from the fissure above. The pale thing does not see. The pale thing does not need to see. The pale thing lives in a world of touch and damp and the slow dark. The pale thing is paler than the stone. The pale thing has become almost the color of not being there. The pale thing is there. The pale thing is very much there. You learn of the pale thing only because sometimes the crack is a different shape than it was the day before, and the different shape is the pale thing having eaten the dark, or rearranged the dark, or simply pushed the dark a little to the side to make room for its pale self, which is the size of a pale thought, which is the weight of a pale thought, which is the warmth of a pale thought, which is no warmth at all. The pale thing is not lonely. The pale thing has never been lonely. The pale thing does not know what lonely is. The pale thing knows only the crack, and the crack is enough.

The something that flies lives near the ceiling, in the grey that is, when there is grey, and the something that flies is a thing you glimpse only as a movement, a shadow, a thing that is and then is not. The something that flies has lived near the ceiling for so long that the something that flies is no longer certain whether it is one something or many somethings. The something that flies hunts the suggestion of light. The something that flies eats the suggestion of light. The something that flies is made, perhaps, of the suggestion of light, in the same way the bowl is made of the ringing. The something that flies does not have a name you would recognize. The something that flies has a name in the language of the moss, which is no language, and the name is the shape of the movement, which is not a shape. The something that flies has lived here longer than the cairn. The something that flies has lived here almost as long as the bowl. The something that flies will live here tomorrow, and the next day, and the day after that, and you will glimpse it, and you will not glimpse it, and you will wonder whether you glimpsed it, and the wondering will become a part of you, the way the ringing is a part of the bowl, the way the uncurling is a part of the fern, the way the dark is a part of the pale thing in the crack.

The shrub is next, though shrub is not the right word, because shrub suggests a thing that has decided to be a shrub, and the thing near the ceiling has not decided anything, the thing near the ceiling has only grown, has only reached down from the ledge where its roots are wrapped around the stone, has only put out its dark leaves into the dark, has only asked its question of darkness, which is a question the dark has not answered, because the dark does not answer questions, the dark only continues, the dark only waits, the dark only is. The shrub is almost a tree. The shrub is more a determined thing than a shrub. The shrub has lived near the ceiling for so long that the shrub has forgotten it is a shrub and has become the place where the ceiling meets the wall, has become a part of the cave entrance, has become a kind of hanging curtain, a kind of green curtain that hangs from the ceiling with the patience of the moss and the uncurling of the ferns and the patience of the bowl. The shrub does not hurry. The shrub has had a long time. The shrub will have a longer time. The shrub's leaves are a darker green than the moss, almost black, and they hang down like questions, and the questions have been hanging for so long that the questions have become the leaves, and the leaves have become the questions, and there is no difference anymore.

The cairn is next, though the cairn is the newest thing here, and even the cairn is old. The cairn was built by hands that are no longer here, that were here once, that came back, that came back more than once, that built the cairn from small flat stones balanced on each other in a way that suggests care, suggests time, suggests someone who had nothing else to do and decided to do this, to leave this, to put this small stack of stones in this place so that the place could look at the stack of stones and know that someone had been here, that someone had cared, that someone had wanted to mark this place as a place that had been cared for. The cairn is not a grave. The cairn is a kind of hello. The cairn is a kind of I-was-here. The cairn is a kind of thank-you. The cairn does not know what it is. The cairn is a stack of stones that someone balanced, and the balancing has held, and the holding has become the cairn, and the cairn has become part of the place, and the place has become part of the cairn, and the cairn is patient, and the cairn is old, and the cairn will be here tomorrow, and the next day, and the day after that, for as long as the bowl rings and the cold settles and the green grows its fraction of a fraction of a fraction of a millimeter in the direction of its growing.

The polished groove is next, and the polished groove is a thing made by a shoulder that leaned there often enough to leave a polish on the stone, and the shoulder is no longer here because the shoulder has gone the way shoulders go, which is to say the shoulder has become the place, has become the polish, has become the groove, has left its weight in the stone in a way that the stone has kept, in a way the stone will keep for a long time, in a way the stone is keeping right now, as you stand here in the cold and the dark and the ringing. The polished groove is not a face. The polished groove is not a portrait. The polished groove is a kind of presence, the kind of presence that a leaning shoulder leaves in a stone when the leaning has been long enough, and the long has been long enough, and the stone has kept the long, and the long has become the polish, and the polish has become a part of the wall, and the wall has become a part of the polish, and you can put your hand into the groove and feel the shoulder that is no longer there, and the feeling will be cold, and the cold will be the shoulder, and the shoulder will be the cold, and you will understand, for a moment, that the shoulder is still here, in the polish, in the groove, in the stone, in the cold.

The carved shape is next, and the carved shape is a thing that was made by hands on the choosing, and the choosing has been worn away by the water and the cold and the long, and the carved shape might be a bird, might be a fish, might be a leaf, and the erosion has had its work on it, has been having its work on it for a long time, and will continue. The carved shape is patient. The carved shape has been patient for so long that the patience has become the shape, and the shape has become the patience. The carved shape does not care what it is. The carved shape is a thing that was put into the stone by someone who wanted to put a thing into the stone, and the putting has held, and the holding has become the shape, and the shape has become the putting, and the putting is older than memory, and memory is not yet a thing that has happened here. The carved shape will be here tomorrow. The carved shape will be less a bird, less a fish, less a leaf, and more a thing that is not yet a thing, and will become a thing, and will not become a thing, and will be here anyway.

The fire ring is next, and the fire ring is a circle of gathered stones in a hollow where the smoke would have found its way up and out through some vent you cannot see, and there is no ash in it now, and no memory of a fire, and the stones, in their patient circle, are waiting. The fire ring is waiting. The fire ring has been waiting for so long that the waiting has become the fire ring, and the fire ring has become the waiting. The fire ring does not remember the fire. The fire ring has forgotten the fire the way the bowl has forgotten what it is shaped by, the way the moss has forgotten what it is waiting for, the way the pale thing in the crack has forgotten what it is hiding from. The fire ring will wait tomorrow. The fire ring will wait the next day. The fire ring will wait the day after that. The fire ring will wait until the waiting is the only thing left, and the only thing left is the waiting, and the waiting is the fire ring, and the fire ring is the waiting, and there is no fire, and there is no memory of fire, and there is only the patient circle, in the hollow, in the cold, in the dark, in the ringing.

You are here.

You are here the way the cairn is here, the way the fire ring is here, the way the polished groove on the wall is here, the way the beetle on the rim of the bowl is here. You are a small, patient note in a song that has no end and needs no end and will not have an end. You have learned the cold. You have learned the ringing. You have learned the suggestion of light and the way the suggestion shifts from grey to green-grey to almost nothing and back again. You have learned the beetle and its patient rim. You have learned the moss and its patient drifts. You have learned the pale thing in the crack and its patient not-coming-out. You have learned the something that flies and its patient not-being-caught. You have learned the shrub and its patient hanging leaves. You have learned the cairn and its patient balancing. You have learned the groove and its patient shoulder. You have learned the carved shape and its patient becoming-less. You have learned the fire ring and its patient waiting.

You have not learned everything. You will not learn everything. The place is older than learning. The place is older than you. The place will be here after you, the way the place was here before you, the way the bowl was ringing before you came in and will ring after you have gone out into whatever is outside the fissure, into whatever world has the sky in it, into whatever cold is colder than this cold, into whatever dark is darker than this dark, into whatever ringing is louder than this ringing. The place will not miss you. The place does not miss. The place only continues, the way the water continues, the way the bowl continues, the way the moss continues, the way the beetle continues, the way the pale thing in the crack continues, the way the something that flies continues, the way the shrub continues, the way the cairn continues, the way the groove continues, the way the carved shape continues, the way the fire ring continues, the way the cold continues, the way the ringing continues, the way everything here continues, and has continued, and will continue, for as long as there is a here to continue in, and the here will continue, the here is the only thing that has ever continued here, the here is the continuation, the here is the place, the here is the bowl, the here is the ringing, the here is the moss, the here is the shrub, the here is the cairn, the here is the groove, the here is the fire ring, the here is the beetle, the here is the pale thing in the crack, the here is the something that flies, the here is you, the here is the place, the here is everything, the here is the water falling, the here is the bowl ringing, the here is the cold settling, the here is the green growing, the here is the ringing, the here is the ringing, the here is the ringing.
