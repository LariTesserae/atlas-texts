# The Inhabitants of the Sounding

The place has no people. It has something more specific than that, and also less.

---

## The Shelf Itself

The granite is the first thing that lives here, and it lives the loudest.

It does not live the way a rock lives in a field, which is to say, not at all. It lives the way a struck bell lives—sustained, declarative, full of a self it has only just discovered it possesses. The hum that runs through it, the low B-flat that moves in the stone the way blood moves in a hand, is not a metaphor. It is the sound of a mineral that has recently remembered it is also a note. The shelf was thrust up out of the warm water some short while ago, in whatever unit of time applies to a thing that large deciding to exist, and it has not finished being surprised at itself. The surprise is the hum. The hum will go on for as long as the surprise does, and the surprise will not end soon, because the shelf is still adding to itself, small accretions of new granite along its upper face, the way a thought adds a clause to itself before it is spoken. The shelf grows, and the fractures widen, and pieces of the edge go into the water below and are accepted, and the shelf does not mind, or minds in the only way it can, which is to keep growing where it is not falling away. It is, in its patient and entirely impersonal way, the steward of itself. It will be here for a long time. It will be here long enough for someone to stand on it and not feel hurried.

---

## The Listening Crust

The black lichen-like thing is not lichen. It is what lichen would be if lichen were invented on the spot by a stone that had recently been introduced to the concept of a surface.

It is a thin crust of living geometry, spread along the cracks where the water has settled longest. Each unit of it is a hexagon the size of a thumbnail, faintly faceted, giving back a different piece of the dim gold light. A patch of them, taken together, looks like the floor of a cathedral seen from a great height, or like a honeycomb that has decided to be a tide pool. They do not photosynthesize. They have not heard of photosynthesis. What they do is listen. They listen to the hum of the granite through the wet stone, and from the listening they metabolize something—heat, or information, or the slow chemical turning of one mineral into another—and they give back, in return, a faint mineral smell, like struck flint, like the air after a long thought. The smell is the by-product of their digestion, the way heat is the by-product of ours. The crust does not move. It does not need to. The granite is moving for it, the hum is moving through it, and the hexagon it is, this particular thumbnail-sized facet of the colony, is a mouth with a great deal of patience, and the patience is being fed. When a heavier pulse of water comes down, and the hum rises, the listening crust retreats a small distance from the loudest places. It does not run. It withdraws, the way a tide withdraws, and when the loudest place migrates, the crust follows, at a distance, like a shadow that is a little late. It is the second-longest thing in the place. It will be here when the shelf has decided what kind of shelf it wants to be.

## The Breathing Mounds

At the far end of the shelf, in a place the water does not fall on directly, there is a cluster of small dark mounds, each the size of a fist or a head, spaced apart from one another with an interval that does not look accidental. They are wet. They are dark. They breathe.

A person standing near them notices, first, that they have become still. The breath stops. The whole colony holds itself, the way a deer holds itself in a moment before it decides whether to run. The stillness is the first sentence they speak. It is the only sentence that is not in their language; the rest of what they have to say is in the slow rhythmic expansion and contraction, and the tiny sound that comes with it, like wet paper being crumpled, almost lost in the roar. When a person steps back, the breathing resumes. The mounds do not have eyes. They have a way of knowing.

They are not animals. They are not plants. They are the next thing the granite is trying, the way the crust was the thing the granite tried before, and the jointed coral will be the thing the granite tries after. They are the granite's second draft of itself. They are made of the stone, and the warm water, and the hum that runs through both, and the time it takes for a mineral to want something. What they want is unclear. What they do is breathe, and be still when watched, and resume when not, and exist in a spacing that is not the spacing of accident. The spacing is, perhaps, the spacing at which a hum is most clearly heard. They are arranged, one might say, the way ears are arranged. They are arranged to listen. They are the granite's ears, and the granite is, slowly, learning to hear.

---

## The Hollow Stalks

In a few of the cracks, taller things have raised themselves. They are thin, jointed, hollow, three feet tall in the largest of them, and they are a kind of coral that has not committed to being coral. They are still in negotiation. They ring faintly in the wet wind, each one its own small and uncertain pitch, and the ringing is a sound no louder than a tuning fork held at arm's length, but it is enough to make the place a grove.

The stalks bend away from the loudest water, the way a person bends away from a shout. Their color is a black with a green undertone, and the green is the same green as the light, and they lean, with a deliberation that is not quite tropism and not quite intention, toward the direction that contains the most of the listening. The most of the hum. The most of the gold-green light. They are, in their slow and jointed way, looking for the place where the place is most itself, and growing toward it, and becoming more of themselves the closer they get. They do not flower. They have not yet decided what they are above the ground. They are the granite's third draft of itself, and they are the most ambitious, and they are the closest, among the things that grow here, to having a posture. A stalk leaning in the wet wind, listening, is a thing that has begun to have an opinion about where it would like to be. That is a kind of inhabiting. That is, for the moment, enough.

## The Ring and the Marks

The low fitted ring of darker stone near the center of the shelf is the only thing in the place that has the air of having been thought about. Three courses high. No roof. No door. Inside diameter about as wide as a person can lie down in. The stones are not the granite of the shelf; they are bluer, and they ring at a different pitch, and when the two rings happen to overlap, the place where they meet is a slow sweet dissonance that lives in the sternum. The ring is older than the shelf. Maybe. It is here in the way that a word is here before the sentence that uses it. It was waiting. It has been used.

The marks inside the ring, on the stone floor, are not writing. They are not quite not writing. A circle, a smaller circle inside it, short lines radiating outward, a single long line through the center that does not quite continue out the other side. The pattern wants to be a pattern and isn't quite. The lines have been scored into the granite by something patient, by a point dragged many times across wet stone, and they are old, and also, in a way that cannot be argued with, fresh. When a person looks at them, the marks hold still. When a person looks away, the marks change slightly, the way a face changes when no one is attending to it.

There is no person to make the marks. There is no sign there has ever been a person. The marks were drawn by something that is still drawing. The marks are what the ring is doing while it waits. The ring was set down on the shelf, or carried up onto the shelf, or grew up out of the shelf the way the stalagmite grows up out of the cave floor, by something that wanted a place to be marked, and the marking is ongoing, and the ring is the place, and the place is the marking, and the marking is the only argument the place has yet made that there is a difference between a thing and the record of a thing. The marks are the place's memory. The marks are the place's dream. The ring is the place that has decided it would like to keep a record, and is, with great patience, doing so.

No one is here to read them. The reading is not, evidently, the point.

---

## The Water, Provisionally

The water does not inhabit the place. The water is the place, in the way that the air is a place. The water is everywhere, above and below and falling, and the shelf is the only thing that insists on being a thing inside the water, and the water accepts this insistence, mostly, and goes on being water.

But the water, in the warm column and the drifting mist, has a kind of attention. The pricking at the back of the throat. The taste of depth. The way the gold light pools where it should not and refuses where it should. These are not the behaviors of a substance that is indifferent. The water here is, perhaps, the most awake thing in the place, in the way that the sea is awake, which is to say, it is awake on a scale and in a register that does not have anything to do with the scale or the register of the small things on the shelf. The water does not know the shelf is here. The water knows the shelf is here. The water goes on falling. The water will go on falling when the shelf is gone. The water is what the place is, and the shelf is what the water is making, briefly, in passing, and the things on the shelf are what the shelf is making, in its slow and ringing way, and the water does not mind, or minds only in the way that the tide minds the shore, which is to say, by continuing.

---

## What Lives Here, in Sum

A shelf, still ringing with the surprise of itself.
A crust, listening.
A cluster of mounds, breathing, holding still when watched.
A grove of jointed stalks, leaning toward the loudest listening.
A ring of older stone, marking the floor of itself.
A mark, still being drawn.

The water, falling.

The place is not empty. The place is at the very beginning of not being empty, and is, with the great patience of things that do not yet have the concept of patience, going on with the not being empty, one hexagon, one breath, one scored line at a time. There is no one here to witness it. There is no one here to make it count. It counts, anyway, in the only currency the place has, which is the hum, and the hum is constant, and the hum says, in the long and quiet way that stones say things: *it is here, it is here, it is here, it is here, it is here.*
