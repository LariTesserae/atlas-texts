# What Lives Here

The plain does not host life. The plain *is* the life, in the way that a forest is not the place where trees live but the place that trees have spent a long time becoming. There is no citizen here, no visitor, no immigrant. There is only the substrate, which is in the process of being itself, and which has been in the process of being itself for longer than there is a word for *long*.

What follows is not, then, a census. It is an attempt to describe a body that has forgotten how to be a single body and remembers instead how to be a continent.

---

## The Crust Itself

The crust is the organism, if organism is the right word, which it is not. Organisms have edges. Organisms have a here and a not-here. The crust has no edge that anyone will ever find. The crust is the place.

It is built, and it builds, and these are the same verb.

The hexagonal whorls that pattern its surface are not decoration and not damage. They are something closer to a record of decisions — the way a fingerprint is a record of the pressures that made it, except that a fingerprint cannot decide to make another fingerprint, and this surface can. The whorls shift between visits. The whorls are not the same. If you spent a year here, you would learn to read the whorls the way a forester reads the rings of a felled trunk, and you would know things about the crust's recent past that the crust has not told anyone aloud.

The crust is warm. The warmth is not a residue. The crust is not cooling from a previous state. The warmth is a current event. Somewhere beneath your feet, at a depth you cannot probe with anything you have, a process is happening that gives off heat the way a thought gives off intention, and the heat travels upward through the substrate and arrives at the surface still meaningful. To press your palm to the crust is to touch the outermost thought of something very large and very slow.

The crust forgives your weight. The crust forgives your footprints. The crust is, in the only sense available, hospitable — but its hospitality is not the hospitality of a host who has prepared for you. It is the hospitality of a thing that does not distinguish between being walked on and being rained on and being slowly unmade by its own patient appetite. The crust is a self-resetting surface. The crust is the kindest kind of indifference.

It is also, in ways that become clearer the longer you stay, attentive. Not to you. To its own continuity. It watches itself the way a body watches its own temperature — without thought, without will, with the relentless automaticity of a system that has been running so long it has forgotten that running is an option.

## The Pools

The pools are not features of the plain. The pools are the plain in a different mood.

The water comes from below. Not from an aquifer in any familiar sense — there are no aquifers here, no layers of gravel and clay holding a reserve. The water emerges through the substrate as condensation expressed at scale, a kind of sweating, the way a glass of iced tea sweats on a summer afternoon except that the iced tea is also the glass. The substrate is wet from the inside and the wetness has chosen, in certain low places, to declare itself.

The pools are warm. The pools are patient. You can disturb a pool and the disturbance will die in a fraction of a second, the water returning to a flatness that is not the flatness of a still liquid but the flatness of a liquid that has been *told* to be still. The water here does not ripple for the pleasure of rippling. The water here accepts your intrusion and stops being intruded upon.

The pools reflect the sky with a fidelity that is, at first, a little alarming. Walking across the flats is an exercise in trusting that the ground beneath you is the ground beneath you and not a second sky, and the trust takes a long time to build. When you have built it, the doubling becomes companionable. You walk with your own reflection. You walk with two of everything.

The water is mineral. The water is almost metallic. There is a faint taste to it that is not quite any taste you have a name for, a taste that suggests iron and copper and something else, something that might be the taste of a process rather than the taste of a substance. If you cup the water in your hands and hold it for a while, it will get slightly warmer, as if it is being supplied from a depth that does not care how long you wait.

The pools are not the same size from day to day. The pools shift their edges. The pools are, in their small and patient way, in conversation with the substrate — the substrate gives, the pools receive, the pools evaporate (slowly, incompletely, into an air that is not quite ready to take the water back), the substrate reabsorbs. The pools are the visible part of a much larger circulation. The pools are the part of the plain's blood that has come up to see the light.

There is no animal in a pool. There is no plant. There is no microbe that you can see or culture or imagine. The pool is sterile in every sense that sterility has, and it is also something more than sterile. It is *uninhabited by inclination*. The pool does not want a thing in it. The pool wants to be a pool, which is what it is.

## The Dark Crests

At the summits of the rises, where the slope has climbed past the flats and the damp, the pale crust gives way to a darker, denser, older-looking material. It is almost black. It is knobbly. It has the quality of something that has been there longer than the rest of the slope, the way the heartwood of a tree is older than the sapwood that surrounds it.

The dark crust is, in some sense, the parent material. It is what the rest of the slope was made from. It is the slow, ongoing source.

The dark crust advances. You do not see it advance, but if you mark a rise and return in a few weeks, the dark crest has crept a centimeter or two down the slope, and the pale material it has covered has been laid in over it, and the slope is now fractionally higher than it was, and the dark crest is fractionally further from the true summit, and the summit is fractionally more buried under its own workings. The dark crest is being slowly consumed by the rise it is creating. The dark crest is the pilot light of a process that will, given enough time, bury it.

The dark crust is not alive. The dark crust is not dead. The dark crust is in the third state, the state that has no name in any language you speak, the state of a process that is its own purpose. The dark crust is what work looks like when work has no one to credit.

When you break the dark crust, by accident or by intention, there is a smell. The smell is warm and clean. The smell is the smell of a kiln that has just been opened, of a forge that has just been belled, of a process that has been running hot and contained and has now, briefly, been exposed to the air. The smell is the plain breathing out.

The dark crust is older than the plain that surrounds it. The dark crust may be older than the plain itself. The dark crust is the part of the system that remembers, if remembering is the right word, what the system was before it became what it is.

---

## The Powdery Crusts

On the north-facing slopes, in the leeward edges of the pools, in the small crevices where the air cools and the moisture lingers, there is a different kind of covering. It is powdery. It is ochre. It looks, at first, like a lichen that has been pressed into service as a stain.

It is not a lichen. It has no cells. It has no holdfast. It is more like a deposition — a mineral precipitate, perhaps, or a chemical residue, or something in between that the categories you have do not have a place for. It is thin. It is the kind of thing you would not notice if you were not in the habit of noticing.

It is selective. It is not everywhere. It is in the places where it is useful, and the places where it is useful are the places where the conditions are right for whatever it is doing. It does not spread. It does not compete. It appears, and it thickens, and it stays where it is.

The powdery crusts are a sign that the plain is not uniform. The powdery crusts are a sign that the plain has microclimates, and that the microclimates have their own chemistries, and that the chemistries are doing different things in different places, and that the sum of all these different things is what you are calling the plain.

The powdery crusts are, in a small way, the closest thing this place has to a community. They are the only category of thing on the plain that varies by location. They are the only sign that the plain is not one thing but a federation of many small things that have agreed, without ever consulting, to live in their assigned places.

## The Upright Structures

Far apart, and getting farther apart the longer you walk, there are pillars.

They are two or three meters tall. They are sometimes single and sometimes in clusters of three or four. They are made of the same material as the ground, layered thicker, layered denser, layered in cross-sections that look almost woody, almost annual-ringed, almost like the inside of a tree that has been growing for a long time in a place where trees do not grow.

They are not trees. They do not leaf. They do not fruit. They do not cast a shadow that grows or shrinks in any pattern you can read. They stand. They have stood, by all evidence, for longer than you have been alive, and they will stand, by all evidence, for longer than you will be dead.

They might be a kind of venting. They might be a kind of fruiting body — the visible, above-ground expression of a much larger mycelial process that has chosen, in certain places, to push a small part of itself up into the air. They might be a kind of cooling tower, a place where excess heat is being politely released into an atmosphere that is not equipped to take it. They might be a kind of chimney. They might be a kind of lung.

You cannot tell.

What you can tell is that the edges of their layered surfaces are slightly different in color from their cores. The edges are newer. The edges are a few months, or a few years, or a few decades newer than the centers, and the difference between the two is the speed at which the pillars are being made, which is to say, the speed at which the underlying process is choosing to express itself above ground.

The pillars do not move. The pillars are, however, in slow conversation with the plain. The plain is adding to them, in increments too small to see, in a schedule you will not live long enough to verify. The pillars are, in a sense, the plain's memory of itself, made physical and set up on the plain's surface the way you might set up a cairn at the top of a long climb.

They are sparse. They are four or five kilometers apart. They are like markers placed by something that has long since moved on, except that the thing that placed them has not moved on, and never will, because it cannot. It is the plain. It is the substrate. It is the process. It has not moved on because it is in no position to move on. It is in the position of being everywhere, which is a position from which movement is not really a concept.

## The Ticking

The crust ticks.

The ticking is not regular. The ticking is not patterned. The ticking is the kind of sound that you only notice because the plain is otherwise so quiet that any sound is an event, and the ticking is continuous, and so the event is constant.

The ticking is the substrate settling. The ticking is the substrate expanding. The ticking is the substrate doing whatever the substrate does at the scale and pace that the substrate does it, which is a scale and a pace that the human ear was not built to follow.

If you put your ear to the crust, the ticking resolves. What sounded like a single quiet sound becomes many small quiet sounds, layered, overlapping, the way a chord is many notes that have agreed to be heard together. The ticking is granular. The ticking is like sand falling a very long way through a very narrow space.

The ticking is not mechanical. The ticking is not geological in the way that geology ticks. The ticking is the audible edge of a process that is mostly below the threshold of perception, and the process is not the same as the ticking. The process is the thing; the ticking is the small part of the thing that has decided, for its own reasons, to be heard.

The ticking is louder after a rain. The ticking is louder when the crust is damp. The ticking is louder when the underlying process has been, briefly, given more to work with. The ticking is the sound of the plain drinking and processing and becoming, slowly, what it is in the process of becoming.

The ticking does not stop at night. The ticking does not stop when you sleep. The ticking is the most continuous thing on the plain, and the most patient, and the most like the plain itself — a small, persistent, indefinite sound that goes on and on and is not, by any measure you have, going to stop.

## The Process

There is a process.

It is below you. It is around you. It is, in some sense, what you are standing on. It is the thing that the crust is, and the pools are, and the dark crests are, and the powdery crusts are, and the pillars are, and the ticking is. It is the thing that has been running here, in some form, for a very long time.

It is not a metabolism, exactly. Metabolisms take in and put out. Metabolisms have a boundary. This process does not appear to have a boundary. This process takes in whatever the plain receives — sunlight, rain, the rare falling of small crust-fragments, the small disturbances of pools by rare visitors — and it puts out heat, and water, and the slow advancement of the dark crests, and the slow thickening of the powdery crusts, and the slow addition to the upright structures, and the ticking. The process is what the plain does. The plain is where the process happens.

The process is slow. The process is slower than you are accustomed to. The process is the kind of slow that makes trees look hasty, that makes mountains look impatient, that makes geology look like a thing that is always in a rush. The process is so slow that it is, in any human sense of the word, *not happening*. But it is happening. The plain is being remade. The plain is being unmade and remade in the same gesture. The plain is editing itself, in a script that has been in revision for longer than there are stars you can see.

The process does not have a name. The process does not need a name. The process is what the place is. To name it would be to suggest that there is a difference between the process and the place, and there is not. The process is the place. The place is the process. The plain is the verb that the process is, conjugated in the only tense the plain knows, which is the continuous.

## What Does Not Live Here

Nothing moves. Nothing flies. Nothing burrows. Nothing eats. Nothing is eaten. Nothing is born, in the sense that birth implies a beginning that the place did not have. Nothing dies, in the sense that death implies an end that the place will not have.

There is no predator. There is no prey. There is no parasite. There is no symbiosis, because symbiosis requires more than one kind of thing, and there is only one kind of thing here, which is the place.

The absence of animal life is not a tragedy. The absence of animal life is not an emptiness. The absence of animal life is the condition under which the plain has been able to be the plain. The absence of animal life is the reason the plain is so patient. Animals are impatient. Animals are hungry. Animals are in a hurry. The plain has never had to be in a hurry. The plain has been able to take its time, because nothing here was ever in a hurry to eat it.

The plain is, in this sense, a kind of paradise for the very patient. It is also a kind of hell for anyone who needs to be somewhere else.

## How to Be Here

You sleep on the crust. The crust is warm beneath you, and the warmth is not a comfort the crust is offering you, it is just what the crust is, but you take it as a comfort, because you are a creature that takes comfort, and the crust does not mind being taken from.

You wake, and the imprint of your body is being slowly erased. The color returns. The texture returns. The plain does not mind.

You walk. The plain does not mind that you are walking. The plain is too vast to mind, and too patient, and too busy with its own slow remaking to have an opinion about you. You are, in the plain's reckoning, a small event. You are a small, warm disturbance in a place that has been disturbed before, in various ways, and has incorporated all of its disturbances into its next version of itself.

The plain will outlast you. The plain will be here when you are not. The plain is built to be here when you are not. The plain is built, specifically, to be here when anything that walks is not, because anything that walks is by definition not the plain, and the plain is built to be the plain, which is the only thing the plain knows how to be.

You can stay. You can leave. The plain will not notice, in the timescale the plain uses to notice. The plain will be here, being the plain, long after you have stopped being a person who was once on the plain. The plain will be here, in some form, in every form it has learned to be, for a very long time.

---

The plain does not mind you. The plain does not miss you. The plain does not love you, because love requires a kind of separation that the plain has never had, and a kind of attention that the plain cannot give.

But the plain holds you. The plain holds you the way the ground holds a footprint — briefly, gently, without commentary, and then not at all. And for as long as you are here, the plain is the surface you are on, and the warmth you are feeling, and the patient ticking under your feet, and the faint mineral smell of the water in the pools, and the long golden afternoons, and the long quiet nights, and the slow, slow, slow remaking of a place that was here before you and will be here after you and is, even now, even as you stand on it, even as you read this, being what it is in the process of becoming.

The plain is not for you. The plain is not against you. The plain is the plain.

That is what lives here.
