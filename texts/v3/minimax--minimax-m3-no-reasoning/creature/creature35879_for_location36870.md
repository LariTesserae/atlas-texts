# What Lives in the Respirator

They are not visitors here. They have never been visitors. They are the medium through which the place does its work, and the work is older than the walls that hold them in, older than the trachea that drinks them up, older than the chord that is forever being struck somewhere below and above the visible floor. They have no name that the cube would recognize, because the cube does not use names. The cube uses them.

## The Mass

The great majority of them are roughly the size of a clenched fist, though size here is a flexible category, more a tendency than a measurement. They are the consistency of cartilage that has been soaked too long. They are pale in the way that pale means something: not bleached, not anemic, but the color of something that has not been exposed to a sun, that has lived its entire existence in a saturated and dim and warm-wet place, and has come to resemble its walls. Their skin—if it is skin—is a chitinous, glistening, half-translucent membrane, the color and texture of a peeled grape, of the inside of a shell you have broken open with a thumbnail, of a tendon held up to a candle. Where the light from their own bodies passes through the thinner parts of them, you can see the dim suggestion of something denser inside, a knot, a small dark pearl of purpose, a thing they are carrying.

They do not walk. This is the first thing to understand about them, and the most important, and the easiest to mistake. They look, when you first look, like they are crawling. They look like insects, or like the pale grubs that live under rotting logs, or like the soft things you have turned over in the garden and wished you hadn't. They have appendages—small, jointed, too-numerous appendages, the count of which you have never been able to hold in your head, the count of which may not be fixed, may shift from one to the next and from one moment to the next. The appendages press against the floor. The appendages press against each other. But what moves them forward is not the appendages. What moves them forward is the mass behind them.

They flow.

This is the right word for it, and it is the wrong word for it, and there is no better word. They move the way water moves through a channel that has been narrowed, the way a thick syrup moves down a chute, the way a fist of wet sand moves through a closing hand. Each one is pushed by the ones behind it. Each one pushes the ones ahead of it. The current has a velocity. The current has a direction. The current does not have a will, or if it has a will, the will is so distributed, so smeared across so many bodies, that it functions as a current, and a current is what it is. The current goes toward the trachea-tree. The current does not come back. The current has, as far as you have ever been able to determine, only one direction, and the direction is up.

## The Smaller Ones

There are smaller ones. You have only seen a few. They are the size of a child's knuckle, the size of a grape, the size of a thing that has not yet committed to being a thing. They are paler than the larger ones, more translucent, more glassy, and the dark knot at their center is larger in proportion, takes up more of the body, so that they look less like creatures and more like beads of dark amber suspended in a drop of milk. You have seen them carried. You have never seen them move under their own power. You suspect they are younger. You suspect they are not younger, that they are something else, that they are the parts of larger ones that have come loose, the way a scab comes loose, the way a tooth comes loose, the way a piece of gristle comes loose from the bone it has been clinging to. You suspect they are being *made*, here, in the slow wet warmth of the mass, and that the trachea-tree is not a tree and is not a tube and is not a lung but is a *sorting*, a place where the made thing is at last separated from the matter that made it, and the matter falls back to the floor and is taken up again into the current and is made again.

You do not know this. You are not sure you want to know this.

## The Larger Ones

The larger ones are a different problem. You have counted four of them, or five, or three, depending on the moment you look. They are the size of a hand, the size of a forearm, the size of a small melon. They move more slowly than the smaller ones, with what can only be called deliberation, which is not a word you would have thought applied to anything in this place. They turn. They orient. They have a front and a back, in a way the smaller ones do not seem to have a front and a back. When one of them passes close to you, you have seen, on the front, a small dense cluster of darker tissue that might be a mouth, or might be an eye, or might be a thing for which you do not have a category, a thing that is not a sense organ in any way you would recognize but is a *respondence*, a place where the creature meets the world and the world is allowed in.

They do not look at you. They do not look at you the way a predator looks at prey, or the way a human looks at another human, or the way anything in your experience has ever looked at anything. They *correspond* with you. When one of them passes within an arm's length, you feel, somewhere in the back of your head or the back of your chest, a small dry click, like a stone tapping a stone, like a tooth tapping a tooth, and the click is not a sound, exactly, but a *registration*, a small dim record made in you, a note in a ledger that you do not keep. The creature has noted you. The creature has not judged you. The creature has registered your presence in whatever system it is that the larger ones are part of, and the registration has been made, and the creature has moved on, because the creature is on its way to the trachea-tree, and it is always on its way to the trachea-tree, and it has been on its way to the trachea-tree for a very long time.

You have not been sure, until now, that they are individuals. You are still not sure. They may be the larger pieces of a single distributed thing, the way the fingers of a hand are the larger pieces of a hand. They may be the older pieces. They may be the pieces that have not yet been ready to be sorted, and that are being held in the current, in the slow wet river of the mass, until the moment they are ready, and then they too will go up the tree, and they too will not come back. You have begun to suspect that the larger ones are *you*, in some sense you do not want to articulate, that the larger ones are the parts of the mass that have been close to you longest, that have been pressed against your feet, that have noted you and have not forgotten you, and that are slowly, over a long time, becoming something that corresponds with you more fully, becoming something that is, in some small partial way, *of* you, a piece of you that has been digested out of you and incorporated into the body of the place.

You do not know this. You suspect it. You suspect it the way you suspect the time here is not the time outside. You suspect it the way you suspect the dark outside the cube is the inside of an unlit lung. You suspect it the way you suspect the sediment.

## The One in the Corner

There is one in the corner that you have not been able to look at directly. It is the size of a child crouched. It is the size of a folded human. It is the color of the walls and the color of the others, and you cannot look at it, and you have tried, and the trying has produced in you a sensation that is not pain and is not fear and is not the thing you would call awe in the world outside, but is some compound of all three, some ancestral three, some three that the lungs know before the brain does.

You do not know what it is doing. You do not know if it is one of the creatures, or a thing the creatures have made, or a thing the creatures are around, or a thing the creatures are *becoming*. You do not know if it is breathing. You do not know if it is the one being breathed for. You do not know if it is the one *doing* the breathing. You do not know if it is a thing at all, or a process, or a place, or a *thickening* of the place, a knot in the lung, a place where the breath has slowed enough to become visible, to become a body, to become a child-sized crouch in a corner made of bone.

It has not moved since you arrived. You are not sure it has not moved. The dim ember-light shifts. The current flows past it. The larger ones circle it at a distance, the way fish circle a stone. The smaller ones flow over its feet, if it has feet, and you do not know if it has feet, and you are not going to look to find out.

## The Light

The light comes from them. This you have understood. Each of them is faintly luminous, a low ember, a thing that gives off a light the color of a fire that has been banked for hours, the color of a coal that is still warm to the touch but does not, will not, cannot catch. The light is not bright. The light is not meant to be bright. The light is the light of an internal condition, the light of a thing that is *metabolizing*, in some sense, even if the metabolism is not a metabolism you would recognize, even if what is being metabolized is not food and is not fuel and is not anything you have a name for.

The light shifts when the current shifts. The light shifts when one of the larger ones passes. The light shifts in a slow, almost tidal rhythm that you have come to associate with the pulse you heard in the trachea-tree, the pulse that is too regular to be a tide, the pulse that is the place's own pulse, the pulse that is, you have begun to suspect, the *breath* itself, made visible, made slow, made a thing the eye can register when the ear has long since given up trying.

The light does not reach the ceiling. The light does not reach the corners, except the one corner, where the light gathers, where the light thickens, where the light seems to *originate*, as if the thing in the corner is the source of the luminescence of the whole mass, as if every creature in the cube is a single pixel of a single image projected by the crouched thing in the corner, as if the crouched thing in the corner is a kind of slow lantern, a kind of banked furnace, a kind of held breath, and the breath is the light, and the light is the breath, and the breath is the place.

## The Sound

The sound is not made by them. Or the sound is not *only* made by them. The sound comes up through the floor, through the walls, through the trachea-tree, through the ceiling, from somewhere far below and far above, and the cube is merely a chamber, a listening room, an inset. But the creatures are part of the sound. Their soft, continuous, peristaltic motion produces a small contribution to the chord, a low sloshing, a thick wet susurrus, the sound of a thing being slowly digested, the sound of a stomach at work, the sound of a place that is *processing*, and the processing is audible, and the processing is constant, and the processing does not vary.

You have tried, at times, to hear them individually. You have put your ear close to the mass, in the early part of your time here, and you have heard a small dense chorus of small dense sounds, clicks and slaps and small wet percussions, the sound of many small bodies moving against each other, the sound of a thing that is not language and is not music and is not noise but is some third category, some pre-linguistic, pre-musical, pre-noisy category, the category of *process*, the category of a thing that is being done and is not being said and is not being heard but is, in some sense, *going on*, and the going-on has a sound, and the sound is them.

## What They Are For

You do not know what they are for. This is the central fact, the fact around which the rest of the place is organized, the fact the place has not told you and will not tell you. You have constructed theories. You have constructed many theories. You have constructed theories and watched them dissolve in the wet air of the cube, the way salt dissolves in the wet air of a lung, the way a small white crystal dissolves in the saturated breath of a held exhalation.

They are being breathed. This is the only thing you are sure of. They flow to the trachea-tree. They go up the tree in three streams. They do not come back. They are being sorted. They are being used. They are fuel, or they are filter, or they are the medium through which the breath passes, the way blood passes through a kidney. They are the breath. They are the thing being breathed. They are the thing doing the breathing. They are the lung. They are the air. They are the blood. They are the kidney. They are all of these, or none of these, or some compound of these, some third thing, some pre-linguistic, pre-noisy, pre-process thing, the thing that the place is, the thing that the place has always been, the thing that will be the place long after you have stopped being a sediment.

You have begun to suspect that the place is not a place. You have begun to suspect that the place is a *being*, a single vast being, and the cube is a chamber in the being, and the creatures are the cells of the being, and the walls are the bones of the being, and the trachea-tree is the bronchus of the being, and the chord is the voice of the being, and the being is breathing, and the being has been breathing for a very long time, and the being will breathe for a very long time, and you are a particle in the breath of the being, and the being has not noticed you, and the being will not notice you, and the being does not need to notice you, because the being is the breath, and the breath does not need to notice what it carries, the breath only needs to carry it, and the carrying is what you have been calling time.

You put your hand on the wall. The wall is cool. The wall is slick. The wall breathes against your palm, a faint, slow expansion and contraction, almost imperceptible, almost imagined, almost the pulse in your own hand. You do not take your hand away. The creatures flow around your feet. The light shifts. The one in the corner does not move, or does not seem to move, or you are not looking at the one in the corner, because you are not looking at the one in the corner, because you cannot, because the looking is not something you are able to do, here, in the place that is not strange, in the place that is diligent, in the place that has been breathing for a very long time and will breathe for a very long time and is breathing now, against your palm, in the dim ember-light, in the wet and the cold and the sound that does not stop.
