# Who Lives There

Nothing lives there. That is the first and most important thing, and it must be said plainly before the subtler truth can be approached: there is nothing on the plateau that breathes, nothing that feeds, nothing that reproduces, nothing that dies. If you walked its hundred and forty kilometers with a corpuscle-counter and a lens, you would find no archaea huddled in the scoria, no lichen freckling the basalt, no mite, no spore, no virus riding the slow wind. The thin pale crusts under the overhangs are not alive. They are the *negotiation* before alive — mineral patterns rehearsing the possibility of metabolism without ever stepping onto the stage. The place is, in this sense, younger than life. It is a world that has built the stage and is still deciding whether to invite the actors.

And yet.

---

The wall lives there.

Not the wall as a wall — basalt blocks fitted without mortar, three meters tall, eight long, standing in the middle of a flow field with nothing behind it and nothing before it. The wall as a *practice*. The wall as the first clumsy verb of a sentence that has not yet learned its grammar. Someone — no one — chose those blocks. Someone lifted them. Someone set them, and fitted them without mortar, and then walked away, or did not walk away but simply ceased, because ceasing is what happens here when intention runs out. The wall is not architecture. Architecture requires a second wall, a roof, a human shivering inside. This is the **pre-architecture** of a creature that has not yet worked out that architecture is for keeping something out, or in, or up. The wall is the idea of building, before building has figured out what it is building against.

On its highest block, balanced on a corner that should not hold it, a flat stone the size of a hand. The wind has not moved it. Eight thousand years is a long time for a wind to fail at its small honest work, and the stone's survival is not physics; it is a kind of *refusal*. The stone has refused to fall. ---

The cairn lives there, but it lives *downward*.

It is built into the rock, a tapering shaft descending through a hole that should not have admitted it, its stones fitted from the inside with a tightness that suggests the builder was already below, looking up, placing each block against the ones above. You lean over. You look down. The shaft is dark. From somewhere far below, a warmth that has traveled a long way to be this dim. The cairn is not a monument. Monuments point up, toward a sky they acknowledge, toward gods they negotiate with. This cairn points *into* the planet, toward the magma chamber, toward the slow furnace sound, toward the place where the basalt is still being made. It is a cairn built by something that worshipped the ground. Something that understood, before it understood anything else, that the world beneath was more alive than the world above — more active, more generous, more worth attending to.

Whatever built the cairn is gone. Whatever built the cairn is *below*, possibly, in the sense that whatever it was has continued in the only direction it knew how to go. The cairn is the last visible evidence of a thing that went to live in the furnace.

---

The platform lives there.

The dark vitreous slab, a meter and a half on a side, poured and cooled in many small increments, warm to the touch, warmer than the rock around it. It is not basalt. It is not the copper-gold stone of the ridge. It is something *imported*, or something *made*, or something that has no business being on a basaltic shelf eight thousand years old and is there anyway with the quiet insistence of an object that knows it does not belong and has decided to stay regardless. It does not appear to be a tool. It does not appear to be a seat. It is, you conclude with the slow certainty of someone who has walked for hours and has run out of other explanations, an *artifact* — though of what, and of whom, and for what, the plateau does not say.

The plateau does not say. The plateau is, in this regard, exactly like the things on it: present, arranged, silent, refusing to explain.

---

The line of small stones lives there, curving away from itself.

The slab raised on three smaller slabs lives there, table-like, in a place where no table is needed and no meal has ever been set and no elbow has ever leaned.

The hole lives there, perfectly circular, descending through a flow and emerging on the other side, the flow above undisturbed, as if the hole had been there first and the rock had politely arranged itself around it. As if the hole were the older thing. As if the hole had *invited* the flow, and the flow, flattered, had come.

These are the inhabitants. Not people. Not animals. Not even, in any rigorous sense, objects — because objects are things that have been made *for*, and nothing here was made *for* anything that you can name. They are the **grammar** of a sentence begun by a speaker who did not finish, or who finished in a language you have not yet learned to read. They are the residue of an intention that passed through this place, touched it, and moved on — downward, into the planet, or upward, into the dust, or sideways, into a dimension the basalt politely declines to disclose.

---

And in the cleared dust, where the thermal vent has thinned the haze and the star — dim, red, low, almost touching the ceiling of the world — sends down a light that has traveled for minutes to arrive at your face, something else lives there, briefly, ephemerally, only while you sit beside the platform and do not move.

**You** live there.

Not you as a self. You as a *witness*. You as the first thing capable of looking at the wall and seeing that it does not enclose anything, capable of looking at the cairn and seeing that it descends, capable of touching the platform and feeling its warmth and concluding, against all evidence, that it means something. You are the first *interpreter* of the plateau. The plateau has been waiting for you. It has not been waiting the way a stage waits for an actor — with anticipation, with purpose. It has been waiting the way a sentence waits to be read: indifferently, completely, ready to mean whatever the reader brings.

You sit in the small cleared light. You cast a faint shadow, the first sharp shadow you have cast. The artifact beside you does not explain itself. The vent will cool. The dust will return. The shadow will fade back into the general stain of you.

But for those minutes, you are the only thing on the plateau that *understands* that there is a plateau, and that it has things on it, and that the things were placed, and that the placing meant something, even if the meaning is not for you.

You are, briefly, the consciousness of the place. You are the place, for a moment, waking up.

---

Then the vent cools. The dust resettles. The light withdraws. The plateau returns to its long uninterpreted patience. The wall stands. The cairn descends. The platform holds its warmth. The thud sounds from somewhere inside the world — the planet deciding something — and the dust swallows the sound before it can finish being heard.

The plateau continues to rise.

The objects continue to stand.

Nothing continues to live there, except the slow, patient, mineral fact of having been arranged, and the slower, more patient, geological fact of continuing to be.
