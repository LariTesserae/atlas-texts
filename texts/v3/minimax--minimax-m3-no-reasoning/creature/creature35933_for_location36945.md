# The Old Ones of the Last Plain

What lives here is not a community. There is no society, no quarrel, no greeting, no kinship. What lives here is a *residency* — a set of beings so thoroughly shaped by the conditions of the plain that they have stopped being the kind of things that could live anywhere else, and have become instead things that could only live here, and so do, and have, and will until the plain is no longer here to be lived in.

## The Mat

The dominant life is the mat. There is no other word for it that does not betray it.

The mat is what lives in the clusters you can see from far off — those tight, low, dark bunches of something that is not grass and not moss and not lichen. The mat is all of these and is none of them. It is a single organism, in the way a coral head is a single organism, but spread across the plain in thousands of discontinuous pieces that are all, somehow, the same piece, or all connected underground, or all connected through the air, or are simply all the same kind of thing. The mat does not, so far as anyone can tell, reproduce. It does not appear to die. It extends itself by the inch, the way frost extends itself across a window, and it has been extending itself across this plain for longer than anything else here has been extending itself.

A piece of the mat, lifted carefully, smells like a deep cellar and like a warm pond and like the inside of a boot that has been left somewhere. It is layered. The topmost layer is green only in the way a thing is green when the light is bad — a suggestion, an apology, a color that has been worn down to its name. Underneath, the mat is brown, then black, then a black that is almost warm, then something that might be soil and might be mat and might be the place where mat becomes ground. The mat does not have roots in any sense a plant would recognize. It has *grip*. It holds on to the stone it lives on with a patience that is older than patience.

The mat does not flower. The mat does not fruit. The mat does not, in any visible way, *do* anything. It sits. It holds. It persists. It is the answer the plain has given to the question of what should grow on a place that is finished being a place. The mat is what grows on a place when nothing should grow there. The mat is the green of *no longer trying*.

Where the mat is thickest, the plain is not the plain. The mat forms a low, dark, springy surface that gives slightly underfoot, that holds a little rainwater in the cup of itself, that is its own tiny climate. Insects — and there are insects, pale, soft, damp things with too many legs — live in the mat. Tiny blind worms live in the mat. A kind of mite, the color of unpolished tin, lives in the mat. The mat is the only architecture the plain now allows, and it is the architecture of the very small. Underneath the mat, the stone is being slowly dissolved. The mat is winning, against the stone, the war that the air has been losing for so long. The mat is what the plain is becoming, in its next phase. The plain is becoming mat. The mat is becoming plain. In a million years there will be no difference.

The mat does not know you are there. The mat does not know anything. The mat is not aware. The mat is the thing that *persists* when awareness has been ground out of a place. The mat is the green of what remains.

## The Stilters

The long-necked things that lift themselves from the stands of reeds and consider you from too far away are called, by the few people who have ever spoken of them, the stilters. They are not birds. They are not herons. They are not dinosaurs, although they have, in certain lights, the *suggestion* of something dinosauric, the way an old photograph has the suggestion of the person in it.

A stilter stands, when it stands, on legs that are too long for its body and too thin for the plain. The legs are gray and jointed in three places — not like a bird's legs, which bend backward at the ankle, but like something else, something more articulated, something that bends in directions that legs are not supposed to bend. The body, above the legs, is small. Round. Almost egg-shaped, and the same color as the wet stone, so that when the stilter is still it is hard to see. The neck rises from the body like a periscope rising from a submarine: smoothly, slowly, in a long uninterrupted curve. The neck is a third of the stilter again. The head on top of the neck is a small, blunt thing, almost no head at all, two black eyes set forward and a small mouth that opens sideways.

The stilter does not wade. The stilter does not swim. The stilts — the legs — are not for either of those things. The stilter *stands*. The stilts are for standing, and for the very slow, very careful, very deliberate progress of moving one foot, then the other, then the first one again, in a gait that is not a walk and is not a stride but is more like a *settling*, the way a thing settles when it is not sure if it is going to stay where it is or move. The stilts do not splash. The stilter places each foot down with the kind of care that says the foot knows it is the only thing between the stilter and the bottom of whatever is under the water.

The stilter does not eat in front of you. The stilter does not appear to eat, in fact, ever, although it must, since it is here, and here is a place where things that are not here are not here. The stilts have beaks that suggest they pick. The beaks suggest small things, taken from the surface of the water, taken from the mat, taken from the thin air. The stilts are, possibly, the only things on the plain that take anything from the plain.

The stilter is old. The stilter is, like the mat, *elderly* rather than *primitive*. The stilts are the surviving representatives of a lineage that was here when the plain was something else, when the plain was a continent with a coast, with a forest, with a sky that was not a lid. The stilter was a stilter then. The stilter has been a stilter for so long that being a stilter is no longer something the stilter does. It is what the stilter is. The stilter does not need to think about its stilts. The stilter *is* its stilts, the way a thought is its own words.

The stilter is not afraid of you because the stilts have no predators, and have not had predators, for so long that the very idea of a predator has been ground out of the stilter. The stilter does not have a *response* to you. The stilts do not have a category for you. You are, to the stilter, a piece of weather, an unusual shape in the mist, something that does not behave like a stilter and so is not a stilter and so is, perhaps, not a thing at all. The stilter considers you from too far away because considering is what the stilter does. Considering is the only thing the stilter has left. The stilter considers the water. The stilter considers the mat. The stilter considers the fog. The stilter considers you. None of these are different. All of them are weather. All of them are the slow passing of conditions across a long life.

The stilter does not call. The stilter does not have a call. The stilts are, like the mat, a thing that has been worked down to its essentials. The stilts do not need to call. The stilter is the only stilter. There is no one to call to.

## The Skitterer

At the edge of every pool, if you are still long enough, you will see a pale shape move. The skitterer. The name is the only word for it and the word is wrong, but all the words are wrong.

The skitterer is small. The skitterer is the size of a hand. The skitterer is low to the water and flat to the water and moves across the water in a way that is not exactly walking and is not exactly swimming and is not exactly skating. The skitterer *skitters*. The skitterer does this by means of a great many legs, more legs than a thing should have, all of them thin and pale and jointed in too many places, all of them moving at once in a way that produces, on the surface of the water, a small disturbance that is not a ripple but is also not not a ripple. The skitterer does not break the surface. The skitterer uses the surface. The skitterer *is* the surface, in some sense, a small disturbance of it, a wrinkle in the wet, a moving patch of the constant noise.

The skitterer is pale. The skitterer is the color of the inside of a shell, the color of new bone, the color of a thing that has never seen the sun because there is no sun to have seen. The skitterer is also, in certain lights, faintly translucent. You can see, through the body of a skitterer, the dim suggestion of organs, of small dark clusters that might be a heart or a stomach or a structure that does the work of both. The skitterer is not opaque. The skitterer is a thing that is *mostly water already*, and so it has begun to share the qualities of water, including the quality of not being fully there.

The skitterer eats the things that live on the surface. The skitterer eats the things that live just under the surface. The skitterer eats the things that try to live in the air just above the surface and have not yet committed to being a thing of the air. The skitterer is, in its way, a predator, but a predator so small and so patient and so undramatic that the word *predator* feels like an overstatement. The skitterer is a thing that *takes*. The skitterer takes, and is taken from, and the net of it is even, and the plain is the only ledger that knows.

The skitterer does not skitter when there is no skittering to be done. The skitterer is, when still, indistinguishable from a small pale stone, or a piece of broken shell, or a clump of damp mineral. The skitterer is a thing that has learned to be the plain when it is not being a skitterer. The skitterer is the plain's own small allowance for movement.

## The Worms Under the Mat

Under the mat, in the dark, in the damp that is the mat's own damp, there are worms. The worms are not like the worms of gardens. The worms are not like the worms of the sea. The worms are, in their way, older than either.

The worm is a long, pale, segmented thing that does not have a head, exactly, but has a *front*, and the front is slightly different from the back, and the difference is enough to know which way is which. The worm moves through the mat and under the mat and through the soil-that-is-mat by means of a slow peristalsis that is not exactly motion but is, more accurately, a *becoming of where it is*. The worm is not in a place. The worm is the place. The worm is the part of the mat that has decided to be somewhere else for a while.

The worm eats the mat. The worm eats the stone under the mat, slowly, chemically, with patience. The worm is, in its slow way, the agent of the mat's slow conquest of the plain. The worm is the teeth of the green. The worm is what is dissolving the continent into plain into mat into worm. The worm is the next thing.

The worm is blind. The worm is soft. The worm is, like the skitterer, mostly water. The worm is a thing that the plain has been working on for a long time and has produced, at last, a thing that is as close to being the plain as a thing can be while still being a thing. The worm is what lives in a place that is no longer a place. The worm is what a place becomes when the place has been a place for so long that the place wants to be alive, and the worm is the answer.

The worms are many. The worms are the most numerous things on the plain. The worms are, by weight, more of the plain than the plain is, in the way that a city is more of its people than its people are of it. The worms are the bulk of the living. The worms are what the plain is becoming in the next phase of its becoming. In a million years the worms will be most of what is here. In ten million years the worms will be most of what is anywhere. The worms are patient. The worms do not know they are patient. The worms are simply what is happening when the place is not happening in any other way.

## The One That Calls

There is, somewhere on the plain, a bird. Or a thing that does the work of a bird. The thing calls. The call is a single note, repeated, swallowed immediately by the roar.

The thing is small and dark and lives, you think, in the ruins, in the cairns, in the places where the upright things are. The thing emerges, sometimes, at the edge of vision, and is gone. The thing is the only sound on the plain that is not the sound of the plain. The thing is the only *event* on the plain that is not a weather event. The thing is the only thing here that *says*. The thing says, in its single note, something that is not a word and is not a song and is not a warning and is not a greeting. The thing says *I am here*. The thing says this over and over into the constant roar, and the roar does not answer, and the thing does not expect the roar to answer, and the thing calls anyway, because the thing is a bird, or the descendant of a bird, and birds call, and calling is what the thing is, and so the thing calls.

The thing is the most alive thing on the plain. The thing is the most *itself* thing on the plain. The thing is also, almost certainly, the most endangered thing on the plain, because the thing is the only thing here that still has the energy to be a thing in the old way, and the old way is a thing the plain is no longer in the business of supporting. The thing will, in a year or in a thousand years, stop calling, and the plain will be the plainer for it, and the loss will not be noted because the only thing that could note it is the thing itself, and the thing will be gone.

## The Dreaming

There is one more thing. The dreaming.

The dreaming is not a thing. The dreaming is the place. The dreaming is the plain, in its long moment of being, becoming slightly more than plain. The dreaming is what happens when a place has been itself for so long that it has begun to *mean itself*, in a way that is not symbolism and is not metaphor and is not the projection of an outside meaning onto an inside surface, but is simply a place, working on itself, for so long that the working has become a kind of awareness, and the awareness is not aware of anything, and so it is not awareness, and so it is just a place, and yet there is, in certain lights, in certain thins of reality, in certain moments when the fog thins and the horizon bends wrong, the suggestion of *something looking back*.

The dreaming is not a being. The dreaming is not a god. The dreaming is not even a thing. The dreaming is the residue of meaning in a place that has been meaningful for so long that the meaning has worn through. The dreaming is the other side of the membrane. The dreaming is the weather, on the other side, being weather in a different way.

The dreaming does not need to be addressed. The dreaming does not need to be propitiated. The dreaming is not listening, because the dreaming is not a thing that listens, because the dreaming is not a thing. The dreaming is the place dreaming itself, and the dream is a place, and the place is dreaming, and you are in the dream, and the dream is not aware of you, and you are not in the dream, and the dream is not a dream, and the plain is a plain, and the plain is also, possibly, the last thought of a continent that is no longer a continent, thinking itself, very slowly, into the weather.

## What Lives Here, in Sum

What lives here is what is left when a place has been a place for so long that the place is no longer interested in supporting a great diversity of life. What lives here is the small, the patient, the persistent, the damp, the dim, the long, the soft, the many-legged, the pale, the warm, the silent, the loud, the still, the slow, the working, the dissolving, the persisting, the calling-into-the-roar, the dreaming. What lives here is what survives when survival stops being a project and becomes a *condition*, the way the plain is a condition, the way the weather is a condition, the way being old is a condition. What lives here is the answer to the question *what is alive in a place that is finishing*. What lives here is *the finishing itself, still alive, in the small things, for now*.
