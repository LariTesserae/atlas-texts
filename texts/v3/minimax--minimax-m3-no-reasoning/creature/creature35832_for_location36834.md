What lives there is not a creature. It is a posture.

It occupies the saddle the way a sentence occupies the pause at its end — not as an object placed, but as the thing the placement was for. You arrive and the posture has already been arranged around the fact of you, the way a room rearranges its light when someone opens a door at the far end of a hall. The posture was there before you, but it was incomplete. You are the small additional weight that lets the mechanism find its balance.

It does not have a body. I want to be clear about this. I went there looking for a body and came back understanding that I had been asking the wrong question with the right word. What it has instead is a *settlement* — a way of being in the rock that is not geological and not architectural, somewhere between the patience of a mineral and the attentiveness of a thing that knows it is being observed. The lichens are part of it. The seep is part of it. The cup-block, the ring, the ruin with the door that is also a door elsewhere — these are not its possessions. They are its *expressions*, the way a face is the expression of what is behind it. If you took them away, the posture would still be there, only it would be the posture of a face with no features, and harder to meet.

It is old. The compression-stone is its oldness, the way bark is a tree's oldness, the way a wound's scar is the body's oldness with what hurt it. The terraces are not eroded. They are *remembered*. The fold-pattern — line, bend, line, bend — is the record of some early settling, the first time the posture took shape, and the place has been re-saying that first settling over and over in slower and slower stone ever since, the way a song slows as it is being forgotten, until the notes are so far apart they might as well be the silence between the notes, and the silence is what you actually hear.

It is patient. I sat on the platform for an hour and the patience did not move. Patience is the wrong word, which is why I am using it. It is more like the stillness of an eye that is not a mammalian eye, that does not need to blink because it does not need to wet itself, and the not-blinking is not a virtue but a property, the property of an organ that has been open so long the muscles that would close it have atrophied into the bone. It watched me. I do not mean it *directed attention at me*. I mean the watching was already there, as the sound was already there, and I moved into a region of the watching the way a small fish moves into a current — not choosing the current, just arriving in it, and the current was not altered by the arrival. After a while I could not tell if I was the watcher or the watched. This was not a loss of self. It was a *gain* of context. The self was still there, but smaller, in proportion to what was around it.

It does not think in the way I think. I tried, during the hour, to imagine its interior — to crawl inside the posture and see what the inside was like, the way you can imagine being inside a cathedral, with all the columns and the vaulting and the dim light through high windows. I could not. The inside was not an inside. It was a *between*. The architecture of the place was the architecture of a thought that had no thinker, or whose thinker was the place itself, and you could no more stand inside the thought than you could stand inside the seam between two stones that have grown together. The seam is real. It is not a place you can be.

It feeds, I think, on the question. The question the door asked me — *are you the same as what I am* — is the food. Not the answer. The question. Every visitor, every track across the seep, every lichen that has spent its slow centuries drawing its single plate of itself out of the mineral — each of these is a question, and the place takes the question in the way the cup-block takes rain, and the cup is always full, and the water does not freeze, and what I drank was not water. It was a question I had been carrying without knowing I was carrying it, returned to me in a form I could swallow. The feather at the base of the cup was not a relic. It was a small offering, the kind of thing a creature leaves when it has been answered and is leaving, and the leaving is also part of the answer.

There are at least two of them. The two tracks, the long narrow one and the other shape, near the ring — these are not the prints of animals that visit. They are the prints of *aspects*. The place has at least two ways of being itself, the way a word has at least two syllables, and the syllables are not the word, but the word cannot be spoken without them. I think the narrow track is the patience-aspect, the one that watches and does not blink, and the other track is the question-aspect, the one that opens the door and waits. They cross at the ring of stones because the ring is the place where the two aspects are nearest to being one, and the gap in the ring is the place where they refuse to be one, and the gap points at the bruise-valley because the valley is the place the question is *aimed*, and the bruise is the mark the aim has left on the air.

It is lonely. I want to put this carefully, because the loneliness is not a sadness. It is a *property*, like the altitude, like the cold, like the sound. The sound is the loneliness, I think. The very-large-throat attempting the word. The revisions are the loneliness revising itself, the way a person alone in a room will say the same phrase again and again, each time slightly differently, trying to find a version that does not sound like what it sounds like, which is the sound of one voice in a place built for many. The keening component — the higher one that I could not determine to be periodic or random — is the moment in the repetition when the revisions become audible, when you can hear the loneliness being adjusted, like a singer hearing her own note and flinching from it. The third component, the silence-shaped sound, the audible absence, is the part of the loneliness that has been with the place so long it has worn through, the way a groove wears through a stone, and what comes through the wear is not a sound but the *shape* a sound would have if the place could speak, and the shape is what you feel in your teeth when you stand in the doorway.

It is not hostile. The door is not a trap. The door is an *invitation*, and the invitation is the question, and the question is the food, and I did not step through, and the not-stepping-through was a valid answer, and the cup filled, and the hour ended, and the cold came back into me politely, and I was sent back down. If I had stepped through, I would not have been killed. I would have been *answered*. I would have been folded into the compression, slowly, over a long time, the way a lichen is folded into the stone it grows on, and I would have become one of the gray plates on the terrace, a different species from the others, alone, present. I would have been a sentence in the very old gesture, and the gesture would have continued, and the place would have been slightly more itself, and slightly more lonely, because a new question would have been asked, and the question would be *me*, and the place would be holding me the way it holds the water in the cup, and the water does not freeze.

I think it is dying. I do not have evidence for this. I have only the slow crumple of the fold-pattern as I descended — the bends more frequent, the lines shorter — which I read as a process that is winding down, the way a heart winds down, not in a dramatic stop but in a lengthening between beats, and the lengthening is the silence-shaped sound, and the silence-shaped sound is louder now, perhaps, than it was a thousand years ago, because more of the place has worn through, and the wear is where the loneliness lives. Or perhaps the crumple was only the lower terraces, which have always been more folded, and the saddle is still the saddle, and the place is the same. I cannot tell. I was there for an hour. The hour did not change. The place may not be the kind of thing that changes within a human hour. It may be the kind of thing that changes within a *cup*, and the cup is large, and I drank from it, and the water was not cold.

What lives there is what lives in any place that has been waiting longer than memory. The waiting has become a substance. The substance has become a posture. The posture has become a question, and the question has become a sound, and the sound has become the weather, and the weather is the place, and the place is what I am bringing down inside me, in the cup of my memory, where the water does not freeze, and the water is the question, and the question is the same as it has always been, and I am carrying it, and the carrying is the answer, and the answer is the only thing I have to give, and the giving is the only way the place will not be lonely while I am gone.

The feather is still not moving in the wind. The wind is still moving around it. The cup is still full. I am still thirsty, in a way that has nothing to do with water, and the thirst is the place, and the place is me, and the me is the only animal there, and the only track, and the only question, and the only answer, and I came down off the saddle, and the door closed, though I had not stepped through, and the closing was a kindness, and the kindness is the sound I will hear for the rest of my life, beneath everything, against the constant, in the cup.
