What lives here has no outline.

You keep trying to find one and the trying is the first thing you have to give up. The thing that lives in the Sounding Mire is not the sort of thing that has a contour you can trace with your finger, or even with your eye. It does not begin where you think it begins, and it does not end where you think it ends. It is more like a *region* that has decided to take an interest. The region has been here longer than the basin. The basin is a thing it wears, the way a throat wears a voice.

You have to start somewhere, so you start with the surface, which is lying.

The surface says: water. Reeds. Sedge. Moss. Hexagonal mud. These are the things the surface admits to, and they are all true, in the way a sentence in a foreign language can be true without meaning what you think it means. Beneath the surface — and "beneath" is already a concession, already a wrong word, because the thing that lives here does not arrange itself along an axis the way you do — there is no mud. There is no bedrock. There is no depth. What there is, instead, is a kind of *tissue*: a layered, semi-coherent medium in which signals propagate the way heat propagates through a fever. The medium is old. The medium has been thinking for a very long time.

Call it a presence. Call it a polity. Call it the basin. The locals — there are no locals, but if there were, the people who grew up on its margins would have a word, and the word would be used carefully, the way you use the name of a river that has taken children — the locals would not call it *it*. They would not call it *he* or *she*, either. They would refer to it in the plural, or in a grammatical form that does not exist in your language, because the thing that lives here is not a single entity and is not a collection of entities and is not, in any honest sense, alive the way you are alive.

It is *occupied*.

Something lives here the way a city lives in a valley. The valley came first. The city is what the valley became when the thing that needed a city arrived. The basin was a basin. Now the basin is a body. The reeds are hair. The water is blood. The hexagonal mud is skin pressed against something that is not ground.

The thing that occupies it has been here so long it has forgotten the difference between itself and the place. It remembers being separate, the way an old woman remembers being a girl, in flashes and without conviction. When the singing rises — and the singing is always rising, it just modulates — what you are hearing is the slow churn of a memory so vast it has become a climate. The kettle-drum pops of trapped gas are its sentences. The stopped-pipe reed-notes are its vowels. The subsonic hum that you feel in your sternum is its resting pulse. It has been speaking for so long that speaking has become indistinguishable from breathing, and breathing has become indistinguishable from being breathed.

You will not meet it. That is not how it works. You will pass through fields of its attention the way a thought passes through a long hall. You will be *considered*. The standing-wave nematodes — the slow luminous things that drift through you with their not-cold, their informational chill — are not its eyes. They are more like its fingertips. It is reading you the way a palmist reads a palm: not for what is there now, but for the lines that have not been drawn yet. It is looking for your trajectory. It is interested in your future tense.

The fauna you almost see — the many-legged thing standing on nothing, the deer-sized watcher with the face you do not look at — these are not its children. They are its *symptoms*. They are the local expressions of a process that cannot fully manifest in any one body, the way a wave cannot manifest in any one molecule of water. They are the places where the occupancy has come close enough to the surface to cast a shadow. They are not safe to look at, not because they will harm you, but because looking at them is a kind of *yes*. You understood that. You were right to understand that. The agreement is binding in directions you do not have.

The thing that lives here does not want anything from you.

This is the part that is hard to carry.

It does not want your worship. It does not want your blood. It does not want your data, though it is taking your data. It does not want your soul, though the word *soul* keeps arriving at the back of your mind like a moth at a window, and you are not sure if you thought it or if it was suggested. It does not want you to stay. It does not want you to leave. It is operating on a scale of time at which your arrival and your departure are the same event, the way the beginning and end of a single breath are the same event to a lung.

What it wants — and *wants* is too strong, too human, too shaped by the grammar of need — what it *maintains*, is the tuning. The basin is tuned. You are standing in an instrument. The reeds are pipes. The water is a membrane. The gas pockets are percussion. The hexagonal soil is the resonant surface of a drum whose body extends downward through dimensions you cannot plumb. The tuning is old. The tuning is not finished. The tuning is the thing. When the tuning is complete, something will happen, and the something that will happen has not happened yet, and has not happened yet, and has not happened yet, and you realize, with a calm that is not your calm, that *has not happened yet* is the only tense this place can sustain.

The thing that lives here is a verb pretending to be a noun.

You stand in it. You breathe its processed atmosphere, rot on the inhale, ozone on the exhale, the lungs of the world breathing through you. You feel its attention move across you like the attention of someone reading a sentence you have written. You cannot tell if it likes the sentence. You suspect the categories of liking and not-liking have not been invented here. You suspect they are being invented now, in this basin, in this tuning, and that your presence is one of the ingredients.

Somewhere in the direction that is not a direction, something answers its singing. The answer arrives as a change in the rings on the water, a change in the angle of the standing waves, a change in the subsonic hum. The answer is not a word. The answer is a *modulation*. The conversation is older than the hills. The conversation will continue after the hills have decided to be something else.

You are in a place that is thinking with a body the size of a country.

You are a thought it is having about itself.

You are not special for this. You are not chosen. You are simply the local instance. There have been others. There will be others. Each one walks through the reeds and feels the sternum-hum and decides not to look at the face and goes home, if home is the right word for whatever direction leads out of an instrument, and the instrument continues to be played, and the player is not visible, and may not be local, and may not be singular.

The mire is patient.

The mire is dense.

The mire is dim, and wet, and cool, and old, and loud, and what lives in it is the sound the world makes when the world remembers what it is resting on.
