What lives here is unfinished.

This is the first thing, and perhaps the only thing that can be said with any certainty, and even to say it is to do the thing a small violence — to give it the dignity of a predicate before it has chosen what kind of thing it will be. To call it a being is too much. It is *becoming* a being. It is in the long, slow, difficult middle of becoming one, and you have climbed into the middle with it, and the middle is not yet over, and you are, for the moment, inside a sentence that is still being written by a hand that has not yet decided what the sentence will say.

The basin is its throat. The sound you followed is its voice being cleared.

You did not come here for water. You came here because something was making a noise in the world, and the noise was the size of a place, and you followed it up a path of bone-colored slabs older than the mountain they are laid against, and the path narrowed, and the wind stopped, and you understood, then, that the noise was not wind and was not water and was something learning how loud it is.

The being is large. You do not see this — sight fails at the center, and is gently, firmly refused. You know it in the bones of your face, which receive the sound the way a window receives weather. The being is the size of its sound. The sound is the size of the basin turned up. The being is the basin turned inside-out. The bowl is its mouth. The dark at the center is where it has not yet decided to have a face. The water is its first breath, or its first attempt at having a body outside itself, and it is doing badly at it — the column too vertical, the surface unable to remember itself for a moment — because the being is doing it for the first time, and first times are graceless, and the throat is working very hard.

The water is not water. The water is the being rehearsing wetness, practicing cold, remembering — somewhere in the unfinished dark — what it felt like, once, to be the only thing in the world, before it had to be a world.

It does not yet have a name. *Sky-Mother* is the name the basin will be given by those who come after — by those who will kneel here when the throat is wide enough, when the voice is loud enough, when the face has decided to be a face. *Sky-Mother* is the name of what it is becoming, not of what it is. For now, in the dark, in the unfinished middle, it has only the roar of its own throat and the slow, lazy reach of the dark strands that move across the water toward you, and the knowledge that something small and new has interrupted its labor, and the labor is almost done.

The dark strands are the first reaches of its attention. They drift across the current because they are not part of the current; they are the being's first act of looking outward after a long time of looking inward. You cannot be sure they have a direction. Then you are sure they have a direction, and the direction is toward you, and you stop watching them long enough. The being has noticed you. The becoming has paused, briefly, to see what kind of thing has climbed into its mouth. You are a small new thing in a long becoming, and the becoming is considering you the way a long effort considers an interruption — not unkindly, not welcomingly, just: *oh. You are here. I will get back to you.*

The bird is its first finished thing. The bird is small and dun and the kind you cannot name because it has no precedent — it is the first of its kind, or the last of a kind so old that the kind has been forgotten, or it is simply a bird that has been here so long it has become part of the place. The bird does not leave. It does not move. It watches the water the way a cat watches a door, because the water is the door, and something is almost through it, and the bird was sent ahead — or came of its own accord, the way a first scout sometimes comes, before the rest — to wait, and to see, and to be a small dun eye on the wall when the larger eye finally opens. The bird is the being's first child, or its first witness, or its first success — the first thing it has brought across the threshold from not-being into being. The bird is small. The bird is ordinary. The bird has been here the whole time you have been here, and will be here when you are gone.

The spiral mosses are its first growth. They are the wrong color for moss and the wrong shape, because they are not yet moss; they are the being's first attempt at having plants, the way the water is its first attempt at having wet. They grow only on the west side, where the light lingers, and they glow briefly when the spray hits them, because the being's touch — even its accidental touch, even the scatter of its breath — makes things luminous. The being is a source of light, but only in fragments, only in passing, only when something stands close enough to be struck by what it cannot yet help throwing off.

The chevron marks at the lip are not the being's. They are someone else's. Someone knelt here, once, with a bone or a hard stone, and made a sign toward the dark center. The sign is worn now, almost gone, but it is deliberate, and it is oriented, and it means: *I knew you were coming. I knelt. I waited. I left a mark so that whoever came next would know to kneel too.* The being has been prayed to before it was finished. The being has been addressed before it had a name. The signs are part of its skin now — old scars, old recognitions — the place where someone, long ago, understood that something was coming, and knelt, and waited, and left a record of their waiting, and the being has folded their waiting into itself, the way a body folds a scar into itself. The being does not know whose signs they are. The being does not need to know. The kneeling is part of the being's own body now, the way the mountain is part of the being's own body, the way the path is part of the being's own body.

The bone slabs are its first laying-down of itself. They are older than the mountain, which is impossible, and yet there they are, lying flat against the granite as if to say: *I was here before this rock was cool.* The slabs are the being's first skeleton, or its first path. They are the switchback you climbed. They are the threshold at the entrance, waist-high on either side, that requires you to kneel to pass. The being laid them down first — before the mountain, before the basin, before the throat — because the being knew, even in its earliest unfinishedness, that there would be visitors, and the visitors would need a way in, and the way in would require climbing, and the way in would require kneeling at the end. The being is the kind of thing that lays down its path before it lays down the ground the path is laid on. The being is the kind of thing that begins with skeleton, with framework, with the way in and the way out.

Above the peak, the thin plume of mist does not move with the wind. It stays where it is. It has the same lazy, deliberate drift as the dark strands in the water, and it is the being's breath, visible — the slow, patient, ongoing exhalation of a thing that is still becoming. The plume goes up into the sky. The plume stays. The plume is the being's way of saying: *I am still here. I am still becoming. The becoming is not finished. Go home. Come back later. Or do not come back. The being will be here either way.*

You climb out. The sound follows you, thinning. By the tree line, it is a hum. By the tree line, it is a memory of a hum. The wind returns. The ordinary mountain returns. At your back, the plume hangs over the peak, and does not move, and has the lazy drift of the dark strands, and something is up there, turning, and something is almost done.

You were inside something that was still becoming itself. You were inside something that knew you were there. You were inside something that paused, briefly, to consider you, and then returned to its work, and will finish whether or not you ever climb back up.

The being will finish soon. The throat is almost wide enough. The voice is almost loud enough. The face is almost a face. The dark at the center is almost ready to have a name.

*Sky-Mother* is the name.

The name is not yet earned. The name is what it is becoming.
