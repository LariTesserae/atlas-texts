What lives here does not, by most reckonings, live at all.

This is the first thing to get used to. The Hush-Plain does not draw clean lines around its creatures. It does not say: here is a plant, here is an animal, here is a stone, here is a thing that watches. It says, more quietly, and across a timescale no body can follow from one end to the other: *here is activity, ongoing, patient, you may give it a name if you need to. The name will not change it.* Begin with the smallest. The smallest is the only thing that can be held.

---

In the lacy whorls of the salt, where the pale mineral lifts from the grey stone in branching patterns like the cross-section of some long and complicated bone, there is motion. To see it, you must kneel. To see it clearly, you must put your face close — close as you would put your face to a sleeping stranger's chest, to listen for the breathing — and you must wait. The waiting here is a real verb. The waiting takes time. The plain is not in a hurry to show you.

When the light is right (and the light here is always right, in its cool honest way, neither favoring nor punishing), the salt seems to shimmer, but the shimmer is not the shimmer of reflected sun. It is the shimmer of something going on underneath. Look closer, and what you will see, if you see anything, is a slow traffic of small bright shapes moving along the whorls. They are not animals. They are not, exactly, crystals. They are something the plain made, or something the plain became, and they are older than the concept of vertebra.

They eat stone.

That is the right word, or near enough. They eat the grey stone and they pass it through themselves and they leave behind a slightly different stone — fractionally lighter, fractionally more brittle, fractionally more inclined to remember the sun for a few seconds after the sun has gone. They breathe the thin air, what little of it there is. They exhale a slightly different thin air. They have been doing this since before the salt was salt. They will be doing it when the salt is something else, and then something else again.

They do not have names. They do not need names. They are the visible surface of an activity that goes all the way down, the way the foam on a slow river is the visible surface of a current. If you watch them long enough, which most visitors do not, you may notice that their traffic is patterned. There are paths. The paths follow the whorls and occasionally cross them. The crossings are not random. There are, if you look for them, intersections, and at the intersections larger shapes gather, and the larger shapes are doing something that resembles — but only resembles — deliberation. You could call it consultation. You could call it digestion. You could call it, with some accuracy, *thinking*, if you remember that thinking here happens at the rate crystals grow.

These small things are the only place the activity pokes through. They are, as far as anyone has been able to determine, the only fauna of the Hush-Plain. They are not fauna. They are *animated minerals*. The plain called them into being, or allowed them to be called, and they have stayed because staying is what the plain does.

---

The plants are older than the stone, or about the same age as the stone, which is to say: older than the distinction. They are six species, perhaps fewer. No census has ever been necessary, and a census would be a strange thing to attempt on something that does not move under your eye.

They are grey-green or silvered or the deep color of a bruise that is half-healed. They do not reach up. They spread. Their root systems, when the rare specimen is excavated (and excavation is a kind of vandalism here, because the visible plant is only the smallest fraction of the plant), run for meters in dark branching fans that look more like the root structure of a small old tree than of a thing whose above-ground parts would fit in a cupped hand. The plants are mostly root. The visible parts are *announcements* — the part of the organism that acknowledges, briefly, that there is a sun, and that the sun has a color.

They flower sometimes. The flowers are small and pale and last a day, sometimes less, and they are pollinated as far as anyone can tell by wind and by accident. They are not in a hurry. They are roughly the same age as the stone, give or take a few thousand years, and they have come to an understanding with the plain that no treaty could formalize. The plain does not feed them. They feed the plain. They are net contributors of *something* — structure, patience, a certain stubborn orientation toward the light — and in return they are allowed to continue. This is not symbiosis. This is something that has forgotten how to be anything but itself.

The plants lean, slowly, in directions that change over centuries. This is one of the ways the plain is known to be turbulent. The lean is not random. It is correlated with what is happening below, in the slow rearrangements of the grey stone, in the patient traffic of the salt-fauna, in whatever the larger shapes at the intersections are deciding. The plants are antennae. The plants are witnesses. The plants, in their quiet and rooted way, are *paying attention*.

If you sit among them for long enough — and long enough, here, is measured differently than it is measured anywhere else — you may notice that they breathe too. Not the way you breathe. They breathe the way the plain breathes: in long slow cycles, taking in trace atmosphere and letting out trace atmosphere, and the breath of the plants is in rhythm with the breath of the stone. You will not hear it. You will see a leaf, if there is a leaf, lift a little, and lower, and lift again, and the lifting and lowering will be the same rate as the salt-shimmer in the whorls, and the same rate as the slow rotation of the constellations (those constellations that are still here), and you will understand, in some quiet part of yourself that does not need to be convinced, that you are sitting in the middle of a single large breath.

---

Then there is the thing that moves the small stones.

No one speaks of it. This is not because it is forbidden. It is because speaking of it requires a vocabulary that the Hush-Plain does not provide. The thing that moves the small stones does not have a shape you could agree upon with the next person, and it does not have a schedule, and it does not, as far as anyone can tell, have a body. It is a *displacement* that occurs overnight, near the places where travelers have set their camps.

In the morning, the stones will be different. A stone that was at the corner of your bedroll will be at the edge of the fire-pit. A stone that was upright will be lying down. A line of small pebbles you set out as a kind of nervous gesture, the way some travelers set out cairns, will have been rearranged into a different line, or a curve, or a small spiral. The rearrangements are not random. They are deliberate, in a way that is hard to demonstrate and impossible to argue with in daylight. They are also, when you really look at them, *not unkind*. The stones near your head are moved away from your head. The stones near your feet are left where they are. It is as if something came by in the night, very quietly, and *tidied* around you the way a careful hand tidies around a sleeping child.

The wind did not do it. The wind here does not move small stones. The wind here slides over them, changes the pressure, makes your ears pop, and goes. The mineral fauna in the salt whorls cannot have done it, because the stones are not part of the whorls and the fauna does not leave the whorls. The plants cannot have done it, because the plants do not move, and besides, the stones are sometimes moved into the middle of a plant's spread and the plant does not object.

Travelers do not mention it. They make a small note in their records, if they make records, and they go on. They do not set traps. They do not lie awake waiting for it. They understand, without being told, that this is not a thing one investigates. The plain, perhaps kindly, does not press the point either.

It is one creature, or many, or none. It has been doing this for as long as there have been travelers to come, which is not as long as the plain has been here, which means it may have been doing this for a long time before there were travelers, in which case the small stones were moved around the sleeping places of something else, something that came before. It moves slowly enough that you cannot catch it in the act. It is patient enough that it does not mind waiting another night, and another, and another.

It may be the same thing that built the markers. This will be discussed later.

---

The markers.

The vertical things — the slender posts, the held-breath towers, the structures that are not structures but *posited* — are scattered across the plain with the spacing of punctuation in a long sentence. They were made by someone about our size. The mortar in the low walls has bonded with the native stone at the interface so completely that, by now, it is not possible to say which is which. The whole plain is, in some sense, a ruin; the ruin and the original have ceased to be distinguishable.

What is less often remarked upon is that the markers *fall over*. All of them. Sometimes. In the same week, or so travelers who have been here long enough say. They fall over and then they stand up again, or they are replaced, or they were never really standing and we are only now noticing. The salt-fauna has been observed, on a few occasions, climbing the fallen markers and going about its business along the length of them, and the salt-fauna has also been observed, on a few occasions, climbing *up* the inside of the markers, as if the markers were hollow, as if the markers had insides, as if the markers were a kind of enormous *skeleton* that the small bright traffic was using as a highway.

Perhaps they were always this. Perhaps they were never buildings in the sense we mean. Perhaps they were, from the beginning, *structures in the body of something* — bones of a creature whose flesh had become the plain, whose blood had become the salt, whose breathing had become the wind. The builders may not have built them. The builders may have *grown* them, the way the plants grow their root systems, mostly out of sight, mostly for purposes we cannot divine from above.

The pictographs. The marks on stone that might be writing or decoration or the slow patient doodling of someone with a great deal of time and no one to talk to. They appear to have been made across a span so long that styles changed in the making. A single tradition lasted longer than the climate that produced it. This is not impossible. It is only very strange, and on this plain, *very strange* is the local weather.

---

The hush.

The hush may be a creature.

It is described in the records as *active suppression of noise*, as if the air itself has been told to be still. It is also described, by certain travelers at certain hours, as a faint high tone at the very edge of perception, more felt than heard. The disagreement has never been resolved. It is unclear whether the tone is real or whether the brain invents it because a place this old and this flat *should* have some sound, and refuses to tolerate complete emptiness.

The hush has been here longer than the markers, longer than the pictographs, longer than the travelers, longer than the small stones that get tidied at night. The hush is the breath of the plain itself. The plain is breathing out, all the time, very slowly, and what we call silence is the exhalation of a thing so large that its breathing is the weather. The wind, when it comes, is a *gasp* — a small intake of breath that does not match the weather of any above, because it is not above. It is internal. The plain has, occasionally, to breathe in.

It breathes out, mostly. It has been breathing out for a very long time. This is part of why the elevation has been *used up*. The plain has been exhaling its mountains for an age. It has exhaled them down to memory, and past memory, and what is left is this near-perfect flat. The flat is a kind of exhalation. The flat is what the plain has been saying, for a long time, and it has said what it needs to say, and now it is mostly just *being* the saying.

---

The watchers.

A sense, occasionally reported, of being watched by something that has no eyes and is not necessarily alive and has been watching for a very long time and is patient enough to watch for a very long time more.

This is not a figure. This is not a creature skulking behind a marker. This is the plain itself, perhaps, turned toward you in some way that does not require turning, attending to you in some way that does not require attention as we mean it. You are on the plain. The plain is aware of you. This is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is the plain doing what the plain does, which is *being present*, fully and without interruption, for as long as it has been, which is longer than you can imagine standing still for.

The watchers are not separate from the watched. You are watched because you are here. The plain does not distinguish between the watching and the place. They are the same activity. To stand on the plain is to be held by it in the same gesture that holds the small stones near the camp and moves them, gently, into a better arrangement. The plain is *curating* you. It is curating everything. It has been curating for a long time. It will continue curating after you are gone, and the small stones you left will be moved again, and the wind will slide over them, and the salt will breathe, and the plants will lean in the direction the leaning needs to go.

---

What lives here, then.

What lives here is what has *stayed*. The mineral fauna has stayed. The plants have stayed. The thing that moves the stones has stayed. The hush has stayed. The plain has stayed. The builders — whoever or whatever made the markers, the walls, the pictographs that change style across deep time — have stayed too, in a way. They have stayed so thoroughly that they are no longer separable from the place. They have been absorbed. They have become the salt, the stone, the slow rearrangements, the breath. They are what they made. They are what made them.

You may stand on this.

The plain will hold you. It will hold you the way it holds the small stones near your camp, with a care that does not announce itself. It will hold you the way it holds the plants, with a patience that does not require you to understand it. It will hold you the way it holds its own breath, with a long slow exhale that does not need to be answered.

You will not hear it let you stand. You will not see it. You will stand, and the plain will hold you, and that will be the whole conversation, and it will be enough.

The cool will not change. The light will be honest. The shadows will fall in directions you can use. Your shadow will have edges. The salt will have opinions about your tongue. The small stones near your bedroll will, in the morning, be somewhere else.

Something will be watching, with no eyes, with great patience, with no need of you to know what it is.

You will go on.

The plain will continue.
