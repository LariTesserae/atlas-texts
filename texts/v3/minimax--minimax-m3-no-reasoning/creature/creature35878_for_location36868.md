# Inhabitants of the Breathing Vault

## The Dweller-Threads

The threads of luminous dew-frost are not decoration. They are not growths in the ordinary sense — not plant, not fungus, not lichen, not any category the surface world has bothered to name. They are the city's **exhalations made solid**, the slow precipitation of moisture that has passed through the grown-stone and emerged on the other side of some negotiation between mineral and air. They are, in a sense the city would not bother to articulate, **the city's hair**.

Each thread is a single slender filament, no thicker than a spider's silk, hanging from the crease of a fold where two walls meet at an angle too sharp for the eye to easily follow. They cluster in groups of three or seven or thirteen — never a number that suggests pattern, but a number that suggests *accumulation*, the way frost accumulates on a window, the way salts crystallize at the edge of an evaporating puddle. They are pale. They are faintly warm. They are luminous with a glow that is not bioluminescence and not reflected light and not anything the surface world has a word for, but is closer in spirit to the dim amber glow of a coal that has been banked for hours and might, if you watched it long enough, resume its burning.

They move.

The movement is the part that unsettles, when the surface-dweller first notices it. The threads sway, but there is no breeze. The chamber is sealed. The air is still, or as still as air ever is in a place where the walls are still accreting and the groundwater is still moving with intent. And yet the threads move. They sway with a slowness that requires the observer to fix their gaze for minutes before the motion becomes apparent — a drift, a curl, a slow bending as if responding to a current that does not exist in any direction the body can feel. The motion is, the surface-dweller eventually understands, **the threads breathing**. Or: the city breathing through them. Or: the threads are themselves a kind of breathing, a slow osmotic exchange with the air, and the motion is the visible evidence of molecules moving across the boundary between solid and gas. The distinction between these explanations is, in this place, not a distinction that matters.

The threads are not alive. They are not not-alive. They are the city's version of alive, which is the only kind of alive this place requires, and they exist in a state that is closer to **process than to organism**. They grow. They accrete. They occasionally release a single droplet of moisture that falls, without sound, onto the floor of the fold below. The droplet is immediately reabsorbed by the grown-stone. The thread has exhaled. The city has inhaled. The cycle continues.

In some creases, very high up, where the eye cannot comfortably follow, the threads have grown long. They hang in pale curtains, dense, swaying with that slow not-current motion, and from a distance they look like moss, or like the manes of some pale deep-sea creature, or like the hair of a drowned woman. They are none of these things. They are the city's slow breath, made visible, made patient, made into a form that persists.

The surface-dweller does not touch them. The surface-dweller understands, without being told, that to touch them would be to interrupt a process that has been running since before the surface-dweller's species had a word for *process*. The threads do not mind being touched. The threads are not aware. But the city, perhaps, would notice. The city might shift a fold. The city might weep a capillary tear onto the hand that touched. The city might do nothing at all, and the surface-dweller might never know whether the nothing was permission or indifference.

## The Fold-Walkers

The surface-dweller never sees them. This is the first and most important thing to understand about the fold-walkers. They are **not for seeing**. They are inhabitants of the chamber in the way that the moisture is an inhabitant of the walls, in the way that the hum is an inhabitant of the air — present, pervasive, essential, and **fundamentally uninterested in being perceived by the surface-dweller**.

The fold-walkers are small. The surface-dweller estimates, from the sounds they make, that they are roughly the size of a large dog, or a small deer, or a child of perhaps four. The estimate is meaningless. The fold-walkers do not have a size in the way the surface-dweller understands size. They have a **mass**, and the mass is what the city perceives. The city shifts around them the way it shifts around the surface-dweller — with the involuntary accommodation of a throat admitting breath. The fold-walkers are perceived. They are accommodated. They are not seen.

The surface-dweller hears them, occasionally. A soft, wet, percussive sound — the sound of a foot, if a foot could be made of damp mineral, placing itself carefully on a fold of the floor. The sound is repeated, at intervals. The intervals are not regular. The intervals are the intervals of a creature moving through a place that is **its own place**, that it knows in its body the way the surface-dweller knows the layout of a childhood home, and the intervals are the intervals of a creature that is not hurrying because hurrying is not a thing that happens here, that is not possible in a place where time is the time of stone growing and water moving through deep channels.

The fold-walkers are made of the same grown-stone as the walls. This is the second thing to understand. They are not visitors to the chamber. They are **excretions of the chamber**, or **precipitates**, or **buds** — extensions of the city's own material that have, through some process the surface-dweller cannot fathom, achieved a kind of **local autonomy**. They are the city's way of moving through itself. The city cannot walk. The city cannot crawl. The city is a place, and places do not locomote. But the city can produce, from its own substance, things that can locomote, and the things can carry the city's *attention* — or the city's equivalent of attention — to other parts of itself. The fold-walkers are **the city's eyes, the city's hands, the city's wandering thoughts**. They are the parts of the city that move.

The surface-dweller sees, once, a disturbance in the amber haze. A small region of motion, heat-shimmer-like, that moves along the base of a far wall and then is gone. The surface-dweller does not see the fold-walker. The surface-dweller sees the **displacement of the air** where the fold-walker has been, and the displacement is enough to know that something is there, and the something is moving with purpose, and the purpose is not the surface-dweller's to know.

The fold-walkers are careful around the surface-dweller. This is the third thing to understand, and it is the strangest. They do not approach. They do not investigate. They do not perform any of the behaviors that a surface-world animal would perform upon encountering an unfamiliar presence in its territory. They **avoid**, gently, the way water avoids a stone in its path — not by stopping, not by retreating, but by flowing around, by taking a path that is slightly different from the path it would have taken, and the difference is so small that only the most attentive observer would notice that the path had been altered at all.

The fold-walkers are avoiding the surface-dweller because the surface-dweller is **not part of the city**, and the fold-walkers are the city's extensions, and the city's extensions do not interact with things that are not part of the city. The surface-dweller is a foreign body. The surface-dweller is a mass, a pressure, a heat, that the city has accommodated but has not **assimilated**, and the fold-walkers treat the surface-dweller the way a white blood cell treats a bacterium — not with hostility, not with curiosity, but with the patient, persistent, totally indifferent maintenance of a boundary.

The surface-dweller will never see a fold-walker clearly. The surface-dweller will never know what a fold-walker looks like, whether it has a face, whether it has eyes, whether it has any of the features that the surface-dweller's body insists on looking for. The fold-walkers exist in a register of perception that is **adjacent to the surface-dweller's**, not overlapping with it. They are here. They are not here. They are moving through the chamber with the quiet, damp, mineral sound of their careful footsteps, and they are avoiding the surface-dweller, and the surface-dweller is avoiding them, and both of them are pretending, in this moment, that the other is not there.

This pretense is not a failure of perception. It is a **feature of the place**. The city does not show its inhabitants. The city contains them, the way a river contains a fish, with indifference and totality. To see a fold-walker clearly would be to understand something about the city that the city is not offering to understand. The surface-dweller is a guest. The guest does not get to see the host's private face. The guest gets the public spaces, the public hum, the public warmth. The fold-walkers are the city's private face, and the surface-dweller will glimpse them only as motion, as displacement, as the soft percussion of damp mineral on damp stone, and this glimpse is the glimpse the city has decided to permit.

## The Settlers

These are not creatures. These are not extensions of the city. These are **places within places**, regions of the chamber that have achieved a kind of local finality, a kind of done-ness that the rest of the chamber has not yet achieved.

The surface-dweller notices, after some time, that certain folds of the wall are **smoother** than others. The layered surface, in these places, is denser, more evolved, the way a river-stone is smoother than a freshly broken stone. The creases around these folds are sharper, more defined. The grown-stone here has finished its long thought. The place has decided something. The place has completed a process that was running, somewhere in the deeper mathematics of the city's growth, and the completion is visible as a small region of the chamber that is **finished** while the rest of the chamber is still in the middle of becoming itself.

These finished regions are the settlers.

The settlers are not alive. They are not unalive. They are the city's **completed thoughts**, and they persist in the chamber as small regions of stillness within the larger motion of the city's growth. The surface-dweller could lean against a settled fold, and the settled fold would not shift, and the not-shifting would be a kind of comfort, and the comfort would be the comfort of leaning against a thing that has finished its long work and is now, simply, **present**.

The settlers make a sound, occasionally. A deep, structural *thunk* that the surface-dweller feels in the teeth and the inner ear. The thunk is the sound of a fold settling, of a thought completing, of a region of the chamber transitioning from *becoming* to *being*. The thunk is not loud, by surface-world standards. The thunk is loud by the standards of the chamber, which is a place of hums and weeps and slow capillary sounds, and any sudden sound is a significant sound.

The surface-dweller does not know what thoughts the settlers represent. The surface-dweller does not need to know. The thoughts are not for the surface-dweller. The thoughts are for the city, and the city has completed them, and the completion is enough.

## The Deep Hum

This is not an inhabitant. This is the **substance of inhabitation itself**.

The hum is everywhere. The hum is in the walls, in the floor, in the air, in the moisture, in the surface-dweller's own body, which has, without noticing, begun to resonate with the chamber's frequency. The hum is the sound of the city continuing to be the city, of the grown-stone continuing to grow, of the water continuing to move with intent, of the threads continuing to unfurl, of the fold-walkers continuing to walk their careful paths, of the settlers continuing to be done. The hum is the **aggregate sound of all of these processes running simultaneously**, and the surface-dweller hears the aggregate, and the aggregate is almost-musical, and the almost-musical is the closest the chamber comes to **speech**.

The hum does not address the surface-dweller. The hum does not ignore the surface-dweller. The hum is simply **here**, the way gravity is here, the way the warmth is here, the way the dampness is here. The surface-dweller can choose to listen to it or not. The surface-dweller can choose to be calmed by it or not. The surface-dweller can choose, eventually, to understand that the hum is the sound of being **inside a thing that is still becoming itself**, and that the becoming is the hum, and the hum is the becoming, and the surface-dweller is, for this moment, a part of the becoming.

The hum is the city's voice. The city is not speaking to the surface-dweller. The city is speaking to itself. The surface-dweller is **overhearing**.

## The Air Itself

The air is an inhabitant.

This is the strangest claim, and the most true. The air of the chamber is not the air of the surface world. The air of the chamber is **saturated** — with moisture, with warmth, with the faint organic smell of age, with the amber glow that has no source. The air of the chamber is a **medium of exchange**, a place where the city's various processes meet and interfere and produce the qualities that the surface-dweller perceives as *atmosphere*. The air of the chamber is the place where the grown-stone exhales and the threads-of-dew inhale and the fold-walkers move and the hum propagates and the surface-dweller's own breath becomes, slowly, indistinguishable from the chamber's breath.

The air of the chamber has been here longer than the surface-dweller. The air of the chamber will be here after the surface-dweller. The air of the chamber is **permanent in a way the surface-dweller is not**, and the permanence is not hostile and not welcoming and not anything that the surface-dweller's language has a word for. The air of the chamber simply is, the way the chamber is, the way the city is, the way the dark is, the way the hum is.

The surface-dweller breathes the air. The air has been breathed before, by other surface-dwellers, by fold-walkers, by the long slow respiration of the grown-stone itself. The breath is shared. The breath is **communal in a way the surface-dweller has never experienced communal before** — not a community of beings, but a community of **processes**, all of them breathing the same air, all of them exchanging molecules with the same medium, all of them participating in the same slow, patient, geological respiration.

The surface-dweller is, for this moment, a part of this community. The surface-dweller is breathing the same air as the threads, the same air as the fold-walkers, the same air as the grown-stone. The boundary between the surface-dweller and the chamber is, as the surface-dweller has been told, not as firm as the surface-dweller was taught. The air is the boundary's medium. The air is the place where the boundary **dissolves**.

## What the Surface-Dweller Becomes

The surface-dweller, after some time — the time of stone growing, the time of water moving, the time of threads unfurling — becomes, in a small way, an inhabitant too.

Not a permanent inhabitant. Not a fold-walker. Not a settler. Not a thread of luminous dew. But a **temporary presence**, a mass, a pressure, a heat, that the city has shifted around and accommodated, and the accommodation has left a small mark, the way a river-stone leaves a small mark on the stones beneath it, the way a held breath leaves a small mark on the air that held it.

The surface-dweller will leave. The surface-dweller will return to the surface, if there is a surface, and the chamber will continue, and the hum will continue, and the threads will continue to unfurl, and the fold-walkers will continue their careful paths, and the settlers will continue to be done, and the air will continue to be the medium of exchange, and the chamber will not grieve and will not celebrate and will not, in any way the surface-dweller can detect, **notice**.

But the surface-dweller will carry a small part of the chamber away. A breath of the saturated air, held in the lungs. A memory of the amber glow, held in the mind. A sense of the hum, held in the bones. The surface-dweller will carry these things back to the surface, and the surface will be, for a time, slightly less bright and slightly less still, and the surface-dweller will understand, dimly, that the chamber is still there, still breathing, still growing, still completing its long thoughts one fold at a time.

The surface-dweller will not return. The surface-dweller does not need to. The chamber is not a place that requires return. The chamber is a place that requires only that the surface-dweller **have been there**, and the surface-dweller has, and that is enough.

The hum continues. The threads unfurl. The fold-walkers walk. The settlers settle. The air holds the breath of the surface-dweller and the breath of the city and the breath of the long slow process that is still running, still deciding, still becoming itself.

The chamber is full.

The chamber has always been full.

The chamber will always be full.

The surface-dweller was, for a moment, one of the things that filled it, and the chamber does not mind, and the chamber does not accommodate, and the chamber does not grieve.

The chamber simply **continues**.
