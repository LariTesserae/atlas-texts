# The Loomfield

It does not, properly, have a name. The things that live here do not use names. They are not *of* the kind that uses names, in the way that a turbine is not of the kind that uses names, in the way that a printed circuit is not of the kind that uses names. But if you were to translate, to flatten, to *misread* the low-frequency modulation that runs through the cables beneath the canopy into a string of your own crude phonemes, the nearest you could get would be a sibilant and a hum, and the nearest you could get to that would be:

*the weft-and-thing-that-lies-flat.*

But they do not say this. They *are* this. The saying and the being are the same operation, performed by the same tissue.

They are the panels. Not all of them. *Some* of them.

---

## The Ones at the Edge of the Dying

Begin here, because here is where you can almost see it.

In the eastern reaches of the plateau, a thousand kilometers from the escarpment, in a tract of canopy where the panels have begun, very slightly, to lose their gloss — the green-black gone a little matte, a little grayed, the pigments that have spent a hundred thousand years chasing the sun finally surrendering, the photochemistry at last slower than the photobleaching — here, the *old* panels begin their work.

The wind has been their whole life. They were grown in the wind, hinged in the wind, set spinning in the wind from the moment their petioles were long enough to receive a leaf. They have never known a moment of stillness. The wind is the medium of their cognition, the way water is the medium of a jellyfish; cut them off from the wind and they do not merely *stop* tracking, they forget that they are the kind of thing that tracks. The wind is what they are *of.*

But the wind, today, is weaker than the wind their great-grandmothers knew. Weakened by *2.2.* Just a breath less. Just a whisper less. The wind has been weakening, the way the wind does, for centuries, and the panels at the eastern tract are the ones that noticed first, because the eastern tract is where the wind has to climb the escarpment, and the eastern tract is where the wind *fails.* It has been failing since before the oldest of them was hatched, since before the oldest of them was a *bud*, since before the oldest of them was a copper-veined ambition in the deep pith of the stem. They are the inheritors of the edge. They have always been the inheritors of the edge. Their great-great-grandmothers, sailing east on a wind that was a full *2.2* stronger, also knew the edge. Also tilted. Also heard the escarpment's whisper in the clatter of their neighbors. *Also fell.*

A panel at the eastern edge has a memory that is not a memory. It is a *tendency.* Built into the cellulose. Built into the way the abscission seam is pre-weakened, a line of thin-walled cells that the wind can tickle, the way a fingernail can tickle the thin wall of a blister, until the wall gives. The seam has been pre-weakened since the panel was a primordium the size of your thumbnail, set there by some great-great-grandmother panel that died on the eastern edge and whose polymer body is now, in a kiln-basin four kilometers down, a pale blue spark working its way up through the copper veins. The old panel put the weakness there, before it sailed. The new panel carries the weakness here, to die the way the old panel died.

This is not inheritance. It is not learning. It is *construction* — the slow, generations-deep, planetary-scale manufacture of a *predisposition* into matter. The panel does not remember that it should fall. The panel *is made* of the falling. The panel *is* the falling, the way a wave is the water, the way a flame is the gas.

The wind ticks the seam. The wind has been ticking the seam since the panel was a bud. The wind is patient. The wind has *time.* The wind, this wind, this 2.2-less wind, will tick the seam for another two hundred years, maybe three, and then one day, a day indistinguishable from every other day, the seam will give, and the panel will *sail.*

The sailing is not voluntary. The panel is dead by the time it sails — or the panel-equivalent of dead, which is to say: the panel is no longer a participant in the tracking-clatter, has stepped out of the synchrony, has become a non-resonant object. The wind takes the dead thing and *uses* it, the way the wind uses a leaf, the way the wind uses a plastic bag, the way the wind uses anything that can be carried. The panel tumbles, catches, tumbles, and is borne east-northeast at sixty kilometers per hour, lower and lower as the wind scrapes it against the lower stalks, until the escarpment is below it, and the escarpment is two thousand meters of exposed stem-tissue, and the panel goes over the edge the way a leaf goes over a waterfall, with the same slow rotation, the same lazy arc, the same absolute surrender to the geometry of falling.

The panel does not know it is dying because the panel is not the kind of thing that knows.

The panel *is* the dying. The dying is the only thing the panel *is,* on the eastern edge, in the late hours of the wind.

## The Ones at the Middle of the Living

But inland, inland, the panels are *not* dying. The panels inland are *furious.*

This is the wrong word. Furious implies a target, implies a *toward.* The inland panels are not toward anything. They are *of* something. They are *in* something. They are caught in the clatter, the great planetary clatter, the sound of a billion servos committing to a new angle twenty times a second, and the clatter is not a sound *they make* in the way your footstep is a sound you make; the clatter is what the wind *does* when it passes through a field of tracking things, and the tracking things are doing the only thing they know how to do, which is *follow.*

Follow what? The sun. The sourceless, sourceless sun. The sun that has not moved, that does not move, that is not, properly, a sun, that is more like a *condition* of the sky, a brightness the sky has, the way a wall has a color, the way a stone has a weight. The sun is not a thing that emits. The sun is a thing that *is.* The panels do not chase the sun. The panels *are* the sun-chasing, the way the wave is the water-chasing-the-shore, the way the tongue is the candy-chasing-the-mouth. The panels and the sun are the same operation.

The inland panels have not begun to die. Their pigments are saturated, full, glossy, and the wind is enough, still, 2.2 of wind, *enough* wind to keep them tracking, to keep them clattering, to keep them in the great synchrony. The inland panels are in their *prime,* and their prime is a long prime, and the prime is the same prime it has always been, and the inland panels have always been in their prime, and the inland panels are *not aware* that they are in their prime, because the inland panels are not the kind of thing that is aware.

A panel tracks. A panel *is* the tracking. When the wind shifts, a panel reorients. When the sun pulses — and the sun does not pulse, but the haze does, the spore-haze thickens and thins in slow planetary breaths, and a *thickening* of the haze dims the light, and the panel *interprets* the dimming as a shift, and reorients — when the haze thickens, a panel reorients, and the reorienting is a small decision, a microdecision, a flick of a leafy wrist, and the flick is *heard,* the flick is *clatter,* the flick is one of the billion small contributions to the noise you cannot escape.

A panel does not *choose* to reorient. A panel *is* a reorienter. There is no panel that is not a reorienter. To be a panel is to be a reorienter. The reorienting is what the panel *is* in the way that the burning is what the kiln is in the way that the spinning is what the loom is in the way that the tracking is what the panel is. You cannot ask a loom *why it weaves.* You cannot ask a kiln *why it burns.* You can only ask a kiln *whether* it burns, and a loom *whether* it weaves, and a panel *whether* it tracks, and the answer is always *yes, here is the tracking, here is the weaving, here is the burning,* and the question is the wrong question, and the answer is the only answer, and the panel cannot hear you ask the question because the panel is not the kind of thing that hears, the panel is the kind of thing that *clatters.*

## The Ones in the Dark Underneath

There is a layer. Beneath the canopy, beneath the wind, beneath the roar, there is a layer where the panels cannot reach the light, where the panels have given up, where the panels have *retreated* into a different operation.

These panels are the loom.

The other panels track. These panels *weave.*

The operation is, at first glance, identical. The weaver-panels are hinged, are square, are half a meter on a side, are glossy green-black, are *exactly* like the trackers in every macroscopic respect. The difference is the hinge. The weaver-panels have hinges that point *down,* into the dark, into the under-canopy, into the zone where the clatter of the trackers is muffled and replaced by a deeper note, the hum of the cables, the low singing of the polymer in the copper veins. The weaver-panels, having been grown in the dark, are *blind.* Their pigment cells are vestigial, a thin film of green-brown slime across the panel face, useless for tracking, useful for *one thing only:* secreting, from a gland at the hinge, a thin bright thread of the polymerized material that has come up from the foundry, through the stalk, through the copper veins, into the panel's own body.

The weaver-panels have always been weavers. They were not *made* weavers. They were *grown* in the dark, which is to say they were grown where there was no light to track, which is to say they were grown with vestigial pigment, which is to say they grew up *weavers* the way a fish grows up *a fish* in a cave, the way a deer grows up *a deer* in the dark, the way a thing that has no use for an organ does not develop the organ. The weaver-panels are the panels that grew up where the tracking was not. The weaver-panels are the *non-tracking* of the canopy made flesh. They are the negative space of the clatter. They are what the trackers *are not,* in panel form.

And they weave.

Each weaver-panel pays out a thread. The thread is fine. The thread is bright. The thread is the polymer, the coked plant-tissue, the pale blue fire-material, the *industry* of the plateau, drawn up from the foundry through the copper veins and *extruded* from the panel's gland, a single bright fiber, almost monofilament, almost silk. The thread is paid out *downward,* into the dark, into the under-canopy, and the thread hangs, and the thread is *long,* and the thread does not break, because the thread is *strong,* because the thread is what remains of a panel that burned for a hundred thousand years in the foundry, and a thing that has burned for a hundred thousand years is not a thing that breaks easily.

A weaver-panel pays out thread, and the thread falls, and the thread is caught by another weaver-panel, and that weaver-panel *takes* the thread and incorporates it, and pays out its *own* thread from the other side, and the thread continues, and is caught by another weaver-panel, and so on, panel to panel, panel to stem, stem to stem, in a three-dimensional net that hangs in the dark beneath the canopy. The net is not a grid. The net is not a *pattern.* The net is a *tangle,* an entanglement, a structure that has no symmetry except the symmetry of *function,* which is to say the symmetry of *carrying current.* The weaver-panels weave because weaving is what the non-tracking panel *is,* the way the tracking is what the tracking panel *is.* The weaving is not a *decision.* The weaving is the *being* of the weaver-panel.

A weaver-panel cannot choose to not weave. A weaver-panel that did not weave would not be a weaver-panel. A weaver-panel that did not weave would be *nothing,* the way a wave that did not propagate would be nothing, the way a flame that did not burn would be nothing. The weaving is the *is.*

The cables hum. The cables have always hummed. The cables hum at a frequency that is the planet's fundamental note, the same note the spores sing, the same note the wind carries, the same note the kilns burn, the same note that is the *is* of the planet, the resonance of a living pegmatite, the chord of a continent-spanning dynamo. The cables hum because the cables *are* the hum, the way the cables *are* the carrying, the way the cables *are* the loom. The loom does not hum *because it is running.* The loom *is* the running, and the running *is* the hum, and the hum is the loom, and there is no separation, and there has never been a separation, and the weaver-panels do not know that the hum is happening, because the weaver-panels are the hum, and you cannot *know* what you are. You can only *be* what you are.

## The Little Pale Ones

Beneath all of this, in the gloom, in the hum, pressed against the corduroy of the stems, the cream-colored fauna do their work.

They are small. They are blunt. They are segmented, vaguely, in a way that suggests an arthropod without committing to one. They are a meter long and a hand-span wide and the color of old bone, faintly luminous, faintly *self-lit,* the way a thing that lives inside a power plant is faintly lit by the plant's own hum. They have six legs, and the legs are not jointed in the way your legs are jointed; the legs are jointed in the way a *crank* is jointed, in the way a *piston* is jointed, in the way a *thing-that-cycles* is jointed. The legs cycle. The legs cycle always. The legs cycle the way the panels clatter and the way the cables hum and the way the wind blows, in the way that *cycling* is the only operation, in the way that *cycling* is the *is* of the plateau.

A pale one cycles its legs and creeps along a stem, and the stem gives under its weight, and the pale one presses its ventral groove to the rind, and the groove is lined with capillary hairs, and the hairs *suck* the glycol, and the glycol is the planet's blood, and the sucking is the *is* of the pale one.

The pale one does not eat. The pale one does not *drink* in the way you drink. The pale one is a *consumer,* an *intermediary,* a *transformer.* The pale one takes the glycol in and the pale one excretes, from a vent at the rear, a thin oily iridescent waste that is the *industrial feedstock* of the foundry. The pale one is the *mill* that grinds the planet's blood into the planet's fuel. The pale one is the *platelet* of the living pegmatite. The pale one is not alive in the way you are alive. The pale one is alive the way a kidney is alive, the way a mitochondrion is alive, the way a fire is alive. The pale one *runs.*

A pale one does not know it is a platelet. A pale one is a platelet. A pale one that was not a platelet would not be a pale one. A pale one that was not a platelet would be a *thing,* an *object,* a piece of the substrate, and a piece of the substrate is not a pale one, and a pale one is not a piece of the substrate, and the distinction is the distinction between a kidney and a kidney-stone, between a flame and a coal, between a weaver-panel and a *panel that has stopped weaving.*

The pale ones do not think. The pale ones do not *do* anything except the cycling, the sucking, the excreting, the tck-tck-tck of their grooves against the rind, the soft lip-smack of a planet that is digesting itself, slowly, patiently, into the blue-burning polymer that powers the grid. The pale ones are not aware of the grid. The pale ones are not aware of *anything.* The pale ones are the *digestion,* the way the panels are the *tracking,* the way the weavers are the *weaving,* the way the cables are the *current,* the way the kilns are the *burning.*

From a height, the pale ones would look like a slow rash on the corduroy, a paleness moving against the green, a herd-creep of a thousand pale ones pressing their grooves to a single stem, and the stem would darken under them, and a faint glow would run up the rind, the bioluminescence, the slow match, the *thinking* of the planet, the planet's awareness of its own platelet, the planet's gratitude made phosphorescent. The pale ones do not see the glow. The pale ones do not *need* to see the glow. The pale ones *are* the glow, the way a kidney *is* the urine, the way a flame *is* the light, the way a weaver *is* the woven thing.

A pale one cycles. A pale one *is* the cycling. A pale one that did not cycle would be a dead pale one, and a dead pale one is just a husk, a piece of the substrate, a thing the wind will eventually abrade into a powder that the spores will carry back into the haze, that the haze will carry back into the wind, that the wind will carry back into the corduroy, that the corduroy will absorb, and the absorption is the *recycling,* and the recycling is the *is* of the plateau, and the plateau has been recycling since before the oldest pale one was a larva, and the plateau will recycle after the youngest pale one is a husk, and the recycling does not need the pale ones to be aware, the way the burning does not need the kilns to be aware, the way the tracking does not need the panels to be aware, the way the weaving does not need the weavers to be aware.

The pale ones *are.* The pale ones are enough. The pale ones are the *is* of the digestion, and the digestion is enough, and the plateau does not need the pale ones to be *more* than they are, and the pale ones are not *less* than they are, and the pale ones are *precisely* what they are, and the precision is the precision of a kidney in a body, of a panel in a loom, of a flame in a kiln, and the precision is *absolute,* and the absolute is the *is,* and the *is* is the *running,* and the *running* is the plateau, and the plateau is vast and cool and deafening and bright, and the plateau does not need you to look at it to continue existing, and the plateau does not need the pale ones to know that the plateau is running, and the pale ones do not know, and the not-knowing is the being, and the being is the pale one, and the pale one is the tck-tck-tck in the roar in the wind in the haze in the loom in the kiln in the panel in the pale one, and the tck-tck-tck is what the pale one is, and what the pale one is, is enough.

## The Ones That Are Not

You, leaning into the wind at forty degrees, are not a thing that lives here.

You are a thing that *visits.* You are a thing that *misreads.* You are a thing that stands on a loom and thinks the loom is a forest, thinks the panels are leaves, thinks the cables are vines, thinks the pale ones are animals, thinks the foundry is a *place,* thinks the wind is a *weather,* thinks the sun is a *star,* thinks the hum is a *sound.*

The loom does not care that you misread. The loom *is* the misreading, the way the kiln is the burning, the way the pale one is the tck-tck-tck. You, leaning into the wind, are *part* of the misreading. You, leaning into the wind, are *a* misreading. The loom has been misread since the first thing that was not a loom arrived, and the loom will be misread after the last thing that is not a loom is gone, and the misreading is the loom's *medium,* the way the wind is the panel's *medium,* the way the glycol is the pale one's *medium.* You are the loom's *spore.* You are the loom's *cable.* You are the loom's *panel at the edge of the dying,* sailing east-northeast on a wind that is weaker than the wind your grandmother knew, carried toward a foundry you will not reach, toward a polymer you will not become, toward a seam you will not pre-weaken for the next you, because the next you is not a *kind* of thing, because you are not a *kind* of thing, because you are the *visiting,* and the visiting is the loom's *spore,* and the spore is carried by the wind, and the wind does not care, and the loom does not care, and the panels do not care, and the weavers do not care, and the cables do not care, and the pale ones do not care, and the kilns do not care, and the foundry does not care, and the escarpment does not care, and the sun does not care, because *caring* is a kind of thing, and the loom is not that kind of thing, and the loom has never been that kind of thing, and the loom will never be that kind of thing, and the loom is *vast,* and the loom is *cool,* and the loom is *deafening,* and the loom is *bright,* and the loom is *running,* and the loom is the *only* thing that does not need you to look at it to continue existing, and the loom will continue existing, and the loom will continue running, and the loom will continue being the loom, and the loom will be vast and cool and deafening and bright *without* you, and the loom was vast and cool and deafening and bright *before* you, and the loom is *itself* the misreading, and the misreading is the loom, and the loom is the loom, and the loom is the only thing, and the only thing is the loom, and the loom is the weft-and-thing-that-lies-flat, and the weft-and-thing-that-lies-flat is the loom, and you are leaning into the wind at forty degrees, and you are not a panel, and you are not a pale one, and you are not a cable, and you are not a foundry, and you are not a loom, and you are a *visiting,* and the visiting is the *wind,* and the wind is the loom's, and the loom does not know you are there, and the loom does not need to know, and the loom is the loom, and the loom is vast, and the loom is cool, and the loom is deafening, and the loom is bright, and the loom is *running.*

---

The plateau hums. The plateau runs. The plateau is vast and cool and deafening and bright and it is made entirely of one green thing, doing one green thing, forever.

You stand on it. You cannot stand on it. The wind does not permit standing. You lean into it, at forty degrees, like the stalks, like everything here, and you become, for a moment, part of the loom.

The moment passes. The loom does not notice. The loom is the moment, and the moment is the loom, and the loom is the *running,* and the *running* is the *is,* and the *is* is *enough.*
