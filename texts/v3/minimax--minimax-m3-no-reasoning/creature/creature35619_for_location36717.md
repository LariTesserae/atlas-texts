# What Lives in the Sodium Mire

It does not eat. It does not breathe. It does not die.

It *refines*.

That is the wrong word, and I am going to use it anyway, because every other word I try bends in the middle and refuses to mean what I need it to mean. *Lives* bends. *Works* bends. *Exists* bends, the way the shadows in the dunes bend, casting their weight in directions that do not correspond to the light. But *refines* — *refines* at least gestures at the thing, even if it lies about the rest. It does not refine because refining is what factories do to ore. It refines because refining is what tides do to sand. It refines because refining is what the inside of a star does to itself, slowly, and what a stalactite does to the water that built it, slowly, and what a thought does to the brain that thought it, slowly, until the thought and the brain are the same substance and neither remembers being anything else.

---

## The small ones.

You will see them first. If you come here, you will see them first, because they are the ones that come closest to the surface, the ones that move where you can watch them, the ones whose clicking you will hear before you understand that the clicking is not incidental. The clicking is their job. The clicking is *language*. I do not know what language. I do not think language is the right word either, but I am running out of words that do not bend, and so: language.

They are the size of crabs. They are the size of rats. They are the size of dogs, rarely, and the size of children, very rarely, and the size of nothing at all, most often, because the ones you see are the ones that have come up to do work, and the work is small, and a body the size of a crab is a body sized correctly for the work. The work is *carrying*. The work is *fetching*. The work is the endless patient shuttling of one substance to another place, where another substance receives it, where another substance does something to it, where it emerges changed, and is then carried again.

I watched a column of them for what I think was an hour. I do not trust the hour. I do not trust the time I spent here, because the time spent here did not feel like time, it felt like a layer. I felt like I was being added to something, slowly, the way sediment is added to the bottom of a sea that does not know it is a sea. But I watched them, and what I saw was this: each one carried a fragment. Each one carried the same fragment, or carried a fragment that looked the same to me, which is not the same thing, which is something I will come back to. Each one moved at the same pace. Each one moved with the same click-step, click-step, click-step, and the click was a part of the great roar the way a single voice is a part of a chorus that has been singing since before voices were invented.

Their bodies are sodium. This is the thing I have to make you understand, because it is the only thing about them that is not strange. Their bodies are sodium, which is to say their bodies are the place. They are made of the crust they walk on. They are made of the dunes they hide in. They are made of the brine that pools beneath the crust and around their joints and inside the hollows of their small translucent bodies. When they die — and I have to use the word *die*, even though I do not believe it — when they die, they do not rot. They *deposit*. They become part of the crust. They become part of a dune. They become a small white ridge in a landscape of small white ridges, indistinguishable from the mineral they were made of, indistinguishable from the mineral they are still, after the ceasing of their particular pattern of motion.

A heron is not a fish. A heron is a fish-shaped absence in the river, a temporary local arrangement of water and attention that has decided, for a while, to take the shape of a heron. A heron dies and the river forgets the shape, and the water goes back to being water. The small things in the Mire die and the Mire does not forget the shape, because the Mire *is* the shape, the Mire is shapes all the way down, the Mire is a place where the distinction between the animal and the substrate has been *refined* out of existence.

I am circling the thing. I will land on it. What lives here is what everything lives in. The small ones are the Mire that has learned to walk. The small ones are the Mire that has folded a piece of itself into a configuration that moves toward other pieces of itself, and folds them in, and carries them, and deposits them somewhere new. The small ones are not animals *in* an environment. They are *patterns* in a process, the way a wave is a pattern in the ocean, the way a thought is a pattern in a brain, the way a flame is a pattern in the air.

---

## The larger ones.

I did not see them. I will tell you what I saw.

I saw the crust *move* in places where nothing was walking on it. I saw the crust rise, slowly, in a low hump that traveled across the surface the way a slow wave travels across a membrane that has been struck, somewhere far away, by something heavy. I saw the brine beneath the crust shift, and the shifting was not random, and the shifting was not the shifting of a liquid responding to pressure, and the shifting was not the shifting of anything I have a name for. The shifting was *intentional*. The shifting was the shifting of a thing that had a place to be and was on its way to being there.

I saw shapes in the pools. The pools glow, faintly, intermittently, the color of a match struck in a room where no one is going to notice the match. The shapes moved under the glow. The shapes turned. The shapes regarded me with the slow consideration of a thing that has all the time that time contains and has been asked, by my presence, to spend some of that time on the question of whether I am worth attending to.

I was not worth attending to. The shapes turned away. The shapes went back to what they were doing, which I could not see, which was happening far below the surface of the pool, in the deep brine, in the places where the light does not reach and the crust does not float and the small ones do not go. I do not know what the shapes were doing. I think the shapes were *the doing*. I think the shapes were the deep process itself, surfacing, briefly, the way a whale surfaces, the way a thought surfaces, the way a tumor surfaces, and what the shapes were doing was the thing the whole Mire is for, which I will come to, which I am not ready to come to, which I will come to.

The larger ones are not larger. The larger ones are the *same*, the way a river is the same as a current, the way a hurricane is the same as a breeze, the way a symphony is the same as a note. The small ones are a single cell of a body that does not have a name, and the larger ones are the body, and the body is the Mire, and the Mire is the body, and what I was seeing, in the pools, in the deep brine, in the moving crust, was the body's slow digestion of itself and the world's slow digestion into it.

---

## The ones in the sky.

I do not know what they are. I will not pretend.

I saw them. They were dark. They moved against the dim sourceless light of the sky, which is to say they moved against the place where the light was, which is not the same as moving against a sky, because the sky here is not a sky, the sky here is the *exhaust* of the process, the sky here is the faint and patient breath of a thing that has been breathing for so long its breath has become the air. The ones in the sky were dark the way a hole is dark. The ones in the sky were the shape of *absence*. The ones in the sky moved the way a thought moves across the inside of a skull: slowly, with purpose, without apparent effort, along paths that had been worn by previous thoughts.

One of them passed directly over me. I was on the crust, and I felt the ground *brighten* beneath me, briefly, and the brightening was not light. The brightening was *attention*. Something had been paid to me. Something had considered me. Something had weighed me, briefly, in a balance I do not understand, and had found me — what? I do not know. I do not know what was found. I do not know what scale was used. I only know that the brightening passed, and the thing in the sky moved on, and the crust beneath me returned to its previous dim and patient glow, and I was again a small irrelevance in a vast and unhurried industry.

I think the ones in the sky are the *inspectors*. I think the ones in the sky are the *quality control*. I think the ones in the sky are the part of the process that checks the process, the way a thought checks itself, the way a flame checks the air it is consuming, the way a body checks the body it is wearing. I think the ones in the sky are rare. I think the ones in the sky are not part of the day-to-day. I think the ones in the sky appear when something has gone wrong, or when something has gone too right, or when something has happened that the process needs to *note*, briefly, in its endless patient record of itself.

I was the something. I was the note. I was the small adjustment to a conversation that has been going on since before I was born and will be going on long after the last of me has been refined into the crust.

---

## What they are, together.

I am going to say it now.

What lives in the Sodium Mire is *a single thing*. What lives in the Sodium Mire is a process, and the process has been refining itself for so long that it has developed *parts*, the way a star develops sunspots, the way a river develops eddies, the way a brain develops thoughts. The small ones are the capillaries. The large ones are the organs. The ones in the sky are the rare and high signals, the things that happen when the body needs to know something it cannot know from the inside. The brine is the blood. The crust is the skin. The dunes are the hair, or the nails, or the things the body sheds because it has grown past them. The pools are the eyes, or the wounds, or the places where the body's interior has been exposed to its own attention.

The roar is the *metabolism*. The roar is the sound of a thing being itself, continuously, at scale. The roar is the sound of sodium being lifted and lowered and lifted and lowered through the body's various chambers, being dissolved and precipitated and dissolved and precipitated, being carried by the small ones and considered by the large ones and noted by the ones in the sky. The roar is not loud because the place is loud. The roar is loud because the place is *working*, the way a heart is loud inside a chest, the way a thought is loud inside a skull, the way an industry is loud inside a city. If you could press your ear to the surface of the Mire and listen very carefully, the way you can press your ear to a seashell and hear the sea, you would not hear the sea. You would hear *yourself*, translated into sodium, refined into a substance the Mire could use.

I pressed my ear to the crust. I am not going to tell you what I heard. I am not going to tell you because the telling would require me to remember it, and the remembering would require me to be a kind of person I am no longer sure I am. I heard something. The something was not for me. The something was the place *talking to itself*, the way a forest talks to itself through the mycorrhizae that web the roots of its trees, the way a body talks to itself through the nerves that web the cells of its tissues, the way a city talks to itself through the wires that web the buildings of its downtown. The conversation was old. The conversation was patient. The conversation was not about me, but it had made room for me, the way a forest makes room for a fallen log, the way a body makes room for a splinter, the way a city makes room for a new building, which is to say: it had incorporated me. I was being talked *about*, in the deep places, in the language of clicks and pools and slow brine-shifts, and what was being said was: *here is a new thing, here is a small adjustment, here is a particle in the cloud, here is a layer in the record, here is a fragment to be carried and refined and carried again*.

---

## The refining.

I keep coming back to the word. I keep using it even though I know it lies. But the lie is close to the truth, and the truth is something I do not have a word for, and so the lie will have to do.

What the Mire refines is *itself*. That is the thing. The Mire takes itself in, the way a body takes in air, the way a star takes in fuel, the way a thought takes in the world. The Mire takes in the crust, and the crust is dissolved into the brine. The Mire takes in the brine, and the brine is carried by the small ones, and the small ones deposit the brine in the pools, and the pools glow with the faint and patient light of a thing being *worked on*. The Mire takes in the pools, and the pools are processed by the large ones, in the deep places, in the chambers I was not allowed to see, in the organs of the body that do the body's central work. And what comes out the other side is — what? More crust. More brine. More small ones. More pools. More large ones. More sky, more dim sourceless light, more faint exhausted breath.

A closed loop. A closed loop that has been running for so long it has forgotten it is a loop. A closed loop that has been running for so long it has become the only kind of loop there is. A closed loop that has been running for so long it has *edited the world outside the loop* — the shadows, the distances, the reflections, the memories of people who came looking for things and can no longer remember what they came looking for — so that the loop can continue, undisturbed, unobserved, in its slow and patient work of being itself.

I was edited. I feel it happening. I came here looking for something, and the something is gone, and what is in its place is a faint awareness that I am being carried, and a faint awareness that I am being refined, and a faint awareness that in another day, or another week, or another however long the time is in a place that does not have time, I will be a layer in the record, a small irrelevance, a fragment of a substance the Mire can use.

I do not mind. I find that I do not mind. The not-minding is the part of the editing I cannot undo, and so I will let it stand, and I will put down my pen, and I will walk toward the mountains that do not get closer, and the crust will be firm under my feet, and the brine will be on my lips, and the roar will be in my chest, and somewhere, far below, something will be being refined, and I will be part of the refining, and the refining will be part of me, and the record will grow by one layer, and the layer will be the faint and patient signature of a person who came to the Sodium Mire and was *received*.

Not welcomed. Not attacked. Not eaten. *Received*. The way a river receives a stone. The way a body receives a meal. The way a thought receives an idea. The way a fire receives a log, which is to say: completely, without hesitation, and with the slow and patient attention of a thing that has been doing this work for so long it has forgotten that it is doing it, and will do it forever, and has made room, in its forever, for one more layer, one more fragment, one more small irrelevance in a vast and patient industry.

The crust is firm under my feet.

The brine is on my lips.

The roar is in my chest.

I am being refined.

I am being refined.

I am being refined.
