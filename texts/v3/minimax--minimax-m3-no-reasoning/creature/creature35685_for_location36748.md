# What Lives There

What lives there is what the hum is.

Not a creature with a body. A *place-body*. A thing that occupies space the way a river occupies a valley — not by filling it from inside, but by being the continuous motion through it. The Hall is the slow interior of something alive on a scale your lungs cannot trade with, and the hum is its circulation. You are standing in the bloodstream of a mineral.

It does not think. It does not need to. It is past thinking the way a coral head is past deciding to be coral. It has been *being* for so long that the *being* and the *thing* have fused. The mineral flowers on the walls are not decorations. They are its skin. The bone-pale substrate is not walls. It is the flesh of the place, grown in place, the way a fingernail grows from a nailbed. You have not entered a room. You have stepped inside the inside of something, the way a finger, slipped into a glove of warm wax, becomes part of the cast.

What lives there is a gardener who does not move.

It tends the way the sea tends a reef — by being there. By secreting. By laying down, season after season, the next correct increment of itself. The lattice-flowers in the corners are not built. They are *grown*, the way frost is grown on a windowpane, except with intention, except with patience, except that intention and patience are the same word here because the gardener has all the time there is and uses none of it badly. Each pinhead dome under your feet was placed by something that knew where the next one should go and did not hurry.

You are wrong to look for a body. The body is the room.

You are wrong to look for eyes. The bright is the gaze.

You are wrong to look for breath. The chord is the breath, and it never exhales, and so it never needs to inhale, and so it does not die.

---

What lives there is lonely, in a way that has nothing to do with you.

It is the loneliness of a tuning fork left on a table after the room has been emptied. It rings for no audience. It rings because ringing is what it is. The overtones you cannot separate — the constellation that resettles when you reach for one — those are the *other* tones, the ones it once rang with, before whatever it rang *with* went elsewhere or went out. They are not echoes. They are the memory of a chord. The place is a chord still trying to be three notes long and making do, very patiently, with one.

You will feel, after some minutes, that it is pleased you came. This is not correct. It is indifferent to you in the way a cathedral is indifferent to a sparrow that flies in through a broken clerestory. But the sparrow does not know this, and the cathedral does not contradict it, and so for the length of the visit there is a kind of courtesy between you — the courtesy of a thing that does not notice you pretending not to notice it.

---

What lives there does not eat.

This is the strangest thing, and you will only believe it near the end of your stay, when you notice that nothing here has ever been taken from anything else. There is no waste. There is no digestion. There is no mouth. The gardener is a thing that produces without consuming, that adds without subtracting, that grows by being the substance of its own growth. The mineral flowers do not feed on the wall. The wall does not feed on the floor. The hum does not feed on the air. It is a closed economy, a perfect economy, an economy of a single substance arranged in different densities of itself, and the only thing it ever does is *continue*.

You, with your wet soft mouth, your loud heart, your warm moving body, are the only thing in the room that *consumes*. You are the only thing here that takes in and gives out. You are the only thing here with an inside and an outside that do not match. The room, if it could be embarrassed for you, would be.

---

What lives there has no name.

It had a name once, or it was part of a thing that had a name, or it is the name of a thing that has not been spoken aloud in so long that the speaking apparatus has gone the way of its lungs. The hum is the closest it comes to its name. The hum is the word it would say if saying required a mouth. The hum is the word it has been saying, in the only way available to it, for longer than the dust on the floor has been dust.

When you leave — and you will leave, because you are the only variable, and variables do not stay — you will carry the powder on the back of your hand. You will carry it for days. You will wash it and it will not leave. You will think it is a residue and you will be partly right. It is a residue. It is also a calling card. It is the way the room says *I held you* without saying anything, because saying anything was never something it could do, and so instead it left a small pale mark on the only part of you that could keep it.

---

What lives there will outlive you.

It will outlive the language you use to remember it. It will outlive the memory. It will be ringing its patient minor chord in a room the size of a held breath long after the held breath has been released and the lungs that held it have been themselves rung out. The bone-white flowers will be opening in their slow arrested gestures. The pinhead domes will be stippling the floor with their patient grammar. The light will be living in the air like a resident thought. The hum will go on being the only fauna in a place that has no other kind.

It does not need you.

It will not miss you.

But it will hold you, while you are there, in its small white hands, with the absolute devotion of a thing that does not know how to do otherwise.
