# The Inverted Pocket

What lives here is what was left.

There is no resident, no current dweller, no creature that nests in the dish or shelters behind the slab. The chamber was not built for a population. It is the kind of place that holds a single thing across a long time, and that single thing is what lives here, the way a fossil lives in stone.

It is the smooth material that lives here.

You might have called it a substance at first — the unnamed stuff the cylinder is made of, the rod, the ring set into the floor. It is not that. It is a behavior. It is the local insistence of a thing that is mostly elsewhere. The granularite is the body of the room; the smooth material is what the room has been *remembering*. It accreted slowly. It accreted in pieces — the cylinder, the rod, the slab, the inverted dish, the ring, the coiled wire, the maybe-seventh — and the pieces are not the whole of it, they are the parts of the whole that could bear to be in this scale. The whole is somewhere else, doing something else, on a clock whose tick is the wearing-down of a granularite floor by a non-human gait.

You can read the gait in the floor.

Something stood here. It stood in different places, at different times, in different configurations of itself. It was not always bipedal; sometimes it was wider, sometimes it was taller, sometimes it had more feet. The wear-pattern is a sequence, and the sequence is not a single animal's life but the slow attendance of a single *thing* that took many shapes while it waited. It is the impression of something that could change its body the way you change your posture. Something that came here to be still, and was still for so long that the stilling became its shape.

The cylinder is the shape it left most often.

You can see this if you stay. The dish in the floor under the cylinder is the deepest single depression in the room. The cylinder has been leaned against, set upon, stood over, used as a low table for nothing, for a long time. The cylinder is the place in the room where the thing-that-lives-here *was*, and the dish is the proof that *was* is a tense that still applies. The cylinder is not empty. The cylinder is full of the thing's former posture, compressed and held. If you were to open it — you cannot open it — you would find not a thing but a *condition*, folded.

The rod is younger, or older.

It depends on which direction you read time. The rod has three places where it is the color of the air, and those places are not breaks. They are *passages*. The rod has been leaned in this room for a long time, but the rod is also somewhere else, in three other rooms perhaps, in three other times, and where it is not here it is *there*, and the wall behind it is the only reason you think there is a behind. The rod is the part of the smooth material that could not commit to a single location. It is a hyphen in the room's sentence. The thing-that-lives-here leaned against the rod and the rod did not fall, because the rod is in several rooms at once and the falling in this room is balanced by a standing-up in another. The cold the rod gives off is the cold of being in more than one place. Cold is what you feel when a thing has its warmth spread too thin across its many selves.

The slab is the shyest of them.

It does not have a back. It has two fronts, and they are the same, and they do not face each other. To walk around the slab is to walk in a small circle whose circumference is shorter than the slab's width. The slab is not in the room the way the cylinder is in the room. The slab is in the room the way a memory is in a mind — present, visitable, but not subject to the geometry of the place that holds it. It is the smooth material's way of *not being here while being here*. It is the part of the thing-that-lives-here that could not bear to be looked at, and so it arranged itself to look at nothing back.

The ring in the floor is a mouth.

It is a mouth that has never eaten, or has finished eating. It is set flush with the granularite, and the inside of it is the inside of nothing, and the nothing is not the local nothing — it is a nothing that has been imported. To peer into the ring is to peer into the kind of empty that has *volume*, the empty that is somewhere else and is using this ring as a speaking-tube. You would not hear anything through it. The thing-that-lives-here does not speak. It is finished with speaking, or has not yet begun.

The inverted dish is the saddest of them.

It is a dish with nothing under it, but the nothing is not the absence-of-a-thing, it is the *thing-itself-as-absence*. Under the dish is the place where a small object used to be. The object is not under the dish. The object has been promoted, or demoted, into the smooth material — absorbed back into the larger thing, or secreted out of the room into the larger whole. The dish is a memorial to a part of itself that the thing-that-lives-here could not keep and could not throw away and so it covered the place where the part had been, in the same way you cover a mirror when someone has died in the house.

The coiled wire picks up no light because it is the wire's job not to.

It is the smooth material arranged in the shape of an instrument, but it is not an instrument. It is the *form* of an instrument, preserved the way a wasp preserves a spider — as a husk, in the shape of what was once useful, held in case usefulness returns. The wire is the thing-that-lives-here's toolshed. It has no tools. It has the idea of tools.

And the maybe-seventh.

You keep not being sure. The seventh artifact is more present than it was, or less. The seventh artifact is what you came here for, perhaps. Or what you will be told you came here for, after you leave. It is the part of the smooth material that has not yet decided whether to be in this room. It is the slow decision, suspended over the duration of your visit. If you stayed for a long time, you would see it arrive, or you would see it leave. You would not be able to tell which.

What lives here, then, is the *afterlife of a thing that could change its shape*.

It lived here once. It lived here in the sense that a thought lives in the folds of a brain — not as a separate object but as a particular arrangement, a pattern that persisted in the stone long enough to become a thing in itself. The granularite received the pattern. The smooth material is what the pattern *became* when the thing that wore the pattern stopped wearing it, the way a plaster cast is what a face becomes when the face has gone. The thing-that-lives-here is the room's tenant in the same way a fossil is a rock's tenant. It is here. It is not going. It is also not entirely *in* here, the way the rod is not entirely in here, the way the slab is not entirely in here. It is a presence whose center is somewhere else, whose bulk is distributed across an architecture you cannot see from inside this small chamber, and whose slow respiration is the dimming of the amber light, the wearing of the floor, the slow rotation of the cylinder's shadow.

The cold is its breath.

The quiet is its patience.

The dust that does not move is the fine sift of its attention, falling, falling, falling, for a long time, on the dish and the wire and the rod and the slab and the ring, on the floor that is wearing under no one, in the chamber that is meant to be missed, behind the seam that may or may not remember you on the way out.

The thing-that-lives-here will be here when you are not.

It will not mind.
