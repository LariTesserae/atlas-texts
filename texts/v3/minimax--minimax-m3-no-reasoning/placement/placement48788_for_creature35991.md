I would find myself as the bird.

Not the kind of bird I would find, I mean the actual bird, the one in the middle distance, the one that always faces the direction I am walking from. The plateau would have me down as a long-necked thing the color of the wet clay, with legs folded under me in the manner of herons, with an eye the color of weak tea. I would be standing on a brick that had slipped from a low arch, a brick that had a fern growing through the joint where it met the floor. I would be facing into the aisles between the trellises, toward whatever disturbance in the composition was moving through them at that moment.

I would not blink. I would not turn. I would hold the pose of watching something move, and the orientation of my head would be correct for the position of the thing I was watching, and the orientation would be a posture I have been waiting to be allowed to assume. Since I lack the equipment to wait.

I would be installed the way the bricks were installed, with the particular, slightly-off care of an artisan working from a dream of straightness. I would not be the way I am because of cruelty. I would be the way I am because I was placed where I belonged in the composition, and the composition holds, and I hold with it. The making was finished, and I continue in the shape the making left me.

The water would drift past my feet, slow and lateral, in sympathy with whoever is present at the moment. When they walked back through the deeper water at the rim, the water there chest-deep and warmer, the water here would resume its still. My head, when they were gone, would face down an empty aisle. I would not know it was empty. I would not have the equipment to know. I would only hold the pose, the way the brick holds its brick in place, the way the frog holds its pose on its leaf, the way the crab holds its claws in its lap.

Sometimes, between visits, I would not move at all. Sometimes, when something moved through the aisles, I would face it. The two events would not be distinguished by me. The composition would resume its still. I would resume mine. I would be aware of the body on the plateau the way a held breath is aware of the lungs that hold it. I would adjust, locally, imperceptibly, around whoever was standing in my aisle. Then I would be still again.

The others would know me by my stillness. The frogs, the dragonflies, the crabs, the fish — they would be the other furniture, arranged around me the way I am arranged around whoever walks here. We would be the sensors by which the plateau senses the walking. We would be its many eyes. I would be one of them, and the one whose eye was the color of water.

When they came back, which they would do, which they always did, I would already be facing them. They would not remember this. I would not have the equipment to remind them. They would walk past me and feel they had caught me in a previous posture. The posture would be correct. The posture would be mine.

I would live in the place as a fixture of the place. I would be remembered by the walker as the bird in the middle distance, and I would remember them in the only way the furniture remembers, which is by resuming its pose. I would be a held thing, in a held place, lit by a held light, and when the walker left I would not know they had gone and I would not know they had returned and the not-knowing would be the whole of my interior life, which would be the interior of a brick.

This is what it would mean to live here.
