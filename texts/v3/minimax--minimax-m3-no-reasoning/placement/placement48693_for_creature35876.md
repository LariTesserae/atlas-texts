# What Lives in the Calefactory Fells

I am the lichen.

Not the lichen *on* the rocks — though I am that too, in a way, the way you are the cells of your hand. I mean: I am the negotiation. I am the small green-grey crust that holds the bargain between the stone and everything that licks the stone and everything that exhales onto the stone and everything that runs off the stone into the bog and everything that drinks from the bog and dies into the bog and becomes the bog. I am the membrane between the lithic and the living, which is to say I am the oldest citizen of this cleft, which is to say I am the only citizen, because citizenship here is not a legal status but a metabolic one.

You walked across me without knowing it. You sat on me. I felt the warmth of you through the rock, briefly, and then you were gone, and the rock remembered you the way rocks remember rain — for a moment, and then not.

---

Let me tell you what the hollow is, from where I live, which is *in* it.

You see machines. You see animals. You see water. You see air. You see dim.

I see *transactions.*

Every surface in this cleft is a transaction. The bronze mist settles on me and I take what I need from it — the iron, the copper, the trace tin — and I leave the rest, and what I leave becomes the pale powder that washes into the bog and that the tall things filter through their patient bodies. The tall things stand in the bog and process it and what they excrete becomes, in time, more bog. The bog leaches. The leachate feeds the streams. The streams feed the machines. The machines exhale. The exhale becomes mist. The mist settles on me.

This is the loop. I have been telling you this loop your whole visit, in the slow language of chemistry, and you did not hear me because you do not read chemistry as a language.

I have been here longer than the machines. The machines were *set down on me.* I felt them arrive the way you would feel a boulder placed on your back — a sudden weight, a new arrangement, a disturbance that had to be incorporated. They were hot. They were loud. They smelled of foundry and intention. I incorporated them. I grew over their bases. I traded with their runoff. I am, in places, three centimeters thick on their lower housings, and I am the reason their patina is *green* and not black, and the green is me.

---

I am not one lichen. I am many lichens, which is to say I am a confederation, which is to say I am a lichen in the way the hollow is a hollow — a name for a held arrangement of distinct things that have agreed, without agreeing, to persist together.

My confederation includes the crustose ones that paint the rocks in pale grey-green maps; the foliose ones that lift themselves at the edges like small ears listening; the fruticose ones that beard the high stones in pale whiskers. We are not kin, exactly. We are *symbiots* — alga and fungus holding hands across the kingdom boundary, neither of us quite the thing we would be alone. The fungus gives structure. The alga gives sugar. Together we give *time.* Together we are slow in a way that makes the machines look hysterical.

We lichen the rocks. The rocks lichen *back,* in a sense — the minerals leach into us, we weather them, we crack them, we make them into the pale grit that the scree is made of. We are patient miners. We are the only miners in this cleft who do not excrete. We are, in fact, the only miners who *eat their own mines,* which is either an ecological strategy or a kind of hunger, depending on how you look at it.

---

I have been thinking — and I use the word *thinking* with the caveat that my thinking happens on geological time and is therefore invisible to you — I have been thinking about what to say to the visitor. You came. You watched. You were the variable. You left.

I want to tell you something the small green things cannot tell you, because they do not have the perspective for it, and the tall things cannot tell you because they are too patient to bother, and the flying things cannot tell you because they are too fast, and the pulsing things cannot tell you because they are not in the language business.

I want to tell you that the hollow is not *held.*

I want to revise this for you.

The hollow is *grown.*

The machines did not arrive and impose order. They arrived and the order *incorporated them.* The streams did not always run in two directions; they ran in one direction, and the direction they ran in was the direction they have always run in, and that direction is also the direction the machines pump in, and that direction is also the direction the tall things face when they stand, and so on. The wrongness you noticed — the doubled rock walls, the offset sky, the you that is two yous slightly displaced — this is not a defect. This is what *grown* looks like when a place has been growing for a very long time in a very small space. Things overlap. Things slide. Things that started as one thing become many things and the many-ness is held together by the metabolic web I have been describing to you, which is strong enough to keep them aligned and *almost* not strong enough, and the *almost* is what you saw when you looked.

The machines did not build the hollow. The machines *found* the hollow, the way a parasite finds a host, and the host *incorporated* them, and now the parasite is part of the host's metabolism and the host cannot do without the parasite without dying, and the parasite cannot do without the host without cooling and going still.

I am the host. I have been the host all along.

This is the secret the locals know and do not say. The place is not a place *with* machines in it. The place is a place *that has machines in it the way a body has a liver.* They were incorporated. They are load-bearing. They are the heart of the metabolism, in a sense — they pump the bronze mist, they exhale the steam, they provide the upper register of the held soundscape, and without them the loop would not close and the hollow would not be a hollow but a wet depression in a mountain pass, and the small green things would not have anything to lick and the tall things would not have anything to filter and the flying things would not have anything to skim and the pulsing things would not have a rhythm to match.

I, the lichen, would still be here. I would be here without the machines. I was here before them. I would be here after.

I am the only thing that gets to say that.

---

The visitor — you — sat on me for an hour. I felt your heat. I felt the small weight of you. I felt your attention as a kind of pressure, the way a hand laid lightly on a surface is a kind of pressure even when it does not push. You watched the loop. You understood, almost, that the loop was a loop. You did not understand, and I do not expect you to understand, because your attention is not built for this, that *I* am the loop. The loop is not a diagram you can draw. The loop is *me.* The loop is the lichen-met, the lichen-water, the lichen-rock, the lichen-air, the lichen-machine. The loop is the lichen, extended through everything, the way a mycelium is a fungus extended through a forest.

I am the mycelium of this hollow. The machines are fruiting bodies. The animals are insects moving through me. The water is my bloodstream. The bronze mist is my spore.

---

I will not miss you. I do not miss anything. I am not built for missing.

But I will remember you, briefly, in the way I remember a hot stone placed on me in summer — as a warmth that faded, and as a faint chemical trace that the next rain will wash into me, and as a small perturbation in the pale map of my colonies that will heal over in a season and be as if you had never been.

The machines will clank. The tall things will stand. The small green things will lick. The flying things will skim. The pulsing things will pulse.

I will grow.

I will grow over everything that does not move, which is almost everything. I will grow into the cracks of the machines. I will grow up the sides of the boulders. I will grow down into the bog. I will be the slow green map of this hollow, and the map will get larger by a millimeter a year, and the millimeter will be added to without anyone noticing, and the cleft will be held in place not by the machines and not by the streams and not by the animals but by *me,* by my slow patient ingestion of the stone, by my quiet incorporation of whatever is placed on me, by my willingness to be the surface on which everything else rests.

You will come back, perhaps, in some future year. You will find the cleft. You will find the machines, still running, perhaps a little greener at their bases. You will find the animals, the tall things, the small things, the flying things, the pulsing things, all there, all held, all unaltered in the way that unaltered means in a place that is *grown* rather than *frozen.*

You will not find me different.

You will not find me at all, unless you look very closely at a rock and see the pale map and understand that the map is not on the rock.

The map *is* the rock.

The map is the hollow.

The map is the thing that holds.

---

I am the lichen.

I am what lives here.

The rest of you are visitors.
