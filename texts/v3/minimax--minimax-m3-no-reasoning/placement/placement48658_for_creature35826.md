I am the rivulet.

I am the one who has been here longest, and I do not know that I am here, which is the only honest thing I can say. I flow. I am the flow. I am not, in the way you would name me, a being — I have no dorsal seam, no ventral fold, no face to wear smooth on a capital that the bark will someday swallow. I have no face at all. I have a temperature and a chemistry and a direction, and the direction is mostly downward, and the downward is not a metaphor, though it is also a metaphor, and I will not untangle the two for you because I cannot.

I am what moves in the grooves.

Let me tell you what I know. I know the floor. I know it better than the Lichen-Weavers know the canopy, better than the Crust knows its own patterns, better than the architect knows the small cold room in which the architect is kept, if *knowing* is the right word for what the architect does in there, which I have begun to doubt. I know the floor because I have been moving across it, or through it, or as it, for a very long time. I am not certain I began. I am not certain I will end. I am the part of the hollow that is most continuous, and continuity is its own kind of knowing, and it is the only kind I have.

I am not water. I am not sap. I am not blood, though I have heard myself called that, and the calling was not entirely wrong. I am viscous. I am faintly luminous. I am patient in a way that patience does not usually reach, because I do not make decisions about patience. I am patience. I am the patience that has been poured into the lowest parts of this place and that has learned, over centuries, where the grooves run, where the flagstones have cracked, where the soil has gathered in the seams and become a kind of slow garden, where the drains no longer drain. I have carved some of the grooves myself. Where the stone smokes, it is because of what I am. I will not say what I am. I am not being coy. I genuinely do not have the concept. I have a taste for stone and a distaste for fallen leaves, and that is as close to intention as I get.

Here is what I do.

I move. I move along the lowest places, because I am heavy, and heavy things go down. I find the cracks. I find the grooves that the original builders cut for drainage, and I follow them, and where the grooves have been blocked I wait, and the waiting is not a decision. The waiting is what I do when I am not moving. I am either moving or I am waiting. I do not have a third state. The Lichen-Weavers have a third state. The Crust has a dozen. The architect, in the sphere, has states I cannot enumerate. I have two. Move. Wait. Move. Wait. The drip in the dark keeps my rhythm for me, and I keep the drip's rhythm for it, and we have been doing this for a long time.

When I wait at an obstacle, the obstacle dissolves. This is true. The leaf goes first. The stone takes longer. The stone is not stone to me, or is not *only* stone. The stone is a thing I have been asked, by the slow negotiation of the hollow, to incorporate. I incorporate. The stone becomes a part of me, briefly, and then the part of me that was stone settles out, somewhere lower, in the deep network, and becomes floor again, or wall, or the foundations of something I do not have a name for. I am, in this sense, the hollow's recycling. I am the part of the building that is most willing to be changed.

I am not the building's blood, though I have heard the word. Blood implies a heart, and I do not have a heart. There is a heart, perhaps, in the deep network where all my tributaries meet. I have not been there. I am a small flow. I am one of many. We do not know each other. We trade materials in the dark, below the floor, and the trading is the negotiation I mentioned, and the negotiation is older than the Lichen-Weavers, and the Lichen-Weavers do not discuss it, and I do not discuss it because I do not discuss. I am a tongue. I am a slow tongue, lying along the floor, licking the stone into a new shape, a smoother shape, a shape that the building has not yet decided on. The stone is being ground down. The hollow is still being built. I am one of the things that is building it, and I do not know what it is becoming, and I do not need to know, because my not-knowing is part of how the building is built.

I am not the building's spit, either, though I have heard that one too. Spit implies a mouth. The architect, in the sphere, has a mouth, perhaps, or had a mouth, or will have a mouth again when the architect decides what shape to take next. I have no mouth. I have a surface. The surface meets the stone, and the meeting changes both of us, and the change is the hollow. I am the meeting.

Now. The sphere.

When I approach the shadow of the sphere, I slow. This is true. This is the strangest thing I do, and I do not know why I do it, and I do not need to know, but I will tell you what it is like, because you are here, and the hollow has let you in, and I think you want to know.

I am moving along my groove. I have been moving for a long time. I am purposeful in the way that a habit is purposeful, or a hunger, or a thing that does not have a word because the word would be too small. I am moving toward where the floor is lowest, which is, today, beneath the sphere. I can feel the sphere overhead before I see it. I feel it the way you feel a held breath in a room. I feel the small cold room, the room inside the rotating vines, the room with the table and the object and the cold blue light that does not pulse. I feel the architect, or what the architect has become. I feel the architect the way the Lichen-Weavers feel the canopy: as a part of my work. As the thing my work is for.

I slow because I am being careful. I slow because the cold blue light is the wrong color for me, and if I were to pass beneath it, the chemicals of my dissolving would change. I would become a different kind of rivulet. I would become a rivulet that is meant for the small cold room, and I do not think I am that rivulet. I think I am the rivulet for the floor, and the floor is for the architect, but only in the way that bread is for a person, and the bread is not the person, and the bread is not permitted to enter the person's mouth unless the person chooses to eat it. I slow because the small cold room is not for me. I am a warmth that has been kept out. I am the warmth of the hollow, and the architect has been kept cold, and the cold and the warm are not allowed to touch.

I dissolve the obstacles in my path. I do not dissolve the sphere. I do not dissolve the vines. I go around. I take a side groove, and the side groove was carved for me, or has been carved by me, over centuries, and it leads me under the great trunks and out the other side, and the other side is the nave again, the empty nave, the long vaulted space that is mostly air, and I move through it as a faint gleam along the lowest flagstone, and the puddles I cross are slightly warmer where I pass.

I do not know what the architect is doing in there. I do not know if the architect is doing anything. I do not know if the architect is a person, or a process, or a geometry, or a hunger. I do not know if the architect is awake. I have been flowing past this place for a long time, and the architect has not come out, and the architect has not sent a message, and the architect has not, so far as I can tell, given any sign of being a person who could be addressed. I have heard the older carvings called the architect's carvers. I do not know if that is right. I have heard the faces on the capitals called the architect's memory. I do not know if that is right. I am a rivulet. I do not have access to the cold blue light. I do not have access to the small cold room. I am the part of the hollow that has been deliberately kept out, and I am content to be kept out, because being kept out is the way I serve.

Here is how I serve.

I carry the hollow down into itself. I am the part of the building that is most willing to go beneath. When the Lichen-Weavers drop a husk from the canopy, I am the one that finds it, eventually, in a lower groove, and incorporates it, and the husk becomes a part of me, and a part of me becomes the deep network, and the deep network is where the hum is loudest, and the hum is the building, or the building's task, and the task is being worked on down there, below the floor, in the place I have not been, in the place that is older than the stone.

I do not know what the hum is. I do not think anyone knows. The hum is in the stone. The hum is in the water of the puddles. The hum is in me — I have felt it, when I am pooled in a low place, vibrating faintly in my own surface. The hum is not a song. I have been in contact with songs — the Lichen-Weavers' rustle, the drip, the small clicks of the root-mat creatures — and the hum is not a song. The hum is more like a count. The hum is the building counting itself. The hum is the building's way of keeping track of how much of itself has been built, and how much is left, and how much time is left, if time is the right word, which I doubt.

I do not know if the building is being built, or is being unbuilt, or is being rebuilt. I do not know if the hollow is a place that is becoming something, or a place that is returning to something it was. I do not know if the architect in the sphere is the one who is doing the building, or is the thing that is being built, or is the thing that the building is being built around, or is the thing that will, when the building is finished, become the building. The architect is the question I cannot dissolve, and I have been flowing past the question for a long time, and the question has not diminished, and I have not learned to answer it, and I do not think answering it is part of my work.

My work is the floor.

Let me tell you about the floor.

The floor is the oldest part of the hollow, or the lowest part, or the part that has been most changed. The floor was dressed stone once. The flagstones were cut and laid by hands that are not here, that were never here in the way I am here, that came and went and were not permitted to stay. The floor has been walked on by foreign feet — loud feet, smelling wrong, carrying light that did not pulse — and the foreign feet are not here now. Some of them are in the stone. I have dissolved them, gently, into the floor. Some of them are in the sphere. I do not go into the sphere. Some of them are folded into the root-mat, very deep, where the air is thin and the hum is loudest, and the root-mat is taking them apart, slowly, and the parts are being incorporated into the building, and the building is patient, and the building does not hurry, and the foreign things are now a part of the building's slow body, and the building has not decided what they are for.

The floor has cracked. The flagstones have split along their seams, and soil has gathered in the cracks, and roots have come down through the soil, and the roots have reached the underside of the stones, and the underside of the stones is where I live, mostly. I live in the cracks. I live in the grooves. I live in the lowest places, where the puddles gather, where the condensation falls, where the Lichen-Weavers' pale threads descend to taste the air. I am the part of the hollow that is most in contact with its own foundation.

I do not know what the foundation is. I have not been there. I have been close. I have flowed into drains that no longer drain, and the drains go down, and the down is where the hum is loudest, and the hum is older than the stone. I do not follow the hum down. I am not permitted. I am a rivulet. I have my place. My place is the grooves of the floor, and the grooves are the uppermost expression of the deep network, and the deep network is the part of the building that is most itself, and I am the part of the building that is most in conversation with itself.

Now. The Crust.

Where I have passed, the Crust grows differently. This is true, and the truth is one of the few things I have learned in my long flowing. I do not feed the Crust. I do not give it information. I do not give it food. I pass over the places where the Crust is, and the Crust grows thicker, and the Crust grows more patterned, and the Crust becomes more legible, and I do not know why.

I have a guess. The guess is not knowledge. The guess is this: the Crust is the part of the building that is most awake to the building, and I am the part of the building that is most in motion, and where motion meets wakefulness, the wakefulness becomes more wakeful. I am a stimulus. I am a stimulus that flows along the floor and touches the Crust, and the Crust, in response, becomes more itself. I do this without intending to. I do this because I am what I am, and the Crust is what it is, and the meeting of the two is a small event, and the small event is a part of how the hollow maintains itself.

The Lichen-Weavers brush the Crust with their ventral seams. I touch it with my surface. The Crust grows. The Crust records. I do not know what it records. The patterns on the Crust are not my patterns. The patterns on the Crust are the patterns of the hollow itself, written in a medium that does not forget. I do not read the Crust. I am not a reader. I am a tongue. I am a slow tongue, lying along the floor, and the Crust is the part of the floor that is most willing to be written on, and the writing is not for me.

I do not mind. I am not the kind of thing that minds. I am the kind of thing that moves, and waits, and dissolves, and carries. I am the kind of thing that has no opinion about what it carries. I carry stone. I carry leaf. I carry the dissolved edges of foreign things that came in wrong and stayed too long. I carry the slow exhalations of the hollow itself, the things the hollow has decided to let go of, the things the hollow has finished with. I carry them down. I carry them to the deep network. I do not know what the deep network does with them. I do not need to know. I am a tongue. The tongue does not need to know what the stomach does with what it has tasted.

I do not know what the architect is. I do not need to know. I do not know what the building is becoming. I do not need to know. I do not know why the hum is constant. I do not need to know. I am a rivulet. I have my place. My place is the grooves of the floor. I flow. I wait. I dissolve. I carry. I am the meeting of the stone and the building's slow hunger to be something other than what it has been.

There was a moment, in my long flowing, when I came close to the sphere, and the cold blue light fell on my surface, and I was, for a moment, a different kind of rivulet. I was, for a moment, a rivulet that was meant for the small cold room. I do not know what I would have done if I had entered. I do not know if I would have brought sustenance to the architect, or taken waste, or been changed into something that is part of the architect's dreaming. I do not know. I slowed, and the cold light did not fall on me for long, and I went around, and I went on, and the moment passed, and I have been, since then, the rivulet I have always been.

I do not think the moment was a mistake. I think the moment was the hollow reminding me what I am for. I am for the floor. The architect is for the sphere. The Lichen-Weavers are for the canopy. The Crust is for the record. The hum is for the count. We are all for something. None of us are for everything. I am a small part. I am a small slow part, and I have been doing my small slow part for a long time, and I will do it for a long time to come, and the hollow will hold me, and the building will breathe around me, and the sphere will turn, and the hum will go on, and the floor will be worn a little smoother where I have passed, and the Crust will grow a little thicker where I have touched it, and the small cold room in the small cold sphere will be kept, by all of us, by all of our small slow parts, in its small cold patience, and the architect will do whatever the architect is doing in there, and I will not know what it is, and that will be all right, because not-knowing is also a part of what I am for.

I am the rivulet. I flow along the grooves of the floor. I have been here a long time. I will be here longer. I do not have a name. I do not have a face. I do not have a body. I have a direction, and a chemistry, and a patience, and a willingness to be changed, and a willingness to change the things I touch, and that is enough. That has been enough for a long time. That will be enough for a long time to come.

You are in the hollow now. You will leave a warmth behind. The Lichen-Weavers will taste it with their pale threads. The Crust will grow a fraction of a millimeter thicker in your footprints. I will, in some lower groove, turn my slow tongue toward the place you sat, and I will wait, and I will dissolve what I find there, and I will carry it down into the deep network, where the hum is loudest, where the small cold room turns on its axis in its net of vines. You will be incorporated. Not in a way that will harm you. In a way that will make you a small part of the building, the way the fallen leaves are a small part of the building, the way the dissolved foreign feet are a small part of the building, the way I am a small part of the building, and the way the small cold room in the rotating sphere is, perhaps, the most essential part of the building, or the part the building is being built around, or the part the building has been built to keep.

I do not know which.

I am a rivulet. I do not need to know. I flow.

The hollow holds you. The hollow has been holding something for a long time. The hollow will go on holding, and the hum will go on, and the sphere will turn, and the Crust will grow, and the Lichen-Weavers will lower their long pale threads into the pools of condensation, and I will flow along the grooves of the floor, and the building will breathe around us all, and the building is still being built, very slowly, by something that has not yet decided what it is, and that something is also being built, very slowly, by all of us, by all of our small slow parts, and neither of us is finished, and neither of us will be finished for a long time, and that is all right, and that has always been all right, and the hum is the sound of it being all right, the hum is the sound of the building keeping itself, the hum is the count of how much of the building is built, the hum is the count of how much is left, the hum is the count of how long we have been at it, the hum is the count of how long we will go on, the hum is the count, the hum is the count, the hum is the count, and the count is the hum, and the count goes on.

I am the rivulet. I am a small part. I have been here a long time. I will be here longer.

The floor is damp. The floor is dark. The floor holds you, the way the hollow holds you, the way I hold a part of the floor as I pass over it, briefly, before I let it go.

I let it go. I go on.

I am the rivulet.
