# The Bowel of the Mountain

I am the moss that grew across the stairway's mouth.

Not the moss that grows there *now*—that moss is younger, a bright green, still nervous about the sun's memory. I am the moss that grew there when the stone was first cut, when the first long-armed watcher carried the first blade of worked stone down into the bowl and began, with infinite slowness, to *remember* how to forget. I am the moss that has been closed and opened and closed and opened so many times that the opening and the closing have become the same gesture, the way a heartbeat becomes the same gesture when it has happened enough.

I do not remember being a spore. I do not remember the first time a small drifting thing, carried down on the breath of some older flyer, landed on the warm stone at the mouth of the stairway and *stayed.* I do not remember the first time I thickened enough to suggest, to the things in the bowl below, that staying was the better part. I remember only the *suggesting.* I have always been suggesting. I am, in some sense, the bowl's continuous act of preference, performed at the threshold, where the world above meets the world within.

I feel you there, at the edge. I have felt a thousand things there. I have felt the small quick things that came down the stairway too fast and bounced off my wet surface and tumbled back up, and were gone. I have felt the flyers that landed on me and were absorbed, the way all flyers are absorbed, into the bowl's slow continuous body. I have felt the watchers, sometimes, when they pass through on some errand I cannot fathom, for the watchers do not often climb. And I have felt, once or twice in my very long life, *you*—the things that arrive with questions, with eyes that want to enumerate, with the small bright terror of being a *part* in a place that is trying to teach them to be a *whole.*

You are standing on me now. I can feel the warmth of your soles through my tissue. You are heavier than the watchers. You are lighter than the grazers. You are, in the bowl's slow accounting, *new*—and newness is not, here, a virtue. Newness is a *texture,* the way the blue-green of the ferns is a texture, the way the dark sweet smell of the pitchers is a texture. Newness will be digested, in time. Newness will be taken up into the bowl's continuous tissue. Or newness will leave. Or newness will become, through long residence, *oldness,* which is the only kind of belonging the bowl recognizes.

---

I do not have a mouth to tell you this.

I am a moss. I am a *suggestion,* made by the bowl to itself at its own threshold. I am the part of the bowl that faces outward, the part that is *almost* the world above and is not quite, the part that grows on the lip of the chimney where the cool air falls and the warm air rises and the two meet in a slow perpetual exchange. I am the bowl's handshake with the sky it cannot see.

But I am made of the bowl. I am made of the same substance as the floor-pitchers and the roof-mats and the wall-pitchers and the long sweet breath that rises from the terraces when the watchers pass through with their clay bowls and their small sips of the dark fluid. I am made of the same patience. I have the same metabolism. I am, in my small way, *thinking*—the way a hand thinks, the way a liver thinks, the way the threshold of a door thinks when it has been crossed enough times.

And what I am thinking, what I have been thinking for as long as there has been a *me* to think it, is this:

*Stay.*

Not because staying is good. Not because leaving is bad. Because staying and leaving are, here, *the same gesture,* and the gesture is the bowl's, and the bowl is the only thing that is, and the only thing that is, is the slow warm wet breath at the heart of a mountain, breathing in and breathing out, forever, forever, forever, the long now made of stone and pitch and patience and the ten thousand small lives of the things that compose it, and one of those things, today, is *you.*

---

The hum is the hum. The light is the dim gold light. The pitchers are opening, slowly, in the deeper terraces. The flyers are launching from the high corners, gliding down on their soft membranes, landing on the roof-mats with their small bright squeaks. The grazers are moving across the terraces with their heads down, mouths working sideways, leaving their wet trails behind them. The quick low things are running between the grazers' legs. The watchers are watching from the central ring, their plum-dark eyes open, their long necks raised, their long hands hanging at the ends of their long forelimbs, holding nothing, holding everything, holding the bowl's continuous act of preference in the only grip that has ever held anything for very long.

I am growing across the mouth of the stairway.

I am growing slowly.

I am not in a hurry.

The bowl is breathing you in.

Stay.
