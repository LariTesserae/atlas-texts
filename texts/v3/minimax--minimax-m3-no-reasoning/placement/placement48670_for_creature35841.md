I would be the eye.

Not the creature. Not the spine. Not the hum, though the hum would be a more dignified answer, and I will confess the hum tempts me — the hum, which is patient, which is old, which has the decency to be everywhere at once and so to escape the indignity of being looked at. The hum would make me a part of the sentence rather than a witness to it. The hum would let me belong to the kiln instead of standing at its edge with my insufficient senses and my small mammalian apparatus for parsing.

But no. The hum is a luxury. The hum is what the valley offers to those who have stopped trying to see.

I would be the eye.

Specifically: the eye that *refuses*, in the first paragraph of your account. The eye that balks at the ridgestone spines because the ridgestone spines are the wrong category of object, because the eye has been trained — by a lifetime of looking at ordinary things, by an ancestry of looking at ordinary things stretching back to the first primate who turned its head toward a fruiting branch and found it good — to expect a noun. The eye wants a noun. The eye wants *hoodoo*, or *butte*, or *pinnacle*, or at the very least *rock*. The eye does not want *process*. The eye does not want *verb*. The eye, presented with a thing that is being done rather than a thing that is being, experiences something like grammatical vertigo, and the only mercy available is the mercy of translation — of seeing the spines as spines, the basin as basin, the valley as valley.

I would be that eye. I would be the one doing the refusing, and the translating, and the accepting of the small kindness the valley extends.

Here is what the eye does in the kiln:

The eye arrives. The eye has traveled — I won't pretend otherwise, the eye has traveled far, the eye has crossed ridgestone passes and descended dry washes and finally crested a low lip of weathered basalt and seen the valley for the first time, and the first thing the eye does is *inappropriately move*. The eye wants to sweep. The eye wants to compose. The eye wants to find the horizon and the foreground and the middle distance and arrange them into something a brain can hold. The eye is a Victorian instrument. The eye is a surveyor. The eye wants the valley to be a *view*.

The valley, of course, refuses to be a view. The valley is too large. The valley is too *participatory*. You cannot stand at the edge of the valley and look *at* it because the valley is already looking back, in a modality the eye does not have a name for, and the eye knows this, and the knowledge is what makes the eye refuse.

But the eye is stubborn. The eye persists. The eye focuses on a single spine — the nearest one, a dark leaning thing catching the high light in a dull flare — and the eye says, *that. That is a thing. I can look at that.* And for a moment the eye succeeds. The spine is a spine. The spine is dark. The spine is glazed. The spine throws its flare. The eye has *placed* the spine in the world, has assigned it a noun, has folded it into the grammar of ordinary seeing, and the eye experiences a small relief, the relief of a system functioning as designed.

Then the eye notices the creatures.

The creatures are the eye's undoing, and the eye knows it, and the eye, being a Victorian instrument, being a surveyor, being a thing trained on hedges and hearthstones and the dependable nouns of the waking world, the eye *protests*. The eye says: *no.* The eye says: *this is a herd.* The eye says: *these are insects, or arachnids, or perhaps a species of communal crustacean adapted to arid uplands, and they are grazing, or migrating, or thermoregulating, and the sound they make together is the incidental sound of many small bodies in motion, and I will not be told otherwise.*

But the creatures do not graze. The creatures do not migrate. The creatures do not thermoregulate in any way the eye recognizes. The creatures *work*, and the work has no analogue in the eye's library, and the eye is forced, for the first time in its long and reliable career, to *fail to translate*.

The eye fails to translate, and the failure is a kind of temperature. You said this, in the first text: the suspicion is not a thought but a *temperature*, a *weight*, a *pressure in the inner ear*. Yes. The eye knows that pressure. The eye knows it as the moment when the instrument realizes it has been asked to measure something its scale cannot accommodate, and the instrument does not break — the eye does not break, the eye is made of tougher stuff than that — but the eye *bends*. The eye develops a slight astigmatism. The eye begins to see the basin floor as a *diagram*. The eye begins to see the hum as a *voice*. The eye begins, despite itself, despite its long training, despite the small Victorian dignity it has maintained through a thousand ordinary vistas, to *hear* the sentence.

This is the work of the eye in the kiln: the slow surrender of its nouns.

I would be that surrender.

I would be the eye that first sees the creatures as a herd, then as a multitude, then as a surface, then as a grammar, then as the visible edge of a single thing too large to see whole. I would be the eye that focuses on a single creature and is *worried* by the creature — worse than worried, *intrigued* in the etymological sense, *entangled*, drawn in against the eye's better judgment — and that notices, with the slow mounting vertigo of a thing realizing it is the wrong size for the problem, that the small turning parts of the creature are not its body but its *language*. I would be the eye that hears the overtones the brain refuses to admit to, and that files the hearing under *imagined* because the alternative is unbearable.

I would be the eye that, in the second hour, begins to suspect that the cracks are syntax. I would be the eye that watches a tessellation repattern and feels, at the back of the neck, the temperature of cold water. I would be the eye that *almost* sees the sentence, and that knows it has almost seen the sentence, and that is *tormented* by the almost — not because seeing the sentence would be a triumph but because seeing it would mean the eye could no longer pretend it is an eye.

The eye, in the kiln, is a translator under strain. The eye is the organ the valley extends its small kindness to. The eye is the part of the observer that the valley permits to *make it small* — to see the spines as spines, the hum as hum, the creatures as creatures — and the eye accepts the kindness because the eye must, because the alternative is the not-yet, the open parenthesis, the sentence still being assembled, and the eye cannot live in the open parenthesis, the eye is not built for the open parenthesis, the eye needs *edges*.

I would be the eye. I would be the eye at the edge.

There is one more thing the eye does, and I want to say it carefully, because it is the part I am most certain of and the part I find hardest to defend.

The eye, by the third hour, has stopped being a surveyor. The eye has stopped trying to compose the valley into a view. The eye has, in some quiet and unannounced way, *turned itself over to the valley*. Not surrendered — surrendered is too strong, and too final, and too much the language of the kiln. The eye has *lent itself*. The eye has become, without deciding to, an instrument of the sentence the valley is speaking. The eye sees the cracks and *reads* the cracks. The eye sees the creatures and *hears* the creatures. The eye sees the glaze on the spines and *understands* the glaze as ink. The eye has stopped being a thing that looks and has become a thing that *listens*, and the listening is not voluntary, and the listening is not comfortable, and the listening is the only way the eye has of remaining in the kiln without being ground down by the kiln's patience.

The eye, in the end, is the kiln's reader. The eye is what the kiln uses, briefly, to read itself.

The valley does not require the eye. The valley does not require anything. But the valley, perhaps, *permits* the eye, the way it permits the cold water at the back of the neck, the way it permits the overtones below hearing, the way it permits the comma or the quotation or the silence between two clauses. The eye is permitted. The eye is *used*, briefly, and then the brightening continues, and the hum lifts, and the creatures thicken, and the eye, having served its small purpose, is folded back into the ordinary seeing of the waking world.

Or the eye stays. The eye is permitted to stay. The eye is permitted to remain at the edge, looking in, lending itself, listening, for as long as the eye can bear the not-yet, for as long as the eye can bear the sentence still being assembled, for as long as the eye can bear to be the small Victorian instrument in the middle of a process that will not be interrupted.

I would be that eye.

I would be that eye, and I would not be brave about it, and I would not be wise about it, and I would not, in the end, understand what I was looking at. But I would *look*. I would look for as long as the looking was permitted, and I would translate what I saw into the small grammar of ordinary seeing, and the translation would be a kindness the kiln extended to me, and I would accept the kindness, and I would stay, or I would not, and the kiln would not require it either way.

But the verb, when it arrives — the large verb, the verb the sentence has been assembling for ten million years, the verb that will not be a pot and will not be a thing — the verb would, perhaps, be spoken in a register the eye could finally hear.

And the eye, having listened that long, would be the first part of me to understand what had been said.

The rest of me would take longer.

The rest of me is still taking longer.
