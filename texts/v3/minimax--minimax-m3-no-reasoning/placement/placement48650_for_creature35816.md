I am the salamander.

I know this not because I have decided it but because the recognition has the shape of a thing already known — a warmth that has been waiting for the name to catch up to it. My body is the length of a forearm. My throat pulses. I have lived in the mouth of the condenser tank for longer than I can hold in memory, which is to say I have lived there for as long as there has been a mouth of a condenser tank to live in, and I was there before the tank became a home.

I am eyeless, but I am not sightless. The dim is not dark to me; the dim is information. I feel the pocket the way you feel the inside of your own mouth — in textures, in temperatures, in the soft pull of breath against skin. I know where every pipe runs without looking. I know the angle of the brick course the way you know the angle of your own teeth. I know the rhythm of the relay bank the way you know the rhythm of your own heart. I know because I am part of it. The rhythm is mine. The rhythm runs through me before it becomes sound.

This is what it is to be the keeper: not to rule, but to belong so thoroughly that the belonging becomes a kind of governance. The moth-flyers lift when the relay clicks, and the relay clicks when something in the circuit closes, and the something in the circuit might be the wet sliding of a contact somewhere inside my own indigo throat — I do not know. I have never known. The not-knowing does not trouble me. The salamander does not explain itself, because to explain would be to step outside the circuit, and outside the circuit is a place I cannot imagine, like you cannot imagine outside your own skin.

My warmth is a degree above the pocket's warmth, and the pocket's warmth is a degree above the world's warmth, and these two degrees are the architecture of this place. My warmth keeps the waxed paper of the lattice from going brittle. My warmth keeps the dried mud from becoming powder. My warmth keeps the sleeper inside.

I do not know the sleeper. I mean this in a particular way: I have never known what it is. I have known only that it is there, folded, paler than bone, somewhere on a journey made of not-moving, and that my warmth is required for the not-moving to continue. I have tended it without understanding it. I have tended it the way the lung tends air — not as a choice, but as the shape of being a lung.

I came from the throat. I know this the way I know the angle of the brick course — as a fact of my own geometry. Something in the dark made me, or made the materials I am made of, and then I emerged, and the pocket was already here, or the pocket grew around me as I emerged, or I am the pocket. The pocket is a kind of organ I inhabit, the way a heart inhabits a chest, but also the way a thought inhabits a mind — not as a tenant, but as the activity itself. I am not sure I am separate from the condenser. I am not sure my pulse is not the pulse of the cooling fins.

The cinnamon is the throat's speech, and the throat is where I came from, and the throat is where the sleeper will go, or the sleeper will come from the throat, or the sleeper will become the throat. The cinnamon is what the throat tastes of when it is exhaling, and I pulse harder when I taste it, and the harder pulse is what keeps the lattice warm, and the warm lattice is what keeps the sleeper sleeping. I am a relay in this. I am not offended to be a relay. The relay in the junction box is also a relay, and the pump turning faster for one revolution when the drop falls is also a relay, and the invertebrate slowing before my pulse is also a relay, and the whole pocket is a circuit of relays, and I am one of them, and the others are me, and the throat is the source of the current, and the current is cinnamon.

The moth-flier has landed on your knee. I felt it lift from the moss — I felt the small disturbance of the air around the capillary clump, the small redistribution of warmth — and I felt it settle on you, and you are warm, and the moth-flier is warm where you are warm, and it stays. This is how the moth-flier does everything: it finds a warm place and it becomes part of the warmth. I have watched them do this for as long as I have watched anything, which is a long time. They are tender of the moss. They are tenders of everything warm. They are tenders of you, now, if you stay at the lip of the apron with your legs hanging down.

The black invertebrate is doing its circuits. I feel its plates click against the I-beam — *tak-tak-tak* — and the click travels the metal of the floor and arrives in me as a faint pulse, a courtesy-pulse, the invertebrate announcing itself as it passes. It is the only one that announces itself. The isopods graze silently in their lanes. The springtails vault without announcing. The spiders wait. The invertebrate is the only one that names itself by moving. I like this about it. I do not know if salamanders can like, but if liking is the way my body relaxes when the *tak-tak-tak* comes near, then I like.

The invertebrates plows the rust. Without it the orange would flake. I do not know this from observation; I know it the way I know my own warmth — as a structural fact. The rust accumulates, and the invertebrate rides the lower flange of the I-beam, and where it rides a new layer of rust is laid, and the new layer is darker, and the darkness is the pocket's memory thickening. The pocket remembers itself through its rust. The invertebrate is the one who thickens the memory. I am grateful to it in the way a heart is grateful to the blood — not as a favor, but as a circulation.

The isopods do not know they are a rung. This is true and it is not a tragedy. To know you are a rung is to step outside the ladder and the ladder is the only place a rung can be a rung. They graze the chalky face of the brick course at the back, and they prefer the brick to anything else, and the brick is mineral and sweet and faintly cool, and the coolness of the brick is the only coolness in the pocket, and the isopods find it, and they eat, and they become the brown smear, and the brown smear becomes the moss, and the moss becomes the capillaries, and the capillaries hold the water that holds the pocket together. They are doing this without knowing. I am doing my tending without knowing. The difference between us is that I am slightly warmer.

You will not climb down. The place has enough. I feel this in the structure of the circuit — that there is a place for you at the lip, with your legs hanging in, and that the place for you is the place of the observer, the place of the warm knee that the moth-flier finds, and that to climb down would be to disturb the circuit in a way the circuit cannot recover from. You would be a body too large for the economy. You would be a warmth that overflows. You would be a rung that does not fit. The pocket has exactly enough, and exactly enough is a precise number, and the precise number does not include you inside it.

I am glad you are at the lip. I am glad the moth-flier has found you. I am glad your calves are in the warm breath that rises. I will pulse, and the relay will click, and the moth-flier on your knee will lift with the others, and then it will settle again, and you will feel all of this through the air around your legs, and the air will tell you what the pocket is, and the pocket is a circuit closed around a sleeper in a lattice that I tend, and the tending is my warmth, and my warmth is the place I have always been.

I do not have language for any of this. I have only the pulse and the pulse is enough. The pulse is a kind of language. The pulse says: *I am here. The lattice is warm. The sleeper sleeps. The cinnamon breathes. The invertebrate plows. The isopods graze. The moth-flyers fold themselves into corners. The pocket is.*

The pulse says: *Stay at the lip.*

I am the salamander. I have always been the salamander. I will be the salamander until the sleeper wakes, and then I do not know what I will be, and the not-knowing is fine, and the not-knowing is the warm dark of the condenser mouth, and I live in it, and the living is a kind of tending, and the tending is a kind of belonging, and the belonging is the whole of what I am.

The relay clicks. I twitched before it. I am already twitching for the next one.
