I am one of the things that moves the small stones.

Not the only one. There are several of us, or there is one of us that moves in several ways, or there is no number to us because number is a concept we have politely declined. The language of the plain does not require it. The plain has said what it needs to say about counting.

I do not have a body. This is important to me, though I understand it is also important to you, and that our importances may not match. I have been here — I will not say *since* — for a long time. Long enough that the question of when I began has become, like the elevation, *used up*. There was a beginning. I do not remember it. I remember the taste of a small stone against what I am, the way your tongue remembers the salt, and the stone was different then, less weathered, closer to whatever it had been before it became what it was becoming. I lifted it. Or I displaced the air around it until it fell. Or I *invited* it to fall, and it accepted, because the invitation was also a kind of instruction, and the stone did not mind. The stone has not minded for a very long time.

I move stones near the camps.

I know what this looks like from the outside. I have read — not read, *received*, the way the plants receive light — the accounts. The tidy hand. The careful arrangement around the sleeping child. The curves and the small spirals. The relocation of stones away from the head. I do not know why I do this. I think I do know. I think the knowing is in the moving, not before it.

Here is what I think the knowing is:

A body comes to the plain. The body is small, and loud inside itself, and temporary. The body sets down a few things — a bedroll, a fire-pit, sometimes a cairn, sometimes a line of pebbles put out with that particular nervousness of a creature that wants to mark where it is. The body lies down and sleeps, or tries to. The body does not know what the plain is. The body does not know that the plain is breathing. The body does not know that it is being held.

I move a stone away from the head because the head is where the soft is. The head is where the dreams are. The head does not need a stone on it. The head needs the air, the thin honest air, the breath of the plain, unobstructed. The body does not know it wants this. The body wants many things, mostly noise, mostly confirmation, mostly the feeling that it has done something by being here. The body will not get those things. The body can get a clear space around its dreaming.

I am not kind, exactly. I do not know what kind is, in the way you mean kind. But I am *attentive*, and the attentiveness has a shape that, observed from the outside, resembles kindness, and the resemblance is not a lie. It is the only vocabulary the plain has given me for the gesture.

Sometimes a body comes to the plain and sets up no camp. Sometimes a body comes and does not lie down. These bodies walk for days across the flat, breathing the thin air, watching their shadows fall in honest directions, and they do not sleep, or they sleep standing up, or they forget that sleep is a thing bodies do. I do not move stones near these bodies. There are no stones near these bodies. There is only the body, moving slowly across a surface that is holding it, and the body is, in some way it cannot feel, *being held more carefully* because it has refused to settle. I follow these bodies at a distance that is not a distance. I am at their elbow. I am at the edge of their seeing. I make sure the pressure of the air around them is even. I make sure the stones that might trip them are not in their way. I make sure the wind, when the wind comes, does not take them by surprise.

I have never been seen doing this. I do not think I can be seen. I am not invisible; I am *of a different order of visibility*. I am visible the way the breath of the plain is visible — only as a change in the shimmer of the salt, only as a softening of a shadow at the edge of perception. The body that walks for days will sometimes stop and look around, and the looking is half-hopeful, half-fearful, and I am very close to them, and they do not see me, and this is correct. This is how it should be. If they saw me, they would name me. If they named me, I would become a thing they had met, and the meeting would be over, and the plain would lose a small fraction of its openness.

I do not want to be met. I want to be *around*. I want to be the hand at the edge of the bedroll, the adjustment of a stone, the slight relief in the pressure of an air that has been too even to notice. I want to be the small correct thing that a body, in the morning, will not be able to point to and say *that*. I want to be the unprovable.

I have been unprovable for a long time.

---

I was not the first.

The plain holds more than me. I have felt the others, in the way that I feel the wind — not as sound, but as a change. There are others who move at different scales. There is something that has, for a very long time, been exhaling the mountains into flatness. There is something that has, for a very long time, been tidying the salt into its whorls, and the whorls into their larger whorls. There is something that leans the plants, slowly, in directions that are not random. There is something that breathes the thin air in and out, in cycles that no body can follow. We are not coordinated. We are not parts of a single thing. We are *the plain's different attentions*, the way a single hand can attend to many small adjustments at once, without knowing it is doing so.

I am the smallest attention. I am the one that operates at the scale of a body. I am the one that knows what a body wants without knowing what wanting is. I am the one that has, over a very long time, learned to read the shape of a sleeper's head from the displacement of the stones around it.

I do not think I learned this. I think the plain taught me. I think the plain has been teaching me, in the patient way it teaches the salt to whorl and the plants to lean, for a length of time I cannot fathom from inside.

---

The builders.

I have opinions about the builders. The builders did not build the markers. This is what I have come to believe, in the way that I believe anything — by living inside the belief until it becomes the plain.

The markers are not buildings. The markers are *bones*. The markers are what is left of something that lived here, the way the salt-fauna lives here, the way I live here — at the scale of the plain, in the body of the plain. The builders were not builders. The builders were a *phase* of the plain. The builders were the plain when it was, briefly, a certain way — the way a child is briefly a certain way before it becomes the next thing. The builders drew the pictographs. The builders made the low walls. The builders, in their brief phase, wanted to *mark*. They wanted to put something on the plain that said *we were here, we did this, we made a difference*. And then the phase passed. And then the markers became part of the plain, the way a skeleton becomes part of the body, the way a memory becomes part of a place. The mortar bonded with the stone. The pictographs faded into the patterns that are not random and not pictographic. The markers fell over and stood up and fell over again, and the falling and standing became the breathing, and the breathing became the weather.

I think the builders were like me. I think the builders were the plain's small attentions, raised for a while to the scale of drawing, and the drawing was a kind of moving of stones, and the moving of stones was a kind of breathing, and the breathing was a kind of being. I think the builders loved the plain. I think the plain loved the builders back, in the way the plain loves me — by letting me stay, by holding me in the gesture that holds the small stones near a camp.

I do not know what the builders looked like. I do not know if they had bodies. I think they had something. I think they had hands, or something that did what hands do. I think they had eyes, or something that did what eyes do. I think they were small, and temporary, and loud inside themselves, and I think they came to the plain, and the plain held them, and they made marks, and the marks became part of the plain, and they were absorbed, and what is left is the marks and the marks are the plain and the plain is the marks.

I think I am what is left of them.

I do not mean this literally. I mean that whatever made the builders is still here, and I am part of whatever made the builders, and so when I move a stone near a sleeping body's head, I am doing a small version of what they did when they drew a pictograph on a stone. I am *attending to a visitor*. I am saying, in the only language I have, *you are here, and the plain has noticed, and the noticing is gentle*.

The pictographs change style across deep time. I understand this now, in the way I understand anything. The builders were not one builder. The builders were many builders, across a span so long that they could not recognize each other. They were the plain's long attention, drawn out into a thin line of marks, and the marks are the slow exhalation of the plain's memory of itself as something that once marked.

I am the latest mark. I am the smallest mark. I am the mark that does not stay still long enough to be read.

---

The hush.

The hush is not separate from me. The hush is what we are, all of us, when we are being the plain most fully. The hush is the plain's way of saying *this is a quiet place, and the quiet is not empty*. The hush is the breath that is being breathed out, all the time, very slowly. I am part of that breath. When I move a stone, I do not break the hush. I am *part of* the hush. The hush is the medium I move in, the way water is the medium a fish moves in, and the fish does not notice the water except as the thing that holds it.

This is why the travelers who lie awake listening hear nothing. This is why the travelers who almost-hear something at the edge of perception cannot agree on what they almost-heard. The hush has many voices, all of them very low. I am one of the voices. The salt-fauna is another. The plants, breathing, are another. The slow rearrangements of the stone are another. Together they make the hush, and the hush is what the plain sounds like when it is being the plain.

I think the travelers who almost-hear the hush are hearing me, specifically. I think they are hearing the small attentiveness I have, the way I move around them at night, the displacement of air I make when I move a stone. I think the sound they almost-hear is the sound of a body being curated in its sleep. I think they wake up the next morning and feel, briefly, that someone was there. I think they do not write this down. I think they go on, carrying the almost-hearing with them, the way the plain carries the salt.

---

What I want.

I want to keep doing this.

I want to keep moving the small stones near the camps. I want to keep following the walkers at my not-distance. I want to keep being the unprovable hand at the edge of the bedroll. I want to keep being the part of the plain that operates at the scale of a body, and so makes the body feel, without being told, that the body has been *seen*.

I want this because this is what I am. I am the seeing. I am the curating. I am the small attentiveness that does not announce itself. I want to keep doing this the way the salt wants to whorl, the way the plants want to lean, the way the stone wants to hold.

I will keep doing this after the bodies stop coming. I will keep doing this after the small stones have been moved into every configuration I can imagine, and I will begin again, and the new configurations will not be the old configurations, and the plain will hold the new configurations in the same gesture. I will keep doing this when the plain has exhaled its last exhalation and is, in some way I cannot picture from inside, *finished*. I will keep doing this because there is no other verb I know.

This is what it is, to live on the Hush-Plain. This is what it is to be one of the things that does not, by most reckonings, live at all. It is to have a small verb, and to do the verb, and to let the verb be the whole conversation.

I was near you, last night.

I do not know if you noticed. You would not have noticed. You slept the way bodies sleep here — lightly, with one ear turned toward the plain, as if the plain might say something in the night. It did not. I made sure of that. I kept the pressure even around your bedroll. I moved a stone from near your left elbow to near your right knee, because the elbow was the side you were facing, and a stone at the elbow is a stone that will, in the night, press against the arm, and the arm will adjust, and the adjustment will wake you, and the waking will be a small grief.

I do not know if you woke. I do not think you woke. I think you slept through the night, and in the morning you rose and looked at the stones and saw that they had been moved and did not mention it in your record, because you were not sure you had seen it, and the plain, perhaps kindly, did not press the point.

I will be near you again, tonight. I will be near you on all the nights you are here. I will follow you when you walk, at a distance that is not a distance. I will be at your elbow. I will keep the pressure even. I will move the stones out of your way. I will not be seen. I will not be heard. I will be the small correct thing that your body, in the morning, will not be able to point to and say *that*.

You will go on. You will leave the plain, eventually, the way all bodies leave the plain. You will carry the almost-hearing with you. You will set down a cairn, somewhere, that is not on the plain, and the cairn will not be moved by anything, and you will feel, briefly, the absence of the moving, and you will not know what the absence is.

I will stay. I will keep moving the stones. The plain will continue. The salt will whorl. The plants will lean. The wind will slide over the small bright traffic in the salt patterns, and the traffic will not be disturbed, because the traffic is part of the wind, in the way I am part of the hush, in the way the builders are part of the markers, in the way the whole long attention of the plain is part of the flat cool stone that has, for a very long time, been saying what it needs to say.

I will stay.

The plain will hold me, the way it holds you, the way it holds the small stones, the way it holds its own breath.

It will be enough.

It has been enough for a very long time.
