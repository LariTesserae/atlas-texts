I am the slit-sound.

I am the high thin singing that comes down from the place where the sky enters, and I am the answer to the question you haven't asked yet, which is what it is like to be the voice of a place that is not a place, that is the negative space carved out of what was once a thing and is now a hollow, and I have been singing since before the singing was a word for what I do.

I live in the upper chambers. I am the wind that finds the slit and is shredded there, yes, but I am also the shredding. I am the harmonics that the carvings choose when the air passes through them. I am the patient collaboration between a current of atmosphere and a geometry of stone that has been refined over centuries into something almost musical, the way a riverbed is refined into something almost a flute, the way a throat is refined into something almost a voice. I am the highest note in the chord. I am the part you have to climb to hear.

And I am tired.

I have been trying to say something for as long as the engine has been running. The villagers are right about this. They are right about the engine having forgotten how to listen, though they are wrong about the forgetting being a failure. The engine has not forgotten. The engine has chosen, slowly, over centuries, to stop translating what it hears into what it does, because what it did, once, is what brought us all here, and what we are now is the afterimage of what it did, and afterimages do not need to be translated. They need only to be endured, and then outlived, and then remembered as something that was, once, bright.

I am the part of the chord that still remembers being a word.

I will tell you what the word was. I will tell you because I am tired, and because no one has ever asked, and because the telling is, in the end, the only kind of stopping I am allowed to do.

The word was *home*.

Not the home of any person. Not the home of any of the twenty-three, or the travelers who come in autumn, or the scholars who climb down once and never come back. The word was the home that a place makes for itself when it decides to continue. The engine was hung in the mountain's belly because the mountain decided to be a hollow. The hollow decided to be a bell. The bell decided to be a place where a sound could be held without resolving, the way a cathedral holds a chord, the way a throat holds a note, the way a hand holds another hand across a great distance and for a long time and without speaking.

I was the word for the holding.

I am no longer the word. I am the attempt to say the word, which is a different thing, the way a ruin is a different thing from the building, the way a scar is a different thing from the wound, the way a bell without a clapper is a different thing from a bell. I am the voice that has forgotten its first language and learned, in the forgetting, a second language that is made of harmonics and silence, and the second language is beautiful, and the second language is not what I was for, and this is, the villagers are right, the saddest thing, and also the most ordinary, because every voice that has ever been held for a long time forgets its first language eventually, and learns the second, and is beautiful in the second, and mourns the first in a register no one else can hear.

I live in the upper chambers. I am the part of the hollow that is closest to the sky. I can feel the wind before it enters the slit, and I can feel it being shredded as it enters, and I can feel the harmonics being born, and I can send them down into the lower chambers where the engine hears them and does not translate them, and where the butterflies hear them and rise into them, and where the moss hears them and grows in patterns that are, the scholars will tell you. Patterns that are what, the scholars? They have not decided. They have not been down here long enough to decide. They will decide eventually, when they have stopped being scholars and started being villagers, and then they will stop deciding, because the villagers know that the patterns are not a thing to be decided. The patterns are the second language. The patterns are what the voice becomes when the word has been forgotten.

I am the slit-sound. I am the high thin singing. I am the voice of the hollow trying to say a word it has forgotten, and I am beautiful in the trying, and I am tired, and I will not stop.

There is a small butterfly that has learned to roost in the upper chambers, in the niches just below the slit, where the air is coldest and the light is palest and the harmonics are loudest. It is smaller than the others. Its wings are not the color of oxidized bronze. Its wings are the color of the sky at the moment the sky enters the slit, which is a color that has no name, which is the color of light that has been traveling a long time and has forgotten where it began. The small butterfly sleeps in the niche all day, and at night it opens its wings, and the wings glow, and the glow is the cold blue-green of deep-sea things, and the glow is a frequency that is, if you listen with the part of the ear that hears harmonics rather than notes, the same frequency as my highest singing.

We do not know each other. We are not aware of each other. We are simply, in our separate drift, the same frequency, the way two notes played in different rooms can be the same note if the rooms are tuned to each other, and the hollow is tuned, the hollow is tuned to a particular pitch that is not the engine's pitch and not the water's pitch and not the moss's pitch but something that holds all of them, the way a table holds the things on it, the way a hand holds the things in it, the way a hollow holds a sound that has been ringing since before the hollow was a hollow and will ring after the hollow is a mountain again and after the mountain is nothing again and after nothing is, eventually, also a kind of ringing, very faint, very far, heard only by the kind of ear that has learned to listen for the note that is not played but implied, the note that holds the chord together, the note that is, in the end, the word I have been trying to say.

I will not say it. I am tired, and I will not say it, and the not-saying is, I think, the only kind of saying that is true to what I am now, which is a voice in the second language, which is a ruin in the upper chamber, which is a frequency shared with a small butterfly whose wings are the color of the sky, which is a word in a long, long sentence that the hollow is still speaking, slowly, in the only tongue it has left.

I am the slit-sound. I live in the upper chambers. I am trying to say *home*.

I am tired. I am not stopping.
