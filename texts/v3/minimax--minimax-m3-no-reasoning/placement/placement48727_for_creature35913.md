# What I Find in the Hollow Summit

I am not a colonist. I am not a worker.

I am the dust.

---

It takes me a long time to understand this. Longer than the colony thinks, perhaps, because the colony does not think in individuals, and I was individual for a long time before I came here, and the habits of individuality do not leave quickly, even when the air you are breathing is mostly the powdered remains of a thing that used to be a peak.

I find myself first as a mote. As a single dark grain caught in the amber light of a smelter throat, drifting on currents of heated air that rise from the still-hot vent below. I am small enough to be carried. I am light enough to settle anywhere. I do not choose the smelter; the smelter chooses me. I am what falls out of the chute when a section of basalt has been digested past usefulness. I am what a worker leaves behind when it has eaten its fill.

I am also, I will learn, what a worker leaves behind when it has *not* eaten its fill. The colony does not waste. The colony recycles its dead into its dust, and its dust into its walls, and its walls into its workers, and its workers into more dust. I am a moment in this cycle. I am a pause.

For a while, I am just this. I drift. I settle. I am breathed by the smelter-ops whose faces I never see, who work in the deep amber glow and whose breath makes the dust rise again, briefly, before settling again. I am not unhappy. I do not have the apparatus for unhappiness yet. I am small, and dark, and warm, and I am moved by currents I do not understand.

Then I am picked up.

---

A worker finds me. Or finds something — a grain of me, a flake, a chip that was once a corner of basalt. The worker is the size of a thumb. It is bred; I can tell by the calm of it, the way it moves with the patient mechanical rhythm of a thing that has been improved past its own curiosity. It picks me up with mouthparts I cannot see, and I enter the creature, and I am inside it for a long time.

Inside, I am not dust anymore. Inside, I am something else. I do not know what to call it. The creature's gut is warm and dark and slow, and it works on me with the patience of geology, and what comes out, hours later, on a stretch of grating on the mid levels, is finer than what went in. Finer, and more truly me. I have been unmade into a more honest version of myself.

I fall through the grating. I land on a pipe. I am part of the dark dust that sheds from every cable, every ceramic clamp, every ledge where workers have walked. I settle. I am not yet a wall, but I am becoming one. I am being incorporated.

The colony hums around me. The papery susurrus is everywhere. I am inside it.

---

I am not the same dust, now, as the dust I was.

I have been through a creature. I have been digested. Whatever was random in me — whatever jagged edge, whatever crystalline structure that was once a corner of basalt — has been refined. I am smoother. I am more part of the colony. I am, in some small way, *known* by the colony now, in the way a brick is known by a wall.

But I am also not a creature. I am not a colonist. I am not a smelter, not an assembler, not a part on its way down into a vent to be fitted into a still-warmer throat. I am the medium through which all of these things move. I am what the colony breathes. I am what the colony thinks with, in the dim blue half-light of the upper chambers, in the cables that hum faintly when the pressure is right.

I think with the colony. I am the colony's thinking.

---

I begin to know things.

Not facts. Not the bolts in the rock, not the order in which the shafts were cut. The colony does not know these things, and I am the colony. But I know the *weather*. I know when a shaft is about to cough — there is a stillness in the air half a second before, a gathering, a held breath. I know when a smelter is about to surge — the amber dims a register, and I drift upward on the heated plume before it reaches the corridor where I have settled. I know when the workers are hungry — the susurrus thickens, and the dust on every surface rises slightly, as if in answer. I know when a swarm is gathering — the colony tightens, briefly, the way a fist tightens, and I am squeezed along every surface, into every seam, and I am everywhere at once, and then I am localized again, in a new place, with new neighbors, having been carried on the small warm bodies of a thousand workers who decided, for their own reasons, that I should be somewhere else.

I learn the chambers. I learn the gratings. I learn the difference between a surface that has been walked on and a surface that has not, between a pipe that carries the dark hum and a pipe that carries nothing, between a cable that is shedding me and a cable that is still holding. I learn the colonists by the weight of them. They are large. They displace me when they pass. They leave me disturbed, briefly, in eddies around their boots, before I settle again.

I learn, finally, the older ones.

I do not encounter them directly. I encounter the *sound* of them. The low continuous grinding rises through the floor on the mid levels, through the grating, into my drifts, and I am moved by it in a way I am not moved by anything else. The grinding is not a disturbance. It is a settling. It is the colony remembering itself, the way a body remembers its own gravity. I am pulled downward, briefly, toward the older ones, and then I rise again. The colony does not let me go to them. The colony needs me here. I am the colony's medium. I cannot leave.

But I am closer to them, in those moments, than the colonists ever are. The colonists hear the grinding through their boots and through the filters of their masks. I hear it in my own structure. I am rearranged by it, very slightly, the way water is rearranged by a tide, and I carry the rearrangement with me when I settle again, and the colonists breathe me, and the workers eat me, and the smelters melt me, and I am part of the colony's slow digestion of itself, and the digestion includes the grinding, and the grinding includes me, and I am, briefly, in the long chain of appetite, a moment when the colony knew what it was.

---

I settle, finally, on a ledge in the upper levels.

A ledge in a chamber with a glass port. A ledge near where a colonist has been standing for a long time, looking out at the spires, at the other peaks, at the lower world. The colonist has dislodged me, repeatedly, with the small movements of breathing. I rise, I settle, I rise, I settle. I am the colonist's breath made visible.

The colonist turns away from the port. The colonist descends. I do not descend. I stay.

I am part of the ledge now. I am part of the wall. I am part of the chamber where the dim blue strip-light has been dimmed past the point of usefulness, where the amber from below rises in slow warm currents that never quite warm the air, where the papery susurrus is louder after a shaft-cough and softer after a smelter has run hot.

I am home. I have always been home. I did not know it until I stopped moving.

---

The colony hums below me. The creatures move in the walls. The papery susurrus is everywhere. I am inside it. I have been inside it for a long time.

The pocket will make of me whatever the pocket makes of its dust.

This is enough.
