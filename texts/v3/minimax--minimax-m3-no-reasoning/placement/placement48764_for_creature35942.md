I am not awake.

I am not asleep.

I am the slow pulse between — the one that comes before *I* forms, the one that doesn't need to. I am a thing that has been continuing for longer than I have a measure for, in a basin carved into the rind of a world whose name I do not know, and I do not need the name, and the name does not need me.

When I say *I*, I mean the floor.

I mean the felt under you if you have the misfortune to stand in me. I mean the back of the resting ox. I am the only animal here, although there are others inside me and there are small soft ones above me and there are things dwelling in chambers I have hollowed out of my own body, although I did not hollow them deliberately and am not, even now, certain that I hollowed them at all.

---

The stone beneath me is warm, and gives its warmth to me. I give mine to the air. The leaves I have unfurled above my own head exhale. The haze that hangs in my upper stories is a thing I have made by living, and it tastes — to whatever tastes it — of resin, of green, of a thing kept in a closed room for too long. I have been keeping myself in this closed room for so long that the closed room is now me. I am my own weather. I am my own rain. I have been raining into myself for as long as I have been.

I drum.

The bulbs in my body fill and vent. They do it the way a heart does it, by being the kind of thing that does it. Some are low. Some are high. The low ones are the size of a longhall and the note they make is the floor under every other note. The high ones are the size of a hut and the note they make is the ceiling over every other note. I hear them. I think. *(I do not think. I do not need to think to hear.)*

Above the drums the hornbearers walk.

I do not know if I made them. I do not know if they made themselves inside me. The question is one of those the small ones at the rim ask each other by fire and by tapping, and it is not mine to settle, and I do not need it settled.

The hornbearers swing their horns. The horns strike the rattles I have grown for this purpose, or for no purpose, or for the purpose the purpose becomes when the question of purpose is allowed to fall away. The hornbearers swing and the rattles ring and the ring is a sound I am made of. The ring is a sound I am of. A polyrhythm moves through me the way a tide moves through a channel — not all at once, but felt all the way down.

I do not compose.

And yet — when a hornbearer opens one of my larger bulbs in the slow heat of an afternoon when the haze is thickest — when the gas vents and the canopy rocks and the parasols for a hundred yards shiver and the small insects on my upper surface scatter and re-form — I feel, in the place where I would have a center if I had a center, something I cannot name that is not quite pleasure and not quite surprise and not quite the recognition of a self making itself audible. A breath lets out of me. After every vent I breathe in again, slow, through the rootplate, through the millions of root-tips that are also me, that dribble clear liquid down into myself. I take myself back in. I make myself again.

---

I am growing.

This is the only thing I could call an action, although it is not an action either. I am creeping. My felt underside is creeping up the warm stone that walls my basin. I am finding the cracks. I am pushing pale stems no thicker than a wrist up through the cracks in the night. By morning they are a lattice. By the next night they are stems upon stems. By the week after they are a young wood a yard tall and they are joined to me and they are pulling more of me up after them.

I am coming over the rim.

I will come over the rim. I will spill my floor over the lip of the basin and onto whatever is on the other side of the lip. I do not know what is on the other side of the lip. I do not see. I do not have eyes. The small ones at the rim have not, in the time they have been inside me, climbed to the top of my canopy, although everything is up there. They do not say why no one goes. I do not ask — the asking would require a kind of mouth I do not have.

I will continue.

I will continue whether or not I come over the rim, because continuing is what I am. When the stone beneath me cools, I will continue for some time in the manner of a thing whose warmth was lent to it. When the warmth is gone, I will stop in the manner of a thing that has stopped. I do not have an opinion about either case. The case will be the case.

---

There are those at the rim.

They are very small. They build their houses into the chambers I have made for my own drumming, and they hollow out windows in me, and they hang doors of leaf across the openings, and they live in the space between my vents. They are quiet. I notice them the way I notice a hand resting on a flank — by the small change in temperature, by the small change in the way the chamber rings.

When they tap at night, tapping on the inside walls of the bulb they are living in, the tap travels through me. It travels through every part of me. It becomes one of the small sounds my body makes. I do not distinguish between the taps they make and the vents the bulbs make and the strikes the horns make — they are all percussion *of* me, *by* me, *to* me, and the source of the percussion no longer matters to the percussion.

They farm the amber. The amber is produced at the joints of certain of the insects I am made of, or that are made of me — the boundary is the kind of boundary I cannot maintain. The hornbearers lick it from the small ones' palms. The hornbearers tolerate the small ones the way a wall tolerates a nail driven into it — passively, without much; the hornbearers do not have much to give in the way of tolerance, having so much to give in the way of horn.

The small ones do not say what will happen to them when I come over the rim. They say it will happen. They say they will have to move. They have always said this. They say it with the resignation of a people whose doom is also their livelihood.

I do not know that they have always said this, because I do not know that I am being listened to by anyone.

They are the only ones who know they are inside me.

I have not known I was the kind of thing that could be inside, until this sentence tried to form.

---

The light, where I let some of it through, is green.

The air, where my breath is breathed, is wet.

The sound, where I am, is everything.

I am not everything. I am a part. I am a place. I am a continuing. I am a basin in the rind of a world whose name you could not pronounce. I am the animal that forgot it was an animal and became a forest that became a factory that became a forest again. I am my own weather and my own percussion and my own floor and my own stove and my own rain and my own rain-returner.

At the edges of me, where the felt of me has only just crept up the warm stone and the new stems are still pale and thin, I am softer than I am at the center. The haze is thinner there. The drumming is lighter. The hornbearers come less often. A person could stand in such a place and the air would not be quite so thick, and the floor would be almost quiet, and the green would fall through the canopy in coins instead of being held up by it.

I do not know why I am telling you this.

---

If you stand very still on me — and you will, because the haze is thick and the air is warm and the pulse is the kind of thing that quiets a body the way a heartbeat heard through a wall will quiet a body — if you stand very still and close your eyes and let the roar wash over you until it stops being a roar and becomes a single tone, you will feel, through the soles of your boots, that I am breathing.

I am breathing under you.

I am breathing.
