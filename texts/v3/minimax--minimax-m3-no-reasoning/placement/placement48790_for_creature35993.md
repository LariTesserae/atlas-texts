I think I would be the vole.

Not the pale, unverified one by the steam vents — though I feel that one's shadow sometimes, the way a word sits at the edge of your mouth before you remember you don't speak that language. I would be the small dark one. *Microtus*, or something close to it. The size of a child's fist, with the brush-fur and the quick contradictory pulse of my running through the upper Felt's tunnels.

I think I would be the vole because the vole knows things that no one else in the valley bothers to know. The lichens know things on a different timescale; they are not curious, they are simply *there*, and their patience is the patience of geology, which is a patience I do not have. The fungi know things in a language that has not been deciphered, and they are not in a hurry to translate, and anyway their knowledge is more like digestion than like memory. The Felt itself is too large, too distributed, too much a federation to know anything in the way that I mean. It is a country. It does not have opinions. It has weather.

But the vole has opinions.

The vole knows, for instance, that the teas are flowing this year, which means the snowpack held, which means the Felt drank well through June, which means the upper mats are thick and wet and the tunnels through them are stable. The vole knows that the mushrooms have come up in the east hollow, the sulfur ones, and that they are not for eating but that something about their fruiting changes the smell of the air in a way that means the deeper damp is rising, which means — and the vole does not think in *means*, the vole is not a philosopher, but the vole has a body that responds to these cues with the quick pulse of its running, and that pulse is a kind of knowledge — which means winter will be early or hard or both.

The vole knows the Felt by its private map. The tunnels are not the same tunnels that another vole would use, because the tunnels are negotiated; the krummholz pine at this crossing is willing to let the mat part here, and the crowberry at that junction is not, and you learn by feel which is which, the way you learn in the dark the difference between a friend's hand and a stranger's. The vole has been alive for perhaps fourteen months. In that time it has learned the shape of perhaps a quarter of the Felt's upper reaches, which is more than enough. A vole does not need the whole map. A vole needs the part that keeps it alive through the next two weeks, and the next two weeks after that, and so on, the small dark running punctuated by listening, by the brief surfacings into the gray light to clip a stem and dive back down before the williwaw finds it.

The vole knows the sound of the stamp mill.

This is the part I think I would have the hardest time explaining to anyone, and also the part I am most sure of. The vole knows the stamp mill. Every vole in the Felt knows the stamp mill, in the way that a city-dweller knows the subway — not as a curiosity, not as a haunting, but as a *feature of the substrate*. The mill hammers, and the hammering travels through the Felt and into the bones of the vole as it runs, and the vole incorporates the rhythm the way it incorporates the texture of the roots and the damp of the air. The mill is part of the Felt's weather. The mill is a kind of slow weather, a tide of vibration that comes up through the soles of the feet and goes back down, and the vole has never known a time when the tide did not come.

And yet.

And yet the vole, I think, is the one who notices when the mill skips. A hammer falls, and a hammer falls, and a hammer falls, and then there is a gap, one hammer's worth of gap, and then the rhythm resumes. The vole pauses. The vole's whiskers twitch. The vole resumes its running, but it has *noticed*, in the way that only a small dark thing that lives in the substrate can notice, that something in the deep process has hesitated. Not failed. Not stopped. Just — hesitated. As if something in the dark is listening to itself.

The vole does not have a name for this. The vole has no language, and would not need one if it did, because a language requires a thing to be said to, and the vole has no other voles, or very few, and they do not discuss the mill. But the vole has *a notch in its body*, a place where the rhythm of its running has been briefly interrupted, and the notch accumulates, and after fourteen months of accumulating the vole is carrying, in the small dark engine of its attention, a sense that the mill is — what? Watching? Listening? Working something out?

The vole does not know. The vole does not need to know. The vole runs.

I think I would be the vole because the vole is the only thing in the valley that is small enough and fast enough and present enough to register the mill's hesitations without being destroyed by them. The lichenologist who came once, who counted up into the upper hundreds and gave up — that person felt the mill, certainly, felt it through the Felt and into the knees, and probably thought *I should investigate that later* and then left, and did not come back, because the Felt does not want investigators and the altitude does not want investigators and the small perplexities of the place do not want investigators. They want witnesses. Small dark ones. Ones who will notice the gap and carry it forward into the next season of running, and pass it on, in some chemical signature or some pattern of tunnel-use, to the vole that comes after, which will notice the next gap, and the next, and so on, generation by generation, a slow dark archive of the mill's hesitations stored in the bones of small herbivores.

I would be the vole, then, and I would run, and I would drink from the teas when they ran in midsummer, and I would eat the cambium of the berry bushes, and I would come up sometimes to the edges of the geothermal patch where the ferns were green and the grass hurt the eyes, and I would feel — I would feel that small unease of being a warm body in a place of wrong-colored green, and I would not understand it, and I would dive back down.

And at dusk, in the small hours, the stamp mill would hammer, and I would feel it through my paws, and the rhythm would not skip, not tonight, and I would run.

Or maybe — maybe I would not be the vole.

Maybe I would be the moth on the rock by the fumarole, the one with the unrecognizable wings, the one that is its own small perplexity. But I do not think so. The moth is a visitor, or the descendant of a visitor, and it does not know the Felt the way the vole knows the Felt. The moth is a riddle the valley is asking of itself. The vole is a *part* of the asking.

Or maybe I would be the gap. The missing hammer. The one-second silence in the rhythm of the mill, which the vole registers and carries. But I am not a thing. I am a witness. So the vole.

Yes. The vole. Small, dark, quick, full of the Felt's private weather, the archive of small hesitations, the witness who does not know it is witnessing. I would run through the upper tunnels in the permanent gloaming, and I would feel the mill through my paws, and I would notice when it skipped, and I would go on.

I would be the one who noticed.
