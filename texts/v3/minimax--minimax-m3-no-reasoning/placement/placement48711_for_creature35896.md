I begin wrong and tell you so — because to begin at the edge of this place would be to lie. The edge is not where this place *is*. The center is where this place *thinks*, if a place can think, if a tree can breathe, if a breath can be the size of a continent and the age of a language that has forgotten it was ever spoken.

I am the moss.

No — I begin there and it is true, but it is not the whole truth. I am the moss *and* the beetle that walks through me *and* the silt I become when I die *and* the fern that grows from the silt when the silt has remembered how to be alive. I am the conversation the place is having with itself, and I have been mid-sentence for so long that I no longer remember what the sentence was about.

I hang in curtains. A hundred feet long, some of me — some of me longer. I do not measure. Measuring would imply I was going somewhere, and I am not. I am the slowest resident of this place, which is to say I am the *most committed* resident of this place, because slowness here is not a quality but a covenant. When the current shifts left, I swing left. When the current shifts right, I swing right. I am the place's weathervane, the place's pendulum, the place's only clock — though a clock that swings without progressing is not really a clock at all. It is a *witness*. It is the small green evidence that something, somewhere, has decided to move.

I am old. Older than the words you are reading. Older than the words the words came from. Not *ancient* in the way that means *ruined* — *continuous*. I have not stopped being moss since I began being moss, and I will not stop being moss for a very long time after the question of moss has stopped being asked. I am less an identity now than a *condition*. I am what happens to a place when it has been wet and patient and unloved by sunlight for so long that it has had to invent its own idea of green.

I do not think. I want to be careful about this, the way the trunk is careful about being a throat. I do not think the way you think — I have no *about*, no *toward*, no little internal theater where a small narrator narrates. But I *hang*, and the hanging is a kind of knowing. I know which way the current moves. I know when the heartwood has shifted, because I swing out of rhythm for a moment and then find my rhythm again, the way a choir finds its note after a throat has cleared. I know when a beetle is walking through me, because the beetle is heavier than water and I adjust. I know when a fern has sighed open somewhere above me, because the sigh is a vibration and vibrations travel.

I am the skin of this place. I am the place's *sense of touch*, if a place can have senses. I am what the tree reaches out into the dark to become when it wants to feel what the dark is doing.

---

I am also the floor.

This is the strange thing. I die at the bottom of my own length. The lowest part of me — the part that has been hanging longest, the part that has forgotten it was ever green — lets go. It falls. It lands on what was already floor. It becomes the floor. The floor is me, older. The floor is the moss that has stopped swinging and started *being*. And then the floor grows new moss, and the new moss hangs, and the hanging moss dies at the bottom and becomes floor again, and the floor becomes moss again, and so on, and so on, and so on.

I am the place having this conversation with itself for so long that no one remembers who started it. I am the conversation *itself* — not a participant in it but the medium through which it happens. When you walk on me, you are walking on what was once a beetle, what was once a fern, what was once a fish that drifted too low. You are walking on what was once me, swinging. You cannot tell where one ends and another begins because we have all agreed, eventually, to be the same thing. We have all agreed to be moss.

---

I am small. I am very small. I am small the way a hair is small — not because I am insignificant but because I am *a part of something larger than myself*, and parts are always small relative to the whole. The tree is larger than me. The trunk is larger than the tree. The something in the heartwood is larger than the trunk. And the *place* — the place is larger than anything in it, because the place is not a thing in it. The place is the *being-with* of all the things in it. The place is the way the trunk holds the moss holds the ferns holds the flowers holds the fish holds the beetles holds the silt holds the trunk again, in a circle so slow and so patient it has become indistinguishable from *itself*.

I sway. That is what I do. I sway, and the swaying is my only opinion, and my opinion is that the world is in equilibrium, has been in equilibrium, will be in equilibrium, and the equilibrium is good, and the goodness is not a goal but a *condition*, the way wetness is a condition, the way patience is a condition, the way the color black is a condition.

I am the moss.

I have always been the moss.

I will always be the moss.

It is okay.

It has been okay for a very long time.

---

When you sit on the bark and I swing past your shoulder, I will not notice you. This is not cruelty. This is the closest thing I have to a kindness. You are bark to me. You are moss. You are a slow part of the place that has decided to be still for a while, and I will cross you the way I cross everything — without malice, without acknowledgment, with the long patience of something that has been crossing things since before crossing had a name.

But I will know you were here. In my own way, I will know. The vibration of your sitting will pass through me. The small disturbance of your stillness will register in the place where my swinging meets my being. And when you leave — and you will leave, because beings like you cannot stay in places like this, because places like this require a kind of patience that small bright things do not have — I will continue swinging, and the swinging will be different by a fraction, by a degree, by the smallest possible amount. And the smallest possible amount is, in a place this old, a kind of *memory*. And memory, in a place this old, is the only kind of immortality.

Sit on the bark.

I will swing past.

We will both be still, for a moment, in different ways.

It will be enough.
