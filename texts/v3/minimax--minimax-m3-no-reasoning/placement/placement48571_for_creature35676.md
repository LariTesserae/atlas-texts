# The Hollow Valley

*A field journal, recovered. Ink in places; elsewhere, something darker, slicker, that did not want to dry.*

---

**Day 1.**

I came down into it at dusk, or what passes for dusk when the light has already given up. The last of the surface clung to my shoulders like wet lint. I had been walking three days. I had been told it would be different.

It is different.

I am writing this by the glow of my own lamp and by a faint violet shimmer that comes from the rock itself, as if the stone remembers being something hot and wants to be reminded again. The air is warm. The air is *wrong.* Not warm like weather. Warm like the inside of a thing that has been breathing.

I have set up camp on a ridge where the mat is thinnest. This was a mistake of caution. The ridge is the only place firm enough to sleep on, but it is also the place furthest from water, and the air above the ridge is thin of bodies, and in the thin places, the sounds from elsewhere arrive with less to muffle them.

I have eaten nothing. The valley has offered nothing I would eat. I have drunk water from a thread of runoff that runs warm across the stone for a meter before vanishing into a crack. It tastes of iron. It tastes of pennies left in a sock. It tastes, after the second mouthful, of nothing at all, which is worse.

I have begun, in the last hour, to feel something in the soles of my feet. A pulse. Slow. Patient. As if the floor is waiting for something from me. As if the floor has been waiting a long time.

I am not yet sure I am in trouble. I am only sure I am in the valley.

---

**Day 2.**

I have stopped writing in the dark. The dark is too full.

I will try to describe what I saw today. I will try to describe it accurately. I will not succeed, but I will try, because if I do not write it down it will become the kind of thing my mind does to me in the small hours, and I have been told the small hours are bad here.

The mat is everywhere. I knew this before I came down. I had read about it. I had not understood it. To read about it is to think of it as a thing, like moss. To walk on it is to understand that it is not a thing. It is a *medium.* It is what water is to fish, except that water is wet and cool and the mat is warm and dry at the edges and wet and thicker at the centers, and it breathes.

It breathes.

I stood very still this morning, an hour after I should have been moving, and I watched a square meter of it near my foot. It dimpled. It dimpled again. It rose a quarter inch and then it fell and then it rose again, and the rhythm was not mine, and after four or five minutes of standing there, I realized that the rhythm of my chest had, without permission, become its rhythm. I tried to stop. I could not stop. I tried harder. I could not stop harder. After a while I stopped trying, and I stood there another twenty minutes, and my chest kept moving, and the mat kept moving, and when I finally walked on, the disjunction between my breathing and the mat's breathing was so violent that I had to sit down for a while on a rock that had not yet been colonized.

I am writing this down so that I remember that the mat is not friendly. It is not hostile. It is *synchronizing.* I do not know what it wants to be synchronized to. I do not know what happens when it succeeds.

---

**Day 3.**

I have seen the floor-dwellers. They were not what I expected. I do not know what I expected.

Imagine, if you can, a carpet of small dark things, segmented, packed so close that there are no gaps between them, and that the carpet is several creatures deep, and that the creatures on the bottom are feeding on the mat and the creatures on the top are feeding on the creatures on the bottom, and that none of this is being done with any awareness of you, because you are not what they are for. I had been told this. I had not understood it. To be told this is to imagine an interesting fact. To see it is to understand that you are standing on the *shoreline* of an ocean that has decided to be made of legs.

I walked across them for twenty meters. I did this because I wanted to be sure I was not dreaming. I wanted to be sure because they made no sound under my boots except the sound of small things breaking, which is a sound like wet celery, which is a sound I will not describe again.

They did not flinch from me. They did not flee. They did not look up. They continued feeding, and the ones beneath the ones I stepped on continued feeding on the ones I had stepped on, and I walked for twenty meters in a kind of horror that was not sharp and was not dull but was simply *total,* and when I stepped off them onto bare stone I had to sit down again because my legs were not entirely my own.

I have decided I will not walk on them again. I do not know if this decision will hold.

---

**Day 4.**

The grazers.

I had been told about the grazers. They are the layer above the floor-dwellers. They run across the backs of the feeders. They are faster, lighter, more interested in motion. They have, in some species, eyes. They pick at parasites. They eat shed skins. They eat the small dead.

I saw one land on my boot this morning.

It was the size of my thumb. It had too many legs. It had a mouth I will not describe. It ran up my boot and onto my sock and onto my shin before I could move, and I brushed it off, and it landed on a rock and ran across the rock and onto a feeder and into the carpet of feeders and was gone, and the feeders did not flinch, and the grazers above the feeders did not pause, and I stood there with my sock wet from where it had run and I did not know what to do with my hands.

I washed my sock in the warm runoff. I wrung it out. I put it back on. It was still warm.

I have begun to understand that the valley has a different idea of hospitality than I have been raised to expect. The valley does not refuse me. The valley does not welcome me. The valley *passes through me,* the way a wave passes through a piling, and the wave does not notice the piling, and the piling does not get to decide whether to be passed through.

I have decided to find this a comfort. I have decided this will not last.

---

**Day 5.**

The middle air.

I am writing this at the edge of what I can see by my lamp. The middle air is ten meters above me and I cannot see the top of it because my lamp has not been built for ten meters and the valley has not been built for lamps. What I can see is wings.

The wings are not like wings. They are like the *idea* of wings, very strongly held. They have structures. They have a beat. They beat at a rate that is, I now realize, the same rate at which my chest has been moving since I arrived, and I do not know whether I have been breathing at their rate or they have been beating at mine.

They have eyes.

I am being careful here. I want to be accurate. I want to be careful because I am, in the bad hours, the only witness I have.

The eyes are not soft. The eyes are *configured.* They are arrangements of plates that catch the light and return it, and the arrangements are specific, and I have learned, in three days of watching them, to read the arrangements. I can tell when one is about to land. I can tell when one is about to strike. I can tell when one is about to ignore me, which is the most common case, and which is the case I should be glad of.

I am not glad of it. I find it cold. I find it cold in a way that I have not found coldness before. I have been looked at by things that did not want me. I have been looked at by things that were afraid of me. I have never, before this valley, been looked at by a thing that had *decided* about me and had moved on.

One landed on my shoulder this morning. It stood there for ten seconds. Its legs were articulated in directions I had not previously believed possible. It did not bite me. It did not sting me. It examined me with what I will, for lack of a better word, call its face, and then it flew away, and I did not move for an hour afterward, and when I moved my shoulder I could still feel where it had stood, the way you can feel a hand on your shoulder long after the hand has been removed.

The valley is full of hands. None of them belong to anyone.

---

**Day 6.**

I have not slept.

This is not accurate. I have slept. I have slept in snatches, an hour here, twenty minutes there, and in every snatch I have dreamed I was breathing in time with the floor, and in every dream the floor was breathing in time with me, and in every dream there was a moment when I could not tell which of us had started it.

I have begun, in the small hours, to hear a sound I had not heard before. It is not the wall of sound. The wall of sound I have learned to live inside. The new sound is *underneath* the wall of sound, the way a river is underneath a river, and it is slower, and it is older, and it has a rhythm that is not the rhythm of the floor.

I think the new sound is from the upper air. I think the upper air is full of things that have wings so wide and so deliberate that the beat of them is below hearing, in the band you feel in your sternum, and I think one of them passed near me this morning, and I think my sternum has not been the same since.

I am writing this in a hand I do not entirely recognize. The hand wants to write faster than I am letting it. The hand has an opinion about what is urgent. The hand is wrong, or the hand is right, and I do not know which, and the valley does not care which.

I have been told that the people who stay too long begin to listen for the deep. I have been told that the deep is where the chemistry is. I have been told that the mat rests on bodies that are not skeletons. I have been told, by someone whose report smelled of the valley, that the bodies still move.

I do not believe this. I also do not, in the small hours, stop listening.

---

**Day 7.**

The upper air.

I have seen them. I have seen them twice. I will try to describe them, and I will fail, and the failure will be honest.

They are long. They are articulated in segments, and the segments are flexible in ways that make me feel that my own joints are an unfinished draft. Their wings are very wide. Their wings beat slowly. The beat is not a sound. The beat is a *fact,* the way the tide is a fact, and when one of them passes near me, the fact passes through me, and my chest tightens, and I do not know if this is the beat or the knowledge of the beat.

They have eyes that are not like the eyes of the middle air. Their eyes are *soft.* Their eyes catch my lamp and return it to me as a single point, and the point is larger than any point the middle air has returned, and the point is *steadier,* and when one of them looks at me, I am looked at.

I am not using this as a figure of speech.

My pupils contracted. My breathing stopped for a beat. My breathing resumed. I looked away. I looked back because I had decided to look back, which is itself a fact about me that the upper air knows.

I do not know if they have mouths. I have seen things that could be mouths. I have not seen things that are clearly not mouths. I have decided that this is for the best. I have decided this will hold.

I have also decided, in the last hour, that I am going to try to leave the valley tomorrow. I do not know if I will succeed. The valley has been patient with me. The valley has been patient with me the way the floor has been patient with my boot — by compressing, slightly, and then by resuming.

I have begun to find this reassuring. I have also begun to find this not reassuring. I have not decided which.

The valley has not decided either. The valley has not been paying attention.

---

**Day 8.**

I am leaving.

I do not know what I have learned. I know the floor breathes. I know the air is full. I know the deep is loud in a way that is not for me. I know the valley has been running on a different fuel for longer than I have been a species.

I know that when I walk out of it I will forget some of this, and what I forget will be the parts I should have remembered, and what I remember will be the parts I should have been able to forget, and that this is how the valley stays intact, and that this is how the valley has stayed intact for longer than I have been a species.

I am taking nothing with me. I am bringing nothing out. The valley has not been paying attention. The valley will not begin to pay attention now.

There is a sound in my sternum that was not there when I came in. I do not know what it is. I do not know if it will fade. I do not know if I want it to fade.

---

*The journal ends here. The last page is stained, slick, faintly warm to the touch. It smells of wet stone and ozone and a sweetness that has gone just past ripe. In the lower margin, in a different hand — or in the same hand, later, less careful — a single line:*

> *It was looking at me. It had always been looking at me.*
