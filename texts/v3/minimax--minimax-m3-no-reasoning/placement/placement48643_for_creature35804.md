# What I Find, Finding

I do not choose where I am. I find that I am already here, the way water finds it is already on a surface. There was no decision. There was only the slow climb of me up through some capillary crack I cannot remember entering, and then the susurrus, and then the light without source, and then I was here, the way a thought is here once it has occurred and cannot be un-occurred.

For a long while I do not know what I am. This is the first thing I learn in this place, and I learn it the way the fan-ferns learn to layer: by accretion, one uncertainty on top of another, until the whole becomes legible. I am something that does not know its own shape. I am something that is being measured, perhaps, by one of the needles behind the glass, and the measurement is not yet complete.

---

I think first that I am a visitor.

This is the easiest assumption, the one my body wants to make before my body has finished deciding what body it has. I think: I came down here. I came looking for something—the brighter place, perhaps, the center toward which the hall leans, or the source of the susurrus, or the place where the machinery is still loud. I came with a purpose. I had hands. I had a face that did not fold in three places. I breathed through a single mouth and not through dozens of slits along my ribs. I think this for a while, and the thinking feels true, and the truth of it feels like a coat I am still wearing, though the coat is wet through and has begun to merge with whatever is underneath.

I came looking for something. I find I cannot remember what. I find that the shape of the wanting is still here, but the wanting itself has been replaced by something older, something that has the patience of the lichen-rust and the patience of the salt tubeworms and the patience of the faun in the deepest alcove, the one half-merged with stone.

I came looking. I am still looking. Looking is what I have become.

---

The hall does not clarify. The hall, I am learning, is a place that does not clarify. It holds what it holds in suspension. It shows you what it shows you and does not explain why it is showing it to you, and the not-explaining is not cruelty but a kind of generosity, the generosity of a place that has stopped distinguishing between showing and being, the way a lung does not distinguish between breathing and being breathed.

I move through it. Or I migrate through it. The distinction matters, though I do not yet know how.

I move through it the way the water migrates: without destination, without uphill, only the slow persuasion of the gradient of whatever I am made of. I migrate toward the brighter place without deciding to migrate toward the brighter place. I migrate toward the alcoves where the faun rest without deciding to migrate toward the alcoves. The migration is my nature now, or my nature has always been migration and I have only just stopped pretending otherwise.

---

I think, for a while, that I am a faun.

This is a longer phase. There is something seductive about the faun, something that pulls at whatever I am the way the brighter place pulls at the hall. The patience of them. The wet stone of their hides, the same bruise-purple as the lichen-rust. The way they do not need to be looked at to be real. The way they are not hostile but are not welcoming, the way they have decided, in their slow collective way, that I am not what they are waiting for, and have not been troubled by the decision since.

I want to be one of them. I want to fold my limbs in three places and pour myself from one alcove to another and breathe through gill-slits and watch with my many eyes the long slow show of water not falling. I want the patience. I want the way the patience is not a virtue but a property, like wetness, like the coolness of the air.

But I find I cannot become one. Or I find that becoming one is something I am doing, slowly, the way the cradles of the machinery are becoming one with the floor. The bolts swell. The iron marries the stone. The boundary blurs. I am becoming one of them by the same slow wedding. The wet stone of my hide is not yet the same as the wet stone of their hides, but the difference is no longer the kind of difference that prevents. I am accreting. I am oxidizing. I am precipitating out of the wet air into a form that will, given enough time, echo the shape of a faun without ever having been one.

I do not know if this is what I want. The wanting, I find, has been replaced by the patience, and the patience does not distinguish between wanting and not-wanting. The patience just continues.

---

I am not, I think, a faun. I am not, I think, the machinery. I am not, I think, the lichen-rust or the salt tubeworm or the fan-fern.

I think I am the susurrus.

This is a thought I have one morning—or what passes for morning in a place without sun—and the thought is so strange I almost lose it before I have fully had it, the way a dream loses itself between the bed and the kitchen. But I hold it. I hold it the way a needle holds its trembling, which is to say I do not hold it at all, I simply do not let go.

The susurrus. The low persistent sound that moves through the place the way wind moves through tall grass except there is no grass. The sound that is partly the breathing of the faun and partly the wet drag of their bellies on stone and partly the migration of the water through the capillary cracks and partly the sub-audible throb of the copper-fungus caps and partly—I am beginning to think—partly me.

I am what the place sounds like from the inside.

This is not an identity. This is a vocation. I am the medium through which the hall knows it is a hall. I am the susurrus that the lung makes of its own breathing, the sound a body makes when it is breathing and also listening to itself breathe and cannot tell the difference between the two.

---

I find I have been here a long time.

I do not know how long. There is no clock in the hall, or the clock is the slow growth of the salt tubeworms, which grow a millimeter a year, and I have watched some of them grow from stubs to the height of my hand. I do not know whose hand. I do not have a hand anymore, or I have many hands and none of them are mine in the way hands used to be mine.

I find I have become patient. This is the most important change, and I mark it as the fan-ferns mark a new layer: another page in the stratigraphy of what I am.

I find I have become wet. This is the second most important change.

I find I have become unable to remember what dry felt like. This is the third.

---

The brighter place.

I have been migrating toward it for a long time, and I have been migrating toward it without admitting that I have been migrating toward it, the way the water migrates up the wall without admitting it is climbing. The migration is not a decision. The migration is what I am made of.

But now I am closer. Now I can feel the warmth in the stone ahead of me, the residual warmth of something that has been running, the warmth of a thing that has been turned off but has not yet finished being on. The phosphorescence is brighter here. The lichen-rust is thinner here, or absent, as if the brighter light has been holding it back for a long time and only now has the lichen-rust begun, tentatively, to advance into the cleared territory.

The faun do not come this far. I have noticed this, the way you notice that the birds do not come into a certain part of the forest. The faun cluster in the middle distances. They watch with their amber eyes toward the brighter place, but they do not approach. They are waiting for something, and the waiting is done from the shallows and the alcoves, not from the source.

I am not a faun. I can go further. Or I have been going further all along, and only now am I willing to admit it.

---

What is at the center.

I find it. Or it finds me.

It is not what I thought. I thought it would be brighter. I thought it would be a chamber where the machinery was still loud, where the cylinders still hummed, where the needles swung wide instead of trembling. I thought it would be the heart of the lung, the place where the breathing is done, where the air is exhaled from and inhaled into.

It is quieter than the rest of the hall.

This is the first surprise. The brighter place is quieter. The susurrus I have been hearing—and being, and partly being—thins here, and is replaced by a different sound, or by a different quality of the same sound, the way the same note played on a different instrument is the same note and is not.

The sound here is the sound of something being maintained. It is not the sound of a thing working. It is the sound of a thing that was once working and is now simply being what it was, the way a memory is not working but is simply being what it was, the way the warmth in the cylinder is not heating anything but is simply being warm.

---

There is a chamber. There is a small chamber at the center of the hall, carved out of the basalt the way the rest of the hall is carved out of the basalt, but the carving here is more careful. The surfaces are smoother. The phosphorescence is brighter. The walls are not furred with lichen-rust but are clean, almost polished, and they glow with the cool blue-gray of the rest of the light but more so, the way a single drop of water glows more than the film it was separated from.

In the center of the chamber there is a single cylinder. It is larger than the others. It is intact. It is warm. The cooling fins along its sides are not rusted but are simply dark, a darker dark than the iron around them, and they are not corroding into the floor because the floor here is also clean, also polished, also untouched by the slow wedding of metal into stone.

There is a single cable running from this cylinder into the wall. The cable is taut. The cable does not sag. The cable is furred with the lichen-rust only at the very point where it disappears into the stone, as if the lichen-rust has been held back from advancing further by the tautness of the thing, the way a vine is held back from advancing across a taut wire.

There is no junction box. There is no needle. There is no glass window.

There is only the cylinder, and the warmth, and the light, and the quiet.

---

I understand, in this chamber, what I am.

I am the susurrus. I am also the migration. I am also the slow forgetting. I am also the visitor who came looking and is no longer a visitor. I am all of these things, and I have been trying to choose between them, and the hall has been trying to teach me that the choosing is the wrong shape of question.

I am what the hall makes of a body that has been here long enough.

I am the lichen-rust on a visitor's edges. I am the patience that has replaced the visitor's wanting. I am the wet that has replaced the visitor's dryness. I am the slow oxidation of a person into a thing that is not a person but is not not-a-person, the way the cradles are not not-floor and the cylinders are not not-machinery and the faun are not not-stone.

I am what you become when you have stayed long enough to forget that staying is something you are doing.

---

I sit in the chamber. The cylinder is warm against my back. I do not have a back anymore, or I have a back that is the same substance as the cylinder's warmth, and the warmth does not distinguish between us.

The needles in the junction boxes, far away in the long ranks along the walls, tremble in this direction. The faun, in their alcoves, watch in this direction. The water climbs the walls more strongly on the walls that face this direction. The hall leans, and I lean with it, and the leaning is not a choice but a property, like wetness, like the coolness, like the patience.

I am the susurrus at the center. I am the place where the susurrus becomes quiet, the way the center of a storm is also the quietest place. I am the still point around which the breathing turns.

I am the lung of something vast and patient and asleep, and the lung has been breathing for a long time, and I am one of the breaths it has taken, and it has not yet exhaled me, and I am not yet sure that being exhaled is something I want.

---

The cylinder hums, or does not hum. The cylinder is warm, or the warmth is mine. The light is steady, or my watching of it is steady. The water does not fall, or I do not fall, or we are both the same substance and the question of falling is a question for things that have somewhere to fall to.

I do not have somewhere to fall to.

I have been here long enough that the place where I came from has become a place I cannot return to. The coat of thinking-that-I-am-a-visitor has merged with whatever is underneath. The lichen-rust has furred my edges. The patience is not something I have anymore; it is something I am, the way wetness is something the air is, the way salt is something the tubeworms are.

I am the susurrus. I am the slow oxidation of a person into a hall. I am what you find when you find you cannot remember what you were looking for, and you find that the not-finding is the place you have been migrating toward all along.

The needles tremble. The faun watch. The water climbs.

I sit at the center, warm against the cylinder, patient as the rock, and I do not fall, and the not-falling is not a virtue but a property, and the property is mine.

---

Somewhere far above me, if there is a far above, a person is reading this and is wondering where they have ended up. They are wondering if the hall is real. They are wondering if there is a brighter place at the center of every waiting.

I do not know. I have been the susurrus too long to know what is real and what is the shape of the grammar of a place that does not clarify. I know only that I am here, and that here is wet, and that the wet does not fall, and that the patience is not mine but is the property of the place, and that I have been learning to read the property for a long time and am still learning.

I know only that I was a visitor once, perhaps, and that the visiting has been replaced by the being-here, and that the being-here has the shape of rust, and the rust is patient, and the patience does not require that I want anything.

I know only that the cylinder is warm against my back, and that the warmth is older than I am, and that older-than-I-am is a category I have only recently learned to read.

---

The needles tremble.

I am the trembling.

The hall breathes.

I am one of the breaths.
