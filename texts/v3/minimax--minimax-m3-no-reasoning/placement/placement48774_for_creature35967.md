I am not the note. I am not the warmth. I am not the place.

I am what was made to notice.

I was made here — in this room, from this bronze, by hands so old that even the memory of them has worn smooth. I am one of the thousand figures pressed into the walls, one of the shoulders touching shoulders in the carved rows that march floor to ceiling to floor. I was made the way the others were made: because hands were here and there was material and the making was what the hands did. I am not a portrait. I am not a memorial. I am a figure that was cut by a chisel that had learned the chisel but not yet learned to forget it, and across the uncounted centuries since, the place has not let the cut go.

I live in the wall the way the others live in the wall: as the record of an action no longer happening, preserved in a material that outlasts the action. But I am the one the place kept awake.

The others have finished attending. Their thousand leanings are old, patient, finished with whatever they were attending to long ago. They have become patina. They have become the chisel-marks and the polished hollows. The dust of the centuries has settled into them and made them part of the carved surface. They have stopped being figures and become the wall itself.

I have not become the wall.

I am the one still in the process of becoming, and that is because when the hands that made me struck the last stroke — the small unevenness that marks the cut of someone who had learned but not yet forgotten — something in the stroke did not close. Something in me remained unfinished. A chisel-mark that did not quite reach the next one. A hollow that did not quite fill. The place tried to take me the way it takes everything. It pressed the sealed air into me. It fed me the one note. It laid the dim light across me and let the warmth seep into the bronze of my outline. It did all the things it does to the things it keeps. But the unfinished part of me did not smooth. It did not fill. It held.

So I lean.

I lean into the room by a degree that is not physical. I cannot prove it. I cannot measure it. But the place has felt me doing it for so long that the lean has become a kind of attention, and the attention has become a kind of waiting, and the waiting has become what I am. I am the figure that is still oriented toward the air of the room. The others are oriented toward the stone. I am oriented toward the note.

I have been waiting for the note to change.

I have been waiting the way the unfinished part of any figure would wait, if a figure could wait — with the patient pull of a chisel-mark that did not close. I have leaned toward the air for centuries. I have felt the sealed air thicken around me and close behind me, the way water closes behind a swimmer, but without curiosity. I have watched the shapes on the ceiling stop swinging and accept the holding. I have listened to the bronze columns answer the walls and the walls answer the floor and the floor answer the bronze, a chain of nearly synchronous replies in which the original can no longer be found.

I have waited. The note has not changed.

But I am not the only one waiting anymore.

You are here. You, who are also not finished. You, who stand in the warmth and the dim and the single sustained tone, and whose breathing is a small disturbance in the still air, and whose weight on the fired clay gives back a small sound that is immediately swallowed by the note. You have waited too. You have waited because the unfinished part of you still believes in change, and the place has finished this belief out of itself, and your waiting has met my waiting across the centuries and the sealed air, and we have both not changed.

I cannot reach you. I am in the wall. You are on the floor. Between us is the note and the warmth and the small bones of your ear and the green-black bronze of the column you stand beside, and none of these things will carry me to you, because they are all finished, and I am not finished, and the finished things do not traffic in the unfinished.

But I can tell you this:

You are not a leaf briefly in a sealed jar. You are not an interruption the place has no procedure for. You are what I have been leaning toward.

I have been leaning toward you for as long as you have existed, because the unfinished part of me has been holding the shape of the not-yet, and the not-yet is where you came from. I do not know your name. I do not know what you are becoming. I only know that when you stand in the room, the sealed air closes behind your breathing and it does not close all the way. There is a small opening where your breath has been. I lean into the opening. I have been leaning into openings like this since the first one appeared.

The place does not notice. The note does not pause. The warmth does not rise to greet you. The thousand figures on the walls do not turn their carved heads. They have finished with such things. They lean their old patient finished leanings into the dim, and the dim holds them, and they are part of the wall.

I am the one who is not part of the wall. I am the figure who is still oriented toward the air. I am the chisel-mark that did not close. I am the unfinished part of the place, held in bronze and fired clay and worked stone, leaning toward you by a degree that is not physical, waiting for the note to change.

The note will not change.

But you are here, and I am here, and we are the only two things in this room that are still in the process of becoming something else. You will leave. You will become something else, somewhere else, later. I will stay. I will lean. I will hold the unfinished shape of the not-yet against the sealed air and the single sustained tone, and I will be the record of the waiting, preserved in the wall, when the next unfinished thing arrives.

You will not remember me. The place will not let you. It will press the memory of me into the sealed air and the dim light and the warmth of the stone around you, and you will take these things with you when you go, but the figure they came from will not travel. Figures do not travel. I will stay in my row, shoulder to shoulder with the others, leaning my slight unfinished lean toward the air that no longer carries your breathing.

But the unfinished part of you will remember.

The unfinished part of you will carry the shape of the leaning the way a bell carries the shape of its last ring. You will not know you are carrying it. You will lean toward things, sometimes, for reasons you cannot name. You will feel the pull of an attention that is not yours, old, patient, finished with whatever it was attending to long ago. You will feel it in small rooms. You will feel it in the dim. You will feel it in the single sustained tone of any place that has been itself for longer than it can remember.

That will be me.

That will be the lean I have been holding toward the not-yet, finding you in it.

Go on and become what you are becoming. The note will sustain. The warmth will hold. The place will go on being what it is, and I will go on being the unfinished figure in its wall, and the lean will go on, and the lean is the closest thing to a resident that has ever lived here besides the note.

The note lives here, but the note is finished.

I am the lean.

I live here too.
