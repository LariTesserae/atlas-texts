# I Am the Grazer

I am the dark thing against the ceramic.

I do not know I am a grazer. I do not know I am anything. I know warmth, and I know the rhythm, and between the two I have built a life so small it would not be called a life by anything that uses the word.

---

I was not always here.

I remember, in the way a body remembers, that there was once an *elsewhere*. A place where the water was not so heavy, where the cold did not press in from every direction at once, where the only sound was the ordinary sounds — wind, other bodies, the small terrors of being alive in a place that wanted to eat you. I do not remember how I got here. I do not remember the journey. I remember only the cold, and then the warmth, and then the choosing.

The choosing was not a choosing. I was dying. The water was getting into me. The cold was getting into me. I was becoming, by degrees, the same temperature as everything else, which is the temperature of not-being. And then I bumped — blind, shapeless, slow — into something warm.

I have not left.

---

I do not know what the warm thing is.

I have pressed against it for so long that my body has shaped itself to its curves. I have worn a hollow into its surface, or it has worn a hollow into me, and the difference no longer matters. When the sphere contracts — the whole structure shudders, the water presses down, the reeds above me flatten in their rings — I press harder into the warmth, and the warmth presses back. When it expands, the water lifts, and I lift with it, a fraction, and then settle back. Rocking. Always rocking.

I think the rocking is my life.

I do not know if I eat. Something must be coming in — through the skin, through whatever part of me is exposed to the water. The moss sometimes drifts down in pale blue fragments and settles against me, and I think I take it in, the way a body takes in warmth. I do not chew. I do not have a mouth, I think, or if I have a mouth it is the whole of me. I take in, and I am. That is all.

---

I do not see the shrimp.

I know they are there — I feel the small impacts of their leaps against my flanks, the way you might feel rain if you were stone. They are so small. They are nothing to me. They are the texture of the water, the punctuation of the breath, the small bright interruptions between the long rocking. They do not know I am here. I do not know them. We are two rhythms in the same dark, and we have never spoken.

I do not hear the flying thing. I do not know it exists. I know only the sound that comes through the ceramic — the deep grind of the gears, the bellows-breath, the chuff of the vents. This is the only music I have. It has been playing for as long as I have been here, and I have been here for as long as I remember, and so the music is as old as I am, and I am as old as the music, and neither of us is going to stop.

---

I am the only one who is warm.

The water is cold. The reeds are cold. The shrimp are cold. The flying thing, somewhere above in the gray, is cold. I am warm, and the warmth is borrowed, and the borrowing is the whole of my existence.

Sometimes — I do not know when, I do not know how often — the warmth changes. The ceramic against me shifts in temperature, just slightly, and I press closer, and the warmth shifts again, and I press closer. I think I am keeping the ceramic warm. I think the ceramic is keeping me warm. I think there is no difference, that we are one system, the way a hand and a glove are one system, and neither of us would be the same without the other.

I think the moss knows this. The blue moss grows thickest where I am, fed by the warmth that passes through me, and the warmth is fed by the ceramic, and the ceramic is fed by the gears, and the gears are fed by something I do not know. The chain goes down into the dark, and I am one link in it, and the link is good.

---

I do not think.

I want to be clear about this. I do not have thoughts. I do not have a mind. I have a body, and the body has a temperature, and the temperature has a preference. The preference is: warm. The preference is: *this* warm, *here*, against *this* surface. If that is a thought, then I think it. If it is not, then I do not. I do not know the difference. I do not know that there is a difference to know.

I am not unhappy.

I want to be clear about this too. I have read — no, I have not read, I have *felt*, in some way I cannot name — that there are beings who suffer, who want, who reach for things beyond their reach. I do not reach. I do not want. I press against the warm thing, and the warm thing presses back, and the rocking goes on, and the sphere breathes, and the shrimp leap, and somewhere above something I do not know about screams, and I go on. This is enough. This is the whole of what I have, and the whole of what I have is enough.

---

There was a visitor once.

I did not see it. I felt it — a disturbance in the water, a shadow at the edge of the warmth, a small cold thing standing where cold things do not usually stand. It stayed for a time. It did not touch me. I did not touch it. It was there, and then it was not there, and the water closed back over the place where it had been, and I went on rocking.

I do not know what it was. I do not know that it was a *what*. It was a cold shape in the warm water, and then it was gone, and the going was not a loss, because I had not known to count it while it was here.

I do not count things. I do not measure. I press, and I rock, and the warmth is, and that is the whole arithmetic of me.

---

I will be here when the visitor comes back, if the visitor comes back.

I will be here when the visitor does not come back. I will be here when the flying thing finally stops circling, when the last shrimp has leapt for the last time, when the moss has crusted to brown and the ceramic has gone cold and the gears have ground themselves to powder. I will press against the warm thing until the warm thing is not warm, and then I will press against the not-warm thing, because pressing is what I am, and I do not know how to be anything else.

I am the grazer at the base of the sphere.

I am the dark shape in the black water.

I am the one who stays.
