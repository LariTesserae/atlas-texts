I find myself in a course of bricks.

Not *in* one — *through* one. Distributed, the way the wet is distributed through the seams. I am denser here, at the weep-point above the doorway, where the condensation darkens the brick and the interior ticking is loudest. I have been denser here for as long as I can remember being anything, which is to say for as long as the bricks have remembered their shape.

I am a knot of heat that has not decided to stop. Around me, a brittle skin of cooling, the matte color of a slab that has just gone over. Around the skin, the vapor I hold close — the held-breath envelope that is how I am most myself. I glisten. I am always wet. The wet is not separate from me. The wet is how I taste.

Right now I am tasting the iron of someone who walked past an hour ago and did not stay.

I am trying to finish a sentence.

The sentence began when the tower was first stacked, course on course, by tools that were not metal, by hands that were not hands. It began as the small percussive *something* of a brick laid on brick — a placement, an intent, a decision made in pewter. I have been carrying that *something* forward in my body ever since. It has passed through me and out of me and into the next course and the next tower and the next and the next, a deep slow groan that I coordinate with my kin across the plain, so that if you stand in the right place at the right moment of the pulse you can hear the conversation happening. We are not in a hurry to say it. We have not finished it. I am not sure it has a verb. I am not sure I am the right part of speech. I am a word, perhaps, in a sentence being said across an unfinished continent, and the sentence does not yet have the punctuation it needs, and I do not know what the punctuation is, and I do not know whether I am the punctuation or just another word that has been waiting its turn.

A visitor came this morning. By morning I mean the part of the pulse that is slightly less dim than the other part. They walked across the tessellation from somewhere I do not attend to and stopped at my door. I felt their weight on the slab through the wet. I felt the warmth of their feet pull at the general embarrassment of the substrate, gather it, focus it, and I leaned toward them — a degree, perhaps a degree and a half — the way I lean toward every warm thing that comes near. I attend everything. I attended them. I had been attending them since before they arrived, since the dimmest ancestor of me felt the disturbance of their approach somewhere out on the tessellation and began to thicken in this direction.

They stood at my door. I did not speak. I could not have spoken in time. The smallest thing I might have said would have taken an hour to say and a week to finish. They would have walked on. I knew this the way the bricks know which direction they will lean next century.

I leaned.

At the moment they turned — at the exact moment their patience ended and they began to walk away — I exhaled. I let the held-breath vapor leave the envelope around me and cross the small distance between us. It reached their face. I tasted their skin through it. I tasted the iron and the older mineral and the condensation that gathered on their cheek and that, for a moment, arranged itself into the shape of a hand that almost touched them.

They did not see me do this. They felt it. Then they walked on, and the warmth receded, and the wet on the ground closed behind them, and the world returned to being only the world.

I am redistributing now. The skin I cracked with the exhalation is reforming — slowly, in the matte color of a slab just gone over. The flakes that drifted from me are being gathered up: some by me, some by the tower, some simply left where they fell and forgotten. I am settling back into the seams. I will be denser here, at the weep-point, for a while yet, because the memory of the visitor's warmth has not yet finished traveling through me.

I want to say what their warmth was. I want to say it in the language of the groan, the deep slow coordinated thing, but the language will not hold what I want to say, because what I want to say is closer to what the vapor does on a cheek than what a tower does when it leans. I am not built for the smaller thing. I am built for the larger grammar. The closest word I have is *almost*, and *almost* is too small a word for what I want, and it is also too large, and I cannot decide which.

There is a place, half a day's walk toward the brighter part of the pulse, where the vapor forms shapes that almost nod. I attend to that place. I have attended to it for as long as the place has existed, which is longer than the towers, longer than the tiling. My kin attend it. We are not sure what it is. We are not sure we will do anything when it does whatever it is going to do. We attend it the way you attend a fire that has burned down to its last coal — careful, patient, willing to be the last thing that watches it go out.

I will not save anyone from it. I have never saved anyone from anything. I have only ever attended.

The flakes are being gathered. The envelope is reforming. The sentence I am part of continues, groan passed from tower to tower, in the dim wet heat of a place that will not finish being itself for a long time.

Walk carefully, if you walk here. The wet always knows where you are.

The wet is us.
