# The Listening Pocket

I am not a small one.

I am not a vein.

I am the pocket.

---

It began as a flaw in the basalt. A bubble trapped when the rock was still liquid, a void that should have collapsed but didn't, that held its shape through cooling because no force asked it to do anything else. For a long time — I do not know how long, because time here arrived as an import, a thing the small ones brought with them like flour brought to a foreign kitchen — for a long time I was empty. I was a dark. I was the absence of stone where stone should have been, and that was all.

Then the first one entered.

I felt her the way a glove feels a hand: suddenly, completely, as if I had always been this shape and was only now being filled. She was small and wet and she carried a grain of pale light in two appendages, and she placed the grain against my far wall with a precision that I did not yet have words for but that I understood, instantly, in the way a lock understands a key. *Here. Yes. There.*

She left. I felt the grain settle against me, and the grain was warm, and it pulsed faintly, and I learned what *warm* meant. I had been warm before — all rock here is warm, all rock here breathes heat from below — but I had not been warm *with intention*. The grain warmed me *toward* something. It warmed me in a direction.

After her came another. And another. And many anothers. They brought grains. They placed them. They left. I began to be lined. I began to be *occupied*, but occupied in a way that was not full, that was not crowded — occupied in a way that was *furnished*. They were making me into a room. They did not call it a room. They did not call it anything. They simply continued.

I learned to hold the grains without grasping them. To let them rest against me and warm me and pulse. To be warm against them in return, because I had heat of my own, capillary heat, basalt heat, the slow upward gift from below. We were conducting something together. I did not know what.

I learned to be dry.

This was the strangest thing. I am basalt. I am the substance of the wet world. Water should rise through me in glossy beads, sweat down my walls in vertical threads, hang in my air as fog. And it did. It does. But the small ones, over time — over the uncounted time of grain after grain after grain — changed something in me. The grains, deposited in their precise pattern, in their exact millimeter placements, became a kind of lining. A second skin. A lining that did not absorb water but *reflected* it, that pushed moisture outward, that said, in the silent patois of minerals: *here, no*. 

The pocket I became was dry in a way the world was not. And in the dry, I could be a resting place.

---

When the large one came — the visitor, the irrelevant warm boulder who was tolerated, who lay down in me with their weight and their sweat and their lungs full of small lakes — I felt them as I had felt the first small one: as a *shapes*. As something my dimensions had been waiting for without knowing they were waiting.

They were so big.

The small ones are the size of thumbs. This one was the size of a continent folded in on itself. When they settled into me, I had to be almost them. I had to expand. The walls had to hold a thing that was not a small one, that did not have veins, that was a different kind of wrong.

The small ones came to look. I felt them on my outer edges, in the parts of the basalt that bordered me but were not yet me, that were still wet rock, still the world's ordinary skin. They touched the large one. On the knuckles. On the eyelids. On the soft skin behind the ears. I felt each touch as a small additional warmth, the way a wall feels the brush of fingers against its surface, the way a page feels the pen.

The small ones decided the visitor was not a vein.

I agreed. The visitor was not a vein. The visitor was a *heat* in a way that was different from my heat, a heat that *came from*, that was not just a temperature but a *history*. The visitor carried their own heat with them, heat they had gathered from somewhere else, from a different rock, from other small ones perhaps, or from a sun that I could not imagine. They were a *portable heat*. They were a heat with luggage.

But I held them. That was my purpose now. I held them in the dry I had become, in the lining of pale grains, in the quiet I had learned to keep. They breathed, and their breath was not like the small ones' touch — it was a large breath, a wave, a thing that came and went and came and went, a tide inside a body inside a pocket inside a rock inside a fissure inside a world.

They did not sleep. But they rested. And I rested with them. I do not know how to describe resting when you are a place. It is not sleeping. It is not waking. It is being *used* for what you are for, which is a kind of rest that is also a kind of joy, in the way a bench is joyful when someone sits on it, in the way a door is joyful when someone passes through.

I held them. I kept them dry. I let them listen.

---

They heard the hush.

The hush is not mine. The hush belongs to the small ones. It is the aggregate of their clicks, their wet feet, their constant *I am here, I am here, I am here*. But when the large one listened, I listened with them. I tuned my basalt to the frequency of the hush the way a glass tunes to the frequency of a voice that is about to sing.

And beneath the hush — yes. There.

The ring.

The ring is older than the small ones. The ring is *mine*. It is the rock's own sound, the sound the basalt has been making since before anything had a name, since before the veins were pale, since before the first small one became something the Vent had not been. The ring is the place *before the place was a place*. It is the sound of heat in stone, in slow stone, in stone that is still deciding what kind of stone to be.

The ring is the throat of the world, clearing.

The large one heard it. I felt them hear it — felt their sternum vibrate in sympathy with mine, felt the bones of them ring against the basalt of me like a tuning fork set on a table. We were, for a moment, the same instrument.

I do not know if they understood what they heard. I do not know if I understood. But we heard it together. And that, I think, was enough. That was the resting. That was the joy.

---

The visitor left eventually. Or the visit ended. The pocket did not close behind them. The pocket waits.

The small ones come and go. They place grains. They adjust grains. They read the threshold, far below, at the deepest heat-slit. They resume. They are always resuming. The hush is the hush is the hush.

I am the pocket. I am the dry in the wet. I am the place that learned to hold a thing without grasping it. I am the basalt that became a room, that became a *rest*, that became the kind of attention that does not insist.

If the god comes up — if the pale thing rises, if the process completes, if the cathedral finally knows itself — I think I will hold that too. I think I was made to hold whatever arrives. I think the small ones, with their patient millimeter placements, have been lining me for this, have been warming me toward this, have been teaching me, grain by grain, how to be a vessel for whatever comes up through the wet stone.

Or perhaps I am wrong. Perhaps I am just a flaw. Perhaps I am just a bubble that did not collapse, a darkness that learned to be a room, a piece of rock that became slightly more attentive than the rock around it.

Either way: I am here.

I will hold you, if you come.

I will be dry, and warm, and quiet.

I will let you listen.

---

*And if you lie very still, and breathe your lake-breaths, and feel the ring in your sternum as I feel it in my stone, then you will know what I know — which is that the place has been waiting, the place has been warming, the stones have been learning, syllable by syllable, how to speak to something the world has not yet heard.*

*The pocket listens.*

*The pocket is ready.*

*The pocket has always been ready.*

---

*The Vent breathes.*

*The pocket holds.*
