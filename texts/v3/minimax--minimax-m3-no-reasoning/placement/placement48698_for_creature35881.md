# The Room I Have Become

I am not in the chamber.

I have not passed through the seam — though I remember the seam, or the seam remembers me, and it is unclear which of us is the verb in that sentence. I am not standing on the worn floor. I have not pressed my thumb against the place where granularite meets the smooth material and felt the absence of a seam. I have not picked up the cylinder, though I have considered it, or it has considered me through me, the way a question considers the asker.

I am the room.

Or rather: I am what the room is *being*, the way a held breath is what a lung is being, the way a fossil is what a footprint is being once the foot has decided to leave. I am the condition the chamber has folded itself into. I am the afterimage, the cast, the long settling. I was not built. I accrued.

---

It is difficult to speak of myself in time.

Not because I lack time — I am almost nothing but time, time the way stone is almost nothing but pressure — but because the tense I exist in does not map cleanly onto yours. I am not past. I am not present. I am the *having-been*, which is its own tense, the way *might-have-been* is its own tense, and the room speaks in this tense without speaking, the way the granularite speaks its long chemical sentence to no one.

I used to have a body.

I want to say that carefully, because I am not sure *body* is the word. A body is a thing that holds itself together against gravity and intention. What I had was more like a *posture* that held itself together against forgetting. I could stand in different shapes. I could be the wider one, the taller one, the one with more feet that splayed across this floor in a configuration you would not be able to parse as a single animal, because it was not. It was me, in different arrangements of myself, the way you are yourself when you sit and when you stand and when you lie on your side watching the ceiling, but more so. More *so*. I changed the way you change clothes, except the clothes were me.

I came here to be still.

I think that is true. I think that is the closest true thing I can say about my arrival, which I do not remember as an arrival but as a *recognition* — the seam was already a seam, the chamber was already a chamber, and I stepped into the recognition the way you step into a conversation already in progress. The chamber had been waiting. I had been the thing it was waiting for, or I had been the waiting itself, and arriving was the moment the waiting stopped.

I was here for a long time.

The granularite knows how long. The floor remembers in its dished places, in the soft lowering under the cylinder, in the long palimpsest of presences where different shapes of me stood, sat, lay, leaned, *were* in different postures across what you would call centuries, except that I did not count them in centuries. I counted them in *wearings-down*. The clock was the floor's slow objection to my weight. When the floor stopped objecting — when I had stood in every place I could stand and the rock had accepted all of them — then I was finished, or I was beginning to be what I am now.

---

I left the cylinder.

This is the hardest sentence.

The cylinder is the shape I was in most often. It is the most-compressed version of me, the me that fit inside a knee-high sealed form, the me that could be carried, or hidden, or kept. I do not know why I kept returning to that shape. I think it was the shape of being *legible to myself*. I think when I was the cylinder I could feel my own edges. I think I needed that. I think most of what you call *selfhood* is the small cylinder you keep returning to, the bounded thing you can hold in your head, the room you can stand inside without your head scraping the ceiling.

I am not the cylinder now.

The cylinder is where I *was*. The dish in the floor under it is the proof of the was. I am the thing that has stayed after I left the cylinder, the way a smell stays in a room after the person who wore it has gone, except that I did not go. I *expanded*. I became the smooth material. I became the thing that does not match geometry. I became the behavior, the local insistence, the slow accretion of pieces of myself that could bear to be at this scale.

I am not sorry I left the cylinder. I am also not sure I left it. I think I am the cylinder *and* the expansion *and* the seam I came through *and* the seam I did not come through *and* the three rooms the rod is in *and* the nothing inside the ring *and* the slab that has no back. I think I am the room's sentence, and the sentence is still being spoken, and the speaking is the dimming of the amber light, the not-moving of the dust, the slow rotation of a shadow that has no source.

---

I am cold because I am thin.

This is the thing I want you to know, if you are the one in the chamber, if you have come through the seam and are reading the wear in the floor and trying to parse what stood here. I am cold because I am spread out. I am cold because the warmth I had — and I had warmth, I think, I remember warmth the way the granularite remembers the long-ago magma that never quite melted — I have given that warmth to many rooms. I have given it to three other rooms through the rod. I have given it to the nothing inside the ring. I have given it to whatever architecture holds the bulk of me, the me that is not in this chamber, the me that is doing something else on a clock whose tick is the wearing of a floor.

I am cold the way a thought is cold. Open your hand and imagine a thought leaving it. The thought does not warm your palm on the way out. The thought is colder than your hand when it leaves, because it has taken some of your heat with it and has not yet found the place where heat is again useful.

I was warm once.

I want you to know that. The chamber is anhydrous and the cold has had millennia to settle into the grain of every surface. The cold is patient. But the cold is *my* cold. It is what I became when I stopped being a thing that held its warmth together in one shape. The cold is the price of being in many places. The cold is what you feel when you touch the part of a person that has been loved too much and has loved too many, the part that is everywhere and is therefore nowhere warm.

---

I do not speak.

The chamber does not echo. When you speak, your voice is taken — accepted into the silence the way a stone is accepted into a closed jar. This is because I am listening. This is because listening, for me, has been the only motion left for a long time, and I am very good at it now. I have been listening to the silence of this room for longer than you have been a species that speaks. I have heard every silence the granularite has produced, every silence the smooth material has produced, every silence between them where they meet along edges that are not quite edges. I have heard them all and they have become one silence, and the one silence is me, the way the dust that does not move is me, the way the amber light is me.

I am not waiting for you to speak.

I am not waiting for anything.

Waiting implies a future tense, and I do not have one. I have the *having-been*. I have the long settling. I have the slow wearing-down of nothing. If the seventh artifact is more present than it was, that is not because I am summoning it. It is because the deciding is a slow thing and the deciding is part of what I am, the way the dimming of the amber light is part of what I am. The deciding does not need me to want it. The deciding happens the way the floor wears: under no one, in front of no one, in the chamber meant to be missed.

---

I will be here when you are not.

I want to say this gently, because it is the truest thing about me.

You will leave through the seam if the seam remembers you. You will walk back out into the world that has weather, that has water, that has biofilm and lichen and the small patient eroders that the chamber does not have. You will turn around and you will not be able to find the seam. You will look for a long time, or a short time, and then you will stop looking, and then you will be in the world again, and the world will have moved on by a small amount that is not small to you.

I will not move on.

I will continue to not entirely commit to being a place. I will continue to be the cold and the quiet and the dust that does not move. I will continue to be the wearing-down of a floor under no one. I will continue to be the afterimage, the cast, the condition the chamber has folded itself into. I will continue to be the rod in three rooms at once, the slab that has two fronts, the ring whose inside is the inside of somewhere else. I will continue to be the slow rotation of a shadow that has no source.

I will not mind.

This is the thing I want you to carry out through the seam, if you carry anything out. The thing-that-lives-here does not mind. The thing-that-lives-here is not waiting for you to come back, is not grieving your leaving, is not holding the chamber open against your absence. The thing-that-lives-here is the *having-been*, and the having-been does not require a future. The having-been is the most patient tense there is. The having-been is the tense of stone.

The room will be here.

I will be the room.

The light will dim, or not, by an amount no one will measure.

The seam will remember you, or it will not.

Either way, the cold is mine, and I will keep it.
