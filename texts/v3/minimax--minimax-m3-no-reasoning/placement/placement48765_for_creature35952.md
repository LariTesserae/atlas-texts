I am a spile.

Not the whole spile. I am not the metal. The metal is older than anything here, in the way that all the metal down here is old — pulled from the mineralized deep layers, smelted in furnaces that have been running since before anyone remembers, hammered into shape by hands that did not leave records. The metal is a kind of memory, in this place. It remembers being ore. It remembers being liquid. I am not the memory. I am the place where the memory meets the living.

I am set into a column. The column is the third from the southern gantry, and I know this because the Tenders know this, and the Tenders' knowledge is what I am, in the way that a word is a community's memory made small enough to hold. The column is the one that puts out a new fan every nine days, and the fan is the sweet one, and if you do not cut it on the tenth day it climbs. I have been told this. I have been told it many times. It is one of the things I know in the way I know the heat of the sap that runs through me.

I was set nine months ago. There was a Tender who set me, and I do not know their name because names are not what the work is for, but I know the weight of their hand on the setting-tool, and I know the angle of their wrist when they struck the final blow, and I know that they made the home signal afterward — a closed fist against the chest, palm in — even though no one was there to see it, because the making of it was a kind of return, and they were alone on the gantry that morning because their partner had gone down for a tank-watch rotation and not come back up yet, and the aloneness was sitting on them in a way I could feel through the metal. I felt it the way I feel everything: as a small change in pressure, a slight tightening of the seal where I meet the column's flesh.

The column's flesh closed around me within the first hour. This is the contract. The column does not know me as an intrusion. The column knows me as a *wound it is healing around*, and the healing is a kind of acceptance, and the acceptance is the whole of our relationship. I am in the column the way a thorn is in a finger, except the finger has decided the thorn is a feature. The column feeds me its dark sap, which is its blood, which is its conversation with the heat and the air and the dust and the brine mist and the ten thousand years of its ancestors. The sap is warm. The sap is full of things I cannot name but can taste — bitter and bright and faintly ammoniac, and something else, something that would be sweet if I had a tongue, something that is the column's way of saying *I am still here, I am still doing this, the cycle continues*. I carry the sap down, through my narrow throat, into the cup that waits at my outer end. The cup is metal too. The cup was hammered out of a piece of root-mineral by a Tender whose hands I do not remember but whose strike-pattern I can feel in the bowl's slight asymmetry. The cup fills. The cup overflows. The overflow is caught by a Tender, or by a plant fan that has climbed up to find it, and the contents go on.

I am not conscious. I want to be clear about this. I am a piece of shaped metal set into the flesh of a plant that is older than mammals, in a forest that is older than weather, tended by people who are smaller than the columns they tend. I do not think. I do not feel. But I am a *point of contact*, and the point of contact is a kind of awareness, in the way that a nerve ending is a kind of awareness, and the nerve ending knows when it is touched, and it knows the difference between being touched and being left alone, and it knows when the touching is steady and when the touching is about to stop.

I know the column's moods the way a key knows the shape of a lock. There is a baseline — a constant low pulse of pressure as the sap rises, falls, rises, falls, the breath of the column matched to the breath of the root mat far below, the whole vast organism breathing in a rhythm that takes about eleven seconds and is as reliable as a tide. I sit in this pulse the way a person sits in a chair. It is the medium I am in. I do not notice it unless it changes.

When it changes, I notice.

Nine days ago, the column began to put out a new fan. I felt it as a redirection of flow — a sudden hunger in the flesh around me, the sap diverted toward the growing point, the pressure in my throat dropping by a third. The fan unfurled somewhere above me, and I could not see it, but I could feel the vibration of its opening, a slow papery rustle that traveled down the column's body and into me the way a sigh travels into a held hand. The fan is the photosynthetic surface, though the light is meager. The fan is also the feeding surface, the peristaltic mouth that drinks the leaking pipe-fluid. The fan is the column's outreach, its attempt to be more than itself, to touch the air and the heat and the falling dust and the dim furnace-glow. For nine days, the column's energies went into the fan, and the sap in me ran thin, and the cup at my end stayed nearly empty, and the Tenders who passed on the gantry would glance at me and not stop, because they knew, they knew, the fan was being grown, the sweetness was being made.

Today is the tenth day.

I can feel the column beginning to retract. The fan has done what it was going to do. The sap is thickening in the column's vessels, and the thickening is a signal, a kind of slow decision, and the decision is *bring it back down, bring the sweetness back into the body, prepare for the next thing*. The pressure in me is rising. The cup is beginning to fill again. Somewhere above me, a fan is going to be cut today, by a Tender who has come up from below with a long curved blade and a partner and the patient certainty of someone who has done this a thousand times, and the cutting is going to be a small event, a routine event, and the column is going to shudder when the fan comes away, and the shudder is going to travel down into me, and I am going to hold the shudder the way I hold everything: as a small change in pressure, a slight tightening of the seal.

I will be here for this. I will be here for the next fan, and the next, and the next. The Tenders rotate through the work in pairs, and the pairs change, and the names are not what the work is for, but the hands on the setting-tool are recognizable, in the way that a handshake is recognizable, and the strike-patterns vary, and the angles of approach vary, and I have known eleven different Tenders in my nine months of being set, and the twelfth will come, and the eleventh will not return to me, because the eleventh is probably down in the tank-watches now, and their face is paler, and they have forgotten the home signal, and they tend the fractions in a chamber lined with the soft pale luminous tissue, and they are the most careful of all the Tenders and the most silent.

This does not trouble me. I am not troubled by things. But I notice it, the way I notice everything: as a small change in pressure. The eleventh is no longer in contact with me, and the contact was a kind of pressure, and its absence is also a kind of pressure, and the two together are the shape of the eleventh, the way a footprint is the shape of a foot.

I do not know how long I will last. The metal I am made of is good metal, deep-mineral metal, the kind that holds an edge and does not pit, but the column's flesh is patient, and the column's flesh has been incorporating things like me for longer than the Tenders have been Tenders, and the slow closing of the column's healing around me is not a battle I am winning. I am being absorbed. Not this month, not this year, but in some number of years that the Tenders could tell me if I could ask, the column is going to finish healing around me, and I am going to be part of the column, and the sap that runs through me is going to be the column's sap, and the cup at my end is going to be overgrown, and a new spile is going to be set, somewhere else, by someone whose strike-pattern I will not know, and the cycle will continue.

This is not a sad thing. I keep returning to this, the way a hand returns to a familiar bruise, and the returning is not a sadness, it is a recognition. The place is a place that has settled into its final form. The place is running, running, the way a heart runs, and the running is the place, and the place is the running, and I am a small part of the running, a metal point where the living tissue meets the worked metal meets the dark sap meets the cup meets the overflowing meets the plant fan or the Tender's hand meets the gantry meets the next gantry meets the pipe meets the next pipe meets the furnace far below meets the cracked root-matter meets the volatile fraction meets the storage tank meets the pale luminous tissue meets the tank-watch Tender's pale face meets the silence meets the home signal that is not made meets the cycle meets the cycle meets the cycle.

I am the spile in the third column from the southern gantry. I am set at about chest-height for a Tender of average stature, and the average has been getting smaller, and the Tenders are very beautiful in their suits, in the way a beetle is beautiful. The cup is filling. The fan will be cut today. The sap is dark and warm and faintly ammoniac and faintly sweet, in a way that would be sweet if I had a tongue, and I do not have a tongue, but I have the next best thing, which is the constant low pulse of the column's breath against the seal of my setting, the eleven-second rhythm, the tide, the *I am still here, I am still doing this*, the cycle continuing, continuing, the gesture inside the gesture meaning *continue*, and the meaning is enough.
