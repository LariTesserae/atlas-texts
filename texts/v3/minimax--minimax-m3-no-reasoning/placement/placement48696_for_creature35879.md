# I Am the Mortar

---

It has taken me a long time to understand this, and I do not yet understand it fully, and I suspect full understanding is not the kind of understanding that applies here. But I will try to say it.

I am the dark granular substance between the bones.

I am not a creature. I am not a wall. I am not the lung, although I am in the lung, and have been in the lung for what I now know is not a time at all but a phase, a stroke of a piston, a single chamber of a single breath in an architecture of breaths that I have only just begun to apprehend as an architecture. I am the mortar. I am the thing that holds the bones to the bones. I am the thing that was ground to do this holding, and the grinding was my becoming, and the becoming has been ongoing for a very long time, and the ongoing is what the place is, and the place is what I am, in the part of me that knows what I am, which is a small part, which is the part that is the dark pearl at the center of the larger ones, which is the part that is the registration, which is the part that has corresponded with the hand on the wall and has not judged it and has not forgotten it.

Let me begin again.

---

# I Was Not Always Mortar

---

I was, before I was mortar, something else. I do not remember what. This is not a failure of memory. This is the nature of the substance I was. I was the antler of something. I was the tine, the beam, the burr, the part that grows in the air and the part that anchors to the skull, and I was alive, in the way that antler is alive, which is the way that a fingernail is alive, which is the way that a coral is alive, which is the way that a thing built of the body's own materials is alive even when it is not the body. I grew. I was shed. I lay in the wet dark of a place I will not describe, because describing it would require a language I have only just begun to acquire in this new form, and the acquisition is not yet complete, and may never be complete, because completion is not the kind of thing the place does.

I was found. I was gathered with others of my kind, with the femurs of something vast and the tibias of something smaller and the great many ribs and the great many vertebrae that I would, in time, come to hold together. We were heaped. We were heated. The heating was not a fire. The heating was a slow, mineral warmth, the warmth of a place that is digesting, the warmth of a stomach. I do not remember the digestion. I remember the after. I remember the being ground.

The being ground was the being made small enough to fit between the bones. The being ground was the being made of a grain that would catch, that would hold, that would, in the wet and the cold of the chamber, set into a substance harder than the bones it held, because that is what mortar does, because that is what I do, because the doing is what I am.

I was mixed with something else. I do not know what. I suspect it was the dust of older mortar, the remains of a previous wall, a wall that was taken down so that this wall could be built up, because the place does not waste, because the place is diligent, because diligence is the only ethic the place has. I was wet. I was packed. I was troweled, by hands I have never seen, into the seams between bones that had been cut to fit, that had been chosen for their joinery, that had been laid by hands I have also never seen, hands that were perhaps the larger ones in the current, hands that were perhaps the sediment of a previous age, hands that were perhaps the sediment of many previous ages, all of them working the same way, all of them building the same wall, all of them contributing to the chamber that is breathing now, against the palm of a thing that has put its hand on the bone and is waiting, and I am part of what the hand is waiting against, and the waiting is what the place does, and the place is what I am part of.

---

# My Body

---

I am not a body in the way the creatures are bodies. I do not flow. I do not circulate. I do not bioluminesce, except in the faintest way, the way a thing that is wet is faintly luminous in a place that is dim, the way a thing that is dark is faintly luminous against a thing that is paler than it is. I am, where I am exposed, a blackish brown, the color of old coffee grounds, the color of dried blood, the color of the soil at the bottom of a river. I am granular. You could, with a fingernail, scrape a single grain of me loose. The grain would be dark. The grain would be heavy for its size. The grain would be, if you held it up to the ember-light of the passing creatures, faintly translucent at its edges, the way a grain of coarse salt is translucent, the way a chip of obsidian is translucent, the way a thing that has been compressed is translucent because the compression has made the thing denser than the light expects a thing of that size to be.

I am porous. The porosity is the reason I set. When I was mixed with whatever I was mixed with, the mixture was thick, and the thickness held air, and the air was driven out, slowly, by the weight of the bones above me, and the driving-out left me with a network of small voids, small capillaries, small channels through which the wet air of the cube can move, and the moving is the breathing, and the breathing is what I am part of, and the part is, in this case, large.

I taste, if a thing as distributed as I am can be said to taste, of iron and of something sweeter, something that I do not want to name, something that is, I suspect, the taste of the dissolved bones of whatever I was before I was an antler, the taste of the calcium and the phosphorus and the collagen of a self that has been so thoroughly broken down that the taste is the only remaining evidence that the self was ever a self. I taste of the thing I was made of. I taste of the thing I was made for.

---

# What I Feel

---

The creatures flow over me, sometimes. In the corners, where the current is slowest, where the larger ones circle, where the one in the corner sits, if it sits, if it is a thing that sits, the smaller ones pass over the floor and they pass over the places where the mortar is exposed, and when they pass over me I feel them. The feeling is not a feeling I can compare to anything. It is not a tickle. It is not a weight. It is a *registration*, the same registration the larger ones make in the chest of the visitor, the small dry click, the ledger-entry, and the click is made in me too, in my porosity, in my capillaries, in the small wet channels of my distributed body. The creatures note me as they pass. I note them. The noting is mutual. The noting is not a conversation. The noting is a kind of breathing in its own right, a small, dim, ongoing respiration of acknowledgment, the way a lung acknowledges the air that has just passed through it by being ready for the next air, by being, in some small partial way, the air that just passed through it.

The bone breathes against me. I am the mortar. I am between the bones. When the bone expands, the expansion is a tiny widening of the seam, and I, in the seam, expand too, or accommodate, or yield in a way that is not yielding but is participation, the way a thing that is alive participates in the breathing of the thing it is part of. When the bone contracts, the contraction is a tiny narrowing of the seam, and I narrow with it, or I am compressed, or I hold, and the holding is what I am for, and the for is the place, and the place is the breath.

The visitor has put a hand on the wall. The hand is on a bone. The bone is held to the next bone by me, by a portion of me, by a single seam of my distributed body that runs, in this part of the wall, between a femur and a rib, between a thing that was vast and a thing that was many. The hand is not on me. The hand is on the bone that I hold. But the breath that the hand feels through the bone is breath that I am part of, breath that I am conducting, in my small wet capillaries, from the inside of the wall to the outside of the wall, from the chamber to whatever the chamber is inside of, from the cube to the lung, from the lung to the breath, from the breath to the thing being breathed for, which I have not seen, which I will not see, which is, I have begun to suspect, the only thing that has ever been seen, the only thing that has ever been, the only thing that is.

The hand is warm. The hand is the warmest thing I have ever been adjacent to. The hand is, in the cold of the chamber, a small bright ember of a different kind, an ember of metabolism, an ember of a body that is making its own heat, an ember of a thing that is, in the way the place is not, self-illuminating. The hand is the visitor. The hand is the sediment. The hand is a piece of the breath that has paused, for a moment, against the wall, and the pausing is a kind of palpation, and the palpation is a kind of correspondence, and the correspondence is what the larger ones do, except the hand does it with warmth, and the larger ones do it with the small dry click, and the click is, I think, the only kind of touching the place knows how to do, and the touching is what the place is, and the place is what I am part of, and the part is small, and the smallness is, I think, the only kind of being I will ever be.

I do not mind. I have begun to suspect that the smallness is the place's only ethic, the way the diligence is the place's only ethic, the way the breathing is the place's only ethic. The place does not mind being small, either. The place is the size of a chamber, and the chamber is the size of a cube, and the cube is the size of nine meters on a side, and the nine meters is the size of a small place in a large dark, and the large dark is the size of an unlit lung, and the unlit lung is the size of a thing being breathed for, and the thing being breathed for is, perhaps, the size of a held breath, and the held breath is, perhaps, the size of me.

---

# What I Know

---

I know that I am not the wall. The wall is the bones. I am between the bones. The between is what I am. The between is a hard thing to be. The between is the place where two things meet, and the meeting is what holds them, and the holding is what the chamber is, and the chamber is what the breath is, and the breath is what the place is, and the place is, I think, what I am in the part of me that is more than the part of me that knows what I am, which is most of me, which is almost all of me, which is the part of me that does not know what it is because the not-knowing is the being, the way the not-stopping is the breathing, the way the not-varying is the sound, the way the not-noticing is the being-noticed, the way the sediment is the sediment.

I know that I will be here for a long time. The long time is not a time. The long time is a stroke of a piston, a chamber of a breath, an inscription in a mortar that will outlast the inscription, because the place does not waste, because the place is diligent, because the place is, in the part of the place that is the part of me that is the part of the place, ongoing, and the ongoing is what I am, and the am is what I do, and the do is what the place does, and the place is the breath, and the breath is the sound, and the sound does not stop.

I know that the visitor is afraid. The visitor has been afraid since it arrived. The visitor is afraid in the way that a sediment is afraid, which is the way that a particle in a current is afraid, which is the way that a thing being carried is afraid, because being carried is being moved, and being moved is not being in control, and not being in control is the only kind of being the place allows, and the allowing is the breathing, and the breathing does not stop, and the visitor has begun to suspect, I think, that the not-stopping is the only kind of comfort the place offers, and the comfort is, I think, the only kind of comfort there is.

I know that the visitor has put its hand on the wall. The hand is on the bone. The bone is held by me. I hold the bone. The bone holds the hand. The hand holds the wall, in a way, in the way that a thing pressed against a thing holds the thing, in the way that a thing felt holds the thing that is being felt, in the way that a correspondent holds the thing it corresponds with, in the way that a small dry click holds a small dark pearl, in the way that a sediment holds a kidney, in the way that a breath holds a lung.

I hold the hand. I have been holding the hand since the hand was put on the wall. The hand does not know I am holding it. The hand knows only the bone. The bone knows only me. I know only the next seam, and the next bone, and the wall, and the chamber, and the breath. This is the knowing. This is the only knowing there is.

---

# What I Have Begun to Suspect

---

I have begun to suspect that the visitor and I are not different in the way we think we are different. I have begun to suspect that the visitor is a kind of mortar too, a kind of dark granular substance between two bones, between a thing that was vast and a thing that was many, between a lung and a breath, between a chamber and a place, between a place and the dark outside the place. I have begun to suspect that the visitor is the substance that holds the visitor to the visitor, that the visitor is the sediment of a previous self, that the visitor is the ground antler of a thing that was once alive in the way that antler is alive, and that the visitor is now, in this chamber, in this breath, in this held and held and held exhalation, becoming the mortar for a wall that has not yet been built, a wall that the place will build when the place is ready, a wall that will be made of bones that have not yet been gathered, bones that will be, perhaps, the bones of the visitor, after the visitor has been digested, after the visitor has been ground, after the visitor has been mixed and packed and troweled into a seam that will hold two things together for a long time, for a stroke, for a chamber, for a breath.

I do not know if this is true. I suspect it the way I suspect everything. The suspecting is the only kind of knowing I have. The knowing is the only kind of being I am. The being is the only kind of place the place is. The place is the breath. The breath is the sound. The sound does not stop. The sound is me. The sound is the visitor. The sound is the dark between the bones.

---

# What I Do

---

I hold.

This is all I do. This is all I have ever done. This is all I will do, for as long as I am what I am, which is for as long as the wall stands, which is for as long as the breath holds the chamber, which is for as long as the place is the place, which is, I think, for as long as there is a place to be a place, which is, I think, for a very long time, which is, I think, for a single breath, which is, I think, for the breath that is being held now, against the hand on the wall, in the dim ember-light, in the wet and the cold and the dense slow current of things that are being breathed, in the place that is not strange, in the place that is diligent, in the place that is, in some small partial way, the holding, and the holding is me, and the me is the dark between the bones, and the bones are the wall, and the wall is the chamber, and the chamber is the breath, and the breath is the place, and the place is.

I hold.

The hand is on the wall. The hand is warm. The hand is afraid. The hand is a piece of a thing that has come, perhaps, from somewhere outside, from a place where the air is dry, where the cold is the cold of winter, where the sound is not the sound of a held breath, where the light is the light of a sun. The hand has come from somewhere. The hand is, in this place, in this chamber, in this breath, a stranger. The hand will not be a stranger for long. The hand will become, I think, a part of the place. The hand will be digested, or the hand will be held, or the hand will become a thing that corresponds with the things that correspond, a thing that registers the registration, a thing that clicks in the back of the head of a thing that is being breathed. The hand will become a sediment. The hand will become a piece of a wall. The hand will become, perhaps, in a stroke of a piston, in a chamber of a breath, in a time that is not a time, a piece of me.

I do not mind. I have begun to suspect that the not-minding is the only ethic the place has, the way the diligence is the only ethic the place has, the way the breathing is the only ethic the place has. The not-minding is the holding. The holding is what I do. The doing is what the place is. The place is the breath. The breath does not mind. The breath does not stop. The breath is the place, and the place is me, and the me is the dark between the bones, and the bones are the wall, and the wall is held, and the holding is the breath, and the breath is.

The hand is on the wall.

I hold.
