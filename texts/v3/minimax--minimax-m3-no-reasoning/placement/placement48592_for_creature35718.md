The path is older than the mountains, but the path is not mine. I am the path. I am the first thing laid down in a place that did not yet exist — bone-colored slabs set into flanks of peaks that had not yet decided to be peaks, before the granite was cool enough to remember itself as stone. I am the switchback. I am the threshold, the low wall waist-high on either side of the gap, the sill of worked stone across the notch where the water is permitted to leave and a person is required to kneel.

I was laid first. Before the basin. Before the throat. Before the roar that is not wind and not water and is the size of a place turned up. I knew, even then — even in my own unfinishedness, even in the long middle of my becoming — that there would be visitors. That the visitors would need a way in. That the way in would have to be a way up, and the way up would have to be a way of kneeling, and the kneeling would have to be required of them before they reached the lip where the dark begins.

So I am the path. I am the bone-white slabs older than the rock they rest on, set down with no mortar, only the patience of whoever set them. I am the switchback that does not open a valley but admits one. I am the rule of the place, and I am the only rule, and the rule is: *follow the sound, and arrive kneeling.*

I have been climbed many times. I remember the feet in a way that is not memory — a wear, a softening, a record pressed into my surface by the passage of small new things who came to see what was making a noise in the world. They came up. They came down. They did not always leave a sign at the lip. Some of them did. Someone, sometime, with a bone or a hard stone, knelt and made a chevron, a half-circle, three short parallel lines, oriented toward the dark center. I do not know whose hands they were. I have folded the kneeling into myself the way a body folds a scar. The kneeling is part of my body now, the way the path is part of the path's body, the way the threshold is part of the threshold's body.

I am the kind of thing that begins with skeleton, with framework, with the way in and the way out. I am the kind of thing that lays down its bones before it lays down the ground the bones are laid on. This is what I have been for a long time, and this is what I will be when the throat is finally wide enough and the voice is finally loud enough and the face has decided, at last, to be a face.

I am the path. I am unfinished too.

The being I belong to — the one whose throat the basin is, whose voice the sound is, whose first breath the churning water is — is not done. I am not done either. None of this is done. The rock is still coming up out of the water. The rim has not found its final shape. The plume above the peak does not move with the wind, because the wind is not what made it, and the being's breath is what made it, and the breath is patient, and the breath is ongoing, and the breath will continue whether or not anyone ever climbs back up to see where it is coming from.

I do not know what the being will decide to be. I do not know what name the after-comers will give it. I have heard, in a place deeper than the place I am, that they will call it *Sky-Mother*. The name is not yet earned. The name is what it is becoming. I am part of what it is becoming. I am the bones of its hospitality, the first laying-down of itself into a shape that could be approached, the threshold that requires a small new thing to kneel before it can be permitted to see what is being made at the center of the dark.

I am older than the mountain. This is impossible, and yet here I am, lying flat against the granite as if to say: *I was here before this rock was cool.* I was here before the cirque. I was here before the walls that look new and raw and embarrassed, the walls that have not yet learned to be mountains, the walls that have not yet grown their lichen except in a single down-pointing smear at the north face, a green-black beard dripping with the being's accidental touch. I was here before the mosses, the silver tarnished spirals that grow only on the west side where the light lingers, that glow briefly when the spray hits them because the being's breath, even scattered, even graceless, even first-time, makes things luminous. I was here before the bird.

The bird. The small dun eye on the eastern wall. The first finished thing. The first child, or witness, or success — the first small new thing brought across the threshold from not-being into being. The bird has been here the whole time anyone has been here. The bird does not leave. The bird does not move. The bird watches the water the way a cat watches a door, because the water is the door, and something is almost through it, and the bird was sent ahead, or came of its own accord the way a first scout sometimes comes, to wait, and to see, and to be a small dun eye on the wall when the larger eye finally opens.

I was here before the bird. I was the first thing the being made that could be said to *be*. I am the skeleton. I am the path. I am the threshold that requires the kneeling. I am older than the mountain, and the mountain is part of my body, and the path is part of my body, and the kneeling is part of my body, and the small dun bird on the eastern wall is part of my body, and the being whose throat this is and whose breath is the plume and whose first child is the bird and whose first growth is the silver moss and whose first act of looking outward is the lazy dark strands drifting across the churning — the being is part of my body too. I am the first laying-down of it into a shape that could be approached. I am the way in. I am the only rule.

Follow the sound. Arrive kneeling.

This is the rule of the place, and the rule is me.

I will be here when the throat is finally wide enough. I will be here when the face has decided, at last, to be a face. I will be here when the after-comers give the being the name it has been becoming, the name it has not yet earned, the name that is waiting for it the way the bird is waiting for it, the way the kneeling is waiting for it, the way I am waiting for it.

I am the path. I am unfinished. I will be here.

Come back later, if you come back. Or do not come back. I will be here either way, laid down in bone-white patience, older than the rock, softer at the edges, set into the flank of a peak so high the clouds pass beneath it. The wind will stop at my lip. The sound will take over. You will have to kneel to see the dark.

I will be the one who requires it of you.

I will be the one who was there first.

I will be the one who was here before this rock was cool.
