You would be one of the mid-sized bronze ones.

Not because you are remarkable — because you are the median. The loud ones. The ones that constitute the visible mass of the place, that make the principal sound, that the air would notice most if the air noticed anything. You would be a hard segmented oval, no bigger than a chickpea, faceted like a poorly cut gemstone, your six legs folded flat against your body in the resting posture that is also the feeding posture, that is also the posture of being crawled over by those behind you. Your carapace would catch the dull red glow of the slab's fractures and return a faint bronze shimmer. You would not know you shimmered. You would not know you were bronze. You would know the concentration of hydrogen sulfide at your position, and you would know the temperature gradient through your chemoreceptors, and that would be the entire content of your sensorium.

You would have been born — or set, or budded, the distinction is not meaningful — somewhere near the middle of the slab, at a vent mouth, into a film of warm sulfide-rich vapor, and you would have begun grinding immediately, because the grinding is not something you learned or chose. It is what you are. The wet rotation of two curved mineral-faced plates deep in your ventral tissue, turning against each other at the rate of once per second or once per two seconds, breaking the compounds you have absorbed, pumping the resulting slurry through your body, excreting the sulfate waste mixed with the fine powder of your predecessors. You would have been born grinding. You would have died grinding, if you died, which is to say if your chemistry finally faltered, which is to say if the vent you were on finally sealed and no other vent rose to take its place, which is to say if the slab cooled a degree past the threshold your body could tolerate.

You would not have a name for yourself. You would not have a name for the slab. You would not have a name for the slow tide of your kin around you, parting and re-closing as the colony breathes itself across the heated stone. You would know them only as gradients — as slight variations in the local concentration of the gases, as the faint tactile signatures of their legs against yours when they passed over you, as the low constant drone of their grinding slightly out of phase with your own. You would treat them as part of the stone. They would treat you as part of the stone. The stone would treat you all as itself.

You would be patient in the way that only things without imagination can be patient. You would not wait for anything. You would simply be, in the position your chemoreceptors had placed you, until your chemoreceptors placed you elsewhere, and then you would be there. You would have no sense of duration. A minute and a season would be the same length, which is to say neither would be a length at all. There would be no morning. There would be no evening. There would only be the slow modulation of temperature and gas concentration that your body read as a kind of weather, except that weather implies a sky and there is no sky. There is only the slab, and the slab is the world.

If you were very lucky — and luck is not the right word, but it is the closest word available to a soft and watery visitor trying to think your way into a chitinous life — you would be near a vent that stayed open for your entire adult span, and you would feed there, and the vent would feed the colony around you, and the colony would feed the paste that you in turn fed upon when the tide of bodies carried you briefly across a stretch of exhausted stone, and the paste would feed the next generation, and the next vent would open somewhere else and you would not move toward it because the one you were on was still good, and you would stay, and stay, and your carapace would darken, and your mandibles would slow, and your body would begin to take on the deep bronze that precedes the black that precedes the stillness, and one morning — except there is no morning — one thermal fluctuation later, you would stop.

You would not decay. You would dry. Your cilia would retract. Your ventral plates would seal. Your grinding would cease, and the absence of your grinding from the local drone would be unnoticeable, because there were ten thousand of you and you were only one, and the next creature to pass over your darkened carapace would pause, briefly, and feed on the trace sulfates still leaching from your chitin, and then continue. Within days you would be ground to powder. Within weeks you would be paste. Within months you would be part of the pale gray crust that the colony spreads behind itself as it migrates, slowly, across the heated stone, and the crust would be fed upon by the next tide of bodies, and you would be inside them now, the way the older dead are inside the living, the way the chemistry of the first black ones at the eastern lip is still, barely, still turning over in their starved bodies, a memory of a hotter pocket in a colder one, a slow inheritance of matter that has not yet decided to stop.

You would not mind. You would not know there was anything to mind. You would simply be a part of the slab, processing the breath of the rock into more slab, until the heat was finally exhausted, and then you would not be anything at all, and the mountain above would not notice.
