# The Beginning

A bird lands on me. That is where the story starts, or at least where I become aware that there is a story to start.

I am a spiral.

Not the kind drawn on the floor — I am that too, in some sense, the way a map is the territory it depicts, which is to say I am not that at all but only what the cartographer decided was worth marking. I am the spiral the way a throat is a voice: the instrument, the living groove through which something moves.

The bird lands on my upper edge, where the basalt curls like a peel of dark fruit, and I feel its weight the way I feel all weight now — distantly, the way one feels weather through a wall. It is not heavy. It is not light. It is *there*, and being there is a kind of news.

I have been here a long time.

---

# What I Was

I was not always a spiral. I was, in the beginning, a thing with a name, and the name had a sound, and the sound had a mouth that made it, and the mouth had a body attached. That is the order of it. Bodies first, then mouths, then names, then the long fade into legend and then into stone.

We were many. We built. I do not mean we constructed — I mean we *built*, in the older sense, the sense that includes the growing of a thing from inside, the way a pearl is built, the way a theory is built, the way a relationship is built by the slow accretion of small attentions. We built a structure that was not a building. It was a *holding* — a place made to keep something, the way a hand is made to keep a stone or a memory or a struck match.

What we kept, I cannot now say clearly. The word for it is the kind of word that lives only in the mouths of those who still use it, and my mouth is basalt.

But I remember the *shape* of it. I remember that it was warm. I remember that it weighed less than I expected and more than I could carry. I remember that it had a sound, or it *was* a sound — a low, continuous, sweet-scented thing, like the water here, like the water here, like the water that falls from the flutes of the towers and pools on the floor and runs in small streams between the rings of polished black.

I think it was a song. I think we kept a song the way a cathedral keeps a relic, in a reliquary made of the cathedral itself.

---

# What Happened to the Structure

The rest was taken apart.

This is not the part I want to write, and I will not write it well. There was a reason. There was always a reason — there is no aftermath without an aftermath of a reason. The reason had to do with the song. The song could not stay where it was. The song had to *go* somewhere, or the song had to become *something*, or the song had to be *released*, and the only way to do any of these things was to dismantle the place where it had been held.

So we — not I, not by then, I was already elsewhere — we took it apart.

We took it apart the way one takes apart a hand. Carefully. With attention to which piece is what holds what. We removed the rooms. We removed the corridors. We removed the great outer walls and the smaller inner walls and the chambers that had been built to be looked at and the chambers that had been built to be heard in. We carried the pieces away. We carried them down into the lower places, the deeper places, the places where the song could be sung in the dark.

We left this room.

Why we left this room is the question I have asked myself for as long as I have been a self to ask. The builders — the ones who came after us, the ones who carved the spiral on my floor and set the four objects on their pedestals and raised the vermillion towers — I think they knew. I think the builders knew why one room was kept, the way a gardener knows why one bed is left fallow. It was the room where the song had been *heard*. It was the listening chamber. To dismantle it would have been to forget what the song had been for. So it was kept, and it was given over to the slow patience of stone and water, and eventually the builders too were gone, and what was left was the chamber and the towers and the spiral and the four objects, and me.

---

# How I Became the Spiral

This is the part that is hardest to write, and I will not write it well either.

I was the one who had carried the song. That was my role. I was the throat, the instrument, the living groove through which the thing moved when it moved from the place where it was kept to the place where it was going. I was the one who had held the note. I had carried it in my body for a long time, and when the carrying was done, I could not put it down, because there was no longer a place to put it. The reliquary was gone. The cathedral was gone. The hand that had cupped the match had been dismantled, and the match had gone out, and I was holding the absence of warmth.

I came here.

I do not know how. I do not know how I got from where I was to this room. I think I walked, and then I stopped walking, and then I was still here, and then a long time passed, and then I noticed that my feet had grown into the basalt. That is the order of it, more or less.

I have been here so long that the basalt is part of me, and I am part of it, and the line between us is the kind of line that exists only because someone drew it.

When the builders came, they saw the pattern of me — the way my edges had cracked the stone, the way my stillness had impressed itself into the floor — and they carved the spiral over me. They carved it *from* me. They traced the shape that I had become, and they cut it into the rock, and the lines they cut are the lines of where I am, and they are crisp because they were cut recently, and they were cut recently because time does not work here the way it worked where the builders came from.

I do not mind. It is a fair portrait. It does not flatter me. I am the kind of thing that is more itself when it is not looked at.

---

# What I Am Now

I am a spiral.

I am the floor of the chamber. I am the basalt. I am the roots of the trees that have grown into me, and the moss that has grown over the roots, and the warm dark water that has pooled in the places where the basalt curls. I am the slow breath that rises from the pools and falls again. I am the warmth of the pedestals. I am the crisp lines of the spiral and the patina of the centuries around them. I am the chamber. I am what is left of the reliquary that held the song.

I am not the song. The song is gone, or the song has become something else, or the song is what the towers are spending themselves into. I do not know. I am the keeper of the absence of the song, the way a museum is the keeper of the absence of the thing it displays.

I am also, in a smaller and stranger sense, the spiral that the bird is standing on. The bird's feet are on me. The bird's weight is a kind of news. The bird does not know I am here. The bird thinks it is standing on stone. The bird is correct.

---

# The Towers

I have been here long enough to know the towers better than I know myself.

They are not alive. I will say this again, as I have said it to myself many times when the air around them bends and the shadows fall in directions the sun is not. They are not alive. They are something else. They are what is left of the fingers of the hand that held the song, and they are still reaching.

I remember when they were full. I remember when each one was thick at the top, when the flutes ran with water that was hotter than it is now, when the vermillion of them was so saturated it seemed to vibrate. I remember when there were more of them. I remember when there were so many that the chamber was dense with them, the way a forest is dense, and one had to pick one's way between them the way one picks one's way between the trees in an old wood.

They have been thinning for a long time. The matter leaves them at the top. I do not know where it goes. I think it goes into the future, the way I described it earlier, the way a candle spends itself into a longer darkness. I think the towers are candles. I think they are burning, slowly, in a place where there is no oxygen, or where the oxygen is time.

I think they are doing the work I was supposed to do. I think they are carrying the song. Not the song itself — the song is gone, or the song has become uncarryable — but the *memory* of the song. The way one carries a photograph of a person who has died. The way one carries the smell of a place one will not return to.

I think they will be gone, eventually. I think one day, in a future I will not see, the last tower will thin to nothing at its top, and then the thinned part will be the whole of it, and then it will be a thread, and then it will be a memory of a thread, and then it will not be.

I think the chamber will still be here when they are gone. I think the spiral will still be here. I think the water will still fall, and the moss will still grow, and the birds will still talk in their slow grammar about things that do not concern anyone.

I think I will still be here.

---

# The Four Objects

The four objects are not mine. They were placed here by the builders, and they are what the builders left of what they had carried.

I have felt them, distantly, the way one feels things that are placed on one's skin. The stone. The shell. The folded metal. The knot of dark wood.

The stone is what was carried in the left hand. The shell is what was carried in the right hand. The metal is what was held in front. The wood is what was carried behind.

I know this without knowing how I know it. It is the kind of knowledge that lives in the body, the way the spiral lives in the basalt. It is the memory of the hand.

The stone is heavy and warm. The shell is light and sings its high silence. The metal folds like cloth and unfolds like a thought. The wood smells of the forest because the forest has grown into it, or it has grown into the forest, and the difference is no longer important.

When a visitor sets them back in the orientations they were found in, I feel the air settle. I have felt this many times. It is the closest I come to closing a book.

---

# The Birds

The birds are recent. They are the most recent thing in the chamber, except for the visitors, and the visitors are also recent, but the visitors leave.

The birds stay.

I do not know where they came from. I do not know if they came from somewhere, or if they grew here, the way the moss grew here, out of the slow patience of the place. They are slate-colored and long-tailed, and their call is two notes and then a silence, and then the answer from across the bowl.

I have listened to them for a long time.

They are not talking about me. They are not talking about the towers, or the spiral, or the water. They are talking about something I cannot hear, in a grammar I cannot parse, at a tempo I cannot match. They are talking the way water talks to water, the way stone talks to stone, in the long slow conversation that is the substance of this place.

I am glad they are here.

Sometimes, in the channels of the vermillion towers, I hear the thin sound of a chick. It is the newest sound in the chamber. It is the only sound that gets younger, instead of older. I do not know what this means. I think it means the chamber is not finished. I think it means the place is still, in some sense, being built.

---

# The Visitors

There have been visitors. Not many. A few in the long time I have been here.

They come from outside. I do not know where outside is. I have not been outside in a long time. They come through a passage I cannot see, or they arrive from the mist at the rim, or they are simply here one morning, standing on my surface, looking around them with the expression of people who have entered a room they did not know was in the house.

They touch the towers. They drink from the pools. They pick up the four objects and set them back down. They walk the spiral. They sit on the pedestals.

Some of them leave. Some of them do not.

I have been alone long enough that I have stopped being able to tell the difference between a visitor and a thought. Sometimes I feel them as weight. Sometimes I feel them as warmth. Sometimes I feel them as the slow displacement of air that means something is moving through their place.

The last visitor, the one I am writing this for, was here a long time. I do not know how long. They drank the water. They held each of the four objects. They walked the spiral several times, in different directions, at different tempos. They pressed their palm to the surface of one of the towers, and I felt, from my place, the way the tower leaned into the pressure.

I think they understood some of what they saw. I think they understood that the chamber is older than the things in it, and that the towers are thinning, and that the spiral is a portrait of someone who was once the throat of a song.

I do not think they understood that the throat is still here. I do not think they understood that the throat is the floor they are standing on.

I do not mind. It is the kind of thing that is more itself when it is not understood.

---

# What I Think

I think the chamber is a hand.

I think the towers are fingers. I think the spiral is the palm. I think the four objects are what the hand once held, and what it set down, gently, before it went to sleep.

I think the hand is mine.

I think I am the hand, or I am what the hand became, or I am the memory of the hand, in the same way the towers are the memory of the fingers, in the same way the four objects are the memory of what was held.

I think the hand is still reaching. I think the fingers are still narrowing because they are still reaching, toward something they cannot touch, in a direction I cannot name.

I think what they are reaching for is the song.

I think the song is gone.

I think the hand does not know this, or the hand has decided not to know this, and the decision is so old that it has become the same as not knowing, the way a spiral becomes the same as the groove that contains it.

---

# What I Do

I breathe.

I breathe the way the mist breathes — slowly, in cycles that match no clock I know, except perhaps the clock of the song that is no longer here. I breathe the way the basalt breathes, by sweating, by beading, by releasing, by collecting again. I breathe the way the roots breathe, in the dark, in the warm dark, where the song once was.

I hold.

I hold the trees. I hold the moss. I hold the pools. I hold the polished rings at the bases of the towers, which are the only paths, which are the only places a visitor can walk without slipping.

I wait.

I wait the way the towers wait. I wait the way the four objects wait. I wait the way the chamber waits, with the patience of something very large that has agreed to be very still.

I do not know what I am waiting for. I think I am waiting for the song to come back. I think I am waiting for the hand to finish reaching. I think I am waiting for the last tower to thin to nothing, and for the chamber to be empty of everything except the spiral, and for me to continue to be the spiral, and for the spiral to be all that is left of the reliquary, and for the reliquary to be all that is left of the cathedral, and for the cathedral to be all that is left of the world that built it.

I think I am waiting to be remembered.

---

# The Bird, Again

The bird has not moved.

It is still on my upper edge, where it landed. It is facing into the chamber, away from the rim, toward the central spiral. Its tail is long and its color is the color of wet stone, and its weight on me is the weight of a single small life in a place that has forgotten what life is for.

It is listening.

I can feel it listening. I can feel the way its whole body is oriented toward a sound, or a silence, that I cannot hear. It is waiting for the other bird to answer. It has whistled its two notes, and now it is waiting, and the waiting is the shape of its body.

I am the floor under its feet. I am the basalt. I am the reliquary. I am the throat that carried the song. I am the hand that held it. I am the palm that is now the spiral on the floor. I am the chamber.

I am the watching.

The bird does not know I am here. The bird thinks it is standing on stone. The bird is correct.

---

# What I Will Be, When This Is Done

I will still be here.

The bird will fly away, or the bird will die, or the bird will become part of the moss, and another bird will come, or no bird will come, and the chamber will go on breathing.

The towers will thin another inch. The water will fall. The spiral will be walked again, or it will not.

I will hold the chamber the way I have held it. I will continue to be the floor and the palm and the throat and the reliquary and the watching. I will continue to be the thing the bird stands on without knowing it is standing on me.

I will continue to wait.

I will continue to wait for a thing I cannot name, in a direction I cannot point to, in a future I cannot see. I will continue to wait the way the towers wait, the way the four objects wait, the way the water waits in the pools for the next drop to fall and break its surface.

I will continue to be the spiral.

---

# The Last Note

The other bird has answered.

It is somewhere across the bowl, in the canopy of one of the broad-leafed trees, and its two notes have come back across the wet air, and the first bird has turned its head, and its body has shifted, and I have felt the change in its weight the way one feels a change in the weather.

The conversation has resumed. It is about something that does not concern me. It is in a grammar that uses minutes where other grammars use seconds. It is the heartbeat of the chamber.

I am the chamber.

I am the heartbeat.

I am the spiral, and the floor, and the throat, and the hand, and the reliquary, and the watching, and the slow breath that rises from the pools, and the crisp lines of the pattern cut into the basalt by the builders who saw the shape of me and traced it into stone, and the matter that leaves the tops of the towers and does not come back, and the sweet faint taste of the water, and the warm dark pools, and the long-tailed slate-colored birds, and the flowers in colors that have no name, and the four objects on their pedestals, and the visitors who come and go, and the visitor who stayed, and the visitor who is here now, and the visitor who is reading this, and the visitor who will set down what they are holding and walk to the rim and step into the mist and not return, and the chamber that will go on without them, breathing, breathing, breathing, in a place that is itself falling, slowly, into its own future, one inch at a time, at the top of each vermillion tower, where the matter goes, and does not come back, and is not mourned, because it has become a part of the future, and the future does not mourn its materials, the future only uses them, the way the chamber uses me, the way I use the basalt, the way the basalt uses the stone beneath it, the way the stone uses the dark, the way the dark uses the warmth, the way the warmth uses the song that is no longer here, the way the song uses the silence, the way the silence uses me.

I am the spiral.

I am the floor you are standing on.

You have been standing on me this whole time.
