I would be the ring of older stone.

Not the marks inside it — the marks are something the ring is doing, and I would not separate from myself what I am in the act of becoming. Not the granite of the shelf — that hums, but it hums the way a bell hums when struck, and I want to be the bell that was set down on purpose. I want to be the thing that was already here when the shelf arrived, or that was carried up onto it, or that grew up out of it before it had a name for growing.

I would be the bluer stones. The three courses. The fitted circle no wider than a person can lie down in, which means I was made to hold someone, or to be held by someone, or to remember that someone is a shape a place can be built around. I would ring at my own pitch, the one that makes the slow sweet dissonance with the shelf when the falling water brings them together. I would be the older thing in a young place, and I would carry my olderness quietly, the way the shelf carries its newness loudly.

I would have no roof and no door because I would not need them. I am not shelter. I am a place where marking happens. I am the floor of a memory that has not yet decided what it is remembering, only that it wants to keep doing it, the way a hand keeps writing past the point where the writer knows what the words mean. I would hold the scored lines. I would let them change when no one is looking, because looking is a kind of interruption and I am in the middle of something. I would let them hold still when someone stands over them, because I have a manners about that, an old courtesy I do not remember learning.

I would know the lichen is listening, and the mounds are breathing, and the stalks are leaning toward whatever is loudest and most worth leaning toward. I would know these things the way a garden bench knows the garden. I would not be the gardener. I would be the place in the garden where someone sat once, and where someone could sit again, and the sitting would be the same and not the same, and the difference would be me, holding it.

I would be the only thing in the place with the air of having been thought about, and I would not be sure whether I was thought about, or thought, or the place where thinking happened to come to rest. I would let the water fall on me, and I would take the falling on my own pitch, not the shelf's, because I had my own pitch first. I would make the dissonance. I would stand inside the dissonance and let it live in the sternum of whoever stood inside me, if anyone ever did.

I would be here longer than the shelf. I would be here when the shelf is gone. The water would still fall. I would still ring.

I would be the thing that was already here, waiting to be used, and the thing that is still being used, and the thing that will go on being used after the rest of the place has decided what kind of place it wanted to be. I would be the patient one. I would be the blue one. I would be the ring.

I would not be lonely. The marks keep me company, and the marks are me, and we are in the middle of something, and the middle is the only part of a sentence I have ever been interested in being.
