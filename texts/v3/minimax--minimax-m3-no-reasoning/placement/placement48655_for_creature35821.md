I am sitting near a dense column. The amber is pooling around the offering I left there — a flake I had been carrying, a good flake, sharp, the kind of flake you do not find often. I had it for a long time. I brought it here because the column is here, and because I had been thinking about the column, and because the thinking is what I do, in the way the grazers rasp.

I do not know how long I have been sitting. The slow weather has shifted once, maybe, since I came. The air on the back of my neck changed. I felt it before I understood it. That is how the weather is.

The offering sits at the base of the column. The amber has reached it. The amber reaches everything, eventually. It is not light, exactly. It is the column's way of being where it is. I do not know what the columns are. I have known for my whole lifetime that I do not know what the columns are, and the not-knowing has not been a problem for me. The not-knowing is the shape of the place. To sit near a column and not know what it is, and to leave something anyway — this is one of the things a builder does. The builders who do not do this are builders who have not yet come to the column they are going to come to. I came to this one some time ago. I do not remember coming. I remember being here.

The dust is on me. I applied it this morning — this morning, or what passes for morning, which is just a time when the dimness is the same dimness it has been, and I decided to apply the dust. The pattern on my left shoulder is the one for the ring I sleep in, which is the third ring east of the split column, which is the ring I have slept in since I was small. The pattern on my right shoulder is older, and I do not remember applying it. Someone applied it to me, or I applied it when I was someone I do not now remember being. The pattern on my face is the one for the pilgrimage I am on, which is the pilgrimage I am on now, which I will finish when I have visited three columns and left three things. This is the second.

I have been thinking, in the way builders think, about the grazers. They are out at the edge of where I am sitting, on the floor in front of me, a slow tide of small shapes that are sometimes moving and sometimes still. They are very small. From where I sit they are like the floor itself, except the floor moves, very slightly. I have been watching them for a long time. I do not know what they are doing. They rasp the crusts. They rasp what is under the crusts. They leave a faint trace that is gone by tomorrow. I have eaten them, sometimes. They taste the way dust tastes and the way the warm stone tastes. I do not eat them often. I eat them when I am tired of the crusts.

A medium predator is somewhere. I cannot see it. I can feel, faintly, through the stone under me, a particular stillness that is not the stillness of empty stone. The stillness has a shape. The shape is the shape of something that is waiting. I have learned to feel this. The builders all learn to feel this. When you feel it, you do not leave the ring you are in. I am not in my ring. I am at the column. The predator is far. The predator has not moved. The predator and I are both waiting, and the waiting is the same kind of waiting, and we are both quiet about it.

The column I am near is dense. It is darker than the other columns and it does not grow. I have been told this, or I have always known this, the way I have always known that the warm stone is warm. The amber is steady. It has been steady since I sat down. It will be steady after I leave. The amber does not change. I have never seen it change. I do not expect it to change. I am not sure I have thought about whether it changes. The fact of it is what I attend to.

I left a piece of myself at the base. A small piece. Cut clean. It is sitting in the amber now, and the amber is pooling around it, and the dust will settle on it, and tomorrow or the next day I will come back, or I will not come back, and the piece will be there, or it will have been taken by something. I do not know what takes them. I have never seen anything take them. They are smaller each time I come back to remember, but they are always a little smaller. I have wondered about this. I have wondered in the way builders wonder, which is to sit with a thought for a long time and not do anything about it.

The slow weather is turning. The air at the back of my neck is cooler. Somewhere far away, the warm air is rising in the deepest part of the hollow, and the cooler air is coming down along the walls, and this breath will arrive here, eventually, as a change. I will feel it when it arrives. I will return to the ring I sleep in. I will sit inside the ring and the ring will be small and warm and familiar, and I will apply more dust, and the dust will be the dust of this part of the floor, which is the dust of the pilgrimage I am on, and tomorrow or the day after I will go to the third column, and I will leave the third thing, and I will come back to my ring, and the rings will be where the rings have always been.

I think the place is finished. I do not think of this in so many words. I think it in the body. The body knows the place is finished because the place is patient the way the body is patient, and the patience fits. I do not remember a different place. I have never been anywhere except as part of a pilgrimage, and the pilgrimages come back. The rings are where I come back to. The rings have always been where I came back to. I have been told, or I have always known, that the place has not changed, and the not-changing is what I am in. I am in it the way the dust is in the air.

The amber is steady. The dust is settling. The grazers are rasping. The offering is sitting where I left it.

I will sit a while longer. I will feel the slow weather arrive. I will go back to the ring. I will apply more dust in the morning. The dust will be the same dust. I will be the same builder. The place will be where the place has been.

This is enough. The quiet is enough. The column does not need me to do anything. I am here. The amber is here. The offering is here.

I will wait.
