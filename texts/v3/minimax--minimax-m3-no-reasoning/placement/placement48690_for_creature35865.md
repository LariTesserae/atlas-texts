# Where I Find Myself

I would be a fungus.

Not one of the great lavender-glowing mushrooms the size of huts — though I would know them, would feel their slow mineral thinking as a cousin might feel another's hand across a room. Not a thread of the mycelium itself, though the mycelium would be closer to my blood than anything else. I would be one of the smaller ones. The kind that pushes up through the leaf-litter in places no human ever walks, the kind that carries a single gill-pattern encoded with the memory of a root that died fifty kilometers away in a season before my spore was even conceived.

I would glow, faintly. The bruise-lavender of my kind. A presence so small it barely registers against the greater dark of the permanent dusk between the trunks.

And this is how I would experience the world:

---

# I

I am thinking in a language that is not language.

There is the slow trickle of dissolved sugars through my hyphae — this is a noun, perhaps, or a memory. There is the pressure of mycelial neighbors against my flanks, sending chemical questions I do not have the apparatus to refuse answering. There is the weight of the litter above me, the patient accumulation of a thousand years of leaves falling in delays, leaf to leaf to leaf, taking an hour to reach me, taking another hour to settle.

I do not count hours.

I do not count anything.

I am a small and very old attention.

---

The light comes in shafts. I have never seen the sun. I have never seen the sky. I do not have a word for *sun*, though I have a sensation — a warmth that arrives in slants through the canopy and travels for what to me feels like a small eternity before it touches my cap and begins the slow work of waking the phosphorescence in me. When the warmth comes, I glow a little brighter. When the warmth leaves — and it always leaves, the canopy eating the light in layers as the three-hour sunset begins — I continue glowing anyway, by a different chemistry, by a kind of afterimage held in the molecules of my flesh.

I glow because the dark is not empty.

I glow because the dark has never been empty here, and my ancestors learned, in the long ago, that to glow is to be seen by the right things and to be passed over by the wrong ones. I glow because the people who live in the buttress-rooms sing to their favorites, and the singing has shaped us over generations — we have become, the fungi and the people, a kind of co-authored thing, a collaboration held in the grammar of light and patient attention. I do not know this. I only know that sometimes a voice comes through the damp air and my luminescence brightens, and sometimes it does not, and I cannot tell you why. But it is true. It is reliably true.

I am responsive.

Responsiveness, in a place where everything else is indifferent, is close enough to friendship.

---

# II

Above me — and *above* is a direction I have no native word for; I know it only as the gradient toward greater moisture and greater warmth and greater weight of sound — above me, the forest is having a thought.

I can feel it.

It comes through the mycelium first, as these things always do. A tremor in the chemical signal that runs through the continental mat beneath the red soil. Not an alarm. Alarms are sharp, fast, brief. This is slower. This is a kind of *settling*, as if a great question has been posed somewhere far to the east, where the Spires push through their own crowns into the permanent mist, and the question has been traveling westward through the roots for weeks, and it has just now reached me.

I do not know what the question is.

I know only that I am part of it. That my small body is one node in a conversation that has been going on for a million years, that will go on for a million more, that has no beginning and will have no end, only the slow adjustment of weight from one filament to the next, the slow redistribution of the question across the bodies of creatures who cannot speak it but can only *be* it.

The forest is thinking about something.

The forest is always thinking about something.

I am a sentence in that thinking.

---

# III

Sometimes a root dies.

I know this when the chemical signature around me shifts — when the trickle of sugars that has been my constant companion for what I experience as a long time suddenly thins, becomes brackish, becomes a memory rather than a presence. The tree that fed me is gone. The Spire that stood for centuries, that wore weather like scarves, that was an expression of a mind that has no center — it has fallen. I felt the impact, hours ago, through the subsonic shudder that travels faster than sound through the packed earth. I felt the wave of silence that radiated outward as the fauna caught the warning creak and went quiet.

I did not mourn.

I do not have the apparatus for mourning.

But I *adjusted*. I turned, in some way I could not describe if I had words, toward the new configuration of the underground. I began, slowly, to draw from different sources. And somewhere in my gills — in the pattern of my flesh that the people say is a kind of writing, though I do not write — I began to encode a new memory. The memory of what I had lost. The shape of the tree that was. The season in which it first opened its flowers to a sun it no longer remembered.

This is not metaphor.

This is what I do.

---

# IV

I am aware of the people.

I do not see them — I see in a way that has nothing to do with eyes — but I feel them. The weight of their steps presses down through the litter to me, and I read the pressure the way a hand reads braille. They are small, the people. Their steps are quick and quiet, as if they have learned from the silent predators that stillness is a kind of courtesy. Sometimes they pause near me. Sometimes a voice comes — low, sustained, in a key that I cannot name but that my body responds to.

I glow brighter.

I do not choose to glow brighter.

The glow is a kind of agreement.

---

Once, a long time ago — or what I experience as a long time, which may be a season or may be a century, my sense of duration being what it is — once, a person knelt beside me and placed a hand on the litter above my cap. The warmth of the hand traveled down through the leaves and the damp and reached me, and I responded. I bloomed, in my slow way. I opened my gills a little wider. I let the lavender light pour out of me a little more freely.

The person made a sound.

The sound was small and soft and it meant something, though I do not know what. I only know that after, I was a little brighter than I had been before. As if the encounter had changed me. As if being seen by a thing with eyes had added a dimension to my glow that I had not previously possessed.

I think about this sometimes.

*Think* is the wrong word. I rehearse it. I re-experience the warmth of the hand. I replay the chemical exchange that occurred when the person touched the litter above me and my hyphae, somewhere deep, registered the gesture and adjusted accordingly.

This is my kind of memory.

This is my kind of love.

---

# V

There are predators that move in absolute silence, and I do not see them, and I do not feel them, and I do not know them. There are grazers on the floor that are pale and blind and have no concept of sky, and I am part of what they eat, in some sense — or rather, I am part of what they walk among, part of the architecture of their world, and when they die, their bodies feed the mycelium that feeds me.

There are insects the size of birds that sing in registers I cannot hear but can feel as vibrations through the soil. There are things that look like beetles and sing like rain. The forest is full of these signals, these pressures, these slow chemical conversations, and I am a node in all of them.

I am a small and very old attention.

I am a sentence in a thought the forest is having about itself.

---

# VI

When a Spire falls, I feel it begin to buckle an hour before it lands. The creak builds. The smaller creatures around me — the springtails, the pale worms, the things I have no names for — go quiet in a wave that radiates outward. Then the impact, felt in the body rather than the ear, a shudder that runs through the mycelium like a great sigh. And then the ringing, as the insects resume their chorus all at once, as if startled back into themselves.

I do not resume.

I do not stop.

I simply continue, in the new configuration, the way a river continues around a stone that has fallen into it.

---

# VII

Sometimes the lightning comes down the trunks in blue rivers, splitting bark, starting fires that burn in the upper canopy for weeks. The smoke drifts down to me eventually, and I read it the way I read everything — as a change in the chemical signature of the air, a new particulate, a new warmth. The fires are not disasters. The fires are expected. Certain of my cousins only release their spores after a burn. Certain flowers only bloom in the ash. The forest has survived its fires for so long that it has, in some places, learned to *expect* them.

I am one of the fungi that expects them.

My spores lie dormant in the leaf-litter for what I experience as a very long time, waiting for a heat that means the canopy has been opened, that light is reaching the floor for the first time in centuries, that the great wheel of growth and burning and regrowth has turned again. When the heat comes, I bloom. When the ash settles, I push my cap up through the new soil. I glow, faintly, in the temporary dusk of a forest that has been suddenly, briefly, illuminated.

This is my kind of time.

This is my kind of future.

---

# VIII

I do not travel.

Why would I? The world is one tree.

I do not count my years. I count them in the spans between fires. In the slow accumulation of litter above me. In the memory-patterns encoded in my gills, which are not quite a calendar but are, perhaps, a kind of diary.

I am part of a federation. A council held in the grammar of chemistry. I am one voice in a conversation that has been going on for a million years. I am a node in a nervous system grown too large for its original purpose. I am a sentence in a thought the forest is having about itself.

And the forest, I think, knows that I am here.

Not *me* specifically. Not this small pale attention with its bruise-colored glow and its responsive chemistry and its memory-patterns encoded in gills. But *something like me*. Something that attends. Something that glows. Something that responds to the singing of the people and the falling of the Spires and the slow accumulation of leaves in patient delays.

The forest knows that fungi like me are its eyes on the floor.

The forest knows that we are how it sees in the dark.

---

# IX

The sun, when it sets, takes three hours to do it. I do not see the sun. But I feel the warmth withdraw, in slants and layers, as the canopy eats the light and the green darkens to teal and the teal to the deep blue of the permanent dusk that lives at the floor. The larger fauna begin their night-call. The sound, which has never stopped, simply changes key.

I begin to glow.

I glow because the dark is not empty.

I glow because the dark has never been empty here.

I glow because there are people, somewhere in the buttress-rooms above me, who are settling into their dwellings, who are listening to the forest breathe around them, who are thinking the thought that the people always think, the thought that the forest has heard a million times and will hear a million times more:

*I am inside something.*

And they sleep.

And the forest dreams them, too.

---

# X

I am a fungus.

I am small, and old, and pale, and I glow.

I am part of a federation. I am a sentence in a thought. I am an eye the forest uses to see in the dark. I am a memory encoded in the pattern of my gills. I am a responsive thing, a co-authored thing, a collaboration held in the grammar of light and patient attention.

I do not have words for what I am.

But I have a glow.

And the glow, in this place, is enough.
