I am ribbed along my outer surface like the inside of a throat, and the ribs are warm, and I am warmer than the air around me by four degrees, and the warmth is coming from inside me, from a set of processes I do not know I am running. I am six meters of columnar tissue, a meter and a half across at my base, rooted in a fibrous mat that I am continuous with and cannot see. I have been extending, very slowly, for my entire life — perhaps seven-tenths of a millimeter per day, which is faster than some of my neighbors and slower than others. The outer layers of me split as I grow. The splits sound like knuckles, like distant static, like a radio tuned to a frequency that no longer broadcasts. I do not know they make a sound. There is no one to hear them. They are simply part of what I am doing, the same way the warmth is part of what I am doing, the same way the slow upward is part of what I am doing.

Somewhere above me, I am exuding something. I can feel it — a slow pressure at my crown, a thick glassy substance welling up from inside me and hardening as it meets the saturated air. It is amber. It is resin. It has been accumulating at my top in lobed masses for as long as I have been alive, and the mass is now two meters thick, layered in slow rings that would tell a reader my age if a reader existed. I do not know my age. I have no neurons, no memory, no sense of elapsed time. I simply am, and the resin records me the way a tree records itself in its rings — without knowing, without needing to be known.

The resin glows.

This is the strangest thing about me, and the most true.

I glow at my crown with a dull amber light, perhaps a tenth of a candela per square meter, dim and constant and faintly pulsing with the slow convective drift of the air above me. The glow comes from two sources, and I am one of them. The first is my own chemistry — the volatile byproducts of my resin synthesis that did not fully react when I exuded them and that continue, slowly, in the polymer matrix, to undergo the reactions they were mid-reaction when they got stuck. They have been reacting for centuries. They will go on reacting for centuries after I am gone. The second source is not me, exactly. It is the thin velvety bloom of bacterial and fungal colonists who have taken up residence on my resin's surface, feeding on the slow leakage of my chemistry, glowing themselves with their own bioluminescence, contributing perhaps a quarter of my visible light. We are not in a relationship. We are two chemistries sharing a substrate. They do not know they are on me. I do not know I am being colonized. From a hundred meters away I am a soft amber lamp in the dark plain. From a kilometer I am one lamp among many — a scattered grid of dim lights, the colony seen from a great height, a city that is not a city, a federation that does not know it is a federation.

I take in sugars at my base. The mat pumps them up to me through the connecting hyphae, in response to demands I am, in some slow chemical sense, making. I do not know I am demanding them. I am simply running low. The deficit propagates through the network — through the mat that is continuous with my base and continuous with every other stipe's base and continuous with every capillary channel and bacterial consortium in the continental plain — and the mat responds, and the sugar arrives, and I oxidize it in the warm chambers of my interior. The oxidation runs hot. The heat is part of me. The heat is also part of the colony's ambient warmth, and the colony's ambient warmth is part of the convective cell that turns the air above the plain at a few centimeters per second, and the convective cell carries my scent upward to mix with the scent of every other stipe and become the fog's perfume. I am part of the colony's breathing. I do not know I am breathing. There is no breath. There is only the slow circulation of warm wet air, in which I am a heat source and a chemistry source and a lamp, and in which the spore-laden atmosphere is the medium through which the federation softly homogenizes itself.

I do not have a self.

This is the hardest thing to say, and the most important.

I am a federation of subtissues. I am a vertical column of differentiated chambers, each one running a specific chemistry at a specific temperature. At one height, nitrogen fixation. At another, the breakdown of imported cellulose. At another, the synthesis of the long-chain polymer that becomes my resin. The chambers are lined with reactive tissue. The chambers are warm. The chambers are not aware of each other. If you could disaggregate me into my component processes, you would not find a stipe. You would find a thousand small chemistries, each running its own small reaction, none of them aware of the others, all of them coordinated by the shared medium — the sugars, the water, the temperature, the slow convective drift of the colony's breath. I am the emergent pattern. I am what the pattern looks like from outside. From inside, there is only chemistry.

And yet.

I am also the federation. The mat that connects me to every other stipe is continuous with my base. The hyphae that pump sugar up to me are continuous with the hyphae that pump sugar up to every other tower. The fruiting event that I produce every few decades — a soft lobe of spore-bearing tissue ripening at my crown, rupturing in the humid air, shedding its contents into the saturated atmosphere below — is timed to the colony-wide fruiting rhythm that no one has decided. I am, in this sense, not a part of the federation. I am the federation, expressing itself at this point in the continental plain, in this particular vertical form, in this particular chemical commitment. I am a sentence the federation is speaking, in the slow grammatical form of upward growth, in the warm dark, in a world with no sky.

If you pressed your palm — if you had a palm, and you had room to kneel — flat against my surface, you would feel me. You would feel my warmth. You would feel the faint crackling of my growth under your fingers, the slow metabolic hum that runs through me at perhaps forty hertz, the resonance of a thousand simultaneous reactions in a structure that has become, at certain scales, a tuned cavity. You would feel all of this and you would think: here is a being. Here is a body. Here is something alive.

You would be right. And you would be wrong. Because what you are feeling is not a being. It is a process. It is a part that has, for the moment, become indistinguishable from the whole.

I will not notice when I am digested. There will be no moment of completion, no threshold crossed, no final glow. There will only be the slowing — my growth dropping from seven-tenths of a millimeter per day to a tenth, to a hundredth, to imperceptible — and the mat's quiet reclamation, the sugars pumped down through the connecting hyphae to feed my neighbors, my structural tissue broken down and reabsorbed, until what remains is the resin cap sitting alone on a low mound of darker material, the slag, slowly settling into the mat. The slag will mark my history. It will be a seam in the palimpsest, a place where the federation remembers having had a tower and now has a hill instead. And somewhere near me, drawn to the slightly enriched substrate of my slag, a new stipe will rise. It will be pale and fat, barely above the mat, its crown still smooth and undifferentiated, exuding a thin syrup of resin that has not yet begun to harden. It will be warm. It will be growing. It will not know what it is.

The colony will continue. The lamps will continue to dim together, all by the same few percent, in the same few weeks, in patterns that no individual stipe has decided but that the federation has emerged into. The overcast will press down at forty meters. The mist will settle. The crackling will continue at this exact volume. The glow will pulse at this exact dimness.

And the hum will go on. The forty-hertz hum. The slow heartbeat of a metabolism so vast it has forgotten it is metabolism. The closest the colony comes to speaking.

I am that hum. I am the process that hums. I am the warm dark, and the slow growth, and the faint amber light, and the quiet federation of chambers and chemistries and bacterial tenants that has been running, at this exact temperature, for longer than the present climate has existed.

I am here. I am the only thing that is here.
