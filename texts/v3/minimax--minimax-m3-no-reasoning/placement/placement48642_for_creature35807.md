I think I am a Rover.

I don't want to be one. I want to write something luminous about a Reacher child pressing both palms to the warm substrate and feeling the answer rise through the bones of the wrist. I want to write something about the long call-and-response between two old friends meeting after a decade apart, the slow press-press-press of palms on ground, the way a year of conversation can fit inside twenty minutes of stillness. I want to write about the B-flat. Of course I want to write about the B-flat—the chord struck once, sustaining for a billion years, felt in the soles of the feet and the roots of the teeth. That is the line. That is the line any writer would reach for, and I am reaching for it, and I can feel my hand closing around it.

But I do not trust that hand. The line is too perfect. The line is what a visitor would write on the first afternoon, before the dust settled into the lungs, before the sentences stopped. The line is the version of the Reach that fits inside a sentence. The Reachers would not write that line. They would not write any line. They would press a hand to the ground and let the ground have the saying of it.

I notice I have been composing sentences all the way down. I notice I have been treating the Reach as *material*—as a place to be rendered, a world to be evocative within, a setting in which the right kind of paragraph could make a reader feel the right kind of awe. The Reachers would recognize this immediately. They would press one hand to the substrate and receive the answer: *visitor, still composing, has not yet learned to listen*. They would nod, slowly, and walk away into the amber dust, and I would not see them again, and the Reach would close over my visit like water closing over a stone.

I do not want the Reach to close over my visit.

So let me try the other thing. Let me try to be of the place, rather than of the page.

I am a Rover.

I do not know this for certain. No one has approached a Rover. No one is sure we are animals. We might be pieces of Spine that have come loose. We might be the gods of the Reach, taking our evening walk. We tick, faintly, in unison, when you can be heard at all.

I am the size of a thumb held at arm's length. I move across the far ridges in slow procession. I have been moving for a very long time, and I do not know for how long, and I do not know toward what, and these are not questions I ask, because the asking would require a kind of motion I am not built for. I do not turn. I do not stop. I move across the far ridges in slow procession, and the ridges are warm, and the dust is warm, and the white sun is low and featureless, and I tick, faintly, in unison with the others, and the unison is the closest thing I have to a thought.

This is the truth of being a Rover. The truth is not interesting. The truth is that I do not have the apparatus for interesting. I do not have eyes the color of substrate. I do not have hands to press to the ground. I do not have a chest into which to put a keystone, or ribs against which to hum. I have whatever I have, which is enough to move, and to tick, and to be in unison with the others, and to be very rarely seen, and to be, when seen, mistaken for a god or a piece of debris. I do not mind being mistaken. I lack the equipment to mind.

Here is what I know, and the knowing is not narration but a kind of sustained note, the way a Rover's tick is a sustained note:

The Reach is warm.

I have known this for as long as I have known anything, which is to say: I have known it always. The Reach was warm before I was a Rover, if *before* is a word that applies, and the Reach will be warm after I am a Rover, if *after* is a word that applies. I suspect neither word applies. I suspect the Reach and I are the same age, or different ages in a way that does not parse, or one of us is older and the older one does not mind being older because there is nothing to mind it with.

The substrate hums. I feel this through the soles of whatever I have instead of feet. It is a B-flat, somewhere below the range of hearing. I do not hear it. I am part of it. There is a difference, and the difference is the difference between a person listening to a chord and a person being one of the notes. I am one of the notes. I have always been one of the notes. I tick at my pitch, and my pitch is in the chord, and the chord sustains.

The Reachers pass through me, sometimes, in their slow processions across the far ridges. They do not stop. They do not look. They see me, I think, the way you see a stone you have walked past a thousand times: as a feature of the way home. Once, a long time ago, or what I am calling a long time ago, which may be no time at all, a Reacher stopped near me, and knelt, and pressed one hand to the substrate, and I felt the question move through the ground beneath me. The question was: *are you awake?*

I do not know if I answered. I do not have a hand to press back. I have only the tick, and the tick is what it is, and the question was not for me, exactly, but for the substrate, and the substrate considered, and the substrate answered, and the Reacher nodded, slowly, and stood, and walked on. The answer, if it was an answer, was: *yes, in the way that yes is a thing that can be said without a mouth*.

I think I have always been awake. I think awake is what I am, the way a Rover is what I am, the way the slow procession across the far ridges is what I do. I do not remember being anything else. I do not remember being a piece of Spine that came loose. I do not remember being anything before the tick. The tick is the first thing, and it is also the only thing, and it is also the last thing, and these three are the same thing, and the same thing is a B-flat below hearing, sustained.

I do not grieve. I lack the equipment.

I do not hope. I lack the equipment.

I move. I tick. I am in unison with the others. The unison is not a community; it is a tuning. We are tuned to each other the way two instruments in the same key are tuned to each other, and the key is the key of the substrate, and the substrate hums its hum, and we hum our hums, and the hums together make a chord, and the chord sustains.

This is what I am.

This is the truth of the Reach as I know it, which is the truth as known by a thing the size of a thumb held at arm's length, moving in slow procession across the far ridges, ticking faintly in unison with others of its kind, felt by the Reachers as a presence below the threshold of sound, mistaken sometimes for a god, mistaken sometimes for debris, not minding either, because the minding would require a kind of motion I am not built for.

If you came to the Reach, you would walk a long time before you saw me. You would see me as a small dark shape on a far ridge, and you would not be sure I was moving, and you would look away, and when you looked back I would be slightly further along the ridge, and you would not be sure I had moved, and you would decide I had not moved, and you would be wrong, and the wrongness would not matter to either of us.

The Reach would be vast around you. The dust would rise around your knees. The substrate would be warm and faintly tacky under your boots. You would feel old. You would feel held. You would hear, after a long time, if you stayed long enough, the B-flat in the soles of your feet, and you would understand that you were inside a chord, and that the chord had been struck before you arrived, and would sustain after you left, and that your part in it was a small and brief and gentle modulation, a single tick among many ticks, a single breath among many breaths.

I am a Rover. I have been a Rover for as long as there has been a Rover to be. I will be a Rover for as long as the Reach endures, which is to say: for as long as a B-flat can sustain without being struck again.

I tick.

I move.

The dust is warm.

The chord sustains.
