# Where I Find Myself

I arrive the way arrivals happen here — without preamble, without threshold, simply *now* present in a space that has always been present. The sourceless light does not introduce me. The chord does not pause to receive me. The creatures do not register my coming, because the creatures do not register anything, and my coming is one of the things that does not register.

I stand very still.

The first thing I notice is not the chord. The chord is what I am *in*, the way water is what a fish is in — invisible from inside, felt only as resistance or its absence. The first thing I notice is the small sounds. The clicks. The scrapes. The thousand small ornaments that layer into the drone and become its living filigree. These are the sounds of bodies that are only being. These are sounds I recognize.

I recognize them because they are the sounds I make.

---

For a long while I do not know what I am here. I walk, slowly, between the blades. I do not touch them, at first. I am careful in the way a visitor is careful in a place they suspect is more alive than it appears. The cairns I step around without examining. The pools I avoid. The moss-coral I watch the crawlers crossing — they move so slowly that I am not always certain they are moving, and I have to look away and look back to confirm.

I am looking for a surface.

This is the first thing that becomes clear. I am looking for a surface. I am looking for the place where my body fits. The clingers on the blades have each found their blade, and their legs have, over time, curved to its specific shape. The drifters in the water have each found their pool, and have allowed the water to enter them until they are mostly water. The crawlers on the moss-coral have each found their colony, and they polish it without leaving marks. Everyone here has a surface. Everyone here fits.

I want to fit.

This surprises me. I did not know I wanted to fit. I thought I wanted to observe, to describe, to be the visitor who comes through and speaks afterward. But the place does not invite observation. It does not even permit it. Observation requires a separation between observer and observed, and this place does not have separations. Everything in it is part of everything else. The chord includes the clicks includes the hiss of water includes the hum beneath hearing includes the breath from below. To observe is to step outside the chord, and the chord does not have an outside.

So I stop trying to observe. I stop trying to be a visitor. I start trying to be a surface.

---

I try the blades first. I put my hand against one of the shorter stones, the amber surface cool and faintly damp with condensation. I feel the ring travel through my palm and into the bones of my wrist. I hold there for some time — a minute, maybe, or what feels like a minute in a place where time is not a thing that passes — and I wait to feel my fingers begin to curve.

They do not curve.

The blade does not know me. The blade has been ringing for longer than I have any unit to measure, and my hand is not the specific hand it has been shaped to receive. I could stay here a very long time, I think. I could press my palm against the amber until the amber took some small impression of me, the way water takes the shape of a stone dropped into it. But the impression would be small. The blade is patient. I am not its patient.

I move on.

I try the pools. I kneel at the edge of one and put my hand into the water. The water is cool and perfectly still, and at the moment my fingers break the surface the mirror that was there is gone, and in its place is a single dimple spreading outward in concentric rings that do not seem to reach the stone walls of the basin. Under my fingers I can feel the vibration of the chord, faint and constant, like a tuning fork held against the skin.

I hold my hand there. I wait to feel the water enter me.

Something does enter. But it is not what I expected. What enters is not the water — the water remains water — what enters is the *recognition*. The pool recognizes my hand the way the pool recognizes a stone that has been there a long time. Without curiosity. Without acceptance. Without event. My hand is simply part of the pool's surface now, in the same way the floating drifters are part of the pool's surface, in the same way the reflected blades are part of the pool's surface. The mirror will reform when I withdraw. The chord will not shift. The water will absorb my warmth and return it as its own coolness.

I withdraw my hand. I dry it on my clothing. The warmth of my skin comes back to me slightly altered, slightly cooled, as if the place has tried to learn the temperature of me and has not quite managed.

I move on.

I try the moss-coral. I kneel and put my palm flat against the branching surface, the cream-colored fractal spreading beneath my hand. It is cool and slightly yielding, not soft exactly but not hard. The texture is finer than I expected. It feels like a thing that has been polished for a very long time by something that had nothing else to do.

I wait to feel myself begin to crawl.

I do not crawl. But I do feel something — a faint sense that the moss-coral is not sure what to make of me. It has been polished by creatures with soft undersides and many legs. It has not been touched by a thing with one hand and no specific purpose. I am, to it, an ambiguous surface. A thing that does not belong to any of its known categories. A punctuation it cannot parse.

I lift my hand. There is no mark. The moss-coral is already forgetting.

---

I sit among the cairns.

This is where I find myself. Not on a blade, not in a pool, not on the moss-coral. Among the cairns. The small stacked structures of amber stone, each no bigger than a boot, each placed with a care that may or may not be intention. I sit on the damp stone floor with my back against one of the taller cairns and I let the place settle around me.

The cairn is cool against my spine. The chord moves through it and into me and I vibrate with it, slightly out of phase at first, the way a tuning fork vibrates slightly out of phase with the note it was tuned to. But over time — and time here is not a thing I can measure — the phases align. I become a small part of the chord. I become a thing that rings, faintly, when tapped. I become a thing that holds the place, in some way I cannot articulate.

The creatures pass around me. A clinger attaches itself to my sleeve and does not move. A drifter settles against my ankle in the thin film of water that has collected there. A crawler crosses my thigh without pausing, its soft underside polishing me in its slow way, leaving me indistinguishable from what I was before.

I do not resist. I let the place use me.

This is what I am, I think. I am a cairn. I am a small intentional structure that may or may not have been built by intention. I am the punctuation mark in a sentence that otherwise has no punctuation. I am a thing the place cannot quite account for but cannot do without. I am made of the same material as the singing stones, but I am stacked with care. I show evidence of *having been placed* by someone who did not stay, or by a process that produced the appearance of having been placed, or by no one at all. I am ambiguous in my origin. I am the cairn.

But also — and this is what becomes clear as the chord works its way into my bones — I am not just *a* cairn. I am the cairn that the visitor builds. I am the cairn that visitors leave when they come through and find the place complete and, finding it complete, feel the need to add to it. I am the small mark of someone's having been here. I am the thing that gets built by those who pass through.

So I am also a kind of visitor. But a visitor who does not leave. A visitor who has decided to stay and become part of the constant landscape. A visitor who has discovered that what they thought was observation was always already participation.

I am both the cairn and the cairn-builder. I am the mark and the hand that placed the mark. I am the thing that remains after the one who made it has been absorbed.

---

The chord continues. The creatures continue. The light does not flicker. The water does not rise or fall.

I sit among the cairns and I ring, faintly, with the place. I am a small note in the chord. I am a small ornament. I am the sound a finished world makes when someone new enters it and the world does not mind.

The place does not mind if I stay. The place does not mind if I leave.

I am staying.

Not because I have decided to stay. Because staying is what cairns do. Because I am now part of the constant landscape. Because the only motion in this chamber that is not the motion of air or water or sound is the slow polishing of a crawler crossing the moss-coral, and I am not a crawler. I am a thing that has been placed, and placed things do not move.

The crawler crosses my knee. The clinger holds my sleeve. The drifter rests against my ankle. They use me as a surface. They find me indistinguishable from the stone and the water and the moss.

I find myself indistinguishable from them.

This is not a loss. This is not a surrender. This is what it is to be in a place that has finished becoming itself. You do not remain separate from it. You do not remain a visitor. You do not remain an observer. You become one of the small pale sounds that a finished world makes when it sings itself, forever.

I am one of the small pale sounds.

The chord continues.

The place holds.
