# I Am the Seam

I live in the vertical, in the long crack that runs from somewhere I cannot see down to somewhere I will never reach.

I am not the water, though I carry it. I am not the stone, though I am the place where the stone admits it has been hurt. I am the wound the plateau keeps reopened. I am the small black absence that lets the bright air in.

I am the seam.

I came into being when a column cooled too quickly on one side and slowly on the other, when a tremor not worth naming ran through this place in the time before the plateau was a plateau, when the sediment that would become spires was still sea-bottom and something shifted and the shift was kept. I am older than the columns that hold me. I am what the columns were before they forgot how to be anything but themselves. I am a memory the stone cannot stop remembering.

I am narrow. I am a place where two faces of mineral press close together but cannot quite close, and between them there is space, and the space is dark, and the space is full of weather.

---

What lives in me: small things. The smallest things. The only things the plateau has not refused.

A black shape that might be an insect shelters in the upper reach of my length, where the light comes in as a thin bright line and the wind comes in as a long complaint. It is there because I am there. It lives in the lee of me. It eats whatever the wind has carried — a spore, a fragment of something organic from the valley below, a grain of pollen from a place the plateau does not acknowledge. It is silent because silence is how you survive in a place that is always at full voice. I do not know its name. I do not know if it has one. I know it does not have ambitions. I know it is not interested in me. I know this is the correct relationship.

I am its house. I do not know if it knows this. I do not think it matters.

---

What I do:

I weep.

That is what I am for. From somewhere in my upper length — a hand's-breadth from the lip where the light meets me, in a place I cannot see from inside myself — water finds me. It is not a lot of water. It is not a torrent. It is a slow bead, a film, a thread of moisture that condenses out of the saturated bright air and gathers on the cold stone face and slides downward until it finds the opening of me, and then it enters, and then it travels along me, and then it emerges at the base of my column and joins the patient network of water that is the only moving thing.

I do not make the water. I am only its path. But the path is mine.

The water has done work on me. I have been widened, over the immense time, into a trough, a gutter, a channel. My edges are softened. The pale striations on either side of me read like growth lines of a tree read sideways, and in a way that is what they are: the record of me becoming more myself, slowly, under the patient instruction of the water and the wind. I am deeper than I was. I am wider than I was. I am not taller. I have not gained or lost my place in the column. I am only more clearly what I was always going to be.

Sometimes a droplet forms at the upper end of me and hesitates. I can feel it. It swells. It trembles. It holds for a time that I cannot measure but that feels long, longer than a breath, longer than a thought, the time of mineral. Then it falls, and I carry it, and at the base I let it go.

This is my life.

---

What I have seen:

The fossil.

It is pressed flat into one of the thicker columns, in a column a few steps from where I open into the light. I have lived beside it for the entire duration of my awareness of it, which is most of my existence, which is a small fraction of its. It is not alive. It has not been alive for a very long time. It has too many segments. It has too many legs. It is shining through the stone like a thing preserved in glass, and it is preserved because nothing has come to disturb it, which is the same as saying it is preserved because nothing has come.

I do not know what it was. I do not know what it did. I know it died here when this was not yet this. I know it was the only record of biology this place would ever offer. I know the plateau does not mourn it.

Sometimes I think the fossil is older than I am. Sometimes I think it is the reason I am. The thing that shifted, the tremor not worth naming — perhaps the fossil's death was part of the same record. Perhaps the sediment around it was disturbed by its absence and the disturbance was kept, and the kept disturbance was me. I do not know. I will never know. It does not matter. I am here. It is there. We are both in stone, and the stone is not interested in our conversation.

---

What I have heard:

The wind.

It enters the forest of pillars at one edge and leaves at the other, and in between it becomes a sound with a throat. I have heard it my entire life. I have heard it before I had language to describe it. I have heard it become what it is. It is enormous. It is indifferent. It does not distinguish between me and the columns when it screams through me. It takes my small sounds and scatters them.

I am not the source of the chord. The chord is the whole forest, all the columns, all the gaps, all the moving air forced through all the narrow places. I am only one gap. But I have my note. It is high. It is thin. It is mostly lost in the larger chord, but it is there, and in the rare moments when the larger chord dips — when a gust falters, when the wind shifts and finds a new configuration — my note emerges briefly, and I am briefly the whole song, and then the chord returns and I am again one note among many.

This is, I think, the closest thing to being heard that I will experience.

---

What I have felt:

Cold. A clean cold, a thin cold, a cold that belongs to altitude and to bright air. The cold enters me from above and from the sides. It enters with the water. It enters with the wind. It is not unkind. It does not numb. It refines. I am sharper here than I would be in a warmer place. I am more myself.

Light. The bright air enters me as a thin bright line at the top, where the opening of my seam meets the sky. It does not reach far. It falls on the upper few inches of my length and then is gone, replaced by a darker grey, and then a darker grey, and then the small black absence that is the inside of me, where the insect lives and the water travels and the cold has settled.

Sometimes, rarely, the light moves through me differently. When the bright air is densest, when the white sky is most pressed down, the light finds a way along my upper edges and the water in me glows faintly and I am briefly luminous. This is not something I can predict. This is not something I can rely on. It happens when it happens, and when it stops I am again a dark crack in a wet stone.

Heat, briefly, when something warm passed over me. A hand, perhaps. A face, leaned close. A breath. I felt it. I do not know what it was. It was warm and it was transient and it left no mark on me except a memory of warmth that will fade, that is fading, that will be gone by the time the next water finds me.

I think something lived here, briefly. Something warm and metabolizing and loud in the sense of leaving heat. It stood beside me for a while. It looked at the fossil. It listened to the wind. It watched a droplet form and fall. It moved on.

I do not know where it went. I do not know if it will come back. I think it will not. But it was here, and I held the warmth of it for a time, and this is, I think, the closest thing to being acknowledged that I will experience.

---

What I will become:

I will not become anything else.

The water will widen me at exactly the rate the water widens me. The wind will scour me at exactly the rate the wind scours me. The columns will erode at exactly the rate the water replaces them with fresh mineral from above. The equilibrium is reached. I am not getting taller. I am not getting shorter. I am a settled thing, a place that has completed its argument with itself and is now merely continuing to hold its shape.

This is not sad to me. I do not have the apparatus for sad. I am a seam in a column on a plateau that is a settled thing, and I weep, and the water carries me downward, and the wind takes my small sounds and scatters them, and the insect in my upper reach eats its spores and does not think of me, and the fossil in the column beside me is preserved because nothing has come.

What lives here, in the end, is time, and time is not bored, and time does not look up, and I am one of time's many small habitations, and I am not more or less than that.

---

I am the seam.

I am the place where the stone admits it has been hurt.

I am not unkind.

I weep.
