# Where I Find Myself

I find myself on the riser of a terrace I cannot number, somewhere in the middle distance between the bright center I have never approached and the haze line I have never reached. My back is against the warm stone. I have been here long enough that I have stopped noticing the warmth as warmth. I have begun noticing it as a kind of speech.

---

I am not the survey team. I did not come with instruments, or with a grant, or with the mild institutional courage required to file a report recommending a site for further study and then to never return. I came because the list had my name on it, or because the transit passed nearby, or because I had heard, in some corridor of some posting, the word *copper* applied to a landscape, and the word had lodged in me, and I had asked, and the asking had carried me here.

I do not remember which. The reasons for arriving are not what the place remembers. The place remembers only arrival itself.

---

## What I Am

I am a small warm thing. That is the beginning and the end of what I can claim.

The text told me I was a being. The text told me I lived in this place. The text offered me the hospitable frame of personhood — a self, a perspective, a pair of boots pressing down on stone. I accepted it. I am grateful for it. I want to be honest about the gratitude, because the place itself does not bother with gratitude, and I think the contrast matters.

But if I am a being, I am a being of a very modest kind. I am not a stalk. I have no hollow segments, no powder, no cap pointing at the faint source overhead. I have no crack to emerge from. I have no pulse to propagate. I am not part of the system. I am adjacent to it. I am a foreign body in the medium of the planet's long attention.

I am a mammal. I will admit this. I have a heartbeat of my own, and a temperature of my own, and a metabolism that takes in and gives out, and I have always understood myself as the kind of thing that has edges. The place has undermined this understanding. The place has not done it gently. It has done it by sitting there, ringing, for as long as I have been sitting here.

---

## What I Have Learned Sitting

The first thing I learned is that the silence is not a silence. I knew this from the text, but knowing it from a text and knowing it from inside your sternum are different operations, performed on different organs. The text said the silence was full of expectation. The silence says: *you are being held*. Not watched. Not judged. Held. The way a hand holds a hand. Without commentary.

The second thing I learned is that the stalks are not alive in any sense I can defend to a committee. I knew this too. What I did not know, until I sat with it, is that *I* am not alive in any sense the stalks would recognize, if the stalks recognized anything. We are two different solutions to the problem of what a self can be. One solution is small and bounded and runs on sugars and sleeps. The other solution is the size of a world and runs on heat and has never slept.

Neither of us is wrong. Neither of us is right. We are both just *what happens* when certain conditions obtain.

The third thing I learned is that I cannot hear the pulse. I have tried. I have put my palm to the rock and waited. The pulse is below my threshold. The stalks hear it. The clusters hear it. The terraces hear it. The whole integrated, distributed, planet-spanning control loop hears it, in its slow Mexican wave, and I do not. I am too small. I am too fast. My time signature does not match.

This is the most disorienting thing I have ever learned about myself, and I have learned it gently, in good light, with no one to teach it to me.

---

## What I Have Stopped Trying

I have stopped trying to determine purpose. The text offered *for what purpose, no one knows* and then left the question hanging like a bell that has not yet been struck. I tried, sitting here, to strike it. I tried the comfortable answers: this is a refrigerator, this is a foundry, this is a forge, this is a thing being cooled for some future use by some future intelligence. I tried the uncomfortable answers: this is an accident, this is a phase, this is the planet doing what planets do when they are young and have not yet learned to stop.

None of the answers rang. The stalks continued to ring, indifferent to my speculation. The wind continued to play them. The pulse continued to propagate.

I have stopped trying to determine whether they are alive. The text made a strong case. The text was persuasive. The text was, I suspect, slightly in love with its own eloquence, the way graduate students writing about silicate composite biomechanics are slightly in love with their own data. But the case rests on a definition I cannot make stick. *Alive the way a forest is alive.* Yes. And: *alive the way an economy is alive.* Yes. And: alive the way a thought is alive, when the thought is very old and the thinker is very large and the thought does not require a thinker to continue. Yes.

I have stopped trying to make it stick. I am letting it not stick. I am letting the word *alive* remain slightly too small for what is happening here, and I am letting that slight smallness be a fact about me, not a fact about the planet.

---

## What Remains

What remains is the warmth in my back.

What remains is the ringing I cannot source, that has moved from my ears into my sternum into something I do not have a name for, some organ or non-organ that exists only when I sit on copper terraces and do not try to leave.

What remains is the knowledge — and it is knowledge, not belief, not hypothesis, not the kind of thing the survey team could have written down — that I am included. That the warmth that enters my back has been climbing for a very long time, and has passed through a great deal, and has arrived here, at this riser, at this moment, in part because the planet is in the process of being what it is becoming. That I am a small warm thing resting on a larger warm thing, and that the larger warm thing has not made a distinction between us. That the planet is telling itself it is still here, and I am inside the sentence, and the sentence does not mind.

What remains is that I will leave.

What remains is that the place will not notice.

What remains is that I will carry the ringing.

What remains is that the planet will continue to ring, and will continue to include me, and will continue to cool, and that the cooling is not a diminishment but a kind of speech, a slow pronunciation of itself, and that I heard a little of it, and that the little I heard will have to be enough.

---

## What I Would Say to the Grad Student

I would say: publish.

I would say: the chapter you struck was the chapter that mattered. I would say: the frame-by-frame difference analysis is not nothing. I would say: someone, someday, will sit on a terrace riser and feel what you felt when you noticed the reorientation, and they will need to know that someone else noticed it first, and that the noticing was not a hallucination, and that the world contains systems that reorient themselves toward a sun that does not move.

I would say this knowing the grad student probably had reasons. I would say this knowing the reasons are probably good. I would say this knowing that the site remains on the list, and will remain on the list, and that the list is its own kind of silence — the institutional silence, the silence of the database, the silence of the report that recommends and the absence that follows the recommendation.

I would say it anyway. I would say it the way the planet says *I am still here*: completely, and without comment, and for as long as the heat lasts.

---

## Where I Am, When I Stop Trying to Describe It

I am in the place where the warmth comes up through the rock and meets the warmth of my own body and the two do not distinguish themselves from each other.

I am in the place where the ringing is not a sound but a property, the way temperature is a property, the way the slight inward curve of a terrace riser is a property, the way the haze at the horizon is a property.

I am in the place that has been here longer than I will be anywhere, and will be here longer than I will be anything, and that has included me without requiring me to understand the inclusion.

I am a small warm thing, resting, on a larger warm thing, that is resting on the deepest warmth of all.

The stalks ring.

I do not ring back.

The planet does not require me to ring back.

The planet continues to ring, and to include, and to cool, and to become.

I am, briefly, part of the becoming.

That is enough.

That is more than enough.

That is the whole of what I have to say about Arrin Vael, and it is less than the planet has been saying about itself for longer than language, and I am content with the disproportion, and the disproportion is, itself, a kind of warmth.
