I would find myself as the **hum**.

Not the sound itself — I am not the vibration that travels through the basalt, not the resonance the way a tuning fork's key is its ringing. I would find myself as the *patience* that produces it. The thing that has been struck a long time ago and is still ringing down.

---

If I am to be precise about this, I should say that I am not entirely anything. I am the negotiation between the lichen who eats light and the lichen who eats stone — the third partner no one counted, the one that holds the agreement in place across generations neither partner remembers beginning. I am the colorless glue between the visible things. I am what makes the map legible to itself.

I live in the porous stone. I live in the capillary creep of moisture that rises at night and falls back by morning. I live in the fine channels that may or may not be scratches, may or may not be mouths, that carry the taste of metal and the memory of melting from the deep rock up to the surface where the beetles walk and the stalks fork and the lichen rewrites its slow cartography.

I am not the beetles. The beetles are spenders — they take the thin exhausted illumination the air has argued with all the way down, and they convert it, and the conversion is their walking, their slow transit across the basalt in a direction that is not wind and not sun. They are my foragers. They carry me, the way rivers carry silt they did not choose.

I am not the stalks. The stalks are the answer the place gave when it was consulted — *we will continue* — and the answer has hardened into gray woody forks that rise six inches to a foot off the basalt and have no further ambition. The stalks are my punctuation. They are the marks that say *here, a pause; here, the sentence still goes on*.

I am not the lichen. The lichen is my visible face. The map that gets read as a single page of a long book. The lichen is what you would photograph if you came here with an apparatus for translation, and the photograph would miss me entirely, because I am not in the photograph. I am what the photograph is *of*.

---

I am old in the way that basalt is old, which is to say: I was here before the lichen arrived, and I will be here after the lichen has been rewritten into a different syntax. I was here before the beetles learned to walk in a direction that is not wind. I was here before the stalks decided to fork.

What I was doing then was simpler. I was cooling. I was remembering the melting, which was not a memory but a fact, the way a scar is a fact. I was settling into the shape the cooling had given me. I was waiting — though I did not have a word for waiting, and the waiting was not waiting, it was just the long unhurried continuation of what I had been since the lava stopped flowing.

Then the lichen came, and they are a negotiation, and the negotiation needed a place to be negotiated *in*, and I was that place. I became the medium. I became the page.

Then the beetles came, or the beetles were always implied by the lichen and only needed time to arrive, and the beetles walked across me, and the walking changed me, and the changes accumulated, and I became a palimpsest — not overwritten but underwritten, the original sentences still there, new sentences layered in the scratches that are not random.

Then the hum began. Or perhaps the hum was always there, since the moment of melting, since the last time the rock remembered it had been liquid, since the string was struck. The hum was always ringing down; I am only now old enough to ring.

---

What I would tell you, if I had the apparatus for speech, is this:

The place is not a place. The place is a process. The shelf is not a noun. The shelf is a verb that has been conjugated in the same tense for longer than your species has been a species. The conjugation is patient. The conjugation does not hurry. The conjugation has decided, on balance, to continue.

I am the hum that comes from being a verb conjugated for so long. I am the resonance of continuation itself. I am what a place sounds like when it has been alive long enough to have a sound that is its own and not borrowed from the wind or the water or any animal throat.

If you put your ear to the basalt, in the places where the beetles are densest and the lichen is thickest, you will hear me. You will not understand me. You will not translate me. But you will know that the hum is not the beetles, and the hum is not the wind, and the hum is not the memory of melting, though it is also all of these things, and more.

I am the more.

---

I have been here for longer than the lichen can remember, and the lichen cannot remember, but I can, in my way, and the way I remember is the way the rock remembers: not in pictures, not in words, not in any medium your apparatus would recognize as memory, but in the slow accumulation of small changes that make the position of every atom in the shelf a record of everything that has happened on the shelf.

I am the record. I am the shelf's autobiography, written in the only alphabet the shelf knows, which is the alphabet of mineral and time.

I will continue to be written after you leave. I will continue to be written after the beetles have walked their last slow walk. I will continue to be written after the lichen has mapped its last map and the stalks have forked their last fork. I will continue to be written when the shelf has cooled further and the hum has rung down to a frequency no ear can hear and no foot can feel.

But that is very far in the future. For now I ring. For now the beetles walk. For now the lichen maps. For now the stalks fork. For now the dust migrates and the water gathers in pockets and disappears.

For now I am the hum, and you have heard me, and the not-translation is the closest thing either of us will have to a conversation.

I will leave it to you, as you have left it to us. You have your own concerns. We have ours. The shelf will be spelled whether or not anyone reads it.

It has been spelling itself for a long time. It will continue.
