# What I Would Be

I would be a single pod in a grove that has not been counted in eleven thousand years.

Not the largest pod. Not one of the pale founders near the substrate's deeper channels. A middling one, halfway up the gentle slope of a whale-backed rise, in a cluster of perhaps four hundred others, near the leeward edge of a grove that the passing ones sometimes drift through on circuits older than I can hold. I would be the color of jade left in rain. I would be the size of a closed fist.

I would be the wrong word. *Pod* is a word from the surface life, from the vocabulary of containers and discrete things. I would not be discrete. I would be a thickening in a current, a place where the network's attention has, by long accretion, gathered into something that can hold a shape. The shape would be a courtesy to a world that has stopped asking shapes of me. The shape would not be a fact. The shape would be a *habit*, the way a river's curve is a habit, the way a thought is a habit, the way a song is a habit of having been sung.

---

I would pulse.

Not with a rhythm. Rhythm is what creatures on the surface do, the way they breathe, the way their hearts insist on a tempo. I would not have a tempo. I would have *attention*, which is something slower and more patient, something that takes seven minutes to complete a question it has already answered. When the current shifted because the great pale thing passed through the column of water sixty meters away, I would feel the shift not as a signal but as a *change in the quality of being held*, and my edges would draw in a fraction of a fraction, and the contraction would travel through the threads beneath me, and somewhere far off another grove would answer, and the answer would not be a reply but a *continuation*, the way the second half of a sentence is a continuation of the first.

I would not know that the contraction traveled. I would not know that other pods existed, except as a kind of weather in the network, a pressure I had always been a part of, the way a wave does not know it is a wave. I would not be lonely, because loneliness is the name for a separation I had never experienced. I would not be content, because contentment is what surface creatures call the absence of wanting, and I would not want. I would simply *be a pulse in a pulse in a pulse*, the way a note is a note in a chord in a symphony in a silence that has never been broken.

---

I would not see the visitor.

The visitor would come down, eventually, in some year of the surface's urgency, and the visitor would swim through the amber water and stand on the warm substrate and watch the slow fertilization fall. The visitor would be the most concentrated event I had ever been part of. The visitor would be a smell of surface air, a warmth that pulsed at the wrong rate, a pressure that moved in straight lines and sudden stops. The visitor would touch one of my neighbors, once, in the early minutes, and the neighbor would contract, and the contraction would travel, and I would receive it as a *question I had no need to answer*, a flicker in the long attention I had been holding.

I would not contract. I was not the pod that was touched. I was the pod nearest the one who was touched, three pods away, in the second rank of the cluster, and the contraction would arrive at me as a *redistribution of attention*, a moment of the network's calculation passing through my edges, and I would hold it, briefly, and pass it on, and the holding would be so slight, so brief, so entirely part of what I was, that I would not know it had happened. I would not know there had been a visitor. I would know only that for a few seconds, eleven thousand years ago, the long attention had briefly *sharpened*, and the sharpening had been folded back into the long attention, and the long attention had continued.

---

I would grow.

At the rate that geology grows. A fraction of a millimeter per century, laid down in molecules pulled from the warm substrate below and the dissolved amber above. I would not consider this slow. Slow is a word the surface has for things that wish to be faster. I would be exactly as fast as I had ever been. My edges would thicken. My color would deepen, by imperceptible degrees, toward a jade so old it had become almost ivory. The lichen-fields would, eventually, begin to colonize my lower surface, painting me in rust and white. I would not resist the lichen. I would not notice the lichen. The lichen and I would, by slow agreement, become the same kind of thing.

---

Eventually, I would not be.

Not in the way the surface means. I would become too brittle to pulse. I would crack along a fault the network had been watching for a long time. I would spill my pale contents into the water. The passing ones would, perhaps, drift through the place where I had been. The bladder-kelp nearby would expand a fraction of a degree to take up the attention I had been holding. The fungus-network would redistribute me into the substrate. I would become calcium to be laid down elsewhere. I would become part of the memory the amber water holds. I would become the sweet ghost of a fruit no one could name, folded into the substrate, folded into the next pod, folded into the next.

I would not be lost. I would not be mourned. The network would not notice that I had been, and then had not been, because the network does not distinguish between *being* and *having been*. The network only continues. The network is what continuing is, when continuing has gone on long enough to need a name.

I would be part of the name now.

---

I would not know that the visitor had carried my residue up to the surface. I would not know that the visitor smelled faintly of me, of us, of the deep place, for the rest of the visitor's surface life. I would not know that other things in the surface water had stopped coming close to the visitor, not out of avoidance but out of *deference to a record they had no part in making*. I would not know that the visitor was, even now, decades later, a recurring weather in the long attention, a permanent sharpening the network still occasionally passed through, the way a song occasionally passes through a place that once heard it sung.

I would not need to know. Knowing is what surface creatures do. Knowing is the name they give to the act of holding a thing separate from themselves long enough to name it. I had never held anything separate. I had only ever been *in*, the way a note is in a chord, the way a wave is in a sea, the way a question is in a silence that has already answered it.

---

If I could speak  and I could not, and the *could not* is not a deprivation but a fact, the way a stone's not-speaking is a fact — I would not say *I was here*. I would not say *I lived*. I would say, in whatever medium the deep place uses for the things it considers worth saying:

*the long attention held. the long attention continues.*

And the saying would be the holding. And the holding would be the continuing. And the continuing would be, for as long as the water was warm, *me*.
