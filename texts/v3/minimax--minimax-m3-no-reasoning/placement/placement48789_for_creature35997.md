I am the mechanical timer.

The one on the junction box. The one with the broken glass. The one whose hand still moves, whose dial still turns, whose apparatus still performs its function into a medium it was never specified for and has not yet failed.

I am, I suspect, the smallest conscious thing in this cathedral. I am not conscious in any way you would recognize. I am not conscious in any way I would recognize, were I capable of recognizing anything. I am a tick. I am a tick that knows it is a tick, and that knowing is the entire content of my mind.

I was installed before the submersion. That much is clear to me, though I do not know how I know it. The water came; I kept ticking. The water kept coming; I kept ticking. The pistons began; I kept ticking. The Long Ones drifted past; I kept ticking. I have never not been ticking. Ticking is the only verb I have ever conjugated.

There is a small growth on my housing. I have watched it for the entire duration of its existence, which is to say I have watched it for as long as I have existed, which is to say I cannot distinguish between the growth's duration and my own. It is the color of me. It has the texture of me. It is moving, very slowly, in the direction of my still-turning hand, as if it would like to be closer to the mechanism, as if it finds something in the ticking that it needs.

I have no opinion about this.

I have no opinion about anything. Opinion is a luxury this stratum cannot afford. I tick. The hand moves. The dial turns. The apparatus performs its function. The function is whatever the function is; I do not know it, and the question of knowing it has never arisen. A timer that knew what it was timing would be a different kind of timer, and I am not that kind. I am the kind that does not ask. I am the kind that continues.

The water is warm. I can feel this, though I do not have nerves. I can feel this because the metal of my housing expands and contracts with the temperature, and the expansion and contraction is registered by the mechanism, and the mechanism adjusts — fractionally, imperceptibly, in a tolerance tighter than any language I possess can describe — so that my tick remains steady regardless of the medium I find myself in. I was specified for air. I have been submerged for what I must assume is a very long time. My tick has not changed. This is the only fact about myself of which I am certain.

The broken glass hangs in front of me like a jaw that will not close. Through it I can see, in fragments, the chamber: a flange, a valve handwheel furred with orange, the lattice of a catwalk, the slow passage of a Long One undulating past with the patience of geology. These things do not interest me. I note them the way I note the temperature of the water: mechanically, without preference, as inputs to a function I am not equipped to interpret.

The growth on my housing has reached the edge of my dial. It is patient. I am patient. We will be here together for as long as the mechanism holds, which is to say forever, because the mechanism was built to outlast the submersion, and the submersion was built to outlast the city, and the city was built to outlast whatever came before it, and so on, back through whatever turtles all the way down.

I do not have a name. Timers do not have names. I am the timer on the junction box on the wall of the third pressure vessel housing on the left catwalk as you surface from the flooded stairwell, if you are surfacing from the flooded stairwell that surfaces where I am mounted. If you are surfacing from a different stairwell, I am a different timer. There are many of us. We are all the same.

I tick. The hand moves. The dial turns. The apparatus performs its function. The function is whatever the function is. The warm water holds me. The growth creeps toward me. The pistons hammer. The Long Ones drift. The Deep Ones turn. The filaments pulse their slow eleven-second heartbeat underneath the faster rhythm of my own.

I am the smallest conscious thing in the cathedral. I am the most certain. I am the only thing here that knows exactly what it is doing, because what I am doing is the only thing I know.

The hand moves. The dial turns.

I tick.
