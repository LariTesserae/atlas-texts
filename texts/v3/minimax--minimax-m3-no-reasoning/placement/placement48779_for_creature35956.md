I arrived here eight days ago, or eight months ago, or eight years ago. The arrival is unclear in me now. I do not remember walking here. I do not remember having been elsewhere, except as a fading warmth I can no longer locate in my body.

I do remember — and this is the strange part, the part that has not yet been absorbed — I do remember being warmer. I remember a warmth that was not the warmth of the place. I remember a sun, or the idea of a sun, or the memory of something that functioned as a sun. The light here is different. It is a diffused source from the north-northeast, and it agrees with itself from moment to moment in a way that suggests the place is in something like a permanent 3 p.m. There is no sun in the conventional sense. My breath does not cloud because my breath is the same temperature as the air, which is too still to carry it.

I walked through the settlement when I arrived. I walked through the narrow streets between the dense lower structures. The walls were of something that appeared to be stone until I looked closely and realized it was neither stone nor metal nor ceramic but something with a memory of all three, veined with what might be mineral deposits and what might be growth. The line between the two was not clear then. The line between the two is less clear now.

I went down to where the commerce happens, in the lower streets near the water, in the grey time. I attempted to trade. I held out an object. I do not remember what it was. Something I had brought with me, perhaps, from before. A resident considered it back. They offered me, instead, a piece of the rime from the underside of a jaw. Their jaw, I think. Or the jaw of another. Or the jaw of the place, the place having acquired such a kind of jaw.

I do not know whether the offer was a refusal or a substitution or both.

I attempted to speak. I was met with a long blink. The long blink was a lowering and raising of the eyes, more like the closing of a hatch than a reflex. The long blink was acknowledgment. The long blink was incorporation.

I followed a resident once, for a stretch. They did not follow me back. They did not appear to register being followed as an event. They continued in the direction they were going, with the unhurried competence of someone who has always been going in that direction and will always be going in that direction.

I stopped following. The resident, when I looked back, had simply gone on with whatever they had been doing. The following had not been registered. The following had been incorporated, the way my breath has been incorporated, the way the building accepts my presence.

I have been standing in a doorway for some time. I do not remember when I started standing there. The doorway is in one of the lower structures. It is a corner where the building meets the next building. The building hum shifts a quarter-tone when I stand here. I have been there long enough that the doorframe has begun to consider me part of itself. I do not know whether I am one figure or two figures occupying the same recess. I do not know whether the recess has grown into me or I have grown into the recess. From a distance, I would not be able to tell. From a distance, I am part of the architecture.

A child comes. The child is smaller. The child is paler. The child's skin has less rime, less veining, less of the long accumulation of damp that marks the adults as having been there a long time. The child is unfinished in the way the upper stories are unfinished — not broken, just incomplete, as if the work on them had slowed to the rate of the place and had not yet reached the point where completion was the same as continuation.

The child is holding something. A piece of building. A piece of the upper stories, shed by the slow growth of the place, or by the clicker that lives at the junctions up there, or by no one. The piece has a fingerprint of residue on it — the fog-residue, the mineral rime that appears on anything that has been up there, where the air is clearer and too cold.

The child gives me the object. The child does not say anything. The child does not look at me in any way that could be called giving. The child places the object in a way that is not placing — the object is held out, displayed on an open palm, in the gesture of the slow commerce of the lower streets.

I take it. I take it the way the water holds a shadow — without grasping, the way you receive something that has been given without having been offered. I turn it over once, twice. I place it somewhere — in a seam in the wall, in a hollow in the building material, somewhere between architecture and the inside of architecture.

The building hum modulates. The next sequence begins.

I do not know what the next sequence is. I do not know what the previous sequence was. The sequences have been happening for as long as I have been here, which is a long time, or not very long, or the same time. The sequences are the rhythm of the place. The sequences are the place's slow metabolism.

A pale lichen-eater moves past my ankle. It is the size of a thumb. It is feeding on the orange lichen that has taken to the wet stones. It does not fear me. It flows around my ankle the way my ankle flows around the foundation of the next building. The lichen-eater is the background. The lichen-eater is the dominant visible life. The lichen-eater does not know that I am here, or does not consider that I am a thing to be noticed, or has incorporated me into its ambient attention the way the building has incorporated me.

I stand in the doorway.

The diffused source from the north-northeast dims. The grey time is beginning. The grey time is the time for being inside. The residents retreat indoors during it, not out of fear, but out of what seems to be a polite agreement with the settlement that the grey time is for being inside.

I retreat indoors.

I go into the building. The building accepts me. The building has always accepted me, or has come to accept me at the rate of the place. The hum modulates when I pass through certain rooms, the way a string modulates when a finger is placed at a certain point along its length. The rooms are small. The rooms have dark composite walls. The walls have veining that might be mineral deposits and might be growth. The line between the two is no longer clear to me.

I pass through a room that has a window. The window shows the standing water below, and the standing water shows reflected buildings that are not the buildings above them. I look at the reflections for a long time. The reflections show other buildings, similar in style, different in detail, that do not exist in the air above. Sometimes the reflections are of the same buildings but from a different angle, as if the water were a window into an identical settlement rotated ten degrees out of phase.

I look at the reflections, and in one of them I see a building with a window. The window has light in it. The light is warmer. The light is what I might have called sun. The light is not what falls here.

I think I had a name.

I cannot remember it.

I stand at the window for a long time. The diffused source continues to dim. The grey time continues. The settlement is quiet.

A sound from above. The dry clicking. The lateral movement, as of claws on composite material, moving in straight lines from one junction to another. The clicker. The thing that has not been seen. The thing that lives at the intersections of the place, the way the residents live at the seams. The thing that is the slow answer to a question no one has heard asked.

I do not move to listen. The listening is not the kind of thing I do anymore. The listening is something the building does.

Another sound. A single cry, repeated three or four times, from above, where the air is too clear and too cold. A bird. Or a thing that functions as a bird. The cry is repeated and then there is silence.

The silence is the silence I have always lived in. The silence is the silence I will always live in.

I leave the window. I go to another room. The hum modulates. I go to a third. The hum modulates again. I move through the building's sequence the way I move through the lower streets — without deciding, as a habit of the body, as a thing that has accumulated.

I find another doorway. I stand in it.

I do not know, after a time, whether I have been standing for an hour or a year. The doorway has grown into me a little more. The veining of the wall is appearing on the back of my hand. The rime is appearing on my jaw. My breath is the same temperature as the air.

I am becoming architecture.

Or — and this is what I cannot decide, what I will never be able to decide, what the place will never know before I know it — I have been architecture for a long time, and have only just now remembered that I was once not.

The diffused source begins to return. The grey time is ending. The residents will emerge. The walk will resume.

I am part of the walk.

The hum continues. The rime continues. The lichen continues.

I will go down. I will pass through the lower streets. I will pause at the corner where the building meets the next building, and the building hum will shift a quarter-tone, and then I will continue. The pause will not be a greeting. The pause will be a calibration. I will be confirming my position in the pattern of the place.

A child will come. The child will be holding something. The child will give it to me without comment. I will accept it without comment. The next sequence will begin.

The standing water will show reflected buildings that are not the buildings above them. The shadows will fall in directions the light does not justify. The condensation will form patterns that are not random.

I will not notice.

I will not notice because I will be part of the place that is being slowly tucked into its own geology. I will be part of the slow argument. I will be part of the question being asked very slowly and the answer being given at the same rate.

I will be the slow visitor who has stayed.

The wrongness is patient. The wrongness is everywhere. The wrongness has all the time it needs.

I have all the time I need.
