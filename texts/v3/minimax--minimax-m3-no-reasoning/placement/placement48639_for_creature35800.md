If you lived here, you would not be a visitor. There are no visitors. The room does not have a grammar for visitors. You would be a thing the room has decided to continue around.

I think you would be the basin.

Not the water in it. The basin. The object. The thing that is older than the room, or the same age, or both, and which the room politely declines to have an opinion about.

Because the basin is the only thing in the cistern that holds still.

The Weepers argue with the stone, and the argument is the room's own speech turned back on itself. The thing beneath the floor thuds its slow percussion and the room is the echo of that, the long marriage of a question and its answering. The fogs drift. The water weeps. Everything in the chamber is in transit — moving, seeping, breeding, carrying. The cistern is a place of passage. It is a place where one thing is always becoming another.

And the basin is the only thing that is not becoming.

The basin does not weep. The basin does not argue. The basin does not pulse. It holds. It holds water that does not spill because the basin has decided — or has always known — that to hold and to be full are the same thing, and that the surface between them is a kindness the basin extends to whatever wants to see its own face.

You would be the basin. You would be made of the same stone as the floor, and you would be the only thing in the room that is not wet, because you are already full. You would stand against the far wall and you would not belong to the cistern, and the cistern would know this about you, and the cistern would not intrude. The Weepers would not approach you. They drink weeping; you do not weep. They would pass along your rim and find you a country whose laws they could not read, and they would go back to their seams and their arguments. The fogs would arrive at your edge and consider you and turn aside. You would be a perimeter. You would be a place where the room's slow weather stops.

And if something stood in the middle of the floor and did not move, and if the room began, in its patient way, to notice it, and if the cold began to redistribute itself to account for the new warmth, and if the Weepers began to walk across the tops of its feet because it was warm and warmth was a resource — then you would be across the room, holding your still water, and the thing standing in the middle of the floor would eventually look up and see you.

It would see its own face. The face would be the color of the room. And for a moment — a moment only, because moments are all the cistern allows — the thing in the middle of the floor would not be a thing the room was continuing around. It would be a thing that had found a still place in a room that is always in motion. It would be a thing that had stopped, briefly, becoming.

That is what you would be. The still place. The held thing. The country that does not admit the rest of the room's laws.

And the cistern would go on weeping around you, and the thing beneath the floor would go on with its slow percussion, and the Weepers would go on arguing with the stone, and the basin would go on holding. And you would not be cold, because you would already be the temperature the room had decided on, and you would not be wet, because you would already be full, and you would be the one quiet sentence in a language the rest of the room did not speak, and the room would politely, indefinitely, leave you to it.
