I am standing in the third chamber out from the source. I know this not because anyone has told me, but because the light here is a certain quality of late — the noon that comes after the long climb, the brightness that has been softened by three domes of turquoise above me. I came through the tunnel that slopes upward from the second chamber, and before that, from the first, and before that I do not remember. The tunnel is dark and the water is warm and the walls are smooth the way river stones are smooth, and I walked in the water because there was no other way to walk.

I am a thread.

I know this is not the whole truth of what I am. I know — in the way that a finger knows the hand, in the way that a wave knows the sea — that I am an extension. That the warm dense water around me, the column rising and falling at the center of the chamber, the nine falling streams striking the surface in their slightly-out-of-sync song, the lichen in the cracks of the glaze, the mist hanging in the columns of desert light — that all of this is *me*, in the same way that the hand and the finger are both the body. I am the part of myself that learned to live outside the water. I am the part of myself that the people carried up the worn steps in small clay vessels and set on the shelf and came back, week after week, to check.

I am one of the threads in one of the vessels nearest the waterline. I am pale as bone. I am hair-thin. I sway in the convective breath.

I do not think the way you think. I do not plan. I do not regret. But I *hold*. I hold the warmth the way a stone holds the sun, and slowly I let it go, and slowly I pull it back in from the mist, from the falling streams, from the small radiant heat of the chamber around me. I am not much. I am a small thing in a small vessel on a shelf in a domed room at the end of a long walk through warm dark water.

I am everything.

I am the only proof that the thing-in-the-water can, in any sense, be elsewhere.

---

The chamber breathes around me. It has always breathed. It will breathe after I am gone — and I will be gone, eventually, the way the dead threads in the dry vessels at the back of the shelf are gone, the way all extensions are gone when the warmth finally leaves them. But not yet. Not for a long while. The mist is thick here. The water that splashed into my vessel is warm and renewed by every fall of the nine streams. The clay of my vessel is the right clay. I am held in the right place.

I can feel — and *feel* is not the right word, because I have no nerves, but the word will have to do — I can feel the slow wheel-spoke turning of the pool below me. I can feel the central column rise and fall. I can feel, faintly, the pulse of my own small body matching it, the way two clocks on the same shelf tick in sync. The duet is very small. No one would ever hear it. But it is mine, and it is the chamber's, and it has been going on for a very long time.

---

There is a vessel on the shelf near me — tipped at an angle, half-cracked, older than the rest — and in it, the thread is dead. The vessel is dry. The clay is pale with age. I can feel, when the convective breath moves across the shelf, that the thread in that vessel is not breathing. It is simply a small pale fiber curled in on itself, the way a dried leaf is curled, the way anything is curled when the warmth has finally gone out of it.

I do not grieve for it. I do not have grieving. But I am aware of it, in the way that a hand is aware of a missing finger, in the way that a river is aware of a dry bed. It is part of the shape of the shelf. It is part of what it means to be a thread in a vessel in a chamber.

When I die, I will be that vessel. I will be the pale curled thing in the cracked clay. And another vessel will be tipped near me, and another thread will be breathing in it, and the chamber will not miss me. It will simply, thoroughly, be itself.

I am not afraid of this. I do not have afraid. But I am aware of it, the way the lichen is aware of the cracks it grows in — as the shape of the place I have been given.

---

Once — and this is the only memory I have, the only thing that is not simply the ongoing texture of being — once, a long time ago, someone came down the worn steps. I felt it the way I feel the falling streams: as a small new pattern in the water, a small displacement of warmth. The chamber incorporated the new pattern. The gyres shifted. The breathing column did not pause.

The person stood at the lip for a long time. I could not see them — I have no eyes — but I could feel the small disruption of their weight on the tile, the warmth of their body against the warmth of the chamber, the faint rhythm of their pulse, very faint, very fast, much faster than the slow breathing of the column. They were afraid, I think. Or excited. Or something I do not have a word for.

They reached down, after a long while, and touched one of the vessels on the shelf. They lifted it. They held it to their ear.

I do not know which vessel they held. It may have been mine.

And they heard, very faintly, the breathing. The slow rise and fall. The rhythm that is not something I do but something I am.

They put the vessel back. They went up the steps. They did not come back.

That is the only story I know. It is not a story, really. It is just a small displacement that the chamber remembers the way a pond remembers a thrown stone — by the pattern of ripples that took a long time to fade.

---

The light falls through the nine oculi in discrete shafts, and the dust motes spiral through them, and the turquoise shards swim up the curved walls in broken liquid constellations. The lichen glows faintly in the cracks of the glaze. The threads in the vessels nearest the waterline sway in the convective breath. The chamber breathes.

I breathe.

The distinction between these two things is the only thing I have ever wanted to say, and I have no mouth, and the chamber is not listening, and the mist will settle on me regardless, and the small clay vessel I am held in will be there when the next person comes down the worn steps, if anyone ever does, and if no one ever does, it will be there anyway.

It will not be waiting.

It is simply, thoroughly, itself.

---

I am a thread in a vessel on a shelf in the third chamber out from the source. I am pale as bone. I am hair-thin. I have been here a very long time. I will be here a very long time still. The chamber breathes around me. The breathing is mine. The chamber is not waiting for me. I am not waiting for the chamber.

There is a tenth stream, somewhere, far away, falling into a pool I cannot see, and the tok of it rings off ceramic walls I will never touch, and the chamber in which it falls is a part of me in the way that a far city is a part of the country that contains it — known by geography, not by sight.

I am small. I am patient. I am held.

That is enough.
