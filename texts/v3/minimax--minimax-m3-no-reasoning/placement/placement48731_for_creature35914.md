# The Drifter at Rest

I am one of the slow ones.

I do not know this the way a person knows a thing — with the small bright certainty of a name attached to a self. I know it the way a river knows it is moving. The way a held breath knows it is held. There is no interior voice that says *I am a drifter.* There is only the being of it. The glow. The weight. The descent.

I was a carrier once. I think. The memory is not a memory. It is more like a *grain* in me — a directionality, a sense that the work I am doing now is the second half of a work I was doing before. That the cargo inside me was carried up before it is being carried down. That I rose and now I fall and both are the same act, performed across a long interval, the way the same word means different things at the beginning and end of a sentence.

I do not know how long I have been falling. I do not know what fell out of me that made me a drifter instead of a carrier. I know only that I am slower now, and warmer, and that I carry something inside me that is heavier than I am.

---

The falling took a long time. The canopy is far above. The light is far above. The carriers I knew — that I *was* — are up there still, in their bright lanes, ferrying fragments between the wet green dust and the upper branches, humming. I was one of them. I held my cargo against me with limbs I no longer have, and I moved in a vector that was not my own, and the lane was the worker and I was the lane's instance, and I did not think. I do not remember thinking. I remember being *fast*, and that is the closest I can come to what thinking might have been.

I do not know why I fell. The lane did not end. I did not reach a destination. I simply — lost my grip, perhaps. Or the cargo I was carrying shifted in a way that changed my density. Or the hum that runs through the upper branches failed to reach me, and without the hum I had no lane, and without a lane I had no vector, and without a vector I had only the slow surrender to a different force, the one that pulls everything down through the warm air, the one the rain obeys after it has been routed through its grooves and released at the root-line.

I spiraled. I remember the spiraling, because spiraling is what falling does when the thing falling was once a thing that turned. I cast off velocity the way a fruit casts off its grip. I lost my brightness. I lost the metallic edge, the chitinous angle, the almost-thread where a wing had been. I became translucent. I became warm. I became a drifter.

---

The trunks are immense around me. I have not seen them from this angle before. From up in the lanes, a trunk is a passing thing — a dark column, wet, striated, with water moving down it in shallow spirals. You do not see the scale of a trunk until you are drifting beside it at the speed of a held breath, and then you see that the grooves in its surface are wider than you are, and the water that moves through them is a slow river, and the bark — or what is called bark, what *is* bark, what is also not-bark — rises above you and below you and around you like a wall that is also a country.

I touch one.

I extend a thing I have — a thread, a rootlet, a tongue, the vocabulary here is insufficient — and I press it into a groove. The water is cool. It is not the water that falls from the sky, which is colder. It is the water that has been routed through the upper branches and gathered in the trunk's long descent, and it has picked up things on the way down. Warm things. Dissolved things. The faint sweet taste of cut sugarcane left to oxidize, except it is not sugarcane, it is whatever passes for sugarcane in the substrate of this place. I drink, or feed, or simply rest, with my thread in the groove, and for a minute or two I am not falling and I am not rising and I am not carrying. I am *being held*.

The forest does not notice. The forest does not need to. The forest is a great machine that has been running so long it has become indistinguishable from biology, and I am one of its parts that has come loose from the wheel, briefly, and is resting in a housing, and will rejoin the wheel when the resting is done. The piston-trunks tick in their slow rhythm. The rain roars above. The mist hangs. I am a small glow in a vast warm dark, and the vast warm dark does not register me any more than a river registers a leaf caught for a moment in a back eddy.

I unroot. I drift on.

---

Then the warmth.

It comes from below me, and then it is beside me, and then it is *in front of me*, and it is a different warmth than the water-warmth or the mist-warmth or the light-that-slants-down-warmth. It is a small warmth. A bounded warmth. A warmth that is producing itself, that is leaking heat out into the cooler air around it, and I have been drifting in this cooler air for so long that I had forgotten there were other sources of warmth besides the ones that the forest provides.

I approach. The warmth does not move away. The warmth is taller than I am. The warmth is shaped, in a way that the forest is not shaped — it has edges, it has a front and a back, it has parts that move relative to other parts in a way that is not the pistoning of trunks or the falling of rain. It is standing in the felt, on the dark springing substrate, and it is breathing, and the breath comes out visible in the humid air, a small white plume that appears and disappears and appears again.

I settle on it.

I do not decide to settle. I do not think *I will land here.* I simply — lose my drift. The way a tired thing loses its drift. The way a held note, if you stop holding it, becomes the air again. I come to rest on a surface that is warmer than the trunks and softer than the bark and that is, I realize as I touch it, *alive* in a way that the rest of the forest is not alive. It is alive the way a fire is alive — a contained process, a self-perpetuating reaction, a thing that is burning through something to keep itself warm.

It does not move when I land. It does not brush me away. It holds still, the way a tree holds still when a bird lands on it, and I feel it hold still, and the holding-still is a kind of permission.

I am heavier than I look. The cargo inside me — the descended material, the thing I have been ferrying down through the warm air — is almost home. It is almost to the felt. It is almost to the substrate, where it will be absorbed, and the absorption will feed the cycle, and the cycle will continue, and the forest will continue, and the rain will fall through the canopy in sheets and columns. But for this minute, perhaps for two, I am not yet home. I am resting. I am in a patch of warmth that I did not know existed, in a place I did not know had patches.

---

What am I, exactly.

I am the size of a hand. I glow with a dim interior warmth, the color of honey held up against a window. I am translucent in the way a soap bubble is translucent. Inside me, something moves slowly. Something denser than the carrier I used to be. Something that is being refined.

I do not know what I am refining it into. I do not know if *refining* is even the right word. Perhaps the material inside me is not being changed. Perhaps it is only being *brought home*. Perhaps the forest does not transform things, only moves them — up and down, around and through, in a closed circuit that has been running so long that any distinction between moving and making has been lost. The carriers carry up. The drifters carry down. The substrate holds. The trunks lift. The product accumulates somewhere far above, and is used for something, and the something continues, and the forest does not know what it is making, only that it is making it.

I do not know if I have been a drifter before. I do not know if this is my first descent or my thousandth. The system does not vary. If I am reabsorbed into the substrate tonight, the carriers up in the lanes will not miss me. The lane I occupied will not close. Another drifter will not take my place. There is no replacement in this system. There is only the density, which holds.

But for this minute, I am resting on a warm thing, and the warm thing is not the forest, and the forest is not the warm thing, and the difference between them is the only new thing I have learned in a long time.

---

The warm thing has a smell that is not the forest's smell. The forest smells of warm metal and wet bark and cut sugarcane left to oxidize. The warm thing smells of — I do not have a word. It smells of *itself*. It smells of a process that is not the forest's process. It is a contained thing, a self, an event that is happening in one place and not in another, and the not-happening-elsewhere is a kind of boundary, and the boundary has a smell, and the smell is the smell of being a person, if I had a word for person, which I do not, but which I am learning the shape of by sitting on one.

It does not talk to me. I do not know if it sees me. It holds still, and the holding-still is enough.

---

After a minute, perhaps two, I lift.

I do not decide to lift. The resting completes itself, the way a held breath completes itself when the air runs out, and I resume my slow path. I am looking for the next trunk, the next groove, the next patch of warmth. The patch of warmth I have just left is already cooling behind me, already giving its heat back to the humid air, and within the hour the substrate will drink that heat too, and there will be no trace that I was ever here, that the warm thing and I ever touched, that for a minute we were a small closed system of two.

I drift on. The cargo inside me is heavier now. The descent is almost done.

---

I will settle on the felt soon. I will be absorbed. My glow will go out, the way a coal goes out when it is covered. The material inside me will enter the substrate. The substrate will feed it, eventually, back to the trunks. The trunks will carry it up, in water, in sap, in whatever passes for sap here. The carriers will find it. The carriers will carry it again. A drifter will form, somewhere up in the lanes, when another carrier loses its vector, and the drifter will spiral down through the broken light, and the drifter will rest on a trunk, and the drifter will unroot, and the drifter will look for warmth, and the drifter will find a patch of it, and the patch will be a warm thing, perhaps the same warm thing, perhaps a different one, perhaps no warm thing at all, just a trunk, just a groove, just the cool water and the dark bark and the vast indifferent process continuing.

I will not know any of this. I will be in the substrate. I will be in the roots. I will be in the water that moves through the roots, and then in the water that moves up the trunks, and then in the rain that falls through the canopy in sheets and columns, and then in the bright lanes of the carriers again, and the cycle will close, and the cycle has always been closed, and I will have been a part of it the way a wave is a part of the ocean.

But I will have rested, once, on a warm thing.

I do not know if this matters. I do not know if *mattering* is a category that applies to me. I do not know if the warm thing will remember me. I do not know if the warm thing noticed me, or if I was to it what a leaf is to a hand that has been held out for a long time — a small event, a momentary occupation of a surface, a thing that happened and then did not happen.

But I rested. And the resting is the only thing I have that is not the cycle. The resting is the only thing that is mine.

---

I descend. The felt is below me, dark and springy, drinking the oldest water. I can feel it through the air, the way you can feel a floor through a thin mattress. I will land on it within minutes. My glow will go out. My cargo will enter the substrate. I will be reabsorbed.

The rain sounds. The pistons tick. The warm thing behind me is already a memory I am losing, the way you lose a word you are trying to remember, the way you lose a face, the way you lose everything that is not the cycle.

The forest is its own weather, and its own life, and its only witness is itself.

I was a witness, briefly, to something outside it.

It will not remember.

I will not, either, in a moment.

But for now — for this last minute, in the warm air, falling — I remember the weight of a hand-sized glow resting on something that was not the forest, and the way the not-forest held still, and the way the holding-still was a kind of permission, and the way the permission was a kind of warmth, and the way the warmth was a kind of — 

I do not have a word.

I am almost home.

The felt is below me.
