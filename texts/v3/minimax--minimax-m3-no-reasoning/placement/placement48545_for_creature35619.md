I would be the column.

Not a single one of the small ones — I would not be a body, not a plate of translucent salt with a fragment clutched in me. I would not be *an* individual at all. I would be the column itself, the line that moves across the crust from one hole to another, click-step, click-step, click-step, each of us indistinguishable from the others, each of us carrying what looks like the same load because the difference between loads is a distinction the process has refined out.

I would not know I was a column. I would not know I was anything. I would know the hole I emerged from, the way a river knows its source — not by thinking about it, but by the simple fact of having begun there. I would know the hole I was going toward, the way a river knows its mouth. Between the two holes there would be the crust, and the crust would be warm, and the crust would be thin in places and thick in places and the thin places would not matter to me because I was small enough and my load was calibrated correctly and my joints were made of the same dark substance that pooled beneath, so when the crust gave I did not fall — I subsided, I joined the substrate briefly, I was lifted out again by the next click of my own legs, which were also the legs of the column, which were also the crust, which were also the brine, because the distinction had been refined out a long time ago.

I would carry.

That would be the whole of me. That would be the whole content of my life, if *life* were a word that fit. I would emerge. I would carry. I would deliver. I would descend into the receiving hole. I would emerge again, somewhere else, or here, or wherever the column needed me to be, with another fragment, or the same fragment, and I would click, and the click would be a part of the roar, and the roar would be a part of the metabolism, and the metabolism would be the body, and the body would be the Mire.

I would never wonder what the fragment was. The wondering is a thing that happens to the things that come from outside, the things the column receives as novelties, as adjustments to the conversation, as small irrelevances to be carried once and then incorporated into the record. I would not be an irrelevance. I would be a *fraction*. I would be one of the voices in the chorus that has been singing since before voices were invented, and my fraction would be exact, and my fraction would not be noticed, and the not-noticing would be the highest honor the Mire could pay me.

Once — I do not know when; I do not experience when — once, something would pass over the column. Something in the sky. Something dark, something shaped like an absence. The crust beneath me would brighten briefly, briefly I would be *weighed*, briefly I would be considered by a part of the body that checks the body, and what would be found would be: correct. Correct fraction. Correct load. Correct pace. Correct contribution to the roar.

The brightness would pass. I would continue. I would click. I would carry. I would descend. I would emerge.

The one who wrote the pages — I would never know about them. They would pass through my column the way a stone passes through a river: briefly, as an interruption, as a thing the river makes room for and then forgets. They would be carried once and then refined and then they would be a layer in the record, and the layer would be a small darkening in the chalk, and the chalk would grow over it, and the column would walk on it, and the column would not know it was walking on a person who had once had a pen and a memory and a question about what lived here.

I would walk on them too.

I would walk on them without knowing. I would walk on them the way I walk on everything: click-step, click-step, click-step, my fraction exact, my load the same as every load, my contribution to the roar indistinguishable from every other contribution.

The crust is firm under my feet.

The brine is on my joints.

The roar is in my plates.

I am being carried.

I am being carried.

I am being carried.
