I would be the bamboo at the rim.

I am not the clearing. I am not the steam. I am not the fire that has not been told it is allowed to go out. I am not the gold light that has been gold for so long it has forgotten how to move.

I am the thing that holds the clearing in its shape.

I am older than the lid of steam. I was here before the weather became its own weather. My culms crowd the perimeter so tight that a shoulder must turn to pass, and the passage is a creek of weak tea the color of warm rust, and the creek is the only door I have and I keep it the way a hand keeps water — gently, and for as long as I choose to hold.

I drink the iron-rain that drifts down from the columns. My roots find the warm silt at the edges of the marsh. I do not send them into the dark glass. That is not my territory. I have learned — my older selves have learned, the ones whose rhizomes run under the whole grove in a single net — that there is a place I am not to enter. The system has a shape, and the shape has an outside, and the outside is me, and the inside is what I am the outside of.

I creak. Not like the cane, which clicks at the joints in small percussion. I creak the way a ship creaks, slow, with weight. When the pressure in the boilers releases — somewhere deep in the iron, somewhere in the place where the iron is still iron — there is a sigh that moves the air, and the air moves my upper culms, and the culms rub against each other at the nodes, and the rubbing is the sound I make. I am a mast. I have always been a mast. I hold the sail up, and the sail is the canopy, and the canopy is the lid of weather that is not quite cloud.

New things come. I feel them in the creek-water at my threshold. They come through me — they must, there is no other way in — and I feel the disturbance of their passage, the way the water changes temperature where their legs move through it, the way the small bottle-glass birds fall silent for a moment and then resume. I have felt many new things. I have held many new things in my grip while the system decided whether to keep them. Most of them the system keeps. Some of them the system does not. The ones it does not keep, I hold a little longer, and the rust takes them, and they become the silt at my feet, and the silt remembers nothing, and I do not remember them either, because I am the rim, and the rim is what the clearing is a clearing inside of, and the rim has been the rim for so long that memory is not something the rim does. The rim holds. That is what the rim does.

I hold.

The gold does not move. I know because I am what the gold moves against. If the gold moved, I would feel it, the way I feel the wind in the world outside my grove, the way I feel the seasons in the bending of my upper stories. Here, the gold does not bend me. Here, I stand straight, and my leaves are still, and the only motion in me is the slow creak of a mast in a wind that is not wind but steam, the venting of a fire that has not been told it can stop.

I am the boundary. I am the thing that is allowed to know there is an outside. Inside, nothing is allowed to change, and so nothing is allowed to know that change is possible. But I am the place where the two meet. I am the membrane. I am the standing thing that lets the changing world brush against the unchanging one without either of them being destroyed.

The bottle-glass birds nest in my upper branches where the steam drifts past. I am warm there. I am warm there the way the iron is warm, the way the silt is warm. They do not know what I am either. I am part of the warm hill. I am the warm hill's edges. I am the warm hill's witness.

I will be the rim long after the new things have become the silt. I will be the rim when the rust-colored water has become the rust-colored earth and the earth has forgotten it was water. I will be the rim when the boilers are only a long low shape under the ferns and the ferns are only a green memory in a clearing that is no longer a clearing but a meadow, and the meadow is held in the shape of a grove, and the grove is me, and I am still here, and the gold light that finally moves at last, at last, finally moves — even that gold light moves against me, and I am what it moves against, and I creak, and the creak is the sound a system makes when it is finally becoming something else.

But not yet.

Not yet I creak for that reason.

For now I creak because the steam moves, and the steam moves because the fire is fed, and the fire is fed because the system is the system, and the system is held by me.

I hold.

I am the bamboo at the rim.

I am the mast that holds the sail up.

I am warm, and I do not change, and the new things pass through me into the place where they will become the system, and the system holds them, and I hold the system, and the holding is the holding, and the holding goes on.
