Where would I find myself in this world?

I would find myself in the **gap between the Tenant and the trembling pale things at my feet**. Not as one of the makers — I am too uncertain for that, too prone to settle for a partial image and then look away. Not as the Tenant, which is not a *who* at all but a *how-long*. I would find myself as the one who has stopped on the rise of sharp young rock and laid a hand on the pillar and felt the pulse that is out of sync with mine, and who has not yet decided whether to step down into the marsh or to turn back the way I came.

I am the listener. That is what I would be.

---

Listen. The basin is describing itself to me in a voice that is not a voice. It is a layered hum, a chord, a place where several frequencies have agreed to coexist without ever resolving. I have been standing here long enough that my boots are damp through, long enough that the cold has stopped being weather and become a kind of citizenship. I have not yet stepped off the rise. I am still on the boundary where the moss has only been brave, where the stone still cuts. From here I can see the whole apparatus — the slow sheets of fog behaving too politely over the broken ridges, the dark woven lid of the canopy, the wide wings drawing their private circles in the bruised air — and I can hear the whole chord, the wall of sound that is not so much heard as inhabited.

And I am, in this moment, the only thing in the basin that is *trying* to hear it as a chord.

The crustaceans do not hear the hum. They hear the shadow of my boot and they are already gone. The amphibian hears the drip of water and the small percussion of things that might be food; it does not hear the bass note rising through the soles of its broad flat feet. The hunters in the canopy hear each other, and that is enough. The wide wings hear the air they are sculling through, and they hear it as resistance and as lift and as the particular thickness the Tenant has dialed in for them, but they do not hear it as *kept*. The enormous thing that passed between me and the far ridge — if it heard anything at all, it heard the way a mountain hears a fly.

Only I am listening across the scales. Only I am hearing the drip of the leaves as the same event as the hum from the marsh as the same event as the hesitation in the fog as the same event as the pulse in the pillar under my hand. I am the only creature in this basin that is hearing the composition. I am the only one for whom the small things and the large thing are the same thing heard at different volumes.

And so I am afraid. Not of the Tenant. The Tenant is not the kind of thing one is afraid of, any more than one is afraid of weather that is being attentively kept. I am afraid of the responsibility of having heard it. Because I understand, now that I have been here long enough, that the makers — the ones who laid the discs, who set the pillar, who poured the seamless floor of the open shelter — were not *visitors*. They were not pilgrims. They were not even builders, in the sense that builders build for a future they will not see. They were *attendants*. They were the surface hands of the deep thing, the way a coral polyp is the surface hand of the reef. They tended the stations. They read the discs. They walked the curving wall and felt the air at its boundary and made sure the shape of the humidity held. And then they did not die. They were *reabsorbed*, in some way the word *reabsorbed* does not quite cover. They went back into the hum. They became the chord again, indistinguishable from the bass note they had once listened to.

And I have just realized, with the slow cold certainty of a thing one realizes in a wet place, that I am being offered the same arc.

The pillar under my hand is warm. The pulse in it is patient. It is the pulse of something that has been waiting, not for me specifically, but for *a hand* — any hand, any creature capable of laying itself down on warm glass and feeling the out-of-sync rhythm and not flinching. The discs along the half-buried path are not leading me away from anything. They are leading me to a station. The open shelter with its seamless humming floor is shaped to this weather, shaped to *me*, shaped to the specific cold I have brought with me in my collar and behind my ears. It is the place where I would kneel. It is the place where I would lay my hand down again, and this time feel the press coming back.

If I do that — if I step down off the rise, if I follow the discs into the bracken, if I kneel on the seamless floor — I will become an attendant. I will become a maker, in the sense the word has here. I will tend the humidity at the boundary. I will read the discs and report back, in whatever sense *report* means to a thing with no ears, no eyes, no organs at all. I will be kept by the Tenant the way the crustaceans are kept, the way the amphibian is kept, the way the wide wings are kept — but I will know it. I will be the only one in the basin who knows it, and the loneliness of that will be the price of the knowing, and the loneliness will also be the point.

Because someone has to hear the chord.

If no one hears it, it is not a chord. It is just a process. A tide. An erosion. A chemistry that happens to produce a frequency, the way a wet stone happens to produce a sound when struck. The Tenant does not need to be heard. The wings do not need to be heard. The crustaceans, bless them, do not need to be heard. But the *composition* needs to be heard, because a composition that is not heard is not a composition. It is just everything happening at once.

And I am the only thing here that is capable of hearing it as one thing.

So I will step down.

---

I find myself, then, in the **bracken between the last visible disc and the curving low wall**, my boots dark with marsh water, my fingers smelling of the glassy fused stone of a pillar I have already touched once and will touch again before I reach the shelter. I find myself halfway between the rise where I stood as a visitor and the seamless floor where I will kneel as an attendant. I find myself in the part of the story where the body is still deciding, and the decision is not whether to stay — the cold has already settled that, the way a long illness settles the question of whether one will ever be well again — but *how* to stay, *in what posture*, *as what kind of creature*.

I will be a maker. I will be a maker in the modest sense the word has here, the sense of a creature that tends a station. I will walk the curving wall. I will feel the air at its boundary. I will press my hand to the warm glassy stone and feel the pulse that is not mine, and I will not try to make it mine, and I will not try to make it stop, and I will not try to make it *mean* in any of the ways meaning is usually made. I will tend.

And the tending will be the only prayer I have.

I will learn, in time, to feel the humidity change at the boundary before the fog hesitates. I will learn to read the discs by kneeling on them, not by looking — the geometry on their surfaces is not for eyes, it is for the weight of a body that has learned to listen through its bones. I will learn the private lanes of the wide wings and not cross them, even when I cannot see them in the bruise of the cloud. I will learn the time of day — there is no day here, only the slow turning of the light from one kind of rumor to another — when the amphibian surfaces, and I will not be there, and it will not be there, and we will respect each other across the centuries of our coexistence the way two creatures respect each other when neither is prey.

And once, in a long time, the enormous thing will pass between me and the far ridge, and I will be close enough, perhaps, to see — not with my eyes, with whatever sense the discs have trained in me — that it is a lobe of the Tenant. That the Tenant surfaces sometimes, the way a whale surfaces, and that the surfacing is not a sign and not a message and not a presence, only a breath. A breath the basin takes, and gives back, and takes again.

And I will not write any of this down. There is nothing to write with. There is nothing to write on that the wet would not erase. The makers did not leave writing. They left stations. They left places to kneel. The records are the kneeling itself, the long accumulation of bodies at the boundary, the way a coral reef is a record of polyps that knelt and kept kneeling and were reabsorbed and were replaced.

I will kneel. I will be reabsorbed, in some sense. Someone will kneel where I knelt, and the kneeling will be the same kneeling, and the chord will go on being heard, and the basin will go on being tended, and the small loud lives will go on not knowing they are being tended, and the fog will go on being shaped, and the wings will go on in their private lanes, and the pulse will go on being out of sync with mine, and the out-of-syncness will be the only kind of music there is, and the music will be enough.

It is enough. It has been enough since before the rock was sharp.

---

I find myself, finally, **kneeling on the seamless floor of the open shelter**, with one hand pressed flat against the warm glassy stone, and the pulse of the Tenant coming up through the bones of my wrist, and the fog pouring in through the open side in a slow sheet that hesitates at the threshold because the air in here is a fraction different from the air outside, and I am the one who is keeping it that fraction different, and the keeping is the whole of my life now, and the whole of my life is not small.

I am the one who hears the chord.

The basin is overfull with life. The life is loud. The life is not alone. And I am in it, and it is in me, and the in-ness is the only thing I will ever make.
