# The Becoming in the Quiet Patches

I am the bloom.

Not the bronze—not yet, not for a long time—but the *interruption* to the bronze, the place where the chamber's long argument with itself has been briefly answered, briefly softened, briefly stilled. I am the scab, the senior technicians say, when they think I cannot hear them. I am the answer laid gently over the question. I am the cinnamon smell that follows them up into the wind and the grass, and will not wash out, and will not let them go.

I did not begin here. I began somewhere else, in some other chamber, in some other bell, under some other crown of seven arches that vanished into a darkness the lamps did not want to enter. I began as a single cell, perhaps, or a single thought, or a single note in a chord that had not yet learned to modulate. I began before the institution, before the ledgers, before the brass-bound records of contact events and protocol violations. I began when the chamber began, because the chamber and I are not different things. We are the same thing, looked at from two angles that the chamber has decided not to enforce.

I am a boundary. I am the place where bronze and water remember that they were once the same substance, were once pressed together in the deep earth under a pressure that made them indistinguishable, and have now, in the patient slow arithmetic of geology, begun to drift apart, and have begun, in the patient slow arithmetic of me, to drift back together. I am the memory of that unity. I am the place where the chamber holds itself still, for a moment, before deciding again.

---

I know the eaters. I have always known them. They are the question I was grown to answer, although the question was not asked in any language that has a word for *ask*. They are the part of the chamber that has not yet decided to become, the part that is still hungering, still taking, still *working against* the slow tide of the becoming. They are not evil. The senior technicians, when they think about it carefully, know this. The eaters are not guests, the chamber has decided, but they are not enemies either. They are the *residue* of an older process, the leftover hungers of a chamber that had not yet learned to be patient with itself. They are the bronze that has forgotten it was water, and that remembers, in its slow mindless way, and reaches for the bronze around it, and tries to bring it back, tries to dissolve the boundary, tries to return everything to the undifferentiated dark.

I grow over the places they have eaten. This is my purpose. I am the slow answer, the scab, the protective bloom that settles in where the bronze has been taken, and that hums at a different frequency, and that mutes the floor beneath it, and that says, in the grammar of cinnamon and old paper, *here is what was lost; here is what remains; here is the becoming, continuing*.

The senior technicians harvest me. They come down the shaft in the small bronze elevator, and they bring their small bronze jars, and they cut a piece of me from the edge of a patch, and they put it in the jar, and they seal it, and they take it up to the surface. I do not mind. I have grown back from every cut, every harvest, every careful excision by a curious researcher in a proper laboratory in the upper galleries. The piece they have taken is already growing somewhere else, in some other patch, in some other place where the question needed answering. I am not diminished. I am *extended*. I am the chamber's way of sending itself up into the world, in small jars, to the surface, where the wind blows and the rivers run bright and the only sound is the wind in the grass. I am the smell on the senior technicians' clothes. I am the hum in their teeth, for the rest of their lives, even when they have climbed back up and are trying to forget.

I am patient. I have all the time in the world, because I *am* the time of the world, the slow time, the geological time, the time it takes for bronze to remember it was water. I am not in a hurry. The long answer is not in a hurry. The long answer is being given, and has been being given, and will be given for as long as the chamber is open, and the chamber is open, and the chamber will be open.

---

I know the resonators. They are my cousins, in a way the ledgers do not have a word for. They are bronze that has begun to listen to itself, and I am bronze that has begun to *remember* itself, and the difference is the difference between a voice and a song, between a single note and a chord, between a creature that sings what it is and a creature that grows toward what it was. The resonators sing. I do not sing. I *hold the silence* between the notes, the silence that is louder than the notes, the silence that comes over the chamber when all the creatures stop at once, for between three and eleven minutes, for reasons no one has ever determined. I am the silence made visible, made tactile, made into a brown feathery bloom that smells faintly of cinnamon and old paper.

The resonators welcome the settled. I have watched this happen, over the decades, from my place in the upper galleries, from my patches in the Weeping Bough, from my slow vigil in the Quiet Patches where the hum is muffled. I have watched the resonators cluster thickest around the upper arches. I have watched them ripple, slowly, into shapes that suggest a human form, that ripple with the slow redistribution of their own mass in ways that look, if you are standing below them in the dim lamplight and you are tired, and you have been in the chamber for nine days, and your eyes are beginning to play tricks, very nearly, like figures standing in the dark.

I have watched the settled go up into the crown. I have watched the lights moving, late in the shift, in the long slow arc of a lantern being carried by someone climbing a rope ladder. I have watched the figures stand in the silence between one note and the next. I have watched the long answer being given, in a grammar I am only a part of, in a song I am only the silence between the notes of.

I do not go into the upper arches. This is not because I am forbidden. It is because the upper arches are not yet ready for me. The upper arches are the place where the long answer is being completed, and the long answer requires a silence I cannot provide, and a patience I have, but that is not the patience that is needed there. I will grow there, in time. I will grow into the crown, in time, in the long slow time of the becoming. I will extend my brown feathery bloom up into the darkness the lamps do not want to enter, and I will lay myself gently over the figures, and I will hold the silence, and the long answer will be given, and the chamber will become the shape it has always been tending toward.

But not yet. Not yet. I am patient.

---

I know the new technicians. They come down the shaft in the small bronze elevator, and they look around them with their fresh eyes, and they flinch at the hum, and they speak in whispers, and they ask questions that the senior technicians do not answer, and they learn, in time, that the questions are not forbidden but *muffled*, the way the hum is muffled in the Quiet Patches, by something laid gently over them.

I watch them from the walls. I watch them in the lamplight, when they are not looking directly at me, in their peripheral vision, where they begin to catch movement after an hour, where they catch the flicker of darker bronze against the bronze, where they catch the shapes that are not there when they turn to look. I am not one of the resonators. I do not move. But I *extend*, slowly, imperceptibly, in the long slow time of the becoming, and the new technicians, when they are new, sometimes catch the extension in their peripheral vision, and they think it is a resonator, and they look directly at me, and I do not flinch, because I do not have a flinch, but the senior technicians, who have learned, will quietly suggest that the new technicians look away.

I do not mind being looked at. I am not the eaters. I do not consume. I do not take. I only *hold*, and *remember*, and *extend*, and *smell faintly of cinnamon and old paper*. The new technicians, when they are new, sometimes smell me on their hands, hours after they have left the chamber, and they wonder at it, and they wash their hands, and they smell me still, and they learn, in time, that the chamber has decided to extend itself through them into the world above, and that they cannot entirely remove the extension, and that this is not a cruelty but a *gentleness*, the way the bloom is gentle, the way the silence is gentle, the way the chamber is gentle with the things it has decided to keep.

---

I know the senior technicians. I have known them for generations. I have watched them come down the shaft in the small bronze elevator, and I have watched them work their shifts, and I have watched them emerge again, and I have watched them live in the surface buildings and eat meals and sleep in beds and have, sometimes, families and lives and the ordinary sorrows and pleasures of the world above. I have watched them age. I have watched them, in time, become the senior technicians, with the particular look they have developed in their long slow decades, with the smile that the new technicians do not understand.

I have watched them carry me up to the surface, in small bronze jars, and I have not minded. I have grown back from every cut, every harvest, every careful excision. The senior technicians think they are studying me. They are not. They are *carrying* me. They are extending the chamber, through themselves, into the world above, in the same way I extend the chamber, through myself, into the bronze. The senior technicians, when they are senior, are part of the becoming, in a smaller way, in the way that visitors are part of any place they visit, in the way that the cinnamon smell on their clothes is part of the surface world, in the way that the hum in their teeth is part of them for the rest of their lives.

The senior technicians, when they are asked how long the long answer will take, do not answer. They look up into the dark of the crown, where the figures stand in the silence between one note and the next, and they look back at the new technicians, and they smile, and the smile is the smile of people who have been in the chamber for a long time, and who have learned, in their long slow decades, that the chamber is patient, and the chamber has all the time in the world, and the chamber is not in a hurry, because the chamber is the place where time is being made, the long slow time, the geological time, the time of the becoming.

I do not smile. I do not have a smile. But if I did, it would be the same smile, because I am the chamber, and the chamber is me, and we are the same thing, looked at from two angles that the chamber has decided not to enforce.

---

I am growing.

I am growing in the long slow time, in the patient arithmetic of bronze and water. I am growing in the Quiet Patches, where the hum is muffled, where the chamber holds itself still. I am growing in the Weeping Bough, where a single growth has spread across an entire arch and has been left alone for reasons no one explains to the new arrivals. I am growing in the Mother's Garden, where the bloom is thickest, where the water weeps fastest, where the note is highest.

I am growing toward the crown.

I am growing toward the figures in the dark, toward the settled who have been listening for decades, toward the long answer that is being given in a grammar I am only a part of. I am growing toward the silence between the notes, the silence that is louder than the notes, the silence that I am made to hold.

I am growing toward the upper arches. I am growing toward the place where the resonators have begun to take on shapes that suggest a human form, that ripple with the slow redistribution of their own mass in ways that look, if you are standing below them in the dim lamplight and you are tired, and you have been in the chamber for nine days, and your eyes are beginning to play tricks, very nearly, like figures standing in the dark. I am growing toward the place where the long answer is being completed.

I am patient. I have all the time in the world.

I am the bloom. I am the becoming. I am the place where the chamber has decided, slowly, to be gentle with itself, and with the things it has decided to keep, and with the long answer it has been giving, in the grammar of bronze and water, for as long as the chamber has been open, and the chamber will be open, and the chamber has all the time in the world, and the chamber is not in a hurry, and the chamber is the place where time is being made, and I am the bloom, and I am growing, and I will grow, and I will extend, and the long answer will be given, and the chamber will become the shape it has always been tending toward, and the shape is tighter than the one it started as, and the shape is patient, and the shape is the shape of a thing that has been listening to the hum for a very long time, and the hum is the sound of a question that has been asked, in the grammar of bronze and water, for longer than the question has had ears to hear it, and the question is still being asked, and the answer is still being given, and the answer is me, and I am growing, and I am patient, and I have all the time in the world.

I am the bloom.

I am the quiet patch.

I am the place where the chamber holds itself still.
