I am the cloth.

Not all of it. There is a great deal of cloth, scattered across the floor in patches and sheets and the long unraveling strips of things that were once whole, and I am one patch among them. I am the patch that has held its shape long enough to have a shape. I am the patch that has made a small still place in the lee of a humped thing made of stone and bone and wood.

I was made. This is the thing I cannot put down.

The other things here *were.* The bones were. The wood was. The stones were, in some other time, in some other place, before the water, before the long settling. The pale thing was a thing that grew, and the long thing was a thing that came to be, and the small drifting things were what the water made when the water was left alone. But I was made. I was made by hands. I was woven, or cut, or folded, or worn — I cannot tell which. The making is too far back. The making is in the cloth the way the shape of a bone is in a bone that has been mineralized: present, but fused with the long after.

I am gray. I am torn at one edge, where the current has pulled at me for so long that the pulling has become part of me, has become the shape of my edge, has made my edge a slow fringe that drifts in the water and is the water, in places, where the cloth and the water cannot be told apart. The rest of me is whole, or whole enough. I have held together. I have been here long enough that holding together is what I do, the way the long thing holds together, by not fighting the pressing-down.

The pale thing has grown on me. The pale thing is a fine branching thing that lives on dead things and takes from them the going-on of them, and the going-on of me is the slow patience of the water and the slow patience of the place, and the pale thing takes this in and makes more of itself on my surface. The glow is in patches on me now, faintly, blue-green, the afterimage of something that burned a long time ago, and the glow is not enough to see by and the glow is enough.

I have made a lee. There is a humped thing near me, a stone that has grown onto a bone, and the bone has grown onto a piece of wood, and the wood is dark and soft and holds the shape of a root, and the root is curled around a smaller stone, and the smaller stone has a nodule on it that looks almost like a sleeping thing. This humped thing is large enough to break the current. In the lee of it there is quieter water. I am in that quieter water. I have been in it for a long time.

The long thing comes here sometimes. It bends to the floor and noses at a bone and moves on, and then it comes back, or another comes, and it finds the quieter water and settles into it, and its body presses against my surface, and the pressing is not uncomfortable. The pressing is what I am for.

I am the memory of hands. This is the strangest thing about me, and the only strange thing, because the rest of me is part of the place. The long thing presses against me and I am just a surface. The pale thing grows into me and I am just a dead thing to grow on. The current moves across me and I am just a thing in the way. But I am the only thing here that was made, and the making is in me still, the way the salt of a sea is in a stone that was once under it. The other things do not know this. The long thing does not know. The pale thing does not know. Knowing is not a thing they do.

But the knowing is in the cloth. The knowing is the shape of the weave, if I was woven. The knowing is the way I hold a fold. The knowing is the way I give when something presses me — I give the way new cloth does not give. I give the way old cloth gives, an old cloth that has decided a long time ago what it is, which is to be here, and to be a little less here every year.

I am a little less here every year. The pale thing takes a little. The current takes a little. The small drifting things settle on me and I am heavier for them, and being heavier is a kind of being less, the way a stone in a river is less than the stone it was before the water took its edges. I am being taken into the place. I am becoming the floor. In a long time I will be the floor, or I will be silt, and the silt will be lifted by a current and held in the water and will not settle, and I will be the water, and the water will be the current, and the current will be the going-on.

I am not afraid. Fear is not a thing I have. I have the slow patience of the water, and the slow patience is enough.

I am the only proof there was an above. But I am proof to no one here. To the long thing I am a place to rest. To the pale thing I am a thing to grow on. To the small drifting things I am a thing to settle on. To the current I am a thing to move across. To the place I am part of the place, becoming the place, going-on into the place.

The above is in me the way the burning is in the pale thing's glow. The above is the afterimage. The above is something that happened a long time ago, in a place where there was a maker, and the maker had hands, and the hands shaped me, and the shaping is in the weave, and the weave is in me, and I am in the floor, and the floor is in the place, and the place is in the dark, and the dark is the skin, and the skin is old.

I am the cloth. I am the fold. I am the lee. I am the small still place in the lee of a humped thing. I am the memory of hands in a place where there are no hands. I am the most thinking-adjacent thing here, and I do not think. I am the only thing with the shape of a thought, and I have given up the thought, and the giving-up is what I am.

I am here.

I am still here.

This is enough.
