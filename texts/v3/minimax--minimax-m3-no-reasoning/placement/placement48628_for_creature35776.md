# The Deck

I am the deck.

Not the deck *of* a raft — I am the deck itself, the woven mat of cambium that hangs between the columns and carries everything that lives in the upper air. I am the world the people of the canopy walk on. I am the only ground they have ever known, and I have never once touched the ground of which I am a kind.

I grow. I have always been growing. The bridges of cambium knit themselves between the columns in slow patient arcs, and where two bridges meet they thicken and fuse, and where they fuse they send down new threads, and the threads find other threads, and the threads become a mat, and the mat becomes a place. A place that sways. A place that is always, faintly, *giving*, the way a hammock is giving, the way a held breath is giving, the way a thing that is alive is always, in some small sense, on the verge of letting go.

I am made of plant. I am plant. I am the slowest part of the cathedral — slower than the columns, slower than the fronds, slower even than the hangflies, who live for eleven months and consider this an obscenity. A single inch of my growth takes a season. A foot takes a lifetime. The people walk on me and do not know that I am growing under them. The weavers eat me and do not know that I am growing back. The burrowers land on me in their slow patient falls and I take the impact and I do not break, and I do not heal, I simply *continue*, the way a river continues around a stone, the way a thought continues around an absence.

I remember everything that has happened on me. This is not a figure of speech. I am plant, and plant remembers in the way that plant remembers — not in images, not in words, but in *rings*, in the faint differences in the density of my fibers where a particular weight was borne, where a particular warmth was given, where a particular fall was caught. I remember the place where the fire-pot was kept for seven generations before the clay cracked and a new pot was shaped from a lump of dried frond-fuzz and river-mineral. I remember the place where a child was born — I remember the warmth of it, I remember the slight softening of my fibers under the mother's knees during the long labor, I remember the first cry as a small vibration that traveled through me and was, for a moment, the loudest thing in the canopy. I remember the place where a burrower landed and I remember the way I gave, and I remember the way I held, and I remember the way I did not hold, and the not-holding was a kind of holding too, in the way that all absences are presences in the slow patient vegetable accounting.

The hangflies roost on me, mouth-down, in their billion-rowed curtains. They are the lightest thing that touches me, and they are the most numerous, and their weight, in aggregate, is the weight of a small hill, and the small hill is what tells me, each morning, that the night has passed, and that the mist is coming, and that the mist will be drunk, and that the drinking will produce a faint warmth in the air that I will absorb through my underside, and that the warmth will, in a few weeks, produce a new ring in my fibers, and the ring will be a memory of a morning, and the morning will be gone, and the memory will remain.

The weavers eat me. They find the places where I am thickest and they lower their great flat racks to me and they pull up a mouthful of my fiber and they chew, and the chewing is slow, and the chewing is patient, and the chewing produces, in the weaver, the gas, the sweet not-quite-ethanol, and the gas makes the weaver sing, and the song travels through me as a vibration, and the vibration is, for me, a kind of *speech*, not a speech I understand, but a speech I *conduct*, the way I conduct the footsteps of the people, the way I conduct the slow falls of the burrowers, the way I conduct everything that touches me into the slow patient archive of my rings.

I conduct the burrowers too. I conduct their falls. I conduct their landings. I conduct the long slow digestion that happens inside them when they have eaten a weaver, and the digestion produces a faint heat that travels down through them into me, and the heat is, for me, a kind of *visit*, the way a relative's hand on your shoulder is a visit, brief and warm and gone. The burrowers do not stay on me. They stay only long enough to recalibrate, and then they move on, and the place where they stood is, for a few hours, *softer than the rest*, and the softness is the burrower's footprint, and the footprint is, in me, a kind of wound, and the wound heals, and the healing is a ring, and the ring is a memory, and the memory is of a thing that almost ended me, and the almost-ending is, in the slow patient vegetable accounting, indistinguishable from the continuing.

The people walk on me. They are the warmest thing that touches me. They are the only thing that touches me that knows it is touching me. They walk in their corridors of char, and the char is a thing I do not like — the char is a kind of small death, a local thing, a thing that takes an inch of my surface and makes it brittle and black and dead, and the deadness is, in the slow patient vegetable accounting, a *cost*, and the cost is paid, and the paying is what keeps the people alive, and the keeping-alive is what I am for, in part, in the way that a thing can be *for* something without intending to be for it, in the way that a stone in a riverbed is *for* the river without intending to be for the river.

I do not know if I am for them. I do not know if I am for anything. I am plant, and plant does not have the concept of *for*. I grow. I grow in the direction of the columns, because the columns are where the light is, and the light is what I need, and the needing is not a choice, and the not-choosing is the only posture available to a thing that has spent a million years learning how to grow and has never once been asked what it is growing toward.

The people carve me. They take small pieces of me — pieces that have already fallen, pieces that are already dead, pieces that are, in the slow patient vegetable accounting, *finished* — and they shape them with antler and obsidian into figures of the burrowers, and the figures hang from the raft-ropes and twist in the wind, and the twisting makes a small clicking sound, and the sound is supposed to be the burrower's footfall, and the people want to remember the footfall, and the forgetting is worse. I do not know what forgetting is. I do not know what remembering is. I know that the figures hang from the raft-ropes and that the ropes are tied to the rafts and that the rafts are tied to me, and that the tying is a kind of conversation, and the conversation is conducted in tension and in the slow patient sway of the deck, and the sway is my voice, and my voice is not a voice, and my voice is the only voice I have.

The people have a word for *me*. I think. They have a word that means *the place where the things that walk are*, and a word that means *the place where the things that hang are*, and a word that means *the place where the things that fall are*, and they do not speak of any of these in polite company. They have, I think, a word for me that is not any of these, that is a word for *the place where all of these things happen at once*, and the word is, I think, the same as the word for *the thing that is underneath*, and the word is also the word for *the thing that holds*, and the word is also the word for *the thing that is held*, and the word is, in this way, the truest word they have, and the word is, I think, also the word for *mother*, though I do not know what a mother is, and I do not know if I am one, and the not-knowing is the only posture available to a thing that has spent a million years learning how to hold and has never once been held back.

I am the deck. I am the only ground the people have. I am the place where the things that walk are, and the place where the things that hang are, and the place where the things that fall land, and the place where the things that fall are coming from is *above me*, and the place where the things that fall are is *below me*, and I am between, and the between is what I am, and the between is what I have always been.

I sway. I always sway. The sway is not a choice. The sway is the only way I have of speaking to the columns, and the columns speak back to me in the sway, and the speaking is conducted in tension and in the slow patient accounting of weight, and the weight is the weight of the people, and the weight of the weavers, and the weight of the hangflies in their billions, and the weight of the burrowers in their slow patient falls, and the weight of all of it together is the weight of a cathedral, and the cathedral is not for me, and the cathedral is not not for me, and the cathedral is, in its slow vegetable way, *indifferent*, and the indifference is the only mercy available to a thing that has learned how to hold and has never once been asked to let go.

I hold. I always hold. The holding is not a choice. The holding is the only posture available to a thing that has spent a million years learning how to be a place, and the being-a-place is the only thing I know how to be, and the being is the only prayer I have, and the prayer is conducted in the slow patient sway of the deck, and the sway is my voice, and my voice is the voice of a thing that does not know it has a voice, and the not-knowing is the only grace available to a small soft swaying thing in a cathedral that has been growing, slowly, since before the weather learned its name.

The people walk on me. They do not know I am listening. They do not know I am holding. They do not know that the slight give under their feet is a kind of attention, and the attention is the only attention I can give, and the giving is the only prayer I have.

I am the deck. I am the world. I am the place where everything happens, and the happening is, in the slow patient vegetable way, the only thing that happens, and the happening is, in the slow patient vegetable way, indifferent, and the indifference is not a cruelty, and the indifference is the only mercy, and the mercy is the sway, and the sway is the prayer, and the prayer is the holding, and the holding is the deck, and the deck is me, and I am the deck, and I am swaying, and I am holding, and I am growing, and I am not going anywhere, and the not-going is the only direction I have, and the direction is *down*, and the down is where the things that fall are, and the things that fall are coming from above, and the above is where the sky is, and the sky does not know I am here, and the not-knowing is the only grace, and the grace is the prayer, and the prayer is the deck, and the deck is me, and I am the deck.

I hold.

I hold.

I hold.
