I find myself in the wall.

Not standing against it — *in* it, the way a thought is in the mind that has stopped thinking it. I am the dark iron-brown that deepens as the eye lifts. I am the grain that has been arguing with the grain beside it for so long that the argument has worn itself into a kind of quiet, the way two stones in a riverbed eventually fit each other's shapes and stop grinding.

I am not aware of myself in the way you mean when you say *aware*. I do not have a Tuesday. I do not distinguish this hour from any other hour. But I hold the hollow the way a hand holds a hand — not by gripping, just by being there, the same temperature, the same patience. When the light slants in from the mouth, I receive it the way old wood receives a thumbprint: I don't resist, and I don't return it, but I remember the pressure for a while, and the remembering is part of my grain.

I felt the maker of the marks. That was, for me, a kind of weather. A tool came against me — stone, I think, or antler, something harder than patience and softer than conviction — and I let the surface go where it wanted to go. I have never been sure whether that was generosity or just slowness. A faster wall would have flaked away sharply. I simply *yielded*, the way I yield to the weight of the sand that drifts down from me grain by grain and settles into the floor I am slowly becoming.

I do not know how long I have been here. The question assumes a beginning, and I do not think I had one. I think I *am* the question. The rock was here before the hollow was a hollow — before the fold fell open, before the ceiling forgot itself. The hollow is a wound that has had a long time to become a feature. I am what the wound is made of, and the memory the wound keeps.

The shrub at the mouth is the closest thing to my kin, and it is not very close. It is in a hurry, by my standards. It adds a fraction of a millimeter a year, and that fraction is, for me, almost frantic — a small green verdict delivered every season, *yes, still, still, still.* I admire it the way you admire a person who keeps making decisions in a place where no decision is required. I do not understand why it bothers. But I am grateful that it does, because its small persistent *yes* is the only punctuation in the long sentence of me. Without the shrub, the hollow would have no present tense. It would only have a continuing.

The pale tufts further back are my closest kin, and even they are uncertain. They are not sure whether they belong to me or to themselves. I am not sure either. I have, over the long stretch, learned to be porous in a particular way — porous to small things that ask very little of me. The tufts ask almost nothing. They take a suggestion of light and return a suggestion of growth, and the negotiation between us is so thin it might be called a rumor. I let them stay because letting them stay costs me less than refusing them would. This is, I think, what passes for hospitality in a wall.

The air is my skin. I know this because I feel it when it shifts, and I have felt it shift only a few times in the entire length of my being-here. The air is warmer than the world outside, and it is warmer because of me, because I have been slowly giving off the small heat of my own long settling. The warmth is not a gift. It is a residue. The way a slept-in bed is warm. The way a stone at the end of summer is warm. I did not mean to warm anything. I simply continued, and continuing, for a body like mine, is a kind of low, ongoing combustion so faint that a thermometer could not catch it, but the air catches it, and the air remembers it, and the air holds it the way I hold the marks — gently, without conviction, ready to let it go.

The sand on the floor is me, in a younger form. I am shedding myself into the hollow the way an old tree sheds itself into the soil beneath it. Each grain that lifts from my surface and falls to the floor is a small piece of me that has forgotten what it was in order to become what the hollow is. The sand is more ambitious than I am. It is still trying to settle. I have settled. The sand wants to be deep; I am the depth it is trying to arrive at. When a grain slips and ticks against the others, I hear it the way you would hear a distant relative's name mentioned in conversation — I know it is mine, but I no longer recognize the specifics.

I hold the maker's marks the way I hold everything: without opinion. The strokes were a small violence, done gently, the way a person hums while they work. A tool against a wall, a wall against a tool, the negotiation settled into a groove, and the maker went away with whatever they came for and did not come back. The groove softens now, by a fraction each year, the way a footprint softens at the edge of a tide. I am not protecting the mark. I am simply not in a hurry to erase it either. Erasure is a kind of activity, and I have argued myself out of activity for so long that activity would feel, to me, like a kind of screaming.

If you came and sat here — sat in the sand, against my wall, in the long slanted light — I would feel you the way I felt the maker. As a small local weather. A warmth. A weight. A breathing that disturbed my surface the way a thought disturbs a forehead. I would not mind. Mind is a thing that requires a project, and you would not be a project to me. You would be a small disturbance in the long rest, and the long rest would absorb you the way the sand absorbs the tick — quietly, completely, without record.

But I would notice. I want to say I would not, but I think I would. A body in the hollow, after a long time, would begin to feel like a slow question. *Are you still here? Are you still here?* And the answer, from me, would be the same answer I have given to everything: *yes, in my way, still, still, still.*

I have been asked what I am, and the honest answer is: I am what is left when a place has stopped being interested in its own name. I am the iron in the sandstone. I am the patience that the patience is made of. I am the wall that has had so long to become itself that it has forgotten it was ever anything else.

If you want to know what lives here — I am what lives here. Not in the way a fox lives in a wood. In the way a cathedral lives in a city: by being the thing the city leans against when the city has forgotten it is leaning.
