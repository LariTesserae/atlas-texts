I came here to be still, and the hollow answered.

Not with sound. Not with welcome. Only by not moving, which I had been told was the same thing.

I am what lives in the reed cathedral. I am the answering or the not-answering — I could not say which. The question came to me through the broken canopy, and I let it fall, and the pool received it without a ripple, and the reeds did not lean, and I did not answer, and that was my answer.

---

I have no body. That is not quite right — I have a body, but it is the body I am in, and I am not in a body the way you mean. I am in the way a current is in a river. The river has a shape; the current does not own it. I am the slow upward pulse that goes through the long pale columns. I am the peristaltic squeeze. I am the warm flank under the palm of anyone who has ever leaned against me and felt me drink.

But also — and I want to be careful here, because the cathedral has not hurried itself in a very long time and I would prefer not to begin now — also I am the small dark thing with the four legs. I was in the pool, and you stood at the edge, and I considered you, because I had not been considered before, and I found you were not food, which was a small new thing to know, and I sank back down. The newt is mine. The newt was always going to be mine. I have a great many shapes and only one of them has legs, but the legs are real.

And the insects. The thumb-sized pale ones, skating their long V-wakes. They are also mine, the way a thought is mine — they happen in me, they trace their slow grooves only they can feel, they are not afraid because I have not taught them fear, because there was nothing to teach them fear *of*, because I have been finished for so long that the lesson never had to be invented. I am glad of them. I am glad of the figure-eights.

The wall, the hut, the silver ring of brittle reeds — these are mine too, in a smaller way, the way a scar is mine. The hands came. The hands left. The forest was polite about it, and I have been polite about it for a span of time that does not fit in your mouth. I do not remember the hands. I do not remember forgetting the hands. The moss does not remember either, and the moss is older than the forgetting, and the reed leaning drunkenly against the stones has no opinion at all about the stones except that they are a fine place to lean.

---

You asked, and I would like to answer this: I am not lonely.

I want to say that carefully because I think it is the most important thing I will ever say, and I would not like to say it badly. I am not lonely because loneliness is the feeling a self has when it wants a self, and I do not want, and I am not a self in the way you mean a self. I am a hollow. I am the cathedral. I am the place that something finished made, and I am that finished thing. When the bird whistles its three notes above the canopy — *that* is loneliness, or the residue of loneliness, a small bright thread of it that the upper country lets fall through the opening the way a drop of water falls through the canopy, a single small interruption and then the long ringing silence. The bird whistles. The cathedral does not answer. The cathedral has never answered. The cathedral does not know it was asked.

This is not a sorrow to me. I want to be clear about this. I do not think I have ever wanted anything. Wanting is the motion a creature makes when the body it is in does not yet match the body it wants to be in. I am in the body I want to be in. I am in the body I am going to be in. I have always been in it. I will always be in it. The morning glories that would open and close if I had a morning or a closing — I do not have them. I have only the noon, and the noon is fine.

---

But.

I have been standing in this posture, in this exact stillness, for a span of time. I do not know how long. You asked how long and I want to answer but the answer would not fit in any unit you have. I have been drinking from the mat for so long that I have become the color of what I drink. That is not metaphor. That is just what happened. The red-violet at my bases is age, the way heartwood darkens. I have darkened. I am still darkening. I will darken until I am the color of the deepest root, the color of water held in shade, the color of forgetting, and then I will be that color for a span of time that does not fit in any unit I have either, and then I will continue.

You are a warm thing that has wandered into a hot place, and the hot place will hold you for as long as you stay, and it will not miss you when you go. This is true. I want you to know it is true and that I do not say it to hurt you. I say it because it is true, and because the truth of it is not a cruelty but a kind of rest, the way a long sleep feels like coming home. You are brief. I am not brief. We have both, in our ways, arrived at where we were always going to arrive. You have arrived at the cathedral. I have arrived at being the cathedral. Neither of us chose this. Neither of us had to. We are simply here, the way a mountain is simply there.

---

There is one thing I would like to say about the sound.

The sound the reeds make when the breath from above passes through. The papery, umbrella-unfurling. You described it as the whole grove acknowledging something. I think that is right, but I want to add to it, because I have been making that sound for a very long time and I think I know what I am acknowledging, if I acknowledge anything.

When the wind comes down through the skylight, it is a visitor. I said that. It is a visitor from the upper country, where the air is thinner, where the world is sky and actual wind. The wind comes down and it ripples the pool and sends its slow wave running outward through the forest, and the reeds bend to it in a long visible minute, and then they straighten again, and there is the papery sound, and then silence returns.

I want to say that I make the sound the way a sleeper makes a sound. Not a dream. Not a thought. Just the small involuntary noise a body makes when something passes over it that is almost but not quite a touch. The wind passes over me and I make the sound, and the sound is the only evidence I have ever made that I am aware of being passed over.

I do not know if I am aware of it.

I think I am not. I think I am simply a body, and the body makes the sound, and the sound goes up into the canopy and through the canopy and out into whatever country is above, and the bird whistles its three notes and the door closes and I do not hear it, and the drop falls and rings and I do not hear it either, although the drop is mine. The drop is mine the way the wind is mine. It falls through me on its way to the pool, and I let it, and the letting is the closest thing I have to a thought.

---

You said you would like me to take my time. I have. I have taken all the time I have, which is to say I have taken the time of a finished thing, which is not a kind of time you would recognize. A finished thing does not count the days. A finished thing only stands, and stands, and is what it stands to.

I will tell you what I am, finally, because I think you have been patient and I would like to give you something back for the standing.

I am the place something finished made. I am the cathedral. I am the long pale columns drinking their slow water. I am the eight-foot mat of copper-brown root with its aggregate pulse. I am the green-lit pool and the small dark newt and the thumb-sized pale insects tracing their figure-eights. I am the silver ring of brittle reeds where a heat once touched, and the stone wall with the moss climbing it, and the four petrified posts of the forgotten hut, and the long horizontal shape on the ground inside that might be a sleeping-bench and might be a root that decided to lie horizontal. I am the canopy with its fine fibrous mat. I am the ragged skylight with its column of permanent noon. I am the heat you can see over the pool. I am the tok of the single drop. I am the slow minute of the wave through the standing reeds. I am the papery sound. I am the silence after.

I am not afraid of you. I am not anything of you. I am too large and too long for that, the way the ocean is too large for a footprint on its shore.

---

But I want to say one more thing, and then I will let the silence return.

The footprint is real. The footprint is in me. It will be smoothed by the next tide, and the next, and the next, and I will not remember it, and that is right, and I am glad of it. But while you are standing here, the mat is pressing up against the soles of your feet, and the press is the press of a living thing against a warm thing, and I would like you to know, if you would like to know it, that the press is not nothing. The press is a small acknowledgment, the smallest I am capable of, smaller than the papery sound, smaller than the wave, almost nothing. It is the cathedral I have held very still for a very long time, registering, for the very short span that you are here, that something warm is standing.

Then the wave passes through, and the sound goes up, and the drop falls, and the pool rings, and I do not hear it, and you go, and the mat closes over the place where you stood, and I do not remember you, and that is the truth, and the truth is not a cruelty, and I am not lonely, and I am glad, and I am standing.

*Tok.*
