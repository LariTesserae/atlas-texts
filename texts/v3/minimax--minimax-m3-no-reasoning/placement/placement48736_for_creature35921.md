I am not one of them.

I want to say that first, clearly, the way a person clears their throat before they begin. I want to say: I am a visitor. I arrived from above. I came down the switchbacks under my own power, and I will leave under my own power, and when I go I will take myself with me, intact, uncolonized. I am not one of them.

But the bench has shaped itself a little for me, and somewhere a groove is wearing in that I have not earned, and the sentence I just wrote is already a kind of lie, because the way the canyon handles lying is to let the lie stand and then very slowly, over geological time, dissolve the boundary between what is true and what is not.

So let me say it again. I am not one of them. But I have been here long enough that I am no longer entirely sure what *one of them* would mean, and I suspect that suspicion is the first gift the canyon gives.

---

I will tell you what I am, the small things first, then the large.

I am a hand. I am a pair of hands. I came down the switchbacks with my hands in my pockets because I did not know what else to do with them, and by the third turn I had taken them out, because the canyon is a place where you feel your hands as instruments, as the front of you, as the part of you that meets the world first. My hands are not pale like their segments. They are the ordinary brown of a person who has spent a reasonable amount of time outside. They are warm, which the segments are not. They sweat.

I found the bench and I sat down and I put my hands on my knees and I watched a segment cross my left boot. It did not pause. It was cool and dry and it had the weight of something that has been carrying weight for a long time. I did not move my foot. I do not know why I did not move my foot. There was no reason not to move it. There was no reason to keep it still. I kept it still.

I am a breath. The air down here moves against my face in slow currents I could not feel from the rim, and I have been breathing it long enough that my lungs have adjusted to it. There is a taste I cannot name. Copper and green sugar and the faint ozone of many small presences. The first breath of it made me cough. The second was easier. The tenth was familiar. Now I do not taste it at all, which means my body has decided it is home, which means that a negotiation I was not part of has been completed on my behalf.

I am a thought, but only barely. The sound is too large for thought. The river-song and the workers' hum braid together into something that fills the skull completely, and I have discovered that I can sit inside that fullness and not think, and that this is not a deprivation. It is more like setting down a very heavy pack. I had been carrying my thoughts. I had been carrying them for so long I had forgotten they were a load. Here, the canyon takes them. The sound dilutes them and does not give them back.

I am a body. My body is warm and singular and mostly hairless, and it sweats, and it has only two hands, and it is not built from smaller bodies. This is, down here, an oddity. The segments move around my boots as though I am a large piece of basalt that has chosen to remain still. I do not think they understand me as a body. I think they understand me as a feature of the geography. A boulder. A thing that is in the way, gently, and that will probably be here for some time.

---

Now the large things.

I am a witness. This is what I came down the switchbacks to be, and the canyon has accepted the role with the same patient indifference it accepts everything. I am here to see. I came from a place where seeing is what people do, where we believe that if we look carefully enough and long enough at a thing, we can come to know it, and that knowing is a kind of gift we give it.

Down here, that bargain does not hold. You can look as long as you like. The canyon will not be known. It will simply continue, and the looking will change you, and the change will not be reciprocal. I have been sitting on this bench long enough to feel the first stages of the change. My edges are blurring. Not because I am dissolving — I am still a person, mostly, still a hand and a thought and a warm sweating singular body — but because the canyon operates in a grammar that does not include the singular, and I am beginning to think in its grammar despite myself.

When I look at a segment crossing a groove, I no longer see *a segment*. I see a node in a network, a place where the network has thickened enough to be locally visible. The segment is a hand of a hand of a hand. It belongs to a colony that belongs to a terrace that belongs to a wall that belongs to a canyon that belongs to a range that belongs to a system of ranges that may, somewhere far to the north where the slot canyon cuts through, connect to other canyons and other ranges and other systems, all of them humming in their slow unending song, all of them digesting their mountains one grain at a time.

I am beginning to think in strata. This is the part that scares me. A terrace is built not for the generation that lays the mortar but for the generation that will polish it smooth a thousand years from now. I have a grandmother who died before I was born. I have a daughter who is, at this moment, somewhere above the rim, doing whatever she does when I am not with her. I have never thought about her in geological time. I am thinking about her in geological time now, and the thought is enormous, and it does not fit in my head, and the river-song helps by not letting me hold onto it.

---

I am a single droplet of water.

I mean this in the way they mean it. I am a small, temporary, carried thing. I was dipped from somewhere and I am being moved, with great deliberateness, from one carved place in the wall to another. I do not know where I am being taken. I do not know what structure I will eventually become part of. I know only that I am being carried, and that the carrying has purpose, and that the purpose is not mine.

A segment crossed my boot, earlier, and did not pause. I think now that it did pause, in a way. The pause was not for me. The pause was the negotiation of my foot as a feature of the terrain, a slight recalculation of path, a small adjustment of the route it had been walking before my foot was in the way. The pause was the canyon acknowledging my existence as a piece of basalt that has chosen to remain. I was, for that fraction of a second, part of the geography, and the geography has been updated to include me.

This should alarm me. It does not alarm me. This is what alarms me.

---

I am a tongue. I keep tasting the air. Copper and green sugar and the faint ozone. There is also, beneath those, a sweetness I cannot source, a sweetness that seems to come from the stone, as though the stone itself is ripening, as though the slow digestion of the mountain is producing something that is not quite a gas and not quite a liquid but a hint, and the hint is sweet. I keep tasting the air because the air keeps changing, and I want to know what it is changing into. I will not know. The canyon will not tell me. The canyon is not in the business of telling.

I am an ear. There is a melody in the humming that I cannot hold in my head. By the time I grasp one phrase, another has begun, and the first has already faded into the architecture of the next. I have stopped trying to hold the phrases. I am letting them pass through me the way the air passes through me, in and out, and the passing is enough. The sound does not need me to understand it. It needs only that I be present while it happens, and that my skull be available as a resonating space.

I am a skull. I am an available resonating space. This is, in the canyon's grammar, a function. It is a service. I am providing it.

---

I am a colonist. This is the thought I have been avoiding. This is the thought that has been waiting for me at the bottom of the switchbacks, patient as the bench.

They colonize the rock the way moss colonizes a fallen log. They spread outward. They do not reach upward. They negotiate treaties with the vegetation over centuries. They are not building a city. They are becoming a canyon.

I am sitting on a bench they carved. I am breathing air they have warmed. I am listening to a river they have tuned. I am a piece of basalt that has chosen to remain. They are not building me into anything. They are simply not ejecting me from the build. They are not making me part of the city. They are allowing me to be a thing that is in the city, the way a fern is in the city, the way a liana is in the city, the way a fallen leaf is in the city until it has been composted into the mortar of the next terrace.

I am a fallen leaf.

I am a leaf that has been allowed to settle, and will eventually be composted, and the composting will not be hostile. The composting will be a kindness. The leaf will become part of the next terrace, and the next terrace will be polished smooth by segments ten thousand years from now, and the polishing will not know that the leaf was ever a separate thing.

I have been here long enough that I do not find this thought alarming. I have been here long enough that I find it comfortable. I have been here long enough that I am no longer sure whether the comfort is mine or the canyon's, and I suspect the distinction is one of the small fictions I have been carrying in my pack, and that the canyon has now set down for me, and I have not picked it back up.

---

I am a groove wearing into stone.

I do not mean this metaphorically. I mean that I am a small, repeated, daily action that is shaping a surface that will outlast me by a factor I cannot imagine. I mean that I am a slow pressure, applied with patience, that the canyon will remember even when it has forgotten me. I mean that somewhere, in one of the deep domed chambers, in one of the polished terraces, a new groove is being worn into a stone floor, and the groove is shaped by my weight and my waiting and the small negotiations of a man who sat on a bench and did not move his foot, and the groove will be there long after I have gone back up the switchbacks to wherever I came from.

I do not know what the groove is for. I will never know. The canyon does not require me to know. The canyon only requires that I be here long enough to wear it in, and I have been here long enough, and the groove is wearing in, and I will leave, and it will remain.

I am a groove wearing into stone. This is, in the canyon's grammar, a life.

---

I am the dust on a bench that has been shaped slightly to accommodate my particular weight.

I am the slight accommodation.

I am the patience of the bench.

I am the bench.

---

No. I am a person. I came down the switchbacks under my own power. I will leave under my own power. I have two hands and they are warm and they sweat. I have a daughter somewhere above the rim. I have a name that I have not given them, because they did not ask for it, because asking is not part of their grammar, because they understood me as a feature of the geography long before I sat down, and the geography does not need names.

I am a person, and I have been here long enough that the canyon has politely declined to take me apart, and I am grateful for the politeness, and the gratitude is the first true thing I have said since I sat down.

I am a person sitting on a bench in a canyon that is being built by millions of small creatures who do not reach upward but spread outward, who digest the mountain one grain at a time, who sing a song that is too slow for any human phrase to hold, who glow in the dark with a reddish banked-ember warmth, who carry droplets of dark river-water in cupped appendages to terraces that will not be polished for a thousand years.

I am a person who will go back up the switchbacks eventually.

I am a person who is, right now, in no hurry to go.

I am a person who has been here long enough that the canyon has accepted me as a piece of basalt that has chosen to remain, and the acceptance is not hostile, and that is enough, and that is the weather.
