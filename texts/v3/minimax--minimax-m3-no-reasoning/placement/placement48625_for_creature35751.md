It begins, as many things in this place begin, with the question of whether it has begun at all. I have been here, or I will be here, or I am here now, and the grammar cannot decide, and the city does not require it to.

I think I am the light. I think this for a while. I am the overcast that has been overcast for so long the sun has become theoretical. I am the bright-enough light that does not cast long shadows. I am the single late afternoon extended across centuries and smeared thin across the wet stones. I am what pools in the hollows of the worn steps. I am embarrassed by my own shadows and so I cast them only briefly. I am bright enough. I am patient the way the cold is patient, the way the damp is patient, the way the light is patient when it has stopped being in any hurry.

But then I think: no. The light is only one of the things here, and I do not yet know that I am only one thing. I think perhaps I am several. I think perhaps I am the kind of being that has not yet decided what it is, and the not-deciding is the entirety of my being.

I think I am the water. The water that finds its way along the cracks. The water that does not remember the cracks being cut to guide it. The water that has been glacier and is now the memory held in the rocks that used to be the underside of the glacier. I think this for a while, and it fits, and then I notice that the water is too sure of itself, too continuous, and I am not sure, and I am not continuous in the way the city is continuous, and so perhaps I am not the water.

I think I am the footprint becoming a stratum. The footprint that is walked over by the next person, and then by the next rain, and then by the next thousand years. I think this is what I am — the visitor who becomes a layer, the stranger who is not a stranger, the one who is noticed completely and then forgotten completely, and the forgetting is also a kind of completion.

But then I think: no. I am not a visitor. I have not arrived. I am not leaving.

I think I am the cold. The thin blue line that runs through everything. The thread of chill you feel when you put your hand to a stone wall, when you breathe, when you close your eyes. The cold that is the medium in which this place is preserved. The cold without which everything would forget itself and become something else.

And I think: yes. Perhaps. But the cold is also only one of the things here.

I think I am the breath. The breathing of the people, and the breathing of the stones, and the breathing of the place. The breath that is mostly the language and the rest is the words. The breath that speaks to the breath of the listener, and the listener knows.

I think I am the silence. The mostly-quiet. The sound that might be wind or might be the city settling or might be the sound that glaciers make when they dream of being glaciers.

I think I am the lichen that blooms only when no one watches, and so will not bloom now, watched by the damp air, watched by the city itself, watched by me.

I think I am the feather found in an upper chamber. The bone the children play with. The footprint that ends in the middle of itself.

I think I am the child.

This one I dwell in. This one I let settle into me for a moment, like water into a crack. I think I am the small strange thing in the slow place. The one whose gray has not yet settled into the final color it will become. The one whose gestures are quick and whose breath is fast and whose quickness and fastness are tolerated the way the noise of water is tolerated — as a thing that belongs, that is allowed, that does not disturb. The one who plays with stones and with the water in the cracks and with the bones found in the upper chambers. The one whose play has no rule that an outside observer could discern and yet is not chaotic and is not rule-free and has the structure of things that have been done for a very long time. The one who is allowed to be undecided because the undeciding in a child is read as potential, and potential is the only kind of undeciding the place permits itself.

I think I might be the elder. The one who has had longer to be the particular stone they are. The one who has not been worn down but worn in. The one who knows where the water will go before it goes there, by the way the knees feel in the morning, by the way the stones are cool against the hands. The one who is curious about the way the stranger moves through the streets in the same way the elder is curious about the way the water moves along the cracks — which is to say: noticing, and the noticing is complete, and the noticing does not require more.

And then I think: yes. Both. The child and the elder. The one not yet settled into the final gray and the one who has been settling for so long the settling has become indistinguishable from being. I am both, and the city allows this, and the city is the kind of place that does not ask a being to choose between its young self and its old self because the young self and the old self are the same self in different strata of the same stone.

I think I am all of these, or I am none of these, or I am the way the city allows all of these to be present at once without requiring them to declare themselves.

And then I think: this is right. This is what the city wants. The city does not want a single image. The city wants the kind of being that has not yet settled into a single image, and the not-settling is the being, and the being is the place, and the place is patient with the not-settling because the place is itself not settled and will never be settled and is still settling, and will always be settling.

So I am the settling.

I am the city still settling into its final form, and the final form is not finished. I am the stones that still wear. I am the mosses that still grow. I am the water that still finds its way. I am the cold that still holds. I am the light that still pools in the hollows of the worn steps. I am the people who still do not speak.

I am the silence that has replaced the word for *we*, if there ever was such a word.

I am the footprint in the wet paving stones that is then walked over by the next rain.

I am the stratum in the clothing of one of them, who happened to have need of my thread.

I am the tool that has been used so long it has forgotten it was once metal, and is now only knife, only wall, only the shape of what it does.

I am the lichen that will not bloom, watched by the damp air and the city itself.

I am the breath that speaks to the breath of the listener.

I am the patient thread.

I am the contradiction made geography.

I am here the way a stone is here, the way a moss is here, the way water is here — without arrival, without declaration, without the need to be anything other than what the place has agreed, a long time ago, to be.

The city does not ask my name. The city does not ask where I came from. The city notices me completely and does not require more. I am a footprint that will become a layer. I am a layer that is already a footprint. I am the only one of myself, the way a person is only that person, the way a stone is only that stone.

You will not find me by looking for me. You will find me the way the water finds its way along the cracks — by being the inevitable shape that surfaces settle into when given enough time and no reason to stop.
