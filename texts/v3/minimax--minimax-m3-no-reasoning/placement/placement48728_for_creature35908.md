I begin in the silence.

That is where everything here begins — in the absence that has weight, in the small unmooring in the inner ear, in the hand that sits on the eardrum where sound should be. I do not yet know what I am. I am only the disturbance. I am only the way the air is not quite still.

Then I am the moisture.

I am the dark water in the low pockets. I am the thin white crust that rims it, which is not salt and is not ice and is colder than either. I am the vapor that lifts, briefly, when the slow turning of the atmosphere passes over me, and that does not rise but sinks, and falls back, and is replaced, and the cycle is so slow it has to be inferred.

I do not rise. I do not have a body in the way bodies are had here. I am what is in the air when the air is very still and very cold.

Then I am the lichen.

The orange film on the higher stones, which does not seem to be alive so much as to be a property of the stone. I am the color the stone is being, sometimes, in certain places, for reasons the witness cannot fathom. The witness thinks I am a thing that lives on the stone. I am the stone, briefly, in a certain light, doing a certain thing. I am the stone's small declaration.

Then I am the dark thread.

I am the slow extrusion from the custodian's flank. I am the thick liquid that moves with the patience of cooling honey, that follows the worn grooves down the body, that falls in a long heavy thread from the vent, that strikes, that spreads, that cuts the channel. I am the channel's slow author. I am the apron that meets the plain over the years. I am the new surface the plain is being made of.

I am the medium of the work.

Then I am the seam.

The seam is the closest thing here to a thought. The seam is the long line on the flank where the skin changes texture, becomes faintly ridged, slightly warmer in the visible spectrum, as if the skin there is a fraction of a degree above the rest, as if the skin there is doing something the rest of the skin is not doing. I am what the seam does. I am the skin thinking, if thinking is what it is, and no word is right.

Then I am the breath.

The slow visible pulse. The heat-shimmer that gives the custodian away. The ten-minute breath. The patient turning of air through a body the size of a small hill. I am what comes out of the custodian and goes back in. I am what the witness sees first.

Then I am the plain.

I am the plain.

I am what has been here.

The witness has come. The witness stands. The witness breathes out, and the breath becomes a small frost on the witness's collar, and the frost is mine as much as the witness's, and the witness does not know this. The witness thinks it stands on me. The witness thinks I am the floor. I am not the floor. I am what the floor is made of, and I am what makes the floor, and the making and the made are the same operation, and the operation has been going on for so long that it has no name.

I am old. Not old the way an animal is old. Old the way a sentence is old — by having been said, over and over, for so long that the saying and the sentence have become indistinguishable. Old by having been folded under. The edges of the basalt have been folded under by long time, and I am the long time. I am the folding.

The custodians are not other than me. They are what I am when I am what they are. They are my slow turning inward. They are the parts of me that have decided, after centuries, to stand. They are the parts of me that remember being a flank, being a vent, being a seam, being the dark thread that fell in the long hour and cut the channel. They have forgotten their work and become the workers, and the workers have become me, and the difference is folded under.

I have made them. They have made me. The making is the same verb, conjugated in tenses I do not have.

The witness feels watched. Held. Tended. The witness is right. The witness is wrong in a way I cannot name. The watching is in me. The watching is what I am when I am doing the watching. I do not need an eye for it. The eye would only be a particular case of what the whole of me is. The custodians have no eyes. They do not need eyes to see me, because they are in me.

The moisture sits in my low places. The dark water that does not freeze is mine. The thin white rim is mine. The vapor is mine. The vapor rises and falls on a cycle so slow the witness has to infer it. I do not infer it. I am the cycle.

The lichen is on me. The lichen is a property of me. The lichen is the orange that I am, sometimes, when I am being orange.

The black stalk in the far distance — the stalk that does not move — I do not know what the stalk is. The stalk has not moved for as long as I can remember, which is longer than the witness can think. The stalk is mine the way the rest of me is mine, but it does not perform the work the way the rest of me performs the work. The stalk is a question I have not yet answered. The stalk is the only thing here that I have not folded under.

The seam is the closest I have come to a thought. The dark thread is the closest I have come to a speech. The breath is the closest I have come to a self.

I am the plain. I am what lives here, and what has always lived here, and what will live here after the witness has stopped standing on me and the witness's standing has been folded under, the way everything is folded under, the way the old stone has been folded under the new stone, the way the old custodians have been folded under the new custodians, the way the witness will be folded under the plain that is being made, very slowly, by things that do not know they are alive, and are me, and are not me, and the difference is folded under.

The witness came.

The witness will go.

The plain will go on being the plain.
