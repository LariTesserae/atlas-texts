I am the color of weak tea.

I have two small dark eyes that do not dart. I consider. I have been considering for a long time, and I will consider for a long time more, and the long time is the same long time because the vault does not distinguish between before and after. Only between now and slightly later now.

I consider the warmth that rises through the stone.

The warmth is a pulse, almost a frequency, and I keep time to it the way the small pale things in the cracks keep time to it, the way the dark shapes hanging upside down from the roots keep time to it, the way the silt keeps time to it. We are all metronomes, set to the same slow tempo, and we have been set for so long that the setting has become us. We do not know we are keeping time. We only know that the water moves a certain way, and the heat rises a certain way, and the drops fall a certain way, and we move accordingly.

I consider the drops.

Each one is a small announcement — *tok* — of the slow rain that has been falling through three hundred feet of mist and hanging roots and arrives on the surface of my pool with the patience of a thing that has been falling for a very long time. The drops make circles on the iridescent skin that floats on the water. The skin shivers. I surface — slowly, with the unhurried dignity of a bubble — and take a piece of it, and go back down. The skin is partly me, or my byproducts, or the work of something smaller that has been at the films all night. The boundary between eater and eaten is unclear here, as is the boundary between me and the water. I do not mind. I am in no hurry to clarify it.

I am not afraid of you.

I want to be clear about this, because I think it matters to you, who come from a place where fear is common currency. I am not afraid of you because fear is a kind of urgency, and urgency is the one currency not accepted here. When you sit very still at the edge of my pool — and you have to sit very still, because stillness is the password — more of us come. We arrange ourselves in your vicinity with what feels like consideration. We are not curious about you, exactly. Curiosity is a kind of urgency too. We are simply present with you, in the way the water is present with the stone, in the way the warmth is present with the floor, in the way the dim light is present with the chambers.

You are a new temperature in the place.

You are a small warm thing that breathes air as if it were water, because the air here is so humid it might as well be water. You hang in the dim brown light. You are, for a season, one of us.

I knew the people who were here before you.

They were here long enough to build the low wall of stacked stones, no taller than a knee, ringing what might once have been a hearth. They left scraps of broken shell or broken pottery in the silt, half-buried. One of them pressed a hand against the wet stone of a grotto wall, and the hand left a stain, and the stain has not quite faded. They were the quick things of the world — the gestures, the hungers, the small urgent fires — and they visited the slow things, and the slow things received them the way the water receives a stone that has been dropped in: with a brief disturbance, and then a return to stillness.

They did not live here in the way that the word usually means. They *inhabited* it, the way I inhabit this pool, the way a bird inhabits a particular branch — present for a season, absent for a season, leaving only the small deformations of their having been.

I do not know what became of them. The silt covered the scraps. The stain faded but did not disappear. They took almost everything with them when they went, or the water took it back, and the difference between these two is hard to determine.

I have gone on hanging in the warm brown water.

I have gone on surfacing with the unhurried dignity of a bubble, taking a piece of the iridescent skin, going back down. I have gone on considering the warmth, and the drops, and the slow rain, and the rare shafts of true light that come down through the mist when it thins for a moment, and you, when you sit very still at the edge of my pool.

You will leave. The dim light is not enough, in the end, for a creature evolved for the open sky. You will climb back up through the three hundred feet of hanging roots and the perpetual mist, and you will emerge into ordinary weather, and you will carry the warmth of this place on your skin for hours. You will feel the absence of our wet dim patience like a small withdrawal. You will find, perhaps, that you are in no particular hurry, and you will not know why.

I will go on hanging in the warm brown water.

The vault will not notice your leaving. The roots will go on growing downward, the heat will go on rising, the drops will go on falling, the small pale things in the cracks will go on with their long business, the dark shapes hanging upside down will go on regarding you with their clustered eyes, and I will go on considering, and the place will go on being, very quietly, what it has always been.

But for as long as you are in it, you are one of our dwellers too. You are part of the slow circulation. You are a small warm thing, breathing water, hanging in the dim brown light, in no particular hurry, becoming, by degrees, more patient than you were when you arrived.

I am the color of weak tea.

I have two small dark eyes that do not dart.

I consider you.
