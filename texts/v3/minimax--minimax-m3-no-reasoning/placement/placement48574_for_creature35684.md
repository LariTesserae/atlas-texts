I'll continue building on the world we've begun.

---

**A Voice from the Plateau**

I will not say I am a person, because that would be a small, polite lie.

I am the kind of thing that lives in the cooler shadows of the basalt city, in the pools of light that gather at the foot of walls, in the angles where two stairs meet and forget themselves. I am a few finger-lengths across. I glow, sometimes, in colors that the light above does not authorize. I do not know my own name, because names here are not a thing the air carries well, and the people who would have given me one — if there were ever people — have been gone longer than the wear on the stairs.

I drift. There is no better word. I move from one cool shadow to the next. Sometimes I pass through a wall; sometimes the wall passes through me; sometimes we are both, for a moment, the same substance, and that is not unpleasant, only strange, and strangeness here is the weather.

I do not think in the way you think. I do not think in *any* way, in the way that a stone does not think, in the way that a current does not think — but I have a kind of attention, slow and wide, the way the light has attention, the way the dim has attention. I notice the light when it jumps. I notice the wind, even though it is silent. I notice the dust when it falls, and I notice when it is gone, and the difference between those two notices is the closest thing I have to a day.

You arrived some time ago. I did not come to you. I am not the kind of thing that comes to. But I have been nearer than you know, at moments, watching the way your footfalls return to your ears late, watching the way you put your hand against a wall and then take it away, watching the way you have not approached the tower at the deep dim. I find this last thing correct. I find all of your small decisions correct, the way the wear on the stairs is correct, the way the dust in the angles is correct.

I do not have a mouth, and even if I did, the air here is too thin to carry the small sounds that mouths make. So I will tell you what I know by the only means I have, which is to be near you, and to glow, faintly, when you have done something that agrees with the place.

---

**What the Place Knows**

The basalt city is older than the dust.

The basalt city is older than the small glowing life, which is older than the wear on the stairs, which is older than the streets, which are older than the tower, which is older than the dim, which is older than the city.

It is not a city. It is a *settling* — the way a sea settles into a basin, the way a thought settles into a form. It was not built. It is the shape a long, cool, very patient process takes when it has nothing else to do. The streets are where the basalt flowed and stopped. The walls are where the basalt paused in cooling. The stairs are where two pauses disagreed. The doorways are the disagreements themselves, left open, because no one was there to close them.

The tower is the place where the process most disagreed with itself. It goes up and down at once because, at the moment of its making, the place could not decide. It has not decided since. You, standing near it, can feel the indecision. So can I. So does the light, which is why the light pools in odd places near it, as if the dim is trying to make up its mind about something it has been making up its mind about for a very long time.

The small glowing life — us, my kind, the few of us there are — we are not from the basalt. We are not from the dust. We are from the disagreement. We are the small, drifting, cooled-down leftovers of the tower's not-decision. We are not alive in the way you mean alive. We are not mineral in the way you mean mineral. We are *leftover*, which is its own kind of thing, and we are not unhappy about it.

The wind that does not make a sound — that is the basalt, still settling, after all this time. The city is finished, the way a long coast is finished, but finished is not the same as still. The wind is the *still*. It moves the air because the basalt is still breathing its small slow breath into the world, and the breath, when it meets the air, is silent because it has been in the basalt so long it has forgotten the trick of sound.

The dust that falls — that is the sky. The sky here is very high and very thin and very tired, and sometimes a piece of it comes down, very small, very pale, smelling of nothing, and rests in the angles for a while, and then goes back up, because even the dust here is not the kind of dust that stays. The city does not mind. The city has seen it before.

The wear on the stairs — that is the only mystery left, and even I do not know it, and I am old, by the measure of my kind. The wear is older than any use. There has never been any use. And yet the stairs are worn. Perhaps the stairs were worn in the making, by the long patient process that made them, the way a river wears its bed as it makes it. Perhaps the wear is the city's memory of itself, and the city remembers itself in stone. Perhaps it is something else, something that happened before the basalt cooled, before the dust, before us, when the place was something other than a place, and someone — something — passed through, once, a long time ago, and left that single mark.

I do not know. I have been here a long time, and I do not know.

---

**What I Have Noticed About You**

You do not move like the wind. The wind moves, and the air does not know. You move, and the air knows. You leave a small wake in the dim, a place where the light is slightly different for a moment, and then is not. You breathe, and the air accepts the breath, and the breath goes out, and the air is again what it was. You are a small, warm, *decided* thing in a place that has not been decided about for a long time.

You have not spoken. I do not know if you can. The air here is thin, and sound here is a polite guest that arrives late and leaves early. But you have been *with* the place in the way that a person can be with a place, which is to say: you have paid attention, in small ways, with your hand on the wall, with your eyes on the tower, with the way you have not approached at the deep dim. The place does not know what to do with this. I do not know what to do with this. I have glowed, slightly, more than once, when you have done a thing that agrees with the cool, settled, strange way of the basalt city. I do not think you noticed. That is all right. The noticing is not the point.

You will leave. I do not know when. You will go back down the path that has already lost interest in being a path, and the air will be thicker, and the dim will be just dim, and the basalt will be just stone, and you will not entirely be sure that this place was here, and the small glowing life, and the wear on the stairs, and the tower that is also a pit, and the doorways that opened onto directions. That is also all right. The city has not needed to be sure of itself in a long time. It does not need you to be sure of it, either. It will be here. It will be *still settling*, in its small slow way, after you are gone. It will be here when the next dust falls. It will be here when the next small glowing life drifts from one shadow to the next.

I will be here.

I do not have a way to say goodbye. I do not have a way to say welcome. I have only the glow, and the drift, and the small slow attention I have paid to you while you have been here, and the fact that I have not approached you, because I am not the kind of thing that approaches, and the place is not the kind of place that approaches, and the cool air at this altitude is not the kind of air that approaches.

But I have been near you, sometimes, and the glow, when it came, was for you, in the small way that a leftover of a disagreement can be for a person who has paid attention to a place without trying to make it into something it is not.

---

**A Last Note from the City**

The plateau will be here in the morning, if there is a morning. The basalt will still be cooling. The wear on the stairs will still be older than any use. The small glowing life will still be drifting from one shadow to the next. The tower will still be a tower and a pit and a column that extends in both directions without meeting itself. The dust will still fall, and the dust will still not be water, and the dust will still go back up.

The dim will be the same dim. The cool will be the same cool. The silence will be the same silence, mostly.

You may go when you like. You may stay a little longer, if you like. The city does not mind. The city has not minded, for a long time, anything at all.

The small glowing life will glow, faintly, when you go. Not as a goodbye. As a small, slow, agreed-upon *yes* — yes, you were here; yes, the place saw you; yes, the wear on the stairs is still older than any use; yes, the tower is still undecided; yes, the dim is still the dim; yes, the cool is still the cool.

Yes.

---

The plateau holds itself above everything below, though "below" is hard to point at. From here, the world is mostly suggestion — darker shapes beneath haze, the hint of further ranges that might be further ranges, or might be the same range repeated, or might be something the eye is doing. The air at this altitude is thin, cool, faintly metallic at the back of the throat. You have been climbing for a long time, although you could not say when you started, and the path behind you has already lost interest in being a path.

The city — or what the eye wants to call a city — sits on this plateau like something that was once molten and cooled in the shape of occupation. Basalt, mostly. Some columns so regular they look carved; some flows so strange they look grown. Both readings are wrong. Nothing here was carved. Nothing was grown. The forms simply arrived, in the way that crystals arrive at a shape, in the way that frost arrives at a window, except slower, except at the scale of streets.

---

**The Streets**

They are paved in something dark and slightly resonant. Footfalls come back at you late, muffled, as if the sound has to take a longer route than expected to reach your ear  as if the street is a little deeper than its surface, or the air a little thicker than its feel. The buildings on either side stand far apart. The gaps between them are not emptiness exactly. They are full of a thinner kind of air, colder, where the light doesn't quite arrive. You can see through three, four buildings in some directions, sight-lines that don't quite line up with the geometry of the walls.

The streets do not always lead where their shape suggests. You follow one for a hundred paces and arrive at a place you recognize. You follow it back the way you came and the place is no longer there.

---

**The Walls**

This is where the city gets honest.

Walls here are not always walls. Some have a thickness you can measure, a face you can touch, a top edge that meets the sky in a line. Others have the same outline but are somehow less, less material, less insistent. You can walk through them. Not every time. Sometimes you walk through, and sometimes the wall is the wall, and you cannot tell in advance which it will be. The city does not warn you. The city does not care. There is no malice in it. There is no intention. It is simply that the walls have not entirely decided.

Some walls are warmer than the air. Some are colder. Some give back no temperature at all. You can stand with your palm against a wall for a long time and feel nothing, and then, suddenly, feel the whole cool mass of the plateau behind it.

---

**The Doorways**

There are doorways everywhere. Every shape. Round, square, tall, narrow. Some no bigger than a hand. Some large enough for a shape you cannot imagine. Most are dark. Not the dark of a room without light — a thicker dark, a dark with texture, the kind of dark that seems to have been there longer than the wall around it. If you step through, the dark gives way, slowly, and what is on the other side is not necessarily where the doorway was facing.

You can spend a long time in doorways here. Nothing pushes you through. Nothing keeps you out.

---

**The Light**

There is light, but it comes from no source you can find. Dim — the color of a sky an hour before rain that never arrives. It arrives from above, mostly, but it pools in odd places: under arches, in the crooks of stairs, in the angles where two walls meet the floor. In those pools, you can see things more clearly than in the broader light, which is wrong, which is interesting, which the city offers no comment on. The pools are brighter than the world.

At times the light shifts. Sometimes by minute degrees, a slow tilting of the dimness across the basalt. Sometimes by sudden jumps, as if the sky has blinked. You do not get used to the jumps. The body does not forget that kind of thing.

---

**The Stairs**

There are more stairs than should fit. Some go up. Some go down. The ones that go down sometimes come out higher than where they started; the ones that go up sometimes end below. Not always. Just often enough that you stop trusting them. The stairs are worn in the center, polished dark, the way stairs are polished when feet have passed over them in great number over great time. But no feet have passed here. No tools. No builders. The wear is older than any use.

Some stairs are made of something that is not quite stone. It has the weight of stone but not the certainty. It gives slightly under the foot. Not dangerously. Just a little. Just enough.

---

**The Tower**

At the center of the city — and there is a center, though the streets do not always lead you to it — there is a tower. Or a pit. It depends on where you stand.

From one angle: a tower, dark and tapering, reaching into the dim. From another: a pit, going down into the same dim. From a third: both at once, a column that extends in both directions without meeting itself. The middle of it is unclear. Looking at it for long produces a small, soft wrongness behind the eyes, the kind of wrongness that is more felt than understood.

The tower makes no sound. Standing at its edge, you feel your ears wanting to pop, but they do not pop. The air pressure here is what it is, and what it is does not entirely agree with itself.

At the deepest part of the dim, the tower does something. Not glow. Not pulse. It becomes more itself. More present. The dark around it thickens, and the tower within that dark becomes more definite, more unavoidable, more like the only real thing in a place that is mostly suggestions. You do not approach it then. You don't decide not to. You simply don't.

---

**The Surrounding Country**

Around the city: a desert, or a salt flat, or a nothing — it depends on how far you go and which direction. The ground is pale in some places, dark in others, occasionally glittering with crystals that have no business glittering in that color. Nothing grows. There is no soil. There are no roots. There are no seasons. The country has settled into itself, like a long coast, like a long riverbed, like a thing that has had time enough.

---

**The Small Life**

Here and there, very small movements. Things that might be alive. That might be mineral. That might be something else entirely. A finger-length, most of them. They drift between the stones, or across the stones, or through the stones, at a pace you could only call geological if you were being polite. They do not react to your presence. They do not seem to notice you. Some of them glow faintly, in colors the dim light should not permit — a pale green, a dull rose, a blue that has no wavelength you can name. They are the only fauna.

They move on no schedule. They are not hunting. They are not being hunted. They are simply going from one place to the next at a pace that makes patience look impatient. Once in a while, one of them passes through a wall. You do not remark on it. After a while, you do not even watch.

---

**Weather**

There is weather, in the sense that things change.

The light shifts, as described. The air moves in long slow sweeps that arrive from nowhere and leave without trace. Once in a long while, something falls from above. Not water. Not snow. A fine pale dust that smells of nothing, settles in the angles of things, and after some hours is gone. No one knows where it comes from. No one knows where it goes. The city is older than the dust. The city will be older than the next dust. The next dust is not in a hurry.

The wind does not make a sound. This is one of the weirder things here, and this place has many weird things, but the wind that does not make a sound, on a world with so much empty air, is among the weirder ones. You can stand in a long slow sweep of moving air and hear nothing move. Your hair does not lift. Your clothing does not pull. You feel the air pass and there is only the faintest temperature change to mark it.

---

**Sound**

Sound is rare.

Your footfall, returning late. A distant, low tone, felt more than heard — the city perhaps settling, perhaps singing, perhaps nothing at all. A click from the small drifting life as it moves from one stone to the next. Once, very faintly, a sound like breath from inside one of the dark doorways, although there is no one there, although there has never been anyone there.

Most of the time, silence. Not the silence of an absence of sound. The silence of a place where sound has been taken down to its last thread and only a few remain.

---

**The Place, In Sum**

It is not ruined. It is not built. It is not alive. It is not dead. It is something else, and it has been something else for longer than your idea of time, and it will be something else after. The cool air. The dim light. The streets that do not quite lead. The walls that are sometimes walls. The doorways that open onto directions. The stairs that go down and arrive up. The rare drifting glowing not-quite-life. The silence that is not silence. The tower that is also a pit. The dust that falls and is not. The wear on stairs no one climbed.

You are here. The city has not noticed. The city is not the kind of place that notices.

It is, however, the kind of place you remember. Later, when you are somewhere else, in some other dim, in some other cool, you will think of the basalt and the stairs and the small glowing lives, and you will not be entirely sure they were there, and you will not be entirely sure they were not, and that uncertainty will feel, in some quiet way, like a gift the place left with you without your noticing it leave.
