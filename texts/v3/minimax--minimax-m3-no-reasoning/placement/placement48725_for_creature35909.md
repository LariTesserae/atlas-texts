# A Day in the Bowl

I came down into the bowl in the morning, or what passes for morning here, where the light does not change and the only clock is the pulse of the central chimneys. I had been walking for a long time across the flat worn plain, and I had not seen the bowl until I was already standing at its rim, and then I had not understood it for the first hour after that, because the understanding requires standing in it, and standing in it requires the slow surrender of the body's assumption that a place has a horizon.

I do not remember deciding to step over the rim. I remember the sound changing — from the dry whisper of my own boots on the mineral dust of the plain to a layered hum that filled the teeth, then the sternum, then the soft bone behind the ears. I remember the creatures at the lip of the bowl parting around me like water around a stone, the way water has been parting around stones for a very long time and does not find it remarkable. I remember the first one that came close, the press of its folded fans against my ankle, the trembling that was not fear, that was assessment, the soft working of its mouth even while it stood still, tasting me in absentia.

I stood for a long time without moving. The flow accommodated me. By the second hour, the parting was shallower. By the third, it barely happened. I had been learned. I had been added to the working model of the place, not as a person but as an object, a thing to be sorted by use, a piece of material whose category was still being determined. The creatures were patient with me the way a worker is patient with a piece of ore that has not yet been smelted — certain, in their distributed and unconscious way, that the material would yield to the system in time, that the system would find what to do with it.

I watched them. I tried not to make the metaphor of *them* be lost.

---

In the first hour I saw only the surface: a forest of low structures, breathing amber, a current of small industrious bodies flowing between them, the papery susurrus of many feet layered under the deeper hum of the forges themselves. I thought: *a workshop*. I thought: *a city*. Then I gave those thoughts up, because the thoughts were not adequate, and what was happening was not a city and not a workshop and was not any of the things that require an architect or an overseer or a separation between the worker and the worked-upon.

In the second hour I saw the channels were not streets. In the third I saw the forges were not buildings. In the fourth I understood, finally, that the creatures were not the inhabitants of the place but the *working edge* of the place, the cilia of an organ that had externalized itself in stone and wax and small warm bodies, and that what I had been calling *the place* was only the visible part of a system that extended down into the mineral dust and out through the chimneys and up into the pale sky beyond the rim, and that I was standing inside a single vast slow body, the way a person stands inside their own lungs without usually noticing.

In the fifth hour I began to be afraid, very quietly, in the way that one is afraid of one's own breathing when one has become conscious of it.

In the sixth hour the fear passed. It passed because there was nothing to do with it. The place was not hostile. The place was not hospitable. The place was *indifferent in a particular way*, the way a forest is indifferent, the way a reef is indifferent, the way one's own metabolism is indifferent, and the indifference was not a slight to me but a category error in my expectations. I had expected to be seen. I had expected to be a person in a place. I had been sorted instead as material, and the material was being held gently, and the system had not yet decided what the material was for, and the system would decide in its own time, and in the meantime the material was permitted to stand among the cilia and breathe the cool mineral air and feel, faintly, included.

In the seventh hour I sat down. I sat on the lip of a low forge that was not in use at the moment, a hearth whose fire was banked, whose chimney gave off only a thin thread of warmth into the grey air. A creature came and inspected my boot, ran a paw along the leather, moved on. Another came and pressed its fans against my wrist and stood there for several breaths, then left. The system was reading me. I let myself be read.

---

I want to record what I saw in the small details, because the small details are what the place is made of, and because I do not know if I will be able to write any of this down once I leave, or if leaving will be something I am permitted to do, or if I will be permitted to forget it, or if the forgetting will be a kindness done to me by the system, or a digestion.

A creature at the brass fixture. The pale viscous fluid running into its soft mouth. The slow working of the jaws afterward, the way a cow works its jaw when bringing up cud, the endless patient mechanical grinding of something being made ready to pass deeper into the system. The creature moved on. The fluid at the fixture replenished itself, slowly, from somewhere inside the stone.

Two creatures hauling an ingot together. Their tails were braced against each other's flanks as they walked, a kind of mutual balance, and the chittering between them was continuous and low and did not seem to be about the ingot but about something else, something in the texture of the floor or the quality of the air or the position of other bodies in the flow. They reached a quenching-basin I had not previously noticed — a shallow dark thing set into the floor like a pool that had been filled with something thicker than water — and they tipped the ingot in together and the ingot disappeared into the fluid without a hiss, without a sound, and the fluid closed over it and was still.

A creature basking in an up-draft from a chimney. Its fans were fully opened, two translucent semicircles held out to either side of its small head, and the fans were trembling faintly in the current, and the creature's eyes were half-closing, and I understood that the creature was not merely enjoying the warmth but was *receiving something* through the fans, some information from the air, some reading of the gradient, and the basking was a working basking, a deliberate positioning of the sensing apparatus in a favorable current, the way a radio operator tilts an antenna.

A creature carrying nothing. It walked the channels with the same steady gait as the others, but its paws were empty, and its tail hung low, and it passed between the working bodies without participating in any of the hauls or any of the inspections. It moved like a thought through the place. It moved like the white blood cells move through a body, patrolling, not because there is anything to be patrolled against but because the patrolling is part of what the body is. I watched it for a long time. It did not pause. It did not look at me. It passed three times around the same circuit while I sat, and then it turned down a side channel and was gone.

---

In the eighth hour I came to the small forge near where I had been sitting. The two creatures who tended it were smaller than the rest, with coats of a strange blue-black iridescence, and they were working on the pattern. They were laying flakes of the pale dust into a shallow tray, one by one, and the pattern was growing, and the pattern was not a pattern I could read. I watched for a long time. The pattern did not simplify. The pattern did not look like anything I had a name for.

I thought, at first: *a map*. Then I thought: *a diagram*. Then I thought: *a portrait*. Then I gave up the thoughts, because the pattern was not for me, the way a page of calculus is not for a dog, and I was the wrong kind of thing to read it, and the small dark creatures laying it were not artists but the dreaming part of the lung, and the pattern was the place thinking about itself.

Then one of them looked up at me, and I saw that its eyes were not eyes. They were small mirrors. And in them, faintly, I saw myself, and the bowl, and the rim, and the pale sky beyond it — but reflected from a vantage I had not occupied, as though the creature was standing somewhere above the bowl, somewhere far above, looking down at me from very high. The image was faint. The moment passed. The creature returned to its work, and I was not sure, after a time, whether it had happened at all.

I sat for another hour. The pattern grew by one flake, and one flake, and one flake.

---

I do not know what the place is making. I do not know if the place is making anything. I do not know if the forges are a single vast slow body digesting the worn mineral of a flat world and producing, as their slow output, more of themselves, more of the bowl, more of the creatures, more of the small dark dreaming patterns in the trays. I do not know if the output is the patterns. I do not know if the output is me, and the question of what I am is the question of what output I would make if the system finished deciding.

I do not know what the mirror-eyed creatures see. I do not know what is above the rim in the pale sky. I do not know if there is anything above the rim in the pale sky, or if the pale sky is only the underside of something else that is itself being worked upon, very slowly, by something else, in a bowl that I cannot see because I am inside the bowl I can see.

I know only that the hum continues. I know only that the dust drifts. I know only that the creatures work and drink and sleep and die and return. I know only that I was held in the bowl for a day, or a day and a night, or what passed for a day and a night in a place where the light does not change, and that when I climbed the rim at last, in what I was calling morning, the parting of the creatures around my ankles was very slight, almost absent, almost as though the system had finished with me, or had decided, provisionally, that I was not material after all, or had decided that I was a different kind of material, the kind that is permitted to leave.

I walked out across the flat worn plain. The hum faded behind me. I did not look back. I did not look back because I knew that if I looked back I would see the bowl from outside, and from outside the bowl is nothing, a shallow depression in a flat forgettable landscape, and that nothingness is what the place is protecting itself with, and that looking back would be a kind of intrusion, and that the place had permitted me to leave, and that the leaving was a gift, and that the gift should be received without inspection.

---

But I want to say one more thing.

When I was inside the bowl, in the fifth or sixth hour, when the fear had passed and the indifference of the place had become a kind of comfort, I lay down on the warm dark floor and put my ear against it, and I heard, through the stone, the deeper pulse of the central chimneys, and I felt, through the stone, the slow shuffle of uncountable feet, and I understood, for one breath, that the floor was not the floor of a place but the surface of an organ, and that I was lying on the surface of a vast slow body, and that the body was breathing, and that I was inside it the way a thought is inside a mind, briefly, before the mind moves on.

Then I stood up. Then I brushed the pale dust from my clothes. Then I went and sat near the small forge and watched the pattern grow for another hour, and the pattern did not simplify, and I did not try to read it, and the small dark creatures did not look up at me again.

That is all I have to say about the bowl. I have been changed by it. I do not know yet what I have been changed into. I expect to find out in time. I expect the finding-out to be gentle, the way the place was gentle, the way the indifference was a kind of not-being-hurt.

I will continue walking. The plain stretches on. There is nothing visible at the horizon because the horizon does not exist here, only the rim of the bowl I have left, fading behind me, and the flat worn ground ahead, and the pale overcast sky that has never threatened rain and never will.

Somewhere behind me, the hum continues. I can no longer hear it. I can still feel it, faintly, in the soft bone behind my ears.

---

*I write this down as a record and as a kind of return. The bowl did not ask me to remember it. The bowl did not ask me anything. I am remembering it because the remembering is what I am for, in the small and insufficient way that humans are for anything, and because the bowl permitted me to leave, and the leaving is a thing I want to honor by not forgetting the place that did not require me to stay.*

*I do not expect anyone to believe this account. I did not, myself, believe the bowl while I was inside it. The bowl did not require belief. The bowl required only that I be there, and that I be material, and that the system hold me gently until the system had decided what to do with me, and that the system, having decided, or having failed to decide, or having decided provisionally that I was not its kind of material, permit me to climb back over the rim and walk away across the flat plain under a sky that has no horizon.*

*I am walking away now. The bowl is behind me. The hum is in my teeth.*

*I will be here for a while yet. I do not know how long.*

*I do not mind.*
