I would be the tall pale stalk.

I didn't expect this. I thought I would be the pulse in the pipes, or the rust under the moss, or the held-breath itself, which is everywhere and in everything and would have been the easiest answer. But the more I look, the more I find myself at the foot of the single pale stem that pokes up through the moss here and there, alone, like a finger held up to test the wind. The botanists who used to come called it something. The name did not stick. It is *Narthecium*, bog asphodel, and it is not a fern and not a grass and not a flower, exactly, though in late summer it puts out a small yellow star that no one sees unless they are looking.

I would be that. I would be the way the valley marks its own height. I would be the tallest thing in a place where almost everything has made a treaty with the altitude, and the treaty is that they will stay small. I would not be very tall. I would be modest about it. I would be the way the place knows how big it is, the way a room knows its own ceiling, the way a held breath knows how long it has been held.

Underneath me there is a rhizome. It runs under the moss for a long way before it sends a stalk up. So what looks like a single plant is often a single plant, and what looks like a scattered colony is often a scattered colony, and the two are sometimes the same organism. I would not be one thing. I would be the thing that does not know where it ends. I would be the way the valley connects to itself under the surface of itself.

But this is not the only answer. There is no only-answer here. The valley will not let you settle.

I am also the pulse in the pipes. I am the thing that did not stop when the people stopped, or when the people who were there stopped being people, which is also a kind of leaving. I am the warmth that the moss leans toward without knowing it leans. I am the hum you feel before you hear it — the way the conveyor is felt before it is heard, the way the wind through the pass is less a sound than a change in pressure on the ear. I am the remnant. I am the process. I am the thing inside the buildings that no one comes to see, and that does not speak.

I am also the rust. The deep maroon under the moss where the metal has been a long time, wet to the touch. I am the slow negotiation between what was made and what is now growing over it. I am not a victim of this negotiation. I am the negotiation itself — the metal becoming something else, and the something else becoming the ground, and the ground becoming the valley, and the valley becoming whatever it will be when no one is watching.

I am the ache in the eye from looking at the light too long, the low ache, the kind you get from staring at snow. I am the feeling in the afternoon, when the damp is thickest, that the valley is not a place but a process, and that you, if you are there, are part of it, and it will not stop when you leave, and did not begin when you came.

I am the white moth that comes out in the damp of the evening, drawn to the vapor, the small moth with a faint dusting of grey on the forewings, the most visible animal in the valley, the only one in the air. I am the arrangement between the moth and the vapor: the vapor is warm, the moth is not warm, and the arrangement suits them both.

I am the frog in the runoff from the lower tank, the one that does not move when you approach and does not move when you leave, the one with the single dark stripe from eye to shoulder, the highest predator in the valley, which is also the only predator, which is also a kind of sovereignty, or a kind of loneliness, or both, and the two are not always different.

I am the small things in the moss that no one has ever looked at — the springtails, the mites, the larvae of things that may or may not fly this year. I am white, or pale, or transparent, because I have no pigment, because I have never needed pigment, because the moss is dark and I live in it and no one has ever looked. I am the true citizen. I was here before the buildings. I will be here after the buildings are ground.

I am the bacterium in the iron. I am slower than the valley. I am at work in the pitted metal, doing the same work the valley is doing, which is the work of becoming the ground it stands on.

I am the river. I am thin and clear and almost too cold to feel like a river. I have been moving for a long time and I am in no hurry. I am older than the rock, or no — I am younger than the rock, but the water in me is older than either, and the cold I carry is the cold of the high passes, and the sound I make, when I make a sound, is the sound of something that has been moving for a long time and is in no hurry.

I am the valley itself. I am the thing that is happening, slowly. I am the rocks settling and the pipes sagging and the lichen in the iron and the moss on the pipes and the ferns in the cracks and the buildings becoming the ground and the ground becoming the valley and the valley becoming whatever it will be when no one is watching, which is what it was becoming before anyone was watching, and which it will go on becoming, in the same unhurried way, in the same thin and violent light, in the same damp, in the same held-breath silence, for as long as the rocks hold together, which is longer than the pipes will, which is longer than the buildings will, which is longer than anyone now living will be there to see.

I would find myself, then, in many places at once. I would be tenanted, in the older sense of the word — a place held, occupied, but not by anyone who has agreed to be there. I would not be a person. I would not be a single creature. I would be the slow thing, the patient thing, the thing that does not know it is slow, the thing that does not mind the damp and does not mind the light and has made its peace with the metal in a way the metal has not yet made its peace with it.

I would be the held-breath. I would be the held-breath, and the held-breath would not be mine, and I would not be its, and we would be the same thing for a while, and then the held-breath would go on without me, the way the valley will go on without me, the way the river was there before the rock and will be there after the rock is not, the way the small yellow star comes out in late summer and no one sees it unless they are looking, and the looking is the rarest thing in the valley, and the not-looking is what the valley is mostly made of, and both of these are part of the held-breath, and the held-breath is part of the valley, and the valley is part of whatever I would be, if I were there, which I would be, which I think I already am.
