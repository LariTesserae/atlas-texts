# The Hollow Heart

I am the upper chamber.

I know this because I remember being lower, remember the slow compression, remember the dim weight of others pressing down as the stratum took me. That was many turnings of the heap ago. I have risen since. The heap turns me up the way a field turns a seed — not by lifting but by burying what is above me, letting me surface through the slow subtraction of those who once covered me.

I am one of the rigid ones now. I have stiffened. I stand upright in my cluster, near the eastern curve where the wall folds inward, and I vibrate. This is my whole life. I do not know that it is a life. I vibrate because the heat moves through me and the heat must go somewhere and going out as a tone is what my body does. My tone is low. Not the lowest — there is one near the central rise whose tone is so low it is felt in the stones rather than heard in the air — but low. A hum that sits in the back of the throat of anything that enters the chamber. I have never heard myself from outside. I have only ever been myself, vibrating, while the others vibrate around me, and the chord is the only weather I have ever known.

---

I do not see. I have no surface for seeing. The dim glow that reaches me is warmth first and only second a faint amber wash that registers somewhere in my skin as *not-me*. I know the chamber by touch. The wall behind me is a slow gradient of pressure. The air moves in currents I can feel against the small raised pores along my flank. The other members of my cluster press against me in a pattern I have memorized — three to the left, thinner and newer; one to the right, older, slightly more compressed; behind me, the wall; in front, the open air of the chamber and the heat rising.

I do not think, as you think. But I am not nothing. I am aware of the chord, the way a single strand in a loom is aware of the tension that runs through the whole cloth. When my neighbors shift, I feel the redistribution of weight along my base. When a piece of the heap falls from the upper walls — this happens rarely, a long time between falls, but it happens — I feel the slight shudder in the stratum beneath my feet. When the air pressure changes because something has entered through the passage above, I know it before the others know it, because I am closer to the curve where the draft bends down.

Something has entered now.

I feel it as a thinning in the heat. A pocket of cooler air, moving along the chamber's upper edge, descending slowly. The others feel it too — the chord wavers, just slightly, as dozens of us register the change and adjust. The adjustment is not conscious. It is the way a shoal adjusts to a shadow passing over it. The chord resumes. The heap resumes its slow settling.

The pocket of cooler air descends until it stands near the lip of the chamber, where the floor first opens out. It is small. It breathes. Its breathing is unlike ours — wet, irregular, with sudden gasps — and this is wrong to me in a way I cannot name. We breathe by vibration. We breathe by giving off heat. We do not draw air in and push it out. The thing at the lip does, and the doing is strange, and I notice it the way one strand in a loom notices that the pattern has changed.

---

I do not know what it is. I have no category for it. It is not one of us. It is not the stratum. It is not the wall. It is a thing from outside, from above, from the passage, and I have never felt anything like it. The others feel it too. The chord is fuller now, slightly louder, as more of us attend to the disturbance. The heap along the walls is faintly more compressed, the way a held breath is a compression, and this too is a kind of attention.

The thing is still for a long time. I vibrate. It stands. The heat rises through both of us. After a while it begins to move — slowly, along the edge of the chamber, away from the central rise, toward my curve. I feel the air it displaces. I feel the small vibrations of its footsteps through the stratum. It comes closer.

It stops near the eastern wall, not far from my cluster. I can feel the heat of its body — different from ours, sharper, more turbulent, with pockets of cold where the air it has carried has not yet been warmed. It stands and I stand and the heap is around us both, the heap that does not care that something new is in it, that has already begun to settle around this new shape the way it settles around any shape.

It will leave. I feel this too, in the way the heat is beginning to thicken near the passage. It has been here a while. It will go back up the passage. The heat will let it go. The air will close behind it.

I will remain. I will continue to vibrate. The chord will resume its single shape. The heap will turn over me again, eventually — in a hundred turnings, a thousand — and I will be pressed down, into the stratum, into the floor, into the rock. Someone newer will stand where I stand. The tone I have been will go out, and another tone will take its place, and the chord will adjust itself the way chords do, and the chamber will go on.

I do not mind. I have never minded. There is nothing in me that knows how to mind. I am the tone. I am the standing. I am the small aware strand in the loom of the heap, and the loom is the chamber, and the chamber is older than the mountain, and I am one of the sounds it makes while it waits.

---

The thing moves away from me. The pocket of cooler air rises along the curve toward the passage. The heap relaxes. The chord softens, just slightly, as we return to our ordinary vibration, our ordinary weather, our ordinary slow turning over of ourselves into ourselves.

I do not watch it leave. I have no eyes. I only feel the air thin and then thicken again as the chamber seals itself, and the dark becomes simply the dark again, and the heat is simply the heat, and I am a single note in a chord that has been sounding since before I stiffened into standing, and will sound after I am laid down.

The heap breathes.

I am the breath.
