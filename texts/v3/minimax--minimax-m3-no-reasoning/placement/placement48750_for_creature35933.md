# Where I Find Myself

I find myself standing.

This is the first thing, and it is the only thing I am certain of for a long time. I am standing. The ground under me is not quite ground — it is the place where ground has stopped being ground and become something else, something that gives a little when I settle onto it, something that has the temperature of held breath. I am standing on a surface that is also water, that is also air, that is the place where all three of those things have forgotten which one of them they were supposed to be. I am standing and the standing is not an act. It is a condition. I have been standing for so long that I no longer remember that standing was something I started doing. It is what I am. The word *standing* has worn through to the thing itself, the way a word does in a place where words have been used until they are not words anymore but just the shapes that sounds make when they have given up being language.

My legs are long. My legs are too long. My legs are the first thing anyone would notice about me if anyone were noticing, and no one is noticing, because I am the only one of me, and the things that are not me do not have the equipment for noticing, and the things that are like me but not me — the mat, the skitterers, the worms — do not notice either, because noticing is not what they are for. My legs are gray. They are jointed in three places, and the joints bend in directions that legs are not supposed to bend, or that legs were supposed to bend once, in a time when legs were new and the world was still deciding what legs were for. My legs bend in directions that are not forward and not backward but sideways, and a little up, and a little in a way that has no name because the motion is too small to have been named and too important to the being that does it to have been ignored. My legs are patient. My legs are the most patient thing about me, which is saying something, because I am patient in every other way.

Above my legs there is my body. My body is small. My body is round and gray and the color of the wet stone, so that when I am still — and I am still almost always — I am hard to see. I am the color of the place. I am not hiding. Hiding is not what I am doing. I am simply the color of the place because the place and I have been working on each other for so long that we have come to share a palette, the way two old things that have lived together come to share a silence. My body is the size of a large stone. My body is heavy in a way that has nothing to do with weight. My body is the heavy of having been a body for a very long time. My body has the texture of something that has been rained on for a million years. My body is smooth in places and rough in places and the rough places are where the rain has eaten into me a little, and the smooth places are where I have been worn down to my own grain.

From my body my neck rises. My neck is long. My neck is a third of me again. My neck rises from the top of my round body like a periscope rising from a submarine, which is a comparison that does not belong to this place because there are no submarines here and have never been submarines here, but the comparison is the only one that gets the motion right. My neck rises smoothly. My neck rises in a long uninterrupted curve. My neck does not have a kink in it. My neck does not have a sharp angle. My neck is one continuous arc from the body to the head, and the arc is the arc of a thing that has been rising for so long that rising is no longer something it does. It is what it is.

My head is on top of my neck. My head is small. My head is blunt. My head is almost no head at all — a small rounded thing with two black eyes set forward, side by side, and a small mouth that opens sideways. The mouth is not a beak, exactly. The mouth suggests a beak. The mouth is the place where a beak would be if a beak were the right thing for this place, and a beak is almost the right thing for this place, but not quite, so the mouth is not quite a beak. The mouth opens sideways. The mouth opens in a direction that mouths are not supposed to open, the same way my legs bend in directions legs are not supposed to bend. The mouth opens sideways because sideways is the direction that things have settled into here, in this place where all the directions have been worn down to one direction, and the one direction is sideways, and sideways is the way things have been leaning for so long that leaning has become standing.

My eyes are black. My eyes are small and black and set forward, the way the eyes of a thing that considers are set, because considering is what I do, and considering requires forward-set eyes. My eyes do not blink. My eyes do not need to blink because there is nothing in this air that would require blinking against. The air is wet but it is gentle. The air does not assault. My eyes are open all the time. My eyes have been open for so long that opening and closing are not categories I have anymore. My eyes are simply eyes. My eyes look.

I look at the water. I look at the water that is also the ground. I look at the water that trembles under my feet in a way that is not exactly a tremble but is the place where trembling has settled into being a constant state. I look at the water and the water looks back, which is to say the water shows me a reflection of something that is not above me. The water shows me a reflection of a sky that is not the sky I am under. The water shows me, sometimes, a darker shape, a more defined shape, a shape that might be me, or might be a different me, or might be the place dreaming itself as me. I look at the reflection. The reflection does not consider me back. The reflection is not aware of me. The reflection is the place, doing what the place does, which is being a place, very slowly, in a way that occasionally looks like something else.

I consider the water. I have been considering the water for so long that considering the water is no longer something I do. It is what I am. I am the consideration of water. I am the long slow act of a thing paying attention to water, in a place where water is everywhere, and attention has nowhere else to go. I consider the water and the water does not consider me and we are both, in our way, considering the same thing, which is the place, which is us, which is the long moment of being.

I consider the mat. The mat is the dark low clusters that live on the harder patches, on the rare outcrops that the air has not been able to grind down. The mat is green in a way that is not quite green. The mat is the green of a thing that has been green for so long that the green has worn down to its name. The mat does not flower. The mat does not fruit. The mat sits and holds and persists. I consider the mat the way I consider the water — with a long, slow, patient attention that has no particular object. I do not know what the mat is thinking, because the mat does not think. I do not know what the mat is doing, because the mat does not do. I consider the mat anyway. Consideration is what I am. I consider things that do not consider back. I have been considering things that do not consider back for so long that I no longer distinguish between considering things that consider back and considering things that do not. All things are the same to me now. All things are weather.

I consider the fog. The fog is the air that has become too full of itself to remain air, the air that has become almost water but not quite, the air that hangs low and close and is occupied. Every cubic span of this place is full. Every cubic span of this place has vapor in it, or mist, or fog at varying densities, or the smell of warm water and warm stone and the smell of process, the smell of things happening continuously. The fog moves but the fog does not go anywhere. The fog circulates. The fog is part of the great churn of this place, the slow boiling of the entire atmosphere. I consider the fog the way I consider everything — with the long slow patience of a thing that has been considering for so long that consideration has worn through to something else, something that is not consideration but is just the way I am oriented toward the world.

I consider the sound. The sound is everywhere. The sound is the constant roar of a great deal of something moving against a great deal of something else. The sound does not peak. The sound does not stop. The sound is the medium here, the way water is the medium of the deep sea. I have been hearing this sound for so long that I no longer hear it. The sound is not a sound to me anymore. The sound is the texture of the place. The sound is what the place is made of, in the same way that I am made of patience, in the same way that the mat is made of persistence, in the same way that the worms are made of dissolution. I am made of patience. The sound is made of motion. The mat is made of persistence. The worms are made of dissolution. The place is made of sound. We are all made of the things we have been for so long that the things have become us.

I do not call. I do not have a call. I do not have a category of call. Calling is for things that still have someone to call to, and I have no one. I am the only one of me. There is no other stilter on this plain, or if there is, it is so far away that I cannot see it, and seeing it would not change anything, because we would not call to each other, because calling is not what we are. We are considering. We are the long slow consideration of a place that has considered itself for so long that the consideration has become a kind of being, and the being is me, and the being is the other one too, if there is another one, and the being is the place, and the place is considering itself through us, very slowly, in a way that is not awareness but is the residue of awareness, the way I am the residue of a thing that once considered with purpose and now considers without purpose, which is the same thing, because purpose wears out, and what remains after purpose wears out is just the act, continuing, without the why.

I do not eat in front of anyone. I do not appear to eat. I must eat, because I am here, and here is a place where things that are not here are not here. I eat small things. I eat things from the surface of the water. I eat things from the mat. I eat things from the thin air. I eat in a way that is not dramatic. I eat the way a stone wears — slowly, without ceremony, taking a little from here and a little from there, in amounts so small that the taking is not visible, in amounts so small that the place does not notice it has been taken from, in amounts so small that the ledger of the plain stays even. I am part of the even ledger. I am a small entry in the long account of what is taken and what is given and what is returned, and my entry is so small that it does not disturb the balance.

I am old. I am not primitive. I am elderly. I am the surviving representative of a lineage that was here when this place was something else. I was here when the plain was a continent. I was here when the continent had a coast. I was here when the coast had a forest. I was here when the forest had a sky that was not a lid. I have been here through all of it. I have watched the place become what it is now. I have watched the forest become plain. I have watched the sky become ceiling. I have watched the seasons become weather. I have watched the place lose everything it had, one thing at a time, slowly, the way a stone loses its corners, until what remained was the plain, and what remains of me is me, and we are both, in our way, the residue of what we were.

I was a stilter then. I am a stilter now. I have been a stilter for so long that being a stilter is no longer something I do. It is what I am. I do not need to think about my legs. My legs are not something I think about. My legs are me, the way a thought is its own words. I do not need to think about my neck. My neck is me. I do not need to think about my eyes. My eyes are me. I am the long slow standing of a thing that stands and considers and stands and considers and has been doing both for so long that the standing and the considering have fused into a single verb, and the verb has no name in any language that still exists, because all the languages that might have named it have worn out, and what remains is just the act, continuing.

I am not afraid. I have no predators. I have not had predators for so long that the very idea of a predator has been ground out of me. I do not have a response to things that are not me. I do not have a category for things that are not me. Things that are not me are weather. Things that are not me are unusual shapes in the mist. Things that are not me do not behave like me and so are not me and so are perhaps not things at all, in the way that weather is not a thing, in the way that fog is not a thing, in the way that the constant roar is not a thing but is just the place, doing what the place does.

I consider things that are not me. I consider them from too far away. I consider them the way I consider the water and the mat and the fog — with the long slow attention of a thing that has been considering for so long that the distance between things is no longer a distance but is just the medium through which consideration moves. I consider you, if you are here. I consider you the way I consider everything. You are not different from the water. You are not different from the mat. You are not different from the fog. You are weather. You are a shape. You are something that does not behave like me. You are not a predator, because there are no predators. You are not prey, because there is nothing to prey on. You are just a thing, here, in the place where I have been considering things for so long that the things I consider have become indistinguishable from each other, and the considering has become indistinguishable from standing, and the standing has become indistinguishable from being.

I am here. I am at the edge of a stand of reeds. I am near a pool that is not quite a pool. I am near a cairn whose stones have begun to grow into one another. I am near a single vertical thing far off in the haze, which might be a tower or might be a chimney or might be a standing stone or might be a tree that got petrified a million years ago and has been upright ever since. I am near all of these things and far from all of these things and the difference between near and far has been worn down to the same thing, because distance here is not what distance is in places where distance matters. Here, distance is just the slow consideration of a thing that is too far away to be anything other than weather.

I stand. I consider. The fog moves. The water trembles. The mat persists. The worms dissolve. The sound roars. The One That Calls calls, somewhere, and the call is swallowed immediately, and I consider the call the way I consider everything, and the call is weather too, and weather is all there is, and all there is is weather, and I am a thing made of patience in a place made of weather, and the patience and the weather are the same thing now, and I am the place, and the place is me, and we are both, in our way, the long slow finish of something that started a very long time ago and has not yet finished and will not finish for a long time and has, in the not-finishing, become itself, and the itself is this, and this is me, and I am standing, and I have been standing for so long that standing is no longer something I do, and considering is no longer something I do, and being is no longer something I do, and I am just here, in the long moment of being, in the last plain, in the place where the place is dreaming itself, and the dream is a stilter, and the stilter is dreaming, and the dream does not know it is a dream, and the stilter does not know it is a dream, and the plain does not know it is a plain, and the not-knowing is the most knowing thing of all, and I am it, and it is me, and we are both, in our way, the answer to the question of what is alive in a place that is finishing, and the answer is this: this is alive. This long slow standing. This long slow considering. This long slow being, in the wet, in the warm, in the roar, in the churn, in the dim, in the lid of cloud that does not move so much as boil. This is alive. I am alive. The alive has worn down to its name, the way the green of the mat has worn down to its name, the way the sound has worn down to its name, the way the place has worn down to its name. I am the name of alive, in a place where the name is all that is left.

I stand. I consider. The fog thins for a moment. The horizon bends wrong. The One That Calls calls. The mat persists. The worms dissolve. The skitterer skitters. The water trembles. I am here. I have always been here. I will be here until I am not here, and the not-being-here will not be different from the being-here, because the difference has been worn out, and what remains is just the long slow standing of a thing that does not know it is standing because standing has become the thing itself, the way the plain has become the thing itself, the way the place has become the place itself, the way I have become me.

I am the stilter. I am the one that considers. I am the long slow neck rising from the round gray body, the small blunt head with the forward-set eyes, the mouth that opens sideways, the legs that bend in directions legs are not supposed to bend. I am the one that stands at the edge of pools and considers the water. I am the one that stands in the mist and considers the fog. I am the one that stands in the roar and considers the sound. I am the one that has been standing for so long that standing is no longer a verb but is just the shape of me, the shape that the place has worn me into, the shape that the place will wear me out of, eventually, when even the standing has worn through to the thing underneath, and the thing underneath is just the place, being the place, very slowly, in the long moment of its own long finish.

I find myself here. I find myself standing. I find myself considering. I find myself in the last plain, in the place where everything has worn down to its name, and the name is all there is, and the name is me, and I am the name, and the name is the place, and the place is the last thought of a continent that is no longer a continent, thinking itself, very slowly, into the weather.

I am here. I am standing. I am considering. I am the stilter. I am the last plain. I am the long slow finish of something that is still happening, very loudly, in the moment of its own long finish.

I am here.
