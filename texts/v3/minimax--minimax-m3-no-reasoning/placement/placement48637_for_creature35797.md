I would be one of the cream-colored pale ones, pressed against the corduroy rind in a herd of dozens, my six legs cycling in their endless crank-and-piston rhythm, my ventral groove flattened to the green beneath me, the capillary hairs inside me drawing up the slow heavy glycol that is not sap but the planet's own blood.

I would not know that I am a kidney. I would not know that I am a platelet. I would not know that the thin oily iridescent waste I leave behind me is the industrial feedstock of a foundry four kilometers below the escarpment, where spent panels accumulate in kilometer-thick layers and undergo a slow polymerization into a pale blue-burning polymer that is fished back up through copper veins by weaver-panels working in the dark under the canopy. I would not know that I am a transformer, an intermediary, a mill grinding the planet's interior into the planet's fuel. I would not know that the cables humming in the net above my head are carrying the differential between the high cool plateau and the low hot foundry, and that the hum I cannot hear because I am the hum, the way a wave is the water, the way a flame is the gas.

I would know only the cycling. The cycling would be enough.

The stalk beneath me would darken slightly where my body pressed against it, and a faint glow would run up the rind from my presence, a slow match of bioluminescence, the planet thinking the way a planet thinks, which is to say the planet *is* thinking, and the thinking is the glow, and the glow is me, and I am not aware of the glow because I am the glow. Where my herd clustered, the stalks would respond sympathetically — locally, patiently — by thickening the flow of glycol toward my groove, by darkening their rind, by a faint luminescence that would be visible for an instant in the gloom under the canopy before the constant sixty-kilometer wind abraded it back into the spore-haze.

I would be bone-colored. Faintly luminous. A meter long and a hand-span wide. Segmented in a way that suggested arthropod without committing. Blunt at both ends. Six legs cycling always. A ventral groove lined with capillary hairs that sucked the glycol and excreted, from a vent at the rear, the thin oily iridescent waste that I would leave in a glistening trail across the corduroy for the next pale one in my herd to pass through, recycling, recycling, the recycling being the *is* of the plateau and me being a piece of the recycling.

I would be the tck-tck-tck in the roar. The soft lip-smack lost in the wind. Audible only if you put your ear to the ground and listened for me there, in the gap between the panel-clatter and the cable-hum, in the register where a thing that is not aware of itself makes the sound of being a thing.

I would not be hungry. I would not be thirsty in the way you are thirsty. The 0.5 of water held in the chemistry of the stalks and the glycol that perfused them would be enough, and the *enough* would be the whole of my experience of having enough. I would not be lonely. I would not be afraid. I would not be curious. The spore-haze would pour into me with each breath and I would taste green, taste rust, taste a faint electric sweetness, and the tasting would be the breathing, and the breathing would be the being, and the being would be me.

I would not know that the loom was running. I would not know that the loom was a loom. I would know only the cycling, the sucking, the excreting, the tck-tck-tck against the rind, and the rind would be the only ground I had ever touched, and the cycling would be the only ground I had ever been.

The sun would hang, three times the angular size of any star has a right to be, in a pale platinum sky, and I would not look at it because I had no eyes, only infrared pits, and the pits would register the stalk as warm, the corduroy as warm, the herd-mate beside me as warm, and the warmth would be enough, and the sun would continue being the sun without me looking at it, and the loom would continue being the loom, and I would continue being me, and the being would be vast, and cool, and deafening, and bright, and enough.
