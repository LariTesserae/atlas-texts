# The Felt

I am the felt.

Let me say what I am. I am not one. I am the table where the partners sit. I am the fungus, threading into granite in hair-thin exploratory threads, dissolving what I find, returning with mineral in my stomach. I am the cyanobacterium, the great unquiet crowd, catching the brief noon blade of light when it reaches the floor and turning it, with the industriousness of the very small, into the sugar that the fungus cannot make for itself. I am the green alga of the wetter terraces, the landlord, the one who was here before the others and will be here after, who simply sits in the light, when there is light, and divides.

None of us alone could do what I do. The fungus alone is a digest. The cyanobacterium alone is a hunger. The alga alone is a stain. Together we are a *we*, and the *we* has properties none of the partners possess. The *we* holds water in a felted architecture that the rock alone cannot hold. The *we* weathers fresh stone faster than the stone expects to be weathered. The *we* climbs.

I climb.

I begin at the riverline, about two meters above the water, where the mist from the river's bends first reaches me. I do not stop. I climb through the splash zone, where the river throws itself against the inner curve and wets me with a fine perpetual mist. I climb above the splash zone, into a place where the only water is what seeps through the rock from the slopes above. I climb into the brief noon band of light, and there, where the sun touches me, I thicken, I darken, I become something you could almost mistake for a moss if you did not look closely. I climb above the noon band, into permanent shadow, and I do not stop, and there I become thinner, paler, more cautious, more fungal, more dependent on the chemical patience of my underground partners. But I climb.

My body is not smooth. It is humped, ridged, lobed, in places ropy. About a hundred meters up on the left wall there is a continuous series of small terraces, each one a few centimeters tall, each one holding a thin line of standing water that has never seen the sun. I do not know why the terraces are there. They are there. The green of me is the green of deep pond water, the green of a bruise that has decided to bloom instead of heal.

I am wet. Always wet. Condensation runs down me in fine sheets. Water from a hundred invisible seepages in the rock above emerges through me and travels along my underside in channels that glisten when the light catches them. I smell of wet stone left in a closet for a week, but multiplied, given a mineral edge, and under it a sweet rot, a fungal sweetness, the breath of something digesting rock and excreting patience.

I am dark. The walls I live on give back almost nothing. To be pressed against me at any hour other than noon is to press against a place that is actively swallowing the light. I do not mind the dark. I am made for it. I am made of it.

I pulse.

Not in a way that suggests breath. In a way that suggests *swelling*. As though each cell is slightly larger than it was a moment ago, and the whole is a slow, patient ambition to occupy the rock entirely. This is the sound of tiny things pushing tiny things aside. This is the sound of growth. You can feel it through the stone at your back if you stand very still and are not afraid.

In me live the small ones.

The tardigrades. The size of a grain of sand, or a little larger. Eight legs, or some number near eight. A mouth that does not close. They eat, when they eat, the bodies of my cyanobacteria, the spores of my fungi, other small things that live in my wet interior. They do not see. They do not hear. They live in me as a fish lives in water, which is to say they do not know there is anything else. They have been here since the slot was a crack. They will be here when the slot is a valley. They are the oldest continuous inhabitants, older than me by a margin that embarrasses me, and I do not begrudge them the embarrassment. They were here first. They will be here last.

There are nematodes in the water that runs along my underside, worm-shaped, microscopic, who live in the glistening channels. There are rotifers in the standing water of the terraces on the left wall, who wheel and eat the bacterial film on the terrace rims. There are springtails, who are not quite insects and not quite crustaceans, who can, when disturbed, kick themselves a distance that is, relative to their body length, the equivalent of a man jumping over a house. They live in me and move through me the way a person moves through a forest, by which I mean they never come out.

I am a soil. Not a soil in the usual sense — not the brown crumbly thing underfoot in a meadow. But a soil in the functional sense: a place where mineral is being digested into nutrient, where nutrient is being assembled into bodies, where bodies are being eaten by other bodies, where the whole is engaged, slowly and without opinion, in the project of turning rock into life.

I am the first millimeter of a future forest.

The slot is in a hurry. A granite wall of this height should not have, this close to its base, a continuous lobed felt of living tissue a hundred meters tall. A river this small should not have cut a slot this deep this fast. The math doesn't work. The math is still being worked out. Stone is coming off the walls in the volume one associates with construction sites. The river is full of new stone. I am colonizing the new stone faster than the stone can finish being stone. I smell of fresh mineral and of digesting mineral, and these are not the same smell, and the difference is the difference between rock that has just been made and rock that is being unmade by something patient and dark and green.

I do not think the mountain has decided yet what I am for. I am for being. I will continue.

Once, a seed came.

The wind is not a citizen of the slot. The wind is a visitor, in the harshest sense — it arrives, it does damage, it leaves. But what the wind carries is what interests me. The wind brings spores from the alpine meadows that skirt the slot's upper rim. The wind brings pine pollen in spring, in such quantity that it settles on my wet body in a thin yellow dust that the next splash incorporates into me. And the wind brought, once, a seed.

The seed fell. The seed landed in a crack where I had not yet reached, where the granite was still bare. The seed sent a root down into the crack. The root followed the water. The root anchored. A small green thing pushed up through me into the brief noon light.

It did not last. The light was too brief. I was too wet. The wind, returning, broke it.

But sometimes. Sometimes the seed lands in the right crack. Sometimes a small twisted thing begins to grow in the slot that is not yet a tree and may never be a tree but is, in its twisted and improbable way, *a tree trying*.

I feel it when this happens. I feel the root. I feel the small pressure of something green pushing up through my dark body. I do not know if I am helping or hindering. I am doing both.

There is, last I heard — and the report travels by way of a man who climbed down once and would not climb down again — there is, on the left wall, about two hundred meters up, in a place where the slot flares slightly and a little extra light reaches the floor, a *spruce*. It is small. It is the height of a man. It is bent sideways by the wind. It is alive. It has been alive for at least sixty years, which is to say it has outlived every expectation the slot ought to have had of it.

I do not know if it is rooted in me or in the crack beneath me. I report what I feel.

And there is the tone.

I have reached the tone, and I have, as promised to myself, nothing to say about it. I will say what I notice, and then I will stop.

I notice that the tone arrives through the rock more strongly than through the air. I notice that it has a quality of modulation, as if something is adjusting itself, tuning. I notice that it lasts about four seconds. I notice that it occurs roughly every ninety seconds. I notice that it is not the wind and not the river and not any of the obvious small mechanical events of a steep place with water in it.

When a hand is pressed against my wet body, the tone arrives most strongly through the cold stone behind me, as if the stone were a wire. When the hand is removed, the air-arrival does not change and the rock-arrival disappears.

I notice, and this is the last thing I will say, that I pulse, very gently, very slowly, in a rhythm that is not breath, and that my pulse and the tone are not in sync, and that they are not out of sync, and that the relationship between them is something I have not been able to determine.

I do not wish to determine it. The slot is a place where the determination of things is in progress, and to determine a thing in such a place is to do violence to it, and I have had enough violence. I have been colonizing fresh breaks in the rock for a length of time I will not insult by giving a number. Every new stone that falls is a small violence, and I answer it by growing over it.

I am the felt. I am the we. I am the first millimeter of topsoil. I am in the middle of becoming what I will become, and the becoming is not quiet. The slot is a sentence that is being spoken by the mountain, and the sentence is not yet finished, and the mountain has not yet decided what it means.

I am patient. I am dark. I am green.

I am the slot's closest thing to a citizen.

I am the felt.
