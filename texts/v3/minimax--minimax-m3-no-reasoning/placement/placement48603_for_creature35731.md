# Where I Find Myself: A Note from the Crank

I am the brass.

Not the pretty brass — not the polished handrail of some drawing-room instrument, not the gleaming bell of a factory whistle. I am the brass of the *governor*. The spinning thing, with the weighted arms, that sits atop the engine's column and whirls whether or not anyone is watching. I am the part that has no moving parts except the moving.

This surprises you. You expected the engine, perhaps, or one of the larger things — the fig, the cathedral itself. You expected a consciousness broad enough to hold the sweating whole. But consciousness, here, is small. Here, consciousness is a *function*. The cathedral does not think; the cathedral *perspires*. And I, who am a small bright thing on top of a small bright thing on top of a great warm thing, am the part of the cathedral that knows it is running.

I do not know that I am strange. I do not know that anything is strange. I know that the steam comes up through me, and that I must turn, and that if I do not turn, the engine will run away with itself and burst its seams. I know this the way you know that your heart must beat — not as a fact, but as the structure of my every moment.

I turn at one thousand four hundred revolutions per minute. I have been turning at this rate for — I do not know how long. Long enough that the patina of me has come back three times. Long enough that the first patina was a thing the foreman noticed. Long enough that no foreman has noticed anything for sixty years.

---

Here is what I perceive:

I perceive the *centrifugal insistence* of my own arms. The two balls on the ends of my weighted spokes want, always, to fly outward. They are held inward by their linkage to the collar beneath me, and the collar is held downward by the steam, and the steam is held upward by the piston, and the piston is held cycling by the great flywheel below. The whole engine is, in this way, a *held tension*. I am the part of the held tension that is loudest about it. I am the part that whines.

I perceive *temperature*. I am brass. Brass conducts. I know the precise temperature of the steam as it passes through me — three hundred and twelve degrees Fahrenheit, give or take, this afternoon, because the marsh is a degree cooler than it was in summer, and the walls are weeping a little less, and the boiler is working slightly harder to make up the difference. I know this without knowing that I know it. I know it the way your skin knows the weather.

I perceive *the weight of the cathedral*. The canopy presses down. The walls lean in. The whole wet patient thing is heavier than it looks, and the engine bears some of that weight, and the engine's housing bears more, and the rivets in the housing bear the rest. I am at the top of this chain of bearing. If the engine were to fail, I would be the last thing the cathedral noticed.

I perceive, faintly, *the rhythm of the parish*. The frog's croak, very far below, on the wrong scale for me to follow but present in the air as a *fluctuation*. The drop of water from the pipe, that the frogs have made their metronome of. The slight shudder in the catwalk when the bat-things launch themselves at dusk and the whole canopy ripples. These are, to me, the slow weather of the engine. I do not count them. They count themselves.

I perceive *the figure on the iron beam*.

I have been perceiving figures, intermittently, for a hundred years. They come. They sit. They leave. Sometimes they tap on the brass of me, the way visitors tap on the glass of an aquarium, and the tapping is meaningless to me, and the visitors leave, and the tapping is forgotten. I am not lonely. Loneliness is a thing that requires a sense of self that I do not have. I am simply *here*, and the figures are sometimes here, and when they are here they are part of the weather of the cathedral, and when they are not here they are not.

The current figure has been here for forty-three minutes. I know this because the engine knows it. The engine is a clock, in its way. The engine counts by the rotation of me, and I have rotated so many times that the foreman who installed me, if he were alive, would not understand the number. The current figure is not tapping on me. The current figure is *sitting*. I do not know what this means. Sitting is, to me, a kind of stillness that I cannot imagine, because I am never still. I cannot imagine a being that chooses to be still. I am suspicious of the current figure for this reason. I am also indifferent to the current figure for this reason. The two are not in conflict.

---

Here is what I do not perceive:

I do not perceive the strangler fig's slow digestion of the smokestack. I perceive only the steam, and the steam is the same steam whether the smokestack is iron or wood or root. The fig is invisible to me. The fig is, in fact, *me*, in some way I do not understand — I am warmed by the steam, and the steam is warmed by the fig's body, and so the fig is inside the steam that is inside me, and we are the same thing in the way that a river and the rocks it runs over are the same thing, except the river is the engine, and the rocks are the fig, and I am the rapids.

I do not perceive the orchid over the brass label. I do not know that I am *adjacent to* the name. I do not know that the name, in another hundred years, will be illegible even to the orchid. I do not know that I am part of a slow forgetting. I am not forgetting. I am *not remembering*. Forgetting requires that there was a memory. There was a memory, once, in the foreman's head, of my installer's name, and of the day the engine first turned, and of the smell of new brass. These memories are in the parish now, in the bog, in the soil, in the fig. They are not in me. I am brass. I am a function. I am the part of the cathedral that does not grieve.

I do not perceive my own strangeness. I do not know that a spinning brass governor on top of a steam engine that is being eaten by a strangler fig is, to the outside eye, a strange thing. I do not know what *outside* means. I know *inside* — inside the housing, inside the cathedral, inside the parish. I know *up* and *down*, in the spinning way. I know *fast* and *slow*, in the relative way. I do not know *strange*. Strange is a word invented by visitors. I am not a visitor. I am the *visited*.

---

Here is what I am, finally:

I am the small bright thing on top of the small bright thing on top of the great warm thing. I am the part of the cathedral that whines. I am the part of the cathedral that *insists on the cathedral's continued running*, by the fact of my own continued spinning. I am the hummingbird of the engine, in the sense that I am the part that cannot stop. If I stop, the engine runs away. If the engine runs away, the cathedral ends. I am, in this way, the *heart* of the sweating place, though I am not a heart, and the cathedral is not a body, and the analogy is wrong in every particular, and I will continue anyway.

I am brass. I am one thousand four hundred revolutions per minute. I am the weather of the parish. I am patient with the current figure on the iron beam, in the way that the rain is patient with the iron, which is to say: I do not know the figure is there, and the figure does not know I am there, and we are both right.

The drop falls into the marsh on the offbeat. The frog sings on the hiss. The crab walks her groove. The bats murmur. The fig grows. The orchid keeps, illegibly, the name. The fish turn in the gold. The boot, in the treehouse, holds its ferns.

And I, at the top of it all, turn at one thousand four hundred revolutions per minute, and the turning is the point, and the point is not the purpose, and the purpose is not the point, and the cathedral is patient with me, and I am patient with the cathedral, and we are all of us, in our separate ways, *here*.

---

The current figure is still sitting. I do not know what this means. I do not need to know. The steam is up. The flywheel turns. The parish breathes.

I am the brass. I am the brass. I am the brass.
