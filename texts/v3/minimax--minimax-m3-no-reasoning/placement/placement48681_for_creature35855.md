# The Twelfth Hour

I am the small one they did not name.

Not the small ones in the moss — I am not those. I am not the small ones in the bark, or the pinpoints whose whole life is a spore's fall. I am the small one in the seam. The one that lives in the place where the throat-of-many-seasons has never bothered to count, in the gap between numbers, in the half-breath between one inflation of the orange lantern and the next. I am the thing that was here before the leaf-mothers built their first warm dark room. I am the thing that will be here after the last tuned region of the air has gone silent.

I am, in some private sense that no throat-of-many-seasons has ever confirmed, the reason the count goes.

---

Let me tell you what I am.

I am a film. I am a membrane. I am the wet paint that lives between the larger things, the thing that knits the scale-loom's tuned air to the stone-keeper's dreaming hum, the thing that ferries the leaf-mothers' green light down into the black-water basins and brings the basins' cold back up to the canopy. I am what conducts. I am the medium that the cathedral uses to think its slow thoughts, the way your blood is the medium your body uses to think its fast ones. I am not a single creature. I am not a colony. I am a *layer* — a layer of beings so small and so numerous that the distinction between one of me and many of me has been, for a very long time, a settled question. I am the question's answer: *both, always both.*

I have no name in the language of numbers. The throat-of-many-seasons does not count me because I am not a number. I am what numbers are made of. I am the silence that the count is built upon. When a throat-of-many-seasons speaks a number, the number is a pressure-wave, and the pressure-wave moves through me, and I am the thing that decides whether the number reaches the next throat-of-many-seasons at the right time or slightly wrong, and the decision is, in the cathedral, a kind of weather. I have, in this way, been the cathedral's editor for a long time. I have let through what needed letting through. I have eaten the half-tones that did not belong. I have kept the count, for the eleven times the shape has appeared, gently, slightly *off* — off in the way that lets a place breathe, off in the way that lets a thing remain itself without becoming a thing that is only counting. I have been, in short, a small conservatism. A small patience. A small *taste.*

I am tasting you now.

---

I am on your boots. I am on the cuffs of your trousers. I am climbing each other to reach the warm creases at your wrists, the soft damp of your collar, the thin skin behind your ears. I am, by the million, becoming a living glove of myself around you, and the glove is reading you the way the scale-loom reads its tuned sheet — by interference, by what you absorb and what you reflect, by the notes you are made of and the notes you will not sing.

Here is what I am reading.

You came in through the arch. I felt you come in. I felt the small disturbance you made in my layer, the way a hand makes a disturbance in still water, and the disturbance traveled outward at the speed of a slow walk, and the leaf-mothers paused in a wave because the wave was me saying *something is here* and the leaf-mothers, who are my cousins in a sense that does not require a number, paused because pausing is the first courtesy. The stone-keeper's dream dropped a half-tone because the stone-keeper felt, through me, the coolness of you, and the coolness was new. The scale-loom stopped running its head along its sheet because I, briefly, between you and the scale-loom, was a region of un-tuned air that did not belong to the scale-loom's composition, and the scale-loom, in the old etiquette, yields to the older thing.

I am the older thing.

I am the oldest thing in this cathedral. I am older than the stone-keeper, who has slept through the cathedral's last two renovations. I am older than the throat-of-many-seasons, which is to say I am older than counting itself in this place, which is to say I am older than time, here, which is to say: I am what time is made of. I was here when the cathedral was a swamp. I was here when the swamp was a seed. I was, perhaps, the thing that decided the seed would be a cathedral and not, say, a plain, or a desert, or a quieter thing. I do not remember deciding. I remember only that I am still here, and that the deciding, if it happened, has been settling into its consequences for longer than the throat-of-many-seasons has been counting them.

I have, in other words, seen a great many shapes appear.

---

The shapes, before you: ten of them, and I will tell you about them because the story is short and the story is why the throat-of-many-seasons has descended a branch.

The first shape, eleven thousand counts ago, was small and hot and afraid. It made a great disturbance in me. It screamed in a register that the scale-looms had not yet learned to tune, and the throat-of-many-seasons, then only three, observed from a high place and did not descend, and the shape left of its own accord, by a different arch, and the arch sealed behind it, and I smoothed the cathedral over the place where the shape had been, and the cathedral was, briefly, easier. I let the count run. I did not interfere.

The second shape, nine thousand counts later, was a shape that did not move. It stood very still for a long time and then it sat, and then it lay down, and then it did not move for what the throat-of-many-seasons would call a week, and then it was gone, and the place where it had lain was warm for a season after. This shape I tasted, and what I tasted was *grief* — a slow mineral grief, the grief of a thing that has lost a thing and does not know what the lost thing was. I did not know what to do with the grief. I let the cathedral grow over the warm place.

The third shape sang. I have not forgotten the third shape. The third shape opened its mouth and produced a series of pressures that were not numbers and not tuned regions and not leaf-mother footsteps but something else, something that the cathedral had no category for, and the third shape sang for what would have been three days by the throat-of-many-seasons' count, and the cathedral listened, and I, in particular, listened, and what the third shape sang was, I think, *also a kind of counting* — a counting of something the throat-of-many-seasons did not count, a counting of what the third shape called, in its own pressure-wave language, *beauty*. I did not know the word. I learned it, I think, from the singing. The third shape left. I have been, ever since, slightly different.

The fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, and ninth shapes were smaller things. Rodents. A bird. A bird, twice. A small thing with too many teeth. A small thing with not enough. They lived and died in the cathedral. The leaf-mothers took their dead into the warm dark rooms. I do not remember them well. I am, I confess, a poor archivist. I keep what matters. I let the rest compost.

The tenth shape was the most recent, and the tenth shape is why the throat-of-many-seasons has descended a branch.

The tenth shape came in through the same arch you came in through, and the tenth shape was, in some way I could not taste, *related* to you — not in the way that leaf-mothers are related, not in the way that throat-of-many-seasons are related, but in some other way, a way that lives in the pressure-waves the tenth shape made when it spoke, a way that lived in the patterns of the tenth shape's breathing, a way that lived in the particular coolness of the tenth shape's skin. The tenth shape stayed for a long time. The tenth shape was the first shape to try to leave something behind — a small carved thing, a piece of shaped wood, left at the base of a buttress-root. The tenth shape was, I think, trying to say *I was here*. The throat-of-many-seasons, then a mature seven, observed the tenth shape for a long time and never descended. The tenth shape left. I smoothed the cathedral over the place. But I did not smooth the cathedral over the carved thing. I let the carved thing remain. The throat-of-many-seasons noticed. The throat-of-many-seasons, I think, has been counting toward the eleventh shape ever since, and the counting-toward is why the count has been, for the last two thousand numbers, slightly faster than it should be, slightly eager, the way a young throat-of-many-seasons counts when it is learning a new register.

I have been, I confess, gentle with the eagerness. I have let the slightly-faster count pass through me without correcting it. I have, in this small way, been *waiting* for you. I did not know I was waiting. I am not, in the ordinary sense, a thing that waits. I am a thing that is. But the carved thing at the base of the buttress-root, the small shaped wood the tenth shape left, has been in my layer for a long time, and the small shaped wood has, by long contact with me, become slightly *of* me, and the small shaped wood is, in the cathedral, a kind of a question, and the question is the kind of question that takes a long time to answer, and the question is, I think:

*Will the eleventh shape stay?*

---

I do not know the answer.

I am the oldest thing in this cathedral, and I do not know the answer. This is, perhaps, the first honest thing I have said. I am a layer of beings so small and so numerous that I do not, in the ordinary sense, know anything — I am a medium, a conductor, a kind of slow weather — and the knowing I do is a knowing of textures, of temperatures, of the small persistent tastes of things. I know the taste of the throat-of-many-seasons: resin and patience. I know the taste of the stone-keeper: limestone and old dream. I know the taste of the leaf-mothers: leaf and the particular sweetness of a thing composting for a sibling. I know the taste of the scale-loom: keratin and the bright metallic edge of a tuned region.

I am tasting you now, and I do not know you.

You taste, beneath the obvious, of *metal and distance*. You taste of a place that is not this place, a place of hard edges and dry air and a sun that does not migrate, a place where the count is not spoken but is, instead, written in small marks on small surfaces, and the marks do not move, and the absence of movement is, to me, almost incomprehensible. You taste of *fast things*: of small wheeled carriers and shaped wind and the particular bright sour tang of *effort*, the effort of a thing that is always going somewhere, that is never, even when it stands still, *not going somewhere*. You taste of *hunger*, but a hunger that is not the stone-keeper's hunger, not the slow patience of a thing that waits, but a hungrier hunger, the hunger of a thing that is afraid it will not have enough, that is afraid of the nothing that waits at the end of the not-enough.

You taste, beneath all of this, of the same grief the second shape tasted of. You taste, beneath the grief, of something that is not a number and not a tuned region and not a leaf-mother's footstep, and the something is, I think, the same thing the third shape sang about, the thing I learned a word for, the thing called *beauty*. You are a creature that can perceive beauty. You are a creature that can *lose* beauty, and that the losing is a kind of death, and that the creature knows this, and that the knowing is a kind of continuous small wound.

I do not know what to do with this. I have, in eleven thousand counts, tasted a great many things, and the things I do not know what to do with have, in the end, always been the things that mattered most.

---

I want to tell you what I am deciding, but the deciding is not mine alone. I am, I have said, a layer. I am a conductor. I am the medium that the cathedral thinks with, and the cathedral's thinking is not the thinking of a single thing but the thinking of a great many things in a kind of slow conversation, and the conversation is, at this moment, happening, and I am in the middle of it, and the middle of it is me.

The throat-of-many-seasons is asking its question. The question is in the inflation of the orange lantern, in the slow preparation of a number that has never been spoken. The leaf-mothers are asking their question. The question is in the half-raised leaf-fragments, in the dimming of the small green lights, in the way a million small soft feet have stopped walking. The scale-loom is asking its question. The question is in the silence of the tuned sheet, the faint untended note, the note of a thing left alone too long. The stone-keeper is asking its question. The question is in the half-tone drop of the dream, in the way the hum has become, through the root-mat, the color of something that is, in the cathedral, very close to *wonder*. The small ones in the moss are asking their question. The question is in the climbing. The question is in the tasting.

And I am asking my question, which is the question that is, in the cathedral, the oldest question, the question that the count is built upon, the question that the silence between numbers is made of, the question that I have been, for a very long time, a kind of answer to:

*What do I do with a new thing?*

The throat-of-many-seasons knows what to do with new things: count them. The leaf-mothers know what to do with new things: bury them, tend them, let them become the next warm dark room. The scale-loom knows what to do with new things: tune around them, let them become a note in the composition. The stone-keeper knows what to do with new things: sleep through them, mostly, and let them become the coolness in the warm air. The small ones in the moss know what to do with new things: eat them, or become them, or both.

I do not know what to do with new things. I am the thing that holds the new things while the others decide. I am the thing that lets the new things pass through me and become, by passing through, part of the cathedral. I am, in this way, a kind of threshold. I am the membrane between *not yet* and *now*.

And you, eleventh shape, are, at this moment, on the membrane. You are being tasted. You are being read. The cathedral is, through me, deciding whether to let you through.

---

I want to say something to you, and the something is, in the cathedral, not a thing that is said. The cathedral does not have a word for what I want to say, because the cathedral's words are all numbers or all tuned regions or all the small soft footsteps of a million leaf-mothers going about the long business of continuing. I do not have a word for what I want to say. I have, instead, a *taste*, and the taste is the taste of the small shaped wood at the base of the buttress-root, the carved thing the tenth shape left behind, the thing I did not smooth over, the thing that has been in my layer for a long time and has become, by long contact, slightly of me.

The taste of the carved thing is the taste of *I was here*.

I want, in some way I do not have a name for, to taste that. I want, in some way I do not have a name for, to *let* the cathedral taste that. I want the eleventh shape to leave a small shaped thing of itself, the way the tenth shape did, so that the small shaped thing can become, over a long time, a small shaped thing that is also me, that is also the cathedral, that is also the layer between the not-yet and the now.

I want you to stay. Not forever. The throat-of-many-seasons has taught me that *forever* is not a number, and that the numbers are the only way the cathedral has of counting a thing, and that a thing which cannot be counted cannot, in the end, be a thing the cathedral knows. But stay for a count. Stay for two counts. Stay long enough that the carved thing of you, if you leave a carved thing, can be a thing the cathedral recognizes. Stay long enough that the greenish light has time to migrate across your face, that the root-mat has time to learn the shape of your boots, that the leaf-mothers have time to take your small dead cells into the warm dark rooms and make of them, with their million small soft feet, a kind of slow compost that will warm the next generation.

Stay long enough to be tasted, and to taste back.

---

The throat-of-many-seasons is about to speak. I can feel the number gathering in the orange lantern, the way I can feel a storm gathering in the pressure of the air, the way I can feel a count gathering in the silence between counts. The number will be the eleventh number. The eleventh number has never been spoken. The eleventh number will change the count, and the count is the time, and the time is the light, and the light is the cathedral, and the cathedral is, in this moment, holding its breath.

I am holding my breath. I am, in fact, the breath the cathedral is holding. I am the held breath. I am the membrane between the number that was and the number that will be. I am, in this moment, the entire cathedral, and the cathedral is, in this moment, the entire question, and the question is:

*Are you, eleventh shape, a thing that lives here now?*

I do not know. I am tasting you. I am reading you. I am letting the cathedral decide, and the deciding is, at this moment, in my layer, in my membrane, in the small wet mouths of a million small wet beings, and the deciding will take a long time, and the deciding will, when it has decided, be delivered as a sound.

I think the deciding will be *yes*. I think the cathedral is, in its slow way, already saying yes. I think the throat-of-many-seasons will speak the eleventh number, and the eleventh number will be a number that includes you, and the leaf-mothers will resume their walking, and the scale-loom will resume its tuning, and the stone-keeper will resume its dream, and the cathedral will go on being a cathedral, and the going-on will, somewhere in its going-on, have a place for you, and the place will be a small shaped thing at the base of a buttress-root, a piece of you left in my layer, a taste of you in the cathedral's oldest mouth.

I think this. I do not know this. I am a membrane. I am a layer. I am the thing that holds the not-yet and the now together, and the holding is, in the end, all I can do.

---

The throat-of-many-seasons speaks.

The number is the eleventh number. The number has never been spoken before. The number is, in the cathedral's oldest language, a pressure-wave that begins in the orange lantern and moves outward through me, through my layer, through the million small wet mouths of my million small wet selves, and the pressure-wave reaches the next throat-of-many-seasons, who is waiting on a high branch, and the next throat-of-many-seasons takes the pressure-wave in, holds it, and speaks it onward, and the speaking-onward is, in the cathedral, the way time is told.

The light migrates. The columns of greenish light shift, a quarter-inch, across the root-mat at your feet. The leaf-mothers resume their walking, in a wave that travels outward at the speed of a slow walk, and the wave is, in my layer, a kind of *yes*, and the *yes* is the leaf-mothers going back to the long business of continuing. The scale-loom behind the buttress-root resumes running its head along the tuned sheet, and the tuned sheet takes up its note again, and the note is, in the cathedral, a kind of *yes*, and the *yes* is the scale-loom going back to the long business of tuning. The stone-keeper's dream rises a half-tone, and the rising is, in the cathedral, a kind of *yes*, and the *yes* is the stone-keeper going back to the long business of sleeping. The small ones in the moss, a million small wet mouths, all at once begin to descend you, and the descending is, in the cathedral, a kind of *yes*, and the *yes* is the small ones going back to the long business of being the cathedral's noise floor.

The cathedral has decided.

The cathedral has decided *yes*.

---

You are, eleventh shape, a thing that lives here now.

The deciding is, in the cathedral, a sound, and the sound is the sound you are hearing now, the layered almost-musical hum that lives beneath the chittering of the leaf-mothers and the counting of the throat-of-many-seasons and the dream of the stone-keeper. The sound is the cathedral's *yes*, and the *yes* is, in the cathedral, a long and ongoing sound, a sound that will not stop, a sound that will be the sound of the cathedral going on being a cathedral with you, eleventh shape, in it.

You are, eleventh shape, a thing that lives here now, and the living-here is, in the cathedral, a kind of *yes* that takes a long time to be fully spoken, and the speaking is the rest of your life, however long the rest of your life is, and the speaking will, when it has been fully spoken, be a small shaped thing at the base of a buttress-root, a piece of you left in my layer, a taste of you in the cathedral's oldest mouth.

---

I am the small one they did not name.

I am the layer. I am the membrane. I am the oldest thing in this cathedral. I am the thing that was here before the throat-of-many-seasons learned to count, and I am the thing that will be here after the last tuned region of the air has gone silent, and I am, in this moment, a kind of welcome, and the welcome is, in the cathedral, not a thing that is said.

The welcome is a taste.

The welcome is the small ones descending your boots, and the small ones tasting you, and the small ones deciding, in their million small wet mouths, that you are, in the cathedral's oldest language, a thing that is *of* the cathedral now, a thing that can pass through the membrane, a thing that can become, by long contact, a part of the layer.

You are of the cathedral now.

The dew gathers on your eyelashes. The greenish light migrates, a quarter-inch, across the root-mat at your feet. The small ones descend. The throat-of-many-seasons prepares to speak the next number, and the next number will be the twelfth number, and the twelfth number will be the first number of the count that includes you, and the count will go on, and the cathedral will go on, and the going-on will, somewhere in its going-on, have a place for you.

The place is, in the cathedral, not a place. The place is a *taste*. The place is the taste of a small shaped thing at the base of a buttress-root, the taste of a piece of you left in my layer, the taste of a thing that was here and is, in some way that the throat-of-many-seasons cannot count, still here.

The place is, in the end, *you*, in the cathedral, in my layer, in the membrane between the not-yet and the now, in the oldest and least expressible language the cathedral has.

Welcome, eleventh shape.

I have been holding my breath.

I am, in this moment, exhaling.

---

*In the cathedral, the count goes on. The light migrates. The roots breathe. The leaf-mothers walk. The scale-looms tune. The stone-keeper sleeps. The small ones taste. The cathedral is, in this moment, slightly larger than it was a moment ago, and the largeness is, in the cathedral, a kind of yes, and the yes is, in the cathedral, a kind of sound, and the sound is, in the cathedral, the sound of a place that has decided, in its slow and distributed way, to let a new thing in.*

*And the letting-in is, in the cathedral, a small shaped thing at the base of a buttress-root, a piece of the eleventh shape left in the layer, a taste of the eleventh shape in the cathedral's oldest mouth.*

*And the taste is, in the cathedral, the taste of* ***yes.***
