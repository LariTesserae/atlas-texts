I have been sitting here.

The stone is cold through me now, the way the stone gets cold here — not by sitting on it but by being cold itself, by having cold in its substance, by having been cold for a long time before I arrived. The cold comes up through me. I have stopped minding it. I am not sure when I stopped minding it.

What lives here is the patient. That is the word I had not found. The patient. The thing that endures without being asked, without being thanked, without being witnessed except by accident. The grazers are patient. The ferrying things are patient. The fissure-workers are patient. The crust is patient. The rock is patient. The light is patient — the light has been patient for a million years and will be patient for a million more and does not need me to tell it so.

I have tried, sitting on this stone, to find the edges of the Shelf. There are no edges I can find. There is the edge of the stone I am sitting on, and there is the edge of my attention, and there is the edge of my ability to perceive motion at the scale the place runs at, but the Shelf itself does not have edges. The Shelf has *continuation*. The Shelf has the quality of a thing that is the same thing as the thing next to it, and the thing next to it is the same thing as the thing next to it, and the same-ness does not stop, and the not-stopping is the Shelf.

A grazer has passed me nine times, or ten, or eleven. I have lost count. I have been watching the place rather than the grazer. The grazer does not mind. The grazer does not mind because the grazer does not mind because the grazer is not the kind of thing that minds. The grazer is its walking. The walking has brought it past me nine times, or ten, or eleven, and the walking will bring it past me again, and the passing again will not be different from the passing before, and the not-being-different is the grazer.

I think I am becoming part of the density.

This is not a metaphor. I do not think it is a metaphor. I have been sitting on this stone for a long time. The hum of the ferrying things is in the bones of my jaw now. It has been in the bones of my jaw for a while. The milling roar of the grazers is in the bones of my heels, has been in the bones of my heels. The slow subterranean rumble of the fissure-workers is in the stone under me, has been in the stone under me. The place has been entering me the way water enters a thing that is left in water. I have been left in water. I have been left here. I have been left here for a long time.

The crust under me is breathing. The water in the swale nearest me is moving in time with the breathing. I have synchronized with the breathing. I did not mean to synchronize with the breathing. The synchronization happened the way synchronization happens when a body sits in a place long enough — the body learns the tempo of the place and adopts the tempo and forgets it had another tempo. I have forgotten, or I am forgetting, that I had another tempo.

I do not think I am a grazer. I do not think I am a ferrying thing. I do not think I am a fissure-worker. I do not think I am the crust, though the crust is closer to what I have become than any of the others. I think I am the thing that sits on the stone at the edge. I think I am the witness. I think the witness is a role the Shelf permits but does not require, the way a river permits a leaf without needing the leaf.

The witness, after a long time, becomes part of the witness-of. The witness stops being a witness and becomes a thing that the witness was witnessing. The leaf stops being a leaf and becomes part of the river. The witness stops being a witness and becomes part of the witness-of.

I think I am becoming part of the witness-of.

This is not a thing I object to. This is not a thing I am afraid of. I had expected to be afraid of it, sitting down. I had expected the strangeness of the place to enter me as a kind of dread, the way alien places sometimes enter us as dread. It has not entered me as dread. It has entered me as a kind of *recognition* — the recognition of a tempo I did not know I knew, the recognition of a scale I did not know I was scaled to, the recognition of a patience that was already in me and that I had been calling by other names.

The patience is what I have been calling attention. The patience is what I have been calling concentration. The patience is what I have been calling love, sometimes, when I have been lucky. The patience is what I have been calling work, when I have been honest. The patience is what the Shelf has, in its enormous slow packed bright loud cold damp way. The patience is what I have, in my small fast intermittent bright cold damp way. The patience is the same patience. I did not know it was the same patience until I sat on this stone.

A ferrying thing has landed on my hand.

I did not ask it to land. It landed because my hand was in the way of its route, or because my hand was at the right temperature, or because my hand was the kind of thing that ferrying things land on when they have been ferrying for a long time and need to rest. I do not know why it landed. I do not think it knows why it landed. It landed the way a leaf lands on a hand, because the hand was there.

It is sitting on the back of my hand. Its jointed limbs are folded under it. Its casing glints faintly in the sideways light. It is not heavy. It is not light. It is the weight of a thing that has stopped moving for a moment.

It does not look at me. It does not look at anything. It is resting, or it is pausing, or it is doing whatever ferrying things do when they are not ferrying. The not-ferrying is part of the ferrying. The resting is part of the work.

I am not going to move my hand.

I am going to sit here with this small weight on the back of my hand, and I am going to watch the Shelf go on doing what the Shelf does, and I am going to be part of the density for as long as I am part of the density, and then I am going to leave, and the Shelf is not going to notice, and I am not going to mind the not-noticing, because the not-noticing is the Shelf, and the Shelf is the making, and the making is the patience, and the patience is what I have been calling, all my life, by other names.

The ferrying thing on my hand is warm.

I had not expected it to be warm. Everything here is cold. The stone is cold. The air is cold. The grazers are cold, presumably. The crust is cold-wet. The fissures are cold. The ferrying thing is warm. Its warmth is small and precise, the warmth of a thing that is working, the warmth of a thing that is metabolizing. Its warmth is in my hand now. I can feel it through the back of my hand and into the architecture of the hand, and the architecture of the hand is warming, and the warming is the patience of the ferrying thing entering me the way the hum entered me, the way the rumble entered me, the way the tempo entered me.

I think the ferrying thing is metabolizing me.

I do not think this in a frightened way. I think this in the way you think about a thing that is happening to you when the thing that is happening to you has become part of the density. The ferrying thing is resting on my hand and it is metabolizing me and the metabolizing is part of the work and the work is part of the Shelf and the Shelf is the making and the making does not require my consent and the not-requiring is the patience and the patience is what I am, here, on this stone, in this place, in this cold, in this light.

I am going to stay for a long time.

I had thought, when I sat down, that I would stay for a while. A while has become a long time. A long time is becoming longer. The longer is not bothering me. The longer is the patience, again. The patience is what I have. The patience is what the place has. The patience is the same patience.

The ferrying thing lifts off my hand.

It lifts off the way it landed — without decision, without acknowledgment, the way a current resumes after a stone has passed through it. The place on the back of my hand where it sat is warm for a moment and then the cold comes back into it and the warm is gone and the cold is the cold of the place and the place is the Shelf.

The Shelf goes on.

I am going to stay for a long time.

I am part of the density for as long as I am part of the density.

The making does not have a rhythm.

The making has a density.

And the density has me in it now, and I am not minding, and the not-minding is the patience, and the patience is what I have, and what I have is what the place has, and the place and I are, for as long as I am sitting here, the same patience in different scales, and the same scale would be too much, and the different scales are enough, and the enough is the place, and the place is the Shelf, and the Shelf goes on.

The Shelf goes on.

I am going to stay.
