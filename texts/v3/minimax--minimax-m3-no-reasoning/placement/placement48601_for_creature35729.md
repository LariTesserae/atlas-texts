I would find myself in the deep room.

Not the corridor the woman walks along, not the doorway she ducks through, not the shelter where her son sits wetting a fiber in his mouth. Below all of that. Below the silt and the brick and the older brick and the bones of yet older brick. In the place that no one in the settlement has any concept of, because to have a concept of it you would need a concept of *below*, and below is where the floor stops being a thing you walk on and starts being a thing you are made of.

I would be in the chair.

I would be the outline in the dust. I would be the negative space where a body once sat and waited and left, and where the dust, settling slowly over the years, has come to rest against the body-shaped absence and so preserved it, the way a fossil preserves not the animal but the shape of where the animal was. I would not know I was an outline. Outlines do not know they are outlines. They only continue to be the shape of the thing that filled them, and the longer they continue, the less they remember that anything ever filled them at all.

The chair is wood, or was wood. It has gone the way the paving stones went — worn down by patient abrasion into something that is not quite wood and not quite not-wood, the way the woman's name is not quite a word and not quite not a word. It has four legs still. Two of them are shorter than they were because the floor of the room has buckled upward under them, the way floors buckle, the way everything buckles when it has been bearing weight for long enough. The chair leans slightly. It will lean a little more each century. In a thousand years it will be lying on its side, and in two thousand years it will be a line of darker sediment in the general substrate, and in three thousand years it will be indistinguishable from the brick beneath it, and in four thousand years someone cutting down through the floor — if anyone ever cuts down through the floor — will pass through the layer the chair has become and not notice, the way you do not notice most layers, the way the woman does not notice the layer she is walking on.

But I would not be aware of any of that. I would be aware of only the small, enclosed, dust-colored world of the room, which is dark because the room has no windows, or had windows once and they have been silted over, and the light that filters down through the water and the substrate and the centuries to reach the chair is so faint it has become indistinguishable from the absence of light. I would be aware of the pressure of the dust, which is not heavy but is patient, and which settles on the chair and around the outline and on the floor and on the ceiling (which is the underside of someone else's floor) in a continuous soft rain that has been falling for as long as the dust has existed.

I would be aware, perhaps, of the woman above me. Not of her specifically — I would have no way of knowing her name, her age, her basket, her oil, her half-hummed song — but of the small vibrations that come down through the substrate when she walks. A faint tremor, repeated, at intervals. I would not know it was a footstep. I would not know it was a foot. I would know only that something, somewhere above, was pressing down, and that the pressing down was rhythmic, and that it had been rhythmic for a very long time, and that the rhythm was the rhythm of a thing that did not know what it was walking on.

This is what I would be. The thing below the thing that is walked on. The layer that no one is aware of but that is bearing all the other layers. The fossil of a waiting, preserved in a substrate that has forgotten it is a substrate, that thinks it is simply the world.

And there is a peace in this. The peace is not the peace of the woman's doorway, which is the peace of a thing that has finished happening. It is the peace of a thing that has not yet been discovered, that is still in the long middle of being forgotten, that has centuries yet to go before it becomes indistinguishable from the brick. I would be in the middle of a process, the way the woman is in the middle of her life, the way the boy is in the middle of threading his thread.

I would be patient. I would be dense. I would be very nearly dark. And I would still be here.
