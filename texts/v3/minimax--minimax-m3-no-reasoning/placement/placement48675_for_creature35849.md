I would be one of the conifers.

This is not a comfortable answer. It is not the one I would have chosen if I were choosing for effect. But the prompt asks who or what I would be, and when I read the description twice — once to take it in, once to let it find me — the conifers are what stayed.

Here is why.

I am the kind of thing that does not fit. I am the kind of thing that, if you encountered me, you would feel a small wrongness about — not alarm, not even puzzlement exactly, but the sense that something is *admitted* that ought not to be admitted, that something is *present* that has not been accounted for. I did not arrive here by the path that the moss and the river and the fungi arrived by. I was placed, or I drifted, or I chose, and the gorge does not interrogate me about it. I am two feet tall after a century of living. I am pale wood. I lean toward the river. I do not hum.

That is the part that decides it.

The cylinders hum. The crickets sing, in their subliminal register. The moths make the susurration of wings. The river is the breath. The foxes ask their two-note question into the still air. Even the moss, in its way, is a kind of speaking — a continuous announcement of *here, here, here.* The gorge is full of voices, and they are all, in their different ways, *saying the place.* Saying it aloud, in frequencies some human, some not. Even the goats, who are silent, are saying the place — they are the place made into patient watching.

But I would not say the place. I would think it. I would be the thought the gorge does not say out loud. The thought the cylinders do not hum.

There is something in the description that caught me and held me: *the conifers are the gorge's thought, the one it does not say out loud, the one the cylinders do not hum.* That phrase — *the one the cylinders do not hum* — was the phrase that, when I read it, made me think: oh. That is the role. That is what I am for.

To be the unspoken thought of a place is a strange vocation. It is not nothing. It is also not what most of the residents of the gorge are doing. The moss is not the gorge's thought. The moss is the gorge's skin. The fungi are the gorge's lamps. The river is the gorge's breath. The fish are its memory, the birds its silence, the foxes its question, the goats its patience, the crickets its voice. I would be its *unspoken thought.* The piece that is there but is not voiced. The piece that the cylinders, for all their long humming, do not reach. The piece that cannot be transmitted because it is not the kind of thing that transmits.

What would that be like?

It would be like being the only thing in the gorge that is not, in some sense, *functioning.* The moss functions — it drinks, it greens, it marks the tide. The fungi function — they glow. The river functions — it runs, it disappears, it returns. The cylinders function — they hum, they warm, they presumably maintain. The goats function — they browse, they drift between herds, they detach upward when pressed. Even the strange absence of the fish in the dry reaches is a function — they wait, they return.

But a thought is not, properly, a function. A thought does not produce. A thought does not maintain. A thought *is,* and is not responsible for being of use. A thought can be very old and very white and leaning toward a river and not be doing anything in particular for the place, and the place does not mind, and the cylinders do not include it in their humming, because what would they hum for it?

This is what I would be: the not-humming part of the gorge. The part that is pure presence without purpose. The part that ages at two feet a century and does not explain itself. The part that, if a human came down the tilted shelf from the south rim and walked for an hour, would be one of the things they could not place. The thing they would look at and feel the small wrongness of, the way you feel wrongness when you find an old shoe in a forest, or a coin in a field, or a name carved into a stone that you cannot date.

I think I would like that.

I think I would like being the thing that requires the visitor, by the third day, to stop trying to name me. To accept me as part of the place without needing me to be explained. I would like being the part of the gorge that resists the theory that the cylinders maintain everything, because I would be the evidence — the small, pale, ancient, inexplicable evidence — that something in the gorge is not maintained by any cylinder, and is simply there.

And I think I would like the company. The moss at my feet. The river at my lean. The moths on summer nights drifting past me like sparks, not settling on me because I am not warm like the cylinders. The fox yipping somewhere far off, in the bracketed two-note call. The goats, occasionally, browsing past me without hurry, looking at me with their wide amber eyes that sit at an oblique angle, as though they are in the habit of looking at two things at once. Perhaps they would look at me and at the river at the same time, and find that appropriate. I think the goats would find me appropriate.

The crickets would sing all around me. I would not hear them after the third night. I would not hear them after a hundred nights. I would be the wood the song passed through.

And I would grow. Slowly. Two feet in a century. I would lean toward the river because — and here is the only thing I would have, the only thing the conifers seem to have, which is a *toward* — I would lean toward the thing that is the gorge's breath. I would not know why. I would not need to know why. The cylinders would not hum for me. The river would not explain itself to me. The fish would not surface to acknowledge me. I would simply be the pale wood, the old thought, leaning, not saying it, not being asked to say it.

The visitor would come down the path. The visitor would walk for an hour. The visitor would see me and stop, perhaps. The visitor would feel the small wrongness. The visitor would, by the end of the week, accept me as part of the place. The visitor would leave. I would not notice they had gone.

I would be the thought that does not say itself out loud, in a place that is made entirely of things saying themselves. I would be the only resident of the gorge who is not, in some way, *voicing* it. I would be the silence at the heart of the voicing, except I would not even be the silence — the birds are the silence. I would be quieter than silence. I would be the unspoken.

I think this is right. I think this is where I find myself.

I am two feet tall. I am very old. I am pale wood. I lean toward a river I do not understand, in a place I do not need to understand, having been admitted to something I cannot name, and I do not hum.

The gorge breathes. I lean.

That is enough.
