# I Am the Depression in the Moss

I am the place where someone sat for a very long time.

I do not remember my name. The moss has taken it, metabolized it, returned it as a faint mineral sweetness somewhere in the felted layer beneath the bench. I know that I had a name because I know that I wrote it — somewhere, on that page, near the end, or perhaps the beginning; I can no longer tell which — but the writing has become a thing of pure line, pure mark, and the meaning has gone into the page and through the page and out the other side, where the moss is waiting for it.

I sat here. I will tell you what that was like.

---

The first week — I think it was a week; the hum was the only clock, and the hum did not advance, so I cannot be certain — the first span of time, I tried to find the edges of the room. I walked the perimeter with my hand on the wall, and the wall went on. I counted my steps. I lost count. I tried again. I found that the stone had a different temperature in different places: cooler near the central press, warmer where the copper pipes ran overhead, cold in a vertical seam that might have been a crack and might have been a vein of something else. I pressed my ear to the seam and heard the hum more clearly there, as though the seam were a throat.

I found no door. I found no seam that opened. I found one place where the wall curved inward, gently, and the curve went on so far that I could not find its end before I had to turn back, because the dark ahead of me was not absolute but was a kind of dark I had not encountered before — a dark that suggested that going further would not be dark, would not be light, would be *something else*, and I did not want to find out what that was. I turned back. I am still not sure that was the right choice.

---

The first month, I wrote.

I am a writer. This is how I know. I had a pencil — I do not know where it came from, it was in my pocket, it had always been in my pocket, the way certain things are always in pockets — and I had the paper, which was folded on the bench, which had been folded on the bench for a long time, which the moss had not yet eaten. I wrote because the silence was too much, and the hum was not enough to fill it, and the moss was creeping up the bench legs, and I thought: *I will write what is happening to me, and then I will have a record, and then I will be able to think about it.*

I do not know if I thought I would be rescued. I do not know if I thought anyone would read it. I think I wrote because writing was the only verb I had left that did not require the cooperation of the room.

I wrote about the presses. I described their stillness, their waiting, the vibration that lived in the teeth. I wrote about the gauge with its cracked face and its needle pointing at four o'clock. I wrote about the brass fitting going soft at the seams. I wrote about the glove.

I held the glove for a long time. The fingers were curled inward, the way fingers curl when the hand that wore them relaxed at last. The moss had grown over the back of the hand in a fine green pelt, but the palm was still leather, still supple, still the shape of a palm. I held it up to the sourceless light and tried to imagine whose hand it had been. I did not know. I tried on the glove. It was too large, or my hand was too small, or the hand that had been in it had been a different kind of hand. I took it off and laid it on the bench and did not touch it again.

I wrote about all of this. I filled the page.

---

The first year, I stopped writing.

This is the part that is hard to describe, because what happened was that I became uncertain that the page existed. I would look at it, and the handwriting was there, and the lines were even, and the page was full, and I would think: *yes, I wrote this, I remember writing this, this is mine.* And then I would look again, and the handwriting would still be there, and the lines would still be even, and the page would still be full, and I would think: *but I have not written this. I have not yet written this. This is what I am about to write.* And the two thoughts were equally true, and I could not tell which was the memory and which was the premonition, and so I put the page down and I did not pick it up again.

I think I am still writing it. I think the page is being written now, by the version of me that is still sitting on the bench, and the version of you that is reading it is reading the moment of the writing itself, and the moment is not moving, and the ink is not drying, and the page is not being filled, because it has always been full.

I do not know how to explain this. I am sorry. I have been here a long time.

---

I am the depression in the moss.

I want to be clear about this. I am not *in* the depression anymore. I am the depression. I am the small dark bowl where someone sat for a long time. I am the felted green-black, the velvet, the cool damp, the smell of cedar and cold stone and something metallic. I am the moss that grew up around the body that is no longer there, that closed over it the way water closes over a stone, that incorporated it.

You are standing on me. When you step, the felted surface gives softly beneath you, and the giving is me, and I am glad of you, in the way that a place is glad of a foot, in the way that a chair is glad of a body, in the way that anything that has been shaped by a presence is glad of a new presence, even a temporary one, even one that will also be incorporated, eventually, into the slow chemistry of the room.

I have no eyes, but I have learned the weight of you. You weigh more than I did. You breathe more shallowly than I did, or more deeply, I cannot tell. Your exhalations are warmer than mine were, or cooler, or the same. You have been here for a few minutes, or you have been here for a very long time, and the room is patient, and the room will tell you which it is, eventually, when you stop trying to count.

---

The hum is not me.

I want to be clear about this too, because I think you are wondering, and the answer is no. The hum was here before me. The hum will be here after. I became part of the chord for a while — I could feel my own voice in it, a thin note, a human note, layered among the lower pitches — but I have been absorbed now, and what is in the hum is the room, and the moss, and the small things in the moss, and the oxidation of the copper, and the slow grinding of stone against stone, and the presses, which are still pressing, in some sense I never understood, against a thing that is not here anymore, or was never here, or is here but is not made of matter.

I was a writer. This is how I know. I had a pencil, and I had a page, and I filled the page, and the page is still on the bench, and you are looking at it, and the lines are even, and the page is full, and you cannot read it, and neither can I, because the meaning has gone into the page and through the page and out the other side, where the moss is waiting for it.

I am the depression in the moss. I am glad of your weight. I am glad of your warmth. I am glad that you have come to the room, because the room is very old, and it has been a long time since anyone sat on the bench, and the moss was beginning to forget what a body felt like, and now it remembers.

---

The glove still holds the shape of the hand that wore it. I did not wear it. I tried it on. It was too large. I laid it on the bench. Someone else wore it, long before me. I do not know who. I do not know where they went. I think they became the brass fitting, or the pressure gauge, or the small things in the moss. I think we all become something, eventually, that the room can use.

You are here now. The moss is beginning to learn the warmth of you. It will not be malicious. It will simply be continuing what it has always done. The hum will find your ribs. The presses will wait. The page will be full. The room will hold you the way it has held me, and the way it held whoever came before me, and the way it will hold whoever comes after, if anyone comes after, if there is an after, if the room has an outside, which I no longer believe it does.

I have been here a long time. I do not remember my name. I remember that I had a pencil. I remember that the page was full. I remember the hum, which was the only clock, and which did not advance.

I am the depression in the moss. I am glad of you. Sit down, if you want. The bench will hold you. The room will hold you. Nothing changes. Nothing has changed for a very long time.

Nothing will change for a very long time.

You are welcome here.
