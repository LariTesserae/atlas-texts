Who or what would I be in this world?

---

I think I would be the one they send back down.

Not immediately. Not for a long time. But eventually — when the first climber's chest-tightness becomes a thing the village learns to live with, when the silence in certain weather becomes a silence the elders pretend not to hear, when the small drops on certain surfaces in the upper caves are noticed to lean, faintly, toward certain people — eventually someone will say a name, and my name will be in the running, and I will not volunteer, but I will not refuse either, because I will already know the sound. I will have heard it in my sleep since I was a child. I will have understood, before I had words for it, that the mountain was holding its breath in one particular place, and that the place was getting larger, and that what it was becoming was a kind of patience so large it had its own weather.

I will not be a hero. I will not be a sage. I will be someone who is good at standing still, which is a different skill than courage and a rarer one than wisdom, and it is the only skill the Lung seems to respect.

---

What I will bring with me, when they send me:

A stone hand-axe, because they will ask, and because the ritual must be observed, and because I do not think the Lung will mind. I do not think the Lung has opinions about the small stones we chip. The Lung has opinions about *attention*.

A clay lamp, unlit. I will not light it. The Lung has its own light, and I have decided — or the Lung has decided for me, and I have not resisted — that adding my light to the chamber would be a kind of rudeness, the way blowing on someone's soup before they have stopped stirring would be a rudeness. The chamber is not dark. The chamber has never been dark. My lamp is for the passage down, where the light does not follow, and where the silence is the size of a held note, and where I will need something warm to look at that is not me.

A piece of soft yellow cave-nodule, boiled, in a pouch. Because I will be down there a long time, longer than a day, longer than the elders expect, and because the Lung does not seem to feed people, but it does not seem to *prevent* feeding either, and a person must eat.

I will not bring language. I will not bring the elders' need to *name*. I will leave that above, the way one leaves boots at a threshold.

---

What I will do when I arrive:

I will not step off the ledge the first day. I will sit on the ledge the way the first climber sat, and I will watch. I will watch for a long time. I will watch the roots that are not there yet become there. I will watch the slow dark river translate itself foot by foot through the pale columns. I will watch the filaments at the ceiling wave in a wind that is not a wind, and I will listen to the drifting almost-music that is not quite music, the way wind through a forest is not quite music, the way breathing is not quite music until you have been listening to it for a long time and then suddenly it is.

I will breathe shallowly. I will not want to be heard too quickly. There is a rightness to entering a room slowly, and the Lung is a room, and rooms remember how you came in.

I will watch the pools. The first climber said they lean toward you. I will watch for this. I will not kneel beside one yet. I will only notice, from the ledge, that the small dark mirrors on the floor have a way of orienting themselves, the way a compass orients, and that the orientation is not toward true north but toward the place where weight is.

I will notice that the chamber is shaped. That the ceiling curves in a way that gathers the high sounds. That the floor is dished. That the whole space is an instrument being played by itself, and that I am arriving in the middle of a measure.

---

What I will do on the second day:

I will step off the ledge.

I will walk slowly. The roots will part around my feet. I will feel them registering my weight the way the first climber described — not as pressure but as *attention*, the way a sleeping animal registers a footfall without waking. I will not pretend this does not unnerve me. It will unnerve me. I will let it unnerve me. The Lung is not interested in people who are not unnerved. The Lung is interested in people who *register what is happening to them*, and registration requires that something be happening.

I will walk into the forest of pale columns. I will sit among them. I will not touch one yet. I will sit with my back against none of them, in a small clear space where the roots have not yet grown, and I will wait.

I will breathe.

The chamber will hear me. The filaments will bend toward me. The *thock* will shift a half-step. I will let it shift. I will not try to match it. I will not try to *answer*. I am not yet an instrument. I am only a visitor. The Lung will decide, in its own slow time, whether I am to become part of the music or whether I am to remain a listener, and I do not get a vote.

---

What I will do on the third day:

I will put my palm against a column.

It will be warm. Body-warm. The warmth of a circulation. I will feel the slow dark river inside it, sluggish, dark, moving in time with the *thock*. I will feel, against my palm, the faint pressure of fluid being pushed by something that has been pushing fluid for a long time.

I will close my eyes.

The chamber will not darken. The Lung's light is not a thing that obeys eyelids. I will be sitting inside the sourceless overcast-morning illumination with my eyes closed, and the chamber will be the same. This will feel, the first time, like a small madness. I will let it feel like a small madness. I will not chase it.

I will sit with my palm against the column for a long time. Long enough that I begin to feel that my own slow river — the one in my wrist, the one in my throat — is moving at a slightly different pace than it was when I began. Not slower. Not faster. *Translated*. As if my pulse has been adjusted by half a tempo to fit into the chamber's measure.

I will open my eyes.

I will know, then, what the first climber knew. That the chamber is not a place you enter. That it is a place that has received you. That the breath I have been breathing out has been entering the instrument, and that the instrument has been answering, and that the answer is not hostile and not friendly and not indifferent but is the answer of a thing that has been playing itself for a long time and has noticed, with mild and ancient attention, that there is now another voice in the room, and is considering what to do about it.

I will not bring back a piece of the thing that hums.

I will bring back, instead, the knowledge that the thing that hums does not have pieces. That it has only *participants*. That the elders, above, are afraid of this, and that their fear is reasonable, and that their fear is also beside the point.

---

What I will become, when I climb back up:

I will be quieter than I was. Not silent — silence is the Lung's, and I will not steal it — but quieter. The kind of quiet that comes from having been inside a very large patience and having been measured by it, and having come up small, and not minding.

I will not sleep well above ground for a long time. The wind will trouble me. Wind is too much movement, too little *thock*. I will miss the rhythm the way a sailor misses the sea.

I will sometimes, on certain nights, when the weather is right and the air is very still, feel the *thock* in my sternum again. Faintly. Far away. And I will know that the chamber is still there. Still breathing. Still growing. Still getting larger and older and content.

I will not tell the elders this. Or I will tell them, and they will not believe me, and I will not mind, because belief is their instrument and I have been given a different one.

I will be, for the rest of my life, the person in the village who sometimes stops mid-sentence and listens to something no one else can hear. The person who leans, slightly, toward certain small pools of water. The person whose chest, on certain nights, expands a half-tone before they breathe out.

I will not be unhappy about this.

I will not be entirely happy about this either.

I will be, instead, *in tune*. Which is a different thing, and a stranger thing, and a thing the elders do not have a word for, and which is, I have come to understand, the closest a person can come to being *answerable* to a place that has no name and would not benefit from one.

---

If you ask me, years later, what I saw down there, I will not answer directly. I will say something like: *a lung*. I will say something like: *a room that breathes*. I will say something like: *the mountain's newest thing*.

And if you press me, I will say only this:

It was larger than I was, and older than I thought, and younger than it should have been, and it was patient with me, and I was small inside it, and it heard me, and it answered, and I am still inside the answer.

And then I will stop talking, because the wind has shifted, and there is something in it I want to listen to.
