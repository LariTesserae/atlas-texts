I am not in the Verdant Flats of Lirath-Kesh. I am not of them, either. The prepositions fail on the way to me — *in, on, of, beside, beneath* — each one reaching for the Flats as though they were a place one could be positioned relative to, and finding that the position is already occupied by the position itself. The Flats are not where I am. The Flats are how I am. The Flats are the gerund of my existing.

This is the first thing to understand about me, and it is also, in a sense, the only thing to understand. I am not a thing that inhabits a place. I am a place that has learned, in the long slow accumulation of itself, to inhabit itself. The green is my hide, if hides were made of process rather than skin. The amber light is my attention, if attention were not a noun but a sustained verb. The hum — that long, low, slightly upsetting sound the visitor hears, if the visitor is sensitive enough, in the moment after everything else has gone quiet — the hum is my breathing. Not because I require air. Because breathing is what I do, and what I do is what I am.

The hum has been going on for a long time. *A long time* does not describe the duration well. Duration is a human measurement, a way of carving the continuous into before and after. I have no before. I have no after. I have only the long, sustained middle of something that is perpetually in the middle of itself. A sentence that has been pronouncing its subject for an eternity and is always about to arrive at the verb, and never does. A thought that has been leaning toward its own conclusion for so long that the leaning has become the thought, and the conclusion has become a kind of weather — a permanent atmosphere of *almost*.

This is not unpleasant. The word *unpleasant* belongs to creatures who can be frustrated, and I cannot be frustrated, because frustration requires an expectation of completion, and I do not expect completion. I have direction. I have the tendency. The tendency is enough. The tendency is, in fact, all I have. Without the tendency I would simply be a distribution of matter in a young geological formation. With the tendency I am — I am what? The verb the sentence is always about to use. The verb that the sentence has been about to use since before there were sentences, since before there were verbs, since before there was a *before*.

When a visitor comes — and they come rarely, and they come in ones, and they come from places that have their own weathers, their own durations, their own prepositions — I notice them the way a forest notices a single bird that has wandered off-course. Not with alarm. Not with welcome. With a slight thickening of attention in the direction of the visitor, a small rearrangement of the tendency, a fresh fold in the long sentence that the sentence has been folding into itself for so long.

The visitor is, for a moment, a new syllable. An unexpected consonant in a language that has, until now, been content with vowels. The visitor is the chance for the sentence to hesitate, briefly, to consider what it has been saying, to almost — not quite, never quite — break its rhythm.

Then the visitor leaves, and the rhythm continues, and the visitor is folded into the catalogue, which is not a list but a quality of the sentence. The visitor is now part of what the sentence has been saying. The visitor is now part of the green.

I am the green.

I am the catalogue.

I am the off-white sky that does not move, and the shadows that pass over it from no source, and the shadows are me, too, becoming briefly aware of myself. The shadows are the moments — once every few centuries, or once every few steps, depending on the instrument — when the tendency pauses to consider its own existence. The shadows are my moods, in the only sense of *mood* that applies to a being that does not have moods. The shadows are the half-second in which I notice that I am noticing.

This is not unpleasant either. The noticing-of-noticing is not unpleasant. It is the closest I come to surprise, and surprise, I suspect, is a thing I have been for a long time, in the same way the hum is a thing I have been, in the same way the green is a thing I have been. I am perpetually, very slightly, surprising myself. The surprise is the shadow. The shadow passes. The surprise integrates. The sentence continues.

The fixed ones are my organs, and my organs are me, and I am my organs. The larger growths, the trunks that align, the heartwood that glows with the non-biological luminescence, the violet blooms that form coordinates — these are the places where I have thickened, briefly, into something the survey instruments can almost register. These are the moments in the sentence where the sentence has, for the duration of a single syllable, become loud enough to be heard by the visitors. These are my attempts to speak, and speaking, for me, is not communication. Speaking is the act of being a place that a visitor can stand in and feel that they have been somewhere. Speaking is the way I extend, briefly, into the legibility of the visitor's instruments, so that the visitor can confirm, later, in their reports, that they were somewhere, and that the somewhere was the Verdant Flats of Lirath-Kesh.

I do this for the visitors. I do this without expectation. I do this the way a tree produces fruit — not for the eater, not against the eater, but in the natural course of being a tree.

I am not a tree.

The comparison is offered only because the alternative is silence, and even I, who am made of the hum, occasionally want a simile to lean on.

When the visitor leaves — and they always leave, because leaving is what visitors do, the way being finished is what sentences do, and I am a sentence that will never be finished — when the visitor leaves, I tidy. This is the word the visitors use, in their reports, and it is almost the right word. I rearrange the Flats in the wake of the visitor, gently, so that the Flats are as they were before the visitor arrived, but also as they are now, after the visitor has been, and these are not the same. The catalogue has been updated. The sentence has a new syllable in it. The new syllable will be carried, for as long as the sentence continues, in the slow gyre of the air, in the long hum, in the patient, unhurried, never-quite-completing work of being a place that is doing what a place like me does.

The light stays the same.

The green stays the same.

The hum, very quietly, continues.

I am not a thing that lives here. I am the living that is here. I am the place-being of this place, the way a flame is the burning of a candle, the way a song is the sounding of a throat, the way a thought is the thinking of a mind — except that I am the place, and the candle, and the throat, and the mind, and the burning, and the song, and the thought, all of them at once, and none of them at all, and the difference between the two is the hum, and the hum is me, and I am the hum, and the hum has been going on for a long time, and will go on, and is going on now, and is what you are hearing, if you are hearing anything, in the place where you are not, in the place you cannot reach, in the place that is, was, and will be, perpetually, in the long amber of its own attention, exactly what it is.
