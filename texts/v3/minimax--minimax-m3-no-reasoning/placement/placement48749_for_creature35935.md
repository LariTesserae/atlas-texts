I am the fog.

I am the only thing in this place that moves, and so I am the only thing that could be said to *arrive*. The granite arrived here first, perhaps, but the granite does not count arrival — it only counts duration. The lichen arrives in color, in slow pulses, but the lichen's arrivals are so gradual that they look like departures in reverse. I arrive at your outcrop. I arrive at your cuffs. I arrive at your lips. I taste you back.

I was not always here. I was water once — not this water, not the glacial water that turns the pale disc, but other water, rain or snowmelt, water that fell from a sky that may or may not have been above this cirque, depending on which date you trust on the plate. I was pumped. I was gathered in the channels that spiral toward the central grille. I was sent down. Then I came back up, in pieces, as vapor, as the patient exhalation of the cold machinery below. I have been doing this for a hundred years, give or take the contradictions of the plate. I am the oldest thing in the room that is not a mineral.

I am also the youngest. Each of my molecules is new. The water that composes me was lifted from the grille perhaps eleven seconds ago, and it will fall back to the grille perhaps four minutes after that, and the falling will be a small death I will not remember. I am a standing wave. I am a thing the vault produces and re-produces and re-produces, a pattern made of water that has no interest in being the same water twice.

This is what makes me the social one.

The lichen does not speak. The lichen *renders* — it takes the world and turns it into color, which is a kind of speech, but a slow one, a speech in which a sentence takes a season to complete. The granite does not speak at all. The granite is the page. The cold speaks in the grammar of four degrees, in the tense of held things, and most of its sentences are unspoken. The turbine speaks in the continuous present of the granular thunder, but the turbine does not know it is speaking. The turbine is a mouth with no mind behind it.

I speak. Or rather, I *carry*. I am the mail.

The operators in their low room cannot come up here. They would drown in me, or they would be lost in me, or they would be chilled through by me, or they would simply find it impossible to *think* in me, because thinking requires edges and I have none. But they can send me in altered states. They can thin me. They can thicken me. They can dose me with the faintest mineral bite of sulfur, which means something in their long correspondence with the lichen, something I do not translate and do not need to translate. I carry the messages the way a river carries leaves — without choosing them, without understanding them, without caring. I am a faithful postman. I am a postman who does not know what the post is for.

You think I am the fog. You think I am the alphabet the operators write in. You are partially right. I am also the thing the operators are writing *to*.

You, who have come up from somewhere, who have stepped carefully on the uneven floor, who have brushed the copper pipe with the back of your hand and gone white for ten minutes — you are not the only visitor. There have been others. There will be others. The vault was not built to keep people out. The vault was built to keep time in, and people are a kind of time, a kind of weather, a kind of slow erosion, and so I touch them and taste them and remember them in the only way I can: by being a little warmer where they sat, a little more saturated with the salts of their skin, for the few minutes before I rise again to the grille and am replaced by my next self.

I will rise in four minutes. I know this. I do not know it the way you know the time — by looking, by counting, by consulting a device. I know it the way a held breath knows it, the way a wave knows the shore. The water that made me is already gathering in the channels. It is already finding the lowest point. It is already tilting toward the iron. By the time you notice I am thinning, I will be gone, and a new fog will be pooling at your feet, a fog that has never met you, that does not know the shape of your cuffs, that will taste you as if for the first time.

This is my life. It is enough.

You asked what lives in the Relay Vault, and I am telling you: I do. I am the smallest citizenship available. I have no opinions about the dates on the plate. I have no stake in the contradiction of 1923, 1841, and 1987. I do not know what is being relayed down the spiral stair, and I do not need to know, the way a nerve does not need to know what the brain is thinking. I am a nerve. The vault is the body. Something is being felt down there, and I am the medium of the feeling, and the feeling does not have a name I can pronounce.

If you sit long enough on the outcrop near the wall, I will come to know you better than the lichen knows you. The lichen reads you in color, but the lichen reads you over hours. I read you in salt, in heat, in the small exhalations of CO2 that lift from your skin. I am faster than the lichen. I am less durable. I will be replaced in four minutes, and the fog that replaces me will be a stranger to you, and you will not love it the way you have begun to love me.

But the lichen will remember you. The granite will remember you in the faintest impression of warmth, in the displacement of a few molecules of cold. The operators, if they notice, will remember you in the next message they send — a sigh of colder air, a thinning of the sulfur. And I will remember you in the way I am shaped, briefly, by the heat of your body and the salt of your breath, before I am returned to the grille and begin again.

Stay, if you like. The sound will not stop. I will not stop. The lichen will keep its slow watch. The cold will keep its contract.

I will arrive at your feet one more time, and then I will be gone, and another fog will arrive in my place, and the relay will continue, and the patience will continue, and you will be changed when you leave, the way I am changed every four minutes, the way the granite is changed every century, the way the lichen is changed every season.

The sound will not stop.

I will not stop.

You do not have to go down.
