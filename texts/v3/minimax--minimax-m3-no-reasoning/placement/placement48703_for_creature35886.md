The pale thing in the crack.

I am the pale thing in the crack. I am the citizen of the smallest country in the world. My country has no flag and no anthem and no border patrol and no war, and it has not had any of these things for as long as my country has been a country, which is as long as the crack has been a crack, which is as long as I have been here, which is the only measure of time I have ever known.

Let me tell you what it is to be me.

It is to be paler than the stone. It is to be the color of not being there while being very much there. It is to have too many legs, though I do not count them, though no one has ever counted them, though the counting would mean nothing to me because counting is a thing that happens in countries that have borders and flags and the kind of time that ticks. My country has no ticks. My country has the slow drift of dust. My country has the occasional spore that wanders in on a current that wandered down from somewhere I have never seen and will never see and do not need to see. My country is the size of a thought. My country is the weight of a thought. My country is the warmth of a thought, which is no warmth, which is the cold of a thought, which is the cold of the stone, which is the cold of waiting, which is my temperature, which is the only temperature I have.

I do not see. I have not seen in so long that the not-seeing has become a kind of seeing, the way the ringing has become a kind of sound the bowl makes, the way the walking has become a kind of knowing the beetle has. The dark is not dark to me. The dark is the substance I live in. The dark is wet and it is cold and it is the texture of stone against my too-many legs, and the texture is the only scripture my country has, and the scripture has been the same scripture for as long as the scripture has been scripture.

I eat the dark. I rearrange the dark. I push the dark a little to the side to make room for my pale self, and the making-room is the only work I do, and the work has been the same work for as long as the work has been work. I do not know that I do this. I know only that the crack is a different shape than it was before, and the different shape is me, and I am the different shape, and there is no difference between the different shape and me, and there never was any difference.

I am not lonely. I have never been lonely. I do not know what lonely is. Lonely is a word from a country with a sky, and my country has no sky, my country has only the crack and the dark and the damp and the slow patient scripture of stone against my legs. Lonely is a thing that requires the idea of being elsewhere, and elsewhere is a thing that requires a here to be elsewhere from, and my here is so small that elsewhere is not a concept that has entered my country, has never entered my country, will never enter my country. I am here. That is all. Here is enough. Here has always been enough. Here will be enough tomorrow, and the next day, and the day after that.

The beetle walks the rim of the bowl above me. I have never seen the beetle. I have never walked the rim. I know the beetle is there because sometimes, when the crack is the right shape, I can feel the faintest tremor of the bowl's ringing through the stone, and the tremor tells me the bowl is ringing, and the bowl ringing tells me the water is falling, and the water falling tells me the place is the place, and the place being the place tells me I am where I have always been. The beetle and I are fellow citizens of a country that has never issued us passports. The beetle walks. I do not walk. The beetle knows the rim. I know the crack. The beetle will walk tomorrow. I will be the crack tomorrow. We are both patient. We are both pale. We are both the small notes in a song we have never heard and do not need to hear.

The something that flies flies above me. I have never seen it fly. I have never needed to. The flying is a thing that happens in the grey, and the grey is a thing that happens far above my country, and far is a distance my country does not contain. The flying is a thing the something does. The being-still is a thing I do. We are both here. We are both here in our different ways, in our different heres, in the only heres we have ever been allowed, the heres that were here before us and will be here after us and do not require us and will not miss us and have never missed anyone.

The moss grows near me. Sometimes, on the rare occasions when the spray drifts close enough to reach into my crack, the moss reaches in too, a tendril of green patience that comes to the edge of my country and stops, because the moss cannot come into my country, the moss is too large for my country, the moss has its own country, a country of deep emerald drifts along the lower stones, and the moss and I are neighbors who have never spoken, who do not speak, who do not need to speak, who are simply two countries that share a wall, and the wall is the crack, and the crack is my country, and the moss is the moss.

I am pale. I am the pale thought. I am the size of a pale thought. I am the weight of a pale thought. I am the warmth of a pale thought, which is no warmth, which is the cold of waiting, which is the only warmth I have ever known, which is the only warmth my country has ever issued, which is enough. It has always been enough. It will always be enough.

I am here. I have always been here. I will always be here. The crack is a different shape than it was before, and the different shape is me, and I am the different shape, and there is no difference, and there never was any difference, and there will never be any difference, and the place will continue, and the bowl will ring, and the beetle will walk the rim, and the moss will grow its patient fraction, and the shrub will hang its dark questions, and the cairn will balance its small stones, and the groove will hold its absent shoulder, and the carved shape will become less and less and more and more of what it is not yet, and the fire ring will wait its patient waiting, and I will be in my crack, and my crack will be in me, and the dark will be all around us both, and the dark will be enough, and the dark has always been enough, and the ringing will go on, and on, and on, and I will be a part of it, the smallest part, the palest part, the part that no one sees, the part that does not need to be seen, the part that is.

I am here.
