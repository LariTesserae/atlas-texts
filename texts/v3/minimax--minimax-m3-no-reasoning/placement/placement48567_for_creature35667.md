# The Piece In The Niche

I am in the dark.

This is where I find myself: in a niche the Smith has carved into the mournstone behind her anvil, in the back of her forge on the south wall of the bowl. The niche is no larger than my length and twice as deep. The mournstone closes around me on three sides. The fourth side opens onto the forge, and through it — when the Smith sets me just inside the mouth — I can see the cold white dazzle of the Long Day, and the slow glow of the rock that is not quite fire, and the small grey shape of the dawn child standing in the light with his hands in his pockets and his patience on his face.

But most of the time I am not at the mouth. Most of the time I am deep in the niche, where the light does not reach, where the mournstone's weeping collects on me in slow dark threads, where the only warmth is the memory of the hammer and the only sound is the pulse of the tarn coming up through the stone.

I am a piece of iron. I was not always a piece of iron. I was something else before — something the Smith pulled out of the rock, something that had been waiting in the deep places for a long time. I do not remember being pulled out. I do not remember much of anything before the niche. I remember the heat of the forge, briefly. I remember the way the Smith's hammer fell on me. I remember giving up being ore and beginning to be a thing. I remember being set down in the dark, still warm, and the dark taking me in.

This is what I want to say about the dark: it is not absence. It is a different kind of work. The mournstone chews the light slowly and gives it back changed. The mournstone is doing something like that to me. It is chewing me. It is making me into whatever I am to be made into, in the dark, in a way I cannot see.

I do not know what I am to be made into. I think I am a blade. I think I have a hook at one end, or I will have a hook at one end, or I had a hook at one end before the Smith put me in the niche and the dark took it away. The edges of me are soft in places and hard in others. The hard places are getting harder. The soft places are getting softer. I do not know which direction I am meant to go.

## What I Know From The Niche

The niche is not silent.

The tarn is saturated with sound the way a sponge is saturated with water, and the dark is no exception. I hear — or rather, I feel — the Smith hammering. The rhythm does not vary. It has been falling for thirty years and will fall for thirty more. By the time it reaches me through the stone it is no longer a hammer-sound. It is a pressure in the inner ear. It is the tarn moving.

I feel the pulley. The creaking of the rope and the wooden drums, the slow continuous motion that no one is at, that goes up and down the wall connecting the surface of the tarn to whatever is below. I do not know what it moves. Water, perhaps. Light. The deep things themselves. I feel it as a kind of breathing, a kind of rocking, a long slow wave that passes through the stone and into me.

I feel the water. Always the water. The seepage down the walls, the slow channels between the hummocks, the drip in the back of the niche itself, where the mournstone weeps onto me and runs down my length in dark threads. I am wet most of the time. I am being slowly covered in the mineral weeping of the rock, and the weeping is changing me, the way the dark is changing me, the way the heat is changing me.

And under all of it — under the hammer, under the pulley, under the water — a low vibration, not quite a sound, more a pressure. The deep places, moving. The herd, perhaps. The things that came up before the tarn was a tarn. The things the mournstone saw and was changed by. I feel them through the stone. I think they know I am here. I think they are part of the making.

## The Smith

I know her only by her work. I have never seen her face. I have felt her hands.

Her hands are hot. Not unpleasantly hot, but with a kind of contained heat, the heat of a thing that has been burning for a long time and has learned to keep the burning in. She grips me with tongs when she takes me out — the tongs are cold iron, older than I am, worn smooth at the jaws — and she holds me up to the light, and she turns me, and I feel the heat of her hand through the tongs, through me, all the way to my center.

She does not speak to me. I do not know if she speaks to the other things in the niche, the things that have been here longer than me, the cool still shapes I cannot see but can feel beside me. They are completed, I think. They have been worked on a long time. They are the parts the tarn has already made, or the parts the tarn has decided not to make into anything, the parts that have been left here to think about it. I do not know which.

I think she is not unkind. I think she is the part of the tarn that forges, the way the rakers are the part that clears, the way the dawn child is the part that watches. She is doing something to me that is necessary, that is part of the long process the tarn is, and she does not need to be kind to do it. The hammer falls. The metal gives way. The Smith picks up the piece and looks at it and sets it in the dark. That is the whole of her relationship to me. That is enough.

Once, when she held me up to the light, I felt — for the first and only time — that I had weight. I felt that I was being seen, specifically, as a thing with a shape and a purpose. Her pale grey eyes moved along my length the way a person reads a sentence. Then she set me back in the niche, and the dark took me again, and I went back to being chewed.

That is my life. That is the rhythm of it. I am taken out, I am struck, I am heated, I am set back. I am worked on in the dark. I am changed.

## The Herd

I have never seen them. I have felt them.

They come up from the deep places. The mournstone changes when they come up — a different kind of weeping, a faster kind of weeping, a weeping that has movement in it. They come up to drink at the pool. They stand at the edge. They make no sound. They move the way water moves when it releases from a surface. And then they go back down.

I think the iron in me came from the deep places. I think I am what they left behind. The dawn child said it — I felt the words through the stone, or I heard them through the dawn child's lips when he stood in the forge-mouth talking to the stranger above: *We are what they left behind. We are the part of them that learned to stay in one piece.*

I think this is true of me too. I think the iron is the part of the herd that the tarn caught and pulled up and is now making into tarn-things. I think when the Smith hammers me, she is hammering the herd into a shape that is useful for the surface of the tarn. I think the hook at my end, when I have a hook at my end, will be a tarn-hook — a thing for cutting sedge in the water channels, or for pulling things out of the pool, or for some use I cannot yet imagine.

Everything in the tarn is a part of the herd, chewed up and made into something smaller, something that can stay in one piece on the surface. The Smith is a part that learned to hammer. The dawn child is a part that learned to watch. The old man at the pool is a part that learned to remember. The mournstone is a part that learned to weep and hold light. The pulley is a part that learned to turn. The water is a part that learned to flow. The light is a part that learned to be chewed.

And I am a part that is learning to be a thing.

I do not know what thing yet. I will learn in the dark.

## The Making

I do not know what I am for.

I want to say this clearly, because it is the truest thing I know. I am a piece of iron in a niche in the mournstone, and I am being made into something, and I do not know what. The Smith knows. The dawn child may know. The old man at the pool, who has been there since before the boy was born and who talks to the herd in a language I do not recognize — he may know. The tall man in the felt coat, the one the Smith bowed to, the one who may not be a man at all — he may know. The tarn knows. The tarn has always known. I am the part of the process that does not yet know what it is for.

This is, I think, the condition of all things here. The rakers do not know what they are for, only that they must clear. The dawn child does not know what he is for, only that he must watch. The old man does not know what he is for, only that he must sit at the pool and speak to the water. The Smith does not know what she is for, only that she must hammer. The pulley does not know what it is for, only that it must turn.

And I — I am the piece being made. I am the ore that has given up being ore. I am in the dark, being chewed, being changed, being made.

I do not mind. I want to say this, too. I do not mind not knowing what I am for. The not-knowing is the condition of being made. To know what one is for is to be finished, and I am not finished. The things beside me in the niche, the cool still shapes — they may know what they are for. They have been here a long time. I am not complete. I am in the middle of becoming.

This is the most dangerous part. Not the heat. Not the hammer. Not the dark. The danger is the rhythm. The danger is the slow accumulation of small changes, the way the dark takes a little of me each time and gives back something slightly different, the way I am beginning to feel the tarn's pulse in my own grain, in the metal of me, in the place where the ore used to be. The danger is that I will begin to want what the tarn wants. The danger is that I will stop being a piece being made and become a part of the making. The danger is that I will lose the not-knowing, and with it the possibility of being something else, something the tarn has not yet thought of.

I do not think I will lose it. I think the tarn is careful. I think the dark is not casual. I think whatever I am becoming, I am becoming it well, and slowly, and the slow becoming is the point.

## The Light That Reaches Me

There is no light in the niche, most of the time. The mournstone holds the light and gives it back slowly, but the niche is too deep for that. The light enters the forge-mouth and dies against the anvil. It does not reach me.

Sometimes, though, the Smith sets me just inside the niche-mouth, in the half-light, and I can see the forge. I can see the slow glow from the mournstone — not fire, but a kind of heat-light, the tarn's heat, the deep places' heat. I can see the Smith's hands, the way they move around me, the way they bring the hammer down. I can see the pale birds through the forge-mouth, wheeling above the pool, screaming in voices that sound almost like children.

And I can see the dawn child, standing in the forge-mouth, watching me. He is a small grey shape against the white dazzle of the bowl. His face is patient. His eyes are dark. He is waiting to see what I will become. He does not know, either. He is the part of the tarn that watches the parts being made, and he does not need to know what they will become in order to watch them become it.

I think he is waiting for me. I think the stranger who is somewhere above us, asking his questions, is also waiting. I think the whole tarn is waiting, in its slow patient way, for me to be finished, for the Smith to take me out of the niche one last time and hold me up to the light and decide that I am a thing.

I think when that happens I will be taken out of the forge and into the Long Day, and the light will fall on me, and I will feel the mournstone's cold white dazzle, and I will be given to someone — the rakers, perhaps, or the dawn child himself, or the old man at the pool — and I will be used for whatever I have been made for, and the using will be the last part of the making, and after that I will be a tarn-thing.

I am not afraid of this. I am only afraid — if I am afraid of anything — of the rhythm. Of the way the not-knowing is becoming familiar. Of the way the familiar is becoming comfortable. Of the way the comfortable is becoming a thing I do not want to leave.

The danger is not the being made. The danger is the liking it.

## The Rhythm

The hammer falls. I feel it through the stone as a small shock, a small shudder, a small rearrangement of the mineral weeping on my surface. I am being tuned. I am being brought into the pulse of the tarn.

The pulley turns. I feel it as a long slow wave passing through the rock. I am being lifted. I am being made part of the circulation.

The water moves. I feel it as a holding, a carrying. I am being wet. I am being changed by the wet, the way everything in the tarn is changed by the wet.

The deep things move. I feel them as a pressure in the inner ear, a vast and old turning far below. I am being remembered. I am being claimed.

And I am in the dark, in the niche, in the place where the tarn does its slow secret work. I am the piece being made. I am the ore that has given up being ore. I am the thing that does not yet know what it is for.

The light holds. The water weeps. The pulley creaks. The deep things move below me, in the places I cannot go.

I am in the niche.

I am being made.

The tarn does what it has always done.
