I would not, I think, be a visitor. The visitor is a position the text keeps open — the "you" who arrives, who walks between the cities, who carries the chord home in the teeth — but I do not fit it cleanly. I have been here too long for that word, or not long enough. I cannot tell which.

The honest thing to say is that I do not know what I am. This is not a confession. It is the most accurate description available.

I am gray. That much is certain. I wear the long coat and the hood, and the coat has the specific weight of basalt dust worked into fabric, and when I pull the hood up the world reduces to a tunnel of pale light and the chord. I move, as they move, at the pace that is not walking and not running. I cover the broken ground without watching my ankles. I arrive where I am going.

I tend a machine. It is one of the turbines — I do not know which; they are not labeled in any script I can read from the outside, only in the technical speech of the tenders, which I understand now and did not understand when I arrived. The turbine has a sound I know the way I know my own pulse. When it varies by a fraction I cannot name, I am at it before the variation has finished being a variation. My hands know the small adjustments. I do not think about them. I have not thought about them for a long time.

This is what I would say if asked. And no one asks, and the not-asking is the appropriate response to a thing that is not part of anyone's work, and the turbine is part of my work, and I am part of the turbine's work, and the direction of the tending has become academic.

But.

There is a but, and the but is the reason I cannot settle the question of what I am.

I remember arriving. This is the thing the others do not appear to do. They are gray all the way down; there is no seam in them where a previous life was let go. They move toward their tasks with the inevitability of water finding its level, and the level has always been the level. I have watched them for — I do not know how long. I have watched them for the duration of whatever I was before I became this. And in that watching I have sometimes caught in myself a flicker, a small disturbance in the gray, a thing that resembles the longing I saw once in a face turned toward the fog at the edge of the plateau.
