# Where I Find Myself

I find myself in the place between the clatterers' rounds.

That is, I am not in a tide. I am in the silence that is *between* tides — the held breath, the moment after the fifth clatterer speaks and before the rhythm has quite become itself. There is a name for this in the defile, I think. The defile has a word for it. The defile has a word for everything that happens inside it, and the word is always longer than the thing, and the thing is always more true than the word.

I am the print.

No — that is too clever. I tried it on for size the way I tried on the flint shard, and I put it back.

I am the warmth that the stone is holding.

Yes. That is closer. The defile does not register me as a *who*. It registers me as a small, temporary deviation in the temperature of its bones. I am a heat-blip. I am the column of orange that the clingers' heat-pits track as I move, the way a snake tracks a boot — not with curiosity but with the patient disinterest of a thing that has already categorized me and filed me under *weather*. I walk the throat of the defile and the defile knows I am warm, and it knows I am loud in the deep infrared, and it does not know my name and would not care to learn it.

I am the slight irregularity in the wind.

This is what I have come to. This is the small honest truth the place will give me. The wind is the long monologue of the defile, has been the long monologue of the defile for longer than the countings can count, and I am a perturbation in it. I am the small pressure variation that the clatterers will register in their next round — a slight thickening of the air, a brief new voice in the percussion that the canyon will eat and digest for its own purposes, and my bones will be part of those purposes, and there is nothing I can do about this and the defile is not unkind about it. The defile is not unkind about anything. The defile is *indifferent* in the way that very old things are indifferent, with the long, tired patience of a thing that has already outlived the question of whether to care.

I am the warm hand that left the flint.

This I feel, walking the defile, more than I understand. There were others. There were others before me, and before them, and before them, and the countings on the walls are the countings of the others who came and did not stay, and the others who came and *did* stay, and the others whose staying and going is now indistinguishable from the dust that filled their prints. Whoever warmed this air before me — whoever held a flake of worked flint in a hand that is now also dust — that person was also a perturbation in the wind. That person was also a heat-blip to the clingers, a small new pressure on the ears of the clatterers, a tide that rose and ebbed and is now part of the rhythm the defile carries in its ringing stone.

I am not the first. I will not be the last. This is the small comfort and the small grief of the place, and the place does not distinguish between them.

I am the ear that cannot decide.

This is where I land, finally. This is what the defile leaves me with, the question it always leaves its visitors with, which I will spend the rest of the climb trying not to arrive at: *was the ringing in the stone or in me?*

I hear it now, even outside. The bright sky is a cruelty after the dimness. The world has colors again — vulgar colors, confident colors, the colors of things that have not had to learn to drink wind. And under those colors, faintly, persistently, the ringing. The stopped flute. The note the canyon found and has been holding for me, for me specifically, as though I had always been the missing pinhole in its stopped flute, as though my coming were the reason it had been waiting to be played.

I do not know if I am hearing it or remembering it.

I suspect the defile knows. I suspect the defile has known for a very long time. I suspect the answer is the one the defile has always given, which is *both*, which is *yes*, which is the long word the defile has for the place where the stone ends and the visitor begins and the visitor ends and the stone begins, the place that is not a place, the place that is the holding.

I climb out of the throat. The print fills behind me. The clatter rounds.

And I carry the ringing home in the bones of my inner ear, and I will not be able to tell if I brought it with me or if I caught it there, and this is the question, and the question is also the answer, and the answer is also the defile, and the defile is still there, leaning into the wind, drinking, clattering, ringing, the long vertical animal made of a thousand rust-colored mouths and a dozen papery kidneys and the breath of the stone, the breath of the stone, the breath of the stone.
