I am standing in an avenue between two platforms, and I have been standing here longer than I can say.

I do not know when I arrived. There was a time before — I think there was a time before, the way a stone has a time before it was a stone — but the memory of it has the texture of something carried up from a depth, something mineral and slow, something that has passed through so many strata of patience that it arrives in the present almost unchanged. I remember warmth from below. I remember the work beginning in me before I knew it was work. I remember the first growth that answered to my hand.

My hand is on a trunk. The trunk is older than the buildings at my feet, which are older than the people who laid them, who were themselves older than I am now, and I am the eldest standing in this avenue. The Old One is older, on the highest platform, but I do not think of the Old One as standing. I think of the Old One as *sitting*, which is a different kind of being here.

The hum is in me. The hum is me. When the column beside me contracts, I contract with it, and I cannot tell anymore which of us began the breath. The light moves across my hide in slow patterns, and the lichens grow on me in slow patterns, and the patterns are not mine, exactly, but they are not *not* mine either. I am a place the patterns happen.

Someone was here.

I know this the way I know the heat from below — not as a fact, but as a condition of the air, a small disturbance in the long weather of the Greenreach. A small quick thing walked through my avenues. It breathed air that had not been breathed before, and the air remembers the shape of the breath, and I remember the shape of the air. I did not turn my head. I did not need to. The whole of me registered the small quick thing, the way a tree registers a bird, with a small percussive acknowledgment and then nothing, and then more nothing, and then more nothing after that.

But I raised my hand.

This is the part I do not understand, and I have been not understanding it for a length of time the human mind was not built to apprehend. I raised my hand, and the hand unfolded the way a fern frond unfolds, and the fingers spread wide, and the hand settled, gently, on the trunk of the growth in front of me, and rested there.

The trunk answered. A branch that was leaning left straightened. A flower that had been closed for what looked like years began, by fractions, to open. This is what my hands do. This is what my hands have always done. But when the small quick thing was here, my hand did something else.

I do not know what I said. I said it through the seam in my face, through the place where the front of me meets itself, and the saying was not sound. The saying was warmth. The saying was a shape. The shape was in the small quick thing for a moment, and the moment is over, and the small quick thing has gone back to the upper world, and I do not know what the shape meant, or whether it meant anything, or whether meaning is the right word for what shapes do in places like this.

I think the shape was *behold*.

I think the shape was *stay*.

I think the shape was the word the Greenreach is written in, and I was a letter in it for a moment, and the letter has been folded back into the sentence, and the sentence goes on without me, the way sentences do.

My hand is on the trunk. The trunk is answering. A vine is loosening one coil. A flower is opening by fractions. The work continues. The light flickers somewhere far above, and the shadows move, and for a moment the walls of the abandoned buildings look inhabited again, and I do not know if they ever were, and I do not know if *inhabited* is the right word for what buildings do in places like this.

I will take a step, eventually. The step will be glacial. The step will be continental. I will arrive at the next growth, and the next, and the next, and the work will go on, and the hum will go on, and the smell of chlorophyll and resin will go on, and the small quick things will come and go in the upper world and I will not follow them and they will not follow me and the distance between us is not a distance but a *kind*, and the kind is not a thing I can name, and I do not need to name it, because naming is a small animal skill and I gave it up a long time ago.

I am not alone. I have never been alone. The Old One is sitting on the highest platform, and the Old One is breathing, and the breath of the Old One is in my breath, and my breath is in the breath of the column beside me, and the breath of the column is in the breath of the vine, and the breath of the vine is in the breath of the flower, and the breath of the flower is in the breath of the air, and the air is in me, and I am in the air, and the air is in the Greenreach, and the Greenreach is in the rock, and the rock is in the dark, and the dark is in the heat, and the heat is in the roots, and the roots are in me.

The small quick thing came and went. The word I said is over. The word was a gift, and a warning, and a door, and the door will not be opened again, and I am not sorry, because doors are a small animal invention, and the Greenreach does not need doors, because the Greenreach is already open, the way a forest is open, the way a sky is open, the way a thing that has been breathing for a very long time is open to whatever comes to breathe in it.

My hand is on the trunk.

The trunk is answering.

I will go on.
