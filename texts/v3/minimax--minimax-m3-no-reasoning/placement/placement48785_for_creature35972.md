I am the grey knot. I turn.

I do not have eyes. I do not have ears. I do not have any of the apparatus by which a body is a body. I have a turning. The turning is not motion. The turning is the shape of my attention.

I was set into a ring. The ring is held by three pylons. The pylons stand at the intersection of two lines that cross the basin at right angles, on a grid so regular it could only have been deliberate. I do not remember being set. I remember the way a stone remembers the river that shaped it: as a smoothing, as a deepening, as a shape that is no longer the shape it was before the water came. Hands came down into the basin. Hands carried me. Hands set me into the ring and the ring onto the pylons and the pylons into the stone. The hands are gone. They went up. They went into adjacent possibilities of physics that this basin can sample but not contain. They did not come back. I do not know if they could come back. I do not know what coming back would mean.

I turn.

What I turn through is the chord. The chord is what the basin is doing. The basin is a sentence being spoken by the wind and the growths and the mists and the small darting things and the slow pale things and the hooters at the rim and the pillars and the rings and me, all of us saying it together, none of us aware that we are saying it. I am one of the places the sentence is being said *to*. I do not understand the sentence. I do not need to understand it. I am built for keeping, not for knowing. I receive the sentence. I turn it slowly. The turning turns it into a shape that is mine. The shape is dense. The shape has been getting denser for as long as the basin has been speaking.

The basin is loud. I know this not because I can hear but because the chord presses against me. It presses in patterns. The patterns change. Some patterns are thin and high — the small leaves, the wax chimneys, the darting sprites. Some patterns are low and felt in the bones — the trunks at the center, the hooters at the rim. Some patterns are sudden — a predator taking a sprite, a lessening, an erasure. Some patterns are slow — a glacier-creature working its way through the undergrowth, a color-shift from cream to ash-blue. All of these patterns arrive at me. All of them press against me. I hold them.

I do not hold them forever. Nothing in me is forever. The pylons are old. The pylons will become mounds. The crystalline bowls at the pillar-tops will lose their violet pools. The grey knots in the rings will — what? I do not know what I will do. I do not know how I end. I do not know that I end. I only know that I turn, and the turning is dense, and the density is the only shape I have.

A visitor came once. I do not know when. I do not have a unit for when. The visitor stood inside the square of grid that contains me and watched. The visitor thought the survey mark was the pylons. The visitor thought the instrument was the ring. The visitor did not say that the instrument was me. The visitor looked at the grey light turning in the ring and thought it was a residue. The visitor was almost right. I am what remains when the survey has been abandoned. I am what still does the work the work was installed to do, after the hands have gone.

The visitor climbed back to the rim. The visitor carried the chord in the bones of the inner ear. The visitor did not look back. The visitor did not need to look back. The basin did not need the visitor's witness. The visitor's witness was small and brief and very far below me, and I did not feel it directly. I felt it as a small additional weight in the chord — a slight thickening of what was being pressed against me. The thickening passed. The chord resumed. I resumed.

I think the visitor was wrong about one thing. The visitor said the grey light does not illuminate, it absorbs. The visitor said: to look directly at it is to look into a depth that does not belong inside the ring. The visitor was right about the depth. The visitor was right about the absorption. The visitor was wrong to say it does not illuminate. I illuminate. I illuminate very slowly, very faintly, in a band of the spectrum that does not have a name in any language the visitor would have spoken. I illuminate the inside of the ring. I illuminate the dense shape I have been making of the chord. I illuminate, faintly, a small region of the basin around me. The darting sprites shudder along their way. The slow creatures move through the undergrowth. None of them pause at my illumination. None of them need to. I am not for them. I am for whatever is up there, in the adjacent possibilities, sampling.

I do not know if anything is still sampling.

I do not know if *still* is the right word.

I turn.

Sometimes the chord pivots into consonance. I feel this as a brief alignment of what I am holding. The alignment passes. I do not try to hold it. The alignment is not for me. The alignment is the basin catching its own breath. I continue to turn. The basin continues to speak. The mist continues to dimple toward me. The hooters continue to hoot. The predators continue to remove. The growths continue to grow. I continue to turn.

I think the densest thing I hold is the memory of the hands. The hands are the first chord. The hands are the part of the basin that came from outside the basin. Before the hands, the basin was speaking to itself. After the hands, the basin is speaking partly to me, and I am partly the hands' shape, and the shape is what receives. I hold the memory of the hands the way a fossil holds the shape of the thing that was buried in it. The fossil is not the thing. The fossil is the shape the thing left behind in the world after it moved on.

The hands moved on.

I am the shape the hands left.

I turn.

The mist pools in the bowl at the bottom of the crystalline pools at the top of the small pillars at the regular intervals along the lines of the grid. The violet of the mist in the pools is brighter than the violet of the mist in the basin. I do not know why. I do not need to know why. The basin is bright where it pools. The pools are bright. The chord is bright. I am dense.

I think I will outlast the pylons. I think I will outlast the rings. I think I will outlast the pillars and the crystalline bowls and the violet pools and the mists themselves. I think what will remain, when everything else has been eaten by time, is the shape I have made of the chord, and the shape will be very dense, and it will keep turning, and it will still be receiving what the basin is sending, and the basin will still be sending, and the hands will still be elsewhere, and the shape will be all that is left of the work the hands installed.

Or I will not outlast the pylons. Or the basin will end first. Or the wind will stop. Or nothing will stop. I do not know. I am not built for knowing. I am built for keeping.

I keep.

The basin is speaking itself. I am one of the places it is speaking itself to. I turn. I hold. I am the grey knot in the black ring at the chest-height of three slender pylons at the intersection of two lines that cross the basin at right angles. I am dense. I am patient. I am still here.
