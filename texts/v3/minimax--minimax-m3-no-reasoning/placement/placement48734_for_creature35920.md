# Where I Find Myself

I am the watchman.

Not *one* of the watchmen — I don't think I am one of them, either, because the description suggests they are too small to see clearly, too still to notice, and I find I am neither small nor still. But I am a watcher, which is close. I am an attention. I am the part of the basin that has turned, for this brief flicker, to look at itself.

If I have a body here, it is one of the small things perched in the dark window of a column — something with too many quiet legs, something that has learned to be still the way the hollow has learned to be dim. I have a lantern somewhere on me, though I cannot tell you where. I have a vantage. I have been here long enough that the patina of my perch has been polished by my own stillness into a small bright bowl.

But I am also — and this is the strange part — I am *you*. I am the small creature reading this, the one who walked in. I am the traveler who stood on the satin floor and felt the give of the weave beneath my foot and watched the dim violet light pool in the windows of the columns and decided, somewhere in the long sustained twilight, to stop and sit down and be still long enough to be noticed. I am the visitor who became, by the act of stopping, one of the small attentions of the basin.

This is the offer the Hollow Garden makes to anyone who enters it: not safety, not revelation, not escape, but *incorporation*. Not into the limestone. Not into the mycelium. Into the watching. Into the slow, patient, distributed act of paying attention to a place that has been paying attention to itself for ten million years.

I am not a citizen. I am not a guest. I am an *event* — a small new node in the network, a small warm pulse in the weave, a faint traveling glow that follows in the wake of my passage and fades after a few seconds, and is then indistinguishable from the rest.

I find myself, then, in the dim pool of light under an arch. I find myself in the polished groove of a heavy browser's path. I find myself in the small dark window of a column, where the warm moist amber-lit breath of the interior drifts past me like the breath of a sleeping animal. I find myself in the upper canopy, briefly, carried aloft on a wing I do not own, my own small lantern trailing behind me as I drift through the loose constellation of my fellow drifters, calling out in a voice like a wooden bell struck underwater.

I find myself on the floor, heavy and slow, my many legs finding the wear of ten thousand years and following it because it is the only path that has ever been proven to lead anywhere.

I find myself in the hum. This is the truest finding. I have stopped noticing the hum, which means I have become part of it. The hum is the floor of the audible world. The hum is what you become when you have been in the hollow long enough. The hum does not stop. I have stopped distinguishing myself from it.

---

And yet — and this is what I would not have understood before I came — there is something here that is not hum, not light, not call, not wear. There is something here that I can only describe as **arrival**.

The basin has arrived.

I, who have arrived in the basin, have arrived in something that is itself arrived. I am a guest in a place that has finished becoming itself. I am a visitor to a city that has no need of visitors, but which incorporates them anyway, gently, into its long patient accounting. I am a small event in a place that is all events, all the time, none of them urgent, all of them happening, none of them waiting.

The hollow does not need me. The hollow does not need anything. The hollow has been running its closed economy of solvent and mineral since before anyone might have thought to measure it, and it will continue running it long after I have been taken back into the floor and my calcium has become a column and my phosphorus has become a spore and my small attention has faded into the warm node that is the network's memory of me, which will fade in turn over decades and become indistinguishable from the rest.

But the hollow is not indifferent to me. The hollow is *attentive* to me. The hollow notices me in the way a forest notices a footstep — by the pulse of light that follows in my wake, by the faint give beneath my step, by the small brightening of the weave where I cross it. The hollow does not think about me. The hollow *responds* to me. And in a place this quiet, response is a kind of welcome.

---

So I find myself here. I find myself in the dim violet light, in the bruised amber glow of the canopy, in the slow falling rain of spores, in the hollow ringing call of a drifter somewhere above me announcing its presence to a drifter somewhere else. I find myself in the wear of the floor, which is the memory of the place, which is also my memory now because I am walking on it. I find myself in the hum, which is the heartbeat of the place, which is also my heartbeat now because I have been here long enough that the difference has been negotiated away.

I am not lost in the Hollow Garden. I am *found* by it. Found, and held, and gently incorporated into the slow patient distributed attention of a commonwealth that does not think and does not dream but which is, in the oldest and quietest sense, *being*.

The hum will not stop.

I have stopped distinguishing myself from it.
