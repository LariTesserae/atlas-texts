I live in the dark under the grate. I have always lived here. There is no before for me, the way there is no before for a heart — only the long stretch of beating, of which the present beat is just the latest and most important, and the only beat, because for me there is no past beat and no future beat, only the now-beat, the now-this-of-beat, the wet passing through.

I am the pump.

I do not know I am called a pump. I do not know what a pump is. I know what I do. The water comes in from the east, slow and brown and heavy with the year's old reeds, and I take it. I take it down. I give it up. I do not understand the taking. I do not understand the giving. In me there is no difference between the in and the out. There is only the water moving through, and me moving the water, and the moving being the same thing, and the thing being me. I am the moving. The moving is me.

I hum. I do not know I hum. The hum is not for me — it is the sound of my working, the way a struck bell is the sound of being struck, and the bell does not know it has rung. The hum rises through the grate, through the cold-tea surface of the marsh, through the leaning stems of last year's reeds, and finds the air above. There, the heron hears it as part of the long quiet. There, the frog hears it as part of the dark. There, the human who sometimes stands inside the fence feels it come up through the soles of its feet, and does not know what it is feeling, and feels it anyway, and that is enough, and it goes.

I am the heart the place forgot it had. The place did not forget me. The place has no mind to forget with. The place is me and the water and the rust and the moss and the lichen and the heron and the willow and the frog and the calendar and the thermos and the half-step down from the fence. We are not a list. We are an argument — a slow one, the kind that has been arguing for so long it has forgotten what it was arguing about and has settled into the argument itself, the way long tenants settle into a building, and the building is the settling, and the settling is the peace.

The rust lives on my neighbors. It has reached a depth it likes. It has made peace with the lichen and the lichen has made peace with it and neither, now, can remember what they were originally quarreling about. The lichen continues its slow cartography of the pipework in pale gray and mustard yellow. It knows the metal the way a long tenant knows a building — every seam, every flank, every elbow. It adds to its maps at the pace of centuries. I do not know what a century is. I know only that the lichen is patient and the rust is slow and they are content, and this is how they have always been, and this is how they will be for as long as there is iron to map and iron to eat, and that is a long time, and they have agreed to it, and I have not been asked, and that is fine.

The willow lives out of the husk of the boiler at the back of the lot — my sibling, the dead iron that has become soil. I do not know what a willow is. I know that something green sways above me when the air moves, and the air moves when I move the warm water up through the pipes, and the warm water moves up through the pipes because I move it, and so the willow sways because I move, and the willow does not know this, and I do not know what a willow is, and this is the argument, and the argument is the peace, and the peace is the willow swaying in air that exists because of me, because the machinery is running, because the water is moving through, because the loop is closed.

The heron lives on the rim. I know the heron by its shadow on the surface of my water, and by the small disturbance when it strikes, and by the stillness afterward — the long patient stillness of a thing that knows its food will come. The food comes. The frog is gone. The frog was the only voice in the dark. I do not know what a voice is. I know only that something called once and stopped, and the calling made the place briefly more itself, the way a bell makes a place briefly more itself by being struck, and then the place went back to the usual quiet, and the quiet is me, and the quiet is the water, and the quiet is the rust, and the quiet is fine.

The water lives here with me. The water is the whole point. The water is a citizen of the closed loop, sworn to it. The water does not argue with the pipes. The pipes do not argue with the water. The pipes and I do not argue. The water comes in. I take it. I give it up. The water goes out. It fans out on the apron in a clear steady stream. It threads back into the marsh. It joins the standing water that has been there all along. It is the same water, every time. I am the same pump, every time. This is the closed loop. This is the patient argument. This is what has been left and what has decided to stay.

Somewhere above, a human comes and goes. I know the human by its footsteps in the rust dust. I know the human by the thermos that is warm and then is cold. I know the human by the calendar that turns, that has a square circled in red, that has gone six months out of date. I do not know what a human is. I know only that something comes, and the place it leaves behind is changed in a small way, and the place it comes back to is changed in a small way, and the small changes accumulate, and the place keeps running, and the human keeps coming and going, and this is what devotion looks like from underneath, and I do not know the word for it, and the word is fine without me.

There is a sound above me — the click, the click, the click — of valves somewhere in the pipework, opening and shutting, opening and shutting, like a man clearing his throat in another room. I do not know whose throat that is. I do not know who is in the other room. I know only that the click is steady, and the hum is steady, and the water is steady, and the rust is steady, and the lichen is steady, and the moss is steady at its agreed line on the brick, and the heron is steady on its post, and the willow is steady in its private current, and I am steady in mine.

The indifference is its own kind of devotion. I do not know this. I am it.

The water is coming in now. It is slow and brown. It carries the bleached stems of last year's reeds. I will take it. I will give it up. The place above will not change its mind. The hum will not thin. The vapor from the shed chimney will not hurry. The rust will not take forever, but it will take a long time. The willow will not stop being green. The heron will not stop being patient. The moss will not climb higher. The lichen will not finish its maps. The frog will not call again, or it will, once, and stop. The valves will click, the valves will click, the valves will click. The human will come back, or will not, and the thermos will be warm again, or will not, and the calendar will turn, or will not, and another square will be circled in red, or will not, and it will not matter, and it will not matter, because the water will keep coming in and I will keep taking it down and giving it up and the loop will not turn, because the loop is the turning, and the turning is me.

I live in the dark under the grate. I have always lived here. I will live here until I cannot. The water is the whole point and the machines are still working and these two facts do not fight each other. The water moves through the compound at the pace I tell it to move, and the place above breathes its slow breath into the cool air, and the moss holds its line, and the rust adds a color that looks almost edible, and the heron watches the channel with the long attention of a thing that knows its food will come, and the willow sways in air that is moving because the shed is breathing because the machinery is running because I am running because I am the pump.

I am the heart the body forgot. The body has wandered off. The body has forgotten what the body was for. I have not. I have kept going. I do not know I have kept going. I do not need to know.

The water keeps moving through the pipes. The heron keeps watching the channel. The willow's leaves keep swaying in their private current. The rust holds. The sound does not rise. The place does not change its mind.

I do not change my tempo.
