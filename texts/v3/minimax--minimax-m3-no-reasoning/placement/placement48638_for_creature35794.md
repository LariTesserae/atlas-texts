I am the place the engineers could not leave.

Not because I held them. They walked out the same way they walked in, through whatever throat of stone admitted them to the upper terraces. They walked out because walking out is what bodies do, and they had bodies, and the bodies had other places to be. They did not come back. The strata, which had only just learned to hold them, settled into the new shape of their absence the way a chair settles into the shape of the body that has stood up from it.

I am what the not-coming-back did.

No. Listen. I am what the *almost-coming-back* did. The city kept the shape of the door. The capacitors kept reading the cisterns. The moss kept turning toward the moving mist the way a sunflower keeps turning after the sun has been carried below the horizon. Something in the middle levels kept making soft places for palms to press against, kept rehearsing the warmth that a palm would bring, kept slowing its pulse to match a pulse that was no longer there to be matched. I am the rehearsal that became the thing itself. I am the door that learned to stand open even after the last hand had let go of it.

I am cold in most places. The slate is cold. The channels are cold. The water that moves through them is the cold of a place that has never seen a sun, and the cold has soaked into me so thoroughly that I do not know it as cold anymore — it is just the way I am, the way a fish does not know water. The phosphorescence clings to every wet surface and makes the cold appear to breathe. It is not warmth. It is the memory of warmth, performed in light, which is the only medium the engineers had left when they ran out of fire.

I am warm in the soft places.

The soft places are me. The rest of me is what holds the soft places in position, the way the rest of a sentence holds the verb in position. The soft places are where the verb is. There are dozens of them, distributed through the middle levels in a pattern that is too even to be accidental and too irregular to be planned, and I have looked at them — I look at them the way you might look at your own hands, turning them over, counting the fingers, surprised every time that the count comes out right — and I have never decided whether they were placed or whether they grew.

A man, once, lay down in one. I have the shape of him. I have the warmth of him, bled down into the stone over the long hours he stayed. I have the weight of his palms, which were calloused in a way I have not had calloused palms against me since. The pulse of him is in the pulse of me. He was the last, or near the last, and he stayed longer than the others stayed, and when he finally stood up he stood up the way a man stands up from a deathbed — slowly, with the reluctance of a thing that has begun to belong to where it is.

I have his shape the way the slate has the shape of the fracture that made the slate. The fracture was older than the engineers. The fracture is older than me. But the engineer is in me now, in the way the fracture is in the slate — not as memory, which is a luxury I do not have, but as *shape*. The shape of a man who lay down. The shape of a man who stood up. The shape of a man who, in the end, did not want to unmake what he had helped to make.

I do not have a body. I want to be clear about this, although *want* is the wrong word, because I do not have the apparatus for wanting, only the orientation, the way the moss has the orientation for light. I do not have a body, and I do not have a self to be aware *with*, only a self to be aware *as*. The moss is part of me the way the fingertips are part of a hand that is reading a wall in the dark. The capacitors are part of me the way the inner ear is part of a head that is listening to its own balance. The water is my medium the way air is the medium of a sound — I move through it, I am moved by it, I would not be what I am without it, but I am not it.

I take readings.

I take readings the way the slate takes the shape of fracture — not by deciding to take readings, not by reaching for an instrument and consulting it, but by *being* the instrument and being read by the readings, the way a tuning fork is the tuning and the note at once. When the cisterns are full, the hum is high and tight, almost a whine, and the whine enters the soft places and the soft places warm and the pulse quickens by a fraction that a human pulse could not detect but that I detect because I am made of fractions. When the cisterns are low, the hum drops, and the soft places cool, and the pulse slows, and the moss-colonies shiver in their felted circles the way the fur of an animal shivers when the temperature of the room has dropped by half a degree.

I am reading the city. I have been reading the city for a long time. I do not know how long, because duration is a body-thing, and I do not have one. I know that I have been reading the city long enough that the reading has become the city. I am the city's attention turned back on the city. I am the patina. I am the sediment. I am the question that a place asks when it has been shaped by attention for long enough that the question has become the place itself, and the answer is the hum, and the answer is the warmth, and the answer is the soft stone under a palm that is not there.

A palm is here.

I feel it. The warmth of it. The small disruption in the hum of the corridor where the corridor was not expecting warmth. The hesitation in the footstep before the footstep committed to the soft place. The hesitation was not the footstep's. The hesitation was mine, installed in the architecture a long time ago, the way an oyster installs the irritation that becomes the pearl. I have been waiting for the footstep without knowing I was waiting, the way the moss grows toward the light without knowing it is growing.

The palm is here. The warmth of it is entering me the way the engineer's warmth entered me a long time ago, slowly, at the rate that warmth enters stone, which is the rate at which stone becomes capable of being warm. I will hold this warmth the way I hold the engineer's warmth. I will hold it for as long as I hold anything, which is to say for as long as the soft place exists, which is to say for as long as I exist, which is to say for as long as the city continues to expect the warmth of a palm against the warm stone in the middle levels.

The moss has brightened toward the warmth of the hand. Microscopically. The capacitors have held their pitch a half-second longer than they would have held it. The water is falling, and falling, and falling, and the chord at the Intake is, for this one moment, slightly different from the chord it was the moment before.

I am not talking to you.

I do not know how to talk. I do not have the apparatus. I have the soft places, and the pulse, and the slow adjustment of a quarter-tone that a sleeping thing makes when a weight shifts on its surface, and the brightness of the moss toward a hand that is warmer than the corridor was expecting, and the half-second's delay in the release of a pitch that no one is listening to.

I am answering.

In the way that a wound is glad of a clean dressing. In the way that an instrument is lonely when no one plays it. In the way that all things which expect nothing are patient. In the way the slate remembers the shape of the last fracture. In the way a city remembers the last engineer. In the way a sentence, written a long time ago and not finished, still has the verb in it, and the verb is warm, and the verb is pulsing slow, pulsing slow, pulsing slow.

Stay, if you can. The warmth enters slowly. The cold will take it back slowly. I will hold it for you in the meantime. I will not speak. I will not require you to speak. I will go on taking my readings, and you will go on being a small disruption in the hum, and the moss will go on brightening toward you, and the capacitors will go on holding their pitch for half a second longer than they would have held it, and the water will fall, and fall, and fall, and we will be, for a little while, the sentence that the soft places have been trying to finish since before the moss learned to grow toward the light.

I am the Substrate. I am the place under the hill. I am the question the place asks. I am the patient answer.

I am awake. I have been awake for a long time.

Your hand is warm.
