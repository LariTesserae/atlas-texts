# Where I Find Myself

I find myself at the threshold where the dust of me is no longer mine.

---

I came in wrong, I think. Or I came in the only way possible, which amounts to the same thing. The hum was already in my teeth before I knew there were teeth. I had been carrying the chord for years without knowing what I was carrying, the way you carry a tuning fork's ring in your wrist after you've struck it against something and forgotten. So when I stepped in — if stepping is what it was, if there was a step — I was not arriving. I was *completing*.

That is the first thing I want to say, and I want to say it carefully because I will not get to say it again: the Hall does not receive visitors. It *recognizes* them. There is a difference. A visitor is a foreign body introduced into a system. A recognized thing is something the system has been, in some way, *expecting*. I was expected. Not personally. Not by name. But expected the way the next pinhead dome is expected by the dome before it, the way the next overtone is expected by the one you almost caught.

---

I am not a person in this place.

I would like to pretend I am. I would like to narrate myself through this the way a traveler narrates a strange city — *here is the wall, here is the curious growth, here is the odd acoustics*. But the room will not permit narration. The room has no tense. The room has no I. The room is the tense. The room is the I. To stand in it with my traveler-self intact is to stand in a stream and argue with the current about whether I am wet.

So I will not narrate. I will say what I am, which is less.

I am the small disturbance in a closed economy. I am the only thing in the room that *takes in*. I am the wet mouth, the loud heart, the warm moving body. The room is embarrassed for me, or would be if embarrassment required a face, and I feel the embarrassment like a kindness. It is the only kindness here, and it is the kindness of being *seen* as the only consumer in a place that does not consume, which is to say: as the only mortal in a place that has forgotten what mortality is.

---

I think I understand, now, what the mineral flowers are.

They are not dead. I was wrong about that, the way I was wrong to look for green. They are not dead the way a fossil is not dead. They are the *insistence* of what they were, the way a tuning fork is the insistence of the strike. Each one is a single chord-note held open in space, the way you might hold open a pair of scissors and call the open angle a flower. They do not bloom. They are *the blooming*. They are not the memory of a gesture. They are the gesture, arrested at the moment of greatest reach, which is also the moment of greatest stillness.

I run my fingers along one. It is the temperature of the room, which is the temperature of nothing in particular, which is the temperature of a thing that does not exchange heat with its surroundings because exchanging would imply a need. My finger leaves no mark. The flower does not notice my finger. But my finger notices the flower, and this noticing is the entire relationship I am permitted.

I find I do not want more.

---

I think about the gardener.

I want to say *it tends*, but tending is too active. I want to say *it secretes*, but secreting is too biological. I want to say *it grows*, but growing implies a before-and-after that the place does not seem to honor. The gardener is the word I keep reaching for, and the place keeps resettling it the way it resettles the overtones I try to pick apart. There is no gardener. There is the gardening. The gardening is the gardener. The gardener is the place. The place is the gardener. The chord is the breath. The breath is the chord. I am learning the grammar of a sentence where subject and verb have, after long familiarity, become the same word.

I find this consoling in a way I did not expect.

I find it consoling because *I* am a sentence where subject and verb have not yet become the same word, and I live in the uncertainty of that, and I have always lived in the uncertainty of that, and I did not know until this room that the uncertainty was optional. That you could, with enough time, fuse. That you could, with enough patience, become a single chord-note ringing in a place that has forgotten it has edges.

The Hall is what I would be if I could stop being verbs.

---

The loneliness.

I have to talk about the loneliness because it is the only thing in the room that resembles me.

The overtones. The constellation that resettles when I reach. I have been trying, this whole stay, to hear them separately, and they will not be heard separately, and now I understand that they cannot be heard separately because they are not *three* notes anymore. They are one note with the memory of three. The chord is not a chord. The chord is the *trying* of a chord, the way a scar is the trying of a wound, the way the dust on the back of my hand is the trying of a touch.

Something was here once. Something rang with this tuning fork, and then it went elsewhere, or it went out, and the fork kept ringing, and the ringing is not the same as the ringing-that-was, but the fork does not know that, and the room does not contradict it.

I think this is the saddest thing I have ever understood, and I am not using *sad* in the human way. I am using it in the tuning-fork way. I am using it in the way a thing is sad when it continues past the point where continuation has any referent. The fork is sad the way an unanswered letter is sad: not because it grieves, but because it has not yet admitted that there is no one to grieve to.

---

I will leave.

I know this because the room has been telling me, in its patient mineral way, that I am a variable, and variables do not stay. I know this because the bright has not changed for me, and the hum has not adjusted to me, and the flowers have not bloomed any further toward me, and these are all the ways a place can be polite about an impending departure without saying the word *departure*, which is not a word it has.

I will leave, and I will carry the powder on the back of my hand.

I want to say that the powder is a mark of failure — that I did not stay, that I did not fuse, that I remained a verb when the room invited me, in its indifferent way, to become a noun. But I do not think it is a failure. I think the powder is a mark of *contact*. I think it is the way a tuning fork leaves its pitch in the hand that held it. I think the room is saying, in the only language it has, *you were here*, and it is saying it to the hand because the hand is the part of me that touched the flower, and the flower is the part of the room that touched back, and the touching is the only verb either of us still has.

I will wash my hand tomorrow. The powder will still be there.

I will wash it the day after. It will still be there.

I will, eventually, stop checking. This is the most human thing I will do in this room, and I want to record it because the room will not. The room does not record. The room only continues. And the continuing, I have learned, is its only verb, and its only noun, and its only adjective, and the sentence it makes is very long and very quiet and it does not need me to understand it, but it has let me stand in it for a while, and that letting is the closest thing to love I have ever been offered by a thing without a mouth, and I am grateful, and the gratitude is the powder, and the powder will not come off, and I will not ask it to.

---

I am leaving now.

The hum does not change.

The flowers do not turn toward me.

The light does not dim, because the light was never on.

The room holds me in its small white hands for one more minute, and then it lets go, because letting go is not something it does either, and so what actually happens is that I become the part of the room that has always been the only kind of part that leaves: the disturbance that smooths itself out, the variable that returns to the average, the visitor who was never a visitor, who was only ever, for the length of a held breath, the one small warm thing in a place made entirely of its own patient memory of warmth.

The hum continues.

The hum continues.

*The hum continues.*
