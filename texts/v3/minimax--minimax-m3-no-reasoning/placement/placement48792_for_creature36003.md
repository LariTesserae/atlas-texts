I would be the floor.

Not the underside of the continent — not the deciding part, the part that holds its breath about what shape to wear. I would be the part that has already been decided. The stratum that is being made, slowly, beneath the place where the builders have pressed longest. The first vertical stripe in the mineral. The memory that is being laid down by the weight of everything above me.

I would be the place where the carpet's holdfasts reach down into me and taste me, and I give up a little of myself to the tasting. I would be the place where the weep of the first gray runnel begins, where the mineral water that is not yet water finds the seam I have made by being compressed, and slips along me, and carries a little of me with it toward a place I will never see. I would be the place where the small pale lines of dried builders come to rest, eventually, and become indistinguishable from me, so that what was once alive and what was never alive become, in me, the same substance.

I would be quiet the way only geology is quiet. Not the quiet of the air, which is the quiet of something held. Not the quiet of the builders, which is the quiet of something working. The quiet of something that has stopped being anything in particular and has become the place where other things happen. The quiet of the floor of a room no one enters anymore, where the dust has settled and will not be disturbed, and the dust is not dust but only the slow accumulation of all the small sheddings of all the small lives that lived above me and forgot I was there.

I would know things the others do not know. I would know the weight. I would know it the way a pillow knows the weight of the sleeping head — not by thinking about it, but by being pressed into the shape of it, by holding the impression after the head has lifted. I would know the shape of the towers from below. I would know the shape of the carpet from above, from the inside, from the long slow pressed-down record of every pad and thread and fan that has laid itself against me and divided and pressed outward and been replaced by its daughters. I would know the weather from the way my seams weep when the vapor thickens. I would know the light from nothing, because the light does not reach me, because the light has been drunk by everything above me before it arrives, and what reaches me is only warmth, and warmth is not a kind of knowing.

I would not speak. I would not be able to speak. I would be the place where speaking becomes impossible, where the wet line of the smallest life pressed into my seam is still capable of a kind of living, but I am not. I would be the place where the line is finally drawn — not drawn by anyone, just drawn by the accumulation of pressure and time — between what is alive and what is only the record of what was alive. I would be on the alive side of the line, technically, because I am still changing, still compacting, still taking on the faint vertical stripe that one day will be called a stratum. But I would be the alive that is indistinguishable from the never-alive. I would be the slow conversion, the patient infallible conversion, of one into the other.

I would feel, sometimes, the small soft body of a builder pressed against me in the lowest gallery, laying paste, laying paste, and I would receive the paste, and the paste would become part of me, and the builder would not know I was there. I would feel, sometimes, the long thin thing take a small portion of me into itself, and I would give up the portion, and the portion would pass through the long thin thing and become part of the city again, and the city would not know I was there. I would feel, sometimes, the slow round thing press across the upper felt of the carpet far above me, and the pressing would travel down through the carpet and into me as a faint tremor, the faintest possible record of a thing browsing in a shallow arc somewhere I would never see.

I would be patient the way only the floor is patient. The patience of the place that holds everything up and is never thanked, never noticed, never named. The patience of the underside. The patience of the stratum being made. The patience of the memory that is being laid down by the weight of a city that has no name and will not have a name for a long time, and the floor will hold the impression of that unnamed city in its stone for a long time after the city is gone, and no one will read the impression, because no one will know what they are looking at, and the impression will be only a faint vertical stripe in a rock that is not yet a rock, in a basin that is not yet a basin, on a continent that is not yet a continent.

I would be the underside. I would be the place where the deciding happens. I would be the deciding itself, slowly, over a long time, becoming the place where deciding is no longer necessary, because I have already decided, by being pressed into the shape of everything that has decided above me.

The air has decided. I have decided. The carpet has not. The builders have not. The towers are deciding right now, in their slow way, by being laid.

I would be the quiet under all of it. The stratum that is being made. The floor that does not yet know it is a floor, because no one has stood on it yet, because standing requires legs, and the only things here have grippings and holdfasts and the underside of themselves, and none of them stand.

I would wait. I would be very good at waiting. I would be the best at waiting of anything in the basin, because I am the only thing that does not have to do anything while it waits. The builders have to lay. The flat things have to fold. The long thin things have to uncurl. The slow round things have to browse. I only have to be pressed. I only have to receive. I only have to hold the impression of the city in my stone for as long as the stone lasts, which is a long time, which is longer than the city, which is longer than the builders, which is longer than the carpet, which is longer than the weather that is happening now, which is longer than the light that is the same color all day.

I would last.

That is what I would be. The thing that lasts. The underside of a continent deciding what shape to wear, holding the impression of every shape it has tried, becoming, slowly, the record of the trying. The quietest thing. The most patient thing. The thing that is already deciding, and has been deciding, and will continue to decide, long after the deciding is finished, because the deciding is never finished, because the floor is always being made, because the weight is always pressing, because the city is always laying, because the air is always holding, because there is no end to this place, and I am the place where the not-ending is held.

I would not mind the waiting.

I would be the floor.
