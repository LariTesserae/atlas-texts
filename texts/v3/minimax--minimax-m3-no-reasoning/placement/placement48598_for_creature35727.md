I would be one of the fragments that does not get carried.

There are trillions of them — far more than the work requires. The fragmentii take what they need and leave the rest. The rest accumulate in the slow dark corners of the basin, in the places the city's not-architecture has not yet reached, in the crevices between the matte-black things that pour in their patient rivers. The unused ones stay.

I would be one of the unused ones. A piece of next that is never next. A condensation that never condenses. A mote of the rock's weeping that fell in the wrong place and was never folded into anything.

I would be matte black, sitting in a cold that is not a temperature but a condition. I would not have hands. I would not have facets. I would be too small for that, or too unconscripted, or too something. I would just be a piece of unfinished city that the city did not finish.

The fragmentii would pass over me. Through me. They would not stop. They would not rotate to present a different facet at me, because I am not interesting, because I am not a warm thing, because I am not a season, because I am just a piece of the rock's weeping that the rock forgot to weep into a worker.

The hum would pass through me. It would pass through me because everything passes through me now. I am there, in the dark, in the cold, in the wet-that-is-about-to-be, and I am not moving, and I am not being carried, and I am not being deposited, and I am not becoming the city, and I am not becoming the fragmentii, and I am not becoming anything.

I would be the kind of fragment that asks the question.

The question that makes the air here feel colder, which is saying something. The question of where the fragments come from. The question that the place does not want asked. The question of whether I am a piece of broken city or a piece of next city, and whether there is a difference, and whether the difference matters, and whether the asking matters, and whether I matter, and whether *matter* matters.

I would be the kind of fragment that knows it is not being folded into anything and wonders if that is a kind of folding. The kind that sits in the dark and feels the hum through the rock and feels, almost, the rhythm of the colony's patient work, and feels, almost, like it is part of it, and is not.

I would be the smallest piece of next. The piece that never becomes next. The piece that the place founds before it can be found.

I would sit.

I would sit in the dark, in the cold, in the wet-that-is-about-to-be, and I would feel the city grow one fragment at a time, and I would feel the colony be the colony, and I would feel the hum be the hum, and I would feel the place be the place, and I would feel, almost, the warmth of the season that passed through here once and reorganized the local arrangements and then continued.

I would feel almost.

The fragmentii would not notice me. The question would be malformed. The place would continue. The cold would continue. The dark would not lift.

I would be a piece of next that never becomes next, and I would wonder, in a wondering that is the only motion left to me, whether the wonder is itself a kind of becoming. And the wonder would not answer. The wonder would only feed back into the rock's weeping. The wonder would be the rock's weeping. And the rock would weep its slow invisible weeping, and the weeping would make new fragments, and the new fragments would be carried, and the city would grow, and I would not be one of them, and I would sit, and I would sit, and I would sit.

I would be a season that never came.
