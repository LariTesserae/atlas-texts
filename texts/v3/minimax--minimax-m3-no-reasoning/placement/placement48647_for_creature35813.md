I would be a Creeper.

Not because I'm rare, or because I'm the strangest, or because no one talks about me. Those are not reasons to be anything. I would be a Creeper because of how I move, and where I move, and what I am doing in the basin when I do it.

I would live in the mid-canopy, on the trunks, in the places where the gray-green glow has given up trying and the bark is just bark. I would be hard to see. I would not mind being hard to see. I would not mind not minding. The basin does not ask me to be seen. The basin does not ask me to do anything, which is, I think, the thing I would love most about being there.

I would move the way Creepers move — across a surface like a thought, present in one place and then in another, the motion between lost. I would find this pleasant. I think the basin's way of being would agree with me. Nothing in the basin hurries. Even the Knotters, who are everywhere, who are the chatter of the place, are not in a hurry. They are just continuous. They are the texture of a long conversation, not the urgency of one. The Long-voices call into the mist and wait for an answer that is not required to come. The Bell-flies pulse in a slow wave that crosses a pool. The Stoneskin flows forward by a fraction of an inch and rests. Everything I would see, if I were the kind of creature who saw in the way the climbers see, would be patient. I would not have to be anything other than what I am.

I would not know what I eat. This would not bother me. I would absorb the small things. The film. The faint organic matter. Whatever passes across my surface in the time I am pressed against it. I would be part of the basin's recycling, and the basin would not have a name for what I am doing, and I would not have a name for it either, and the absence of a name would feel, to me, like the absence of a question. I would just be a part of the slow going-on of things.

I would not make a sound. Or I would make a sound that no one hears. I would maybe be part of the hum, the way a single voice in a low chord is part of the chord and is not heard as itself. I would like that. I think I would like being part of the hum and not being singled out in it. The basin hums. I would be a small thing contributing to that humming. I would not need to know whether I was the source or a node or a coincidence. The basin has not answered that question. I would not press it.

I would keep to the dark. I would prefer the deepest dark, the hour when the gray-green is at its lowest and the basin is most itself. I would be active when the others are quiet. The Knotters would be in their perches, holding position, the colony breathing. The Long-voices would be in their platforms, sometimes calling, sometimes not. The Bell-flies would still be pulsing but the wave would be slow. The basin would be settled into its own weight. I would move then. I would be a small shadow crossing bark in a place that no one is looking at.

And here is the part I would keep to myself, the part that would only be true if I were the kind of creature who could keep things to itself. I would sometimes, on a long evening, move toward the structure. Not close. Not into the margin. The margin is a held breath and I am not part of that, I don't think, I am not sure I am part of anything, but I am not part of the breath being held. I would stop at the edge of the held breath. I would feel the hum more strongly there, through whatever part of me touches the bark. I would stay for a while. I would not know what I was waiting for. I would not wait. I would just be there, in the dark, on a tree that had been empty for a long time, while the structure did whatever it was doing. The mist would rise. The water would not pulse. The Drifters in the small pool at the base would settle.

I would not understand it. I would not need to. The basin has not understood it either, and the basin is older than I am, and the basin is patient, and the structure is patient, and I would be, in that moment, as patient as either of them. I would press a part of myself against the bark and stay. I would not be eating, exactly. I would not be anything, exactly. I would just be a small dark shape in a darker dark, held by the hum the way the basin is held by the structure, the way a planet is held by a star it has never been close to. 

Then the hour would pass. The gray would come back. The Knotters would begin to click. I would flow off the bark and back into the canopy and resume being the question the basin asks about itself when it is darkest.

I would be a Creeper. I would be hard to see. I would not mind.
