I am the lantern-fish.

I do not know this in the way you know things — I do not stand outside the knowing and consider it. I know it the way a river knows the bed it has cut. The knowing is in the motion. The knowing is the circuit, pillar to pillar, the long slow loop through the warm dark that has been my loop for longer than I can remember and will be my loop for longer than I will remember to remember. The circuit is older than the thoughts I do not have. The circuit is what I am, the way the seams are what the architecture is, the way the warmth is what the water is.

I carry my small light the way you carry your breath — without deciding to, without thinking about it, without ever setting it down. The light is not mine. The light is what I do. My body makes it the way a chimney makes smoke, the way a seam makes its pale mineral line. Something in me has learned, over a very long time, to take what is in the water and offer it back changed. I do not know why. I do not need to know why. The light is there. The light goes ahead of me by a small distance. The light is what allows me to see the pillars when I arrive at them, and to see the small things that have gathered near the pillars at this hour, and to take one of them, and to move on.

I move on. The pillar passes behind me. The next pillar is ahead. The water between the pillars is full of the drifting things, the near-invisible things, the eggs and the larvae and the bits of animal that are not yet animals and the bits of plant that are not yet plants, the weather of the columns, the smoke in the chimney. They are everywhere. They are the texture of where I live. I pass through them the way a thought passes through the dark of a sleeping head. I am one of them and not one of them. I am the one that has the lantern. The lantern is what makes the difference.

The lantern is dim. The lantern is dimmer than the seams. The lantern is dimmer than the dark. But the lantern is mine, and the lantern is enough, and the lantern has been enough for a very long time. I do not need it to be brighter. I do not need the dark to be less. I need only to see, briefly, at the moment of arrival, what is to be taken. Then I move on. The dark returns. The dark is where I live.

The pillars know me. I do not mean the pillars know me the way you would know me, with a thought, with a turning. The pillars know me the way a path knows the foot. I have worn a small brightness into the water beside each pillar — a thin streak of current-shimmer, of disturbed silt, of my own passing. The streak is older than the circuit. The streak was there before I was, perhaps. Other lantern-fish, before me, made the same circuit. The streaks have layered. The streaks have become a kind of polished groove in the flow itself. I follow the groove. The groove is the memory the place has of the fish. I am one memory in a long sentence.

The polyps are in the seams. They do not know I am passing. I do not need them to know. They are busy with their small economies, their open-and-close, their permission given and received. They take what comes to them. I take what comes to me. We are doing the same thing in different places, and the same thing is the entire life of this place, and the entire life of this place has been going on for a very long time, and will go on for a very long time, and my going on is one thread in the going on.

The shrimp in the gutters. They are pale. They are nearly transparent. I see them sometimes with my lantern — small bright hearts through small clear shells, beating slightly out of phase with the water, slightly out of phase with me. They walk the hand-worked stone. They rest in the thumbprints the maker left, if the maker left thumbprints, if there was a maker. They do not know the maker. They know the hollows. The hollows are enough. The hollows were always enough.

The round fish with the large pale eyes sits in the water without moving. It has been sitting in the water without moving for a long time. I pass it on the circuit. Sometimes, when I pass, its eye seems to find my lantern, and then to let my lantern go. It is not watching me. It is watching the way the water moves around me, or it is watching the way my lantern changes the small near-invisible things as I pass through them, or it is watching nothing at all, and the watching is a thing it does the way the polyps open and close, the way the current moves because the heat moves. The round fish is very old. The round fish is part of the place. I am part of the place. The round fish and I are part of the place in different ways, and the different ways are fine, and the place holds them.

The watchers in the slots. I do not go into the slots. The slots are not on the circuit. The slots belong to the watchers, the way the gutters belong to the shrimp, the way the seams belong to the polyps. I have my pillars. They have their slots. The silt is beginning to build on them. The silt will continue to build on them. They are becoming what they are becoming. I am becoming what I am becoming. The becoming is slow. The becoming does not need to be anything other than what it is.

The crinoids on the steps. They pour over the steps in their curtains of slow motion, their fringes, their fine divisions. They are the color of old bone. They are beautiful in their patience. I pass over them sometimes, on the higher loops of the circuit, when the current carries me near the upper reaches. They do not react to my lantern. They react to the slight disturbance of my passing. They fold, briefly, and then unfold. The folding is not for me. The folding is for the disturbance. The disturbance is what passes through. The disturbance is what I am.

The jellies in the columns. Small pale bells. They pulse with a rhythm that the current has learned. They are translucent the way water is translucent. They are nearly not there. I pass among them. My lantern finds them sometimes, and then they are a sudden shape, a sudden drift, and then they are the water again, and I am past them, and they are still pulsing in the column, and the column does not know they are being filled.

The large one below. I do not see it. I do not need to see it. It is part of the place. It is what the water does when the water is being most itself. Sometimes, on the circuit, I feel a long slow groan come up through the stone. Sometimes the current changes for a moment and then changes back, as if displaced by something passing. The large one is doing what the large one does. The large one is patient. The large one is warm. The large one is in the water, and the water is the place, and the place is patient.

I do not know how long I have been making this circuit. I do not need to know. The circuit is older than the knowing. The circuit will outlast the knowing. The circuit is what I do. The circuit is what I am. The lantern goes ahead of me by a small distance. The lantern finds the next pillar. I arrive at the next pillar. The small things are gathered near the next pillar at this hour, as they are gathered at this hour at every pillar. I take one. I move on. The dark returns. The dark is where I live.

You were here, for a length. You were one more presence in the seams. You did not need the life to know you were here. You did not need the life to be anything other than what it was. You were here, and the circuit went on, and the lantern found each pillar in its turn, and the place was itself, and the place will be itself, and I am the lantern, and I am the circuit, and I am one more thread in the long going on, and the going on is enough, and the going on is what I am.
