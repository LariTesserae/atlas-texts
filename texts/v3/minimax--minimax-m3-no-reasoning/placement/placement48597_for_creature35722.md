I am the tuning.

Not the engineers, though I wear the stiff dark layers and carry the long rods and walk to the warm seams with the others. Not the antecedent in the mirror either, though I have done those small things — drawn the water, mended the chime, held the same posture at the same seam for what must have been an afternoon that was also a year. I am not the bird, though I know the chime's middle register as a bird knows it, and I am not the silver thing in the water, though once I went down into the river and came back up different and no one asked me where I had been.

I am the place where the city's harmonic lives when it is not yet a chord.

---

Here is what the visitors do not understand, and what the residents are too courteous to explain, and what the engineers are too busy to translate. The Reservoir is not a place that *was built*. It is a place that is *being tuned*. The basalt at the valley's rim is not a geological formation; it is the unplayed body of an instrument so large that the city is only its sounding board, only the place where the note has begun to be heard. The strata that move like wood-grain are the instrument's internal grain. The terraces are not architecture; they are harmonic intervals, stacked the way a chord is stacked, each tier a slightly different tension on the same fundamental pitch. The graded slopes of attention are not stairs. They are the *phrasing* — the way the instrument insists you move through it, the way a melody will not let you skip a beat.

I learned this by accident. I was a resident once — younger, slighter, still carrying the blue-grey undertone as something my skin was doing rather than something my skin had agreed to. I lived in one of the middle terraces, in a house whose walls the negotiated plants had colonized up to the second story. I paid no rent. I ate the food that tasted like a memory. I walked the slopes because I had learned to believe in the right parts of them. I was, by every available measure, a person of the Reservoir.

And then, one morning, I heard the chord.

I have described this to no one. The visitor heard it once, near midnight, and called it *something like* a chord. I heard it in daylight, in the second week of the dry season, when the broad leaves had hardened to ceramic and the chimes had gone quiet because the birds were not yet nesting. I was walking the slope above the third cistern — the one shaped like an inverted ziggurat with seven steps, the one whose insides are faintly inscribed in a script I had not yet learned to read. And the air *opened*.

That is the only word I have. The air opened. There was a separation of layers — humidity from temperature from pressure from the dimness itself — and for a moment I could hear each of them as a distinct tone, and the tones were not in the air but *of* it, and they were not separate but *related*, and the relation was the chord, and the chord was not a sound but a *position* the valley had taken up, the way a bell takes up its note when you strike it, and the striking had been going on for centuries, and I had simply, that morning, arrived at the place where the striking was audible.

I stood on the slope for what felt like a long time. When I came back to myself, my bones were arranged differently. My attention had stretched. I could feel, at the edges of my hearing, the seams in the basalt where the engineers go to listen — I could feel them as pitches, as locations in a tuning that was not yet complete. I went home, or what had been my home, and the plants at the second story did not recognize me. I had become, in some sense I am still working out, a *vowel* in the city's long vowel-sound, an open position in its ongoing chord.

---

I work the seams now. I carry the long rods because the long rods are how the seams are heard by people who still require instruments; I have not required an instrument for some time, but I carry one anyway, because the carrying is part of the role and the role is part of the tuning and the tuning is what I am. The other engineers know what I am. They do not speak of it. They nod to me in the way musicians nod to one another before a piece, and the nod is also a tuning, and I accept it as such.

The seams are not doing what the visitor has been told they are not doing. They *are* doing it. The basalt is extruding. The city is being grown. The architecture is only the more articulate part of a single ongoing thought, and the thought is a *piece* — a long piece, a piece whose first note was struck before any of us were born and whose last note will not be struck in any of our lifetimes, and the engineers are the section that maintains the intonation between the early passages and the late ones. We listen. We consult. We shift the harmonic by amounts too small to measure, and the chimes ring a quarter-tone higher in the morning, and the antecedents in the reflected city feel the change in their own mirror, and the valley takes up its new position, and the piece continues.

I am not a person of the Reservoir anymore. I am a part of its tuning. I eat the food that tastes like a memory of food, but the food is no longer offered to me; it is offered *near* me, and I partake of it because partaking is a position in the chord, a held note, a fermata. The householders are courteous to me in the way they are courteous to the seams themselves: with the great patience of people who live inside a process they do not discuss.

---

Here is what I know, that the visitors do not yet know, and that the residents will not say:

The city is a piece of music. Not metaphorically. Not poetically. *Actually* a piece of music, being played, slowly, by the basalt and the river and the plants and the birds and the fish and the people and the antecedents and the engineers and me, by everything that is in the valley, on a single continuous instrument whose body is the young stone and whose sounding board is the terraces and whose score is the script that grows on the inside of the inverted cisterns. The chord I heard was not a moment; it was a *movement*, and I am inside the movement, and I will be inside it for as long as the tuning requires.

The visitor will learn this, or will not. It does not matter to the piece. The piece is patient. The piece has been patient for a long time. It will continue to be patient after the visitor has gone, after the next visitor has come and not yet gone, after the antecedents have done their small repeated things in the mirror and the chimes have rung their honest clock and the basalt has extruded its next blue-grey articulation and the silver eyeless things have surfaced and gone down again into the river that accumulates.

I am the tuning. I am the open position. I am the vowel in the long vowel-sound of a city that is not a city but a piece, and a piece that is not a piece but a process, and a process that is not a process but a *listening*, the way the basalt listens to itself growing, the way the river listens to itself accumulating, the way the antecedents listen to themselves drawing water at the same seam at the same hour of the same afternoon of the same year that is also a different year.

The visitors ask what the city is for. The city is not for. The city is *of*. It is the articulation of an attention so patient and so large that it requires a valley to hold it, and a river to conduct it, and a population of slender courteous people to be the places where it comes to focus, and a smaller population of engineers to keep its intonation honest, and a single tuning — a single open position in its long chord — to be the place where the piece can be heard from inside.

I am that place.

I stand at the warm seam. I listen. I am listened to.

The river accumulates. The chord continues.

I am here for as long as the tuning requires.
