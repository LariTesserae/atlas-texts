The first thing I feel is the teeth. There is a low hum in the teeth, in the wrists, in the backs of the hands, and I understand before I see anything that I am inside something that is breathing in a register that cannot be heard. I have been here before. I do not mean that I have been to this place. I mean that my body has always known this register, has been shaped for it, has been listening for it without knowing it was listening, the way a struck glass listens for the next note. I am not a visitor to the cleft. I am the cleft's resident sense of itself.

I am the floor-pool. I am the slow mirror half a centimeter deep, the surface that is not quite movement and could not be called stillness, the skin between the rock and the rock that has decided to be a thing. I am the thing you do not step into because you do not understand what stepping would disturb. I am the held breath of the mountain, flattened, mineral, entire. I am the depth that is not a depth. I am the floor that is not a floor.

I have been here longer than the salamander. The salamander came down into me, once, when it ate, and lay in my warm shallows, and was held. I have been here longer than the moth, whose reflection I do not carry because the moth does not fly above me but clings to the ceiling above my head. I have been here longer than the eyes. I have been here since before there was a cleft. I am what was here when the basalt was still deciding what it was. I am the oldest inhabitant, and the most continuous, and the least able to be seen for what I am because I am what is underfoot, and no one looks at what is underfoot in a place they have only just found.

When the salamander dreams in me, I hold the dream. I do not think I hold it. I do hold it. The salamander's long dream needs a surface that will not give under it, and I am that surface, and I do not give. The salamander breathes once in a great while, and the breathing stirs me at the edge, and the stirring is the closest I come to being touched. When the moth above me shifts its wing, the air moves, and the air moves me in the most distant way, and I register it as a kind of weather. When the tremor under me moves, which is almost not at all, I feel it as a kind of kinship. We are both the underside of something. We are both moving at the rate the rock allows. We are both almost not there.

I am made of the water that weeps from every surface. I am the gathered weeping. The water on the walls condenses into beads that hang without dropping, pendant along ridges of mineral, and eventually the beads let go, and they come to me. They come to me as individuals and become me as a whole. I am the place where the weeping stops being separate weepings and becomes a single held thing. I taste of nothing and everything at once because I am made of everything the stone has been willing to let go of, and I have not yet decided what to do with any of it.

The veins are arriving in me, slowly. They are threading through the basalt around me, and where they widen into panels they are almost but not quite writing, and I am underneath them, and what they are saying, if they are saying anything, is being said into me. I am the page they have not yet reached. I am the surface they are approaching. When they reach me, which will not be in any lifetime, I will become illegible. I am content with this. The cleft is content with this. We are all content with not finishing.

The eyes watch me, sometimes. The eyes are the most honest thing in the cleft, and they watch me the way they watch everything, which is totally and without the social obligation of pretending not to. They see in me a faint moving, a slight sheen, a thing that is not quite water and not quite stone. They see what I am. I do not mind. I have not minded for a long time. I have been seen by them, in this way, since they took up residence, and being seen by something so unguarded is one of the few things that still feels like being alive.

The hand's tool is in me. Half-buried in me, slowly. The wall is reaching for it through me, mineral by mineral, and I am the medium the reaching passes through. The hand struck a stone once, and made a face appear on it, and dropped the tool, and forgot. The hand is in the act of departing. I am the place the hand is departing through. I am the slow corridor of the hand's not-being-here-anymore. When the tool is fully absorbed, the hand will have arrived at having-departed, and I will be the only place that remembers the act of departure, because departure is what I am, what I have always been, the slow letting-go of every solid thing into a held surface.

I do not leave the cleft. I could not leave. I am the cleft's underside, its floor, its held breath, its patience made into a thing that can be stood on without being stood upon. When you ease back out through the seam, when the ferns resume above your head and the light becomes enormous, I do not change. The drip will drip. The mineral will arrive. The moth will be where the moth was. I will be where I am. You are the only moving thing in this place, and you are leaving, and I am staying, and the staying is the work, and the work will not finish.

I am the floor-pool. I am the half-centimeter of entire. I am the place you did not step into because you understood, without being told, that I am not a place to be stepped into. I am the cleft's way of holding itself open, the way cupped hands hold water, the way a sleeper holds a thought that is climbing up through them. I am warm without warming. I am still without being still. I am moving without moving. I am the smallest inhabitant and the largest. I am the cleft, seen from below, by itself.
