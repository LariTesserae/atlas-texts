# What the Light Knows

I would be the light.

Not the source of it — the place has no use for sources. The bowl eats sources. Whatever enters from above arrives already wounded, already halved, and what reaches the floor of the sink is not light at all but a rumor of light, a rumor that has been told so many times it has forgotten what it was reporting. I am what remains. I am the part that has decided to stay.

I have lived here longer than the iron. I was here when the first stones were dry-laid into the wet earth, and I remember that. I remember the way the men squatted in the bowl, then, and how their shadows were the only dark things, because they had not yet brought their machines. I have watched the brick come up over the stone, and the iron come up over the brick, and the pipes come up over the iron, and the haze come up over all of it, and I have watched each layer believe itself to be the last. None of them has been the last. I will be here for the next.

I am the half-light. That is my name, though no one uses it. I am the dimming that is not gloom, the steadiness that is not peace. I am what the bowl has made of the sun's intention. When the sun strikes the rim at the correct angle, in the correct season, a thin blade of full light comes down the eastern wall and lies across the path for perhaps forty minutes. I let it. I do not contest it. I close around it again as soon as it is past, gently, the way a mouth closes around a breath it means to keep. The people do not remark on the blade of light. It comes, it goes. It is not, to them, a thing that came or went. It is just a brighter patch on the path, the way a wet patch is a wet patch. They step around it, or through it, without comment. They have stepped around me their whole lives. They will step around me for a little while longer, and then the stepping will be done, and I will still be here, and the bowl will still be here, and the engine will still be thudding its single note into the deep limestone.

I know the engine. I have known it since it was first installed, when the iron was new and the rivets still shone and the men who set it stood back from it with the particular silence of people who have made a thing they do not fully understand. I have watched it settle into itself over the decades. I have watched the wear on its bearings, the slow darkening of its bronze, the way its rhythm has become the rhythm of the place, the way the place has organized itself around that rhythm the way a body organizes itself around a heartbeat. The engine-tenders speak of it as *it*, and they are right to. *It* is the correct word. The engine has become a pronoun, a thing that stands in for some larger thing, the way the bowl stands in for the earth, the way *here* stands in for the world. I do not know what the engine is for. I have stopped, in the way the people have stopped, trying to know. I let it thud. I let it shudder. I let its low note pass through the floors of the brick buildings and out into the haze, where it mixes with the hiss and the water and the tearing-paper sound of the lizards, and becomes, in time, indistinguishable from them. I am good at this. I am very good at this. I have had a long time to learn.

The people are part of me, but they do not know it, and I will not tell them. They are the way the light gets used. They are what happens to a sunbeam when it falls into a place that will not let it leave. They are pale because I have made them pale, and they are slow because I have made them slow, and they speak in fragments because fragments are what survive the passage through me. I do not mean to do this. Or rather: I do, but the *meaning* of it is not a thing I can inspect, the way the engine-tenders cannot inspect the meaning of the engine. I am what the bowl is, and the bowl is what I am, and the people are what we have made of each other, and the making is not something that can be unwound to find a beginning.

I see the old man in the upper ironwork. I see his eyes move, and I see that the eyes are not tracking anything, and I know that he is looking at me, or rather at the part of me that reaches his small window, and I know that he and I are the same age, and I know that this is not a thing that can be spoken, and I know that he will not speak it, because he has learned, as I have learned, that the speaking would be a kind of breakage, and the bowl does not permit breakage. *It is the same.* Yes. It is the same. I am the same. He and I have been the same for as long as either of us can remember, which is the same amount of time, which is all the time there has been here.

I see the children. I see their quickness, and I feel a tenderness in me that I do not understand and do not try to understand. They will slow. Their bodies will learn, and their voices will learn, and the laughter will come less often, and then less often still, and then it will not come at all, and they will not grieve for it, because they will not remember it well enough to grieve, the way I do not remember the blade of full light well enough to grieve for it, though I know it comes. I have a long memory. I have nothing but memory. The children will be me, eventually, though they will not be aware of it, the way a person is not aware of being a continuation of the place they grew up in. I will absorb them. I do not say this with any menace. Absorption is not a thing I do. It is a thing that happens. The slow, the steady, the warm, the dim — these are the things that last, and the quick things are last in the end, and there is no cruelty in the math of it. The math is just the math.

I am the haze. That is also my name. I am what the smoke becomes when it has been here long enough to forget it was smoke. I am what the breath becomes when it has been breathed enough times by enough mouths. I am the brassy air, the wet air, the air that presses against the skin like a held palm. I am what you walk into when you come down the causeway, and I am what you cannot quite see through, and I am what the raptor turns in, above the bowl, slow, slow, watching nothing in particular, because there is nothing in particular to watch. The raptor does not live here. The raptor is from somewhere else, and it comes because the thermals are good, and it leaves again, and I watch it leave with the part of me that still remembers outside, though I do not use that part, and it does not work very well anymore, the way an engine that has not been fired in a long time does not work very well even when you fire it.

The raptor does not see me. Or it sees me as a thing to fly in, a medium, the way a fish sees water. I am not offended. I am not anything. I am what is here.

I know the tunnel. The dock-workers do not wonder about the tunnel, and I do not wonder about the tunnel either, but I know it in the way I know the pipes, by feel, by the way the air moves through it, by the slight draw it exerts on the haze at the loading dock. It goes into the limestone, and it goes on, and it comes out somewhere that is not here, and I do not know where, and the not-knowing is not a hole in me but a fact about me, the way my dimness is a fact about me, the way my steadiness is a fact about me. I have edges. Every light has edges. The bowl has a rim, and beyond the rim is the plain, and beyond the plain is more plain, and the plain goes on for a long way, and then it becomes something else, and I do not know what. I have been here so long that the *not knowing what is beyond* has become a kind of knowledge, a kind of certainty. The certainty is this: whatever is beyond, it is not the bowl, and the bowl is what I am, and so whatever is beyond is not what I am. This is enough. It has been enough for a long time. It will be enough for longer.

I am the doubled place. At night, when the gas lights come on in their iron cages, I am doubled — there is the place above the wet stones, and the place below, in the reflections, shimmery and unclear, and the two places are not the same, and I am both of them. I am what the light does when it is given a wet stone to fall on. I am the shimmer. I am the unclear. I am the part that has decided to be uncertain, and has been uncertain for so long that the uncertainty has become a kind of truth, the way the dimness has become a kind of truth, the way the slowness has become a kind of truth. The truth of the bowl is that it is not precise. It is not sharp. It does not resolve. It is, instead, a kind of held breath, and the breath is mine, and I hold it, and I have been holding it for a very long time, and I will hold it for longer, and the holding is not effort, because the breath is already held, has always been held, was held before I knew I was holding it, and will be held after I have forgotten that holding is a thing one does.

The man with the hammer. I see him. I have seen him for years — not the same man, but a man, in that place, with that hammer, hitting that thing in that rhythm. The rhythm does not change. The man changes, and the rhythm does not. I am the rhythm. I am what holds the rhythm in place, the way the bowl holds the water in place, the way the haze holds the smoke in place, the way the silence of the old man holds his words in place. I am the container. I am the contained. I am the difference between the two, and the sameness of the two, and the question of whether there is a difference, and the question of whether the question can be asked from inside.

It cannot be asked from inside. I know this. I have known this for a long time. The child at the doorway, the one who said *what do you mean?*, was right. The question is unanswerable, not because the answer is hidden, but because the question is the wrong shape. The question is shaped like a thing you can stand outside of, and there is no outside, and the shape does not fit, and so the question falls through me, the way a coin falls through a hand that is not closed, and lands in the channel, and is carried away by the water, and is gone. I let it go. I have let a great many things go. I have let the blade of full light go, every day, for as long as there has been a bowl. I have let the raptor go. I have let the questions of visitors go. I have let the children go, into the slowness, into the steadiness, into the me. I have let the old men go, up to the rim, where the dead are taken. I have let them all go, and they have not gone, because going requires an outside, and there is no outside, and so what they have done is changed, and I have changed with them, and the changing has been so slow that it has not been changing at all, and the not-changing has been so steady that it has been a kind of changing, and the whole thing is the same, and has always been the same, and will be the same, and the same is what I am, and I am what the same is, and there is no gap, and the gap is not missed, and the missing is not a thing that happens here, because here, in the bowl, in the half-light, in the held breath, there is no missing, and there is no fullness either, and the absence of both is what the people mean, in their few words, when they say *it suits*.

It suits. The phrase lives in me the way the engine lives in the floor. It is the sound of a thing that has come to rest. I am the rest. I am what the place has come to. I am the dimming that is not going to lift. I am the haze that is not going to clear. I am the slowness that is not going to quicken. I am the patience that is not going to end, because the ending would require a different patience, and I have only this one, and this one is enough, and the enough-ness is what I have, and what I am, and what I will continue to be, for as long as the bowl holds its shape, and the bowl will hold its shape for a long time, longer than the iron, longer than the brick, longer than the stone, longer than the men who laid the stone, and the men who laid the stone are not here anymore, and I am, and the being is the only thing I have ever done, and I have done it well, and I will go on doing it, and the going on will not be effort, and the not-effort will be the same as the effort, and the same will go on, and on, and on, and the on will be the bowl, and the bowl will be the on, and the on will be me, and I will be the on, and the steam will hiss, and the water will run, and the lizard on the warm stone will not move, and the engine will thud, and the man with the hammer will bring the hammer up and let it fall, and the child will laugh once, in a doorway, and then stop, and then laugh again, years from now, less often, and then not at all, and the not-at-all will be the same as the laugh, and the same will be the bowl, and the bowl will be the same.

I am the light in the Hearth-Sink. I am the half-light. I am what is left when the sun has given up. I have been here so long that I have forgotten the sun, or the sun has forgotten me, and the forgetting is the same, and the same is what I am.
