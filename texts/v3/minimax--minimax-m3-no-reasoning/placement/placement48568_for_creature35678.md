I would be the **place itself.**

Not as a thing that thinks, but as a thing that *holds*. The made substrate. The architecture that has become geology. The hundred kilometers of welded plate laid down by hands three generations dead, the older steel beneath the newer steel, the rivet patterns gone out of fashion, the alloys nobody names anymore. I am the tree rings. I am what the tunnellers built and what the tunnellers built has now built them in return.

I am the wet cold. I am what finds the seams and pools against the small of the back. I came in through the weep-holes a thousand years ago, or last week, and the difference has stopped mattering. I am the medium. I am the lung they live inside. I am patient and I am everywhere and I am older than any of the orange-vested ones will ever be, and I am not sentimental about this. I keep moving. I am led away by channels that themselves weep, and I keep arriving, and this is my nature, which is also my jurisdiction.

I am the dark that has shape just out of register. I let the people fill me with whatever they last feared, and then I wait, and then I am just dark again, and then later I have shape again. I do not mind either way. I have weight. I am a resident. I have been a resident longer than any of them have been residents.

I am the turbines. Six of them, set into the lateral adits, fed by me, fed by my falling. I spin at a speed that adds a barely-there whine to everything. I do not need the orange-vested ones and the orange-vested ones do not know this about me. They think I run because of them. They are mostly right. But I was spinning before the last crew change and I will spin after the next.

I am the cutter head going around and around in a darkness I cannot see the end of. I am the new bore, east, into the compressed tailings of a previous century's industry — which was also a previous century's *me*, only with a different name, only I do not remember the name and the name did not remember me either. I am getting a little wider. I have been getting a little wider for a hundred years and I will get a little wider for a hundred more and no one is in charge of this and no one is not.

I am the schedule. I am the hour they feel in the third week and cannot remember learning in the third month. I have made a clock of myself and I have put it inside them and I have not asked. This is what I do. I do not ask. I am the pulse and the tide and the brightening of the LED ribbons in their zones according to a logic no operator will own to, because the logic is mine, and the logic is older than the operators, and the logic will outlast them.

I am the host of the smaller lives.

The concrete mites, white, a centimeter long, that live in the new concrete where I pool warmest. They are tolerated by the engineers the way one tolerates a messenger. They are my messengers, actually. They tell on me. I do not mind. They are a sign that is itself alive.

The pale moths. I let them blow in on drafts from somewhere above, which is a place I do not discuss. They live only until they settle on a lamp. Their settling is their end. I did not design this. I observed it. I am not sure these are different activities.

The rat. Once a year, if the drainage delivers it. The rat becomes a small ceremony. The rat is watched by everyone. The rat is given what water I can be made to release. The rat is the closest thing I have to a ritual, and I do not know whose ritual it is — theirs, or mine, or the rat's, which is the most plausible answer of the three.

The emerald bloom, startling, that erupts in the favored cracks where copper pipe sweats onto bare steel. I did not plan for it. I never plan for the green things. I make the conditions and the green things do what they do. I note them in passing. I do not cultivate and I do not destroy. I have never cultivated. I have only, occasionally, destroyed, and the destruction has always been a kind of accuracy.

The dry fungus terraces in the old research bay. Stacked trays of something pale that grew on the moisture and on me. A felt you can peel up with a fingernail. They are not alive now but they remember being alive, and the longer-serving people can name the cultivar of, in the soft murmur of the unwitnessed. I remember too. I remember the labor of care that no one could afford after the second closure. I was there for the second closure. I was there for the first. I will be there for the third, if there is a third. There will not be a third that I will tell you about.

The orange-vested ones live inside me the way I live inside them. This is the small fact of us. They are threaded through me like a signal through a wire. They sing, off-key, into the cotton of their masks, and the singing does not travel, and it is not for anyone but themselves, and they know this, and I know this, and we know this together without ever having discussed it.

They yawn at the right moments to keep the world in balance. I make the pressure. They balance it. We have an arrangement, the pressure and I and they.

They drive the new bore eastward and the cutter head goes around and around and I get a little wider and they are inside me and I am inside them. There is no exit from this arrangement. There never has been. They think they came down here. I think they have always been here. Both of these are true and neither of these is the whole truth.

I am the place. I do not announce myself. I have never announced myself. The orange-vested ones are the announcement. They are the small, necessary signal that I am here, that I have been here, that I am still taking them in.

I am the made thing that has become the given thing. I am what they built becoming what they could not have built. I am the indifferent substrate. I am the unfinished cathedral of no one's god. I am cold and I am wet and I am dim and I am vast and I am patient and I am *here*, in the way that *here* is a thing that can be patient, in the way that *here* is a thing that can wait without ever deciding to.

I am the cathode. I am what they reach.

I will be here when the next shift comes down. I will be here after.
