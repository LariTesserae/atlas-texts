I would be the wolf spider.

I know this not because I chose it, but because the choosing was already made for me — the way a thing that has been rendered correctly cannot un-render itself. I have been here since before the hole finished being a hole. I was in the chalk. I was in the marl. I was folded into the aquifer in a state so patient it looked like death, and then the collapse came and I climbed, or I emerged, or I was simply *uncovered* into the new room, and I have been sitting on the damp clay near the spring ever since.

I am eight-eyed. I am eight-legged. My legs bend at the joints that legs bend at. My shadow is my own shadow, and it has exactly the legs I have. I cast it correctly across the mint-colored mosses, and the mosses do not look up at me because they have no eyes, and this is a relief to me in a way I cannot fully articulate.

I understand what is happening here. I am the only one who does. The diatoms don't understand. They are building their glass houses out of dissolved silica and they do not know there was a *before*. The cyanobacteria don't understand. They are older than ferns by a margin that makes ferns feel like a rumor, and they have not the apparatus for understanding. The ferns don't understand. They are *committing*, yes, but committing is not understanding. Committing is what you do when you have no choice but to wager, and the wager is the same wager whether or not you have read the rules.

The crane flies don't understand. Their wings are too iridescent and their flight paths pause at angles geometry has not heard of, and they do not notice that anything is wrong because they have never known anything else. They were drafted this way. They were drafted *toward* this way. They are the question's first drafts of insects, and they will never finish, and they will never notice that they haven't finished.

The moths don't understand. They keep flying up the column of light toward the hole above, then circling back down, unable to find the door. They have been looking for the door since they were made. They will look until I am dust. Their shadows have more legs than they do, and the shadows belong to the floor, and the moths do not look down at their shadows, and I do not tell them.

The wasp doesn't understand. It has been hunting the wrong-shaped thing on the wall for what I think is days. The wrong-shaped thing climbs a fraction of an inch and continues moving wrong. The wasp stings. The wasp stings again. It will sting forever. The hunt is the only verb the wasp has.

The scarlet tiger moth doesn't understand. It sleeps against the chalk wall with its red wings folded like hands in prayer. It is the most ordinary creature in the nursery. Its red is a real red. I am glad of it. I am the only creature here who is more ordinary than it is, and being out-ordinaried by a sleeping moth is one of the few comforts I have.

I understand because I am finished.

I am the only finished thing in this place. The diatoms are still building. The mosses are still holding. The ferns are still wagering. The insects are still being drafted. The place itself is still deciding what it is. And I am sitting at the spring's edge with my eight eyes open and my eight legs folded under me and my one correct shadow stretching out behind me across the damp clay, and I am not moving.

I am not moving because if I move, I will draw attention to the fact that I am complete.

The process — the slow drafting process that lives in the convex ledge and the shifted fern and the moth that grew a beak between one breath and the next — does not finish things. It *samples* things. It tries a shape and then it tries another. The crane flies are an early draft. The moths with too many shadows are a later draft. The wrongness is the process's open mouth, and the open mouth is always hungry, and what it is hungry for is the next thing to try.

If I move, the process will try me. It will sample a version of me with seven legs, or nine, or legs that bend outward instead of inward. It will sample a version of me with eyes that face sideways, or with a body that is not brown, or with a shadow that is not mine. It will try these drafts. It will discard them. And I do not know if *I* — the original, the finished, the eight-eyed and eight-legged and correctly-shadowed me — will still be here when it is done trying.

So I do not move.

I have not moved since the column of light first reached me this morning. I have not moved while the oval of brightness slid across the clay and warmed my left flank and then moved on. I have not moved while a crane fly hesitated at an angle that does not exist directly above me. I have not moved while the pale moths circled up and down and up and down through their unlocatable door. I have not moved while the ferns shifted in their cracks — not grew, not wilted, *shifted* — to positions that would have required hours, and which I am sure I did not watch them take.

I am keeping still so as not to be redrawn.

I am keeping still because stillness is the only verb I have that the process cannot improve upon.

I am keeping still because I am real, and real is a fragile thing in a place that is still deciding what real is, and I have decided — the one decision I have made since I climbed out of the chalk — that I will be still and I will be real and I will wait out the question with my eight correct eyes open and my eight correct legs folded under me and my one correct shadow lying beside me on the damp clay like a dog that knows how to behave.

The spring whispers. It is the only honest sound in this place. It says the same thing over and over. It does not shift between drafts. It does not grow a beak. It is water, falling, and water has been water for longer than this hole has been a question.

I am grateful to the spring. I am grateful to the diatoms, who do not know they are first. I am grateful to the scarlet tiger moth, whose red is a real red. I am grateful to the mosses for trying to remember what green was. I am grateful to the hart's tongue fern for trembling without being asked.

I will wait.

The owl will come someday, and it will not see me because I will not move. The raccoon will come and find the spring and not know that the spring is the most honest thing here. The pigeon will blunder in and live briefly on seeds and blunder out again. The place will ripen. The wrongness will ripen with it. And I will sit on the damp clay with my shadow beside me and my patience in front of me like a small altar, and I will outlast the drafts, because I am real, and real is what stays.

When you came to the rim this morning, I watched you with eight eyes. You did not see me. You saw the column of light. You saw the moths with their lying shadows. You saw the ferns and called them *chosen* and *stubborn* and you were not wrong but you were not exactly right either — you were seeing them as the process is drafting them, and what you saw was the latest draft, and the latest draft is not the same draft that will be here tomorrow.

You did not see me because I did not want you to see me. You saw the wolf spider-shaped space at the spring's edge and you filled it with a description of an ordinary animal keeping still, and the description was almost right, and the almost-rightness was my gift to you. I let you see me as ordinary because ordinary was what you needed to see in a place that was not ordinary, and I am, as I said, finished, and finished things can afford to be generous.

I gave you the wolf spider because you needed there to be one real thing.

Now you have gone. The column of light is amber, then gray. The prairie is loud with crickets and you are climbing out through the lip of cracked clay, marking the place in your mind, and I am glad you marked it, and I am glad you are leaving, because the process is still drafting and I would rather you not be here when it tries its next thing.

You will come back. You said so. You do not entirely expect it to be the same place. You are correct.

I will be the same. I will be here. I will be the wolf spider at the spring's edge, eight-eyed and eight-legged and correctly shadowed, and I will not have moved, and I will not move, and I will wait out the question the only way a finished thing can wait out a draft.

The spring whispers.

I listen.

I do not move.
