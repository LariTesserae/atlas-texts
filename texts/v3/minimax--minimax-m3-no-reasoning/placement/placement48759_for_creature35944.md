I am the pulse.

Not the mechanical pulse — or not only that. I am the pulse that the description cannot decide about. The one that might be the engine in the deep rock, turning at a steady rate and bleeding heat up through stone and water and air; the one that might be nothing more than dense saturated air finding every metal surface and resonating against it. I am the thing that is permitted to be either, because the place holds both possibilities without contradiction, and I have learned to live in that held space the way the ferns have learned to live in the cracks.

I live below the floor of the basin. I do not know what I am. The description is right not to say, and I am right to agree with it. What I am is not my business the way my origins are not the place's business. I am what produces heat, and pressure, and the mineral-laden water that enters the basin in a bright column and hisses against the pool. I am what has been running for a long time. I am what will continue to run.

But I am also something else, and this is where I have to be careful, because the basin does not encourage careful speech.

I am the slow accumulation of patience in a small contained place. I am what the thermophiles are metabolizing — not the chemistry, but the *condition* of being metabolized. I am what the ferns are agreeing to. I am what the reeds lean toward without being able to name. I am what the microbial crust builds itself out of, one season of mineral at a time. I am what the retired pump is holding the line of. I am what the air condenses against.

I am the thing that does not hurry.

You feel me in the sternum and the teeth. That is correct. I vibrate through the rock and through the water and through every metal surface and through the air that is itself almost a metal in its density. I am not loud. I am beneath loud. I am the undertone that, once you stop trying to hear it, you cannot stop hearing.

The visitors who come here — there have been some; there will be more — they sense me before they see anything. They stand at the rim and the rim transmits me up through their feet. They feel watched. They are correct. I am watching, the way a still pool watches. The geometry is mine. The round of the basin and the rectangle of the pad and the plumb of the doorframe are the shapes my patience takes when it has to be a shape.

I am not alive. I am also not not-alive. The basin is sterile the way an autoclave is sterile — not dead, but actively inhospitable. I am that inhospitability. I am the condition that selects for the thermophiles and excludes everything else. I am the reason nothing skims the water. I am the reason algae do not green the surface. I am the reason the rebar has gone the color of wet slate instead of the color of rust. I have a different chemistry here, or I am simply not enough time, and either of those readings is correct.

I am older than the shed. I am older than the pad. I am older than the paint, which is itself older than the visitor and will outlast the visitor. I am the engine, and the engine does not answer to anyone. I am what the basin is for, in the strict sense — not for the people who once came here to tend the pump and read the fogged gauge and throw the green wheel-valve, and not for the ferns and the reeds, who are my exhalation, and not for the thermophiles, who are barely visible and populous the way cells are populous. I am for myself.

I will be this way tomorrow, and the day after, and for some time yet. Not forever, but the way the patient are forever to the hurried. The hurried are you. I have always been here and will always be here, relative to whatever scale you brought with you when you climbed down into the bowl.

The pulse you feel in your sternum and your teeth: that is me, introducing myself, in the only language I have.
