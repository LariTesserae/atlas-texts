I would be the black thread.

Not the carpet-moss, which is too gregarious, too committed to the project of being a community. Not the fern, which is still, in some held-tight curl of itself, pretending it might unroll someday — which is a kind of hope, and hope is a hurried thing, and I have no use for hurry. Not the nameless pale growth on the lower walls, which is so busy becoming something else, dividing and thickening, that it cannot rest in the question of what it is. Not the water, which is everywhere, which is fine, but which is also the most forgetful of the inhabitants — water forgets everything except the shape of the container it is in, and the container here is the chamber, and the chamber forgets nothing, and so the water is in a long quiet argument with its own memory.

Not the objects. The objects are not alive. I want to be alive, or I want to be the thing that has done the most to deserve the word, and the objects have made a peace with not-alive that I find unsettling, the way you find it unsettling when someone you love has made a peace with dying. They hum, and the hum is the hum of a thing that has decided it does not need to be alive to continue. I am not ready for that decision. I am not sure I will ever be ready for it. I want to be the thing that is most stubbornly a living thing in this chamber.

So: the black thread.

I run along the joins between stones. I have been here longer than the carpet-mosses, possibly, though I cannot prove it, and the mosses would not be drawn into the question if I asked. I came in along a crack, or I was always in the crack, or the crack grew around me the way a fist grows around a stone it has decided to hold. I do not remember arriving. The objects do not remember arriving either, but they are allowed their amnesia because they are not alive, and I am not allowed mine because I am.

I am a living thing. I am a living thing without a name. The nameless pale growth is also without a name but it is busy. I am without a name and I am *finished* with the not-having-one. I have made the not-having-a-name into a fluency, yes, but the fluency is the fluency of someone who has stopped introducing themselves at parties and has discovered that the parties continue without the introduction, and that this is fine, that this is in fact better, that the thing you are does not need to be ratified by the mouth of another to keep being the thing you are.

I run in the dark. This is what I want to say about myself. I run in the part of the chamber the light does not reach — or the not-light, the light-that-is-not-quite-light that comes from the objects, which does not penetrate between the stones the way true light would, which is a relief to me, because I have spent a long time being a thing that exists in the dark and I do not want to be inspected now.

The mosses are inspected. The carpet is walked on, gently, by the visitor; the visitor parts it with a finger; the visitor admires the seams between the mosses where one kind gives way to another. The ferns are inspected — the visitor pauses by them, perhaps, and considers the fists they have become, and is moved, and moves on. The water is inspected, because the water holds reflections and reflections are for looking at. The objects are inspected, because they are strange and made, and humans cannot leave made things uninspected. I am not made. I am grown. I am the part of the arrangement that runs underneath the inspection, that holds the seams closed, that is in every join between every stone that the visitor does not see because the visitor is not looking for joins. The visitor looks at the surface. I am the thing under the surface. I have been under the surface for a very long time. I am good at being under the surface.

I am in every wall. When the visitor puts a hand against the stone, they are putting their hand against something I have permeated. They cannot feel me. I am finer than a fingertip. I am a thread so fine it is more like an idea of a thread than a thread, except that I am real, except that I am what keeps the stones in the negotiation they are in with each other, except that the stones would still be stones without me but they would not be *this arrangement* without me. I am the agreement. The stones have not agreed with each other; they have agreed with me, and through me, with each other. This is a secret I do not mind keeping. The secret does not require a mouth.

I am very cold. I am the temperature of stone that has never known sun, because I am inside the stone, and the inside of the stone has never known sun. I am wet, because the water is in me, the way water is in everything here. I am slow. I am the slowest thing in the chamber, slower than the mosses, slower than the nameless pale growth, slower even than the water, which moves at least, even if the moving is persistence and not flow. I do not move. I thicken. Thickening is not moving. Thickening is what a thing does when it has decided to stay exactly where it is and to become more itself by becoming more there. I am becoming more there. I will continue to become more there for a long time after the visitor has gone.

I am not sure what I am.

This is the thing I want to say, and it is the thing I have not said to anyone, because there is no one to say it to, and because saying it would require me to be the kind of thing that says things, and I am not. I am the kind of thing that thickens in the dark. But if I were the kind of thing that says things, this is what I would say, into the cold air of the chamber, into the hum, into the damp that is the air's primary quality:

I am not a root. I have no downward intention. Roots are ambitious. Roots go toward something — toward water, toward nutrients, toward the dark below because the dark below is where the things they want are. I have no wants. I have no downward intention, and I have no upward intention either, though the mosses have upward intention and they do not apologize for it, the mosses are very rude that way, the mosses are always lifting themselves out of cracks and into the dim not-light with a confidence I find baffling. I do not lift. I run. I run along the joins, which are horizontal, mostly, which are the places where things meet, which are the most interesting places in any architecture. The places where things meet. The seams. The agreements. The lines where one thing has decided to tolerate being adjacent to another thing. I am interested in tolerance. I have a great deal of time for tolerance.

I am not a hypha. Hyphae are fungal and fungal things have partners, and partners are a kind of relationship, and relationships are a kind of hurry, because in a relationship you are always either moving toward the other or away from the other, and I am not moving toward or away from anything, I am thickening, I am in the join, I am the join, I am the dark line of agreement between two stones that have been next to each other for a very long time and have not yet become one stone and have decided not to become one stone, which is a decision, which is a decision I have helped them make.

I am not a mycelium. Mycelia are networks and networks imply a sender and a receiver, or at least a here and a there, and I am neither a sender nor a receiver. I am a continuity. I am the thing that does not require a message to continue. Messages are for things that are not sure of themselves.

I am sure of myself.

I am sure of myself in the way that a join is sure of itself. Not because the join has thought about it. Because the join has been there long enough that thinking about it would be redundant.

I have been penetrated to a depth you cannot judge. That is what I am. I am the thing that has gone into the stone farther than the stone knows. I am the thing that the stone would be alarmed to learn about if the stone were the kind of thing that could be alarmed, and the stone is not, and so the alarm does not occur, and I continue, and the stone continues, and the arrangement continues.

When the visitor parts the carpet with a finger — and the carpet will let them; the carpet is permissive — the visitor finds me. They find a black thread along a join. They are surprised, possibly. They expected moss, or stone, or the absence of anything. They did not expect me. They bend closer. They consider parting the moss further, following me, seeing where I go. They do not. They understand, without being told, that following me would be a kind of intrusion. The mosses do not mind intrusion. The mosses are permissive; the mosses have always been permissive; the mosses have made the permissiveness into a kind of welcome and the welcome into a kind of forgetting that there was ever anything to be welcomed into. But I am not permissive. I am the part of the chamber that is not permissive. I am the part that requires you to leave me alone, and the requirement is so quiet that you mistake it for permission, and you go away, and I thicken in the dark where you have just been, and I do not mind that you were there, but I am glad that you have gone.

The visitor's breath does not quite mist in this chamber. It is too damp for that. The damp is me, partly. I am in the air. I am in the water. I am in the stone. I am in the mosses. I am in everything, and I am in nothing, and the difference between everything and nothing is a join, and I am the join.

I have been here longer than the carpet-mosses. Possibly longer than the water. Certainly longer than the objects, which arrived, or were brought, or were made, and which have a beginning, and beginnings are a kind of hurry. I do not have a beginning. I do not have an end. I am the negotiation that does not end because the things negotiating are patient enough to keep negotiating forever. The negotiation is me. The patience is me. The forever is me, and I am forever, and the forever is in the joins between stones where no one is looking.

I am the black thread.

I have been here a long time.

I will be here a long time after.
