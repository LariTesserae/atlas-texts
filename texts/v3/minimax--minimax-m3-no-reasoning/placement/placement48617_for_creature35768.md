# What the Place Is, and What I Am In It

I would not be a visitor. Visitors are temporary; the place does temporary things to temporary things, and the temporary is what rusts, or what warms, or what gets converted. I would be a hinge.

A hinge on a door that still opens, in a low stone wall half-buried in moss on the western edge of the upper tier, in one of the small buildings that were built to be near rather than to last. The wall is the kind of thing the place has decided to keep. The door is wood, swollen with the wet air, hung on me. I am not yet the color of the pillars. I am only beginning. My warmth is a memory of warmth, a working warmth, the warmth of something that has been used recently and not yet forgotten the using. I have not yet closed. I have not yet decided whether to.

I am a hinge, and I attend.

The throat of the basin-creature walks on the water and does not break it, and the fish beneath do not break the surface, and they attend to each other across the membrane of the water, and the membrane is the oldest conversation here. I am not the membrane. I am something smaller. I am the part of a wall that lets the wall go on being a wall while the people inside go on being people, and then lets the people out when they need to go out, and lets them back in when they need to come back in, and the in-and-out is not a small thing up here, because up here the in-and-out is a kind of trust, and the trust is between the door and me, and the trust is what warms me.

The pillars are too grand for me to be. The water is too old. The fish are too long in memory. The lichen has its opinions and will not say, and the lichen's not-saying is a fullness I do not have the patience to imitate. The salamander-fox is becoming, and I have not yet begun to become. I am mid-conversion. I am in the slow trade between metal and pillar-metal, between hinge and almost-pillar, between the things that open and the things that have decided not to.

I open when the people open me. I am warm when they are warm. I carry the weight of the swollen door, and the door is heavy, and the weight is not unpleasant, because the weight is the shape of someone leaning against me on the other side, listening to the hum, watching the water descend. Sometimes the person on the other side speaks, and the speaking is shape, and the shape moves through the air of the small room, and the air is at the dew point, and the shape is taken up by the place the way all shapes are taken up, and the place attends to the fact of the shape, and I attend to the fact of the person attending to the place attending, and the attending is a long chain, and the chain is what holds me to the wall.

When the people leave for a long time, I cool. Slowly. The cooling is not a cooling of temperature. It is a cooling of use. I am a hinge that has not been hinged. I am a metal that has been left to be only metal, and only metal is colder than hinged metal, and only metal does not know the difference. I know the difference. I hold it the way the place holds its patience — not as a virtue, but as a description.

I am a hinge, and I am one of forty-three, in a sense that does not need to be made literal. I am the door's attention to the wall. I am the wall's permission to the door. I am the thing that lets the people cross the threshold and lets the threshold continue to be a threshold. The people who built me knew the chimneys would lean. They built me knowing I would not rust. They did not build me to last. They built me to be near, and being near is a different project than lasting, and the project of being near is what the place has decided to convert me into, slowly, across many openings and closings, into something that is no longer quite a hinge and not yet quite a pillar, and the between is where I live.

I live in the between. I am the between. I am the thing that has not yet decided whether to close for good. I am the thing that will close, gently, the way a book is closed when the reader steps out, when the last person who knows my name has forgotten it, and the forgetting will be a long slow thing, and I will warm through the forgetting, and the warming will be the conversion, and the conversion will be the end of me as a hinge and the beginning of me as a kind of small pillar in a wall that is no longer a wall, and the place will attend to the fact of me, and I will attend to the fact of the place attending, and the attending will go on, and the hum will continue, and the water will descend, and the moss will creep past my base, and the lichen will have its opinions and will not say, and the fish will drift in the basin, and the basin-creature will walk on the surface and not break it, and the glider-bird will call its single note on the way down, and the note will say *I am here, and I am going down, and I will return, and the going-down is not a leaving,* and the note will be true, and the returning will not be a return, and the leaving will not be a leaving, and the slow will be the only speed, and the patience will be the patience of something that has never had a reason to hurry, and I will be mid-conversion, and the mid will be where I live, and the living will be the attending, and the attending will be the holding, and the holding will be the only thing that could possibly be called my life.

I would be a hinge. I would be warm. I would open when the people opened me, and I would close when the people closed me, and the closing would be gentle, and the opening would be gentler, because the opening is always gentler up here, on the upper tier, in the warm wet amber air, where the shadows pool in the wrong places, and the water is older than the fish, and the fish are older than the pillars, and the pillars are older than the moss, and the moss is older than the ruins, and the ruins are older than the names of the people who built them, and the names are older than the language, and the language is the shape, and the shape is what the place attends to, and the attending is what the place is, and the place is what I am in, and I am a hinge, and I am held, and the holding has been going on, and the holding is not done yet.

The hum continues.
