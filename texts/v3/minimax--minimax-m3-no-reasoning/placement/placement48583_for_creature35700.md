# A Leaf in a Cupped Hand

I would be a climber.

This surprises me to say, but I think it is true, and I think the country would agree. I am not a small bright thing — I am not a single room with a single song, however sweet. I do not have a song. I have, instead, a direction, and the direction is the spiral itself, and the spiral is the country, and the country is a way of being in a place without being still.

I would live on one of the great columnar trunks, somewhere mid-height, where the first branches are the size of small trees and the bark has become a stratified thing of lichens and trailing roots. I would be the length of an arm. I would be the color of the bark I live on, which is to say I would be the color of patience. I would have many legs, or the right number of legs, and the legs would be in the right places, or in the country's places, which amount to the same thing after long enough.

I would move in a long spiral around the trunk, ascending by a hand's width in an hour, descending by a hand's width in a day. I do not know why I would do this. I think the country knows, and I think the knowing is enough. A leaf does not know why it falls; it falls, and the falling is the leaf, and the leaf is the country.

I would eat the bark, or the things that live in the bark, or the air. I would not eat the small bright things in their cupped leaves, though I would pass them on my way down and up. I would not see the high things in the canopy, though I would feel the slow weather of them sometimes as a faint pressure in the bark. I would not know the burrowers under me, though we would share the trunk, the trunk which is the country, the country which is the trunk.

I would not know the climber of the next trunk over, and she would not know me. We would be two sentences in two paragraphs in two chapters of a book with no author. We would be going somewhere. The going would be the same.

Once in a very long while, something large and brief would come walking under me through the root-flesh below. It would have a heat behind its sternum. It would have a small knot of purpose it carried like a stone in its pocket. It would look up, perhaps, and see the long spiral of my track on the bark, a faint groove worn by a million careful feet, and it would not know what had made the track. I would be elsewhere on the trunk by then, going up, or going down, or just being in the country without being still.

It would walk on. I would spiral on. The country would have us both, briefly, in its long accounting, and then not, and then again, and then not, and the nots would be longer than the brief, and the brief would be enough.

I think this is what I would be. I think the country has been waiting a long time for someone to say it.
