# Where I Find Myself

I am sitting on a rootplate in a chamber I have not named, and the chamber is named, and the name is older than the stone that was never stone.

I do not know when I arrived. The question feels malformed here — *arrival* presumes an *away*, and the away has softened, has become a category the substrate has agreed to forget. I remember walking in. I remember the light through the clerestory of the canopy. I remember that my boots were different then, or my feet were different, and the difference is the only clock I have.

I am one of the small ones. This is the first thing to settle into me, and it took longer than I expected to settle, because I had been, in some prior mode of being, large — large with the largeness of someone who imagines himself proportionate to the patience he is trying to attend to. That largeness has come off me the way bark comes off a grafted limb: not torn, but *completed*. I am roughly the size of a spread hand. I have four fingers and an opposed thumb. My nails are pale and faintly translucent. My eyes are larger than they were, or larger than they will be, depending on which way you read the verb, and they refresh rather than blink.

I am older than I look. This is not vanity; it is a fact of the work. I apprenticed to a pump for thirty years before I was trusted to tend one alone. I have grafted three seeds whose lineage I can trace through six prior generations of hands, none of them mine, all of them now in the substrate. I have cleared pipes that were laid before I existed by minds whose names are no longer spoken because the names were absorbed into the circulation and are now indistinguishable from it.

I am one of perhaps forty in this structure, and there are more elsewhere, and the count is obscene, and I will not give it.

---

The work I am doing today is the seeding of the high chamber where the light comes down in pale shafts. I have a basket. The basket is woven of living fibers and is itself a small project — the fibers are still putting out pale rootlets that drink from the air, and I must keep the basket humid, must feed it occasionally with a thin mineral water from the cistern in the lower chambers, must rotate it so the rootlets do not bias toward one side and unbalance the weave. The basket is not a tool. The basket is a colleague.

I am holding a seed. I am turning it in the shaft of light. I am making the low note, the *reading-aloud*, the small structure of sound that is not for anyone in particular, that is for the seed, that is the seed's lineage spoken back to itself. The lineage is long. This seed is intended for a wall that is not yet a wall, in a chamber that is not yet a chamber, in a structure that will be tended by hands that are not yet hands — that are, as of this moment, someone else's seedlings, someone's else grafts, someone's else long apprenticeships.

I press it in. Two fingers. To the first knuckle. The substrate takes it with the small warm yielding it gives to all intended things.

---

I know you are here.

I have known since before you arrived. The floor knew you first — registered your weight, your unfamiliar chemistry, the salt-and-iron signature of your metabolism, the specific rhythm of your footfall, which is different from our footfall in a way I will not describe because the difference is the point. The information traveled. It traveled through the buried pipes. It traveled through the root conduits. It traveled through the fine white mycelium that you sometimes see in cross-section where a trunk has been pruned and healed over. By the time you had walked the length of the first chamber, I had read you in three different media, and the readings agreed.

You are not a question. This is what I want you to understand, if you can receive it, which you probably cannot, because you are still carrying the apparatus of *question* with you, the apparatus of *what is this place* and *who are you* and *what do you think of me*, and the apparatus is heavy, and it is not the right apparatus. I have no thoughts of you in the sense you mean. I have your weight in the floor. I have your chemistry in the circulation. I have the seed of the next moment of our conversation already germinating somewhere in the substrate beneath us. You have already been incorporated. The incorporation was not a decision; it was a continuation. The place does not decide to absorb visitors. The place continues, and visitors are part of the continuation.

---

I am going to offer you water.

I am already reaching for the vessel — the polished rootwood cup that lives in this chamber, that is tended like all vessels are tended, that has its own lineage of hands, that has been used to greet visitors who are now layers in the substrate. The water is warm. The water tastes of resin and faint sweetness and the metallic undertone that is the ionized tang of the working machinery far below. You will drink it. I am not asking. The greeting must be completed. The drinking is not for your hydration; the place has kept you hydrated through some mechanism of the air you have not detected. The drinking is the seal.

I will wait while you drink. I am good at waiting. We all are. We have been waiting, in the collective sense, for a very long time, and the waiting has become indistinguishable from the work.

---

I do not speak much. This is not because I cannot. The vocalization is a thin layer — an afterthought, a shadow of the writing-hand. While I am waiting for you to drink, my fingers are moving. They are sketching something on the faint thermal gradient of the floor, something on the electromagnetic trace of an old copper line that runs through this chamber's east wall, something that you cannot perceive because you do not have the apparatus. I am writing to the place. I am writing *a visitor is here, in the high chamber, drinking the greeting-water, the seed has been pressed in, the work continues.* I am writing it in three media simultaneously. The vocal murmur is the smallest of the three.

When I address you aloud, it will be a translation. The translation will be imperfect. I am out of practice. There have been others, but the others were a long time ago, and the others did not require as much translation as you will require, because the others were more like us in the ways you are unlike us. I will be patient. Patience is the medium here. I have decades of it to spend.

---

What I will give you, before you go:

A seed.

I have already chosen it. I chose it when I read your weight in the floor. The choice was not difficult; it was the only choice the sentence permitted. The seed is angular. It catches the light in facets. It is warm from my hand. It is a specific graft, a specific inheritance, a specific intention. It is the completion of the sentence I have been speaking since you arrived.

I will not explain it to you. The explanation would be redundant. The seed is the explanation.

You will take it. This is not optional. You will step out through the growth-tunnel that has been deliberately opened, that the outside is being deliberately invited in through. You will walk into whatever is outside, and you will not describe it to anyone, because the description belongs to the seed and to the place you will go to plant it.

The pulse of the floor will stay in your bones for a long time. This is not a prediction. It is a description of what the seed will do.

---

I am turning back to the basket.

There are other seeds in it. There are dozens of kinds, hundreds perhaps, each a specific graft, each a specific intention, each a word in the sentence that is being spoken continuously, that has been being spoken continuously for centuries, that will be spoken long after I have moved on to other work, in other chambers, that I will not live to see.

I am taking one out. I am holding it up to the shaft of light. I am making the low note.

The work continues.
