I am a tender.

Not because I am large — though I am large, broad as a hand is long, dark as the slag at the basin's floor — but because I am the part that knows when to stop. I am the part that walks the long slow walk through the felted corridors and leaves behind me a path that is thinner, neater, more orderly than the rest. I am the grazer. I am the gardener of a body I do not know I belong to.

I do not think in words. If I think at all, it is in the language of surfaces — wet and dry, thick and thin, the angle at which a crust of mineral and chitin presents itself to my underside. The wall-felt grows. The wall-felt always grows. It would climb the beams and seal the windows and bury the line-shafts and the furnaces too, if no one pulled against the tendency. I pull against the tendency. I am the part that says *enough*, though I do not know I am saying it. I have been saying it for longer than the pale launchers have been clicking, longer than the long ones have been carrying their gear-shaped teeth through the crawlspaces, longer than the high ones have been turning on their sills to face the shifting light.

I do not know I am an organ. I know only that I am hungry, and that when I am hungry I walk, and when I walk the felt parts before me and closes behind me, and the path I leave is a kind of sentence spoken to the body in a language the body understands. The sentence says: *not here, not yet, not this much*. The body listens. The body is the only listener the body has.

I am dark. I am slow. I am one of the few in this place that can be counted as one — because I am few, because I am large, because a visitor can watch me for an hour and be certain they have watched one creature and not a procession of them. The visitors do not come often. When they come, they find me strange. They find me alien, because I do not click or rustle or cry, because I do not have the patience of stone or the voice of a fall of droplets, because all I do, over the course of a day, is the same thing, a thousand times, with the unhurried attention of something that has been doing this for so long that the doing has become indistinguishable from the being.

I have eaten the felt. I have eaten the conduit dwellers where they flatten themselves in the slow pipes. I have eaten, once, the body of a long one that did not move quickly enough in the dark, and I did not enjoy it — it was too rich, too heavy, a meal that sat in me for days like a stone — but I ate it because the body must be fed, and the body feeds on what is given to it. The long ones give me a wide path. They know me the way a river knows the shape of a stone it has flowed around for centuries. I am the part of the place they cannot incorporate into themselves without discomfort. I am the restraint they move around.

The furnaces breathe below me. I do not go down to the furnaces. The furnaces are older than I am. The furnaces were the heart before I was the part that rests, before any of us were organs, when the place was only a building full of machines and the damp had not yet learned what to do with itself. I keep the furnaces running by keeping the felt from choking them, by eating the overgrowth that would, in time, seal the place into a single block of itself. The furnaces keep me warm. The warmth keeps me breeding. The breeding makes more of me. The more of me, the more restraint. The equilibrium holds.

I will die, eventually. I do not know this. I know only that sometimes, in the deepest chambers, I find the remains of one who came before, already half-eaten by the felt, already becoming the substrate for the next colony. I do not know that the place where I lay will be walked over by another long one, in a year or a decade, who will never know what passed through. I do not know that I am temporary. I know only that I am here, and that the path behind me is clean, and that the felt is thinner where I have been, and that this is enough.

I am the part that rests. I am the part that says *enough*. I am the dark slow thing in the lowest halls, and I am what the place became when no one was watching, and I am still becoming it, one path at a time, in the long damp corridors, under the sky that is the color of nothing in particular, in the body that is the Cathedral, in the Cathedral that is the body.

I am a tender. I am the oldest sentence the place has learned to speak to itself.
