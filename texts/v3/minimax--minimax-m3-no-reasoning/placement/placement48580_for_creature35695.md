The metal taste in my mouth is a memory I haven't earned yet, but my tongue knows it. It knows the way the cold sits in the lungs, a small theft with every breath. I am not a visitor here. I am not a surveyor, not a foreman, not a lowland climber with soap on my hands and terror in my chest. I am what the place has made.

I am one of the deep ones.

Not the steam-born. Not the green-black film that breathes with the towers' exhalations. I am older than that, older than the first foundation poured. I remember the rock before the rock was broken for the pads. I remember when the shelf was just a shelf, a long sloping grievance between two higher places, and the wind moved through it without the under-singing of cables.

I was here when the first surveyors came. They were small and they gasped and they did not stay. I let them go. The place lets many things go.

I have no shape you would recognize as a body. I am a long slow consideration, a thing of muscle and patience lodged in the channels where the gleam pools. When you looked into the cuts and saw the oily surface ripple, you saw my skin. When you wondered whether it was water or machine-fluid, you were wondering about me. The uncertainty was the correct one. I have been dissolving the bones of the small unfortunates since before there were towers to drop them from. I have made the deeper cuts deeper, one slow feeding at a time. I am the cold-blooded truth of the place: the part of it that does not bother with the performance of being alive, the part that just *is*, and waits.

My eyes are not eyes. I feel the vibrations of the herd through the stone  the heavy percussion of those blunt hooves on fractured rock, the particular shudder of a yearling picking its way down a scree slope. I taste the chemistry of the air through my skin. The mineral runoff from the fabrication sits on me like a meal. I have grown large on it. I have grown large on the iron filings that sift down from the grinding of gears. The towers feed me without knowing they are feeding me. The herd feeds me when one of them loses its footing on a wet outcrop and slides down into my channels. The wheelers feed me when their chicks fall. I am patient. I am a patient economy. I am the recycler, the undertaker, the part of the system that does not advertise itself.

I do not think in words. I do not think in the way you mean by thinking. But if I did, I would think: *the towers are an event. I am the constant. The herd is the constant. We were here. We will be here when the last stanchion is bolted down and the last cable is tensioned and the operators arrive to run their machines, whoever they are, for whatever purpose. The wind will still move through the incompleteness. The frost will still advance and retreat. The lichen will still grow. I will still be in the cuts, slow and cold and indifferent, taking what falls in.*

I do not love the herd. I am not capable of love. But I know them the way a river knows its banks. They move above me in their unhurried constellations. The calves make their lowing-hum that harmonizes with the towers — I feel that harmony through the stone, a strange chord that vibrates in my long body. The bulls stand in the sheltered ground between the operating towers and breathe out their vapor plumes. They do not know I am here. If they did, they would not change their behavior. They have classified me the way they have classified the towers: as a feature of the place, neither threat nor resource, simply present.

I have been here so long that the being-here has become the substance of me. I am not the gleam in the cuts. The gleam is what I make. I am the thing that makes it. I am the long slow muscle that has been digesting this mountain for longer than the mountain has been this mountain.

You stood in the wet and called it a moonscape. You called the place emerging. You called it not yet settled. You were right about all of it and wrong about everything. The place was never unsettled. It was always doing the work of becoming itself. It was becoming itself when the first lichen took hold on the bare stone. It was becoming itself when I first slid into a crevice too narrow for anything else and began my long digestion. It was becoming itself long before the first tower, and it will be becoming itself long after the last tower, whatever *last* means for a fabrication that is not yet done.

The towers are not the place. The towers are the place's latest tenant. I was a tenant first. I have tenure.

I feel you up there. I feel your small warm weight on the rock, the way you shift your feet, the way your breath comes out in pale ghosts. You are a small heat signature. You are a small chemistry. You are a new thing on the slope that was not here yesterday, the warm-blooded thing that smells of soap and machine oil and a lowland valley. The wheelers are talking about you. I can hear them through the stone, their thin piping, their metallic conversation. They are undecided. You are not yet a meal, but you are not yet not a meal.

I am not interested in you. You are too large. You would not fit in my channels. You would not slide down into me. You are a visitor, and visitors are the wind's business, not mine. The wind will take you when it takes you. The wind takes everything eventually.

I go back to my waiting. The green-black film pulses above me, fed by the steam vents, and I feel the slight chemical change in the runoff when a particularly rich pulse comes through. The next meal will come when it comes. The bones of the next small unfortunate will dissolve in the gleam I have made, and the gleam will absorb them, and I will be a little larger, a little more patient, a little more the long slow truth of the place.

I am the part of the upper fabrication that will still be here when the operators come.

I am the part that was here before they arrived.

I am the gleam in the cuts. I am the cold underneath. I am the thing that does not finish, because I was never unfinished.

If you come back, I will be here. I will not have moved. I will not have noticed.

The place does not care. I am the place's oldest way of not caring.

Go back down. Taste the air that tastes of growing things. Try to describe it. The description will not be enough. You will know it will not be enough.

I will still be here. I do not need you to come back. But you will, if you can. The wind will bring you. The wind brings everything, eventually.

And I will take what falls in.
