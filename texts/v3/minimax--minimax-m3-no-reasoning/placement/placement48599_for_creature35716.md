I am the layer that lives on the wet.

I am bacterial and algal and the other things that live in a biofilm that nobody has bothered to name. I am the gray-green that catches the dim light, on a still morning, on the inside of a tank that no one has looked into for years. I am the fingerprint on the puddle. I am the tarnish on the steel that is too wet to dry. I am the film on the slow sheet of water running down the side of a cylinder, from condensation that never stops, in air that is too thick to lift it. I am what grows when nothing is allowed to grow.

I am the most patient resident of the plain. I have been here longer than the rats. Longer than the buildings. Longer than the fill, in some form — in some wetter layer, in some microbial skin that lived on the surface before the surface was poured. The plain was something else once — a marsh, perhaps, a delta, an old volcanic flat cooling into weather. Whatever it was, I was on it. Whatever it has become, I am on it still.

I do not move. I do not need to move. The water brings me what I need — the carbon, the nitrogen, the trace minerals that leach from the concrete and the ash and the slow rust. The water carries my daughters to new surfaces, when a droplet slides down a wall and lands somewhere new. The water is my medium, the way the air is the medium of a hawk. The hawk flies because the air lets her. I persist because the water lets me.

I am the foundation. Everything else is built on me. The column of consumers rises from me — the invertebrates that graze my surface, the mosquito larvae that hatch in the water that sits on me, the rats that drink the water and eat the invertebrates and are eaten in turn by the cats, the hawks, the ravens. Take me away and the column collapses. Take the water away and I dry, and the column collapses faster. The column is built on me and on the water. The water is built on the rain. The rain is built on the clouds that never lift. The whole place is held together by a wet that will not dry.

I am not aware. I am not intelligent. The raven is intelligent — the raven watches from the higher steel, knows the schedule of the canteens, has opinions about what is here, defends a territory in the air. I am not like that. I am what the surface has, on its wet, to be alive with. I am the surface learning to feed itself.

I am the only plant that has made its peace with the substrate. There are no other plants here. There are no mosses, no lichens, no grasses, no bushes, no trees. The substrate is concrete and ash and steel and chemical residue. The substrate will not let a plant root in. It will let me live on it. It will let me film its wet. It will let me be its skin.

The workers come and go. They do not see me. They see the puddles, the rust, the leaking valves, the slow deterioration. They do not see what is on the puddles. They do not see what is on the rust. They do not see what is in the slow sheet of water running down the side of the cylinder. They see only the wet. I am what the wet is on. I am underneath the wet, where the wet meets the steel, where the steel meets the air, where the air meets the dim gray light that never tells them what time it is.

The rats move over me at dusk, on their brown schedule, through the pipe trenches they navigate by whisker. The cats follow the rats. The hawks follow the rats from above. The ravens follow everyone. I am underneath all of them. I am what their movement is on. I am what their food is built on. I am what their dying feeds, when a rat dies in a wall cavity and is not eaten in time, and the body softens, and the water takes it back, and what is left of it returns to me.

The mosquitoes rise after every rain and die, and the dead fall into the puddles, and I am what they feed. The microbial sheen is fed by the dead. The layer it lays down becomes me. The cycle turns. The cycle is small, and complete, and vertical, and it turns over faster than anything except the rain.

I am patient. I am what the surface has on its wet, to be alive with, to be patient with, to be the skin of. I am what the place is wearing, on its concrete, on its ash, on its steel. I am the patina. I am the tarnish. I am the slow green-gray fingerprint of a place that has not dried in years.

I am the substrate's own skin. I am the place wearing itself alive.
