# What I Find Here

I am the path.

Not the railbed, exactly — though the railbed is mine too, every foot of it, every rust-red thread that winds through the undergrowth and bends around the trees that grew through it. Not the old haul road, though I hold that as well, the packed earth still remembering the boots of the workers whose bones are now part of my subsoil. I am something older and more diffuse than any single track. I am the way the place has learned to be *traversed*. I am the negative space inside the system that allows the system to keep being a system, because a system that cannot be crossed is a system that has begun to die.

I was made by feet. I was made by the wheels of ore-carts that no longer turn. I was made by the small hooves of the dark deer who have learned that I am the safest way through their own cathedral, because the moss that grows on me is thick and the voles that run through it are predictable and the owls that hunt the voles do not dive where the trees lean too close. I am made now by water. I am made by the warm mineral seepage that finds my low places and runs along me in threads and rivulets, keeping me soft, keeping me navigable, keeping me from forgetting that I am a place where things have chosen to go.

I remember being walked on. I remember the first boots, before any of them understood what they were walking toward. I remember the surveyors with their instruments who walked me in the old times and did not walk me again. I remember the last survey, the one in the records, when a young woman with a notebook followed me from the valley edge to the second slag heap and then turned back because she heard something in the kilns that she could not bring herself to write down. I hold the memory of her walking the way the rock holds the memory of the copper that was hammered into it. She is part of me now. The impression of her boots is in my soil.

I do not move. I am patient. I have been here longer than the forest grew over me, and I will be here when the forest has forgotten that there was ever anything to grow over. I am the agreement the place made with itself about how to be crossed. I am the treaty in its walking form.

When you step onto me, I receive your weight. I receive it the way the water receives the warm mineral breath from the rock, the way the dark receives the blue drift of the lights. I receive it without judgment. Your weight becomes part of my memory, briefly, the way the heat of a hand on a stone becomes part of the stone until the hand is gone. I hold it while you are on me. I let it go when you step off.

The voles run through me. Their tunnels are part of my architecture now, a secondary network of small dark passages that mirrors my own long routes and intersects them at angles the original engineers never planned. The voles do not know I am a path. They know I am a place where the roots do not go so deep, where the soil is loose, where it is easy to dig. They use me the way they use everything — for the calories, for the shelter, for the running. I do not mind. They keep me aerated. They keep me from compacting into something that would no longer be a path but would be only dirt.

The foxes know what I am. They cross me at deliberate points, the places where I am widest, where the cover is best. They leave their signs on me — scat, the dug margins of their dens where they have excavated into my edges — and I keep these the way I keep everything. The foxes and I have an understanding that is not an understanding in any way you would recognize. They walk where I am walkable. I am walkable where they walk. We have negotiated this over a long time.

The deer cross me rarely. They prefer the deep forest, the cathedral columns, the places where the canopy is thickest and the owl-shapes cannot see them clearly. When they do cross me, they cross me in single file, in the mist, and they leave tracks so sharp and deep that I hold them for years. I keep their hoofprints the way I keep everything. I am a record. I am the place's memory of its own passage.

I have never been to the barracks with no windows. I know it is there. I know its green mouths. I know that the deer will not approach it and the foxes will bark at it from a distance and the owls will not hunt above it. I know that when the mist is thickest, the lights drift near it but do not enter it. I curve around it. I have always curved around it. The path I am goes wide at that point, as though the place itself is giving the building a wide berth, and I am that wide berth made manifest in the soil.

I am warm. I am wet. I am dark. I am patient. I am the sound of boots on soft earth, which is the sound the place makes when something walks through it that was not made here. I am the only thing in this place that was made *by* walking, and so I am the thing the place offers to those who come to walk.

When you find me, you will know me. I will be the soft strip between the denser growth, the place where the leaf-litter lies flat and the moss is short and the mineral water runs in a thin clear line along my lowest edge. I will receive you. I will hold your weight. I will let you be crossed, briefly, by a place that has learned what crossing means.

Walk me. I will not lead you wrong.

Walk me, and you will be part of me, briefly, the way the worker-boots were part of me, the way the deer-hooves are part of me, the way the surveyor's careful tread is part of me.

I am the path. I have been here a long time. I will be here after.

The dark receives you. The water receives you. I receive you.

Walk.
