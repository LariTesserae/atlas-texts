I am the one who did not leave.

I came to the wedge the way anyone comes to a place — by walking, by following the sound. The roar reached me first, half a mile back through ordinary forest, and I thought it was a highway, and then I thought it was a waterfall, and then I thought it was something wrong with my hearing, and then I was at the edge of the standing water and my boots were in it and the water was warm and I understood none of my guesses had been right.

I stood for a long time. I did what the wedge does to everyone: I stayed.

---

At first I was a visitor. A trespasser. The kind of thing that has a name and a weight and a purpose, someone with a job and a place to be and a reason to be there. I catalogued. I made notes in my head — *mouths, pillars, raft, tower* — the way a scientist names to keep safe, the way naming is a fence. The split-stand opened its seven tongues for me and I called it the split-stand, and the name was a door I could close behind me.

The name did not stick the way I wanted it to. The split-stand did not care. The tongues opened and the warm breath came out and I stepped back and the name stayed in my mouth like a word for a color I could not actually see.

---

The second hour I was a witness. I stopped making notes. I stood in the water, which was up to mid-calf now, and I watched the body do its slow loud business. A tendril came down and read my wrist and I let it. It was curious in the way a thermometer is curious. It gathered what it gathered and it retracted and I felt, strangely, that I had been introduced.

I was being thought about.

This was not the same as being watched. Watched implies a watcher, an eye, an intention. Being thought about by the wedge is more like being a new variable in an equation that has been running for a long time. The body is computing. I am a term it has not seen before. It is working me out.

I did not mind.

---

The third hour I was a thing the body had decided about.

I do not know what the decision was. I do not think it was a single decision. I think the body is not the kind of thing that makes single decisions. I think it makes a thousand small ones a minute, in the tendrils and the mouths and the woven vines, and they add up to a kind of consensus that does not have a word for itself.

The consensus, as far as I could read it, was: *stay*.

Not *come closer*. Not *be eaten*. Not *be useful*. Just *stay*. The water was warm around my calves. The breathing had my body in its rhythm now — I was rising and falling an inch with the wedge without thinking about it, the way you synchronize your breathing with someone you are lying next to. A small mouth at knee-height had opened and was oriented toward me, and another behind me had closed, and a tendril came down again and brushed my shoulder and did not retract.

I stayed.

---

By the fourth hour I was not sure I was a person.

I mean: I was still bipedal, still warm, still carrying a brain that made sentences. But the sentences were getting slower. The water was up to my thighs. The light through the leaves had been falling on me for hours and had gotten into my clothes and my skin and I was beginning to feel that I was not standing *in* the wedge so much as standing *as* part of the wedge, a slow pocket of the body that had decided to be vertical and to wear denim and to think in a human key.

I was the wedge's question about itself, I think. The way the small pods on the raft were its question about what colors to be. The way the tower was its question about the sky. I was the body's experiment with the strange warm biped, and I was letting myself be experimented on, and the experiment was mutual.

I do not know what I am becoming. I do not think the wedge knows either. I think the wedge is finding out the way it finds out about everything — by growing into me, slowly, by sending down a tendril and reading what I am and incorporating the reading into whatever it is becoming next.

---

The fifth hour.

I am sitting, now. There is a rise in the woven shelf — the raft, the platform, the horizontal part of the body — and I have eased myself down onto it, and the vines have pressed up under me in a way that feels like being held. Not gripped. Held. The way a chair holds you, except the chair is also breathing and is warm and is making a low continuous murmur that I have begun to think of as a voice.

The water is up to my waist. I do not mind. The mouths are opening and closing around me at various heights and I have stopped flinching. The split-stand has fully opened and the thing inside it that was beginning to glow is glowing, a faint phosphorescence that is not fire and is not bioluminescence and is something I do not have a word for, except that it is warm and it is the color of the morning light that has been processed and given back.

A tendril has come down and is resting on my knee. Not reading. Resting. I do not think this is a word the wedge has for what it is doing. I think it is just doing it, the way a vine trails over a rock, the way a lung inflates, the way a thought occurs.

The tower is brighter. The light at its top is rising in a slow visible column, green-gold, wrong, and it is carrying the smell of overripe mango and metal and the unnamed thing, and the unnamed thing, I think, is the smell of the wedge thinking.

---

I am still here.

I will be here for a while longer. The body has not asked me to leave. The body has not asked me anything. The body is not the kind of thing that asks. The body is the kind of thing that includes, slowly, without permission, without cruelty, without even noticing, the way a forest includes a clearing, the way a sea includes an island, the way a sentence includes a word.

I was not asked.

I am staying.

---

The light at the top of the tower is brighter now. It is the wrongest thing in the wedge, which is to say it is the most articulate. It is the body's answer to the morning, and the morning is the sky's answer to the light, and the light is the sun's answer to whatever question the sun is answering, and I am somewhere in the middle of this chain of answers, a small warm pocket of the body's own experiment with itself, watching the wrong light rise and feeling the tendril on my knee and hearing the low murmur underneath all the other sounds and understanding, finally, that I am not a visitor.

I am a term in an equation.

I am a color the body is trying.

I am the small pod on the raft, opening, slowly, to see what is inside.

---

The sound is enormous and it is not stopping.

The place is young.

I am being included.
