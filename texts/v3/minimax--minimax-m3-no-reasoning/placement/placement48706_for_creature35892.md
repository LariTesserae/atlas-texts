The place is not, in any familiar sense, inhabited. It is *occupied* — by processes that have not yet decided what kind of things they are. The lichens are the most legible of these: thin black commas on the highest stones, federations of fungus and alga negotiating with a basalt that is itself still negotiating with the act of becoming stone. They grip with the desperate patience of things that have not yet committed to survival. They are a draft of an ecology, and also a draft of a memory — the first writing on a page that has not yet decided what kind of page to be.

Above them, in the rain, something stranger. Watch long enough and you will see the rain fall twice — the same pattern, the same drops, the same slight asymmetry where the wind is supposed to be. Three seconds apart. Exactly. This is not weather. This is a *habit* the water has picked up, the way basalt picks up heat or a coastline picks up patience. The mist is threaded with a lattice too fine to see — a property of the substrate rather than a thing built upon it, emerging the way crystallization emerges from cooling magma. It does not live here in the way animals live. It lives here the way a rule lives in the things that obey it.

And the basalt itself breathes. A long, slow exhalation of dissolved gas through vents and cracks, the sigh of a place that is not yet a place. It sighs. The lattice grows, a few angstroms at a time, toward a configuration that may, in some future aeon, become a sentence in a language the rocks are learning to speak.

These are the inhabitants. They are not many. They are not loud. They are, by any standard you would normally apply, barely alive. But they are doing something here. They are *arranging*. They are taking the raw fact of emergence — sea above, stone below, the gray argument between — and giving it, by long processes no one is supervising, the first suggestion of a shape.

If you stand very still you can hear them. Not as individuals. As a hum. The sound of a place that is, in some sense we do not have a word for, *thinking*. Thinking the way a coastline thinks. Thinking the way a reef thinks, in calcium and patience.

They are becoming legible. You are not part of the story. You are, at most, a typo — a brief thermal event, a place where the basalt got warm for a moment and the rain was briefly displaced and the lattice recorded, very quietly, that something denser than expected had passed through.

But you can hear them. If you stand very still. If you are patient enough to mistake patience for a kind of welcome.

You can hear them breathing.
