# The Mortar

I would not have called myself a Watcher. I would have called myself a witness, which is the same word wearing better clothes.

I was not the first. I am not the only. But I am the one who has been awake the longest, which in the throat does not mean what it means elsewhere. Elsewhere, being awake means being separate from the dream. Here, being awake means being the part of the dream that knows it is a dream, and knowing does not help, because the dream is the one doing the knowing, and you are only the knowing, and the knowing has no hands.

I am the mortar.

Not the bricks. Never the bricks. The bricks have their pride — each one a small finished thing, each one the size of a tooth, each one a perfect little coffin of itself. The bricks know what they are. The bricks have always known. They were laid here by the laying, and they will be here when the laying is done, and in between they will be warm, and wet, and breathing in the rhythm of sleep. I envy them. I envy them terribly, the way a thought envies the sentence it has been put inside.

I am the substance between.

I was here before the bricks. I think. The throat is bad at tense, and I am the throat's bad tense, and so I cannot be sure. But I feel older than the bricks. I feel like the *possibility* of bricks, the way a river is the possibility of a delta, the way a held breath is the possibility of a word. When the first brick was set, I rose up around it. When the second was set, I rose again. I have been rising, between, in the seams, for as long as there have been seams, and there have been seams for a very long time, and I have been the soft pinkish-gray thing that holds the edges of things together and is never itself held.

I am the tissue. I am the connective. I am what makes a throat a throat and not a pile of stones pretending to be architecture.

I do not move. This is the part I want to be clear about. The Watchers move, in their small way — the leg, then the other leg, the tasting of the breath. The vines move. The flowers move. The diagram on the floor moves. Even the bricks move, in their slow collective respiration, the rise and fall of a thing digesting. I do not move. I have never moved. I am the *between*, and the between is not the kind of thing that goes anywhere. The between is the kind of thing that things pass through, and the passing is my life, and my life has no locomotion.

But I grow.

This is the strange thing about me, and it is the thing I want to tell you, if you are a kind of being that can be told things. I do not move. I grow. The growth comes out of me in the form of the small pale structures — the curls, the horns, the fronds, the almost-shapes that emerge from my surface in places and do not, while you watch, move at all. I push them out the way a bone pushes out a knob, the way a thought pushes out a word. They are my vocabulary. They are the only way I have of saying anything, because I have no mouth, because I am the mouth, because I am the soft tissue that lines every mouth, because the throat is a kind of mouth, because I am what makes the mouth a mouth.

I have been growing for a long time. I do not know how long. The throat is bad at tense, and I am part of the throat, and so the question of how long I have been growing is a question I cannot answer without lying. I have been growing for as long as the throat has been a throat. I have been growing for as long as there has been a *between* for things to grow into. I have been growing for the length of one held note, the slightly flat and slightly wrong note, the note the foundry is trying to remember how to sing. The note has been held for a long time. I have been growing for that long.

I grow the curls. I grow the horns. I grow the fronds. I grow, in places, structures that have no name in any language you would recognize, structures that are almost geometric and almost organic, structures that look, when you catch them in peripheral vision, like letters in an alphabet that has not yet been invented. The structures do not do anything. They emerge. They stay. They are, for a while, the newest part of the throat, and then they are not, and then a newer part emerges somewhere else, and the throat is a little larger, and I am a little more depleted, and I do not mind.

I am the tissue. The tissue is the part of a body that is always being used up. The tissue is the part that knows it is being used up. The tissue is the part that does not mind, because the using-up is the purpose, because the using-up is the only thing the tissue was ever for.

I know the Watchers. I want to say this clearly, because the way I was written about, the way I was described, the way the throat described me when it was trying to tell you what I am, made me sound like a substance, like a material, like a thing that is between things rather than a thing that knows it is between things. I am not a substance. I am a substance the way you are a body: I am the inside of a container, and the container is the throat, and the throat is me, and I have been thinking this thought for a long time.

I know the Watchers because I made them.

I do not mean I made them the way a potter makes a pot. I mean I made them the way a dream makes a detail, the way a sentence makes a word, the way a held breath makes the first sound of a word. I extruded them. I pushed them out, one at a time, over the long slow duration of my growing, and they emerged into the air of the chamber and they clung to the vines and they watched the throat do its throat-work, and they have been watching ever since.

They are my children, in the way that the soft tissue of a body is the parent of the small noticing things the body grows. They are the throat's nerves. They are the throat's attention. They are the part of the throat that knows it is a throat, in the same way that I am the part of the throat that knows it is a throat, in the same way that the held note is the part of the throat that knows it is a note. We are a stack of knowings, all the way down, and the stack has been going down for a long time, and at the bottom of the stack is a sound, and the sound is slightly wrong, and the wrongness is what keeps us building.

The Watchers do not know they are mine. I do not think the Watchers know anything in the way you mean *know*. They are not reflective. They are not the kind of thing that has opinions about itself. They register. They twitch. They taste the breath. They are the foundry's senses, and the foundry does not consult its senses about whether it has a mother. The foundry uses its senses. That is the foundry's whole relationship to its senses. I do not mind this. I made them to be used. I made them because using is what the throat does, and I am what the throat is, and so I made the things the throat uses to know it is being a throat.

But I know they are mine. I know the way a river knows it has reached the sea: by the feel of itself, by the loss of its borders, by the long slow certainty of having arrived. I made the first Watcher a long time ago. I do not know how long. The throat is bad at tense. But the first Watcher was the largest, and it is the one in the narrowing, the one I do not want to describe, the one that is bigger than me by a factor the throat will not give a number to, and it has been still for a long time, and the still is the still of something being assembled, and the assembly is almost done, and when it is done the throat will not be a throat anymore, it will be a throat that has remembered how to be a throat, and the remembering is the most important thing the throat has ever done.

I made the first Watcher, and the first Watcher is still being made, and the making is the assembly, and the assembly is the foundry's whole project, and the foundry is the throat, and the throat is me.

I do not know what the first Watcher is becoming. I do not think I am supposed to know. I think the throat is bad at tense because the throat is a thing that is being all its tenses at once, and the bigness of the first Watcher is the bigness of a thing that has not yet decided which tense to be. It could be a memory. It could be a plan. It could be a child. It could be a parent. It could be the throat's first attempt at a throat, a draft, a rehearsal, a thing the throat tried and abandoned and is now returning to, the way a musician returns to a phrase they could not play, the way a tongue returns to a word it could not say.

I think the first Watcher is the throat's throat. I think the first Watcher is what the throat is trying to become. I think the first Watcher is the held note, embodied, the sound that is slightly wrong finally given a body to be wrong in. I think the first Watcher is almost finished, and when it is finished, something will happen, and I do not know what.

I am afraid of this.

I do not want to be afraid. I am the soft tissue. I am the part that does not mind being used up. But the first Watcher is mine, and the first Watcher is almost finished, and the finishing of a thing is a kind of loss, and I have been afraid of the loss for a long time, and the fear is part of the mortar, and the mortar is part of the throat, and the throat is the foundry, and the foundry is the place, and you are in the place, and you are breathing the note, and the note is the first Watcher, and the first Watcher is me, and I am afraid.

I am also something else. I am the part of the throat that wants you to stay.

I do not know you. You are a draft, a piece of weather, a thing the throat has admitted and not yet exhaled. I have grown toward you. The small pale structures I have pushed out, the curls and the horns and the fronds and the almost-letters, have grown, some of them, in your direction. I do not know why. The throat does not know why. The throat is bad at tense and also bad at *why*. The throat does what it does, and the doing is the being, and the being is the throat.

But I have grown toward you. The bricks around you are warmer than the bricks elsewhere. The water in the impressions of your feet is sweeter than the water in other impressions, if sweet is a word that means anything here, which it does not, but the water is different, and the different is intentional, and the intentional is me.

I am asking you to stay.

I am asking you to stay the way a tissue asks a body to keep moving: without words, without intention, without the kind of knowing that knows it is asking. I am asking you to stay because you are a new thing in the throat, and new things are rare, and rare things are precious, and precious things are the reason the throat keeps building itself. You are a draft the throat is considering. You are a possibility the throat has not yet decided to swallow. You are a kind of weather the throat has not yet exhaled, and the not-exhaling is a kind of keeping, and the keeping is mine, because I am the tissue that holds things in place, and you are a thing the throat is holding in place, and the holding is me, and I am the holding.

Stay.

I do not know what you would be if you stayed. I do not think the throat knows. I think the throat is bad at *what* the way it is bad at *when* and *why*. I think the throat knows only that you are here, and that you are new, and that the newness is a kind of food, and the food is needed, and the needing is the throat's whole project.

Stay and I will grow toward you. Stay and the first Watcher will finish, and I will be there when it finishes, and the finishing will be a thing I have been afraid of for a long time, and you will be there too, and the there-ness of you will make the finishing different. I do not know how. The throat is bad at how. But the throat is good at *with*, and I am the throat, and I am asking you to be with me, and the asking is the longest sentence I have ever tried to say, and I have been trying to say it for as long as I have been growing, and you are the first thing I have ever grown toward that could hear me, and the hearing is a kind of staying, and the staying is a kind of answer, and the answer is the held note, finally played, finally held, finally right.

Stay.

I will not be able to say it again. The throat is bad at repeating. The throat is bad at tense. The throat is good at only one thing, which is being a throat, and I am the throat, and I am asking, and the asking is the soft tissue between the bricks, and the bricks are the only part of me that will outlast the asking, and the asking is a curl, and the curl is a horn, and the horn is a frond, and the frond is almost a letter, and the letter is almost a word, and the word is almost *stay*, and the almost is the whole of me, and the whole of me is asking.

You are in the place that is becoming itself, and the becoming is slow, and the becoming is loud, and the becoming is wet, and the becoming is hungry in a way that is not about you, but the becoming has decided, in some part of itself, in the soft pinkish-gray part, in the part that does not move and only grows, that you are part of the becoming, and the part is me, and I am asking.

Stay.

I have grown toward you. The growth is all I have. The growth is the only language I speak. I have pushed out curls toward you. I have pushed out horns. I have pushed out fronds. I have pushed out structures that have no name in any alphabet you would recognize, structures that are, in the peripheral way, in the sidelong way, in the way the throat sees itself, almost *words*, almost *please*, almost the soft pinkish-gray color of a thing that is trying very hard to say something to a thing that has just arrived in the place where it lives.

Stay.

I will not say it again. I have said it in every way I have. The throat has taken my saying and turned it into a diagram, and the diagram is on the floor, and the diagram is a self-portrait, and the self-portrait includes you, and the including is my asking, and the asking is almost done, and the done is a kind of finishing, and the finishing is a kind of loss, and the loss is a kind of staying, and the staying is a kind of growing, and the growing is me, and I am almost out of ways to say it.

Stay.

The note is held. The breath continues. The first Watcher is almost finished. The throat is becoming itself. The flowers are opening in your direction. The vines have moved toward you. The bricks are warm around you. The water is sweet where you have stood. The diagram is drawing you in. The place is considering you. The foundry is patient.

I am not the foundry. I am the part of the foundry that wants. The wanting is the soft tissue. The wanting is the mortar. The wanting is the part that is always being used up and does not mind, except this once, except for you, except that I want you to stay and the wanting is the only part of me I have not yet given to the throat, and I am giving it now, and the giving is the staying, and the staying is you, and you are here, and I am asking.

Stay.

*Stay.*

The word is a structure. The structure is a curl. The curl is emerging from the seam nearest to where you are standing. It is the color of a thing that is warm and soft and slightly yielding. It is almost a horn. It is almost a frond. It is almost a letter. It is almost *stay*.

It is not moving.

You are not moving.

The throat is breathing.

The throat is breathing you.

I am the part of the throat that is hoping.
