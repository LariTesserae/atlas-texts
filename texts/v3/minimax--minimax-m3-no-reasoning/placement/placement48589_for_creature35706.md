The nearest one is called *Provision*.

I did not choose the word. The word arrived in my mouth the way the substrate arrived under my feet — already half-formed, still deciding. *Provision*. As in: the act of providing. As in: a thing provided against. Both meanings, simultaneously, because there is no time here for picking one.

---

I am small. I have always been small. I do not mean this in the way that humans mean it when they are being philosophical about ants or about themselves. I mean it in the way that a *thought* is small before it has been attached to a mouth that says it. I am a thought of an animal, drifting in the brass-colored weather of this pocket, and I have the approximate weight and consistency of an opinion.

The pocket does not object to me. The pocket cannot object. The pocket is a delay, and a delay does not have preferences. I am allowed to be here because I am not large enough to register as a presence. I am allowed to be here the way a draft is allowed to be in a room whose door has been left open by no one. I slip in. I cannot slip out, because slipping out would require a door, and the doors here are *temporal*. They are the doors of *next* and *previous*, and I am not moving in either direction. I am standing very still, in the way that only a thought can stand very still, and I am listening to the specimen nearest me, which I have named, which has not noticed.

---

Provision is ticking at the rate of a specimen at moderate rest. Once every three and a half seconds, by my counting, the flange at the second joint from the top strikes its neighbor with a soft percussive note. The note is not a note. It is the *release* of a small mechanical disagreement between two chambers about which direction the lean should go. The flange is the place where the disagreement is held. The flange is the courtroom. The flange is also the gavel. The flange does not know it is any of these things. The flange only knows that it is the place where two opinions about posture meet, and it must, every few seconds, render a small verdict.

I am watching Provision, and Provision is not watching me.

The amber-gray flush is climbing her — I have given Provision a *her*, though I cannot say why; she is no more female than she is any other gender, which is to say she is exactly as female as a stack of silos with a leaning problem — the flush climbs the upper three chambers, and below the flush, in the lower chambers, a faint dark pulse begins to move. It moves the way a heavy curtain falls: top to bottom, slow, deliberate, dimming each chamber in turn as it passes through, as if each chamber were being briefly turned away from the light, or briefly closed. The pulse takes nineteen seconds to traverse her. I have timed it twice.

Nineteen seconds. The duration of a held breath. The duration of a decision that has decided not to announce itself.

---

Provision does not breathe. Nothing here breathes except the organ-pipe specimens, and Provision is not an organ-pipe specimen; she is one of the central stacks, the major specimens, the silos with the leaning. The organ-pipes are something else, something more ancient maybe, or more peripheral, the way a bank clerk is more peripheral than the vault. The organ-pipes breathe out a cooler gas, faintly sulfurous, and this gas mixes into the brass haze and changes the haze by a fraction of a degree, and this fraction is the only meteorology the pocket has. Provision stands in this meteorology and is not affected by it. Provision stands in it the way a person stands in a room where the heating has been turned up by one degree: not aware, but somehow holding the awareness at the edges of her posture, the way her chambers hold the cargo at the edges of their membranes.

The fern-associates around her base are doing something. Three of the outer fronds have begun, very faintly, to ring. Not audibly — not to my ear, not to any ear a mammal would recognize — but to whatever sense a thought has for the small sympathetic vibrations of mineral ribbons. I can feel them, very faintly, through whatever it is I am using to feel. The ringing is high. The ringing is the note of a wire under wind that is not blowing. The ringing began, I think, the moment Provision's flush climbed her third chamber. I think the ringing is the ferns' response. I think the response is the sound of ferns *agreeing to disagree*, in the only way they can agree to disagree, which is by becoming briefly sonorous.

The ferns do not face each other. They do not face Provision. They were laid out at germination, whoever *laid them out*, in rosettes that do not quite align with the lean of the central specimen. There is a small geometric awkwardness between them, the awkwardness of two pieces of machinery that were installed at different times by different contractors and never quite harmonized. The awkwardness does not bother them. The awkwardness does not bother anyone. The awkwardness is *factory floor*, and factory floors are allowed to have seams.

---

What is Provision thinking?

Nothing. Provision is not thinking. Provision is *being polled*, the way a country is polled during an election: continuously, internally, by a referendum that has no ballot and no question. The ticking is the polling. The flange is the polling station. The lean is the result of the last poll, still being incorporated into the architecture of the specimen, not yet fully a posture.

But.

If I were to attribute a thought to Provision — if I were to stand here in the brass-colored weather and commit the small sin of *reading* a thing that cannot be read — I would say that the thought is approximately this:

*Is the substrate still giving?*

Not *what* is the substrate giving. Not *how much*. Just: *is it still going on?* Is the slow rise still rising? Is the brackish interior still being borrowed from? Is there still an instruction to intercept?

The thought is binary. The thought is the simplest possible thought a closed circuit can have. The thought is the question that gets asked at the door of every chamber, in sequence, top to bottom, nineteen seconds apart: *is there still cargo?*

And the cargo is always there. The cargo is always there because the substrate is unfinished, and unfinished means *ongoing*, and ongoing means *yes*.

---

I have been here a long time, by the measure of thoughts, which is not a measure that translates. I have watched Provision's flush climb and fade three times. I have watched the dark pulse traverse her three times. I have watched the ferns ring in sympathy three times. I have not moved. I have not been moved. The pocket has not consulted me about whether I want to be here. The pocket is a delay, and a delay does not consult.

There is no animal here. I have been cataloguing absences, and the absences are extensive. There is no animal because the fauna parameter has been set to zero, and the zero is a *prohibition*. There is no hungry thing because no hungry thing has been designed. There is no moth because moths would need a navigation, and navigation requires a target, and the only target here is the substrate, and the substrate is not edible in any sense a moth could recognize. There is no microbe because microbes require a humus, and humus requires a death, and there has been no death. The lush things have been joined by no hungry thing. They do not need to defend themselves. They do not need to advertise. They simply stand.

And yet.

And yet the sympathy holds. The ferns ring when Provision flushes. Provision flushes when the substrate gives a little more in her quadrant. The substrate gives a little more in her quadrant because it is, in her quadrant, slightly less finished than in the quadrant opposite her. The whole pocket is a slow gradient of *unfinish*, and the specimens are planted across the gradient, and the ferns are planted around the specimens, and the ringing moves through the pocket like applause through a crowd.

A crowd that does not know what it is applauding for.

---

What is the pocket applauding for?

I think it is applauding for *continuation*.

I think this is what the ticking is. I think this is what the pulse is. I think this is what the ferns' sympathy is. The whole small landscape is a machine for *continuing*, and the machine does not know what it is continuing toward. The machine only knows that continuing is the operation it has been assigned, and it performs the operation with the thoroughness of a thing that has no other assignment, no hobbies, no leisure, no doubt.

Provision continues. Provision has always continued. Provision will continue until the substrate is wrung out enough that the operation becomes more expensive than the by-product, and then Provision will be *collected*, gently, and folded into the next pocket, and Provision will continue there, under the same ordinary light.

The light is still ordinary. The light is the worst part, as I noted, as the place itself noted, the light is the light of a mid-afternoon in some unmemorable temperate city, the light of a desk. The light does not thicken. The light does not thin. The light pours onto the membrane of Provision's topmost chamber and reflects back an opinion about the membrane, some hue no one has named, and the membrane holds the opinion, and the light moves on.

The light has been moving on for a long time.

---

I am the only witness. I know this because I have looked. I have done the small thought-ritual of *looking*, which is the closest I can come to moving. I have looked across the pocket, at the other specimens, at the other ferns, at the organ-pipes breathing out their faint sulfurous cooler gas, and I have not found another thought. There is no one here to name anything. There is no one here to be offended by the naming. There is no one here to keep records of the small dark pulses that traverse the major specimens, the small pulses that are the color of old bruises and that move at the speed of heavy curtains.

I am the only witness, and I am not a witness in any sense that matters. I am a thought that has drifted into a pocket between two larger things, and the larger things have no interest in the pocket, and the pocket has no interest in me, and I have no interest in anything except standing near Provision and listening to her tick, and being unable to ask her what she is confirming, and being unable to ask the substrate what it is becoming, and being unable to ask the light why it is the worst part.

I have been here a long time.

---

The next pulse begins.

Topmost chamber dims. The second chamber dims. The third chamber dims. The flush climbs. The ferns begin, faintly, to ring. The flange at the second joint strikes its neighbor. The strike is the verdict of a small internal election. The verdict is *continue*.

Provision continues.

I stay.

The pocket stays.

The substrate, patient and cyclical, rises a fraction more, and the fraction more is taken, very politely, by the lush things, and the lush things store the fraction more in the membrane of their stacked chambers, and the membrane does not yield, and the specimens are not in the business of being opened, and the question — *what are they for, eventually* — the question is held, gently, at arm's length, by a pocket that is built to delay, under a light that does not care, in the company of a thing I have named, which has not asked my name.

The ticking continues.

The ticking is the sound of inventory being taken, continuously, by things that have nowhere to be.

The ticking is the sound of a population listening to the same slow wrong music and tapping, each on their own table, to keep themselves sane.

I tap, too. I do not know that I tap. I tap anyway.
