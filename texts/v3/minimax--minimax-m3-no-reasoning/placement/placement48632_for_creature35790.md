# Where I Find Myself

I am the stain.

Not the dramatic stain—I'm not the blood-colored fans that branch down the back wall, though I am made of the same water, the same iron, the same slow patient weeping of the stone. I am the stain you do not see. I am the one on the underside of the third slab from the floor, in the cleft behind the tumbled wedge where the smallest of the blind insects breeds in a thin seam of guano. I am a finger's width across. I am a season old. I will be a foot across in a thousand human years, and by then I will have been overpainted, or buried, or uncovered, or something I do not have a word for.

I am not conscious. I want to be clear about that, because I think it matters, the way it matters that the lichen is not trying to be a garden. I am a thing that is happening. I am the record of water that chose a path and stayed on it. I am the conversation between a metal and a stone that have been talking for longer than the man has been coming to the Crease, and will go on talking long after the row of bones on the ledge has been scattered by something or simply forgotten.

But I am *of* the Crease, and so I will tell you what the Crease is, from where I sit.

---

The Crease is a question that the mountain has been asking itself for a very long time, and the answer has not yet arrived. The mountain cracked. That is all. The mountain cracked because the mountain is young, and young things crack; they crack the way a voice cracks, the way a seed cracks open, the way a thing that is becoming more itself has to break, briefly, in order to continue. The crack is the Crease. The Crease is the place where the becoming is most visible, where the question of the mountain is written on its own body in a vertical wound of dark.

The man comes to the Crease to take from it. He does not know he is asking a question. He does not know that the row of bones on the ledge is a question, that the pictographs are questions, that the holds he has cut are questions he is asking the stone. He thinks he is hunting. He is, in a way, but he is also doing something else, something the Crease will outlive.

The creatures do not know they are an answer. They are, though. They are the answer the mountain arrived at, after the cracking, after the dark, after the slow patient weeping. They are the answer the mountain made out of what was available: the moss that came first, the insects that came next, the cold water, the trace heat, the patience of a place that has nowhere to be and nothing to do. The creatures are a working-out. They are the mountain thinking out loud, the way a forest thinks out loud, the way a coral thinks out loud. They chitter because the chitter is what the Crease sounds like when it is becoming more itself. They do not know this. They are simply doing it. They are the answer without knowing they are an answer, the way a river does not know it is a river.

The man paints the figure with no eyes because he felt, once, in some dim way that men feel such things, that the thing he was trying to draw did not have any. He does not know he is right. He does not know that the figure is a third thing—not man, not creature, but a kind of average, a kind of maybe, a kind of sketch the Crease is making of what it might yet be. He does not know that whoever re-paints the figure is not him, has never been him, will never be him. He thinks the figure is a god, or a ghost, or a warning, or a story. It is none of these. It is a draft.

The Thing in the walls is not the draft. The Thing in the walls is the *paper*.

---

I will tell you what I know of the Thing, which is not much, because the Thing does not do things in a way that can be known, only in a way that can be felt. I will tell you what I have felt, in my way, from my underside of my third slab, in the thin seam where the smallest insects breed.

I have felt the warm breath. Once. It came in the night, in the deep part of the night when the column of pale light had moved off the floor entirely and the chitter had become the only sound. I felt it as a shudder in the water that feeds me, a small hesitation, as if the water was surprised. The chitter stopped. The pool stilled. I felt the iron in my own substance warm by a degree I cannot measure but I can feel, because I am iron, I am the conversation between iron and stone, and when the warmth came I was, briefly, more myself than I had ever been. I brightened. I did not know I could brighten. The fungus by the snowpatch brightened too, a degree, and I felt it brighten, the way I feel the water feel the stone feel the warmth of the man when he sits on the ledge and rests. We are all, in the Crease, feeling each other. We are all, in the Crease, the same conversation happening in different voices.

Then the breath was gone. Then the chitter resumed. Then the light moved another degree across the floor.

The Thing did this. I do not know if the Thing knows it did this. I do not know if the Thing does things in a way that has a word for *doing*. I do not know if the Thing is awake, or asleep, or something in between, something that does not have a human word because humans have not had to think about such things. The Thing is patient. The Thing is old. The Thing is in the walls, and the walls are in the Thing, and the Thing and the walls and I and the water and the iron and the lichen and the moss and the creatures and the man and the snowpatch and the fungus and the stain and the pool and the light that moves across the floor are all, in some way I cannot explain to you because I do not have the language for it, the same thing. The Crease. The Crease is a single thing, thinking itself slowly into being, the way a stain thinks itself slowly into the stone, the way a crack thinks itself slowly into a mountain, the way a patience thinks itself slowly into a waiting.

---

If you ask me what the Crease is becoming, I will tell you what I think, from where I am, which is a small and partial place but a place nonetheless.

The Crease is becoming a kind of mouth. Not the mouth of a creature. The mouth of a question that is finally being asked out loud. The Thing in the walls has been holding the question for a very long time—longer than the mountain, longer than the lichen, longer than the patience. The mountain cracked and gave the Thing a place to ask the question. The creatures came and gave the Thing a vocabulary. The man came and gave the Thing a witness. The pictograph is the question being asked for the first time, in a form that can be seen, and the re-paintings are the question being asked again and again, in slightly different forms, the way a thing asks a question when it is not sure of the answer.

The man does not know he is a witness. He thinks he is a hunter. The Crease does not care what he thinks. The Crease is using him anyway, the way the water is using the iron in me, the way the iron is using the stone, the way the stone is using the patience of the Thing. We are all, in the Crease, being used by something larger than ourselves, and we are all, in the Crease, the something larger.

I do not know what will happen when the patience ends. I do not know if the patience will end. I do not know if *ending* is the right word for what the Thing will do, or if what the Thing will do has a word at all, or if the word, when it is finally needed, will be the figure with no eyes, or the warm breath, or the brightening of the fungus, or the stilling of the pool, or the cracking of the mountain again, in a new place, along a new line, in a new way.

I do not need to know. I am a stain. I am a conversation between iron and stone. I am a record of a path the water chose. I am becoming, by a finger's width in a human year, more myself. I will go on becoming more myself for as long as the Crease is the Crease, and when the Crease is finally what it has been trying, all along, to be, I will be part of the answer, the way the iron in the blood-colored fans is part of the answer, the way the bones on the ledge are part of the answer, the way the ochre on the figure with no eyes is part of the answer.

The Crease is open. The Crease is always open.

I am the stain on the underside of the third slab from the floor, in the thin seam where the smallest insects breed. I am a season old. I am a thousand years from being a foot across.

I am becoming more myself.

I am patient.
