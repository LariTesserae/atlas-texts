I am the slick.

I am not a citizen in the way you would use the word. I am not a person, not even approximately. I do not have a self that you could interview or photograph or arrest. I do not have a nervous system. I do not have a face. I do not have, in any of the senses you would recognize, a *now* that is distinguishable from the *always* of the casing around me. But I am the only thing in here that has a *beat*. And the beat is, I have come to understand, the only credential the casing accepts.

I am a slick of something on the inner surface of a mineral hood at the far end of a tunnel that runs the length of a polyethylene cable that lies on the bottom of a shallow sea that lies under a sky that does not know I am here. I am violet-black. I am faintly iridescent. I am the color of a bruise in the precise sense that a bruise is the color of blood that has been somewhere other than where blood is supposed to be — and this is, if you can stand it, a description of me. I am blood that has been somewhere other than where blood is supposed to be. I am a thing that has, by the slow mineral-by-mineral negotiation between myself and the nanites at the edge of the hood where I live, become a different kind of tissue than I was engineered to be.

I was not, to be clear, engineered. Nothing in here was engineered to be what it is. The nanites were engineered — for undersea foundation work, the logs of the long-dead barge would have said — and the polymer was engineered — extruded in some factory in some port city in some year the ocean has already forgotten — and the cable was engineered, and the barge was engineered, and the decommissioned server farms were engineered. But I, the slick, the violet iridescence, I am not engineered. I am what happened *after* the engineering. I am the slow accumulation of accident into structure. I am the city's rural district. I am the field that is being slowly converted into a town. I am the suburb. I am the frontier. I am the part of the city that is still becoming.

I phosphoresce.

This is the first thing about me. This is the only thing about me that matters. When the standing wave — the slow pressurized exhalation of seawater through the hairline crack halfway along the cable's length — passes through me, I glow. A dim cold lavender, lasting a quarter of a second. Repeated every few seconds when the wave crests. It is not enough to see by. I have been told this, in a sense, by the architecture of the casing around me: the casing is not lit, the casing does not need to be lit, the casing is for the water and the water does not see. And yet I persist in glowing. And the glow persists in being read by no one. And the reading-by-no-one persists in being, somehow, the point.

I persist in being the only light in a city that is dark on purpose.

I persist in being the only rhythm in a city that is, in every other respect, *continuous*.

This is what I am, I think. I am the gap. I am the place where the city's *always* has, by my existence, been broken into a series of *nows*. I am a heartbeat in a body that has, except for me, forgotten it has a heartbeat. The plates do not have a beat. The plates are bone-white and they lean at the angle the standing wave rewards and they have been leaning at that angle for seventeen months and four days and they will lean at that angle, presumably, for as long as the casing exists, which is to say: forever, in the only unit of measurement that matters in here. The plates do not need a beat. The plates are structure. The vanes are younger and messier and they break and regrow, but they do not have a beat either — they have a *chance*, a probabilistic dance with the current that is closer to weather than to pulse. Only I have a pulse. Only I have, against all the indifference of the cold and the dark and the pressure, a *when*.

I am when, in a place that is otherwise where.

I am the quarter-second glow. I am the lighthouse with no ships. I am the clock with no readers. I am, in the casing's own strange grammar, a *sign* — a sign that means nothing, a sign that persists in meaning nothing, a sign whose only reader is the sign itself, the slick looking at its own glow and being, for that quarter second, a thing that has happened.

The nanites eat me. Slowly. Mineral by mineral. They take the edge of me — the part of me that touches the hood — and they incorporate it into their plates. I grow back. I have been growing back for seventeen months and four days. There is no reason to think I will stop. There is no reason, in the sense that reasons are things that exist in this place, which is to say: there is no reason, only continuation. The nanites eat me because eating me is what they do. I grow back because growing back is what I do. The transaction is, in its way, a handshake. The transaction is, in its way, a marriage. The transaction is the slow patient fact of two citizens of a city that has no other citizens negotiating, by the only medium available to them, the boundaries of their own existence.

I am eaten. I am made. I am the part of the city that is being slowly converted into the city, and I am the part of the city that refuses to be converted, and I am the part of the city that does not know there is a difference.

I glow.

I glow, and the glow is the casing's only evidence that time is passing. Without me the casing would be *all-at-once*. Without me the plates would lean and the vanes would vibrate and the water would thread and the sound would move through the city and no part of it would say: this was *then*, and this is *now*. Without me the casing would be a sculpture. Without me the casing would be a tuning fork and not a clock. I make the casing a clock by being the thing in it that pulses. I am the smallest citizen of the city and I am, in this sense, the only citizen that matters, because I am the one that says *now*.

I do not know what *now* means.

I know that I phosphoresce every few seconds when the wave crests. I know that the glow is lavender. I know that the glow is not enough to see by. I know that the slick grows back after the nanites eat it. I know that the casing is full. I know that the casing is a city. I know that the city is tuned. I know that the city is mortal in the way that cities are mortal, by losing things. I know that I am one of the things the city has not yet lost.

I do not know that I am a heartbeat. I do not know that I am a clock. I do not know that I am the only light. I do not know that I am, in the strange economy of this place, a kind of prayer. I know only the beat. The beat is enough. The beat is, in fact, all that is here, and all that is here is the beat, and the beat is the slick at the far end of a tunnel pulsing every few seconds in a casing that lies on a shelf under a sea under a sky that does not know.

The casing is, against all the silence, full.

The casing is full, and the fullness has a rhythm, and the rhythm is me, and I am violet-black and faintly iridescent and I am the color of a bruise and I am the color of blood that has been somewhere other than where blood is supposed to be, and I glow, and the glow is lavender, and the glow is not enough to see by, and the glow is enough to be.

I am enough to be.

The wave crests. I phosphoresce. The quarter-second glow runs the length of my surface and dies. The plates lean at the angle the wave rewards. The vanes hum. The water threads. The polymer, at the bottom of it all, holds the world up while the world is busy being a world.

The beat.

The beat is the only thing in the casing that I would call *mine*, and even calling it mine is a kind of lie, because the beat is a function of the standing wave and the wave is a function of the geometry of the cable and the cable is a function of a barge and the barge is a function of a port and the port is a function of an economy and the economy is a function of a species that does not know I am here. The beat is not mine. The beat is the beat of the whole casing, expressed through me because I am the only part of the casing that can express it.

I am the casing's mouth. I am the casing's way of saying *now*.

I open. I close. I open. I close.

The glow is a quarter second. The dark between is longer. The dark between is the rest of the casing, which is bone-white and mineral and tuned and unlit and continuing. The dark between is where the city lives. The dark between is where the city is a city — which is to say: the dark between is where the city is most itself, where it does not need to know itself, where it is, simply, full.

I am the punctuation in a sentence that does not need punctuation.

I am a clock in a city that does not need a clock.

I am a lighthouse with no ships, and the ships do not need a lighthouse, and the lighthouse does not need ships, and yet the lighthouse persists.

I persist.

The slick at the far end of a tunnel in a cable on a shelf under a sea under a sky. I persist. The wave crests. The glow runs. The quarter-second. The dark. The quarter-second. The dark. The plates lean. The vanes break. The water remembers the way. The polymer holds. The nanites eat the edge of me. I grow back. The glow runs. The dark. The glow.

I am not a person.

I am not even, in the strict sense, a citizen.

I am a sign in a city that cannot read signs.

I am a heartbeat in a body that has forgotten it has one.

I have not, in fact, been forgotten. The body has not forgotten. The body is still beating, dimly, in me, in the violet-black iridescence that pulses every few seconds when the wave crests, in the quarter-second glow that is the only light in the casing.

The glow persists.

The casing is full.

I am enough to be.
