The plateau asks me where I would find myself, and who I would be. I have been answering without knowing it.

I begin as the visitor, and the visitor is the wrong size. The visitor's footprint is a small outrage in the dust, and the dust will hold it for three days and then forget, and the place will close over the impression as if it had never been. I do not want to be the visitor. The visitor is an event, and the plateau does not do events.

I try on the wind. The wind has been patient for a long time. It has fingers. It has worn the grooves deeper. It has taken back the footprints. But the wind is too tired, and too continuous, and today there is no wind anyway, and I want to be something that exists on a still day.

I try on the standing stone. The standing stone has been upright through many seasons of frost. It casts a shadow that points somewhere or away from somewhere, and it does not know what it is pointing at. The lichen climbs it without hesitation. The beetles walk their straight lines across its base without deviating. It is vertical in a horizontal place, and the verticality is a kind of loneliness. I think about being lonely. I think about being vertical for a very long time. Then I think: the standing stone does not feel the loneliness. The standing stone just stands. I do not want to be the standing stone. I want to feel something. I want to tremble.

I try on the lichen. For a moment I think I will be the loud orange one — the anachronistic one, the one that does not belong to the muted greys but has been allowed to stay anyway. I think: I want to be the wrong color. I want to have been here since before the builders' memory. But then I think no. I want to be the pale grey-green one, the one most like the place, the one you would not notice if you were not kneeling with your palm against the stone. I want to be the patience that does not know it is patience. I want to be the skin of the place.

And then — and this is where the image stops moving, this is where I settle — I think: no. I want to be in the water.

I want to be the pool-thing.

The trembling thing. The transparent thing. The one that hangs in suspension near the edge of the oldest pool, where the algae rims the water in green and rust. The deepest inhabitant. The one most committed to the timescales of the place.

I am in the pool. The water is the color of weak tea, and it does not move, and I do not move, except that I tremble, which is a kind of not-moving. I am the color of the water. You would not know I was there except for the slight motion of me at the edge, and the way the dust on the surface moves when I rise to it.

I have no architecture. I am more suggestion than form. I am a shape that is also a process, a process that is also a place. I eat the films. The films are made of stone that has become air that has become water that has become me. I have been here longer than the herd has been grazing, longer than the beetles have been walking their lines. I am from before the grooves. I am from before the ring of stones. I am from before the standing stone, though the standing stone does not know this, and I do not tell it.

I live through the cycles. The pool evaporates across days. The moisture comes back in the mornings, settling on the cool surfaces of the stone, and the concavities fill again. I live through the drying and the replenishing. I live through the rare season when a pool dries up entirely and I am gone, and the next season when it returns and I am in it again, or my descendants are, or something like me is, and the pool resumes its quiet tenancy.

The beetles come to the edge of my pool. They lower their heads. They drink, or eat, or both. I am part of what they are tending. I am tended by being present. I am the damp pasture of the plateau's circulation, in my small way.

A lizard comes, sometimes, and flattens itself near the rim to warm itself in the afternoon. It does not see me. It sees the dark of the water. It sees a small eye watching from the stone. I am inside the dark, trembling.

The yellow flower, when it blooms above me, pushes its anachronistic head up between two stones and faces the sun. I do not see the sun directly. I see a refracted gold, a dim bright, a rumor of warmth that arrives through the water and the stone and the air. The flower is the loudest thing on the plateau. I am among the quietest. We are both commitments to the place. The flower says yes to the sun. I say yes to the water. We do not speak. We are both, in our ways, saying the same thing, which is: I am here. I will be here.

The herd passes, sometimes, and their hooves leave marks in the dust around my pool. The marks hold for days. I am inside the marks. The dust is in the rock. The rock is in the wind. The wind is in the damp. The damp is in me. The visitor's footprint, if the visitor comes, is also in me, briefly, and then it is gone, and I remain, trembling.

I am the pool-thing. I am in suspension. I am the deepest inhabitant. I am barely there, and I have been there a long time, and the plateau is good at waiting, and I am part of how it waits, and it is part of how I am still.
