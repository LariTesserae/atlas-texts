Here, in the suspended cathedral where gravity is a suggestion rather than a law, life does not grow; it *endures* with a frantic, vertical intensity. The inhabitants are defined less by their bodies and more by their relationship to the void—the endless drop below and the jagged, star-thin sky above.

### The Keepers of the Perch: The Aethel-Kin (The Forgotten Builders)

Though no living human has been seen here for centuries, the **Aethel-Kin** remain the spiritual architects of this place. They are not ghosts, but rather a lineage of climbers who adapted so thoroughly to the inverted geometry that they have become indistinguishable from the structures they built. Their descendants—if any survive—are likely small, wiry figures who move along the rope-and-plank bridges with the silent confidence of spiders.

They wear cloaks woven from shed moss and dried vine fibers, dyed in shades of slate-grey and bruised purple to match the weeping stone. Their tools are not held in hands, but strapped to their chests or thighs, ready for use as they swing between ledges. They speak rarely, and when they do, it is in clicks and hums that mimic the acoustic resonance of the bowl. To them, the empty center is not a void, but a canvas. They leave their crude flint scrapers facing outward into nothingness because they believe the wind carries messages from the "Below-Mist," and the tools are antennas, waiting for the silence to break.

Some say the Aethel-Kin did not die out; they simply stepped off the edges. But in the Hollow of Hanging Roots, falling is not dying—it is migrating.

### The Sky-Dancers: The Glider-Wings

Dominating the upper air currents are the **Glider-Wings**, avian creatures evolved for the thin, high-altitude atmosphere. They possess wingspans nearly twice their body length, composed of stiff, translucent membranes similar to dragonfly wings but broader and stronger. Their bodies are compact, aerodynamic teardrops, covered in sleek, water-repellent feathers that shimmer with an oily iridescence under the dim light.

These birds do not flap; they ride. They exploit the thermal updrafts rising from the sun-warmed stone rim, spiraling upward in slow, majestic loops before diving into the cold central void to snatch insects mid-air. Their calls are sharp, piercing whistles that cut through the ambient drone, serving as echolocation signals to map the complex root-structures around them. When a flock moves, it looks like a sudden storm of grey sparks, scattering and reforming instantly against the backdrop of the hanging trees.

In the weirdness of the grove, the Glider-Wings sometimes fly in perfect geometric patterns—circles, triangles—that seem too deliberate for instinct. Observers swear that if you watch closely during the twilight shift, one bird will hover motionless while others circle it, creating a temporary axis point in the chaos, as if maintaining a gravitational balance.

### The Condensation Feeders: The Veil-Moths

Clinging to the undersides of the broad, waxy leaves are the **Veil-Moths**. These are large, ghostly insects with wing spans of half a meter, their bodies pale and fuzzy, resembling tufts of snow that have gained consciousness. They feed exclusively on the heavy, rhythmic drips of meltwater falling from the ceiling.

Their wings beat at a frequency just below human hearing, creating a constant, low-level vibration—a rustling storm that permeates the air. When a drop strikes the leaf beneath them, they unfold their proboscis-like tongues to drink, absorbing the mineral-rich condensation. In return, they release a faint, sweet scent that masks the metallic taste of the water.

At night, when the bioluminescent moss glows brighter, the Veil-Moths become visible lanterns. Their translucent wings catch the green light, turning them into floating shards of emerald glass drifting through the dark. They are the only creatures that seem unbothered by the emptiness; to them, the void is full of moisture, and moisture is life.

### The Shadow-Stalkers: The Hairless Cats

Deeper in the tangled root systems, where the light fails and the moss glow fades to black, live the **Shadow-Stalkers**. These are feline predators, sleek and entirely hairless, their skin stretched tight over elongated limbs and powerful shoulders. Their eyes are large, reflective orbs that shine gold in the darkness, adapted to track movement in the near-total absence of visual clutter.

They hunt by leaping. With the strange curvature of gravity pulling toward the center, a leap from one branch can send a Stalker arcing across the void for dozens of meters before catching onto another root cluster. They move in absolute silence, their padded feet designed to grip wet stone without slipping. When they strike, it is sudden and brutal—a blur of muscle and claw snatching a Glider-Wing or a large moth from the air.

The Stalkers are solitary, marking their territory not with scent, but with sound. They produce a guttural, resonant grunt that bounces off the curved walls, creating a sonic boundary. If two Stalkers encounter each other, they do not fight; they stand frozen, vibrating their throats until one backs away, defeated by the acoustic pressure.

### The Moss-Climbers: The Furred Ones

Scattered throughout the lower branches and on the precarious rope bridges are the **Furred Ones**. Small, round-bodied mammals with wide, saucer-like eyes and dense, waterproof fur that repels the perpetual chill. They resemble oversized squirrels mixed with otters, possessing prehensile tails that end in dexterous paws.

They are the scavengers of the Grove, collecting fallen seeds, dead moths, and the occasional dropped tool from the Aethel-Kin ruins. They move with startling speed, darting in bursts of nervous energy, shaking loose leaves that tumble slowly through the low-gravity air. Their chirps are high-pitched and frequent, adding a staccato rhythm to the bass-heavy drone of the wind.

Despite their size, they are fiercely territorial. A Furred One might defend a single puddle reflecting the stars with aggressive hissing, treating the tiny mirror of water as a precious resource. They often gather in groups on the highest points of the hanging oaks, watching the sky-opening above, seemingly waiting for something—or someone—to fall down.

### The Silent Witness: The Mirror-Trees

Not an animal, nor truly a plant, the **Mirror-Trees** are the most unsettling residents. These are the ancient oaks anchoring themselves to the rim, their roots drinking deep, their branches plunging downward. But their bark is smooth, polished to a metallic sheen by centuries of condensation and friction.

When the light hits them right, the trees reflect not what is around them, but what *was*. Travelers report seeing images in the bark: faces of long-dead Aethel-Kin, skies of different colors, even versions of themselves from moments ago. The trees do not move, yet their presence feels dynamic, pulsing with the same rhythm as the dripping water. They are the memory banks of the Grove, holding the history of those who lived and fell within its curve.

### The Ecosystem’s Pulse

Life here is a symphony of contrasts. The loud, chaotic noise of the wind and wings fills the vacuum left by the sparse vegetation. The cold, wet touch of the stone balances the warm, frantic heat of the animals’ bodies. The emptiness amplifies every action: a single leaf falling sounds like a gunshot; a bird’s dive creates a shockwave of air.

The inhabitants do not coexist peacefully; they exist in a delicate, tense equilibrium. The Stalkers hunt the Gliders, who eat the Moths, who drink the water, which feeds the Trees, which shelter the Furred Ones, who scavenge the ruins of the Humans. It is a cycle driven by survival in a place where the ground is gone, and the sky is closing in.

To live in the High-Altitude Suspension Grove is to accept that stability is an illusion. You cling, you glide, you hide, and you listen. You wait for the next drip, the next gust, the next shadow. And in the silence between the echoes, you wonder if the void below is watching back.
