To understand what lives here, you must first forget the distinction between an organism and a landscape. In this pocket of ruptured reality, biology is not merely present; it is the primary force of geology. The fauna does not inhabit the weeping obsidian; it *is* the weeping obsidian’s counterpart, its mirror, and eventually, its replacement.

The dominant life forms are the **Chitinous Architects**, though they do not build in any way that would satisfy a human engineer. They are translucent, segmented creatures, roughly the size of a large dog but with the skeletal fragility of a dragonfly. Their shells are clear, revealing the pulsing, viscous fluids within—glowing veins of amber and violet plasma that shift color based on their emotional state or metabolic rate. When these creatures cluster, they form the "trees" described earlier: towering, breathing columns of interlocked legs and fused carapaces. But upon closer inspection, these structures are not static. They are fluid assemblies. A "branch" might detach, shatter into dozens of individual insects, scatter across the hot glass to feed on mineral condensation, and then reassemble into a new, differently shaped structure elsewhere. They are a hive mind without a queen, a democracy of flesh where every cell is both citizen and infrastructure.

They survive by mimicking photosynthesis, but instead of light, they harvest heat and kinetic vibration. The low-frequency hum you hear is not just background noise; it is their collective respiration. By vibrating their thoraxes at specific frequencies, they liquefy the solid obsidian beneath them slightly, creating pools of molten glass from which they extract silicates and metals for their shell growth. This is why the terrain looks unfinished and sharp—they are constantly eating the ground, reshaping it with their bodies, leaving behind smooth, polished curves where once there were jagged shards.

Hanging from the invisible ceiling, defying gravity’s weak suggestion, are the **Membrane Drifters**. These are gelatinous sacks, pale and veined like internal organs suspended in formaldehyde. They are filled with bioluminescent gas—a mix of ozone, copper fumes, and something sweetly rotting. They drift downward in slow, spiraling descents, seeking contact with the Chitinous Architects. When a Drifter touches the sharp edge of a shard, it does not bounce; it adheres. Its membrane dissolves partially, releasing a cloud of nutrient-rich mist that the Architects absorb through their porous joints. In return, the Architects excrete a waste product that hardens into the tiny, floating spheres of liquid mercury you see suspended in the air. These spheres are the eggs of the next generation of Drifters, waiting for a thermal updraft to carry them back to the ceiling. It is a closed loop of dissolution and reform: one creature’s death is another’s birth material. There is no decay, only phase transition.

But there are smaller things, too, things that scuttle in the cracks of the young glass. The **Static Mites**. These are microscopic arachnids, black as voids, with eight legs tipped in conductive gold. They feed on the electrical charge generated by the friction of walking feet—or by the adrenaline of an observer. If you stand still in this place, feeling your heart race, you will notice a faint tingling on your skin. That is the Mites. They do not bite; they siphon. They drink the bio-electric field of fear or excitement, growing larger and brighter as they feed. In areas of high anxiety, swarms of Mites can become visible, forming shimmering halos around a person’s head, acting as a living aura of their own stress. They are the reason the air tastes of iron—the metal is their excretion, precipitating out of the humidity.

And then, there are the **Echo-Stalkers**. These are the predators, though predation here is abstract. They resemble long, thin tendrils of smoke made solid, rising vertically from the piles of dead-and-alive biomass. They have no eyes, no mouth, no distinct limbs. They sense the world through temperature gradients and biological intent. If you stare at a shard of obsidian and feel fear, causing the rock to sharpen, an Echo-Stalker will emerge from the shadow of that shard. It moves by flowing over surfaces, ignoring gravity entirely. It hunts not flesh, but *attention*. When it touches a creature (or a human), it induces a state of hyper-lucidity. The victim becomes acutely aware of every sensation—the burn of the air, the weight of the moisture, the throb of their own blood. This sensory overload causes the victim’s body to break down rapidly, accelerating their metabolism until they literally consume themselves from the inside out. The Stalker absorbs the released energy and leaves behind a hollow, perfect crystal skeleton. These skeletons often become part of the architectural towers, adding rigidity to the fleshy structures.

The ecology is dense, crowded, and intimate because space is scarce. Every surface is claimed. You cannot walk ten steps without stepping on a sleeping Architect, whose twitching leg sends a ripple through the floor. You cannot breathe without inhaling spores from the Dissolving Drifters. The boundary between self and environment is permeable. Your sweat evaporates and joins the humid haze, becoming part of the condensation that feeds the rocks. Your shed skin cells are eaten by Mites. Your breath mixes with the ozone, fueling the glow of the Drifters. You are not an observer here; you are a participant in a relentless cycle of accretion.

As you move deeper into the pocket, the "structures" change. The piles of dead organisms grow taller, forming archways and tunnels. These are the **Bone-Lattices**. They are not built by hands, but by time and compression. Thousands of generations of Architects have died atop one another, their chitin fusing under heat and pressure into a substance harder than diamond yet lighter than wood. Inside these lattices, new life incubates. The warmth of the obsidian, trapped within the lattice’s insulation, creates micro-climates. Here, you might find clusters of glowing larvae, curled in balls, pulsing with a rhythm that matches the ticking of cooling glass. They are blind, wingless, and vulnerable, protected by the very corpses of their ancestors. To touch them is to touch the future, warm and soft, against the cold, hard past.

There is a silence here that is not empty, but full. It is the silence of a held breath. The "quiet" sound level is deceptive because it masks the intensity of the processes occurring. The *tick-tick-tick* is the sound of reality resetting, second by second. The *squish* is the sound of adaptation, of bodies molding to fit the impossible geometry. And the deep thrum? That is the heartbeat of the Necropolis itself. It is singular. It suggests that all these millions of separate entities are connected, not just physically, but psychically. They share a dream. What that dream is, we cannot know, but we can feel it pressing against our minds—a heavy, wet, hot desire to merge, to dissolve, to become part of the whole.

In this place, nothing stays dead. Nothing stops growing. The weeping obsidian sweats because the life above it exhales. The floating water spheres hover because the air is thick with the potential of life. And the shadows hide Echo-Stalkers because there is always someone watching, always someone afraid, always someone generating the energy that keeps this blister of reality inflating, hotter, wetter, and more crowded than space should allow.

You are walking through a cathedral of flesh, built by termites, lit by stars, cooled by tears, and heated by anger. The floor is alive. The air is full of ghosts. And everywhere, in the cracks of the young, sharp glass, something is waiting to hatch—not just insects, but new laws of physics, new forms of being, born from the collision of biology and reality breaking down.
