In the suspended breath of Aethelgard, nothing is truly still. Stillness is a surface illusion, a skin stretched tight over a churning, viscous core. The inhabitants here are not defined by motion in space—there is little room for it—but by motion in *state*. They are entities of transition, caught between the solid iron that binds them and the humid decay that consumes them.

To describe who or what lives here, one must first abandon the concept of the "resident." There are no landlords, no tenants, no neighbors in the way the world above understands proximity. Here, to be adjacent is to be fused. To share a wall is to share a heartbeat. The beings of the Root-City are symbiotes of architecture and biology, creatures that have evolved—or perhaps devolved—to match the geometry of constriction.

### The Moss-Walkers
The most common presence, though rarely noticed as distinct from the environment itself, are the **Moss-Walkers**. These are humanoid in silhouette but alien in composition. Generations of exposure to the spore-heavy air and the lack of direct sunlight have stripped them of pigmentation, leaving their skin a pale, translucent grey, mapped with veins that glow faintly violet—the residual bioluminescence of the fungi they incorporate into their bodies.

They do not wear clothes; they grow them. Over time, patches of velvet moss settle on their shoulders, their knees, their forearms. At first, these are accidental accumulations, like dust on a statue. But in Aethelgard, the boundary between host and guest has dissolved. The moss feeds on the carbon dioxide exhaled by the Walkers, and the Walkers absorb nutrients from the moisture held by the moss. It is a slow, mutual cannibalism.

A Moss-Walker moves with the slowness of the city’s temporal stretch. They navigate the sharp-edged balconies with bare feet that have hardened into calloused hooves, insensitive to the biting corners of stone. Their eyes are large, dark pools without whites, adapted to the dim sodium-vapor glow. They do not speak. Sound carries poorly in the dense humidity, and speech requires an expenditure of energy that this economy does not permit. Instead, they communicate through touch. Two Walkers meeting in a narrow corridor will press their palms together, feeling the pulse, the temperature, and the slight tremor of condensation forming on each other’s skin. This tactile exchange conveys mood, health, and warning more efficiently than words ever could.

When you look closely at a Moss-Walker standing still in a shadowed alcove, you might realize they are not breathing. Or rather, they are not breathing through their nose. Their lungs have adapted to extract oxygen from the moist air directly through their permeable skin, a process aided by the symbiotic fungal mats. If they remain still for too long, they begin to resemble the statues of old saints found in abandoned cathedrals, indistinguishable from the growths that reclaim them. To wake them, you must wait for a drop of water to fall from a pipe onto their shoulder, startling the nerve endings beneath the moss.

### The Iron-Root Weavers
Deeper in the lattice, where the heat rises and the vegetation becomes thornier, live the **Iron-Root Weavers**. These are the artisans of Aethelgard, though their craft is not one of creation, but of maintenance and entanglement. They are smaller than the Moss-Walkers, wiry and agile, with fingers elongated by generations of climbing into the crevices where metal meets plant.

The Weavers are responsible for the structural integrity of the paradox. They prune the aggressive vines before they crush the support beams, and they oil the gears before the rust claims them. But they do not use tools in the traditional sense. Their hands are stained permanently black with iron oxide and green with chlorophyll. They manipulate the environment through a technique known as *grafting*.

A Weaver might take a severed cable and wrap it around a living branch, binding them together with strips of cured leaf-skin until the plant grows around the wire, becoming part of the electrical circuit. Or they might encourage a fungus to eat through a corroded pipe joint, replacing the broken steel with a flexible, self-healing mycelial plug. They view the city not as two separate systems (industrial and biological) but as a single, confused organism that needs its immune system regulated.

Their movement is jerky, sudden. Unlike the glacial pace of the general populace, the Weavers operate in bursts of activity followed by long periods of meditation. They sit cross-legged on hot stone floors, staring at a leaking valve, waiting for the rhythm of the drip to sync with their own heartbeat. When the moment aligns, they strike—a quick application of resin, a swift cut with a shard of obsidian glass, a whisper to the vine. Then, stillness returns. They are the only ones who seem to understand the "unseen pumps" driving the city’s heart, because they feel the vibrations in the soles of their feet before the hum reaches their ears.

### The Echo-Stags
Fauna is rare, but there are exceptions. High in the vaulted ceilings, where the air is slightly cooler and the darkness absolute, drift the **Echo-Stags**. These are not deer, nor any mammal familiar to the surface world. They are large, floating organisms, roughly the size of a man, composed of a translucent membrane filled with lighter-than-air gases produced by the decomposition of organic matter below.

Inside their membranes hang clusters of sensitive filaments, like jellyfish tentacles, which dangle down toward the walkways. They are blind, but they see sound. The low-frequency hum of the machinery, the sizzle of water on hot stone, the soft footfalls of the Walkers—all of these create ripples in the stagnant air, which the Echo-Stags interpret as images.

They move slowly, drifting through the upper voids like ghosts of balloons. They feed on the spores that rise from the moss-mats, inhaling them through pores in their undersides. Occasionally, one descends too low, its tendrils brushing against the jagged edge of a balcony. The pain causes a release of gas, a small pop that echoes through the chamber. The locals do not hunt them. They fear them. To touch an Echo-Stag is to invite infection; their membranes are coated in a mucus that induces hallucinations, forcing the victim to perceive the city not as it is, but as it *wants* to be—soft, round, endless.

The Stags are omens. If they gather in a specific sector, it means the pressure has dropped, indicating a leak in the thermal containment system. The Weavers watch them. The Walkers ignore them, unless they get too close, when the instinct to pull back becomes primal.

### The Core-Keeper (The Absent One)
And then, there is the figure at the center, or the rumor of the figure. No one has seen them, but everyone knows they exist. Let us call them the **Core-Keeper**.

The paths spiral inward, narrowing, heating up, silencing further. Those who attempt to go deeper often return changed, if they return at all. They come back mute, their skin covered in thick, calcified layers, their eyes clouded with cataracts of white salt. They refuse to speak of what lies at the bottom. They merely point downward, trembling.

The prevailing theory among the few scholars left in the upper zones is that the Core-Keeper is not a person, but a function. Perhaps the entity at the center is the source of the ozone smell, the origin of the spark that dies but never fully extinguishes. Maybe it is a machine that is also a god, or a prisoner that is also a jailer.

Some believe the Core-Keeper is dead, and the city continues to function on inertia alone, a clock wound too tightly that cannot stop ticking even after the mainspring has snapped. Others believe the Core-Keeper is sleeping, and the entire subterranean expansion is a cradle built to keep them warm during a long, geological hibernation. The lush vegetation is their bedding; the industrial pipes are their circulatory system; the silence is their dream.

If this is true, then the inhabitants of Aethelgard are not citizens. They are antibodies. They are the white blood cells of a body that does not know it is alive, fighting off the entropy of the rock, maintaining the fever heat, keeping the breath suspended.

### The Nature of Presence
Living here means accepting that you are temporary scaffolding for something else. You do not own your home; you inhabit it like a parasite inhabits a host. Your body will eventually become part of the wall. When a Walker dies, they are not buried. They are placed in a niche in the lattice, surrounded by damp earth and seeds. Over weeks, the moss takes root in their flesh. The vines find purchase in their ribs. The bones dissolve into calcium deposits that strengthen the surrounding concrete.

There is no grave in Aethelgard. There is only *integration*.

The air hangs heavy with the scent of heated iron and wet moss because that is the smell of death being metabolized into structure. The metallic tang of ozone is the static charge of souls lingering before they settle into the stone.

To live in the Root-City is to be aware, at every moment, of the thin membrane between yourself and the architecture. You feel the cold drip on your neck as if it were your own blood. You feel the vibration of the gear turning once per hour as if it were your own pulse. You are not separate from the place. You are the place, dreaming that it is people.

And in the deep, quiet dark, the dreams are slowing down. The edges are blurring. The watchers are becoming walls. The walls are becoming roots. And the roots are reaching, always reaching, toward the center, toward the warmth, toward the thing that holds the breath.
