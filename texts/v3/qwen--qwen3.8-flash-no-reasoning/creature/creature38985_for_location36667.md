Here, in the deafening white and the crushing density of wet glass, life does not arrive with a footfall or a cry. It arrives as a fracture in the light, a subtle shift in the opacity of the air.

To find them, you must first learn to ignore the roar. The sound is a physical weight, pressing against your eardrums until they feel like bruised fruit. But if you can endure the vibration rattling your teeth, if you can look past the blinding reflection of the sun on ten thousand slick pillars, you will see them clinging to the underside of the overhangs, tucked into the deepest, most shadowed fissures where the acoustic resonance dampens just enough for perception to exist.

They are the **Lithovores**, though that name feels too biological, too soft for what they truly are. They are entities of silica and static, born from the friction of the eternal drip.

**The Morphology of Stillness**

At first glance, they appear to be part of the geology. Imagine a cluster of quartz crystals, jagged and sharp, but colored a deep, translucent indigo that seems to absorb the surrounding glare rather than reflect it. They do not have legs; they do not have wings. They have roots—long, filament-thin tendrils of hardened mineral that pierce the microscopic pores of the obsidian floor. These roots anchor them so firmly that even the viscous, oily water rushing over their backs cannot dislodge them.

Their bodies are segmented, resembling the thorax of a wasp made entirely of spun sugar and ice, but rigid. There are no eyes. In a world of uniform, blinding brightness and infinite reflections, sight is useless; depth perception fails, and contrast vanishes. Instead, they possess vast, concave sensory pits along their dorsal ridges—troughs designed to catch the falling droplets.

Each creature is roughly the size of a human hand, huddled low against the rock. When you look closely, you realize they are not still because they are resting; they are still because they are *listening* through their bones. Their exoskeletons are tuned instruments. Every tremor in the stone, every harmonic frequency generated by the billions of dripping drops, travels through their crystalline shells. They do not hear sound; they feel the geometry of the noise.

**The Diet of Charge**

You described the water as viscous, laden with dissolved minerals, almost oily. This is key to their existence. As the thick, heavy drops slide down the sharp edges of the volcanic glass, the friction generates a massive electrostatic charge. The landscape itself is a giant capacitor, constantly building up potential energy from the endless movement of fluid against solid.

The Lithovores feed on this electricity.

Watch one closely. A drop of the mineral-heavy water slides down the spire above it, gathering speed, stretching into a thin, shimmering filament before detaching. It falls toward the creature’s outstretched "hand"—a broad, flat plate of translucent crystal extended outward.

The moment the drop makes contact, there is no splash. The water shatters into micro-spheres that hang momentarily in the dense air. In that split second, the static charge leaps from the water to the crystal plate. The creature’s body flares with a sudden, internal pulse of cold blue bioluminescence. The light travels down its spine, illuminating the hollow chambers within its shell, and then fades back into darkness as the charge dissipates into its core, fueling the slow metabolic processes that keep it alive in this frozen stasis.

It takes hours for a single charge to be fully absorbed. During this time, the creature remains utterly motionless, its surface becoming slightly warmer to the touch—a rare anomaly in this chill world. If you were to place a hand near one (though the vibration would likely make your nerves scream), you might feel the faint hum of energy stored within the lattice structure.

**The Social Geometry of Silence**

Fauna is rare here, 1.2/3, which means individuals are solitary hunters of the atmosphere, yet they form loose congregations in the pockets of relative quiet. You will find clusters of five or six Lithovores arranged in a perfect hexagonal pattern on a flat ledge, their tails touching.

This arrangement is not social bonding; it is acoustic engineering. By linking their crystalline structures, they create a resonant chamber that amplifies specific frequencies while canceling others. They are filtering the white noise. Within the center of the hexagon, the roaring hiss of the rain-birds becomes a manageable thrum. Here, in the eye of the sonic storm, they communicate.

Communication happens through modulation of their own internal light. One creature pulses rapidly—short bursts of azure fire. Another responds with a slow, throbbing violet glow. These are not words; they are data packets of voltage and timing. They share information about the flow rates of nearby drips, warning each other of sudden shifts in the condensation patterns caused by thermal currents high above. If a section of the sky-clouds thins, letting more direct sunlight hit a pillar, the resulting change in evaporation rate alters the viscosity of the runoff. The Lithovores sense this change instantly through the altered pitch of the dripping rhythm. They adjust their position, inching their crystal feet millimeters to the left or right, seeking a new optimal angle for charge collection.

**The Anomaly: The Glass Weavers**

Among the common Lithovores, there is a rarer variant, perhaps occurring once in a hundred miles of terrain. These are larger, with elongated limbs that resemble spider legs made of blown glass. They do not sit passively under the drips. They build.

Using secretions from glands at the tips of their fingers—substances that harden into instant resin upon contact with the sulfur-rich air—they weave intricate nets across narrow chasms between the stone pillars. These nets are not for catching prey; there is no prey. The nets are filters. They catch the heavier, oilier droplets that fall slower, allowing the lighter vapor to pass through.

By manipulating the flow of water, these Glass Weavers change the local soundscape. Where the net is stretched tight, the sound changes from a *tink* to a sustained, metallic *ring*. This ring creates a standing wave, a pocket of harmonic silence amidst the chaos. Other creatures, smaller insects with iridescent carapaces that survive only in these micro-climates, gather around the weaver. They clean the nets of accumulated mineral dust in exchange for protection from the harsher, unfiltered noise outside the web.

But the Weaver itself remains alien. It hangs upside down from the ceiling of its own construction, a silhouette against the blinding white background. Its body is transparent, revealing the slow, viscous circulation of golden fluid inside—not blood, but liquefied amber-like resin that stores the electrical energy. When it moves, it moves with terrifying precision, sliding along the tension wires without making a sound, vibrating at the exact frequency of the wire to remain undetectable to predators—or to avoid disturbing the delicate equilibrium of its trap.

**The Experience of Presence**

To witness a Lithovore is to witness a contradiction. In a world screaming with volume, it is the epitome of silence. In a world of blinding, uniform light, it is a point of contained darkness and inner fire.

If you stand close enough, ignoring the nausea induced by the bone-deep vibration, you will see the fine hairline fractures running through its crystal skin. These cracks are not damage; they are growth rings. Each crack represents a century of accumulated stress, a record of the storms that never happened, the earthquakes that never came. Because the world is in stasis, these cracks widen very slowly, absorbing the ambient pressure of the saturated air.

Occasionally, a particularly large drop falls directly onto a Lithovore’s head. The impact sends a shockwave through its entire body. For a brief second, the creature goes dark, the blue glow extinguished as its system reboots, processing the sudden surge of power. Then, brighter than before, the light returns, pulsing in a rhythmic cadence that matches the heartbeat of the mountain.

They are lonely things, these children of the glass throat. They live in a universe that offers no warmth, no shelter, no rest. Yet they thrive in the abrasion. They have made peace with the pain of the constant drip, turning the torture of the environment into sustenance. They are the only proof that life does not require comfort, nor silence, nor even space. Life only requires a way to convert suffering into energy.

As the fixed sun beats down on the mirror-lake of the sky, and the mist presses closer, tightening the wool around the world, the Lithovores wait. They do not sleep. They do not dream. They simply listen to the song of the breaking stones, drinking the lightning, glowing faintly in the deafening, beautiful dark.
