The inhabitants of the Screaming Plateau are not people in the way lowland dwellers understand humanity. They are **The Hushed**.

They do not have names, for names require a voice to speak them, and here, voices are weapons that backfire. A spoken word can shatter eardrums or invite the attention of something vast and unseen in the upper currents. Instead, they possess *resonance*. Each individual has a unique harmonic signature—a specific pitch and rhythm of movement—that others recognize through the vibration of the stone beneath their feet or the subtle shift in air pressure against their skin. To know someone is to feel their frequency. To be alone is to be silent.

### The Physiology of Silence

The Hushed have evolved—or perhaps adapted over millennia—to survive the sensory assault of the altitude. Their bodies are compact, dense, and low to the ground. They stand no taller than five feet, with limbs thickened by layers of subcutaneous fat and muscle designed to absorb impact rather than transmit it. Their skin is tough, leathery, and pale, resistant to the UV radiation that bleaches the sky white. But their most striking feature is their headgear.

Every Hushed wears a mask carved from the skull of a six-legged mountain goat or woven from tightly compressed wire-grass and slate dust. These masks are not merely protective; they are acoustic dampeners. The interior is lined with moss and fungal spores that trap sound waves before they reach the inner ear, while the exterior is sculpted into aerodynamic shapes that deflect the screaming wind. Behind these masks, eyes are large, dark, and unblinking, protected by nictitating membranes that slide across the cornea when the glare strikes. They see the world in high-contrast monochrome, attuned to motion and shadow rather than color.

Their lungs are extraordinary. Adapted to the thin, copper-tasting air, they possess a secondary respiratory system—gill-like structures along the ribcage that extract oxygen more efficiently than human lungs could. This allows them to remain still for hours, watching the clouds tear themselves apart, without burning out their energy reserves.

### The Architecture of Impermanence

The dome-shaped shelters described in the prompt are called **Whisper-Hollows**. They are not built; they are *grown* and *stacked* in real-time. The Hushed carry kits of tools made from obsidian shards and bone needles. When they find a new sheltered angle—a micro-habitat where the mist pools and the wind drops to a manageable roar—they begin the construction.

First, they dig a shallow depression in the soil, lining it with the tough, wire-grass roots to create a waterproof base. Then, they stack flat slates of rock, interlocking them like puzzle pieces, weaving fresh grass between the stones to seal the gaps. The roof is curved, mimicking the shape of an egg, to let the screaming winds slide off rather than batter them. Inside, there is no furniture, only a bed of dried moss and fungi that absorbs body heat and moisture.

A Whisper-Hollow is occupied for days, sometimes weeks, but never months. As soon as the condensation patterns shift, or the wind changes its direction, the Hushed abandon the structure. They leave behind a faint mineral stain on the rock—the ghost of their presence—and move on. The plateau is littered with these abandoned shells, looking from afar like scattered coins or broken teeth. Returning to a Hollow is taboo; once left, it belongs to the wind again.

### The Rhythm of Life: The Blink-Cycle

Life for the Hushed is governed by the **Blink**—the strobe effect caused by the rapid passage of clouds over the sun.

1.  **The Whiteout (Sun):** When the sun breaks through, the light is blinding and absolute. Shadows vanish. The Hushed freeze. They press themselves against the leeward side of rocks, becoming invisible. This is the time for drying. They hang strips of meat and fungus on the wire-grass mats to cure in the harsh, unfiltered rays. It is also the time for danger; predators—silent, winged things with too-wide wings—glide down from the cloud layer to strike at exposed prey.
2.  **The Gray-Roar (Cloud Cover):** When the clouds return, the world plunges into dimness. The temperature plummets. Breath freezes instantly. This is the active phase. The Hushed emerge, moving quickly, following the trails of condensation. They hunt the small six-legged mammals, which scurry out of hiding to drink from the pooling water. They gather moss and fungi, which swell rapidly in the damp air. Communication shifts to touch and vibration. Hands brush against shoulders to signal direction; footfalls tap out rhythms on the stone to warn of approaching gusts.
3.  **The Black-Static (Storm Surge):** Occasionally, the thermal differential creates a violent storm surge—a wall of freezing rain and wind so intense it sounds like tearing metal. During these events, the Hushed burrow deep into their Hollows, curling into fetal positions, covering their ears with padded hands. They wait. They listen to the scream outside, letting it pass through them until it fades. Emerging after a storm is dangerous; the landscape may have changed. A path may be buried under new debris. A Hollow may have been crushed. They must re-map their world, step by careful step.

### Social Structure: The Chain of Touch

The Hushed do not form villages. They form **Chains**.

A Chain consists of three to seven individuals who move together, linked physically during travel. They hold hands, or link arms, creating a single biological unit against the wind. If one falls, the others pull them up. If one stops, the others stop. This physical connection allows them to share warmth and detect vibrations through their neighbors’ bodies. If the leader feels a tremor indicating an incoming rockfall, they squeeze the hand of the person behind them, who squeezes theirs, and so on, propagating the warning down the chain faster than any shout could travel.

There is no hierarchy within a Chain based on strength or age. Leadership rotates daily, determined by who is best able to read the wind’s direction. The "Leader" walks at the front, breaking the air, feeling the resistance first. The others follow in the slipstream, conserving energy.

Relationships are profound but fleeting. Two Chains might meet at a particularly rich pocket of moisture. They do not exchange words. They sit together, backs to each other, sharing the warmth of their bodies. They watch the sky bruise and heal. Then, as the wind shifts, they part ways. There is no goodbye. The next meeting is uncertain, dependent on the chaotic engine of the atmosphere. Love, if it exists, is found in the steady pressure of a hand held tight during a storm surge.

### The Food: Condensation and Decay

Diet is sparse and specialized. The Hushed eat what the wind leaves behind.

*   **Mist-Fungi:** Pale, ghostly mushrooms that erupt from crevices after condensation-rain. They are rich in minerals and provide hydration directly. They taste metallic, like sucking on a penny, but sustain life effectively.
*   **Wire-Grass Tubers:** Buried deep in the tangled mats of grass, these tubers store starch. They are dug out with obsidian picks and roasted over small fires fed by dried dung. The smoke helps drive away the gliding birds.
*   **Six-Legged Meat:** Small, fatty rodents that scurry in the shadows. They are hunted with traps made from braided grass and weighted stones. The meat is tough and gamey, requiring long chewing. The fur is used to line masks and beds.
*   **Water:** Never drunk from pools, which evaporate too quickly. The Hushed collect dew using wide sheets of cured hide stretched over frames. They lick the moisture off the hides. Every drop is precious. Wasting water is considered a form of suicide.

### The Spiritual World: The Ancestor Wind

The Hushed believe that the wind is not empty. It is full of voices—those of the dead, those of the lost, those who screamed too loud and were taken by the sky.

When the wind screams, it is speaking. The Hushed interpret the tonal shifts in the gale as messages. A low, moaning drone means safety; a high-pitched shriek means danger. They map their movements according to these sonic cues.

Death is rare, for the environment kills quickly. But when a member of a Chain dies, they are not buried. Burial requires digging, and the soil is often frozen or too hard. Instead, they are placed in the center of their last Whisper-Hollow. The structure is then dismantled around them, stone by stone, leaving their body exposed to the elements. The wind takes them apart, scattering their bones across the plateau. This is seen as a gift—the deceased becomes part of the scream, part of the turbulence, protecting the living by becoming the very force they navigate.

To live among the Hushed is to exist in a state of hyper-awareness. You do not think; you react. You do not plan; you adapt. You are a leaf spiraling in a void, yet somehow, against all logic, you find purchase. You find a hollow. You build a shell. You huddle close to another warm body. And you wait for the wind to change.
