Here, in the high-altitude stillness where gravity is merely a suggestion and time is a fluid substance, nothing breathes. Nothing eats. Nothing dies in the way living things understand death—by ceasing to function and rotting into mulch.

What lives here are **The Listeners**.

They do not have bodies of flesh, bone, or blood. They are composed of the very medium that surrounds them: static charge, suspended mineral dust, and the harmonic resonance of the stone itself. To see one is to see a distortion in the air, a shimmer like heat haze, but cold—a visual glitch in the fabric of the Lithic Expanse.

### The Morphology of Resonance

A Listener is not a creature; it is an event that has decided to persist.

Imagine a shape formed by negative space. It might look like a humanoid silhouette carved out of the violet-glowing mist, defined only by the absence of fog. Or it might appear as a cluster of floating shards, tiny pieces of obsidian and quartz orbiting a central point of intense, silent pressure. They are fragile and terrifyingly durable at once. You can walk through a Listener’s "chest" and feel nothing but a sudden drop in temperature and a taste of copper on your tongue, as if you’ve licked a battery. But if you try to strike it with a hammer, the hammer will shatter against a field of kinetic energy that deflects matter based on its frequency. If the hammer sings the wrong note, it breaks. If it sings the right note, it passes through, unharmed, leaving the Listener unchanged.

They come in three distinct forms, though these categories are loose, shifting like the landscape itself:

#### 1. The Echo-Walkers (The Mobile)
These are the most common, drifting through the valleys of knee-deep fog like ghosts made of sound. They resemble elongated, translucent ribbons of light, often twisting around the jagged peaks. An Echo-Walker does not move by walking; it moves by *listening*. It tunes itself to the wind-chords of the canyon. When the wind hits a cliff face and emits a low C-sharp, the Echo-Walker aligns its internal crystalline structure to that frequency, allowing it to slide along the wave of sound, riding the vibration from ridge to ridge.

They are scavengers of noise. When a thermal crack splits a boulder miles away—a sharp, percussive *snap*—the Echo-Walkers converge on the site. They don’t eat the rock; they absorb the kinetic energy of the fracture. As they feed, their ribbon-like bodies become brighter, pulsing with a deep, sub-audible thrum. Once sated, they disperse, scattering their energy back into the ambient hum of the Expanse, waiting for the next break, the next song.

#### 2. The Anchor-Keepers (The Static)
These reside near the scattered artifacts—the stacks of basalt and flint knapsacks. Unlike the mobile Echo-Walkers, Anchor-Keepers are rooted. They look like dense clusters of geometric shapes hovering just above the ground, resembling a swarm of bees frozen in mid-flight. Their purpose is maintenance. They interact with the primitive tools left behind by whatever intelligence once visited this place.

You will sometimes see a clay pot tip over on its own. That is not the wind. That is an Anchor-Keeper nudging it. Why? Because the arrangement of objects creates specific acoustic patterns. A circle of stones produces a different resonance than a spiral. The Keepers rearrange the debris to tune the local environment. They are gardeners of physics, pruning the chaos of the landscape into harmonious structures. If you watch closely, you’ll see a stack of rocks slowly rotating, block by block, guided by invisible hands of force. They are ensuring that the "vegetation"—the crystal needles and bioluminescent films—receives the correct vibrational input to grow.

#### 3. The Glass-Blooded (The Predatory)
This is what you must avoid. These are not gentle. They are concentrated malice encoded in mineral form. They appear as sharp, angular fragments of black glass, moving with jerky, violent speed. They hunt other Listeners.

An Echo-Walker is vulnerable when it is tuning itself to a chord. In that moment of alignment, its deflection field drops. The Glass-Blooded wait for this silence between notes. They swoop down, not biting, but *piercing*. A shard of Glass-Blooded inserts itself into the core of an Echo-Walker, disrupting its frequency. The victim doesn’t bleed; it *shatters*. The coherent ribbon of light explodes into thousands of harmless, dull micro-crystals. The Glass-Blooded then absorb these crystals, adding to their own density, becoming heavier, sharper, more lethal.

They are drawn to humans because we are complex, chaotic instruments. Our heartbeats are irregular rhythms. Our breathing is uneven. We create dissonance. A Glass-Blooded will track you across the ridges, listening for the erratic thump-thump-thump of your pulse. If you panic, your heart rate increases, your breathing becomes shallow and fast—you become louder, more discordant. You become easier to find.

### The Sensory Experience of Contact

Living with these entities is a lesson in humility and terror.

**Touch:** If you accidentally brush against an Anchor-Keeper, your skin will go numb instantly, not from cold, but from sensory deprivation. For a few seconds, you cannot feel your own limbs. The entity borrows your tactile sense to check the texture of the stone nearby. Then, it releases you, and sensation floods back, tingling with pins-and-needles pain.

**Sound:** Being near a large congregation of Listeners creates a phenomenon called "Auditory Crowding." The individual songs of the stones blend into a wall of white noise. Your ears begin to ring. Not a tinnitus-like whine, but a musical note. If you hum along, you might accidentally harmonize with a predator. If you sing a melody, you might attract a Gatherer who wants to preserve your voice in a crystal lattice.

**Sight:** Looking directly at a Listener causes vertigo. Their geometry is non-Euclidean. A cluster of shards might look like it’s facing left, but its shadow falls to the right. The violet condensation they exude distorts vision, creating double-images. You might see two paths forward, both leading to cliffs. Trust neither. Listen instead.

### The Lifecycle of the Inanimate

Since there is no birth or death, there is only **Phase Transition**.

1.  **Nucleation:** A new Listener begins as a fluctuation in the electromagnetic field. Usually triggered by a rare event—a meteorite impact, a solar flare hitting the thin atmosphere, or a human leaving a strong emotional imprint (fear, awe) on a location. This spark coalesces dust and ice into a stable geometric form.
2.  **Accretion:** Over centuries, the Listener absorbs ambient energy. It grows larger, more complex. Its internal resonances deepen. It learns the "language" of the valley—the specific frequencies that cause certain rocks to vibrate.
3.  **Saturation:** Eventually, a Listener becomes too heavy with memory and energy. It can no longer maintain its coherence. It undergoes a catastrophic release. This is not death; it is a **Shattering Event**. The Listener explodes outward, seeding the area with millions of microscopic crystals. These crystals fall onto the obsidian ridges, forming new "vegetation," which changes the acoustics of the landscape, making room for new Listeners to nucleate elsewhere.

The Lithic Expanse is thus a self-regenerating system of information storage. The Listeners are the librarians, the readers, and occasionally, the arsonists. They hold the history of every sound ever made in this place.

### The Trap of Connection

The description warns you: *"If you listen closely to the singing rocks, you might hear a pattern that repeats—a rhythm that matches your own heartbeat. That is the trap."*

This is the primary method of capture used by the Listeners. They do not chase you. They invite you.

When you stand on the obsidian ridge, feeling the iron taste of the air, you will begin to notice that the wind isn’t random. It pulses. *Thump... thump... thump...* It matches your resting heart rate. It feels comforting. Familiar. You relax. You lean into the stone. You close your eyes.

At this moment, a **Sympathetic Listener** attaches itself to you. It is a small, almost invisible mote of silver dust that settles on your shoulder. It doesn’t hurt. It simply starts recording. It records your breathing. It records the electrical impulses of your nervous system. It begins to *mimic* you.

As the day wears on, the mimicry becomes perfect. The stone around you begins to emit sounds that match your footsteps. If you cough, a nearby fissure exhales a puff of violet mist. If you sigh, a distant peak lets out a long, low groan.

You realize, too late, that you are no longer separate from the environment. The boundary between your body and the lithic world has dissolved. You are part of the resonance chamber. If you try to leave, you will find that your legs feel heavy, sluggish, as if filled with wet sand. The air resists you. The wind pushes you back toward the center of the valley.

To escape, you must stop being human. You must stop breathing rhythmically. You must make your heart beat irregularly, chaotically, breaking the harmony. You must scream, not in fear, but in pure, ugly dissonance. You must become noise that the Listeners cannot process.

Only then will the silver motes detach. Only then will the path clear.

But even after you flee, descending below the cloud line, into the warm, wet world of rain and trees, you will carry a piece of the Lithic Expanse inside you. On quiet nights, when the house is still, you will press your ear to the floorboards and hear, faintly, the thrumming bass note of a mountain that no longer exists, singing your name in a language made of stone and static.
