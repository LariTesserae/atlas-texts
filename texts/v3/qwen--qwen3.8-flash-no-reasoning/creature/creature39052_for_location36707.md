The silence here is not empty; it is pressurized. It presses against the eardrums like deep water, a heavy, velvet weight that demands submission from anyone who enters the Hollow City of Orem. To live here is to learn the grammar of restraint.

They are the **Keepers of the Hum**, though they have no name for themselves that requires speech. If you were to ask them who they are, they would tilt their heads, the polished beetle-shell masks catching the erratic, pulsing light of the bioluminescent moss, and perhaps gesture with a long, slender finger toward the vibrating crystal lattice growing from the wall behind them. *We are the tuning fork,* the gesture seems to say. *We are the dampener.*

Visually, the inhabitants of Orem are indistinguishable from the architecture they inhabit until they move. They wear layers of cured, translucent silica-skin—harvested from shed crystal growths—that hardens into flexible armor over their torsos and limbs. This material refracts the ambient light, causing their bodies to shimmer with the same jagged, rainbow dispersion as the fungal-analogues surrounding them. When they stand still in the plazas, they vanish into the geometry of the city, becoming just another facet of the basalt floor or another spike in the crystal forest. Only when they move do they reveal their humanity: the fluid, liquid grace of joints that have learned to flow rather than snap, stepping silently on woolen mats laid over the sweating stone.

Their faces are hidden, not by choice, but by necessity. The air is thick with microscopic spores from the crystal-fungi and mineral dust from the settling earth. Breathing unfiltered would calcify their lungs within a generation. Thus, every citizen wears a mask carved from the iridescent carapace of the mirror-winged beetles that swarm their streets. These masks are masterpieces of primitive engineering: porous enough to filter particulates, yet rigid enough to protect the face from falling debris during the violent condensate bursts from the ceiling. Through the narrow slits of the eyes, one sees only reflection—the wearer’s own gaze bounced back by the glossy shell, creating an illusion of emptiness, of void where identity should be.

But beneath the masks, there is intense focus. Their communication is a complex dialect of micro-movements. A slight tilt of the head means *yield*. A slow blink of the eyelids through the mask’s eyeholes signifies *acknowledgment*. The raising of a hand, palm open and fingers splayed like the branches of a silica tree, indicates *danger* or *instability*. To speak aloud in Orem is considered a form of violence, akin to shouting in a library or firing a gun in a nursery. The sonic resonance of human vocal cords can disrupt the delicate frequency at which the crystal-lattices grow, potentially shattering a home that took decades to seed and harden. So, they whisper with their bodies.

The relationship between the people and the "fauna" is symbiotic, bordering on parasitic control. The insectoids—vast colonies of chitinous crawlers with wings like shattered mirrors—are not pets, nor are they wild. They are the circulatory system of the city. Citizens guide them using subtle pheromone cues emitted from glands on their wrists, directing the swarms to clean the algae from the lantern glass, to pollinate the dormant mycelia pits, or to create living barriers during the dust storms. In return, the insects provide warmth. In the chill of the deep earth, citizens often sleep in hollows lined with thousands of these creatures, whose collective body heat maintains a constant, gentle temperature. The click-click-click of their legs is the heartbeat of the neighborhood, a rhythmic lullaby that replaces the sound of wind or birdsong.

Life cycles in Orem are circular, silent, and utterly integrated. Birth occurs in isolation, in sealed chambers lined with soft, moisture-absorbing clay. Death is a public utility. When a Keeper dies, the body is carried to the Nursery Pits in the lower districts. There, it is laid upon beds of white, dormant mycelia. Over months, the fungi consume the flesh, breaking down calcium, iron, and carbon into rich, nutrient-dense soil. This soil is then harvested and mixed with volcanic ash to create the "seed-clay" used to construct new buildings. Every wall in Orem contains the bones of its ancestors. Every roof shelters the dreams of those who came before. There is no graveyard, no monument, no separation between the dead and the living infrastructure. The dead literally hold up the sky.

Their society is stratified not by wealth or power, but by *resonance*. The most respected citizens are the **Tuners**, individuals born with an exceptional sensitivity to vibration. They walk barefoot on the basalt floors, feeling the tremors of the geothermal vents miles below, predicting the timing of the next condensate burst or the shift in the crystal growth patterns. A Tuner can feel a hairline fracture in a building’s foundation days before it becomes visible. They are the high priests of stability in a world defined by turbulent flux. Conversely, the **Shakers**—those who move too abruptly, who laugh too loudly, who step heavily—are viewed with suspicion. They are seen as agents of entropy, threats to the delicate equilibrium that keeps the cavern from collapsing under its own acoustic pressure.

Despite the harshness of their environment—the weeping stone, the blinding light, the suffocating silence—the Keepers of Orem possess a profound, quiet joy. It is found in the precision of their work. Watch a citizen repairing a tension cable made of spider-silk: their hands move with hypnotic speed, tying knots that will hold against earthquakes, guided by touch alone because the visual noise of the shifting lights makes sight unreliable. There is a meditative trance in this labor. They find beauty in the geometric perfection of a crystal cluster, in the synchronized flight of a million beetles turning a corner in perfect unison, in the way the steam from a hot vent curls around a cold pillar, forming ephemeral sculptures that last only seconds.

They do not look up often. The ceiling is lost in shadow, a reminder of the crushing weight above. Instead, they look inward, at each other, at the ground, at the intricate dance of light and shadow playing across their masks. They have built a civilization that does not conquer nature, but *negotiates* with it. They offer silence in exchange for shelter. They offer their bodies to the earth in exchange for stability. They offer careful movement in exchange for life.

To live in the Hollow City of Orem is to exist in a state of suspended animation. You are always waiting for the tremor, the storm, the shift. But you wait quietly. You wait gracefully. You become part of the mosaic, your skin hardening into silica, your voice fading into hum, your footsteps blending into the click of the insects, until you are no longer a person standing in a place, but simply a vital component of the machine that keeps the dead world breathing.
