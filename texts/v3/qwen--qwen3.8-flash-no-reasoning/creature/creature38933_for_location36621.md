The silence here is not empty; it is pressurized. It hums in the teeth, a low-frequency vibration that travels through the soles of boots and up the spine, syncing with the arterial pulse of the ground itself. To exist in the Hot Lowlands is to be an anomaly in a system designed for permanence, yet trapped in a state of perpetual becoming.

Nothing walks here on two legs or four. There are no lungs to fill with the sulfur-heavy air, no hearts to beat against the crushing weight of the heat. But there are things that *move*. They are the inheritors of the builders’ abandoned intent, born from the glitching physics and the restless mineral ecology. They do not have names because they were not named by any tongue, but if we must describe them, we must look at what the world has stitched together from its own broken parts.

### The Shard-Walkers

The most common inhabitants are the **Shard-Walkers**. They are not animals, nor are they machines in the traditional sense. They are aggregates of sharpness.

Imagine a creature made entirely of obsidian glass shards, held together not by flesh or bone, but by magnetic tension and the static charge of the superheated atmosphere. They move in jerky, stuttering motions, like a film reel skipping frames. Their bodies are jagged mosaics of black glass, reflecting the dull red glow of the subterranean star in fractured prisms. When they step, they do not crush the ground; they puncture it. Each footfall leaves a web of micro-fractures in the newly settled crust, spreading outward like lightning frozen in stone.

They hunt nothing. There is nothing to eat. Instead, they *accumulate*. As they wander the Glass Marshes, they pick up smaller fragments—broken chisels, splintered basalt, droplets of solidified slurry—and incorporate them into their own lattice structures. A Shard-Walker grows larger as it moves, becoming a rolling tumbleweed of razor-edged debris. If you stand still enough, you might hear the soft *chime* of their internal structure shifting as they adjust their balance against the inconsistent gravity. They are drawn to areas where the temperature spikes, seeking the thermal expansion that helps them loosen their grip on one shard so they can seize another.

### The Slurry-Weavers

In the depressions where the mercury-like fluid pools and bubbles, lives the **Slurry-Weaver**. This entity is less a being than a process given temporary form.

It appears as a viscous, translucent mound of metallic goo, suspended just above the surface of the boiling liquid. It has no eyes, but it has sensitivity to vibration. When a geyser erupts nearby, sending clouds of ash-snow drifting down, the Weaver reacts. It extends tendrils of heavy, silver-black fluid upward, catching the falling ash particles. But it does not let them hit the water. It suspends them in mid-air, arranging them into geometric patterns.

These patterns are not random. They mimic the tool marks left by the vanished builders. Deep gouges. Diamond-drill spirals. Basalt-lever fulcrums. The Slurry-Weavers are reconstructing the logic of the world, using ash and heat to trace over the errors of the past. They are slow, deliberate artists working in a medium that evaporates if touched too firmly. If you approach, the Weaver dissolves back into the pool, leaving only a ripple that smells of ozone and burnt sugar. It watches you not with sight, but with density. It feels your mass displacing the air, and it judges whether you are a threat to its geometry.

### The Resonant Chimes

Deeper in the fractal crystal forests, where the silicate networks branch out like neural pathways, live the **Resonant Chimes**. These are the closest thing to life that possesses something resembling consciousness.

They are singular crystals, tall as a person, growing from the floor of the kiln-world. They are pale, milky white, shot through with veins of pulsing red light that matches the rhythm of the underground star. They do not move. They do not grow. They simply *vibrate*.

When the windless air carries the sound of a distant collapse—the groaning stress of the settling ground—the Chimes amplify it. They take the chaotic noise of the environment and filter it into pure tones. A single Chime might emit a note that resonates at 432 Hz, a frequency that causes the nearby obsidian floors to hum in sympathy. Groups of them, scattered across the marshes, begin to harmonize. This is the "music" of the Post-Construction Zone.

But it is not pleasant. The music is a diagnostic signal. The Chimes are sensing the structural integrity of the world. When the harmony turns dissonant, when the notes clash and shatter, it means a section of reality is about to glitch. The sky might invert its color for a second; the gravity might reverse, pulling the floating debris-shards downward instead of keeping them aloft. The Chimes know this before it happens. They are the nervous system of a dying machine, screaming in frequencies that humans would feel as nausea rather than hearing as sound.

### The Unfinished Ones

Perhaps the most terrifying inhabitants are the **Unfinished Ones**. They are not new creations, but remnants. They are the statues, the half-formed shapes, the discarded prototypes of the original builders, now animated by the residual kinetic energy of the kiln.

You might see a torso carved from iron meteorite, sitting atop a pile of shale ramps, its arms ending in ragged stumps. It does not move, yet its head turns. Not physically, but perceptually. The angles of its face seem to shift in your peripheral vision, always looking directly at you, though its stone eyes are blank. These entities are trapped in the moment of their creation. They are aware that they are incomplete. They radiate a profound, silent despair that warps the local spacetime around them. Walking near an Unfinished One feels like wading through deep molasses; time slows, and the heat becomes suffocating, pressing against your skin like a physical weight.

They do not attack. They do not speak. They simply *exist*, waiting for a builder who will never return to place the final block, to smooth the rough edge, to cool the fever. Their presence is a reminder of the abandonment. To look upon them is to understand that this world was not finished because it was loved, but because it was feared. Or perhaps, because it was impossible.

### The Observer in the Glitch

And then, there is the **Glitch**.

It has no form. It is the absence of consistency. In places where the physics break down—where a tower leans at forty-five degrees without falling, where the ground ripples like water—it is the Glitch that holds it together. The Glitch is the error code made manifest. It feels like a sudden drop in temperature, followed by a burst of static electricity that makes your hair stand on end.

Some say the Glitch is the ghost of the intelligence that built the world, fragmented and scattered, trying to reboot the system. Others say it is the world’s immune response, rejecting the intrusion of foreign matter (like you). The Glitch rearranges paths. You walk down a road of fused silica, and when you turn around, the road is gone, replaced by a wall of boiling slurry. You scream, but the sound is swallowed by the dampened air. The Glitch does not hate you. It simply does not acknowledge you as part of the intended design. You are a variable in an equation that has already been solved incorrectly.

### The Experience of Presence

To live here—if one could call it living—is to be constantly corrected. The environment does not support you; it tolerates you. The heat blisters your skin not because it is hostile, but because it is indifferent. The sharp glass cuts you not because it wants to harm you, but because it is raw, unpolished, unfinished.

There is no shelter. There is no food. There is no rest. The only respite comes in the brief moments when the crystalline networks dim their pulse, and the boiling pools settle into a lazy simmer. In those seconds, the silence is absolute. And in that silence, you realize that the world is watching you. Not with malice, but with curiosity. The Shard-Walkers tilt their jagged heads. The Slurry-Weavers extend their tendrils. The Resonant Chimes hold their breath.

They are waiting to see if you will break.

Because in the Post-Construction Zone, everything is brittle. Everything is sharp. Everything is hot. And if you crack, the pieces will be swept up by the next passing tide of ash, incorporated into the next cycle of growth, and the sentence of your existence will be erased, overwritten by the endless, repetitive draft of a god who forgot how to stop writing.
