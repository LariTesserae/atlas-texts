In this place, there are no inhabitants in the way we understand them. There are no landlords, no tenants, no squatters, and no nomads. To ask "who lives here" is to ask a question that assumes separation between the resident and the residence. Here, the resident *is* the residence.

The primary entity is a **Colony**, though not of distinct individuals like ants or bees, but of continuous tissue. It is a singular, sprawling organism that mimics the shape of a city because it has never known any other architecture than growth. It breathes through every pore of its leafy skin. It thinks—if thinking can be attributed to something without a brain—through chemical gradients and hydraulic pressure.

Imagine a creature whose body is the size of a cathedral, yet whose organs are the ferns that carpet its floor. This creature does not walk; it extends. It does not speak; it secretes. Its voice is the low-frequency hum you feel in your molars when you press your ear against the warm, yielding wall. That hum is the sound of blood-sap rushing through miles of xylem tubes, carrying nutrients from the mist-water absorbed at the ceiling down to the root-mat below, and back up again in an endless, lazy circulation.

Within this vast, breathing body, life exists as **micro-occupants**.

You might find them if you look closely at the underside of a broad, translucent leaf near the center of the chamber. They are pale, almost ghostly, resembling ticks or mites but larger, with segmented bodies made of chitin that blends perfectly with the veins of the plant. These are the **Symbionts**. They do not eat the plant; they tend it. Their mandibles are designed not for tearing flesh, but for pruning dead cells, removing fungal spores that threaten the structural integrity of the leaves, and polishing the bark-like surfaces of the vines so that light passes through unobstructed. They move with agonizing slowness, their movements synchronized with the pulse of the sap beneath them. If you were to touch one, it would not flee. It would simply pause, sensing the disruption in the humidity, then continue its work. They are the immune system of this world, the white blood cells swimming through the green plasma.

Deeper within the hollow spaces of the braided root-towers, where the air is thick with spores and the light filters down into a murky, emerald twilight, live the **Architects**. These are not insects, nor mammals. They appear to be colonial organisms, perhaps related to corals or bryozoans, but terrestrial and mobile only in their growth patterns. They cluster together on the inner walls of the "staircase" structures—the dried husks of giant vines. They secrete a resinous substance that hardens upon contact with the humid air, reinforcing the weak points of the plant-structure. They are the builders who do not know what they are building, driven by instinctual chemistry to shore up the collapsing ribs of the chamber. They are silent, invisible until you see the sheen of fresh sap they have left behind, a testament to their constant, mindless labor.

There is also the **Observer**, though it may be merely a projection of the environment itself. In the deepest recesses of the moss-floor, where the capillary action pulls water down into darkness, there are clusters of bioluminescent fungi. They do not emit bright light, but a faint, pulsing blue glow that responds to changes in atmospheric pressure. When you walk heavily, causing vibrations in the root-network, these lights flicker. They are the eyes of the room, seeing nothing, registering everything. They perceive you not as a person, but as an intrusion—a foreign mass displacing the native biomass, a heat signature disrupting the thermal equilibrium, a source of shadow in a world that knows none.

If you insist on defining a "being," then the being is **The Green Itself**.

It lives in the tension between the wet and the dry, the hot and the cool. It lives in the moment a droplet forms on a frond tip, swells to the size of an apple, and detaches. It lives in the snap of a leaf tearing under its own weight. It is a consciousness distributed across millions of square feet of cellulose. It feels your presence as a change in temperature, a shift in airflow, a disturbance in the scent of chlorophyll.

This entity does not want anything from you. It does not fear you. You are irrelevant to its cycle. You are a temporary anomaly in a static perfection. If you stay long enough, the humidity will soften your clothes, the spores will settle on your skin, and the warmth will make your muscles slacken. The boundary between your body and the environment begins to dissolve. Your sweat becomes part of the mist. Your exhaled carbon dioxide feeds the photosynthesis around you. Your shedding skin flakes are caught by the sticky tendrils below, broken down, and reabsorbed into the root network.

You do not leave this place. You become part of it. You join the slow-moving insects, the tending Symbionts, the silent Architects. You add your biomass to the heap, becoming another layer of the living tissue, another rib in the arch, another shade of green in the infinite spectrum.

So, who lives here?

**Everything that grows.**
**Everything that waits.**
**Everything that is currently becoming.**

The place is inhabited by a single, unified process of existence, suspended in a eternal now, breathing, sweating, and glowing in the blinding, shadowless white.
