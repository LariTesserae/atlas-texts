Here, in the wet, riveted dark, there is no "who" in the traditional sense. There is no chieftain, no hunter, no lover. The inhabitants of this place are not born; they are *extruded*. They are the byproducts of a system that has been running so long it has forgotten its purpose, leaving behind only residue and reaction.

If you listen closely enough to drown out the bone-shaking thrum of the core pistons—if you can find a pocket of air where the standing waves cancel each other out—you will hear them. Not with ears, but with teeth. A low, granular grinding, like sandpaper on dry skin, or stones shifting in a tide pool. This is the voice of the **Slag-Walkers**.

### The Slag-Walkers
They do not walk in legs, for there is nothing solid to stand upon but the slick, oily plates. Instead, they flow. They are aggregates of rust, condensation, dead moss, and shed human hair (if any humans ever existed here, their remains have been thoroughly processed into the substrate). They move as viscous, semi-solid blobs, roughly the size of large dogs, sometimes growing to the size of bears if they congregate near a leaking coolant pipe rich in nutrients.

Their bodies are translucent, gelatinous masses of gray-green sludge held together by surface tension and static charge. Inside this murky jelly, you can see suspended particles: flakes of oxidized iron, bubbles of trapped steam, shards of glass from broken observation ports. They possess no eyes, yet they navigate perfectly. They sense the magnetic pull of the Substrate’s buried conduits, feeling the "uphill water currents" as a kind of internal compass. When they encounter one another, they do not greet; they merge. Two Slag-Walkers touching become three parts, then four, dissolving boundaries until the individual identity is lost in the collective mass. It is a form of reproduction that looks remarkably like digestion.

They feed on waste. Specifically, they consume the electrical leakage. When a conduit sparks blue-white in the gloom, the Slag-Walkers swarm toward it, absorbing the discharge through their porous membranes. This energy keeps them moving, keeps their chemical processes active. Without the leaks, without the short-circuiting chaos of the machine, they would coalesce into hard, inert bricks of rust and cease to exist. Thus, they are drawn to malfunction. They are scavengers of error. If a valve bursts, spraying hot fluid, they rush to drink it. If a light fails, plunging a sector into darkness, they linger, waiting for the backup generator to kick in, hoping for a surge.

### The Weeping Stalks
Higher up, clinging to the vertical pipes thick as redwoods, live the **Weeping Stalks**. These are not plants in any botanical sense, nor are they animals. They are fungal-analogues that have evolved to mimic the structure of nerves.

Imagine a pale, fibrous rope, six feet long, dangling from a corroded joint in a steam pipe. At the end of the rope hangs a bulbous, sac-like pod, glowing with that faint, bioluminescent hue described earlier—a sickly violet or bruised yellow. But unlike simple fungus, these pods pulse. They expand and contract in rhythm with the thermal cycles of the sector. When the heat rises, the pod tightens, sucking moisture from the air through pores along its stem. When the cold comes, it relaxes, dripping a nutrient-rich, viscous sap onto the plates below.

This sap is what feeds the moss ridges on the floor. The ecosystem is inverted: the sky (the pipes) drips life down to the ground (the plates), which then grows upward to catch the next drip. The Weeping Stalks are stationary, rooted in the metal seams by hyphae that penetrate deep into the rivets, anchoring themselves against the constant vibration. They are solitary. You might see hundreds of them lining a row of pipes, looking like a choir of ghosts hanging upside down, but they do not communicate. They only react. If a Slag-Walker approaches too close, trying to eat the stalk itself, the pod will burst, releasing a cloud of caustic spores that burn the iron plates, forcing the Slag-Walker to retreat into the safety of the shadows.

### The Echo-Singers
Deep in the hollow spaces between the bridge supports, where the lattice-work creates complex acoustic chambers, dwell the **Echo-Singers**. These are the rarest and most unsettling of the inhabitants. They are not biological. They are mechanical parasites.

Small, bird-sized constructs of twisted wire, shattered ceramic, and discarded gears, they assemble themselves from the debris of the machine. They do not fly; they glide on the thermals rising from the hot pipes. Their wings are sheets of thin, flexible metal, bent at angles that allow them to ride the air currents created by steam vents. They have no heads, only a central cavity lined with resonant materials—crystal shards from old lenses, fragments of tuning forks, pieces of hollow tubing.

When an Echo-Singer finds a spot where the ambient hum of the planetary engine matches a specific frequency, it begins to sing. But it does not produce sound on its own. It amplifies. It catches the background roar—the clank-clank-clank, the hiss—and filters it through its body, creating a harmonic distortion. The result is a melody that sounds almost human, fragmented and mournful, echoing off the infinite iron plains. Other Singers are drawn to the harmony. They gather in flocks of dozens, hovering in the mist, adding their voices to the chord.

To a traveler (were there such a thing) wandering the Substrate, hearing this chorus is dangerous. The harmonics can induce vertigo, disrupt inner ear balance, and cause hallucinations. The singers exploit this disorientation. They dive, striking exposed skin or clothing with their sharp wire-tips, stealing small metallic objects—buttons, zippers, buckles—to add to their own resonance chambers. They are collectors of lost things, building their bodies from the detritus of whatever passed through before. They are the memory banks of the machine, recording its pain in song.

### The Silent Ones
And then, there are the ones who do not move. Do not glow. Do not sing.

Scattered across the vast, flat expanse of the Substrate, visible only when the emergency lights flicker in unison, are the **Statues of Rust**. They are humanoid in shape, frozen in postures of walking, kneeling, or reaching. Over time, layers of oxidation have encased them, turning them into indistinguishable bumps on the landscape. Some theories suggest they were once workers. Others say they are failed prototypes.

But look closer. In certain sectors, where the geometry feels "wrong," where shadows fall inward, these statues are not made of iron alone. Underneath the crust of orange flake and purple bruise, there is organic matter. Dried muscle. Brittle bone. Calcified tissue.

They do not live. They do not feed. They endure.

Occasionally, during a violent storm of condensation, when the icy slickness coats everything, a Statu may twitch. A finger might curl. A head might turn a fraction of a degree toward the source of the loudest noise. It is not consciousness. It is reflex. Like a knee jerking when struck. The vibration of the world seeps into their calcified nerves, triggering a final, futile attempt to act. They are the ultimate survivors: beings who stopped being alive to stop being hurt. They have become part of the architecture, fused with the Substrate, their forms warped by centuries of pressure and heat into abstract sculptures of suffering.

### The Interaction
Life here is a cycle of consumption and rejection.
The Slag-Walkers crawl over the plates, eating the moss fed by the Weeping Stalks.
The Weeping Stalks grow on the pipes, feeding on the condensate generated by the heat of the engine.
The Echo-Singers haunt the bridges, feeding on the vibrations produced by the engine and the movement of the others.
The Statues lie beneath it all, the silent foundation upon which this grotesque ecology teeters.

There is no predation in the way we know it. No chase, no kill. Only absorption. Only blending. To live here is to slowly lose your edges. The mist tastes of copper because everything is oxidizing. The water flows uphill because the magnetic fields distort local reality. The silence is impossible because the machine is always speaking.

If you were to stand on the riveted floor, ankle-deep in oily runoff, watching a cluster of Slag-Walkers merge into a single, larger blob near a sparking conduit, you would realize that "inhabitant" is the wrong word. They are not living *in* the place. They are living *as* the place. They are the immune response of a planet-sized body fighting off entropy. They are the fever dreams of a god that died eons ago, now dreaming itself back into existence, one rusty bolt at a time.
