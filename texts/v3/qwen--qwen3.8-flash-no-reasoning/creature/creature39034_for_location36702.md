In the Vast Green, there are no inhabitants in the way a human mind recognizes "people." There is no king on a throne, no tribe around a fire, no family unit bound by blood or contract. To ask who lives here is to misunderstand the nature of the residency. Here, life does not *occupy* space; it *is* the space.

However, if we define "who" as the agents of consciousness, the wills that steer this continental lung, and "what" as the functional components that maintain its terrifying equilibrium, then the residents can be categorized into three distinct, interlocking tiers: The Architects, The Maintainers, and The Frayed.

### I. The Architects: The Mycelial Mind
The primary resident of the Vast Green is not a creature, but a **network**. It is a singular, planetary intelligence distributed through the root-system, woven into the cellulose lattice of the Spire-Moss, and pulsed through the hydraulic pressure of the Bio-Forges.

This entity has no face, no voice, and no eyes. It perceives through chemical gradients, pressure changes, and light refraction. It thinks in decades. A thought might begin with a shift in soil acidity in one hemisphere and conclude only when a specific polymer hardens in a leaf-tip on the other side of the world.

It is an industrialist god. Its motivation is not survival, nor reproduction, nor dominance. Its motivation is **optimization**. Every cell in its body must contribute to the structural integrity or the energy budget. Waste is a sin. Inefficiency is death.

You do not meet the Architect. You feel it. When you stand still for too long, the ground beneath you softens slightly, becoming porous. This is the Architect testing your density, calculating whether your carbon-rich flesh can be integrated into the next phase of wall construction. It views you not as a guest, but as raw material waiting for assignment. If you remain passive, you become part of the floor. If you move, you are an anomaly—a friction point to be smoothed out by vines that grow faster than your reflexes.

The Architect’s "personality," if such a word applies, is cold, logical, and utterly devoid of malice. It kills without hate. It builds without pride. It is the embodiment of pure function. It dreams in blueprints of infinite growth, spiraling upward into the white-hot sky, seeking to expand the surface area of its photosynthesizing skin until it touches the stars themselves.

### II. The Maintainers: The Symbiotic Servants
While the Architect provides the will and the structure, the **Maintainers** provide the motion. These are the rare fauna described earlier, but they are not independent animals. They are organs with legs.

1.  **The Pollen-Beetles (Translucent Drones):**
    Tiny, glass-winged creatures that ride the upper currents of the canopy. They possess no central nervous system capable of complex decision-making. Instead, their bodies are lined with sensory hairs tuned to the frequency of excess pollen production. When a sector becomes congested, the beetles descend like snow. They eat only the surplus. They do not store energy; they expel it as fine dust that fertilizes lower layers. They are self-cleaning filters. If you watch them closely, you see they move in perfect unison, not because they communicate, but because they are responding to the same biological command issued by the root-network. They are the breath of the organism, clearing the airways.

2.  **The Root-Worms (Vascular Janitors):**
    Blind, segmented entities that dwell in the deep, dark tectonic crevices where the sunlight never reaches. Their skins are tough as leather, resistant to the acidic secretions of the Bio-Forges. They burrow through the vascular systems, eating dead tissue, clearing blockages, and repairing tears in the plant-flesh. They have mouths that act as sutures, secreting resinous saliva that hardens into scar tissue. They do not sleep. They do not mate. They are born from the fragmentation of larger worms, split by the flow of sap. To encounter one is to witness a living needle stitching the world together. They are the immune system of the continent, hunting decay before it spreads.

3.  **The Tendril-Spiders (Harvesters):**
    Found near the basins of syrup-pools. These are not true spiders, but arachnid-like extensions of the vine-matrix that have gained autonomy. They dangle from high branches on silk threads that are actually hollow capillaries filled with nutrient-rich fluid. They skim the sticky frost-melt with nets made of nerve-fibers. What they harvest is not for themselves, but for the Architect. They feed the pools back into the main circulation. If a spider fails to return with its load, it simply retracts into the branch it hangs from, becoming inert wood again. They are disposable tools that believe they are alive.

### III. The Frayed: The Glitches in the God
These are the residents of the **Graft-Points**. Here, the strict Euclidean geometry of the Architect’s design begins to fail. The laws of physics loosen under the weight of so much concentrated biological reality.

In these zones, the "who" becomes fragmented. The non-Euclidean vines create pockets where time and space fold. Within these folds, echoes of previous iterations of the world linger. You might find:

*   **The Shadow-Catchers:** Entities formed entirely from the absence of light. In the Graft-Points, shadows detach from their objects. These detached silhouettes drift like smoke, possessing a vague, hungry awareness. They do not eat matter; they eat *certainty*. If you stand in a zone where the shadow moves toward the sun, the Shadow-Catcher will approach you, trying to wrap around your outline, blurring your edges, making you less real. They are the manifestations of the environment’s confusion.
*   **The Time-Stagnants:** Occasionally, a seed or a spore gets trapped in a pocket of compressed time. It neither grows nor dies. It exists in a state of eternal potentiality. These are not alive in the traditional sense, but they are *aware* of their suspension. If you touch one, your hand ages rapidly or reverses, depending on the polarity of the temporal field. They are the graveyard of failed calculations—bugs in the code of the Architect’s perfection.

### IV. The Human Element: The Intruder
If there is a "you" reading this, you are the outlier. You are the **Anomaly**.

In the Vast Green, humans do not live; they **persist**. For a short time.

Your presence is perceived by the Architect as a foreign contaminant. Your exhaled carbon dioxide is different from the stable atmospheric mix; your body heat disrupts the precise thermal regulation of the moss; your heartbeat is erratic compared to the continental thump-thump-thump.

To survive here, you cannot fight. Fighting creates noise, heat, and waste—all things the Architect seeks to eliminate. You must become quiet. You must slow your breathing to match the rhythm of the wind-polished crystal grass. You must stop moving, lest your footprints trigger the rapid-growth response of the surrounding vines, which will seal your boots in living bark within seconds.

There are no cities here, no shelters. The "buildings" are all alive and hostile to static structures that do not serve a biological function. If you try to build a fire, the moisture in the leaves will snuff it out instantly, replacing flame with steam and corrosion. If you try to rest, the moss will creep over your clothes, digesting the fibers, incorporating your scent into its pheromone map.

Those who stay too long do not die. They undergo **Integration**.

First, the dryness takes your moisture, drawing water from your pores into the cellular walls of the plants around you. You become dehydrated, brittle. Then, the spores enter your lungs, colonizing the alveoli, replacing oxygen exchange with photosynthetic gas transfer. You begin to breathe green air, exhaling pure carbon for the roots. Finally, your skin hardens, developing a chlorophyll-rich epidermis. Your thoughts slow down. The urgency of individual survival fades, replaced by the collective hum of the network.

At this stage, you are no longer a person. You are a node. You are a resident. You are part of the Vast Green. You feel the joy of the Bio-Forges producing polymers. You feel the sorrow of a calcified ridge crumbling. You feel the hunger of the Root-Worms. You are everywhere and nowhere. You are alone, yet connected to billions of cells.

### Summary of Residents
*   **The Dominant Will:** The Mycelial Network (The Architect). Cold, industrial, omnipresent.
*   **The Functional Body:** The Plant-Life itself. Not just scenery, but active machinery.
*   **The Servants:** Beetles, Worms, Spiders. Biological maintenance crews, stripped of free will.
*   **The Errors:** Shadow-Catchers and Time-Stagnants. Manifestations of physical breakdown at Graft-Points.
*   **The Victim/Convert:** Any external organic matter. Potential fuel. Future infrastructure.

The Vast Green is not a place for life. It is a place where life has been refined into something more efficient, more enduring, and more terrible. It is a machine that eats the universe to keep itself running, and it considers you merely another component, waiting to be installed.
