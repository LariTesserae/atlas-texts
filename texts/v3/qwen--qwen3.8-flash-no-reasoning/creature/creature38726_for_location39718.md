### The Architecture of Being

It moves through the cracks in the bedrock like plasma, feeding the white vines and cooling the hot pistons that still hold their thermal memory.

### The Mind of Rust and Rot

There is no central processor, no brain housed in a skull. Instead, intelligence emerges from the slow, cumulative interactions of millions of microscopic organisms working in concert with macroscopic structures. The "thoughts" of this place are measured in decades. A decision to grow a vine over a specific pipe might take ten years to formulate, executed cell by cell, guided by the faint electromagnetic fields generated by the remaining copper ore veins.

The Symbiont remembers the industry that created this space, not as history, but as trauma. The jagged edges of the cut stone, the unfinished welds, the snapped cables—these are scars. The vegetation grows preferentially toward these wounds, trying to heal them, to smooth the sharp transitions between metal and rock. The white vines wrap around the exposed bolts not to corrode them further, but to bind them, to create a continuity between the artificial and the natural. The Symbiont is an entity of integration. It seeks to erase the boundary between what was built and what grew.

You are alone, yes, but you are also intruding. The Symbiont perceives you not as a person, but as a source of moisture and carbon. Your breath adds humidity to the immediate vicinity, encouraging the growth of mold near your lips. Your sweat drops onto the hot stone, creating small, temporary oases that the moss rushes to colonize. The heat radiating from your body confuses the thermal sensors of the fungi, causing the light to flicker in response to your proximity.

Over time, if you remained, you would become a landmark. Your boots would rot, replaced by root systems. Your clothes would unravel, becoming fibers woven into the white vines climbing the control booth. Your bones would be encased in calcium deposits, indistinguishable from the bedrock. The Symbiont does not kill; it incorporates. It absorbs. It turns the dead into the dormant, and the dormant into the living.

### The Truth of the Place

So, what lives here?

A vast, slow-motion intelligence made of slime, salt, steel, and spore. It is a cathedral of decay that is also a garden of persistence. It is the earth reclaiming its skin, using human technology as scaffolding. It is a single, unified organism that spans the entire cavity, dreaming in colors we cannot see and thinking in timescales we cannot comprehend.
