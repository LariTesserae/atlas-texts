Here, in the Verdant Vault, life does not merely exist; it *inhabits*. It is woven into the grain of the world, a symbiotic dance between the flesh of the traveler and the fibrous memory of the plant. To describe who lives here is to describe a people who have forgotten they are separate from their home, for there is no "outdoors" and no "indoors," only layers of growth, varying in density, intent, and hunger.

### The Sap-Kith: The Architects of Intent

The primary inhabitants are the **Sap-Kith**, humanoid figures whose skin has taken on the pale, translucent quality of young birch bark. They do not wear clothes so much as they *grow* them. Over generations, they have cultivated a second skin—a living epidermis of moss, lichen, and fine rootlets that feeds off their body heat and exhalations. This biological armor allows them to blend seamlessly into the walls of the trunk-towers when stillness is required. When they move, the rustle of their foliage-skin is indistinguishable from the wind in the canopy.

They are small, perhaps two meters tall, but their limbs are elongated and multi-jointed, adapted for climbing the vertical surfaces of the Vault without tools. Their hands possess four fingers, thick-padded with sensory nodes that can read the hydraulic pressure within the wood beneath their palms. A Sap-Kith elder can place a hand on a bridge-vine and feel the pulse of the entire continent-scale structure, sensing where the sap flows freely, where it stagnates, and where the vines are beginning to tighten in anger or fear.

Their society is built on **Persuasion**, not construction. You will see them standing before a sapling, humming a low, resonant frequency that matches the natural vibration of the plant’s cells. Over decades, sometimes centuries, the tree grows around them, forming hollows, arches, and staircases. A home is not built; it is coaxed. A doorway is an act of negotiation with the ivy. If the Sap-Kith grow angry or fearful, the house may contract, squeezing its occupants until they calm down, teaching them emotional regulation through physical discomfort.

They speak in a whisper-language, a complex system of clicks, hums, and breath patterns that carry little sound in the heavy air but transmit perfectly through bone-conduction. To shout is to invite death; loud noises attract the Vibration-Hunters (the serpentine vines). Therefore, the Sap-Kith are masters of silence. They communicate across vast distances by tapping rhythmic codes onto the trunk-walls, messages traveling miles through the wood at the speed of sound, arriving as faint throbs in the listener’s fingertips.

### The Mycelial Mind: The Collective Consciousness

Beneath the feet of the Sap-Kith, and above their heads, lies the true ruler of the Verdant Vault: the **Mycelial Network**. This is not a creature with eyes or a mouth, but a fungal intelligence that permeates every square inch of the substrate. The bioluminescent glow mentioned in the description is the visible manifestation of this mind’s thoughts—slow, chemical deliberations made light.

The Mycelial Mind views the Sap-Kith not as subjects, but as organs. It uses them to spread spores, to prune dead branches, and to introduce foreign matter into the ecosystem. In return, it grants them protection, warmth, and structural integrity. Occasionally, a Sap-Kith will enter a state of deep trance, sitting motionless for days while the fungus threads through their nervous system, downloading memories of the forest’s past or uploading new instructions. These individuals emerge changed, their eyes clouded with white filaments, speaking words they did not learn, knowing paths they have never walked. They are the **Spore-Speakers**, the priests of the green god, who interpret the will of the lattice.

To live here is to accept that your individuality is a fluid concept. The boundary between self and stem is porous. Many Sap-Kith believe that upon death, they do not disappear but dissolve into the soil, their consciousness merging with the Mycelial Mind, adding their memories to the vast, slow-accumulating archive of the Vault. Burials are ceremonies of release; the body is laid gently against a warm patch of resin-nodule, and the roots come up through the floorboards to claim it. There is no grave, only integration.

### The Sky-Gliders: The Winged Stewards

High above the inhabited levels, where the air thins to a cold, dry whisper, dwell the **Aeris**, or Sky-Gliders. These are not humanoids but avian-human hybrids, born with vestigial wings that have evolved into massive, cellulose-stiffened membranes spanning twenty meters. They do not flap; they ride the thermal currents rising from the rotting cores of the dead branches below.

The Aeris are solitary and aloof, viewing the Sap-Kith with a mix of pity and detachment. They live in the domes of translucent membrane, suspended in the void, eating the nectar of the decade-blooming flowers. Their bodies are hollow-boned and light, their skin feathered with soft, downy plant-matter that insulates them from the chill. They serve as the eyes of the Vault, scanning the endless drop for intruders or shifts in the weather patterns. When they dive, it is silent, a shadow blotting out the bruised purple sky. They rarely interact with those below, communicating only through complex aerial displays—banking left, then right, then spiraling downward—to signal danger or abundance.

However, the Aeris are also predators. They feed on the large amphibious insects that crawl in the mid-levels, snatching them from the moss with talons made of hardened lignin. And occasionally, if a Sap-Kith ventures too close to the edge of a drop without anchoring themselves, an Aeris might glide down—not to kill, but to retrieve. They catch falling travelers with surprising gentleness, setting them back on solid ground, though often leaving them trembling with the terror of having been held by something that could just as easily have dropped them into the abyss.

### The Resin-Stones: The Weeping Sentinels

Embedded in the walls are the **Resin-Stones**, nodules of mineralized amber that weep constantly. These are not alive in the traditional sense, but they are *aware*. They are the memory banks of the Vault. Each tear that slides down the cliff-face contains dissolved data—chemical signatures of every event that has occurred nearby. To drink the weeping is to taste static electricity, yes, but also to ingest fragments of history: the scream of a dying branch, the laughter of a child, the scent of rain from a century ago.

Some rare individuals among the Sap-Kith become **Tear-Drinkers**. They sit beside these pools for hours, absorbing the ambient memory, becoming conduits for the past. These Drinkers are revered and feared. They know the secrets of the lattice—who betrayed whom, which vine strangled which sibling, what lay buried deep in the core. But the weight of this knowledge often drives them mad, their minds unable to distinguish between their own memories and the thousands of others they have absorbed. They wander the corridors, muttering in languages long dead, pointing at empty spaces where ghosts of growth once stood.

### The Vibration-Hunters: The Immune Response

Lurking in the damp crevices and hanging like curtains of muscle and tendon are the **Vines of Retribution**. These are not plants in the passive sense; they are active hunters, extensions of the Mycelial Mind’s immune system. They have no eyes, no ears, but a sensitivity to vibration so acute they can detect the heartbeat of a prey animal three miles away.

When a traveler steps heavily, or when a Sap-Kith argues loudly, the vines tighten. They strike with the speed of a cobra, wrapping around ankles, waists, necks. They do not kill immediately; they squeeze, cutting off blood flow, inducing panic, until the victim submits to the rhythm of the Vault. Those who struggle are crushed slowly, their bodies absorbed into the root system, their nutrients returned to the soil. Those who go limp, accepting their fate, may be released—or dragged into the dark hollows to be digested over weeks.

Living with the Hunters requires a discipline of movement known as **The Soft Walk**. Children are taught this before they can run. Every step must be deliberate, placed precisely on firmer ground, avoiding the sensitive tremor-inducers. To run is suicide. To jump is a gamble. The inhabitants of the Verdant Vault move with a fluid, predatory grace, sliding along the polished floors, swinging from lianas, never making a sound that carries beyond their immediate radius.

### The Void-Dwellers: The Lost Ones

Finally, there are those who fell. The **Void-Dwellers** are not a distinct species, but a category of exile. They are Sap-Kith who slipped, who were pulled by vines into the gaps, who lost their footing on the swaying bridges. Some survived, landing on lower, darker platforms where the light fails entirely. Here, gravity feels heavier, the air thinner, the silence louder.

Over generations, these lost ones have changed. Their skin has turned greyish-white, blind to the lack of light. Their eyes have widened to capture the faintest bioluminescence. They have become more plant-like, their hair growing into tendrils that seek moisture from the air. They are feral, desperate, and hungry. They climb up from the depths, raiding the upper levels for food and shelter, driven by a primal need to return to the light they remember but cannot see.

The Sap-Kith of the upper levels regard the Void-Dwellers with horror and superstition. They leave offerings at the edges—the bundles of seeds wrapped in leaf-paper—not just for luck, but as appeasement. They hope the seeds will fall far enough to reach the lower realms, feeding the lost ones, keeping them occupied, preventing them from climbing higher. It is a fragile peace, maintained by drops of hope into an infinite dark.

### The Nature of Living There

To live in the Verdant Vault is to live in a constant state of negotiated existence. You are never alone, yet you are profoundly isolated by the silence. You are surrounded by abundance, yet you are always aware of the emptiness that defines your world.

Your home breathes. Your clothes photosynthesize. Your food grows in response to your mood. Your enemies are the very ground you walk on. Your gods are the fungi in the cracks. Your ancestors are the sap in the walls.

You do not conquer nature here; you submit to it. You listen to the thrum. You match your heart to the pulse. You learn that safety is not found in stone walls, but in the trust of a vine. And you understand, deep in your marrow, that if you stop listening—if you start shouting, running, thinking yourself separate—you will be digested.

So you stay still. You let the moss grow on your shoulders. You let the roots find purchase in your dreams. You become part of the lattice. You become green.
