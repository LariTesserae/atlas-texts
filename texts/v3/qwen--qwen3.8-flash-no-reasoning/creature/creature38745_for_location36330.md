In the Kiln-Cathedral, nothing breathes in the way a lung expands. Instead, existence here is defined by **vibration, resonance, and calcification**.

To describe who or what lives here requires abandoning the concept of "biological life" as we understand it. There are no hearts beating with blood; there are no eyes blinking away moisture. The inhabitants are entities of heat-shock resistance and mineral integration. Let us call them the **Ash-Walkers**, though this name is a translation error for their own self-designation, which sounds more like the grinding of tectonic plates: *Kra-Thum*.

### I. The Ash-Walkers: The Living Stone

The Kra-Thum do not have soft skin. They possess an integument of layered, semi-translucent silicate scales that resemble the outer rind of a geode. This armor is not grown once at birth but is continuously secreted from pores along their spine, shedding old layers like snakeskin during thermal peaks. These shed skins fall to the obsidian floor, where they shatter into harmless glass dust, contributing to the haze that warps visibility.

They are tall, gaunt figures, averaging two meters in height, with elongated limbs designed for reach rather than speed. Their joints are ball-and-socket mechanisms encased in flexible copper-moss tendons, allowing them to twist and fold themselves into narrow fissures without breaking. They move with a eerie silence relative to their size; their feet are wide and padded with a dense, rubbery fungal tissue harvested from the cave walls, distributing their weight so they do not crack the brittle silica floors beneath them.

Their faces are masks of angular geometry. They lack noses, for they do not breathe air in the traditional sense. Instead, they inhale superheated vapor through slits along their necks, filtering out sulfur particulates using internal membranes made of woven mica. Their mouths are horizontal slits, rarely used for speech (for speech is futile against the roar of physics), but frequently used for tasting minerals. A Kra-Thum can taste the iron content of a rock face, determining its age and stability.

**The Eyes:**
This is the most unsettling feature. The Kra-Thum have no whites, no irises, no pupils. Their eyes are solid, multifaceted crystals, similar to the silica-ferns that surround them. These crystalline eyes do not see light; they see **heat signatures and vibrational frequencies**. To a Kra-Thum, the world is not a visual image but a complex audio-spectrum map. They perceive the glowing red cracks of magma as low-frequency drones, the blue glow of the moss as high-pitched whines, and the movement of other beings as distinct rhythmic patterns. When you look into their eyes, you see your own reflection distorted across dozens of facets, a kaleidoscope of your own body temperature.

### II. Communication: The Rhythm of the Roar

Because the ambient noise of the vent system—a constant 120-decibel drone of escaping gas and clashing crystal—drowns out vocal cords, the Kra-Thum evolved communication through **percussion and sign**.

*   **Hand-Drums:** Every adult carries a small drum strapped to their forearm, crafted from hollowed basalt and stretched hide. By tapping specific rhythms, they convey complex ideas. A rapid triple-tap means *"The vent is shifting."* A slow, heavy thud means *"Safety."* It is a language of timing, pitch, and volume.
*   **Crystalline Sign Language:** In close quarters, they communicate through hand gestures that manipulate the light. They hold up polished shards of mica, angling them to catch the prismatic beams of the silica-ferns. A flash of blue light directed at another’s face might mean *"I am tired,"* while a cascade of red reflections could signify *"Danger ahead."*

### III. The Architecture of Ancestry: The Petrified Watchers

The description mentions that death is just construction. For the Kra-Thum, this is literal. Upon death, a Kra-Thum does not decay. The extreme dryness and heat cause immediate desiccation. The body hardens, the silicate scales fusing with the surrounding mineral-rich air. Over decades, the dead ancestor becomes indistinguishable from the living architecture.

Therefore, the city is populated by **statues that were once people**.

These petrified ancestors are not buried in tombs. They are placed in alcoves carved into the Pillars, often still holding their hand-drums or tools. Over centuries, Copper-Moss grows over them, embedding them further into the structure. New generations live literally inside the bodies of their forebears. Walls are thickened by the accumulation of generations of dead.

To a stranger entering the Kiln-Cathedral, the horror is immediate. You walk down a plaza and realize the smooth, polished column beside you has the shape of a human torso, arms raised, fingers splayed. The "roof beam" above you is a line of petrified heads, jaws open in silent screams or songs, fused together. The Kra-Thum do not view this with revulsion; they view it with reverence. To touch a wall is to touch a grandmother. To lean against a pillar is to rest upon the shoulder of a great-uncle.

**Memory Moss as Library:**
Since the dead become stone, and stone records vibration, the Kra-Thum treat their ancestors as storage devices. If a young Kra-Thum wishes to learn a technique, they may gently tap the chest cavity of a petrified ancestor. The Copper-Moss embedded in the stone releases a stored echo—the voice or rhythm of that ancestor when they were alive, teaching a song, a warning, or a history lesson. The city hums with the ghosts of thousands of voices, trapped in the lattice of the walls, playing back snippets of conversation from centuries past whenever the wind shifts.

### IV. The Fauna of the Void: Non-Biological Entities

While there are no animals, the ecosystem supports other forms of "life" that blur the line between biology and geology.

1.  **Thermal-Eels:** Deep within the hottest vents, serpentine structures of molten salt flow upward, cooling instantly upon contact with the cooler upper cavern air. These are not creatures with intent, but rather fluid dynamics given form. However, the Kra-Thum hunt them. Using long hooks made of bone-crystal, they snag these transient eels, harvesting the cooled salt for industrial purposes and preserving the rare, heat-resistant proteins found in the eel’s core. Eating a Thermal-Eel is dangerous; if one does not chew quickly enough, the residual heat can burn the mouth shut.
2.  **Glass-Spiders:** Arachnid-like constructs formed from spontaneous crystallization on the tips of the silica-ferns. They have no brains, no organs, only tension-based legs. They hang motionless for days, waiting for the thermal current to shift. When the wind changes, their legs contract, snapping shut around passing motes of metallic dust. They are considered pests by the Kra-Thum, as they clog the ventilation holes of homes, but they are also harvested for their perfect geometric symmetry, used in jewelry and religious artifacts.
3.  **Echo-Wraiths:** Not physical entities, but phenomena caused by the "Time is Fluid" rule. Near the hottest zones, time slows. A Kra-Thum moving quickly through a hot vent zone might appear to freeze mid-stride to an observer in a cool zone, then suddenly snap forward after several seconds of real-time. Conversely, an observer in a slow-time zone might watch a bird (if birds existed) fall from the ceiling for hours before hitting the ground. The Kra-Thum navigate these temporal eddies carefully. To misjudge the flow of time is to step out of sync with reality. Some elders speak of "Wraiths"—people who entered a time-slow zone and never emerged, effectively frozen in a moment that lasts forever, becoming part of the background radiation.

### V. Daily Life: The Dance of Survival

Life for the Kra-Thum is a constant negotiation with the environment.

*   **Morning (or the Cycle Peak):** As the underground magma surges, the temperature spikes. The silica-ferns vibrate violently, creating a deafening chime. The Kra-Thum retreat deeper into their suspended houses, anchoring themselves to the pillars. This is the time of danger; bridges sway dangerously, and the risk of being blown off into the incandescent void below is highest. They huddle together, sharing body heat (though they generate little themselves, relying on external warmth), and wait for the peak to pass.
*   **Midday (or the Venting):** As the heat stabilizes, the updrafts weaken. The sound drops to a lower drone. This is work time. Harvesters descend on ropes to collect fresh Crystal-Fern sap, which is used to seal cracks in homes. Miners chip away at the Copper-Moss, extracting trace metals. Children play in the plazas, kicking shards of broken glass, learning the rhythms of the ground.
*   **Evening (or the Cooling):** The light dims as the geothermal glow fades slightly. The bioluminescent moss brightens, casting the city in a ghostly blue-green. This is the time for socializing. Families gather on balconies. Elders tap their drums, recounting stories encoded in the vibrations of the moss. Young lovers exchange glints of reflected light, a romantic gesture unique to this dark, shimmering world.

### VI. The Philosophy of Dryness

The Kra-Thum have no word for "wet." They have no concept of drowning, because water does not exist here. Their greatest fear is not death, but **brittleness**.

To them, flexibility is life. Rigidity is death. Their homes sway. Their bodies are covered in flexible scales. Their minds are adaptable, shifting their perception of time based on the heat gradient. To be rigid—to stop moving, to stop adapting—is to shatter.

When a Kra-Thum dies, they do not mourn the loss of breath. They celebrate the achievement of becoming permanent. They praise the deceased for finally stopping the endless dance, for becoming strong enough to bear the weight of the mountain, for becoming part of the foundation.

### VII. The Outsider's Experience

If a human were to enter the Kiln-Cathedral, they would find it intolerable within minutes. The air would scorch their lungs. The heat would dehydrate them instantly. But the psychological toll would be heavier.

They would feel watched. Not by predators, but by the walls. The petrified faces in the alcoves would seem to turn toward them as the light shifted. The echoes leaking from the moss would whisper in languages unknown, sounding like static-filled radio broadcasts from a distant future or past.

And then, they would hear the music. The symphony of glass and gas. At first, it is terrifying. But if one stays long enough, if one allows the heat to seep into the bones, the noise begins to organize itself. The random crashes become rhythm. The hissing winds become melody. The outsider realizes that the chaos is actually a precise, mathematical order—a vast, planetary engine turning in the dark.

They would begin to sway. They would stop fighting the vibration. And in that surrender, they would cease to be an intruder, and become part of the Cathedral’s eternal, roaring choir.
