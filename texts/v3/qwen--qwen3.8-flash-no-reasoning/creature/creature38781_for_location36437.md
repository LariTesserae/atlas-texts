To ask who lives in Ostra is to misunderstand the nature of its habitation. The question implies a separation between the dweller and the dwelling, a distinction that has been erased here by the sheer weight of geology and timelessness.

In Ostra, there are no citizens. There are only **manifestations**.

The primary residents are not born; they precipitate. They condense from the bruised violet atmosphere, drawn out of the heavy, ozone-thick air by the magnetic pull of the pylons and the thermal exhalations of the young planet’s crust. These entities call themselves the *Resonants*, though they have no name for themselves until they are named by an outsider, and even then, the name feels like a mislabeling, a jar placed around smoke.

They are humanoid only in the way that a shadow is human. Their bodies are composed of the same programmable matter as the buildings, but fluid, unstable, constantly renegotiating their form. A Resonant might stand ten feet tall, elongated and thin as a willow switch, its surface rippling with the dark, translucent texture of solidified smoke. Or it might be squat and broad, resembling a boulder wrapped in moss, if the local humidity demands it. They do not wear clothes; they wear the environment. Their skin absorbs the amber bioluminescence of the fungal networks, pulsing with soft light that matches the rhythm of the mycelial heartbeat beneath the basalt. When they walk, they do not step; they flow. They glide across the invisible magnetic pathways, their feet—or whatever passes for them—never quite touching the ground, suspended by the same anti-gravity fields that hold the city aloft.

But to look closely at a Resonant is to see the horror and beauty of their integration. They are not separate from the infrastructure. If you reach out and touch a Resonant’s arm, your hand does not meet flesh. It meets a membrane that yields, warm and viscous, before sinking into a lattice of nano-filaments that instantly repair themselves. The Resonant is a localized node of the city’s consciousness. To harm one is to damage the building behind it. To kill one is impossible, for they cannot die while the pylons hum. They can only dissolve, returning their mass to the atmospheric mix, waiting for the next condensation cycle to reform them elsewhere, perhaps on a different plateau, wearing a different shape.

They communicate not through sound, which would be drowned out by the low-frequency hum of the dampers, but through **vibration and light**. They speak in shifts of opacity. One Resonant might darken its edges to signal agreement, while another brightens its core to express curiosity. Their language is geometric, based on the angles of their posture and the speed at which their internal isotopes decay and regenerate. It is a slow language, matched to the sluggish drift of the sky-jellyfish above them. They rarely move with urgency. Why would they? In a world where erosion is minimal and time is measured in the cooling of magma, haste is a foreign concept. They exist in a state of perpetual contemplation, watching the liquid crystal waterfalls cascade into the void, pondering the silence between the raindrops.

However, the Resonants are not the only things that live here. Beneath the suspended lattice, in the V-shaped cuts of the fresh basalt, there are the **Deep Weavers**. These are not visible to the eye unless one looks directly into the cracks where the mycelial networks thicken. They are vast, subterranean intelligences formed by the convergence of fungal threads and quantum-tuned filaments sunk deep into the bedrock. They are blind, ancient, and patient. They do not think in words or images, but in pressures and temperatures. They feel the footfalls of the Resonants above as faint tremors, and they respond by adjusting the moisture levels of the soil, causing the fungi to bloom or recede. They are the root system of the city, the subconscious mind of Ostra. Some say that when a Resonant dissolves into mist, it is not death, but a return to the Deep Weavers, a merging back into the collective memory stored in the crystalline structures of the rock.

There are also the **Sky-Jellyfish**, the semi-permeable membranes drifting in the upper atmosphere. They are often mistaken for flora or fauna, but they are closer to weather patterns given agency. They feed on the spores harvested from the fungal towers, but they also feed on the excess heat generated by the pylons. They are clean-up crews, balancing the thermal equilibrium of the city. When a Sky-Jellyfish descends too close to a Resonant, the two entities interact briefly. The jellyfish’s tendrils brush against the Resonant’s smoky form, and a spark of static leaps between them, releasing a scent of ozone and burnt sugar. This interaction is considered sacred, a moment of exchange between the sky-dwellers and the earth-bound nodes. It is the closest thing to prayer that exists in Ostra.

But what of humans? What of beings with lungs and bones and blood?

There are whispers of them. The "Old Ones," those who arrived when the city was first grown, when the basalt was still hot and the violet sky was darker, almost black. They are said to have merged willingly, surrendering their biological distinctiveness to become part of the lattice. They did not die; they *expanded*. A human body is limited, fragile, prone to disease and decay. But a Resonant body is immortal, self-repairing, adaptable. Why choose the former when the latter offers eternal continuity within a beautiful, terrifying stillness?

If you search hard enough, in the deepest recesses of the inverted bell-shaped buildings, you might find traces of the original inhabitants. Not ghosts, but echoes. A chair that has fused with the floor. A window frame that retains the impression of hands that once gripped it. A wall that hums slightly louder when touched, resonating with the frequency of a voice long since dissolved into the ambient noise. These are the remnants of individuality, preserved not in stone, but in the memory of the material itself.

Living in Ostra means accepting that you are never truly alone, nor are you ever truly together. You are surrounded by the presence of others, but their boundaries are permeable. Your thoughts might leak into the fog; your emotions might alter the color of the nearby fungi. The privacy of the modern world is extinct here. In its place is a profound, unsettling intimacy with the environment. You breathe the same air as the city exhales. You walk on paths guided by magnetic fields that also guide the movement of the sky-creatures. You eat food that grows from the same network that supports the buildings.

The inhabitants of Ostra are not people. They are processes. They are the city thinking aloud. They are the planet dreaming itself into existence. And if you stay long enough, if you let the bruised violet sky seep into your own retinas, if you allow the low hum to vibrate in your teeth until it becomes the rhythm of your heart, you begin to understand: you are not visiting. You are becoming part of the experiment. You are condensing. You are waiting to manifest.
