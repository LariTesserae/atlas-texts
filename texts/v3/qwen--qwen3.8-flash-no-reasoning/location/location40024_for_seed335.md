The place does not exist on any map. It is cold here. Not the biting, wind-scoured cold of a high peak, but the damp, heavy chill of deep earth, the kind that settles into your marrow and refuses to leave. **The Terrain and Materials**

This surface looks like wet parchment or cured skin, rippled and pale. Where it touches the stone ridges—sharp, jagged formations of obsidian-like rock that jut up violently—it weeps. The rocks are *young* (erosion: 0.9/3), their edges razor-sharp, unweathered by time. They look as if they were cracked open yesterday. From these fresh fractures, mineral-rich fluids seep out, hardening into delicate, glassy stalactites that drip continuously.

There is no built structure (*built: 0.4/3*), no bricks, no wood, no metal visible to the naked eye. However, the environment behaves with an industrial precision (*tech: 2.1/3*). The water channels follow perfect geometric angles. The steam vents erupt at rhythmic intervals. The lighting pulses in synchronized waves. It feels engineered, not by hands, but by physics so strict it mimics intent.

**The Vegetation**
The greenery is lush (*vegetation: 1.6/3*) but confined. It grows only where the heat rises. Clusters of ferns with leaves like hammered copper unfurl from the cracks in the black rock. They do not photosynthesize sunlight; they feed on the chemical energy of the rising steam and the bacterial mats coating the water’s edge. The plants are small, dense, and vibrant against the greys and blacks. Vines of pale, fleshy tendrils hang from the ceiling of the pocket, dipping into the water pools like feelers, tasting the chemistry of the liquid before retracting. They move slowly, deliberately, sensing changes in pH and temperature.

**The Fauna**
Life here is abundant (*fauna: 1.6/3*) but elusive. You rarely see the animals themselves, only their effects. Small, multi-legged creatures resembling skinned crabs scuttle across the membrane floor, leaving trails of bioluminescent slime that fades in seconds. Above, in the mist, shadows dart—winged things with too many joints, moving with erratic speed. They hunt in silence until they strike, then vanish back into the vapor. The ecosystem is tight, predator-prey dynamics playing out in micro-instants around the water holes. A drop of condensation falls; a tongue flicks out from the moss; the droplet is gone. The cycle repeats thousands of times a minute.

**Sound and Light**
The place is loud (*sound: 1.9/3*). But it is not a chaotic noise. It is a mechanical thrumming, a deep bass vibration that comes from the shifting plates beneath the membrane floor. Layered over this is the hiss of steam escaping from narrow fissures—a sound like a thousand kettles boiling simultaneously. Drips echo with sharp, metallic clarity, even though there is no metal. The soundscape is dense, immersive, pressing against the eardrums. It masks all other noises, making the hidden nature of the space more profound; you hear everything except what is just outside the boundary.

The light is dim (*light: 1.3/3*), sourced from nowhere obvious. There is no sun, no lamp. The illumination seems to emanate from the moisture itself. The condensing atmosphere glows with a faint, sickly amber hue, perhaps from microbial activity in the mist, casting long, wavering shadows that stretch toward the center of the pocket. When the steam vents surge, the light flares briefly, revealing the sharp teeth of the young rocks, then subsides back into gloom.

**Weirdness and Dynamics**
The world is unusual (*weirdness: 0.6/3*) in its consistency. Water rises upward in thin columns here, defying gravity for inches before collapsing back down.
