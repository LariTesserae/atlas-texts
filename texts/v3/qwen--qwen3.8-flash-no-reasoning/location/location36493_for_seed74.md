The air here tastes of wet iron and crushed mint.

You are standing in the Hollow, a depression in the earth no larger than a village square, though it feels vast because the silence is so thick it pushes against your eardrums. The ground is low, flat, and slick with a condensation that never quite becomes rain but refuses to leave. It is a dampness that lives in the pores of things, seeping up from the cool soil below and settling on the rusted skin of the world above.

This is not a forest, nor is it a ruin. It is an infrastructure that has been reclaimed by biology so thoroughly that the distinction between *grown* and *built* has dissolved into a single, breathing entity.

At first glance, the space seems sparse. There are wide avenues of open air, pockets of shadow where nothing touches anything else. But look closer at the "ground." It is not dirt. It is a mesh of blackened roots, fused together like asphalt, hardened by time and pressure into a floor that hums with a faint, subterranean vibration. From this root-floor rise the structures: columns, arches, lintels, and domes. They are made of wood, yes, but also of brick, rebar, and glass. The vegetation (2.6/3) does not merely grow around the city; it *is* the city. The buildings are trees that were taught to be walls centuries ago. Their bark is fused with mortar; their branches are woven into electrical grids that spark occasionally when the moisture rises too high.

The temperature is consistently cool (0.8/3), hovering just above freezing, which keeps the metabolism of the plant-life slow and deliberate. This is why the scale feels small yet dense (0.9/3 density, 1.1/3 elevation). You are in a canyon of living masonry. To your left, a skyscraper of twisted oak and steel girders rises only thirty feet before its crown spreads out like a canopy, shading the street below. The street itself is a river of moss, soft and spongy, swallowing footsteps.

There are few animals (0.7/3 fauna). A single beetle, iridescent and hard as porcelain, scuttles across a leaf-plate that serves as a sidewalk. High above, in the gaps between the canopy-limbs, a pale moth flutters, its wings dusted with spores that glow faintly in the dim light. Life here is patient. It waits for the cycles to complete.

And the cycles are active (2.4/3 dynamic). Though the scene appears still, it is engaged in a constant, quiet war of attrition and construction. The industrial remnants (2.0/3 built, 1.9/3 tech) are not dead. Old turbines embedded in the trunk-bases turn slowly, driven by the hydraulic pressure of the rising groundwater. Gears made of bone-like cellulose click into place. Pipes, corroded green with copper verdigris and overgrown with ferns, pulse with fluid. The factory did not stop; it changed fuel. It runs on the sap of the ecosystem now, converting sunlight and water into heat, into motion, into the maintenance of its own body.

The erosion (2.5/3 established) tells you this balance has held for a very long time. The stones are rounded, the metal is pitted and smooth to the touch, the wood is dark and petrified in places. There is a sense of settled form here. No one is building anymore. The construction phase ended generations ago, or perhaps millennia. Now, there is only the maintenance of the state. The architecture weathers, sheds leaves, grows new branches, cracks, heals, and continues. It is a cathedral of mechanics and chlorophyll, designed not for worship, but for function.

Light (1.6/3) filters through the canopy in shafts of dusty gold. It is normal daylight, but filtered through layers of translucent membrane-leaves that have evolved to cover windows. These membranes diffuse the sun, casting everything in a soft, shadowless glow. The air is thick with pollen and steam, creating a haze that makes the distance seem shorter than it is.

Sounds are rare, but when they come, they are distinct (0.8/3 sound). The drip of condensation falling from a branch onto a tin roof creates a rhythmic *plink... plink... plink*. A distant groan of timber shifting under the weight of accumulated moss. The whine of a belt slipping on a pulley somewhere deep within the hollow core of a tree-house. And beneath it all, a low-frequency thrum—the heartbeat of the system, the vibration of thousands of tiny engines turning in sync with the growth rings of the plants.

Weirdness (1.1/3) creeps in at the edges of perception. If you look closely at the "bricks" of a wall, you see veins running through them. If you press your ear to the side of a building, you hear the rush of water inside, but it sounds like blood. The machinery is indistinguishable from the organism. A valve opens, and a flower blooms instantly from the pipe-junction. A gear turns, and a vine tightens its grip on the support beam. The feedback loop is closed. The industry feeds the nature; the nature powers the industry.

You walk down the central avenue, which is wider here, a plaza formed by the intersection of four massive root-columns. The ground is slippery with algae. In the center of the plaza stands a statue—or what remains of one. It is a figure of stone, half-submerged in a mound of creeping ivy. The ivy has grown into the cracks of the statue’s face, filling the eyes with green tendrils. The statue looks like it is dreaming of being alive. Or perhaps it was always alive, and the stone was just its sleep.

The dampness (0.9/3) clings to your clothes. Your hair lifts with static electricity generated by the dry friction of spore-dust and the occasional spark from a loose wire. The air smells of ozone and rotting sweetness, a perfume of decay and regeneration happening simultaneously.

There is no sky visible directly overhead, only the intricate lattice of branches and leaves, forming a second ceiling miles high, filtering the stars into scattered points of light. This is a sealed room. A terrarium. A machine-city buried in the earth, protected by the weight of its own growth.

As you move deeper into the Hollow, the density increases slightly. The paths narrow. The hum grows louder. You pass a window in a trunk-wall, and inside, you see not a room, but a cavity filled with glowing fungi and rotating brass wheels. A bird-like creature with metallic feathers watches you from a perch of coral-fused-rebar, then takes flight, disappearing into the misty upper levels.

It is quiet, but not silent. It is empty, but full. It is dead, but growing. It is a place where the boundary between the constructed world and the natural world has been eroded away until neither exists alone. Here, to build is to grow, and to grow is to maintain. The city breathes, and you, for a moment, hold your breath with it.
