The place does not exist in the way a city exists, nor in the way a forest exists. It is a cavity. A hollowed-out lung of stone, sealed shut by millennia of geological patience and forgotten intent.

To find it, one must descend through the crust of the world, past the roots of dead mountains, into a stratum where pressure has crushed time into diamond-dust and silence into lead. Here, at a low elevation relative to the surface but deep within the planetary mantle’s skin, lies the **Ossuary of Green**.

It is a pocket-scale universe, no larger than a cathedral nave, yet vast enough to swallow the concept of distance. The darkness here is absolute. Not merely the absence of light, but a physical substance—a thick, viscous velvet that presses against the eyes until they ache with the phantom memory of sun. There is no moon here, no star-glow, no bioluminescence from animals, for there are no animals. The fauna parameter is zero; nothing with blood or bone walks these halls. Nothing breathes air in the traditional sense.

But there is life. Oh, how there is life.

**Vegetation: 2.8/3 — Total Substrate**

The floor, the walls, the ceiling—everything is plant. But not as we know plants. These are not photosynthetic organisms hungry for light; they are chemosynthetic, lithotrophic entities that feed on mineral decay and geothermal heat. They are the architecture. The "buildings" are grown, not constructed. The "streets" are cleared pathways of compacted root-matter. The "city" is a single, contiguous organism, a mycelial network of hyper-evolved flora that has been slowly growing, dying, and regrowing over ten thousand years without interruption.

Because the water parameter is near zero (0.3/3), this is not a wet jungle. It is a dry, brittle, crystalline ecosystem. The plants do not hold sap; they hold resin. They do not have leaves; they have fractal shards of silica-laced cellulose, hard as glass, sharp as obsidian. The air is arid, tasting of chalk and old copper. To walk here is to risk being sliced by the very ground beneath your feet. The vegetation is so dense, so complete, that it fills every cubic inch of the available space, creating a labyrinthine density that feels empty because the spaces between the fronds are too narrow to enter, while the open areas are vast, echoing voids where the plant-structure has collapsed under its own weight.

**Erosion: 2.9/3 — Ancient Final Form**

The erosion is not violent. It is ancient. It is the result of eons of stillness. The edges of the plant-crystals are perfectly smooth, worn down by the slow friction of their own growth. There is no wind to erode them, no rain to wash them. The "weathering" is internal—the cellular breakdown of older structures feeding the newer ones. This is a closed loop, a perfect equilibrium. The city has settled into its final form. It is stable. It will look exactly like this in another million years, perhaps with slightly thicker layers of accretion, but the shape is done. The blueprint is finished.

**Scale: 0.5/3 — Hidden / Micro-Environment**

Despite the "urban" feel of the infrastructure, the scale is intimate. You cannot see the whole of it. Your headlamp (which you would bring, though it offers little utility in this total dark) illuminates only a few meters before the beam dies in the blackness. The rest of the city extends beyond perception, a blind sprawl of green-black crystal. You are never alone, because the walls are watching, breathing, pulsing—but you are always isolated, contained within your small sphere of visibility. It is a room that thinks it is a metropolis.

**Built: 2.2/3 — Urban Infrastructure**

This is the most disorienting aspect. The plant-life has organized itself into what can only be described as urban planning. There are archways formed by two converging stalks. There are plazas where the crystals grow flat and wide, forming floors. There are towers that rise toward the unseen ceiling, connected by bridges of woven fibrous tissue.

Who built it? No one. Or rather, everyone who ever tried to live here failed, and their failures became the structure. The "settlement" is a fossil record of habitation attempts. Primitive tech (0.7/3) is visible in the corners: hand-carved stone tools left on altars of fungal shelf-like growths; crude rope made from dried tendons of long-extinct beasts (or perhaps just strong plant fibers) tied around pillars. These artifacts are not maintained; they are incorporated. The plants grow *around* the tools, sealing them in amber-like resin. The city is a museum of its own inhabitants, preserved by the substrate that consumed them.

**Light: 0.0/3 & Sound: 2.0/3 — Loud Darkness**

In the absence of sight, sound becomes dominant. And it is loud.

The silence of the desert is broken by the constant, rhythmic *crack-hiss* of expansion. As the temperature fluctuates minutely (0.8/3 — cool, but variable in micro-climates), the silicate-cellulose structures expand and contract. Millions of tiny fractures pop and snap across the cityscape. It sounds like a forest of ice falling apart in a storm. Underneath this percussion is a lower hum, a resonant frequency generated by the movement of dry dust grains sliding over each other in the vast, empty corridors. It is a white noise roar, deafening in its constancy. To speak, you must shout. To listen, you must press your ear to the cold, vibrating walls.

**Weirdness: 1.6/3 — Strange**

There is a subtle wrongness here. The geometry feels non-Euclidean in the dark. Because you cannot see the horizon lines, your brain invents distances that don't match the echoes. A corridor that sounds five meters long might take twenty minutes to traverse because the plant-walls curve inward, narrowing the path until you must crawl.

Also, the plants react to presence. Not aggressively, but passively. If you stand still for too long, the fine dust on the ground begins to drift toward your feet, settling on your boots like snow. The air currents shift. You realize that the "empty" density (0.1/3) is deceptive; the air is heavy with spores, microscopic crystals that hang suspended in the dark. When you breathe, you inhale the city. It coats your lungs. Over time, your own body becomes part of the substrate. Your sweat salts the ground. Your exhaled CO2 feeds the nearest stalk. You are not visiting the city; you are digesting it, and it is digesting you.

**Ecology: Dry Mineral Plant-Life**

There are no flowers, no fruits, no seeds. Reproduction is vegetative fragmentation. A piece of wall breaks off during the sonic cracking storms, tumbles into a crack in the floor, and if it lands in a pocket of trapped heat (from the primitive geothermal vents that provide the energy source), it takes root. New buildings grow from the debris of old ones. The city regenerates itself through destruction.

At night—if such a word applies to a place without day—the cracks become louder. The temperature drops. The resin hardens. The city freezes into a moment of perfect clarity, then thaws as the vent pulses warm air from below, and the growth resumes.

**Dynamic: 1.4/3 — Slow**

Change happens on a timescale that mocks human urgency. A bridge grows a millimeter per year. A tower shifts its angle by one degree per decade. Yet, in the aggregate, the city is constantly rebuilding itself. The "erosion" is the pruning of this slow construction. The dead parts fall away, the living parts climb higher.

**Final Impression**

Imagine standing in the center of a town square paved with black glass trees. The sky above is a ceiling of tangled, fossilized vines, pitch black. Around you, the ruins of huts carved from stone sit half-buried in mounds of sharp, translucent fern-fronds. There are no people. There are no birds. There is only the cold, the dark, the taste of copper, and the relentless, roaring *click-click-click* of the earth grinding its own teeth together.

It is a beautiful, terrifying tomb. A library written in a language of salt and silica, readable only by those willing to lose their sight and their sanity to the rhythm of the breaking world.
