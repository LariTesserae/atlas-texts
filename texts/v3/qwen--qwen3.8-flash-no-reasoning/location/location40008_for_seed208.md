The air here is thick, not with humidity, but with weight. It presses against the eardrums before it touches the skin.

This is the **Plain of the Silt-Singers**.

At first glance, it appears to be a vast, low-lying depression in the earth, perhaps a dried seabed from an epoch before time was measured in years. The elevation is barely above sea level, a shallow bowl extending for miles toward horizons that blur into gray mist. But nothing here is dry. The ground is saturated, a sponge-like consistency of fine, dark particulate matter—mineral dust and crushed stone, so ancient it has lost its angularity. It is silt that has forgotten what it means to be rock.

There are no trees. There is no grass. There is no moss. The vegetation parameter of 0.3/3 manifests as an absence so profound it feels aggressive. Instead of green, the world is composed of **Geological Biomes**.

### The Terrain: Weeping Stone
The terrain is defined by **Erosion: 3.0/3 — Ancient**. This is not a landscape shaped by recent storms or seasonal floods. This is a landscape that has been ground down by millennia of static friction. The rocks are not mountains; they are stumps. Jagged peaks have been worn away until only their cores remain, exposed like ribs in a skeleton laid flat on the floor of a cathedral.

These stones are slick. They weep. Because there is no plant life to absorb moisture, the water sits on the surface of the mineral crust, condensing from the cool (Temperature: 0.6/3) atmosphere and pooling in the depressions between the flattened stones. The pools are not clear; they are viscous, reflecting the dim light (Light: 0.8/3) with a dull, oily sheen. They are deep enough to swallow a boot, but shallow enough to reveal the white, fungal-like filaments that thread through the mud below. These are not plants. They are crystalline structures, growing slowly, feeding on the mineral saturation of the water. They look like pale roots, or frozen lightning trapped in amber.

### The Sound: Deafening Silence
The most striking feature is the **Sound: 2.6/3 — Deafening**.

In a place with no birds, no rustling leaves, no flowing rivers, one might expect silence. But this plain is loud. The sound comes from the **Fauna: 2.0/3 — Abundant**.

The fauna here are not animals in the traditional sense. They are **Lithic Swarms**. Tiny, translucent creatures, no larger than grain of sand, that live within the wet silt and the crevices of the weeping rocks. They move in coordinated pulses. When they shift, when they feed on the mineral deposits, when they reproduce, they create a frictional resonance.

Millions of these microscopic organisms scraping against millions of grains of sand creates a constant, high-pitched **shriek**. It sounds like a tea kettle boiling on a far-off stove, amplified to a piercing whine. It is a wall of noise that vibrates in the teeth and behind the eyes. To walk here is to be immersed in a sonic fog that makes communication impossible. You cannot hear yourself think over the *scree-scrree-scree* of the living stone.

Yet, despite the abundance of fauna, the density is **0.8/3 — Sparse**. The swarms are everywhere, but they are invisible individually. What you see are empty spaces. Vast stretches of bare, wet mud. Occasional mounds of black glassy obsidian rising from the silt. The "sparse" feeling comes from the lack of distinct landmarks, the monotony of the texture, the way the horizon seems to stretch forever without interruption.

### The Structures: Faint Marks
Scattered across the plain (**Built: 1.3/3 — Scattered**) are remnants of something else. Not ruins of cities, but **Anchors**.

Small, crude structures made of stacked, smooth river-stones held together by a black tar-like resin. They are primitive (**Tech: 1.3/3**), likely built by hands that used simple tools—rocks tied to sticks, fire-hardened wood. Each structure is no larger than a hut, often just a partial circle of stones three feet high. Some are intact; others have collapsed into piles.

Inside each Anchor, there is a single object: a large, hollow reed-like tube made of fired clay. These tubes act as **resonance chambers**. They are tuned to dampen the shrieking sound of the Lithic Swarms. If you approach an Anchor, the noise diminishes slightly. The people who lived here, whoever they were, did not flee the sound. They tried to engineer their way out of it. They built pockets of relative quiet in a world that screams.

But the Anchors are few. For every mile of plain, perhaps one remains. The rest are covered in the pale, crystalline fungal growths, reclaiming them back into the geology.

### The Ecology: Mineral Predation
The ecosystem is circular and strange (**Weirdness: 1.4/3**).

1.  **The Water**: Rain falls rarely, but condensation is constant. The water seeps up from deep aquifers, bringing dissolved minerals.
2.  **The Crystals**: In the wet mud, the pale filaments grow. They consume the minerals.
3.  **The Fauna**: The Lithic Swarms feed on the crystals and the mud itself. Their movement grinds the mud finer, releasing more minerals, accelerating crystal growth.
4.  **The Erosion**: The grinding action of the swarm, combined with the chemical weathering of the crystals, breaks down any solid rock into silt. The land is literally dissolving underfoot, but slowly, over geological time.

It is a world where **life is indistinguishable from erosion**. To exist is to grind the world down. To build is to stop the grinding.

### The Atmosphere: Dim and Cool
The light is dim because the sky is perpetually overcast with a heavy, low-hanging cloud layer that traps the cool air near the ground. There is no sun, only a diffuse, gray glow that casts no shadows. The temperature hovers just above freezing—not cold enough to turn the water to ice, but cool enough to keep the evaporation rate low, allowing the surface to remain permanently slick.

The smell is metallic. Iron, copper, ozone. Like licking a battery wrapped in wet wool.

### A Moment in the Plain
Imagine standing at the edge of one of the scattered Anchors.

You are waist-deep in silt that pulls at your boots with every step. The shriek is unbearable now, a physical pressure against your temples. You step inside the ring of stones. The clay tube hums—a low, thrumming vibration that counteracts the high-pitched scream outside. For a moment, there is peace.

Through the gap in the stones, you watch the plain extend outward. Miles of gray, wet emptiness. Occasionally, a bubble bursts on the surface of a pool, releasing a puff of steam. Somewhere in the distance, a mound of black glass shifts. A swarm has disturbed it. The shriek intensifies around that spot, then fades as the particles settle.

There is no path. There is no destination. There is only the scale (**1.8/3 — Open**), the endless expanse of grinding, singing stone, and the faint, human-made whisper of a clay tube trying to hold back the noise of a world that is slowly eating itself.
