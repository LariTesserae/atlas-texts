The world is not a planet. It is a tank.

It is a spherical containment vessel of planetary scale, suspended in the void of space, but its interior is filled entirely with water. There is no sky here, only the endless, crushing blue-black depth that stretches for thousands of kilometers in every direction. The "surface" is merely a theoretical concept, a membrane of pressurized gas bubbles trapped at the top of this global ocean, far above where any light can reach with clarity. But you are not there. You are below.

You are standing on the floor. Or rather, you are floating just above it.

The floor is not rock. Rock has been consumed, dissolved, or replaced. The entire substrate of this world is constructed. It is a seamless expanse of industrial composite, a dark, matte material that feels like ceramic fused with steel, stretching to the horizon and beyond. This is the **Total Build**. There is no natural soil, no bedrock, no sediment layers. Everything from the walls to the ground was manufactured, assembled, and welded into place by hands long gone or minds long forgotten. The geometry is vast, sweeping in gentle curves that mimic the curvature of a sphere, but every line is deliberate. Every angle is calculated.

Because the elevation is zero relative to the surface, and the density is near-void, the structure is hollow inside its own skin. The walls are thin, transparent to certain frequencies of energy, and they hold back nothing but their own mass. Inside, the volume is mostly empty space occupied by water and silence—until the machines wake up.

### The Atmosphere of Water

The water is 2.8/3 on the submersion scale. It is not just present; it is *total*. The medium through which you move is viscous, cool (0.9/3), and clear enough to see for miles, yet distorted by the pressure. The temperature is constant, hovering just above freezing, kept stable by the massive thermal regulators embedded in the walls. This cold is not biting; it is numbing. It slows movement. It makes thoughts feel heavy.

There is no wind. There is no rain falling from clouds. Instead, there are currents. These are not tidal forces driven by moons, but engineered flows. Great rivers within the ocean, channeled by invisible fields, sweep through the mid-water columns, carrying nutrients, waste, and heat from one sector to another. These currents are loud. They roar like freight trains when they pass close to your hull or suit, vibrating through the bones of anyone foolish enough to be outside without dampening gear.

### The Young Erosion

Despite the age of the construction itself—which may be millennia—the features emerging from the floor are young. The erosion parameter (1.0/3) suggests sharpness, freshness, recent activity. New structures push up from the built environment daily. These are not geological formations but architectural blooms. Spire-like vents, jagged cooling fins, and needle-thin sensor arrays pierce upward from the floor, still warm from their fabrication. They have no moss, no coral, no bio-fouling yet. They are raw, metallic, and gleaming under the artificial lights.

These new growths are part of an ongoing cycle. Old sections are dismantled, melted down, and re-extruded as new components elsewhere. The landscape is in a state of perpetual, slow-motion renewal. A city-block-sized slab might detach and sink into a processing trench, while a kilometer-high lattice rises in its place, fully formed in hours, cooled in minutes, and submerged immediately.

### The Light

Light is normal (2.4/3). Not sunlight—there is no sun—but the illumination is sufficient, steady, and shadowed. The walls glow with bioluminescent panels or LED arrays, providing a consistent, flat brightness that casts no dramatic shadows. It is office-lighting stretched over a continent. It reveals everything and hides nothing. The lack of natural twilight creates a sense of eternal noon, or eternal night depending on the sector’s calibration, but always with visibility. Colors are true. Red rust looks red. Blue coolant looks blue.

### The Ecology of the Made

Vegetation is sparse (1.1/3). There are no forests. No grasses. No trees. What little plant life exists is cultivated in high-density vertical farms integrated into the side-walls of the major transport corridors. These are small patches of green, yellow, and purple, enclosed in glass tubes, fed by hydroponic streams harvested from the main body of water. They are precious, isolated, and fragile against the vast emptiness of the rest of the world.

Fauna, however, is abundant (1.5/3). But what kind of animals live in a made-world?

They are not evolved creatures. They are *assembled* organisms. Bio-mechanical hybrids. Fish with titanium skeletons and gills made of synthetic membranes. Whales whose bodies are half-submarine, half-living tissue, used for long-range communication and sonar mapping. Crabs the size of houses, their shells forged from impact-resistant alloys, scuttling across the floor, hunting smaller drones. Jellyfish with neural nets for brains, drifting in the upper waters, filtering particulates.

These creatures do not compete with humans; they are part of the infrastructure. They are the immune system of the machine-world. When a pipe leaks, the repair-swarm arrives. When a predator threatens a farm, the defense-shoal moves in. They are loud, aggressive, and numerous. Schools of metallic tuna blast past in coordinated waves, their engines humming beneath their scales. Leviathans groan as they shift course, their propellers spinning slowly in the thick water.

### The Sound

Sound is loud (2.2/3). In water, sound travels faster and farther than in air. This world is a cacophony. The hum of the thermal regulators vibrates through the floor. The grinding of metal-on-metal as new spires lock into place echoes like thunderclaps. The roar of the currents is a constant background drone. And the fauna add to it: the shriek of sonic pulses from hunter-drones, the low-frequency rumble of moving leviathans, the click-click-click of crustacean joints working against the pressure.

Silence is impossible. Even in the deepest trench, if you stop your engines, you hear the world breathing.

### The Rules of This World

1.  **Nothing is Free:** Every object, every liter of water, every calorie of food is produced, transported, and managed by the system. If it is not assigned a function, it is deleted.
2.  **Movement is Fluid:** You do not walk; you glide. Gravity is weak, masked by buoyancy. To go forward, you must push against the water. To stop, you must create drag. Momentum is king.
3.  **Isolation is Danger:** The density is low (0.4/3). Vast spaces between presences mean that if you drift away from a hub, you are alone. No rescue will come unless you signal. The empty dark is indifferent.
4.  **Construction is Life:** The boundary between biological and mechanical is blurred. Your house is alive because it grows. Your car is dead until it breathes coolant. To touch the wall is to touch something that was recently born and will soon die.

### The View

Imagine yourself in a small, pressurized pod, hovering ten meters above the floor. Below you, the black composite ground stretches out, dotted with fresh silver spires that catch the ambient light. Around you, schools of chrome-scaled fish dart in synchronized patterns, creating swirling vortices of shimmer. Above, the water fades into darkness, punctuated by distant glows of other habitats, miles away, connected by cables thicker than skyscrapers.

A sudden tremor runs through the water. Somewhere, a section of the floor is being recycled. Dust—metallic dust—clouds the water nearby. A giant crab, its shell etched with maintenance codes, skitters toward the disturbance, mandibles clicking. It ignores you. You are too small, too temporary.

This is not a wilderness. It is a factory floor that never closes, submerged in an ocean that never dries, inhabited by animals that were designed to survive it. It is cool, it is bright, it is deafeningly loud, and it is completely, utterly artificial. Yet, in its own way, it is alive.
