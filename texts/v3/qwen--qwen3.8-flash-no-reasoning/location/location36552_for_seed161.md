The place is called **Ossuary Peak**, though no one has named it in three thousand years.

It sits at the roof of the world, a jagged, flattened scar upon the highest ridge of a mountain range so old that its bones have fused into a single, continuous plateau. The elevation is absolute; here, the air is thin enough to taste copper, and the sky is not blue but a bruised violet, pressing down with a weight that feels physical. There is no water. Not a drop. No ice, no vapor, no condensation. The concept of moisture has been erased from the local physics. What exists instead is **static charge**. The atmosphere here is saturated with dry electricity, a low-humming potential that makes hair stand on end and causes small sparks to jump between fingertips when touched.

Because there is no water, life does not grow from soil. It grows from **stone** and **sound**.

### The Terrain: Primordial Flatness
Erosion here is ancient, primordial. The mountains were once sharp peaks, tearing the clouds apart, but eons of wind and thermal expansion have ground them down. Yet, they have not disappeared. Instead, they have become *dense*. The terrain is a vast, undulating field of compressed rock, smoothed to a texture like poured concrete, colored in deep ochres, rust-reds, and charcoal grays. There are no loose grains of sand; the surface is solidified, vitrified by centuries of lightning strikes and heat cycles. It is hard, unyielding, and utterly silent underfoot until you step onto a resonance plate.

### The Vegetation: Lush without Life
Vegetation rating: 1.5/3 (Lush). But this is not green. This is not leafy. In the absence of water, plant-like structures have evolved into **Crystalline Filaments**. These are translucent, amber-colored spikes that erupt from the rock in dense clusters, reaching up to two meters high. They are "lush" in the sense that they occupy every available cubic inch of space, layering over each other, forming thickets that resemble glass coral reefs. They do not photosynthesize sunlight; they absorb the ambient static charge of the atmosphere. When the light dims—which it always does here, as the sun is filtered through permanent high-altitude dust—the filaments glow with a faint, internal bioluminescence, pulsing slowly like heartbeats.

They are brittle. If you break one, it shatters into fine, needle-like shards that carry an electrical sting.

### The Scale and Density: A Cathedral of Noise
Scale: 0.9/3 (Small). The entire inhabited area is bounded by the natural amphitheater of the peak’s rim. It is roughly the size of a city block, perhaps 200 meters across. But within this small space, density is extreme. The Crystalline Filaments press against the structures of the settlement, leaving almost no empty air. Every surface is covered. To walk here is to move through a forest of glass and stone, where your shoulders brush against glowing tendrils, and the ceiling is a canopy of interlocking mineral lattices.

Built: 1.8/3 (Urban). The inhabitants, the **Keth’ari**, have built their homes not *on* the rock, but *into* the acoustic landscape. Their architecture is made of hollowed-out basalt drums, stacked vertically and horizontally. These are not buildings for shelter from weather—there is no rain, only cold and thin air—but for **resonance**. Each house is a tuning fork. The walls are curved inward to focus sound. The streets are paved with piezoelectric tiles that generate a spark when stepped on, lighting the dim corridors with sudden, brief flashes of white-blue fire.

### Tech: Primitive Craft
Tech: 1.4/3 (Primitive). The Keth’ari use hand-tools made of obsidian and bone. They do not forge metal; they cannot. The temperature is too unstable, and there is no fuel for smelting. Instead, they craft using vibration. They shape stone by singing to it. Using large, handheld resonators carved from mammoth tusk (fossilized, since there are no living mammals), they hum specific frequencies that cause micro-fractures in the rock, allowing them to split basalt slabs with precision. Their technology is entirely analog, tactile, and auditory. They do not write; they record history in complex knot-patterns woven from the shed fibers of dead crystalline plants.

### Light and Atmosphere: Dim and Electric
Light: 0.6/3 (Dim). The sun never fully breaks through the upper atmospheric haze. It appears as a diffuse, pale disc, casting no shadows, only gradients. The primary source of illumination is the **Glow-Flora**. As the day wanes into twilight, the crystalline thickets brighten, shifting from dull amber to a vibrant, sickly teal. The air itself seems to shimmer with charged particles. Vision is limited; colors bleed into each other. Depth perception is unreliable due to the lack of atmospheric scattering contrast. You see things because they glow, not because they reflect.

### Fauna: Abundant and Auditory
Fauna: 2.3/3 (Abundant). There are no birds, no insects with wings (the air is too thin for flight dynamics). Instead, the fauna consists of **Chitin-Walkers** and **Echo-Burrowers**.
*   **Chitin-Walkers**: Armored, six-legged creatures the size of dogs, covered in overlapping plates that click together when they move. They graze on the lower portions of the crystalline filaments, grinding them down with rasping tongues. Their shells are highly reflective, making them appear as moving mirrors in the dim light.
*   **Echo-Burrowers**: Blind, worm-like beings that live beneath the flat rock surface. They navigate entirely by sound. They emit constant, high-frequency clicks that map the subsurface structure. When they burst from the ground to feed on fallen crystal shards, they scream—a short, piercing shriek that alerts the Keth’ari hunters.

There are thousands of them. The ground vibrates constantly with their movement. The air is filled with the clicking of chitin and the thrumming of burrowing.

### Sound: Loud and Structured
Sound: 1.7/3 (Loud). This is the defining characteristic of Ossuary Peak. It is never quiet. The wind, though dry, howls through the gaps in the crystal forests, creating a natural flute-song that shifts in pitch depending on the temperature. The Keth’ari houses amplify these winds into harmonies. When you enter the settlement, you are greeted by a roar—a wall of sound generated by the interaction of wind, stone, and human activity. Conversations must be shouted or conducted through hand-signals accompanied by rhythmic stomping. Silence is dangerous here; silence means a storm is building pressure in the electrostatic field.

### Dynamics: Slow Cycles
Dynamic: 0.8/3 (Slow). Nothing changes quickly. The crystals grow millimeters per year. The erosion happens over millennia. The Keth’ari age slowly, their skin taking on a grayish, leathery texture to resist the dry abrasion. The day-night cycle is slow, stretched out by the high altitude. A single "day" lasts thirty hours. The shift from dim amber to bright teal takes four hours. People move with deliberate care, conserving energy, speaking rarely, listening often. Time feels thick, viscous.

### Rules of the World
1.  **Dryness is Sacred**: Water is poison. A drop of moisture causes rapid corrosion of the crystalline flora and disrupts the electrostatic balance. Rain would destroy the ecosystem. Therefore, the Keth’ari wear sealed suits made of waxed hide when venturing beyond the settlement’s core, to prevent even sweat from contaminating the external environment.
2.  **Sound is Currency**: Resources are not traded by weight or volume, but by frequency purity. A tool is valuable if it rings true. A song is valuable if it calms the wind. Debt is paid in performances of harmonic stability.
3.  **Touch is Dangerous**: Because everything is charged, touching another person directly can cause painful arcs of electricity. Intimacy is conducted through intermediaries—long sticks, fabric layers, or shared resonance chambers where bodies vibrate near each other but do not contact.

### Emerging Image: The Evening Chime
As the sun dips below the horizon, the valley darkens. The violet sky deepens to black. The crystalline forests begin to pulse brighter, a sea of teal light rising from the stone floor. The wind picks up, funneled through the narrow passes of the mountain. The hollow basalt houses begin to sing—a deep, cello-like groan that vibrates in the chest.

A child steps out onto a piezoelectric tile. *Zap.* A flash of white light illuminates her face for a fraction of a second. She holds a tuning fork carved from bone. She strikes it against the side of her house. *Ping.* The note hangs in the air, joining the chorus of thousands of others. Above her, a Chitin-Walker freezes, its mirror-shell reflecting the teal glow, before skittering away into the denser thickets.

The place is alive, not with breath, but with vibration. It is a symphony played on a dry, electric string, echoing endlessly across the top of the world.
