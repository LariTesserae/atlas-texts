It begins with the sound. Not a roar, not a crash, but a frequency so high and so dense that it occupies every cubic centimeter of the air, vibrating in the marrow before the ear can even register it as noise. It is the sound of water screaming under pressure, a deafening, white-hot static that drowns out thought, memory, and breath.

You are not underwater. You are *in* the rain. But this is no ordinary precipitation. The water level here is 2.8/3—submerged, all-liquid—but at an elevation of 2.5/3, meaning you are suspended in a column of falling ocean, held aloft by some impossible atmospheric tension. You are inside a vertical river that falls forever from a sky that does not exist, rising from a ground that has not yet formed.

The scale is hidden (0.3/3). This is not a landscape you walk across; it is a pocket, a blister on the skin of reality, roughly twenty meters wide and infinite in height. A micro-environment contained within a void of turbulent chaos. Outside the membrane of this sphere, there is nothing but the dark, churning energy of creation, but here, inside, the rules are locally consistent, though barely.

**The Terrain: Embryonic Geology**

The erosion parameter is 0.4/3—emerging, raw geology. There are no cliffs, no valleys, no established stones. Instead, the "ground" beneath your feet—if you can call it that—is a slurry of semi-solid mist and crystallizing vapor. It feels like walking on wet ash that hasn’t learned to be rock yet.

Every few seconds, a pillar of calcium-rich sludge shoots up from the floor, solidifies into a jagged, translucent spire for ten seconds, then dissolves back into liquid under the weight of the crushing sound-pressure. The terrain is alive, breathing, constructing itself and tearing itself down in a rhythmic, violent cycle. It is pre-formation. You are standing on the blueprint of a mountain range, drawn in water and erased by wind.

**Vegetation: Sparse and Skeletal**

At 1.3/3 vegetation, life here is minimal, desperate, and strange. There are no trees, no grasses. Instead, clinging to the fleeting stone pillars are tufts of black lichen-like moss that pulse with a faint bioluminescence. They are not green; they are the color of bruised plums. They feed not on sunlight, but on the kinetic vibration of the deafening rain. When the sound peaks, the moss contracts; when the sound dips for a fraction of a second, it expands, exhaling tiny bubbles of oxygen that rise instantly into the torrent.

There are also "glass-ferns"—fragile structures made entirely of silica precipitated from the water, grown over decades in these pockets. They shatter easily, their shards floating in the current like silver dust. They are the only permanent things here, and even they are slowly being worn away by the turbulence.

**Built Structures: Scattered Artifacts**

Density is 0.3/3—empty. The space between objects is vast, terrifyingly hollow. Yet, scattered throughout this column of falling sea are remnants of something else. At 1.4/3 built, there are small, inexplicable structures.

A wooden chair, barnacled and bleached white, sits upright on a patch of emerging mud. A child’s shoe, made of a material that resembles woven kelp, floats near a shard of glass. A section of copper piping, green with verdigris, protrudes from a dissolving stone wall, humming with a low resonance that cuts through the higher pitch of the rain.

These artifacts do not fit the environment. They were dropped here. Or perhaps, they are memories of a world that was flooded and lifted, trapped in this suspension chamber. They are primitive (tech 0.6/3)—hand-crafted, simple, old-world things. A hammer rests on a ledge that vanishes three seconds later. A clay pot, cracked, hangs suspended in mid-air, held up by a vortex of water, rotating slowly.

**Fauna: Rare and Ghostly**

At 1.3/3 fauna, you rarely see anything move. But if you look closely, into the gaps between the roaring sheets of water, you catch glimpses. Translucent, eel-like creatures with too many eyes dart through the mist, feeding on the microscopic algae in the air. They make no sound; their mouths are sealed shut. They are evolved for silence in a place of deafness.

Occasionally, a larger shape passes—a whale-sized shadow that moves against the flow of the rain, swimming upward through the downward waterfall. It is rare, seen once in an hour, and always at the periphery of vision. It leaves no wake, no ripple. Just a displacement of light.

**Atmosphere and Light: Normal Light, Strange Shadows**

Light is 2.5/3—normal, bright, daylight. But because the medium is constantly shifting, the light behaves erratically. Sunbeams (or whatever source provides this illumination) pierce the upper canopy of the pocket, striking the falling water and fracturing into thousands of rainbow shards that dance on the emerging rocks.

However, the shadows are wrong. They are too sharp, too deep. Objects cast shadows that point toward the source of the sound, not the source of the light. This creates a disorienting, dreamlike quality. The cool temperature (1.2/3) bites at exposed skin, a chill that comes from the evaporative cooling of the endless rain. You are always damp, never soaked, because the water is moving so fast it strips moisture away as quickly as it lands.

**Weirdness: The Rules of the World**

The weirdness factor (2.2/3) manifests in the physics of time and causality. Because the environment is embryonic (erosion 0.4), cause and effect are loose. If you drop a stone, it doesn't fall straight down. It spirals, slows, accelerates, or sometimes hovers, caught in eddies of the sonic pressure.

More strangely, the "built" artifacts seem to age rapidly and then reset. The wooden chair rots in minutes, turning to mulch, which then gets sucked into a vortex and reappears as a fresh plank of wood elsewhere. The system is recycling itself, trying to build a stable world but failing, stuck in a loop of creation and destruction.

The sound is the key. It is not just noise; it is the engine of this pocket. The deafening roar (sound 2.8/3) is the energy source. It agitates the water, keeps the particles suspended, powers the growth of the moss, and holds the structure together. If the sound stopped, the entire pocket would collapse into a single, heavy splash of ocean water crashing onto a nonexistent floor.

**Ecology and Dynamics: Turbulent Balance**

The dynamic state is turbulent (2.7/3). Chaos is the norm. Every second, the arrangement of the world changes. A rock becomes a tree becomes a cloud becomes a raindrop. There is no stillness. Even the air vibrates.

Yet, within this chaos, there is an ecology. The moss grows where the sound resonates most strongly. The glass-ferns grow where the water is clearest. The artifacts gather in the quiet spots—micro-eddies where the sound pressure drops slightly, creating pockets of relative calm. These calm zones are dangerous, because the lack of vibration causes the emerging geology to solidify too fast, trapping things in brittle, unstable cages of stone and ice.

To survive here, one must move with the turbulence. To stand still is to be eroded away or crushed by the sudden formation of a new stone pillar. One must ride the wave, follow the current of the falling ocean, touch the artifacts only briefly before they dissolve or fly apart, and listen—not with ears, but with bones—to the rhythm of the creation-scream.

This is a place of unfinished business. A flood that forgot to drain, a storm that forgot to end, a construction site abandoned by gods who died of deafness. It is beautiful in its violence, terrible in its clarity, and utterly, profoundly alone.
