It sits low to the ground, barely a dip in the earth’s skin, perhaps no larger than a modest cathedral nave if you were to draw walls around it with your mind. But there are no walls here. Only the sky above, heavy and gray, pressing down on a landscape that has forgotten how to change.

This is the **Sump of Stasis**.

It is ancient beyond reckoning. The bedrock beneath the soil has been worn so thin, so perfectly flat, so utterly settled into its final form, that it feels less like geology and more like memory. There are no jagged peaks, no fresh cuts from recent landslides, no chaotic boulder fields. Just smoothness. A profound, unsettling smoothness that extends in every direction until the eye gives up and finds only a uniform line where the damp earth meets the air.

The water is everywhere and nowhere, a sheen over everything, making the surfaces slick and reflective, turning the world into a blurred mirror of itself.

And then there is the green.
