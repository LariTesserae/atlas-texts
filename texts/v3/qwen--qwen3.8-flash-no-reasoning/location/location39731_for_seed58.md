The heat is not a weather event here; it is the architecture. It presses against the skin with the weight of wet wool, thick and suffocating, rising from below rather than falling from above. We are in the **Under-Vat**, a subterranean industrial settlement carved into the bedrock of a dormant volcano’s flank, where the earth’s own fever has been harnessed, channeled, and sold.

This is a place of **dense, low-ceilinged intimacy**. The scale is claustrophobic but organized—a hamlet-sized labyrinth of tunnels, shafts, and hollowed-out geodes, bounded by walls of slick basalt and reinforced iron grating. There is no sky. There is only the ceiling, three meters overhead, sweating.

### The Atmosphere and Water
Water exists here as **condensation made manifest**. With a moisture level of 0.6, the air is perpetually on the verge of rain, but it never quite falls as droplets large enough to wash anything clean. Instead, it clings. Every surface—the rusted pipes, the polished stone floors, the faces of the workers—is coated in a fine, lukewarm mist that tastes of sulfur and copper. Pools form in depressions, stagnant and oily, reflecting the dim light in rippling distortions. These are the "veins" of the Under-Vat, feeding the steam engines that keep the settlement alive.

### Light and Vision
Light is scarce (1.3/3), provided only by flickering sodium-vapor lamps housed in cracked glass domes bolted to the tunnel arches. They cast a sickly, orange-yellow hue that renders shadows deep and purple. The darkness between the lights is absolute, swallowing corners and doorways. This creates a visual rhythm: a bright pool of visibility, then a wall of black, then another pool. To move through the Under-Vat is to navigate by these islands of illumination.

### Built Environment and Tech
The construction is **industrial-organic hybridization** (built 2.4, tech 1.6). The city did not build *on* the rock; it built *inside* it. Tunnels were bored using early-stage pneumatic drills (erosion 0.6, young features), leaving behind sharp, unpolished edges of stone that have not yet been worn smooth by foot traffic or wind. The infrastructure is layered: primary conduits for geothermal fluid run along the ceiling, insulated with asbestos-like white fibers. Secondary pipes for steam distribution snake along the walls. Floor-level cables carry power to the few electric lifts that haul goods between levels.

Buildings are not separate structures but **carved rooms** connected by narrow bridges of riveted steel plate. Dwellings are stacked like honeycombs—small, circular chambers dug directly into the volcanic tuff, sealed with heavy iron doors. The density (2.2/3) is high; bodies press close, machinery hums inches from sleeping quarters, and the air circulation relies on massive, rattling fans that push the humid heat through filtered vents.

### Vegetation and Ecology
There is no animal life (fauna 0.1/3). The silence of the beasts is replaced by the mechanical drone of pumps and the hiss of escaping steam. But there is vegetation, sparse and strange (veg 1.0/3). In the crevices where condensation pools long enough, **fungal mats** grow—pale, translucent sheets that glow faintly blue in the dark. Near the hottest vents, **thermophilic mosses** cling to the metal pipes, turning them velvet-green. These are not decorative; they are harvested. The moss is dried and used as insulation for the engine cores; the fungal slime is scraped off to make a sticky adhesive for sealing leaks. The ecosystem is entirely parasitic upon the industry.

### Sound and Dynamics
The soundscape is **mostly quiet but actively textured** (sound 1.4, dynamic 2.2). There are no birds, no insects, no footsteps on gravel. What you hear is the *pulse* of the place:
*   A low, rhythmic thumping from the main turbine hall two levels down.
*   The intermittent *drip-drip-drip* of condensation hitting a metal tray.
*   The occasional sharp *clank* of a pipe expanding under thermal stress.
*   The soft whir of ventilation fans, barely audible over the dampness.

It is an active environment because the heat changes constantly. Shifts in pressure cause bursts of steam to vent suddenly, flooding corridors with white fog for minutes before clearing. The temperature fluctuates within small ranges, causing materials to expand and contract, making the very walls creak and groan. The world here breathes mechanically.

### Weirdness and Rules of the World
The weirdness (0.7/3) lies in the **inversion of natural order**. Normally, heat rises. Here, heat is trapped, forced downward, and recycled. The water does not flow downhill to a sea; it flows uphill through pipes, driven by pumps, only to evaporate, cool on the ceilings, and drip back down. It is a closed loop, a self-licking ouroboros of steam and stone.

The rules of the Under-Vat are simple:
1.  **Keep dry.** Moisture rots cloth and short-circuits wiring.
2.  **Respect the veins.** Never block a condensation channel; if it backs up, it will burst a seam and flood a room with scalding water.
3.  **Listen to the rock.** If the stone stops creaking, the pressure has equalized—and that means the next release will be violent.

### Emerging Detail
As you stand in one of the residential niches, looking out at the central atrium—a hollowed-out geode cavity filled with suspended catwalks—you notice something new. The erosion parameters suggested "young, fresh features." Indeed, in the corner of the atrium, a section of the floor has recently buckled. Not from age, but from a **fresh fissure** opened yesterday when a magma pocket shifted beneath the foundation.

From this crack, a jet of pure, superheated vapor escapes, silent and invisible until it hits the cold air, creating a permanent, swirling cloud of mist right in the center of town. People do not flee. They gather around it. Children reach out to touch the edge of the cloud, their fingers coming away steaming. The engineers have cordoned off a five-meter radius with yellow tape, but the crowd presses against it, drawn by the raw, primal energy leaking from the planet’s gut. This is not just a utility; it is a spectacle. The place is alive, and it is bleeding light and heat into the dark.
