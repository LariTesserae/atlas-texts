The world is not a place you walk upon; it is a volume you are suspended within.

Imagine a subterranean cavity so vast that its ceiling is lost in the glare, and its floor is indistinguishable from the horizon. This is the **Cathedral of Green Glass**, located deep below the surface of a planet that has forgotten how to hold heat. The elevation parameter places us far beneath any crustal plate, yet the pressure does not crush. Instead, the air here is thick with spores and vapor, heavy enough to support the weight of the architecture but thin enough to let light pass through with terrifying purity.

The temperature is a biting, absolute zero that exists only in the shadows between the structures. But because the light intensity is near-maximal (2.9/3), there is no darkness. There is only *white*. The light is cold, hard, and relentless. It comes from everywhere: bioluminescent veins pulsing within the plant-matter, refraction through crystalline sap, and an ambient glow that seems to emanate from the empty spaces themselves. You cannot close your eyes to rest, because the eyelids glow when pressed against them.

### The Substrate: Plant as Architecture

Vegetation here is not soil-cover; it is the geology. The "ground" is a tangled root-system of petrified wood and living mycelium that spans kilometers. Above this, rising like stalagmites made of woven vines and hardened resin, are the **Spires**. These are not trees in any traditional sense. They are hollow, tubular structures formed by the aggressive, rapid growth of a single, dominant species—a moss-like organism that secretes silica and calcium carbonate as it expands.

Because the erosion level is young (1.1/3), these structures are jagged, fresh, and sharp. They look as if they were grown yesterday. The edges are razor-thin. The surfaces are slick with condensation, reflecting the blinding white light in fragmented shards. There is no weathering, no smoothing by wind or rain over millennia. The plants are still actively tearing into the void, their tendrils lashing out in slow-motion bursts, creating new arches and buttresses that collapse almost immediately due to their own fragility.

### The Atmosphere: Turbulent Stillness

The dynamic parameter (2.6/3) suggests chaos, but this is not a storm of wind and rain. It is a **storm of growth**. The turbulence is internal to the ecosystem. Vines whip outward at high speeds, snapping like cables. Sap explodes from severed stems in pressurized jets. The air itself churns with thermal convection currents caused by the contrast between the cold stone-below and the heat generated by metabolic processes above.

Yet, the sound is mostly quiet (1.0/3). Why? Because the density is low (0.5/3). The space between the Spires is vast, empty, and hollow. Sound waves dissipate before they can travel far. A vine cracking fifty meters away sounds like a distant thunderclap, muffled and soft. A person shouting ten meters away would be heard clearly, but anything beyond that is swallowed by the sheer volume of empty, damp air.

The water parameter (0.9/3) manifests as **condensation cycles**. There is no river, no lake. Instead, the humidity is so high that every surface is coated in a film of ice-water. Periodically, the temperature drops slightly, and the moisture freezes into delicate, fern-like frost patterns on the green glass. Then, the bright light causes localized heating, and the frost sublimates directly into vapor, rising in shimmering columns. This creates a perpetual mist that dances around the base of the Spires, glowing white under the illumination.

### The Ecology: Rare Fauna in a Bright Void

Life is sparse here. The fauna parameter (1.4/3) indicates rarity, but not absence. What creatures exist are adapted to the light and the cold. They are translucent, almost invisible, moving through the mist like ghosts.

One such creature is the **Glass-Moth**. It has no scales; its wings are membranes of chitin so thin they refract light rather than reflect it. It feeds on the exudate of the Spires, landing on the wet, cold bark to drink the sugary sap. When it flies, it makes no sound. It is seen only as a distortion in the air, a ripple in the white glare.

There are also the **Root-Walkers**, six-legged arthropods the size of dogs, armored in living bark. They move slowly across the subterranean floor, navigating the labyrinth of roots. They are blind, sensing the vibrations of the turbulent growth above them. They do not hunt; they scavenge the fallen debris—the snapped tendrils, the collapsed arches—that the plants shed in their chaotic expansion.

### The Built Environment: Scattered Remnants

The built parameter (0.9/3) reveals that humans—or whoever—once lived here. But the civilization was small, scattered, and primitive (1.1/3).

You find them occasionally: a hut woven from dead vines, now half-swallowed by the living moss. A stone circle, arranged precisely, but now tilted by the shifting earth-roots. Tools made of bone and flint, left on ledges, covered in a layer of glittering frost. These are not ruins in the traditional sense; they are fossils of intent. The structures are too few to form a village, too isolated to suggest a network. They feel like waystations for travelers who came down here to die, or to hide.

One common artifact is the **Lens-Pillar**: a crude cylinder of polished quartz, driven into the ground. These seem to have been used to focus the intense light, perhaps to start fires (though fire is dangerous here, where everything is damp and volatile) or to signal across the vast emptiness. Many are cracked, split by the freezing and thawing cycles. Others remain perfect, catching the white light and throwing sharp beams across the mist, cutting through the gloom with surgical precision.

### The Rules of the World

1.  **Light is Danger:** The brightness is not warm. It is a laser-like intensity that bleaches color and dries out any exposed skin. To survive, one must stay in the shadows of the Spires, which are scarce and constantly shifting as the plants grow and collapse.
2.  **Growth is Violence:** The vegetation is not peaceful. It is an explosive, cellular frenzy. Standing under a Spire is dangerous; it may shed its outer layers, dropping shards of silica-laced bark without warning.
3.  **Sound Does Not Carry:** You can speak loudly, but no one will hear you beyond a few meters. Communication is visual: hand signals, flash-lights, the positioning of bodies in the open spaces.
4.  **Cold Burns:** The temperature is low enough to cause immediate tissue damage if unprotected. The condensation freezes instantly on contact with warmer objects. If you touch a metal tool with bare hands, your skin sticks to it. If you exhale, your breath crystallizes in front of your face.

### A Moment in the Cathedral

Stand in the center of one of the large hollows. The floor is a mosaic of dark roots and white ice. Around you, twenty meters above, a massive Vine-Arch twists slowly, its fibers groaning with the strain of growth. It is bright enough to hurt your eyes, yet the scene is monochromatic: shades of green, black, and white.

A drop of condensation falls from the Arch. It hits a shard of broken quartz on the ground. *Tink.* The sound is small, sharp, and lonely. It echoes once, then vanishes into the vast, empty density of the air.

Somewhere in the distance, a Root-Walker shifts its weight, grinding stone against root. The vibration travels up through the floor, a subtle tremor in the cold. The Light-Pillars flicker as the mist swirls around them, casting long, dancing shadows that look like reaching hands.

This is a world of beautiful, silent destruction. A cathedral built by a plant that hates silence, trying to fill the void with noise and structure, while the void itself remains vast, empty, and indifferent. You are a guest in a body that is feverishly dreaming itself into existence, surrounded by the wreckage of those who tried to map the dream.
