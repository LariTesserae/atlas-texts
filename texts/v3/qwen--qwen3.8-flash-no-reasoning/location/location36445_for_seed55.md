The world is not a planet in the way we understand planets. It is a sphere of infinite, cold black water, sealed within a shell of ancient, eroded stone that has been worn down to its absolute limit by eons of pressure and current. There is no sky, only the weight of the ocean above, pressing down with a force that defines existence here. The temperature hovers just above freezing, a constant, numbing chill that seeps into the marrow of anything that dares to exist in this place.

This is the **Abyssal Carapace**.

### The Substrate: Made-Environment
There is no natural earth here. Every surface you see, every structure that rises from the dark depths, was *built*. But "built" is too active a word for what remains. This is a civilization’s final resting place, or perhaps its beginning, frozen in time so long ago that the distinction between architecture and geology has vanished.

The terrain is composed of massive, interlocking blocks of a substance that resembles basalt but feels like polished obsidian when touched through the gloves of the few who wander here. These blocks are fitted together with a precision that defies modern engineering, yet they are covered in layers of calcified mineral deposits—ancient erosion turned into armor. The buildings do not stand upright; they sprawl. They have been flattened, compressed, and reshaped by the crushing depth until they form gentle, rolling hills of stone, vast plateaus of constructed matter that stretch for hundreds of miles without breaking.

Because the elevation is low (relative to the sea floor) and the scale is vast, these structures feel less like cities and more like mountain ranges made of dead hands. A single "street" might be ten kilometers wide, paved with tiles the size of houses, all fused together by millennia of sediment and pressure. The built environment is total: there is no patch of bare rock, no unconstructed void. Even the spaces between the structures are filled with intricate lattices of corroded metal frameworks, now soft as sponge, colonized by mineral growths.

### The Water: 3.0/3 — Submerged
Water is not merely present; it is the medium of reality. It is thick, viscous in its darkness, and utterly still except for the slowest currents. Because the light level is near zero (0.2/3), the water appears solid black, swallowing any torch or beam of light within meters. To move through it is to move through ink.

But this water is alive with chemistry. It carries the dissolved remnants of the builders’ technology. Iron, copper, and strange alloys have leached out over thousands of years, tinting the deepest currents with a faint, sickly green luminescence that is barely perceptible to the human eye. The water tastes of rust and salt. It is cold enough to cause instant shock, but the true danger is the silence. The density of the water dampens sound, making every movement muffled, as if listening through a wall of felt.

### Vegetation: 2.4/3 — Lush
In a place with no sunlight and no animal life, "vegetation" takes on a different meaning. Here, lushness is fungal, bacterial, and mineral.

Carpets of pale, ghostly slime mold cover the lower slopes of the built structures. It pulses slowly, expanding and contracting in rhythm with the thermal vents far below. Towers of crystalline kelp-like organisms rise from the crevices in the stone, their fronds wide and translucent, filtering nutrients from the water column. They sway gently, hypnotically, in the slow currents.

The most striking feature is the **Iron-Vine**, a bio-mineral composite that grows along the edges of the ancient roads. It starts as a thread of oxidized metal, then branches out into complex, leafy structures that are hard as wood and flexible as wire. These vines weave through the ruins, binding broken stones together, creating a living mesh that holds the crumbling city together. In the darkness, they emit a faint, bioluminescent blue glow, providing the only source of light besides the occasional spark from striking tools. The effect is breathtaking: a forest of glowing, metallic flora draped over the bones of a drowned metropolis.

### Fauna: 0.4/3 — No Animal Life
There are no fish, no whales, no crabs. The absence of animal life makes the silence heavier. There is no predation, no hunting, no flocking. The ecosystem is autotrophic, relying entirely on chemical energy and mineral decay. This gives the landscape an eerie peace. You will never hear a splash other than your own. You will never see eyes watching you. The only moving things are the slow drift of particles and the gentle sway of the Iron-Vines.

### Sound: 0.8/3 — Mostly Quiet
Sound travels poorly here, but when it does, it carries strangely. The primary sound is the **Hum**. It is a low-frequency vibration generated by the pressure of the water against the dense, layered structures of the city. It resonates in the chest, a constant, sub-audible drone that suggests the world itself is breathing.

Occasionally, a stone shifts. With erosion at 2.8/3, the structures are unstable in their stability. A tile may loosen, slide, and fall, creating a dull *thud* that echoes across kilometers of flat stone. Or a bubble of trapped gas escapes from a fissure, rising with a soft, wet gurgle that sounds almost like speech. These sounds are rare, isolated, and haunting in their brevity.

### Dynamic: 0.6/3 — Slow
Time moves differently in the Abyssal Carapace. Changes occur over centuries, not days. A vine may grow one meter in a year. A current may shift direction once a decade. The builders, if they were humans or something akin to them, used simple tools—hand-forged chisels, ropes, pulleys—to construct this place before the flood. Now, those tools are buried under inches of silt.

The processes here are patient. Erosion does not carve jagged cliffs; it smooths surfaces into gentle curves. Sediment does not pile up rapidly; it settles in thin, even layers, like dust on a forgotten shelf. The world is settling into its final form, a state of equilibrium where nothing changes fast enough to notice, but everything changes eventually.

### Tech: 0.8/3 — Primitive
The technology visible here is primitive, but monumental. There are no machines, no engines, no electricity. What remains are tools of leverage and craft.

Massive stone gears, each thirty feet in diameter, lie half-buried in the sludge, their teeth worn smooth. They were part of some ancient pump or lift system, now useless, repurposed by nature as pedestals for iron-vine growth. Hand-chiseled steps lead nowhere, ending abruptly in sheer drops into deeper trenches. Ropes of woven plant fiber, petrified into stone, still hold bundles of bricks together.

The primitive tech suggests that this was not a high-tech utopia, but a labor-intensive project. Thousands of workers must have lived here, carving, lifting, placing. Their ghosts are in the uneven tool marks on the stone, the hastily patched walls, the crude drainage ditches now overflowing with moss.

### Light: 0.2/3 — Darkness
Darkness is the default state. Any light source must be carried. Torchlight burns with a sickly yellow flame, struggling against the oxygen-poor air and the cold. It illuminates only a small circle around the bearer, beyond which lies absolute void. The darkness is not empty; it is full of presence. It presses against the skin. It hides the distance. You cannot see the horizon because there is no horizon—only the curve of the water meeting the top of the frame of vision.

### Ecology & Rules of the World
1. **The Law of Compression:** Nothing stands tall. All structures lean, sink, or flatten. Gravity and water pressure are the architects here, not the original builders.
2. **The Cycle of Rust:** Everything decays toward oxidation. Metal turns to red-brown powder, stone to sand, sand to mud. The color palette is monochromatic grays and blacks, punctuated only by the rust-red of decay and the blue-green of microbial life.
3. **The Silence of Depth:** Sound is the last thing to fade. If you stop moving, you become invisible not because you hide, but because you cease to exist acoustically. The world listens to itself.
4. **The Absence of Predators:** Without animals, there is no fear of being eaten. The only dangers are environmental: hypothermia, drowning, structural collapse, and the disorienting loneliness of the dark.

### The Experience of Being There
To walk here—if walking is possible, given the viscosity of the water—is to walk through a dream that has calcified. You move past walls of smooth, black stone, lined with glowing vines. Above you, the water stretches infinitely upward, a ceiling of liquid night. Below, the ground is layered with the history of a thousand years of sediment.

You might find a room, perfectly preserved except for the moss growing in its corners. Inside, a table carved from a single block of stone, with three bowls sitting on it, still waiting for meals that were served five centuries ago. The bowls are empty. The stone is slick with algae. Outside, the vast, quiet expanse of the drowned city rolls on, untouched, unmoving, eternal.

It is a place of profound beauty and profound emptiness. It is a monument to effort that has been swallowed by time, yet transformed by it into something serene, something that asks nothing of you but witness. The cold bites, the dark watches, and the silence hums.
