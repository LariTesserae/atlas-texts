The air here does not smell of rain, though it tastes of iron and old static. It is a high-altitude place, where the thinness of the atmosphere makes every breath feel like sipping cold water through a narrow straw, yet the moisture clings to everything with unnatural tenacity. This is the **Lithic Expanse**, a region suspended in a state of geological suspension, where the rules of erosion have settled into a permanent, humming equilibrium.

You stand on a ridge of black obsidian glass, smooth as a mirror but fractured by hairline fissures that weep not with water, but with condensation that glows faintly violet. The light is blinding, a harsh, white-bright glare from a sun that seems too close, casting no soft shadows—only sharp, absolute lines between illumination and void. Because there are no plants, no trees, no grasses to break the wind or catch the snow, the landscape is entirely mineral. It is a world of stone, shard, and dust.

But it is not dead stone. It is *listening* stone.

### The Terrain of Broken Physics

At 2.7/3 elevation, you are above the cloud line, above the weather systems that govern the lower world. Here, the clouds do not form; they shatter. They descend from the upper atmosphere as sheets of translucent, icy vapor that hit the jagged peaks and disintegrate into fine mist. This mist never evaporates upward; it falls downward, pooling in the crevices, creating a perpetual, knee-deep fog in the valleys below your vantage point.

The terrain is dense (1.5/3) not in vegetation, but in structure. Every square foot of ground is packed with geometric anomalies. Rocks are not rounded by millennia of gentle weathering; they are faceted, cut at impossible angles. A boulder might be perfectly spherical on one side and a sharp tetrahedron on the other, with no tool marks, no chisel scars. It looks grown, but nothing grows here.

The "vegetation" parameter is zero because life has been replaced by **crystalline mimicry**. Where ferns should be, clusters of silvery needles rise from the rock, vibrating slightly in the wind. Where moss should carpet a stone, layers of translucent film stretch across surfaces, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic bioluminescence—or perhaps chemiluminescence—that reacts to the movement of air. These are not plants. They are mineral formations caught in a feedback loop with the ambient energy field, behaving with the persistence of biology but lacking its warmth.

### The Sound of Loud Silence

The sound level is 1.5/3, loud, but it is a confusing volume. There are no birds, no insects, no rustling leaves. The noise comes from the rocks themselves.

As the wind moves across the high ridges, it does not whistle. It *sings*. The stones have been shaped over eons (erosion: established) into natural resonance chambers. A single gust can cause a cliff face to emit a low, thrumming bass note that vibrates in your chest cavity. When two winds meet in a canyon, they create harmonics—complex, shifting chords that drift up and down the scale.

Because there is no fauna, the soundscape is purely physical, yet it feels populated. You hear footsteps when no one is there—the crack of a thermal expansion joint miles away. You hear voices in the wind, not words, but the cadence of speech, rising and falling in patterns that almost make sense before dissolving into shrieks of escaping gas.

### The Reality Breakdown

This is where the weirdness (2.7/3) takes hold. The laws of causality are loose here, stretched thin by the altitude and the density of exotic matter.

Water behaves strangely. Puddles on the flat obsidian surfaces do not reflect the sky accurately. Look into a still pool, and you may see the sky as it was an hour ago, or as it will be. Condensation forms on warm rocks and drips *upward*, defying gravity for inches before falling back down, creating a localized rain that only wets the underside of overhangs.

Time is also eroded here. The established forms (2.0/3) suggest stability, but the reality is fluid. A mountain peak visible in the distance might vanish if you blink, replaced by a different formation, then return later. The landscape is in a constant, slow-motion flux, rearranging itself according to algorithms we cannot perceive. The "density" of presence means that objects often overlap. You might walk through a wall of solid granite and find yourself on the other side without breaking it, leaving a smear of cold dust on your clothes that smells of ozone.

### Scattered Artifacts of a Primitive Mind

Despite the lack of organic life, there are traces of intelligence. Built structures (1.4/3) are scattered across the expanse, small and isolated. They are not houses. They are **anchors**.

These are stacks of rough-hewn basalt blocks, piled into crude towers no taller than a person. Around them, simple tools lie abandoned: flint knapsacks, bone needles (though no animals exist to provide them), clay pots sealed with resin. The technology is primitive (1.1/3), hand-made, pre-metal. But the materials are wrong. The flint is actually shards of the glowing violet glass. The "bone" needles are crystallized filaments harvested from the silent forests of mineral spikes.

Who made these? No one lives here. The artifacts remain, untouched by decay because decay requires biology. They are preserved in the dry, freezing air. Some piles are arranged in circles, others in spirals. Occasionally, a pile will shift, block by block, rearranging itself into a new configuration while you watch, then freeze again, as if waiting for a specific alignment of stars or a change in the wind’s pitch.

### The Ecology of the Inanimate

The ecosystem (dynamic: 1.6/3) is active, but it is a cycle of phase changes rather than birth and death.

1.  **Condensation:** As the bright light hits the cool surfaces, atmospheric moisture turns to liquid, pooling in the geometric depressions.
2.  **Vibration:** The wind causes the resonant rocks to hum. The frequency builds until it matches the natural oscillation of the water droplets.
3.  **Sublimation/Shattering:** At critical mass, the droplets don’t just evaporate; they explode into micro-crystals, shooting upward into the air like silver spray.
4.  **Reformation:** These crystals fall elsewhere, accumulating into new mineral growths, adding to the "vegetation," which slowly reconfigures the shape of the rocks, changing their resonance frequencies.

It is a self-tuning instrument played by the wind and the temperature differential. The landscape is tuning itself.

### The Experience of Being There

To stand here is to feel small and permeable. The boundary between you and the environment is blurred. Your skin prickles with static electricity that jumps from your hands to the nearest rock. The cold is not biting; it is invasive, seeping into joints, slowing thoughts.

You look out over the valley. Below, the fog thickens, hiding the base of the obsidian spires. Above, the sky is a hard, brilliant blue-white, devoid of clouds because all moisture has been stripped away and condensed onto the earth. Between these extremes lies the Lithic Expanse: a graveyard of geology, a museum of impossible physics, a place where silence screams and stones remember things they were never taught.

There is no exit path marked by footprints. The way forward is determined by listening. Follow the loudest chord. Avoid the discordant notes. Step where the air feels thinner, less charged. If you listen closely to the singing rocks, you might hear a pattern that repeats—a rhythm that matches your own heartbeat. That is the trap. If you start walking to that beat, you will never stop. You will become part of the resonance, another facet in the dense, humming geometry of the world.
