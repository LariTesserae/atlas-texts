The place is called the Hollow Choir.

It is a sealed cathedral of mineral and brass, no larger than a modest hamlet yet so densely packed with structure that it feels infinitely recursive. Every surface is built; there is no untouched substrate, no natural stone or dust that was not first shaped by hand. The entire volume has been carved, cast, riveted, and tuned over what must have been geologic ages until it settled into its final, immutable form.

The air is warm, dry, and thick with the scent of hot iron and ancient resins. There is no water anywhere — not even a memory of moisture. Chemistry here is anhydrous, based on volatile silicates and sulfur compounds that never needed to flow. What little atmosphere exists is a heavy, inert soup of argon and sulfur hexafluoride that carries sound like a struck bell.

Light is dim, almost ecclesiastical. It comes from within the Choir itself: slow phosphorescence leaking from thousands of hairline fractures in amber-colored glass lenses set into the architecture, and from the dull glowing filaments of primitive thermocouples buried in the walls. The glow is the color of old honey and dried blood. Shadows are absolute.

The architecture is claustrophobic and total. Every cubic meter is occupied. Towers, buttresses, choir stalls, organ pipes, and spiraling staircases have been crushed together, folded, and interlocked until the distinction between wall, floor, ceiling, and ornament has vanished. Surfaces are covered in dense, low-relief carvings — not decoration, but functional resonators. Every column is a tuned tube. Every arch is a baffle. The entire structure is one continuous instrument.

And it is deafening.

A single, unchanging chord thunders through the Hollow Choir without pause or variation. It has sounded for so long that the metal and stone have annealed around the vibration. The sound is not music; it is tectonic. It is the note that remains after every other note has died. Bass frequencies so low they are felt in the teeth and sternum. High overtones that arrive like needles through the eardrums. The chord never swells or fades. It simply is — an equilibrium so perfect it has become geologic.

There is no vegetation. No lichen, no fungus, no microbial mat. Life never reached this place, or it was scoured away so thoroughly that even its chemical signature is gone. The only complexity belongs to the built environment.

No animals. No movement. The Choir is locked in perfect dynamical stasis. Nothing has changed here for longer than the concept of “longer” can meaningfully contain. The primitive tool-marks of its anonymous makers — bronze chisels, stone mallets, bone scribes — are still visible on inner surfaces, preserved by the total absence of erosion. This is not merely ancient. It is the oldest stable state a made thing can achieve before it becomes indistinguishable from the crust of a dead world.

Elevation is deceptive. The entire structure sits high inside a larger sealed cavity — perhaps a bubble within an ancient planetary body, perhaps a deliberate vacuum-sealed reliquary floating in some airless gap. Inside the Choir itself, the cramped architecture rises and falls in tight spirals and sudden vertical shafts that feel like climbing the inside of a cathedral that has been crumpled in a giant fist.

Weirdness accumulates in the details.

The proportions are wrong. Doorways are the perfect height for no known body. The carved figures that line the walls have too many joints. Some of the immense pipes bend back into themselves in ways that should collapse under their own weight, yet they have stood since before oxygen was common in any atmosphere. In certain recessed chambers the endless chord cancels itself into pockets of eerie silence so complete that breathing feels like sacrilege.

And always, beneath the deafening sound, there is the sense that the Choir is not merely emitting the chord.

It is listening to itself.

Waiting, in perfect equilibrium, for something that will never come.

This is the final form of a machine built by a species that understood resonance better than it understood time. They tuned an entire worldlet into a single eternal note, sealed it, and walked away — or were absorbed into the structure so completely that no separate bones remain.

Now only the Hollow Choir remains. Warm. Deafening. Utterly still.
