The world has no name because it has not yet decided what it is.

You emerge into it as if surfacing inside a storm that has learned to be vertical. Everything is water, yet the water is not merely liquid. It is a slow, cold, planetary-scale fluid that behaves like an atmosphere trying to remember it was once an ocean. Pressure and gravity argue constantly. At these elevations the hydrostatic column above you is so immense that the water itself is compressed into a luminous, sapphire syrup. It glows with its own bioluminescence born not from life but from pressure shearing the electron clouds of dissolved minerals. Normal light still reaches here, filtered through hundreds of vertical kilometers, arriving as a cold, even, platinum-white radiance that never quite finds a source or a horizon.

There is almost nothing to see.

The scale is continental yet embryonic. Vast empty volumes yawn between the few objects that have begun to crystallize out of the flux. These objects are not islands, not continents, not even properly stone. They are proto-geologies still deciding whether they will become matter. Colossal ribs of raw mineral rise in slow motion from the deep, distorted by the turbulent currents like half-melted wax sculptures being pulled upward by invisible strings. Their surfaces are glass-smooth in some places, violently fractured in others, as though the planet is trying to birth mountains before it has invented erosion. Some of these nascent massifs are already hundreds of kilometers tall and still growing, their peaks lost in the upper layers where the water thins toward vacuum.

The sound is continuous, deafening, architectural. It is not the roar of waves. It is the sound of the world arguing with itself in frequencies that vibrate your bones before your ears can parse them. Deep harmonic booms roll through the water like continental plates being struck by god-sized bells. Layered over that is a constant high metallic keening, the shriek of hyper-pressurized fluids racing through invisible fault-lines in the fluid itself. Every few minutes a new frequency appears, as though an unseen choir is testing which note will make the proto-continents shatter.

Nothing lives here. Not yet. The turbulence is too savage, the temperatures too borderline, the chemistry still inventing itself. Even the concept of “biology” has not quite arrived. Yet the water is not sterile. It is pregnant. Strange laminar flows twist through the cold blue emptiness like living equations, folding and unfolding into geometries that should not be stable. These are the first attempts at structure, the world’s own thoughts trying to become matter. Sometimes they almost succeed and a perfect crystal lattice blooms for a few heartbeats before the turbulence tears it apart again. The fragments become new seeds that drift for thousands of kilometers before latching onto the growing mineral spines.

The place feels unfinished on a cosmic scale. You sense that you are standing inside the engineering bay of a planet that is still on the drawing board. The “tech” here is the raw machinery of physics itself, running at a level where quantum foam and classical hydrodynamics have not yet agreed on the rules. Occasionally the water simply forgets to be coherent and a pocket of hard vacuum blooms like a silver flower, only to collapse again in a detonation that sounds like every cathedral bell on Earth ringing at once.

There is no up or down that can be trusted. The cold pulls in every direction. Light falls evenly from all sides. The great half-formed mountain-things drift past at imperceptible speeds, so large that their motion registers only as a shift in pressure across your body. Sometimes, far off in the empty distances between them, a new spine begins to extrude from nothing. It rises with glacial violence, shedding sheets of super-critical fluid that flash into plasma before dissolving back into the roar.

This is not an ocean.  
This is not a sky.  
This is a world still being shouted into existence, syllable by syllable, and you are suspended inside the shout.

Everything is loud.  
Everything is empty.  
Everything is becoming.
