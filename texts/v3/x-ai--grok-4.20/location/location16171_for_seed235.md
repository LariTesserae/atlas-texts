The place has no name because names require time, and time has been cancelled here.

You stand on the lip of a colossal crater-valley that should not be able to exist. The elevation is murderous—thin, scalding air claws at your lungs—yet the heat is so absolute that sweat cannot evaporate; it simply rolls down your skin in fat, perpetual beads that never dry. The sky is the colour of molten brass poured over milk. Light feels thick, almost oily. It arrives from everywhere and nowhere at once.

Water is everywhere and nowhere. It does not fall as rain; it *perspires* from the rock itself. Every cliff face, every tower of stone, every folded ridge weeps in slow, clear sheets that never reach the ground. Halfway down they simply forget to fall and drift sideways as hovering veils of mist. Condensation beads on the air. You can cup your hands and fill them just by standing still. The water is hot—bathwater left too long in the sun—and it tastes of iron and lilies.

The city—if it can be called a city—grew inside this weeping stone the way coral grows inside a reef, except the reef is the fossilised memory of a mountain that died of vertigo. Tier after tier of terraces, balconies, colonnades, and spiralling streets have been carved, fused, and melted into the living rock over what must have been geological epochs. No tool marks remain. The architecture looks *secreted*, as though the mountain itself once dreamed in the shape of temples and avenues and then forgot it was only dreaming. Every surface is glossed with a thin film of flowing water that reflects the brass sky into a thousand trembling duplicates.

Vegetation has taken the stone like a fever. It is not green so much as *violent*. Ferns the size of houses burst from windowsills. Vines thick as ship’s cables braid themselves through colonnades, flowering with fleshy white blooms that open only for a single eternal instant and then simply remain open forever. Everything is locked in the same second of peak ripeness. Nothing rots. Nothing matures. Nothing falls.

The sound is the worst part.

It is not one sound. It is every sound the world has ever made, compressed, folded, and played simultaneously at a volume that should liquefy bone. Yet the roar is perfectly stable, perfectly balanced, a deafening chord that never changes its note. You hear waterfalls that do not exist, the groan of continental plates, the scream of every cicada that ever lived, cathedral bells struck by lightning, and beneath it all a low, patient heartbeat that might be your own or might be the planet’s. After a few minutes the noise stops feeling like sound and starts feeling like weather. You do not hear it anymore; you wear it.

There are no animals. Not a single insect, bird, or lizard disturbs the leaves. The only movement is the slow, perpetual drip and drift of water that has learned to ignore gravity in interesting ways. Streams run up vertical walls. Lakes hover in mid-air like silver lenses, their surfaces perfectly still, reflecting the same unchanging sky.

Reality here is not broken so much as *healed wrong*. The wound has closed, but the scar tissue grew into the shape of a city.

You can walk for hours—days, perhaps—and the light never changes. The heat never lessens. The roar never falters by a single decibel. Every new terrace reveals another identical, impossible version of the same place: more dripping colonnades, more impossible hanging gardens, more stone that looks as though it is still softly breathing. Perspective collapses. You can see the curve of the crater wall twenty kilometres away as clearly as you can see the veins in the leaf beside your face. Distance has been cancelled along with time.

This is not a ruin.  
This is not a city that was abandoned.  
This is a city that achieved perfect equilibrium with its own extinction and then simply *stayed*, like a scream frozen mid-breath.

If you sit on any balustrade long enough, you will notice the water flowing over the stone is spelling something in a language that has no words yet. The letters keep changing, but the meaning never does.

The meaning is:

*We were here at the exact moment everything became eternal, and we were so loud about it that the universe agreed to never move again.*

And the hot, weeping cliffs keep weeping, and the brass sky keeps burning, and the sound keeps sounding, and nothing, ever, for the rest of forever, will be any different.
