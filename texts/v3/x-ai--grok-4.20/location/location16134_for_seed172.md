The place is called the Hollow Forge.

It is not a world. It is a single, self-contained pocket-universe no larger than a cathedral, yet it feels infinitely tall because every surface refuses to acknowledge a ceiling. You stand inside a vast cylindrical shaft of machined metal and crystal, embedded like a bullet inside something far larger that will never be seen. The walls rise forever in perfect verticality, ringed by spiraling galleries, catwalks, and gantries that repeat every thirty meters with mechanical precision. There is no sky, only an unreachable vanishing point where the rings blur into a single white thread of light.

Everything here is built. There is no natural substrate, no stone that was ever quarried, no mineral that was ever mined. The matter itself was precipitated into existence already formed — titanic plates of black iridium, lattices of white ceramic, struts of sintered diamond, and vast ribbed conduits of something that behaves like frozen mercury but never melts. The geometry is young, almost violently fresh: every edge is still sharp enough to cut neutronium, every joint still shows the microscopic teeth-marks of whatever unimaginable machines birthed them. Nothing has been worn down. Nothing has been forgiven by time.

The temperature is a killing −217 °C. The air (if it can be called air) is a thin, non-breathable soup of noble gases and exotic fluorocarbons that have never known water. Sound travels through it like a bullet. And sound is everywhere.

The noise is deafening and eternal. It never changes. A single, compounding roar composed of ten thousand industrial frequencies that have reached perfect equilibrium: the bass-matter thump of continent-sized pistons cycling once every eleven hours, the scream of magnetic impellers spinning at 80% lightspeed, the constant glass-shattering harmonic of phase-locked plasma arrays, the subsonic growl of matter printers the size of office blocks extruding fresh segments of the cylinder itself. All of it has found a fixed, screaming chord. The sound is no longer an event. It is the ground state of this place. Newcomers feel their bones ringing in sympathy within seconds; after an hour the vibration becomes religious.

There is almost no vegetation. Only sparse, deliberate growths — brittle gray fronds of metallic silicon crystal that sprout from expansion joints like steel wool. They are not alive in any organic sense; they are slow semiconductor colonies that feed on stray voltage and waste heat. They never spread. They simply persist, trembling in the constant roar.

No animals. No bacteria. No dust. The only motion is mechanical.

Light is harsh, actinic, and perfectly even. Banks of plasma emitters mounted every twenty meters bathe the cylinder in a cold, shadowless brilliance that makes every surface look simultaneously brand new and already eternal. The light never flickers. The temperature never rises or falls. The roar never modulates. The Hollow Forge is a place of absolute dynamic equilibrium — a machine that has achieved the terrifying perfection of never needing to change again.

And yet it is still being built.

Along the inner walls, colossal tiered machines the height of skyscrapers crawl upward on magnetic rails, slowly, relentlessly extruding new rings of the cylinder above them. They will never reach the top. There is no top. They have been crawling upward for what the embedded chronometers claim is four hundred and nine thousand years, adding one perfect ring every 41 hours. The cylinder lengthens itself into infinity at a fixed, eternal rate while the bottom-most machines, far below where any eye can see, quietly devour the oldest rings and recycle them into raw stock for the printers above. A closed loop. A breathing dragon eating its own tail in perfect silence beneath the scream.

The strangeness is this: perspective does not work correctly here. Walk ten paces and the rings above you appear no closer. The scale is intimate — you can reach out and touch the nearest machine’s housing, feel it vibrating with enough force to liquefy bone — yet the mind insists you are standing inside something the size of a galaxy. Both are true. The Hollow Forge is pocket-sized and vaster than comprehension at the same time, folded in on itself like a metal origami axiom.

There is no weather. No decay. No evolution. Only the roaring, the cold, the white light, and the slow, patient, endless birth of new wall where old wall used to be. A completely constructed industrial womb that has achieved nirvana through noise.

Welcome to the place that finished becoming itself an eternity ago and has never stopped finishing.
