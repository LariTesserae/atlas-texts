The place has no name and does not need one.

It is a high, warm, utterly vegetable world that has decided—long ago and without consultation—to abandon the distinction between ground and growth. What should have been rock or soil instead breathes. Every surface, every slope, every hollow is a single continuous organism: a pale-green-to-gold living substrate that has folded itself into hills, valleys, cliffs, and basins the way lungs fold into lobes. This is not vegetation growing on rock; this is vegetation that has become the geology while it was still deciding what shape to become. The world is embryonic and total at once.

You stand on what feels like a high plateau, yet the land is still inventing its own elevation. Great soft ribs of living matter rise slowly beneath your feet, each ridge trembling with vascular pulses that travel at the pace of a deep breath. Between the ribs lie wide, shallow basins filled with a warm, heavy mist that condenses as fast as it evaporates. The air itself sweats. Water is not a visitor here; it is a permanent negotiation between the plant-mass and the sky. From the undersides of colossal leaf-bridges and from the joints where vast stems thicken into buttresses, clear water drips in steady, musical curtains. The droplets strike resonant hollows tuned by the plant itself, so the entire landscape rings and gongs in layered, overlapping rhythms. The sound is loud but never chaotic—more like a cathedral organ played by wind and root pressure.

Everything is made, yet nothing is built by hands or machines. The architecture is pure biology in a state of permanent, deliberate becoming. Colonnades of fused stalks form vaulted corridors that stretch for kilometers, their ceilings dripping with condensation that falls in perfect straight lines into moss-lined channels below. Arches of braided rhizomes span ravines that have not yet decided how deep they wish to be. In places the living mass has grown into delicate lattices so fine they appear to be carved ivory, yet each thread thickens and lengthens by the hour. You can watch the place constructing itself if you sit still long enough: a new buttress swelling, a fresh leaf-roof unfurling to shade a newborn valley, a glistening membrane of sap stretching across a gap to become a translucent window.

The color palette is strangely gentle for something so loud. Greens run from celadon to almost-white lime; golds and ochres appear where older layers have begun to oxidize into stiffened support tissue. Here and there veins of vivid carmine pulse just beneath the translucent epidermis, carrying heated sap upward against gravity. Normal daylight falls through a perpetual haze of transpired moisture, turning every beam into a soft, volumetric blade. Shadows are rare and velvety.

Fauna is almost absent. Once in a great while a translucent insect the size of a hand drifts past on wings that beat too slowly for ordinary physics. It looks like a cross between a dragonfly and a sheet of wet paper. You suspect these creatures are less animals than mobile extensions of the plant-mass itself—pollinators that are also seeds that are also nerves. They make no sound. Nothing here needs to hunt or be hunted; the equilibrium is absolute.

And the strangeness is this: the world is still choosing its final form. You can feel the indecision in the air. Some ridges are trying to become mountains; others have changed their minds and are collapsing back into wide, soft plateaus even as you watch. A distant escarpment that looked permanent an hour ago is now rippling like a slow green sea. Nothing is fixed. Yet nothing is hurried. The plant-mass has all the time it will ever need, and it is using every second of it to remain unfinished on purpose.

There are no stones. There is no bare earth. There is only the one living substrate, endlessly self-sculpting, weeping warm water from ten thousand pores, singing to itself in resonant bass and bell-like tenor, stretching across a high, open country that has decided to be everything at once while remaining almost empty.

If you lie down and press your ear to the warm, yielding surface, you can hear the slow hydraulic heart of the place beating beneath you—steady, loud, eternal, and still, somehow, only just beginning.
