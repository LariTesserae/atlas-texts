The place is called the **Weeping Lattice**.

You step through a fissure no wider than a shoulder and the world opens only enough to admit you, then seals again behind. Everything here is built. There is no bare stone, no untouched soil; every surface has been engineered, grown, folded, or spun into existence by technologies that treat physics as a set of polite suggestions.

It is a pocket the size of a modest cathedral, yet it feels oceanic because every cubic meter is saturated with layered, near-touching presences. The ceiling (if it can be called that) is a dense mesh of pale, living conduits hung so thickly they form a false sky only four meters above your head. From these conduits weep slow, warm tears of condensed water that never quite fall straight. The droplets drift, orbit one another, or slide along invisible field lines before they are drunk by the thirsty architecture below. The air itself is humid enough to bead on your skin and taste faintly of iron and lilies.

Temperature is a steady, drowsy 31 °C. The warmth is not from a sun—there is none—but from the slow metabolic pulse of the Lattice itself. You can feel it as a gentle thrum against the soles of your feet.

Sound is almost violent. A layered, bone-deep roar composed of:
- the constant hydraulic sigh of water being pumped, filtered, and exhaled,
- the overlapping choral drones of ten thousand micro-fauna singing in fractal counterpoint,
- the low tectonic groan of structural members that are simultaneously growing and being eaten by maintenance organisms,
- and, beneath it all, a subsonic heartbeat that arrives every 9.4 seconds, as though the entire pocket is a single lung.

The light is dim, amber-gold, and sourceless. It seems to ooze from the wet surfaces themselves, reflecting and refracting through millions of moving droplets until the air appears to glow with its own fever.

Vegetation is sparse in the classical sense—no grasses, no leaves—but the built environment is densely colonized by something that behaves like vegetation. Glossy black filaments, each no thicker than a hair, grow in tight velvet mats across every engineered surface. They drink the weeping water, photosynthesize the dim light, and bloom every few hours with tiny translucent sacs that pop open to release clouds of metallic spores. These spores are both pollen and repair-nanites; they drift like glittering plankton before settling and fusing into new structural tissue.

Fauna is absurdly abundant and almost entirely small. Thumb-sized velvet crabs with mirror carapaces scuttle through the dripping latticework. Clouds of luminous midges drift in slow, deliberate spirals that map out magnetic anomalies only they can sense. Fist-sized jellyfish-like things pulse through the humid air on bioelectric sails, trailing tendrils that taste the condensation for trace chemistry. Larger presences exist but remain mostly hidden: you occasionally glimpse a sinuous shadow the length of your arm slipping between conduits, or hear the wet click of something large rearranging itself in the upper dark.

Everything is in balance, yet nothing is still. Erosion here is not destructive; it is husbandry. The Lattice has existed so long that its weathering has become aesthetic. Edges are rounded by ten thousand years of deliberate micro-abrasion. Cracks have been colonized, widened, and turned into deliberate aqueducts. The entire structure is simultaneously ancient and still being born.

The rules of the world are simple and absolute:

- Water is currency. Nothing is allowed to remain dry for long.
- Nothing is allowed to remain alone. Every organism, every mechanism, every structural member is plugged, grafted, or symbiotically bound to at least three others.
- Scale is enforced. Anything that grows too large is gently dismantled by translucent scavenger eels that sing while they work.
- The heartbeat must never stop. If it does, the Lattice will begin to forget what it is.

You stand in the warm, deafening, dripping dark, surrounded by a billion moving pieces that have all learned to cooperate inside a room that should not be able to contain them. The air kisses your face with condensation. Something small and warm lands on your wrist, drinks for three seconds, and flutters away. The heartbeat thuds through your ribs like a second, slower heart that is not yours.

This is not a ruin.  
This is not a garden.  
This is a single, vast, engineered organism that happens to be shaped like a hidden cathedral, and it is crying, singing, breathing, and repairing itself all at once, forever.
