The place is called the Hollowed Basin.

It is a wide, low-lying depression of stone and rust, roughly the size of a small inland sea that has long since given up pretending it was ever water. The ground is not soil; it is an endless pavement of mineral crust, fractured and re-fused over centuries into plates the color of dried blood and old copper. Moisture gathers only in the seams—thin, glassy films of condensation that bead along hairline cracks each dawn and evaporate by mid-morning, leaving behind rings of faint white efflorescence like the ghost of salt. Every few hundred meters a shallow pan of standing water appears, never more than ankle-deep, perfectly still, reflecting a sky that always looks the same.

There is no vegetation. Nothing green has ever been here. The only living architecture belongs to slow organisms that are less than lichen and more than mineral: vast, paper-thin sheets of black and ochre pseudofungus that grow across the stone in geometric lattices, following fault lines with mathematical patience. They do not spread; they simply *are*, unchanging for lifetimes.

The air is warm—thickly, persistently warm, like the inside of a machine room that has been running since before anyone alive can remember. It smells of hot iron, ozone, and something faintly electrical, like the breath of old transformers.

Sound dominates everything. A deep, industrial drone lies beneath the world the way blood lies beneath skin. It is not a single engine but the accumulated voice of many: vast subterranean turbines, distant piston hammers, and pressure valves that have been opening and closing on geological schedules for centuries. The noise never rises or falls. It simply *is*, a constant 70-decibel cathedral tone that makes the jaw ache after an hour and the bones feel pleasantly loose after a day. Newcomers sometimes mistake it for the planet itself breathing.

Scattered across the basin at wide, irregular intervals stand the Machines.

They are not ruins. They are not in use. They simply persist—enormous, half-sunken iron-and-brass assemblies the size of cathedrals that have been slowly settling into the mineral crust for what must be millennia. Some rise only ten meters before the stone claims them; others still thrust skeletal gantries fifty meters into the flat, even light. Their purpose is no longer legible. Great flywheels have seized solid. Conduits the width of train cars disappear into the ground and never reappear. Every surface is covered in the same slow patina of rust and pseudofungus, as though the planet is gently digesting them without hurry.

The light is ordinary daylight, yet it never quite seems to change. There are no clouds. The sun (or whatever passes for it) sits in the same quadrant of sky at all times, a dull bronze coin. Shadows are soft and permanent.

Fauna is rare. Once every few days a lone, armor-plated creature the size of a small horse will drift across the middle distance on six jointed legs, its carapace patterned in the same rust-and-black as the machines. It never approaches. It never feeds. It simply traverses from one invisible point to another and vanishes behind a ridge of fractured stone. Nothing else moves.

The entire basin is in perfect equilibrium. Nothing is being born. Nothing is dying. The condensation cycles, the pseudofungus grows at a rate measured in microns per decade, the machines sink another millimeter every century, and the great drone continues without variation. Even erosion has reached its final form: the land has been worn down to a state that will not meaningfully change again until the star itself cools.

It is a landscape that feels post-industrial and pre-biological at the same time, as though someone built an empire here, then left, then the empire’s bones fossilized, and then even the idea of empires forgot this place existed. All that remains is warm stone, patient machines, and a sound that has become the texture of eternity.

If you stand still long enough in the middle of the largest pan of water, you can feel the vibration of the turbines through your soles and up into your teeth. The water trembles in perfect concentric rings around you, never breaking, never still, reflecting the same bronze sun that has always been there.

This is the Hollowed Basin.

It does not wait for you.

It has already finished waiting.
