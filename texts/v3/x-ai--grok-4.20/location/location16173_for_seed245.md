Here is the place that answers to all those parameters at once.

You stand at the bottom of a world that is still being born and is already dying. It has no name because names would imply completion, and nothing here has ever been allowed to finish.

The land itself is low and planetary in scale: an endless, shallow basin the size of continents, yet so flat and raw that the horizon feels only a few kilometres away before it dissolves into thermal haze. Every surface is engineered, every contour deliberate. Vast plates of black volcanic glass, still warm from whatever furnace extruded them, lie tilted at impossible angles like half-buried monoliths. Between them run geometric trenches and half-formed canals, cut with a precision that suggests intelligence, yet abandoned mid-stroke. The geometry never quite closes; every pattern falters, every line warps, as though the builders changed their minds while the stone was still molten.

Water is everywhere and nowhere. It does not flow so much as sweat. The air itself is supersaturated, a hot, breathable mist that condenses on every cold surface in slow, oily beads. These beads do not fall; they crawl. You can watch them migrate across the glass in deliberate, almost nervous rivulets, gathering into trembling lenses the size of shields before they suddenly boil away with a soft hiss. The ground exhales. Every few minutes a new fumarole opens with a wet pop, releasing a gout of warm vapour that smells of iron and wet chalk. The water never pools long enough to reflect the sky; it is always being born or murdered by the heat.

There is no vegetation. Nothing green has ever been permitted here. Instead the only living (if that word applies) things are slow fungal lattices of pale silica that grow in the joints between plates. They are not plants. They are closer to mineral infections, fragile lattices of glass threads that bloom in perfect geometric flowers, then shatter into dust the moment the temperature shifts a few degrees. The dust itself then reorganises elsewhere, as if the world is trying to decide what shape it wants to be and keeps rejecting its own attempts.

The heat is oppressive, wet, and heavy. It does not burn so much as press. Your lungs feel full of warm syrup. Sound travels strangely; every noise is soaked and muffled, as though the air itself is listening. Mostly the place is quiet, but the quiet is not peaceful. It is the quiet of something holding its breath. Every so often a low, sub-audible thump rolls across the basin; not a sound so much as a pressure change that vibrates the fluid in your inner ear. When it happens, distant structures can be seen rearranging themselves. Whole cliffs of black glass slide sideways on invisible rails, new trenches open like parting lips, and fresh plates rise steaming from below, still soft at the edges.

There is no animal life. The only movement belongs to the world constructing and deconstructing itself. Occasionally you will see what look like tools: primitive, hand-scale things of ceramic and raw copper left lying on the glass. Some are half-melted. Some appear to have been used only minutes ago. There are no hands to have used them. The place makes them the way a wound makes pus.

Light is dim, perpetual twilight filtered through thick steam and low, bruised clouds that never rain. The sun, if there is one, is only a sickly orange smudge that drifts in wrong directions. Sometimes two smudges appear at once. Sometimes none. Shadows behave poorly. They lengthen when they should shorten. They point toward each other. They pool in places where nothing is casting them.

Reality here is not broken so much as unfinished and impatient. The rules keep being rewritten mid-sentence. Walk ten paces in a straight line and you may find the ground has tilted while you weren’t looking. Look away from a structure and when you look back it has grown baroque ornamentation that was not there before, or it has simplified into brutal minimalism. Cause and effect are still being negotiated. Entropy and creation are fighting in the same room and neither is winning.

This is a made world that was never allowed to cool. A machine the size of a continent that is building something it has forgotten the purpose of, using only the crudest tools and the wettest, hottest materials. It is a fever dream expressed in black glass, condensation, and slow geological panic.

And it is still, even now, clearing its throat to begin.
