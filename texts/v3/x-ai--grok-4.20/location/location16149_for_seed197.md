The place is called the Hollow Basilica.

It is a single, self-contained living cathedral no larger than a modest hamlet, yet inside it feels simultaneously cramped and oceanic. Every surface—floor, walls, vaulted ceilings—is made of living vegetation so densely interwoven that it has become both substrate and architecture. There is almost no bare earth; the ground underfoot is a thick, breathing mat of mosses, liverworts, and fibrous root-felt that gives like living carpet. From this base rise colossal trunks, vines, ferns, and fungal shelves that have fused over centuries into buttresses, columns, and ribbed arches. The “stonework” is entirely vegetable: pale lignified stems hardened by time, bark lacquered black by repeated condensation, emerald mosses packed so tight they read as emerald marble from a distance.

The scale is intimate and vast at once. You can walk from one side of the Basilica to the other in ten minutes, yet the ceilings soar thirty meters above you, lost in a hazy green twilight. Between the colossal plant-pillars there is almost nothing: great hollow volumes of air, empty as a cathedral nave at midnight. This is the paradox the parameters demanded: total vegetation (2.6) existing inside near-void density (0.2). The plants have eaten the space and then, having eaten it, left most of it open again, as though respecting some ancient agreement.

Water is everywhere and nowhere. It does not flow in rivers but condenses. The air is warm (1.6) and perpetually damp (1.4); every breath feels like inhaling soup. Moisture beads on every leaf and drips in slow, irregular percussion from the vaulting far above. In places the living floor dips into shallow, tea-colored pools lined with bladderwort and sundew. These pockets appear and vanish with the breathing of the structure itself; some mornings entire transepts are ankle-deep in warm water that simply drains away by afternoon through hidden root-channels.

Light is normal but heavily filtered. Sunlight arrives only as a diffused, cathedral-grade glow, stained green and gold by countless layers of translucent leaves. Shafts of it appear and disappear as clouds of midges and tiny luminous moths move through the upper vaults like living dust motes.

The sound is deafening (3.0). It never stops. Millions of creatures—tree-frogs the size of thumbnails, clicking beetles, cicadas with bodies like amber jewelry, colonies of bats that roost in the root-caverns, vast choruses of unseen birds—sing, shriek, drone, and thrum in overlapping waves. The vegetation itself participates: hollow reed-like stems whistle when wind moves through them, seed pods snap open with rifle-cracks, and the great trunks emit low, subsonic groans as they expand and contract with humidity. The combined roar is so total that after ten minutes inside you stop perceiving it as noise and begin to feel it as weather.

Fauna is absurdly abundant (2.3). Every cubic meter of the hollow air seems to contain drifting insects. Geckos with transparent skin run across the vaulting like living jewels. Slow, sloth-like creatures the size of sheep hang upside-down from the ceiling arches, their fur colonized by orchids. Predators and prey move in plain sight because there is nowhere to hide; the emptiness between the plant-columns makes every hunt theatrical. A six-legged monitor lizard stalks a flock of iridescent flightless birds across an open stretch of moss-floor while a cloud of predatory dragonflies wheels above them like silver smoke.

The ecosystem is in frenetic, constant motion (2.4). Nothing is still. Vines visibly lengthen and retract with the moisture cycles. Flowers open and slam shut in minutes. Fungal lattices bloom overnight across the columns and are devoured by morning by armies of translucent snails. The entire Basilica is a single super-organism that has achieved a kind of urban organization (built 1.7) without any tool-use whatsoever (tech 0.3). Its “streets” are the open airy naves between columns; its “districts” are zones of different dominant plant species that have segregated themselves over centuries; its “cathedral square” is a perfectly circular clearing where no large trunks grow and the floor is a single unbroken sheet of emerald moss.

Yet the real signature of the place is the weirdness (2.6). Reality here is not broken so much as softly melted. Perspective refuses to behave. Walk toward what looks like a distant pillar and it is suddenly looming over you; turn around and the entrance you came through has closed behind a curtain of new growth that was not there thirty heartbeats earlier. Certain vaulted chambers replay sounds from hours before, as though the air itself has memory. In the deepest, darkest chapels—where the plant-mass grows so thick it forms almost solid walls—you can sometimes see your own silhouette moving half a second out of sync with your body.

The Basilica has been here long enough (erosion 2.0) that it feels inevitable. It is not growing so much as it has already become, and now simply maintains itself in a state of roaring, dripping, deafening equilibrium. It is low (0.7), warm, wet in pockets, dry in others, empty in its bones and yet so full of life that the life has replaced architecture, theology, and time itself.

You are inside an ancient, hollow, deafening, breathing church that grew rather than being built, and the church is still growing, and it is never, ever quiet.
