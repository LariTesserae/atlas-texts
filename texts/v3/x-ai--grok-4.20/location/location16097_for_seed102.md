The place is called the **Amber Vault**.

It is not a cavern in any ordinary sense. It is a single, self-contained pocket of reality lodged inside the dry, lightless crust of a world that has never known surface water. The Vault measures perhaps two hundred paces across at its widest and rises no higher than the reach of a tall tree, yet it feels both immense and suffocatingly intimate. Its boundaries are unnervingly sharp: glossy black mineral walls that look as though they were poured yesterday, still wet from some primordial kiln. They curve with the impossible smoothness of a blown glass bubble and refuse to echo. Whatever lies beyond them is simply *not*.

No water has ever touched this place. Instead, the air itself is a thick, warm, resinous exhalation that smells of hot myrrh, crushed beetle shells, and something electrical. The temperature never deviates. It is always the exact warmth of a body that has just died.

Light is scarce and sour. A diffuse, bruise-colored glow leaks from fist-sized nodules of living crystal embedded in the ceiling like slow stars. Their radiance is just enough to make silhouettes theatrical and to turn every surface into a theater of soft shadows. The crystals pulse in a rhythm too slow for a heartbeat and too fast for geology.

From the black floor—fractured, glossy, and still warm to the touch—grow the **lung-trees**. They are not plants in any earthly taxonomy. Their trunks are supple, fleshy columns the color of old bone, branching into dense, wet canopies of translucent leaves that tremble constantly even though the air is dead-still. The leaves are veined with slow-moving sap that glows faint turquoise. When they brush one another they produce a wet kissing sound. The entire forest is lush, almost indecently so, yet the individual trunks stand far apart, giving the Vault the strange atmosphere of a crowded room where everyone is politely ignoring everyone else.

Between the lung-trees the ground is scattered with the **First People’s remnants**—not ruins, but single, perfect artifacts dropped as if their owners simply ceased mid-gesture. A stone hand-axe with a carved spiral that hurts to stare at for long. A clay doll with too many joints. A bronze mirror no larger than a palm that reflects yesterday instead of today. These objects never weather, never gather dust, never move. The Vault does not permit change.

The fauna is abundant and loud and deeply strange.

Huge iridescent millipedes the length of a man’s arm clatter between the trunks in ceaseless caravans, their legs clicking like shaken bones. They sing—actually sing—in deep whale-like chords that overlap and interfere until the air itself feels bruised. Clouds of thumb-sized moths with mirror wings dart through the lung-tree canopy, reflecting the bruise-light in sudden, blinding flashes. When they land they fold their wings and look exactly like flakes of rust.

Most unsettling are the ** Hollow Choir**—creatures that resemble skinned monkeys made of wet red glass. They have no faces, only smooth concave mirrors where faces should be. They move in total silence until they begin their performance. Then the Vault becomes deafening. Their voices are not voices; they are perfect imitations of every sound that has ever occurred inside the Vault since its formation. They scream with the voices of extinct First People, they reproduce the exact clatter of millipede feet from three thousand years ago, they moan with the slow cracking of the ceiling crystals. The effect is a roaring, layered chorus that never repeats and never ends. Yet nothing ever changes. The same individual Hollow Choir members return to the same perches, sing the same sequence of stolen sounds, and relapse into mirrored stillness.

There is no wind. There is no weather. The lung-trees do not grow or shed. The crystals do not dim or brighten. The artifacts do not corrode. Even the echoes have memorized their routes and repeat them with mechanical piety.

This is a young world that has already reached the end of time.

It is a pocket that was sealed the moment it achieved perfection, and the perfection it achieved is loud, warm, lush, claustrophobic, and permanently out of balance with any sane ecology. The First People who left their tools behind understood something about finality that later peoples have forgotten. They built nothing more than a few ritual shelters—low huts of black glass and resin—but mostly they simply stood among the lung-trees until the Vault decided it was finished with motion.

Now only the Hollow Choir still moves with purpose, stealing every sound the Vault has ever made and flinging it back into the warm amber air forever.

If you stand very still and cup your hands over your ears, you can just hear the resinous air breathing in and out of the lung-trees in slow, contented sighs.

It has been breathing the same breath for fourteen thousand years.

It will never stop.
