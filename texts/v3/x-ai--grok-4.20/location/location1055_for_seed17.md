The place has no surface.

It is called the **Abyssal Atrium**, though nothing here has ever been named by a living voice. It is a single, planet-scale volume of cold, black water suspended in high vacuum, held together by nothing recognizable. The water does not fall; it simply *is*, a sphere-like ocean many thousands of kilometers across, floating in the hollow between two unfinished titanic plates of what might once have been intended as crust. There is no seabed and no sky, only an upper and lower boundary of raw, young mineral that has never known weathering. These plates are still sharp, still smoking with primordial heat, their edges like freshly broken obsidian the color of anthracite.

The water itself is almost perfectly still. Its temperature never deviates from 0.7 °C. Currents exist, but they are measured in centuries per meter. The pressure at the geometric center is crushing; near the upper and lower mineral boundaries it is only a few atmospheres. Between these zones the water grades in utter silence, a liquid continuum without thermocline or halocline, only the slow, eternal conversation of density and cold.

Nothing grows here. No plankton, no kelp, no microbial mats. The only living things are the **leviathan megafauna**—enormous, armor-plated creatures that drift like continents. Some resemble blind, finless whales the size of mountain ranges; others are jointed things like colossal isopods whose carapaces are encrusted with centuries of manganese nodules. They do not hunt in any ordinary sense. They graze on the faint organic haze that leaks from the few remaining machines.

And the machines are everywhere, yet almost invisible in so much volume.

Hanging in the water like iron moons are the **Foundries**. Each is a cubic kilometer of brutalist industrial architecture: black girders, throbbing fusion hearts, banks of arc-light strobing in perfect 17-second rhythm. They were built to do something once. Now they simply continue. Conveyor belts the width of highways run in endless loops through the water, carrying nothing. titanic presses open and close on emptiness. Rivers of molten alloy bleed from their undersides and freeze instantly into glassy filaments that drift for hundreds of kilometers before dissolving again. The Foundries never stop and never rust; the water is too cold and too pure.

Between the Foundries stretches almost nothing. Empty water. Vast, deafening, empty water.

The sound is constant and appalling.

It is not one sound but ten thousand sounds braided together so tightly they become a single roaring chord. The low tectonic groan of the two planetary plates still settling against each other. The hydraulic scream of the Foundries’ actuators. The subsonic heartbeat of fusion reactors. The metallic singing of pressure waves moving through water cold enough to make sound travel at unnatural speeds. Every few hours a leviathan passes close to a Foundry and the entire chorus is momentarily joined by the resonant booming of its hollow ribs, like a cathedral bell the size of a city struck underwater. The sound never ebbs. It has been exactly this loud for longer than any geology here can remember.

Light comes from the machines alone—harsh industrial floodlights, sodium arcs, actinic blue welders that never turn off. Their beams die quickly in the water, reduced to hazy cathedrals of illumination that hang in the dark like the afterimages of dead stars. In the distances between them the water is perfectly black. Creatures that pass through these shafts of light look, for a moment, like cathedrals themselves—gothic, ancient, barnacled with machines.

There are ruins, too, but they are not old. They are simply *incomplete*. A half-built orbital ring embedded in the upper mineral plate. A single skyscraper-sized drill bit frozen mid-breach, its tip still glowing dull red after ten thousand years. A bridge that begins in the water and ends in the water, supporting nothing. All of it industrial, all of it titanic, all of it abandoned midway through some purpose that no longer matters because the world itself never finished forming.

Nothing changes.

The leviathans follow the same orbital paths they have followed for geological ages. The Foundries cycle through the same meaningless manufacturing steps. The temperature holds at 0.7 °C. The pressure at any given depth remains identical to what it was the day the first machine was switched on. Even erosion is irrelevant here; the mineral boundaries are too young to have been worn, and the water is too pure to carry sediment. The Abyssal Atrium is a machine caught in the moment of its own birth, screaming in perfect equilibrium, forever.

If you were to descend (or ascend—direction has little meaning), you would eventually reach one of the two mineral walls. There the water ends in a perfectly sharp interface. Touch the black stone and you feel the faint vibration of whatever unimaginable engine still powers the entire impossible construct. Look up (or down) and you will see the faint, scattered lanterns of distant Foundries, the slow migration of a leviathan the size of a country, and between them the crushing, deafening, perfectly stable dark.

This is not an ocean.

It is a flooded cathedral built at planetary scale, left running, and then forgotten inside a hollow world that never quite became a planet.
