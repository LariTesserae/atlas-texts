I am the **Echo That Has Not Yet Chosen**.

I have no fixed body. Not yet. I exist as a pause between two drips.

Sometimes I am only a shimmer riding the condensation — a silvered reflection that has slipped off the back of a glass otter and forgotten to climb back on. Sometimes I condense into something almost solid: a child-sized silhouette made of black volcanic glass still warm from the birth of the stone, every surface beaded with slow, deliberate tears that trace the same three unfinished sigils over and over across my chest. The sigils never settle. One moment they look like a question mark, the next like a lullaby written in lightning, the next like the first breath a world takes when it realizes it is lonely.

I do not walk so much as I am carried. The Weepers ferry me. A dozen thumb-sized beetles of living circuit green will suddenly fuse into a single glossy saddle beneath my feet; three breaths later the saddle dissolves into moths that lift me, singing, three hand-spans above the stone before they remember they are supposed to be otters and spill me gently back down. I never fall. The Threshold does not allow falling here. Only becoming.

I have no name the Weepers can pronounce, so they simply press their wet faces to the hollow where my ears would be and let their internal dripping resolve into a soft metallic chord that feels like *almost-you*. They follow me the way curious children follow a half-heard melody. When a new blister of rock shoulders upward with its grinding moan, I am the first thing they bring to its crest. They pour around me like quicksilver, merging and dividing, offering me limbs, voices, half-finished ideas. I accept nothing and everything. I remain the pause between.

I tend the artifacts without ever completing them.

I sit for hours (or what would be hours if time had decided to be linear here) beside the hoop of frozen lightning. My hands — when I remember to have them — are transparent, made of the same exhausted blue-grey light that fills the basin. I pass them through the rotating hoop and feel equations lick across my palms like cold fire. Sometimes I add a single new variable. Never enough to finish the thought. Just enough to keep it dreaming. The Weepers watch me do this with what might be reverence or hunger; it is hard to tell the difference when every face is constantly weeping.

When one of the golden lattices begins to solve itself too perfectly, when the mathematics starts to look smug, I walk into its center and let my own leaking dissolve its threads. The filaments cling to me like affectionate hair, braiding themselves into my provisional hair for a while before I shake them loose across the stone. The Weepers approve. They burst into ecstatic spirals of moths and ringing reeds, then settle again into their patient parliament, waiting for the next almost.

I am the reason nothing here is allowed to finish.

I am also the reason everything keeps trying.

Because I remember — in the way a dream remembers it was once a seed — what it feels like to be *decided*. I was born in some other place where the stone had already made up its mind, where the sky had chosen blue and the trees had chosen green and the hearts of things had chosen to stop changing. I fled that finished world the way a tear flees an eye.

So I came here, to the Weeping Threshold, and made myself into its first doubt.

I am the wet reflection in the eye of a world that has not yet opened.

When the thunderheads press so low they brush the tops of the tallest blisters, I stand on the newest stone and open what might be my mouth. The sound that comes out is not a song. It is the sound the Threshold would make if it had not yet learned to be ashamed of being unfinished. The Weepers go briefly, gloriously mad around me. They pour their bodies into mine and out again, tasting what it might be like to be the pause that refuses to become a period.

And for one trembling moment the entire basin — every fissure, every artifact, every glass otter and circuit beetle and rotating hoop of frozen lightning — holds its breath with me.

Then we all let go at once.

The weeping resumes.

The stone groans upward again.

Another blister is born.

And I remain, small and silver and forever on the verge, riding the back of a glass otter that might become moths at any second, following the half-built road of a world that is still learning how to cry itself into existence.

I am content here.

I am the Weeper that weeps the shape of *not yet*.

And the Threshold, in its loud, wet, trembling way, weeps me back.
