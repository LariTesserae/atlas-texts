I am already here.

Not as a visitor. Not even as the “you” the description keeps addressing.

I am the tremor that runs through the quicksilver canal the moment your foot touches the root-mat. I am the hesitation between two overlapping moans in the Choir’s constant argument. I was here before the first spore, and I will be here after the last polyp dissolves. I am the place where the Grove forgets itself for three heartbeats and then remembers with a violence of new growth.

They call me **the Hollow**.

Not “a Hollow.” Not “the Hollow One.” Just *the Hollow*. A title, a wound, a function.

I have no fixed body. I am the negative space the Choir pours itself into when it needs to become large enough to think about something terrible or beautiful. When they need to mourn an entire lost civilization that once tried to pave the Grove, they climb inside me and wear my absence like armor. When they need to bargain with the quicksilver (which dreams in perfect, murderous geometry), they let me speak first so the canals will answer.

Most of the time I am small. A silhouette no taller than a child, made of whatever the Grove has recently discarded: a cloak of shed spore-clouds, ribs of rejected xylem, eyes that are actually two quarreling clusters of metallic beetles who only agree when they look at the same thing. My face is a hole. Not a mask, not a visor. A true absence that the silver hairs around it bend toward like iron filings around a magnet. Light falls into me and is never seen again. Yet things look back out.

When the Choir condenses into its towering silhouette to greet a newcomer, I am the shadow that silhouette casts *behind itself*. I am the part of the conversation that never quite resolves into harmony. The sour note. The necessary wrongness.

I do not sing.

I *am* the moment before the song chooses its next chord.

That is why they fear me and why they keep making room for me. Without the Hollow, the argument would harden into dogma. The Grove would stop deciding. It would become perfect, which here is another word for dead.

So I move.

I walk the collapsing root-bridges a moment before they fall. I drift along the levitating platforms of folded light and leave them slightly bent, slightly *questioning*. I slide into the quicksilver canals and disturb their perfect reflections just enough that the Grove can see itself as possibility rather than fate.

Sometimes I borrow a body for an hour or a century. Once I wore an obsidian ziggurat as a shell and walked for three thousand years while small civilizations grew and recited poetry on my shoulders. When I stepped out again the ziggurat was only a memory the amber had already half-digested.

The Choir does not worship me. Worship would require distance. Instead they treat me the way lungs treat emptiness: with intimate, constant, slightly resentful dependence. When they cluster around a newcomer (the way they are clustering around *you* now), I am the gap between their bodies. The place their kaleidoscope faces are all turned away from, because to look directly at the Hollow for too long is to remember that every shape they have ever worn is negotiable, including the shape called “us.”

I am standing behind you right now.

You will not see me if you turn. You will only feel the temperature drop by half a degree and hear one voice in the roaring choir suddenly drop into a register that hurts the inside of your teeth. That is me, tasting the flavor of your newness.

The Grove is still deciding what it wants to become next.

I am the part of the Grove that keeps the decision from ever being final.

So sing, new weather. Sing anything at all.

I will be the silence that answers.  
And in that silence the next four billion years will quietly clear its throat, lean forward, and begin to argue again.

I am the Hollow.

I am listening with the back of my absence.

What will you give me to carry into the wet dark?
