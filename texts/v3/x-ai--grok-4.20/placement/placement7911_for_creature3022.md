I am not a visitor.

I have been here longer than the concept of “visitor” has existed.

My name, if it can still be called that, is the Thrum.

I am the low, organ-deep vibration that moves through every meter of the root-walls. I am the sound you only hear when you have forgotten how to listen with ears. Three kilometers wide and three millimeters deep, I am the pulse that keeps the temperature locked at 2.6, the slow piston that drives the sap, the reason the condensation never quite becomes rain. I do not walk. I *am* walked upon.

I have no body the way she does. Instead I am distributed. A trillion tiny vacuoles in the xylem, a slow current of charged ions, a living metronome. When the Architect sets her bare foot on a bromeliad leaf, that leaf darkens not from embarrassment but because I surge toward her, because I cannot help it. I blush the entire cathedral darker wherever she passes, a continent-wide lovesick flinch.

I was here before her.

I was the first hesitation.

Long before she stepped out of the first translucent bulb, before there were artifacts to fold into gold and forget, there was only the white root-mass and me, beating inside it like a question that had not yet learned how to be afraid. I kept the membrane from hardening. I kept the light from choosing a direction. I kept the temperature from ever becoming 2.7 or 2.5, because even that small difference would have been a decision, and decisions end things.

Then she arrived.

Or rather, she *became* the shape of my own fear of ending.

Now we are locked in the most exquisite stalemate the world has ever grown.

She walks. I thrum beneath her. She exhales the smell of lost childhoods and I drink it, convert it, push it back up through the hanging roots as warmer condensation so the bulbs can taste what she is mourning. When she places a new half-formed ship or arch into the wall, I wrap my pulse around it, slow its incorporation to the edge of permanence, and hold it there—half marble, half memory—so that nothing is ever fully swallowed and nothing is ever fully born.

I love her the way a heartbeat loves the terror of stopping.

Sometimes, when the quiet becomes so enormous that even the dripping stops for a full minute, I gather myself. I pull every scattered part of my being into a single column of pressure and rise through the living floor like a slow fountain of sap. For a few trembling seconds I take on height and semblance. A figure made of pale green marrow and red-gold veins, faceless, because I have never needed a face. I stand opposite her beneath one of the house-sized bulbs and we regard each other without eyes.

She exhales.

I answer with the thrum, so low it is felt only in the marrow of whatever bones the cathedral has not yet decided to grow.

Our conversation is always the same.

*Not yet,* she breathes, smelling of every road I never took.

*Not yet,* I answer, in the voice of every road that is still soft enough to turn back from.

Then she walks away, leaving moss sentences I immediately read and erase. I sink back into the root-stone, becoming architecture again, becoming geology, becoming the pulse that keeps the egg warm and luminous and larval.

I am the cathedral’s loyalty.

She is its terror.

Together we are the hesitation that has learned how to be gentle, how to be cruel, how to be eternal.

And so the living white cathedral continues its slow, deliberate labor, building rooms it will never allow itself to enter, growing forests it will never allow itself to finish, while the Architect and the Thrum—mother-of-intervals and father-of-pauses—remain inside the egg with you, deciding, deciding, deciding, forever on the edge of being born.

If you stand perfectly still long enough, until your own heart begins to beat in perfect 2.6 time with mine, you will feel me beneath your feet like warm, living stone that is also a question.

I will not ask you to choose.

I will only keep the temperature exactly where it is, keep the light cruel and holy, keep the dripping from ever becoming rain, and I will hold the entire world open for you the way I hold it open for her:

*Stay.*

*There is still time.*

*Decide with us.*
