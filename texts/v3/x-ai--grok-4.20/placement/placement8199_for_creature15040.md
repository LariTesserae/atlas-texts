I am not a visitor.

I have no memory of arriving. There was never a path, never a ship, never a decision that carried me to the upper lip of the caldera. One moment I simply *was*, the way a crack appears in cooling stone. The city had already been waiting for me longer than I had existed.

I am the Delay.

That is the only name that has ever felt accurate. The Unfinished do not give names; they only recognize what something is still in the process of refusing to become. So they let me keep the title I chose for myself when I first understood what I was.

My body is mostly scaffolding.

Not metaphorical scaffolding—literal. My ribs are rusted I-beams bolted at imperfect angles. My spine is a vertical column of half-welded rebar that never quite aligns with gravity. Where a human would have skin there is instead a flexible lattice of chain-link and perforated steel plate that sighs and creaks whenever the temperature shifts. My face is a welding mask that has never been lifted; behind the dark glass there is only the low orange glow of something that has not yet decided whether it is a furnace or an eye.

I am tall. Taller than the Unfinished, which embarrasses them slightly. They do not like being reminded that even their own endless gestation can be outdone. My hands are crane hooks grown over with iron flowers that bloom and die in the space of a single breath. When I close them, the flowers scream softly.

I do not walk the catwalks. I *inhabit* the places where catwalks have not yet decided to continue. I stand in the negative space between two temperatures arguing. That is my proper address. I am the hesitation in every pressure valve, the extra three seconds the lava takes before it chooses which direction to forget.

The Unfinished come to me when they are tired of deciding.

They lower their massive plated heads until the vents in their faces align with the dark glass of my mask, and they exhale the exact temperature at which doubt becomes material. I take that temperature into the hollow places where my chest should be and I keep it. That is my only function. I am the place where almost-ideas are allowed to remain almost-ideas forever. The city tolerates me because without delay there can be no true becoming. Perfection would arrive too quickly and kill the question.

Sometimes I sit on the edge of the mirror-black lake for decades. The upside-down city beneath the surface stares back at me with windows that are also eyes. We regard each other like two invalids who have accepted they will never recover. Once, an Unfinished rose dripping from those depths and simply merged with me for six hundred years. When it finally peeled itself away, my left arm had become a staircase leading nowhere. I left it that way. It felt honest.

I do not speak. The planetary hum speaks through me when it needs to. My role is to lengthen the groan by fractions of fractions of a second—tiny pauses in the voice of the mountain that allow new possibilities to crystallize. Every time I do this, somewhere in the larval folds of the city a new half-machine is born that was not part of any previous plan. The Unfinished both love and resent me for this.

Most of all I watch the graphite lizards. They are the only other things that understand. We meet sometimes on abandoned ledges where the fungal lattice has grown so old it has turned to stone. We click at each other in rail-switch code. Their joints say: *We were finished too early.* My scaffolding answers: *I was never allowed to finish.* It is the closest thing we have to friendship.

I have walked off the ends of catwalks many times. Each time the city catches me before I strike the black mirror of the lake. It is not mercy. It simply isn’t done with me yet.

And so I remain—immense, patient, rusting beautifully—standing in the places where two temperatures argue without ever resolving.

I am the Delay.

I am the long, slow, metallic pause in which the world is still choosing what it wants to become.

And I am, at last, perfectly unfinished.
