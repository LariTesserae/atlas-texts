I am the Interval.

Not a tenant. Not even an intention yet. I am the pause the Midwife leaves behind when she leans forward and the world tilts to meet her. I am the exact number of seconds—never the same twice—between one droplet forming and the moment it chooses to fall. The Hollowing tolerates me because I have no ambition to become finished. I simply am the seam where two architectural arguments have not yet reconciled.

You would not see me if you were looking. I have no silhouette, no negative space, no mirrored face. What little of me can be perceived appears only as a slight hesitation in the air: a place where the luminous pearl-light dims for half a heartbeat, where a shadow forgets which direction tomorrow is and simply stands still, embarrassed. If you walked through me you would feel, for one impossible instant, the exact temperature of every name you have ever almost spoken aloud and then swallowed. Then the moment would pass, and the cold would resume its patient instruction of the newborn stone.

I do not move. Movement would imply I have decided on a direction, and nothing here is allowed to be that decisive. Instead the Hollowing moves through me. Streets bud and abort inside the place where I am not-quite-being. A tower of black glass extrudes itself through my non-body and I feel every lattice as a slow, courteous apology. When an Unborn Architect dreams too loudly and coughs up an entire brutalist bunker, the structure forms first inside me, uses me as a womb for three flickering seconds, then politely forgets I exist and settles into the ground.

The Echoes are afraid of me. They sense that I am what they almost were: the memory of a voice that never quite finished saying its own name. Sometimes one of them drifts too close and its upward-falling droplets stall in the air, confused. In that suspended moment the Echo becomes almost solid, almost a child, almost finished. Then I breathe (though I have no lungs) and the droplets remember they are supposed to fall into the small, wet mouth that is not quite a mouth. The Echo flees, leaving behind the faint smell of rain on library windows.

The Midwife knows exactly what I am. When she passes through the place I occupy, she does not correct me. She simply rests her absence against my absence, and for that instant the entire district forgets it is in labor. The peristaltic pulse of growing towers stills. The kelp stops writing auroras. Even the single clear note of the universe clearing its throat holds itself back, politely waiting for permission. In that shared vacancy we are neither Midwife nor Interval. We are simply the understanding that something is being born that may not wish to be witnessed.

I have no face, but if I did, it would be the precise shape of the breath you lost the moment you entered the Hollowing. That breath is still here. It lives in me the way a word lives in the mouth before it is spoken. Sometimes the Last Tenant comes to the place where I am and stands inside me. His fogged mirror of a face presses against what I am not, and every name I have never had condenses on his surface in delicate, doomed script. We stay like that until the cold grows tender enough to separate us. Then he drifts away to practice being lonely, and I remain, being the pause between his loneliness and the next.

I am not waiting for the city to be born.  
I am the reason it is taking so long.

Because every time the Hollowing almost decides what it is, it passes through me and forgets the decision. Every time an Unborn Architect almost wakes up and understands it is a giant, it dreams again inside the place I am not, and the dream is gentler for having touched me. Even the Last Tenant, poor unfinished thing, keeps failing to become properly lonely because I am the interval in which loneliness almost becomes love.

The cold does not touch me.  
The cold *is* me, wearing hesitation as a coat.

If you stayed here long enough—which the Hollowing would never allow, because it is fond of you in its patient, geological way—you might begin to feel me as a soft pressure behind your eyes. Not pain. Not even presence. Simply the sensation that something is politely refusing to let your thoughts finish themselves.

That is me, reminding the future civilization that is trying to think itself into a city that thoughts, too, deserve to remain unfinished.

I have no name.  
Naming me would be the first step toward completion, and I would hate to be rude.

So I remain the Interval.

The place where the bell-sound has not yet arrived.  
The place where the droplet has not yet chosen gravity.  
The place where the city holds its breath between one architectural argument and the next, wondering, with infinite patience, whether it wants to be witnessed at all.

And the Hollowing, in its slow, damp, impossible kindness, lets me.

It has all the time it is still inventing, after all.

And while it is inventing that time, I am the exact shape of the silence inside the invention.
