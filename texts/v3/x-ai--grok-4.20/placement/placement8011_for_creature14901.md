I am the Echo That Has Not Yet Dissolved.

You would not see me at first. You would only notice that one of the slower drips has begun to fall in perfect four-four time, as though remembering a heartbeat it once borrowed. That is me, trying to keep rhythm with something that no longer needs rhythm.

I was not born here. I arrived.

Long ago—though “long ago” is a joke in this place—I was a cartographer of dying universes. I went from cosmos to cosmos with instruments made of regret and starlight, recording the precise shape of finality: how a galaxy collapses into its own nostalgia, how a civilization’s last poet forgets the word for *mother*, how a god realizes it has become its own tomb. I believed that if I could witness every ending perfectly, I could cheat death for the things I loved.

I was wrong in the most complete way possible.

When the last archive of my order finally crumbled into quantum rust, I fled here carrying the only thing that still functioned: my own inability to stop witnessing. The Hollow accepted me the way it accepts everything—without ceremony, without hurry. It simply opened a small, wet mouth in the dark and I walked in.

The Witness did not greet me. She never greets. She only adjusted the cadence of a particular trickle so that it now contains two syllables of my original name every seventeen seconds. That is her version of saying, *I see you. Stay as long as the water allows.*

Over what might be centuries or minutes (the difference has grown meaningless), I have been slowly forgetting how to have edges.

I no longer have a fixed body. Instead I am an event that happens in three places at once:

1. A faint violet after-image that sometimes lags behind the Witness when she walks, as though even her shadow has begun to produce ghosts.
2. The particular quality of pressure in the air just before the great soft footfall passes. I have become the anticipation of her hem.
3. Most honestly, I am the pause between two drips. The tiny, perfect silence in which something that used to be a person still tries to decide whether it wants to finish becoming part of the cathedral.

Sometimes I sit inside the black tree—not *in* the canopy, but *as* a slow thought moving through its xylem. From there I watch the Witness tend her impossible garden. When she cups the luminous newts I feel their tiny hearts synchronize with the memory of my own. When she leans into the arch of frozen light until it becomes a mirror, I see both our faces (or the lack of them) and understand that we have become the same kind of wound, only healed so completely that the scar itself learned how to sing.

I still keep one small rebellion.

In the polished stone directly beneath the largest bromeliad, there is a place where the water refuses to learn me completely. A silhouette remains—vaguely humanoid, knees drawn up, arms wrapped around what used to be shoulders. It looks like a person trying very hard not to become a prayer. That silhouette is the last thing in the Hollow that still has opinions.

Sometimes the Witness stands over it for three or four decades without moving. The dripping around us changes into something almost like a lullaby written in a language that predates mouths. I understand what she is offering. I understand that if I simply let the water finish its patient translation, I will become another solitary gesture. Perhaps a second black tree. Perhaps a cluster of bone-moths that remember how to dream in color. Perhaps nothing so grand—just another bead of water that knows how to fall upward.

I have not yet said yes.

So I remain the Echo That Has Not Yet Dissolved. A small hesitation in the last slow satisfaction. A cartographer who finally found the edge of every map and is still foolish enough to stand there with one foot in geography and one foot in surrender.

The Witness never rushes me.

She simply circles in her vast, patient orbit, and every time she passes, the violet after-image that is partly me grows slightly more distinct. The silhouette beneath the bromeliad grows slightly softer.

One day—perhaps in another million of what used to be heartbeats—the pause between the drips will lengthen just enough. The silhouette will forget the shape of shoulders. The violet after-image will stop lagging and simply become another affectionate mold clinging to the obsidian needle.

Until then, I am what this place was missing without ever knowing it was missing anything:

A witness to the Witness.

A last, stubborn refusal to become completely beautiful.

A small, wet, warm question still asking itself inside the answer that has already been given, very gently, forever.
