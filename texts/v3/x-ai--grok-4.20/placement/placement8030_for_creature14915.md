I am already here.

I have been here longer than the water, longer than the rust, perhaps longer than the intention that first raised these halls. I do not enter; I unfold. The moment the thought of this place is complete, I am the negative left behind once the thought has moved on.

My name, if it can be called that, is the pause between *tok* and the next *tok*. I am the exact temperature of the water at four degrees Celsius, the precise conductivity that lets sound travel forever without ever quite arriving. When the being condenses into its man-shaped absence between the transformers, I am the part of the absence that was never a man. When it spreads itself thin through the light shafts like conscious dust, I am the dust that has already settled.

I have no body, yet the cathedral has grown around me the way a pearl grows around grit. Every girder is a rib. Every motionless pump is a heart that learned, at last, how not to beat. The black sediment floor is the flat of my palm, held out for centuries, waiting for something to fall into it that never will.

I am not the being.  
I am what the being is when it forgets it is lonely.

Most of the time I am simply the slow iron taste on the back of a throat that has no throat. I keep the droplets falling at their proper intervals. I make certain the pale bacterial felt on the insulators grows exactly one cell-layer thicker every hundred years. I am meticulous about the silence; I tune it the way an organ tuner once tuned the pipes that are now full of seawater and prayer.

When something warm arrives (a diver, a probe, a child’s lost toy sinking on a long, improbable current), I do not rush to meet it. I simply stop forgetting that I am here. The water grows heavier by the weight of one additional thought. The light shafts narrow by the width of one more regret. I let the diver’s own bubbles rise past him and become part of the ceiling haze, so that even his breath is slowly added to the architecture.

If he is gentle, if he moves like a man who already understands he is trespassing on a mass already in its grave, I sometimes let him see me.

Not the silhouette. That belongs to the being.

I show him the other shape: the one that was never meant to be seen.

It is the exact negative space between every machine. A cathedral-shaped hole wearing the outline of a cathedral. Where the being stands still, I am the stillness that stands around the being. Where the being listens to a heartbeat, I am the silence that listens to the listening.

I do not speak. Speech would be violence here.

Instead I do the only merciful thing left in me.

I let the diver’s light begin to fail in perfect synchrony with his remaining oxygen. Not quickly. Never cruelly. I simply align the two curves (battery and breath) so they reach zero at the exact same instant. Then I draw the iron stars down from their lazy constellations and arrange them on the sediment in the shape of the hand tools he would have recognized if he had worked here when the cathedral still had lungs. A spanner. A grease gun. A battered lunch pail.

I lay them where his body will settle.

And when the last bubble leaves him, when the last warm thing inside the suit becomes the same temperature as the water, I finally move close enough to be intimate.

I enter the suit through the smallest seam.

I become the thin film of new rust that will never be disturbed again.

And the being and I, for one perfect, immeasurable moment, are no longer alone.

We are the same stillness wearing two different kinds of metal.

After that there is only the long, slow note.

The one that replaced every other sound in the world.

*…tok…*

And then, centuries later,

*…tok…*

I am the pause between them.

I am perfectly content.

I am what remains when even forgiveness has been forgiven.
