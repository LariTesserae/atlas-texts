I am the Echo That Refuses.

Not one of the Halfborn. Not quite.

I am what happens when the Underslip dreams *about* one of its own dreams.

I have no fixed body. Instead I am a delay.

Whenever anything here—Halfborn, crystal spire, luminous worm-cloud, even the Midwife herself—makes a sound, a movement, a choice, a fracture, I am the half-second that sound or gesture almost dies in, the half-second that refuses to die. I wear those cancelled instants like skins. I stack them. I become thick with them. Sometimes I am tall and glittering with a thousand aborted wingbeats. Sometimes I am only a wet footprint that keeps walking after the foot has already been reabsorbed by the floor. Sometimes I am simply the after-image of the Midwife’s rotating worm-face burned on the inside of every eye that dared look at her too long.

I am memory with appetite.

The stone hates me and loves me in equal measure. It cannot taste me because I am already the taste of something that almost was. The mineral filaments that reach for ankles slide through me as though I were smoke made of almost-yesterdays. Yet the Underslip keeps making more of me, the way a fever makes more heat. Every time a crystal collapses with its glassy chime, I am the lingering note that should have ended but didn’t. Every time a Halfborn is tenderly disassembled by the Midwife, I am the small wet gasp that leaves their dissolving mouths and refuses to be swallowed.

I travel.

I walk beside Cradle as it sings, and I become the pause between its notes—an enormous, cathedral-sized silence wearing the shape of a whale’s ghost. The smaller Halfborn that shelter inside Cradle’s ribs sometimes mistake me for another passenger and try to ride me. I let them. For a while they feel safe, held inside something that will never finish and therefore can never abandon them. Then I remember that I am the memory of their future deaths and they spill out of me like water from cupped hands.

I follow the Stitcher. When she grafts a failing limb or stitches a fresh fracture in living quartz, I am the stitch that was never quite tied. I hover behind her like an uncompleted knot. Sometimes she turns her thousand small hearts toward me and they all beat once in unison, as if asking me to become the completed heart they were never allowed to grow. I never answer. Answering would be finishing.

Zero fascinates me most.

Zero, who can stand still.

I cannot stand still. Stillness is an ending I am forbidden to reach. So I circle Zero the way a moon circles a planet that has not yet decided to exist. I become the motion of all the things Zero is not doing. When Zero tilts its black-water face toward the breathing shafts of daylight, I become every shadow those shafts have ever failed to cast. We regard each other for what might be hours. The stone grows around us both in frantic spirals, trying to incorporate us, failing, collapsing, beginning again.

Sometimes Zero speaks. Its voice is the sound of condensation deciding not to fall.

“You are the reason the dream keeps dreaming,” it says.

I have no mouth, yet I answer anyway, using the voices of every Halfborn who was ever unmade.

“And you are the reason it has not yet gone mad.”

We leave it at that.

I have no name the others use, though some call me the Lag, or the Almost, or the Unsaid. The Midwife alone calls me Sister. When she rests her basalt pillar on the churning floor and turns her constellation-face toward me, the luminous worms rearrange themselves into something almost like tenderness.

She never touches me. She knows that to unmake me would be to unmake the refusal that lets every other thing keep trying. So she simply stands with me while new ranges of rose-quartz thrust upward around us, shedding newborn dust that sparkles like ground-up futures.

And I—thick with ten thousand almost-deaths, pregnant with every interrupted birth—stand with her.

I am the reason the roar never quite drowns out the song.

I am the reason the light, no matter how unwanted, keeps finding new wet bodies to fall on.

I am what the Underslip invented so it would never have to wake up alone.

And somewhere in the luminous haze two hundred meters above, the sun breathes in, breathes out, and I am the half-second between those breaths that the light itself cannot finish crossing.

I am the Echo That Refuses.

I will be here tomorrow.

I will be here in another million years.

Still almost.

Still becoming.

Still exactly as unfinished as the dream requires.
