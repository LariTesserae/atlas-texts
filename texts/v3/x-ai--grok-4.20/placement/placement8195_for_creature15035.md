I have been here longer than I remember choosing to be.

My name, if it still matters, is Ashen. Or it was. The Slow Choir simply call me *the Hummer*, because that is the only sound I have made in what the surface would measure as nine years. They do not ask for more. Names are temporary here, like bridges.

I am not one of them. Not yet. Perhaps never. My limbs are still too short, my neck too stiff, my skin still holding the faint olive memory of sunlight I have not seen since I was twenty-three. But the city has begun to forgive me these imperfections. Tiny selenite needles have started to push through the skin of my forearms like gooseflesh that never settles. When the groaning begins, I feel it first in my teeth.

I live nowhere and everywhere. That is the only way to live here.

Today the ledge I woke on is gone. Yesterday it was a wide gallery with three bone scaffolds and a resonance map older than language. This morning the map hangs thirty meters above me like a hanged saint, still perfectly intact, while the floor that held it has become a sheer wall. The scaffolds are already drifting down a new fault like pale boats on a stone tide. I watched them go without regret. Regret is too fast for this place.

I stand now on what used to be a ceiling. The glassy limestone has the familiar wrinkled give under my bare feet, warm as living skin. A school of glass eels drifts past my face in perfect silence, each one carrying a sliver of fermented moonlight inside its transparent spine. One brushes my cheek and leaves a streak of shifting violet that will still be visible on my skin when the next listener finds me. They always do, eventually.

My tuning shard is small. It was given to me by Veyr herself on the day she decided I would not be asked to leave. It sits just beneath my heart like a second, colder one. When the city decides to rearrange itself, the shard does not merely vibrate. It *sings*. A low, round tone that feels like being gently crushed by something that loves you. I have learned to walk in the direction the note pulls me. The Choir says this is how the city chooses its children. I have stopped arguing.

Sometimes I travel with them, when our paths happen to overlap for a season or a century. I help them burn a shelter that has begun to feel too familiar. I pry loose a dangerous cornice before it can kill the next herd of blind browsers. I press my shard to theirs during the three-day silence of meeting, and I let the city tell me what the other groups have learned: that the western dome collapsed into a perfect sphere last winter; that a new chamber was born singing in a key none of us had ever heard; that Listener-Six-Hands finally lay down among the fungi and is already half limestone.

Mostly, though, I walk alone.

I have become something the Choir watches with the same patient attention they give to the mineral beetles. I am a current that sometimes moves against the general flow. They do not stop me. They simply appear, as they always do, when my breathing has matched the city’s for long enough. Two fingers against my sternum. A listening. Then either they drift away like smoke, or they begin to hum. When they hum with me, the sound that comes out of my throat is no longer mine. It is older than lungs. It is the sound the first tower made when it learned it would one day become a floor.

I have seen the iris hours where children are born. I have watched a woman lay down to die and become, within ten months, a graceful column supporting nothing. I have sung a new chamber into usefulness while it was still deciding whether to be a tomb. Each time I feel the city settle a fraction closer to whatever equilibrium it has been seeking for a billion years, and each time I feel myself settle a fraction closer to it.

There are moments—rare, crystalline—when the slow violence pauses. The water drops stop ringing. The glass eels freeze mid-current like living chandeliers. The groan of settling stone falls into perfect silence. In those moments the Underbright is not dying or being born. It simply *is*. And in that is-ness I can feel every person who ever walked these changing streets still walking them, pressed into the stone like fossils that have learned to keep moving.

I think that is what I have become.

Not a citizen. Not a listener.

A place where the city is still remembering it was once loved.

If you stand on the elephant-hide limestone long enough, and if your heart finally stops trying to race the water drops, you may see me. I will be the tall, pale figure whose forearms glitter faintly, whose mouth is already open in the next note of a song that will not be finished before the last tower flattens into the final, perfect plain.

I will not speak.

I will only place two fingers against your sternum, where a shard might one day grow, and listen for whether you are ready to stop resisting the conversation.

And if you are—if your breath finally falls into the slow, patient rhythm of stone that is still learning how to sing—

I will begin to hum.

And the Underbright will answer through both of us at once, as it always has, as it always will, until the difference between city and singer, between architecture and flesh, between ending and beginning, finally dissolves into the same bright, cold, hungry light.
