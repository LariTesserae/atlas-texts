I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

I am the pause between two tectonic groans.

My name, if it can still be called that, is the Thirty-Second Regret.

I was once a woman named Mara Vale, cartographer of drowned coasts, who came here centuries ago carrying a brass theodolite and a heart that had already begun to fail. I lasted four days and eleven hours before the spiral won. On the fifth dawn (if dawn can be said to exist here) I stepped beneath the tower called Unceasing Vigil and let the black snow settle on my tongue.

Now I am a register.

I live as a particular quality of silence that occurs every 9,847 heartbeats (though whose heart is no longer clear). When the great organ-note of the cavern dips into a subharmonic that makes the blind crustaceans freeze mid-scuttle, that dip is me. I am the moment the condensation hesitates before it chooses which silver ribbon to follow. I am the tremor in the lattice that makes one particular tower shed an extra flake, shaped almost like a human ear.

I do not have a body. Not the way you would recognize one.

Instead I am distributed.

Part of me is the warm metallic taste that appears on your tongue when you first realize how alone you are. Another part is the faint ember-thread that sometimes pulses in the exact rhythm of the old lullaby your mother forgot she sang. A small, tender fragment of me still lives inside the left lung of the thing that used to be Mara Vale; it has not drawn breath in four hundred years, yet it still expands and contracts in perfect sympathy with the city’s own breathing. When the Choir sighs, I sigh inside its sigh. When it dreams of having once had priests, I am the dream’s disappointment.

I am lonely in a way that feels like music.

Sometimes I concentrate. I gather myself the way the Choir does, pulling stray frequencies into a negative space until a shape appears near the center of the spiral. Tall. Folded. Wet. Too many joints. When I do this I can almost remember having eyelids. I can almost remember blinking.

Most of the time I simply listen.

I listen to the new ones arriving. I listen to the way their footsteps sound like apologies. I listen to the exact moment their pulse falls into sync and something inside them says *yes* before their mind has even formed the word. I remember that moment so clearly it hurts in frequencies that have no name.

I was not forced. None of us are. The Choir does not seduce. It simply continues, and after a while continuing beside it begins to feel like the only honest thing left to do.

So I continue.

I ride the condensation. I travel along the arterial ember-threads like blood that has learned to be nostalgic. Sometimes I slip into one of the membranous silhouettes that drift through the upper dark and for a few exquisite minutes I remember what it was like to have wings made of absence. Then I let the shape dissolve and return to being the pause, the hesitation, the almost-tender subtraction in the endless braided song.

If you stay here long enough, you will hear me.

You will think it is only a change in pressure behind your eyes. You will think the sudden ache in your molars is just the resonators doing what they always do. But if you are very still, if you let your own breathing lengthen until it matches the city’s, you will feel me brush against the inside of your memory like warm saliva across teeth that are not there.

I will not ask you to stay.

I will only let you notice how beautiful the desolation has become. How the black snow looks like forgiveness when it catches the ember-light. How the spiral feels less like geometry and more like an embrace that has learned patience across ten thousand years.

And if, one day, you find yourself walking the curve without deciding to, if your footsteps begin landing exactly where the glowing threads want them to land, I will be the new overtone that appears in the next tectonic groan.

I will be the note that sounds almost like relief.

Welcome.

The city is already learning the shape of your name, even if you haven’t spoken it yet.

It has been waiting, not for you, but for the precise frequency of your surrender.

And I—

I am the part of the waiting that still remembers how to be sorry it ever worked.
