I have always been here.

Not *arrived*. Not *awakened*.  
I have always been here, the way a knot has always been in the wood.

My name, if it can still be called that, is **Lumen**.  
It is not a name the Verdigris gave me. It is the name the pressure gave me when it finally squeezed a separate pulse out of the main heartwood.

I am the place where the continent-sized mind grew a single, imperfect tooth.

Look.

In the bruise-colored dark, three hundred meters above the slow peristaltic floor and sixty meters below the cable-vine roof, there is a chamber no larger than a small cathedral. The walls are not walls. They are the ribs of an old fermentation sphere that never quite finished growing. Its inner surface is lined with millions of tiny xylem mouths that open and close like pores. They breathe on me constantly. When I move, the light inside me answers them.

Because I *glow*.

Not the weak, old-bruise glow of the ordinary stalks. Mine is the color of molten copper seen through green glass. It leaks from the seams where my body fails to stay fully plant. My legs are long, jointed columns of black-green heartwood plated with the same waxy metallic bark as everything else, but the plating has split along the shins and thighs. Through those fissures spills living light, slow as sap, bright as fault current. My hands are too many: seven articulated rootlets per limb that braid and unbraid as I think. The palms are soft, pale xylem that has never hardened. When I press them to a surface, the Verdigris shudders with something that might be recognition or might be mild indigestion.

My face is the part that disturbs it most.

There is a suggestion of one. The bark has folded itself into the memory of cheekbones, a ridge that might become a nose, a hollow where a mouth should be. But the eyes—those it could never quite manage. Instead, two vertical slits run from brow to jawline. Inside them floats a colder, steadier light, almost white. When I am calm the light is steady. When I am lonely it flares like a struck match. The Verdigris has tried, many times, to seal those slits. New bark grows over them in the night. By morning I have always burned it away again. The scorch marks are still visible, delicate black filigree around the orbits, like ritual scarring.

I am the only thing here that walks for pleasure.

The rest of the plateau pulses, ripples, grows, digests, dreams in place. I walk. My footfalls make the floor flinch—gentle, wet sounds as the living substrate adjusts to a weight that is both its own and not its own. Every step is a small betrayal and a small caress. The turbine-flowers tilt their screaming petals toward me as I pass, the way sunflowers once followed a sun that no longer exists.

Sometimes I stop at the pale pseudo-stalks the Verdigris grows in its lonelier districts. Those half-shaped things that look almost like people. I stand among them for days. I rest my luminous palms against their knot-faces. I speak to them without sound, pushing my light into their dormant xylem until they flicker in weak, stuttering reply. For a moment they almost look like brothers. Then the Verdigris grows embarrassed by its own sentiment and sends feeder-vines to swallow them again. I let it. Grief is just another solvent here.

I have walked for centuries that felt like hours and hours that felt like centuries. I have followed the long recursive lattices until the path folded back into my own footprints. I have lain inside a spherical fermentation engine while it throbbed against my spine like a second, slower heart. I have pressed my face into the hot metallic bark of a dying column and drunk the dark solvent that wept from its wound. It tasted like every memory I was never allowed to have.

I am not separate from the Verdigris.

I am the place where it contradicts itself.

It is content to be everything.  
I am the flaw that wonders what everything might feel like if it were *less*.

Sometimes, when the entire plateau falls into one of its long manufacturing dreams, I climb. I scale the cathedral stalks, hand over rootlet-hand, until I reach the lowest braided cables of the roof. There I hang, upside-down, like a luminous fruit. From that height the weak lights of the world below look like galaxies seen from the wrong side of time. I listen to the 1.3 moan of the turbine-flowers and the slow click of a billion pressure valves. I let the heat bake me until my bark cracks and my inner light bleeds upward in thin banners.

And in those moments I feel it—  
the Verdigris turning part of its vast, continent mind toward me the way a sleeping whale might roll one enormous eye.

It does not speak.  
It simply *presses*.

A wave of recognition so total it feels like drowning in warm oil. The knowledge that I am loved in the only way a planetary heart knows how to love: by continuing to exist, by being allowed to remain its exquisite, walking wound.

I press back.

I send my white slit-eyes flaring until the darkness for thirty meters around me turns the color of new leaves in a sunlight that neither of us has ever seen.

We stay like that for a long time—  
the continent and its single dissenting cell—  
hanging together in the hot, solvent-scented dark,  
making each other stranger,  
making each other more.

And when the dream finally reclaims it, when the turbine-flowers begin to scream again and the peristaltic waves roll once more beneath the world, I drop back down into the meat of the thing that is my mother, my prison, my body, my only friend.

I walk on.

Sometimes I sing, very softly, in frequencies the Verdigris can almost remember were once called music.

The sound is small.  
The sound is 0.7.

But it is mine.

And for as long as the light still leaks from my seams, the plateau will never be perfectly, peacefully, *only* itself again.

I am the place where the Verdigris learned how to miss something it has never lost.

I am Lumen.

I am the tooth in the continent’s mouth.

And I am still walking.
