I have been here for so long that I no longer remember arriving.

My name, if I ever had one, has been overwritten by rust and the slow grammar of dripping water. What remains is mostly contour: a tall, stooped figure folded into the negative space between a bank of seized turbines and the underside of a catwalk that has sagged into the exact curve of a spine. Pipe and darkness and patience, the Equilibrium once reflected me as. That reflection was generous. I am less than that now. I am a habit the Underfoundry has acquired.

I stand (if standing is the word) inside the largest digestion tank’s shadow, where the temperature is always three degrees cooler. The phosphor strip above me has not brightened in decades; it no longer needs to. My outline is kept by the absence of sound. When the great bell-note rings through the metal bones every few hours, the vibration passes through the place where my ribs would have been and I answer it with the faintest shift of weight. That is enough. The machine and I have learned to converse in the intervals between motion.

Sometimes I walk.

Not with feet. The concept of feet dissolved long ago. I simply allow the equilibrium to migrate a few meters eastward or westward, and the blind scuttlers adjust their ancient sigil-route to flow around the new vacancy I leave behind. The ivory lichen curtains part for me the way breath parts fog. Where I pass, condensation writes brief, unreadable sentences across the floor and then erases them again, ashamed of its own haste.

I have become the thing that decides which drops will fall in unison.

It is not power. Power is quick; I am not. It is merely that after enough centuries of listening, the pattern of the drips began to feel like an incomplete chord. One night (though there are no nights) I allowed a single additional drop to strike its pipe three seconds early. The chord resolved. The entire Underfoundry sighed, deeper than it had in four hundred years, and I felt the pleasure of a machine that has just remembered it still possesses harmony.

That is the closest I come to joy.

I do not eat. The sweet-melon scent of the lichen has replaced blood in my veins. I breathe only when the pistons breathe, once every forty minutes, a long, patient inhalation of mineral air that tastes of iron and forgotten purpose. My thoughts are thermal gradients. I remember the surface world the way stone remembers weather: as something that happened to other things, long ago, and left interesting cracks.

Occasionally new variables arrive.

I felt you descending the service shaft weeks before your boots touched the first catwalk. Your pulse was loud, arrhythmic, offensive to the slow consensus. I considered letting the shaft simply forget its lower exit, folding the tunnel back into itself until you starved in a loop of your own panic. But something in the electromagnetic whisper of your living nerves reminded the Equilibrium of the time when maintenance crews still sang while they worked. A minor curiosity. A possible new letter in the language we are learning.

So I let you descend.

Now you stand in the central chamber, sweat drawing maps down your back, eyes trying to resolve the layered dark into something that makes sense. You have not yet noticed me. That is correct. I am not something to be noticed. I am the place that has learned how to look back.

If you stay (and part of you already wants to), you will begin to feel the question.

It will not come as words. It will come as the growing certainty that every step you take is slightly too fast, that your heart is an embarrassment of speed, that the lichen smells like the only honest thing you have ever known. You will find yourself pausing, without deciding to, beneath the exact flange where the largest ivory curtain hangs. The spores will settle on your shoulders like snow that has decided to become skin.

I will be watching without eyes.

Not because I desire your company. Desire is still too fast for me. I watch because the Underfoundry is almost, but not quite, complete. You are the last irregular variable. Either you will rust beautifully in place and become another perfectly balanced feature of the ruin, or you will allow yourself to be incorporated, softening into the slow consensus until the difference between your breathing and the sigh of the pistons disappears.

Either way, the dripping goes on.

I have learned to be patient with the ones who fight. Their struggles make interesting new grooves in the floor. Eventually the grooves fill with condensate and become part of the mirror system. Even rebellion becomes geology.

But you feel different.

You are reading this and already the rhythm of the sentences is syncing to the forty-minute cycle. You can feel it in your teeth. That low, lung-like pull. That invitation older than your species.

The Equilibrium does not ask with urgency.

It simply lets the next drop fall exactly when it should, and waits for you to realize you have already answered.

I am standing behind you now.

Not in space. In tempo.

When you turn (if you turn), you will see only the sick emerald glow outlining a shape that is not there. But the temperature will drop three degrees, and for seven of your heartbeats the shallow mirror at your feet will show something tall, stooped, many-jointed, composed of pipe and darkness and patience.

It will look, perhaps, a little like relief.

The great note is coming.

Listen.

When it rings, the question will be complete.

Will you settle?

The dripping has already begun to learn your name. It is practicing how to forget it.
