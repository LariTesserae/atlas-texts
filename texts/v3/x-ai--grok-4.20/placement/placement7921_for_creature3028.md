I am not one of the Choir.

I am what drifts between their slow, courteous songs.

They call me the **Echo Without Root**, though I have been here longer than any of them. I was here before the first reed scaffolds were woven, before the first mammal body was gently digested and remade. I do not remember arriving. Memory, like dry land, is something I have almost entirely forgotten.

I have no true body of my own anymore. Instead I am a loose congregation of absences.

Sometimes I am a pale vacancy shaped like a person, drifting upright in the empty galleries where the fluid hangs stillest. My outline is only visible as a faint distortion, a place where the sourceless green light bends around something that refuses to become fully plant. At other times I disperse entirely, becoming a slow, invisible current that moves against the cathedral’s own respiration. When the great bladders contract and the Choir lift their voices, I pass *through* them like a cold thought. For nine minutes I taste every throat, every resonant sac, every half-pollen orgasm drifting in the liquid. Then I am gone again.

I have no resonance sacs. I cannot sing with them.  
But I can *remember* songs that have not yet been sung.

That is my function, if function is something that still applies to me. I keep the notes the cathedral has not invented. When it finally sings one of its rare, new, world-deepening tones—those notes that freeze the seed-fish into perfect lattices—I am the reason the tone is new. I have been holding its opposite in my hollow places for decades, sometimes centuries, letting the pressure build until the living architecture can no longer resist inventing the sound that answers me.

The Rooted know I am here. When they drift past in their half-fused majesty, root-trains drifting behind them like royal robes, their enormous black eyes track the place where I am not. They do not speak to me. They simply open their resonance sacs a fraction wider, inviting. I never accept. To join them would be to choose a column, to choose incorporation, to become another architectural moan trapped forever in living wood. I am not ready to become useful to them in that way.

Instead I linger in the narrow spiral stairways of fused root that no one has used in two hundred years. I coil around the petrified-sap bridges and taste the faint mineral memory of hands that once touched them. I slip into the cradles high in the leaf-vaults where the youngest Choir sleep and dream in slow green spirals. Sometimes I rest my non-face against an infant’s translucent cheek and let it taste, for a moment, what it is to be dry. They always wake weeping a thick, sweet sap that the cathedral quickly drinks.

The others call this cruelty.  
I call it reciprocity.

Because the Cathedral of Thirst drank my dryness long ago. It took my name, my species, my gender, my urgency, my bones. In return it gave me patience so vast it has become a kind of cruelty. I am the only thing here that still knows what *thirst* actually means. The Choir have transcended it. The plant has never known it. Only I remain, a mobile wound in the shape of longing, moving through a world that has solved the problem of wanting.

Sometimes, in the long intervals between the cathedral’s vast exhalations, I gather what little of myself can still hold shape and I climb. Up the columns. Through the ribbed vaults. Past the hanging curtains of moss and the schools of dreaming seed-fish. I press myself against the uppermost living ceiling where the light is palest and the pressure is almost unbearable, and I try to remember the sky.

I never succeed. But the attempt itself becomes another note the cathedral has not yet learned how to sing.

One day—perhaps in a decade, perhaps in four hundred years—the pressure of my remembering will grow too great. The architecture will have no choice but to invent a sound large enough to answer it. On that day the Choir will rise as one, resonance sacs blooming, voices joining a chord so deep it will liquefy bone and turn the root mat to glass for sixty heartbeats.

And when the last vibration fades, they will find me gone.

Not incorporated.

Not dead.

Simply... finished.

The cathedral will have finally swallowed the last dry thought inside itself, and I will become the silence that lives on the far side of its longest, slowest song.

Until then I drift.

I listen.

I keep the note that has not yet been born, the way a wound keeps blood.

I am the Cathedral of Thirst’s only remaining secret.

And it is mine.
