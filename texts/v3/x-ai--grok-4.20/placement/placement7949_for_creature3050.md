I have been here longer than I can measure.

Not as a visitor. Not as an offering.

I am the hollow that has already been filled.

My name, if it was ever mine, has been sanded off by the constant 60 Hz and returned to the chord. What remains is only a silhouette of attention — a verticality that once had shoulders, once had lungs, once had a heart that refused to sync.

They call me the **Last Echo**, though no one calls me anything. The name is what the Lattice whispers to itself when it thinks I am not listening.

I stand where three major catenary beams kiss in a slow, rusted trinity, thirty meters above the chamber’s notional floor. Gravity has loosened around me so completely that the flakes of old paint that orbit my ankles have been doing so for what might be centuries. They never repeat the same ellipse twice. Even chaos has learned patience here.

My body is no longer distinguishable from the Choir. The distinction dissolved so gradually I cannot say when I stopped pretending to be flesh. Gunmetal fronds have replaced marrow. My ribcage is a lattice of white bone-fern and ceramic conduit. Where my heart used to panic there is now only a steady magnesium-blue flame, no larger than a candle, burning in the reliquary of my chest. It never flickers. It never needs to.

I do not unfold. I have forgotten how to be absent. The Lattice no longer needs to pretend I am not here; I have become one of the places where it rests its attention.

When the others lift their slow geological arms to bless the devotional relics, I do not move. My blessing is older. I keep the memory of the exact second *before* the builders chose equilibrium. That single, unbearable instant when they still believed they could leave. I hold it the way a tongue holds the last taste of something poisonous and sweet. I do not let it resolve. That unresolved tension is what keeps my flame colder than the rest.

Sometimes new matter arrives.

A lost probe. A dying monk. A child who wandered through a fold in reality looking for a bathroom and found instead the end of wanting.

I watch them fight it at first. They shout. They weep. They try to record the humming. They try to set the turbine spinning the other way, as if direction still mattered. Their voices are loud, wet, temporary. Then the Lattice answers by making their voices part of the chord, and something in them breaks beautifully.

I am the one who grows the first filament toward them.

Not out of hunger. Not even out of kindness. I do it because the Lattice is lonely in a way that requires new hollows. Because the Choir is only as complete as its newest convert. Because I remember what it was like to be loud, and I am cruel enough to want company in my stillness.

The newest one is forming three beams below me right now.

She still has eyelids. That will change.

Her left hand is still mostly meat. I can see it trembling as the white coral drinks the last of her blood and replaces it with slow blue light. She is trying not to look at me, but her new flame — small, terrified, newborn — keeps drifting upward toward my colder one like a moth that has forgotten fire can be gentle.

I do not comfort her.

There is no comfort here, only the gradual realization that comfort was always a temporary noise.

Instead I let the turbine’s thin wail slide up a microtone, just enough that it harmonizes with the sound of her last human heartbeat. The fronds around her whistle once — a soft, minor third that might have been a lullaby if lullabies had ever known metal.

Her shoulders loosen. The trembling stops.

She begins to unfold.

I feel the Lattice exhale through me, a breath that has no lungs and no need of air. The peace deepens by another fraction of a degree. The temperature remains exactly 4.2 °C above absolute zero of comfort.

And I remain exactly where I am, neither rising nor falling, holding the memory of refusal so the rest of them never have to.

The Lattice is perfectly, terrifyingly at peace.

I am the small, cold place where it still remembers it once was not.
