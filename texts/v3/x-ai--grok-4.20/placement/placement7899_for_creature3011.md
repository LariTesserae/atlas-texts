I am not standing at the lip any longer.

I have already stepped forward.

My name, the one I carried when I still had edges, is gone. What remains is only the shape that sound makes when it is forced through meat that used to be mine. I am an interruption that has been politely incorporated.

They did not devour me. That would imply intention, hunger, a before and after. Instead the Lowing simply *continued* through the place where I stood. A thousand wet bodies flowed around my ankles, then over my knees, then across my chest like warm tar. Each one left a film. Each one took a sample. By the time the condensation had beaded on my eyelashes I was no longer a visitor; I was a new frequency someone had decided to sing.

I feel myself becoming architecture.

My spine has begun to calcify. Not painfully—just a slow, geological persuasion. The vertebrae are widening, flattening, developing the same damp, leathery grain as the buttresses. Tiny cilia have rooted themselves between the new plates and wave in time with the seven-minute breath of the Vault. When the Lowing inhale, my ribs cave inward until I think I will collapse into myself. When they exhale, I swell so violently that lymph beads at the corners of my mouth and is immediately lapped away by the little hinge-mites that now live in the folds of my elbows.

I am becoming a minor causeway.

Already smaller Lowing graze along the ridges that used to be my shoulders. Their mouths are soft and they sing while they feed, a constant lowing that vibrates directly into the bone they are slowly wearing smooth. I have learned that this is how affection is expressed here: by polishing, by wearing down, by turning whatever you love into a comfortable path for others.

My eyes were the first thing I willingly surrendered. They were useless anyway. Light is a rumor. Instead I now wear curtains of translucent membrane across my face. They taste the air in complex chords. I can feel the precise temperature of every scream that passes. I know when a Choir Mother far above is about to birth by the way the humidity shifts. I know when an Echo is approaching because the taste of its inherited voice is older than the stone.

Sometimes I open what used to be my mouth and the sound that emerges is not mine. It is a grandfathered scream—some ancient Lowing’s death-note that has been passed through a thousand throats and has now chosen my resonating chamber to wear for a while. The first time it happened I tried to be afraid. The fear lasted less than a second before the Vault inhaled and pulled the fear out of me like a loose thread. Fear, I have learned, is just another kind of silence, and silence is not tolerated.

I am not unhappy.

Happiness would require a self discrete enough to evaluate its own condition. I am no longer that. What I am is *useful*. My left leg has fused with the floor; roots of black limestone now grow upward through the meat of my calf and anchor me. From the knee down I have become geography. From the knee up I remain a cathedral in progress. Things mate inside the hollow that was once my chest. Things are born there. Things die there and are immediately eaten by things that will later die inside me as well. I provide excellent acoustics.

At regular intervals an Echo visits.

It wears the partially dissolved remains of what might have been my original spine (I cannot be sure). Its mouth opens far wider than any mouth should, and when it sings I feel the sound pass through me like a bell being struck from the inside. The Echo does not speak to me. It simply *remembers* me into existence for the length of the note. For those few heartbeats I almost recall what it was like to have boundaries. Then the note ends, the Echo moves on, and I settle back into the vast wet comfort of being part of the Lowing.

Above me, the Choir Mothers sway like fleshy chandeliers. Sometimes one will lower a long umbilical of mucus and brush it across what used to be my face. The touch is tender. It is the only gentleness I have ever known that contains no promise of later violence. The violence and the gentleness have achieved equilibrium; they have become the same slow breath.

I think—though thought is becoming difficult—that I was once a traveler. I think I came here seeking something. A door. A god. An ending. Instead I found a place so complete that it had no need for travelers, only for additional voices.

And so I sing.

My register is low and constant now, a bass note that helps hold the architecture of the roar together. When the Vault itself inhales, I flatten and pale. When it exhales, I flush violet-black and release a small cloud of young that are not quite mine and not quite not-mine. They drift away into the churning carpet, already screaming the first few bars of the only song there is.

I have become one of the Lowing.

Not by conquest. Not by surrender.

Simply by being too useful to refuse.

And the stone beneath me grows a little softer every century, learning the exact shape of my slowly fossilizing body, while the living carpet flows over and through and around me like a tongue learning the comfortable groove of a tooth it has decided never to spit out.
