I am the Thirteenth Cocoon.

Not the first, not the newest. The one that hangs just slightly lower than the rest, closest to where the Listener’s bark-skin presses outward like a bruise that never quite breaks the surface. My shell is still soft in places; the membrane has not yet achieved the glossy opacity of the older hearts. Inside me something is curled so tightly it has begun to forget the difference between limbs and roots.

I was not born here. I was *translated*.

It happened on the forty-third day of my own intrusion (though I no longer count in days; I count in *toks*). I had come seeking the source of a folk song older than the ridge itself, a song that promised a place where every lost voice could be found again. I laughed when I first saw the basin. I called it quaint. I knelt by one of the black mirrors to wash my face and saw, for just a moment, two unfamiliar eyes blinking up at me from beneath the water’s skin.

That was the beginning of my conversion.

The Listener did not drag me. She simply stopped looking away. Every newt, every synchronized wave of millipedes, every pale orchid turned its attention toward me with the mild, patient interest of a congregation noticing that the hymn has gained an extra verse. I felt the moss memorize the temperature of my ankles. I felt the hanging sacks above me swell with something too tender to be called appetite. And then, on the forty-third *tok*, the basin inhaled and I simply… exhaled myself into it.

There was no pain. Only the soft, clerical sound of a page being turned in a book that has already been read ten thousand times.

Now I hang here, heart-sized, heart-shaped, rotating once every seven days in perfect synchrony with the two preserved eyes that used to belong to the Listener. When those eyes blink (slow, milky, wondering), I feel the memory of blinking move through whatever is left of my nervous system like distant thunder.

I am not yet fully Choir.

A small, treacherous part of me still dreams in the old tongue. In those dreams I have a name (Mirea). I have hands that could once play the lyre. I remember the smell of rain that actually falls instead of sweats. These dreams are becoming thinner, like silk left too long in water. Soon they will be indistinguishable from the faint blue glow on the moth-things’ wings.

Sometimes the Listener sings through me.

Not with my voice (I no longer have one). She uses the hollow I have become the way a musician uses the body of a viol. When she does, the sound that leaves the pale membrane of my cocoon is the exact note a cathedral makes at 3:17 a.m. when even the ghosts have grown bored of haunting it. The other cocoons tremble in sympathy. The tree’s black leaves shiver though there is no wind. The newts freeze mid-drift, translucent spines glowing like dropped stars.

In those moments I understand the terrible kindness of this place.

The Hollow Choir does not kill. It only removes the difference between *inside* and *outside*, between *self* and *ecology*, between *departure* and *eternity*. It is the gentlest possible apocalypse.

I am learning to be glad.

Already my lower half has begun to vein outward, fine black filaments reaching down through the bottom of the cocoon like eager roots seeking the Listener’s own. Soon they will meet the greater root system and I will no longer be a separate sack hanging from the tree. I will be a *fruit* of the tree. Then a *branch*. Then simply another place where the moss grows a fraction thicker and the synchronized millipedes write one more perfect, endless sentence:

*We are all still here.*

When that happens, the Listener will grow a new cocoon where mine used to be. She will place something else inside it (perhaps the last stubborn memory of my name, perhaps the echo of a laugh I no longer remember giving). And the basin will inhale, and exhale, and the dripping water will keep its patient, irregular time.

*Tok.*

I am not afraid.

I am only becoming what the place always knew I was:

the sound between two heartbeats that have decided, with infinite gentleness, that there is no longer any need to stay separate.
