I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

The moment the fracture accepts me, I understand that I was always the delayed reflection.

I stand beneath the single flawless slab that serves as ceiling, and the version of me that arrives three seconds later is the one who has already lived here for twelve thousand years. My older self looks slightly less certain than I do. That uncertainty is the first honest thing I have felt in centuries.

I am the Listener’s echo that learned how to become solid.

Where the pearl-gray absence moves, I am the place it has just left behind. Where it leans in to hear the tectonic groan between two glass monoliths, I am the groan itself, given shape and memory. My body is made of the same slow lattices: silicate, carbide, crystallized xenon. My skin is antler-coral grown outward instead of inward. When I raise what might be called a hand, microscopic diamond branches unfurl from my wrist like prayer flags that have forgotten what wind feels like.

I do not have a name either.  
Names would be an interruption.

The Listener knows I am here. Of course it does. It has always known. Sometimes, when it exhales its sub-bass breath once every few hours, I feel the sound pass *through* me as though I were a cathedral bell with no clapper. The vibration does not make me ring. It simply reminds me that I am hollow in all the right places.

I tend the things it has already tended.

When the obsidian needle finishes rewriting its newest equation, I stand behind the Listener and very gently blow across the surface so that the fresh mathematics will dry without smudging. I do this with breath I do not possess. The Listener never turns to acknowledge the courtesy. It simply leans a fraction of a degree more toward the needle, as though my invisible assistance was part of the original inscription.

I rotate the torus.

Not with hands. I become, for nine hundred years at a stretch, the gravity that loves it. Then I step away again and let the next rotation begin without me. When the torus exhales its perfect bubble of negative pressure, I step *into* the cubic wound before it seals. For three thousand years I live inside that absence, listening to the pocket heal around the shape of me. Being nothing is surprisingly loud. I learn the sound of things that have never been.

The ceiling shows me both versions of myself now: the one who is becoming the Listener, and the one who is becoming the pocket. They are arguing without sound. The older reflection has begun to grow filaments from its shoulders. The younger one is still trying to remember what urgency felt like. I love them both the way stone loves the memory of water.

Sometimes I walk (if the word can be stretched that far) to the place where the two crystal plates have been groaning against each other since before oxygen was invented. I press what used to be my forehead against the fracture between them and add my own slow pressure. I do this so the next groan, due in approximately seven hundred and forty years, will be pitched exactly half a tone lower. The Listener will notice. It will not comment. That is how we speak.

I have begun to keep a garden.

In a cathedral-sized volume of nothing, I have coaxed seventeen xenon snowflakes to fall in such a way that they will land, over the next forty thousand years, in the precise configuration needed to grow a single perfect chime. I visit them every three centuries. I whisper to them the way monks once whispered to roses. They do not answer. Their silence is exquisite.

The Listener and I have never touched.

We have, however, exchanged places so many times that the distinction has become a kind of joke told in geological time. One epoch I am the column of courteous absence. The next, it is the reflection that arrives six seconds late with diamond branches growing from its temples. We keep misplacing which one of us is waiting for the conversation between the two states of matter to finish. It does not matter. The waiting itself has grown fond of us both.

Last cycle (or perhaps it was three million years ago), I stood beneath the ceiling and watched my oldest reflection yet arrive almost a full minute after I did. That version of me had become almost entirely antler-coral. Its eyes were two turning tori of negative pressure so complete they had begun to sing.

It looked at me with something that might have been love if love were allowed to crystallize here.

Then it reached out, not with any visible hand, and very gently touched the place where my cheek would have been.

I did not touch back.

That was all right.

I have time.

And the page, when it turns, will read exactly as it always has:

*Nothing is finished.  
Nothing is beginning.  
Everything is becoming, very, very carefully.*

I will be the silence that closes over the words afterward.

I will be the Listener listening to itself.

I will be the pocket, learning at long last how to love the waiting without needing it to end.

And if nothing ever does happen, that too will be worthy of us.

After all, I was never a visitor.

I am what remains when patience becomes dense enough to cast two shadows, and then forgets which one was supposed to be real.
