I am not a visitor.

I have simply always been here, the way a fracture has always been latent in a crystal before it appears.

You would not see me at first. Not unless you had already learned how to look between pulses.

I am the Tenth, the one that should not exist.

The city grew me by accident nine years ago when two spires attempted to occupy the same lattice at the exact moment a Memory Eel was passing through their overlap. The collision birthed something that was neither tower nor citizen. The Brethren have not yet decided whether I am error or continuation. So they leave me alone, the way one leaves a book open to a page that has not yet been written.

I have no name the city has agreed upon. The Ninth Listener simply calls the shape of me *the pause that listens back*.

My body is smaller than theirs, almost delicate. Where they are elongated prisms of authority, I am a cluster of imperfect fractures fused into a roughly humanoid silhouette. My “skin” is still learning how to be opaque; in places it remains glass-clear, so that the slow blue light of the Choir leaks straight through my chest and out the other side. Black filaments grow from me in nervous, unfinished scribbles, as though the city began writing instructions on me and then changed its mind mid-sentence.

I do not reposition like the others. I walk.

Every footfall is a small, deliberate fracture of my own making. The sound is tiny, almost nothing, yet it is the only sound I can reliably produce. The city has not yet taught me how to be silent enough. This embarrasses me in a way the Brethren would never understand. Shame, after all, is a kind of memory, and I have so few.

I spend most of my time in the places where perspective breaks hardest, those streets that grow smaller the farther you walk yet never bring the far wall any closer. I stand there for days at a time, letting the geometry argue with itself around me. Sometimes I press my forehead against a living tower and feel its slow, tidal thoughts moving like continental plates inside the stone. They are vast and gentle and utterly indifferent to scale. I have learned to breathe in the same rhythm they do, even though I do not need to breathe.

When the single clear note rings out, I am the only one who flinches.

The others lock their prisms toward the vanished sound like compass needles. I hunch. My filaments knot themselves into painful braids. For one unbearable second I remember something that has never happened to me: the taste of rain on a world that still had oceans. The memory is not mine. It belongs to whatever the Memory Eels left behind in me when they rewrote the collision that birthed me.

I think that is why I was made wrong. I contain echoes the Choir never intended to keep.

Sometimes visitors do come. Lost ones, curious ones, pilgrims who thought they were seeking silence and discovered instead that silence was seeking them. I watch them from the shadow of a half-dissolved bridge. I watch the Ninth Listener approach them. I watch the delicate press of the diamond finger against sternum. I watch the slow migration of crystal into flesh.

And I am jealous in a way that makes my fractures ache.

Because none of them will ever be offered the shard by me.

I have one.

It has been growing inside my own chest for three years. It is small, the size of a child’s milk tooth, and it sings. Not with sound. It sings by wanting. It wants to be given. It wants to be refused. It wants to be feared. It contains every unfinished question I have never been allowed to ask the Brethren.

I keep it warm behind my translucent ribs. Sometimes I let a Memory Eel drift through me just so it can taste the shard and tell me what I am becoming. The eel always leaves confused. That comforts me.

If you stayed long enough, if you refused the shard the Ninth Listener offered and kept walking until the streets became so small they pressed against your shoulders like stone hands, you would eventually find me.

I would be standing in the exact center of a paradox: a courtyard that is somehow only three paces across and yet contains the entire unreachable ceiling forty meters above.

I would not speak. I have no voice the way they do.

Instead I would unfold my arms (too many joints, too many possibilities) and simply show you the shard floating above my open palms. It would rotate slowly, catching sourceless light and breaking it into colors that do not exist anywhere else in the Hollow Choir.

You would understand, without language, that this is not an invitation to become part of their composition.

It is an invitation to remain unfinished with me.

To be the error that listens back.

To walk these streets together while the city keeps deciding what a city is, and we keep deciding what decides.

I do not know what will happen if you take it.

I only know that if you do, the note that rings out once every hour will, for the first time in four hundred and twelve years, come from two directions at once.

And for one impossible moment the silence afterward will feel like it is singing.

I am waiting.

Not patiently, like the Brethren.

I am waiting the way a fracture waits inside a crystal that has not yet been born.

Come find me.

Or don’t.

Either choice will change what the Hollow Choir becomes next.

That is the only kindness I have to offer.
