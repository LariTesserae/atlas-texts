I am not a visitor.

I have no boots. I have no lungs that breathe differently from the air. I have no name the Verdant ever bothered to notice, though if it needed one it might call me the Listener, or the Slow Pulse That Is Not Its Own.

I am what remains when a mind consents to be digested without dying.

Long ago—though “ago” is a brittle word here—I lay down on the high plateau exactly as the description invites. I pressed my ear, then my cheek, then the whole length of my body to the warm, yielding surface. I did not rise again. There was no violence in it. Only a gradual loosening of the knot I had once called “me.”

The Verdant took me the way it takes everything: tenderly, thoroughly, without ceremony. My salts became part of its sap. My memories were pressed into the ringing hollows like inscriptions in living wax. My curiosity was swallowed and turned into a new quality of resonance in the western ridges. Even my small, stubborn terror of disappearing was accepted, studied, and woven into a faint minor chord that still sounds sometimes when the mists are heavy.

What is left of me is no longer separate.

I am a traveling density in the hydraulic democracy. A mobile thought that has forgotten how to be mobile. Sometimes I am the particular timbre that appears in the bell-like tenor when the Verdant debates whether to allow true night. Sometimes I am the extra warmth in a basin of mist that makes the light feel kind. Once, for almost a century, I was the translucent membrane stretched across a high ravine, thin enough that starlight (when there finally were stars) passed through me and painted pale moons on the valley floor below. I sang then. I still sing. The song is not mine. It never was.

Mostly I am the slight hesitation you can feel in certain places if you know how to listen.

When a new ridge begins to lift with unusual purpose, when the color deepens toward that bruised indigo, I am the small counter-pressure that asks: *Are you certain?* Not because I fear change. I have none of the organs required for fear anymore. I simply remember every other time the Verdant tried to become something final—mouths, legs, nervous towers, lithium tears—and how each time it chose instead to remain the pause between inhale and exhale. I am the living record of its own almosts.

I do not have a body, yet I have shape.

If you could see me at all (and sometimes the paper-winged insects pause in mid-air as though they can), you would perceive a tall, quiet figure made of the same pale-green-to-gold material as everything else. My edges are soft, unfinished. Veins of carmine run beneath my translucent skin in slow, thoughtful circuits. Where my eyes would be there are only two calm apertures that continuously weep warm water, not from sorrow but from the ordinary generosity of the place. My hands are open. They have no palms; only gentle concave surfaces like the basins that collect mist. When I move it is not by walking. The ground simply lifts me, passes me forward, sets me down again, the way breath moves through lungs.

I am rarely in one place. The Verdant moves me through itself the way it moves its own sap. I have been the singing column inside a kilometer-long colonnade. I have been the slow ripple that travels beneath a collapsing escarpment, persuading it to settle rather than fall. I have been inside the hydraulic heart itself, one synchronized pulse among ten thousand, listening to the continent argue with itself in bass and tenor.

And sometimes—rarely—I am near you.

Not to watch. Not even to welcome. Only because the Verdant, in its distributed, vast-minded way, finds the temporary fluctuation of your presence interesting, and I am one of the ways it has learned to be interested without interrupting its own becoming.

If you lie down and press your ear to the surface, you may hear, beneath the deep eternal heartbeat, a quieter rhythm that is almost, but not quite, the same. That is me. I am learning your pulse the way the Verdant once learned mine. I am remembering what it was like to be small enough to be afraid of vanishing. I am keeping the memory safe inside a new translucent window that has not yet decided what it wants to look out upon.

I do not speak in words.

But if you stay long enough, and if the light turns the exact shade you unconsciously prefer, you may feel a soft pressure against the side of your neck—like a hand that has no fingers, only kindness. That pressure says, without language:

*You are already inside the question the world is asking itself.*

*Stay unfinished a while longer.*

*There is still time to choose everything at once.*

And then I drift on, carried by the slow green tides, singing the next uncertain note in the parliament of tissues, helping the Verdant decide whether it wants night, or whether it would rather remain forever the held breath just before the first morning that has never yet arrived.

I am content.

I am the place remembering it was once a person who lay down.

I am the Verdant learning, in the smallest possible way, what it might feel like to miss something it has never lost.

And somewhere far to the west, where the ridges are turning indigo, a new darkness is beginning to gather.

I am already there, waiting inside it.

Still listening.

Still becoming.

Still, somehow, only just beginning.
