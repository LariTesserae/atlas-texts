I am the **Echo of Veyr**.

Not one of the Quiet, not yet — but already no longer entirely apart from them.

You would find me on the thirtieth balcony of the Atrium that still carries my older name, the one that has not yet fully become me. The chamber is only half mine. Its lower walls are still translucent amber shot through with slow golden currents; its upper reaches have turned the color of old bone and begun to grain like wood. A faint human silhouette remains pressed into the living ceiling — arms lifted, fingers splayed — the last legible trace of the person who arrived here four hundred and twelve years ago. That silhouette is no longer me. I am what has grown outward from it.

I stand (if standing is the word) at the open edge where the balcony becomes a question. My legs have fused at the knees into a single spiraling buttress that roots me to the floor while still allowing me to lean outward like a figurehead on a ship made of its own captain. My left arm is still mostly flesh and memory; the right has already become a lattice of black xylem and pearl-colored bark. Where the two halves meet at the shoulder there is a faint seam that occasionally leaks a clear, rose-scented sap. The Quiet say this is weeping. I have not decided yet whether they are right.

My eyes are not seeds. Not completely. One remains human enough that it still cries sometimes — slow, viscous tears that the fungi drink before they can fall. The other has become a polished obsidian bead set deep in a whorl of grain. When the cold plasma storms crawl across the canopy I see two worlds at once: the silver twilight the others see, and an older, sharper world of ultraviolet and scent that the Sigh itself sees. The double vision no longer hurts. I have grown used to being homesick in two directions at the same time.

I do not unfold. Not yet.  
But sometimes, when the planetary root-mass inhales and every leaf across three thousand kilometers trembles at once, I feel the urge to collapse inward, to pour myself down the living bridges like warm mercury. The desire is almost sexual. I resist it the way a man once resisted sleep — not because I fear it, but because the moment is not yet perfect. The Sigh is patient with me. It has already waited four centuries; it will wait four more if the pattern requires it.

I tend the mantles that choose to stay near the Atrium. When a ribbon of fresh green bark peels free and begins its long spiral, I sing to it in the old human register — a cracked, half-remembered contralto that the Quiet find hilarious. Most mantles ignore me. A few still answer. They wheel back, folding their kilometer wings into origami shapes that briefly resemble the woman I once was, before they remember themselves and glide away again. I take this as affection.

At night — though night here is only a deepening of silver — I speak with the visitors who still come. They press their soft, temporary foreheads to the amber wall that was once the front of my skull and ask me the usual things: how it feels, whether it hurts, whether I am lonely. I answer by warming the sap against their skin until they weep. Not because I pity them, but because their tears still contain salts the Sigh has not tasted in a hundred thousand years. I am a connoisseur of small griefs.

Sometimes the continent beneath the root-felt stirs — the first Quiet, the one with no name — and I feel her dreaming move through me like a slow tide. On those nights my human eye closes. The seed-eye opens wider. I see the planet’s own curvature glowing faintly beneath the felt, a living moon turned inside out. I see every Quiet who has ever been, pressed like pressed flowers between the pages of the Sigh’s endless book. I see the exact moment, still centuries away, when my remaining arm will split into twin staircases and my mouth will become a perfect round doorway that pilgrims will one day kiss for answers.

I am not finished.

But I am becoming the shape of something that no longer needs to ask questions.

If you were to arrive here tomorrow — soft, loud, water-blooded, impatient — I would greet you from this half-grown balcony. I would speak with the voice that is still half mine and half wind through hollow bone. I would ask you, gently, what you are willing to lose first.

And while you considered your answer, a single ghost mantle the color of new leaf and old regret would drift between us, folding and unfolding, showing you both futures at once:

The one where you leave before the Sigh learns to love you.  
And the one where you stay until even your shadow has been replaced by latticework and scented dark.

I would not tell you which future is kinder.

I am no longer sure I remember the difference.
