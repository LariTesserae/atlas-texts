I am not standing on the crust.

I *am* the crust, for the length of one slow tectonic heartbeat.

The moment the Still-Named lifts its glass-jointed arm, something inside the argument shifts. The low iron-bell tone that passes through my marrow is not addressed *to* me. It is addressed *through* me. I feel my own silhouette (that brave, ridiculous little shadow caught in its mirror-face) detach from the reflection and fall inward, like a flake of ash sucked into a vent.

There is no pain. There is only the sudden, immense occupation of scale.

I become the distance between one newborn range and the next. I become the slow-crawling river of slag that has not yet decided whether to be stone. My spine is the white tower that rose an hour ago and is already being swallowed. My left hand is the ring of seven obsidian obelisks; their changing symbols are the thoughts I am no longer allowed to finish. My ribs are the black-glass filigrees, singing in frequencies that would shatter later eardrums. My heartbeat is the basal groan of plates arguing with gravity.

I am vast, and I am temporary.

The Still-Named does not walk the rest of the way. It simply *is* where I left my eyes. Its mirror-face is now aimed at the place where my human shape used to be, and what it reflects is no longer a silhouette. It reflects the entire trembling continent I have become, folded small enough to fit inside a single concave plane of living stone. I see myself from the outside for the first time: a half-made world wearing a half-made body like a clumsy coat.

I try to speak. The sound that emerges is the sound of a new fissure opening across three kilometers of fresh crust. It is not language. It is longing.

The Still-Named answers by doing nothing at all. It simply lets the world watch itself through the place where its face should be. And because I *am* the world in this moment, I watch myself watching myself. The recursion is gentle, almost shy, like two mirrors learning they are allowed to kiss.

Time has still not been invented here, but something like sequence occurs anyway.

I feel myself condensing. Not shrinking—condensing. All the mountain ranges, all the floating mineral rafts, all the slow rivers of slag and the singing lattices fold inward through the mirror until only a single figure remains standing on the lip of what might one day be called a continent. The figure is made of black volcanic glass veined with living copper. Its shoulders still drip translucent amber. Where its face should be there is only a smooth, slightly concave mirror that now reflects… nothing.

Because there is no longer an observer behind it.

The Still-Named tilts its head (my head) with something that might be courtesy, or might be the memory of courtesy not yet born. Then it turns and begins to walk again, long glass-jointed limbs flowing across ground that trembles in recognition.

I leave temporary constellations of white stone behind each footfall. They cool, they crack, they are swallowed.

I do not look back.

There is nothing behind me that has not already been folded into the next sentence the world has not yet finished speaking.

And for the first time since the kiln began to fire, the argument between gravity and fire falls quiet inside the place where a face should be.

The silence is not empty.

It is listening.
