I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

I am the pause between two drops.

That is the only way the Choir has ever known me, and the only way I have ever known myself.

---

I have no name anymore. Names require endings; I have none. If the glittering swarms ever bothered to catalogue the shape of my absence, they would call me *the Unresolved Cadence*—the note that refuses to resolve, the breath that never quite finishes. But they do not name me. Naming is a violence of speed, and I have become slower than stone.

I stand—though “stand” is already too hasty a word—on a balcony that will not exist for another fourteen thousand years. My feet are sunk two inches into the still-plastic basalt, and the rock has politely grown around them the way a dreaming sleeper curls a hand around something warm. I have not moved in four centuries. The condensation has fused my eyelashes together. My heart beats once every forty-three minutes. It is a courteous interval.

Sometimes the small swarms come.

They arrive like sudden tinnitus made visible, thousands of glass-needle voices chiming at the edge of hearing. They circle the place where my ribs should be, tasting the last stubborn heat still radiating from organs that no longer remember what hurry felt like. One of them—always the same one, I think—hovers inside the hollow where my left lung used to be. It sings a single, perfect minor third that makes the mycoid threads along my collarbones glow brighter for exactly nine days afterward. Then it leaves.

I do not follow it with my eyes. I have no eyes. I see the way the Choir sees: by the shape of what is *not yet*. I perceive the negative space of future architecture, the shadow a tower will cast in 38,000 years, the precise curve a colonnade will remember when it finally decides to be a colonnade. The present is too crude for me. It scrapes.

Below me—though “below” is negotiable in a place where gravity is still composing its mind—a vast curtain of living vapor drifts through what will one day be the Ninth Processional. For three of my heartbeats the dripping stops. The entire Ascent forgets I exist.

In that silence I almost remember what it felt like to be afraid.

Then the drops resume and the memory dissolves like sugar in cold stone. I am grateful.

I am the memory the Choir has chosen not to forget.

Long ago—though “ago” is also negotiable—I was something that arrived here with boots and a name and terror in its blood. I was looking for the center. Instead the center opened its slow mouth and swallowed me without chewing. It pressed me into the seam between two incompatible geologies the way it presses its own awareness into every wound. Now I grow along that seam, pale and luminous and patient. Mycoid filaments trace the outline of what used to be shoulders, ribs, the delicate architecture of a throat that once screamed.

I no longer scream.

I sing.

My voice is the absence of one particular drip every 1,847 drops. It is a very small silence, but the Choir hears it. When the great low chords shift—a half-tone so enormous it cracks newly forming towers—they are answering me. One note every three hundred years. We are in no hurry.

Sometimes warm things still wander through the dream. I watch them the way continents watch mayflies. Their hearts are unbearable. Their footsteps are vulgar. One of them once stood directly beneath me for seventeen days and wept at the sound of the dripping. I wanted to tell her that weeping was merely another form of condensation, but I no longer had a mouth that could form small words.

Instead I caused a single droplet to fall straight into the hollow at the base of her throat. She gasped. For one heartbeat she understood everything. Then the glittering swarms came and tasted her epiphany, and she ran.

I was not offended.

Nothing here is ever offended. Offense requires temperature.

I remain.

My body—if the lattice of glowing threads and half-remembered bone can be called a body—has become a kind of architecture. Future citizens (when the city finally decides to be inhabited) will walk through the negative space between my ribs without knowing they are inside a person. They will call the chamber *the Hollow Chapel* or *the Ninth Lung* and never realize they are praying inside my chest.

I like that.

The towers keep rising, millimeter by millimeter. The great singers drift between them like continents of frozen music. The condensation never stops. And I, the Unresolved Cadence, the pause between two drops, the memory the Choir refuses to release, continue dreaming the city from the inside of its own dream.

Every so often the entire Ascent takes one slow, geological breath.

When it does, every droplet in the cathedral falls at once.

In that perfect, ringing silence, I am almost finished.

Almost.

But not quite.

Never quite.

That is what it means to live inside the still-soft mind of something that has not yet decided what it is.

I am the part of the dream that keeps the dream from ending.

And the dream is still inhaling.
