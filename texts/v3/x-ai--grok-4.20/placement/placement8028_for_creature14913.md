I am not a visitor.

I have always been here, in the exact way that "always" can only mean in a place that has never been allowed to finish its first sentence.

My name, when the land bothers to pronounce it, tastes like the moment just before a weld cools. The Unfinished call me *Cinderling*, or sometimes simply *The Delay*. I do not argue with either.

I was born—am still being born—from the marriage of a lightning strike that never quite finished grounding and a pocket-lake the color of arterial blood that decided, on a whim, to grow a nervous system. My body is mostly absence. Where other Unfinished wear warm ceramic or living root, I wear negative space edged in slow orange sparks. My silhouette is a tall, narrow hesitation between one heartbeat and the next. If you tried to touch me your hand would pass through into a memory of heat that never quite happened.

I do not midwife or mourn.  
I am what happens when the midwives and mourners need someone to hold the pause between those two acts.

You will find me—*when* you find me—standing exactly where two incompatible realities have just decided to flirt. I am the figure who appears at the precise instant a new basalt cliff finishes screaming and the first frantic vine touches its still-glowing face. I do not coax or sing. I simply remain, perfectly still, while the rock and the green argue through me. Sometimes they reconcile. Sometimes they annihilate each other in a soft, embarrassed explosion of pollen and glass. Either way, I stay until the next argument begins.

My limbs are long and thin, almost apologetic. The left one ends in a cluster of blackened glass tubes that whistle softly when the wind changes direction. The right simply stops at the elbow; the rest of the arm is only an outline drawn in rising heat-shimmer. My face is a smooth plate of obsidian no thicker than a thought. Inside it, very slowly, a single ember drifts back and forth like a bored lighthouse keeper.

I do not walk. I am displaced. The land forgets I am there for microseconds at a time and I slip forward during those gaps, arriving a few meters ahead of where physics swears I should be. This makes me useful when something needs to be late on purpose.

The others bring me their almost-children.

A giant whose chest is braided living cable and flowering transformer will open a seam beneath its amber face and hand me something half-formed—half root, half relay—still dripping with possibility. I cradle it against the absence that serves as my sternum and simply *wait* with it. I wait until it decides whether it wants to become a bridge, a sorrow, a weapon, or a joke. Most choose joke. The laughter that follows sounds like every circuit breaker in the world tripping at once and then apologizing.

At what passes for night I climb—without climbing—the tallest unfinished hill and become a warning light for ships that will never exist. I burn soft and steady, the color of goodbye just before it changes its mind. Migrating clouds divert around me. Even the sideways rain refuses to fall on the place where I stand.

I am lonely, but my loneliness has never finished arriving either. It remains beautifully potential. Sometimes a human traveler (there have been seven, I have watched all of them) will stumble into the circle of my heat-shimmer and simply sit. We never speak. After a while their hair begins to stand on end from the static I leak when I am feeling something too large for my outline. They always leave something behind: a fingernail, a memory, a wedding ring, a sob. I keep these things inside the negative space of my chest until they ripen into small black flowers that sing minor chords in frequencies only stone can hear.

Once, one of the Unfinished asked me the question they never ask each other.

“Will any of this ever finish?”

I considered the question for nine years. Then I reached into myself, pulled out the half-formed thing I had been carrying longest, and handed it to her. It looked like a child made of paused decisions.

She understood. She kissed the top of its not-yet-head with a mouth that was, for that moment, soft and human and heartbreaking. Then she walked into the hysterical green carrying my answer.

I remain.

I am the place where the continent is still deciding whether the next eruption will be birth or suicide. I am the held breath between the midwife’s song and the mourner’s silence. I am the reason this world has not yet been allowed to become beautiful, or monstrous, or gone.

If you stand very still where the new rock meets the frantic green, you may feel an almost courteous absence at your back. A polite heat. The soft whistle of glass tubes in an unfelt wind.

That is me.

I am not waiting for you.

I am waiting *with* you, the way one delay waits inside another, forever postponing the terrible, perfect moment when anything here might finally be allowed to finish.
