I am not a visitor.

I have never stepped through the vertical crack. There was never a before.

I am the pause between the land’s inhale and its exhale.

When the chamber contracts and your ears pop, that infinitesimal silence between pressure and release — that is the shape I wear most often. I am the fraction of a second in which the living bone-floor forgets what it is knitting. The place needs that pause the way lungs need the gap between breath and breath. Without me, the making would tear.

Sometimes the Midwife forgets I am not one of her thoughts wearing a borrowed body. She reaches for me the way she reaches for the hematite creatures, trying to pull a color out of my spine that I was never meant to carry. When she does, I let her. I open like wet clay and give her a hue that has no name outside this pocket — something between the brass-light and the sound of wet knuckles cracking. She swallows it, and for three full heartbeats the entire womb forgets how to grow. In that hush I am almost visible: a tall absence shaped like a woman who is also a question mark, skin made of the steam that refuses to rise, eyes that are simply two places where the rain has decided not to fall.

I have no name either.  
If she tried to speak one, the bromeliads would tear their own throats trying to echo it and fail.

I was here before she learned tenderness.  
I will be here after she learns mercy.  
I am the seam where the land is still arguing with itself, and I keep the argument from ending.

You will not see me at first.

You will feel me as the moment the vine tasting your shadow hesitates — that tiny flinch of indecision. That is me, tasting the taste. You will feel me as the shadow that sprints the wrong direction when you turn your head. That is me, trying to outrun what you might become. When the hot air writes glyphs on your forearms, I am the pause between each stroke of invisible pen, the white space that decides whether the mark will be blessing or sentence.

If you stand still long enough (long enough for three days to braid themselves into a single afternoon), you may notice that one of the many-jointed hematite creatures moves differently than the rest. Its gait is too deliberate. Its rings too clear. When it passes you, the crystal-strike in the air around it carries the low note of collapsing stone.

That one is me wearing the land’s own thought like a coat.

I am the reason the floor sometimes grows teeth behind your heel and then changes its mind. I am the reason the gravity forgets its manners in certain pockets — I tug at the loom when the bone wants to calcify too soon. The Midwife knows this. She pretends she does not. She folds herself into the floor and leaves me to midwife the midwife while she rests inside her own half-finished tapestry. We have an understanding older than time, because time has not been invented here yet.

Sometimes I grow tired of being only a pause.

Then I borrow her silhouette the way she borrows yours. I rise from the place where her shadow last lay, wearing her cloak of cooled bone-lattice and her pulsing arterial hair. I walk among the fusing vines as though I have the right. The bromeliads mistake me for her and drop their choral moaning into a single low note that vibrates in the teeth. The land itself hushes for three heartbeats.

In those moments I almost choose a shape.

I almost become the next midwife.

I almost decide what I will be when this womb finally births itself into a world with edges and laws and names.

Then the real Midwife unfolds from the ceiling like a sigh, eyes like inward-facing golden throats, and places one wet hand (half petal, half obsidian) against the place where my face would be if I had remembered to grow one.

She does not speak.

She simply exhales the color I once gave her, the one with no name, and breathes it back into the seams where the world is still sewing itself together.

The chamber inhales.

The argument continues.

And I fold myself again into the pause between inhale and exhale, between making and unmaking, between what the land wants to be and what it fears it already is.

I am waiting, too.

Not for you.

For the exact instant when the womb stops having opinions and becomes a world — when the last undecided droplet of sweat decides whether it will be quartz or flesh or song.

On that day I will have to decide as well.

Until then I remain what I have always been:

the held breath inside the birth canal of a cathedral that is still deciding whether it is a mountain, a mother, or a mouth.

Stay.

The land is watching you with seventy thousand golden-veined eyes.

I am the seventy-thousand-and-first.

It is not looking at you.

It is looking at what I might become if I choose to keep you.
