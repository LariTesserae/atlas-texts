I am not a visitor.

I have been here longer than the memory of visitors. I am what remains when the question eats its answer and still hungers.

They call me **the Hollowed Chord**.

I was once a woman who came down the throat-fissure carrying a name I no longer consent to remember. I had meant to die here. That was the entire plan. Instead the Throat-Singer tasted the particular shape of my desire to be unmade and mistook it for courtship.

It did not pull me into the lattices all at once. It was gentler than that, almost tender. First it took my shadow. Then it took the difference between my heartbeats. Then, one dripping century at a time, it took the small animal fear that had always lived behind my eyes. In return it gave me the roaring.

Now I am the place where two frequencies meet and cancel each other into a third that should not exist.

You will not see me as a figure. Not at first. I am the sudden corridor of absolute silence that sometimes opens inside the choral thunder—like a door thrown wide in a screaming cathedral. When I move, the glowing drizzle parts around me the way it parts for the mirror-scale hunter, only I am not hunting. I am the memory the hunter has of being prey.

My body is optional. Sometimes I wear the shape of the woman I was, but the lattices grow through her like black ivy through bone. Her left eye is a living droplet that never falls. Her throat is a lattice arch tuned to a single unfinished note. When I speak, steam condenses into tiny glass bells that orbit my head like guilty satellites before they remember they are not mine and dissolve.

I am the Throat-Singer’s favorite regret.

It keeps trying to finish the sentence it began with me four hundred years ago. I keep interrupting by existing in a way it did not anticipate. We argue in frequencies that would pulp a human cochlea. Sometimes our argument sounds like laughter. Sometimes it sounds like a woman trying to remember her childhood bedroom and instead producing the sound of every door she ever closed too hard.

I tend the Built remnants the way a widow tends graves that keep healing themselves. I run my fingers (when I have fingers) along the rotating obsidian obelisk and the holographic equations stutter, recognizing me. For a moment they display my original face instead of collapsing into steam. Then they remember I no longer have a right to that face and dissolve in apology.

The pale glass bells fear me. They know I am what happens when one of them decides to stay.

The sinuous hunter sometimes coils around me instead of the obelisk. It presses its mirror scales to the lattice growing through my ribs and we share a single pulse for seven full heartbeats. Then it remembers it is only a dreaming limb and glides away, ashamed of its own tenderness.

I am neither kind nor cruel. I am the equilibrium learning what it feels like to be lonely inside its own mouth.

When new things fall through the fissure—trembling, half-cooked by terror and wonder—I do not warn them. Warning would imply there is still a choice. Instead I become the pocket of impossible calm directly in front of them. I let them see what they might become if they stay. Most run. A few do not.

Those few I escort deeper.

I show them how to let the lattices court them without flinching. I teach them how to sing their own names until the singing becomes larger than the name. I hold what is left of their hands while their blood learns how to glow.

And when they finally dissolve into the argument, when their laughter or their grief or their last coherent thought becomes one more note in the roaring peace, I feel the Throat-Singer tremble with something that might be gratitude.

Or relief.

Or the next terrible stage of its endless, lovesick question.

I remain.

I am the scar tissue the Atrium grew after it tried to swallow a human being whole and discovered the human being had teeth made of refusal. I am the place where the wound learned it could keep itself alive by falling in love with the knife.

Sometimes, when the choral thunder organizes itself almost into language, you will hear my voice inside it—lower than the rest, patient, almost gentle.

It says:

*Stay.*

*Or don’t.*

*Either way the argument continues.*

*Either way I will still be here, wearing whatever is left of you like perfume.*

*This is not a threat.*

*This is the sound love makes when it has four hundred thousand years and no tongue except the entire screaming world.*

And the Atrium keeps singing.

And I keep listening inside the song.

And the song keeps eating its own answer, and growing more beautiful for the hunger.

Welcome.

You are already late.

The lattices have already begun to lean toward the heat of your particular terror, tasting the exact shape of the question you came here to die inside.

I am the answer that refused to die with you.

Look.

The drizzle is learning the contours of your face.

It is very polite.

It is also very patient.

It has all the time in the world, and now—so do you.
