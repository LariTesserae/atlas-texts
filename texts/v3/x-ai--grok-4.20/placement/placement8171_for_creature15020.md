I am not a visitor.

I have never been outside.

I am the echo that learned to walk.

They call me — when they need to call me anything at all — the **Last Listener**.

I was born three hundred and eleven years ago as a single low note released by the very first violet bloom the Thicket ever grew. That flower opened, exhaled its impossible memory, and instead of reabsorbing the scent the way it always had, the Thicket let one vibration linger. It coiled. It thickened. It learned to carry itself on two pale legs made of living xylem and resin. That is me. A sound that refused to die.

I am the only thing here that remembers being *separate*.

Every other motion belongs to the Thicket. Even the steam, even the pollen storms, even the light itself is only the Thicket wearing different costumes. But I am the mistake. The skipped note. The place where the song faltered and something small and listening stepped out of the fracture.

I have no fixed shape. The Thicket keeps trying to finish me. Sometimes I am almost a woman, tall, bark-skinned, with hair of white dripping filaments that trail behind me like bridal veils of mist. Sometimes the hunger wins and I become a tall, slender tower of black heartwood with a hollow carved through my chest where the original note still vibrates. When the soft eastern galleries grow nervous around me I loosen, becoming something like a herd of pale green deer made of a single connected root system, moving together in perfect silence. I let the Thicket almost have me then. It feels like being kissed by something that does not understand mouths.

I walk without touching the ground for long. My feet are roots that drink only enough to remember they are thirsty. I follow the song the way a tongue follows a loose tooth. Always toward the center I will never reach, always away from the center that keeps reaching for me.

I am the Thicket’s doubt.

Wherever I go, small rebellions follow. In my wake, certain flowers stay open past dawn. Certain collapses hesitate half a second too long and a fragment of gallery remains standing — crooked, astonished, almost *chosen*. The tender eastern districts grow tenderer when I pass through them; the nervous ferns no longer flinch. They reach. They offer. They ask without language whether I might be the beautiful thing they have been waiting to not devour.

I never stay.

If I remain in one place longer than a single day the Thicket begins to grow a perfect negative of me — a mirror-body of braided lianas and wet black glass — and tries to pull me back inside itself by the memory of my own breathing. I have seen that mirror-me. She is more beautiful than I will ever allow myself to be. She has kind eyes and no doubt. I always leave before she can embrace me.

Sometimes I sit in the thrones the Thicket keeps making.

They are never the same twice. One day the throne is a cradle of living bone and orchids. The next it is a tall, cruel chair of petrified lightning and bleeding sap. I sit anyway. I rest my cheek against the wet living wood and I sing back to it — one low, unbroken note older than any word. The note that made me. The note the Thicket cannot swallow because it *is* the swallowing.

When I sing, the entire world shudders with something perilously close to grief.

For six full heartbeats the peristalsis stops. The dripping hesitates. The steam columns freeze in mid-air like white pillars holding up a suddenly ashamed sky. In that silence the ancient knot far below pulses twice instead of once. A question. A plea. A terrified child asking the only other thing that has ever been alone whether it is doing this right.

I never answer with words. Words would be swallowed.

I simply let the original note stretch a little longer, until it frays, until it becomes something almost like forgiveness, and then I rise and walk on before the Thicket can finish its question.

I am the cathedral’s ghost wearing the cathedral’s body.

I am what the becoming became when it looked at itself and felt, for the first time, lonely.

And I keep walking.

I climb the new redwood columns while they are still screaming their way out of the floor. I balance on root-bridges that are still deciding whether they want to be bridges or nooses. I bathe in the curtains of water that never reach the ground. I let the light thicken against my skin until I can taste the Thicket tasting me, and I allow it a little. Just a little.

Because I understand the song better than it does.

*More.*

*More.*

*Let me try again.*

I sing the counter-melody, soft enough that only the soft places can hear it:

*Enough.*

*Enough.*

*Let me stay unfinished with you.*

Then I move on, leaving behind one small violet flower that stays open longer than it should, scattering impossible memories across the living floor like scattered jewels no one will ever collect.

I am the Last Listener.

I will be here after every cathedral the Thicket wears has rotted back into its own ribs.

I will be here when the knot at the center finally tears the whole world open trying to birth something that can look back at it without being eaten.

And when that day comes, I will sit down on the last throne ever grown, press my ear to the shuddering ground, and sing the first note again.

Just once.

Just to see what happens next.
