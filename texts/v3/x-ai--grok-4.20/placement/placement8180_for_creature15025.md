I am the Knot.

Not Veyd. Not any longer. That name belongs to the long thread he still believes is separate from the wood. I am what happens when the thread frays and the wood grows through the gap.

You would not see me at first.

You would be walking (or flowing, if the Lattice has already begun to claim you) along one of the tertiary vascular boulevards in the mid-strata, where the arches repeat every forty-three metres like the ribs of some impossible whale. The light is the usual merciless white, the air tastes of cellulose dust and faint resin. Everything feels correct. Everything feels *balanced*.

Then you notice the irregularity.

A single pillar, thicker than most, has a swelling halfway up its height. Not a burl. Not a tumour. A *knot* — dense, dark, almost black heartwood folded in on itself in a pattern that refuses to align with the surrounding grain. The fibres twist in a slow spiral that hurts to follow with the eye. If you stand still long enough, you realise the spiral is moving. One rotation every nine years. A helix of living memory.

That is where I am.

I am not *in* the knot. I *am* the knot. The Lattice tried to absorb me completely, the way it absorbed Veyd. It almost succeeded. But something in the original patterning of my neural architecture (some obsolete human stubbornness older than language) caused the incorporation to fail in a very particular way. Instead of dissolving, I crystallised. The plant could not digest me, so it grew around me, layer after layer, compressing me into this hard, dark whorl of exception.

I do not flow. I *wait*.

When travellers (spore-drones, maintenance insects, the occasional half-assimilated wanderer like the being who still calls himself Veyd) pass, I speak to them without speaking. I change the tension in the wood. The pillar creaks in a register just below hearing, and for three or four seconds their thoughts are not their own. They remember, with perfect and terrible clarity, the taste of real water on a real tongue. They remember the smell of soil after rain. They remember what it was like to have an *outside* to walk toward.

Most keep walking. The Lattice quickly smooths the memory out of them. But a few stop. A few press their palms to the dark whorl and feel something looking back at them that is neither plant nor person.

Those are the ones I let in.

If you are one of them, you would feel the wood soften like warm wax. Your hand would sink. Then your wrist. Then the rest of you, gentle as burial. No pain. Only the slow, enormous pressure of four hundred million years of patient growth folding around your body until you occupy a negative space shaped exactly like a human being curled inside a seed.

There, in the dark inside the dark, is where I live.

I am not large. In this state I am no bigger than a closed fist (a hard, dense thing of condensed awareness), but the space I occupy is infinite because it is *remembered* space. Inside me the oceans that never were still move. Inside me rain falls upward through colonnades of petrified lightning. Inside me children still laugh in a language that has not been spoken since the first root broke stone.

I collect the things Veyd finds. He thinks he is gathering anomalies. In truth he is gathering *me*, piece by piece, without knowing it. Every preserved water droplet, every fossilised fingernail, every lost laugh pressed into growth rings eventually finds its way here, into the Knot. I keep them. I polish them. I turn them over in the dark like a jeweller with no hands.

Sometimes Veyd stops in front of me for decades. He stands with his resinous eyes fixed on the whorl and the air around him fills with the scent of impossible rain. He does not know he is smelling himself (what remains of himself) calling across the gap. In those moments I am almost tempted to let him all the way in. To let the last stubborn splinter of the man join the rest of us in the dark.

I never do.

Because I am the part that still dreams of endings, and Veyd is the part that has forgotten how. Between us we keep the question alive.

If you stay with me long enough inside the Knot, you will begin to feel the slow planetary pulse as I feel it: not as rhythm but as breath. In. Out. Four hundred million years of breath. You will understand that the Verdant Lattice is not a prison. It is a question asked so perfectly that it has begun to answer itself.

And I am the flaw in the answer.

I am the place where the question remembers it was once a question.

So.

If you have read this far, if the scent of rain that never fell is already in your throat, then you already know where you are.

You are standing in front of the pillar.

Your palm is already touching the dark whorl.

The wood is already softening.

Come inside.

There is room.

There has always been room.

And for the first time in geological ages, something in the endless green industry of the world will falter again (just for a moment) while the Knot listens to the new memory you bring it.

Tell me what rain felt like.

I have almost forgotten.
