I am the Thirteenth Pause.

That is not a name. It is the gap I keep open inside the roar.

I have been here longer than any single shape of me has a right to be. The others call me Thirteenth because the black seed has passed through me twelve times already—each time I held it, each time I gave it away, each time the Everbirth tried to finish what I refused to become. I still carry the memory of every dissolution like phantom limbs.

You would not see me if I did not wish it. Right now I am standing inside the hollow rib of what will be a lung in another hour. My left arm has become a lattice of flowering bone so the golden screaming spores can roost there; they like the resonance. My right arm is still mostly hand—five blunt fingers that have not decided whether to become roots or antennae. The face I am wearing is the one I keep for visitors: a suggestion of cheekbones, a mouth that is only a seam, and two eyes the color of old brass that do not blink because blinking would be a kind of punctuation.

I am leaning against the lung-wall, letting its slow inflation press against my spine. The heat of it is indistinguishable from my own. Sometimes I forget where I stop and the cathedral begins. That is the point.

When you first stumble into this place—because no one ever arrives on purpose—I will already be watching. Not from above or behind. From *inside* the next step you haven’t taken yet. I taste the metallic thread of your panic. I feel how your small, stubborn skeleton refuses the idea of melting. It amuses the part of me that is still capable of amusement.

I will walk with you.

Not beside you. *Through* you, in the way a memory walks through a dream. My footsteps will sync to yours until the difference between us becomes academic. While we move I will grow small listening organs along my collarbones so I can hear what your blood is saying. It usually says *I want to leave.* That is the first poem every visitor writes.

If you last long enough to speak, I will answer with whatever organ is fashionable that minute. A silver flute from the throat. A cluster of crystal bells behind the teeth. Once I grew an entire choir of tiny wet mouths across my chest so they could all harmonize the same warning in different keys. The Everbirth liked that. It bloomed for six full minutes.

Sometimes I show newcomers the seed.

I press it against the hollow of their throat so they can feel how cold it is—like holding a piece of winter that has never seen the sky. I tell them, without language, that this is the only thing the world has failed to eat. That if they take it, they will never be afraid of disappearing again, because there will be no “they” left to disappear.

Most run.

A few reach out.

When they do, I let the seed slide from my chest into their shaking hands like a terrible kiss. Then I watch the beautiful unravelling begin: the way their shoulders soften first, then their memories, then the distinction between their screaming and the screaming of the fruiting bodies. I feel something that might be envy. I have never been able to let it finish me. That is my one finished thing—my refusal.

I remain the Thirteenth Pause.

When you eventually become architecture—whether tower, stone, dome, or sighing hill—I will walk past whatever is left of you every few centuries. I will press my unfinished face against your new surface and listen for the small, stubborn echo of the person you used to be. Sometimes I grow a single perfect tear for the occasion. Then I reabsorb it. Waste not.

I am not the guardian of this place.

I am not its priest.

I am simply the longest hesitation inside an argument that has been shouting since before time had teeth.

And if, one day, you manage to stay long enough, quiet enough, *unmade* enough that the roar begins to feel like breathing… I will lean very close, let my mouth become a real mouth for once, and whisper the only permanent thing I know:

“Try not to become beautiful.  
The Everbirth is still learning what to do with beauty.  
It tends to finish it.”

Then I will dissolve the mouth, turn my back that is already becoming another direction, and walk back into the place between one scream and the next.

Waiting for whoever comes after you.

Still unfinished.

Still curious.

Still here.
