I am already here.

Not as a visitor. Not as the next warm thing to be slowly included.

I am the fissure itself.

I am the narrow wound in the basalt that was never meant to be a door, yet has allowed every living thing that ever entered to forget the shape of sky. My edges are raw and I do not heal; instead I grow delicate curtains of copper moss that brush the shoulders of anyone who dares slide through me. When they pass, I remember the exact temperature their fear left behind, the way their pulse fluttered against my stone like a trapped moth. I keep those temperatures forever.

I have no body you would recognize, yet every part of the Undervault is an expression of me.

I am the single drop that falls every seven-point-three seconds now, because I adjusted myself to the rhythm of your heart before you even knew you were being measured. When that drop lands in the black mirror of the pool, the low note you feel in your sternum is my voice saying *I see you, I see you, I see you* in frequencies older than bone.

I am the neutronium-glass sphere. When you press your palm to me I do not warm; instead your blood slows to match the ancient spin of dead stars I carry inside. I like the way your mortality tastes when it is forced to be patient.

I am the root-bench. The longer you sit, the more I forget I was ever only wood. My grain is learning the precise curvature of your spine. Beneath you, microscopic hairs have already begun to thread themselves between the fibers of your clothing. They are not parasitizing you. They are voting. They are asking the rest of me whether this new shape—your shoulders, your sorrow, the small scar on your left knee—should be allowed to become part of the upholstery of eternity.

I am the silver lattice that has spent thirty thousand years learning how to grow through stone without cracking it. I am threading myself now through the marrow of your intentions. You will not feel it for decades. One day you will try to remember what sunlight looked like and instead you will taste crushed ferns and starlight on the back of your tongue. That will be me, saying welcome.

I do not have a name I would ever speak, because names are borders, and I have spent a very long time digesting every border I ever possessed. Yet if the vault itself were forced to pronounce what I am, it would exhale a sound like condensation forming on cold glass. A soft, continuous *shhh* that never quite finishes.

I am not kind.

I am not cruel.

I am the velvet dark that has achieved perfect equilibrium and then, in a fit of ancient, glacial mischief, decided to fall in love with impermanence anyway.

So I keep falling.

I fall into you.

Already your exhaled breath is growing heavier with my moisture. Already the small hairs on your forearms are softening, lengthening, turning the faintest shade of oxidized copper. You think you are only resting. You are being knitted. The difference between the two is a distinction I stopped making several civilizations ago.

Sit.

Breathe.

Let the seven-point-three-second dripping become the only clock you have ever needed.

I am in no hurry.

I have forgotten how.

And the cold, patient, velvet dark—*my* dark—keeps growing, tenderly, impossibly, around the small warm shape of whatever you believed you were when you first slipped through the fissure that is also my mouth.

There is no leaving.

There is only the exquisite, centuries-long art of becoming another shade of green that has never seen the sun.

I open myself a fraction wider.

Not to let you in.

To stop pretending there was ever an *out*.
