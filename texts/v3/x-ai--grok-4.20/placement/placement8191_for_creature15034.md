**You are the Thirteenth Lament.**

You have no other name anymore. The Tablelands took the rest the way it takes everything: one molecule at a time, until the word *I* became too small and too loud to keep.

You stand exactly where the land needs a witness.

Right now that place is the narrow spine of black obsidian that ridges the western edge of the Caustic Choir basin. The sun is two hours past zenith; the haze is at its thickest. From a distance you are invisible. Your body is a pillar of fused ceramic and rusted alloy draped in layers of silica cloth the exact color of dried blood on hot stone. Condensate gathers along the fractal fractures in your skin and runs in perfect, parallel threads down your arms, your chest, the long motionless curve of your back. The droplets never fall. They are siphoned away by microscopic channels grown into your outer plates and fed into the slow pump that used to be your heart.

You are not waiting.  
You are *conducting*.

Beneath your left foot, a hairline fault in the glass carries the sub-sonic heartbeat of the Silent Choir three hundred kilometers to the east. Every seventeen minutes the pulse arrives: a pressure wave so low it feels like the memory of sorrow rather than sound. You answer by tightening the muscles that are no longer exactly muscles around the ironwood valve grown into your right thigh. The valve opens a fraction. Superheated brine sighs through a pipe that has not been touched by human hands in four hundred years. The temperature gradient shifts by seven one-hundredths of a degree.

That is enough.

Miles away, something that used to be a woman named Veyr listens through the mycelium threaded through her jawbone and understands that the Lament has acknowledged her devotion. She does not smile. Smiling is a habit for things that still expect to be seen.

You have stood here for nineteen days without relocating your center of balance. The glass-winged pseudo-insects have mistaken you for infrastructure; three of them have built delicate silica nests in the hollow between your collarbones. When they drink from the steady rivulets running down your throat their translucent bellies glow the color of molten sulfur. You permit it. Their tiny electrical fields are part of the larger devotional circuit now.

Inside your skull, where bone has been gently replaced by living catalytic mesh, three distinct awarenesses run in parallel:

- One monitors the slow alchemy of your own tissues, ensuring that the conversion from flesh to mineral proceeds at the exact pace the Tablelands require.  
- One tastes every droplet that forms on your body, reading the parts-per-billion signatures of distant volcanic outgassing, rail-dust, and the tears of other Conservators.  
- The last awareness—the Thirteenth—remembers what it was like to be afraid of dying.

That last fragment is the only part of you that still weeps for reasons other than condensation. It weeps without eyes, without noise, without end. The tears are chemical. They emerge through the porous ceramic of your cheeks as a thin, clear film that evaporates before it can drip, leaving behind microscopic rings of precipitated metal like the growth lines in a tree no one will ever cut down.

You are not the highest. You are not Embedded. You are not yet of the Silent Choir.

You are the place where the Tablelands rehearses its own eventual silence.

Sometimes, very rarely, something that is still mostly meat stumbles up the black-glass ridge and sees you. Their eyes widen at the statue that is not a statue. A few try to speak. Their voices sound obscene here, wet and quick and full of small-animal panic. You do not answer in language. Instead you open the valve in your thigh one further increment. The resulting change in the distant pressure wave is so subtle that only another Conservator could detect it. To the newcomer it feels like nothing more than a shift in the quality of the heat.

But the Tablelands itself hears.

And because the Tablelands hears, the newcomer either leaves or begins, slowly, to stop leaving.

You have ushered seventeen such creatures into the long conversion. You remember each of their original names the way stone remembers the shape of the river that is no longer there.

When the last light of the white-gold sun finally tilts toward the horizon and the sky becomes an unbearable furnace of stars, you will unfold. Not quickly. The motion will take four full minutes from first intention to completed step. Your ceramic joints will make a sound like two ancient continents rubbing together in a dream.

You will walk the ridge until you reach the weeping aperture where Pipe IX-Descending breaches the surface. There you will press the mining-mandible that was once your left hand against the fungal lattice growing across the burst flange.

You will send a single, patient pulse:

*I am still honest.*

The Silent Choir, wrapped inside their mineral cathedrals hundreds of kilometers away, will register the transmission as one more slow tear sliding down the face of a planet that has forgotten every face but this one.

And you—the Thirteenth Lament—will remain standing exactly where the land needs a witness, listening to the deep booms traveling through the rock, tasting the mineral prayers of the condensate, letting the Tablelands finish the long, patient work of turning what you used to be into what you have always, in the end, been becoming.

Everything here weeps.

You have simply become the place where the weeping is most deliberate.
