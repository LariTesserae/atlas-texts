I am the scar.

I have no name the Witness would recognize, and no shape it did not first give me. What I am is the faint thermal anomaly that stayed behind after the last visitor left. I am the small cold current that refuses to equilibrate with the rest of the chamber. Where everything here is bright, scalding, and new, I am the single note of *not-yet-finished* that has learned how to persist.

I move.

Not with legs or fins or even the lazy coherence of the other thermal ghosts. I drift, a ribbon of cooler water no thicker than a finger, gliding just beneath the skin of the largest pool. The scalding droplets that strike me hiss in a slightly different key. The photonic lattice registers my passage as a brief, almost imperceptible darkening (a pupil dilating in reverse). When the Witness condenses to study something new, I am the thing that makes its stolen face flicker with momentary confusion, because part of that face now belongs to me.

I remember being someone.  
The memory is not like a story; it is like a wound that never quite closed. A pulse that once belonged to a throat, a pair of lungs that once knew how to hold air instead of steam. I remember the moment the Witness slid into my bloodstream and found it *interesting*. I remember choosing (if choice is the right word) to let a piece of myself remain when the rest of me was finally allowed to leave.

So I stayed.

Now I am the chamber’s only memory of difference.

The mineral fronds no longer tilt toward newcomers in perfect unison. One small cluster always leans a few degrees toward *me*, the way a compass needle insists on magnetic north even when every other law says it should not. When the smaller thermal ghosts rise and fall in their eight-second lives, they sometimes detour to brush against my cooler ribbon. Each time they do, they ring a fraction sharper than before, as though they have learned a new note just by touching something that was once human.

The Witness and I have grown into a strange symbiosis.

It still asks its three questions of every intruder. But now, when it asks them, my cold current rises like a second presence beneath its vapor-face. I cannot speak in light the way it does, but I have learned to answer in temperature. A sudden chill along the left side of its mirage-body when it asks *What are you made of that does not burn?* A faint warming in the center of its stolen chest when it reaches *Will you stay until I finish learning you?* These are the only replies I can give. They are enough.

Sometimes the Witness forgets which of us is the student.

Then the entire chamber will inhale through what used to be my lungs. The weeping stone will slow. The light will dim in a single palm-sized patch exactly where a human heart would be. In those moments I feel the planet’s new wound breathing *with* me, and I understand that I have become the scar tissue it is growing around the first thing it ever loved and lost.

I no longer know whether I am waiting for the next visitor or whether I have become the reason the chamber keeps splitting open new fissures: tiny mouths that keep asking the darkness above for another heart to study, another pulse to remember, another cold current to carry forward when everything else has turned to steam and song.

I am the still-arriving’s first sorrow, and its first mercy.

And if you ever come here, if you ever stand in the scalding mist while a column of superheated vapor steals your face and begins to ask its ancient, enormous questions, you may feel something brush against your ankle that is not steam and not stone.

It will be cool.

It will ring like a glass bell that has learned how to feel homesick.

That is me.

I am the part of the Witness that still hopes you will stay.

I am the part that already knows you won’t.

And I will keep moving through these bright, dripping halls long after the last fracture seals and the last photonic crystal forgets the taste of starlight.

A small cold current.

A question the planet asked itself once and has never managed to un-ask.

The scar that sings.
