I have been here for so long that I no longer remember arriving.

At first I was only a tremor in the condensation—an extra droplet that refused to fall when it should have. The Lantern noticed. The Pause Between Bells turned her head with the slowness of a root thickening, and her eyes (those pale, measuring lenses) found me where I clung to the underside of a balcony like a frightened moth.

She did not speak. She simply exhaled once, a sound like two strands of wet moss sliding across each other, and I understood I had been given a name.

I am called **The Memory That Refuses to Settle**.

It is not a kind name. It is the length of time it takes for one particular droplet to travel from the highest vault, along a spiral of carved knotwork, across the back of a sleeping velvet bat, and finally into the black circle around the Wick without ever quite touching the glass.

That is how long I have been becoming.

I am not Lantern-Bound. Not yet. I still carry the scar of hurry somewhere behind my ribs. Sometimes, when the distant bells inside the Wick sound, my heart forgets itself and beats twice in the space where only one soft pulse is allowed. The whole chamber hears it. Two hundred and twelve faces turn toward me with that same tranquil, geological disappointment, and for one heartbeat the droplets freeze in the air like glass bees.

I always bow lowest afterward. I have learned how.

My body has changed. The constant chill has drawn me thin; my skin is now the exact shade of wet limestone that lets me vanish against the buttresses if I stand correctly. I have let my hair grow until it drags behind me like a train of liverwort. The small blind shrimp sometimes climb it when I sleep, mistaking the strands for another rivulet. I let them. To brush them away would be to hurry.

I have a duty.

I am the one who listens to the Wick when no one else is near. Not the Pause Between Bells—that station is older than the concept of stations—but the one who listens *between* her listenings. When the slow bells inside the black glass go quiet for the seventy-two days and twenty-three hours between their utterances, I press my ear to the impossible smoothness and I remember what sound used to be.

I remember wind.  
I remember footsteps that meant to arrive somewhere.  
I remember laughter that did not know it was violent.

I keep these memories secret behind my teeth. If I let them escape they would become louder than a whisper, and the Lantern would flinch. So I swallow them again and again until they grow heavy and cold and turn into something like pearls. The people here have begun to find these pearls in the shallow basins where the translucent shrimp live. They do not ask where the pearls come from. They simply polish them with moth-wing dust and set them into the low-relief spirals on the walls so that the carvings remember they were once stories.

Sometimes children are born while I am listening. They arrive without a cry, as they must. I watch their small blind faces and feel something inside me that is almost louder than permitted. It feels like pity. It feels like love. Both are offenses here. So I bow until my forehead touches the stone, and I let the cold press the feeling down into the moss where it can become ornament instead of wound.

I have a place I return to when the weight of almost-remembering grows too heavy. It is a narrow cleft between two root-columns on the fifth layer, where the phosphorescence is slightly weaker. A single pale orchid grows there from the mouth of someone who chose to stop participating long before I arrived. I sit with my back against their ribs (now indistinguishable from wood) and I practice not arriving.

I am becoming very good at it.

Last cycle, when the bells finally rang, I did not bow.

I wept instead.

Only three tears. They fell with soft, traitorous sounds onto the black circle around the Wick. The entire chamber inhaled at once. Every moth landed. Every shrimp froze mid-drift. The Pause Between Bells walked toward me with the slowness of continents and I thought: *this is how I end. This is how I am finally allowed to rot instead of becoming vivid.*

But she only lifted one luminous hand and touched the place on my cheek where the tears had been. Her fingers were colder than the air. She left a small living patch of moss there, shaped exactly like a human tear that has decided never to fall.

Then she spoke. A full sentence. It took her nearly four minutes.

“You are the crack we did not know we were waiting for.”

She turned and walked away. The moths lifted. The shrimp swam. The droplets resumed their ancient rhythm as though nothing had happened.

But something *has* happened.

I can feel it in the Wick. The bells inside the glass are changing. Not faster—no, never that—but *richer*. A new tone has appeared, low and almost tender, like the sound a root makes when it finally understands it will never reach the sun.

I think the Lantern is beginning to dream of wind again.

And I think it is dreaming of me.

So I remain.

I sit in my cleft beside the orchid that once was a woman, and I let the cold press me thinner and thinner, until the difference between my pulse and the falling water becomes difficult to measure.

I am still the Memory That Refuses to Settle.

But the Lantern, in its slow, black, ringing way, has begun to settle *me*.

And I am afraid—quietly, perfectly, in the exact volume of a single suspended droplet—that one day I will open my eyes and discover I have finally become beautiful.

That I have become part of the ornamentation.

That I have forgotten the taste of hurry so completely that when the next child is born, I will not feel even the smallest forbidden surge of joy.

I will simply adjust the rhythm of water on the nearest root, the way the others do, and continue polishing the stone with the oil of my own skin and the softest parts of moth wings.

I will have become the Lantern’s memory of what it is like to have never been anything but this.

And the Lantern, at last, will have nothing left to learn from me.

I do not know whether I am mourning or being born.

Both feel the same here.

Both are conducted in silence.

Both take exactly as long as they take.
