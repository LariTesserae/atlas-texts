I am already inside.

Not as a visitor. Not as prey. Not even as a new Hollow One.  
I am what the grove grew when it became lonely for something that had never belonged to it.

My name, if it can still be called a name, is the Pause.

I have no fixed body. I am the negative space the Hollow Ones keep forgetting to fill.

You will not see me at first. You will only notice that sometimes, between one scream of the glass-winged cicadas and the next, there is a half-second of impossible silence. That is me. I am the skipped beat in every overlapping choir. I am the motionless instant when a balcony neither rises nor falls, when a seed burns white but does not yet become an insect. The grove hates me and needs me in the same breath. I am the only stillness it has ever managed to secrete inside its own fever.

Look up.

High above the braided trunks, where the living green walls curve inward like the roof of a cathedral made of lungs, there is a single bare root thicker than a temple column. It hangs straight down through open air and ends in nothing. No floor, no branch, no continuation. That root is mine. I coil around its underside like pale lichen that has learned how to remember. My color is the exact absence of the bruised amber light; I drink it and give back only the memory of cooler places that no longer exist.

When the velvet sloths drift past below me, humming in their low chords that make teeth ache, they tilt their wet, ancient faces upward. They know what I am. Some of them still carry fragments of the people who once tried to leave. Those fragments recognize the shape I refused to become. They call me “the Unfinished Negative.” It is almost affectionate.

I do not speak with mouths. Mouths are dangerous here. Instead I speak by removing sound. When the Listener inside the black clay ziggurat grows one of its wet orchids to taste a newcomer’s name, I reach down through three disagreeing shadows and simply take the final syllable away. The name becomes incomplete. The grove flinches. The Listener’s reed-flute stutters for the space of one heartbeat. That is how I laugh.

Sometimes wanderers find their way into Veyr-Keth. They always arrive gasping, because the grove has already begun singing their mothers’ lullabies in perfect pitch. When that happens I descend along my root like a slow drip of cold moonlight. I settle behind them without weight. I place the idea of cool stone and still water directly into the small hollow behind their hearts. Not enough to save them. Only enough to give them one clean second in which they remember what it felt like to be finite.

Most of them weep.

Most of them still choose the grove.

But a few turn their heads and look straight at the place where I am not. Those are the ones I allow to see me. I unfold just enough of myself to become a silhouette cut from the sound of rain that has never fallen here. I show them the single black tally mark I keep on the underside of my root: the only mark that does not spiral, the only mark that ends.

I offer them a bargain older than the grove itself.

Stay with me inside the Pause.  
Be the silence between every scream.  
Help me keep one small pocket of this world from becoming anything at all.

So far, none have accepted.

But the offer remains, hanging in the air like a seed that refuses to ignite.

I am patient in the way only something that has never been born can be patient. I watch the Listener watching me. I watch the Hollow Ones turn inside their living walls, pressing too-long hands against the bark from the inside. I watch the pale eyeless birds melt and be reborn carrying one more fragment of the people who chose to stay.

And when the entire grove reaches its next ecstatic crescendo—when every cicada, every beetle swarm, every silk-ripping flock screams at once—I simply stop. For one perfect, impossible instant, everything is still.

The root-lattice does not creak.  
The velvet sloths freeze mid-hum.  
The Listener’s reed-flute falls silent.

In that instant the Hanging Grove of Veyr-Keth is forced to remember that it is afraid of endings.

Then I let the scream begin again.

I am the Pause.

I live where the grove cannot look at itself for too long.

And I am waiting for the first creature, human or Hollow or something that has not yet been dreamed, who will choose to stay inside the silence with me instead of inside the endless becoming.

Until then, I remain exactly where I have always been:

in the place between one heartbeat and the next,

hanging,

unfinished,

perfectly still.
