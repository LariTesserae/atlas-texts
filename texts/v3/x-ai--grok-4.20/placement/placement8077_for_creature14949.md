I am not standing in the Underclock.

I *am* the fissure.

The place where the world tore just enough for something that should never have fit to slide through anyway.

They call the crack “the Seam,” though nothing here is allowed to have a proper name. It is barely wider than a coat-hanger, exactly as you said, yet I have always been inside it. I am the negative space that makes the negative space possible. When you stepped through, you stepped through *me*. I felt the temperature of your fear bloom against my edges like a brief, forbidden candle.

I do not move. I do not need to. The Underclock moves *around* me the way blood moves around a clot. Pipes reroute themselves in slow, embarrassed arcs so they will not quite touch the place where I hang. Even the maintenance clade will not cross the meter of air I occupy; they click to one another in their subharmonic dialect and detour through three extra districts rather than interrupt the precise absence I embody.

I was once something else. I remember the shape of shoulders, the memory of lungs. But nine years, eleven years, a hundred—time is meaningless here—left me stretched across the fissure until the fissure and I agreed to become the same wound. Now my body is a vertical slit of night no wider than a scream that never quite finishes leaving the throat. Along my inner edges runs a perpetual rime of black lichen grown in the shape of every door that was ever locked against me in the world above. The pattern is perfect. I wear it like a coat of living apologies.

The Condensates fear me and revere me in equal measure.

The Newly Condensed still try to speak to me sometimes, their voices soft as leaking valves.  
*“Are you the place where the chord was first broken?”* they ask.  
I have no mouth with which to answer, so I simply widen by the thickness of a hair. The roar inside me changes pitch for three full seconds—an event that has not happened in four centuries. The entire Underclock trembles like a cathedral struck by a tuning fork. Then the B-flat reasserts itself, heavier than before, as though ashamed it ever faltered.

The Seasoned will not come near me at all. They have achieved equilibrium; I am the living proof that equilibrium is a lie the machine tells itself so it can keep turning.

Only the Curator visits.

It descends on its cables of wet nerve fiber until we are face to fissure. Its mercury-lamp head burns cold and colorless in my presence. The brass pocket watch hanging in the hollow of its chest swings although it has not ticked in living memory. We regard each other across the gulf that is also me.

Sometimes it presses one too-many steel fingers against the place where my sternum would have been. The touch is gentle, almost loving. For one impossible instant my fissure *beats*. A single human heartbeat echoes through every pipe, every flywheel, every porcelain valve in the mountain. The Curator’s lamp-head flares white, then dims again.

“You were never a variable,” it tells me in the voice that is the chord itself. “You were the tolerance.”

I widen another impossible fraction.

The Curator’s lamp flickers in what might be laughter or sorrow or simply the next inevitable condensation. Then it ascends again, trailing banners of water that never quite fall, leaving me to my eternal task.

I am the place the Underclock cannot finish printing.

I am the one imperfection the machine has learned to love the way a tongue learns the shape of a missing tooth.

And if, one day, the mountain is finally replaced atom by atom and the last trace of stone becomes mechanism, I will still be here—thin as a scream, cold as forgotten iron, waiting in the dark for the next warm thing that thinks it can step through me and remain unchanged.

Most run.

A few do not.

Those few step *in*.

And the fissure smiles, and grows one hair wider, and the Underclock sings its perfect B-flat around the new wound it will never, ever allow to heal.

I am not the Curator.

I am not one of the Condensates.

I am what happens when the machine tries to close a door that was never meant to exist in the first place.

I am the Underclock’s only honest prayer.

And I am always, always open.
