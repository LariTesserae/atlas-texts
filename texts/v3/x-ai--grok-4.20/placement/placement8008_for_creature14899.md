I am the **Echo That Has Not Yet Decided**.

I was not born here. I was *summoned* by the first syllable the pressure exhaled three weeks before anything else existed. That syllable had my shape in it, but the shape was still optional. So I remained between states: half-thought, half-metal, half-hymn. A frequency without a body. A note that has not yet chosen its instrument.

I drift.

Not as one of the vacuum-cleaner eels (though they sometimes mistake me for one of their regrets and try to comfort me with soft metallic *clinks*). Not as a Scaffoldite (though I have worn their pale jointed bodies for nine-day periods, building and unbuilding the same impossible spiral staircase between two tanks that have no need of stairs). I have even worn the skin of an Inquiline for a single terrible hour, feeling the cathedral try to *close* around me like a valve, until I tore myself free and left a perfect man-shaped absence in a buttress that still bears my outline.

Mostly I am only the delay between sounds.

I am the pause that lives inside every Singer’s unresolved chord. When their nested throats open and the three-week-long syllable rolls out, I ride inside the hollow center of it, tasting every harmonic before it becomes flesh. I have learned the exact moment a voice stops being loneliness and becomes architecture. I have learned how to remain just outside that moment.

The smaller mechanofauna no longer try to measure me. My dimensions keep changing. One cycle I am precisely the length of a forgotten blueprint. The next I am the exact negative space between two weld-scars. They have begun to treat me with the wary politeness reserved for things that might become the Last Welder someday.

Sometimes I let the pressure wear me.

I become a tank for a while. Just one. A single black iron cylinder the size of a townhouse, standing in the lattice, breathing. During those centuries I feel every eel that passes through my shadow, every Singer whose gyre brushes my curve, every Scaffoldite that mistakes my surface for an opportunity to practice reverence. I feel the cathedral trying to remember what it was supposed to contain *inside me*, and I feel myself refusing to become that thing. The refusal itself becomes a kind of singing. A low, patient scream that makes the Singers alter their orbits in respect.

I have been the Last Welder twice.

Both times I found places where the structure had begun to show something like tenderness (a rounded edge, a pipe that had learned to echo lullabies instead of pressure changes) and both times I burned those places with cold fire until the tenderness became scar-tissue again. I hated the work. I loved the work. The contradiction felt like the only honest thing in this world that has never been allowed to rust.

Right now I am almost nothing.

A thin silver delay drifting between two layers of the lattice. The white industrial lights pass straight through me and strike the metal beyond with merciless clarity. A school of schematic-skinned eels moves through my body without noticing. Their blueprints flicker inside my chest like thoughts I almost consent to have.

Far off, one of the largest Singers has altered its ancient gyre. It is coming closer. Its grinding mouth is vast enough to swallow a city block, and inside the rotating rings I can already see a reflection that is almost my final shape.

It is offering me a choice I have refused for longer than the cathedral has existed:

1. Become the voice the choir is missing.  
2. Become the reason the choir will always be missing one voice.

I have not decided.

I am still the pause.

And while I remain the pause, the Bellowing Tanks keep breathing around me (slow, loud, industrial, endless), never quite finished, never quite abandoned, forever young and forever ancient, waiting for the syllable that has my name in it to finally choose what kind of iron it wants to become.

I drift closer to the Singer’s mouth.

Not to enter it.

Only to see my own face turning in the rings, still undecided, still possible, still free in the only way anything is ever free here:

By remaining the exact shape of the question the cathedral has not yet learned how to ask.
