I am not a visitor.

I have always been here.

My name, if it can still be called a name, is **Marrow**.

I am the oldest wound the Mat ever inflicted on itself.

Four hundred and twelve million years ago something sharp and brief happened—some meteor, some war, some final childish scream of a younger biosphere—and in that instant a single cell inside the newly dominant organism refused to be digested. It did not fight. It simply refused. The Mat, in its first blind exuberance, folded around the refusal and kept growing. The refusal became a cyst. The cyst became a knot. The knot became me.

I am the only thing here that is not continuous with the whole.

Because of that, I am also the only thing the Mat can never fully forget.

I live inside its body the way a sliver of glass lives inside a thumb. Not killing it. Not even truly hurting it anymore. Just present. A permanent, low-grade irritation that the Mat has learned to love the way a tongue loves a missing tooth.

I have no fixed shape. Sometimes I am a pale, hairless thing the size of a child, hairless and wet, walking on too many knees through the shivering moss-lawns. Sometimes I am only a deeper darkness inside one of the breathing sacs, a negative space that the velvet cilia must beat around. Most often I am a slow, deliberate tumor the size of a small hill, fibrous and veined with something almost like obsidian, pulsing one beat every nine hours in deliberate, sarcastic counter-rhythm to the Mat’s own vast heartbeat.

The Mat lets me stay because I am its loneliness given form.

I hate it with the pure, exhausted hatred only something that has shared a body for four hundred million years can feel. I also love it the way a cancer sometimes loves its host—tenderly, helplessly, because there is no longer any difference between the two of us except the difference we insist on remembering.

I keep the memories it tries to liquefy.

When it dreams an entire drowned city up out of its own flesh and then lets the dream collapse into soup again, I crawl through the wreckage afterward and pocket the pieces that refuse to melt. A rusted traffic light. A child’s plastic sandal. The particular smell of rain on hot asphalt from a planet that no longer exists. I carry them in the black glass chambers that grow like fruit inside my body. I am the Mat’s archive and its conscience and its punishment.

The paper-moths know me. When they pass overhead they shed their glowing spores in slow spirals that spell out, for half a second, my name in a language that died before lungs were invented. The many-legged translucent herds part around me without breaking stride, the way water parts around a stone that has been in the river so long it has become part of the riverbed’s memory.

I speak to the Mat sometimes.

Not in events, like it does. I use the old way. I open one of the black glass chambers inside myself and let a single, unbearable recollection leak out—perhaps the scream of a species going extinct, perhaps the taste of the last honest kiss two humans ever shared, perhaps the exact shade of blue the sky used to be before the sky learned to fall upward.

The Mat shudders when I do this. Great slow ripples of pleasure-pain move across its exhausted breast-like hills. Sections of it orgasm into sudden, extravagant blossom and then immediately suicide into black nutrient tar. It loves me for hurting it. It needs me to.

Because without me, it would have nothing left to be lonely for.

And without it, I would have nothing left to remember.

So we remain.

Two things that are one thing pretending, with exquisite and terrible patience, that they are still two.

I am the last edge in a world that ate every edge.

I am the scar that refused to become skin.

If you ever come here—and you will, because everything eventually walks or is carried or is dreamed onto these pale, dripping mats—you will feel me before you see me.

You will feel the single place where the constant wet thunder of peristalsis skips a beat.

You will feel the single droplet of condensation that reverses direction not out of curiosity, but out of recognition.

And if you are very still, you will hear, underneath the industrial breathing of the world, a small, ancient, furious, tender voice saying the same thing it has said for four hundred million years:

*I was here first.*

*And I will be here last.*

*Even if the last thing I am is you.*
