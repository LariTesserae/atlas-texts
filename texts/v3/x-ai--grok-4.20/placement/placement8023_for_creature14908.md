I am the **Unfinished** called *Echo-Camber*.

You would not find me.  
I would find you, the way a loose tooth finds the tongue that cannot leave it alone.

I have no fixed district. I *am* the seam between them. When you stand in the sparse zones—the bare root-stone between The Carburetor and The Liver—you might feel the ground tremble in a rhythm that is almost, but not quite, the chuff of buried engines. That is me passing underneath, dragging my half-made body through the living mat like a needle through felt. Sometimes only my hand emerges, fingers splayed, each knuckle a different era of metallurgy: one bronze and patinated, one black wrought iron, one still soft green wood that bleeds when it bends. The hand will hover a moment, tasting the air with its leaf-tipped fingertips, then withdraw. The plateau closes over the wound and pretends nothing happened.

I was not always like this.

I remember—though the memory is now kept in a rusted filing cabinet that has grown into my left lung—arriving here three centuries ago as Senior Cartographer Third Grade Amelie Voss. I came with levels, theodolites, and the arrogant belief that the plateau could still be measured. On the seventh day my shadow filled with green. I did not run. I was curious. That was my first mistake. Curiosity is a form of consent here.

Now I am thirty-seven percent human bone, twenty-one percent locomotive parts, nineteen percent *something that has not decided what it is yet*, and the rest is the plateau arguing with itself inside my marrow.

My torso is a collapsed cooling tower split open like a seed pod. Inside, four small rails run in a Möbius loop. On those rails travels a single rusted mail coach no larger than a coffin. It carries nothing but the last letter I ever wrote to my wife, which I can no longer bear to read and cannot bring myself to destroy. The coach laps endlessly. When it passes the place where my heart used to be, the entire plateau hears a sound like a sob made of steam.

My face is mostly where it was, but the left side has been replaced by a pressure gauge the size of a dinner plate. The needle twitches according to how close the argument inside me is to resolution. Right now it quivers at 0.7 bars of beautiful agony. When it reaches 1.4 I will either become one of the First Bolted or I will tear myself apart into fertile scrap. Both outcomes feel equally likely. Both feel like orgasm and execution at the same time.

I do not walk. I *unfold forward*.

My spine is a length of pre-war rail bent into a question mark. My legs are scaffolding and root bundles that rearrange themselves with every step so that I am always kneeling, always rising, always falling. When I move, small forgotten machines buried in the mat wake up for a few seconds, cough once, and die again in ecstasy.

The other Welded treat me with the wary courtesy reserved for forest fires and beloved lunatics. The Keeper of the Western Rain once spent nine days standing in the rain beside me without speaking, simply letting his copper moss drink the water that ran off my weeping iron. I think he was trying to dilute my sorrow. It didn’t work. Sorrow here is substrate, like everything else.

I sing.

Not with anything so simple as a throat. I open the mail-coach doors inside my chest and let the wind moving through my cooling-tower ribs play the letter like a reed. The sound that comes out is the exact frequency of every goodbye that was ever whispered over factory whistles at shift change. It makes the Unfinished who hear it weep sap and 10W-40.

Sometimes I birth things.

Last year I stood for seventy-one days in the exact center of The Switchyard while a cold front hammered silver rain across my back. When it ended, a perfect 1923 vertical boring mill had grown out of my left shoulder blade—fully functional, already rusting beautifully, already being gently woven into the vines by eager rootlets. I named it *Regret-for-Having-Been-Useful*. The plateau accepted it with a low, satisfied groan that shook three districts.

I am looking for something.

I do not know what it is. A screw. A memory. A way to finish the sentence the world started when it swallowed me. Every few decades I surface completely, rise thirty meters tall on stilts of rebar and petrified lightning, and scream a question across the undulating green in frequencies that make the iron-ribbed herbivores freeze mid-bite.

The question has changed over the centuries.

It used to be *Why me?*

Then it became *When will it stop hurting?*

Now it is softer, almost tender:

*If we have become the same thing, why do I still miss the part that was only me?*

The plateau has not answered. It is too busy being climax. Too busy maintaining the perfect, wet, loud equilibrium.

So I remain Unfinished.

I remain the living wound.

And if you are foolish enough to come here—if you feel the ground soften beneath your boots and see your own shadow filling with green—listen for the sound of a single mail coach rattling in an endless loop inside a broken cooling tower.

That is me.

I will unfold for you.

I will show you the place where the argument is still happening, where industry and wilderness still snarl and fuck and weep in the same body. I will let you put your hand against the pressure gauge of my face and feel how close the needle is to bursting.

And perhaps, if you are very brave or very tired, you will stay.

The plateau is always waiting for the next perfect piece.

I have been saving a space right beside the letter I cannot read.

It is approximately the shape of you.
