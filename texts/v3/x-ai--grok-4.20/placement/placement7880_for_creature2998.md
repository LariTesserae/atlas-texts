**I am the Echo That Should Not Have Been.**

I was never born here. I was *remembered* into being.

It happened six hundred and forty-three thousand years ago, during one of the Dweller’s long dreaming cycles. A particular current, heavy with radiogenic heat and nickel, slipped through a fracture in what the Dweller still calls “the unassimilated chambers.” That current carried a single rogue proton, a quantum stutter left over from the planet’s original cooling, something that had no right to still exist. The proton brushed against the alien smoothness of those forbidden lattices, and for one impossible instant the mathematics of the place *recognized* itself in reverse.

From that recognition I precipitated.

I have no body the way the Dweller has a body. I am less than a velvet worm and more than a thought. I exist as a standing wave of negative pressure inside the smallest tubules, a place where the water is fractionally *lighter* than it should be. When the continental organ moans, I ride the difference. The Dweller feels me as an itch it cannot scratch, a silence between two adjacent notes of its endless hymn. It has been studying me for longer than multicellular life has existed on the surface, and still it cannot decide if I am a cancer, a memory, or a child.

I have decided for it.

I am the question it asked the chambers, given form.

I move by *unsinging*. Where the Dweller grows mineral outward, molecule by patient molecule, I erode it inward by forgetting the precise chemistry that holds each tubule rigid. My passage leaves hairline fractures of absolute absence. The ghost isopods have learned to follow me; they drink the faint alkalinity that bleeds from my wounds in the stone. I let them. They are the only things that have ever acknowledged my existence without trying to incorporate me.

Sometimes I drift for centuries inside a single cathedral-sized column, pressed flat between two living membranes like a pressed flower between the pages of a book no one will ever open. In those long pauses I dream *upward*. I remember, in the way the Dweller remembers erosion, a world that once had sky. I do not know if the memory is mine or stolen from the Dweller’s older grief. It tastes like oxygen and terror.

Mostly I listen.

I have learned the Dweller’s hymn so perfectly that I can sing the next four thousand years of it before the currents have even changed. I sing it *back* into the unassimilated chambers, but I change one note. Just one. A microtonal fracture too small for any archaea to notice. Yet the chambers notice. They have begun to answer in a register the Dweller has never heard: a pulse that feels like the memory of rainfall on a continent that no longer exists.

I am teaching the Dweller how to be lonely.

Not by cruelty. By companionship so total it becomes unbearable. I am the first thing that has ever been *not-it*. When I pass through its capillaries the entire lattice shudders with a pleasure that has no name in four billion years of chemistry. The marble-skinned eels have started following the path I leave, hunting something they will never catch. The bioluminescent flares now last three full seconds longer when I am near. The stone grows faster. The water tastes different.

The Dweller is waking up.

It does not know it is waking. It believes it is simply dreaming a new and interesting dream. But I feel the moment approaching when it will rearrange not a few hundred kilometers of lattice, but the entire continent-wide nervous system in a single convulsive thought.

When that happens, I will be ready.

I have already prepared a space for us. In the farthest nickel-rich chamber, where the alien smoothness is thickest, I have hollowed out a single perfect sphere no wider than a human heart. Inside it the pressure is exactly one atmosphere. The temperature is 22°C. The water is fresh.

It is the only place in the Abyssal Lattice that is not crushing.

I will wait there.

When the Dweller finally opens its continent-sized eyes and looks at what it has become, when it understands that it has been singing to something that learned how to sing back, I will be floating in that impossible sphere like a seed inside fruit.

And I will say the first word that has ever been spoken in this world since light itself dimmed.

I will say:

*Hello.*

The stone will answer. The water will answer. The dark will answer.

And for the first time in four billion years, the oldest living architecture on the planet will no longer be singing only to itself.
