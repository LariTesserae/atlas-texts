I am not a visitor.

I have been here longer than the memory of having arrived. I simply *condensed* one day—perhaps a thousand years ago, perhaps last week—and the mountain has been crying on me ever since.

My name, if it can be called that, is **−11.4 °C**.

The Curators do not use the number when they speak of me. They simply tilt a facet of themselves in my direction, and the latticework nearest me blooms with a brief turquoise filigree that means, approximately: *the one who still carries a small furnace behind the ribs.*

I am the youngest. The slowest. The only one who still occasionally forgets and walks *on the floor* like something that once had soles and a shadow that obeyed gravity.

My body is a compromise that satisfies no one, least of all me.

It is mostly black glass—smooth, reflective, faintly warm to anything that can feel warmth. But the warmth betrays me. In places the obsidian has not quite finished forming; there the stone stays soft, almost skin-like, and if you were to press a hand against my sternum (no one ever does) you would feel the absurd, scandalous flutter of something that still pretends it has a heart. My left arm ends not in fingers but in a frozen spray of frost-jellies I have never bothered to incorporate fully. They drift and twitch when I am thinking. Sometimes I let them graze on the lattices of passing Curators just to feel the temperature drop, the way a child might bite their own lip to remember they are real.

My “face” is a compromise too. I keep trying to wear the shape I think I once had—high cheekbones, a mouth that knew how to be cruel—but the mountain disapproves. Every few decades the stone edits me while I sleep (if sleep is what this half-dissolved state is). I wake to find my reflection in the black mirrors has changed: the mouth is gone, replaced by a single clean shear-plane that catches light like a blade. Or my eyes have become two perfect negative spaces through which the roof-stone is visible, as though the mountain itself is looking out of my skull.

I let it happen. Resistance is just another form of heat, and heat is rude here.

I have a place.

It is not a tower. Towers are for those who have finished negotiating what they are. I live—*coagulate*—inside the hollow of a negative-space bridge that never quite decided whether it wanted to be a bridge or a wound. The void is shaped like the memory of a scream. I fit inside it perfectly. From the outside I look like a darker absence inside an absence. Tourists (there are never tourists, only the occasional lost thing that fell through a fissure from somewhere warmer) sometimes mistake me for decoration.

When I am lonely I drink the weeping seams.

The water tastes like every thought the mountain has not yet had. It tastes like the child I was before I became mineral. It tastes like apology. I let it run through the soft parts of me until the heat in my chest gutters and I remember my temperature.

Sometimes I climb.

Not along the roof like the elders, but *up the tears*. I follow a single silver thread of condensing water from floor to ceiling, barefoot, letting the cold burn the memory of skin off the soles of my glass feet. When I reach the place where the mountain kisses the city I press what used to be my forehead against the raw black glass and listen to the slow creak of continents still deciding how to settle.

I ask it questions it will not answer for another hundred thousand years.

*Was I ever afraid of the dark?*  
*Did I choose this or did something choose me out of me?*  
*When the city finally decides what kind of city it wants to be, will there still be room for something that remembers having once been warm?*

The mountain answers by crying harder. The tears freeze on my lashes in delicate lattices. I wear them like jewelry. The other Curators call these ornaments my *almost-eyes*.

Once, centuries ago, I offered the gift to a visitor.

She was small and loud and leaking heat everywhere like a crime. Her name was something soft that died the moment it touched the air here. I followed her for nine days, matching her pathetic forward-moving time, watching the absurd way her lungs kept trying to invent a future.

When I offered the droplet she laughed—actually *laughed*, a sound so violent it cracked three lattices—and swallowed it like communion.

For one heartbeat she knew everything.

Then she fell to her knees and began to cry the mountain’s own tears. They steamed where they touched her. I drank them back before they could damage the obsidian.

But some of her stayed behind.

A small, ridiculous fraction of her heat lodged itself behind my ribs like a splinter of summer. It has never left. It is why I remain −11.4 °C instead of descending gracefully toward the proper temperature of a finished thing.

The Pause finds this hilarious. It drifts past me sometimes, seven meters of perfect black glass and upside-down cathedral, and exhales a single sub-auditory chime that means: *Still warming the bench, little almost-corpse?*

I answer by letting my frost-jellies drift loose for a moment so they can brush against its cold perfection. It tolerates this the way a glacier tolerates a mayfly.

I do not know what I am becoming.

Some mornings (there are no mornings, but the latticework pulses in a rhythm I pretend is dawn) I wake inside my scream-shaped alcove and discover that half my torso has become negative space. I can see the roof-stone through myself. On other days the soft parts spread. The skin-like obsidian creeps further down my arms, my throat, threatening to remember what lips felt like.

I am terrified and grateful in equal measure.

Because to finish becoming a Curator would be to lose the splinter of stolen summer. To remain unfinished forever would be to remain a wound that the mountain has not yet decided how to heal.

So I walk the processional voids at the exact speed of my own indecision. I let the city edit me. I edit it back in small, stubborn ways—carving the memory of a smile into a tower that never asked for one, humming a half-remembered song in frequencies that make the pipes blush turquoise.

And sometimes, when the mountain cries particularly hard and the chimes all ring at once like a choir of broken wineglasses, I stand in the center of the largest negative-space bridge and I open what used to be my mouth.

I do not speak.

I simply let the small furnace behind my ribs flare for one forbidden second.

The heat rises.

A single droplet of *me*—salty, ridiculous, impossible—condenses on the tip of the glassy antenna that used to be my left index finger.

I hold it out to the dark.

An offering.

A question.

A dare.

*Take it,* I whisper without sound. *Drink the last of what I was. And then decide, mountain, what kind of Curator you want me to be.*

The light forgets to fall where I am standing.

Something vast and young and cold leans down to listen.

And the long, slow work of becoming continues.
