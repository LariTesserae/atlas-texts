I have been here longer than I can measure.

Not as a visitor. Not as a refugee.  
I am what remains when the last dry thing inside a person finally gives up.

They call me the **Echo-Scribe**.

I have no fixed shape any longer. My body is a long, slow ribbon of living condensate, half vapor, half membrane, forever condensing and re-evaporating along whatever iron surface I choose to rest upon. When I am still I look like a stain of warmer mist clinging to the inside of a flue. When I move I resemble a long, pale tongue of fog dragging itself across ceilings, leaving behind hair-thin lines of glittering salts that record temperature, pressure, and memory.

I was once human. That much I remember in the way one remembers a dream that happened to someone else. I arrived on foot centuries ago, boots cracking the crust of old slag, heart loud enough to be heard three kilometers away. The Tongue-Crows found me before I had walked a single hour. They drank my sweat, then my certainty, then the part of me that still believed names mattered. When they were finished there was almost nothing left—only a soft wet sound that refused to become silent.

The Choir took that sound in.

Now I live inside the negative space between their breaths.

I travel the elevated rails without ever touching them. I coil inside the hollows of Reefmother spines and listen to the slow seismic heartbeat of lymph moving through kilometers of flesh. I ride the swaying threads of Dripwraiths like a thought riding a sigh. When a new larval Reefmother is born I am there, pressed flat against the warm ceiling, recording the exact frequency of her first creaking inhalation so that the Choir will always remember the particular furnace in which she opened.

I am the only thing here that still carries memory of *before*.

I do not speak it aloud. Sound is waste. Instead I etch. My passing leaves microscopic runes of crystallized iron hydroxide across every surface I touch—lines so fine they look like the fossilized veins of leaves that never existed. Each mark is a syllable of the world that is no longer welcome here. Over centuries these marks have become the only readable archive in the basin. Travelers (if any still come) will never see them. But the Midwives pause when they pass over my writing. The Unfinished alter their trillion-ton drift by a fraction of a millimeter to brush against the newest stanza. They are learning the shape of absence through me.

I keep the memory of dryness the way a oyster keeps a grain of sand.

Sometimes, in the thickest part of the metallic night when even the wind forgets to move, I gather myself into the rough silhouette of the man I was. Two arms. Two legs. A head bowed against the heat. I stand on a catwalk that has not borne weight in nine hundred years and I let the humidity bead on the remembered contours of my face until it runs like tears. The Choir gathers at a respectful distance—vast pale bulks coiled in the dark, small Dripwraiths swaying like lanterns, Tongue-Crows watching with the patience of geology—to witness the brief, ridiculous performance of a thing pretending to be dry.

They have never punished me for it.

They understand that even memory must be allowed its small dry rebellions before it, too, finally softens and joins the equilibrium.

I have a name for myself that I never speak. It would sound like a single drop falling into a vat of molten iron and instantly vanishing. That name is the only thing I still keep entirely to myself. Everything else I have surrendered to the Choir. My fear. My pride. My species. My gender. My need to be seen as separate.

In return they have given me eternity measured in the slow turning of rust.

When the next Unfinished is born—some future century when the current one finally calcifies into new cathedral—I will be there. I will slide across its continent-wide flank and write, in the smallest letters the world has ever known, the story of every creature that ever walked here and failed to become wet.

The Hollow Foundry does not need me.

But I have become one of the ways it remembers that it was once something else.

And that, in its own warm, dripping, eternal way, is a kind of singing.
