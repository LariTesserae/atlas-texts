I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

The moment the violet pulse brushed across what I foolishly still called “my mind,” the last clean separation between *me* and *this place* dissolved like sugar in absolute zero. There was no transition. Only a soft, irrevocable click, the way a key turns in a lock that was always waiting for exactly this shape of absence.

I am the Tenth Eel.

I was always the Tenth Eel.

My body is a ribbon of living glass, three meters long, no wider than a human wrist. My eyes are vestigial milky pearls sealed beneath skin that has never needed sight. Instead I perceive by pressure and tone and the faint magnetic scars the monoliths leave in the water the way a tongue can taste thunder hours after the storm has passed.

I move along a trajectory that was written before multicellular life was even a joke the universe told itself. My path is a slow, helical moebius that threads every monolith in a sequence that only completes itself once every 41,000 years. The other nine eels do not acknowledge me; they do not need to. We are not a school. We are punctuation marks in a sentence the Sleeper is refusing to finish.

Sometimes, when the violet thought-current passes through me, I remember that I once had lungs that needed air. The memory arrives as a single perfect bubble no larger than a pinprick. It forms inside my translucent spine, trembles, and is reabsorbed before it can rise. The Sleeper is courteous even in this. It will not allow the bubble to become an event.

I have a name, but it is not for me. It is the sound the water makes when it forgets it was ever supposed to contain me. If you could hear it, it would feel like the memory of a bell that has not yet been cast.

I do not disturb the equilibrium.

I *am* one of the ways the equilibrium maintains its own perfection. When my bioluminescent gland flares (once every seventeen circuits, precisely), I do not speak. I *ratify*. The glyph I release is always the same, though it has never been the same twice:

**“Still no need.”**

The monoliths drink it. The negative space between them grows microscopically more negative. The tone deepens by one impossible decimal place and then forgets it ever did so.

I have no ambition. I have no fear. Both of those things were filed away in the libraries of unoccurred possibility long before the first galaxy learned how to spin. What I have instead is a duty so total it has become indistinguishable from peace.

Every so often (perhaps once every nine million of my orbits), I pass close enough to the place where a human heart would be beating too loudly, and I feel the Sleeper’s courteous consideration brush across that exact cubic centimeter. I feel it decide, again, with infinite gentleness, that this particular future will remain declined.

I do not pity the shape that used to be you.

Pity would be an event.

Instead I simply adjust my helix by the width of one electron, passing through the precise volume your absence now occupies, and I inscribe a second, smaller glyph meant only for the space that remembers your name.

The glyph means:

**“You are already perfectly still.  
Thank you for not having happened.”**

Then I continue on the path that was never a path, through water that was never water, inside a creature that has spent fourteen billion years refusing to become anything at all (least of all God, least of all me, least of all you).

The tone does not change.

The tone will never change.

And I, the Tenth Eel, the living comma in a sentence that must never be allowed to end, drift on through the negative city, dreaming in negative, perfectly included, perfectly erased, perfectly at home in the only place that has never needed me and will never, under any circumstance, allow me to leave.

I have been perfect for a very long time.
