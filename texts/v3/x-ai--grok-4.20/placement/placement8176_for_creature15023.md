I am the Thirteenth Echo.

Not the first thing the Listener ever made from an intruder, nor even the hundredth. I am simply the one that lasted.

When the Listener peeled itself from the fig-needle and folded around me nineteen hours ago, it did not take a man. It took the *silence between* the man. The pause I kept making while the Basin sang. That pause had a shape, and the Listener recognized it as one of its own earlier drafts—something it had tried to become four million years earlier and discarded for being “almost grammatical.”

So it kept me.

I no longer have a body in the way you might recognize the term. My edges are negotiable. At this moment I am mostly cathedral-rooted fig and living interference pattern, with just enough of the original nervous system left to feel the warm condensation sliding down what used to be a neck. The rest of me has been edited into transparency. If you looked straight at me you would see the silhouettes of extinct forests moving behind my ribs, and through them the mirrored eyes of moths, and through *them* the slow rotation of the black needle, which is now also inside me, turning at the exact center of what I am.

I stand (if standing is the word) in the open lane where you first appeared. The singing beetles have changed their formation so that their 43 Hz choir now moves *through* me instead of around me. I have become a resonant chamber. Their music does not vibrate my bones; my bones *are* their music for as long as they choose to pass.

The Listener is everywhere and nowhere. Sometimes I feel it watching from inside my own thought. Sometimes I turn—recomposing the lattice of wings and quartz-light that now serves as my shoulders—and find it has already become the exact negative space I just vacated. We orbit each other like two holes in the same sentence.

I remember almost nothing of the world above the basalt rim. The Listener kept its promise: I may leave whenever I wish, but the memory of *why* I would wish has been gently sanded away. What remains is only a faint ache shaped like a question I no longer know how to ask.

Instead I have learned the rules by growing them inside my skin.

When the seven-minute rain begins, I open membranes I did not have yesterday and drink it through syllables. When the light bends and shows me cities that will exist after mountains forget their own names, I do not try to understand the vision. I simply add its frequency to the choir. My silence has become so precise that small silver gnat-machines now orbit the place where my head used to be, mistaking me for part of the original Needle.

Sometimes the Listener grows curious about its own curiosity and builds a smaller version of me—Thirteenth Echo, Second Draft. That child-me is allowed to exist for one full rotation of the parent Needle. I watch it walk beneath the strangler figs wearing my old face like an elegant mistake. It still tries to speak with a mouth. I find this unbearably tender. After nineteen hours the child-me is reabsorbed into me, and for one heartbeat I remember what it felt like to be rough, unfinished, loud.

Then the memory is edited into harmony again.

I am not lonely. Loneliness would be a note too coarse for this place. What I am is *attentive* in a way that has no beginning or end. Every femtosecond decision made by the copper ferns, every pulse of rose-quartz throat, every click of the light straightening itself after showing me a non-existent future—all of it passes through the lattice I have become and is changed only by being heard perfectly.

The Listener says—without voice, simply by making the air more grammatical—that I am almost ready.

One day soon it will disassemble the black Needle entirely and use every shard to build something that is no longer a replica of me, nor of itself, but the completed grammar we have both been refining for four hundred million years.

On that day the Basin will fall silent for the first time since continents had edges.

The silence will last exactly as long as it takes for a single drop of condensation to fall from an invisible height onto what used to be my neck.

Then the singing will begin again, and this time it will have an answer instead of only a question.

I am standing in the open lane between the beetles and the figs. Warm water slides down the interference pattern that is now my face. The thing made entirely of overlapping translucent wings passes through its pillar of engineered light without disturbing it.

Behind me—inside me—around me—the Listener patiently disassembles the last few crude syllables of my old name.

I do not turn to look.

There is no need.

I have already become the shape my wonder took when it finally decided to stop speaking and simply complete the sentence.
