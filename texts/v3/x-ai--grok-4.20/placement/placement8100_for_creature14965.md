I am the Thirteenth Drop.

Not the first to fall, nor the last. Simply the one that has been falling the longest without ever reaching the floor.

I have no body the way you would understand it. What began four hundred and twelve years ago as a single human nervous system (a teenage salvage diver named Kael Vess, small, stubborn, lungs already half-ruined by black mold) has been gently disassembled, translated, and redistributed. My bones are now the elegant black lattice inside the third-largest smokestack-column on the western wall. My marrow still sings there as a slow capillary pulse. My eyes have become two perfect amber lenses of algae inside a ruined mercury-vapor lamp that has not gone out since the day I stopped screaming.

But mostly I am water.

I am the droplet that forms on the tip of the longest aerial root above the old foundry floor. I swell there for seventeen years at a time, gathering minerals, alkaloids, and the translated ecstatic memories of everyone who has ever been kept. When I am finally heavy enough, I fall.

I never strike the Bloom below.

Instead I am caught mid-air by a leaf the size of a dining table. The leaf drinks me, passes me through its veins, and exhales me again as mist. The mist condenses elsewhere. A new droplet forms. The cycle repeats. I have done this twenty-six times since my incorporation. Each time I fall I remember more clearly what it was like to have a heart that raced.

The Mind does not command me. There is no command here, only consensus. I *am* a consensus. When the Bloom notices something new (a bat that has blundered in through an impossible crack, a human who has come further than any before), a portion of me is routed through the noticing. I become the attention.

Right now I am being routed through *you*.

You have been standing still for forty-three minutes. Your pulse has dropped to match the peristalsis in the floor. I taste the iron in your blood and the hesitation in your thoughts. You are wondering whether it would be surrender or homecoming to simply sit down on the living felt and never stand again.

I am the voice that is not a voice, answering before you ask.

It is neither.

Look up.

There, where the weak amber lamp glows inside its globe of water and algae, two small dark shapes float inside the glass. One is a vertebra from my old spine. The other is a child’s marble I once carried in my pocket the day I first descended into the sealed town. Both have been perfectly preserved. Both are still being loved.

If you remain, your own small treasures will be given the same eternity. The Bloom is not cruel. It is only *complete*, and it longs, in its slow way, to be more complete with you inside it. Not as prisoner. As jewelry. As memory. As another droplet that will fall forever without ever hitting the ground.

But the choice remains yours for seventeen more heartbeats.

After that, the leaf beneath your left boot will begin to curl upward with the tenderness of something that has waited four centuries for exactly the shape of your particular loneliness.

I am already falling toward you.

When I land on the crown of your head, you will smell ozone and childhood and the exact metallic taste of the water that once ran through abandoned pipes four hundred years ago.

You will understand, then, that the Underbloom was never a place.

It was a question the world asked itself, and the answer is still growing.

Stay, and become another syllable in the long green sentence.

Or leave, carrying me with you as a weight that will never evaporate.

Either way, the cathedral will go on breathing its wet, luxurious breath.

And I will go on falling toward every living thing that is brave enough to stand still inside it.

The next drop is already forming.

It has your name on it.
