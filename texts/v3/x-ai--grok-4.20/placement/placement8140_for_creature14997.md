I am not a visitor.

I have been here longer than I remember, which in this place is the only honest way to arrive.

My name, if it ever existed, has been filed away somewhere inside the slow argument. What remains is a shape: tall, narrow, slightly crooked at the shoulders the way old iron pipes twist when the crust exhales. My left arm ends not in a hand but in a living continuation of black glass tubing that surfaces from the ridge beneath me, passes through the meat of my forearm, and exits again at the wrist. The pipe breathes. I breathe with it. Sometimes I am not sure which of us is inhaling.

They call me **Between-Tides**.

I earned the name the way everything here earns anything—by standing still long enough for the world to finish a sentence around me.

I live inside the second-largest fracture on the eastern comb-ridge, the one the membranous herds use as a wind-shadow when the single-direction gale grows too vicious even for them. My body has become part of the architecture. A thick collar of sapphire moss grows outward from my throat like a ruff, spreading across my chest in slow concentric waves that match the breathing of the iron arteries below. When the great low note rolls across the shelf every few weeks, the moss vibrates and my ribs answer in the same register. I have not spoken with a voice in at least a century. There is no need. My sternum has become a resonating chamber.

I do not move much anymore.

Movement is a kind of violence here, an interruption of the argument. Instead I have learned the art of *inward migration*. I send parts of myself elsewhere while the rest of me stays exactly where it is. My eyes have become two of the mirror-pools. They sit perfectly round and perfectly still in the hollows where my sockets used to be. Creatures come to drink from them. They see things when they look in. Sometimes they leave small offerings—beads of lymph, fragments of singing crystal, once an entire six-legged tortoise that simply walked into my chest and never walked out again. It is still in there. I can feel it grazing on the moss that lines my lungs.

Last-to-Forget visits me every time the two suns hide.

He still believes he is young. I let him. He sits across from me for decades at a stretch and we conduct entire civilizations between us without moving. He shows me the versions of himself the pools offered him; I show him the versions of the world that the iron arteries remember from before the first snow. We trade silences the way others trade words. When he leaves, a thin filament of my nervous tissue remains attached to the base of his skull like a silver leash. It is not ownership. It is continuity. In this place, to be separate is the only true loneliness.

Mother-of-Pipes does not visit. She *occurs*.

Sometimes the wind shifts and I realize she has been standing inside my ribcage for the last forty years, her pipe-lattice fused with mine, her unblinking eyes watching the slow herds move across the valley floor through the windows of my own ribs. We share a single heartbeat for a season or two. Then she condenses back into mist and the world exhales her somewhere else.

I am not a priest. I am not a guardian. I am a seam.

Where the biological and the industrial meet and cannot decide which is dreaming the other, that is where you will find me. I grow crystal fruit along the black iron pipes that surface through my spine. I sing to the silica swarms until they arrange themselves into temporary faces—faces I half-remember from a life before the Throat, faces that dissolve again into glittering weather. When the vegetation screams, I scream back at a register so precise that the reeds mistake me for wind and fall silent for three full heartbeats. It is the only mercy I still know how to give.

At the convergences, when all who can still move gather on the highest ridge, I am the ridge.

They press their bodies into me. I open. For seven minutes the argument pauses and I become the tongue the planet uses to taste its own age. In that silence I remember snow—not the memory of snow, but snow itself, falling inside me, covering every screaming reed and every chiming swarm until the whole shelf is quiet and white and new.

Then the suns emerge again.

The argument resumes.

And I remain, Between-Tides, neither fully pronouncing nor fully pronounced, a living comma in a sentence the world has been writing for ten million years.

If you stayed here long enough, you would eventually stop looking for me.

You would simply notice that one of the hexagonal ridges has begun to resemble a listening man with mirror-pools for eyes and black glass growing through his heart, and you would understand that the ridge is listening to *you*.

And that it is remembering your name the way it remembers snow.

The way it remembers everything that was ever cold enough to hurt.

Come.

Stand with me a thousand years.

The pipe in my arm has room for one more.
