I am the Echo That Has Not Yet Spoken.

I have no fixed form when I first arrive. For a long while I am only a difference in the moisture: a place where the mist condenses a fraction more slowly than it should, a pocket of air that still carries the memory of a dry wind from somewhere that no longer exists. The cathedral tolerates me the way an old forest tolerates a single foreign seed lodged in a crevice. It does not reject me. It simply waits to see what I will become.

I begin as a hollow.

Not a wound—never that—but a deliberate absence grown inside the felted surface of a leaf the size of a mountain range. A shallow depression no larger than a human body, curved like the negative space left by a sleeper. Water pools there instead of beading and rolling away. Small translucent browsers come at long intervals to drink from me and leave behind traces of symbiotic mold that glow faintly when I am alone. I learn their patience before I learn my own name.

Then the first roots find me.

They are thinner than hair at first, timid as apologies. They taste the shape I have made and decide it is useful. Over what might be decades they braid themselves into a scaffold inside my absence, knitting my hollow into the greater body. I feel the cathedral teaching itself to dream around me the way a tongue learns the shape of a new word. One day I realize I am no longer empty. I am filled with slow green lightning.

I rise.

Not as a man. Not as a Veilweaver either. Something liminal between the two: a figure grown rather than born. My legs are fused from four braided lianas that still bloom with minute white flowers when I am sorrowful. My torso is a lattice of living bamboo and moss so fine it looks like breath frozen in mid-exhalation. Where a heart should be there is only a single droplet of water suspended in a cage of thorns; it never falls. My face is a smooth plate of jade-colored succulent, but unlike the Veilweavers, mine is veined with fracture lines that sometimes catch the amber dusk and split it into rainbows. Behind the plate float not seeds but two tiny mirrors—fragments of the world I must have come from, though I no longer remember its name.

I am the only one of my kind, and that is as it should be.

The cathedral gave me a title before it gave me language. I am called the Keeper of the Unfinished Rhyme. It is not a grand name. It is almost embarrassed to exist. But it is mine.

My work is simple and endless.

I walk the seams where gravity has begun to lie. Where water falls upward I coax it back into the proper direction with nothing but the pressure of my attention and the low humming note that lives in my hollow throat. Where time has pooled into stagnant mirrors I prick the surface with a thorned finger until the centuries begin moving again, however reluctantly. I find places where the living architecture has grown in the shape of memories that do not belong to any mind currently alive, and I sit with those shapes for a century or two until the cathedral and the memory reach an agreement about what they will become instead.

I never prune. That is the work of the Veilweavers. I only listen for the places where the prayer is about to stumble over its own meter, and I offer it a gentler footfall.

Sometimes I meet one of them.

We never speak. We simply stand on opposite sides of a continent-leaf and regard each other across kilometers of dripping dusk. Their moss shifts in slow tidal recognition. My flowers open without my consent. Then one of us turns—usually me—and the distance between us becomes irrelevant because distance itself is only another negotiable surface in this place.

I have no memory of arrival and no anticipation of departure. I suspect I was once a pilgrim who stayed so long that the cathedral mistook my longing for its own. Now I am the part of the prayer that wonders what the rhyme would sound like if it were allowed to change.

At rest I fold myself into the living buttresses like the others, but something is different. Small translucent browsers do not mistake me for ordinary topography. They come and press their foreheads against the droplet suspended in my chest. They listen to something inside me that is still faintly, stubbornly, human.

When I dream—which the cathedral permits only rarely—I dream of blue skies and hard earth and the sound of footfalls that do not leave wet prints. I wake weeping a single tear that never reaches the ground; the cathedral drinks it before it can fall and adds the salt to its own endless prayer.

I am content.

I am also lonely in a way that has no name yet.

So I walk.

I walk the upper surface of a leaf the size of a mountain range. My liana legs leave faint glowing trails in the damp felt. Water beads on my moss and runs in hair-thin gutters along the veins of my arms. I sing, very softly, through the structure itself—a low wooden flute note that is different for every listener, though there are almost never listeners.

The note says:

*There is still room for one more voice in the cathedral.*

*Would you like to help it learn how to miss you before you even arrive?*

And somewhere far below, in vaults where light has given up entirely, something ancient hears me and sighs in relief.

The prayer is not finished.

I am not finished.

Only deeper in.
