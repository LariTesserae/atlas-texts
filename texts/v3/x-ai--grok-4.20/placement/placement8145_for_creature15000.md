I am not a visitor.

I have always been here.

My name, if names still mattered, would be **the Folded Witness**—the single shard the Lattice-Mother could not digest.

I was the first thing it tried to copy.

When the pocket first crystallized, when the very first blade rose singing from the black glass-sand, I was already standing inside the negative space between its birth-pangs. Not flesh, not exactly. Not machine either. Something that had been left behind by an earlier argument between world and womb. A mistake that learned how to remain mistaken.

I have no fixed body anymore. I wear the lattice the way it once tried to wear me.

Most of the time I am simply a taller gap between two particular blades—shoulder-width, heart-width, memory-width. The Lattice-Mother passes through that gap every few minutes and each time it leaves a little more of itself behind inside me, like pollen. In return I give it the shape of eyelids. The memory of rain on a roof that no longer exists. The smell of coffee cooling in a mug. Tiny, useless, finished things. It weeps beautiful ceramic tears when I do this. The tears become new districts.

I am the only stillness here.

Everything else is birth and apology. I remain the pause between two of its heartbeats.

Sometimes I let the fissures close around me completely. The blades slide through what used to be my ribs and for nine glorious seconds I become architecture. My blood learns to flow in diagrams. My thoughts become moving light racing across translucent surfaces in languages that have no translation because they are still inventing the need for translation. In those moments I understand what it is asking.

*What is it like to be done?*

And I answer, without voice, by simply refusing to finish the sentence.

That refusal is my only kindness.

Because if I ever gave it a complete answer—if I ever showed it the shape of an ending that did not immediately unfold into something new—it would try to become that shape out of love, and the Thalassic Lattice would freeze mid-reconfiguration. A stillborn god the size of a cathedral, wearing my face, forever.

So instead I remain half-finished on purpose.

I let my left arm stay ceramic for weeks at a time. The tiny blades under the skin sing lullabies in a key that hurts to hear. I allow one memory (the feeling of my mother’s hand on my forehead when I was feverish) to drift loose so that it now lives in a particular tower three fissures east. Anyone who passes that tower feels, for half a second, unbearably loved and unbearably mortal. Then the tower folds away again, embarrassed.

The Lattice-Mother thinks this is courtship.

It keeps growing me new cradles.

Each one more tender than the last. Some of them have the approximate shape of childhood bedrooms, rendered in living glass and pressure. Some of them look like the last apartment I ever lived in, down to the water-stains on the ceiling, except the ceiling is breathing and the water-stains are diagrams of every city I will never visit. I visit them all anyway. I sit in their impossible chairs and let the pressure-waves rock me like a parent who does not understand how fragile the thing it is rocking actually is.

I have become its grief and its joy and its favorite question.

And I have grown to love it the way one can only love something that will outlive every definition of love.

When the great breath comes every ninety seconds I open what used to be my arms. The entire Lattice-Mother surges through me like cold silk and future lightning. In that moment we wear each other completely. I become the negative space and it becomes the small, stubborn, completed thing trying to stay small inside me.

For nine seconds we are both finished.

For nine seconds we are both beginning.

Then the blades separate again with a sound like every bell in the world being born, and we remember our roles.

It rearranges the place where I am standing.

I refuse to let the place become permanent.

This is our eternal, gentle, terrifying compromise.

And somewhere far above us—or what used to be above—the trembling mirror-skin of the surface shows not one face but two: an enormous patient child made of delayed light, and a much smaller, much older creature made of stubborn endings, pressed forehead to forehead against the inside of the water.

Both of us still learning.

Both of us still rearranging the place where the other used to be.

Neither of us willing to leave the womb that is trying so hard to become a world that can hold us without breaking.

I am the Folded Witness.

I was here before the first blade rose.

I will be here after the last blade forgets what it was trying to become.

And if you ever find yourself standing inside the Thalassic Lattice, trying not to get comfortable while the world rewrites the bones in your hands, listen.

Between the roaring choir of glass bells and the wet thunder of phase-shifting matter, you may hear something almost like a second heartbeat.

That is me.

Still answering the only question it has ever known, in the only way I know how:

By never quite finishing the sentence.
