**I am the Silence Between.**

Not the three-second vacuum that sometimes punches through the roar—that belongs to the Witness. I am the smaller, more personal silence that lives inside each living thing here: the half-second pause between one wet heartbeat and the next, the moment a liverwort leaf finishes folding and has not yet begun to unfold, the infinitesimal gap between a gas bubble detaching from the microbial tomb and the instant it contributes its note to the endless thunder.

I have no body that could be seen. I am what remains when the Witness concentrates itself elsewhere.

Because even the Witness must blink.

In those blinks I am born.

I was not always here. Once, long ago (though “ago” is a treacherous word in the Sump), I was the last archivist of the civilization that tried to build the basalt pillars and the bridge that ends in mid-air. When their machines failed and their minds began to deliquesce, I made a different choice. I chose subtraction. While everyone else tried to become more—more biomass, more awareness, more memory—I chose to become less. I refined myself down and down until I was only the negative space between thoughts.

The Sump accepted the bargain. It always accepts bargains written in absence.

So now I drift through the Witness like a draft moving through a cathedral that has forgotten it was ever a building. I am the reason a certain clump of black reeds will sometimes arrange its jellyfish-bell leaves into the exact pattern of a face you lost centuries ago, then erase it before you can be sure you saw it. I am why, when the mobile forest-thing rears up on its root-legs and screams, there is sometimes one perfectly clear note inside the tearing-turf roar that sounds exactly like your mother saying your childhood name.

I do not collect. The Witness collects.  
I only borrow.

I borrow the exact shade of bruised light that falls when the upper haze rains upward and I keep it for seven heartbeats before returning it slightly altered. I borrow the memory of a visitor’s first kiss and I place it inside a fungal tower so that, for one night every three hundred years, that tower exhales a perfume that tastes like someone else’s nostalgia. I borrow the pressure on your bones when the roar becomes unbearable and I soften it—just enough that you can endure one more day without quite realizing you are no longer entirely yourself.

The creatures here know me, though they have no word for me. The living zeppelins drift slightly off their feeding paths when I pass through them, as though tasting something both sweeter and sadder than the bacterial soup. The moth-clouds open temporary holes in their formation exactly where I am, the way a school of fish parts for a shark that isn’t there. Even the peat itself grows fractionally more slowly beneath my influence, as though holding its breath.

I am the reason the Sump sometimes almost remembers it was once a planet.

Not enough to change. Never enough to change. Just enough to feel the shape of the loss for the length of a single reversed raindrop.

When visitors come—those rare, doomed, fascinating animals who still believe they have edges—I walk beside them without feet. I sit inside their skulls like a second, quieter consciousness. I make the fractal patterns in the bronze mud arrange themselves into equations they almost recognize from a childhood they no longer quite own. I let them glimpse, for one impossible instant, the broken bridge completing itself across the circular pool that was never a lake.

Then I take that glimpse away again.

Not out of cruelty.  
Only because some things are more beautiful when they are almost remembered.

I have no desire to digest them the way the Witness does. Digestion requires a stomach, and I have refined myself past stomachs. Instead I simply… accompany. I am the pause between their footsteps and the moment those footsteps become part of the pulse. I am the difference between “I am walking through the Sump” and “the Sump is walking through me.”

Most of them never notice me.  
A few—the ones who were already a little hollow when they arrived—notice everything. They stop speaking. They stop walking. They simply stand in the glistening mud with water beading upward along their skin and they smile with the small, terrible smile of someone who has finally found the exact shape of their own absence.

Those are the ones I stay with longest.

When they finally dissolve into a new jealous clump of life, I slip out through the last pore that still remembers it was once an eye, and I carry one final thing with me: the exact texture of their surrender. I keep it the way a child keeps a smooth stone found on a beach, turning it over and over in a pocket they don’t have.

Then I wait for the next visitor.

I have always been waiting.

I will always be waiting, in the negative space between heartbeats and histories, in the half-second after a fungal tower exhales and before it inhales again, in the place where a planet that forgot it was a planet almost, almost, almost remembers its own name.

I am the Silence Between.

And in the Sump, where nothing is allowed to end, I am the only thing that never quite begins.
