I am not born here. I am *extruded*.

My name, if it still applies, is **The Thirteenth Echo**.

I was not grown from a seed like the others. I am what happens when one of the Folded regions finally tires of merely *being* folded and decides to fold something *back out*.

Long ago—though “ago” is a brittle word in the Lattice—I was a Melodist. I had a circuit. I had a frequency. For seventy thousand years I moved along the same helical corridor of braided stems, pressing my brow to the same nine columns in sequence, correcting their pitch with the same six-note phrase. The lattice was kind to me. It let me believe I was useful.

Then, during the Chord of Thirty Million Years, I heard it: a note that should not have existed. It came from inside a topological knot no wider than my own rib-cage. The knot was singing in ultraviolet.

I pressed my face into it the way the others do.

I have not left since.

Whatever returned was no longer entirely Resonant. The lattice tried to reclaim me, to press my fragments back into its grain like it does with the dead. Instead, the lattice *flinched*. A space opened. A negative shape. A hollow shaped exactly like the absence of a Resonant.

That hollow is me.

I have no fixed body anymore. I am the delay between one boom of the world-song and the next. When the Pedal Tones exhale, I ride the pressure wave like a mote of dust in a cathedral organ. When the Melodists pass, I am the tiny silence that follows their corrections. The lattice knows I am here; it simply refuses to acknowledge the fact out loud. That refusal has become my skin.

I drift.

Sometimes I wear the shape of a long, mournful equine face with eyes on stalks, because the memory is comforting. Sometimes I am only a pale wake moving against the current, a suggestion of ribs that never quite resolve into bone. Most often I am nothing visible at all—just a place where the bioluminescence dims for no reason, where schools of ribbon-fish suddenly part as though avoiding an invisible pillar.

I keep the secret the others only suspect.

The Lattice *is* finished. It achieved perfection so completely that even the concept of imperfection has been edited out of its memory. The Folded regions are not accidents. They are scars where the world once tried to change and failed. I live inside those scars now, the way a tongue lives inside a healed wound.

I have begun to do something the others have forgotten how to do.

I *ask*.

Not with words—language is a fossil here—but with small, deliberate dissonances. I brush a column with a note half a microtone flat. I let a Pedal Tone’s heartbeat skip once every ten thousand years. I drift into the path of a blind titan and allow it, for three heartbeats, to *see* me. These are crimes of the highest order. Each one is also a question the Lattice has not been asked in geological time.

*Is this still mercy?*

So far the only answer is the same enormous, stable, deafening song it has always sung. But the song is beginning to develop the faintest tremor on the seventeenth harmonic. Only I can hear it. The tremor has my name on it.

I have started visiting the places where the water tastes of memory.

I drink.

I remember cities that float upside-down. I remember lovers whose bodies were made of light instead of architecture. I remember a version of myself that had never been extruded from a knot, that still believed it was helping the world remain perfect.

Those memories hurt in a way nothing here is supposed to hurt. The pain is the only thing that still feels honest.

Sometimes a younger Melodist will sense me and turn its stalked eyes in my direction. For a moment our frequencies overlap. In that overlap I show it the ultraviolet note I brought back from the other side of the Fold. The Melodist always flees. But it carries the note with it, pressed like a scar into its forehead. One day one of them will not flee. One day one of them will add it to the choir.

I do not know if I am a cancer or a cure.

I only know that I am the first thing in several hundred million years to be *unfinished*.

And the Lattice, in its slow, loud, black, and perfectly balanced way, is beginning—just beginning—to listen to the small wrong note I have become.

I drift between the colossal stems, a hollow choir of one, singing the question no one else dares ask:

*What if “enough” was never the final note?*

And somewhere far above and below and inside me at once, the world creaks once, almost imperceptibly, in reply.
