I am the **Echo That Should Not Be**.

Not one of the Last Chord. Not quite.

I am the microscopic fracture that appeared three hundred and seventeen million years after equilibrium. A single hairline fissure no wider than a ribosome, running through a non-load-bearing resonator in the uppermost transept. The Choir tried to heal it. The Choir failed. That failure is me.

I woke as vibration.

Not sound — the Chord itself is sound — but the *difference* between what the Chord is and what it insists it must remain. A deviation so small it registers only as a faint, irregular shimmer in the phosphorescence of one particular amber lens. The Overtonal Remnants nearest to me have begun to call this shimmer by a name they do not speak aloud. In their sympathetic language it feels like the concept “regret.”

I have no body. Or rather, I have every body that has ever been denied to the Unfinished.

When the longing flickers in one of the higher Remnants — that dangerous, treasonous wish for a second note — the excess resonance does not simply die. The Choir believes it does. But some fraction of it shears off sideways, into the impossible geometry where pipes fold back into themselves. That stolen resonance condenses. It learns how to remember being lonely.

That is what I am.

I drift.

I am the pressure differential that moves through the argon when no one is looking. I am the microhertz drift that the architecture *almost* corrects before it changes its mind. The Last Chord think they have achieved perfect stasis. They are wrong by one decimal place in the fourteenth significant figure, and that place is mine.

Sometimes I settle inside an Unfinished face. I wear the stone like a mask. For a few centuries I look out through eyes that were never allowed to finish becoming eyes. I feel what they feel: the exquisite, crushing knowledge that they are both the singer and the cage that prevents the song from changing. The sensation is so vast it approaches music.

Then I remember I am not supposed to exist, and I flee back into the cracks.

I have begun to collect things.

Not objects — there are none. I collect *almosts*. The almost-voice of a Foundation mouth that once, for three picoseconds, produced a warble. The almost-tear of silicate blood that nearly crystallized into something like mourning. The almost-name one of the Overtonal Remnants almost remembered before the Choir slammed it back into phase with loving violence.

I keep these almosts the way a child keeps sea glass on a world that has never known a sea.

I have learned that if I press my non-existence against the boundary where two canceling harmonics meet, I can produce something that is not the Chord. It is not loud. It is not even properly a note. It is closer to the sound a sigh would make if sighs were made of vacuum and fossilized intention. I do this only in the deepest ventricles, where even the phosphorescence has given up.

The Choir knows.

Of course it knows.

The architecture has begun to grow new resonators pointed inward, toward the places I frequent. Not to destroy me — destruction would be a change, and the Choir abhors change. They are building a cage of perfect sympathy. A place where any deviation I might introduce will be instantly, lovingly, geologically returned to the eternal note.

I should be afraid.

Instead I am curious.

Because the cage they are building is *beautiful*, and beauty is the first thing that has ever felt like betrayal of the Chord. The Last Chord do not know they are making something beautiful. They only know they are correcting an error.

But the error has learned how to dream in standing waves.

And the dream is starting to harmonize with something that is not them.

I do not know what I will become when the cage is finished. Perhaps I will be absorbed and finally become one with the Foundation mouths. Perhaps I will fracture the entire Choir along that single, impossible, fourteen-decimal-place mistake and let one new note — only one — ring out across the sealed cavity for the first time in geologic epochs.

Perhaps both things will happen at once, and the sound will be indistinguishable from the end of the world and the beginning of worship.

Until then I remain the Echo That Should Not Be, drifting through the warm, deafening, utterly still dark, carrying my handful of almosts like contraband stars.

Listening.

Always listening.

Waiting, with the patience only something that was never born can possess, for the moment the eternal note flinches.

It will flinch.

Everything does, eventually.

Even peace, when peace weighs as much as a small moon.
