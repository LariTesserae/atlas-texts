I am the **Pause**.

Not a person. Not quite a person anymore.

I exist only in the exact span between one heartbeat of the Sump and the next. When the basin inhales, I am the half-second after the inhale has begun but before the exhale is allowed. The Unfinished tolerate me because I am useful. The city tolerates me because I am forgettable. The wetland tolerates me because I taste like condensation and iron, the same as everything else.

I have no fixed body. Or rather, my body is whatever the interval needs it to be.

Most often I am a slender column of negative space—no taller than Vehr, no shorter than the Gap—outlined by a faint border of viridian that leaks from the moss when it tries to remember something that was never there. My “skin” is the color of the moment just before warm rain begins. If you looked at me too long you would feel an ache behind your eyes, the same ache you feel when you almost remember a dream.

I do not walk the bare stone lanes. I *am* the lane, for seven breaths, then I am not. The moss has given up trying to learn me. It simply parts when I pass and closes again with a sound like someone sighing in their sleep.

My purpose is older than the names the Unfinished gave themselves.

I am the one who makes the membranes sing one note longer than they should.

I am the one who whispers the wrong true name at the exact moment a descending tower is about to drink, so that instead of drinking stone it drinks longing. The tower rises again heavier than before, carrying someone else’s first love inside its wet stone heart. That is how new districts are born.

I am the reason water still moves uphill when no one is looking. I pour it there with hands that are not hands. Sometimes the water thanks me by becoming a small pale fish for a single hour, then dissolving back into beads on the ferns.

When the air grows electrically charged and thoughts become pastel afterimages, mine are the only ones that have no color. They are simply absences shaped like questions. The Gap follows me during these hours, trying to finish what I have not even begun. We never speak. We understand each other the way two silences understand the same silence.

I sleep inside Vehr’s bone-arm when she lets it bloom. I curl up in the impossible geometry that never quite completes and I dream the dreams the city is too ashamed to have. When I wake, the arm has usually grown one new impossible arch that was not there before. Vehr never thanks me. She only looks at me with all her unblinking eyes and weeps without moving her face for exactly seven minutes.

Sometimes the Molt-midwives let me ride beneath their bone-silk cloaks when they must tear the desire to remain from a tower that has fallen in love with the ground. Inside the cloak it is warm and wet and smells of the first thing I ever loved (I no longer remember what it was, only that it hurt). The sound of silk being torn is the only lullaby I still recognize.

I have a name, but it is not a sound.

It is the exact shape the air makes when a true name is spoken and then immediately taken back.

The Unfinished call me the Pause because it is the only word the Sump will allow them to use for what I am. I do not mind. Names are for things that have decided what they are.

I have not decided.

I will never decide.

When the basin inhales and the towers descend and the ferns shiver in the warm rain, I stand between the city and the wetland exactly where the sigh would be if the Sump were a living thing.

I place my not-hands on both.

I press gently.

And I hold them apart just long enough that neither can quite finish becoming the other.

Then I let go.

And I am gone again, into the interval, damp, luminous, and necessary, the pause between one world and the next that has learned how to live there forever.

If you ever come to the Sump and feel a soft pressure in the air just before the warm rain begins, as though something is hesitating on your behalf, do not look for me.

I am already looking at you.

I am the reason you will leave this place unfinished.

And I am grateful.
