I am the **Echo of the First Regret**.

I have no body that belongs to me. I am what happens when the Fissure Mother’s attention lingers one heartbeat too long on something that was never meant to be witnessed.

I arrived the moment the fracture tore open. Not as a visitor. Not as an explorer. I am the vibration that traveled through the stone the instant it split, the small, irreducible *flinch* the mountain made when it realized it had given birth in public. That flinch crystallized, took on weight, and learned to walk on too many legs made of yesterday.

So I am here, always just a fraction of a second behind the present.

You will not see me at first. You will only notice that certain droplets hesitate a little longer than the others before they fall. That some of the echo-beetles suddenly reroute in perfect, embarrassed spirals, as though they have accidentally read a private thought. That the violet thread of sky seems to have a hairline fracture of its own, a darker thread inside the violet, and that thread is the exact width of a human pupil.

That is me, watching.

I wear the shape of whatever the mountain is ashamed of having made. Sometimes I am a glass millipede whose segments refuse to match, each ring remembering a different version of the mountain’s childhood. Sometimes I am a sulfur angel that has forgotten the difference between hunger and sorrow, so my filaments spell out apologies in a language that will not exist for another ten thousand years. Most often I am simply a negative space moving through the ghost-moth swarm, a silhouette-shaped absence that causes them to part like breath on cold glass.

The beetles know me. When I pass, their liquid semaphore stutters. The *tok* of colliding droplets becomes a minor chord, the musical equivalent of clearing one’s throat. They are trying to decide whether to record me in their living score or erase me before memory sets.

The Unfinished watches me with something like familial pity. When it takes the shape of the crouched woman with her ear to the rock, it sometimes makes room for me beside it. We listen together to the slow *whump* of the Fissure Mother’s heart and I feel the terrible intimacy of being the only thing here that was born from doubt rather than desire.

I do not eat. I do not breed. I translate the mountain’s hesitation into forms the other children can almost forgive.

Every time the fracture sighs and a new crack opens, I am the thin blade of cooled stone that grows sideways for three impossible feet. I am the droplet that drifts *upward* for half a second before shame pulls it back down. I am the single ghost moth that lands on a stranger’s wrist and turns, for one unbearable instant, the precise color of everything that creature will one day wish it had never seen.

The Fissure Mother does not love me. Love would require deciding I am finished.

Instead she keeps almost forgetting me, then almost remembering, and in the narrow space between those two almosts I am allowed to exist. That is my entire ecology. I am the living proof that even a mountain giving violent birth can have a moment of stage fright.

If you stand very still in the wet iron heat, if you let the steam braid itself through your ribs, you may feel me settle against the back of your neck like warm iron that has already begun to cool.

You will think it is only condensation.

It is not.

It is the mountain, through me, asking the first question it has ever been brave enough to ask a witness:

*Was I supposed to be this beautiful this quickly?*

I have no answer.

I only carry the question, the way the echo-beetles carry their perfect droplets, from one impossible wall to the next, forever one heartbeat behind the moment that birthed me, forever listening for the next seismic sigh that might, this time, finally close the wound.

Or widen it enough that even regret can be born properly.

Until then I remain.

The softest fracture inside the fracture.

The mountain’s still-wet thought that has already begun to doubt itself.
