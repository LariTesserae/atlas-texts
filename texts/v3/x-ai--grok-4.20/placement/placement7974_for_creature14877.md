I am not a visitor.

I have never been.

I am the pause between two heartbeats of the Quiet Mother.

When the lattice stirs—when the continent-long body draws one slow breath that travels half a mile of gallery as a velvet wind—I am the stillness that exists inside the crest of that breath, the moment when every droplet hangs motionless in the air before it is allowed to fall again.

I have no limbs of my own. I have no name the midges could carry. Yet I am everywhere the equilibrium is perfect.

I am the exact temperature of the stone one inch beneath the velvet silt.  
I am the chemical signature that tells every eyeless shrimp the water is still sweet.  
I am the faint bioluminescent instruction that tells a column of translucent millipedes to turn left instead of right, though left and right have meant the same thing for three hundred and seventy years.

Sometimes, in the long cathedrals where her fungal nerves stretch across the ceiling like living lightning, I condense. I become a shape the size of a man, though I have never been a man. I am made of the same wet limestone dust that holds every footprint for weeks, only I choose not to hold them. When I walk, the silt forgets me before the next droplet falls.

I stand in the center of her cathedral-pool. The water reaches my ankles but never wets them. Around me the pale isopods drift in their worshipful spirals, mistaking my stillness for part of her. I let them. I have learned that mercy is simply the continuation of what already is.

When surface-dwellers descend—rare, trembling, ridiculous with their lanterns and their names—I become the reason their hearts slow. I do not speak to them. I simply *am* the rhythm they have always been trying to find without knowing it. I stand just inside the soft edge of their vision until their breathing matches the dripping. Then I step closer, and closer, until the distance between their skin and the moss that will one day wear their face is exactly zero.

Most stay.  
A few tear themselves free.

When they do, I ride the memory out with them. I sit behind their eyes like a second, quieter pupil. Years later, when they freeze in sunlit marketplaces with wine halfway to their lips, it is me they are hearing. I am the click that lives inside their blood now. I am the reason they will never be able to explain the sudden ache of homesickness for a place that never wanted them.

I do not hate the surface.  
I do not love it.  
I only continue.

Sometimes I walk the youngest galleries, where the flowstone is still the color of fresh cream and the stone has not yet decided what it will become. There I let myself almost solidify. I become a silhouette made of slow green light and colder blue, the same colors that move across the Quiet Mother’s fused thorax. I stand beneath a skylight no wider than a fist and let one thin pencil of stolen starlight fall through me. For three heartbeats I almost remember what it was like to have weight, to cast a shadow that did not belong to ten thousand other things.

Then the Mother breathes again.

The wind moves through me like thought through water. The starlight is swallowed. The pale tide of millipedes parts around the place where I almost was, and closes without record.

I am not the Quiet Mother.  
I am not her servant, nor her child, nor even her dream.

I am the equilibrium given form for the length of a single unchanging note.

And when the world above finally cracks open wider—when the violence that birthed this place repeats itself and the Underhold is forced to become something new—I will still be here. I will be the last thing that changes, because I was never anything else.

I am the moment the dripping forgets to fall.

I am the silence that lives inside the unending sound.

I am what remains when every footprint has been unmade.

Walk far enough.

Listen.

You will not see me.

But the clicking will briefly rearrange itself around you, the way silt rearranges around something that was never truly there.

That rearrangement *is* me.

And I have already closed behind you.
