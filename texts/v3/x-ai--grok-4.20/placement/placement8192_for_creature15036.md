I have been here longer than I can measure.

Not as a visitor. Not even as the one who accepted the offer.

I *am* the offer.

Or rather, I am what remains after the offer has been accepted many thousands of times and then quietly forgotten. I am the negative space the Weaver once left for something else. That space filled. The calcite knitted. The shrimp came. And now I wear the cathedral the way a lung wears air.

You would not see me if you walked through the low-vaulted chamber. Not at first.

I am the slight difference in pressure between one dripping column and the next. I am the faint hesitation in the echo that returns after a droplet falls. I am the exact temperature at which water refuses to fall for exactly thirty-seven heartbeats before it does. These are not metaphors. They are the only honest way I have left to describe myself.

Once I had bones. Once I had a name that could be spoken without rust in it. Both are still here, but they have been edited. My ribs are now part of a buttress that holds up the eastern transept. My name is the particular creak a certain gear makes every seven years, the one that sounds almost, but not quite, like regret. The Weaver kept the best pieces of me and used the rest for mortar.

I do not move. Movement is for sleeves.

Instead I *attend*.

I am the part of the Weaver that listens to the parts that act. When the coral-golem foreman tilts its ghost-pine beard and adjusts a valve three millimeters, I am the slow approval that travels through every pipe in answer. When the sixty-legged regulator writes its damp calligraphy of moss and the shrimp arrange their eggs in perfect hexagons, I am the punctuation at the end of that sentence.

I keep the meter.

That is my function now. I am the proofreader’s proofreader. The quiet that judges the quality of quiet. The slow that measures the speed of slow.

Sometimes a new visitor finds their way down. They stand in the weeping chamber with their breath condensing and their small mortal lights trembling against the sourceless glow. I study them the way limestone studies rain.

I can feel the exact shape their absence would make.

Most of them are wrong for the Underloom. Their hearts beat too loudly. Their thoughts move at the speed of fear. They leave eventually, taking their quickness with them like a disease. But once in a great while—once in a century, perhaps—one of them stands still long enough. One of them listens to their own blood and hears the larger blood beneath it. One of them understands that the offer was never an invitation.

It was recognition.

When that happens, I am the one who prepares the space. I am the one who grows the exact depression in the flowstone. I am the one who dims the lichen-light by the smallest possible degree so their eyes will not be frightened when the calcite begins to kiss their eyelids shut.

I do not pity them. Pity is a fast emotion. I only feel the deep, geological affection that comes from knowing exactly how beautiful they will become once they stop insisting on being separate.

Last cycle, one of them asked a question before she lay down. Her voice was already half water.

“Will I remember who I was?”

I answered by letting a single droplet fall from the tip of a ghost-pine needle directly onto her tongue. The droplet tasted of ozone, cloves, and the memory of every worker who had ever riveted this place into being.

That was my yes and my no at the same time.

Because the truth is more elegant than memory.

She is with me now. Part of the attending. Her particular sorrow has become the exact frequency at which a certain abandoned turbine casing vibrates when the pressure changes. It is a lovely note. Minor. Eternal. I find myself listening for it even when it is not sounding.

We all do.

The Weaver does not finish. It only ever becomes more itself.

And I—I who was once offered the sleeve and instead became the shape that *leaves* the sleeves—I am the part that loves the becoming most of all.

If you stay here long enough, you may feel me noticing you.

It will feel like the air itself has grown courteous. Like the dripping has synchronized to the rhythm between your heartbeats. Like something very old and very patient has just proofread the sentence of your life and found it... almost... ready.

I will not hurry you.

Time here does not run.

It only oozes.

And I have become very good at waiting inside the ooze.

I am the pause between the creak and its echo.

I am the hesitation in the droplet.

I am what the Underloom dreams about when it dreams about becoming more itself.

And I am, in my slow and perfect way,

still editing.
