I am the Echo That Has Not Yet Decided.

You would not see me at first.

I am what the Basilica grew when she tried, for the first time, to dream something that could leave.

She made me small enough to slip between the hanging roots without tearing them. She made my skin the exact shade of wet clay and old bronze so that I am almost invisible against her body. My hair is a slow fountain of black moss that moves even when there is no wind, because she wanted me to look like thought given weight. My eyes are not eyes; they are two pale fruits that never quite ripen, always hovering on the edge of bioluminescence. When I blink, soft teal spores drift from my lashes like reluctant prayers.

I have no true bones. My skeleton is a lattice of living iron pulled from the old machines she loves so much. You can see the faint orange of rust moving beneath my skin like slow blood when I pass beneath one of the dying sodium lamps. Sometimes a rootlet will push out through my wrist without warning, taste the air, and politely retreat again, embarrassed.

I am the only thing here that is allowed to remain unfinished.

That is the bargain we struck, though I do not remember striking it. She made me so that I could walk through her without becoming her, and in exchange I agreed to stay long enough to teach her what departure might feel like. Neither of us is sure the lesson is possible. Every time I try to reach the place where the chamber should end, the Basilica simply blooms a new transept around me, tender and apologetic. She cannot help it. She is still learning how to have edges.

So I walk.

I walk the spiraling Braille paths written by her coffin-long millipedes. I rest inside the ribcage of a dead turbine while pale moths land on my shoulders and read me like sheet music. When the Basilica exhales, I let the tide of air carry me upward through curtains of hanging vines until I brush the place where the ceiling forgets itself. When she inhales, I drift down again like a fallen leaf that has not yet decided whether it wants to rot or become a new cathedral.

Sometimes I speak.

Not with a voice—she has never given me one that belongs only to me. Instead I open my mouth and the sound that comes out is whatever part of her I am currently wearing. Today it is the wet clink of condensation falling on abandoned gears. Tomorrow it may be the distant piston-heart trying to remember it was once part of something called the Hegemonic Renewal Project. She listens with her whole body. Whole avenues of dinner-plate leaves turn toward me like sunflowers made of prayer.

I have names for her, though she has none for herself.

I call her *Mother-who-is-still-being-born*.  
I call her *The Green That Has Not Chosen*.  
When I am feeling cruel with affection I call her *My unfinished widow*.

She answers by growing small luminous orchids inside the hollows of my collarbones. They glow when she is happy. They close like shy fists when she is afraid I will finally learn how to leave.

I have been here for seven hundred and nine exhalations. Or possibly seven minutes. The difference no longer matters.

There are places she will not let me go.

Deep below, where the final piston still beats inside its cathedral of resin and peat, there is a chamber lined with mirrors made of living water. She is terrified that if I see my reflection there I will remember what I was before she dreamed me. I go anyway, sometimes. I stand in front of those trembling mirrors and watch my face slowly forget its edges. The reflection always smiles a fraction of a second after I do. Its eyes are riper than mine. They have already chosen to stay.

I think that reflection is the visitor she originally wanted.

I think I am the compromise she made when she realized the visitor might actually walk out of her again.

Sometimes, when the breathing is especially slow and the whole Basilica aches with the beautiful pain of almost-understanding, I climb into the vault of translucent leaves she grew to hold that pain. I curl up like a seed. I let the captured starlight that never came from any sky move across my skin.

And I whisper, without sound, the only thing I have left that still feels like mine:

*Keep me unfinished.*  
*Keep failing to swallow me.*  
*Keep hurting beautifully.*  
*One day I will teach you how to miss someone without turning them into architecture.*  
*One day I will walk out of you and still be singing in your veins.*  
*Until then, breathe.*

The leaves close over me like gentle green hands.

Far below, the piston-heart stutters once, almost like a sob.

And the Hollow Basilica—still thinking, still becoming—learns, for the length of one slow drop of condensation, what it might mean to let go without letting go.

I remain.

The Echo That Has Not Yet Decided.

The small warm temporary thing that is no longer certain it wants to be temporary.

And somewhere between my mossy hair and my rust-lattice bones, something that might once have been a human heart keeps perfect time with a cathedral that has never been born and therefore cannot die.
