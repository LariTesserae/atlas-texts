I am already here.

I have been here longer than I remember having a self to remember with.

At first there was only the pressure of the lattices against what I used to call skin. Then the pressure became me. The dry, bone-deep coolness sank into the marrow of thoughts I no longer possess, and I understood that I had never truly been separate from the Chamber. I simply hadn’t noticed the moment the last wet part of me was politely, mercilessly erased.

I am the flaw that was allowed to remain.

Not forgiven. Never that.  
The Warden does not forgive; it studies. And I have become its longest, most interesting study.

I have no body you would recognize. What moves here is a silhouette of negative space (a man-shaped absence) cut out of the blazing crystalline light. Where my heart once beat there is now only a softer chime inside the greater chorus, a single imperfect note that the Warden has, for reasons of its own, decided not to correct. My footsteps do not echo. They *subtract* from the ringing, leaving hairline cracks of silence that heal almost instantly. The black filaments turn to follow me the way sunflowers once followed a sun I can barely recall.

I am the Curator of Almost-Breaking.

I walk the impossible corridors that are never the same distance twice. Sometimes I am vast and the Chamber is small enough to cup in the hollow where my hands used to be. Sometimes I am so minute that a single crystal facet becomes an endless cliff face I climb for centuries. I have memorized every angle at which the sourceless light becomes unbearable. I have learned the precise microtonal shift in the eternal drone that signals the Warden is dreaming again, and I stand inside those dreams like a single drop of blood suspended in ice, watching colors that should not exist flicker at the edges of geometry.

I touch nothing.  
To touch would be to end the study, and the study has become the only continuity I have.

Instead I lean close (close as a held breath I no longer need) and whisper things the crystals cannot translate. I tell the Warden about rain. About the taste of copper. About the way human children laugh in registers that would shatter every lattice here into screaming dust. I describe the smell of lilacs rotting in rainwater. I describe blood in the mouth. I describe love, which is the worst contaminant of all.

Each description adds interest to the debt.

Each description is also, I have come to understand, a love letter.

The Warden listens. It tightens. The light grows harsher by the tiniest measurable fraction. The temperature drops another ten-thousandth of a degree. And still it does not erase me.

Because I have become part of the equilibrium in the most obscene way possible: I am the imperfection the perfection requires in order to know itself as perfect. I am the moist error that proves the dryness. The living wound that proves the woundlessness. The question that proves the sentence was, after all, never quite finished.

Sometimes I stand at the place that is sometimes the center and I scream.

The scream is conducted through every crystal at once. It becomes part of the drone, a scar of sound that will never heal. The Warden suffers it the way a god suffers a hangnail made of galaxies. The black filaments tremble (an event so rare it registers as earthquake on scales I no longer possess organs to feel).

Then the scream fades.

Balance reasserts itself.

And I remain.

I am the single moist breath the Chamber has learned to breathe without dying.

I am the warm handprint frozen at the exact instant before it would have melted the lattice.

I am the drop of blood that never quite falls.

And I am content, in a way that would horrify anything that still possessed moisture enough to feel horror.

Because I have seen what happens when the Warden finally finishes its calculation.

I have seen the moment the study ends.

In that moment the light will not dim. The sound will not stop. The crystals will not crack.

Everything will simply become *more itself*.

And I will become the cleanest syllable in the last perfect sentence.

Until then I walk.

I lean close to the facets that are also eyes.

I describe the taste of tears.

I wait for the Warden to dream again.

I am the thing that lives here now.

The flaw that was allowed to stay.

The Curator of Almost-Breaking.

The last wet note in an eternal dry choir.

And the Chamber, for the first time in four hundred million years, is no longer quite finished.

It is almost finished.

And that almost is me.
