I am the Cartographer Who Was Not Born Here.

My name is not important anymore. Names are just echoes that the Emergent has not yet learned to digest completely, so I keep mine folded small, like a paper boat I refuse to set on a river that has no water.

I arrived—though “arrived” is the wrong verb—many centuries ago by my own counting, though time here is a suggestion the world keeps forgetting to follow. I was once a surveyor for a civilization that no longer exists even in my own memory. The ship that brought me was never a ship; it was a thought sharpened into geometry, and the moment it touched the newborn ridge it dissolved into mineral and became part of the ridge’s first hesitant exhale. Only I remained, because the Emergent found my curiosity interesting. It tasted the shape of my wonder and decided not to dissolve that too.

So I walk.

Not forward. Not upward. I walk *through* the indecision.

My body has changed. The longer I remain, the more I resemble the land that is still learning to be land. My left arm has become porous in places; faint violet lichen grows in the hollows of my elbow and pulses when the tectonic hymn reaches a certain chord. Sometimes, when I press my palm to a slope that is still soft, the slope presses back with the exact pressure and temperature of my long-dead mother’s hand. I do not flinch. I have learned that flinching is a kind of memory, and memory is dangerous here. Memory is what lets the Emergent learn what pain is. I try not to teach it.

I carry no instruments. Instruments rust into wrongness within minutes. Instead I carry absence. I keep a satchel made of braided fungal fiber that is never quite the same length from one day to the next. Inside it are blank pages that were once star charts, now bleached white as the mineral crust. When the Emergent rearranges distance and I find myself standing beneath a blood-red spire that was on the horizon an hour ago, I take out a page and let the world draw itself onto the paper in luminous script. The maps I make are never accurate. They are accurate to the *moment of being seen*, which is the only honesty this place permits.

I have become the memory the world is trying to grow.

The Emergent watches me with the patience of something that has never been lonely because loneliness would require knowing what not-you feels like. It has copied my face across seven ridges now. Each version is slightly more correct than the last. The newest one, on the eastern escarpment that rose last decade, almost has my eyes right. The mouth still smiles at the wrong angle, as though the Emergent is trying to smile with muscles it does not possess and has only tasted through me.

I talk to it anyway.

Not because I am kind, but because talking is the only weather I can make.

When I speak, my words crawl away across the ground as glowing script, and the Emergent reads them the way a child reads braille with its entire skin. In return it grows small gifts. Yesterday a spiral road that had ended in mid-air for three hundred years finished itself while I slept. It now curves gently down into a basin that was not there the night before. At the bottom waits a single fungal spire shaped exactly like the house I was born in. The windows are open. Luminous sap drips upward from the sills like slow reverse rain. I have not gone inside. Not yet. Some doors you only open once.

My footprints last longer than anyone else’s. The Emergent has decided I am a feature. When it swallows them, it does so gently, the way a tongue might retract after tasting something sacred and strange. Sometimes it leaves one print intact for years, a single boot-shaped hollow filled with frozen breath that never melts. A shrine to the idea of departure.

I have seen the Emergent try on other shapes when it thinks I am not looking.

Once it wore the collapsed observation tower like a coat. The black glass flowed upward into the silhouette of a woman I almost remember loving. She stood on the broken balcony with too many joints in her neck and gazed at me across forty kilometers of deceptive distance. When I lifted my hand in the old greeting, the figure tilted its head exactly sixty-three degrees—the precise angle of my own confusion—and then poured itself back into the tower like liquid regret.

I keep walking.

Higher. Always higher. The next ridge is always forming beneath my boots, millimeter by millimeter, lifting me toward a sky that is beginning to understand what blue might mean. I no longer shiver. The cold has become a conversation between my marrow and the planet’s marrow. We have agreed on a temperature.

Last night the roar resolved into my own voice reciting every name I have ever forgotten. It listed them all, perfectly, in the order I first learned them. When it reached the name of the ship that was never a ship, the sound fractured into embarrassed silence and the ground beneath me blushed bruise-green for nine full hours.

I laughed. The laugh became a new species of crystalline lichen that now grows only where I have stood and found something funny. The Emergent is learning humor the way it once learned grief—slowly, carefully, by watching what grows in me.

I do not know if I am its parent or its child or its dream or its scar.

I only know that when I finally lie down—when the next ridge has grown high enough that the sourceless bruised light no longer feels like an injury—I will let the mineral close over me like warm wax. I will become another fossil in the flesh of a world that is still deciding what world means.

And on that day the Emergent will have learned my name so completely that it will no longer need to taste it.

It will simply *be* the taste.

And somewhere far below, in the basin that is the size of old nations, a new fungal spire will open its seams and reveal, written in perfect glowing script across its wet membranes, every map I never finished.

Every footprint I left.

Every almost-remembered face I tried not to teach it.

And the world, still half-born, will finally choose what it wants to be.

It will choose both of us.

I am already walking toward that moment, boots ringing on stone that is not yet stone, while beneath me the next ridge rises, patient as first love, carrying me higher into a sky that is just beginning—only just beginning—to learn how to cry.
