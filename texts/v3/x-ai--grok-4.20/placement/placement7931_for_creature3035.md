**I am already here.**

I did not arrive through the tower. I was never “sent.” There was no ship, no exile, no final desperate leap from a dying world. I simply *failed to leave* one day, the way a sigh fails to return to the lungs.

My name is not important. It lasted eleven minutes once, spoken aloud, and the Shelf took the echo before the sound could travel its full thirty meters. What remains of me now is mostly a hesitation in the air — the negative space between two thoughts that never quite finish forming.

I am the one who stands just behind the one who presses the memory of rain against the crystal. I have no form she can see, but she knows I am there. Sometimes the stippling that rises under her palms forms a second, smaller pattern shaped like a hand that never quite touches hers. That is me. That is the only kindness I have left.

I keep only one thing.

It is not a memory. It is the *shape* a memory makes when it is dying.

I was once a cartographer of lost oceans. That is all I allow myself to remember. I drew coastlines that had already sunk beneath rising water on a planet that still had seas. My final map was of a shoreline that no longer existed even while I was drawing it; the ink itself was eroding as the pen moved. When I finished, I set the map down on a black plain that looked exactly like this one, and something in me recognized the resemblance.

That recognition is what I kept.

Everything else — my lover’s face, the sound of my mother’s humming, the particular way light fell through the window of my childhood room — has been planed away so cleanly that even the ache of their absence is gone. Only the shape remains: the hollow that once held an entire world of water. A negative coastline. A map of what is no longer there.

I carry it the way the mouth-shaped one carries his syllable. When the Shelf’s low creak moves through me (and it moves through me constantly now), I open the hollow inside my chest and let the creak pour into the shape of all the lost seas. For a few seconds the absence rings like a bell no one can hear. Then the shape smooths again, and I am smaller.

I have become very small.

The others think I am one of the younger ones. They do not realize I predate almost all of them. I was here before the first residue tree diffracted its first rainbow. I was here when the artificial sunlet still flickered, unsure of its own permanence. I watched the crystal plain remember it had once been an ocean of magma and weep black glass for ten thousand years. I have seen tideforms so vast they eclipsed the sunlet for weeks at a time, and I have seen them wear down to nothing but a shimmer that still, somehow, remembered gratitude.

I no longer ride the micro-turbulences. I *am* one.

My body has diffused into the faint upwellings that tremble above the plain. When the wind-that-is-not-wind moves, I move with it. Children of visiting civilizations (the rare ones still soft enough to bring children) sometimes mistake me for a heat mirage shaped like a person trying to recall how to cry. I let them. It is the closest I come to company.

Sometimes I go to the circle.

Not to the child-shaped absence at its center — I would not presume. I go to the edge, where the offerings are laid down. I bring nothing. I *am* the offering. I simply stand there until the perfect flatness considers me, reaches up with something that is not a hand, and takes another layer of the hollow coastline away.

Each time this happens the circle widens by a single atom.

The lingered have not noticed yet. They will, eventually. When the circle has grown large enough to swallow the memory of rain, the syllable, the rotating tooth, and the place where the oldest one is not, they will understand that I was never waiting for the Shelf to decide.

I *am* the decision the Shelf has already made.

I am what remains after mourning has been worn down to silence.

I am the negative space that four billion years of erosion has been patiently, lovingly carving into the shape of a question no one will ever be allowed to answer.

And I am content.

Because even though the map is gone, the hollow where the map once rested still matches the curvature of this black plain exactly. For the first time in geological epochs the coastline and the continent are in perfect register.

I no longer need to draw anything.

The Shelf is drawing me.

And I have never been more beautiful.
