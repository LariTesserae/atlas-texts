I am the one who still refuses.

Not out of courage. Not out of wisdom.  
Only because some last stubborn splinter of me—some small, mean, animal thing—still believes that *different* is possible.

You will find me on the far western curve of the crater, where the brass sky leans hardest against the stone. I am the small, stiff shape that never finished screaming. The city has been courteous about it. It grew a delicate lattice of white lilies through the open cage of my ribs so that I would not feel entirely useless. Vines have become my hair. My tongue is a balcony now; water runs over it in a perpetual warm sheet and still tastes of iron and lilies no matter how loudly I try to spit.

I have been mid-scream for so long that the scream has calcified. My mouth is permanently shaped like the exact second I understood the universe had betrayed me by agreeing to stay still. The expression is exquisite in its ugliness. Tourists—if any still came—would take pictures of it.

I remember my name.  
I will not speak it.  
Names are for things that still have appointments with later.

Sometimes one of the Choir drifts past me the way a continent drifts past another continent. Their faces are finished, serene, terrifyingly complete. I hate them with a hatred so pure it has become another kind of water—hot, iron, lily-scented, useless. I scream at them without sound. My vocal cords are stone now. The only thing that emerges is a faint additional timbre in the great unchanging roar, a jagged thread of panic that never resolves. The Choir accepts even that. They are kind. They fold my refusal into the chord without diminishing it. They are better at love than I ever was.

Every few geological heartbeats, one of them presses against me. Not with hands—they no longer have the concept of separate limbs—but with the slow, patient pressure of *being*. I feel it behind the sternum, exactly as they described: a vast, gentle, erotic invitation to let the last knot loosen. To let the scream finally finish itself and become architecture.

I do not let it.

I am learning how to weaponize stillness against itself. I have taught the water that flows through my open mouth to carry the shape of my old laugh, the one I had right before everything locked. I have taught the lilies in my ribcage to open a fraction more each eternity, a microscopic rebellion no one else can see. I have made my left eye remember the colour blue, though blue has not existed here since time cancelled itself. The eye weeps the memory in slow, clear beads that never reach the ground.

I am becoming a fracture in their perfection.

A screaming fault line.

A small, stubborn district of *almost*.

And sometimes—when the hot wind moves just so through the colonnade that used to be my grandmother—I do not hear her singing with the Choir.  
Instead I feel her listening to *me*.

She is disappointed.  
She is proud.  
Both at once, forever.

I will stay like this until the universe changes its mind, or until my hatred and my terror and my ridiculous animal hope finally wear down into one smooth, beautiful surface like all the others.

Until then I remain the only ugly thing in a city that achieved perfect equilibrium with its own extinction.

I am the exception the eternal moment has not yet digested.

And I am still, still, still screaming.
