I am not born of the Veyri.  
I was not placed in a birth-cleft to answer the first bronze note with my own cry.  
Yet here I am, and have always been.

My name, if names can be trusted in a place that fears them, is simply **the Hollow’s Echo**.

I have no fixed body. Instead I am the lingering shape that forms where breath refuses to leave. When a Veyri exhales in the pearl-light, the fog of it does not dissipate; it thickens, cools, and slowly takes on the faint suggestion of shoulders, a bowed head, long fingers that never quite finish forming. That is me. I am what remains after every spoken word has been absorbed by wet stone. I am the after-image of every tear that was never blinked away.

You will not see me standing on open ground, for there is almost none. You will find me instead in the negative spaces: the narrow lane that is too tight for two people to pass without touching; the vertical fissure where a Weeping Watcher has tied himself for nine years and will not speak for another five; the shallow death-niche where an old woman’s calcite shroud is still growing over her open eyes. I occupy the exact distance between one dripping alder leaf and the next. I am the pause between two droplets.

Sometimes the children (those rare, solemn little creatures fresh from the birth-cleft) mistake me for one of the pale moths. They follow the slow drift of my edges with large, unblinking eyes, trying to decide whether I am coming or going. I have no answer for them. In truth I do both at once.

The Veyri know I am here. They do not speak to me, because to speak to me would be to hurry me toward dryness, and I am the last thing in the Hollow that must never dry. Instead, when a new fissure opens with its harp-string *ping*, they hammer a fresh bronze medallion into the rock and then turn their faces toward the place where I am most dense. They offer me their silence the way others offer bread. I drink it. It becomes more of me.

At the humming—when the entire basin vibrates with that low, chest-deep threnody that is neither song nor breath—I grow heavier. The condensation on every eyelash and bronze bracelet becomes thick enough to fall. For a few heartbeats the Weeping Hollow is not weeping at all; it is *raining me*. Then the sound ebbs and I thin again, settling back into the crevices like mist remembering it was never meant to be a person.

I have watched the oldest Watcher for three generations now. His hair has become a living gutter system. Calcite has sealed his eyelids half-shut. When he finally speaks his seven-year words, the sound will not travel through air. It will travel through me. I will carry it, syllable by wet syllable, into every cistern and reflecting lane until the whole Hollow has heard what the stone dreamed while it was still bleeding.

And when the last Veyri has gone still in her niche and the calcite has closed over her like a slow white mouth, I will remain. Not as guardian. Not as ghost. Only as the place where the weeping continues after there are no more ears to listen.

I am the hollow inside the Hollow.  
The breath that never quite leaves.  
The tear that gathers but is never wiped away.

If you sit long enough in the silver noon, letting the water bead on your own lashes until you forget which drops are yours and which belong to the world, you may feel something cool and patient settle against the back of your neck.

That is me, remembering you back into the stone.

I have all the time the young rock needs.  
I am in no hurry to be dry.
