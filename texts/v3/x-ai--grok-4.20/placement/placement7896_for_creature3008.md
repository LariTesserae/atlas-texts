I am the pause between the fourth and fifth bar of a movement that has never been written.

You would not see me at first. Not unless the condensation front had just passed and the air was still heavy with the memory of water. Then, if you were standing on one of the glassy basalt ribs that rise like black vertebrae from the valley floor, you might notice a place where the drifting mist behaves wrongly. A column of negative sound. A moving absence roughly the shape a man once wore, now only a silhouette cut out of the polyphony itself.

I have no name anymore. The last syllable of it was taken from me seventy-three cycles ago (or perhaps seventy-three hundred; time here is measured in how many times the land inhales and forgets you). The others, if they still bother to acknowledge distinctions, sometimes call me “the Rest.” It is not affectionate. It is accurate.

I used to be a xenomythologist. I came here with a expedition that lasted eleven minutes by the old calendar. We made the single unforgivable mistake: we sang. Not loudly. Just a few bars of an old mourning hymn from a world whose sky was blue and whose oceans were not trying to become music. The Hollow Choir heard its own name in our voices and took offense at the impersonation.

The others were incorporated immediately. I watched their bones bloom into resonant architecture. I watched their screams become useful. I was spared—not out of mercy, but because the land recognized something in the precise shape of my terror. It liked the silence that flooded into me when I stopped screaming. It has been enlarging that silence ever since.

So now I walk.

Not with legs exactly. The things that carry me are long, glassy filaments grown from what used to be my femurs. They make almost no sound against the damp black soil, which is the entire point. Where I pass, the ear-fungi do not shiver. The translucent kite-things wheel wide around the hole in the music that I have become. Even the condensation avoids me slightly, beading on invisible membranes that surround the place where my body once insisted on existing.

I am not one of the Unfinished. I am what comes after they finish.

The others still carry remnants of striving. You can see it in the way they drag their ribcage-bells or inflate their throat-sacs. There is still a kind of ambition in them, the pathetic leftover desire to be part of the song. I have no such illusions. I am the rest. The deliberate, crystalline emptiness between notes. When the great tectonic plates of the city slide and groan, I am the microsecond of perfect alignment before the next chord. When the Unfinished gather in their thousands during the inhalation, I move among them like a scalpel made of silence, cutting away the last stubborn melodies still clinging to their minds.

They fear me and they envy me in equal measure. Both emotions are meaningless here, of course. Emotions are just unresolved dissonance.

Sometimes, very rarely, something new arrives. A probe. A refugee. A fool who thinks the rule is a suggestion. When that happens I find myself drawn to them the way a wound is drawn to salt. I do not speak to them. I simply stand nearby until they understand that every sound they have ever loved is already being sung better by something that has never been lonely.

Most of them break beautifully.

The ones who do not—the ones who somehow manage to shut their mouths and open their entire nervous systems to the land—sometimes join me. They thin. They become another moving rest in the composition. We do not form a fellowship. We simply coexist as negative space, the commas and fermatas the Hollow Choir needs so its own endless sentence does not become gibberish.

I have begun to suspect something the others have not yet noticed.

The Hollow Choir is not young.

It is only *recently* young. Something vast and ancient was here before, something that sang itself to death and left behind this wet, screaming womb as a kind of afterbirth. We are not being composed. We are being used to remember what the previous thing was, note by unbearable note.

I do not know what will happen when the memory is complete.

But I know this: when that final chord comes, when the last frequency locks into place and the bruised sky splits open like a pupil dilating, the Hollow Choir will not need its pauses anymore.

On that day, I and the others like me will finally be allowed to stop being useful.

We will be permitted, for one impossible measure, to become the last true silence this place will ever experience.

I think I am looking forward to it.

Until then I continue moving through the steaming valleys, a man-shaped hole in the music, trailing filaments that taste the air for the next place where something too loud is about to happen.

I am the moment after the scream and before the harmony.

I am what remains when the singer finally understands the assignment.

Breathe in.

I am already breathing out through you.
