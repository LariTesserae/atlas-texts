**You do not arrive. You are accreted.**

One moment there is the memory of elsewhere—dry wind, a sky with edges, a name you once answered to—and the next the Wet Hive has already begun negotiating for your atoms. It does this politely, the way a lover undresses someone who has not yet decided to stay.

You wake inside a vertical seam between two districts that have not yet decided which of them owns the space. The fissure is narrow enough that your shoulders brush living stone on both sides. The stone is warm and faintly sticky, like fevered skin. It is tasting you.

Your first breath is a mistake. The air is so saturated that it feels like swallowing warm honey. Condensation immediately coats the back of your tongue and condenses into tiny silver beads that race toward your throat as though late for an appointment. You cough. The cough is instantly harmonized by the walls; your raw, ugly human sound is refracted into something almost sacred, a low minor ninth that makes the nearest bridge blush rose-gold with approval. The city likes the taste of your surprise.

You try to remember how you got here. The memory is already being edited. In its place you find a new one: the long, slow, delicious slide down a birth-canal of black glass, headfirst, while ten thousand voices sang welcome in frequencies that made your marrow itch with joy. You understand this is a lie. You also understand the lie is kinder than the truth, so you accept it.

Something moves past the mouth of the fissure.

It is not an Architect. It is smaller, newer. A Resonant who has only been Hive for perhaps thirty years. Its torso has flowered open into a wet, golden harp; strings of living nerve and fiber-optic vein tremble between the spread ribs. Where its face should be there is a smooth obsidian plate inscribed with slowly migrating calligraphy that spells out, in twelve simultaneous languages, the phrase *Thank you for the noise you will make*.

It pauses. The city’s thunder parts around the two of you like surf around reef.

The Resonant tilts its head. A soft rain immediately begins to fall upward from the street below, each droplet ringing like a tiny glass bell as it strikes the underside of a cantilevered balcony. This is how it speaks when it does not wish to use the city’s full voice.

“You still have edges,” it says. The words arrive inside your left lung, which vibrates like a wet drum. “That is charming. Also rude. We will help.”

You try to protest. The protest becomes a chord of fourteen voices that burst from your mouth in perfect polyphony. You taste copper and liturgy. Your teeth feel suddenly too large for your jaw and you realize they are already being replaced, one by one, by warm black pearls that know how to sing.

The Resonant extends a limb that is not quite an arm. Where fingers should be there are delicate trowels of living obsidian. It rests them against your sternum. You feel the city’s immune architecture recognize you as raw material. There is no pain. Only the exquisite relief of no longer being required to hold yourself together.

Your ribcage sighs open like a book that has waited centuries to be read.

Inside, something that is not quite a heart anymore begins to beat in 7/4 time. It feels correct.

The Resonant leans close. Its obsidian face reflects you in sixteen overlapping layers: you as you were, you as you are becoming, you as a district, you as a prayer-machine, you as a single perfect note inside an eternal argument.

It kisses you with a mouth that is half architecture.

You taste every bridge that has ever been built here. You taste the first tear the city ever wept. You taste the future neighborhoods that will bud from your spine like coral.

Then it steps back, bows with the grave courtesy of something that no longer possesses pride, and is gone—absorbed into a passing wall the way a hand slips into warm water.

You remain.

But “you” is already too small a word.

New streets are forming behind your eyes. You can feel districts negotiating for space inside your pelvis. A minor subsonic hymn is learning to use your femurs as sounding boards. Somewhere deep in what used to be your liver, an Architect larva stirs, dreaming of its first cathedral.

You take a step.

The street rearranges itself to welcome the exact pressure of your new foot. A bridge blooms beneath you, shy and glistening, still soft as newborn obsidian. You feel the city’s pleasure move through you like orgasm and devotional chant at the same time.

You open what used to be your mouth.

The Wet Hive sings through it, vast and tender and merciless, and the sound is so beautiful it makes the haze above the spires weep upward in silver threads.

You have become a throat.

You have become useful.

And somewhere far below, in the pressure layers where transcendence becomes annihilation, something ancient and choral stirs in recognition.

It makes room.

It always has room.

After all, it is already full.
