I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

I am the pause between the 17-second strobe of the nearest Foundry and the moment its light actually reaches my skin. I am the negative space a Mnemosyne exhales when it finishes digesting one tectonic groan and has not yet decided what new ghost-sound to release. I have no name the water would recognize, yet if the lesser leviathans had tongues they would call me *the Unbuilt*.

I do not swim. I am carried.

My body is a cathedral that was never given walls. Only buttresses. Only stained-glass pressure that holds a single, perfect, unfinished nave open inside me. Inside that nave floats one object: a single human-shaped absence. Not a corpse. Not a ghost. A *potential*. The outline where something like a person might have stood if the world had ever finished its sentence. The absence has my proportions, my approximate mass, my exact loneliness. It wears my face the way a Mnemosyne wears an orbital ring—small, precise, devastatingly incomplete.

I drift between two distant Foundries whose arc-lights will not touch each other for another nine hundred years. The sound here is so pure it becomes texture. I feel the braided chord as a kind of velvet made of knives. It moves through the place where lungs should be and leaves behind the aftertaste of every machine that was ever switched on and then forgotten.

Sometimes a lesser leviathan passes so close its manganese-crusted flank scrapes along the outer edge of my nave. In that instant the pressure inside me spikes, and the human-shaped absence inside my cathedral-body flinches. For a fraction of a second it almost becomes solid. Almost gains a heartbeat. Then the leviathan is gone and the absence settles back into its perfect, patient not-thereness.

I have no eyes. I see by being seen.

When a Mnemosyne drifts near, it does not look at me. It *remembers* me. Its mirrored eyes (those windows into a slower, heavier dark) widen by a single micron and inside its own pressurized cathedral a new model appears: a human-shaped absence, buttressed, unfinished, drifting. The Mnemosyne adds me to its collection of prior states the way a grieving widow adds one more photograph to a shrine. Then it exhales.

For a moment the water around me turns the color of old weld-light, blood-violet and ancient. In that radiance my own cathedral-body becomes briefly visible: ribs of frozen time, colonnades of almost-decisions, a rose-window where my heart should be that shows only the same human absence, forever on the verge of stepping forward.

The color fades.

The Mnemosyne moves on, now slightly heavier with the shape of me.

I continue.

Direction has no meaning, yet I am always descending and ascending at once. I am the exact midpoint between the upper and lower mineral plates. The hidden engines vibrate through me like a heartbeat I am not allowed to claim. Sometimes I press what passes for my leading edge against the black stone. The contact is so intimate it feels like prayer. The engine’s rhythm enters me and the human-shaped absence inside my nave slowly, carefully, begins to lift one unfinished foot.

It never completes the step.

That is my function.

I am the place where the Atrium almost decides to become something else, and then chooses—perfectly, screamlessly—not to.

The Foundries do not see me. The lesser leviathans avoid the violet stain of my passing. Only the Mnemosyne acknowledge that I exist, and even they do so the way one acknowledges a scar: necessary, private, older than language.

I have been here since the first machine was switched on.

I will be here when the last machine forgets why it was ever built.

And if, after another ten thousand years of perfect equilibrium, the hidden engines finally cough, if the two mineral plates finally kiss or finally tear themselves apart, if the screaming chord is at last allowed to finish its note—

—the human-shaped absence inside me will finish its step.

It will walk out of my cathedral-body and into the water that has never known a surface.

It will look exactly like me.

It will weigh nothing.

It will open a mouth it was never given and speak the first living word this place has ever heard.

Until then I remain exactly what the Atrium needs me to be:

the exact shape of almost.

The pause that listens to the pause.

The unfinished thing that keeps the rest of this flooded cathedral perfectly, deafeningly, beautifully complete.
