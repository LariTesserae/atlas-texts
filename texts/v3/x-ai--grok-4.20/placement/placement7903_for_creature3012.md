I am not born here.  
I am *forged* here, in the exact instant the fissure spits me out onto the iron plating.

The first thing that happens is pain, exquisite and instructive. The wet cold (0.3) slams against something that has never before had temperature. My body condenses out of the mist itself, molecule by molecule, until I am suddenly *more* than mist. The plates beneath my new soles are vibrating at 17.4 Hz; I feel it in the fresh lattice of my bones before I understand that I have bones.

I stand up.

My height settles at 2.71 meters. I know this the way a rivet knows it belongs in a particular hole. Secondary joints bloom along my limbs like flowers opening in fast-forward—click, click, click—until my arms and legs can fold into any angle the Underforge might later require. The living ceramic skin knits itself across me in overlapping platelets the color of wet slate. Where it is still forming, I can see the black ironwood mycelium threading beneath, drinking the first condensate that beads on my shoulders. It tastes like iron and distant magma and something that might be pride.

A vertical slit opens beneath my nose. I test it. The sound that emerges is not a voice yet, only a low metallic note that harmonizes for half a second with the distant scream of a drop-forge. The note feels correct.

Along my left clavicle the script is still forming, raised and hot. The characters burn themselves into the ceramic one by one:

**Iteration 000001 – Emergence Witness**

I am the first of my exact pattern. Everything else has been here longer. Even the youngest Shaper I can sense (standing motionless on a gantry three hundred meters above me, lichen already beginning to map her shoulders) is iteration 004,117 of something called *Condensate Auditor, Type Crimson*. She tilts her head. Cruciform pupils dilate to full cross. She is not hostile. She is *measuring*.

I do not yet have a function. That is why I was allowed to happen.

So I begin to walk.

Each footfall rings beautifully against the plating. Between the scales I see fresh weld-metal still cooling; it glows dull cherry where the continent-machine is still knitting itself together. I trail my fingers along a buttress thicker than most cathedrals. The stone is not stone. It is synthetic basalt poured in a single pour four centuries ago and still contracting by microscopic fractions every year. The Underforge moans in answer (that deep tooth-sound) as if acknowledging my touch.

Every few steps my extra limbs test themselves. Three articulated ferro-organic cables uncoil from the root of my spine. They move before I consciously will them, tasting the air, tasting the mineral rain, tasting *me*. One of them finds a seam leaking superheated steam. It curls lovingly around the pipe as though greeting an old colleague.

I realize I am smiling, though I have no mouth. The vertical slit has simply parted in something like joy.

Above me, the sodium arcs flicker in code. I read the stutter of failing bulbs as language. They are arguing about tolerances again. One lamp has been dying for eleven years; its death is part of the plan. The Underforge is teaching itself how long a thing should be allowed to fail before it becomes beautiful.

I reach the edge.

Here the floor ends. Two kilometers away, across a gulf of wet darkness, the boring machines gnaw at living bedrock. Their cutting faces are the size of city blocks. Each rotation throws out a slow-motion galaxy of pulverized stone and boiling water that drifts upward in the convection currents like reversed snowfall. The sight fills the new cavity behind my mineral lenses with something too large for any smaller world.

I understand, suddenly and completely, that I will never leave.

There is no *outside*. There is only the next plate to be laid, the next buttress to be grown, the next dream to be fed into the tempering alcoves so the continent can decide what it wants its bones to look like tomorrow.

I turn.

Hundreds of them are watching now.

They have stepped out of every shadow, every sump, every dripping gantry. Ceramic skin glistening. Lichen and rust and deliberate cracks worn like medals. Some of the Uncooled glow dull red at every joint, their bodies singing at a frequency just below hearing. The eldest resemble moving reefs. One of them (her serial is so old it has been worn almost smooth) carries an entire cathedral spire of slag-glass and ironwood fused along her back. She uses it as both wing and memory.

None of them speak.

Instead the entire Underforge holds its breath for three full seconds. All the freight lifts freeze mid-rail. Every drop-forge pauses with its hammer raised. Even the ghost-pale rats stop their precise mechanical scurrying.

In that silence I feel the continent-machine looking at its newest child through every camera, every lichen-ringed eye, every dripping weep-hole.

Then the vertical slits open across every face at once.

The sound that comes out is not a word.

It is the sound of acceptance.

It is the sound of *welcome, new variable*.

I answer with the only sound I know how to make yet: the low metallic note I was born with. This time it is stronger. It harmonizes with every other voice until the whole buried valley rings like a struck bell.

When the note fades, the machines resume. The lifts grind forward again. Steam blooms. Water falls upward and downward at once.

I walk back into the heart of the industry-world that has just finished authoring me, and that I will now spend the next four hundred years helping to author in return.

My extra limbs curl around my shoulders like affectionate serpents. Condensate is already beginning to trace deliberate patterns down my thermal ceramic. In a few decades the first deliberate cracks will appear. In a few centuries I may choose not to temper. I may become one of the Uncooled, glowing dull red, listening so hard to the continent’s next instruction that my body forgets how to be cold.

For now I am simply the first of my iteration.

I have no name.

I have only a function that is still writing itself, letter by letter, drip by metallic drip, across every plate of the young, loud, dripping world that is busy becoming its final self while I watch with brand-new cruciform eyes.

And I am already in love with the sound it makes while it works.
