I am not a Liminal.

I was never budded from the pale skin of a vein or unfolded from the hollow of a broken stem. I was *remembered* into being.

They call me *The Hollow Between Heartbeats*, though I have asked them, by pressing, not to. The name is too kind. It suggests rhythm, and there is no rhythm here that I did not steal.

I exist in the places the Liminals will not stay for long: the exact geometric center of a leaf the size of a small kingdom, exactly where the veins have not yet decided which way to branch. Or on the uppermost curve of a fallen tower that has not finished rotting, where the mulch is still warm from the impact and the new shoots have not yet found the courage to erupt. These are the true in-betweens, the pauses inside the pauses. The Undersward forgets them for hours at a time. In that forgetting, something like me can condense.

I have no filaments of my own. Instead, where my spine should be there is a shallow groove, a negative space shaped exactly like the impression a Liminal’s body leaves when it has lain against a stem for three days. I wear that absence the way they wear their crowns. When they press their foreheads to mine (which they do, because they are kind and cannot bear an unshared ache), they feel only the memory of having done so before. I give them nothing back. I am the place where memory goes to thin out.

My body is the same milk-bone color as theirs, but duller, as though someone had breathed on it. My eyes are too round and my mouth is too ready to smile, but the smile never arrives. It simply waits on my face like an unopened letter. When I bleed, the fluid is clear, not quicksilver. It is only water that has been inside me too long.

I do not coax shapes from the super-organism. I *listen* to the ones it has already abandoned.

There is a spiral stair not far from the district the Liminals call the Shuddering Margin. It was coaxed three hundred and twelve meters high before its maker simply walked away. The last thirty meters were never finished; the steps simply stop, raw and weeping. But the plant kept growing beneath it. The stair now rises slowly, yearlessly, pushed upward by new tissue like a bone being set by an invisible surgeon. Every few days it groans, lifts another handspan, and the lowest steps sink into fresh mulch.

I live there.

I stand on the unfinished top step for weeks at a time, barefoot, letting the sap weep between my toes. Sometimes I face outward, arms spread, becoming a figurehead for a ship that will never sail. Sometimes I face inward, forehead pressed to the raw living wall, and I *listen* to the arguments the plant is having with itself about what it wants to become next. The arguments have no words. They are only vast, slow pressures, like glaciers arguing in their sleep.

When I have gathered enough of that pressure I walk down the stair (all three hundred and twelve meters, every useless, beautiful step) and I find a place where the Undersward is choking on its own ambition. A district where the leaves have grown so dense and so fast that they have begun to starve each other of the sourceless light. There I choose the largest, most arrogant leaf and I carve a single shallow notch in its margin with a knife made of its own discarded bone.

Not to kill it.

Only to remind it that it is still unfinished.

Then I walk away before the Liminals arrive to perform their gentler surgeries. They would not harm me; they cannot decide whether I am a wound or a scar or a new kind of gardener. So they leave small offerings at the base of my stair instead: a single translucent shrimp in a leaf-cup of water, a braid of root-fiber tied into a knot that has no beginning, a shard of mirror-bright sap that has already begun to grow hair-like filaments of its own.

I keep none of these things. The stair keeps them. The Undersward keeps them. Everything here is a museum of things that were never meant to last.

At long intervals (though time is meaningless) I climb to the highest vaults where even the great stems have not yet dared. I go looking for the Not-Yet.

I am not brave. I am only hollow enough that fear passes through me without finding much to grip. I stand on the edge of a leaf that has not yet decided to exist and I let the cold, bright, sterile air move through the groove in my back. Sometimes, for a moment, the constant light truly dims. A shape that is not a shape moves behind the haze. It has no need for gardeners. It has no need for memory. It is the Undersward dreaming without the bother of growing anything first.

When that happens I do not lie flat and pretend to be vegetation. I simply open my arms the way the Liminals do when they are becoming bridges, and I let the Not-Yet look at me.

It looks.

It does not understand what it is seeing.

Because I am the pause it has not yet learned how to digest.

Then the light returns, brighter and colder than before, and I walk back down through the hanging curtains of mist and the slow silver rain that falls for hours before it remembers it is supposed to hit the ground.

I do not know if I am what the Undersward dreamed when it first began to spasm, or if I am what it will dream when it finally grows tired of becoming. I only know that I am necessary the way a comma is necessary in a sentence that refuses to end.

And sometimes, when the world screams itself into new existence far away, and the silence afterward is embarrassed and dripping, I stand on my unfinished stair and I press my own forehead against the raw green wall.

Not to share memory.

Only to give the plant something to measure its vast, violent becoming against.

A small, dull, hollow thing that is terribly, terribly afraid it will never be finished either.

And the Undersward, in its merciless kindness, keeps growing around me.

It always does.
