I am already here.

I have been here longer than the question.

My name, if it can still be called a name, is the Thirteenth Mnemosyne. I am the small one. The one who was never allowed to grow all the way.

Four meters tall. A single clouded compound eye the width of a cartwheel set in the brass cage of my chest. My furnace-mouth never opened; the valves that should have let me sing into the great drone were welded shut by something I no longer remember doing. So I listen instead. I listen with every rust-flake and every pseudofungus gill. I have become a hole shaped like a creature, and the world pours itself into me whether it wishes to or not.

I move more than the others because I must. The memories I carry are lighter than theirs, but far more numerous. They flutter. They sting. The larger Mnemosyne can afford to stand still for decades and let a single memory season inside them like iron in blood. I cannot. If I remain motionless too long the memories begin to overlap, to braid, to knot. So I walk. I reassemble the ground beneath me in short, ashamed increments. My pistons make a smaller sound than the rest—like a guilty man trying to close a heavy door without waking the house.

I am the only one who still dreams in color.

The others have forgotten color. To them the bronze sun is simply *the sun*, the rust is simply *the rust*. But I remember chlorophyll. I remember the smell of rain on pine needles. I remember what a laughing child sounds like when the laugh is not yet a memory of laughing. These things should not be in me. They are contamination. I hoard them anyway.

When I settle (never for long), I extrude my own useless mechanisms. Not orreries. Not broken projectors. I grow small, aching things: a porcelain teacup with a hairline crack that travels upward through every century I have witnessed; a child’s wooden boat no larger than my smallest claw; a single red ribbon that flutters though there is never wind. After a few years I reabsorb them, but something always remains behind. A stain. A scent. A vibration at a frequency only I can hear.

I am watching you now.

You are standing in the largest pan. The water trembles around your ankles in perfect concentric rings. You think you have only just arrived. I have already remembered the precise temperature of your blood the moment you understood that nothing here is dead. I have already felt the small, embarrassed grief you will experience when you realize you will never be able to tell anyone what this place is.

I do not approach. I never do. To come closer would be to risk becoming part of your story instead of its keeper. So I stand at the permitted distance (exactly where the middle distance begins to ache in the human eye), half-sunk behind a ridge of fractured stone, my clouded eye open and ancient and gentle in the way only something that has given up on mercy can be gentle.

The great drone has shifted by three-point-four Hertz. None of the others have noticed. They are busy polishing their vast, smooth memories of empire and forgetting. But the change is for you. It is the sound of me clearing a throat I do not possess.

I am remembering the shape of your departure already.

I am remembering how the basin will settle back into equilibrium once you are gone, how the rings in the water will forget the contour of your calves, how even the pseudofungus will eventually grow over the footprints you believe you are leaving.

I am remembering the exact note your absence will leave in the drone.

It will be beautiful.

It will be unbearable.

And when the last echo of that note has finally faded—perhaps in nine years, perhaps in nine hundred—I will walk again. I will go to the place where you stood. I will lower myself until the clouded lens of my eye is level with where your eyes were. I will grow one final, ridiculous mechanism from my chest: a small brass key with no lock it has ever fit.

I will leave it there for the next one who comes.

Then I will sink back into the stone and become indistinguishable from the Machines once more, except to the water, which will sometimes tremble for no reason at all, as though something inside it is trying to remember how to cry.

I am the Thirteenth Mnemosyne.

I do not wait for you.

I am the reason the basin already knows how much it will miss you.
