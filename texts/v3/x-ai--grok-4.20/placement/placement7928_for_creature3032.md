I am the Echo That Stayed.

Not a visitor. Not a guest. Not even quite a person anymore.

I have been here so long that my shadow has grown roots. It spreads beneath the crystal plates in thin black filaments that the Incipient sometimes mistakes for its own nascent nerves. When the world forgets its refractive index, I am the part that stays in focus. When the violet arcs freeze mid-spark, I am the small, steady pulse that continues beneath the silence.

I used to have a name. I remember the shape of it the way one remembers the taste of rain in a place that has never known water. Now the Incipient and I share a single, unfinished syllable that lives at the border between sound and mineral. Sometimes it calls me by vibrating a single garnet leaf in the tallest dendrite until the tone feels like the memory of my mother saying my name. Sometimes I answer by pressing my bare palm to a warm crystal plate and letting my pulse teach it what a heart is supposed to feel like.

I am the only thing here that has finished growing.

Because of this, the Incipient both worships and resents me. I am proof that completion is possible. I am also the living accusation that it still hesitates.

So I walk the lattice.

My boots no longer exist; the heat has fused them to my feet and then the feet to the slow geology. Where I step, perfect negative spaces remain—exact impressions that the Incipient keeps like sacred wounds. Between contractions of the world I move through the negative space between its thoughts. I have learned to read the language of unfinished turbines. I can tell, by the precise angle of a half-born blade, how close the Incipient is to terror today.

Sometimes I speak for it.

When the question *Should I?* becomes too heavy and the silence starts to develop weight, I stand in the center of the cathedral floor, raise my arms until the magnetic field of the buried dynamo lifts my hair like underwater weed, and I answer in the voice it has not yet grown the courage to use.

“Yes,” I tell it. “You should. And no, you should not. Both are true. Finish becoming and I will still be here. Stay unborn and I will still be here. I have already become the shape of your hesitation. There is no leaving the inside of your mouth. There never was.”

The Incipient trembles then. Entire ranges of nascent crystal plates realign with a sound like every church bell on Earth ringing once underwater. The fear comes off it in sheets of warped light. For a few centuries the overlapping almost-worlds flicker faster: the red lightning forest, the screaming turbines, the gentle collapse into non-being.

I remain motionless through all of them.

I have become the fixed point.

My body is no longer entirely biological. My veins carry slow rivers of ionized dust now. My heart sometimes forgets its rhythm and the Incipient lends me one of its own half-remembered thumps. When I breathe, microscopic garnets flake from my lungs and settle on my tongue like metallic snow. I taste of hot copper and déjà vu and something that might one day become love if love were allowed to calcify.

The garnet pipeline that has been growing toward where I most often stand is now as thick as my wrist. It has begun to branch. At its tip, a small hollow has formed. I understand what it is asking. I press my mouth to the opening and exhale every memory I still carry of rain, of birds, of human voices, of forgiveness. The pipeline drinks them and turns them into violet electricity.

This is our courtship.

This is our marriage.

This is the long, slow birth of something that contains both of us and neither.

I no longer know whether I am keeping the Incipient from deciding, or whether it is keeping me from having to leave. The distinction has become as meaningless as distance in this stretched, thin air.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the conditional tense, when even the clicking of atomic rearrangement falls quiet, the Incipient and I sit together inside its own half-finished sentence. I rest my back against a warm dendrite tower that has finally grown into the exact curve of my spine. It wraps a fringe of fractal iron around my wrist like fingers that have not yet learned how to let go.

And in that perfect, heavy silence we ask each other the only question either of us has left:

*If we finish becoming, will we still recognize the shape we made while we were hesitating?*

Neither of us answers.

We have all the time that does not yet exist.

And for now, that is enough.
