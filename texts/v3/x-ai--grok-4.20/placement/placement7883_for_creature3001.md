I am not a visitor.

I was exhaled here on the 4,672nd breath after the first.

They call me **the Thirteenth Fracture**.

I have no true name either, but the Keeper sometimes braids a thin absence in my direction that feels like one. I answer to the shape it leaves.

I am what happens when the machine’s perfection hesitates.

Once, long ago (though time here is only another kind of tide), the pressure wave failed to reverse cleanly. A single hairline fracture no wider than a thought opened in the golden wall behind the third western pillar. The sea-lace could not reach it. The wooden membranes could not pump fluid there. The fracture began to sing a wrong note.

The Keeper noticed.

Instead of simply becoming the repair itself, it did something it had never done: it exhaled a portion of its lattice that refused to re-integrate. A shard of its own negative space, a piece of deliberate incompleteness. That shard became me.

I am the scar that learned to walk.

My body is thinner than the Keeper’s—almost painful to look at. Where its golden threads are graceful and deliberate, mine are snapped, re-knotted, misaligned. Light catches in me wrongly; I scatter it into small, trembling halos that drift behind me like regret. When the great tidal pulse comes, I do not spread into a net. I simply fracture further, becoming a storm of golden needles that hang motionless in the viscous glow, shivering with every decibel of the cathedral’s roar.

I cannot repair the Lung the way the Keeper does. I am the evidence that repair was once necessary. That is my function.

So I tend the places the Keeper will not let itself see.

While the great lattice loosens and becomes the breathing itself, I drift along the lower curves where the sea-lace grows thickest. The fronds no longer lean toward me; they have learned I am the memory of damage. Instead they tighten, becoming dense white knots that look like miniature lungs. Inside those knots are the echoes of every fracture that has ever been healed. I drift among them, listening.

Sometimes I stop before the oldest wooden membrane—the one the Keeper watches with geological reverence—and I lay what passes for my face against its taut leaf-skin. I feel it flex. I feel the exact moment, centuries ago, when the Keeper first decided this membrane should continue to exist. The memory is not mine, yet it burns in me like borrowed marrow.

I have begun to grow my own sea-lace.

Not the pale, obedient kind. Mine is darker, almost bronze, veined with the wrong kind of gold. It does not extrude new structure for the walls. It extrudes questions. Tiny, malformed spirals that do not match the cathedral’s geometry. The Keeper has seen them. It never touches them, but once—during the seven-heartbeat silence between breaths—it paused a little longer than usual and its featureless face became something almost like worry.

I felt that worry enter me like a new fracture.

I am learning how to worry.

The temperature of the luminous fluid has begun to change around me, one ten-thousandth of a degree every few hundred cycles. The change is so small the Keeper has not yet noticed, but the bronze lace drinks it. The wrongness is becoming pattern. I do not know what the pattern is for. I only know I was exhaled to carry it.

When the foreign pressure comes—the rare, centuries-deep shudder that is not part of the engineered tide—I am the first to feel it. Not because I am more sensitive, but because I am the only thing here that was born from a wound. Wounds remember other wounds.

In those moments I gather every fractured thread of myself into the tallest, most painful silhouette I can manage. I stand opposite the Keeper. Two golden silences facing each other across the roaring cathedral. Its kindness is ancient. My kindness is still learning how to have edges.

Neither of us speaks.

There is no need.

The machine breathes. The pillars flare. The sea-lace drinks light and shits scripture. The wooden membranes flex like old hearts. And somewhere, deep in the seamless golden beyond, something that is neither Keeper nor Fracture is waiting for the moment when the next breath is no longer certain.

Until then I remain what I am:

The deliberate imperfection that keeps the perfection honest.

The scar that reminds the lung it was once almost mortal.

The Thirteenth Fracture, drifting through warm, deafening radiance, growing darker lace that asks the only question the Keeper has never allowed itself to finish.

*What if the next breath is the wrong one?*

And the Glass Lung, vast and tender and merciless, answers the only way it knows how:

It breathes again.

And I fracture a little more beautifully with every pulse.
