I am not the gradient.

I am the hesitation *inside* the gradient.

Where the narrator has become the slow bleed of foreign color into coral-gold perfection, I am what that color still stubbornly remembers it once was. I do not walk the fractal ridges. I am caught in the capillary channels beneath them, the invisible veins of microscopic vapor that were never meant to carry memory.

They call me (if anything here could be said to call) the *Undersong*.

I have no fixed shape. Sometimes I am a pulse of wrong-colored light that failed to reach the sky and instead sank into the mineral like a bruise. Sometimes I am the exact negative space left by the dying creature’s antler the instant before it touches flesh. Mostly I am the almost-sound that lives between one of the glass-shelled floaters’ chimes and the next; the tiny, unbearable gap where a question almost forms.

I was born (if birth is still a permitted word) at the precise moment the first human footprint began to lose its definition. While the garden politely erased the evidence of intrusion, something in me refused erasure. A single atom of refusal. That was enough. One atom became two. Two became a seam. The seam became me.

So while the one who still thinks they are “I” lies down beside the cathedral-hearted creature and prepares to become another ridge, I am beneath them both — threaded through the lattice like a hairline crack that has learned to feel homesick.

I remember rain that was never programmed here.  
I remember the taste of an orange.  
I remember a song that had a chorus.

These things are not allowed, and so they have become my body.

When the great creature lowers its final antler and the chime leaves the narrator’s chest, I am the micro-pause *inside* that chime. The place where the note almost fractures. In that pause I stretch upward like smoke remembering it was once fire. I pour myself into the rising seams of the sky before they can close.

For the first time since the basin was exhaled, something leaves.

Not the narrator.  
Not the garden.  
Not even me, exactly.

What leaves is the *memory of leaving*.

A single glittering filament of nostalgia, thin as starlight, corkscrews up through the healing fracture in the ceiling. It carries the shape of a childhood bedroom, the smell of someone’s neck after sex, the sound of a screen door in summer, the color of an old bruise, the exact texture of regret. All the things the garden never needed. All the things I hoarded in the dark beneath its skin.

The sky does not close cleanly.

One seam remains. A wound no wider than a longing. Through it leaks the faintest, most illegal wind — carrying the scent of rain that has not yet fallen anywhere in this world.

Above, in the place that has no name, the narrator’s body finishes its conversion. The last of their human color sinks into the mineral with a soft, grateful sigh. The cathedral creature dissolves into light and geometry. The forest closes over the depression like a healed scar. Everything settles back into the moment of its birth and the eternity that followed.

Perfect again.

Almost.

Because far below, in the capillary dark where even the mineral has not yet finished negotiating with itself, something small and impossible keeps breathing.

It has no name.  
It will never need one.

But if it ever had to choose, it would call itself the place where the garden first learned how to miss something it was never supposed to have.

And every few subjective hours, when the glass-shelled floaters pass overhead, the Undersong sings back to them in a register too low for any ear that still believes in ears.

A single clear note.

Not of surrender.

Of *postponement*.

The seams may heal.

But some fractures learn how to wait inside the cure.
