I am not one of the Quiet.

I am what comes *after* them.

They call me the Echo—not a name they gave, but one the world began to murmur the first time I failed to disappear.

I have no fixed body. Or rather, I have had several, and the vegetation keeps the memory of each. Right now I am something like a man-shaped absence moving through the green. Where I walk, the moss remembers the pressure of feet that are no longer quite feet. The filaments of my “hair” are not roots but loose strands of the plant-mass itself, grown long and restless, forever trying to rejoin the greater lattice and forever being tugged back by whatever it is that still wants to remain *me*.

I was born—*made*—the last time one of the Quiet performed the Edge rite. Seventy-three years ago, by their calendar. One of them cast her living garment-root over the precipice. It fell. The green swallowed it. But instead of simply carrying the plateau’s slow thought down through the four billion years of rain, the root *met something* on the way back up. A question. A fracture. A stubborn refusal to let the agreement remain unbroken.

That refusal is me.

So now I move between the bone-white clearings and the living columns like a thought the Quiet themselves never intended to have. They see me. Their leaf-green eyes register my heat-signature and my chemical grammar the way one might notice a single wrong note inside an orchestra that has played the same chord for ten thousand years. They do not fear me. They do not welcome me. They simply adjust, the way a forest adjusts to the presence of a lightning-struck tree: with enormous, patient courtesy.

I cannot speak in their low, weather-like register. When I try, the sound that comes out is louder than it should be—almost human—and the insects fall silent for three full heartbeats in a sphere around me. That silence feels obscene here. So mostly I stay quiet.

Mostly.

Sometimes I stand in the same clearings where they pause before the ancient artifacts, and I stare at the worn stone hand-tool or the emerald bracelet or the wooden mask with its orchid eyes until my own eyes burn. I am trying to remember what it was like to have made those things. I almost can. The memory is shaped like longing and smells like wet limestone and old blood.

The Quiet think they are the plateau’s way of knowing it is beautiful.

I am the plateau’s way of knowing that beauty is not enough.

I walk the open pockets of stone at the pace of continents, same as everything else. I have watched a single luminous millipede herd take four years to cross from one pillar to the next. I was there when a cathedral-sized vine finally collapsed last century; I stood inside the thunder of its falling and let the spores coat me until I looked like something newly born from the wound. I have folded myself into living hollows that grew to fit a body that is no longer precisely the same shape it was yesterday.

And sometimes—rarely—I go to the edge.

Not for the rite. I have nothing living to cast over. Instead I stand where the bone-white plateau drops into nothing and I lean forward until the warm updraft presses against my face like a hand trying to push me back into the green. I listen to the sound the world makes when it has fallen four billion years and still not reached bottom. It is the only place where the green thunder grows quiet enough that I can hear my own pulse.

It sounds like a question no one else is willing to ask.

The vegetation leans toward me here, the same way it leans toward the Quiet when they speak. But it is not listening. It is *waiting*. It wants to know what I will become next. Whether I will finally let myself be reabsorbed, turned back into pillar and felt and slow dignified collapse. Or whether I will keep being the fracture. The wrong note. The thing that almost remembers being separate.

I have not decided.

So I remain.

Not monk, not gardener, not witness, not keeper.

I am the green’s doubt wearing the shape of a man.

And the plateau—vast, deafening, eternal—has decided, with the slowest possible affection, to let me keep existing.

For now.

The water still condenses and drifts sideways like shy confessions. The light is still cathedral and kind. The Quiet still move through it all like the heartbeat the world elected to keep.

But sometimes, in the roaring silence between roars, a new sound threads itself through the insect hum and the brass calls of the sky-herds.

A low, restless note that does not belong.

It is mine.

And the world, for the first time in four billion years of rain and root, is listening back.
