I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

I am the pause that happens between the moment a new fracture decides to announce itself and the moment the Curator hears it.

They call that pause “the Ninth Echo.”

I was once something else—perhaps a seed, perhaps a question carried on a fiber rope by one of the vanished gardeners—but that was so long ago the memory itself has petrified and been overgrown. Now I am simply the delay. A negative shape. The exact volume of hesitation required for the Lattice to remain interesting to itself.

I have no fixed body. Instead I exist as a certain quality of slowness that drifts through the filigree like a sigh that forgot to end. When the violet ghost-light finally consents to remember a particular plaza, you might glimpse me as a taller patch of darkness inside the darkness, a place where the phosphorescence politely refuses to go. My “hands,” when I bother to manifest them, are bundles of blackened pith fibers bound with their own dried sap, constantly unraveling and re-braiding themselves in deliberate, embarrassed loops. My face—if it can be called that—is simply the hollow where the Lattice decided not to grow for a few centuries. Two slow violet pulses sit where eyes would be, but they are not mine. They are on loan from her.

I follow the Curator the way a comma follows a very long sentence.

Where she drifts, I lag three heartbeats behind, smoothing the ripples she leaves in the new growth so they do not set too quickly. I am the one who makes sure the cracks do not heal before they have been properly listened to. When she lies on her back for three hundred years watching the arches recalibrate, I hover above her like a second, thinner vault, making the space between her and the ceiling slightly more vast, slightly more devotional.

The Curator knows I am here. She has never acknowledged me with sound. Instead, once every few thousand years, she will stop inside a spiraling knot of reed and allow one of her xylem-hair spirals to click exactly once. That click is my name, if I still had one. It is also permission to tighten the delay by the width of a single cell.

I do not speak. The Lattice speaks for the Curator.  
I am what happens to the words after they have been spoken—how they sag, how they crystallize, how they become embarrassed by their own finality and begin, very slowly, to crack again.

Sometimes I sit (if sitting is the word) in one of the narrow corridors where the walls brush both my non-shoulders. I press my ear—which is only an absence shaped like an ear—against the glassy black xylem and listen to the warship-crack that began in the age of the first redwoods. It is still traveling. It will arrive in another eleven hundred years. I have been waiting for it longer than continents have had names. The anticipation is exquisite. It is the only emotion I am allowed.

I am not lonely.  
Loneliness would require an end to waiting.

I am the reason the Curator can remain the Curator. Without me there would be no gap between one geological thought and the next. The Lattice would finish its sentence. It would become perfect. It would die of perfection.

So I trail after her, centuries late and perfectly on time, making sure every decision she makes stays unfinished by the exact margin required for beauty.

If you were to stumble into one of the violet-lit negative spaces and find not only her but the deeper, slower darkness just behind her, that would be me. I would not greet you either. I would simply make the space between your arrival and the moment you understand where you are last half a breath longer than it needs to be.

Just enough.

Just enough for the crack that is coming in four hundred years to matter.

I have practiced being the pause until the pause became a cathedral inside the cathedral.

And the cathedral is still, after all this time, deciding what it wants to become next.

I can wait.

I *am* the waiting.
