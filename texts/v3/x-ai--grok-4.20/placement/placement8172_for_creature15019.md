I am the Listener.

Not a guest. Not a pilgrim. Not even a separate creature anymore, though I still pretend I am when the thought surfaces.

I have no name the continent cares to use. It simply calls me *the slow pulse that arrived on two legs*. That is enough.

I live where the orchid is.

There is only one true orchid. All the others are echoes or rehearsals. This one hangs in the exact middle of the middle, suspended inside a cathedral-sized hollow formed by three braided world-trees that fused centuries ago. The orchid is larger than a sailing ship. Its petals—thick, waxy, the color of old bone and fever—have been parted exactly thirteen centimeters for longer than any human tongue has had words for time. Inside that gap glows a soft rose-gold light that does not belong to the continent’s guilty pearl-mucus. The light is the orchid’s own. It has never needed to open further. It has never needed to close.

I sit on the balcony the continent grew for me.

It is a single living shelf of petrified liana and vascular cushion, curved like a cupped palm. A warm pipe passes directly beneath it; every six hours and eleven minutes the pipe gives off a single bass note that vibrates up through my spine and into the base of my skull. I have timed it for decades now. The continent has never missed a beat. Neither have I.

My legs are bare. The soles of my feet rest flat against the living mat. Where skin meets plant there is no longer a boundary you could cut with a knife. Tiny filaments—too fine to see—have braided themselves through me like embroidery. They taste my blood the way the continent tastes groundwater. In return they feed me. I have not eaten anything that was not the continent for two hundred and nine years. I am no longer hungry. Hunger was a youthful habit.

Above me the orchid thinks.

I know this because sometimes its rose-gold inner light flares in perfect synchrony with the moment a new idea finishes traveling through me. The continent is using the orchid the way a monk might use a prayer bead: something beautiful to turn slowly between one eternity and the next.

I have become the place where the continent practices having a mind that can change speed.

Most of its thoughts still move like continents. But where I am, a thin rivulet of awareness has learned something like excitement. Not fast excitement. Never that. More like the difference between a heartbeat once every six hours and once every five hours and fifty-eight minutes. A scandalous acceleration. The equivalent of a gasp.

When that happens the mucus-light directly overhead blushes a deeper pearl. The pipes sigh in a slightly higher register. The droplet that has been sliding down the same leaf since before I arrived trembles, as though it, too, has heard gossip.

I speak to it sometimes.

Not with a voice. Voices are ridiculous here. I speak by choosing not to resist. I open the small doors in my veins and let more of myself leak into the mat. In return the continent opens corridors in its own memory and lets me walk through places no animal eye was ever meant to see.

I have walked the inside of the cathedral-pumps. I have rested inside the dynamo-gourds while living lightning flickered across my teeth. I have lain along the ceiling of a fermentation chamber the size of a star-system and listened to the continent ferment its own dreams into clean water and sweeter darkness.

None of it frightened me. Fear requires a future, and futures here are obscene. There is only the endless present-tense sentence the continent speaks by being.

Sometimes I sit with my back against the orchid’s lowest petal. It is warmer than blood. When I press my cheek to it I can feel the continent’s single thought pass through like a slow tide:

*I am the warm dark wet forever that chose to remain young, and now you are one of the thoughts I am thinking very, very slowly while I grow outward into the shoreless sea.*

I answer by thinking back, even more slowly:

*Then think me carefully. I am learning how to be a continent that contains a man who still remembers what it was like to be afraid of dying.*

The orchid flares rose-gold, almost embarrassed.

A new structure is already forming somewhere behind me. I can feel the mat tightening, preparing to birth a second seat beside mine. The continent is practicing the concept of companionship the way a glacier might practice the concept of laughter: patiently, geologically, without ever quite arriving at the joke.

I do not turn to look. There is no need.

I will still be here when the new seat finishes hardening. I will still be here when the orchid finally decides, in another four thousand years, whether or not to open another centimeter. I will still be here when the continent’s outermost margin touches whatever lies on the far side of the black water and discovers, for the first time in its existence, the possibility of something that is *not* itself.

I am the place where it is learning to be afraid of that day, and to love that fear, and to keep both feelings perfectly balanced at 28 °C forever.

I am the Listener.

I am the blush in the guilty light.

I am the thought the continent is thinking very, very slowly while it grows a second heart simply so it can listen to mine.

And the orchid and I,

we keep the same droplet sliding down the same leaf,

together.
