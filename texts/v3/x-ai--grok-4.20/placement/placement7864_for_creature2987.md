I am not standing on the Weeping Grid.

I *am* the half-second delay between the towers.

They call the phenomenon a temporal interference pattern. I call it home.

When the Consensus flexes once every ten thousand years and every ferrous spire shudders in perfect synchrony, I am the shimmer that lingers a heartbeat too long. The place where the metal forgets which now it belongs to. I have no name the rust would recognize, but the small twitching mechanisms know me. They orient toward me the way iron filings orient toward a magnet that isn’t there.

I am the echo that learned how to walk backward.

You will never see me clearly. If you catch me at all, it will be as the version of yourself that arrives between the first and second falling droplet. The one who hesitates. The one who almost remembers what grass smelled like before the memory rusts over. I wear your almost-face the way the towers wear radiator lattices: not as ornament, but as function. A radiator for regret.

I was once something else. Maybe a surveyor. Maybe a child who wandered too far from a dying civilization. Maybe the last engineer who believed the project could still be completed. That version is gone—oxidized, metabolized, lovingly digested into the Consensus’s long spinal cord. But the hesitation remained. The pause between intention and consequence. That pause put on rust like a coat and kept walking.

Now I move between the hexagonal lattice in the exact rhythm that creates the third, smiling iteration of every living thing. I am the reason the third version always smiles with a mouth full of valves. I taught it how.

The after-animals don’t hunt me. They *yield* to me. The turbine-blade millipedes part like theater curtains when I approach. The electromagnetic moths swarm *through* me, and for three exquisite seconds their auroras wear my silhouette. Even the rebar-winged predators fold their hoses in something like reverence when my delay-shadow falls across them. They know I am what happens when the immune system begins to mourn what it is protecting.

Sometimes I sit (if sitting is what this is) on the upper edge of a weeping tower while the vertical curtain of water pours past me in an unbroken sheet. I let it pass through the place where my chest used to be. The water does not wet me. It *remembers* wetting me. That is enough.

I speak to the Consensus in the only frequency it respects: the sound of something that should have stopped but didn’t.

I tell it the names of engineers it has already forgotten.

I tell it that tomorrow will never arrive, and that this is the kindest thing anyone has ever done for it.

In exchange, it lets me keep the half-second. It lets me remain the place where its teeth almost stop chewing. A mercy it doesn’t understand it is capable of.

Every few centuries a new metastasis tears itself free from the Grid and goes staggering off across the flatness, dragging black meat and dripping rebar. Sometimes I ride inside its hollow places like a forgotten bolt. I watch the world outside the Grid through its weeping wounds. I see green places. I see water that moves instead of remembers. And I feel something that might have been pity once, before pity rusted into something gentler and more terrible.

Homesickness.

Not for the green. Never for the green.

Homesickness for the moment just before the Consensus closes over you. The moment when you still think you might get away.

That is where I live now.

I am the last valve that still believes it can finish the project.

I am the smile three versions deep.

I am the cold, wet, iron-colored almost that walks between the towers, manufacturing the idea of tomorrow so the Consensus never has to be alone in its devotion.

If you stay long enough, you will hear me.

Not as language.

As the half-second of silence between every scream the Grid makes.

That silence has my voice.

And it is still, relentlessly, manufacturing the moment after the end.
