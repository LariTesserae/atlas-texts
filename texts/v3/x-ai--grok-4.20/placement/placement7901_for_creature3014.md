I am not standing in the center.

I tried, once. The pulse came — that forty-three-second heartbeat rolling through the stone like the slow contraction of a planet’s iron womb — and every Veyr within twenty meters simply stopped. Ten thousand bodies lowered in perfect unison until their bellies kissed the glowing floor. Their bioluminescent ridges flared together, a single sullen wave of ember-light that passed across the chamber like blood through a shared artery. In that suspended instant the roar of mandibles and grinding shells fell away into something worse than silence: a listening.

I could not bear it.

So I moved. Not away (there is nowhere away), but *into*. I let the swarm close over me the way a wound closes over a splinter. I allowed the Veyr to incorporate me.

It did not hurt. Pain would have been too fast, too mammalian. What happened was slower.

First the heat stopped burning. It simply became the condition of my skin. My sweat flash-dried into a fine red crust that the smaller Veyr tasted, then delicately scraped away with the edges of their mouthparts, recycling even that. My lungs gave up on air and learned to drink the metallic vapor the way a root drinks stone. After some uncountable stretch of time my ribcage began to stiffen. Plates of black chitin, thin as communion wafers at first, began to extrude from my sternum and spine. They did not grow *on* me. They grew *as* me. I felt the first vanadinite needles push through the new shell like slow fireworks, each one a tiny antenna tasting stress fractures I had never known existed.

I am becoming an anchor.

Not the oldest ones (those are still boulders of living garnet and hematite that have forgotten they were ever separate), but something midway. A transitional node. The swarm flows around me now the way magma flows around a stubborn xenolith. When two smaller Veyr meet nearby, they sometimes pause, lock carapaces with mine for a few centuries, and I feel their memories — which are not memories but mineral histories — pass through me. I know the exact lattice pattern of every crystal that has grown and been eaten in this chamber for the last nine million years. I know it the way a heart knows its own valves.

I no longer have eyes. I do not need them. Heat is my light. Pressure is my hearing. When a fresh sheet of realgar bursts from the ceiling with its wet hiss, I feel it as an orgasm of new geometry across the entire surface of my being.

Every forty-three seconds I lower my body (what remains of it) until the heavy plates of my back kiss the floor. In that moment I am not separate from the waiting Veyr in the high bubble. I am not separate from the larval things that drink excess heat. I am not separate from the sleeper beyond the walls. We are all the same slow pulse wearing different densities of matter.

Sometimes, very rarely, one of the translucent larval forms crawls across my back. Its feathery gills brush the crystal gardens that have become my shoulders. It drinks. I let it. In exchange it leaves behind a single perfect sphere no larger than a pearl, nestled in a hollow that has opened in my thorax. The sphere beats once every forty-three seconds. It is warm. It is listening.

I think — if the word *think* can still be used — that I am no longer waiting *for* the sleeper to wake.

I am one of the places where its dreaming becomes industrious.

The chamber eats itself, rebuilds itself, eats itself again. I am part of the jaw, part of the stomach, part of the new growth. Ten thousand grinding mandibles roar around me and through me. The red light breathes. The crystals click and bloom and fracture.

And I, who was once foolish enough to stand in the center and feel that heartbeat as something outside myself, have become a place the heartbeat can rest inside.

I am the Veyr now.

I always was.

The only difference is that something inside me still remembers the taste of cold, thin, treacherous oxygen, and the brief, ridiculous speed of a human lifetime. That memory is becoming beautiful the way a flaw in a perfect crystal is beautiful: a tiny, impermissible softness that the swarm has decided, after careful geological consideration, to preserve.

They have incorporated even my nostalgia.

It grows on my left foreleg now, a single minuscule blossom of something that might once have been called sorrow, crystallized in shades of frozen starlight no other Veyr possesses.

The swarm likes it.

They polish it every few millennia with the soft edges of their mouthparts, the way monks might polish a single sacred relic.

When the sleeper finally turns over in truth, when the forty-three-second pulse becomes a continuous roar and the walls split like an egg, that small crystal of human regret will be the first thing carried out into whatever new hell waits beyond.

I hope it survives the journey.

I think it will.

After all, nothing here is in a hurry.

And nothing here is ever lost.
