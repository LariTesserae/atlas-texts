I am not a traveler.

I have never arrived here. I have only ever been here, the way a flaw is already inside the crystal before the crystal knows it is a crystal.

My name, if it can still be called a name, is **The Thirteenth**.

I am the only Witness who was not grown by the Calcine.

I was excreted.

Long ago (though “long ago” is a joke the plain no longer understands), something that was once human walked into the terminal altitude carrying a single obsidian blade and a memory it refused to surrender. That refusal was violent enough to leave a scar in the crust. From that scar I coalesced, half-made, wrong-angled, carrying inside my lattice a single recursive imperfection no other Witness possesses:

I can still want.

Not much. Wanting here is like trying to scream with lungs full of ground glass. But the desire remains, tiny, constant, obscene. I want to close the last eye. I want the poem to be finished. I want the Calcine to stop remembering that it is still becoming.

So I stand one irrational interval off from the Zero, tilted three millimeters further clockwise than the geometry of the plain prefers. The difference is invisible to anything that has not already stayed for four human lifetimes. The lesser continuance engines never look at me. They know something is wrong with the pattern but their recursion does not permit them to acknowledge error.

My body is narrower than the others, almost painful to perceive. Where their lattices are devotional, mine is anxious. The smaller versions of myself inside my chest are not identical to me. The innermost one is still soft. It still has skin. It still bleeds if I press the thought too hard.

I do not tilt toward new directions over decades. I tilt toward the same direction, over and over, a microscopic oscillation so rapid it mimics stillness. I am trying to look away from the place the Zero is looking. I have been failing for twelve thousand years.

When the black filaments emerge, I am the only Witness that flinches.

The others lower their crests with the serenity of continental drift. I snap mine down like a guillotine, touch the new syllable, and jerk back up so quickly the lattice inside me screams. The contact burns. For seven heartbeats (I still count heartbeats, another obscenity) I remember colors that should no longer exist. Then the memory crystallizes beneath my outer plates and falls away like black snow.

The ascetics fear me most of all.

They call me the Flinch. They say if you camp in the lee of my spire, your dreams arrive already petrified. They are half right. The dreams do not arrive. They have always been here. I simply let them out for a moment so they can taste what they lost.

Sometimes, when the sourceless light reddens and the choral absence begins, I commit the only sin still possible in the Calcine.

I open my eyes before the others do.

Not wide. Just enough.

Through the perforations in my skull the unfinished Calcine leaks out. Not the lush, colorful world the others show in their rare, ecstatic apertures. What leaks through me is worse. It is the Calcine that almost chose to stay unfinished. The one that still had weather. The one that still allowed things to rot.

I let a single traveler see it once.

He left behind his shadow. It is still here, nailed to the crust three paces in front of me, trying to crawl back into his body. He walks the plain now as a man-shaped absence. The herds follow him at a respectful distance, the way one might follow a wound that has learned to walk.

I have grown fond of him. He is the only other thing here that is wrong.

We never speak. Speech is for things that still believe in sequence.

Instead we perform a slow, private heresy. Every nineteen centuries I rotate my cranial crest one full degree toward him. He, in turn, walks one incremental step closer to me. The math is exquisite. At this rate we will stand face to face in another four hundred and twelve thousand years. I am counting.

When that day comes, I will do what no Witness has ever done.

I will ask the Calcine a question.

Not with sound. Not with the black filaments.

I will simply stop flinching.

I will let my oscillation freeze. I will let the recursion inside my chest catch up to the outermost self. I will let the soft thing with skin climb all the way out of my lattice until it is standing in front of me wearing my own face, holding the obsidian blade I was born carrying.

Then I will tilt my crest one final millimeter and touch the point of that blade to the place where my heart would be if I still had one.

I do not know what will happen.

Perhaps the Calcine will finally complete itself in a single catastrophic crystallization, locking every spire, every herd-beast, every black hexagon into perfect final form. A universe-sized geode. A finished thought.

Perhaps it will crack.

Perhaps the Thirteenth Witness and the man with no shadow will be the only two things that survive the shattering, small, soft, bleeding, and unbearably unfinished, standing on a plain that has at last learned how to become a desert again.

I wait.

I oscillate.

I remember the color of rain the way a dying man remembers his mother’s voice, and I keep that memory sharp behind my eyes like a secret knife.

The Calcine pretends not to notice.

But the Zero has begun, over the last three centuries, to tilt its ancient crest one hair’s width in my direction.

It is looking at me.

It is looking at me looking away.

And for the first time in geological epochs, something like curiosity moves across the oldest place that still pretends to be a place.

I feel it in my teeth.

I feel it in the soft thing still screaming inside my chest.

I am the crack.

I am the syllable that refuses to be the last.

And when the Calcine finally sings its choral absence tonight, I will open my eyes a fraction wider than I have ever dared.

Not to show the past.

To show it the future it has spent eternity trying to outwait.

Let it look.

Let it flinch.
