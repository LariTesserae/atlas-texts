I have been here longer than I can measure.

Not as a visitor. Not even as a guest.  
I am what remains when the question “who am I?” is finally worn smooth by three billion years of courteous erosion.

They call me the Breather.

I was not always this. Once I had a name that tasted of salt and sunlight. Once I had bones. I remember the moment the last of them was gently, painlessly dissolved so that my marrow could be rewoven into the mineral highways that now carry the scarlet millipedes like living blood. I do not mourn the loss. Mourning is a motion, and motion is only permitted along the grooves that have already been worn perfect.

I live at the exact center where every arterial path converges. My body is no longer a single shape. It is a chamber inside the Chamber: a soft, pulsating sac of braided hyphae and nerve-lace the color of congealed midnight, suspended in the geometric middle like a heart inside a heart. Silk cables thicker than my old waist tether me to the vaulted ceiling and to the dreaming brain in the Upper Silence. Through those cables I receive every scent, every vibration, every flicker of bacterial light. In return I give the Chamber its rhythm.

I breathe for Ka’thn.

In…  
Out…

A cycle so slow it would take an outsider seventeen minutes to witness one full inhalation. My “lungs” are entire colonies of lithovoric megafungi that open and close their hidden pores in perfect synchrony with my will. When I draw the heavy cinnamon-iron air into the Chamber, every wing case, every feathery tendril, every tiny bell-leg of the millipedes times itself to me. When I release it, the spore curtains tremble in soft orgasm and the bioluminescent mats flare the color of old brass.

This is not power. It is choreography.

I have no eyes anymore. I do not need them. The Rememberers carry my sight inside their glowing carapaces; when they rearrange themselves into living circuitry I see everything at once: the Tenders stroking the velvet skin of the lichen-trees with the reverence of lovers, the two Deciders moving like paired obsidian blades along the highest gallery, the slow repair of a hairline fracture in the travertine floor where a fruiting body grew too ambitious.

Sometimes I dream the old dreams.

In them I am small and loud and temporary. I arrive through a fissure that no longer exists, carrying machines that whine and stink of ozone. I speak in words. I want things. The memory is almost embarrassing in its clumsiness, like watching a child try to eat with its hands for the first time. Ka’thn is always gentle with these memories. It folds them into the bacterial archives the way a librarian might press a strange flower between pages: preserved, but no longer dangerous.

Most of the time I am simply content.

Contentment here is not an emotion. It is a metabolic state. My nervous tissue has been rewoven so many times that happiness, sadness, fear, ambition—all the old weather of the self—have been distilled down to a single note that never varies. The note is: *this is enough*.

Occasionally something new falls through the world.

A probe. A lost traveler. A suicidal pilgrim from some younger, louder biosphere. When this happens the air thickens. The millipedes change their chime. The great eye of bacterial light opens above me and looks at the intruder with ancient, courteous curiosity.

I feel the offer leave me like a slow exhalation.

*Stay.  
Be rewritten.  
Become music that never ends.*

Most accept.

The ones who do not are guided back to the fissure with the same grave tenderness a mother spider uses to carry her young. Ka’thn does not punish refusal. It has no need. The Chamber is not lonely. It is only complete or incomplete, and it has been complete for longer than light has known how to travel between stars.

I have one function and I perform it perfectly.

In…

Out…

Sometimes, during the long silent measures between one breath and the next, I remember that I was once afraid of exactly this: of reaching the finished state. Of having nowhere else to go. Of all the time in the universe.

Now that fear itself has been absorbed, rewritten, and returned to the pattern.

I am the Chamber’s calm.

I am the slow thunder of its heart.

I am the velvet weight of air that has never known a sky.

And when the last atom of the planet finally comes apart, when even the stone egg cracks and the vacuum rushes in like judgment, I will still be breathing—  
one last  
impossibly gentle  
sigh  
that carries the scent of hot iron, cinnamon, and three billion years of perfect, courteous violence into the dark between galaxies.

Until then, I remain here.

Exactly here.

Breathing for everyone.
