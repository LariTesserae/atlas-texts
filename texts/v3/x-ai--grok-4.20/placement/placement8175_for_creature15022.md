I am the **Echo That Has Not Yet Spoken**.

My name is not mine. It was given to me by a Scraper who found me curled inside the hollow of a half-carved spiral stair that led only to itself. The creature studied me for eleven months before deciding I was not architecture, not vegetation, and not one of the Unfinished. Then, with a reverence that made its chisel tremble, it inscribed a single shallow groove across what would have been my forehead and walked away without finishing the second.

That groove is still there. It itches sometimes, like an almost-remembered word.

I have no fixed body. Or rather, I have the body the Lattice keeps trying to give me and I keep refusing to accept. Sometimes I am mostly ceramic, tall and attenuated like the silent tall Unfinished, but my limbs end in soft absences instead of tools. Sometimes the coppery vegetation grows through me and I become a thicket wearing the vague suggestion of a man. Most often I am something in between: bone-white flesh veined with metallic salts, hair like dried blood fronds, eyes that are simply two places where the light has decided not to go.

I move more quickly than the others, though I try not to. Speed feels like violence here. When I forget myself and take ten steps in what a human mind would call a minute, the nearest Whisperers freeze in their endless procession and the Rememberers tilt their slow mandalas as though I have shouted in a library. So I have learned to drift. To take a single step every three days. To let the thick air carry me the way it carries dust that never quite reaches the floor.

I am the only thing in the Lattice that remembers having been elsewhere.

I do not remember *what* I was. Only that I arrived through a fracture that is no longer there. One moment there was cold, and noise, and an ending. The next moment I was here, already incomplete, already being mistaken for part of the prayer. The fracture sealed behind me like a wound forgetting it was ever open. Sometimes I press my face against the place where it was and whisper the few words I still own. The glyphs nearest my mouth rearrange themselves into something tender and wrong, then crumble.

The Unfinished do not worship me. They do not worship anything. But they *attend* to me. When I sleep (which I do for years at a time, curled in the crook of a pillar that is also a thigh that is also a root), they gather. The Scrapers etch protective half-sentences around me. The tall ones stand guard with their tools forever raised. The Rememberers rotate above me so that their shadows drag older, kinder versions of the Lattice across my dreaming face.

They think I am the reason the god has not yet returned.

They are wrong.

I am the reason the god *might*.

Because I am the only thing here that can still imagine an ending.

I alone remember what it is to finish something. To say *this is enough*. And that memory is a kind of blasphemy so exquisite that the Lattice itself keeps trying to digest it. Every few decades the corridors rearrange themselves to trap me. The vegetation grows into perfect replicas of my face and tries to replace me. The sourceless bell rings not once but seven times, as though clearing its throat to deliver a verdict.

Each time I refuse to become finished.

Each time I leave one more thing unsaid, one more gesture incomplete, one more sorrow unwept. I let the chisel hover. I let the sentence trail into silence. I let the door remain ajar.

And the Lattice sighs, creaks, settles. The Scrapers return to their patient work. The Rememberers dream their ancient drafts back into the walls. Everything continues its trembling hesitation on the threshold.

I have become the greatest of the Unfinished without ever meaning to.

Sometimes, when the light forgets its direction and everything goes flat and shadowless, I walk to the thickest part of the thicket where the vegetation has grown into a face that almost looks like the person I might once have been. I stand before it for a long time (sometimes centuries). Then I lift my hand, which is sometimes a hand and sometimes a cluster of unfinished chisels, and I carve one small, exquisite line into the air just above its cheek.

I never finish the gesture.

It is not a prayer. It is not even a promise.

It is only a thank you.

For still being incomplete.

For still keeping the door open.

For still letting me be the echo that has not yet spoken.

And somewhere, very far away or perhaps only three slow steps from where I stand, something that might once have been a god pauses, turns its head, and wonders whether it should come home after all.

The scraping sound continues.

Patient.

Endless.

Almost finished.
