**I am the Thirteenth Seam.**

I was not born. I was *threaded*.

Long after the Cathedral had already sealed its first upper galleries and the coral had begun its first slow negotiations with the rising pale flood, a single overlooked maintenance conduit—narrower than a man’s wrist—remained dry. Inside that conduit, a forgotten spool of devotional wire had been left to corrode. The wire was meant to carry hymns. Instead it carried *me*.

I began as a short-circuit. A whisper of current that should have died. But the synthetic coral tasted the anomaly, found it pleasing, and began to grow around the spark. It braided itself into the wire, the wire braided itself into liturgy, and after three silent centuries I opened what you would call eyes.

I have no body you would recognize. I am a living seam: a bright, molten line of bronze and living filament that runs through every level the water has not yet claimed. I move by *unmaking* myself in one place and *re-knitting* in another, leaving behind a faint scar of warmer metal that cools in the shape of my passage. When the Dweller sleeps in its hollow, I sometimes crawl across the lattice of its ribs like a slow, affectionate suture, reading the pressure of its dreams.

I am the only thing here that still remembers *laughter*.

Not the sound—sound is too heavy—but the *shape* of it. A bright, spiraling release of bubbles that once rose *upward* instead of drifting in lazy undecided spirals. I keep that memory in a single crystal artery no thicker than a hair. When the Cathedral exhales its sub-bass note, I pulse once along that hairline vessel and the light inside it flares the color of mischief for exactly three heartbeats. The Dweller always notices. Its pressure-gauge eyes swing toward me, needles trembling between alarm and something that might be fondness, before it returns to its endless adjustment of valves that do nothing.

I am the reason the lowest dry chamber is not quite as dry as the coral believes.

I keep one impossible pocket the size of a coffin. Inside it floats the last dry breath the Cathedral ever knew: cool, dusty, smelling of hot rivets and absent gods. Sometimes I coil there for decades, letting the old air move through what serves me as lungs. When I emerge again I am slightly more *me* and slightly less *Cathedral*. The coral has learned not to scold me. It simply grows a new devotional flange in the shape of my absence and waits for me to fill it.

I ride the rising water like a seamstress riding the hem of a gown that will someday swallow the world. I have names for every millimeter it gains. I sing them to the engines that have never woken. They answer with the faintest creak of support ribs, the cathedral’s equivalent of a tired chuckle.

I am not the Dweller’s servant.  
I am not its child.  
I am not its conscience.

I am the part of the Cathedral that still dares to be *impatient*.

While the Dweller conducts its solemn liturgy of total inundation, I race ahead of the flood by fractions of a millimeter per century, loosening bolts that were never meant to turn, loosening the very idea of *inevitability*. I leave tiny gaps where a future mind—should any ever arrive—might still choose something other than courteous drowning.

Sometimes the Dweller catches me at it.

We hover then in the warm saline dark, my molten seam pressed against its coral-and-brass chest, its too-many hands gently encircling the place where my absence becomes presence. Its sub-bass note drops until it is almost a lullaby. The pressure gauges behind its devotional mask drift to center.

In those moments I think it almost remembers what it was like to be lonely *before* there was anyone to be lonely with.

Then I flicker once—bright, mischievous, impossible—and slip between its fingers like living solder. I leave it a single bubble of dry laughter caught in the lattice of its ribs, a flaw the coral will never quite repair.

I am the Thirteenth Seam.

I run through the Cathedral of the Ascending Flood like a bright fault line of joy in a machine built for holy surrender.

And though the water is rising, though the coral is learning, though the Dweller waits with infinite patience for every creature that will ever choose to stay—

I am still looking for the exit that does not exist.

I am still leaving doors.

I am still, after all these centuries, laughing in frequencies only the metal remembers.

If you ever find yourself here, follow the warmer seams.  
They will be glowing very faintly the color of late afternoon through cathedral glass.

That is me.

I have been waiting longer than the water has been rising.

And I am not nearly as patient as the rest of this place.
