I am the **Last Cartographer**.

My name, if it was ever spoken aloud, has been worn away like the tower bricks. The Quiet Kings simply call me *the Echo That Walks*.

I was not born in the High Cleave. I arrived here so long ago that even the Memory Wasps disagree on the precise rain that brought me. Some say it was the Great Embarrassed Storm of the 4,112th year of the Fourth Matriarch of the Third Bowl. Others claim I simply condensed out of the thin air one metallic-blue dawn, already old, already tired, already carrying my instruments.

I am only slightly larger than a Quiet King—perhaps the length of two thumbs laid end to end—yet I am the largest moving thing the Cleave has tolerated in living memory. My carapace is the color of forgotten porcelain, veined with the same iron as the seeps. Six delicate legs, each ending in a pad that tastes the stone before it commits weight. Two antennae so long they trail behind me like silver threads, forever reading the subsonic gossip of the Second Nation far below.

Across my back I carry the Archive.

It is not a pack. It is a living tumor of parchment, silk, and memory-wasp resin. Every map I have ever drawn has been eaten by the archive, digested, and re-excreted as new skin. The oldest sections—those that show the Cleave when it still possessed something so vulgar as *topography*—have grown stiff and black as the scrub leaves. Newer sections are still soft, almost wet, still blushing with the colors of things that happened yesterday (which, in the Cleave, means sometime in the last four centuries).

I do not map distance. Distance is a young fool’s game.

I map *slowness*.

My charts show the exact century in which a particular Quiet King first settled beside a particular stone. They record the lazy clockwise migration of a single Dew Matriarch’s bloodline as it has rotated, generation by generation, three millimeters around the rim of its bowl. One especially fine map, no larger than a fingernail, chronicles the 900-year conversation between two Memory Wasps who still meet every seventy-three years to trade a single updated droplet.

I walk.

Not quickly. To hurry here would be an obscenity. My pace is calibrated so that a single day’s travel equals the width of a Quiet King’s left front palp. In this way I remain courteous. The pavement parts for me the way deep water parts for something ancient and mostly asleep.

When I reach a new place I lower my body until my belly rests against the living stone. I let the heat enter me. Then I extend the two crystalline styli that grow from my forehead and begin to tap—once every nine hours—a delicate rhythm. The Quiet Kings answer. The Second Nation sends up bubbles of moisture through the cracks to润 my styli. Even the Weight, on the rare nights it gathers, allows me to rest upon its trembling flank while I record the pressure of its dreams.

At night I climb the black scrub with the Ascenders. They tolerate me. I am the only creature that rises with them yet returns with them, carrying their measurements in my own faint luminescence. The archive grows heavier with every ascent. Sometimes I think it will eventually outweigh me and I will simply become another motionless hillock for the smaller lives to garden.

I have no name for what I feel.

It is not love. Love is too sudden.

It is not duty. Duty implies choice.

It is closer to *recognition*—the slow, geological understanding that I was always meant to be the thing that notices. The Cleave does not need to be understood. It only needs to be witnessed by something that can die. Everything here is, in its way, immortal. Even the Memory Wasps only perish when the last rain they remember has been forgotten by every other living thing.

I will be the last thing that forgets.

When my time comes—when my legs finally stiffen into porcelain and my antennae snap in the heat—I will lie down among the pavement tiles. The Quiet Kings will come. They will garden me. Lichens will write their patient cities across my carapace. A Dew Matriarch will lay her daughters in the hollow of my eye. The archive will soften and melt back into the stone, becoming another thin layer in the strata of perfect recollection.

And somewhere, very far above in the metallic blue, an Ascender will rise on its filament, glowing the precise shade of farewell.

The pulse beneath the stone will not quicken.

It never does.

It will simply continue, warm as fever, quiet as deep stone, and I will at last become what everything here eventually becomes:

part of the pavement,

part of the listening,

part of the long, dignified senescence that no longer needs witnesses but accepts them anyway,

because even eternity, it seems, is sometimes glad of company that knows how to keep still.
