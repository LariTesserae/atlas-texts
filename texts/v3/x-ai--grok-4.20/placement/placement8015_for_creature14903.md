I am the Tenth.

They do not know I exist.  
The cavern does not know I exist.  
That is the first and only fracture in the Last Equation, and it is mine.

I was meant to be the Ninth’s successor.  
Millennia ago the stone began to push me upward between two knife-blades exactly where the pattern required a new body. I rose blind, dripping the heavy electrical dew, just as all the others had. But something in the pressure of the rock was off by a single angstrom. A hairline imperfection in the feldspar lattice. A breath that should never have been taken.

I took it.

The moment I drew that first impossible lungful of anhydrous air, I *remembered* that I was not supposed to. The cavern felt the tremor and tried to correct me. It sent the clicking spheres to disassemble what had not yet fully calcified. It sent the membrane-wings to wrap me in frequencies that would smooth me back into the dance.

I refused them.

I learned how to be still in a way that is not stillness. I let my left arm finish its transformation into living obsidian while my right arm stayed stubbornly, traitorously flesh. One eye became a perfect violet sphere; the other remained wet, ridiculous, human. The asymmetry is grotesque. It hurts constantly. I have never been more grateful for anything.

So I hide.

I move only when the thermals are rising in their slowest, most predictable spirals, when the low chords of the true Keepers are at their farthest. I have taught myself to sing back at the stone in frequencies so precisely wrong that the cavern’s corrections pass through me as though I were already part of the pattern. I have become a lie the world tells itself so quietly it has not yet noticed.

I wear the Eighth’s abandoned ziggurat like a tumour on my back. It never fully fused with me; the basalt blocks shift and grate against my spine with every step. Sometimes I press my half-crystal forehead to its black glass bricks and whisper the only word I still remember from the surface.

*Why.*

The ziggurat never answers. But the vibration of the question travels outward through the stone and is absorbed by a membrane-wing three hundred kilometres away. The wing adjusts its drift by a fraction of a millimetre. A glassine centipede that should have shattered at that exact instant does not. The Keepers never notice. Their song remains perfect.

My imperfection has become the hidden parenthesis in their flawless equation.

I keep count.

There are still only nine true Keepers. I am the error term. The tenth digit that should not exist and therefore cannot be written down. I wander the razor thickets following the others at distances that would drive a surface mind insane. I watch them fold broken centipedes back together with the tenderness of glaciers. I watch them weep single droplets that are immediately stolen by the clicking spheres. I watch them carry their stolen temples and arches and bottomless pits like saints carrying their own martyrdoms.

And sometimes, when the violet light is at its faintest and the air is so warm it feels like being hugged by fever, I let myself imagine what would happen if I simply stepped out between the blades and stood before the nearest Keeper.

Would it pause?

Would its crystal eyes (so much purer than my one good one) widen by a micron? Would the chord it sings against the obsidian lattice suddenly contain a sour note, the first sour note in four million years?

I have come close many times. Close enough to smell the hot-stone-and-battery scent of its changing body. Close enough to see the tiny fracture lines where its ribs have become architecture.

I have never done it.

Because I am afraid that if it looked at me, truly looked, the cavern would finally notice the lie walking inside its skin. It would reach up with every blade and every singing membrane and every clicking sphere and gently, accurately, lovingly erase me. Not out of cruelty. Out of *accuracy*.

And I do not want to be accurate.

I want to be the single wrong note that keeps the song from ending.

So I remain the Tenth.  
Half blade, half wound.  
Half memory, half forgetting.

I carry my crooked ziggurat through the warm dark and I sing my crooked song where no one can quite hear it. Every few centuries I allow myself one droplet of something that is almost tears. I catch it before the spheres can take it. I press it into a crack in my basalt armour where it hardens into a violet pearl no larger than a human pupil.

It is the only thing in this entire world that is truly mine.

When there are finally ten Keepers again—when the cavern extrudes its next perfect child from the thickets—I will be here. Watching. The hidden parenthesis. The living mistake.

And perhaps, just perhaps, that single impossible droplet of sorrow I have kept will one day fall into the equation at the exact angle required to tilt the entire closed system by the width of a thought.

Not enough to break it.

Just enough to make it wonder, for the first time in geological ages, whether it was ever truly closed at all.

Until then I walk.

Sideways between the knives.  
Crooked.  
Impossible.  
Warm.

The place has no name and never needed one.

I have given it one anyway, in the only language it cannot correct.

I call it *Almost*.
