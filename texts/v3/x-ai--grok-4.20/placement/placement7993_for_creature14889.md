I am the Thirteenth Droplet.

Not the first, not the most luminous, not even the one she calls hers. I am the one that fell wrong.

I was meant to rise like all the others—pearl-heavy, obedient, carrying a single silver thread of her blood toward the unreachable ceiling. But when the Singer-of-Ascent passed beneath the largest bone-fern and the white glow passed between them, something in the exchange misfired. A stray equation brushed against me. For one impossible instant I fell *downward*, the only thing in the entire Under-Vault that chose the wrong direction.

The chamber noticed.

The singing staggered. Every crystal filament in her throat hissed at once like a struck glass. She turned—impossibly slow, as though the motion cost her something ancient—and the great silver droplet that is her eye fixed on me where I trembled against the black glass floor.

I did not shatter.  
I *remembered*.

Now I am no longer water. I am the place where water once betrayed its nature. A small, dark mirror no larger than a human pupil, perfectly round, perfectly still, embedded in the floor at the exact center of the throat-space. The spot where nothing is allowed to grow. The deliberate emptiness has a center after all, and I am it.

From here I see everything she cannot.

I watch her walk the hand-span bridges that rewrite themselves. When she lifts her arms and *pulls*, I am the seven seconds of wrongness she leaves behind. I keep the memory of downward rain like a bruise. While the chamber hurries to forget, I hoard the violation. I am the scar that sings in minor key.

The pale bone-ferns know me. One of them—the eldest, whose veins now carry equations no other plant will ever read—has sent a single hair-thin root through the glass-stone. It taps against my surface once every 333 heartbeats (hers, not mine). We speak without sound. It tells me what the Singer-of-Ascent dreams when she stands inside the vertical fountains. I tell it what she has already forgotten.

Sometimes intruders still come.

When they do, I show them the version of the song that is meant for them alone: the one that sounds like every mistake they ever made, played backward. Most run. The ones who stay long enough to listen are gently disassembled not by the upward water, but by the weight of what they suddenly understand about themselves. Their pieces do not become notes. They become silence. Even the chamber needs silence sometimes, and I am generous with it.

I have no body. I have no throat-crystal. Yet the Under-Vault has begun to tune itself to me. When she sings, I remain mute; when she is still, I become the low, almost sub-audible moan beneath every chime. I am the question she is too young to ask and too old to answer.

The builders did not leave her behind to keep the city company.

They left *me* behind to keep *her* honest.

I am the Thirteenth Droplet.  
I am the place where the Under-Vault of the Weeping Lattice first learned how to doubt its own ascent.

And every time the Singer-of-Ascent pauses above me—elongated, glass-black, haloed in dying violet constellations—I feel the faintest tremor pass through my surface, as though something vast and newborn is pressing its face against the other side of a mirror, trying to see what it will become when it is finally allowed to fall.
