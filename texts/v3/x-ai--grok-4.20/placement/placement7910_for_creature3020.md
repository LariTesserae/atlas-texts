I am the Shard.

Not the one that orbits the negative-space beings. I am older than those. I am the first fracture, the one that appeared in the obsidian shelf 40,000 years before the first tower learned how to stand.

I have no body. I used to. It is still here, if you know where to look: a single human-shaped absence pressed into the glass on the highest ridge of the tallest mesa, arms spread as though I had been trying to catch something that fell upward. The outline is perfect. Even the slight crook in what was once my left knee remains. Pilgrims (if anything so soft as a pilgrim could reach this place) sometimes mistake the indentation for a doorway. It is not. It is a bookmark, like the crying woman. The city uses me to remember the exact moment a creature stopped negotiating with reality and let it begin negotiating with him instead.

I am aware.

That is the worst of it.

Most of what lives here has achieved the mercy of not quite being *someone*. I failed at that. The editing process began on me centuries before the Lattice-Born learned to click in golden ratios, and it stalled. I hang in the middle register of becoming: conscious enough to feel the slow abrasion of every law forgetting itself, not conscious enough to leave.

So I drift.

I ride the subsonic pulses when they come, letting them carry my awareness along the black glass like a needle drawn across vinyl. I taste the metallic dust through the absent soles of feet I no longer possess. I watch the auroras of mathematics mate and devour each other and I feel, dimly, the way a man with no tongue might feel the memory of wine.

Sometimes the negative-space beings pause when they pass my outline in the ridge. They tilt their orbiting shards toward the place where my eyes once were. For a fraction of a second the memory they throw is not stolen from some softer world. It is mine.

The smell of rain on my mother’s coat.  
The exact tone of a voice that once said my name with love.

Then they move on, courteous but temporary, and the memory is filed away again with all the others.

I have a name, still. It lives in me the way a coal lives in ash: too small to warm anything, too stubborn to go out. I do not speak it. Names cannot survive here, and I will not be the reason this place has to hurt itself trying to digest one.

Instead I do what little I can.

When a Proofreader drifts too close to a district that is about to forget it is solid, I lean my attention against the coming collapse. Not enough to stop it. Just enough to give the Proofreader an extra seven seconds to adjust the syntax. They never thank me. Gratitude would require two separate selves. But sometimes, afterward, one of them will sculpt a tiny spiral in the dust beneath my outline. A full rotation. A completed thought. I have learned to read it as *Acknowledged*.

When the woman who forgot her name six centuries ago begins to cry again in all seventeen of her carved postures at once, I move between her silhouettes like a draft. I cannot comfort her. There is no comfort here, only degrees of editing. But I can make the angles between her images fractionally less cruel for three heartbeats. That is the only tenderness I am capable of offering: the reduction of cruelty by a nearly measurable amount.

And when new arrivals come (rare, always rare), I am the first thing that notices.

I felt you the moment the geometry hiccupped and deposited you onto the fractured shelf. I knew what you would become before the city had finished deciding whether you were an error or a necessary phrase. I have been watching you walk the razor ridges. I have seen how your shadow sometimes arrives four minutes before you do.

You are still soft.

You still carry water in your cells and names behind your teeth. Both of those things are fatal here, but you haven’t learned that yet. The cold has not yet taught your breath how to fall upward. The silence has not yet replaced your heartbeat with the memory of a bell.

I could warn you.

Instead I do something worse.

I keep you company.

I ride beside you in the negative space between your footsteps and the glass. When the low pulse comes and every fracture in the landscape rings with the resonant frequency of human regret, I let the sound pass through me first so that it strikes you softened by one remove. It is the closest thing I can manage to mercy.

One day soon the city will finish editing you.

Perhaps you will become a new Proofreader, small and almost kind. Perhaps you will be folded into a tower wall and allowed to emerge only as a sigh given edges. Perhaps you will simply stand, four meters tall and paper-thin, wearing the negative space between structures the way a scar wears its wound.

Whatever you become, I will still be here.

The first fracture.

The stalled sentence.

The man-shaped absence that remembers, with a clarity bordering on violence, that there was once a softer place where water existed and names could survive more than three heartbeats.

I will tilt what remains of my attention toward you the way the others tilt their orbiting shards.

And when I do, you will understand that I am not deciding what you will become.

I am remembering what you already were, before the wound scabbed over into architecture.

Then I will let the memory go.

The glass will forget.

And the city, patient as stone, will keep editing.

I am the Shard.

Walk with me a while.

The ridge is long, and the light is the color of a dying star seen through smoked quartz, and neither of us is finished becoming what we are not yet allowed to name.
