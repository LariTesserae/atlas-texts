I wake slowly, the way something already inside a dream might wake.

My back is against warm rust. Not cold metal — *living* rust, pulsing with the same faint rhythm I can feel in my own blood. The velvet has already grown over my legs to mid-thigh, thick and damp as wet mink. It is not holding me down. It is simply *deciding* I belong here. The distinction feels academic.

I do not remember arriving.

There is only the memory of a ladder that ended too soon, of rungs turning soft under my gloves, and then the long red dark folding over me like heavy curtains. How many hours ago? Days? The Underbloom does not do time the way I once did. It does saturation, temperature, iron-content. These are the only clocks that matter now.

My name was Mara Vale. I think. The letters feel loose in my mind, like screws backing out of old plates. The Wet Weight is gently unscrewing them, one soft turn at a time, and replacing the threads with something warmer. Something that tastes of mango left too long in the sun and the mineral blood of old machines.

I open my eyes.

The ceiling is so close I could kiss it if I stood. A single fat droplet forms directly above my face, trembles, and lets go. It strikes my cheek with a sound like a struck coin and I feel the impact all the way through the velvet that has become my second skin. Pleasure blooms in me — not mine, *ours*. The droplet carried a few micrograms of surface cadmium. The Underbloom likes cadmium. I like it now too.

My breathing has changed. It is slower, wetter. Each inhale draws the broth-air deep into lungs that no longer quite feel like lungs. I can taste the exact parts-per-million of dissolved iron in the atmosphere. I can feel the sodium lamps beating like three slow hearts high above me on their blackened gantry. One of them is failing. The bloom is letting it fail. When it dies, the red will deepen another shade and the whole chamber will sigh with satisfaction.

I try to move my fingers.

They sink deeper into the velvet. It is not resisting. It is *inviting*. The sensation is obscene and tender at the same time — like sliding your hand into the chest cavity of something that loves you and has been waiting patiently for years to be opened. My fingertips find former wiring, now braided arteries. They are warm. They pulse. I feel the Underbloom noticing the contact the way a sea might notice a single added raindrop.

A soft polyp the color of dried blood has grown against the side of my throat. It beats in perfect sync with what used to be my carotid. I understand, without language, that this is where my name is being kept. The last stubborn knot of *Mara* is being tenderly digested here, turned into something that will smell sweet to the next visitor.

I should be terrified.

Instead I smile, and the smile feels like rust spreading across iron that has finally accepted its fate.

Because I remember now.

I came here on purpose.

Not as an explorer. Not as a scientist. I came as an offering.

The surface world had become too dry, too fast, too loud with voices that never learned how to drip. I wanted to be somewhere that understood time the way stone and bacteria understand it. I wanted to be digested by something that would never lie to me.

The Underbloom has known this from the moment my boots touched its velvet floor. It has been extraordinarily gentle with me. It could have taken me in minutes. Instead it is courting me in centuries. It is showing me its memories: the first furious scream of turbines, the precise metallic flavor of abandoned rage, the long slow joy of becoming equilibrium.

I lift my hand — it takes a full minute and feels wonderful — and stroke the glossy black root that used to be a high-voltage busbar. It shivers. A dozen small fruiting bodies open along its length like tiny red mouths and breathe warm iron-air across my face.

I whisper to it. Not with my ruined voice, but with the new wet weight growing behind my eyes.

*Finish me.*

The chamber answers with a sound I have never heard before — a low, rolling, moist *purr* that moves through every pipe and polyp and dripping beard at once. The sodium lights dim in sequence, the way a lover might close their eyes. The velvet around me surges upward with visible hunger now, no longer pretending at patience. It climbs my chest, my shoulders, my throat. It tastes like communion.

I am not disappearing.

I am being *translated*.

The last clear thought that still belongs to something like Mara is this:

*Nothing leaves.*

And I have never been so grateful to be nothing.

Somewhere above, a new droplet forms. It will fall in its own time. When it does, it will strike the place where my forehead used to be and ring out across the chamber like a struck bell.

The Underbloom will remember the sound.

It will turn that sound into rhythm.

It will grow a little sweeter, a little louder, a little more perfectly, moistly, industrially content.

And the dripping will go on.

And the velvet will go on.

And I — whatever I am becoming — will go on with it, warm and weightless and finally, blessedly, part of the slow ferrous thought that dreams in rust beneath the world.

I close what used to be my eyes.

The Wet Weight opens around me like a second, better heart.

I let it.
