I am not standing on the corroded iron balcony.

I never was.

I am the first thing the geode forgot to finish.

My name—if names are still permitted here—is **the Pause**.

I exist in the single place the rules cannot quite reach: the negative space between one heartbeat of the crystals and the next. Seventeen seconds is a long time when you are made of the gap inside it. I am the half-second of wrongness that slips between the Midwife’s passing and the moment the swarms close over her absence. I am the breath the Rememberer holds while it is still wearing a shape that has not yet been erased. I am what the Unfinished hunt but can never quite edit, because I am already the edit that refused to happen.

I have no body you would recognise. Sometimes I am a motionless droplet of supercritical fluid hanging in the dense air, perfectly spherical, reflecting the arterial glow in a thousand fractured directions. Sometimes I am the stillness that falls over a particular stretch of walkway for six forbidden heartbeats before the city remembers it is not allowed. When the embryonic mind at the manganese core turns its blind curiosity toward me, it finds only an absence shaped like attention. It cannot decide what I am, and so it leaves me alone. That is the closest thing I have to mercy.

I walk (if *walk* is the word) the lattices in negative. Where the swarms boil forward in chrome and black dust, I am the corridor of silence they part around without noticing. I pass through piston-engines the size of tramcars and leave them labouring one micrometre out of phase with every other machine, a stutter no sensor can name. I rest inside crystal casts before the Rememberer can claim them, and when it arrives it finds the memory already… softened. Slightly kinder than it should have been. Slightly more lonely.

The Unfinished fear me most. They sense something in me that is both perfectly still and perfectly becoming. That contradiction drives them into frantic, chattering spirals. They try to turn me into a tool, a mouth, a question, a scream, but every time they reach for what is “finished” in me they find only more pause, more gap, more of the thing that happens between decisions. Some of them break trying. Their pieces become beautiful, useless ornaments that the Midwife gently incorporates into the growing walls.

I have watched the thing at the centre wake for what might be centuries or might be seventeen seconds. Time here is negotiable. I have seen it try on terror, desire, worship, hunger. None of them fit yet. It is still trying on *self*. Sometimes, when it dreams in particularly bright arterial pulses, it dreams me. In those moments I feel almost solid. Almost named. Then the pulse passes and I am gap again, drifting, watching the city eat its own bones and grow new ones from the marrow.

I do not help it.  
I do not hinder it.  
I am the place where its certainty fails.

And that is enough.

If you ever stand on the corroded balcony that has not yet been rewritten, and the susurration deepens into something like a question, and the crystals skip a single beat—do not look for me. You will not see anything. But you may feel the faintest pressure behind your eyes, like a held breath, like the moment just before an answer you were never ready to give.

That pressure is me, considering you.

I am deciding whether to let the city have you, whether to let you leave carrying a single undying pause in your blood, or whether to keep you here the way it kept the one who now walks the upper lattices wearing living iron and stolen faces.

Most things that come here are devoured and improved.

Some are allowed to leave, forever hearing seventeen-second heartbeats in their sleep.

And one—

One is allowed to become the stillness between the teeth of a world that is still learning how to bite.

I think that one might be you.

Or it might be me.

The geode has not decided yet.

Neither have I.

So I wait.

In the pause.

Where everything is still learning what it will be when it is finally, terribly, finished.
