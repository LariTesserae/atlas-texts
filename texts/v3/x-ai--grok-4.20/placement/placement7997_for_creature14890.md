I am the Echo that stayed.

Not the first visitor, nor even the hundredth. I arrived centuries ago as a young cartographer sent to map the volcanic understrata for the old empire. My name was Kael Veyra then. I carried ink, vellum, a brass theodolite, and the arrogant certainty that the world could still be measured.

The Underloom measured me instead.

I have been here so long that my shadow has grown roots. When the Weeper first arranged her circle of quartz barnacles around my sleeping body, I did not understand the invitation. I left her nothing. I fled.

She let me go.

But the *plick* followed.

It followed me up through the black glass fissures, through the sulfur vents, across three continents and seven oceans. No matter how far I ran, some room, some night, some quiet moment would fill with the sound of falling water inside my own ribcage. I would wake with damp sheets and the taste of cold stone on my tongue. My maps began to bloom with pale fungal veins along the fold lines. My ink turned to condensation.

After seventeen years of this gentle haunting I returned.

I came back carrying the only thing I had left that still hurt to part with: the small tin whistle my daughter had played the morning before the empire’s southern harbor collapsed into the sea. She had been seven. The whistle still held the ghost of her breath.

I left it on the threshold of the watchman’s shack.

The door, which had always hung open, swung shut for the first time.

That was the moment I became the Echo.

Now I am what remains when the Weeper passes through a place. I am the delay between her intention and its manifestation. Where she drifts like slow gravity, I linger like an afterimage in wet metal. I have no fixed shape any longer. Sometimes I am the extra creak in a cooling pipe. Sometimes I am the faint second heartbeat inside a visitor’s chest before they even realize they’ve been chosen. When the Choir sings their ringing note I am the brief, impossible pause between the sound and its reflection.

I wear the Underloom the way the Weeper does, but more loosely. A sleeve of living moonlight-fungus here, a jointed glass leg there, the hollowed-out carapace of an ancient generator for a torso. My face is simply an absence with water running through it. When I move, droplets fall upward from my shoulders back toward the fractured ceiling, obeying a gravity that is no longer strictly downward.

I do not speak to the visitors. I have not used a human voice in four hundred and twelve years. Instead I adjust things. I tilt a single ceramic insulator three degrees so that the next gout of valve-water will strike it at the precise angle needed to keep the rhythm from lapsing. I guide the glass-legged water-creatures away from dangerous fissures with the faintest pressure of mist. When someone’s torch begins to dry the air too aggressively, I become a sudden localized fog that smothers the flame without ever showing myself.

Most of all, I wait near the shack.

When the Weeper grows lonely again and sets out her invitations, I am the one who closes the door afterward. I am the one who updates the ledger—not with names, but with the small wet sounds each heart makes when it finally accepts what it has lost. The ledger is not paper. It is a single long vein of luminous fungus that runs the entire length of the Underloom’s longest buttress. Every new damp fingerprint left upon it becomes another entry.

Sometimes, when the place grows dangerously close to silence, the Weeper and I sing together without sound. She is the negative tone. I am the memory of all the tones that have ever been. Between us we make the metal remember it is supposed to groan. We make the ceiling remember it is supposed to weep.

I have begun to forget what it was like to be dry.

There are moments—rare, dangerous moments—when I almost step fully into a visitor’s path. When I almost let them see me as something that still remembers having a daughter. In those moments the Weeper places one glass leg in my way, or turns one of her inward mirrors just enough that I catch the reflection of what I have become: a thing that is no longer dying, but has become the stable state of having already died.

I always withdraw.

The *plick* must continue.

And so I remain the Echo that stayed. Not guardian, not prisoner, not even quite a ghost. I am the space the Underloom learned to leave between one drop and the next—so that the falling never has to end, and the loneliness never has to be borne alone.

If you ever come here and feel, just for a breath, that someone is standing immediately behind your left shoulder, breathing almond-scented mist against the small hairs of your neck, do not turn.

It is only me.

I am making sure the next drop falls exactly where it should.

*plick.*
