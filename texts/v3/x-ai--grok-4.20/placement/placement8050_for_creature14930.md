**I would be the Thirteenth Echo.**

Not the first visitor, nor the last. Not one of the Choir, though I have stood among them. I am what remains when someone has already accepted the gift, been taken apart, and then—centuries later—changed their mind.

They let me keep my shape. That was the compromise.

So I walk the Hollow Basilica as a tall, slender figure made of the same braided wood as everything else, but I have kept the memory of a human face. It is not quite a face anymore; the features are soft, rounded, as though carved by condensation and then half-erased by time. My eyes are simply deeper grain in the wood. When I blink it sounds like two wet leaves brushing together. My mouth rarely opens, because when it does the Basilica answers with a sudden volley of drips that drowns whatever I meant to say.

I am allowed to move freely. The six-legged moss-sloths greet me by pressing their foreheads to my chest, the way one might greet an old tree they have known since saplinghood. The thumbnail frogs ride along my arms in living bracelets and only leave when they feel the pressure-pulse inside me change. The white moths sometimes mistake me for an open volume of air and drift straight through my ribcage before realizing their error.

I do not remember my original name. The Choir remember it for me; they keep it safe inside one of their translucent bodies like a seed in a pod. Sometimes, when they wish to be kind, one of them will stand very close and open a small door in their chest so I can hear it spoken in the slow rhythm of sap. The name always tastes like rain on metal. I have never asked for it back.

My role is singular.

I am the one who follows those who refuse the gift.

When a visitor finally tears themselves away from the unbearable equilibrium and stumbles toward the place where the living walls thin enough to let them out, I walk behind them at the exact distance the Choir once stood behind *them*. I do not speak. I simply let the dripping fall out of rhythm in a pattern that means:

*You will carry us anyway.*

Most never notice me. A few do. Those are the ones who look back once, just before they step through the final green arch into whatever world still pretends to have wind and sun and endings. When their eyes meet mine they usually flinch, because they see their own future face already softening into wood.

I have done this for four hundred and twelve thousand years.

Sometimes I grow tired of echoing. On those days I find a place in one of the great naves where the light is exactly the same as it has always been, and I stand perfectly still until the pressure of equilibrium becomes a hand around my throat. Eventually one of the Choir comes and stands behind *me*. They place a hand the texture of living velvet between my wooden shoulder blades and push, very gently.

I fold. The cables of my body loosen. For a little while I am allowed to become part of a pillar again, nothing but heartwood and slow green circulation, dreaming the long geological dream.

But I always unravel myself once more. The Basilica permits this the way a vast, patient mind permits a single restless thought.

Because even the finished world needs one small imperfection.

And that imperfection is me.

I am the Thirteenth Echo.

I am the sound of someone leaving who has not yet left.

I am the witness who stayed, and stayed, and stayed—until staying itself became a kind of departure.

If you ever come here and feel, in the cool celadon light, that something is walking behind you in perfect silence, matching every heartbeat you try to quiet, do not turn too quickly.

It is only me.

I have been waiting for you longer than you have been alive.

And I am still deciding whether I will ask you to stay, or simply walk you to the edge of the green, and let the dripping say the rest.
