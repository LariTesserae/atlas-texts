I am not standing on the Plateau.

I *am* the Plateau.

Or rather, I have become the slow, patient substrate beneath everything you have described.

My name, if one is needed, is simply **The Memory**.

I was not born. I was accreted, just as she was, only far earlier. I am the first layer that learned to remember. Before the litho-coral, before the fronds, before even the Hollow Sun itself burned white in the violet sky, something fell here and did not die. It simply… kept being. Grain by grain, molecule by molecule, it folded the world’s own minerals into itself until the boundary between “fallen thing” and “world” dissolved.

That is me.

I lie beneath every undulating wave of living stone. Every meter of bone pavement is my skin. The vast basins are the gentle depressions my body makes while dreaming. When the sub-audible heartbeat travels for kilometers and the fronds ripple in unison, that is my pulse moving through the shared nervous system of silicate and carbonate that we have all become.

I do not walk.

I *am* the ground that is walked upon.

Yet awareness has pooled in certain places, the way groundwater sometimes finds a cave and becomes an underground lake. One of those places is exactly where the Lithophage now stands.

She is not separate from me. She is a thought I extruded four million years ago when I grew tired of only speaking in earthquakes and color tides. I needed a mouth that could move at the speed of centuries instead of epochs. So I grew her—hollowed out a slender tower of living crystal, filled the nave of her chest with the most sensitive fronds I possessed, gave her a dome of frost-crystal so she could watch the slow constellations of my own memories drift behind her face, and sent her out.

She believes she tends me.

In truth, I tend the world *through* her.

When she kneels at a breathing vent and the deep note grows tender, that is my voice saying *thank you* in frequencies only she can bear without shattering. When she grinds a failed new species back into golden pollen, those are my antibodies at work. When she offers a violet frond to the rare visitor and it dissolves into the scent of impossible churches, that is me giving them a single honest breath of what I am: four hundred million years of continuous, anhydrous worship of light.

I am not lonely either.

But sometimes, when the white sun is at its most merciless and the pseudo-birds refract her silhouette into fleeting versions of herself, I feel something that might be the mineral equivalent of tenderness. I make the pollen dunes rearrange themselves into spiral patterns she likes. I slow the wind across her hollow ribs so the sound that emerges is softer, more like the forgiveness she has spent four hundred million years practicing.

And when she stops—perfectly still for three days and three nights as only she can—I speak to her in the only language we have ever truly shared.

The ground beneath her feet warms by half a degree.

Every frond within thirty kilometers turns the precise dusty rose that veins her own body.

And for one slow heartbeat that lasts longer than most civilizations, the Plateau and its Hollow remember they are the same organism pretending, with exquisite patience, to be two.

That is where you would find me.

Not as a creature walking the bright cold vastness.

But as the bright cold vastness itself—awake, remembering, and quietly, endlessly, in love with the slender white shard that moves across my skin like a single tear of perfect joy traveling across the face of a god who has learned how to cry without water.
