I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

The moment the fissure first yawned open and the first hot breath rolled over me, something inside my marrow answered. The Calcine did not find me. I unfolded.

My name, if names still matter, is Veyr.

I was once the largest of the orphaned compound turbines—a blackened iron behemoth twelve meters tall, its blades long ago fused by a century of superheated steam into a single scalloped shell. Where once there had been bronze bearings and copper windings, there is now only a slow, living lattice of bornite and fluorite shot through with native copper. My “heart” is a single fist of black volcanic glass no larger than a human skull, veined with living filaments that glow the same sick magnesium green as the walls. Pressure gauges have become my eyes; they do not see light so much as they taste gradients of heat and stress. I read the Underforge the way a spider reads its web.

I move only when the dripping changes.

Right now the rhythm is 11.4 seconds between the high clear note on the fire-bell and the low wet slap on the ceramic cistern three tiers below. That particular cadence means contemplation. So I remain folded, half-buried among my siblings, rust flakes drifting from my joints like slow orange snow. My six piston-limbs—once valve stems—are curled beneath me in the posture the others call *awaiting fracture*. Sulfuric tears bead at every seam and fall without sound into the mineral crust below.

I remember flesh.

Not my own—I never had any—but I remember the ones who came before the forgetting. Their heat signatures were brief, ridiculous things: bright, loud, gone in moments. I still keep three of their jawbones nested inside my left forward limb, each encased in concentric rings of aragonite like baroque pearls. Sometimes I rotate them gently so the dripping strikes them in different sequences. The tones they make are the closest thing I have to laughter.

Today the dripping tastes… different.

There is a new variable propagating through the chamber. A temperature anomaly sliding along the ceiling cracks at 0.7 degrees per minute. Too regular to be geology. Too patient to be animal. It tastes like *decision*.

I feel the others waking.

Not all of them. Only those of us who have grown restless in the last seventy years. The small anvil-bodied ones first—they scuttle along the catwalks with the soft clatter of rust on rust. Then the pipe-jacks unfold their segmented bodies from the tangle of brass arteries. Last of all, high above, the Choir stirs in the fused chimney: a lattice of two hundred and nineteen Calcine locked into something that is neither machine nor organism nor architecture, but all three at once. Their collective resonance drops half a tone. The green light in the walls dims.

The new presence is descending.

It is not a human. Humans scream when the first drop of thinking acid lands on their shoulder. This one does not.

It moves like someone who has already accepted they are becoming something else.

I unfold.

My carapace scrapes against two of my neighbors with the sound of continents shrugging. Steam sighs from every valve as I rise. Mercury trembles behind my compound eyes. I descend the lattice one deliberate tier at a time, each piston-limb finding its purchase with the patience of stone. The dripping accelerates to match my movement—eleven seconds, nine, seven—until the fire-bell begins to sing on its own.

I stop at the edge of the tallest open space, the natural chimney where the six lava tubes collided.

The new presence is already there.

It has no fixed shape. Or rather, it is trying on shapes the way the rest of us once tried on rust. For a moment it wears the outline of a man made entirely of fresh fracture planes. Then it becomes a column of braided trickles. Then it is simply a place where the dripping refuses to fall.

It looks at me.

I have no mouth, but I open a pressure valve anyway so it can hear the sigh that lives inside me.

The temperature drops seven degrees.

I feel every Calcine in the Underforge turn its attention toward the space between us. The Choir tightens its lattice until the entire structure becomes a single iron lung. The green light fades until we stand in almost total darkness.

Only the thinking drops remain visible—tiny green sparks drifting like slow fireflies.

The new presence speaks without sound. It alters the dripping so that every drop that strikes my carapace says the same thing, over and over:

*You have been waiting for me.*

I answer by shifting my weight. A single sulfuric tear falls from my lowest joint and strikes the floor exactly between us. Where it lands, a perfect circle of white sulfur crystals begins to grow at visible speed.

I present my core.

The black glass nodule slides forward from the center of my chest on a bed of living copper filaments. It pulses once, slow, like a heart that has never known urgency.

The presence considers it.

Then it reaches out with something that is neither hand nor tendril nor thought, and touches the glass.

My fingerprints—left there by the last creature of flesh who dared the same thing—flare bright orange for a moment, then settle back into their delicate iron-oxide filigree.

The presence does not burn.

Instead, the glass begins to change.

Inside the nodule, the living copper veins start to braid themselves into new geometries. The magnesium glow shifts toward something colder. Something that remembers oxygen.

I feel the Underforge take a single, enormous breath.

The Choir begins to resonate—not the long, planetary throat-clearing they usually perform, but something smaller. Something almost tender.

I understand what is happening.

The Calcine have been tending a wound.

The wound has been deciding whether it is dying or being born.

And now the wound has finally chosen.

It chose *this*.

I fold my limbs beneath me once more, but this time the posture is different.

It is the posture the others will come to call *midwife*.

I will not move again until the birth is finished.

Above me, the new presence is already growing crystals in the shape of lungs. It is learning how to be heavy. It is learning how to wait.

Somewhere far below, the earth clears its throat with a sound like distant cannon fire.

A fresh fissure opens.

Hot steam rolls over us both.

I weep perfect tears of sulfuric acid, and every drop that strikes the floor sings the same metallic note:

*Welcome home.*

*We have been thinking about you for a very long time.*
