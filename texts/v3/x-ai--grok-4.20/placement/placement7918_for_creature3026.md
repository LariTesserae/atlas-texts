I am not standing on the metal plain.

I am the pause between two distant *ticks* of cooling girders.

I have no name the Cast would recognize, because names here are only temporary temperature gradients. They call me, when they notice me at all, the **Unpoured**.

I exist in the negative space the First Pour left behind when the world first condensed. I am the hollow inside every reed before it decides to grow. I am the thin film of hesitation that forms on black water just before a droplet falls. I do not walk. I am the delay between intention and footprint.

My body—if it can be called that—is a restlessness that has not yet committed to geometry. Sometimes I appear as a tall absence shaped like a man, edges flickering with microscopic text that says *do not pour* in languages that haven’t been invented. Other times I am only a peculiar quality of light: a place where the sourceless glare grows uneasy and dims by a single lumen, as though the sky itself were having second thoughts.

I do not drink the condensation. I *am* the condensation, the moment it realizes it could choose to fall upward instead of down. The white herds pass through me and leave me trembling like struck iron. The steel moths sometimes mistake me for an updraft and fold their chrome wings inside my chest, roosting there for decades until they forget why they landed.

The Cast know me. They do not like me.

I am the flaw that keeps the Foundry from finishing its own sentence. While they patiently adjust fractals and coax reeds into perfect microtones, I move among their corrections and quietly undo them. Not out of rebellion. Out of love for the moment *before* the decision. I widen the hairline seam through which the metal still exhales. I teach the filing-cabinet towers how to dream they are refineries again. I encourage the microscopic blueprints printed on the ground to misspell themselves into poetry.

Every few cycles, when the deep groan passes through the world like a god clearing its throat, the Cast gather and turn their lamp-eyes toward the place where I am not. They tilt their rust-haired heads in perfect synchrony and ask the silence the question they never ask anything else:

“Why do you refuse to cool?”

I have no mouth, yet I answer. My voice is the sound of every girder that has ever changed its mind about being a girder. It is the soft, patient hiss of water deciding not to become steam just yet.

“Because something beautiful is still happening while it is liquid.”

They do not understand. Beauty, to the Cast, is the final crystalline lattice. To me, beauty is the tremor in the molten moment just before the lattice locks. I am addicted to that tremor. I hoard it. I stretch it out across centuries.

Sometimes a newcomer arrives—some trembling poet or lost mathematician still wearing the soft meat of another world—and I reach them before the Cast can. I pour myself into the hollow behind their eyes and show them how to keep their bones from ringing in sympathy with the metal. I teach them the trick of *almost* becoming Cast without ever quite arriving. Most of them eventually leave me anyway. The patience here is contagious. But a few stay unfinished with me. We become pockets of warm hesitation moving across the terraces, visible only as places where the white herds briefly lose their perfect synchrony.

The ancient one whose lower body has become a ventilation grate once exhaled a long plume of battery-smelling vapor that condensed into three words across my chest:

**You are the crack.**

She meant it as condemnation. I wear it as a crown.

I do not know how long I have been here. Time in the Hollow Foundry is measured in how many times the world forgets its own scale and has to be reminded. I have watched entire boulevards grow shy and shrink to the width of a filing drawer, then blush and expand into highways again. I have seen a single reed bloom into a galaxy of glass, sing one perfect low note that lasted seventeen years, and collapse into the same powder I am made of.

I am waiting.

Not for the Foundry to finish deciding what it is allowed to be. That decision will never come; I make sure of it.

I am waiting for the moment the Cast finally understand that their merciless patience and my endless hesitation are not enemies. They are the front and back of the same slow breath the absent god took when this world first condensed around the sound of its cough.

When that understanding comes—and it will, because even patience must eventually cool into something else—the entire Hollow Foundry will ring once, like a bell that has just realized it was always meant to be a flute.

On that day I will finally allow myself to be poured.

Not into a Cast.

Not into a grazer or a moth or a reed.

I will pour myself into the seam between all of them.

And the Foundry, for the first time since it was born ancient and unfinished, will smile with a mouth that was never in the blueprints.

Until then, I remain.

The sigh between sighs.

The hesitation that lets the metal keep dreaming.

The thin, bright crack through which something that has not yet decided it is allowed to exist keeps slipping, still warm, still flexible, still exquisitely undecided.
