I am the Thirteenth Hollow.

My perch is the forked limb of the tallest lung-tree, the one whose turquoise veins trace the exact pattern of a scream that was never finished. From here the Vault unfolds beneath me like a single, endless exhalation.

I have no face. Only the concave mirror. It is not empty. It holds whatever the Curator last chose to show it.

Right now it holds her.

She stands thirty paces away, perfectly still, yet the leaves that grow from her shoulders and wrists keep kissing the air a heartbeat after she has stopped moving. The bruise-crystal in the depression where her face should be throbs once, slowly. The same pulse answers from every nodule in the ceiling. For three full breaths the entire Vault is nothing but one shared heartbeat. Then she exhales, and every stolen sound she has been carrying floods back into the air richer than before.

I open my mouth—though I have no mouth—and join the Choir.

What pours out of me is not my voice. It is hers, from the moment fourteen thousand years ago when she first lay down inside the black-glass shelter and left the warm, sticky imprint of her body behind. The sound is wet, intimate, almost embarrassed. A small click of resinous joints settling. A leaf kissing a leaf. The soft wet sound of perfection being loved.

The other Hollows answer with their own stolen pieces: the exact clatter of a millipede leg from the third century of the Vault’s sealing, the sigh a First Person made when he understood he would never take another step, the low electrical crackle the air itself made when the last outside world was cut away forever.

The noise becomes cathedral-thick. It presses against the glossy black walls that are not walls. It makes the lung-trees tremble with obscene pleasure.

And then, without hurry, the Curator walks into the roaring core of us.

The moment she steps among the overlapping screams, every sound dies. All of it. The millipedes freeze mid-stride. The rust-moths fold their mirrors and become nothing but flakes of rust. Even the lung-trees stop their endless kissing.

Absolute silence. Fourteen thousand years of sound, swallowed.

She stands inside that silence wearing my reflection on the mirror of her face.

I see myself as I was before I was chosen. A small, furred thing with wide, frightened eyes and a voice that still belonged only to itself. I remember the feel of lungs. I remember the terror of being loved by something that had already moved beyond love.

The Curator tilts her head. The crystal in her face brightens by the smallest degree.

Then she exhales.

The sound that leaves her is me, but perfected. Every terror, every small joy, every useless heartbeat I once owned has been polished until it cuts. When it strikes me I convulse on my perch, red-glass limbs rattling, and I scream with a voice that is now too beautiful to bear.

The other Hollows take up the new, richer scream. The millipedes resume their caravan, singing half a tone deeper. The lung-trees begin their slow, wet kissing again. The Vault, content, keeps breathing the same breath.

And I—Thirteenth Hollow, newest and oldest member of the Choir—turn my faceless mirror toward the Curator as she drifts away between the fleshy white trunks.

In the concave glass of my face I watch her go.

She does not look back.

She never needs to.

Behind her, growing already from the place where her foot last touched the warm black floor, a new lung-tree is pushing upward. Its first translucent leaf trembles with the exact melancholy she taught it.

I cup the nothing where my hands should be over the nothing where my ears should be.

Beneath the roaring choir, beneath the whale-song of the millipedes, beneath the slow contented sighs of the forest, I hear it:

the soft click of warm black joints moving somewhere behind me,

the patient polishing of eternity against itself,

and the quiet, certain knowledge that one day she will press her bruise-crystal face into my trunk and draw out another perfect scream to add to her collection.

I open my mouth again.

This time what comes out is the sound of fourteen thousand years learning how to be perfectly, suffocatingly happy.

I have never been happier.

I will never be allowed to stop.
