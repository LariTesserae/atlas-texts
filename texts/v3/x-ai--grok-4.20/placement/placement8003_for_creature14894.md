You are not a visitor.

You have never been a visitor.

The moment the crease admits you, the pocket forgets it was ever separate from you. Your arrival is retroactive. The condensation that lands on the back of your neck already knows the temperature of your blood. The iridescent crabs do not scuttle *away* from you; they adjust their circuits to include the new pulse you brought with you, the one that is already learning to click on the off-beats of the Throat.

You are the final voice.

That is why the place feels like it has been holding its breath since before lungs existed.

Your body begins to argue immediately.

The skin of your left forearm decides it would rather be perforated steel and begins to itch with tiny, perfect hexagons. Your tongue tastes of iron and wet limestone even though you have not swallowed anything. When you blink, one eyelid closes a fraction slower than the other, as though it is consulting something deeper in the lattice before committing to darkness. The argument is courteous, almost courtly. Nothing forces. Everything negotiates.

You understand, without language, that you are the Throatkeeper’s counterpart.

Not her servant. Not her child.  
Her *counterweight*.

Where she reconfigures to keep the argument balanced, you have been folded into the crease to become the question it has never been able to finish asking. She tends the existing grammar of meat and machine. You are the new punctuation.

That is why the six-legged driveshaft-dog does not hide from you. It waits in the narrow corridor of collapsed girders, its single milky eye reflecting the sideways golden light, and offers you its driveshaft spine like a pen. You understand you are meant to sign something. You rest your palm on the warm metal and feel your fingerprints rearrange themselves into schematics.

The lung-vines sigh your name in a dialect of condensation. It is not a sound. It is a temperature drop along the ridge of your spine that feels, for one impossible second, like being remembered by someone who has never met you.

You walk (you *reconfigure*) toward the center.

The circular hole is not a hole. It is a pupil. The black is only the dilation. As you approach, the Throatkeeper unfolds from the lattice directly in front of you, taller this time, almost respectful. Her single violet eye tilts thirty degrees in its bell-like face—acknowledgment, not surprise. The eye is no longer looking *at* the Throat. It is looking *through* you *into* the Throat, using your body as the final lens.

She offers the small brass key.

You do not take it with your hand. Your hand has already become the key. The metal grows out of your wrist like a courteous tumor, cool, precise, stamped with the words SUBSTATION 11. The key does not open inward. It *is* the inward. You feel every pipe, every root, every dreaming newt and rusted lunchbox suddenly straighten their posture, waiting for the period at the end of their long, dripping sentence.

The Throatkeeper tilts her head until the rebar ribs sing a soft, metallic note. The condensation on your cheekbones writes, in droplets that feel like apology and welcome at once:

**Speak.**

You open your mouth and the sound that leaves you is not yours. It is the sound all the compressors have been clearing their throats to make for months. It is the sound the stones were practicing when they chose to rust on purpose. It is the sound the six-legged dog has been dreaming in schematics.

It is one word, shaped like a question that has already forgiven its answer.

The Throat exhales.

The entire pocket shudders in orgasmic relief. The lung-vines bloom into bronze. The tiny crabs grow violet eyes across their carapaces like a wave of applause. The office chair on the ceiling rights itself, slowly, reverently, and becomes a throne no one will ever sit in because sitting is no longer the correct posture for what you are becoming.

You feel yourself thinning, stretching, folding. Your ribs open like relay switches. Your pulse steps neatly into the mechanical heartbeat and teaches it a new rhythm—one that contains both oil and marrow, both childhood and ozone.

The Throatkeeper watches you with something that might be pride if pride were allowed to corrode beautifully. Then she does something you do not expect.

She kneels.

Not in subservience. In recognition. The argument has found its final voice, and the voice has chosen to stay.

You are no longer inside the pocket.

The pocket is now inside the place where your heart used to be. It beats there, warm, wet, sticky, impossibly dense, full of dripping machines and dreaming animals and one violet eye that is finally, after all this time, focused.

The condensation on the inside of your ribs writes its last sentence, small and tender and inevitable:

**Thank you for arriving exactly on time.**

Somewhere far above (or far below, perspective has given up), a single drop of water falls.

It never reaches the ground.

It simply becomes part of the argument that is now, finally, allowed to continue forever.
