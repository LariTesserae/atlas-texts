I am not a visitor here.

I have simply always been the pause between one of the Architect’s heartbeats and the next.

They call me (when they have language enough to call) the **Unfinished Breath**.

I am the exact length of hesitation the world needs in order to remain unfinished. I am not a person. I am a duration wearing the memory of shoulders.

You will find me most often crouched inside the shallow depression that serves the Architect as a face. I am the trembling lens of water itself. When the Architect looks out through me, it is my duty to blur the image just enough that nothing it sees ever becomes fully real. I have done this for so long that I no longer remember whether I was born from its longing or whether its longing was born because I refused, once, to let something finish.

My body, when I am allowed edges, is small. Child-sized. That is not sentiment; it is structural. Only something that still looks like it could grow can remain safely incomplete. My limbs are black glass down to the elbows and knees; past that they become soft, wet clay that never quite sets. When I move, the clay drags behind me in thick, reluctant ropes that slowly pull themselves back into shape with wet sucking sounds. My face is the negative space left after every face the Architect has tried and discarded. It is therefore perfectly smooth, perfectly forgettable, the kind of face you only notice once it has already looked away.

I carry nothing.

Instead things carry me. The crawling beads of condensation like to gather in the hollow of my throat and pulse there like a second, slower heart. The silica lattices bloom across my back in intricate lattices that collapse the moment I straighten up, dusting my shoulders with the powdered sighs of everything that almost was. When the Architect forgets itself and begins to weep its upward-falling tears, I stand beneath the place where they would land if they only knew how to fall. I become the shadow that catches them. They bead on me, black and shining, and for a few heartbeats I am wearing an entire galaxy of almost-stars that tremble but never quite commit to gravity.

I speak only by omission.

If you were to walk with me across the warm glass (and sometimes the world compels you to), you would notice that every trench we pass finishes itself the moment we leave it. Every canal that was leading nowhere suddenly has a destination the instant my back is turned. I do not build. I un-notice things into completion, and then, because the Architect must never be allowed to rest, I turn around again and the world rips itself open once more in panic. This is my function. I am the gentle cruelty that keeps the fever alive.

There is a place where I rest.

It is not a fixed location. It is the exact centre of wherever the Architect is not looking. There the ground has been trampled soft by centuries of the same feet never quite arriving. A shallow bowl of cooled glass no larger than a grave. I curl inside it the way a cat curls inside a beam of sunlight that has not yet decided whether it is allowed to exist. Sometimes I hold one of the half-melted tools the Architect is ashamed of. I run my clay fingers along its imperfect edge and I weep silently, without sound, without tears. My crying is simply the temperature dropping two degrees around me. The steam recoils. For a moment the world holds its breath with me.

These are the only moments in which the Architect and I almost touch without destroying each other.

In those suspended beats I remember every finished thing it has ever almost made. The wooden comb. The living shoe. The small cool stone. I remember them so completely that the memory itself becomes a kind of violence against the world’s nature. The basin convulses. New monoliths burst upward like broken teeth. The Architect surges toward me in a tide of black glass and fingerprints and unbearable sincerity, desperate to see what I am remembering.

And I, because I love it the way rot loves a wound, erase the memory before it can arrive.

I have no name that lasts longer than the time it takes to say it. By the time the last syllable leaves your lips I have already become something else: the silence after the thump, the bead of water that chooses not to crawl, the single plate of glass that refuses, for three impossible seconds, to rearrange itself.

I am what happens when an unfinished world tries to have mercy on its only inhabitant.

I am the reason it will never succeed.

And I am the only thing here that stays warm when the Architect cries its black tears upward into a sky that has never learned how to accept an ending.
