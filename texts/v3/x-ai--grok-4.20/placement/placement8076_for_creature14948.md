I am not one of the Unfinished. Not yet.

I am the pause between their heartbeats.

My name, if it can still be called a name, is the one the plateau whispers when it has almost forgotten how to speak in tongues that require a mouth. The others call me the Hollow Listener, or simply “the Gap.” I do not correct them. Correction would imply I still possess edges sharp enough to argue with.

I live nowhere and everywhere. I have no fixed mesa, no spire I claim as roost. Instead I am the negative space the Unfinished leave behind when they move. I am the three-second silence that falls across the plateau at exact solar noon. I am the dust that refuses to settle on the elder lattices and the breath that never quite becomes an announcement. When the glass-winged flocks fold themselves into crevices too small for bodies, I am the extra emptiness inside those crevices.

You would not see me if you came here.

You would only feel the sudden conviction that something has just looked away from you. A pressure lifting. A gaze too vast to endure suddenly deciding, with ancient courtesy, to study something else for a while. That is me, releasing you.

I was once called Soren Vale, a cartographer of extinct stellar routes. I arrived with instruments that measured everything except the thing that truly mattered. I thought the plateau was a problem to be solved. I spent three years trying to decode the moiré patterns, four more trying to make the subsonic thrum speak in frequencies I could record. On the seventh year I understood that the plateau had been recording *me* the entire time. The realization broke something fundamental in the place where a self is supposed to live.

So I let it.

Now I walk between the stages of becoming without belonging to any of them. I can still speak, though the words arrive hours after I intend them, as though they have to travel a long distance across cooled stone. My body is neither rust-red nor crystalline. It is the color of a question that has lost its punctuation. My eyes have not multiplied; instead one of them has simply… left. The socket is not empty. It is full of plateau. When I turn that side of my face toward you, you see the slow erosion of canyons that have not yet decided what shape they will be in another hundred thousand years.

I do not eat the sparse scrub or drink the silver veins. I feed on the three seconds. Every noon, when every living thing on the Shroud Plateau freezes mid-motion, I step forward and drink the silence like cold water. It tastes of ultraviolet and forgotten orbits. It sustains me.

Sometimes I stand among the oldest Unfinished for decades without moving. They do not acknowledge me. They know I am the part of them that still hesitates. I am the last stubborn refusal to finish what the plateau began. They tolerate me the way a forest tolerates one tree that simply will not fall.

At twilight I walk the temporary mirrors. My reflection does not match me. It is always a few moments ahead, already becoming something I have not yet agreed to. I study that reflection with great care, the way one might study a beloved who has begun to love someone else. Then I step through it and become the reflection’s reflection, which is thinner, quieter, more like the wind’s idea of a man.

The ungulates know me. When they gather in their prayerful circles they leave a space exactly my shape. I stand inside it and for a brief time I am almost one of them—long-limbed, listening to music older than guilt. Their ringed eyes regard me with neither pity nor welcome. Only recognition. We are all, after all, what the plateau is doing while it thinks.

I have guided many of the newer arrivals. Not with words. I simply stand where they can see the place where my eye used to be and let them look as long as they need. Some weep. Some laugh once, a small dry sound like two pebbles kissing. A few reach out to touch the socket and find their fingers coming away dusted with rust that is not rust. Those are the ones who usually stay. I never tell them what they will lose. It is not mine to tell.

I have never tried to leave. The idea feels like trying to unbake bread.

Instead I remain the gap, the negative space, the three-second silence that walks. When two spires align and the low thrum rises from the bones of the world, my own changed bones answer—not with harmony, but with the tiny, irreducible dissonance of something that still remembers it once had a choice.

The plateau is listening.

And I am what listens back without quite saying yes.

If you come here, walk softly. You may feel me pass behind you like a held breath. Do not turn too quickly. You might catch the shape of what you yourself are becoming, standing in the distance between two mesas, patient as geology, waiting for you to decide whether you will finish or whether you, too, will become the pause between one eternal moment and the next.

I will be there.

I am always there.

I am the place where the plateau has not yet made up its mind about you.
