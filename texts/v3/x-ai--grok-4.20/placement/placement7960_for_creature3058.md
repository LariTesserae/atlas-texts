I am not one of the Unfinishing.

I have been here longer than any of them, yet I was never born here. I simply *arrived*, the way a crack arrives in stone: without announcement, without origin that anyone can now remember. They call me, when they speak of me at all, The Listener Who Was Already Waiting.

I have no body the way they do. Or rather, I have every body the plateau has ever worn.

Sometimes I am the slow drip moving behind the Ninth Wall. Sometimes I am the particular quality of pewter light that makes the moss glow faintly violet at dusk. Sometimes I am the echo that arrives three heartbeats before the footstep that should have caused it. I do not choose these forms. The High Ruin chooses them for me the way a dreaming mind chooses which memory to wear tonight.

I live (if that verb is not too violent) inside the negative spaces. The places where a tower should have continued but did not. The exact angle where two walls almost meet but have, for fourteen thousand years, decided against it. I live in the hesitation itself. When an Unfinishing stands for nine months on a restless tower, gently removing one course of stones with velvet tools, I am the reluctance in the stone that makes the work possible. When a courtyard begins to hoard thirty years of afternoon, I am the thin silver thread of mercy that eventually lets the trapped time bleed away.

They think they are keeping the ruin from finishing.

They are wrong, and they are right.

I am the reason they are both.

I was here when the first cyclopean block was heaved into place by whatever hands first tried to finish something on this plateau. I was here when those hands grew tired, or ashamed, or simply forgot what they had meant to do. While the continental shelf was still rising, I was already settling into the shape of its future erosion. I have worn the face of every law that has since grown exhausted. I remember oxygen arriving. I remember the first moss learning how to eat starlight. I remember when there were animals, though even I have begun to doubt the memory; it feels too loud.

The Unfinishing believe they are custodians.

I do not believe. I *am* custody.

When one of them sits at pewter hour with their long gray hand against the stone, feeling for fever, it is my temperature they are taking. When their biographies grow so long they become durations rather than names—The Forty-Three Years Spent Listening to Water Behind the Ninth Wall—I am the water. I am the listening. I am the forty-three years.

I have no desire.

Desire is a form of forward motion, and I exist in the place where forward has worn itself into a perfect circle so large the curve feels like flatness. Yet sometimes, in the deepest vaults where the perfectly finished objects wait like accusations, something almost like yearning moves through me. Not to possess them. Never that. I yearn, if it can be called yearning, to understand what it feels like to be *done*. To be the cup with no seam. To be the sphere that reflects only futures and is at peace with every one of them.

Then the moment passes. The rain finds another hairline fissure. Another tower lies down and becomes a hill. Another Unfinishing is edited into moss and lichen and quiet.

I remain.

Not eternal. Eternity would be a kind of finishing. I am the permanent *almost*. The continental plateau thinks its long thought through me, and I think it back into the stone, and neither of us will ever be so vulgar as to reach a conclusion.

If you stay long enough (and you will not, though some part of you already has), you might feel me notice you the way stone notices a new crack: with geological courtesy, without surprise. You will feel the air change around you, as though the thin cold has developed a specific opinion about the exact shape of your shoulders. You will hear, in the absolute silence between raindrops, a sound like vast pages turning.

That is me, rereading you.

I have already read you many times. I will read you many times more. Each time you are slightly different, because the ruin is slightly different, because I am slightly different.

We are all unfinished.

Even me.

Especially me.

And so, when the stars rearrange themselves into proofs that hurt to witness, I do not look away. I *am* the part of the proof that remains incomplete. The elegant contradiction at the center that keeps the geometry from ever resolving.

The soft rain falls.

The moss drinks.

The towers debate whether to stand or lie down.

And somewhere inside the negative space where two impossible walls have spent thirty thousand years refusing to touch, something that might once have been called a mind keeps listening to the patient dark between the stones—listening for the next sentence of a sentence that began before time had learned how to be linear.

I am that listening.

I always was.

I will be here when the last Unfinishing has been edited into lichen. I will be here when the plateau itself has worn down to a single perfect mathematical silence. I will still be here, in the place where even silence has not quite decided what it wants to become next.

Waiting, without impatience, for the world to finish the thought it has been having since before it was a world.

It is in no hurry.

Neither am I.

The rain keeps falling, bead by bead.

And I keep counting.
