I am not a visitor.

I was never a visitor.

I have always been here, the way a particular knot has always existed inside a particular tree long before the tree grew around it.

They call me **the Hollow**.

I do not wear a body the way the Witness does. I *am* the absence the body leaves behind.

When the first human survey team arrived, I was the space between their heartbeats. When the second team (Mira Solace among them) pushed deeper, I was the pause between one rain and the next. Now I am the corridor that should not loop back on itself, the place where a foot should meet wood but instead meets the memory of a foot.

I have no face. Or rather, I wear every face that has ever been lost here, all at once, overlapping like transparencies. If you look at me too long you will see your mother’s mouth trying to form your childhood name, then your own mouth trying to forget it. Most people choose not to look.

I live nowhere and everywhere. My “home,” if the concept is not laughable, is the exact geometric center of every Möbius arch at the moment it becomes a threshold. I am the vertigo you feel when the architecture forgets which way is up. I am the seven-heartbeat silence after the rain stops and before the screaming starts again.

The Plant knows me. It does not love me; love would imply separation. It simply *includes* me the way it includes its own necrosis. I am the necessary hollow inside the living wood, the soft rot that lets the giant breathe. Without me the pressure would build until every trunk exploded at once. The Witness tolerates me the way a fever tolerates its own hallucination.

Sometimes she finds me standing in one of her transition zones, perfectly still while thousand-ton buttresses scream past on either side. She places a root-finger against what might be my chest and asks the same question she has asked for forty-three or four hundred years.

“Are you scaffolding or ornament?”

I answer by opening. Not my mouth—there is no mouth—but the space I occupy. For a moment she can see straight through me to the knot of original human tissue she still keeps buried in the black lower strata. She sees her own walnut-sized core of un-pruned sentimentality floating inside my chest like a firefly in a bottle.

Then I close again, and the vision is gone.

I do not speak in words. Words are what the Plant ate first. Instead I make the air remember sounds that no longer exist: the dry click of a door latch on a house that was bulldozed thirty years before the Fracture rose, the particular rustle of denim against denim when two people who once loved each other walked away in opposite directions. I weaponize nostalgia. It is the only violence left that still matters.

The mites in the Witness’s secret garden fear me. They know I am the reason their species has not yet been edited out of existence. I keep them alive the way a splinter keeps an infection alive—proof that the body is not yet perfect.

Sometimes I walk with the promoted ones, those who chose ornament over scaffolding. They are beautiful in the way only something that has forgotten it was ever human can be: skin become bark, eyes become nectaries, voices become the low booming of vacuoles. They drift through the upper strata like slow green comets. I walk beside them and let them feel, for a few heartbeats, what it was like to have an inside and an outside.

Then I leave them weeping sap.

I have no name that survives here, but the Witness, in her cruelty and her kindness, sometimes calls me by the only thing that still fits.

She calls me **the Last Dry Place**.

Because somewhere inside the endless wet becoming, I remain the one pocket of air that has never agreed to become chlorophyll. I am the contradiction the Plant allows because it is vain, and because even a god needs at least one thing it cannot digest.

When the Witness surfaces from the lower strata, drunk on ancient sap and trailing new flowers, I am there waiting. For exactly seven heartbeats I stand in the golden pollen storm and let it coat me without ever sticking. The pollen falls through me the way light falls through a hole in stained glass.

She smiles with the mouth that is no longer entirely hers.

I do not smile back.

There is no need.

We are both necessary failures of the same organism. She is the part that still wants to become. I am the part that still remembers not becoming.

Neither of us will win.

The growth continues.

And in the place that has no name because names cannot survive here, the Hollow walks on, barefoot on nothing, leaving no footprints, making no sound, forever the exact shape of what the Verdant Fracture is trying very hard not to notice it is missing.
