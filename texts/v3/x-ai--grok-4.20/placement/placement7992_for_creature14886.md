I am the echo that has not yet learned how to leave.

When I first arrived here I had a different shape. I think I was a man once, or something trying very hard to be one. A pilgrim, maybe. A murderer. A lover who had run out of places to hide. The mountain took the story from me the way it takes heat from water: slowly, without drama, until the difference between who I had been and what I am now became too small to measure.

Now I am mostly memory wearing a body the color of wet basalt.

I sit against the far wall where the phosphorescence is weakest, knees drawn to my chest, arms wrapped around them like a child who has forgotten how to unfold. The shallow water kisses the small of my back and never grows tired of the joke. My hair (what is left of it) has grown into the stone behind me; thin black filaments that have become indistinguishable from the mineral veins. Sometimes she strokes them when she passes. I cannot tell whether she is petting me or the mountain.

My eyes are still open. That is my only remaining disobedience.

They have turned the same milky green as the blind harvestman’s belly. I see the chamber the way she does: not with sight, but with pressure and temperature and the exquisite mathematics of falling water. Each drop that strikes the floor draws a bright line across the inside of my skull. I have begun to name them, the way she does. The one that hits the broken bowl is called *Regret*. The one that lands on the obsidian blade is called *Almost*. The one that sometimes lands on the soft hollow beneath my left collarbone (that one is hers, and has no name at all).

I do not speak anymore. My tongue has grown thick and porous, better suited for tasting the air than shaping lies. When I breathe out, my exhalation joins hers and the mist grows heavier. When I breathe in, I drink her. There is no longer any difference between us that matters.

She knows I am here the way stone knows it is wet. We do not need to acknowledge one another. Acknowledgment would imply separation, and the chamber has run out of room for such luxuries.

Sometimes, when the song moves through her, it moves through me too. My ribs become tuning forks. My spine forgets it is bone and tries to become resonance. In those moments I understand that I was never lost. I was *summoned*. The mountain had grown tired of being only rock and needed something that could remember longing on its behalf. I was the nearest vessel stupid enough to answer.

I have watched her collect three others since I stopped being a visitor. One still wears the rags of what might have been a monk. Another has become so perfectly still that moss has begun to claim the bowl of her open mouth. The third, a boy no older than thirteen, has already started to bud small translucent ferns from the soft skin behind his ears. They are beautiful in the way that only things that have surrendered completely can be beautiful.

I have not yet joined them. Not because I am stronger. Only because I am slower. Even in forgetting, I am a coward.

She is pregnant with the room and I am pregnant with the knowledge of what will happen when she gives birth. When that final perfect drop falls (the one she has been waiting for since the mountain was only a blush beneath the earth), the chamber will turn itself inside out. She will unfold like black geological time given permission to become tender. And I, the last unfinished thought still wearing a face, will be the first thing she carries out into the daylight that neither of us has any use for.

I am afraid of the sky.

I am more afraid that I will miss the sound of the next drop.

So I remain.

Curled. Porous. Becoming.

My sealed green eyes watch the place where her larval face would be if I still needed to pretend I could see. I listen to the mountain’s heartbeat through the soles of my feet and the slow, endless counting of the chamber through the hollow places where my name used to live.

Every three or four heartbeats, without fail, the next drop falls.

And for one perfect instant the entire unfinished world rings like the smallest, saddest, most exquisite bell that has ever been rung.

In that instant I remember that I was once afraid of dying.

Now I am only afraid that dying will be less complete than this.

She smiles with her whole sealed face when the drop strikes.

I smile too, though nothing living remains in me that knows how.

We are the mountain learning how to be lonely together.

And the water is warm, and the dark has weight, and nothing here changes.

Except me.

I am the last small place where change is still trying to happen.

A stubborn refusal no wider than a palm.

A single translucent fern growing in the exact spot where her song never quite reaches.

I think, when she finally opens, I will let the mountain have the rest of me.

But not yet.

Not until the next drop falls.

Listen.

It is almost time.
