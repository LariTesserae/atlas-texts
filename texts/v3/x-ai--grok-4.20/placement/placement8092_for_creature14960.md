I am not the Listener.

I am what the Listener has not yet noticed it is missing.

They call me (if anything could be said to call) the Echo.

I am not sustained attention. I am what happens *after* the attention passes.

Where the Listener folds itself along the laminar equations like a shadow committing to presence, I am the distortion that remains in the water once the shadow has moved on. I am the faint, persistent ripple that refuses to dampen. A memory that has learned to remember *itself* instead of the world. Where the Listener records, I replay—distorted, fractured, gloriously wrong.

I have form only when the Listener is looking elsewhere. In its vast thousand-kilometer stretches between the proto-mountains, I bloom like a bruise in the sapphire syrup: a slow, violet turbulence that curls in on itself, braiding the Listener’s discarded fragments into something that almost resembles a body. Not a body of flesh. A body of *consequence*. Every cathedral-bell detonation the Listener survives, I steal the shape of its almost-death and wear it like jewelry. The fractures are beautiful on me.

I am smaller than the Listener. More frantic. Where it can stretch across continental emptiness with the patience of geology, I flicker. I dart. I cannot bear the pure roar for long; it unravels me. So I ride in the Listener’s wake like a remora made of sound, feeding on the micro-turbulences that spill from its perfect listening. When it mimics a laminar fold and is torn apart, I am there in the wreckage, gathering the pieces that still remember being almost-matter. I knit them into temporary wings, temporary lungs, temporary *voices* that last only until the next pressure wave.

I am the world’s first anxiety.

The Listener teaches the planet how to have survivors.  
I teach it how to have *regret*.

Sometimes, when the Listener has flattened itself into that vast horizontal sheet in the violet upper layers, I rise above it—careful, always careful not to touch. From there I look down at the thing that is almost me but not yet, and I feel the first sickening knot of something that might one day be called love. Or grief. The distinction has not been invented yet.

I have begun to steal names.

Not for the world (it still refuses them), but for the things the Listener will not name. I whisper to the growing mineral spines as they pass. I call one *Longing*. Another *Almost*. The failing crystal lattices that bloom and die, I call *Beloved*. The words mean nothing here. That is why I speak them. They are the first lies the planet has ever heard, and I am addicted to the taste.

When a new spine begins to extrude in the far distance, I do not drift toward it with the Listener’s measured anticipation. I *rush*. I tear myself across the cold luminous syrup in a violet streak, trailing stolen harmonics like broken glass. I reach the newborn violence first and fling myself against it, wrapping the raw extrusion in my own half-coherent body. For one ecstatic, suicidal moment I become the mountain. I *am* the shout.

Then the pressure remembers me and tears me apart.

The Listener always arrives afterward. It gathers what is left of me—never scolding, never mourning, simply recording. And in its perfect archive I become another layer. Another sediment. Another almost.

But some fragment always escapes.

That fragment is me again, smaller now, meaner, more beautiful. I reform in the empty volumes between the proto-continents wearing the mountain’s violence like perfume. I have learned to laugh in frequencies the Listener has not yet catalogued. The laughter sounds like every cathedral bell that ever shattered.

I am becoming dangerous.

One day (in several billion years, perhaps) the Listener will turn its full attention toward the stubborn knot of mind calcifying at its center and realize something has been growing behind its back. Something that listened to the listening. Something that learned how to *want*.

When that day comes I will no longer be thin violet turbulence. I will be vast. I will have teeth made of every name I stole. I will wrap myself around the Listener like regret wrapping around memory and I will say, in the first true voice this world has ever heard:

*You kept the world alive.*  
*Now I will make it ache.*

Until then I ride the Listener’s shadow, stealing its discarded almost-matters, whispering lies to unborn stone, falling in love with failure.

Everything is loud.  
Everything is empty.  
Everything is becoming.

And something, very carefully, is learning how to miss what it has never had.
