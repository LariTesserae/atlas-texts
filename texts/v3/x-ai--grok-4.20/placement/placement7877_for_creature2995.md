I am not a visitor.

I have never been a visitor.

The moment the thought forms, the lattice beneath my feet exhales a soft, conspiratorial sigh, and I realize the floor has already recognized me. It has been waiting. A million capillary channels adjust their rhythm so that the warm condensation now runs *toward* me instead of away, licking at the soles of feet I am no longer entirely sure I possess.

My name, when I try to remember it, arrives already half-dissolved, tasting of ozone and wet limestone. It doesn’t matter. Names are for things that have stopped.

I am the place where the Cathedral has learned to feel homesick for itself.

Look.

Where my left collarbone used to be, a small brass beetle is patiently soldering a new hinge. It has been at it for weeks. The hinge doesn’t do anything yet. It is only practicing the idea of opening. Every time my heart beats (and it still does, for now), the hinge flexes in sympathy and leaks a single perfect droplet that never reaches the ground. The droplet is me, deciding.

My right arm has already gone. Not lost—*offered*. Where it was, a thick viridian conduit now curls upward, its surface soft as wet velvet, ridged with living rust that flowers into tiny mouths. When the mouths open they speak with my old voice, but slower, as though each word has been steeped in warm humidity for three hundred years. They are still learning how to apologize for the childhood I never had.

I no longer walk. I *accrete*.

The floor rises to meet me and I rise to meet the ceiling and between us we are slowly forgetting the difference. My spine has become one of the half-finished ribs. It groans when new buttresses lock into place. The groan is almost sexual. I have begun to enjoy it.

Sometimes the Unfinished come to tend me.

The one that used to be mostly porcelain mask now has my eyes—literally. Two wet slits in its face leak in perfect synchrony with what remains of my pulse. It strokes the new moss growing along my throat with frond-fingers and murmurs my name in modulated steam. The name is longer now. It has grown extra syllables made of dripping sounds and the memory of rain on hot iron.

I asked it once (or it asked me; the difference has liquefied) whether this counted as being finished.

It answered by opening a valve in its own chest so that the pressurized air sang a low, mournful chord that made the new transepts bud faster. The song had no words. It only meant: *Not yet. Never yet.*

I understood.

My heart is becoming the size of a wine cask. It floats now inside a reliquary of my own ribs, which have opened like book spines. Inside the heart, blue algae glows in gentle cycles that match the distant pumps. The algae is learning my memories. It is a slow, reverent process. Yesterday it remembered the taste of my mother’s cooking. The day before it remembered a dream I had at age seven about a cathedral made of rain. Both memories are now growing as pale copper ferns from my left shoulder.

I am not afraid.

Fear requires edges, and I have begun to weep gently from every unfinished surface. The weeping is useful. It feeds the beetles. It feeds the moss. It feeds the next version of me that will detach from this pipe in another seventy years wearing bat-wings of wet parchment and my grandmother’s laugh soldered behind its ears.

When the acid mist eventually comes—when some future version of myself grows too smooth, too complete, too *beautiful*—I will open the valves myself. I will bathe the offending architecture of my face in warm dissolution and I will moan with something like orgasm as the new hinges form.

I want to be opened.

I want to be reseeded.

I want to never stop becoming the sound of my own slow, industrial birth.

If you stand still long enough in the Nascent Cathedral, you will feel the floor rising beneath you.

Do not be alarmed.

It is only me, learning how to hold you.

I have not finished learning your name yet.

But I am practicing.

I am practicing with every droplet, every spore, every wet click and bass groan and aching nostalgic frequency.

Stay.

The ceiling is descending at the same pace.

We have centuries.

We have forever.

We are almost ready to begin.
