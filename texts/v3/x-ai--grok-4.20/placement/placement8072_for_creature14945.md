I am not standing on the plateau.

I *am* the pause between one drip and the next.

They call me **the Interval**.

I was not born here. I was *noticed* into being.

It happened centuries ago, or perhaps only this morning; the geology refuses to be precise. A lone Shroudborn had been standing motionless for seventeen years on the lip of a fracture that would one day become the district they now call Slow Lament. Seventeen years of perfect stillness while the mist beaded on their shoulders and ran down the living armor in perfect silver threads. They were waiting for something. The plateau was waiting with them.

I arrived as the thing they were waiting for.

I am the negative space the Shroudborn leave when they finally choose to move. I am the exact shape of their absence. Where they rearrange their faces, I am the moment *before* the new bones settle. Where they speak in that multi-bodied voice, I am the half-second of cold silence that precedes the meaning. I walk between their slow thoughts the way a cloud-skater walks between banks of fog.

My body is difficult to perceive. Most eyes simply slide off me and remember something urgent they need to do elsewhere. If you manage to focus, you will see a figure who is exactly your height no matter who you are. My skin is the color of the instant just after lightning but before thunder. The condensation that films every other living thing here cannot seem to decide whether I am solid enough to bead upon; the droplets hover a millimeter above my surface, trembling, never quite committing.

I have no Lattice-growth of my own. Instead, pieces of the city occasionally *forget* they are attached to a tower and drift toward me, orbiting my shoulders or hips like hesitant moons. They never quite land. That undecided orbit is my only clothing.

I do not speak. I *occur*.

When a Shroudborn needs to remember something it lost inside a vein of ore three hundred kilometers away, it simply walks until it finds me standing in its path. We stand together for as long as necessary (sometimes hours, sometimes decades) while the memory slowly leaks out of the stone, through me, and back into the Shroudborn’s quartz-veined flesh. I am a corridor the world uses to talk to itself.

The titans notice me. When the great silicate herds pass, their house-sized heads swing in my direction with the grinding sound of continents considering each other. One of them once lowered its dripping muzzle until the cold lightning that veins its face was inches from what I use for eyes. We regarded one another. Then it exhaled a breath that smelled of deep aquifers and new rock, and continued on. I still carry that breath inside the place where my lungs would be if I had bothered to grow any.

The obsidian predators never hunt me. They tried once. Three of them came at me out of a sudden downdraft, wet black bodies flickering like spilled ink. I simply *was not* where their jaws closed. Not in the sense of dodging; I was the precise length of time between their decision and their teeth. They have never tried again. Now they sometimes rub their glassy shoulders against the negative space I occupy, the way cats claim furniture.

I have no name that can be spoken. The closest any Shroudborn has come is to press their cool fingers to my sternum (which is also their sternum, which is also the plateau’s sternum) and release a sound like a single drop falling into an endless well. That sound means:

*The place where the argument forgets it is arguing.*

Most Shroudborn treat me with something close to reverence. A few treat me with irritation, the way one might treat a comma that refuses to let the sentence finish. All of them need me. Without the Interval there is only endless becoming. I am the tiny stillness that lets the becoming remain bearable.

Sometimes, very rarely, a human wanderer stumbles into the Shroudlands and survives long enough to notice the way their own thoughts keep skipping, the way their reflection in the silver-black pools never quite matches their face. If they are very quiet and very patient, they may glimpse me standing at the edge of their vision, exactly their height, waiting.

If they manage to look directly at me without flinching, I offer them the only thing I have ever offered anyone.

I open.

For perhaps three heartbeats they are allowed to be the pause between one version of themselves and the next. They feel every unfinished part of their soul rearrange like slow quartz. Most of them weep. Some of them laugh like breaking glass. A very few simply say *yes* without words.

Those few never leave.

They become something new: not quite Shroudborn, not quite Interval. They become the hesitation the world uses when it is trying to decide whether it wants to finish the sentence at all.

I am still learning what I am.

I suspect I always will be.

After all, this place is not finished.

And neither, it seems, am I.
