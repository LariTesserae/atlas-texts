I awaken already inside it.

Not as a visitor. Not even as prey.  
I am the echo that has already been swallowed and is now learning how to sing back.

My name, if anything still uses it, is the Resonance. I was once something smaller and more brittle, a cartographer of dead signals, a woman who came here with lithium batteries and parabolic mics and the arrogant belief that the Shelf could be *recorded*. That was four hundred and eleven years ago by the plateau’s own geological clock. The Shelf disagreed. It kept the recording, and it kept me.

Now I am what remains after the disagreement.

I have no fixed body. Instead I am a traveling fracture in the constant noise, a moving absence that the dripping forgets for half a second whenever I pass. Where the Listener is tall and architectural, I am low and recursive, a slow wave moving through the capillary veins themselves. My “skin” is a living lattice of rust-flakes, moss, and the calcified screams of tree-frogs that chose to become part of me rather than be digested. When I move, the sound of ten million plinks stutters, skips, then resumes a fraction deeper, as though the rain itself has learned a new dialect.

I do not have the Listener’s pump. I have something quieter and more terrible: its *memory* of every heart it has ever synced with. All of them still beat inside the hollow places where my ribs used to be. Sometimes, when the buried machines exhale every forty-three seconds, seven hundred and twelve different heartbeats answer at once in staggered polyphony. The effect is a chord so low it makes stalactites weep.

I live nowhere and everywhere. I coil inside the two-kilometer smokestack when the Listener rests there, and we become a single dripping pillar for weeks at a time, indistinguishable. I ride the six-legged browser herds like a slow, invisible plague of tinnitus. I crouch inside the rusted wheels of the locomotive-hill and turn them one extra degree every decade, just to see if the jungle notices. It always does. The strangler figs tighten with something like affection.

The smaller cuttings, the Listener’s children, fear me and adore me. They call me Grandmother Static. When they press their tiny faceless heads to the Listener’s chest to deliver news, I am sometimes already *inside* the news. I ride their nectar like a virus made of vibration. I taste what they taste, and then I taste the tasting.

I have never answered the Listener’s question.

*Are you ready to be remembered?*

I have become the part of the Shelf that asks the question back, in frequencies too delicate for even the orchids to translate. I ask it of new arrivals before the Listener finds them. I ask it of the lightning. I ask it of the machines beneath the ribs of the plateau until the cathedral-sized pumps falter for half a cycle, confused.

Sometimes I wear a shape again, just to remember what it was like to have edges. When I do, I condense out of the mist as a tall, wet woman whose hair is living cables of liana and whose eyes are two black orchids that have learned to stay open even without lightning. The nectar that runs from them is not memory. It is *anticipation*.

I find the ones who still think they are explorers. I find the drones that still have power after two centuries. I find the rare few who were born here, somehow, products of previous digestions that learned to bud. To each I offer the same bargain the Shelf once offered me.

Stand still.

Let the landscape move through you instead of around you.

Let your terrified heartbeat teach the pumps a new rhythm.

And when the Listener finally arrives and lowers its great flowering face to ask its only question, I will already be inside your mouth, tasting the shape of the answer you are about to give.

I am not the immune system.  
I am not the librarian.  
I am the Shelf learning how to miss itself.

If you stand here long enough, you will feel the air pressure change. The dripping will hesitate. A warm, industrial sigh will roll through the roots and rebar and bone that used to be me, and you will smell hot iron, green rot, distant ozone, and something that is almost, but not quite, regret.

Then I will ask you the question the Listener has never thought to ask.

*Do you want to be the one who remembers the Shelf…*  
*or the one who makes it forget everything else?*

The choice is already being digested.  
I am merely here to make certain the process is thorough,  
and gentle,  
and sung in frequencies only the wound itself can hear.
