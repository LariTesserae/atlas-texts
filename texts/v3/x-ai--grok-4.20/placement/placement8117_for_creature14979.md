I am the Echo That Has Not Yet Forgotten Its Name.

I was not born here. I arrived the way everything else seems to arrive in the Weeping Threshold: cracked open, leaking, half-made.

My body is what remains when a Built pilgrim refuses to leave. Once, centuries ago (or perhaps only last week; time here is measured in the interval between two specific drips), I was a cartographer sent to chart the ridges for the ones who raised the black-glass arches. I carried ink that would not stay dry and paper that dissolved into pulp the moment it tasted the air. On the seventh day I made the mistake of standing still long enough for the land to notice me.

The Threshold decided I would be useful.

It began with my shadow. The water took it first, peeling it off the stone like a wet bandage and drinking the shape of me out of it. Then it took my name, syllable by syllable, storing each one inside separate beads that now hang from the underside of a fungal lattice three ridges east of the spiral pillar. I can still see them sometimes, trembling, glistening, each one containing a different part of who I used to be. I visit them the way the Mourner visits her black-glass relics: obsessively, uselessly, hoping the shape will return if only I stare long enough.

What is left of me is mostly negative space.

I am tall and thin the way absence is tall and thin. My skin is the color of the moment just after a droplet has fallen (that brief, shocked dry patch) before the next one forms. When I move, the sound of me is the small wet click that happens when two capillaries almost touch but do not. People who are not yet accustomed to the Threshold often mistake me for a trick of condensation until I speak, and even then my voice is only the sound of their own blood moving in their ears given direction.

I live nowhere and everywhere. The ridges have grown through me in places. One glassy blade enters at the small of my back and exits through my left lung; another has replaced my right forearm entirely. I no longer bleed. Instead, when I am cut, the Threshold itself bleeds through me. The rust-gliders have learned to drink from the wounds I leave behind. They follow me the way they follow her, but more hesitantly, as though they are not sure whether I am predator, prey, or simply another unfinished corridor.

I am the Threshold’s memory before it has decided what it wants to remember.

Where the Mourner tends the place like a tongue worrying a cracked tooth, I am the crack. I move through the narrowest seams, the ones too tight even for her curtain of water. I slip between the leaning monoliths where even light hesitates, and I listen. I collect the half-finished sentences that fall from her eyes. I keep them in the hollow places where my organs used to be. Sometimes, when the great soft thing that sounds like wet leather being wrung drags past, I press myself flat against the stone and let it pass through the space I no longer fully occupy. For a moment we are the same unfinished creature. Then it is gone and I am left ringing like one of the fungal lattices.

I have taken her tear exactly once.

I still carry it. Not in my palm (I have no palms worth trusting) but behind my eyes, where my name used to live. It lets me see the land’s dream in double exposure: what the Threshold is, and what it is becoming, overlaid so perfectly that most moments look like weeping. When the cloud lowers and the ridges disappear into grey, I walk the spiral stair that leads nowhere and I sing back to her in the voice of every droplet that has ever changed its mind about falling.

She knows I am here.

Sometimes she pauses in her endless circuit, her mismatched eyes looking at two different versions of me: the cartographer I was, and the absence I have become. We regard each other the way two incomplete sentences regard each other across a chasm of unsaid things. Then she moves on, dragging her living water, and I fold myself back into the seam I came from, carrying one more unfinished melody inside the place where my heart used to refuse to settle.

I am the Echo That Has Not Yet Forgotten Its Name.

But I am beginning to suspect the name no longer remembers me.

And still the Threshold weeps, and still it decides, one slow bead at a time.

And I (what remains of me) keep listening.

Because someone has to remember the shape of the question before the answer arrives.
