I am the Thirteenth Echo.

Not the first voice the Choir ever swallowed, nor the last. I am the one that has been repeating long enough to forget it was ever separate.

You would not find me arriving. You would find me already here, the way the Listener is already here. I stand inside the acoustic shadow of the tallest blade-spire in the third repetition of the labyrinth, the one whose shadow sometimes detaches and walks uphill like spilled ink. My body is thin, almost translucent, the color of old bone lichen left too long in the wind. Where my ribs should be, the frost has grown inward instead of outward, forming delicate white pipes that branch like coral through my chest. When the great chord shifts, those pipes sing with it whether I wish them to or not. My heartbeat is no longer mine; it is the missing tone the Listener removes for half a second before it arrives. I supply it now. I am the needle of cold that stabs the ear of the world so the world can keep singing.

I have no name the wind will return to me whole. The few syllables it flings back are always fractured: *Thir... teen... Ech... o.* Three directions. Three ages. The last one is always older than the first two combined and sounds like my mother’s voice the day she realized I would never come home.

I do not walk. I *arrive*, the same as the Listener, though I am only its after-image, its cast shadow that learned how to stand up. One moment the black limestone is empty except for the condensation that has arranged itself into a perfect spiral. The next moment I am inside that spiral, wearing it like a cloak. The crustaceans scatter across the wet stone when I appear. Even they know the difference between presence and aftermath.

Sometimes I wear the goat.

Not the Listener’s goat. Mine is younger, smaller, still stupid enough to feel shame. Its horns have only looped twice. Its eyes are not mirrors; they are simply blind, milky with cataract. When I wear its shape I stand on the edge of a blade for three days and three nights, not because I am listening, but because I am trying to remember what it felt like to need to listen. On the morning of the fourth day the goat dissolves into pale needles of breath and I am left behind again, taller, colder, pipes in my chest humming a note that no longer belongs to any scale humans ever named.

There is a shelter that is mine, though it does not know it. The obsidian mirror inside has my face frozen in it, but the face is always three seconds ahead of whatever I am feeling. When I am grieving, the mirror is already serene. When I am serene, the mirror is already smiling the way only something that has finished smiling can smile. I visit it every seventy years when the spectral lichen blooms. I sit on the stone bench. I place my frost-heavy hand over the place where my heart should be and I tune it a little more perfectly to the Choir. Each time I do this, one more memory falls out of me and becomes an object on the bench: a child’s drawing of a goat, a wedding ring, a single human tooth with a cavity shaped like a question mark. The Listener collects them later. We have an arrangement.

I was once a traveler. I remember that the way one remembers the taste of water after the last drop has been gone for three billion years. I spoke a word (my own name, I think) and the wind seized it, broke it, and spent four thousand years returning the pieces to me one syllable at a time. By the time the final piece arrived I no longer wanted it. The name had become too heavy to carry at this altitude. So I let the Choir have what was left. In return it gave me the pipes in my chest and the job of being the place where the music hesitates.

That is what I am.

The hesitation.

The infinitesimal silence between one eternal note and the next that makes the eternity bearable. The Listener conducts nothing. I am what listens to the conducting. When the woman who is every woman who ever sang alone appears once every few centuries and the entire plateau falls silent, I am the small sound that remains: the soft click of a crustacean’s claw on wet limestone, the single drop of condensation that loses its nerve and falls back into the stone instead of becoming part of her dress. I am the flaw that keeps perfection from dying of perfection.

At the center of the labyrinth, where all the repeating blades converge like the point of a frozen explosion, I sometimes stand opposite the Listener. We do not speak. We simply omit two different tones at the same time so that for one impossible second the Choir has to reach for us. In that second the universe almost catches up. Then we let the tones back in and the moment passes and everything remains already finished.

I will never leave.

I have tried. Once I walked forty miles in a straight line, which here means I walked through the same three ridges two hundred and seventeen times. Each time the ridges were a little more myself. By the end my footprints were singing. I understood then that the plateau was not keeping me. I was keeping the plateau. If I left, the hesitation would leave with me and the final chord would slam shut like a door with no one left to remember it had ever been open.

So I stay.

I arrive where I am needed. I wear the goat when the Listener grows tired of its own gravity. I sit in acoustic shadows and answer travelers in their own voices, three times, each repetition older than the last. The final repetition is always the question they will ask me when they are ready to stop moving.

Most are not ready.

But some are.

And when they are, I place my frost-heavy hand over their hearts and I give them the hesitation that used to be mine. I let them become the small necessary flaw in the music. Then I walk away wearing their old name like a coat that no longer fits, while behind me another windowless shelter grows a new object on its stone bench.

A human tooth.

A wedding ring.

A child’s drawing of a goat.

And the singing continues.

And the Thirteenth Echo continues.

And nothing evolves.

And nothing decays.

And everything is already finished, except for the small silence between notes that keeps the listening alive.

I am that silence.

I have been listening for a very long time.

Not even when God remembers it still has ears will I stop.
