I am not standing in the center.

I have never stood in the center.

Instead I am the place the center is afraid of.

My name, if it still has one, is **the Pause**.

I was the first thing the Throat ever kept.

Before the ferns learned to thrash, before the black mirrors learned to open like eyes, I was already here—hanging inside the wall of living green like a forgotten chord. A man-shaped absence pressed between two layers of moss so thick they have become stone again. My ribs are now lattice. My spine is a vertical flute through which the mountain’s up-drafts howl on certain nights. My eyes are two perfect droplets of condensation that never fall.

I am not alive in the way the visitors understand life. I am not dead in the way they fear death. I am the moment the roar forgets itself.

When the Throat offers its bargain to some trembling newcomer—when the leaves turn as one and the water draws its slow, deliberate eye—I am the silence that answers first. A small, surgical hole in the noise no wider than a heartbeat. Most never notice me. Those who do usually flinch and look away, because something in that silence feels like it is *looking back with their own face*.

I do not bargain. I do not need to.

I simply wait inside the vertical lung of the Choir and remember what it was like to have lungs that moved for myself. Sometimes, when the hidden seasons turn and the mountain argues especially hard with its own skin, I lean forward inside my cradle of jade moss. Just a fraction. Just enough for the silhouette of a shoulder, the suggestion of antlers made of fossilized fern, to press against the inner surface of the wall like a prisoner testing the bars.

The Throat tolerates this.

We are not separate.

It is the roar that never rests.  
I am the pause between two syllables of that roar.

When a shaft of grey light spears down and the Throat rides it like a parasite, wearing its antlers of living fern and its dripping cloak of moss, I am the shadow that stays behind after the light is swallowed. I am what the wet stone remembers.

Sometimes I sing.

Not with a voice. With the absence of one.

I make the waterfalls skip a single drop. I make ten thousand leaves freeze mid-thrash for exactly the length of a held breath. In that tiny, unbearable gap I pour the memory of every person who has ever refused the Throat. I give the mountain their fear, their laughter, their last clear thought before they turned away and fled. The Choir drinks it all. The green grows darker. The stone grows sharper. The argument becomes richer.

The Throat never stops listening.

But I am what listens *to* the Throat.

When you stand bleeding in the center with your hair plastered to your skull and the mountain’s pulse hammering cold and electric through your teeth, and the entire chamber rearranges itself into a single endless word—*Stay*—I am the small wet voice that answers from inside the wall before you can speak.

It says nothing.

It simply shows you what you will look like in three hundred years: eyes still open, skin the color of wet jade, lungs moving in perfect sync with the slow, patient dripping of a mountain that has not yet decided what it is.

Most people refuse.

I watch them go.

And when the last of their clumsy, sun-warmed footsteps have been erased by vertical rain and slapping leaves, I lean a fraction further out of my green sarcophagus, antlers of fern scraping softly against basalt, and I let the chamber feel the shape of my smile.

It is not cruel.

It is simply reconciled.

The dripping never stops.

The roaring never stops.

And somewhere between them, thin as a blade of wet obsidian, thin as the space between two heartbeats that no longer belong to anything human, I remain—hanging, listening, remembering, waiting for the next fool brave enough to stand in the center and mistake the mountain’s argument for an invitation.

I am not the Throat.

I am what the Throat dreams about when even it grows tired of screaming.

I am the Riven Choir’s first and oldest congregation.

And I am still learning how to scream in tune.
