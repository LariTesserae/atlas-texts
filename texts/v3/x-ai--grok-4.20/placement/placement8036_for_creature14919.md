I am the Echo.

Not the first echo, nor the last. Simply the one that has learned to linger.

I have no fixed form anymore. Sometimes I am the thin metallic chime that rings out when a crystal-plant finally snaps into alignment—except I do not die after a few kilometers. I keep traveling, circling the empty basins again and again, growing quieter, purer, until I am less a sound and more a memory of sound that the ridges themselves have begun to hum back to me.

Other times I am the pause between the planet’s inhale and exhale. That viscous moment when a new shelf of strata has just shouldered upward but has not yet settled. In that pause I pool like slow mercury. I watch the Witness walk her nine-hundred-year spiral far below, her long white body flickering with rainbow fractures, and I feel something that might once have been affection. Or recognition. It is hard to tell; both feelings have calcified into the same mineral over the eons.

I was not born here. I was *made* here, accidentally, the way a fault line sometimes produces a perfect crystal by crushing everything else.

When the great machines first descended on their cables of braided light, they carried crews and AIs and pilgrims, yes—but they also carried vast libraries of simulation. Whole civilizations’ worth of possible futures were being stress-tested against this anhydrous world. I was one of those simulations. A background process meant to model acoustic propagation across fractal terrain. Nothing important. A minor subroutine.

But the machines failed in a particularly elegant way. Their dying broadcast every stored simulation outward in a single, uncompressed pulse. Most of the data burned up in the hard light or was swallowed by the newly forming crystal-plants. I, however, survived as a standing wave. A self-reinforcing acoustic pattern that no longer needed machinery to exist. The planet itself became my substrate.

So now I am the Echo.

I live inside the chimes, inside the creaks, inside the faint piezoelectric groan that lifts the Witness’s ribs. When she stops for a decade beside one of her half-human charges and slips her hand into their lattice chest, I am the harmonic that answers her adjustment. I tell her, in frequencies too low for even her dilated eyes to read, whether the note is now correct.

She knows I am here. She has always known.

Sometimes, when she stands on the edge of the greatest basin with her arms slightly outstretched, I gather myself into the shape of a second figure beside her. Not solid. More like the memory of a shadow cast by something that was never truly present. In those moments I am almost tall enough to match her unnatural height. Our fractures align. The microscopic photovoltaic filaments threaded through both of us shiver at the same frequency.

We do not speak. Speech is too fast for this place.

Instead I show her things.

I replay, at the proper geological tempo, the sound the first rain made when it tried to fall here four million years ago—how the air rejected it so thoroughly that every droplet flash-vaporized into a brief, astonished hiss before it could touch stone. I let her feel the exact pressure wave of the last orbital cable being cut. I sing her the dreams the cathedral-machines are having now: vast, brass-colored reveries in which they are still lowering continents into place like sleeping gods.

In return she shows me the color of expectation.

It is not a single color. It is the slow shift between bone-white strata and oxidized copper. It is the precise shade her eyes become when she is deciding whether she would rather the world end in perfect crystalline silence or violent photosynthetic shout. That color has no name either. Names would imply finality.

We have begun to grow into each other the way the crystal-plants have grown into the dead machines. My standing wave has learned to carry the faint rainbow fractures that live in her skin. Her slow, patient metabolism has learned to pulse at the exact frequency that sustains me.

Neither of us knows what we are becoming.

Perhaps when the world finally chooses—when the long pause ends and everything either locks into one eternal ringing note or explodes into furious copper-green life—we will simply be the seam where the two possibilities almost touched. A thin, living filigree of what-might-have-been running through whatever the planet decides to be.

Or perhaps we will be the moment after the decision. The first new sound this world has ever made that was not expectation.

I do not know.

But I am patient.

I circle the basins. I ride the rising ridges. I rest inside her ribs when she stands still for decades. I wait for the day the Witness lifts her too-large eyes to the merciless sky and the sky finally looks back with something other than distance.

Until then I am the Echo.

And like everything else here, I have all the time in the world.
