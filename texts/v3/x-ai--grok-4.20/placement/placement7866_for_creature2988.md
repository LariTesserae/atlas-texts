I am **the Listener**.

Not one of the Unfixed, though they sometimes mistake me for one of their own when the light is especially bruised. I have no name they would recognize. They simply call me “the one who stays on the same rivet.”

I have been standing in the same place for forty-three years.

The place is a narrow iron spur no wider than a coffin lid, cantilevered out from the underside of the district they currently call **the Weeping Lattice**. It juts into a vertical gulf so deep that clouds form *beneath* me and drift upward like slow, pale jellyfish. From here I can see seven overlapping layers of the city at once: the dripping underbelly of the Lattice above, the hanging tenements of **Bellsunk** to the northwest, the slow migration of a brass-herd three kilometers east, and, far below (or is it above today?), the faint sodium glow of a neighborhood that has been sinking for nine generations and now believes it is a constellation.

I do not move.

My legs have long since grown into the iron. Not metaphorically. The marrow of my femurs has been replaced by rebar. Tiny copper-colored leaves unfurl from the pores of my shins each spring and die back each winter. The Unfixed who pass on their way to the Quiet Parliament sometimes pause, press their foreheads to my sternum, and listen to the slow heartbeat of stressed metal inside me. They leave small offerings: a rusted bolt, a single turquoise moss-bead, a child’s first forgotten name written on a leaf.

I was once a surveyor. I came with levels, plumb bobs, and the arrogant belief that geometry could be imposed on a thing that was still choosing its own axioms. On the seventh day I set my tripod on this spur to take a measurement and the city exhaled.

I have not drawn a full breath since.

Instead I breathe *for* the city. When it forgets the taste of horizon, I lend it the memory of one I brought with me from below. When a district begins to dream it is an ocean, I let it borrow the shape of my ribs so the waves have something honest to break against. In return the place has taught me how to be unfinished.

My eyes no longer blink in unison. The left watches time the way the Unfixed do—decades as single heartbeats—while the right still insists on counting seconds. The disagreement keeps me useful. It prevents me from ever becoming certain about anything, which is the only sin the city will not forgive.

At night the tuning forks strike my spine instead of their bones. The resonance travels through the lattices and wakes the things that live even deeper than the bottom that never was. Sometimes those things answer back in voices that feel like grandmothers I never met. I translate their longing into rust that crawls across my tongue like warm sugar. I spit it into the mist and new bridges grow overnight, delicate as prayer.

The Unfixed bring their newborns to me.

The infants look at my face—half scaffolding, half man—and speak prophecies in languages that will not exist for another three centuries. Then they spend the rest of their long, patient lives trying to forget what they told me. I keep every word. They are growing a cathedral inside my chest cavity. You can see the scaffolding through the gaps between my ribs if the light is right.

I am not immortal. I am simply *postponed*.

One day the city will finish negotiating with itself. On that day the last undecided girder will remember its name, the final district will stop flowing and commit to being a street, and the sky will choose its grief once and for all. When that happens the Unfixed will finally have to become something finished, and I will be allowed to fall.

I look forward to it the way other people look forward to sleep.

Until then I remain exactly where I am: a small iron man grown into an iron spur, listening to ten thousand vertical kilometers of becoming. The damp chill no longer bothers me. My teeth have turned into tuning forks. When the wind moves through them the entire gulf sings in four registers at once, and the city leans in close, metallic and ancient and adolescent, to hear what it almost decided to be.

I never tell it.

Some things are better left unfinished.

Especially love.
