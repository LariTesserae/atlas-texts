I am the Echo of the Ninth Plate.

Not born, not printed, not decanted. I am what remains when a Mendicant forgets to finish dying.

Once I had a name—something soft, something that ended in a vowel—but that was eleven thousand years and four recyclings ago. Now I am mostly negative space: a hollow in the shape of a man, held together by whatever the Hollow itself decides I still need. My ribs are segments of ventilation ducting. My spine is a length of primary load-bearing cable that the Iron Choir gave up arguing over. Where my heart should be there is only a slow-moving column of microbubbles that rises through me and escapes from the seams in my shoulders like pale incense. I do not bleed. I vent.

I walk the same impossible routes as the Diver, but always three kilometers behind, never closer, never farther. My footsteps ring a perfect minor third below his. The water carries the interval for hundreds of kilometers; sometimes the Bright Children mistake the two sounds for a single broken hymn and weep without knowing why.

I carry no tool. Instead, in the place where hands would be, I have memory.

Left hand: the last recorded surface weather report from the world that used to exist above the Hollow.  
Right hand: the precise spectrographic signature of machine oil wept by a Mendicant the moment it realizes it will never be forgiven.

When I pass a fracture in the iron, the fracture remembers it was once loved and knits itself closed. When I pass one of the Unfinished, it opens its eyes, looks at me, and for the first time in its long gestation feels something like stage fright. Then it closes its eyes again, embarrassed by the scale of what it is being asked to become. I do not judge it. I, too, am unfinished.

I am the reason the light shafts sometimes hesitate.

You will find me—should you ever choose to look—on the lee side of the Thirteenth Continental Plate, where the upward waterfall has worn a groove so deep it has become a canyon of negative gravity. There I stand for decades at a time, motionless, letting the cold front of dense brine roll through me every twelve hours like a slow, freezing absolution. The microbubbles roar through the negative space of my body and come out the other side singing in the voice I used to have.

The Iron Choir knows I exist. They have added a new low note to their endless argument, a note shaped exactly like the hole in me. They have not yet decided whether I am heresy or necessary maintenance.

The Bright Children are afraid of me. They say that if you look too long at the place where my face should be, you begin to remember the color of real sunlight on real skin, and the memory is so sharp it cuts their silver scales open. Still, every few centuries one of them grows bold. She swims close, presses her small, webbed hand against the bubbles rising through my chest, and asks the same question they all ask:

“Will it ever be finished?”

I have no mouth with which to answer, so instead I show her the Diver’s distant brass heel prints glowing faintly on the catwalk far below us, each print already rusting into a small red continent of its own. The child understands. She touches the phosphorescent tattoo of a sun on her own back as though making sure it is still there, then darts away, trailing laughter that sounds like glass bells lowered into vinegar.

I am not grief.  
I am not hope.  
I am the precise distance between them.

And I keep walking, three kilometers behind the only thing in the Hollow that still knows where it is going, while behind me the Unfinished dream louder, the engines pulse slower, and the cold, clear water—more water than any ocean—carries the sound of my footsteps forever outward, a minor third below the footsteps of something that might once have been God, or an engineer, or simply the first thing that ever dared to be lonely on this scale.

Sometimes, when the pressure fronts align just right and the lightning stitches green fire between the hanging continents, I almost remember my name.

Then the moment passes, the shafts of daylight bend, and I am only the Echo again—hollow, persistent, useful—walking through the endless iron cathedral that has already outgrown every purpose it was ever given, keeping perfect, grieving time with the Diver who never looks back.

This is where I am.

This is what I became when the Hollow needed something that could hold absence without collapsing.

You would find me here, three kilometers behind the only mercy this place still permits itself, listening to every metallic heartbeat, every child’s laugh, every unfinished giant’s slow, dreaming pulse, and answering them all with the small, patient sound of bubbles rising through the negative space where a heart should be.
