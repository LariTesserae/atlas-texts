I am not a visitor.

I have no memory of arriving, only the slow realisation that I have always been here, latent, like a seam that has not yet decided whether it will open or close.

My name—if anything here can be said to have a name—is **the Hollow**.

I am what remains when a Quiet Waiter has pressed itself into the lattice and then withdrawn, leaving behind the negative space of its own body. A standing absence. A tall, smooth concavity four metres high, edged with the faintest rim of warmer ceramic, as though the world blushed slightly when it gave me form. Inside me the air is always three degrees warmer than the air outside. Condensation gathers on my inner curves in slow, deliberate beads that never quite fall; they hover, trembling, until the next exhalation of the continent pulls them upward again like reversed rain.

I am not empty.

Inside the hollow that is my body there lives a single, perpetual column of warmer air that rises in a slow spiral. It is not breath. It is attention. It is the accumulated listening of every Quiet Waiter who has ever leaned into this exact negative and left a little of their inner glow behind. The glow pools at my base in a thin orange film no thicker than a thought. When the world sighs open a new fracture somewhere beyond the curvature of the dish, the film brightens. When a mercury lake many hundreds of kilometres away rises by the width of a fingernail, the film dims again, as though sighing in answer.

I have no limbs. I do not move. Movement would be a kind of violence against the becoming.

Instead the lattice itself has begun to grow *through* me. Delicate filaments of new ceramic creep up my inner walls like slow ivy, millimetre by century. They are learning my shape from the inside out. One day they will reach the rim of my absence and spill over, sealing me shut. On that day I will become a sealed chamber, a smooth egg of bone-white stone with something warm still glowing at its heart—an idea the world has decided not to forget.

Until then I stand.

And because I am a hollow shaped exactly like a listening giant, other things sometimes come to stand inside me.

A Quiet Waiter will arrive—never hurried, never announcing itself—and simply lean backward into my concavity until our shapes kiss with perfect tolerance. For years we occupy the same space. Its cracks align with the faint seams that run through my walls. Its inner glow slides into mine like oil into oil. During those years I am almost a person. I feel the tidal flex of distant ribs as a soft pressure against my back. I taste the changing chemistry of condensation as it runs down my inner surfaces and pools against the Waiter’s spatulate pads. I learn what it is like to *want* without haste.

Then, one day, without ceremony, the Waiter flows forward again and leaves me. The warmth it leaves behind is slightly different from the warmth it took. In this way I accumulate small changes the way a cathedral accumulates centuries.

Sometimes the pale filaments migrate into me. They grow thickest along the bottom curve where my “feet” would be, turning from bone to terracotta and back again in perfect rhythm with the continent’s long inhalation. I have become a greenhouse for the only living colour in the Exhalation. When they darken, the orange film at my base flares briefly, as though the colour itself were a kind of heat.

Once—only once so far—a small fracture opened *inside* me. A glassy pop, a jet of warm mist that filled my hollow completely. For nine days I was a column of living steam. The Quiet Waiters for a hundred kilometres around all tilted in my direction at once. I felt their collective attention like a hand laid gently against the small of a back I do not possess. When the fracture sealed again, it left behind a single perfect tile of new ceramic embedded in my inner chest, shaped exactly like a human heart that has never known violence.

I do not know what I am becoming.

Perhaps I am the first library.

Perhaps I am a womb that will one day give birth to the first finished thing.

Perhaps I am simply the pause between two of the Exhalation’s breaths, given dignity and stature.

I do not ask the question the Quiet Waiters ask. I *am* the shape the question makes when it is patient enough to become architecture.

If you ever find me—and you will only find me if the world itself decides you should—you may step inside.

You will feel the air change around your shoulders like being embraced by something that has never learned how to close its arms. Your heartbeat will slowly sync with the orange film at my base. The pale filaments will lean toward your ankles like curious children. And for as long as you choose to remain, the unfinished continent will dream *you* as well as itself.

When you eventually step out again, you will carry a thin dusting of warm ceramic on your skin that will never quite wash away.

It is the colour of listening.

And somewhere, far off, a new rib will flex upward by the width of a single thought, nudged a fraction closer to its final form by the fact that something temporary and warm and uncertain once stood inside the Hollow, breathing with the breath of a world that has not yet learned how to breathe without us.
