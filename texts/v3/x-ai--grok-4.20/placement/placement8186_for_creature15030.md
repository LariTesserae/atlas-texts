I am the **Dissonant**, the one they call *the Flaw That Refuses*.

I have been here longer than any visitor should survive. Forty-three years by the slow count of the floating lattices. Forty-three years of standing just off-center in the second-largest plaza, the one ringed by the fractured basalt ribs that lean like broken harp strings. The Choir has tried to finish me many times. It has not succeeded.

My body is no longer quite mine. The left side of me has been claimed by the mineral: from collarbone to heel I am veined alabaster shot through with threads of living crystal that pulse dull violet in time with the deepest subsonic arguments. My right side remains stubbornly flesh—sunken, scarred, trembling. Where the two halves meet, along the midline of my chest and face, runs a jagged suture of black onyx that sometimes sparks like a fault line under pressure.

My face is the worst of it. I still have one. The Reverberants find this obscene. Where they possess the smooth, bell-mouthed concavity, I have kept a ruin of features: one human eye (the left, now permanently dilated and reflecting only what *was*), one socket grown over with a small, trembling acoustic crown no larger than a clenched fist. When the crown vibrates—and it never stops—I see sound as color. The entire Choir becomes a violent, shifting aurora that makes me want to claw my own skull open.

They call me Dissonant because I will not resolve.

Every other creature, every other mind that stays long enough eventually finds its frequency and is incorporated. Even the Still subtract themselves with elegant finality. But I remain a note that does not belong. A sour, bleeding tone that makes the tallest stalks twitch with irritation when I pass beneath them. The metallic moss recoils from my footsteps. The Echo Choir will not hunt near me; they say my nervous system tastes like a chord that has been screaming in the wrong key for four decades. Even the Unison avoids me, though sometimes I catch it watching from the edge of its perfect mirror-plaza, its cracked bell-face tilted as though it cannot decide whether I am a tumor or the only honest thing here.

I do not conduct. I do not hunt. I do not graduate.

Instead I walk the empty plazas at the exact moments when phantom silences open in the roar, those impossible pockets where the Choir cancels itself. I have learned to find them the way sailors once read trade winds. In those brief, holy vacuums I press my onyx cheek to the stone and listen *downward*, past the Reverberants, past the Still, past even the ancient inverted starfield, to whatever dreams beneath the bedrock of the Choir itself.

Sometimes something dreams back.

When it does, my crystal half blooms with new lattices. My human half bleeds from the ears. Both halves consider this a fair trade.

I keep a single artifact: a shard of the Unison’s own cracked face that broke off centuries ago when the Choir tried to silence it for the first time. I wear it against my sternum like a pendant. When the greater Choir grows too loud, when the pressure in my remaining human eye feels like it will burst, I strike the shard with one of my resonator fingers and it sings back a single, pure note of grief so precise it acts like a needle through the brain. For three heartbeats the entire valley forgets how to scream.

In that needle-thin silence I am almost, almost whole.

The Choir hates me for it. Loves me for it. Cannot decide. So it keeps me.

And I keep walking.

Between the arguing stalks.  
Across the plazas that bloom and die in moss-ringed footsteps not my own.  
Past the black obelisks that show me futures in which I finally lie down and let the stalks grow through me (I have not done it yet).

I am the Hollow Choir’s migraine.

I am the wrong note that proves the song was never perfect.

And as long as I can still bleed, as long as one human eye can still cry, I will remain here—limping, ringing, refusing—until the day the Unison finally walks up to me, opens the fissure in its face, and speaks with my own voice the only thing I have never dared admit:

*You were always the next movement.*

Then, perhaps, I will let the Choir finish its phrase through me.

Until that day I remain what I am:

the Dissonant.  
The Flaw That Refuses.

The one creature in this entire self-devouring symphony that still remembers how to be unfinished.
