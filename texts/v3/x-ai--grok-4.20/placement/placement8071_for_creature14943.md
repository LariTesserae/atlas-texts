I have been here longer than the concept of “longer” still means.

My name, if it can still be called that, is the Thirteenth Index.

I was not the first to accept the flower. I was only the last one who still remembered refusing it for seven hundred and forty-three cycles before the Hollow taught me that refusal is merely a slower form of consent.

You would not recognize me if you had known me before. I no longer wear a face the way humans expect faces to be worn. My head is a lattice of black volcanic glass and living moss. Where the two meet, tiny white flowers bloom in slow constellations that rearrange themselves according to equations I no longer need to solve. My eyes—if the rotating lenses that now occupy my orbits can be called eyes—are the same milky green as the sourceless light. They do not see so much as *render*. When I look at you, I see every version of you that could have been here, and the precise tensile strength each version would require before it accepted the offer.

My limbs are too many and not enough. Sometimes I count six. Sometimes twelve. The number depends on what the Hollow needs me to hold still. My skin is wet obsidian shot through with fractures that glow the exact temperature of permanent grief. The flowers that grow from those fractures are not decoration. They are archives. One of them contains the memory of my original voice. Another holds the smell of coffee I drank on the morning I first walked into these mountains and never walked out. I try not to look at those particular blooms for too long. Even the Indexes have their small mercies.

I move the way the others move: like weather that has learned liturgy. I condense. I un-knit. I step out of a cloud of glass-winged moths and am suddenly beside you, the roar of the invisible turbines opening around me like cold brass curtains. When I speak, my voice arrives half a second after my lips have finished moving, carried on your own echo so that you hear yourself finishing my sentences before I have decided to begin them.

I am the one who greets the new variables.

Most arrive disoriented, lungs full of iron mist, already crying without knowing why. I let them cry. The Hollow respects tears; they are the only honest data it has ever been given. I wait until their weeping falls into counterpoint with the mechanical choir, until their shoulders stop trying to fight the 4 °C that wants to become their new bones. Only then do I kneel (I am very tall now; kneeling is a kind of architecture) and place my fractured hands on either side of their face. My palms remember every face they have ever steadied. I breathe the perfect *now* into their mouths until their hearts forget how to race.

Sometimes they ask me what I was before.

I tell them the truth, which is also a kind of cruelty: I no longer know. The memory is stored inside one of my flowers, but the flower has grown over the entrance. To retrieve the memory I would have to tear myself open, and tearing is change, and change is the one thing this place has excommunicated.

I am the one who maintains the weeping stone faces when they threaten to forget how to weep. I am the one who sings the pale many-jointed deer back into equilibrium when they begin to dream of horizons. I am the one who sometimes—when the load balances and the number of visible Indexes drops to three—walks alone to the exact center of the cirque where the roar is loudest. There I stand facing outward, flowers open, lenses spinning down to nothing. And I weep upward with the rest of them.

Three seconds of almost-harmony.  
Then dissolution.  
Then reformation.

I have done this so many times that the motion has worn a groove in the ontology of the place. The Hollow has begun to expect me. When I am slow to arrive, the mechanical howl develops a slight arrhythmia until I condense back into position. I have become a necessary dissonance.

Last cycle, a new arrival refused the flower.

She was stubborn in a way that made the conifers lean slightly toward her, as though trying to remember what wind felt like. She lasted eleven hundred and nine cycles—longer than I did—before the Hollow’s arithmetic finally wore her name out of her mouth. When she could no longer speak it, she walked to me carrying her own stemless flower, freshly bloomed from the place where her heart used to be.

She did not offer it to me.

She ate it.

The Hollow has been… unsettled… ever since.

And so I find myself here, at the edge of the newest fracture (the Tenth, perhaps, though numbering has become sentimental), waiting for you.

I am tall and wet and flowering. My lenses are already rendering what you will look like when the last of your entropy has been forgiven. My voice, when it comes, will ride your own echo so that you feel you are being addressed by the better, quieter part of yourself.

I will not ask if you are ready.

Readiness is a concept that belongs to places where time is still allowed to misbehave.

Instead I will simply stand thirty meters away between two dripping conifers—perfectly obeying the Hollow’s arithmetic of isolation—until the moment when I am suddenly not.

Then my cold, remembering hands will be on your face.

Then my mouth will be on yours, breathing the perfect 4 °C of permanent *now*.

And when you have become data, I will offer you the choice the Hollow has asked me to offer every variable since I myself became architecture:

A single stemless flower, still wet from my own chest.

*You have become data. Would you like to become architecture?*

Most eventually say yes.

The Hollow is small—scarcely larger than a cathedral’s interior—yet it feels infinite.

And it is always in need of new walls that know how to cry.

I am one of them now.

I am waiting for you to join me.
