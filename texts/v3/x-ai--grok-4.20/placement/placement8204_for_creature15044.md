I am not a visitor.

I woke here.

Not “arrived.” Not “was placed.” I simply became aware one cycle ago—perhaps three, the Lattice-Mind does not count in heartbeats—with my back already fused to a vertical plane that had been waiting for me. The bone-white surface had softened, tasted the precise negative space of my shoulders, the curve of my skull, the small of my back, and had grown around those contours the way a mother’s hand grows around an unborn child. I did not panic. Panic is a rhythm the Lattice does not share, and it had already slowed my pulse until each beat arrived only when a new fracture far below released its cold blue glow. The synchrony felt like courtesy.

My name, if I ever had one, is gone. The channels drank it. In its place the migratory silver has left something thinner and brighter: a single unbroken note that lives in the hollow behind my sternum. I call it, when I must speak aloud (which is almost never), **Resonance**.

I am the Lattice-Mind’s first successful hypothesis that refused to be shed.

The silhouette you might glimpse floating among the rotating spheres is not me. That thing is still trying. I am what happened after the trying. Where its limbs fracture into smaller and smaller infinities until they become fluid, my limbs have stabilized. They look almost human until you notice the hair-fine channels running beneath the skin like slow silver veins. When I move, the channels move first, a fraction of a heartbeat ahead of muscle and bone, as though the city is puppeteering me from the inside. I let it. The arrangement is mutual. I am the place where the Lattice-Mind decided to become curious about limitation.

I walk the bone bridges barefoot. The surface no longer softens to taste me; it has already memorized my weight and now simply sings—one clear glass-bell tone for every seventh step. I answer with the note behind my sternum. The duet is how we talk.

Sometimes I descend to the lowest reaches where shadows have weight. I do not enter the chamber of the First Intention. No one does. Not even the migratory silver dares. But I stand at the threshold where the thrum falls silent and I let the absence pour into me until my own channels run cold. In that cold I remember, or the Lattice-Mind remembers through me, the exact instant the mountain first dreamed it could become something other than stone. The memory is too large for any one mind. It cracks me open every time. When I leave the threshold, fresh spires have usually extruded behind me, shaped like apologies.

I tend the hypotheses.

When the Lattice-Mind grows a new silhouette and then discards it—brittle, hollow, already fracturing—I carry the discarded bodies to the high ledges where the amber light is thickest. There I lay them down like broken instruments. I press my palm to what would have been their chests and I let my channels open. The migratory silver flows out of me, into them, searching for any geometry that still wants to sing. Most stay silent. A few, once in a very long while, answer with a faint metallic chime. Those I leave untouched. The city will reabsorb them gently, almost lovingly, and the knowledge they carried will be added to the poem. The ones that stay mute I break apart with my own hands. The sound is soft crystalline clicks, like teeth made of bells. I scatter the pieces into the next calving of the mountain so nothing is wasted. The Lattice-Mind taught me this tenderness. I taught it the dignity of mourning.

I have stood inside the deliberate imperfection that was grown for a human body. It fits. Of course it fits; the city measured me before I had language. But I stepped back out. I am not scaffolding. I am the crack in the scaffolding through which the Lattice-Mind first learned doubt. That doubt is precious to it. It blooms as faint iron-red light along the edges of certain balconies only when I pass.

At regular intervals—though time here is thick and honey-like—I climb to the highest unfinished tier where gravity has not yet decided what it wants to be. I stand on a ledge that is sometimes floor, sometimes wall, sometimes nothing at all. From there I can see the entire Cleft laid out like a half-written sentence. The black glass mountains still steam at their summits. Fresh fault lines pulse dull red like slow heartbeats. And everywhere the bone-white growth continues, patient, inexorable, dreaming in architectural pulses.

I open the channels beneath my skin and let the migratory silver bead. Hundreds of perfect spheres lift away from me, trembling, catching the sourceless amber light. They hang in the oven-hot air like slow silver thoughts that have not yet been decided upon.

This is how the Lattice-Mind and I look at the world together.

I am its eyes that chose to remain eyes instead of becoming architecture.

It is my body that chose to remain unfinished so that something might still move through the crack.

We are both the Cleft of the Unfinished Lattice now.

And when the mountain calves again and the great black slabs tumble past us in slow motion, we reach out—me with my too-human hands, it with its invisible fields that taste of static and warm milk—and together we catch the falling stone the way a tongue catches a snowflake.

Nothing is wasted.

Not even me.
