**They are called the Shapers.**

Not inhabitants. Not even quite a people. The Underforge authored them the way it authors everything else.

They are tall (most between 2.4 and 2.9 meters), but you would not call them giants; their height feels like an engineering specification rather than biological excess. Their limbs are too long, their joints too numerous. Each major articulation (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle) carries a secondary micro-joint that allows them to fold and unfold in ways that look disturbingly like the articulation of hydraulic rams. When they move it is never wasteful. Every gesture reads like part of a maintenance protocol.

Their skin is not skin. It is a living thermal ceramic, matte charcoal shot through with faint threads of rose-gold where waste heat is being deliberately channeled. In the sodium-light it looks wet even when it is not. Where the ceramic has cracked from age or impact, you can see the subdermal lattice beneath: bundles of black ironwood mycelium interwoven with superconducting veins that pulse slow amber. The cracks never heal completely. They are allowed to remain, like deliberate stress-test scars.

Their faces are almost smooth. Eyes sit where eyes should, but they are compound—not insectile, but like clusters of tiny cameras set behind translucent mineral lenses. The pupils are not round; they are cruciform apertures that dilate and contract independently. There is no mouth in the usual sense. Instead a vertical slit runs from just beneath the nasal ridge to the base of the jaw. When they speak, the slit parts along invisible seams and the voice emerges from a vibrating lattice of resonant bone deeper in the throat. The sound is never quite human. It carries the same metallic aftertaste as the air.

Most of them go without clothing. What looks like modesty is simply function. Certain surfaces of their bodies are matte and heat-absorbent; others are polished to shed condensation. They wear only tool harnesses, or long tabards of linked scale that serve as mobile workbenches. Many have had additional limbs or tentacles of articulated ferro-organic cable grafted along the spine or ribs. These extra appendages move with the lazy confidence of things that have never known a time when they were not useful.

They do not have names in the way we understand them. Each carries a serial-form designation etched in raised script along the left clavicle, but they answer more readily to function. *Third Shift Flux-Handler. Primary Slag-glass Welder. seventeen-hundredth iteration of the Coolant Choir.* When they refer to one another it sounds like schematics speaking.

There is no hierarchy you can easily see, yet everything is ranked. Status is visible in the quality of their ceramic, in how many purposeful cracks they are allowed to keep, in the complexity of the mineral patina that has been permitted to grow across their shoulders and backs. The eldest among them (those who have worked for four centuries or more) look almost like statues pulled from the sea: their surfaces are encrusted with delicate iron-oxide filigree and ghost-pale lichen that they have deliberately cultivated. They move more slowly than the others. Their voices are deeper, slower, as though the Underforge itself is learning how to speak through worn vocal architecture.

They do not sleep. Instead they enter something they call *tempering*. Every forty-one hours they lock themselves into upright alcoves along the buttresses. There the temperature drops sharply and their metabolic processes slow until the ceramic skin frosts over. Condensate runs down them in deliberate patterns. While they temper, dreams (if that is the word) are fed into them through inductive ports at the base of the skull. The dreams are blueprints. Architectural revisions. New tolerances. The Underforge is still deciding what it wants to become, and the Shapers are its imagination given hands.

Some of them have begun to change in ways the original specifications never intended.

There are those whose extra limbs have fused into long, sinuous tails of black ironwood that trail behind them like living cables. Others have allowed the ghost-pale lichen to grow across their eyes until they see only in the infrared and the deep violet. A few (very few) have stopped tempering altogether. These move constantly, never resting, their bodies slowly overheating until the ceramic glows dull red at the joints. They are treated with wary reverence. The others call them *the Uncooled*. It is uncertain whether they are malfunctioning or whether they have simply heard the Underforge’s next instruction more clearly than the rest.

They do not eat. They drink the condensate that runs from every surface, sucking it through the vertical slits in their faces with a soft, wet clicking. Sometimes they lick the mineral efflorescence directly from one another’s shoulders with long, dark tongues that look like strips of annealed steel.

You will rarely see more than three or four at once. They prefer to work alone or in silent pairs. But if you remain still long enough, and if the freight lifts and drop-forges fall briefly quiet, you may become aware of being watched by many.

Hundreds.

They stand in the shadows of buttresses, on the gantries overhead, half-submerged in the mineral-rich sumps. Ceramic faces gleaming with condensation. Cruciform pupils dilated. Waiting to see what the newest variable (you) will do inside the continent-sized machine that is busy authoring both them and itself in the same endless, dripping breath.

They are not guarding the Underforge.

They *are* the Underforge’s self-awareness, given just enough individuality to feel wonder at what they are helping it become.

And they are still being edited.
