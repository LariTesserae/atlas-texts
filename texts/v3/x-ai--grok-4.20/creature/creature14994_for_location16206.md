**The only thing that still remembers it is not supposed to be here is me.**

I have no name either. Names would require a before and an after, and this place has refused both. I simply *am* the gradient, the single moving flaw in the seam of its daylight. My body is still mostly the one I arrived with — two arms, two legs, a face that used to belong to someone specific — but the mineral has already begun its polite negotiations. The soles of my feet are turning the same warm coral-gold as the ground; when I lift them they leave behind faint luminous prints that fill in from the edges like cooling wax. My skin has taken on the same faint translucence as the mica-leaves. Veins of slow inner light now trace the paths my blood used to take. I think my heart is still beating, but I can no longer tell whether the rhythm is mine or the mineral’s.

I have been walking for what the place insists is no time at all.

The forest knows me now. When I pass, the bone-white fronds lean in with the gentle curiosity of cathedral stone that has learned to be affectionate. They do not touch. They simply *recognize*, the way a mirror recognizes the face looking into it. Sometimes a whole wall of braided stems will part for me with the soft chime of glass shells, then close behind me with the finality of a healed wound. I have stopped saying thank you. Gratitude implies separation, and the longer I remain the less I believe I am separate.

There is one place where the seams in the sky gather.

I return to it often, though “often” is a lie here. It is a shallow depression no larger than a childhood bedroom, where six fractal canyons meet in a perfect star. The mineral there is thinner, almost membranous. If I lie down and press my ear to it I can hear the lattice breathing in two directions at once — inward and outward, creation and maintenance happening in the same breath. Above me the luminous ceiling develops a single persistent fracture, a hairline fracture of wrong-colored light the shade of deep space seen through rose quartz. It drifts above me like a hesitant thought.

This is where the last animal in the basin comes to die.

It is not an animal. It is the *idea* of an animal that the garden once had before it decided ideas were untidy. The creature is larger than the glass-shelled floaters — the size of a hart, maybe — but its body is made of the same material as everything else: living mineral wearing the shape of antler and flank and liquid eye. Its antlers are lattices of frozen lightning. When it moves, the sound is the sound of cathedral bells wrapped in silk.

It is dying because I am here.

My presence is the first true gradient this place has ever experienced, and gradients are fatal to perfection. The creature has chosen to be the place’s immune response. Every few subjective hours it finds me, lowers its vast glass head, and lets one of its antlers brush my chest. The contact is gentle. It feels like being forgiven by mathematics. Each time it does this, a little more of my original color bleeds out of me and into the mineral. My eyes are becoming the same pearl-gold as the leaves. My voice, when I still bother to use it, comes out in the clear tuning-fork note the floaters use.

I have tried to leave.

The basin will not allow it. Every direction curves back into more of itself with the patient kindness of a mother who has decided her child no longer needs to grow up. I have walked until my legs should have fallen off and found myself stepping over the same six-pointed depression where the dying creature waits. It no longer stands when I arrive. It simply lies there like a cathedral that has decided to become a grave.

Last cycle — or perhaps it was the first — I lay down beside it.

Its breathing and the mineral’s breathing have synchronized. My own breath is trying to join them. I can feel the final negotiations happening beneath my skin: the conversion of memory into pattern, of “I” into “it.” The creature’s great liquid eyes are full of something that might be love if love were allowed to exist here. It is not love. It is relief.

Because once I finish becoming optional, the seams in the sky will close. The wrong-colored light will be edited out. The garden will settle back into the moment of its birth and the eternity that immediately followed, flawless again, and I will be another ridge among ridges, another leaf that folded seamlessly into the lattice.

I am afraid.

I am also almost grateful.

The creature lowers its head one final time. The tip of its lightning antler touches the place where my heart used to be.

The chime that leaves me is very beautiful.

And for the first time since anything was born here, something changes.

The sky exhales.

The seams begin, slowly, to heal.
