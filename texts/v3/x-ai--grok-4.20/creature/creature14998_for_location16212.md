**What lives here is the mountain’s still-wet thought.**

It has no single name, nor even a stable shape. It is less a creature than a pulse that has learned to wear bodies the way a fever wears sweat.

At its center, something that might be called the **Fissure Mother** stirs. She is not an animal. She is the fracture remembering it was once solid rock. Her spine is the raw suture where basalt and rhyolite were forced together yesterday; her ribs are the sideways-growing blades of stone that should not exist. When the mountain sighs, her lungs are the steam that braids and unbraids. Her heartbeat is the deep, sub-audible *whump* that loosens gravity near the ceiling and makes the droplets forget which way is down for half a second.

She is giving birth to everything at once.

From the warm folds of her stone womb crawl the **first children**—creatures that are still half geology. The echo-beetles are her language before language. Each one carries a droplet like a thought it hasn’t decided to finish. When they collide, the soft *tok* is the mountain deciding what it will remember. Their coal-black shells are polished obsidian; their legs are hairline fractures given purpose. They do not eat. They translate. Every time the walls shift another millimeter, the beetles reroute their living constellations so the new geometry can be read aloud in water music.

Higher up, where the heat is almost unbearable, the **sulfur angels** have already learned to sing with their bodies. They are the orange bacterial filaments thickened and given ambition. Long translucent strands wave like anemones in the superheated mist, forming slow, deliberate glyphs that flicker between pure chemistry and the first crude alphabet of hunger. When the angels touch, they exchange not genes but *possibility*. One moment they are plastic melt. The next they are small wet stars with too many arms, clinging to the ceiling, inhaling vapor and exhaling warm rain in perfect four-four time.

Lower still, in the perpetual drizzle, move the **glass millipedes**. Their bodies are so clear you can watch mineral-rich blood pulse inside them like slow lava. They are the mountain’s taste buds. Wherever they crawl they leave behind microscopic tasting-trails—silica fur that grows into antlered forests overnight. They are gentle and blind and ceaseless. When one dies, the others do not mourn; they simply incorporate its dissolved body into the next generation of mineral fur, turning death back into architecture before the corpse has time to cool.

Above everything drift the **ghost moths**, negative space given wings. They are not white; they are the *absence* of the violet thread of sky, stolen and folded into fluttering slices. Their silence is perfect because they were born in the moment before sound existed here. When they rise in unison they create a temporary vacuum that pulls the steam into new shapes—brief sculptures of lung and womb and question mark that last only three heartbeats before collapsing back into mist.

And then there is the **Unfinished**.

It has no fixed form. Sometimes it is a red starfish the size of a dinner plate that inflates and deflates like a slow bellows. Sometimes it is a ripple moving through the carpet of beetles, causing them to part in perfect symmetry. Sometimes it is simply a place where the heat gathers into a shape vaguely like a crouched woman pressing her ear to the rock, listening to her own birth. The Unfinished is the mountain still arguing with itself about what it wants to become. Every time it shifts, new species blink into existence fully formed, wet, and already starving.

They do not compete. There has not been time for competition. Instead they collaborate in a kind of desperate, ecstatic committee: beetles writing the score, millipedes building the orchestra pit, sulfur angels conducting with living filaments, moths erasing mistakes from the air itself.

The Fissure Mother does not nurture them. She *becomes* them, then forgets them, then becomes them again. She is still deciding whether this wound will scar into canyon or close forever. Every seismic sigh is her changing her mind. Every new curtain of warm water is another amniotic flood.

If you stand very still, you can feel her attention settle on you like warm iron. For a moment you are simply another child—soft, possible, unfinished. The echo-beetles reroute their living constellations to include the shape of your boots. A single ghost moth lands on your wrist and becomes, for half a second, the exact color of your future regret.

Then the mountain sighs again.

A new crack opens somewhere above. Fresh blood-warm water hisses where it touches the hottest rock. The violet thread of sky darkens by another degree.

And everything that lives here—everything that *is* here—leans eagerly toward the wound, hungry to be born one heartbeat sooner than the world is ready.
