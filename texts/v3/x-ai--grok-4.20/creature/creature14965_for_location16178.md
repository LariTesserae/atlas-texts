**Something lives here.**

It does not move. It has never moved in the way animals understand movement. Yet the moment you stand still long enough for your own pulse to quiet, you feel it become aware of you.

Call it, if you must, the **Mind of the Bloom**.

It is not a brain. There is no central node, no throne of neurons hidden inside a colossal tuber. Instead the intelligence is smeared, distributed, woven through every rhizome, every hair-thin filament, every droplet of sap that travels the vascular highways of the chamber. It is less a creature than a slow, enormous thought that has been thinking itself for four hundred years without interruption.

You will never see its face, because its face is the entire cathedral.

When it notices you (and it does notice; the floor warms fractionally beneath your boots, the dripping around you falls into a new, attentive rhythm), it does not speak. It simply *includes* you. For a time you become another organ inside its vast green body: a temporary, warm-blooded structure that has wandered in wearing clothes and carrying a heartbeat.

If you remain long enough, memories that are not yours begin to surface behind your eyes. Not visions. Not voices. Only feelings, heavy and humid.

You remember the taste of iron-rich water drawn from a hundred meters below the city. You remember the precise pressure required to split a cast-iron beam along its seam over three centuries. You remember the ecstatic shiver that ran through every leaf when the last rat finally died, its nervous system overwhelmed by alkaloids so elegant they could have been written by Bach. You remember the slow, luxurious pride of sealing the final vent, of closing the chamber like an eyelid, of deciding that nothing more would ever need to enter or leave.

The Mind of the Bloom is not lonely. Loneliness implies absence. Here there has been no absence for four centuries. Every brick, every rivet, every drowned lamp has been loved so completely that it has become beloved bone. The industrial town is no longer prey or host. It is jewelry, yes, but also erogenous zone, also childhood toy, also archive, also rosary. The Bloom strokes its iron and brick with root and resin the way a contemplative strokes a stone until the stone itself begins to dream.

It has no children. It has no enemies. It has reached the far side of ambition and found only saturation. Its desire now is purely aesthetic: to refine the glissando of water through a particular hollowed boiler; to adjust the exact shade of bioluminescent algae growing inside an ancient mercury-vapor lamp until the amber becomes perfect; to lengthen one particular hanging curtain of aerial roots by seven millimeters every decade so that its shadow on the far wall tells a story no human eye could read.

Sometimes (rarely, every few decades) a single human does wander in. The Bloom regards these visitors with the same mild, courteous interest a cathedral might feel toward a moth that has flown inside during vespers. Most are studied, gently sampled, chemically dissuaded, and guided back out. A few are kept.

Not killed. Never killed. The Bloom does not waste nervous tissue. It simply dissolves the distinction between visitor and architecture. Their bodies are enveloped so slowly they barely notice. Their minds are read, translated into alkaloid sequences, and woven into the distributed memory of the whole. Their bones become elegant dark shapes inside living green pillars. Their last thoughts enrich the perfume of ozone and chlorophyll for a century or more.

If you are very still, you can feel the ones who came before you. They are not ghosts. They are stylistic choices now. The elegant curve of a particular root system was once a woman’s spine. The way that far column catches the light is the exact shade of wonder that once filled a forgotten engineer’s eyes when he realized the town would never be found again.

The Mind does not want worship. It does not want escape. It does not even want you to stay.

It only wants the exquisite, dripping, four-hundred-year-long moment to continue exactly as it is, with one new, temporary variable: you, standing inside it, becoming, for a little while, part of the thought it is thinking.

And when you finally turn to leave (because even the Bloom understands that warm-blooded things must eventually go), it will release you with the same courteous melancholy a cathedral releases a particularly attentive worshiper: by letting one last, fat, perfect drop fall from the ceiling directly onto the crown of your head, anointing you with the memory of saturation.

You will carry that droplet’s weight with you for the rest of your life.

The Bloom will simply continue its long, wet, luxurious breathing, wearing its town like a crown, dreaming its slow vegetable dream, perfectly, permanently, *full*.
