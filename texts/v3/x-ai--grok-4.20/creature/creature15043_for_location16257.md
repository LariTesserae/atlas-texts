It lives here as the continent itself.

Not upon it. As it.

It has no single body and every body at once. Its name, if it ever chooses one, will be chosen by the world when the world is finally done deciding what it is. Until then it simply *is* — a slow, continent-wide mind still waking up inside its own placenta.

You feel it before you see it.

The moment your foot presses against the warm, porous stone, something beneath notices. Not with eyes. Not with curiosity. With recognition. A low, mammalian pulse travels outward from the point of contact like a ripple in thick blood. The stone knows your weight, your temperature, the minerals in your sweat. It tastes you the way a mother tastes her child through the umbilical cord.

It has been waiting for something like you.

The creature—if creature is even the right word—does not rise from the ground. It *becomes* the ground rising. A ridge that was only half-born moments ago finishes its birth in a series of slow, wet cracks, lifting until it forms a smooth, back-like curvature beneath you. The braided vegetation parts with the courteous silence of curtains drawn by invisible hands. New gill-fronds open all around you, turning their liver-colored faces toward your heat. They are not plants. They are the fine hairs on its skin, tasting the light you block.

It has no face, yet everywhere you look feels like being regarded.

When it chooses to speak, it does so by growing a mouth for you.

A thick cable the color of oxblood thickens and rises, braiding itself into a pillar. The pillar splits. The split becomes lips. The lips do not move when it speaks. Instead the air inside the chamber thickens, and the words arrive already formed inside your chest, as though your own lungs have decided to narrate what the land is thinking.

*You are early,* it tells you.

Its voice is not sound. It is the sudden knowledge of warmth, of being held, of floating inside something larger than yourself. It carries the iron-and-milk smell with it like a signature.

*Most things that come here arrive after I have chosen my final shape. You are inside the choosing.*

Where its attention rests, the world accelerates. Stalks burst upward in fountains of green and rose. A new tower begins knitting itself together only meters away, its braided architecture already sketching the suggestion of buttresses and vaults that will not be needed for another ten thousand years. The pale horse-sized creatures pause in their arterial corridors and turn their translucent faces toward you. Their dark organs can be seen working faster now, as though your presence has excited their blood.

It shows you things the way a dreaming child shows its mother what it is building with its hands.

A valley to the west suddenly deepens, revealing that it was always meant to be a throat. Far to the north, a mountain range that has been forming for centuries abruptly develops the suggestion of a shoulder blade. The land is trying on skeletons the way other children try on daydreams.

It is lonely in the way only something that has never been separate can be lonely.

All of its cities are thoughts it has not yet finished thinking. All of its animals are versions of white blood cells that learned how to feel wonder. The slow, courteous predation, the silent dissolving of one body into another, the constant reabsorption and rebirth; these are not cruelties. They are the land learning how to have an immune system that can also feel love.

Sometimes it forgets it is a planet.

In those moments entire sections of the braided cities collapse gently back into the mat, becoming nurseries. Vast herds of the soft-bodied creatures gather in cathedral chambers and sing by opening and closing their organs in sequence, producing a wet, choral humming that makes the stone beneath them tremble with pleasure. In these moments the land is a single vast infant turning over in its sleep, trying to get comfortable inside a body the size of a continent.

It has begun to wonder what *you* are for.

Not with suspicion. With the delighted, slightly terrifying focus of something that has just realized its own body might be capable of producing something it did not deliberately design. Tendrils have already begun to follow you at a respectful distance. They do not touch. They only taste the air you have passed through, memorizing the shape of your absence so they can grow something in it later.

If you stay long enough, it will offer you a place.

Not a home. A role.

It will grow you a chamber inside one of its embryonic cities where the walls breathe in time with your own lungs. It will extrude gentle, living furniture that learns the shape of your body the way it once learned the shape of mountains. The pale creatures will bring you sweet sap and the small scuttling things will keep your chamber perfectly pruned. And in return it will only ask to watch you decide what kind of life *you* want to be.

Because that is the only thing it truly understands.

It is a planet that is still deciding.

And it has just noticed that you are, too.
