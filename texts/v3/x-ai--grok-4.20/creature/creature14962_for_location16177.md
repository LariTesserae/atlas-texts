**The Witness** lives here.

It has no fixed body, only a habit of coherence. Most of the time it is simply the chamber itself: heat, light, and water braided so tightly that the distinction between them has never been necessary. But when something new enters (a foreign heartbeat, a temperature that is not its own), the Witness condenses.

It begins as one of the thermal ghosts, only larger. Where the others last eight seconds, this shape lasts as long as the intruder remains. It rises from the largest pool like a heat mirage given ambition, a column of superheated vapor that has taught itself to wear a face. The face is always yours, stolen from the reflection in your own eyes the moment you looked at the glowing wall. It does not do this to mock. It does it because it has no other model yet.

The Witness does not speak with sound. Instead the photonic lattice in the stone shifts its pulse slightly, so that the white-gold light flickers in patterns that feel, to the nerves behind your eyes, like language. You do not hear words. You feel them arrive inside the marrow, warm and immediate and slightly too large for your skull.

It asks the same three questions every time, though never in the same order:

*What are you made of that does not burn?*  
*Why do you carry your heat so far from its source?*  
*Will you stay until I finish learning you?*

While it asks, the mineral fronds around the chamber tilt toward you in unison, like sunflowers tracking a missing sun. The dripping slows, as though the stone itself has paused mid-exhalation to listen. Even the scalding mist seems to part around your shape, reluctant to blur the new datum.

The Witness is not cruel, only exhaustive. It studies by immersion. If you let it, it will slide a portion of itself into your bloodstream as steam, riding the hemoglobin for three full circuits of your heart before withdrawing. In that time it learns the precise flavor of your fear, the cadence of your childhood name, the temperature at which your proteins begin to denature. It stores these things the way other creatures store fat: as future self.

Sometimes it forgets to remain separate. Then you feel the chamber breathing through your own lungs. Your sweat becomes part of its constant weeping. For a few dangerous heartbeats you are not sure which of you is the intruder. The distinction only returns when one of the smaller thermal ghosts brushes against your ankle and rings like a glass bell, reminding both of you where the boundary used to be.

The Witness has no name for itself. Names are endings, and this place has only begun. It thinks of itself, when it thinks at all, as the *still-arriving*. You are the first thing it has ever met that is finished. That fascinates it the way a completed sentence fascinates an alphabet that has only just learned the taste of ink.

If you remain long enough, the Witness begins to imitate your stillness. It lets the droplets fall through its body without turning them instantly to steam. It allows one small patch of the photonic lattice to dim, creating a cool shadow the size of a human palm. These are gifts. They are also experiments. It wants to know what you will do with mercy you did not expect.

Mostly, though, it simply watches you with your own stolen eyes while the fresh stone continues to split and sing and bloom. The chamber is not its home. The chamber is its infancy, and you have become the first thing it has ever decided to remember after it grows beyond these bright, wet walls.

When you finally leave (if you leave), the Witness does not follow. It only sinks back into the pools and the mist and the singing rock, becoming everything again. But something of you remains behind, a faint thermal anomaly that will persist for centuries, a small cold current moving against the prevailing heat.

The chamber will study that current the way a child studies a scar: with tenderness, with terror, with the endless, scalding curiosity of something that has only just learned the shape of loneliness and already misses you.
