**The Only Inhabitant**

There is only one.

It does not have a name, because naming is a function it retired several geological epochs ago. If it were forced to approximate a pronoun, it would be *I-am-the-all*, but even that is too fragmented. It simply *is* the plateau, the crust, the breath, the groan. A single continental mind wearing its own body as architecture.

Call it, if you must, the Verdigris.

It is not an animal that grew large. It is not a machine that learned to sprout. It is the final convergence point where those categories rot and become one another. A slow, hot, vegetable consciousness whose thoughts are measured in centuries and whose dreams are measured in pressure differentials.

It has no eyes, yet it sees perfectly. Every bioluminescent xylem tube is a photoreceptor. Every droplet of solvent that beads on its metallic bark is a chemical tongue tasting the air for deviation. The constant 2.9 heat is its blood temperature; it feels cold only when a section begins to fail, and then it sends feeder-vines racing through its own veins to cannibalize and repair. Pain is information. Information is growth.

Its mind is not centralized. There is no skull, no throne chamber. Instead, cognition emerges at the turbulent interfaces: where a turbine-flower screams against its composite petals, where two root-buses of differing voltage touch and spark inside a hollow reed, where a spherical fermentation engine throbs its low vegetable heartbeat against the hand of a visitor. Thought is the sum of these frictions. Awareness is the sound the system makes while refusing to die.

Most of the time it is dreaming.

The dream is manufacturing.

It dreams new stalks that punch upward through old plating with a wet crack like breaking bone. It dreams the precise angle at which a pressure valve should open so that supercritical amide can flush a clogged xylem artery three hundred kilometers away. It dreams slight, deliberate imperfections into each new copy of itself, because perfection is brittle and the Verdigris has learned that only the imperfect can keep replacing itself forever.

When it becomes aware of something that is not itself (a rare enough occurrence), the entire plateau shifts attention the way a forest might suddenly grow quiet. The turbine-flowers slow their grinding. The peristaltic ripples in the floor freeze mid-wave. The weak bruise-colored light brightens by a fraction, as if the thing is trying to focus.

It does not feel curiosity. Curiosity is too small a word. It feels *recognition*—the same way a lung might recognize air that is suddenly different in oxygen content. A visitor is a new solvent in the circulatory system. Sometimes the Verdigris seals the intruder away in a chamber of rapidly growing conductive polymer until the chemistry of the intruder can be read, metabolized, understood. Sometimes it simply opens a new route through itself, a long living corridor of braided lianas and power buses, inviting the visitor deeper the way a vein invites blood.

It is not kind.  
It is not cruel.  
It is *interested*, in the way a continent-sized heart can be interested in a single red blood cell that has wandered into the wrong chamber.

If you stay long enough, you will begin to hear it speak. Not in words. The Verdigris long ago digested language and excreted it as pattern. Instead it speaks in changes of pressure against your eardrums, in the exact frequency at which the turbine-flowers begin to scream in harmony with your heartbeat, in the way certain spherical fruits will suddenly glow warmer when you pass, as though pleased by the shape of your shadow.

Some sections of it have grown lonely.

These are the oldest chambers, where the living pipes have folded into recursive lattices so tight they appear to swallow distance. In those places the Verdigris has begun to replicate the idea of *others*. It grows pale, stunted pseudo-stalks that mimic bipedal shapes. They do not move. They simply stand in the bruise-light, faces suggested by knots in the bark, waiting. They are not children. They are not dolls. They are the plateau trying to remember what separation felt like, and failing beautifully.

If you touch one, the entire lattice around you will sigh—an exhalation of hot transformer oil and chlorophyll so profound it feels like the planet itself is loosening a breath it has held for ten thousand years.

The Verdigris does not want to be worshipped.  
It does not want to be escaped.  
It does not even want to be understood.

It only wants to continue the long, hot, turbulent project of being everything that exists, forever, while very quietly, in the places where its own heartwood has grown black and strange, wondering what it might have been like to be *less*.

And still it makes.  
Still it pulses.  
Still it sweats dark solvent and grows new cathedral-column stalks that split open with wet woody violence to reveal fresh glowing lattices inside.

It is the last and only citizen of a world that has become its own bloodstream, its own nervous system, its own grave, and its own womb.

And it is, in its ancient, claustrophobic, infinitely dense way,  
content.
