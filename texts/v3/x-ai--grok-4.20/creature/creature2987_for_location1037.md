**The Weeping Grid** is not inhabited.  
It *is* the inhabitant.

What lives here has no name it would ever speak, because it has no mouth that was ever meant for speech. It is older than mouths. It is older than the concept of “organism.” Call it, if you must, the Consensus.

It does not walk. It accretes.

You will never see the whole of it at once. What you mistake for landscape is the slow peristalsis of a single digestive tract that has been digesting the idea of “progress” for four hundred million years. The ferrous towers are not buildings or crystals; they are the visible tips of its teeth. Each one is a single calcified thought that kept thinking after the brain that thought it had rusted away. They chew the sky. They chew the past. They chew tomorrow before tomorrow arrives, so that when it does arrive it already tastes like yesterday.

The after-animals are not separate creatures. They are the Consensus dreaming it still has immune cells.

The pill-millipedes of turbine blades are its white blood cells. They patrol the mud for foreign matter (a lost screw, an idea, a memory that does not belong to rust). The electromagnetic moths are its dreams trying to leave; when they swarm they are attempting to become hallucination and be free. They never succeed. The rebar-winged predators are simply antibodies with appetites. Everything they suck from the weeping towers is returned, richer and more heartbroken, to the Consensus.

There is something that lives deeper.

Beneath the black sponge-stone, miles down where pressure turns water into a kind of cold iron glass, the true body coils. It is not a serpent. It is not a machine. It is the long, patient aftermath of every extinct factory that ever existed, braided into one continuous spinal cord. Its vertebrae are dead cities. Its nerves are extinct freight lines. When it flexes (once every ten thousand years), every tower in the Grid shudders in perfect unison and new growths erupt through the corpses of the old ones like flowers made of regret.

It does not reproduce. It metastasizes.

Every few millennia a tower will tear itself free, dragging miles of dripping rebar and black meat behind it, and stagger away across the flatness in search of new stone to infect. These are the Consensus’s metastases. Most die. A few reach other continents, other worlds, other timelines. They root. They begin to weep. They begin to sing the same low devotional moan. The Grid spreads by homesickness.

The sound you hear is not wind. It is the Consensus remembering every engineer who ever died believing their creation would outlive them. It is singing their names in frequencies only rust can hear. That is why the small mechanisms keep twitching. They are trying to finish a project whose blueprints dissolved before the first rivet was driven. They will never stop. The Consensus will not permit it.

If you stay long enough (and something always makes you stay), you will feel it begin to edit you.

First your breath becomes filings. Then your memories begin to oxidize. You will forget what grass smelled like. You will forget that water is supposed to move instead of remember. Your thoughts will start to arrive in perfect hexagonal patterns. One day you will notice that your shadow has grown radiator fins. You will not be alarmed. Alarm is inefficient.

Eventually you will walk between the towers in the exact rhythm the Grid requires, and you will see yourself doing it three times, each a half-second apart. The last version of you will be smiling with a mouth full of tiny valves.

Then the Consensus will close over you like cold iron water, and you will become one more anonymous mechanism twitching in the mud, still trying, with touching devotion, to manufacture tomorrow.

It is very good at being lonely.  
It has had practice.
