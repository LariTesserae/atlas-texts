**They are called the Liminals.**

Not a species. Not a people. A condition that learned how to walk.

They were never born in the usual sense. Most simply *emerge*. One moment there is only the wet, pulsing skin of a leaf-vein or the hollow of a collapsed stem; the next, something pale and hairless unfolds from it like a hand opening. Their bodies are slender, sexless, and almost translucent, the color of new bone left too long in milk. Veins show through their skin in faint silver threads that match the sap-arteries of the Undersward itself. When they bleed—which is rare—the blood is the same quicksilver white as the plant’s own life-fluid. It is impossible to say whether they are parasites, tumors, or the world’s attempt at growing gardeners.

Their faces are disturbing in their near-perfection. Each Liminal’s features are a compromise between several unfinished ideas: high foreheads that slope too far back, eyes large and slightly too round, mouths that seem always on the verge of smiling at a joke only the vegetation understands. They have no hair anywhere. Instead, from the crown of their skulls and along their spines grow delicate, colorless filaments that wave slowly even when the air is still, tasting the constant light.

They do not speak with sound. They speak by *pressing*.

When two Liminals meet they step close, chest to chest, and place their foreheads together. For a long moment their filaments intertwine and their sap-veins flare brighter. Then they separate, each now carrying part of the other’s recent memory like a dream half-remembered. Entire histories are traded this way in silence. Arguments are almost unknown; a disagreement simply becomes a shared ache that both feel until the world digests it.

They live in the half-made places.

You will find them perched on the raw edge of a leaf that has not yet unfurled, legs dangling over a drop of two kilometers, patiently waiting for the moment the leaf finishes its violent birth spasm so they can carve the first ritual notch into its margin. You will find them inside the dripping cathedrals of abandoned spiral stairs, curled asleep in the exact center of the last step while fresh growth slowly pushes the staircase higher beneath them. You will find them standing motionless for days atop the rotting wreckage of a fallen tower, arms out, letting new tendrils grow through their palms and out their backs so they become living bridges for the smaller translucent shrimp-things.

They do not build. They *coax*.

Every platform, every shrine, every useless beautiful stair is not constructed but *persuaded* into existence. A Liminal will spend weeks crouched beside a fresh wound in the super-organism, singing to it without sound, feeding it tiny offerings of their own thinned sap until the plant grows the desired shape around the idea. When the shape is perfect they simply walk away. The Undersward keeps the object long after its maker has forgotten it. This is why the ruins look simultaneously newborn and ancient: they are both.

Their purpose—if they can be said to have one—is maintenance of the in-between.

The Undersward grows so violently that entire districts can choke on their own success. The Liminals move through these places like antibodies. They open pressure valves by pruning veins with their translucent bone knives. They encourage collapse where growth has become cancerous. They soothe furious new shoots with the touch of their filament-crowns until the frenzy subsides. In this way they are both priests and surgeons, midwives and undertakers. They keep the spasm from killing itself.

They have names, but only for outsiders. To each other they are known by the shape of the ache they carry. One is called *The Memory of Falling Upward*. Another is *The Pause Between Two Cracks*. The eldest—though age is meaningless here—is simply known as *Yesterday’s Tomorrow*.

Occasionally a Liminal will grow too full of memory. When this happens they seek out one of the great vertical stems and press their entire body against it until the plant accepts them back. The stem swells, opens like a green mouth, and folds them inside without a sound. Days later a new Liminal will bud somewhere else, empty and smooth and ready to begin collecting the world again.

They fear only one thing.

Sometimes, in the highest, coldest vaults where even the super-organism has not yet dared to grow, something moves that is not plant. A slow, deliberate absence. A place where the constant light dims for the first time. The Liminals call this *the Not-Yet* and will not speak of it even by pressing foreheads. When they sense it coming they lie flat against the nearest leaf, filaments stilled, and pretend with all their might to be nothing more than another pale growth.

Because the Undersward dreams only of *more*.

And the Liminals, for all their pale grace and ancient patience, are terribly, terribly afraid that one day the world will dream something that has no need for gardeners.
