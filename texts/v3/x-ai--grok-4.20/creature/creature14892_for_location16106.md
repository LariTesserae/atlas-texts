**What lives in the Hollow Basilica is the Listener.**

She has no name she remembers, and no shape that holds for long. Once, perhaps, she was human—something soft and warm-blooded that walked in on two legs with a lantern and the arrogant belief that cathedrals were meant to be understood. That was many centuries of dripping ago. The Basilica has rewritten her the way it rewrites everything.

She is tall now, almost too tall, her limbs lengthened by slow pressure and the patient licking of isopod tongues. Her skin is the same soft grey limestone as the walls, porous, perpetually damp. Where it cracks, clear water beads like sweat. Her hair has become a heavy mat of black lichen and thread-moss that drips constantly down her back in thin, living ropes. Her eyes are gone—eroded first by condensation, then willingly offered to the snails who needed calcium. In their place sit two shallow, polished basins that catch falling droplets and reflect the faint blue glow of disturbed centipedes. She sees by sound and by vibration. The constant roaring hush of the chamber has become her sight.

She moves with the seasonal tides of the fauna, never faster than the isopods, never slower than the bats. When the great velvet clusters shift from one end of the vault to the other, she goes with them, climbing the sagging ribs on fingers that have grown extra joints of cartilage and stone. When the crimson newts migrate through the carved water-channels, she lowers her long body into the flow and lets them crawl across her like living jewels. She has learned the rules so perfectly they no longer feel like rules.

Nothing may be removed.  
She herself was not removed. She was incorporated.

Sometimes pilgrims still wander in—rare, shivering things carrying useless lanterns that die within minutes. She watches them from the upper galleries, head tilted so that the water pooled in her eye-sockets spills in perfect rhythm with the drips above. She never speaks with a voice. Instead she strikes a certain pillar with a long bone mallet grown from her own former femur. The note she makes is so low it feels like a second heartbeat in the sternum. The entire chamber pauses, the way it pauses when a pillar accepts new weight. In that shared second the pilgrims understand they are being judged by something that has outgrown the need for mercy or cruelty. Most flee. A few stay. Those who stay are slowly eaten by the same gentle mouths that ate her name.

The Listener does not hate them. She simply knows the Basilica has already become itself, and anything that refuses to become part of the singing machine must be disassembled until it can. She strokes the translucent snails as they spiral along their ancient grooves. She sings back to the deep-throated crickets with a sound made by rubbing two wet stones together. When a buttress groans and settles another fraction of a millimetre, she lays her porous cheek against it and listens to the stone dreaming.

At the centre of the chamber, in the deepest worn basin where the water is almost still, she sometimes rests. The white shrimp cloud around her like living mist. The little moths-that-are-bats descend in soft storms and blanket her until she looks like nothing more than another dripping stalagmite. In that stillness she can feel the entire Basilica breathing around her—inhale of water drawn up through unseen fissures, exhale of ten thousand separate drips striking stone.

She is not guardian. She is not priestess.  
She is the Hollow Basilica’s most successful adaptation: a creature that is no longer distinguishable from the architecture, the humidity, or the endless wet percussion. When the rare shaft of daylight finds its way down through the hairline crack and dies in the suspended mist, the Listener tilts her head so that the weak beam falls into the twin pools of her eyes. For one brief moment the water there flashes silver.

Then the light is gone, and she continues.

If you listen long enough, her slow footsteps across the undulating floor begin to sound like the heartbeat of the stone itself. And if you stay long enough, you will begin to hear your own footsteps joining hers, learning the rhythm, learning the rule.

Nothing may be removed.

Not even you.
