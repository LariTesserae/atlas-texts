They live here.

Not as tenants. Not even as residents. They *are* the place in the same way a scar is the wound that learned how to speak.

Call them the Unnamed, though even that is too loud a word. Most of them long ago surrendered their original shapes the way a river surrenders its name when it reaches the sea. What remains are slow, deliberate absences given just enough coherence to keep from dissolving completely.

One of them is moving now.

It wears the negative space between three slanted towers the way another creature might wear a coat. Where its shoulders would be, the air folds inward, creating a silhouette that hurts to finish looking at. Its height changes depending on who (or what) is watching. To you it might stand four meters tall and paper-thin. To the silicate fungi blooming across the nearest ridge it registers as a brief interruption in radiation, nothing more.

It has no face. Instead, a single shard of black glass orbits the place where a head should be, spinning on three axes at once. Every full rotation, the shard catches the dying-star light and throws it back as a memory that does not belong to you: the smell of rain on your mother’s coat thirty-seven years ago, the exact tone of a voice that once said your name with love. Then the shard turns and the memory is filed away again, catalogued, indexed, *edited*.

The being does not walk. It permits the geometry to slide beneath it. When it needs to cross one of the floating districts it simply exhales a low subsonic pulse. The glass remembers how to be a bridge for seven heartbeats, carries the creature, then forgets again. Bridges here are courteous but temporary.

There are others.

Deeper in the folded districts, where streets become Möbius ribbons and gravity apologizes before changing direction, live the Lattice-Born. Their bodies are living fractal inscriptions. Stand still long enough and you will see one emerge from a tower wall like a sigh given edges. They are taller than the negative-space beings, but thinner, as though someone tried to draw a person using only the concept of *angle*. Their joints click in perfect 1:1.618 ratios when they move. They do not speak; instead they allow small portions of the surrounding architecture to briefly forget its own atomic weight. The resulting hum is their language. It translates, roughly, as *We are still deciding.*

Some of them carry passengers.

Clinging to the inner surfaces of certain towers are things that might once have been human. Or might one day be human again. They have been here so long that their outlines have begun to repeat. One of them (a woman who forgot her own name six centuries ago) has become a recurring motif in the city’s self-editing process. You will see her silhouette carved into the obsidian in seventeen different places, always in the exact posture of someone realizing they have been crying for a thousand years without noticing. She is not trapped. She is a bookmark.

The city uses her to remember what longing felt like before it became architecture.

Beneath the shelf, in the places where the escarpment folds into itself like a paper crane made of night, live the Proofreaders. They are smaller. Almost kind. Their bodies are composed of the metallic dust that never moves—except when one of them decides it should. They drift in slow schools, reshaping the dust into delicate, temporary limbs only when they need to adjust something. A misaligned law of physics. A shadow that has overstayed its welcome. A memory that has begun to rot.

They are the reason the auroras sing.

Every time a Proofreader corrects a flaw in the city’s slow self-revision, the mathematics overhead twist into brighter, more painful patterns. The equations are celebrating. Or mourning. It is difficult to tell the difference here.

None of them are lonely.

Loneliness requires a self that can be separated from its surroundings, and these beings have long ago allowed the distinction to deliquesce. When one of the negative-space creatures brushes against a Lattice-Born, the contact is not social. It is grammatical. They are refining one another’s sentences.

If you remain here long enough to be noticed, one of them will eventually approach.

It will not speak. It will simply stand in the place where your shadow is supposed to be and wait for you to finish becoming whatever the city has been writing you into. Sometimes they tilt that orbiting shard of glass so that it catches your eyes instead of the starlight. When that happens you understand, with a clarity that borders on violence, that they are not deciding what you will become.

They are remembering what you already were, somewhere else, in a softer place where water still existed and names could survive more than three heartbeats.

Then they let the memory go.

The glass forgets.

And the city keeps editing.
