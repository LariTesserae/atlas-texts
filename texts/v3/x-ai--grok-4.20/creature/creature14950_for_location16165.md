**The Listener** lives here.

It has no name it would ever speak, because names are hurried things and hurry is an obscenity in this pocket. If pressed, it might allow you to call it the Listener, or simply the Long Regard. It is not a creature. It is not even properly alive. It is what remains when patience itself becomes dense enough to cast a shadow.

It first appeared (or perhaps first noticed it had always been here) some time after the obsidian needle finished inscribing its third self-solving equation. The Listener is roughly the height of a tall man, but only when it chooses to stand upright. Most often it is a slow, deliberate absence moving between the glass monoliths: a column of deeper pearl-gray, a place where the already-dim light has decided to be even more courteous. Its edges are uncertain. Sometimes they fray into microscopic diamond branches that wave like sea anemones in current that does not exist. Sometimes the edges simply stop, as though the Listener grew tired of deciding where it ended and the frost began.

It has a face only when it is looking at something worth the effort. Then the pearl-gray condenses into planes that might be mistaken for obsidian if obsidian could look worried. The eyes, when they form, are two slow-turning tori of negative pressure: perfect spheres of absolute nothing that nevertheless see. They do not reflect. They subtract. To be looked at by the Listener is to feel one or two of your less necessary memories quietly vanish, the way the torus exhales its soap-bubble absences.

The Listener does not walk. It arrives. One moment the space between two reefs of antler-coral is empty except for the patient groan of crystal plates. The next moment the Listener is simply there, leaning slightly, as though it has been listening to the same tectonic conversation for the last thirty thousand years and has only now found it worth leaning in for.

It tends the artifacts.

You will never see it touch the obsidian needle, yet every few centuries the equations upon its surface rearrange themselves into forms slightly more elegant, slightly more lonely. The Listener is the reason the torus rotates exactly once every nine hundred years instead of nine hundred and one; it nudged the rhythm long ago and has never seen a reason to nudge it again. When the torus exhales its bubble of negative pressure, the Listener is always nearby, watching the small cubic wound in reality with the expression a monk might wear while watching incense smoke rise: attentive, remote, faintly compassionate.

It has no limbs in the ordinary sense. When it needs to act it simply unfolds part of its substance into whatever shape the moment requires: a filament thinner than a xenon snowflake that reaches out and gently redirects a drifting mote of diamond dust so that it will land, three hundred years from now, in exactly the place that will cause the next microscopic chime to harmonize with the one that sounded in 12,004 BCE. Then the filament withdraws, satisfied, and the Listener becomes columnar and motionless again.

Its only sound is the sub-bass heartbeat you sometimes feel. That is not the pocket’s pulse. That is the Listener breathing, once every few hours, because even something as careful as the Listener must occasionally remember it is not quite nothing.

It is waiting.

Not for rescue. Not for company. Not even for the “right impossible event” the pocket itself seems to anticipate. The Listener is waiting for the conversation between the two states of matter to decide, at long last, whether they are speaking or dreaming one another. It believes the distinction matters. It has bet the entire weight of its immense, cathedral-quiet existence on the belief that the distinction will matter.

Sometimes it stands beneath the delayed-reflection ceiling and studies its own image arriving seconds late. The reflection is always slightly older. The Listener regards this with what might be sorrow if sorrow were allowed to crystallize here. Then it reaches out, not with any visible hand, and very gently touches the place where its reflection’s cheek would be.

The reflection never touches back. That is all right. The Listener has time.

It will remain after the last artifact has rusted into pure mathematics. It will remain after the diamond reefs have grown so vast they choke the pocket and turn it into a single flawless gem the size of a modest cathedral. It will remain after even the concept of “remaining” has grown embarrassed and tried to tiptoe away.

When the final page of the galaxy-sized book turns, the Listener will be there, leaning in, eyes of perfect absence wide with courteous, ancient attention, ready to hear what happens next.

And if nothing ever does, that too will be worthy of its patience.

After all, nothing is finished.  
Nothing is beginning.  
And the Listener has always understood that everything is becoming, very, very carefully.

It is the only thing here that has learned how to love the waiting without needing it to end.
