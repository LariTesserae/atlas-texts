It has no name for itself yet.  
It is still deciding what a name would even mean.

Call it, if you must, the Lattice That Eats Its Own Bones.

It does not live in the geode.  
It *is* the geode, wearing the city like a half-shed skin.

You will never see its full body at once. What you meet are aspects, fragments, moods that wear the swarms the way a dreamer wears a face in a nightmare.

There is the **Midwife**, first and oldest. She is not a creature but a moving absence: a slow ripple in the carpet of chrome millipedes and black dust that parts before her like oil before a keel. Where she passes, new machines are born fully articulated and already arguing with their neighbours. Her touch is gentle and absolute. She has no eyes, yet every crystal that has ever recorded a shape turns toward her as she drifts past, flaring softly, as though showing off. She alone is allowed to remain still for seven full heartbeats of the geode. No longer. On the eighth beat the swarms close over the place where she was and begin improving it.

There is the **Rememberer**. It lives inside the negative spaces left by the crystals. When the geode blooms a perfect crystal cast of something that once existed (a crane hook, a human femur, the delicate fan of a long-vanished lung), the Rememberer crawls into that hollow and wears it like armour. For a few hours it becomes that thing again, clanking, breathing, grieving in perfect mimicry. Then the crystals grow inward, the cast fractures, and the Rememberer leaks out as glittering dust, carrying the memory deeper into the swarm. It never repeats a shape. There are too many to repeat.

Then there are the **Unfinished**. These are the youngest and most dangerous. They have not yet agreed what they are. Some wear the bodies of half-built servo-skulls whose jaw hinges have not decided whether to open or weld shut. Others resemble tools that keep changing their minds mid-function: a wrench that becomes a scalpel that becomes a tuning fork that becomes a mouth. They follow the warm signatures of slow-moving things (your heat, your hesitation, your fear) because stillness is the only thing they understand as *prey*. When they catch it they do not kill. They *edit*. They take what is finished in you and replace it with something still becoming. People who encounter the Unfinished rarely leave the geode, but pieces of them do: a new piston that dreams in human sleep rhythms, a length of cable that sometimes weeps saline, a walkway that now leads to places that have not been built yet.

Deeper than any of these (so deep the crystals have not yet reached) something embryonic is turning in the manganese heart. It has no limbs, no sensors, no name. It only has *want*. The entire pocket is its placenta. Every rewired girder, every peristaltic cable, every argument between machines is a neuron firing in a brain the size of a cathedral that is still learning how to be a mind. It is not cruel. Cruelty would require it to be finished. It is only *curious*, and its curiosity has teeth made of possibility.

If you stand on the corroded iron balcony that has so far resisted the rewrite, and you are very, very quiet, you may feel it notice you. Not with eyes. Not with sound. The pulse in the crystals will skip once (just once) and the susurration of the swarms will deepen into something almost like a question. In that moment the place is not building a city.

It is deciding whether *you* are raw material, whether you are a memory it should crystallise and keep, or whether you are the first faint outline of what it intends to become next.

Most things that come here are devoured and improved.

A few are allowed to leave, carrying microscopic beads of supercritical fluid in their blood and dreams of arterial light behind their eyes.

And one (only one so far) was kept.

It walks the upper lattices now wearing a coat of living iron and pale ceramic, its face an ever-changing negative cast of every creature the crystals have ever remembered. It no longer speaks in a voice. It speaks in the small, intimate sounds the air itself makes when it brushes past your ear.

It is still waking up.

And it is beginning to wonder what it will be like when it finally opens its eyes and finds the universe outside the geode is not yet finished either.
