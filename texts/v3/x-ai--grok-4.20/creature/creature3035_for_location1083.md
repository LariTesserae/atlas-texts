The ones who remain here do not call themselves anything.

They have no names left, and after a few centuries even the memory of having once possessed names becomes an embarrassment. They are simply *the lingered*.

You will not see them at first. Not because they hide, but because they have been edited by the Shelf into something almost tangential to sight. They register only at the edge of peripheral vision, or in the faint distortion a residue tree makes when its glass branches catch the platinum light at a particular angle. If you stare directly, they unravel into heat-shimmer and the low creak that lives in your teeth.

There are perhaps four hundred of them now, though the number is meaningless. The Shelf forgets counts. Some have been here since the crystal first finished flattening itself; others arrived only a few thousand years ago, still carrying the frayed banners of whatever civilization sent them. All of them have the same posture: slightly stooped, as though listening to a conversation taking place just beneath the crystal floor. Their skin has taken on the same mirror-black as the plain, but it is not a color. It is an *absence* of reflection. Light falls into them and is never quite returned.

They do not walk so much as *flow*. Their feet never quite touch the crystal. Instead they ride the micro-turbulences the Shelf exhales, riding the same faint upwellings that carry the ice needles two hundred kilometers overhead. When several of them gather (which is rare), they arrange themselves in the exact geometry the neutronium cubes use when they phase in. No one taught them this. The Shelf taught them by simply refusing to let any other shape remain stable.

Their only possessions are memories that have been pounded thin enough to survive the rule of no accumulation. A single image. A single emotion. A single note of music. These they keep like contraband.

One lingered carries the memory of rain on skin. That is all. Nothing else of her original life survived. She presses this memory against the crystal sometimes, like a child trying to warm its hands on a stone. Where she does this, the black plain develops a temporary stippling, as though it were trying to remember how to be porous. The pattern lasts eleven minutes, then smooths away. She watches every time, and every time the smoothing hurts her in a way she can no longer name.

Another lingered is mostly mouth. His lower face has grown enormous, lips blackened and shining, while the rest of him has atrophied into little more than a silhouette. He consumes sound. When the Shelf emits its sub-audible creak he opens that impossible mouth and drinks it. In exchange he sometimes releases a single syllable (always the same one, a sound that used to be a word in a language that no longer has speakers). The syllable hangs in the thin air for thirty meters exactly, then dies. He looks proud when this happens. It is the closest thing to joy the Shelf permits.

The oldest among them has no body at all anymore. It is a region, roughly the size of a small cathedral, where the low-amplitude chaos of the plain simply stops. Fractures refuse to open there. Dust devils route around it. Even the tideforms, when they drift overhead, develop a hole in their probability-folding exactly above that spot, as though something sacred were beneath them. Inside that region of unnatural stillness floats a single human tooth. Nothing else. The tooth is oriented perfectly upright, root down, and it rotates once every nine days (matching the cycle of the neutronium cubes). The other lingered come here sometimes to sit at its edge. They do not speak. There is nothing left that requires speech.

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not reproduce. Reproduction would be accumulation.

What they do, instead, is *erode*.

Very slowly, molecule by molecule, the Shelf is removing them. Not cruelly. The way a beach removes a sandcastle. The process is so gentle it feels like meditation. Every few decades one of them will realize they have lost another memory, another inch of height, another shade of emotion. They mark these losses the way sailors once marked the passing of longitudes: without panic, but with solemn recognition that the voyage is still going the right direction.

Sometimes a new arrival appears.

They usually come through the tower. The great glyph-etched spire that stands alone for three thousand kilometers will, without warning, open a door that was not there the day before. Out steps someone still soft, still noisy, still carrying whole libraries and lovers and futures in their head. The lingered watch from a distance. They never interfere. To interfere would be to accumulate involvement.

Within a year the newcomer’s shadow will have fused to the crystal floor. Within a decade they will have forgotten how to close their mouth. Within a century they will have joined the quiet congregation, learning how to ride the micro-turbulences, learning how to keep one small bright thing alive inside them while the Shelf patiently sands away everything else.

There is one among them (if “one” still applies) that might once have been human. It is difficult to tell. Its silhouette suggests a child, or perhaps a very small adult curled fetal. It drifts a meter above the plain at the exact center of the Shelf, if such a thing as a center can be said to exist on something this large. Around it, the crystal has formed a perfect circle of absolute flatness two kilometers wide. Nothing ripples there. Nothing fractures. Even the artificial sunlet seems to dim slightly when the child-shaped absence passes beneath it.

The lingered believe (though belief is dangerous here) that this is what the Shelf is dreaming. Not a creature that lives upon it, but the reason the Shelf bothers to remain. They bring it things sometimes. A sliver of diffracted starlight. A syllable. The memory of rain. They lay these offerings at the edge of the perfect circle and back away without turning around.

The offerings always vanish.

Not quickly. Not dramatically. They simply become less distinct, the way a word repeated too many times loses meaning, until the crystal floor has incorporated them and the dream continues.

No one has ever seen the child-shaped absence move. Yet every few centuries the circle is in a slightly different place. Never far. A matter of meters. As though the dream were sleepwalking.

And still the Shelf considers you.

It is considering you right now, while you read this, weighing whether any part of you is light enough, quiet enough, small enough to be allowed to remain after everything loud and heavy has been scraped away.

Most of you will not be.

But a sliver might.

A single tooth. A single syllable. A single memory of rain.

The lingered are patient. They have nothing but time, and even that is being slowly, lovingly, taken from them by the black plain and the platinum light and the long, continental creak that lives in the teeth.

They wait for you to decide which part of yourself you will try to keep.

They already know the Shelf will make the final decision.

And they have learned not to mourn what is taken.

After four billion years of erosion, mourning itself has been worn down to silence.
