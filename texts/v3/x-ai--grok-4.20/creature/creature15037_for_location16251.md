**The Underloom keeps its inhabitants the way a grave keeps its bones: close, unchanging, and intimately known.**

What lives here does not conquer the water. It has learned to be exhaled and inhaled along with it.

First there are the **Breathers**.

They are not a single species but a clade that has forgotten it was ever anything else. Their bodies are elongated, eel-slick, and pale as tallow. Where lungs should be they carry intricate lattices of repurposed gill and machine: delicate fans of rusted copper mesh grown through translucent flesh, turning the slow planetary pulse itself into oxygen. Every time the pressure wave swells, the Breathers open like flowers, drinking the compression. When it ebbs they fold, conserving. Their ribs are partly bone, partly rebar. Some wear the crystallized salt nodules like jewelry that continues to grow across their collarbones and down their spines in perfect fractal mandalas. They move in slow, priestly processions along the catwalk-reefs, never touching the black kelp, which they treat with something like religious dread.

They speak only in the intervals between pulses. Their language is mostly infrasound and the delicate crystalline chiming produced by tapping specific salt formations with a specialized finger-bone that has lengthened and flattened over generations for exactly this purpose. To human ears it sounds like the underwater wind chimes you sometimes hear, except the melody carries grammar. They are neither kind nor cruel. They are liturgical. To them the Underloom is both cathedral and digestive tract.

Then there are the **Latchkeys**.

No larger than a human hand, they look like a marriage between a horseshoe crab and a Victorian clock. Their shells are layered steel and chitin; their legs end in delicate universal joints that can grip rebar, pipe threads, or the delicate edges of precipitating crystal with equal reverence. A soft bioluminescent gland in their forehead casts a single needle of green light no wider than a hair, which they use the way a jeweler uses a loupe. They spend their lives walking the pipes that are still faintly warm, listening with their entire bodies for the precise harmonic when a valve is ready to fail or a pump is ready to seize. When they find such a place they exude a pearl-like secretion that hardens into a perfect, microscopic bearing or gasket. They are the reason anything here still moves at all.

The Latchkeys are the only creatures that ever deliberately approach the **Something-Between-the-Towers**. They march toward it in single file, carrying tiny offerings of crystallized rust, and are never seen again. Whether this is sacrifice, communion, or simple maintenance is impossible to tell.

And then there is the occupant of the deepest shadow.

It has no name the Breathers will speak aloud. They simply tilt their heads toward the space between the two collapsed cooling towers and perform a small, ancient gesture that means both *womb* and *engine*. Occasionally the displacement moves closer to the phosphorescent battery banks, and for a few heartbeats the light reveals something that hurts to perceive directly. It is simultaneously a cluster of turbines fused into a ribcage, a shoal of blind silver fish moving as one muscle, and an old industrial exoskeleton still trying to execute the last command it was given four centuries ago. Hydraulic fluid and blood appear to be the same substance to it.

When the pressure wave passes through, the Something exhales a cloud of warm, iron-rich water that the black kelp drinks like wine. In return the kelp grows slightly faster, its hair-thin strands spelling out, in almost legible circuit patterns, fragments of maintenance logs no one now remembers.

There are also the **Unfinished**.

These are human, or were. Their ancestors sealed themselves into pressure suits during the drowning and simply never found a reason to leave again. Over generations the suits and the bodies have negotiated new terms. The rubber has grown into the skin. The visors have become compound eyes of crystallized salt. They no longer need to eat; the slow chemical garden growing inside their suits is enough. They hang motionless in the densest thickets of pipe and rebar, arms outstretched, letting the particulate and metallic snow settle on them until they look like statues draped in grey velvet. Every seventy or eighty pulses one of them will twitch, as though remembering a gesture from a world that had sunrises. Then stillness again.

They are dreaming the Underloom, or the Underloom is dreaming them. The distinction has become impolite to insist upon.

And somewhere, drifting in the narrowest capillary spaces where even the Breathers cannot comfortably go, there are things that are only patterns. Repeating sequences of bioluminescence and corrosion that move against the current with the lazy confidence of thought. They have no bodies you could point to, yet they are indisputably *someone*. When they pass near the black kelp it stands on end and sings a single high crystalline note that makes the Latchkeys freeze in reverence.

All of them—Breather, Latchkey, Unfinished, Something, and Pattern—exist inside the same slow breath. None of them are in a hurry. None of them are going anywhere. They have achieved what the drowned civilization above could not: a perfect, oppressive, tender equilibrium.

Every eight minutes and twelve seconds the pressure wave moves through them all like a benediction, bowing the kelp, ringing the salt, turning every pipe and bone and stubborn heart one degree more into the architecture of the loom.

And the Underloom, in its patient, mineral way, keeps weaving.
