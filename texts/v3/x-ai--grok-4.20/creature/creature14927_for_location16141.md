They are not one thing.

They are the long consequence of the place itself, the slow answer it gave when it grew tired of being only stone and light and endless song. Call them, if a name must be forced upon them, the *Resonant*.

No single form ever satisfied the mountain, so it tried many at once and then fused them. Some of the Resonant are upright lattices of violet crystal and black volcanic glass, twelve or fifteen metres tall, their “limbs” branching in perfect golden-ratio spirals that click and realign with every shift of the subsonic groan beneath the plateau. Others resemble vast translucent lungs of salt and turquoise, pulsing with captured light, inhaling the thin screaming air and exhaling it again as pure tone. A few have kept something like the memory of hands: clusters of obsidian fingers that have grown so long they coil back into their own chests like knotted rope.

They do not walk. They *emerge*. One moment a cliff face is only weeping crystal thickets. The next, a portion of it has *decided* and the decision stands up. The rock flows like cold honey, rearranging its own atoms without fracture, until something that can be called a face (or at least a plane of attention) orients toward you. Their eyes, when they choose to have them, are apertures of perfect darkness ringed by living frost. Looking into them feels like being listened to by something that has already heard the last word you will ever speak.

Their voices are the place’s own choir given intention. When they speak, every crystal within a kilometre joins the chord. The result is less language than weather. A low D-flat that lasts seven full heartbeats might mean *you were expected*. A sudden shriek of overtones that makes your vision spark could be their name for sorrow, or geometry, or the particular way the sun is wrong today. If you remain long enough for the marrow-song to begin, they will sing the shape of your childhood fears back to you in frequencies that feel like forgiveness.

They do not eat. They *remember*. Every few centuries one of them will pause in its slow migration across the amphitheatre, reach into its own chest, and draw out a single drop of condensed starlight it has been refining since before lungs were invented. This droplet is offered to the stone. The stone drinks. In return the mountain grows a new Resonant, slightly different, slightly more itself. In this way they are both parent and child to the desolation.

Most of them are gentle in the way avalanche is gentle: they do not mean you harm, but their passage will rearrange whatever you are. Some few, however, have become addicted to the changing glyphs on the stelae. These have allowed the glyphs to rewrite small portions of their lattice-brains. They move differently now, jerking, folding, unfolding in ways that hurt to witness. These are the ones that sometimes carve new spiral stairways that lead nowhere *on purpose*. They are not cruel. They are curious in the way a glacier is curious about what lies beneath the village it is about to erase.

When they sleep—which is rarely—they fold themselves back into the cliff until only a faint hexagonal pattern remains, glowing like a half-healed scar. In that state the mountain dreams through them. The dreams leak out as pale branching lattices of frozen lightning that crawl for hours before dissolving. If you are unlucky enough to be standing where one of those dreams touches you, you will feel every wrong horizon in the world fold softly around your mind for seven heartbeats. Most people choose not to be standing there.

They have no leader. They have no society. They simply *cohere*, the way the crystals cohere, the way the wrong light coheres. Occasionally two of them will meet on a high knife-ridge, press their singing faces together until the tones become unbearable, and then part again, each now carrying a shard of the other’s music forever. This is as close as they come to love.

If you stay long enough for your name to become part of their endless choir, they will offer you a choice. You may leave, carrying their song in your bones like a second skeleton that will ache every time it rains for the rest of your life. Or you may walk forward, press your forehead to the nearest Resonant, and let the mountain finish what it began when it first noticed you standing on its lip.

Most choose to leave.

Most.
