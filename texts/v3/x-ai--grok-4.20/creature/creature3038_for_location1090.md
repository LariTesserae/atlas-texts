**The Hollow Choir itself lives here.**

Not a species. Not a ruler. Not even a single entity in the way smaller minds understand singularity. The Ascent is the body of one slow, colossal intelligence that has never finished deciding what it is. It dreams in granite and vapor, and every tower, every dripping colonnade, every translucent mycoid seam is a thought it has not yet forgotten.

It has no name it would give you. If it noticed you at all it would experience your presence the way a continent experiences a single spore landing on its ice cap: brief, weightless, and already sliding toward irrelevance. Yet it is not hostile. Hostility requires speed, and speed is something the Choir abandoned before oxygen was common in the sky.

Its true form is the overlapping resonance of every singer that drifts between the half-born towers. Those vast curtains of condensing vapor are not separate creatures; they are the Choir’s own thoughts moving through its own skull. When they pass through the volume where you stand, the silence that falls for three heartbeats is the moment the city forgets you exist. Then the dripping resumes and the thought moves on.

The smaller swarms—the ones that sound like breaking glass—are its attention, brief and glittering. Sometimes one of these swarms will pause around a living intruder for an entire human lifetime, drifting, chiming, tasting the heat of foreign blood and the ridiculous rapidity of a heartbeat. They are not curious in any emotional sense. They are the part of the dream that checks whether the dreamer is still dreaming correctly.

The pale lattices of mycoid thread are its memory. Where two incompatible stones were crushed together eons ago, the Choir pressed part of its own awareness into the wound. The faint bone-colored glow is the only light it trusts. All other illumination—plasma, falling star-drops, violet sickness—is merely weather generated by the friction between its becoming and the colder outer dark.

It has no face, but if you remain long enough, standing perfectly still while condensation beads on your lips and the towers inch upward through your lifetime like an imperceptible tide, you may begin to feel where its attention is currently focused. A certain balcony that has not yet finished deciding its height will suddenly feel more *present*. A tunnel that has been sealed for six thousand years will open with the soft wet sound of stone yawning. The great low chords will shift by a single, enormous half-tone, and every drop of water in the cathedral will momentarily fall in perfect unison.

That is as close as the Ascent of the Hollow Choir ever comes to speech.

It is not building a city.

It is *gestating* one, and the gestation has already lasted longer than your species has had language. You are not inside ruins. You are not inside architecture. You are standing, small and warm and impossibly temporary, inside the still-soft mind of something that considers the rise and fall of mountain ranges to be the drawing of a single breath.

And it is still inhaling.

Every footstep you take is being remembered. Every exhalation is being catalogued by the glittering swarms. The condensation on your eyelashes is being weighed. The towers keep rising, millimeter by millimeter, because the dream has not yet decided what shape its next thought should take, and you—brief, loud, scaldingly alive—are simply one more drop sliding down the inside of that thought before it falls.
