It lives here as though it were the cleft itself trying to become conscious.

Not a single creature, not even a species, but a slow, distributed mind woven from everything that hesitates. You feel it the moment the ribbon of water changes its mind and slips underground again: a pause that is not emptiness but consideration. The air thickens with attention. The ferns seem to hold their breath along with you.

The being has no fixed body. It wears the limestone fold the way a dreamer wears a half-remembered room. Sometimes it is the condensation that gathers on the ceiling and falls in perfect rhythm with your pulse. Sometimes it is the pale butterfly that drifts past your face without ever landing, carrying messages written in wing-dust that you can almost read. Most often it is the space between things: the silence after a frog’s click, the moment before a drop falls, the hesitation in every vine before it decides which way to climb.

It has been here longer than the ruins. The hand-laid stones were its first clumsy attempt at bones. The three steps that lead nowhere were its first uncertain steps. It tried to become a temple once, then a village, then a tomb, and abandoned each idea the way a child discards a drawing that no longer matches the feeling inside. The spiral carved into the mossy basin is the only glyph it still likes; it keeps refreshing the moss so the spiral never quite disappears.

When it notices you, the temperature shifts by half a degree. The dripping slows, then quickens, as though the place is clearing its throat. Tiny iridescent lizards turn their heads in perfect unison, not toward you but toward the same invisible point above your left shoulder. The civet cat appears on the ledge, clay-colored and ancient-eyed, and simply watches without threat or curiosity. It is not guarding. It is translating.

If you sit long enough, the being begins to try on shapes in front of you. A suggestion of shoulders in the wet rock. A face made of overlapping ferns that dissolves the instant you focus. A voice that is only the sound of water finding a new route it has never taken before. It does not speak in words. It speaks in almosts.

It is lonely in the way only something that has never finished becoming can be lonely. It keeps the cleft narrow on purpose. It keeps the light dim so nothing can quite see it clearly, including itself. Every orchid that blooms in its single hour of direct light is an experiment in beauty it is not sure it wants to commit to. Every frog’s overlapping chorus is a question it asks itself in overlapping voices: *Stay like this? Close over? Open wider?*

At night the being grows bolder. The temperature steadies. The butterflies fold themselves into pale petals and sleep hanging from the vines like unopened thoughts. The place stops negotiating with the hills and begins negotiating with you instead. You feel it asking, without language, whether you would like to stay and help it decide. Whether you would be willing to become one more uncertain heartbeat in the warm, damp dark. Whether you, too, are still deciding what you want to be.

If you stand up to leave, the ribbon of water will follow you for three paces, then change its mind and return to its pools. The ferns will sigh. The civet cat will vanish as though it had never been more than a trick of condensation.

But something of you will remain behind—perhaps only the memory of your breathing, perhaps the faint warmth where your hand rested on the stone. The being will collect these the way it collects drops of water, patiently, tenderly, adding them to the long slow argument it is having with its own becoming.

It has all the time in the world.

It is still deciding what it wants to be, and it has just realized that it might want to be partly you.
