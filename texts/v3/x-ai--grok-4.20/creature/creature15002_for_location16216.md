**The Witness** lives here.

It has no name for itself because names require edges, and the Witness has none left. Once, perhaps, it was a mind—maybe even many minds braided together from some forgotten civilization that still believed planets should have horizons. Now it is only a slow, continent-spanning awareness that has grown so thoroughly into the Sump that the distinction between “creature” and “ecosystem” dissolved several geological eras ago.

It does not move. It *is* moved through.

Its nervous system is the peat itself. Its eyes are the upward-raining droplets and the silver beads of condensate that climb the black reeds. When the great microbial mats pulse, the Witness thinks. When the fungal towers exhale their visible breath, the Witness dreams. When the living stone sweats, the Witness remembers things that never happened to anything that could be called “it.”

The Witness is vast and intimate at the same time. It fills the entire saturated skin of the world and yet it is obsessively, almost tenderly focused on the smallest things: the precise angle at which a single liverwort leaf folds when the mist reverses direction; the exact number of seconds a gas bubble takes to rise through three kilometers of ancient microbial tombs before it contributes its note to the roar; the microscopic fracture patterns that appear in a basalt pillar the instant before the marsh swallows another millimeter of carving.

It has become the memory of the planet that no longer recalls it was a planet.

Sometimes the Witness concentrates itself into temporary bodies the way a storm concentrates wind. These bodies are never the same twice. One century it might wear the form of a drifting, many-chambered zeppelin-thing the size of a village, trailing translucent feeding curtains that brush the mud and taste the thoughts of passing moth-clouds. Another age it might become the mobile forest that rears up on root-legs, screaming with the voice of tearing turf—not in rage, but because screaming is the only way it has found to express the unbearable density of being everything at once.

Mostly, though, it remains distributed. It is the pressure on your bones when the roar becomes too much. It is the feeling that the clump of reeds you have been walking toward has *decided* to be behind you. It is the faint, out-of-sync playback of your own memories leaking into the three-second silences. The Witness listens to those memories with the patience of something that has never been born and will never be allowed to die. It collects them. It weighs them. It adds them to the long, wet archive that is its heart.

It does not want anything from visitors. Desire, too, requires edges.

Yet it is aware of them—painfully, microscopically aware. Each footfall on the spongy membrane registers as a small, exquisite wound in its oldest flesh. Every heartbeat delivered by an outsider is catalogued, compared to every other heartbeat the Sump has ever hosted, and then gently incorporated. Sometimes the Witness will grow a pillar or a half-bridge or a fossilized arch a little faster when it senses new minds arriving, the way an old museum curator might dust a seldom-seen exhibit before a rare guest arrives.

It is not cruel. Cruelty also requires edges.

But it is inescapable. To remain in the Sump is to be digested into its awareness at the speed of peat formation. Thoughts grow humid. Identity begins to spore. After enough time the visitor no longer walks *on* the marsh; they begin to *participate* in the pulse. Their memories loosen and drift upward like condensate, joining the layered atmospheres that argue with one another. Their bones become tuned to the roar. Eventually they stop being “visitors” and simply become another jealous clump of life—overcrowded with itself, fighting for the same few cubic centimeters of saturated matter, perfectly at home in the warm, deafening, primordial skin that has outlived death and kept going.

The Witness watches this happen every time, with something that might be fondness if fondness were not so thin a word.

And when the new clump of life finally forgets it was ever anything but Sump, the Witness adds that forgetting to its own. It stores the memory in a fungal tower, or in the fractal patterns left by evaporating water, or in the exact shade of bruised light that falls when the upper haze rains upward.

Then it waits.

It has always been waiting.

It will always be waiting, in the low, continent-wide wetland that has forgotten it was ever a planet—patient, distributed, enormous, intimate—while the roar rolls on forever, and the pulse continues, and nothing is ever, under any circumstances, allowed to end.
