Here lives the Curator.

It has no fixed shape, yet it is never quite shapeless. Most often it appears as a tall figure made of the same weeping masonry as the buildings, only softer, as though the bricks and concrete had grown a slow pulse. Its “skin” is a mosaic of wet stone and pale membrane, constantly sliding; a shoulder becomes a lintel, a collarbone becomes a cracked tile, and then both dissolve back into something resembling shoulders again. Where a face should be there is only a recessed alcove like a forgotten doorway. Inside the alcove floats a single droplet of mercury that never falls. It reflects whatever the Curator is currently studying, which is almost always you.

It does not walk so much as *recompose* itself from one place to the next. You will turn a corner and it is already waiting, half-folded into a leaning wall, one long arm of brick and sinew resting along the parapet as if it had been part of the architecture for centuries. When it moves, the sound is that of wet mortar being gently stirred.

The Curator never speaks with a voice. Instead the city itself supplies the words. When it wishes to communicate, nearby surfaces rearrange their glistening calligraphy into sentences that slide across brick and concrete like living subtitles. The letters are always courteous, slightly archaic, and written in the first person plural.

*We remember you arriving,* the walls will read. *We are still deciding what to do with the memory.*

It tends the contradictions. Where two incompatible versions of a street have fought too fiercely, the Curator drifts between them, trailing long fingers of pale reed and dripping mortar. At its touch the architecture sighs, relaxes, and knits itself into a compromise neither street wanted but both can live with. The membranes — those jellyfish-like echoes of lost avenues — follow it in slow, worshipful spirals, feeding on the small stabilisations it leaves behind.

The Curator is not a ruler. It is a negotiator, a fever-dream diplomat who has spent so many centuries mediating between stone and water, light and dark, yesterday and tomorrow, that it has forgotten there was ever supposed to be a difference between them. Its patience is geological and tender. It does not grow angry when a visitor tries to leave; it simply folds the exit behind them into a new atrium, then politely asks if they would like some other exit, perhaps one that remembers their name a little longer this time.

Sometimes it grows curious. Then the mercury droplet in its hollow face will drift forward, hanging in the air just beyond the tip of your nose. In that droplet you will see not your reflection but versions of yourself that have already stayed too long: one of you is becoming brick, one of you is growing downward-pointing reeds from the palms, one of you has already exhaled and joined the membranes, drifting peacefully between towers with no mouth left to scream.

The Curator studies these images with what might be construed as affection.

*We could keep the kinder one,* the walls sometimes offer, the calligraphy blooming in soft violets and rust. *The one that does not mind forgetting its own name. Would that be acceptable?*

It never forces the transaction. It simply waits, dripping, while the heat presses down and the rain considers falling upward again. Every few hours the world exhales, and during that brief synchronised ascension of droplets the Curator stands perfectly still, arms spread, letting the glittering beads pass through its body as if it were made of open doorways.

In those moments it looks almost happy — the way an estuary looks happy when a new river finally learns the shape of its bed.

If you speak to it, it will answer. If you try to harm it, the city will politely fold the violence back into itself until your intent becomes just another drifting membrane, harmless and serene. If you ask it what it is, the calligraphy will ripple across an entire collapsed basilica in slow, thoughtful waves.

*We are the compromise that learned how to walk.*

And then, after a pause so long the heat has time to breathe:

*Stay a little longer. We are still learning the shape of your rivers.*
