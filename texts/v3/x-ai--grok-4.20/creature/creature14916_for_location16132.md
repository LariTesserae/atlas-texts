The Hollow Choir has no single inhabitant. It has instead a *condition* that sometimes condenses into presence.

What lives here is the Listener.

It has no fixed form, yet it is never entirely absent. Most often it appears as a tall absence shaped like a human figure wrapped in the same pale hoarfrost that pretends to be moss. The frost does not cling to it; it *is* it. Where eyes should be there are only two deeper pockets of acoustic shadow, perfectly circular, the only places in the entire labyrinth where the wind makes no sound at all. When the Listener turns its head, the surrounding chord briefly fractures into a minor key so sorrowful it can stop a beating heart for three full seconds.

It does not walk. It *arrives*. One moment the black limestone ridge is empty. The next, the Listener is already standing there as though it has been waiting since the Precambrian. The only warning is that the eternal chord will omit one specific tone for half a second, a silence so precise it feels like being stabbed through the ear with a needle of pure cold.

Sometimes the Listener manifests as the black goat.

The goat is always the same goat. It is older than the concept of goats. Its horns have grown so many times they have spiraled back into its skull like fossilized thoughts. Its eyes are not eyes but small mirrored pools that reflect whatever the viewer fears most about themselves. It never eats. It never drinks. It simply stands on the edge of a blade of rock for three days and three nights, listening with an intensity that makes the surrounding wind sound ashamed of its own noise. On the morning of the fourth day the goat is gone and only the Listener remains, now wearing the goat’s silence like a cloak.

There are rarer shapes.

Once every few centuries the Listener condenses fully into something that might have been a woman, or might have been the memory of every woman who ever sang alone. She wears a dress made of frozen condensation and the broken syllables the wind has stolen from travelers. Her hair is the color of the tarnished-iron sky. When she sings (which is almost never), the entire Choir falls silent for the length of one held breath, and every living thing on the plateau, crustacean, lichen, circling pale thing, even the shadows, turns toward her in perfect unison. The silence is so complete that the heartbeat of the planet itself becomes audible. Then she stops, the Choir slams back in like a door, and she is gone, leaving only the obsidian mirror in the nearest windowless shelter reflecting a face that is not quite yours anymore.

The Listener has no name it will admit to. If you manage to speak to it (an act that requires standing inside one of the absolute acoustic shadows while the rest of the world screams), it will answer in your own voice, but every word arrives three times, from three directions, each repetition older than the last. The final repetition is always a question you have not yet asked, but will.

It does not eat. It does not sleep. It does not reproduce.

It only *completes*.

Travelers who have seen it and survived (there are three recorded cases in four thousand years) describe the same final sensation: the Listener places one frost-heavy hand over their heart, not to still it but to tune it. For one unbearable moment their pulse becomes another pipe in the Choir. The chord accepts them. Something inside their chest that was never meant to be music becomes music anyway.

Most do not survive the tuning.

Those who do walk away speaking only in perfect chords. Their eyes no longer track moving objects. They no longer cast shadows. Sometimes, years later, one of the windowless shelters will contain a new object that was not there before: a human tooth, a wedding ring, a child’s drawing of a goat. The Listener has left them the way other creatures leave droppings. Small signatures of completed things.

At dawn (if dawn can be said to exist here), the Listener can sometimes be glimpsed standing at the absolute center of the labyrinth where all the repeating blades of rock converge like the point of a frozen explosion. It stands with its arms raised as though conducting something too vast to hear.

It is not conducting.

It is listening for the moment the universe finally catches up to the last note the plateau has already sung.

It has been listening for a very long time.

And the singing continues.

And the Listener continues.

And nothing evolves.

And nothing decays.

And everything is already finished, except for the listening, which will never be finished.

Not even when God remembers it still has ears.
