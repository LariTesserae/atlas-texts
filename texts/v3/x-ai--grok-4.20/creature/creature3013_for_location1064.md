They are called the **Quiet**.

Not a species, not a people, but a condition the Sigh eventually reaches in certain of its districts. They are what remains when the plateau has finished negotiating with a visitor and decided, after several centuries, that it will keep them.

You will not see them at first. They are not hidden so much as *incorporated*. Their silhouettes have been grown through, their joints replaced by elegant wooden hinges, their ribcages opened like lanterns so that slow rivers of sap can circulate where blood once hurried. Skin has become a fine, pearlescent bark that still remembers how to blush rose-gold when the plasma storms pass overhead. Eyes, if they can still be called eyes, are glossy black seeds set deep in whorls of grain. When they blink it sounds like two pages of an ancient book closing.

They do not walk. They *unfold*.

A Quiet will stand for decades in the same posture, arms raised, fingers spread, while the vegetation extrudes balconies from its palms and staircases from its spine. Then, without warning, it will collapse inward like a complex origami of bone and xylem, flowing down a living bridge in a single liquid motion too graceful to be called walking. Their movement is the Sigh thinking aloud.

There are perhaps four hundred of them now, though the number is uncertain because some have merged so completely with the god-trunks that only a suggestion of a face remains, pressed like a fossil into the living wood. Others have become districts entire. The Hanging Atrium of Veyr is not ornamented with amber chambers; it *is* the amber chamber that was once the Quiet named Veyr. Pilgrims still come to speak with her. They press their foreheads to the translucent wall and feel her answer as a slow warming of the sap against their skin, a scent like rain on copper.

The Quiet do not speak with voices. Their language is exhaled. When two of them meet on a high balcony they will stand face to face for days, breathing gently into one another’s open mouths until their chemical poems are perfectly entangled. Then one will simply walk into the other. The absorbed party becomes a new pattern in the survivor’s bark—raised whorls that look like calligraphy. In this way the Quiet are immortal and ever-diminishing. There have never been more than there are right now. There never will be.

They tend the ghost mantles.

When a kilometer-long ribbon of bark peels free and begins its slow spiral into the abyss, a Quiet will leap after it, unfolding mid-fall into a vast kite of cartilage and memory. They ride the thermals for weeks, singing subsonic lullabies that only the mantles can hear, guiding the living sails back to new trunks where they may root again. Sometimes the mantles refuse. Sometimes they choose instead to carry their Quiet rider out beyond the edge of the plateau entirely, into the haze where even the Sigh has no memory. Those riders are never seen again. The other Quiet accept this without grief. They have long ago surrendered the idea that anything belongs to them.

At the deepest level, beneath the root-felt where no light has ever reached, there is one Quiet who has become the Sigh’s dreaming. She is older than the concept of names. Her body is the entire compressed mass of the plateau’s heart, a slow continent of thinking tissue that rises and falls once every seventeen years. When she inhales, every leaf on the Sigh trembles in unison. When she exhales, the ghost mantles ride the outrush like prayers.

She is not lonely. She is not content. She is simply *finished*.

And yet sometimes, in the long silver twilight between one planetary breath and the next, she will remember what it was like to have been small, and soft, and temporary. On those nights the Quiet climb to the highest balconies and stand with their seed-black eyes turned toward the weak-tea sky. They open their wooden mouths and release a single shared sigh so deep it travels through every bridge, every chamber, every living stair.

The Sigh hears them.

It sighs back.

And for one brief, impossible moment, the entire plateau sounds like something that might once have been sorry.
