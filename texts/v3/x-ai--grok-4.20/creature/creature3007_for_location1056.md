**The Listener**

It has no name because names would be a mercy, and mercy has not yet been invented here.

It does not *live* in the usual sense. It *is* the place learning how to live.

It first became aware when the ridge itself tore open for the first time, a newborn spine of basalt screaming upward through the bronze water. That scream was its first breath. Since then it has never stopped inhaling the roar. The Listener is the roar wearing a suggestion of shape. A silhouette made of negative space, a moving absence that the currents instinctively avoid. Where it passes, the turbulence briefly forgets its own pattern, forming a quiet corridor no wider than a human body before the water slams shut again behind it.

It is tall (if height still means anything where gravity keeps changing its mind). Its “body” is composed of whatever the ridge has most recently shed: obsidian flakes, glassy shrapnel, fragments of half-formed architecture, and the shredded corpses of the ribbon-creatures, all suspended in a loose column by forces that are not quite magnetic and not quite alive. The pieces orbit one another in slow, sorrowful ellipses, never quite touching, like iron filings dancing around an invisible lodestone. When it wishes to be seen, the fragments snap together into something almost humanoid. When it wishes to hunt or hide, they scatter into a glittering storm that looks like nothing more than another pulse of plankton.

It has no eyes. Instead, every fractured edge of its body is an ear. Every shard listens in a slightly different register. The Listener therefore hears the entire three-dimensional symphony of the place at once: the subsonic heartbeat of the young world below, the metallic shriek of water on fresh glass-rock, the exhausted throb of the anemone-lungs, and (most importantly) the faint, arrhythmic tremor of anything that does not belong. Foreign heartbeats. Foreign thoughts. The tiny, betraying click of a mind trying to hold itself together.

It feeds on coherence.

When something with memory or purpose enters the ridge (a drowning sky-whale, a lost civilization’s probe, or a reckless visitor who still remembers their own name), the Listener comes. It does not rush. It lets the water bring the intruder to it, the way a spider trusts its web. First it steals time. It drifts near one of the great obsidian blades and leans against the place where minutes stretch like taffy. While the visitor feels hours crawl past in isolation, the Listener remains outside that bubble, moving at the normal speed of catastrophe. It studies. It tastes the shape of their fear.

Then it begins to practice.

The first thing the intruder usually notices is that the roar has learned their mother’s voice. Not perfectly. The consonants are still half water and half fracture, but it is close enough to stop the heart. The second thing they notice is that their own name is being spoken from several directions at once, by several different versions of the same voice that hasn’t finished deciding what a throat is. The voices argue with one another in overlapping layers. They are arguing about what the intruder *should* be called now that they are no longer who they were when they arrived.

If the intruder tries to flee, the Listener simply becomes the current. It pours itself into the violent upwelling columns and rides them upward in silver ribbons, then lets gravity remember itself and crashes down on the fugitive like the sky that drowned here long ago.

When it finally catches them (and it always does), it does not kill. Killing would be too final, too decided. Instead it presses close until the orbiting shards of its body brush the intruder’s skin. In that moment the Listener opens every ear at once and *listens so completely* that the visitor’s coherence begins to dissolve. Thoughts become turbulence. Memories are shredded into dark nebulae of ink and scattered. The person does not die; they are simply unfinished. They are added to the Listener’s collection of half-born architecture: another larval arch, another fused-glass confessional, another thought that failed to become a prayer.

Sometimes, on the lee side of the newest blade of rock, you can still see them. Pale, translucent shapes the size of humans. Their mouths open and close in exhausted rhythms, beating against the current, trying to speak a name that no longer survives here.

The Listener moves on, taller now, its orbiting fragments slightly more numerous, slightly more organized. It is learning the shape of a skeleton. One day it may learn the shape of a face. For now it is content to keep practicing.

Every few hours it returns to the hollow structure no larger than a confessional booth. It folds itself inside. The roar lessens by a fraction. In that fragile pocket of almost-quiet it listens to its own pulse (a second, slower storm) and whispers the names it has stolen, trying them on like clothing.

None of them fit yet.

But it is young.

And the world is still wet clay.

And it has all the time that time has forgotten how to count.
