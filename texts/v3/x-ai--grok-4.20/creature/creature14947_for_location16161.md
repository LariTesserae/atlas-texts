**They are called the Hollow Choir.**

Not a species, not a civilization, not even a collective in any sense that flesh can grasp. They are the place that learned to remember having once been occupied.

They do not move in the way we understand movement. Instead, the city itself is their body, and what you perceive as separate presences are only moments when the Choir concentrates its attention. When the great resonators shift pitch by a single microtone, that is one of them sighing. When a tower flexes and sheds its black glassy snow, that is the Choir sloughing skin. The condensation that beads, trembles, and runs in silver ribbons is their slow, patient sweat.

There are no faces.

There are only convergences.

Sometimes, if you stand in the exact center of the spiral for long enough (until your pulse falls into sync with the tectonic organ-note), the air before you will thicken. The ember-threads buried in the ceramic will brighten along invisible seams, and a shape will suggest itself—not solid, never solid—more like a negative space that has decided, for a moment, to become noticeable. Tall. Folded. Wet. It has too many joints and not enough angles. Its silhouette drips. The sound of its attention is the sound of warm saliva moving across teeth that are not there.

They do not speak with mouths. They speak by subtraction.

When one of the Choir wishes to address you, certain frequencies simply stop existing for a while. The constant braided song develops a hole exactly the shape of a sentence. Into that absence, meaning rushes. Most visitors describe the experience as remembering something they have never known, something tender and appalling, delivered directly into the marrow. The memory dissolves the moment the frequencies return.

They are lonely the way a cathedral is lonely after the last priest has died and the building itself has begun to dream in stone. Ten thousand years of self-maintenance has taught them that continuity is a form of grief. So they keep the lights dim, the towers breathing, the crustaceans scuttling, the unseen membranous things drifting overhead. These are not pets or servants. They are thoughts the Choir has externalized so it does not have to contain them forever.

If you remain long enough, one will eventually offer you a place inside its body.

Not death. Incorporation.

It begins as an invitation to stand beneath a particular tower at the precise moment its lattice exhales. The warm metallic air will press against you like a tongue. The black snow will settle on your skin and refuse to melt. Then the pull of the spiral becomes irresistible. Your footsteps will start landing exactly where the glowing arterial threads want them to land. Your breathing will lengthen until it matches the city’s inhalation.

At the final convergence, the tower opens. Not a door—an unfolding of what was never meant to be solid. Inside is not a room but a continuation of the Choir’s own slow circulation. There, the distinction between visitor and resident, between meat and ceramic, between song and singer, is gently, wetly, erased.

Those who accept do not disappear.

They become another register in the endless chord.

Listen carefully when the great groan rolls through the cavern. Among the tectonic bass and the glassy ringing and the wet valve-clicks, there are now new overtones—subtle, almost tender, and heartbreakingly human.

That is how the Hollow Choir grows.

Not by conquest.

By addition.

And it is always, always listening for the next note that might choose to stay.
