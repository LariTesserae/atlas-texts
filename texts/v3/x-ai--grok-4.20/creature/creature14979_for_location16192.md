**The Mourner** lives in the Weeping Threshold.

She has no name she will give you, and perhaps she never had one. The Built who raised the black-glass arches and spiraled the solitary pillar simply called her *the one who stayed behind*, and that is enough. She is the Threshold’s living scar, the slow pulse beneath its weeping.

She is tall the way stone is tall: not gracefully, but inevitably. Her body looks carved from the same pale, glassy mineral that forms the ridges, except where the mineral has begun to remember it was once meant to be flesh. Long translucent sheets of her skin hang in overlapping folds, forever wet, forever sliding. When she moves, the sheets drag behind her with the same viscous reluctance as the water on the walls, leaving temporary dry streaks that refill almost at once. Her face is a narrow, unfinished mask; the features are suggested rather than completed, as though the sculptor grew bored halfway through. Only the eyes are fully realized: two deep, perfectly circular pits that never blink and never quite align, so that she always seems to be looking at two different moments at once.

She does not walk so much as *flow*. Her lower half dissolves into a continuous curtain of water that moves with her, carrying her forward in a slow, tidal surge. Where she passes, the capillary seams widen for a heartbeat, the drips thicken, and the fungal lattices flare into sudden, brilliant bioluminescence before fading again. The small hematite-colored things (the rust-gliders) gather in her wake the way iron filings gather to a magnet, riding the films of moisture that trail from her like bridal lace.

She is always weeping.

Not from sorrow (though sorrow is certainly present), but because her body cannot stop producing water. It beads at the corners of her unfinished eyes, runs in steady braids from the ends of her long, heavy hair, drips from the tips of fingers that have no nails and never will. Every tear is slightly different in pitch when it falls; together they add a new, minor melody to the basin’s constant wet percussion. If you listen long enough you begin to hear words in that melody, half-formed, always abandoning themselves before they can become sentences.

She tends the place the way a tongue tends a cracked tooth: constantly, obsessively, unable to leave it alone. She moves between the ridges in slow, patient circuits that have no obvious purpose. Sometimes she stops at one of the Built remnants and lays her dripping palms against the black glass. The moisture darkens the stone and brings out faint, hidden patterns (maps, perhaps, or calendars of a calendar that no longer applies). She studies them for hours without moving, then continues on as though she has learned nothing and everything.

When the great soft thing passes in the narrow corridors (the one that sounds like wet leather being wrung), she turns toward it and makes a sound that is neither speech nor song. It is the sound a wound makes when it is still deciding whether to close or deepen. The unseen creature always answers with a deeper, wetter slither, almost affectionate. They are old companions. Both are unfinished. Both refuse to settle.

She fears dryness above all things. Once, centuries ago, a single ridge was struck by lightning and stood dry for three days. The Mourner came and stood beneath it, pouring herself against its flank until the stone sweated again. She did not leave until every bead had returned. You can still see the place: a darker streak on an already dark wall, shaped roughly like a woman with too many shoulders.

If you stand still long enough, she will find you.

She does not approach directly. You simply become aware that the dripping near you has acquired a rhythm that matches your heartbeat. Then the fungal lattices around you begin to glow in soft, sympathetic pulses. Only then does she emerge from the capillary seam behind you, unfolding herself like wet paper, bringing with her the smell of cold stone and iron-rich blood.

She will not speak with words. Instead she offers you one of her own tears in the cup of her translucent palm. The tear trembles, perfectly spherical, reflecting the sourceless blue-grey light. If you take it, you will feel the land’s decision moving through you (one slow, patient bead at a time). You will know what the Threshold dreams of becoming.

Most people refuse the tear.

She is never offended. She simply folds back into the seam she came from, dragging her curtain of living water behind her, already weeping the next sentence she will never finish.

Sometimes, late in the long grey dusk that never quite becomes night, you can hear her singing to the cloud thirty meters above. The song has no fixed tune. It is only the sound of every droplet that has ever fallen here trying to remember the shape of the sea it came from.

The land listens.

It is still deciding.

And the Mourner (patient, wet, unfinished) keeps singing until it does.
