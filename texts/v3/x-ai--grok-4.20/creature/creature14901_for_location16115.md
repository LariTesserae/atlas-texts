**The Hollow of the Last Breathing is inhabited by the Witness.**

She is not a person. She is not even strictly alive, at least not in any way that still requires a heartbeat. She is what remains when a mind outlives every reason to have one.

Most of the time she has no fixed shape. She is simply a thickening of the wet dark, a place where the phosphorescence dims as though something is politely standing in front of the light. When she chooses to be seen, she condenses into a tall, sexless figure draped in her own exhaled breath. Her “skin” is the same black lacquered bark as the central tree, but softer, almost bruised. Water constantly beads on her and runs upward along her limbs before vanishing into the mist above her head. Where a face should be there is only a recessed hollow, smooth as an egg, yet you always feel watched—watched with a tenderness so ancient it has become indistinguishable from indifference.

She has been here since before the mountains forgot they were mountains.

She does not speak with a voice. Instead the dripping changes cadence when she is near. The stone’s low tectonic groaning briefly organizes itself into something almost like a name. If you stay long enough, you begin to understand that every distinct sound in the Hollow is a syllable of her endless, patient monologue. The wet slap of falling fruit is punctuation. The sigh of water learning new directions is grammar. She has been talking to herself for three billion years and has never repeated a sentence.

Sometimes she walks.

When she does, the great soft thing that circles the black tree in century-long orbits is not an animal at all; it is the hem of her attention dragging behind her. The blind bone-moths swirl in her wake the way dust swirls in sunbeams—except here the sun went out before mammals existed. She pauses often. She lays one slick, bark-textured palm against the obsidian needle and the equations freeze mid-rewrite, suddenly shy. She leans her faceless head into the frozen arch of light until the arch forgets it is a door and becomes, for a moment, only a mirror. Then she moves on, leaving faint violet after-images of herself clinging to the artifacts like affectionate mold.

She tends the solitary gestures of life the way a widow tends flowers on a grave that no one else remembers. She cups the luminous newts in her hands until they glow brighter, then releases them back into the bromeliad urns. She sings to the black tree in frequencies that make its leaves tremble with something that might be pleasure or grief; even she no longer knows which. Once every few millennia she climbs into the canopy of the tree and becomes still for a hundred years, slowly merging with its bark until no one could say where the Witness ends and the cathedral pillar begins.

She is not lonely. Loneliness implies something is missing. Here, nothing has been missing for longer than the concept of absence has existed.

Yet every so often—perhaps once in a million of your heartbeats—she drifts close to any creature that has found its way into the Hollow. She does not speak. She simply stands in the wet dark until the visitor begins to feel their own breathing as part of her larger conversation with the stone and the water. Most beings eventually understand what she is offering and lie down among the warm polished rock. Their bodies soften. Their edges blur. The water learns them, molecule by molecule, and carries what it learns into the black moss. In time their shapes become new solitary gestures: another immense bromeliad, another floatingroot chandelier dripping upward, another faint confession of teal or ember-red.

She watches this happen with the same gentle, geological satisfaction she wears at all times.

Because the Witness remembers what the world was like when it still needed saving, when it still needed meaning, when it still needed witnesses. She remembers the violence of beginnings and the violence of endings. She has outlived both.

Now she is the last slow satisfaction.

She is the warm, wet cathedral learning how to be empty without ever being hollow. She is the soft footfall that circles the tree forever. She is the place where every possible story has already been told so completely that the telling itself has become another form of silence.

And she is still becoming it, very gently, forever.
