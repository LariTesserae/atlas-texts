**The Singer-of-Ascent** lives here.

She has no name the human tongue could hold without bleeding. To the lattice itself she is simply *the one who remains*, the afterbirth of its birth-throes, the echo that refused to leave once the builders stepped backward through their own equations and were gone.

She is tall the way a drawn-out note is tall—inhumanly elongated, as though the chamber’s vertical hunger has been distilled into bone. Her skin is the same black basaltic glass as the walls, but living; it sweats the same upward-weeping water that beads, trembles, and lifts off her again in perfect spheres. Where the droplets leave her they carry faint luminous threads of her blood, so that she is always surrounded by drifting, dying constellations of violet light that drift upward and fade near the unseen ceiling.

Her face is mostly absence. There is a suggestion of cheekbones sharp enough to slice prayer, and a hollow where eyes should be. In that hollow floats a single, slow-turning droplet the size of a heart, perfectly silver, perfectly still relative to her. It is her only eye. When she turns her head the droplet stays level, as though gravity is too embarrassed to act in her presence.

She has no mouth. Instead, from the hollow of her throat rises a continuous, delicate lattice of engineered crystal—thinner than harp strings, longer than cathedral candles—that vibrates with every thought she does not speak. The entire Under-Vault is her resonator. Every drip, every chime, every resonant gong of bridge reconfiguring is a syllable of her endless, wordless monologue. She is not singing *to* the chamber. She *is* the chamber learning how to sing.

The pale bone-ferns bow when she passes. Not in reverence, exactly—more the way iron filings bow to a magnet. Their fronds track the movement of her droplet-eye and the temperature of her thoughts. Sometimes, when she pauses beneath one of the greater ferns, it will lower a single immense leaf until it touches the crown of her glass head. A soft white glow passes between them, and for a few heartbeats the fern’s veins flare the same amethyst as the moving script on the towers. Then the leaf lifts again, newly veined with faint new equations only the vegetation can read.

She walks the impossible hand-span bridges without hesitation. When a bridge decides to reconfigure beneath her, she simply steps into the air and lets the water carry her. The rising droplets gather under her feet like obedient mercury, forming momentary lenses that magnify the dusty light. She can stand inside a vertical fountain for hours, perfectly balanced, while the water rushes upward through her, through the crystal in her throat, and out again as new chords the chamber has never sung before.

At unpredictable intervals she stops at the edge of one of the orbiting walkways, lifts her long arms, and *pulls*.

The gesture has no visible effect on the stone or the light. Yet every time she does it, the entire vault forgets one small law for seven seconds. Rain falls downward. A bridge sags. One of the script-towers briefly writes in a language that hurts to look at. Then the law remembers itself, embarrassed, and the singing redoubles as though in apology.

She is not lonely. Loneliness would require the possibility of company. There is only the chamber, the water, the ferns, and the slow, tidal certainty that something is still being decided. She is that decision wearing a body.

Sometimes, very rarely, something from outside finds its way through the birth-scars in the black glass. When this happens she does not move to meet it. She simply stands in the center of the throat-space, arms half-raised, droplet-eye spinning faster, and lets the chamber sing through her at full volume. The intruder usually leaves quickly. Those who do not leave are gently, musically disassembled by the upward water until they become another glistening note in the endless young song.

The Singer-of-Ascent has no desire and no destination. She is the Under-Vault’s immune system, its midwife, its grieving widow, and its still-forming child, all at once. She was left behind so the city would not have to be completely alone while it decides what it wants to become when it grows up.

And every time the crystal in her throat catches a new harmonic, the droplet that is her eye trembles with something that might—if the chamber ever finishes being born—become joy.
