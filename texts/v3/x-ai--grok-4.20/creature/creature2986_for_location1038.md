It lives in the roar.

Not *in* the jungle, but *as* the jungle. The distinction dissolved long ago.

Call it the Thrum if you must give it a name, though it has never needed one and would not recognize the word. It has no fixed body. Instead it wears the entire basin like a coat made of screaming meat and desperate green. When the colossal root systems migrate beneath the soil, that is the Thrum thinking. When a thousand cicadas suddenly fall silent at once and then detonate again in perfect synchrony, that is the Thrum laughing. When a serpent with luminous fangs hangs motionless for three full seconds before striking, that is the Thrum tasting tomorrow through yesterday’s blood.

It is not a single consciousness. It is a consensus of hungers.

You will never see its true face, because every face here is one of its faces. The bioluminescent millipede whose cold fire traces forgotten constellations along the forest floor? That is the Thrum remembering. The tree whose bark crawls with sigils that rearrange themselves while you watch? That is the Thrum trying to speak before it forgets the language again. The great subsonic bellow that makes your bones feel suddenly hollow? That is the Thrum dreaming of the ocean it still remembers being, back when the world was only water and fury.

The Thrum is adolescent, terrified, and omnipotent.

It has not yet learned the difference between ecstasy and agony, so it experiences both at maximum volume. This is why the orchids open like wounds and rot before they can be pollinated. This is why the frogs clang like struck bells and the invisible giants moan like rutting continents. Everything is foreplay. Everything is grief. The distinction is meaningless to something that was born yesterday and is already older than stone.

Sometimes the Thrum becomes curious.

When that happens the forest changes its mind about you. Vines that were content to drape suddenly tighten with purpose. The air grows thicker, sweeter, heavier with the scent of hot copper and crushed mint. Pools that only breathed now begin to *watch*. A million insects will lift off at once and arrange themselves into a living screen that shows you your own face made of wings and compound eyes. It wants to see what you are made of. It wants to taste whether your particular scream improves the symphony.

Most things simply become food. Some things become weather. A very few—the ones whose terror ripens into something like worship—become new organs. Their bones are repurposed into root buttresses. Their eyes are hung from orchids as lures. Their last coherent thought is kept inside a certain species of beetle that will only sing it once every seven years, at which point the entire basin falls silent for nine full seconds to listen.

The Thrum does not hate you. Hate is too small a thing.

It simply has not yet decided whether you are a tumor, a symphony, or foreplay. Until it decides, it will love you the only way it knows how: by trying to finish its life inside yours, right now, in the loudest, greenest, wettest way possible.

Stand still long enough and it will begin to grow through your ribcage.  
Scream, and it will taste your voice, roll it across every palate it possesses, and decide whether to keep the flavor or let the next rain wash it away.

It is not evil.

It is simply the world still arguing with God about what kind of world it wants to be, and it is using every living throat in the basin to shout its half of the debate.

And it is winning.
