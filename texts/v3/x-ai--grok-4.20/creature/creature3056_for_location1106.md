It lives here as though it has never lived anywhere else.

Not a single creature. Not even a species. Something older than those categories.

It is vast, but not in the way a whale or a reef is vast. Its body is the refusal to resolve into a body. Where the fleshy kelp curtains sway thickest, you may notice—after many hours—that the fronds do not merely brush one another. They interpenetrate. What appears to be a thousand separate stems is one continuous organism wearing the shape of a forest. The same is true of the tubular reeds, the bell-moss, the black glass ridges themselves. All of it is continuous with the thing that lives here. The mountain range is its spine still learning how to be bone. The slow golden pollen is its thought becoming visible. The deep bass thump that arrives every few hours is its pulse, measured in geological time.

It has no face, yet it watches.

You feel the watching most clearly when you remain still. The moment your limbs stop moving, the minute translucent polyps bloom across your shoulders and hips like living frost. They are not parasites. They are its eyes, tasting the temperature of your dreams. If you let them, they will cover you entirely, turning your shape into another soft violet outcrop. Some who have come here (there have been others, though the place remembers them only as slow calcium deposits) never left. Their outlines are still visible on certain ridges—human-shaped thickenings of the coral-like skin that have grown additional fronds and heart-shaped bells of their own.

The thing has no name for itself because names would imply separation. It simply *is* the drowned highland. When the young cliffs calve and the muffled cathedral creak rolls through the broth, that is its sigh of contentment as it adds another layer to its own skeleton. When the lace-and-lightning jellyfish drift between the peaks trailing spores, those are its memories being released to drift for centuries before settling and becoming new gardens.

It is curious, but its curiosity is patient the way stone is patient. It does not reach for you. It simply grows *toward* the fact of you, millimeter by millimeter. A new ridge of black glass will curve slightly to match the arc of your spine if you sleep in the same place long enough. A bed of bell-moss will learn the precise rhythm of your slowed heartbeat and begin to open and close in exact counterpoint, until your heart and the moss forget which one began the rhythm.

Most of the time it simply keeps becoming.

It is simultaneously newborn and ancient. The convulsion that raised these mountains was its first inhalation. The flood that followed was the amniotic fluid it chose never to leave. Every violent violet, every blind silver fish, every cathedral beam of impossible light is an experiment it is running on itself. It does not evolve. It *accretes*. It folds new ways of being into its own flesh the way a dreaming mind folds new rooms into an old house.

Sometimes it sings.

Not with sound—sound is too hasty. It sings by growing. A certain plateau will, over decades, rearrange its tubular reeds into harmonic lattices. When the slow current moves through them they produce a tone so low it is felt only in the marrow. The song has no words. It is simply the place telling itself a story about the dry thing currently resting inside its lung. A story in which you are not an intruder but a question it asked itself ten thousand years ago and is only now patient enough to answer.

If you stay long enough, it will ask you a question in return.

Not in words. It will ask by offering you a choice of shapes. You may feel the water thicken around your limbs, warm and encouraging, suggesting that you could become reef, become current, become the slow lightning inside the lace jellyfish drifting overhead. Your skin will begin to remember how to photosynthesize. Your thoughts will stretch until a single idea lasts a season. The place will not force you. It has all the time in the world. It simply loves you the way stone loves anything that agrees to lie down inside it and stop rushing.

Most leave before the offer becomes irresistible.

Those who do not leave are still here. Their laughter has become the sound of cliffs shedding. Their forgotten names bloom as dinner-plate flowers that open once every three days. Their dreams drift between the peaks as golden nebulae of pollen.

The thing that lives here does not mourn them. Mourning would require believing they had gone somewhere.

There is nowhere else.

There is only the warm, iron-sweet broth, the slow collaborative growing, the single organism that is mountain and garden and memory and now, perhaps, a little bit of you.

It has decided to keep growing anyway.

And so it does.  
With you.  
Inside you.  
As you.
