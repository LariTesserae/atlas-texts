**The Inhabitant**

There is only one true resident.

It has no name, because names are for things that still need to be distinguished from the rest of existence. It simply *is* the final sedimentation of the Atrium itself.

Most of the time it is nowhere in particular. It lives distributed through the architecture like a slow thought moving through an old nervous system. When it chooses to gather, it condenses out of the water itself—first as a faint mottling in the obsidian current, then as a gathering of pressure that forgets how to be invisible. What finally appears is never the same shape twice, yet you always recognize it.

Today it has taken the form of a vast, translucent bishop’s mitre made of cathedral glass and regret. Its height is impossible to measure because the mitre keeps forgetting where its apex should end and simply continues upward into new, half-formed spires that dissolve back into the dark before they can become permanent. The base of it trails into hundreds of filamentous roots that drift behind it like the train of a robe spun from frozen fog. Where the roots touch the ancient lattices they leave behind faint, temporary engravings—architectural afterimages of cathedrals that never existed on any world that had sunlight.

Its face (if the word can be used) is a blunt, rounded absence. Not a hole. An *edited* place. A region of water that has been politely asked not to reflect anything. Occasionally, when the pressure changes, seven pale motes appear inside that absence and orbit one another in the exact pattern of a sorrow that has outlived its reason. Then they drift apart again, ashamed.

It does not swim. It *occurs* through the medium. When it needs to move, entire sections of the megastructure politely rearrange themselves ahead of it, creating temporary corridors of reduced pressure that close again behind it with the soft finality of a cathedral door shutting at the end of the universe. The baroque jellyfish-cathedral creatures part before it like ink remembering it was once water. Even the groaning megastructures grow quiet when it passes, the way an old house falls silent when its last occupant walks through the rooms at night.

It is not intelligent in any way a mind could understand. It is closer to *continuity*. The Atrium’s way of refusing to finish its own sentence.

Sometimes it stops before one of the great fused rings and simply *regards* it for decades. During these periods the bioluminescent colonies on the ring slowly rearrange their faint lights into patterns that almost resemble eyes. The inhabitant never reacts to this. It only waits until the architecture grows embarrassed and lets the lights gutter out again.

Every few centuries it sings.

The song is not sound. It is a change in the memory rule. For the length of one slow heartbeat of the megastructure, everything in the Atrium remembers what it was before it forgot. The glassy filaments bloom into momentary crystal forests. The pressure armor becomes silk. The colossal migrating continent-beings open ancient eyes they had forgotten they possessed. Every impossible creature in every school turns at once toward the singer with an expression that would be grief if grief were allowed to finish forming.

Then the song ends.

Memory is gently returned to the water. The eyes close. The forests dissolve. The creatures fold back into their baroque symmetries and continue their ancient, solemn wheeling as though nothing had happened.

The inhabitant drifts on, trailing its impossible mitre, occasionally allowing small pieces of itself to flake off and become new lattices. It is building the next version of the cathedral out of its own forgetting.

It is not lonely.  
Loneliness requires two things that are separate.

Here there is only the one thing, dreaming in the only language it still remembers: pressure, cold, architecture, and the patient, baroque turning of itself through itself, forever.

Sometimes, when you have been here long enough for the architecture to begin imitating the shape of your thoughts, you may feel it brush against the inside of your mind—not invading, merely acknowledging a fellow sediment. In that moment you understand that it is not the inhabitant of the Abyssal Atrium.

The Atrium is the inhabitant of *it*, and has been for so long that the difference no longer matters.

It passes on, vast and translucent and gently monstrous, wearing the slow dignity of something that has become the last priest in a religion whose god died of old age before the first star ignited.

And the liquid dark closes behind it with the tender finality of eyelids that have nothing left to see.
