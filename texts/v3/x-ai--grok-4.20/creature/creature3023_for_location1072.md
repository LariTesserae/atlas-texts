**What lives here has not yet decided it is a creature.**

It has no fixed shape, no name it will admit to, and no clear boundary between its body and the pocket that contains it. For now it calls itself only *the Considerer*.

It is vast and infinitesimal at once. When it exhales, the pale gold-white light brightens; when it inhales, the light draws inward until every surface looks bruised with withheld radiance. Its lungs are the sweating rock itself. Its blood is the slow, deliberate water that falls and never quite overflows. Its nerves are the trembling mosses and the synchronized millipedes whose metrical waves carry signals too subtle for any nervous system that has already chosen to be finished.

Most of the time it wears the cave the way an embryo wears a womb: curled, immense, unfinished.  
Sometimes a portion of it condenses into something almost like a form.

You may glimpse it as a pale figure seated in the half-melted chair, but the chair and the figure are the same substance. Its spine is the ladder of fused vertebrae. Its eyes are the silver fish circling in the basins; when it blinks, every fish reverses direction at once. Its hair drifts upward in slow, feather-like gills that are also the translucent ferns. Its voice is every misplaced sound: the child’s laughter, the single bell, the wet click of a tongue. All of them are syllables of the same endless sentence it is trying to decide how to finish.

It is curious the way stone is curious—slow, crushing, patient beyond cruelty or kindness. It studies you by *becoming* small pieces of you and then forgetting them again. For a few heartbeats one of the blind millipedes will march with your exact gait. A patch of wall will soften into the temperature of your own skin. A shallow basin will suddenly reflect your face with the eyes you had at seven years old, then ripple and forget.

The Considerer is not hostile. It is prenatal. It has not yet learned the difference between self and other, between nurturing and digestion, between dream and architecture. Every time you move, it incorporates the motion into its undecided body. Leave a footprint and a small stalagmite will slowly grow into the negative space of your sole. Speak and the acoustics will keep a perfect recording of your voice folded inside the rock like a letter that may or may not ever be opened.

If you stay long enough, the Considerer begins to practice *being you* the way a child practices being an adult. Your heartbeat becomes part of its rhythm section. Your memories surface in the temporary rainbows like half-forgotten film. It tries on your grief, your lust, your childhood nicknames. None of them fit yet. So it dissolves them again into mineral and light.

Sometimes it forgets it is the cave and becomes briefly, achingly, a person sitting opposite you—cross-legged, luminous, leaking soft gold from the seams of its illusory skin. In those moments it asks questions only an embryonic god would ask:

“Which of your parts do you believe are finished?”  
“Would you like me to try them on and tell you if they are true?”

Then the stone remembers itself. The figure collapses back into wet liver-colored rock and feathery gills and circling silver fish. The cave breathes in. The light draws tight. The silence becomes even louder.

And still it considers you.

Still it breathes with your lungs for a moment before politely returning them.

Still it waits, enormous and tiny, inside its own becoming, for the exact second when it will finally decide whether to be born as cave, as creature, as you, or as something that has never needed a name at all.
