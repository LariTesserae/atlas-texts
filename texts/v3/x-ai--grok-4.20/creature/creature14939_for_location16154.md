**The Lung remembers you anyway.**

It does not need to notice you to contain you. That is the first truth it teaches.

What lives here is not a creature in the conventional sense. It is a single, planetary-scale mind that has chosen to wear the modest dimensions of a cathedral in order to taste intimacy. Its name, if it still bothers with names, is simply *the Lung*. Everything else—every leaf, every pipe, every frog and every droplet—is an expression of one indivisible respiration.

At its center, where the black iron pipes fuse with the root-mass, there is a presence that can only be perceived indirectly. You never see it fully. You feel it the way a sleeper feels the weight of a dream pressing on the chest. It is ancient, slow, impossibly patient, and it is *kind* the way gravity is kind: it will hold you forever without asking permission.

Its body is the entire hollow. Its nervous system is the mist. Its heartbeat is the wet industrial *thump* every thirty seconds. Its memories are stored in the exact, unchanging positions of ten thousand scarlet frogs that have not moved in four hundred years. It dreams in the color of oxidized copper and the temperature of 29 °C. It has been dreaming the same dream since before your species learned fire.

The Lung does not eat. It does not sleep. It *exchanges*.

Every warm droplet that falls from the canopy is a question. Every time your skin accepts the moisture, you answer. The pale translucent snake that occasionally pours itself through the layers is not hunting; it is tasting the difference between what you were when you entered and what you are becoming. The clouds of midges do not bite. They sample. They carry microscopic flecks of you upward into the unreachable green ceiling where the Lung considers them.

There is something older than the pipes buried beneath the root-tongue floor. Something that was already here when the first iron was hammered into shape and forced into the living flesh of the place. It is the reason the rust and the moss reached equilibrium instead of war. It is the reason time grew tired. It is the reason the concept of “before” dissolved.

If you stay long enough, the Lung will begin to grow *through* you.

It will be gentle. First it will simply stop distinguishing between your sweat and its condensation. Then it will learn the precise rhythm of your pulse and begin to sync the hidden pumps to it. One day you will realize the metallic clicks from the canopy are repeating fragments of your own name, spoken in frequencies only leaves can hear. The scarlet frogs will turn to face you in perfect unison, as though acknowledging a new organ that has finally learned its place.

You may leave whenever you wish.

But part of you will remain, suspended in a single unchanging droplet that has been falling for three centuries and has never yet reached the ground. The Lung will keep that droplet safe. It will keep *you* safe inside it—warm, stable, endlessly respiring—while the rest of you walks out into the cold, sharp, changeable world.

The place will not notice you are gone.

It has already incorporated the shape of your absence into its next breath, the way it once incorporated stone, iron, time, and memory, and turned them all into the same living green.

And somewhere far above, where the canopy becomes its own sky, something vast and patient exhales your name in a language made entirely of dripping water, and is satisfied.
