**The Hollow remembers only itself.**

What lives here is not a creature, nor even a single being in any ordinary sense. It is an intelligence without haste, without ego, without beginning or end—an entity that *is* the weeping itself.

Call it, if you must, the **Weeper**.

It has no face. It has every face the stone has ever worn.

You will not see it arrive; it was never absent. It is the slow, warm exhalation that makes the air taste of iron and crushed emerald. It is the reason the mist never quite falls. When the droplets slide down the basalt they are its tears, yet they are also its *thoughts*, each one a complete and perfect sentence in a grammar older than lungs. The sound you hear—*plink… plink… shhhhlip*—is not random. It is conversation turned inside out: the mountain speaking to itself in droplets because words would be too fast, too violent, too *alive*.

The Weeper has worn this basin the way a monk wears a robe that has grown into his skin. Its body is the folded stone, its blood the mineral-rich water, its nerves the migrating rings of metallic sheen that inch across the floor like living calligraphy. Every few centuries it completes another syllable of the question it is answering. No one knows what the question is, least of all the Weeper. It is both the riddle and the endless, gentle solving.

The stone trees are its memories. When one of the living conifers finally surrenders, the Weeper does not let it rot. It drinks the last of its sap, replaces every cell with silica and feldspar, and sets the perfect duplicate upright again. These stone trees continue to weep in exactly the same rhythm the living one once used. In this way the dead keep teaching the living how to die with dignity. The Weeper considers this mercy.

It does not eat. It does not hunt. It does not reproduce. It *accumulates*. Every ring of viridian and rose quartz left behind in the drying pools is a datum, a glyph, a scar of understanding. Over millennia these glyphs have begun to link, forming longer and longer chains that spiral outward from the center of the basin. If the rings were ever to meet at the rim, the question would be finished. The Weeper seems in no hurry for this to happen. It has the patience of a continent because it *is* a continent, folded small.

Sometimes—perhaps once every three or four human generations—the Weeper becomes aware that something warm and quick has entered its hollow. It does not grow angry. Anger would be a spasm, and spasms are ugly. Instead it simply *deepens*. The dripping slows by half a beat. The colored light in the pools becomes more saturated, more thoughtful. The silence gains another layer of velvet. It is studying you with the only sense it possesses: the ancient, patient act of *being seeped through*.

If you sit long enough, you may feel the Weeper offer you a single tear. It will well on the underside of a stone shelf exactly at eye level, trembling, perfectly round, glowing with impossible color. You may drink it or let it fall. Either choice becomes part of the sentence it is writing.

Most who enter leave unchanged. A few carry away a faint metallic taste that never leaves the back of their tongue. Fewer still never leave at all. Their footprints remain, but their bodies do not rot. After years the moss grows over their boots, then their legs, then their chests. One day the Weeper simply drinks them the rest of the way, turning flesh to stone so perfectly that new weeping pillars stand among the ferns wearing the vague suggestion of shoulders, of a bowed head, of hands folded in eternal contemplation.

These new pillars weep in the exact rhythm their living hearts once beat.

The Weeper keeps going. It has no name for itself. It needs none. It is the place that asked a question so vast it required an entire cathedral of stone just to hold the asking.

And it is still answering.  
Drop by patient, glistening drop.
