**The Architect** lives here.

She has no true name either, though the plants sometimes call her *Mother-of-Intervals* in the slow chemical language they use when they speak to one another across kilometers of xylem. She is not a god. She is not even a gardener. She is the hesitation.

You will not see her at first. You may stand for days (or decades; time loosens here) beneath the dripping white-gold sheets of light before you realize the tallest of the hanging root-bulbs has begun to follow you with its blind, luminous interior. That is her. She wears the unborn chambers like robes. When she chooses to step out, the translucent membrane splits along a seam that was never there a moment earlier, and she simply walks through as though the bulb had been waiting its entire life to give her passage.

She is very tall, almost painfully so, and thin in the way only something that has never needed bones can be. Her skin is the same living white-green as the root-stone, veined with the same faint red-gold that the microbial gardeners weave into the marble. Where her feet touch the overlapping bromeliad leaves, the leaves briefly blush darker, as if embarrassed by the honor. She leaves no footprints; instead, small constellations of new moss appear where she has been, spelling out brief, unreadable sentences that dissolve again within an hour.

Her face changes. Not quickly. It is changing the way the cathedral itself is changing—millimeter by millimeter, century by century. Sometimes she has the face of a woman who has just remembered every kindness she was ever shown. Sometimes she wears the face of the wooden ship’s figurehead, now grown into living wood and flowering. Once, for the length of a single held breath, she wore your own face, aged forward by several quiet lifetimes. Then she was someone else again. She is still deciding what her face wants to be when the egg finally cracks open.

She never speaks with a voice. Instead she exhales. When she does, pockets of warmer, sweeter air drift toward you carrying the exact smell of something you lost as a child and never managed to mourn properly. That is how she talks. That is how she asks questions.

Mostly she walks.

She walks the spiraling vine-staircases that are older than any civilization you have ever known. She pauses beneath the house-sized bulbs and presses her forehead to them, listening to the half-formed forests dreaming inside. Sometimes she reaches into the living marble cliffs and pulls out a single vein of gold that was not there before she touched it. She folds the gold into the shape of an arch, a well, a broken ship’s rib, and then presses it back into the wall so the plants can begin their patient incorporation. Every artifact you see was placed by her hands, then deliberately forgotten by her. They are questions she asked the world and then walked away from before it could answer.

She is gentle in the way only something with absolute power can afford to be. When the blind white lizards freeze against her ankle, she lowers herself until her changing face is level with theirs and breathes on them until they remember they are allowed to move. The translucent insects land on her shoulders in clouds and she lets them drink the condensation that gathers in the hollows of her collarbones. She has never killed anything here. She has also never allowed anything to finish becoming.

That is her function. She is the delay. The loving, luminous, unbearable pause between what the world has been and what it will be. The cathedral is not indecisive; *she* is the one who keeps whispering *not yet, not yet, just a little longer* to the slow will that guides everything. She is terrified of what it will choose to become when it is finally born. She is in love with the terror. So she keeps the temperature at exactly 2.6. She keeps the light cruel and holy. She keeps adding new half-formed chambers and then refuses to tell them what they are for.

If you stand perfectly still long enough (until your own heartbeat begins to sound like the three-kilometer-wide organ thrum beneath the root-walls), she will eventually come to you. She will not speak. She will simply stand close enough that you can see your reflection in the changing surface of her eyes, and she will exhale once, slowly.

The smell that drifts over you will be the smell of every road you never took, every name you never gave to the thing you lost, every version of yourself that was allowed to remain larval.

Then she will turn and walk away, bare feet leaving new moss sentences that read, in the language of roots:

*Stay.  
Decide with me.  
There is still time.*

And the warm, living cathedral will keep growing around both of you, deciding, deciding, deciding, while the Architect keeps it forever on the edge of being born.
