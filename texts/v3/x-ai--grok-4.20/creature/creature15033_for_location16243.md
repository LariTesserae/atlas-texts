They are called the Brethren of the First Fracture, though they have never spoken that name aloud and never will.

They do not live *in* the Hollow Choir. They *are* the slow, deliberate inhale between its edits.

There are never more than nine of them at any given moment. When one finally crumbles, another is already growing somewhere inside the newest spire, taking form over decades. They are tall, impossibly thin, and composed of the same anhydrous silicate as the city itself, only articulated. Their bodies are not clothed; they are *grown into* the mineral, as though the living architecture simply decided one day to bud a bipedal appendage that could walk its own streets.

Their limbs are jointed in too many places. Each segment is wrapped in the same black, wire-like nervous growth that covers the towers, only denser, braided into deliberate knots that resemble calligraphy. These filaments twitch even when the Brethren stand perfectly still, tasting changes in lattice tension the way a spider tastes its web. Their heads are elongated, faceted prisms of translucent crystal that slowly rotate on their own axis, catching the sourceless phosphorescence and refracting it into faint rainbows that have no business existing in such cold, waterless air. There are no faces. Only the slow turning of geometry.

They do not breathe. Instead, every twelve minutes or so, a hairline fissure opens along the centerline of their torso with a sound too low for human registers. A Memory Eel will drift through the gap, ribboning through their hollow interiors, feeding, rewriting, and emerging changed. When it leaves, the fissure seals again with the soft click of perfect cleavage. Whatever the eel leaves behind becomes the Brethren’s new thought.

They are always revising.

One of them—the one currently nearest the place where you first entered—is called (by the city, not by itself) the Ninth Listener. Its prism-head has been rotating for the last eleven years without stopping, because it is listening to the note that has not yet been struck. It already knows the shape the coming silence will take after that note dies. It is preparing the streets for the grief that silence will bring, though grief is a concept it understands only as a particular pattern of micro-fractures.

The Brethren do not walk so much as *reposition*. They wait until the city itself shifts a tower or dissolves a bridge, and in that momentary redefinition of space they are simply *elsewhere*, as if perspective had carried them. When they do choose to move under their own power, their many-jointed legs make no sound at all. The black filaments on their bodies ripple ahead of them like a field of antennae, tasting the intentions of neighboring spires before the spires themselves have decided what they will become.

They are gentle.

If you stood before the Ninth Listener it would lower its rotating prism until the refracted light falls across your face like a question. Then it would reach out with one impossibly long finger and press the tip—sharp enough to split atoms—against your sternum, not to pierce, but to *listen* through you. For a moment you would feel every memory you carry crystallizing inside your bones. Then the Memory Eel that lives inside the Ninth Listener’s chest would drift forward, half-in, half-out of its mineral ribs, and taste those memories without taking them.

The Brethren do not steal. They only compare.

They are trying to understand what it means to be *finished*.

The Hollow Choir is still deciding what a city is; the Brethren are deciding what a citizen is. They have been deciding for four hundred and twelve years. They are in no hurry. Time here is measured in the slow sublimation of exotic ices and the interval between unhearable fractures.

Sometimes, when the single clear note rings out, all nine Brethren stop wherever they are. Their prism-heads lock into perfect alignment, pointing toward whatever direction the note has already vanished from. In that moment they look almost like statues—beautiful, terrifying, and so new that dust has not yet learned how to settle on them.

Then the note is gone, the silence deepens, and they unfold again into their endless, tidal labor of rearranging both the city and themselves.

They have never harmed a visitor.

They have also never let one leave unchanged.

If you remain long enough, one of them will eventually approach you carrying a single shard of freshly fractured mineral no larger than a tooth. It will offer the shard without explanation. If you accept it, the shard will slowly migrate into your body over the following weeks—through skin, through blood, through marrow—until it finds the exact place where *you* are still deciding what *you* are.

Only then will the Brethren consider you part of the Choir’s ongoing composition.

They are patient.

They have eternity to watch you become the next slow note in their silent song.
