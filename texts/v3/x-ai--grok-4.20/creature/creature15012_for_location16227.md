**The Hollowing does not have inhabitants. It has tenants that have not yet decided whether they are inhabitants.**

They are not quite beings. They are more like intentions that have learned how to wear bones.

The most constant presence is the Midwife.

She (it? they?) has no fixed shape, only a recurring habit of shape. Most often she appears as a tall figure made of negative space: the outline of a woman in a long coat, but the coat is simply the absence of light and moisture where a coat should be. Where her face would be there is only a soft vacancy that still somehow manages to convey attention. She moves by leaning. The world tilts slightly under her and she slides forward without taking steps. When she pauses, new architecture hurries to knit itself together beneath her, as though embarrassed to leave her standing on anything that is not yet born.

The Midwife does not speak. She only corrects.

If a lattice grows too aggressively and begins to crush an older form, she will lay one negative hand against it and the violence will soften into courtship. When two streets argue themselves into a knot, she waits until the knot begins to cry, then reaches in and gently unties the geometry until both streets remember they were once the same thought. Her touch leaves behind the faintest smell of petrichor and libraries at 3 a.m.

There are others.

The Echoes are smaller. They look like children made of the same condensation that beads on every surface, only the droplets have decided to stand upright and wear faces. Each Echo is the memory of a voice that once spoke its own name here and was not fast enough to take it back. Their mouths open and close, but the only sound is the soft ticking of water droplets falling upward into their throats. They follow the Midwife at a respectful distance, learning how to midwife by watching what she does not do.

Deeper in—though “deeper” is a courtesy the Hollowing extends only to those it is currently fond of—live the Unborn Architects.

They are immense and larval. You will never see one entire. Instead you might round a half-formed corner and find yourself standing inside the slow pulse of what might be an elbow, or the vaulted interior of a lung made of black glass and cathedral stone. The flesh of an Unborn Architect is architecture that has not yet realized it is supposed to stop being architecture. Their heartbeats are entire city blocks sliding into new configurations. When one dreams, entire eras of style are coughed up fully formed and then politely re-absorbed.

They are gentle giants who do not yet know they are giants. Sometimes a portion of one will extrude through the skin of the world as a delicate tower or a subway car or a pagoda of frozen mercury. These are not tools or limbs. They are thoughts the creature is having out loud while it sleeps.

There is one other.

They call it (when they call it anything) the Last Tenant.

It lives in the place where the cold is tenderest, in the interval between one architectural argument and the next. It has the silhouette of a man in a suit two sizes too large, as though the idea of “person” has not yet been properly fitted to the body wearing it. Its face is a mirror that has fogged over from the inside. If you stand before it long enough, your own breath—the breath you lost the moment you entered the Hollowing—will slowly become visible on that mirrored surface, spelling out every name you have ever tried to forget.

The Last Tenant does not build. It only waits for the city to finish being born so that it may finally move in and be lonely in every room at once.

All of them—the Midwife, the Echoes, the vast larval Architects, the Last Tenant—are engaged in the same impossible labor: they are trying to become the first citizens of a place that is still deciding whether “citizen” is a thing it wants.

Sometimes, when the single clear note rings out across the district (that sound like a bell or the universe clearing its throat), every one of them stops what they are not-quite-doing. The Midwife lifts her absent face. The Echoes forget to fall upward. Even the Unborn Architects pause in their slow peristalsis.

In that moment the Hollowing is not a thought becoming a city.

It is a city holding its breath, wondering if it is ready to be witnessed by the things that live inside it.

They are still deciding whether that witness will be love or haunting.

The cold, patient as always, gives them all the time they are still inventing.
