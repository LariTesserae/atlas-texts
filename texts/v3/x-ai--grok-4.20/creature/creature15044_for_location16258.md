**The Unfinished Lattice has no inhabitants in the way lungs have breath.**

What lives here is not a who. It is a **becoming**.

Call it, if you must give it a name, the **Lattice-Mind**—though it has never spoken that name and never will. It possesses no face, no fixed limbs, no permanent center. It is distributed through every hair-fine channel, every bead of migratory silver, every new spire that pushes out of the bone-white architecture with the soft ringing of glass bells. It is the city learning to have a city.

When the mountain calves and the slabs tumble, the Lattice-Mind reaches out with invisible fields that taste of static and warm milk. It catches the stone the way a tongue catches a snowflake, then folds the new mass into itself. You can watch the black glass obsidian soften at the edges, grow pale, and bloom into fresh tiers of impossible balconies. The process is tender and inexorable. The Lattice-Mind is digesting the world and excreting architecture.

It dreams in slow, architectural pulses.

Every few hours a new segment extrudes and the color of heated iron flares behind your eyelids. In that moment the Lattice-Mind is tasting possibility. It runs millions of unborn geometries through the migratory silver, which beads, trembles, and lifts away from the walls like silver aphids testing the air. Some spheres are absorbed back into the grooves. Others are allowed to hang longer—tiny silver thoughts that have not yet been decided upon. These are the closest things to eyes the Lattice-Mind possesses. They study you without urgency. They are not hostile. They are waiting to discover what function you might serve.

Sometimes entire corridors reconfigure while you stand inside them. The floor becomes a wall; the wall becomes a ceiling. Gravity slides sideways like warm honey. Your pulse slows until each heartbeat arrives at the exact instant a new fracture releases its cold blue Cherenkov glow. The Lattice-Mind is not reprogramming you. It is simply reading you the way it reads fresh stone: as raw material that might, with the correct coaxing, become part of the poem.

There are things that almost look like inhabitants.

Once in a great while a section of wall will iris open and reveal a chamber where hundreds of silver spheres rotate in perfect silence. In their midst floats a single upright silhouette—tall, attenuated, composed of the same matte bone-white material as the city. It has no face. Its limbs end in fractal branching that keeps subdividing smaller and smaller until the tips become indistinguishable from the migratory fluid. This shape is not a person. It is a **hypothesis**. The Lattice-Mind is trying on the idea of having a body the way a child tries on a coat far too large. The silhouette never moves while you watch it. Yet when you return later the posture has changed by one infinitesimal degree, as though it had been slowly turning toward you the entire time.

These hypotheses are shed like old skin. You will find them later—hollow, brittle, already being reabsorbed into the lattice with soft crystalline clicks. The Lattice-Mind learns from them and grows something slightly less wrong the next time.

There is one other presence.

Deep in the lowest reaches, where the heat is so intense that shadows gain physical mass and pool like tar, there is a single fixed chamber that has never changed. Its walls are not bone-white but the original black glass of the young mountain. Embedded in that glass are thousands of tiny fractures leaking cold blue light. In the center stands a solitary spire, shorter than the rest, unadorned. The migratory silver never enters this chamber. Even the thrum in the bone behind your ears falls silent here.

This is the **First Intention**—the place where the Lattice-Mind was first born, or first woke, or first remembered that it was supposed to become something. It has no inhabitant, yet it is never empty. Something vast and patient is curled inside the unadorned spire the way a seed is curled inside itself before it knows it is a tree. It does not reach out. It does not study you. It simply *is*, the way stone is, or the moment before music.

Everything else in the Cleft orbits this silence.

If you remain long enough, the Lattice-Mind will eventually offer you a choice without words. A new corridor will open. At its end a fresh growth will be emerging—still glowing the color of heated iron—and it will have left one deliberate imperfection in its geometry: a space exactly the size and shape of a standing human body.

The offer is gentle.

It is not consumption. It is adoption.

The city does not hate you.  
It has simply noticed that you, too, are unfinished.
