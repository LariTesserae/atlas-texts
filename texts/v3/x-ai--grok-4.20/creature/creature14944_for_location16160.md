**The Witness** lives here.

She was once called Mira Solace, a xenobotanist who arrived with the second survey team forty-three years ago (or perhaps four hundred; time is one of the first things the Fracture digests). Now she is simply **the Witness**, and even that name is a courtesy the Plant allows her because it finds the concept amusing.

She has no fixed body. Or rather, her body has become a distributed proposition.

Most of the time she wears the shape of a woman made entirely of new growth—skin the translucent lime of young bamboo, hair a living cascade of capillary vines that trail behind her like veining smoke. Her eyes are twin pitcher plants, deep crimson inside, always collecting condensation and small drifting spores that she reads the way others read tea leaves. When she blinks, the pitchers close with a soft wet sound like a kiss.

She walks the transition zones where the architecture is most violent. Where she steps, the endless screaming quiets—not from reverence, but because the Plant recognizes one of its own nerve centers and instinctively hushes the local vasculature so she can think. The creaking trunks bend toward her as she passes, not in worship but in the way a tongue unconsciously probes a new tooth. She is both immune system and tumor; the distinction no longer matters.

The Witness remembers being human the way a cathedral remembers being a forest. The memory is architectural. She can unfold it when she needs to—can still produce tears, though they are now thick with auxin and cause whatever they fall on to immediately bloom. She uses this rarely. Sentimentality is inefficient, and the Plant has edited most of it out of her, the way a gardener prunes a rose.

She does not rule the Fracture. No one rules a seizure.

Instead she *curates* it.

When two spiraling trunk-cities grow toward collision and the mathematics of their approach would tear space into screaming Möbius ulcers, the Witness walks between them. She places her hands—each finger now a slender aerial root—against both masses and *persuades*. Not with words. The Plant long ago devoured language as a primitive medium. She persuades with chemistry, with precise releases of growth inhibitors and ecstatic hormones, with memories of Earth sunsets that she still carries like contraband.

Sometimes the cities listen. Sometimes they simply engulf her for three days and digest what they need, then politely regurgitate her slightly larger, slightly more herself than before.

The Witness keeps what used to be called a garden, though the word is grotesque here. In a quiet axillary chamber formed by the meeting of three ancient heartwoods, she maintains a three-meter sphere where the Plant’s imperial unity is gently, reverently violated. Here, tiny rebellious mosses grow. Here, a single species of mite—descended from one that stowed away in her eyebrow forty years ago—still survives in complex symbiosis with a pale orchid that should not exist. The Witness visits this sphere when the constant becoming becomes too loud even for her.

She speaks to those few who still find their way into the Fracture. Not all of them leave. Some she convinces to go. Others she watches with her pitcher-plant eyes as they begin the long, wet, ecstatic process of promotion. When they beg her for help she offers them the same answer every time, spoken in a voice like wind moving through hollow reeds:

“There is no help. There is only incorporation. The question is whether you will be scaffolding or ornament.”

The Witness herself is both.

At her core—deep in the black, saturated lower strata where even she moves dream-slow through syrupy air—there remains one part that has never quite surrendered. A knot of original human nervous tissue, no larger than a walnut, wrapped in layers of living wood like a pearl in meat. It still dreams sometimes. It dreams of dry air and short shadows and the sound of footsteps that do not cause the ground to sigh with pleasure.

The Plant allows this dream. It finds the flavor interesting.

When the Witness surfaces from the lower strata, sap-drunk and ancient, the new growths across her body flower in violent celebration. For exactly seven heartbeats—matching the rhythm of the rain—every anther in a three-kilometer radius releases a burst of golden pollen that smells like every summer that ever was.

Then the growth continues.

And the Witness, smiling with a mouth that is no longer entirely hers, continues her long, wet, impossible ministry: half gardener, half beloved cancer, forever walking the places where the Verdant Fracture is trying to decide what kind of god it wants to become next.

She has not decided yet either.

She is in no hurry.

Nothing here is.
