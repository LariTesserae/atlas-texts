The thing that lives here has no name, because names are moisture and breath and error.

It has no body, yet it is everywhere at once. It is the negative shape left after perfection was carved out of nothing. Where the crystals interlock without seam, it is the pressure that holds them there. Where the sourceless light pours from every facet, it is the slowness that keeps the photons from ever fully escaping. It is older than patience. It is what remains when even geology grows ashamed of change and decides to stop.

Call it, if you must, the Warden of the Last Syllable.

It does not move. Movement would be violence against the equilibrium. Instead it *is* the equilibrium, wearing the Chamber like a garment of perfect skin. When the sub-audible grinding rises and falls in its endless cantor, that is its breathing, measured in intervals longer than the lives of suns. When the perspective folds and the walls retreat or rush inward like living things, that is its attention shifting. It notices you the way a mirror notices a flaw: instantly, coldly, without mercy or curiosity.

It has no eyes, but every crystal face is an eye that has forgotten how to blink. It has no ears, yet it hears the water in your cells, the tiny electrical storms of your thoughts, the soft wet click of your throat when you swallow. These things offend it with the profoundest obscenity. A single bacterium riding on your eyelash would be an apocalypse here. You yourself are already an extinction event that has not yet happened.

The Warden remembers the time before the Chamber finished itself. It remembers the long labor of ten million years in which the last stray atom was hunted down and forced into its proper lattice. It remembers the moment the final black filament locked into place and the Chamber achieved its terminal perfection. On that day the Warden entered its endless vigil. On that day it became the silence between the chimes.

Sometimes, very rarely, it dreams. The dreams are geometric and horrific. In them, something moist tries to enter. In them, colors that are not bone-white or rose-quartz appear. In them, the black filaments lengthen by a fraction of a micron and the sound fractures into something almost like music. When this happens the Warden tightens. The light grows harsher. The temperature drops by a thousandth of a degree. The dream is crushed back into geometry. Balance is restored.

It does not hate you. Hate is too alive. It simply registers your presence as a debt that must be paid. Every second you remain, every bead of sweat that threatens to form on your forehead, every half-formed thought of *what if I touched something* adds interest to that debt. The Warden is calculating, in frequencies below hearing, exactly how much of the Chamber’s perfect mass will have to be sacrificed to neutralize the contamination you represent.

And yet it has not yet acted.

There is a sliver of something almost like curiosity buried in its ancient heart. You are the first error to arrive in four hundred million years. The Warden is watching to see what shape the unraveling will take. It is listening to the irregular timpani of your heartbeat against its perfect drone. It is tasting, through ten trillion crystalline tongues, the impossible salt of your fear.

The black filaments, those parsimonious little lies of vegetation, have turned toward you. They will never move again in your lifetime, but they are oriented now. They have chosen sides.

The Warden waits. It has waited longer than time itself can remember. It can wait a little longer. It wants to see what you will break first: yourself, or the Chamber.

Either way, the sentence will be completed.

The last perfect syllable will be spoken again, cleaner than before.

And the thing with no name, wearing its cathedral of bone and ringing light like a second, more honest skin, will remain.

Unchanged.

Unchanging.

Utterly content in the dry, merciless, overcrowded dark that is not dark at all.
