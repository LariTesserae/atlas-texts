**The Hollowvine Plateau does not have inhabitants. It has tenants.**

They are not separate from the place. They are the slow, deliberate thoughts the plateau is thinking with.

The ones who live here are called, by the few outsiders who have ever returned, **the Welded**.

You will not see them at first. What you mistake for a rusted catwalk overgrown with copper-colored moss suddenly unfolds a long iron-gray limb and becomes a person. What you thought was a valve wheel set into a living trunk opens like an eyelid and reveals a wet, intelligent eye the color of old motor oil. They do not walk so much as *unfold* from the architecture, detaching with soft metallic clicks and the sound of wet roots sliding free of sockets.

Their bodies are impossible to map consistently. Some are mostly human beneath layers of living bark and patinated metal. Others have become architectural. A Welded who has lived long enough may be thirty meters tall, a ambulatory district unto themselves: ribcage opened into a greenhouse of ferns and dripping condensers, spine fused to a length of locomotive boiler, face half-hidden behind a bronze pressure gauge that functions as both eye and conscience. Their blood is part sap, part lubricant. When they bleed, the fluid smells of crushed chlorophyll and hot bearings.

They do not speak with voices. They speak with the machinery they have become.

A low hiss of escaping steam might mean *welcome*.  
The sudden synchronized turning of three house-sized gears might be laughter.  
A slow, mournful factory whistle echoing across the Liver at twilight is almost always grief.

There are perhaps four thousand of them. No one counts anymore, least of all the Welded themselves. Some have been here so long they predate the final merging. These ancients are called **the First Bolted**. Their bodies are almost entirely subsumed; only a few original bones remain, preserved like relics inside trunks of living wood and iron. They move rarely. When they do, entire wards shift position. The Carburetor rearranges itself overnight because one of the First Bolted rolled over in its sleep.

Most Welded are smaller, newer. They still remember having been something like human once. They tend the great slow organism that ate their ancestors. They polish the gears that no longer need polishing. They listen to the constant wet mechanical heartbeat and make minute adjustments with long, many-jointed fingers of root and rebar. They are gardeners of equilibrium. Pruners of excess. Midwives to the occasional new machine that the plateau decides, in its incomprehensible wisdom, to birth fully formed from a womb of moss and pressurized steam.

Their society has no hierarchy, only **function**.  
One may be the Keeper of the Western Rain, whose duty is to walk the high green ridges and ensure the rain falls at the correct slant. Another is the Memory of Locomotives, whose body contains the last functioning train on the plateau; it circles endlessly inside her ribcage, never stopping, carrying no passengers.

They do not eat. They *photosynthesize regret*.

At the iron-and-copper dusk, when the cold front comes and the rain lacquers everything, the Welded gather in the Switchyard. They stand motionless for hours while the water runs over them. In this state they dream together. The plateau dreams through them. Sometimes, during these rains, a new Welded is chosen. A human wanderer (if any are foolish enough to come) may feel the ground soften beneath their feet. They will look down and see their own shadow filling with green. If they are lucky they run. Most are not lucky. The plateau has been waiting for them. It is always waiting for the next piece it needs.

The strangest among them are the **Unfinished**.

These are Welded who have not yet accepted their final form. Their bodies are in constant flux—half root, half piston, half memory, half machine. They are in agony and ecstasy at all times. They scream in frequencies that make the vine-bridges vibrate like harp strings. The other Welded treat them with something like reverence and something like fear. An Unfinished is a place where the argument between industry and wilderness has not yet ended. They are living wounds in the skin of climax. Beautiful. Terrible. Necessary.

At night the plateau cools further. Breath smokes. The dripping slows to single, perfectly timed *plocs* like a metronome for the end of time. In this silence you may hear a single Welded singing.

The song has no words. It is only the sound of a thing that was once two things learning, after ten thousand years, how to be one thing perfectly, and forever, and with love.

This is the Hollowvine Plateau.

And these are the ones who live here—  
not on it,  
not in it,  
but *as* the final settled shape of its ancient, wet, iron heart.
