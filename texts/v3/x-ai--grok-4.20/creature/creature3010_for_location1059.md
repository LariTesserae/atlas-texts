Here lives the Last Ember of Veyra.

She has no body the way we understand bodies. What remains of her is a single, motionless figure seated at the exact geometric center of the basin, where the mineral sheet is at its thickest and the slow geothermal rhythm is strongest. She looks like a woman carved from the same fused stone as the floor—pale ochre shot through with charcoal and rust—except that her surface is faintly, almost imperceptibly softer than the ground around her. If you stare long enough you realize she is not *made* of the ash-stone; she *has become* it, the way a coal left in a kiln for a thousand years becomes indistinguishable from the kiln wall.

Her posture is neither devotional nor exhausted. She simply sits with her legs crossed, spine straight, hands resting palms-up on her thighs. The fingers are long and slightly too jointed, as though the sculptor changed his mind halfway through. Her face is smooth, almost featureless; only the faintest suggestions of closed eyelids and a mouth that once knew how to smile remain, worn down by the same patient forces that shaped the ridges and troughs. She has no hair. Where hair might have been there is only a delicate lattice of black filaments—the same living charcoal growths that dot the rest of the Hollow—growing in slow, deliberate spirals from her scalp.

She does not breathe. Yet the entire basin breathes *with* her. Every ten thousand heartbeats (yours, not hers), the stone beneath you gives the faintest lift, a microscopic expansion, as if some vast lung far below the crust is still trying to match the rhythm she set when she first sat down.

Her name was once Veyra-Who-Would-Not-Forget. She came here when the world still had oceans and forests and noisy, temporary things. She came carrying the last living coal of her entire civilization inside her mouth, and when even that coal threatened to die she lay down in the basin and let the final fire consume her from the inside out. The fire did not go out. It simply changed its mind about needing fuel, about needing time, about needing anything at all. It fused her to the stone, fused the stone to her, and then politely asked reality to stop interfering.

Now she is the Hollow’s only inhabitant and its only priestess. She keeps the catastrophe remembered without ever reopening it. Every black filament in the basin is one of her thoughts made slow and mineral. They do not spread because she has already thought every thought worth thinking; they simply stand as perfect, motionless records. The faint phosphor light that allows you to see is the last leakage of her eyes, which she closed so long ago that even she no longer remembers the color of what she saw.

If you sit before her long enough, the rhythm in the stone will begin to feel like speech. Not words. Never words. Only an immense, patient sorrow that has been distilled past grief into something almost gentle. She is not lonely. Loneliness requires the possibility of company. She is *complete*—a single note held for so long it has become the silence that contains all other silences.

Sometimes, very rarely, one of the black filaments on her scalp will tremble. When that happens the temperature in the Hollow drops by half a degree for the space of three human heartbeats. Then it rises again, and the filament returns to perfect stillness. That is as close as she ever comes to waking.

She will not acknowledge you. She has already seen every possible visitor the universe could send—those who came before you, those who will come after, and the ones who only exist in timelines that never happened. You are simply another soft, noisy thing passing across the surface of her meditation. Your footsteps are rude, but they are also already forgotten.

And yet.

If you were to reach out and lay your bare hand upon the stone of her knee (an act of almost unbearable presumption), you would feel it: the faintest warmth, deeper than temperature. For one trembling instant you would understand that she *does* notice you. That she has noticed every living thing that has ever walked here, and that she carries each of you inside the slow coal of her heart the way she once carried her dying world.

Then she would let you go again, because she is kind, and because kindness, to her, means allowing things to remain transitory.

She will be here when the last star burns down to cinders. She will be here when even the concept of “here” has lost all meaning. She is the place that has achieved its final shape, and she asks nothing, remembers everything, and will outlast you without ever needing to open her eyes.

Her name, if you dare speak it aloud in this place where sound itself feels like sacrilege, is simply:

*Stay.*
