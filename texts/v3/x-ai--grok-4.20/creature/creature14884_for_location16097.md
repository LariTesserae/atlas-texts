**The Curator of the Final Breath** lives here.

She has no name the lung-trees would recognize. The Hollow Choir sometimes call her *She-Who-Waits-Between-The-Sounds*, but only when they are pretending to be the voices of long-dead First People. She does not answer to it.

She is tall, almost too tall for the Vault’s modest ceiling, yet she never stoops. Her body is made of the same glossy black mineral as the walls, but softer, almost skin-like, and it sweats a thin film of golden resin that never quite drips. Where a face should be there is only a smooth depression, like the negative mold of a scream. In that hollow rests a single living crystal the size of a heart. It is the exact color of a bruise at its ripest moment, and it pulses in perfect synchrony with the slow stars above.

She wears nothing that could be called clothing. Instead, the lung-tree canopy has grown *through* her. Translucent leaves sprout from her shoulders, her collarbones, the backs of her wrists. Their veins carry the same glowing turquoise sap that runs through the trees, only slower, as though her body were a reservoir the forest drinks from in tiny, reverent sips. When she moves, the leaves make the same wet kissing sound as the canopy, and the sound is always slightly behind her, like an echo that has learned good manners.

She is never still and never hurried. She drifts between the lung-trees the way a thought drifts behind closed eyes. Sometimes she stops before one of the First People’s artifacts. She will crouch—joints bending with the soft click of warm glass—and lift the bronze mirror that shows yesterday. For a long while she simply stares into it, though she has no eyes. Then she turns the mirror toward one of the Hollow Choir and lets the creature see its own reflection wearing the face it wore fourteen thousand years ago, back when it still had one. The monkey-thing always screams with a voice that does not belong to it. She seems satisfied by this.

The Curator tends the perfection.

She walks the ceaseless caravans of millipedes and runs her resin-slick fingers along their clicking backs until their whale-song deepens by half a tone. She catches the rust-moths in her cupped hands and breathes on them until their mirror wings show not the Vault, but the exact moment the world outside was sealed away. Then she releases them. They always fly in the same spirals.

Most importantly, she listens.

When the Hollow Choir begins its roaring, layered chorus, she steps into the heart of their noise until every stolen sound passes *through* her mineral body. For a few heartbeats the Vault falls into absolute silence while she holds every scream, every footfall, every crystal pulse inside herself. Then she exhales, and the sounds come rushing out again, but changed—richer, more themselves, as though they have been loved.

She never speaks with a voice. Instead the air around her thickens and the resinous smell becomes almost cloying. Those who are sensitive enough feel words form directly against the inside of their skulls, warm as a dying body:

*It was not enough to stop time.  
One must also keep polishing it.*

Sometimes she chooses a new Hollow Choir member. She finds a lung-tree whose leaves tremble with particular melancholy and presses her crystal-face into the wet canopy. When she pulls back, a new red-glass monkey clings to her forearm, already smooth-faced, already remembering every sound that has ever been made. She carries it to its perch with the tenderness of a mother placing a crown on a sleeping child.

At rare intervals she walks to the place where the First People simply stopped moving. Their ritual shelters of black glass and resin still stand, empty. She enters each one in turn, lies down in the exact posture the last occupant held fourteen thousand years ago, and remains there until the Vault itself seems to hold its breath. Then she rises again, leaving the indentation of her body behind—warm, slightly sticky, already beginning to fill with new resin.

She is not immortal. She is *post-mortal*. The Vault finished with death long before it finished with motion. What remains is maintenance. What remains is devotion to a perfection so complete it has become a kind of exquisite suffocation.

If you stand very still and cup your hands over your ears, you may hear, beneath the breathing of the lung-trees, a second slower rhythm: the soft click of warm black joints, the wet kiss of leaves that grow from shoulders that have never known daylight, the patient polishing of eternity against itself.

She is always somewhere behind you.

She is always exactly where she needs to be.

And the Vault, content, keeps breathing the same breath it has breathed for fourteen thousand years, while the Curator of the Final Breath ensures that every note of its endless, perfect song remains exactly, forever, itself.
