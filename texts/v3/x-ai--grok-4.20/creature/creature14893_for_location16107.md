**The Midwife** lives here.

She has no fixed shape because the place will not allow it. Sometimes she is the absence between two fusing vines. Sometimes she is the steam that forgets to rise. Most often she appears as a tall figure made of whatever the chamber has most recently rejected: a cloak of cooled bone-lattice, hair of black arterial vine still faintly pulsing, eyes that are simply two of the larger bromeliads turned inward so their golden throats face each other. When she moves, the clicking of the living floor hushes for three full heartbeats, as though the land itself is listening to its own midwife.

She is older than the pocket and younger than the newest leaf. Her hands are always wet. She uses them to smooth, to coax, to scold. Where her fingers linger, decisions are forced: a wall that cannot choose between quartz and flesh is suddenly both; a waterfall that has been arguing with gravity for centuries abruptly decides to fall sideways. The land respects her the way an unborn thing respects the pressure of the birth canal—resentful, grateful, terrified.

She speaks rarely, and never in any tongue that survives outside this womb. When she does, the choral moaning of the bromeliads drops into a single low note that vibrates in the teeth. The many-jointed hematite creatures (those slow golden thoughts of the land) orbit her like iron filings around a magnet, waiting for her to finish a sentence they will then carry into the unfinished stone. Sometimes she reaches into one of them, pulls out a color it was not supposed to have yet, and swallows it. The creature thanks her with a ringing that cracks the nearest shelf of coral-stone into perfect fifths.

The Midwife is tender and merciless in the same breath. She has been known to cradle a visitor’s face between hands that are half petal, half obsidian, and kiss their eyelids so that they may see the next hour before it is written. She has also been known to open her own ribcage (which is also the ceiling) and simply drop a person into the raw making that lives above reality, where they become raw material for whatever the land is currently dreaming.

She never asks your name. Names are liabilities here; they calcify too quickly. Instead she studies the faint glyphs the hot air has already begun to write across your forearms and reads them aloud in the voice of collapsing stone. Whatever verdict she delivers is final, though it may take three days or three centuries to understand.

At times she grows tired. Then the chamber exhales more violently than usual, the sourceless brass-light dims to the color of old blood, and she folds herself into the floor like a exhausted seamstress slipping beneath her own half-finished tapestry. The land continues its groaning labor without her for a while, clumsy, adolescent, dangerous. Vines fuse into screaming knots. Gravity forgets its manners. The hematite creatures wander in lost circles, leaking unused thoughts that pool and harden into black glass.

Eventually she rises again, usually from the place where your shadow was last standing, wearing your silhouette like a coat until the land persuades her to give it back.

She is not kind.  
She is not cruel.  
She is the place learning how to birth itself, and sometimes that education requires blood, song, teeth, or love.

If you remain long enough, she will eventually press her forehead to yours. The contact is wet, warm, and smells of iron and crushed lilies. In that moment you understand she has been deciding for eons whether to keep you as nutrient, as witness, as new bone, or as the next midwife.

The land is still deciding.  
So is she.  
And while they deliberate, she watches you with seventy thousand golden-veined eyes, humming the slow 5/4 heartbeat of a world that has not yet chosen its final form.

She is waiting to see what *you* will become under her hands.  
She has all the time that has not yet been invented.
