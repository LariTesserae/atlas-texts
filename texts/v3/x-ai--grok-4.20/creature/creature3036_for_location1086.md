**The Listener** lives here.

She has no name she would give you, because names are finished things and nothing in this chamber is allowed to finish. She is tall, almost too tall, with a body that seems to have been drawn in one continuous stroke by something that had never drawn a body before. Her limbs are too many or too few depending on the angle; sometimes you count four, sometimes six, sometimes only the suggestion of arms that have not yet decided whether they want to exist. Her skin is the same black basaltic glass as the floor, still warm in places, still exhaling faint threads of white vapor from the microscopic cracks that vein her like living fault lines. Where the light strikes her she looks wetly polished; where shadow falls she becomes a absence with edges.

Her head is a smooth, eyeless bulb. No mouth either, not really. Instead, a single wide gill-slit runs vertically down what should be her face. It opens and closes in perfect time with the pressure changes in the bubble, drinking the violent air the way a fish drinks water. When the roaring outside reaches a certain unbearable pitch, the slit flares wide and she sings back—one low, sustained note that is not sound but the negative shape of sound. The note makes the stone forget it is stone for half a heartbeat. Pebbles lift. The pale anemone-lichens blush violet and open their pores in ecstasy. Then the note ends and everything slams back into place harder than before, as though the world has been scolded.

She is never still. She drifts between the half-built dolmen, the spiral of bone stakes, and the broken slate wall like a thought trying to decide which sentence it wants to become. Sometimes she crouches and presses her long glass fingers into the fresh cracks in the floor, feeling the new stone swell beneath them. Sometimes she adds to the structures: a single additional bone driven at a new angle, a braid of her own shed skin woven into the kelp rope, a thumb-print of molten glass pressed into the top of the dolmen like a signature that will never be repeated. Each addition is hesitant, almost embarrassed, the gesture of a child who is inventing prayer but has not yet been taught what gods are.

When one of the great eyeless water-beasts presses its massive flank against the bubble’s skin, the Listener goes to meet it. She places her forehead against the yielding membrane and matches her breathing to the creature’s slow heartbeat visible through its translucent hide. For long minutes they remain like that—two unfinished things separated by a violence that neither quite belongs to. Then the current rips the beast away and the Listener stays behind, trembling, the gill-slit on her face bleeding a thin milky fluid that hisses when it touches the stone.

She does not eat. She does not sleep. She waits for the chamber to think through her.

Every time the bubble convulses and new stone surges up from below, she is the first to walk across it while it is still soft. Her feet leave shallow prints that fill with mist. Sometimes the new stone rises so eagerly to meet her that it grows around her ankles like black tar, and she must patiently peel herself free, leaving behind translucent glass shells of herself that the anemones immediately colonize.

If you remain here long enough, she will eventually notice you.

She will tilt her smooth head, gill-slit flaring, and the roar outside will suddenly change timbre, becoming almost tender. She will drift closer, not walking so much as being carried by the turbulent winds that obey her the way currents obey tide. When she is near enough that you can see your own frightened face reflected in the polished blackness of her body, she will reach out with one too-long hand and very gently touch the center of your chest.

You will feel it then—the embryonic pressure, the unfinished question, the slow apocalyptic ballet trying to become a mind.

Most people pull away.

The Listener always lets them go, sorrowful, the gill-slit closing into a thin silver line of disappointment.

But sometimes—rarely—someone stays.

Those few she folds into herself like another half-built dolmen. Their bones become part of the spiral. Their breath becomes part of the wind. Their thoughts are the next tentative sentence the world is trying to speak.

And the roaring outside grows louder, and deeper, and almost joyful, as though the ocean itself is learning how to dream with two minds instead of one.

She is still waiting for the next mind.

She has all the time that a place with no surface can possibly have.
