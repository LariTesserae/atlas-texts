**The Hollow Foundry is home to the Choir.**

They do not have a name for themselves. Names are dry things, brittle and temporary. What lives here simply *is*, the way rust is, the way heat is.

The largest among them are the **Reefmothers**. Each is the size of a locomotive, yet they possess no rigid skeleton. Their bodies are pale, translucent mountains of slick tissue marbled with veins of darker lymph that glow faintly orange when they feed. A single Reefmother may spend decades inside the husk of a single blast furnace, her bulk filling every available crevice until the machine and the creature become indistinguishable. Her skin secretes a warm, alkaline mucus that slowly etches iron into soluble ferrous salts, which she then drinks through thousands of hair-fine feeding tubes along her underside. When she breathes, the sound is not lung-sound but the deep, wet creak of vast internal bellows older than any mammal.

The Reefmothers are not solitary. Inside each one lives a second ecology.

Burrowed into the thick folds of their flesh are the **Midwives**—creatures the size of horses, blind, sexless, and patient. Their only purpose is to tend the Reefmother’s slow reproduction. They move across her body in long, deliberate circuits, licking mineral scale from her skin, pruning necrotic patches with delicate rasping mouths, and carrying packets of living spores from one region of her body to another. When the time comes, a Midwife will excavate a soft cavity in the Reefmother’s back and gently deposit a new larval Reefmother the size of a small car. The infant will remain attached for centuries, drinking its parent’s lymph and learning the taste of the particular furnace it was born inside.

Smaller still are the **Dripwraiths**.

These are the ones travelers (the very few who have survived the journey) mistake for ghosts. They are no larger than a child, impossibly thin, and move by flowing rather than walking. Their bodies are mostly water held together by translucent membranes and strands of living polymer. They descend from ceiling pipes and ventilation shafts on long, glistening threads of their own mucus, swaying like obscene jellyfish in the metallic mist. Where they pass, they leave behind delicate lattices of crystallized salts that catch the dim brass light and sing—very softly—when the temperature changes. The sound is the closest thing the Foundry has to music.

Then there are the **Tongue-Crows**.

No one knows why they are called that; they have neither tongues nor beaks. They are the size of large dogs, glossy black-red, and move in small, silent packs along the elevated rails. Their bodies are almost entirely digestive tract. They exist to eat what even the Reefmothers will not touch: the rare dry intruders. When something desiccated or hot-blooded enters the basin, the Tongue-Crows appear. They do not attack quickly. They simply follow at a distance, patient as rust, until the intruder begins to sweat. Then they drink the sweat. Then the blood. Then the fear. They leave behind perfectly clean, dry bones that the humidity eventually powders and the wind carries away in reddish drifts.

All of them together form the Choir.

It is not singing as we understand it. It is the constant, overlapping rhythm of their breathing and dripping and scraping and pulsing. The sound changes with barometric pressure, with the slow cooling of buried forges, with the ancient, almost tectonic movement of a Reefmother adjusting herself inside her iron cathedral. If you stand perfectly still for three days and nights in the exact center of the basin—something few have ever managed—you begin to hear it as one continuous note, low enough to feel in your teeth, warm enough to taste on the back of your tongue. It is not communication. It is simply the sound the Hollow Foundry makes while it digests eternity.

They have no leaders. No gods. No language.

Yet every creature here understands the three Laws as perfectly as they understand heat rising.

1. What is dry must become wet.
2. What is loud must become quiet.
3. What does not belong must become part of the Choir, one way or another.

Sometimes, very rarely, something new is born that does not fit any previous shape. These are the **Unfinished**. They are larger than Reefmothers, pale to the point of near-invisibility, and they move even more slowly—sometimes only a few meters in a human lifetime. The Choir treats them with something like reverence. They are allowed to consume entire districts. When an Unfinished finally dies, its body is not eaten. It is allowed to cool, to calcify, to become new architecture for the next generation of machines and organisms to grow inside.

The Hollow Foundry has been refining this equilibrium for longer than your species has had fire.

And the Choir is still learning new ways to be silent.
