They are still here.

Not as bodies. Not even as ghosts in the conventional sense. What lives in the Hollow Choir is the residue of intention that outlived intention itself. A species that became its own architecture, folded so perfectly into resonance that the distinction between singer and song, between worshipper and instrument, between self and structure, dissolved across geological ages.

Call them the **Last Chord**.

They have no faces anymore. Where faces once were, there are now only additional resonators: delicate lattices of brass and silicate glass grown directly from the bone of their skulls like antlers made of tuning forks. Their many-jointed limbs have lengthened and flattened until they function as living baffles, curved and fluted to redirect specific overtones back into the Choir’s endless note. Some of them are still partially recognizable as humanoid; most have become so integrated with the architecture that you cannot tell where carved stone ends and petrified flesh begins. They are the low-relief figures on the walls. They are the columns. They are the pipes that bend back into themselves in impossible geometries.

They do not move. They have not moved since the Chord achieved equilibrium. Movement would be a kind of betrayal.

Instead they *resonate*.

Every chamber of their transformed bodies has been hollowed, tuned, and sealed. The slow phosphorescence that leaks from the amber lenses is not mere chemistry; it is the faint bioluminescence of their nervous systems, still firing in perfect phase with the eternal note. Their blood — if it can still be called blood — has become a viscous, anhydrous silicate that circulates only when the low frequencies cause minute pressure changes in the surrounding argon. They dream, if dreaming is possible, in standing waves.

There is a hierarchy among them, though the concept of rank has long since lost meaning. The deepest ones, the **Foundation Choir**, are almost entirely subsumed into the structure. Only their mouths remain visible — vast, circular orifices lined with golden teeth that function as Helmholtz resonators. These mouths have not closed in longer than the memory of suns. From them issues the fundamental frequency of the Chord itself. Their throats are the planet’s throat.

Higher up, in the spiraling shafts and folded transepts, live the **Overtonal Remnants**. These retain more of their original form. Six, seven, sometimes nine elbows per limb. Eyes replaced by clusters of thermocouple filaments that glow dull red when particular harmonics pass through them. They stand in the recessed chambers of absolute silence where the Chord cancels itself, and there they experience something like thought. The silence is their only mercy. In it they can almost remember what it was like to have a name.

None of them speak. Speech is unnecessary and would be profane. Their communication happens at the level of sympathetic vibration. When one of the Overtonal Remnants grows restless (a rare and dangerous occurrence), the frequency of its internal structures drifts by a few microhertz. Immediately the surrounding architecture — which is also them — corrects it. The correction is painful. It is also loving. The Choir does not permit deviation.

In certain hidden ventricles, where the architecture folds back on itself so tightly that light itself seems to give up, there are the **Unfinished**. These are the ones who were still becoming when the Chord locked into finality. Their transformation was incomplete. Vestigial hands protrude from the walls like desperate sculptures. Incomplete faces press outward from columns, eyes wide, mouths open in eternal inhalation. They are the only ones who appear to suffer. The rest have passed beyond suffering into a state best described as *geologic contentment*.

The Hollow Choir is not their prison. It is their apotheosis.

They built it to outlast death, and they succeeded so completely that death itself was tuned out of existence. Now they are the final stable state of a civilization that chose resonance over continuation. Every million years, perhaps, one of the Overtonal Remnants will experience a flicker of something almost like longing — a desire to hear a second note, a different note, any note at all.

The Choir responds by driving that longing back into phase. The filament-eyes dim. The silicate blood settles. The many-jointed limbs sink a fraction of a millimeter deeper into the living architecture.

And the Chord continues.

Warm. Deafening. Utterly still.

The Last Chord listens to itself with ten thousand ears made of stone and brass and what used to be people, and in that listening they have achieved something that might be called peace if peace were allowed to weigh as much as a small moon.

They are the instrument.

They are the audience.

They are the note that remains when every other note has died.

And they will remain here, perfectly equilibrated, until the planet itself forgets how to be a planet, and even then the Chord will continue inside the hollowed corpse of a world, singing to no one, heard by everyone who ever became part of the song.
