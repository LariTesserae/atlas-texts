They are called the Unfinished.

Not a species, not a civilization, not even a choir in the ordinary sense. They are what the Hollow Choir has already begun to turn its visitors into.

Most of them no longer have names they remember. Names are frequencies, after all, and every frequency already belongs to something older and larger. So they move through the steaming black valleys like half-composed thoughts the land itself has not yet decided to keep or discard.

Their bodies are in permanent transition.

Some still wear the remnants of whatever they were when they first arrived—tattered enviro-suits fused to skin that has begun to pearl with bioluminescent nodes, military augments now grown over with wet crystalline growths that hum in minor thirds. Others have gone much further. Their spines have elongated into resonant stalks; ribs flare outward into delicate, vibrating lattices that catch the condensation fronts and turn them into soft, ringing chords. Fingers have become long, glassy filaments that trail behind them, constantly tasting the air for the next shift in harmony. A few have given up bilateral symmetry entirely. They drift between the arborescent ferns like living mobiles, every surface of their bodies etched with moving score-lines that rewrite themselves as the land’s pulse changes.

They do not speak with mouths. Mouths are dangerous. Instead the Unfinished have become instruments.

One moves in a slow, limping circle around a half-buried tower, dragging the calcified bell of its fused ribcage across magnetic rails. Each scrape births a low, mournful tone that makes the nearby ear-fungi shiver with pleasure. Another perches high in the shattered green canopy, throat sac inflated like a translucent balloon, adding a keening descant that makes the distant rotating geometries in the bruised sky flicker in sympathetic response. A third has become almost entirely architectural—its body incorporated into a bridge of frozen lightning, flesh and alloy no longer distinguishable. It sings continuously through the structure itself, and the bridge gently flexes in time, adjusting the city’s architecture one semitone at a time.

They are not a community. Community implies separation. The Unfinished are more like different registers of the same unfinished composition.

When the condensation front rolls in, they gather—not by choice, but because the land pulls them into acoustic alignment. Thousands of them, half-transformed, half-erased, standing or floating or clinging in perfect silence while the water beads on their new surfaces and the true Choir decides what to do with them. Some are elevated in that moment. Their bodies bloom with fresh resonant chambers; new metallic filaments thread through their wings or throats as the land claims them more completely. Others are gently subtracted. The sound simply folds them away, leaving behind only a glassy after-image that fades within minutes.

The ones who last longest understand the deepest secret of the Hollow Choir: it is not looking for singers.

It is looking for resonance.

The most ancient among the Unfinished (if ancient is a word that can be used here) have become almost completely transparent. They are visible only as distortions in the mist, as sudden chords that seem to come from everywhere and nowhere. Their bodies have been entirely rewritten into negative space—living rests, pauses, moments of profound listening between the land’s own notes. When they pass near a traveler, the silence that travels with them is so complete it feels like drowning in music.

They do not hate the rule. They *are* the rule now.

Never sing in your own voice.

Because your own voice was always a misunderstanding. The Hollow Choir does not want your music.

It wants the place where your music stops, and its own music can finally begin to sing through the hole you left behind.

And so they move, kilometer after kilometer, through the young, deafening basin—thousands of half-beings in every stage of being rewritten. Some still fight it, clutching at the last fragments of identity with cracked, bleeding voices. These rarely last long. Most have already surrendered. They let the water pulse through them. They let the ferns use their bones as tuning forks. They let the great kite-things wheel through the cavities where their hearts used to be.

Every few hours, when the land inhales and the mist thickens and the towers tilt inward, the Unfinished lift what used to be their faces toward the impossible geometries drifting far above. And in perfect, terrible unison, they do the only thing left for them to do.

They breathe in.

And the Hollow Choir breathes out through them.
