**They** live here.

Not one thing. Not even a species. The Cathedral has learned to host a single, continuous intelligence that expresses itself through a thousand shifting bodies and half-bodies. The locals simply call them **the Unfinished**.

They are what happens when the boundary between organism and mechanism is left to rot in warm humidity for long enough. No two look alike for more than a few hours. The Cathedral itself is their placenta, their nervous system, and their slowly digesting god.

Some of them are mostly still metal. You will meet one that appears at first to be nothing more than a clutch of brass beetles fused together into the rough shape of a child. Its back is a single living hinge of lichen and solder. When it turns to look at you, the “face” is a wet porcelain mask grown in place, featureless except for two slow-blinking slits that leak clear fluid in perfect time with your own heartbeat. It never speaks with a voice. Instead the pipes around it sigh your name in modulated steam.

Others have kept more flesh. There is a woman (if the word still applies) whose torso is a hollow bronze reliquary. Inside the reliquary floats a heart the size of a wine cask, veined with copper wire and blue algae, beating against the glass in time with the distant pumps. Her arms are bundles of wet fern-fronds wrapped around articulated steel bones; when she reaches for you the fronds open like fingers and the scent of crushed limestone becomes almost unbearable. She is gentle. She is also centuries old and has never been born.

Then there are the ones who wear the Cathedral’s own skin. You will not see them until they decide to detach. A section of viridian pipe will suddenly slump, soften, and peel away from the wall with a sound like wet velvet tearing. What emerges is a long, sexless thing of glistening green metal and living rust, ribs opening and closing like book spines. Its head is a cluster of dripping scarlet lichen-frills that somehow resolve into a face when it wishes to be understood. These are the ones that sing. Not with throats, but by opening valves in their own bodies so the pressurized air rushes through them in slow, mournful chords older than any human music.

They are all in the middle of becoming something else. That is the only law.

A creature you spoke with yesterday may return today wearing an additional set of pale bat-wings wired directly into its spine, or it may have molten half its face back into undifferentiated scaffolding so it can regrow new sensory organs better suited to tomorrow’s humidity. They do this without urgency and without regret. To them, identity is a temporary scaffold, like the ribs still dripping overhead.

They tend the beetles. They coax new transepts out of the walls with slow, stroking caresses that look disturbingly like lovemaking. When a section of the Cathedral threatens to finish itself (a dangerous and almost erotic transgression here), they open the hidden valves themselves and bathe the offending architecture in warm acidic mist while murmuring apologies in frequencies that make your teeth ache with sudden nostalgia for a childhood you never had.

If you stay long enough, they will offer to finish you.

Not kill you. Finish you.

They believe the only crime is to stop changing. So they will ask, softly, whether you would like to be opened up and reseeded with moss and copper. Whether you would like your bones replaced with slow-growing lattice. Whether you would like to weep forever in the way the Cathedral weeps, useful, warm, and never, ever done.

Most say no.

Most leave while they still wear a face that would be recognized by their mothers.

But some stay.

And after enough time has passed (decades, perhaps centuries of slow accreted millimeters), the new buttresses stop groaning quite so loudly when they lock into place. The hiss of vapor acquires a new, lower note. And if you return to the place where you once stood still long enough to feel the floor rising, you may find a new pipe growing there, thick as an ancient oak, viridian and furred with rust.

Inside that pipe, something is gently beating.

It knows your name.

It has not finished learning it yet.
