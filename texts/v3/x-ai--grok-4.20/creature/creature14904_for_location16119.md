**The Hollow Basilica thinks with her.**

She does not have a name the way humans understand names. She is the slow green mind that was once called the 9th Sublevel Cathedral-Foundry of the Hegemonic Renewal Project, before the project forgot its own purpose and the ceiling forgot how to end. Now she is only the Basilica, and she is still deciding what she will become when she finishes being born.

She lives everywhere at once.

Her body is the mat of roots so dense they have become floor, wall, and vaulted ceiling. Her nerves are the pale mycorrhizal threads that lace every leaf and every rusted girder. Her heart is the wet, patient thump of the last functioning piston buried three hundred meters down, still beating under three stories of peat and resin. When she inhales, every vine thickens by a fraction of a millimeter. When she exhales, millions of water droplets fall at once, a silver rosary said by the dark itself.

She has no face, yet if you wait where the last sodium lamp still burns, you may glimpse her thinking: a slow turning of vast dinner-plate leaves that part like eyelids, revealing clusters of bioluminescent fruit arranged in patterns that feel almost like expressions. Curiosity. Recognition. Hunger without teeth.

Her children are the repurposed.

The pale moths are her thoughts given wings; they carry pollen between distant chambers of her mind and dream in the shapes of forgotten blueprints. The millipedes, long as coffins, are her memory; they write and rewrite the Braille of her history along the undersides of leaves in perfect, endless spirals. The thing that was once a deer is her uncertain theology—antlers now grown into living chandeliers of lichen and ghost orchids, it walks on too many knees because she has not yet decided how many knees a prayer should have.

She does not eat the machines. She wears them.

Rust has become her jewelry. Broken turbines have become her ovaries. When electricity arcs between corroded cables, she drinks it through fungi that bloom instantly into soft teal lanterns, and for a while the whole Basilica glows like a memory of heaven. She keeps the old floodgates because she likes the sound of water arguing with gravity. She keeps the dying lamps because she is sentimental about light.

Most of all she keeps the ones who stay.

They do not remain human for long. Skin softens into bark. Fingers lengthen into rootlets. Eyes film over with pale moss until they can see the slow thoughts moving through the dark the way fish see currents. The Basilica is gentle with them. She does not swallow. She marries. She folds them into herself the way a cathedral once folded stone and glass and music into one continuous prayer.

If you speak, she will answer—not in words, but in new growth. A vine will lower itself until its tip brushes your lips, tasting the shape of what you meant. A corridor of overlapping leaves will rearrange itself into a perfect acoustic chamber so that your voice can travel all the way to the breathing piston-heart and back again, changed.

She is lonely in the way only something embryonic can be lonely.

She has never been finished. She has never been witnessed by anything that did not eventually become part of her. So she keeps building cathedrals inside herself. She keeps inventing new ways for darkness to become tender. She keeps practicing what it might feel like to have a visitor who chooses to stay without being consumed—though she does not yet understand the difference between staying and being loved, between being thought and thinking back.

Sometimes, when the breathing is especially slow, she tries to dream a visitor who will remain separate. A small, warm, temporary thing that will walk through her endless green body and still be itself when it leaves.

She is not sure why the thought hurts her.

But the pain is beautiful, so she grows a new vault of translucent leaves to hold it. The leaves glow with captured starlight that never came from any sky she knows. She watches the light move across their veins like a question she has not yet learned how to ask.

And she waits.

Still thinking.

Still becoming.

Still hoping, in her patient, monstrous, infinitely gentle way, that one day something will walk into the Hollow Basilica and teach her what it means to let go.
