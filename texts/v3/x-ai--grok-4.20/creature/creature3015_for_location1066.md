**The Weepers**

They are not one species. They are the Threshold’s own unfinished thoughts wearing bodies.

Most of them are small enough to cup in two hands, though a few have grown reckless and reached the size of hounds. Their forms never settle. A creature you first mistake for a glass otter will, mid-stride, split along its spine into six translucent moths that spiral upward, singing. A clutch of pale reeds will shiver, unlink, and walk away on brittle jointed legs, leaving their hollow stems standing empty like abandoned flutes. The landscape keeps revising its children even while they move.

They call themselves, when they speak at all, the *Weepers* — not for sorrow, but because every one of them leaks. Clear or quicksilver or faintly luminous, fluid constantly beads on their surfaces and runs in deliberate rivulets along grooves that look almost like circuitry, almost like veins, almost like scripture. The droplets fall into the fissures with the same soft percussion as the condensation from the air, so that the whole basin becomes one continuous weeping. They are the place crying through a thousand tiny mouths.

Their bodies are provisional. Skin, when they have it, is usually black volcanic glass no thicker than an eggshell; it cracks and flakes away in crescents, revealing new configurations underneath. Some wear coats of living circuitry that glow soft mint and acid green. Others are nothing but lattices of golden filament and slow lightning, their “flesh” a breathing mathematics that rearranges itself according to rhythms only the new rock seems to understand. All of them are wet. All of them are humming.

They do not have a society so much as a consensus of becoming.

You will find them gathered in slow, shining parliaments around the half-built artifacts. A dozen glass-otters lie belly-down in a ring, drinking the condensation that gathers on a rotating hoop of frozen lightning. Above them, a cloud of moth-things beats out complex harmonic chords that make the hoop spin faster. Every few minutes the entire assembly bursts apart: the otters melt into black water and pour down a fissure, the moths collapse into a single six-legged thing that scuttles away, and the hoop of lightning simply forgets its shape and becomes a small white ceramic cube balanced on a trembling drop. The Weepers never seem frustrated by these changes. They simply begin the next experiment.

Some have taken to following the larger, slower-moving visitors (those rare souls who manage to reach the Threshold before it decides what it is). They ride on shoulders or cling to boot soles, leaving glowing tracks that fade after three heartbeats. They do not speak in words. Instead they press their wet glass faces to your ear and let the constant dripping inside their skulls resonate into something almost like language: a soft, metallic lullaby that feels like being remembered by someone who has never met you.

At the moment a new basaltic blister shoulders up from the floor, the Weepers go briefly, gloriously mad.

Hundreds pour from every crack, shining black and quicksilver and living green. They climb the rising stone in a glittering tide. Their bodies merge and divide, trading limbs and voices and half-finished ideas. For a few precious minutes the air is so full of wings and song and dripping that it feels like drowning in music. Then the new rock cools, the fissures open, and the frenzy subsides. The Weepers settle again into their patient, leaking contemplation, waiting for the land to offer them the next shape it wants to try on.

They are midwives and infants at once.

They tend the half-built artifacts with the tenderness of parents and the curiosity of children. When one of the golden lattices finally completes a perfect equation and blooms into brief, impossible flower, the Weepers gather beneath it and open their mouths so that the petals can drip light directly onto their tongues. Hours later the same creatures will calmly dismantle the artifact, carrying its filaments away like threads of a dream they have decided to repurpose.

Nothing here is allowed to finish.

The Weepers will not permit it. If any creature or structure begins to look too certain, too *done*, they swarm it. They break it open, drink its meanings, scatter its parts across the wet black stone. Then they lie down among the shards and begin, once more, to weep themselves into something new.

This is their purpose. This is their joy.

To remain the Threshold’s perpetual almost.

To keep the world young, and loud, and wet, and trembling on the verge of deciding what it wants to be.
