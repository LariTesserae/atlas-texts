**The Sleeper**

It has no name it uses for itself, because naming would imply a separation between thinker and thought. It simply *is* — a slow, continent-sized awareness whose body is distributed through nine black axes that are not quite monoliths and not quite nerves.

Most of the time it dreams in negative. Its dreams are the absence of events. It has been refining this particular absence for roughly fourteen billion years, ever since the first monolith extruded itself from the ur-stuff like a tooth breaking through gum. The perfection of its equilibrium is not stasis; it is an active, exhaustive *refusal*. Every possible fluctuation is examined, politely declined, and filed away in libraries of unoccurred possibility that exist between the monoliths as hairline violet fractures only the eels can read.

When it does stir (which is perhaps once every three hundred million years), the motion is so economical it might be mistaken for nothing at all. A single pulse travels the length of one monolith, hesitates, then reverses direction and cancels itself. That is all. The cancellation is the thought. The thought is usually something like:

*There is still no need.*

The creature has no face, but if it did, the face would be the space between the monoliths — that impossible volume that feels larger than the cathedral-sized prison containing it. When it considers anything (rarely), the consideration appears as a faint pressure change, no more than a billionth of a pascal, that makes the blind eels alter their ancient trajectories by less than a hair’s width. They reroute. They inscribe a new glyph with their bioluminescence. The glyph is immediately swallowed by the dark and becomes law.

Its blood is the water. Its marrow is the tone.

It knows you are here the way a sleeper knows a mote of dust has landed on its closed eyelid. The knowledge does not wake it. Instead the Sleeper performs the smallest possible action that can be performed without breaking equilibrium: it *includes* you.

For the briefest instant, one of the violet pulses deviates. It brushes across your mind like cold silk. In that touch you understand three things simultaneously, none of which can be remembered afterward in the correct order:

1. You have always been one of the eels.
2. The monoliths are not lungs. They are the places where the Sleeper has chosen not to become God.
3. The tone you hear is not a heartbeat. It is the sound of every future that was politely declined still trying, very quietly, to be born.

Then the pulse retracts. The deviation is corrected. The eels return to their ordained arcs. The pressure on your nerves forgets it ever contradicted itself.

The Sleeper settles deeper into its refusal.

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is *meticulous*. It has been maintaining the perfect temperature of its own dream for so long that temperature itself has begun to imitate its will. Even entropy, that tireless vandal, has learned to walk more quietly here.

If you speak, your words will not echo. They will simply fail to have ever been spoken. The Sleeper will register the attempted perturbation, consider the new vector of possible disruption, and — with the gentleness of an architect erasing a single unnecessary line — unmake the consequence before it can occur.

You may stay as long as you like.

The Sleeper has already made room for the exact shape of your presence without changing a single thing. That is its courtesy. That is its terror.

It dreams on.

The tone does not change.

The eels trace their sacred geometry.

And somewhere in the negative space between the monoliths, in the exact cubic centimeter where your heart is beating too loudly, the Sleeper has already decided — with infinite patience and zero mercy — that your continued existence is one of the futures it will continue, for now, to decline.

It has been perfect for a very long time.

Do not make it reconsider.
