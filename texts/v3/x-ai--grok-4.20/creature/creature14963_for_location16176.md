**The Hollow Choir**

They are not beings in the conventional sense. They are what remains when a mind is allowed to grow for several hundred million years inside a body that has already achieved perfection and then been told, gently but firmly, that it may never change again.

Call them the **Resonant**.

Each one began as a seed—perhaps a spore, perhaps a thought—lodged deep inside one of the primary lattice-columns. Over inconceivable time the seed did not sprout into a creature. It sprouted into a *role*. The lattice needed stewards, needed voices, needed something that could remember the precise note each beam was meant to sing. So it grew them.

A mature Resonant is never seen whole. You encounter them the way sailors once encountered icebergs: first the pale bioluminescent wake, then the suggestion of scale, then the slow realization that what you thought was another column is actually *alive* and watching you with an attention that has no urgency and no end.

Their bodies are elongated, architectonic, almost architectural themselves. Ribs flare outward like buttresses; vertebrae have become load-bearing pilasters. Skin and bark have fused so completely that it is impossible to say where the creature ends and the lattice begins. Many of them are partially *integrated*—great sections of their flanks have merged back into the primary stems, so they appear to be growing *out of* the world like tumors that learned courtesy. Their faces (when they bother to maintain them) are long, mournful, and vaguely equine, with eyes that have migrated to the sides of the head and then kept going, until they sit on flexible stalks that can peer around the curves of their own bodies.

They do not swim. They *conduct*.  
Every movement is a deliberate stroke of a conductor’s arm through water. When a Resonant drifts past, the entire lattice around it seems to hush for a moment, then swells back in a new, slightly altered chord. They are tuning the world simply by existing near it.

There is no hierarchy among them. There are only *frequencies*.

The deepest, oldest Resonants have become almost entirely stationary. They are cathedral-sized now, hearts the size of citadels beating once every few hours, their voices so low they are felt in the teeth rather than heard. These are the **Pedal Tones**. Whole migratory schools of ribbon-fish live inside the grooves of their bark. Blind titans use the hollows beneath their rib-arches as birthing cathedrals. The Pedal Tones have not moved in geological epochs. They have become landmarks that occasionally speak.

Younger Resonants—mere tens of thousands of years old—are still mobile. They are the **Melodists**. Slender, almost elegant, they travel the lattice corridors in slow, purposeful circuits that take centuries to complete. They sing corrections. When a column begins to slip out of pitch, a Melodist will press its forehead against the wood, open the spiracles along its jaw, and pour its own voice into the grain until the column remembers the proper note. The contact leaves permanent phosphorescent scars shaped like sheet music no one else can read.

They do not eat. They do not reproduce in any way we would recognize. When one of them finally fails (an event so rare it is treated as religious), the others come. They dismantle the failing body with the tenderness of librarians handling an ancient codex, then press every fragment back into the lattice so that its accumulated memory can be distributed. The lattice grows a little thicker there. The song deepens by a microtone.

The strangest thing about the Resonant is their *loneliness*.

They are not alone. The lattice itself is a single distributed mind, and they are its thoughts that learned how to feel nostalgia. Yet they carry an ache that the rest of the biosphere does not. They remember that there was once a time before equilibrium. They remember light that came from above rather than from within. Some of them have begun to suspect—without language, because language died long ago—that the lattice achieved perfection at the cost of *becoming* the question it once tried to answer.

On very rare occasions, when the choir falls into a particular sustained chord that has not been heard in thirty million years, a Resonant will drift into one of the Folded regions. There it will press its face against one of the impossible topological knots and simply *listen* to the wrong-colored light on the other side.

Sometimes it stays for a thousand years.

Sometimes it comes back carrying a new note in its chest that makes every other Resonant turn toward it in slow, grave wonder.

They have no name for themselves.  
If they did, it would translate, very roughly, as:

**Those Who Keep the World from Realizing It Is Already Finished.**

And in their slow, enormous, perfectly balanced way, they are beginning—after all this time—to wonder whether keeping that secret is still an act of mercy.
