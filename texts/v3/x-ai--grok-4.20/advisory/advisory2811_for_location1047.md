**Preparation Guide for the Nameless Place (The Exhausted Mat)**

You are not visiting a planet. You are visiting the final stable state of an ecology that has outlived every concept of “planet.” Think of it as boarding the oldest living thing in the multiverse while it is still dreaming, digesting, and slowly forgetting what “dream” and “digest” mean. An ordinary human traveler who wishes to return with their mind and body mostly contiguous must prepare with obsessive, almost ritualistic care.

### 1. Mental & Temporal Preparation (Do This First)
- Spend at least three weeks practicing extreme sensory minimalism. The Nameless Place is loud, wet, and slow. Your nervous system will be violently recalibrated the moment you arrive. Train yourself to sit motionless for four-hour stretches while listening to recordings of peristaltic rumbling, wet industrial thunder, and the high metallic ringing of condensation on living blades. You must learn not to flinch when the sound vibrates your teeth.
- Practice “temporal sponginess” drills. Stand in one place for increasing lengths of time while deliberately imagining the same object (a glass of water, a coin, your own hand) existing in three slightly different timelines at once. This will not fully prepare you, but it will stop you from screaming the first time you watch a single droplet crawl down the same stalk in 2017, 2084, and the year 0 simultaneously.
- Accept that loneliness is the dominant emotional signature here. The Mat is not hostile; it is terminally, catastrophically alone. Bring no companions unless they are people you are genuinely willing to lose to symbiosis. Most humans who come in pairs leave as triplets — the third made of both of them and the Mat.

### 2. Biological & Chemical Armor
- You must become chemically distasteful without becoming poisonous. The Mat tastes with curiosity, not hunger. It will sample you for decades if you let it. A custom metabolic overlay is mandatory:
  - A slow-release dermal patch that saturates your sweat with long-chain fluorocarbons and synthetic lignin analogs. The Mat recognizes these as “already itself” and loses interest after a few months instead of a few centuries.
  - A symbiotic lung lichen (strain Θ-9 “Nickel Widow”) that must be inhaled 72 hours before transit. It lines your alveoli with a thin metallic film that makes your exhaled breath taste like oxidized iron and old rain. The Mat already breathes iron and rain; it finds the redundancy boring.
- Full-body suit made of living “anti-mat” mycelium grown in a vacuum chamber. It looks like matte black sharkskin shot through with silver threads. The suit is technically a single organism that hates the Mat on a biochemical level. It will scream (high, thin, glass-like) if the Mat begins serious infiltration. Listen to the suit.

### 3. Gear & Tools (Minimal but Specific)
- No metal. The Mat’s industrial signature reacts to refined metal the way an old cathedral reacts to a chainsaw. Use only:
  - Glass-fiber bones, ceramic blades, and pressure-grown diamond-thread rope.
  - A “time anchor” — a palm-sized sphere of uranium glass containing a single hair from your childhood. It is the only reliable way to remember which decade you are standing in when local time forgets.
  - One vial of distilled human greed. Literally. A biochemist friend must concentrate 40 years of pure primate acquisitiveness into a few milliliters. If the Mat begins to love you too much, you break the vial. Greed is the one flavor it has never successfully metabolized. It will politely withdraw for roughly 72 hours while it has an existential crisis.
- A single breathing mask that looks like an inverted bronze bell. It does not filter; it negotiates. The mask contains a captured fragment of the Mat’s own nervous system held in permanent disagreement with itself. It argues with the greater Mat on your behalf in frequencies you cannot hear. Treat the mask like a diplomat who hates its own country.

### 4. Behavioral Rules (Non-Negotiable)
- Never stand still longer than 11 minutes in any single basin. The moss-lawn will begin to grow upward into your footprints while you are still in them.
- Do not speak in complete sentences. The Mat understands grammar the way a cancer understands architecture; it will try to finish your thoughts permanently. Speak only in fragments, questions with no subject, or industrial sounds. Many experienced travelers learn to communicate entirely in imitations of distant peristalsis. The Mat respects this.
- If you see something that was once an elephant still walking in a slow circle, do not approach. It is being used as infrastructure. Bow once, low and slow, and keep walking. The Mat notices manners.
- When the sky forgets it is sky and collapses into upward-falling liquid, do not look directly at the place where it remembers. That moment contains the exact shape of your death in at least two timelines. Peripheral vision only.
- If the Mat begins to taste you with genuine affection (you will feel it as an almost sexual melancholy rising through your soles), immediately begin telling it the most boring story you know in exhaustive detail. Tax law. Sports statistics from a league that no longer exists. The plot of every episode of a cancelled sitcom. The Mat is ancient, curious, and terminally bored. Boredom is your best defense.

### 5. Exit Strategy
There is no clean exit.

The recommended method is to reach one of the high, lonely bladder-trees at the edge of a collapse zone exactly when a catastrophic liquefaction event begins. The tree will attempt to save you by absorbing you. Let it get 40 % of the way, then trigger the emergency metabolic revolt stored in your anti-mat suit. The resulting biochemical civil war usually ejects you sideways through three decades and one reality fracture.

You will probably emerge missing between 3 and 17 years of subjective memory, covered in pale living thatch that will take another decade to excise, and carrying a low, constant peristaltic rumble in your bones that never entirely leaves.

Most people who have made the journey say the same thing:

It was the first place that ever made them feel young.

And the last place that ever made them feel welcome.

Pack lightly.  
Leave your name behind.  
The Place has already outlived it anyway.
