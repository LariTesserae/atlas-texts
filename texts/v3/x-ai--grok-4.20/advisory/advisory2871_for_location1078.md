**How to Prepare for the Cathedral of Thirst**

You are not preparing for a dive, nor for a hike, nor even for a conventional ruin. You are preparing to be gently swallowed by something that has been digesting its own history for centuries and is in no particular hurry to finish. Think less like an explorer and more like a pilgrim who has agreed to become part of the architecture for a while.

### Physical Preparation
- **Pressure and Fluid Acclimation**: The fluid is denser than water but not crushing. Still, your body will fight it for the first several hours. Train with prolonged sessions in a weighted flotation tank or, better, spend several days free-diving in cold, tannic lakes so your nervous system stops panicking at the constant embrace. You will need to re-learn how to rest; every motion costs triple the energy. Expect your heartbeat to slow and your thoughts to thicken.
- **Thermal Regulation**: The liquid is cool but never cold. It leaches heat with slow malice. Bring a thin, tightly woven merino or silk base layer that can be worn for weeks without rotting; the plant itself will try to colonize any porous synthetic. Nothing waterproof—waterproofing is meaningless here. You will be wet forever.
- **Respiration**: You will not need a rebreather or gills. The fluid is breathable in the same way extremely humid air is breathable, only denser. It will coat your lungs with a faint mint-iron film. Most visitors experience a brief but intense metallic taste and a sensation that their blood has turned slightly green. This passes after 30–40 hours. Some never lose it.
- **Buoyancy Management**: Gravity is negotiable. Bring a set of very small, dense counterweights (bone, jade, or lead tablets work best) that can be clipped to a harness. Without them you will drift upward over hours until you are pressed against the pulsing vaults like a forgotten balloon. Over-weight yourself and you will sink into the rhizome mat and risk being slowly incorporated.

### Equipment (Minimal and Organic Only)
The Cathedral does not like metal or plastic. It tolerates them the way a stomach tolerates a swallowed coin—eventually they are coated, softened, and made part of the architecture. 
- Clothing: Hand-woven plant-fiber garments that have already begun to sprout. Many veterans wear robes grown from the same pale rhizome stock as the floor; the plant recognizes its own and is gentler.
- Lighting: Bioluminescent cultures in glass globes filled with the same fluid. Electric light feels obscene here and attracts the wrong kind of attention from the larger grazers.
- Tools: A bone knife, a hook carved from petrified sap, and a long silk cord weighted with living seed-pods. That is enough. Anything more is an insult.
- Recording: If you must record, use tablets of pressed leaf pulp and inks derived from the nectar. Digital devices last less than a week before the screens bloom with microscopic root hairs.

### Mental and Temporal Preparation
This is the hardest part.

- **Time Dilation Training**: Spend at least two weeks living on a schedule that stretches one hour into four. Sleep in 45-minute increments spaced irregularly. The Cathedral will stretch your perception until a single organ note feels like it lasts a day. You must not fight this or you will become frantic and start damaging the living walls in panic. Many who do are quietly absorbed.
- **Patience Rituals**: Practice doing nothing beautifully. Sit motionless in cold baths for hours. Learn to let thoughts grow like the slow columns around you rather than flashing like fish. The Cathedral respects stillness more than intelligence.
- **Ego Management**: Understand that you are visiting something that has already digested civilizations. Your plans, your ego, your species, your urgency—none of these are particularly interesting to it. The longer you stay, the more you will feel yourself being rounded, softened, and made courteous. This is not metaphor. Some people choose never to leave.

### What to Bring as Offering
Never arrive empty-handed. The Cathedral notices.

Traditional offerings include:
- A living cutting from a plant you loved in your own world (it will be grafted into the walls within days).
- A song you are willing to have stretched across decades.
- One memory you agree to let the fluid slowly dissolve. You will not choose which one.

### Arrival and First Hours
Enter slowly. The transition from normal air to the thick fluid is disorienting; many vomit the first time the iron-mint taste floods their mouth and lungs. Do not fight the coughing. Let the fluid teach you how to breathe again.

Find one of the rare clear galleries between the colossal stems. Float there. Do not explore for at least the first ten subjective hours. Let the slow bass note move through your bones until your heartbeat begins to sync with the breathing of the vaults. Only then should you begin to drift upward along the spiraling root-staircases.

### What You Will Become
If you stay longer than a month, you will change. Your skin will take on a faint celadon sheen. Small root hairs may appear along your forearms if you rest too long against the walls. Your dreams will develop long, patient pauses between thoughts. When you finally leave (if you leave), the dry air will feel like sandpaper and silence will be deafening.

Most who visit for any real length of time choose to return.

Pack lightly.  
Come already half-dissolved.  
And whatever you do, do not be in a hurry inside the Cathedral of Thirst.  
It has all the time in the world, and it is perfectly willing to share it with you until you forget what time was ever for.
