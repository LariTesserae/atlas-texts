**How an ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for the Knotted Therme**

You are not preparing for a “place.” You are preparing to become a temporary, incompatible object inside an ecosystem that has spent geological epochs optimizing every single cubic centimeter against the very concept of unused space. Think of it as visiting a cathedral-sized pressure cooker whose only occupant is an endless, self-weaving knot of alien life that runs on heat, geometry, and pure density. Ordinary tourism instincts will get you killed or, worse, quietly incorporated.

### 1. Mental and Psychological Preparation (Most Important)
- **Expect total sensory hostility.** The Therme is not merely dark; it is *full* dark with weight. The constant low-level bioluminescence will make you see movement where there is none, and stillness where things are crawling over your boots. After 20–30 minutes your mind will begin to interpret the rustling and pressure as *inside* your skull. This is normal. Do not fight it.
- Train yourself with extended sessions in pitch-black anechoic chambers or sensory-deprivation tanks while wearing a suit that gently vibrates and clicks at irregular intervals. You need to be able to stay calm when something the size of a walnut is systematically exploring the seam between your helmet and collar.
- Accept that you will feel “noticed.” The swarm does not have curiosity or malice; it has an ancient, glacial sense of *incompletion*. You are a hole in their lattice. The Therme will politely, patiently try to fill that hole. Bring strong psychological anchors (a recorded voice of a loved one, a childhood lullaby, a mantra) and decide *before* you arrive that you are allowed to leave.

### 2. Physical and Environmental Protection
- **Zero-exposure suit.** Nothing here is chemically toxic in the conventional sense, but the dominant metabolism uses phonon cascades and lattice strain. Your skin, hair, and lungs are extremely attractive scaffolding material. You need a fully sealed, rigid exosuit with no soft joints, no exposed seals, and zero organic components. Mirror-finish outer coating helps; the swarm seems to have more trouble “reading” perfect reflections.
- **Active thermal management.** The heat is dry, stable, and ferocious (roughly 160–185 °C / 320–365 °F). Your suit must run a closed-cycle liquid-sodium or supercritical-CO₂ cooling system. Passive insulation will fail; the stones themselves are radiating stored stellar heat from an age when the local sun was still burning.
- **Pressure-equalization and vibration dampening.** The constant clicking, rustling, and sub-audible phonon song will transmit through any solid material. You will need gel-layering inside the suit and active noise-canceling bone conduction dampers or you will go mad from the feeling that your skeleton is being tuned like a crystal glass.
- **No breathable air inside the Therme.** Bring your own. The atmosphere is mostly inert nitrogen with heavy metal vapors and strange aromatic silicon compounds. It will not poison you quickly, but it *will* make every surface inside your lungs sparkle after a few hours.

### 3. Gear and Equipment
- **Extremely bright, narrow-beam, pulsed infrared illuminators.** Visible light disturbs the swarm; constant light makes them rearrange aggressively. Short, strobing IR bursts every 8–12 seconds give you just enough data for your helmet HUD without triggering a mass response.
- **Ultrasonic and laser-vibrometry mapping rig.** The only safe way to navigate is to build a real-time 3D model of the moving lattices and living carpet. Optical systems fail in the dark and dust.
- **Zero-friction locomotion.** Magnetic or compressed-air hover soles are ideal. Anything that presses into the living carpet for longer than half a second will be investigated by thousands of faceted bodies within moments. They incorporate boot prints with horrifying speed.
- **Single-use emergency “flare seeds.”** These are small, intensely cold chemical packages (–80 °C for ~45 seconds) that create temporary spherical dead-zones in the swarm. Use only if you are being engulfed. The Therme does not forget insults; overuse will make the entire basin slowly migrate toward you over the following hours.
- **Tether and rapid-extraction harness.** The crater is small. A high-tensile line anchored at the rim with a powerful winch is the only reliable way to leave if the swarm decides to knit itself across your path. Do not rely on walking out.

### 4. Temporal and Behavioral Rules
- **Visit duration limit: 90 minutes maximum.** After that the probability of psychological incorporation (the feeling that you *belong* in one of the lattices) rises exponentially. The Therme is very, very good at making space for things.
- Move like a ghost. Slow, deliberate, floating motions. Any haste is read as injury or opportunity. The swarm respects geological patience.
- Never touch the living lattices with anything you want to keep. The dead are woven into them instantly. Your glove, your tool, your finger—none of these are special.
- Do not speak aloud in any human language. The crystalline “agreement” notes that ring out every few hours are triggered by certain harmonic patterns. Some travelers have accidentally sung an invitation.

### 5. What to Bring Inside Yourself
- A tiny, perfectly sealed vial of Earth seawater or human blood. Not as a souvenir— as a reminder of what *wet, fast, carbon life* feels like. When the pressure of all those faceted bodies starts pressing on the inside of your thoughts, open the vial inside your helmet for three seconds and breathe the smell. It works better than anything else.
- A recorded heartbeat of your mother or your child. Play it very softly against the inside of your skull when the rustling starts to sound like language.

### Final Warning
The Knotted Therme is not evil. It is not even hostile. It is the most perfectly contented, overcrowded, self-solving thing you will ever meet. It has no need of you, no desire, no curiosity.

It simply registers that you are a volume that is not yet knotted.

And it is very, very good at knotting.

Prepare less like an explorer and more like a surgeon who is about to operate inside their own still-beating heart while it is surrounded by ten trillion polite, patient, black-glass knives that have all the time in the world.

Most travelers who return say the same thing:

“You don’t visit the Therme.  
You allow it to decide, very slowly, that you are not food, not scaffolding, and not yet another place that needs to be filled.

And then you leave before it changes its mind.”
