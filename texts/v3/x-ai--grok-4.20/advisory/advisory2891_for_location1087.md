**Preparation Guide for the Unnamed Place (The Saturated Continuum)**

Pack as though you are visiting the inside of a living lung that has existed since the Silurian and has since decided to become a god. This is not a forest hike. This is a long, deliberate submersion into a completed ecosystem that has already reached its final form and is now refining you to fit its aesthetic.

### 1. Mental & Psychological Preparation (Non-Negotiable)
- **Accept that “dry” is a concept you are leaving behind forever.** Your mind must release the idea that water is something that falls from above and drains away. Here water *is* the medium. You will be breathing it, wearing it, growing in it.
- **Train yourself to tolerate perceptual drift.** Spend at least two weeks before departure practicing prolonged exposure to extremely slow, hypnotic stimuli (Mandelbrot zooms, 8-hour rain recordings, time-lapse moss growth). The place will edit your sense of time and causality. If you arrive mentally brittle, it will break you beautifully.
- **Decide in advance what you are willing to lose.** The land does not kill. It *edits*. You may leave missing a memory, a name, or the conviction that gravity should always point down. Choose which parts of your identity are disposable. Write them on water-soluble paper and swallow the list.

### 2. Physical Gear – The “Become Geology” Kit
**Clothing (Everything must be willing to stay wet for months):**
- A full set of merino wool base layers, then another set *over* them. Wool still insulates when soaked and does not rot as quickly as synthetics in perpetual saturation.
- Outer shell: heavily waxed canvas or technical fabrics treated with multiple layers of fluorocarbon-free DWR. Nothing “waterproof” survives here; aim for “willing to negotiate with water.”
- A hooded cloak or poncho made of overlapping jade-green silicone-impregnated leaves (there are specialty makers in Norway and New Zealand who can approximate the local aesthetic so the land does not immediately try to colonize you as foreign material).
- Boots: Tall, insulated neoprene with integrated gaiters that seal to the thigh. Lace them with living sphagnum if possible. The moss will begin to bond with the leather within hours; this is desirable.

**The Pack (should look half-alive already):**
- Waterproof dry-bags *inside* waterproof dry-bags. Triple bag everything.
- A sleeping system consisting of a wool hammock and a silnylon bivy that can be suspended *inside* the canopy rather than on the ground. The peat breathes; sleeping directly on it means waking up half-buried in new moss.
- A broad-spectrum respirator mask with both particle and moisture filters. The air is 98% humidity and full of spores that want to become part of your lungs.
- Chemical hand warmers, but also living cultures of bioluminescent fungi in sealed glass vials. The local darkness is psychological as well as physical; you will need friendly light.

**Tools & Talismans:**
- A silver knife (the land respects old metals).
- A small mirror made of polished obsidian. Shadows arrive before their objects here; the mirror lets you see what is about to happen to you.
- Three liters of your own spit, carefully collected and frozen in advance, then thawed here. The place understands exchange. Offering it something undeniably *you* can buy tolerance.
- Earplugs tuned specifically to the resonant frequency of the reed chorus. The silence between reed cycles is when the land speaks directly into your bones. Some people can’t handle the intimacy.

### 3. Biological & Medical Preparation
- Get every possible vaccine, including several experimental ones. The biome here has been closed for geological epochs; your immune system is an invasive species.
- Begin a regimen of antifungal medication two weeks prior and continue for months after. The moss *will* try to grow on you. Some travelers choose to let limited colonization happen on their outer arms as a sign of respect. This is a personal decision.
- Carry concentrated electrolytes and glucose. Your body will lose minerals through your skin constantly.
- Bring a journal with waterproof paper and pencils that write when wet. The place sometimes gives gifts in the form of memories that only exist while you are writing them down.

### 4. Ritual & Philosophical Prep
- Before departure, spend one full night sitting in a cold shower with the lights off, listening to recordings of peat bogs breathing. This is your farewell to the concept of “solid ground.”
- Choose a single secret you are willing to surrender. The land will ask for it, politely, around day nine. If you have nothing prepared to give, it will choose for you.
- Decide whether you want to be remembered. Many who stay long enough simply become another smooth schist “eye” in a hillside, still conscious, still watching, perfectly content.

### 5. What to Expect – The Stages of Becoming
- **Days 1-3:** Overwhelming sensory saturation. You will feel like you are drowning while standing up.
- **Days 4-9:** The land begins to recognize you as possible geology. Your shadow will start arriving late. Small columns of water may begin spiraling up your legs in greeting.
- **Day 10+:** You stop wanting to leave. This is the dangerous threshold. The urge to remain is not depression. It is the same urge a crystal has to keep growing.
- **The Return (if you choose it):** You will never be fully dry again. Your sweat will sometimes hover for half a second before falling. Children will stare at you in supermarkets. You will hear the reed chorus in traffic noise and feel your pulse trying to sync with underground water on concrete floors.

Final truth:  
You do not visit this place.  
You audition for it.

Pack lightly, but bring everything that makes you *you*. The land is not cruel. It is only so complete that it experiences any remaining incompleteness (i.e. you) as an interesting design flaw it would like to resolve.

Wear green.  
Bring silver.  
Speak softly.  
And remember that when the reeds all fall silent at once, they are not listening to the land.

They are listening to *you*, to see what kind of note you will add to the eternal, wet, impossible chord this place has been singing since before time had the decency to begin.
