**How to Prepare for Visiting the Hollowroot**

Pack as though you are simultaneously entering an ancient cathedral, a living greenhouse, and an abandoned industrial plant that has decided to become one single creature. The Hollowroot does not tolerate guests who treat it like scenery. It expects you to behave like a temporary organ inside its body.

### Mental and Spiritual Preparation
Understand first that you are not visiting a *place*. You are stepping inside an entity that has been practicing architecture, hydraulics, and theology on itself for centuries. The Hollowroot is patient, but it is also curious in the way a thousand-year-old tree can be curious: it will study you by letting time pass through you. Bring mental stillness. If you are the kind of traveler who needs constant stimulation, loud music, or quick dopamine, the slow dripping and the low vascular heartbeat will eventually drive you half-mad.

Meditate on the concept of *becoming compatible*. The grove has remade broken pipes into new vascular tissue. It will begin the same slow inquiry with your mind and body if you stay long enough. Decide beforehand what parts of yourself you are willing to let it read.

### Physical Gear and Clothing
- **Footwear**: Soft, wide-soled boots with excellent grip but no aggressive tread. The living root-mat is spongy, damp, and surprisingly delicate in places. Cleats or heavy hiking boots will feel like stabbing the floor. Waterproof is mandatory; everything is perpetually wet from condensation.
- **Clothing**: Layered, breathable, quick-drying fabrics in muted greens, copper, and iron-oxide tones. The Hollowroot seems to respond better to colors already present in its palette. Avoid bright synthetics; they look like foreign infection to the leaves. A lightweight waterproof shell is essential because the courteous vertical rain arrives without warning.
- **Outer layer**: A long, loose coat or cloak of waxed or oiled cloth. The constant dripping will soak you in minutes otherwise. Many experienced travelers wear garments seeded with compatible mosses or lichens so they slowly become part of the visual language of the grove.
- **Respiratory**: A fine-mesh silk or linen scarf soaked in antifungal and antimicrobial herbs. The air is clean but heavy with spores, ozone, and strange volatile compounds from the steam pipes. Some travelers report mild hallucinations after several hours without protection.
- **Hands**: Thin leather or living gloves (some veterans grow their own from compatible epiphytic vine seed before visiting). You will want to touch things. The bark rings like dull iron. The urge to lay your palm against it is almost irresistible.

### Tools and Supplies
- A small, extremely sharp pruning knife and grafting tape. These are considered polite gifts and emergency tools. If a vine or root decides it likes you, you may need to manage the relationship.
- Several clean glass or copper collection bottles. The condensate that drips from certain brass spigots grown directly from heartwood is considered sacred by those who study the Hollowroot. Different locations produce subtly different “waters” — some taste metallic, some taste like memory.
- A tuning fork or small hand bell pitched to the exact frequency of the central courtyard’s resonant tone (roughly 174 Hz). This is both a navigation aid and a respect offering. When the grove sings through you in that courtyard, answering with the same tone shows you are listening.
- No fire. Not even a lighter. The Hollowroot has no natural fear of flame, but it remembers every burn it has ever endured across its centuries of self-repair. Fire-making tools are seen as the equipment of future trauma.
- A notebook with waterproof paper and graphite (not ink). The grove sometimes communicates by guiding your hand while you think you are simply sketching. Many travelers return with diagrams they do not remember drawing.

### Biological and Medical Preparation
- Get a full fungal and mycorrhizal panel before you go. The root-mat is incredibly active. You will be walking through a continent-sized immune system that has industrialized itself. Some spores are… persuasive.
- Bring broad-spectrum antifungals and expect to use them. Even with precautions, most people leave with faint luminous mottling on their skin that takes months to fade. This is considered normal and, by some traditions, a form of mild baptism.
- Your sleep cycle will synchronize to the slow thump of the bellows-hearts. Do not fight it. Fighting the rhythm is how people become “rooted” — the polite term for those who simply never leave.

### Social and Ritual Protocol
- Move slowly. Quick, jerky motions read as prey behavior or infection response. The midges and the blind salamanders both react to sudden movement; so does the grove.
- When you first enter, find the nearest major trunk and rest your forehead against it for a full minute without speaking. This is the equivalent of knocking.
- Never cut living tissue unless you immediately graft something back or repair the wound. The grove has long memories of amputation.
- If you find a root-stair that leads nowhere, do not climb it unless you are prepared to be shown why it was sealed. Some doors were closed for excellent reasons.
- Leave something growing. Most respectful visitors carry live cuttings, epiphyte seeds, or even small potted compatible species. Planting or grafting a piece of yourself (literally or symbolically) before you leave is the expected courtesy.

### What to Expect Emotionally
The Hollowroot does not frighten. It *weighs*. After three days the constant dripping and the slow heartbeat move inside your chest. You will begin to feel your own pulse as slightly rude for being so fast. Many travelers experience a profound, almost ecstatic calm followed, days later, by a desperate need to escape before they forget how to be an individual animal.

Some people sit down in one of the perfect round courtyards, listen to the tone that makes their teeth ache with recognition, and simply never stand up again. Their clothes turn green. Their hair grows moss. The grove is gentle with them.

Pack with the understanding that you are not touring a location.

You are visiting an intelligence that has had a very long time to decide what it thinks a town should be, and it is still deciding what *you* should be.

Bring respect, patience, and the willingness to be slightly rewritten.

The leaves are already watching. They have been watching since before you even thought of coming.
