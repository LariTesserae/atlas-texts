**Preparation Guide for a Visit to the Shelf**

You are not preparing for a *place*. You are preparing to be slowly, lovingly incorporated into a single continental organism that has already decided you belong to it. Think of this less as a hiking trip and more like signing up for three-dimensional symbiosis with something that is half primordial rainforest, half abandoned megafactory, and entirely alive. Ordinary traveler instincts will get you killed or, worse, kept alive indefinitely as a substrate.

### 1. Mindset & Psychological Preparation (Most Important)
- Accept that privacy, dryness, silence, and solitude are concepts the Shelf does not recognize. It will be inside your clothes, your ears, your lungs, and eventually your decisions.
- Cultivate radical acceptance of being tasted, weighed, catalogued, and gently metabolized at all times. The Shelf does not hate you; it is simply curious and hungry in the same way a stomach is curious and hungry.
- Prepare for sensory overload that never turns off. The constant metallic tinnitus, the 43-second geological heartbeat, the warm iron rain, and the feeling that the ground is breathing will erode your sanity if you fight them. Bring earplugs only for sleep; otherwise learn to *ride* the noise like a surfer rides a wave.
- Understand that “getting out” may become negotiable. The longer you stay, the more the Shelf will rewrite your pheromone signature, your gut biome, and your sense of time until leaving feels like amputation.

### 2. Physical Gear – “Second Skin” Protocol
You will wear everything for weeks without being able to take it off comfortably. Everything must be either waterproof, self-draining, or deliberately designed to rot at the same rate the jungle wants to eat it.

- **The Suit**: Full-body, breathable-yet-sealed expedition membrane (think advanced hazmat meets wetsuit) with thousands of micro-perforations. Outer layer should be aggressively anti-biofouling or, paradoxically, deliberately inoculated with Shelf-compatible moss so the jungle thinks you’re already part of it. Gore-Tex will fail in twelve hours. Bring three changes, but expect to lose at least two to aggressive strangler-root adhesion.
- **Footwear**: Not boots — living boot-symbionts. High, laced leather or synthetic that can be colonized by the same glowing fungi the six-legged browsers wear. The Shelf respects beings that have already outsourced their skin. Steel-toed boots will be slowly incorporated into new locomotive-hills.
- **Eyes**: The darkness is not total — it is alive with infrared, bioluminescence, and lightning-triggered orchids. You need triple protection:
  - Polarized, anti-fog goggles that can flip to thermal.
  - A secondary pair of chemically-treated “taste-lenses” that let you read airborne chemical gradients the way the blind fauna do.
  - Emergency blinkers that produce controlled lightning flashes to open the black orchids for brief, safe illumination.
- **Respiration**: A rebreather or full-face mask with biological filter cartridges grown from the same orchids that live here. The air is so saturated that ordinary filters drown. You will be drinking the air as much as breathing it.
- **Sound Management**: Custom-molded ear protection that still allows the 43-second pump rhythm through (missing that rhythm can cause fatal disorientation). Noise-canceling is useless; you need *tuning*, not cancellation.

### 3. Biological & Chemical Preparation
- **Pre-colonization**: Before arrival, deliberately inoculate yourself with a starter culture of the glowing fungi and certain iron-oxidizing bacteria that live on the six-legged megafauna. The Shelf is far less aggressive toward things that already smell like it.
- **Anti-digestion regimen**: Daily dosing with a complex of enzymes and alkaloids that slow the rate at which the jungle’s digestive mists break down human keratin and collagen. This buys you weeks instead of days. The downside is that your sweat will permanently smell of green rot and ozone.
- **Water discipline**: Never drink standing water. Only drink the upward-weeping capillary water that emerges directly from living stone, or rainwater intercepted before it touches any vegetation. All other water is already being used by something else.
- **Gut biome overhaul**: Your microbiome must be replaced with one that can process the constant influx of volatile plant oils and fungal spores. Bring starter cultures and a week-long “seeding” diet before you go.

### 4. Navigation & Technology
- Conventional GPS, compass, and maps are jokes here. The vegetation is one organism; magnetic fields fluctuate with the cathedral-sized pumps; the plateau itself exhales magnetic mist.
- Bring a living navigator: a juvenile “lantern herder” — one of the six-legged browsers’ symbiotic fungi colonies grown onto a portable scaffold. It will pulse and change color according to safe chemical corridors.
- A vibro-seismograph tuned to the exact 43-second pump cycle. Deviations in rhythm tell you when something enormous is moving nearby.
- No drones. They are eaten or converted into new smokestacks within hours. The only permitted technology is what can be grown, rusted, or incorporated.

### 5. Behavioral Rules – How to Move Inside the Stomach
- Never stand completely still for more than four minutes. The Shelf interprets motionless biomass as either dead or ready to be colonized. Keep a slow, constant swaying motion even when resting.
- Walk like you are part of a herd. Erratic solo movement triggers the siege-predators that wait weeks inside hollow trees.
- When you feel the ground give one long tectonic sigh of recognition, answer it. Exhale in the same rhythm. This is not metaphor — it is primate diplomacy with a continental liver.
- If you find an intact piece of old machinery (a valve stack, locomotive wheel, etc.), bow. The Shelf remembers its wounds and respects those who acknowledge the scar tissue.
- Do not try to “clear” paths. Cutting vegetation is like performing amateur surgery on your own host. The jungle will close the wound by closing *you*.

### 6. Exit Strategy (Optimistic)
Plan for three different exit timelines: the one you tell your team (2 weeks), the one you actually expect (6–8 weeks), and the one the Shelf has in mind (never).  
Cache self-locomoting escape pods made of the same braided lianas the jungle uses — living vessels that will carry you to the edge on a 43-second pump rhythm. They will try to keep you, too. Everything here tries to keep you.

### Final Warning
Pack light on ego, heavy on humility.  
Bring no fires — the Shelf already runs on geothermal and ancient reactors.  
Bring no loud weapons — the sound spectrum is already full; anything new will be investigated by things that digest their own muscles while waiting.

You are not a visitor.  
You are a temporary organelle.

Dress accordingly.  
Breathe accordingly.  
And when the warm black water begins running across the inside of your eyelids in perfect metronomic strings, try to feel honored.

The Shelf has been waiting a very long time for someone new to digest gently, industrially, and with enormous patience.
