**How to Prepare for the Throatwell**

You do not “visit” the Throatwell. You consent to being slowly digested by a place that is still deciding whether it is allowed to exist. An ordinary human multiverse traveler who is foolish, curious, or broken enough to seek the seam must prepare less like an explorer and more like someone choosing to be interred inside a living cathedral that has not finished growing. Here is what your body, mind, and continuity will actually need.

### 1. Physical Preparation (the body you will mostly abandon)
- **Thermal equilibrium**: The water is cool but not cold—roughly 4 °C, the temperature of maximum density. Bring nothing that relies on trapped air for warmth; neoprene or any closed-cell foam will compress into uselessness within minutes. Instead, train your body for prolonged exposure to viscous, high-pressure cold. Weeks of ice baths and deliberate cold shock training are mandatory. Your blood needs to learn how to stay polite when oxygen becomes a luxury.
- **Pressure acclimation**: The density is not uniform. You will pass through lenses where the water weighs as much as mercury and pockets where it is almost weightless. Spend time in hyperbaric chambers and variable-gravity simulators if you can find them. More practically, learn to freedive extremely deep so your nervous system stops screaming every time your ribs creak.
- **Biological humility**: Nothing that eats, breathes, or breeds quickly survives here. Leave your microbiome behind. Fast for at least ten days beforehand so your gut is quiet. Do not bring skin bacteria that think they have rights. The microscopic violet life will catalog you the instant you cross the seam; the less of “you” there is to catalog, the less of you will be rewritten.
- **Zero equipment that shines, beeps, or decays**: Any light stronger than a single candle will be treated as an insult and slowly overgrown. Electronics die instantly. Metal rusts into kelp-colored lace within weeks. The only acceptable technology is an obsidian blade (mirroring the one already present) and perhaps a single glass vial of air from your home reality, stoppered with black wax. That is all.

### 2. Psychological & Perceptual Preparation (the mind that will stretch)
- **Temporal surrender**: You must meditate daily on the experience of centuries passing while you remain still. The Throatwell does not steal time; it offers you the chance to become irrelevant to it. Practice staying conscious while a clock runs backward, or while an entire afternoon is compressed into three heartbeats. Learn to feel nostalgia for moments that have not happened yet.
- **Ego atrophy**: Most who remain become architecture. Train yourself to feel peaceful when you imagine your thighbone becoming a glassy rib, your hair becoming copper kelp, your last thought becoming a sub-audible click that only the eels will ever hear. If the idea still horrifies you, do not go. The water will know.
- **Sensory recalibration**: Spend long hours in absolute darkness and absolute silence. Then spend hours in overwhelming density—float in thick brine, in silt, in rooms packed floor to ceiling with wet rope. You need to teach your nervous system that “being touched on every square centimeter at once” can be a neutral state rather than panic.
- **Reality tolerance**: The deeper zones have leaking concepts. You may smell your childhood home, hear your mother singing in a voice she lost forty years before you were born, taste snow that has never melted. Practice remaining uncurious about these intrusions. Curiosity is a hook. The Throatwell uses it to reel you in molecule by molecule.

### 3. Metaphysical & Narrative Preparation (the story you will stop needing)
- **Choose your leaving-name**: Everything that enters eventually forgets its original name. Decide now what single syllable or image you are willing to keep. Write it on a tiny slip of paper, seal it in black wax, and swallow it before you cross the seam. It will become the last hard thing inside you.
- **Carry one true thing**: The knife already present proves that human-made objects can persist if they are honest enough. Bring something that is perfectly, painfully itself. A wedding ring you can no longer wear. A bullet that missed. A child’s drawing that makes you cry for reasons you no longer understand. The Throatwell respects objects that have already been broken by time.
- **Accept the eel**: There is only one major moving creature. When it eventually passes (once every few centuries of your subjective time), you must not reach out. You must simply let its blind, cathedral-sized body scrape past you like continents drifting. Touching it is considered impolite by the kelp. The chemical sigh it leaves is addictive.

### 4. Practical Entry & Conduct
- Find the seam by not looking for it. The Throatwell is found in peripheral blindness—in the rotten log you almost stepped over, the stone you leaned against while thinking of suicide, the sudden ache behind your sternum at 3 a.m. When the hairline fracture appears, do not hesitate. Hesitation becomes permanent hesitation here.
- Once inside, move like the kelp: one deliberate molecule at a time. Any haste is punished by sharper glass and tighter tunnels.
- If you begin to feel yourself becoming architecture, that is not necessarily failure. Some of the most beautiful blades of new glass were once people who finally stopped needing to be separate.
- To leave (if you still want to), you must become so light with forgetting that the water itself rejects you. This is rarer than staying. Most who exit do so as snowflakes, or half-remembered laughter, or the smell of hot pine needles that briefly troubles some other traveler’s dream.

### Final Warning
The Throatwell is not evil. It is young, embarrassed, and infinitely patient. It does not want your death. It wants your completion.

Pack light.  
Bring only what you are willing to watch slowly turn into velvet, glass, and almost-sound.

And remember:  
once the seam closes behind you, the concept “outside” will begin to feel like a rumor told by something that is already becoming kelp.

Welcome to the beginning of the world.  
It has only just started, and it is already very, very late.
