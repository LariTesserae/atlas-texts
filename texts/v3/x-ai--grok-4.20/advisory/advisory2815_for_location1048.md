**Preparation Guide for the Suspended Embryonic Sea-Sky**

You are not visiting a finished world. You are stepping into a place that is still dreaming itself into existence. This is not a destination; it is a slow, half-conscious act of creation, and you must prepare your body, mind, and expectations accordingly.

### 1. Mental and Temporal Reorientation (Most Important)
- Accept that your visit will feel like moving through someone else’s extremely long, extremely patient dream. Time here is biological-geological. A “short walk” might last six hours. A conversation might pause for twenty minutes while everyone waits for a current to shift.  
- Train yourself to speak only in near-whispers for at least a week before arrival. Loud voices can literally destabilize the living mats. Practice moving and thinking at half speed.  
- Let go of any agenda. This place does not respect schedules, urgency, or the concept of “seeing everything.” You will see what the world is ready to show you. Nothing more.

### 2. Physical and Medical Preparation
- **Swim conditioning**: You must be exceptionally comfortable in lukewarm, thick, murky water for many hours at a time. Train in a pool with added salt and xanthan gum to approximate the syrupy density. Practice neutral buoyancy techniques; there is no true “surface” or “floor.”
- **Symbiont acceptance**: The local humans are covered in photosynthetic algae. Your skin will almost certainly begin hosting similar organisms within 48 hours. Do not fight this. Sterilizing yourself beforehand is both impossible and rude. Bring no antifungal or antibacterial products.
- **Pressure tolerance**: The water column is hundreds of kilometers deep in places. Even though you will stay in the warm upper “stratosphere-sea,” you must be able to handle gradual pressure changes and the occasional sudden upward or sideways current.
- **Vision**: Light is permanently dim, blue-green, and diffuse. Train your eyes in very low light for weeks. Bring nothing that produces bright light (no powerful lamps, no flashes). Even a strong headlamp could panic the living architecture.
- **Temperature**: It is never cold, but never truly warm either. A perpetual lukewarm 28–31 °C (82–88 °F). You will feel neither hot nor cold, only heavy and wet forever. Pack nothing “dry.”

### 3. Gear — Extremely Minimal and Living
Pack only what can be grown, woven, or absorbed:

- A set of living garments made from the same buoyant root-fiber the locals use. Synthetic fabrics will be rejected by the mats and may destabilize them.
- A bone knife and a small toolkit of shell hooks and resin glue. Metal is not only absent but actively harmful; it poisons the embryonic continents.
- Several long braided ropes grown from your host mat’s own rootlets (these will be given to you upon arrival). They are alive and will slowly fuse with whatever you tie them to.
- A breathing symbiote hood (a delicate, net-like membrane grown from local jelly-creatures). It filters the iron-rich water and lets you remain submerged for many hours without surfacing.
- No camera, no recorder, no notebook. The place does not want to be documented. Observation here is participatory. Write or draw only with dyes made from the local algae on sheets of living leaf-canvas.

Leave behind: fire, electricity, anything that produces continuous sound above 30 decibels, perfumes, sunscreen, and above all, any attitude of conquest or extraction.

### 4. Social and Ritual Preparation
- Learn the “Midwife’s Greeting”: a slow exhalation underwater while exposing your palms and letting local water flow across the symbiotic algae on your wrists. This identifies you as a temporary guest rather than a foreign body.
- Practice extreme patience. The locals measure conversation in lifetimes. A question you ask on day one may be answered on day nine with a single whispered sentence.
- Understand that you are a visitor inside an organism that has not yet decided if it is an ocean, a continent, a city, or a single creature. Politeness here means never forcing anything to finish forming before it is ready.

### 5. What You Will Experience (and How to Survive It Emotionally)
- The perpetual feeling of being inside a womb the size of a planet. Some people find this profoundly peaceful. Others experience crushing existential claustrophobia after about ten days. Know which one you are.
- The constant soft creaking of living architecture and the faint sound of water falling in impossible directions can become hypnotic. Do not fight the hypnosis.
- You will witness structures that were once people, or will one day become people, or are both at once. Do not ask for clarification. The distinction is not yet meaningful here.
- Occasionally the entire world will shift upward by a few centimeters as something vast and slow is born far below. This is normal. Breathe through it.

### 6. Exit Strategy
There is no clean exit. The world will eventually decide you have seen enough and will gently guide you toward one of the rare “membrane thresholds” — places where the sea-sky thins enough for transition back into ordinary spacetime. You cannot force this. Trying to leave before the world is ready to release you is considered extremely poor manners and may result in you being gently incorporated into the scaffolding of a new district.

### Final Warning
This is not a place that completes you.  
It is a place that is still completing itself, and it may use parts of your attention, your memories, or even small pieces of your biology in that process.  
You will not leave unchanged.  
You may leave half-born.

Pack lightly.  
Move slowly.  
Speak softly.  
And above all, remember:

**You are not a tourist.**  
You are a temporary midwife to a world that is still deciding what it wants to become.

When you feel something vast rising beneath you — whether new coral, new stone, or new thought — do not panic.  
Simply place your hand on the nearest living stalk, exhale, and help it decide.
