**Preparation Guide for the Hanging Valleys of Veyr**

You are about to step into a place that is not broken, not cursed, and not dying — it is simply *paused*. The universe took one slow breath here several hundred years ago and has refused, ever since, to let it out. Visiting Veyr means agreeing, temporarily, to be paused with it. Most travelers find the experience peaceful. A few find it quietly annihilating. Prepare accordingly.

### 1. Mental & Psychological Preparation (Most Important)
The Hanging Valleys will test your relationship with time, memory, and identity more than any other location in the multiverse.

- **Accept repetition.** You will hear the same three wind notes. You will see the same Alignment happen at the exact same angles of the day. The mind that fights this begins to fray. The mind that surrenders to it discovers an almost narcotic calm.
- **Decide who you are before you arrive.** In Veyr, people do not visibly age and new arrivals often stop aging as well. More dangerously, some travelers report “retrospective smoothing” — they slowly lose the ability to remember *when* they were different. Write down your age, the date you left your home reality, the names of people you love, and three memories that are painful. Keep this note on your person at all times. Read it every time you feel the urge to say “I think I was younger once.”
- **Prepare for the Alignment.** Three times per day the entire valley — every living thing — will face the same cardinal direction for exactly seven heartbeats. Fighting the Alignment (trying to look away, speak, or move) produces a feeling best described as “being unstrung like a violin.” Do not resist it. Face the direction. Become part of the tuning fork.

### 2. Physical Equipment & Clothing
The climate is perpetually cool, damp, and between 4–11 °C (39–52 °F). It feels like late autumn that never becomes winter.

**Clothing (layer aggressively):**
- Base layer: tightly woven wool or modern synthetic that wicks moisture. The mist *gets into everything*.
- Mid layer: thick felted wool or boiled leather. The locals wear garments that are literally centuries old; they never wear out.
- Outer layer: oilskin or treated basalt-fiber cloak. The condensation beads and rolls off these in perfect spheres — very useful.
- Footwear: hobnailed boots with deep treads. The basalt is slick with perpetual moisture and the living fern-root bridges can be treacherous.
- Head covering: wide-brimmed felt hat or hood. The mist collects on hair and drips down your neck for hours if you don’t.

**Gear:**
- A small, extremely loud bell or chime. The local martens are curious but respectful of sharp, high sounds.
- Ear protection that can be removed easily. The sub-audible ringing of the plateau can, after 10–14 days, cause auditory hallucinations or an irresistible desire to lie down and never stand up again.
- A reliable timepiece that is *not* a watch (watches tend to freeze at the same three moments as the Alignment). A small hourglass filled with something heavier than sand (mercury or ground obsidian) works best.
- A notebook with waterproof pages and indelible ink. Memory is the first thing the valley tries to edit.
- Dried or preserved food for at least 21 days beyond your planned stay. Local food is nutritious but tastes... *static*. Many travelers report that everything tastes like it was cooked three hundred years ago and kept warm.

### 3. Medical & Physiological Preparation
- **The No-Decay Effect:** Wounds will not fester, but they also will not heal while you remain in Veyr. Set any broken bones *before* you arrive. Bring rapid-healing salves, sutures, and clotting agents if you expect violence.
- **Fertility and Biology:** Conception is possible but the resulting child will be born with an adult face and will never age further. Most travelers take extremely rigorous precautions.
- **Sleep:** You will not need less sleep, but you will *want* less. The constant ringing and the Alignment make ordinary REM cycles feel like cheating. Melatonin analogues or strong herbal sedatives are recommended for the first week.
- **Addiction risk:** The profound stillness is addictive. Some travelers have to be physically dragged out after their third visit. Set a hard departure date and have a trusted companion waiting on the other side with a loud noise and bright lights.

### 4. Social & Cultural Preparation
- Learn the three wind notes. The locals respond with genuine warmth if you can whistle them back at the correct intervals.
- Never ask “How long have you lived here?” They do not experience the question correctly.
- When someone pauses and whispers “I think I was younger once,” simply answer: “The mist is heavy today.” This is the locally accepted way of saying “I acknowledge your existential dread and will not make it worse.”
- Trade goods that are *deliberately impermanent*: cut flowers, fresh bread that will go stale, ink that fades, candles that burn down. These are considered miraculous and sacred. Permanent things (steel tools, gemstones, plastic) are treated with polite suspicion.

### 5. Exit Strategy
The Hanging Valleys do not want you to leave. The longer you stay, the more the roads seem to curve back toward the same six settlements. The rope bridges grow longer. The new buildings that appear overnight will start trying to look like places you would want to live.

Recommended techniques for leaving:
- Carry a timepiece that is deliberately *wrong*. A clock set to a different universe’s time can act as an anchor.
- Have someone waiting at the basalt lip who will ring an enormous bell the moment they see you. The shock of real change is often enough to break the plateau’s grip.
- The moment you feel genuine homesickness or grief — *lean into it*. Strong negative emotion tied to change is the most reliable exit key. The valley cannot hold sorrow that wants to become something else.

### Final Warning
The Hanging Valleys of Veyr are not evil. They are not even particularly dangerous by multiversal standards.

They are simply a place that has mistaken stillness for perfection, and they are very, very good at teaching visitors to make the same mistake.

Bring something noisy.
Bring something that decays.
Bring the version of yourself that still knows how to end. 

You will need all three to come home unchanged. Most people don’t.
