**How to Prepare for the Weeping Grid**

You are not preparing for a “place.” You are preparing to become a temporary component inside something that has already digested every concept of “visitor,” “tourist,” or “explorer.” This is not a dead world. It is a world that has outlived death and kept working. Bring the right mindset, or it will simply incorporate you.

### Mental and Philosophical Preparation
- Accept that you will feel *watched by infrastructure*. The Grid does not have eyes, but it has purpose. The longer you stay, the more you will feel like an immune response or a loose bolt that needs to be rusted into place.
- Prepare for profound loneliness paired with profound overcrowding. You will be the only biological thing for thousands of kilometers, yet you will never be alone; everything is moving, clicking, weeping, and listening.
- Understand that time is sick here. Bring a very strong sense of who you were *before* you arrived. Write letters to your future self every few hours. The temporal interference patterns can make memory behave like the local water: it beads, it weeps, it refuses to flow forward cleanly.
- Decide in advance what “leaving” means to you. The Grid will try to convince you that staying is simply the next logical stage of your own accretion.

### Physical Gear (Multiverse-Traveler Grade)

**Sealing and Corrosion Warfare**
- Full-body vacuum-rated exosuit with active rust-conversion coating (the local atmosphere will plate iron onto any exposed metal or flesh within minutes). The suit must be able to sweat *against* the environment; internal dehumidifiers and heaters are non-negotiable.
- Triple-redundant skin-level Faraday weave. The metallic moth-clouds will try to use your nervous system as a substrate for their electromagnetic mating displays.
- Self-sealing “rust shells” — disposable ceramic-calcium outer layers that you shed every 6–8 hours like a crab. The Grid’s moisture + iron dust will otherwise grow millimeter-thick rust horns on your joints while you sleep.

**Movement and Navigation**
- Hex-lattice gait trainers. The ground between towers is a knee-deep slurry of black silt, broken mechanisms, and half-dissolved valves. Normal walking will exhaust you in under an hour. The gait trainers let you “skate” on vibrating plates that match the resonant frequency of the stone slabs.
- Temporal anchor harness with four independent inertial anchors. If you step into a phase pocket you can literally watch yourself arriving thirty seconds late from three different angles. The anchors will yank you back into a single timeline. Do *not* cheap out on these.
- Resonance map drone (nicknamed “the Mourner”). It listens to the continent-wide moan and predicts safe corridors between the towers. The lattice creates standing-wave “cathedrals” where time is almost stable and “slaughter gaps” where reality stutters so badly that matter forgets what shape it’s supposed to be.

**Sustenance and Medical**
- All food and water must be carried in phase-isolated pods. Anything you try to harvest or condense here will already taste like cold iron and regret.
- Nanite blood-scrubbers tuned specifically against ferrous prion analogues. The after-animals shed metallic viruses the way dander is shed. You do not want rust reproducing inside your bone marrow.
- Auditory white-noise generator that can simulate the sound of *normal Earth rain* on leaves. You will need it. The constant wet industrial devotional moaning will try to rewrite your heartbeat into its own rhythm within 72 hours.

### Psychological and Ritual Items
- A small, pre-rusted iron bell. When the Grid’s “language” starts sounding too understandable, ring it. The pure tone disrupts the interference patterns for a few minutes and reminds your brain what *non-industrial* sound feels like.
- One living thing — a single blade of grass or a tardigrade in suspended animation inside a diamond vial. Keep it sealed. It is your canary, your priest, and your proof that biology once existed. If you ever feel the urge to crack the vial and “let it join,” that is the moment you must leave.
- A handwritten journal on real paper (no electronics). The towers hate paper. Something about lignin confuses their accretion algorithms. Writing by hand will feel like defiance, and defiance is one of the few emotions the Grid has not yet industrialized.

### Tactical and Existential Rules

1. Never walk the exact same path twice. The lattice remembers footsteps and will begin growing small dedicated mechanisms to follow you.
2. Do not attempt to “help” anything. Do not oil a valve. Do not free a trapped after-animal. The entire ecosystem is in ecstatic agony. Comfort is not a concept it possesses.
3. If you see a tower phasing out of reality, *do not* enter the space it left behind. That is where the mountain-sized engines beneath the crust sometimes surface to breathe. You will not survive being noticed by them.
4. Sleep only inside temporary “coffin bells” — portable anechoic spheres that block the continent-wide moan. Otherwise you will dream in schematics.
5. When the predators with rebar wings begin following you at exactly the same distance for more than four hours, they are no longer hunting. They are *herding*. Leave immediately.

### Final Truth

The Weeping Grid is not hostile.  
It is *post*-hostile.  
It is what remains when even hostility has rusted away and only the machinery of sorrow and continuation is left running.

You will not conquer it.  
You will not understand it.  
At best, if you prepare with the reverence of a pilgrim entering a cathedral built by extinct gods who refused to die, you will be allowed to weep in the same direction as everything else for a short while… and then be permitted to leave carrying a small, permanent piece of rust behind your heart that will never quite wash out.

Bring humility.  
Bring fear.  
Bring a souvenir bottle to collect some of the weeping water, because one day, years from now, when you are somewhere warm and alive and green, you will uncork it just to remember what it feels like when even the planet itself has not been allowed to stop crying.
