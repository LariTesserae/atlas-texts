**How to Prepare for the Hollow Basilica**

You are not preparing for a city. You are preparing to become a temporary organ inside a single, continent-sized fossil that is still metabolizing heat. Think of yourself as a red blood cell that must survive for a while inside a body whose immune system is made of screaming, heat-drinking coral-trees. The following is not a packing list. It is a reconfiguration of your own body and mind.

### 1. Thermal & Chemical Reconfiguration
- You will live at temperatures that routinely exceed 55–68 °C (131–154 °F) in the open plaza. Your core temperature must be allowed to ride higher than you have ever experienced. Train for this in advance: repeated 50–60 °C sauna sessions, ideally combined with infrared lamps. Teach your body that “uncomfortable” is not the same as “dangerous.”
- The air is aggressively oxidizing and bone-dry. Nasal passages, corneas, and lung linings will desiccate in minutes without protection. Pack multiple layers of saline-gel eye drops, a full-face respirator that can accept humidifying cartridges, and thick lanolin or tallow-based balm for every exposed centimeter of skin. Reapply constantly. Your lips will still crack.
- The floor radiates like a kiln. You will need boots with thick, multi-layered ceramic-fiber soles and reflective insoles. Ordinary leather or rubber will cook your feet in under an hour. Consider open-weave rope sandals made from the same living wood the locals use if you plan to stay longer than a week; they breathe and slowly char instead of melting.

### 2. Visual & Light Adaptation
- The ambient light is permanent twilight filtered through amber and rose dust. Your pupils will stay dilated for the entire visit. Bring the darkest sunglasses you own for the first 48 hours simply to give your retinas a rest, then remove them forever. Night-vision or thermal goggles are useless; the entire environment is a thermal bloom.
- Carry a small, dimmable red LED headlamp. White light feels like violence here. The locals will flinch. Use only the lowest red setting when you absolutely must read or treat an injury.

### 3. Auditory & Linguistic Preparation
- The Basilica never stops making sound. You will be living inside a 2 km stone lung that creaks, booms, hisses, cracks, and tears silk twenty-four hours a day. Earplugs are forbidden; you must learn to speak and listen *through* the noise. 
- Practice speaking in a low, perfectly level monotone that projects without rising in pitch. Record yourself and test whether your voice can be understood over loud fan noise, cracking wood, and tearing paper. This is the only register the locals respect.
- Learn the “low-register hum” that precedes a vegetative surge. If you hear the plants drop half an octave and begin a slow, submarine-like thrum, you have less than two hours before that quadrant of the city is swallowed in green fire. Move immediately when you hear it.

### 4. Botanical & Ecological Armor
- The vegetation is not hostile, but it is *indifferent* in the way a reef is indifferent to your fingers. It will grow through you if you stand still long enough. 
- Wear loose, bone-white or pale ochre clothing that matches the mineral exactly. Tight clothing traps heat and gives the plants purchase. Long, flowing robes or wraps are best. The locals will still see you, but the plants will register you as “part of the floor.”
- Bring a bronze pruning hook and a short obsidian blade. Not for defense. For surgery. You will need to cut yourself free of thickets that decide to embrace you while you sleep. The woody stems are incredibly tough; bronze saws work best.
- A fine-mesh silk or living-woodfiber scarf is mandatory. The spore detonations are apocalyptic. When an entire neighborhood decides to fruit at once, the air becomes a blizzard of metallic-violet needles. Inhale enough and your lungs will bloom.

### 5. Hydration & Metabolic Strategy
- There is no free water. The plants do not transpire; they sequester. You must bring your own or trade for it. The locals distill minute amounts from deep thermal condensers inside the oldest pillars. It tastes of iron and pollen and is worth more than gold.
- Pack electrolyte tablets, salt, and high-fat preserved foods (lard, dried meat, nut butters). Your body will burn calories at a terrifying rate simply staying cool. Expect to lose 2–3 kg of water and fat per day until you acclimate.
- Sleep inside the oldest, slowest thickets. The temperature inside their hollow cores drops by almost 8 °C and the constant creaking acts as white noise. Never sleep in open plazas; the radiant heat will cook you from below.

### 6. Psychological & Cultural Alignment
- This is a place with no animals, no birds, no soft edges. The only rhythms are mineral expansion, vegetative detonation, and human monotone. If you crave companionship, variety, or silence, you will break.
- Bring no music, no recordings, no devices that produce novelty. They will feel obscene. The Basilica is already playing the longest, loudest, most patient song in existence. Your job is to become one more repeating motif inside it.
- Prepare for the “disappearing” effect. After ten days your mind will begin to edit out the constant vegetal roar. You will start to speak in the local monotone without noticing. Your movements will slow until you can stand perfectly still for twenty minutes and simply *become* another pale column the plants ignore. This is not meditation. This is successful camouflage.
- Decide in advance what question you came to ask the Basilica. It does not give answers, but it does give balance. Most travelers leave either perfectly calm or perfectly mad. There is no middle path.

### 7. Exit Strategy
- The entrance only appears to those who are “meant to.” This is not poetry. The fossil-organism itself chooses. Carry no map. Instead, carry a single living cutting from one of the small emerald-violet thickets in a clay pot. If it begins to wither and turn bone-white, the Basilica is rejecting you. Follow it out immediately. Do not linger.

Final truth:  
You are not visiting a ruin or even a city. You are visiting an immune response. The Hollow Basilica is the slow, hot heartbeat of something that died before humanity existed and has been dreaming in green and red stone ever since.

Dress like bone.  
Speak like wind over hot iron.  
Move like a leaf that has learned manners.

And never, ever expect it to be quiet. Silence here is a sin, and the Basilica is devout.
