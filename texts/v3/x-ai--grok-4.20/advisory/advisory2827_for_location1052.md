**How to Prepare for the Hollow Basilica of Perpetual Condensation**

Pack as if you are visiting an abandoned mega-cathedral that has spent the last four hundred years turning itself into a jungle and a machine at the same time. The building does not want visitors; it simply tolerates them with geological indifference. Your job is to survive that tolerance without becoming another layer of rust or moss.

### Physical Protection
- **Clothing**: Full-coverage, breathable synthetic layers that can be washed and dried easily. The air is perpetually 28–34 °C with 95–100 % humidity. Cotton rots in days. Choose quick-dry trekking clothes in drab greens, rust browns, and dark brass tones so you don’t stand out against the chrome beetles or oxidized copper ferns. Long sleeves and high collars are mandatory; the condensation falls in heavy, warm drops that feel strangely oily. A wide-brimmed waterproof hat or helmet liner is essential; the droplets hit with surprising weight.
- **Footwear**: Steel-toed, chemically resistant boots with aggressive tread and ankle support. The grilled iron floor is treacherous—slick with condensation, algae, and occasional oil film. The rims of the black mirror-pools are slippery beyond reason. Bring ankle gaiters; the pale tube-lizards sometimes mistake boot laces for convenient pipes.
- **Outer layer**: A lightweight, hooded poncho or rain shell treated with anti-mold and antimicrobial coating. You will be continuously damp. Plan to discard or aggressively disinfect it after the visit.

### Respiratory and Sensory Management
- **Mask**: A half-face respirator with replaceable organic vapor/acid gas cartridges and a P100 filter. The air tastes of warm iron, ozone, and faint mineral rot. After six hours the metallic tang sits at the back of your throat like a coin. The subsonic gearbox rumble will give you a permanent headache if you don’t dampen it.
- **Ear protection**: Custom-molded musician’s earplugs combined with over-ear noise-isolating headphones or a construction earmuff. The soundscape is hypnotic and slowly corrosive. The twelve-minute fan blades produce a chord that feels like it is tuning your skeleton. Many travelers report hearing phantom cathedral bells for weeks afterward.
- **Goggles or sealed glasses**: Anti-fog, impact-resistant. The steam sheets drift at eye level and the dripping is relentless. You will be hit in the face by fist-sized drops from 80 meters up.

### Hydration, Nutrition, and Medicine
- Water: Bring 6–8 liters in metal bottles. The condensation pools look pristine but are biologically and chemically complex. Treat any local water you collect with a high-end portable purifier *and* iodine tablets as backup. The building’s water is slightly alkaline and carries dissolved iron and calcium.
- Food: High-calorie, low-residue bars, nuts, and dried fruit in vacuum-sealed packs. Mold grows on anything porous within hours. Avoid anything that crumbs; the chrome beetles are drawn to organic debris.
- Medications: Broad-spectrum antifungal cream and powder (this place will try to grow on you). Strong anti-nausea medication (the never-ending slow rotation of gears can induce a subtle motion sickness). Headache tablets and electrolyte tabs are non-negotiable. Bring a nasal saline spray; the air will dry your sinuses even while your skin never stops dripping.

### Tools and Navigation
- Strong headlamp and multiple backup chemical light sticks. The brass-colored industrial light is sourceless and uneven; entire districts lie in deep shadow between hanging gardens.
- 100–150 meters of thin, high-tensile climbing rope and rappel gear. Some of the helical pipelines have sagged into ramps and balconies, but others have collapsed. The iron grates have rusted in places; a fall into one of the perfectly circular pools is not advised. No one knows how deep they are.
- Multi-spectrum camera or phone with excellent low-light capability. The reflective beetles and pale moths photograph poorly without adjustment.
- A small, powerful Geiger counter or dosimeter. Some of the deeper ducts still emit trace radiation from whatever the original atmospheric processors used.
- Machete or heavy pruning saw. The vegetation is lush but slow. You will mostly be cutting through calcium-armored vines that have the texture and density of old bone.

### Psychological and Temporal Preparation
- This is not a place you “dash through.” plan on 36–72 hours minimum if you want to reach the central naves. The building’s rhythm is measured in hours and centuries. Trying to hurry here causes mistakes.
- Bring a notebook or voice recorder. The place slowly teaches you its language of drips and distant hisses. Travelers often report the sensation that something enormous is about to speak, then never does. Write it down. The act of documentation seems to keep the mind from sliding into the building’s geological patience.
- Tell someone on the outside you will be unreachable for at least a week. Comms die here; the constant EMF from the slow-turning machinery and the sheer metal mass create dead zones the size of small countries.

### Ritual Items (optional but strangely effective)
- A small container of machine oil or rust dissolver as offering. Many experienced travelers leave a few drops on the largest visible gearbox or pressure valve. The building seems to notice.
- A single non-digital timepiece (mechanical watch). Digital electronics age strangely here; batteries swell and screens bloom with corrosion in under a day. A wind-up watch keeps better time than you expect.

### Final Warning
Nothing here will try to kill you quickly. It will instead invite you to stay until your sense of urgency dissolves into the dripping. The greatest danger is deciding that the slow orchestra of gears, the warm rain, the reflective beetles, and the never-finished gestures are perfectly reasonable, and that leaving would be the real madness.

Pack light enough to move, heavy enough to endure, and disciplined enough to leave when your pre-set timer says so—even if every dripping surface is whispering that the next pressure release will finally resolve into language.

The Basilica has been waiting a very long time. It is in no hurry.  
You must be.
