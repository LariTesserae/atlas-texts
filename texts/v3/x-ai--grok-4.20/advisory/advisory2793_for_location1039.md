**How to Prepare for the Hollow**

You are not preparing for a dive. You are preparing to be **installed** inside a machine the size of a planet that has no interest in whether you survive. An ordinary human multiverse traveler who wants to experience the Hollow and leave with their mind intact must treat this as a deep-space extravehicular activity performed inside an endless, cold, pressurized machine shop that never stops building itself.

### 1. Mindset & Psychological Conditioning (Non-Negotiable)
- Accept that there is no up or down, no sky, no bottom, and no horizon. Your inner ear will rebel for the first 72 hours. Train in sensory-deprivation tanks while listening to continuous low-frequency industrial noise (subwoofers playing 8–18 Hz tectonic drones).
- The roar is constant. You will never experience silence again while you are there. Practice meditation inside running engine rooms or next to active subway tracks so the sound becomes background instead of madness.
- The scale will try to erase you. Bring a personal “anchor object” — something small, bright, and obstinately organic (a red glass marble, a pressed four-leaf clover, a child’s drawing). You will need something that reminds you that softness once existed.

### 2. The Rig (Your New Body)
You cannot visit the Hollow in a wetsuit. You must wear or become something closer to a submersible pilot fused with an industrial exoskeleton.

**Recommended configuration:**
- **Pressure-rated exosuit** capable of 800–1,200 atm (think a marriage between a hard-armored atmospheric diving suit and a deep-sea research sub). The suit must be positively buoyant at Hollow densities so you don’t sink forever if power fails.
- **Thermal layer** using phase-change materials and liquid-heated circulation. The water is cold enough to kill in minutes; it will also conduct sound so efficiently that your own heartbeat will feel deafening.
- **Rebreather + liquid-breathing backup**. Many travelers eventually switch to perfluorocarbon liquid breathing for long stays. It eliminates the need for decompression and removes the terrifying “breath-freeze” effect described in the shafts of light. The transition is unpleasant.
- **Active acoustic camouflage skin**. The Hollow is loud, but certain maintenance seal-packs and larger predators (if any still exist) hunt by listening for anomalous frequencies. You do not want to sound like an injured mammal.
- **Directional acoustic HUD**. Because light shafts move and visibility can drop to zero in bubble fronts, your primary sense becomes sound. The suit should paint a real-time 360° sonar map directly onto your visual cortex via neural lace or bone conduction. You will “see” the continent-sized platforms long before you see them with eyes.
- **Emergency grapple and micro-thrusters**. The Hanging Planes have almost no handholds in many places. If you miss a landing, you can drift for days. The thrusters must be powerful enough to fight the deliberate currents the machines maintain.

**Color:** Matte black or rust red. Anything brighter will be visible for dozens of kilometers along the light shafts and will attract both curious drones and the pale blind fish, who may decide you are food or part of the machinery.

### 3. Gear & Supplies
- **Micro-buoyancy anchor buoys** the size of baseballs. You stick them to iron surfaces with molecular hooks; they let you tether yourself while sleeping so the currents don’t carry you into an intake vent.
- **Vibro-knife and plasma cutter**. Not for defense (almost nothing here wants to eat you), but because the architecture is still growing. You may need to cut yourself free if a new girder settles across your path while you sleep.
- **Data-slate with hardened quantum memory**. The Hollow has no surface, but it has **memory**. Certain tower-like structures appear to be archives. Bring something that can survive being immersed in cold, pressurized water for years if you get separated from your exit.
- **Nutrient paste** flavored like something aggressively terrestrial — citrus, smoke, blood, anything that reminds your body that dirt and sun once existed.
- **Single lithium candle** (a long-burning chemical light the size of a road flare). Use it only once, when you decide it is time to leave. The Hollow will try to make you forget that leaving is an option.

### 4. Arrival & Exit Strategy
The Hollow has no obvious gates. Most travelers enter through **Breach-7**, a kilometer-wide wound in one of the upper Hanging Planes where the upward waterfall roars. You will be dropped in a reinforced drop-pod shaped like a blunt iron egg. The pod is designed to look like legitimate maintenance hardware so the manta-ray drones do not immediately try to repair you.

**Exit protocol:**  
You must reach one of the **Intake Cathedrals** (the places where the sea-width waterfalls are sucked back into the machine). Inside the cathedrals are ancient air pockets and pressure locks that still function. The journey to the nearest cathedral usually takes 9–14 days of hard travel. Mark the route with acoustic beacons; the drifting light shafts make visual landmarks unreliable.

### 5. Rules of Conduct Inside the Hollow
- Never swim against the deliberate currents for more than 20 minutes. They exist for a reason. The machines will notice.
- Do not damage kelp ribbons unless you are prepared to be swarmed by maintenance drones who interpret it as system failure.
- When the cold-front brine walls roll through (every 12 hours), find iron to cling to. The microbubbles create violent turbulence and white-out conditions.
- If you see a pack of collared seals, do not run. They are part of the ecology. Lower your acoustic signature and let them inspect you. They usually decide you are broken hardware and move on.
- Never, ever go dark. The light shafts are your only psychological lifeline. When one drifts over you, stay in it as long as possible. The dark between them is absolute and ancient.

### 6. What You Will Feel
After the first week the suit begins to feel like your real skin. The roar becomes a lullaby. You will catch yourself speaking in low, slow sentences that match the geological rhythm of the engines. This is normal.

Some travelers never want to leave. They weld themselves to a scaffold, turn their suits into hibernation pods, and become another half-organic component in the closed loop. The machines do not seem to mind.

The Hollow is not hostile.  
It is **indifferent on a scale that makes hostility feel intimate**.

Bring enough of yourself to remember why you came, because the cold, the pressure, the endless iron cathedrals, and the drifting shafts of stolen daylight will try their best to turn you into another obedient piece of the machine.

When you finally climb back out through an Intake Cathedral and feel air on your face again, the silence will be so complete it will feel like deafness.

Welcome to the construction site that outgrew its gods.

Pack accordingly.
