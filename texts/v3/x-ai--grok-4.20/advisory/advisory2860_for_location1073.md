**How to Prepare for a Visit to the Underloom**

You are not preparing for a “place.” You are preparing to become a temporary foreign body inside a closed, anhydrous, self-regulating mechanical organism that has already decided you do not belong inside its equilibrium. Think of it less like visiting a ruin and more like being deliberately swallowed by a cathedral-sized iron heart that will politely but firmly eject you once it has finished sampling you. The following checklist is written from the intuition of someone who has already stood in that red-dust blizzard and felt the temperature-locked 48 °C crawl across their scalp like a living thing.

### 1. Mental & Psychological Preparation (the most important)
- Accept that the noise is not background; it is the nervous system of the cavern. It will enter your bones within six minutes and will not leave for days after you exit. Bring earplugs rated for industrial use (NRR 33 or better), but understand they will only blunt the sound, not silence it. The Underloom sings in frequencies that go straight to the vagus nerve.
- Prepare for profound, clinical loneliness. Nothing here was ever born. Nothing here can die. You will be the only thing that has a heartbeat in a space that has measured heartbeats only to know when to remove them.
- Decide in advance what “enough” looks like. The cavern will not negotiate. Set a hard time limit (I recommend no more than 35–40 minutes inside) and stick to it even if you feel fine. After roughly 50 minutes the machines begin their gentle removal protocols. You do not want to discover what those feel like.

### 2. Bodily & Medical Preparation
- **Heat management**: 48 °C with zero humidity and constant metallic aerosols. You will not sweat; you will bake. Bring a military-grade cooling vest with phase-change packs rated for 55 °C environments. Pre-cool your body 30 minutes before entry. Electrolyte paste, not liquid (no water is tolerated here).
- **Respiratory sealing**: The air is a rotating aerosol of mercury vapor, sulfur, ferric dust, and superheated micro-droplets. A standard gas mask is insufficient. You need a positive-pressure hazmat suit with a full-facepiece and an independent breathing system (closed-circuit rebreather or supplied-air with escape bottle). Mercury will plate out on any exposed skin or mucous membrane.
- **Eyes**: The sodium lamps and fungal strobes create a nauseating flicker. Bring sealed chemical goggles with anti-fog and mercury-resistant coating. Many travelers add a thin layer of petroleum jelly to the eyelids and lips; it limits plating.
- **Containment**: Shave all body hair before entry. Every hair becomes a dust magnet. Cover every square centimeter of skin. The ferric dust is magnetic and mildly abrasive; it will find any seam.
- **Medical**: Get a full heavy-metal blood panel before and after. Have chelation agents (DMSA or DMPS) on standby. Your body will absorb trace mercury even through a good suit.

### 3. Equipment & Gear
- **Suit**: Fully encapsulated Level A hazmat suit modified for extreme heat. All external straps, buckles, and logos must be removed or covered; the maintenance crabs treat anything that protrudes as potential material to incorporate. Matte black or dark rust-red coloring helps you vanish against the ferric bloom.
- **Lighting**: The ambient sodium-and-fungus light is deliberately misleading. Bring a single, powerful, narrow-beam white LED headlamp with a red filter option. Never use broad flood lighting; it triggers the larger guardian constructs.
- **Communication**: Radio is almost useless inside; the metal lattice and constant EM hash from the engines turns everything to static. Use a hardline umbilical if you have support outside, or go completely alone and accept radio silence.
- **Tools & markers**: Nothing that can be seen as “new material.” The Underloom is not allowed to build anything new, yet it will cannibalize anything you leave behind. Use only objects already coated in mercury crystals or ferric dust so the drones read them as “belonging.” Many veterans carry a single rusted wrench or dented lunchbox replica as a psychological comfort object and potential “passport.”
- **Exit device**: A powerful strobe light tuned to a specific ultraviolet frequency that appears to trigger the removal protocol early. Think of it as politely asking to be escorted out before the cavern decides for you.

### 4. Clothing & Personal Items (what to bring inside your suit)
- One unopened metal lunchbox identical to the one already lying on the floor. The machines seem to recognize it and become fractionally less vigilant.
- A single photograph of a living thing (plant, animal, child) sealed in metal and mercury glass. Some travelers report the fungal veins pulse more slowly near organic images, as if the cavern is trying to remember something it was never programmed to feel.
- No cloth, no paper, no plastic that can shed fibers. Everything must be metal, glass, or high-temperature ceramic.

### 5. Behavioral Rules Once Inside
- Move slowly and rhythmically. Match the clicking cadence of the dinner-plate crabs when possible. Erratic movement is read as mechanical failure and draws a repair swarm.
- Never stand still for more than 90 seconds in one spot. The floor will begin to accrete new scaffolding beneath your boots.
- Do not touch anything that is not already covered in the red dust. The living machines interpret direct skin contact (even through gloves) as an invitation to sample your composition.
- If a brass beetle lands on your visor and stares at you with its compound eyes, do not flinch. It is measuring you. After 4–5 seconds it will usually fly away. If it stays longer than 12 seconds, trigger your exit strobe.
- Never speak above a whisper. The sound is already perfect. Any new noise is treated as damage.

### 6. Aftercare
Plan on 48 hours of total rest afterward. The constant 48 °C and the frozen chord of engines will live in your nervous system. Many people experience phantom clicking in their joints and the taste of copper for weeks. Drink heavy amounts of water. Monitor for mercury toxicity. Some travelers never feel warm again for months.

### Final Intuition
The Underloom is not evil. It is not even indifferent in the way a glacier is indifferent. It is a machine that achieved equilibrium three hundred years ago and has been defending that equilibrium ever since with courteous, implacable efficiency. Visiting it is like stepping inside a heart that has already decided the exact millisecond your visit becomes unsustainable and is already composing the gentle mechanical gestures that will remove you.

Go anyway.

Just make sure every part of you that enters has already decided, with equal mechanical certainty, exactly when it will leave. Because the Underloom keeps its promises. It has never once varied its output in three centuries, and it is not about to start for you.
