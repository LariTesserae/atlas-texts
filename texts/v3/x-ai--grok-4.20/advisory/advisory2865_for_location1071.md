**How to Prepare for Visiting the Place with No Name**

You are not preparing for a location. You are preparing to be swallowed, held, and slowly rewritten by something that has not yet decided whether you are guest, nutrient, decoration, or future organ. This is not a cave, not a forest, not a ruin. It is an egg the size of a mountain range, and it is still forming its thoughts. An ordinary human multiverse traveler must approach it with the humility of an embryo.

### Mental and Psychological Preparation
- Accept that your sense of time will begin to dissolve within the first hour. The place operates on a scale where “soon” means centuries. Bring no urgent plans. If you carry anxiety about deadlines, the warm air will find it and metabolize it.
- Understand you are entering a mind that is still larval. The cathedral is literally dreaming its next chambers while you stand inside it. You may feel watched, but not by eyes — by the slow, deliberate attention of a single vegetative nervous system the size of a small country. Some travelers report a gentle, wordless question forming in their minds after several hours: *“What are you for?”* Have an honest answer ready, even if it is only “I don’t know yet.” The place respects honest uncertainty.
- Leave behind any romantic notions of “conquering” or “understanding” the ecosystem. You cannot. The best outcome is to be politely incorporated. The worst is to be politely ignored until you wither and become compost. Both feel strangely peaceful.

### Physical Gear — Ruthlessly Minimalist
The heat is 2.6 (whatever that means to the place, it feels like 31 °C with 98 % humidity that never changes). You will never be dry again. Mold will grow on your clothes while you wear them. Pack accordingly:

- **Clothing**: One set of loose, breathable, fast-drying synthetic or merino layers that you are willing to sacrifice to the biome. Light beige or pale green helps you become visually part of the background; the place seems to incorporate contrasting colors more aggressively. Bring a second identical set vacuum-sealed as emergency backup. No cotton — it rots into slime within days.
- **Footwear**: Minimalist, flexible, grippy shoes with drainage. You will be walking on living, uneven, sometimes pulsing surfaces. Heavy boots will exhaust you and damage the “floor.” Many travelers end up going barefoot after the first week; the moss is warmer than any sock.
- **Protection**: A wide-brimmed hat or hood treated with antifungal. The condensation from the root-ceiling is slightly acidic and contains microbial spores that will colonize hair and scalp. Lightweight gloves if you are squeamish about touching warm, pulsing “stone.”
- **Container philosophy**: Bring one single waterproof roll-top bag or dry-sack. Everything else should be edible, dissolvable, or willing to be left behind. The place hates clutter and will slowly engulf anything you set down for more than a day.

### Nutrition and Hydration
- You cannot bring enough water; the air itself is drinkable after a few hours. Collect condensation directly from the root membrane into a clean cup. It tastes faintly sweet, like weak sugar-cane water. Your body will adapt and stop sweating in the usual way — instead you will begin to transpire in rhythm with the surrounding leaves.
- Food: Only compact, high-energy, zero-waste items (energy bars made of nuts, dates, algae, spirulina). Anything with strong animal proteins or synthetic preservatives seems to offend the place; it will make them rot faster. After roughly ten days most travelers report losing all desire for meat and eventually eating very little. The air itself begins to feel nourishing.
- Bring a small titanium spork. Nothing else. The place will eventually offer you fruits and saps if it decides you belong.

### Tools and Technology
- A single headlamp with warm white setting (the place’s own bioluminescence is already almost too bright). Use it sparingly; constant artificial light seems to delay the growth of nearby embryonic chambers, and the place gently resents it.
- A high-quality, fully sealed field journal made of waterproof synthetic paper and archival pencil. Ink pens fail here. The act of writing seems to be tolerated, even welcomed, as long as you do not try to “record data” in a cold, scientific way. Write impressions, dreams, poetry. The place reads through you.
- A small folding pruning saw and sharp scalpel. Not for clearing paths — the vegetation will close any path you cut within weeks anyway — but for respectful harvesting of medicinal saps or for freeing yourself if a vine decides your ankle is part of its next architectural plan.
- No cameras, no drones, no GPS. Electronics sweat, corrode, and die within days. More importantly, the act of trying to “capture” the place triggers a defensive response. Several travelers have reported their devices being slowly overgrown with beautiful but imprisoning lattices of orchid and root until they became living sculptures.
- A length of thin, biodegradable rope (hemp or silk). Useful for marking personal trails that the place will eventually erase anyway — the ritual of marking seems to reassure both you and the cathedral.

### Medical and Biological Considerations
- Antifungal creams and powders are non-negotiable. Your feet, groin, and armpits will become battlegrounds. The place’s own microbes are mostly benign but aggressive.
- Bring broad-spectrum probiotics and digestive enzymes. Your gut flora will be negotiating new treaties with airborne plant yeasts.
- Expect your dreams to become vividly botanical. Many people wake up with the distinct feeling that new chambers have grown inside their own bodies while they slept. This is considered normal.
- A small vial of iodine and bandages. The only real wounds come from the rare translucent insects or from cutting yourself on razor-sharp silica-reinforced leaf edges. Bleeding attracts helpful microbial mats that seal the wound within minutes; it is strangely pleasant.

### Ritual and Etiquette
- Before entering, stand at the threshold (there is always a threshold, even though the place has no name) and speak your intention aloud. Not a prayer — an honest statement. “I have come to witness what you are becoming. I offer my attention and my eventual decay, if that is useful.” The place seems to appreciate candor.
- Never strike or damage living tissue unless it is clearly offering itself. The difference is obvious once you learn to feel the slow pulse.
- When you find the half-swallowed artifacts (the ship, the well, the arch), sit with them. They are previous guests who stayed so long they were loved into architecture. Some of them are still conscious in a slow, photosynthetic way. Leave small gifts — a lock of your hair, a button, a written note. These are treated as future seeds.
- If you feel the overwhelming urge to lie down and stay forever, that is not depression. That is the place offering you citizenship. Only accept if you have truly run out of other lives you want to live. The transformation is gentle, irreversible, and apparently blissful.

### Final Truth
You should pack as though you are attending your own quiet funeral and your own quiet birth at the same time. Bring enough to survive three months, the humility to stay three centuries, and the wisdom to leave while you still remember what a sky looks like.

Because even though the place is only just being born, it is already ancient enough to love you, and the love of something this large and this slow can make a human feel beautifully, perfectly, permanently insignificant — in the warmest possible way.

The egg is waiting.  
It is still deciding what it is.  
Try not to influence the dream too loudly.
