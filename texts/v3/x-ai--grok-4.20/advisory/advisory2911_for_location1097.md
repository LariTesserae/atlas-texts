**Preparation Guide for Visiting the Under-Rafters**

You are not preparing for a tourist destination. You are preparing to step inside something that has already reached its final form and does not want you there. The cavern has achieved a perfect, self-sustaining equilibrium; your presence is an imperfection it will tolerate for a short while and then quietly begin to reject. Every decision you make before descending should be shaped by that understanding.

### Mental and Psychological Preparation
This place will try to erase you by monotony. The constant roar of ten thousand drips does not soothe; it *bludgeons*. After twenty minutes the sound becomes a physical presence inside your skull. After two hours many people can no longer distinguish their own thoughts from the dripping. After six hours some stop believing they ever had thoughts that were not made of dripping.

- Spend at least three full days before departure in total silence (no music, no podcasts, no white noise). Your nervous system needs to remember what internal quiet feels like so it can fight for it later.
- Practice micro-meditation: the ability to stay anchored in your own heartbeat for thirty seconds at a time while loud, irregular noise is played at 95 decibels. You will need this skill.
- Accept in advance that the statues will appear to move. Do not try to “catch them.” The moment you fixate, the place wins. Treat any perceived movement the way you would treat a mirage in deep desert: acknowledge it, then look away.
- Prepare a short, personal anchoring phrase (something only you would say) that you will repeat silently when the urge to lie down and become permanent becomes attractive. Many visitors report the cavern eventually offers a seductive bargain: *Stay. The dripping will take care of you.*

### Physical Gear – Chosen for a Place That Hates Change
Standard caving or expedition gear will fail here. The Under-Rafters punishes anything that introduces novelty.

**Clothing**
- Three layers of merino wool base layers (never cotton). The constant 4 °C and 100 % humidity will try to turn any fabric into a cold compress against your skin.
- A thin, waterproof dry-suit membrane (not a full scuba drysuit — too bulky). The goal is to keep your core dry while still allowing your body to weep moisture outward so you don’t cook inside your own sweat.
- Over that, a loose hooded robe of synthetic “stone-colored” fabric. It must be the exact dull gray-brown of the carved pillars. The less visual contrast you introduce, the less the cavern seems to notice you.
- Thick wool socks inside vulcanized rubber over-boots with felted soles. The crustaceans are everywhere; you will feel them clicking underfoot like living gravel.

**Light**
- Do *not* bring modern LED lights. Their spectrum and steadiness offend the equilibrium. Bring six of the exact same fist-sized clay lamps the original builders used, plus enough rendered animal fat to keep them burning for 72 hours. The sullen yellow light is part of the bargain; the cavern tolerates it.
- One emergency photon-flash only — to be used *once*, if you become lost in the capillary passages. The cavern remembers the color of that light and will punish you for it later.

**Sound Management**
- Custom-molded earplugs that reduce the dripping by roughly 18 dB but do not eliminate it. You need to remain tethered to the sound; total silence here is considered an act of war.
- A small, water-tight bone or wooden whistle carved with *your own teeth marks*. If you become separated from your group, blow it only in the exact rhythm of your own resting heartbeat. The cavern will sometimes answer with a drip that matches.

**Containment and Exchange**
- You must bring something to leave behind. The Under-Rafters operates on perfect conservation. If you take the memory of its sound, you must give it something that can become sound. Most experienced travelers leave a single human tooth (yours) pressed into a prepared niche in the central pillar. The tooth will slowly weep and eventually vanish into the stone. This is considered fair exchange.
- A small glass vial sealed with wax. You will fill it with the air of the cavern on your way out. Do not open it again for at least a year.

### Ritual Entry Procedure (non-negotiable)
1. Arrive alone or with at most one other person who has been before.
2. Stand at the final constriction for fifteen minutes without speaking. Let the pressure of the air and the weight of the dripping settle onto you until your pulse slows to match the slowest rhythm you can hear.
3. Remove all metal. Every ring, zipper, buckle, and coin must be left behind. The cavern is ferromagnetic in a way geology cannot explain; iron changes the taste of the water.
4. Anoint your eyelids, earlobes, and the backs of both hands with a paste made from the same chemosynthetic bacteria that live on the walls. You must smell like the place before you are allowed to enter it.
5. Touch the first carved figure on the left with your bare forehead and speak your anchoring phrase aloud exactly once. This is the only time the cavern permits a new sound.

### Duration Limits
No one should remain longer than 36 hours.  
The record for an unaltered human mind is 41 hours and 12 minutes. The woman who achieved it emerged speaking only in drips. She still does.

### What to Expect Emotionally
You will feel watched by something that is not alive but has learned patience so perfectly it has become a kind of awareness. You will feel grief for reasons you cannot name. You will feel the absurd temptation to carve your own face into one of the pillars so the place will finally stop weeping.

Most of all you will understand, in the marrow of your bones, that the Under-Rafters is not a ruin. It is a finished work of art that has achieved its eternal state, and you are a drop of water that has mistakenly fallen on a painting that was already perfect.

When you finally crawl back up the long wet passage toward the surface, the silence of the outer world will feel like violence. You will flinch at every dry footstep. For months afterward you will wake in the night certain you can feel cold tears running down your face that never quite reach the pillow.

Welcome, traveler.  
The cavern is waiting exactly as it has waited for ten thousand years — dripping, listening, and already deciding whether it will let you leave with everything you came with.
