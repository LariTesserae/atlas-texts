**How to Prepare for Visiting the Embryonic Pocket**

This is not a cave you visit. It is a place that is still deciding whether it *wants* visitors, and whether you will be allowed to remain entirely yourself afterward. An ordinary human multiverse traveler must prepare with the solemnity of someone about to undergo minor surgery performed by an undecided god. Treat the journey as if you are stepping into the amniotic sac of a planet that has not yet been born.

### Mental and Spiritual Preparation (Do this for at least three days before entry)
- Spend time in absolute sensory deprivation (float tank, dark room, no phone, no clocks). The pocket is violently sensitive to unresolved human intention. If you carry unexamined grief, lust, rage, or even strong aesthetic preferences, the cave may try to edit them out of you or incorporate them into its architecture.
- Practice radical non-resistance. Meditate on the phrase “I am willing to be unfinished.” The pocket responds poorly to people who demand consistency.
- Decide in advance what parts of your identity you are willing to let the cave taste. It *will* taste you. Many travelers report returning with one childhood memory now permanently rendered in pale gold-white light, or with the sudden inability to pronounce their own middle name.
- Write a short letter to the cave on biodegradable paper (ideally rice paper or mulberry). It should contain only honest questions you do not expect answered. You will offer this letter upon entry. Do not sign it with your full name.

### Physical Preparation
- **Clothing**: Wear nothing synthetic. The pocket’s light interacts strangely with plastic and will slowly knit it into the stone if left too long. Choose raw wool, silk, linen, or go naked. Many experienced travelers choose nakedness; the cave seems to respect the honesty. Bring one thin silk robe the color of bone for the return journey—your skin will feel too new afterward.
- **Light sources**: Do not bring any. The pocket produces violent, complete illumination and will treat artificial light as an immune response. It may grow temporary eyelids on the ceiling to smother your flashlight.
- **Tools and objects**: 
  - One small obsidian knife (for blood offering if the fissure resists opening).
  - A single human tooth (not necessarily yours) wrapped in red thread. This acts as a “visa.”
  - No metals except cold iron or pure gold. The pocket is in the process of inventing rust; it finds the concept fascinating and may experiment on anything ferrous.
  - A small mirror made of polished silver. Not for vanity—for negotiation. The cave sometimes forgets what a human face is and needs reminding.
- **Body modifications**: Remove all piercings. The cave has been known to finish them into permanent mineral growths. Do not wear contact lenses; several travelers now possess one eye that permanently sees in the cave’s gold-white spectrum and cries tiny rainbows when it rains.

### Sensory and Psychological Kit
- Earplugs made of natural wax. The acoustics will amplify your heartbeat until it sounds like a parade marching through your ribcage. You may need to remind yourself that the sound is still yours.
- A small vial of your own blood mixed with sea salt and honey. If the cave begins to consider you too interesting and starts turning sections of your skin into pages or feathers, a single drop on the tongue can remind it that you are a finished mammal (for now).
- One childhood toy or object small enough to fit in your mouth. If the cave offers you permanent residence in one of its half-dreams, you can bite down on the object to remember linear time.

### Ritual of Entry
1. Fast for 24 hours. The cave is metabolizing; it dislikes competition.
2. At the fissure, press your bare chest against the damp rock and let it read your heartbeat for a full minute. This is the price of admission.
3. Whisper your childhood nickname (the one you hated) into the throat-like opening. The cave collects names that were almost discarded.
4. Leave your letter. If the fissure swallows it, you may enter. If it spits the letter back wet and covered in tiny translucent gills, do not proceed. Come back in seven years.
5. Squeeze through sideways like a child being born in reverse. Do not look back. Something will be looking at you.

### While Inside – Rules of Conduct
- Move slowly and with embarrassment, as though you have walked into someone else’s dream uninvited.
- Never name anything you see. Names will harden it. The cave is still choosing.
- If a section of wall becomes soft skin and begins to breathe in sync with you, gently place your palm on it and say “Not yet.” Then step away.
- When the blind millipedes reverse direction, you must reverse direction too. This is non-negotiable.
- If you hear the laughter of a child, answer with your own most genuine laugh, even if it sounds broken. The cave is listening for authenticity.
- Should the floor forget gravity and you begin to rise, do not fight it. Let yourself drift upward until the world corrects itself with that embarrassed lurch. Fighting causes permanent vertical dyslexia.

### Exit Strategy
The cave will eventually begin to consider you part of its undecided anatomy. You will feel it as a soft, loving pressure behind your eyes and a sudden inability to remember what day it is on Earth.

At that moment you must:
- Take out the childhood object and press it against your heart.
- Speak your full legal name out loud in the most boring, bureaucratic tone you can manage.
- Walk backward toward the fissure while maintaining eye contact with the stone chair (even if it has melted further).

Most travelers emerge changed. Some return missing a memory. A few return pregnant with small, luminous ideas that later become symphonies or religions. One traveler is said to have returned as the cave’s first permanent shadow — he is still in there, politely waiting for the world to finish deciding what he is.

Bring humility.  
Bring curiosity so pure it borders on terror.  
And above all, bring the willingness to be edited.

The pocket is not yet a cave.  
You should not yet be entirely human when you meet it.
