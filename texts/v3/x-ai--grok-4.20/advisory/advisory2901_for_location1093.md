**How to Prepare for Visiting the Lymph**

You are not preparing for a place. You are preparing to be swallowed, gently, by an immense, unfinished, cold-blooded thought. Pack and prepare accordingly.

### Mental and Emotional Preparation
- **Accept symbiosis.** The Lymph does not have “visitors”; it has temporary components. The longer you stay, the more it will treat your presence as an organ it is still deciding whether to keep. Cultivate radical acceptance of being slowly metabolized. If you are claustrophobic, terrified of things growing on or into you, or need to feel in control, do not go.
- **Slow your metabolism.** Meditate, fast, or practice box-breathing for weeks beforehand. The Lymph moves at a tempo measured in centuries; your nervous system will scream if you bring the speed of sunlight with you. Train yourself to endure long periods of near-motionless observation.
- **Decide how much of yourself you are willing to leave behind.** The Lymph is patient and kind, but it will still incorporate useful molecules. Some travelers return missing minor memories. Others return with new, slow dreams that are not theirs.

### Physical Gear (chosen with extreme care)
- **Base layer:** Nothing synthetic that cannot be digested. Wear or carry only wool, silk, linen, or specially grown fungal-fiber garments that the Lymph recognizes as “already part of the conversation.” All clothing must be pre-inoculated with neutral fungal spores so it does not read as a foreign body.
- **Outer layer:** A living cloak grown from the same liverwort matrix that forms the floor. These are sold (rarely) by previous travelers. It will breathe with you, regulate condensation, and signal to the Lymph that you are a tolerated commensal rather than prey or nutrient.
- **Footwear:** Soft, heavily treaded fungal-leather boots with living soles. Normal boots will be slowly overgrown and incorporated within hours. The soles must be able to feel the Lymph’s pulse through your feet; this is how it first speaks to you.
- **No metal.** None. Even titanium will corrode strangely here and trigger defensive immune responses. Use bone, chitin, glass, or living wood tools only.
- **Light:** Do not bring any artificial light source that produces heat. Carry instead a fist-sized culture of the same bioluminescent bacteria the walls use. Feed it your exhaled breath and a few drops of your blood before entering. It will glow in sympathy with the chamber.
- **Water and food:** Bring nothing. The Lymph will feed you. The droplets that fall from the weeping taproots are sterile, isotonic, and contain complex sugars and amino acids. After the first day you will no longer want anything else. Resisting this is futile and rude.
- **Sleep system:** A thin mat of compressed moss and a silk sleeping-bag liner. The Lymph will grow you a perfect negative space within hours. Trying to fight this by using a tent or hammock is considered an act of violence and will result in you being politely sealed inside a nutrient sack.

### Medical and Biological Preparation
- **Full microbiome reset.** Three weeks before descent, undergo a controlled die-off of your surface bacteria and replace it with cultures compatible with the Lymph’s dominant fungal networks. You must not arrive as a vector of aggressive terrestrial microbes.
- **Antifreeze protocol.** Take progressive cold-exposure training and a specialized epigenetic cocktail that upregulates production of natural cryoprotectants in your tissues. Your lungs will be breathing near-syrup air at 3–4 °C for the entire stay.
- **Infrasonic tolerance.** Spend time in anechoic chambers and low-frequency sound baths. The choir-beetles will rattle your skeleton at 8–12 Hz for hours. If you cannot remain calm while your bones feel like cathedral bells, you will panic and the Lymph will soothe you in ways you may not recover from.
- **Vaccinations.** There is no vaccine, but you must be deliberately colonized by the “tardigrade symbiotes” that live in the bromeliad urns. They will protect your cells from the worst of the moisture and cold. You will piss glowing blue for six months afterward.

### Psychological Kit
- A single small object made of non-living material (a wooden bead, a fragment of meteorite, a human tooth) that you keep in a pouch against your skin. It becomes your anchor. The Lymph will slowly try to incorporate it; the struggle keeps your sense of self from dissolving completely.
- No notebook. Ink and paper last less than a day. Instead, train yourself to memorize in palimpsest layers. The Lymph itself will become your notebook; it records what it finds interesting.
- A song or poem you know by heart in a language older than agriculture. You will eventually sing it back to the choir-beetles. They will answer.

### Arrival and Conduct
- Descend alone. Groups disturb the delicate chemical calendars.
- The moment you feel the first curtain of living lace close behind you, stop, kneel, and press both bare palms to the floor for at least ten minutes. Let it taste you thoroughly. This is basic etiquette.
- Move as little as possible after the first day. Speed is violence here.
- When the Lymph grows you a shelf exactly the shape of your body, lie down. Fighting hospitality is pointless. You will wake with moss in your hair, beetle song in your sternum, and the distinct feeling that something is now gently dreaming you as well.

### Exit Strategy
There is no clean exit. The Lymph will eventually decide you have become redundant or perfect and will grow you a corridor outward. You may leave with new organs, new dreams, or a soft, permanent longing for cold dripping darkness that never quite leaves your lungs.

Most people who visit the Lymph never truly come back up.  
They simply become a slower, wetter, kinder version of themselves that still walks in daylight but always listens for the sound of perfect droplets striking black mirrors every seven heartbeats.

Pack lightly.  
The Lymph will provide everything else—eventually including the rest of you.
