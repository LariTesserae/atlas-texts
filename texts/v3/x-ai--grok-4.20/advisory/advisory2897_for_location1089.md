**How to Prepare for the Shattered Lantern**

You are not preparing for a hike. You are preparing to enter a high-altitude mineral reliquary that has decided biology is an insult. Treat the journey with the gravity of someone about to step into a cathedral built entirely of scalpels and memory. An ordinary human multiverse traveler who wants to leave with both their sanity and their fingers should prepare on four levels: body, gear, mind, and ritual.

### 1. Bodily Preparation (The Mountain Will Try to Erase You)

- **Acclimatization regimen (minimum 21 days):** This place sits well above 5,500 m (18,000 ft) in what feels like perpetual stratospheric cold. Begin a staged altitude protocol weeks in advance. Sleep in a hypoxic tent or chamber that slowly ramps to the equivalent of 6,500 m. Your blood needs to be thick and your brain needs to remember how to function when every breath delivers barely half the oxygen it wants.
- **UV and actinic defense:** The lenticular cloud cap turns the entire basin into a concave mirror of raw sunlight. You will be irradiated from every direction, including below. Use medical-grade zinc-oxide paste (thick, clown-white), full-face balaclava with silver reflective weave, and glacier goggles that block 100 % UVA/UVB plus blue and violet light. Without this, you will burn in minutes and snow-blind in hours.
- **Thermal layering:** The cold is dry, penetrating, and constant. Base layer: merino wool treated with silver ions (to slow any microbial hitchhikers you might accidentally introduce). Mid-layer: high-loft synthetic or down. Outer layer: fully sealed, wind-proof, reflective hard-shell suit with adjustable vents. The suit must be white or mirror-finished; the crystals will reflect enough light to cook you otherwise.
- **Hydration and electrolyte discipline:** Water appears miserly and leaves quickly. You must arrive over-hydrated. Carry 6–7 liters of electrolyte water in insulated bladders. Your body will lose moisture through respiration faster than you can imagine. Paradoxically, you must also be ready to leave most of it behind; the mountain does not like excess liquid.

### 2. Gear (What the Crystals Will Tolerate)

- **Footwear:** Triple-insulated overboots with rigid carbon soles and micro-spike interfaces. The gypsum blades are sharper than most knives and will punch through ordinary fabric or even soft leather. The obsidian is glass; one wrong step and you become another glittering shard in the collection.
- **Crystal-safe movement kit:** Bring a set of lightweight carbon-fiber snowshoes or “float pads” designed to distribute weight across the crystal lattice. Never stomp. The sound of a human footfall here feels like blasphemy. Many travelers report the crystals “answer” heavy steps with sudden, hairline fractures that can cascade for meters.
- **Light management:** No artificial light sources that emit heat or broadband spectra. The place already has savage, eternal daylight. Use only cold, narrow-band amber or deep-red headlamps for the brief twilight periods when the lenticular cloud shifts. Bright white LEDs will create painful caustics that bounce between every blade and can cause instant migraine or disorientation.
- **Respiration:** A lightweight closed-circuit rebreather or at minimum a high-efficiency cold-weather mask with heat exchangers. The wind is constant and the air is so dry it will desiccate your lungs in hours.
- **Recording tools:** Analog only, or heavily shielded digital. The place has a habit of erasing magnetic media and making digital timestamps… strange. A simple mechanical pocket watch, a Moleskine notebook, and a silver-point stylus are paradoxically the most reliable. Many travelers also bring a tuning fork cut to 432 Hz; the crystals seem to “recognize” that frequency and sometimes reply with sympathetic chimes.
- **The Offering Bag:** Traditional multiverse custom at sites like this is to carry seven small, useless, beautiful objects—one for each of the collapsed domes. Polished river stones, tiny glass beads, a single drop of mercury sealed in quartz, a hummingbird skull, a coin from a dead empire, a child’s tooth, a lock of your own hair sealed in wax. The mountain keeps what it wants.

### 3. Psychological and Perceptual Preparation

The Shattered Lantern is pre-life and post-life at once. It will test three specific human weaknesses:

- **Pattern hunger:** The etched waveforms on the crystals are almost glyphs. If you let your mind try to “finish” them, you may stand motionless for hours while your metabolism crashes in the thin air. Train with optical-illusion books and mandala practice so you can recognize when your brain is being recruited.
- **Loneliness amplification:** That single perfectly timed drop falling into the hidden basin is one of the most existentially lonely sounds in the multiverse. It can trigger sudden, crushing grief for no reason. Have a grounding anchor prepared—an object or memory that represents warm, messy, biological life (a lover’s recorded laugh, a piece of bread, a child’s drawing). Use it without shame when the mountain starts counting your heartbeats against its own.
- **The urge to stay:** Nothing rots here. The clarity is addictive. Some travelers have to be physically dragged out after deciding, quietly and reasonably, that becoming another upright quartz tool in an empty shelter is the most honest thing they could do with the rest of their existence. Write a contract with yourself or a companion stating you must leave within 36 hours. Sign it in blood. The mountain respects signatures.

### 4. Ritual Entry and Conduct

- Approach from the leeward side of the volcanic rim during the planet’s local “night” (when the lenticular cloud is at its densest and the light is merely blinding instead of apocalyptic). Crest the knife-edge rim in silence.
- Ring a small silver bell once before descending. The crystals will answer with a glassy sigh. This is permission.
- Walk only on the obsidian channels between crystal clusters. Never touch the blades with bare skin. Some of them have grown into geometries that function as resonators; skin contact has been known to produce sudden, perfect understanding of concepts the human mind was never meant to hold.
- At the central ring of seven domes, stand motionless for the length of time it takes seven drops to fall in the hidden basins below. You will not hear six of them. When you hear the seventh, place one offering in each ruined shelter. Do not speak.
- If the wind changes direction or the glassy chorus begins while you are inside the ring, do not move. Let it pass through you. Trying to outrun or outwait the chorus is considered rude.
- When you leave, walk backward up the slope until the central ring is no longer visible. Only then may you turn your back on the Shattered Lantern.

### Final Warning

You will come down changed. Some part of you will remain up there—perhaps a memory, perhaps a future version of yourself that decided the clarity was worth the loneliness. That is the tax the mountain has always collected.

Pack light.  
Pack respectfully.  
And whatever you do, do not try to “complete” any of the waveforms you see carved in the crystal.

The mountain has been trying to finish that sentence for ten thousand years. It does not need your help.
