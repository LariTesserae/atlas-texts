**Preparation Guide for a Visit to the Underforge**

If you are an ordinary human multiverse traveler with a healthy survival instinct, treat the Underforge not as a tourist destination but as a living geological wound that has taught itself to breathe, sweat, and run heavy machinery. It is hostile, alien, and indifferent in a way few places are. Here is exactly what your body, mind, and gear need before you descend the narrow volcanic throat.

### 1. Physical Conditioning & Medical Readiness
- You must be in excellent cardiovascular shape. The heat is brutal (often 45–60 °C / 113–140 °F with near 100 % humidity). Your body will be constantly fighting both hyperthermia and dehydration even while standing still.
- Train in a sauna or steam room wearing full gear for at least two weeks beforehand. Get used to functioning while drenched in sweat that never evaporates.
- Have a full medical workup. Especially important: lung function, heat tolerance, and any history of respiratory issues. The air is loaded with sulfur dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, carbon dioxide, and fine mineral aerosols. Even short exposure can inflame lungs.
- Bring prescription-grade electrolyte tablets, diuretics (to manage fluid in the lungs), and anti-nausea medication. Volcanic gases can trigger migraines and vomiting within minutes.
- Tetanus, hepatitis, and pneumococcal vaccinations are non-negotiable. Every surface is either razor-sharp fresh fracture or encrusted with jagged mineral needles.

### 2. Mandatory Protective Gear (no compromises)
- **Full-body heat-resistant suit** – Think proximity firefighter gear but lighter. Nomex or Kevlar blend with integrated cooling. The outer layer must be able to touch metal at 90 °C without melting onto you.
- **Sealed full-face respirator** with dual-stage volcanic-gas cartridges (acid gas + particulate). Carry three spare sets; filters clog fast with the mineral “snow” that grows in the air. A PAPR (powered air-purifying respirator) is strongly preferred because the environment will fight you for every breath.
- **Reinforced, insulated gloves** – leather or Kevlar with thick palms. The condensation makes everything slippery; the mineral crystals are like broken glass.
- **Steel-toed, heat-resistant boots** with aggressive tread and ankle support. The floors are a nightmare of sharp rebar stubs, loose chains, and treacherous wet mineral crusts that can crumble underfoot.
- **Hard hat with integrated lamp** plus a powerful waist-mounted backup LED (at least 1000 lumens). The native fluorescent green glow is treacherous; it ruins depth perception and makes everything look diseased.
- **Chemical-resistant coveralls** over the suit. The mineral salts (copper sulfate, iron oxides, sulfur compounds) will eat fabric and skin in hours.
- **Ear protection** that also seals. The dripping sounds are hypnotic and disorienting; the deeper subsonic thumps can cause nausea after prolonged exposure.

### 3. Environmental & Navigation Kit
- **Personal cooling system** – Either a chilled-water vest or phase-change ice packs that can be swapped. You will overheat faster than you think possible.
- **Moisture-wicking base layers** that actually move sweat away. You will be soaked within five minutes; staying wet in that heat is dangerous.
- **Laser rangefinder and bright infrared illuminator**. Visible light is unreliable here; the steam and mineral haze scatter it. Thermal imaging is useful but the background heat is so chaotic that it’s only a secondary tool.
- **Redundant mapping system**: physical waterproof map on heat-resistant synthetic paper + a hardened tablet in a cooled, sealed case. GPS will not work. Compass is erratic near the iron mass and volcanic magnetism. You must dead-reckon and mark your path with high-temperature ceramic chalk or carved notches.
- **100 m of lightweight climbing rope, ascenders, and rappelling gear**. Some catwalks have collapsed; new fissures can open without warning. You may need to climb or descend suddenly.
- **Emergency shelter**: a small insulated reflective tent made of the same material as wildfire shelters. If a major steam event or new fracture opens, you need something to crawl inside while the air cooks.

### 4. Psychological & Tactical Preparation
- The Underforge is profoundly lonely and inhuman. There is no life, no birds, no insects, only the wet ticking of a million drops and the slow vegetable growth of crystals on sleeping machines. Many travelers experience crushing claustrophobia or the sense that the entire structure is “deciding” whether you belong.
- Go in with a strict time limit. No more than four to six hours maximum on the first visit. The place erodes willpower.
- Travel with at least one partner. Solo is extremely dangerous; if you fall into a hidden drain or are overcome by gas, no one will ever find you.
- Establish a rigid “umbilical” protocol: fixed ropes, regular check-ins with a surface team, and a hard abort time. The environment will try to lull you into staying longer than is safe.
- Accept that you will feel watched by something that is not sentient in any way we understand. The geometry and the half-glimpsed machines create constant pareidolia. The feeling is real even if the danger is geological rather than supernatural.

### 5. What to Bring Inside (Minimal but Critical)
- Two liters of water in insulated bladders (you will drink more than you think).
- High-calorie, easily digestible rations that can be eaten without removing the mask.
- Chemical light sticks (green ironically works best against the magnesium glow).
- A high-temperature first-aid kit including burn gel, trauma shears that can cut your own gear off, and a one-use emergency airway.
- A titanium or ceramic knife. Steel will corrode fast.
- A small geological hammer and sample vials if you’re there to study it. The crystals grow fast enough that you can literally watch them lengthen; many are beautiful and lethally sharp.

### Final Truth

The Underforge does not hate you. It simply does not register your existence as important. Every surface is either scalding, razor-edged, or coated in acidic mineral sherbet. The air wants to cook your lungs. The geometry wants to cut you. The machines, though long abandoned, still hold pressure and can wake up with almost no warning.

Dress like you are walking into an active volcanic smithy that has been running itself for centuries. Move like you are inside the still-healing ribcage of a young planet. Speak quietly. Touch almost nothing. And always keep one eye on the ceiling, because the next fissure may open with a sound like a cannon shot directly above your head.

If you are not willing to treat this place with the same respect you would give an active lava lake wearing a thin skin of rusted iron and dripping crystals, then do not go.

The Underforge is still deciding whether it is dying or being born.  
Try not to become part of the decision.
