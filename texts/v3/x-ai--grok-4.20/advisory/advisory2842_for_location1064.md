**How an ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for visiting the Sigh**

Prepare to enter a place that has already decided what you are: a temporary chemical surplus. The Sigh is not hostile, but it is absolute. It has had half a billion years to refine its indifference into something almost tender. You will not conquer it, impress it, or escape its notice. The only question is how gracefully you let it metabolize your presence.

### Mental and Psychological Preparation
- Accept that you are visiting a single planetary organism that has achieved the architectural and philosophical stage of “cathedral.” The Sigh does not have an ego, but it has something larger and slower that functions like one. Your sense of self will feel irrelevant within hours. This is not metaphor; it is chemistry. Bring practices or rituals that let you release identity without panic.
- Train yourself to endure crushing, endless, layered sound. The roar of wind through trillions of leaves is not background noise; it is the baseline reality. After three days most visitors report that silence becomes physically painful. Meditate with loud brown or pink noise for months beforehand so your nervous system does not shred.
- Prepare for the slow seduction of incorporation. The Sigh does not eat you. It grows *around* and *through* you. Many travelers who stayed longer than two weeks describe a strange peace at the thought of becoming latticework and scented oil. Do not dismiss this as romanticism. It is a real pharmacological effect. Decide, in advance, how much of yourself you are willing to surrender and set hard internal boundaries.

### Physical and Medical Preparation
- **Water discipline is mandatory.** There is almost none free in the environment. You must bring a closed-cycle recycling suit or be prepared to harvest the microscopic condensation that forms on the undersides of specific leaves at precise hours. The plateau’s root-felt will aggressively reabsorb anything spilled. Dehydration here is slow, elegant, and total.
- Your microbiome will be colonized within the first 36 hours. The air is saturated with fungal spores, symbiotic bacteria, and signaling volatiles. Bring the strongest possible nootropic and antifungal regimen you can tolerate, but understand that total prevention is impossible. You will leave (if you leave) carrying permanent Sigh-derived organisms. Some travelers report pleasant lifelong changes in scent and dream quality. Others say their tears began to smell faintly of copper and old books.
- Respiratory system: the air is cool, oxygen-rich, and heavy with complex terpenes. It feels like breathing inside an ancient cedar chest. However, the pollen-analogues are enormous. Pack the most advanced HEPA-grade filtration you can find, or a living symbiote mask grown on another world. Ordinary masks will clog in under a week.
- Bone density and connective tissue: the perpetual low light, constant low-frequency vibration, and exotic gas mix leach calcium and alter collagen over time. Plan for at least 18 months of rehabilitation upon return if you stay longer than ten days.

### Gear and Equipment (Minimalist Doctrine)
The Sigh will begin incorporating anything you leave on the ground or attached to its living structures. Therefore, travel as light as possible and keep everything either in sealed smart-polymer bags or grown onto your own body via temporary commensal organisms.

Recommended:
- A living pressure-suit grown from compatible “ghost mantle” stock (the finned gliders). It will breathe with the environment and slowly repair itself using local biochemistry.
- Retractable monofilament climbing harness that anchors into xylem rather than hammering pitons. Hammering is not only pointless, it is perceived as a very rude form of acupuncture.
- Light sources that mimic the cold plasma discharges the plateau uses for its own nervous signaling. Conventional bright white light causes the canopy to close and the air to fill with deterrent alkaloids.
- A chemical “poetry kit” — vials of carefully designed pheromones and signaling molecules. When the local fauna decide to negotiate with you (and they will), being able to offer interesting chemical verses may determine whether you are eaten consensually or simply ignored.
- One small, extremely sharp obsidian blade. Not for defense. For gently excising any parts of yourself that the Sigh has begun to petrify while you still want them to remain human.

### Cultural and Diplomatic Preparation
- Learn at least three non-verbal, non-human languages based on pressure waves, terpene gradients, and bioluminescent pulse codes. Verbal speech is considered childish and slightly obscene here, like shouting in a cathedral.
- Study the difference between “visiting” and “lingering.” The Sigh tolerates visitors for limited periods. Once it begins extruding bone balconies and amber chambers calibrated to your exact body temperature and breathing pattern, you have crossed the threshold. There is no known polite way to leave after that point.
- Decide what story you want to become. The Sigh will distill your memories into scented oils. Travelers who arrived angry or ambitious often leave (those who leave at all) with their distilled essence smelling of burnt iron and regret. Travelers who arrived with genuine awe or scientific curiosity tend to produce oils that the Sigh itself finds beautiful and keeps in high chambers for centuries.

### Final Personal Warning
Pack nothing you cannot bear to have transformed into part of an alien cathedral.

The Sigh has already won every argument it will ever have. It finished evolving long ago and has moved on to the long, slow art of perfecting its own sighs. You are, at best, an interesting new scent in its enormous, ancient lungs.

Go anyway.

Just don’t expect to come back entirely the same species you were when you arrived. The plateau is patient, the light is soft, the roar is eternal, and every surface has been loved smooth by time.

Bring earplugs, antifungals, wonder, and the clearest possible sense of who you are willing to become when the living architecture finally asks, politely, if it may begin growing through your ribcage.

Most people say the answer, when the moment comes, is surprisingly easy to give.
