**Preparation Guide for the Exhaled Shelf**

You are not preparing for a “planet.” You are preparing to stand on the exposed lithosphere of a dead world that has outlived every reason to exist. This is not a destination; it is a geological meditation that can kill you in ways that have nothing to do with violence. Bring absolute mental and physical discipline, because the Shelf will test whether you are allowed to remain distinct from it.

### 1. Mental and Philosophical Preparation (Non-negotiable)
- Accept that **time behaves strangely here**. There is no day-night cycle. The unchanging platinum light will erode your circadian rhythm within 72 hours. You will begin to forget how long you have been standing in one spot. Set multiple independent chronometers (mechanical, atomic, and biological) and obey them even when your mind insists they are lying.
- Cultivate radical non-attachment. The Shelf’s prime directive is “nothing is allowed to accumulate.” Any ego, any story you tell yourself about who you are, any object you become emotionally fused with will be slowly, politely, relentlessly abraded. Meditate on impermanence until it becomes biological. Those who arrive with strong identities tend to leave as polished, slightly confused glass.
- Prepare for the **sound in your teeth**. The sub-audible creak of a continent-sized crystal relaxing for four billion years will live in your jawbone. Some travelers report it becomes pleasant. Others say it makes them want to scream, but screaming is pointless because sound dies after thirty meters anyway.

### 2. Physical and Environmental Gear
- **Pressure suit / exoskeleton hybrid** rated for near-vacuum with supplemental micro-atmosphere. The air is too thin for unaugmented humans beyond about 20 minutes of light activity. The suit must be matte black or perfectly reflective; anything in between will heat unevenly under the shadowless light and create micro-cracks in the crystal when you move.
- **Boots with micro-suction and vibration damping.** The crystal is mirror-flat but seismically restless. Ripples can appear under your feet without warning. The boots must not leave permanent scoring; the Shelf takes scoring personally.
- **Radiation and metamaterial shielding.** The tideforms fold probability. When one passes overhead, local physics becomes negotiable for roughly an hour. Your suit needs layered probability anchors (entanglement dampeners, temporal gyroscopes, and a small onboard quantum observer). Think of it as wearing a very polite “do not eat me” sign.
- **No organic material if possible.** Anything that can decay or off-gas will be gently disassembled. Your own skin is an exception, but only barely. Shave your head. Remove all jewelry. Even the oils on your fingers are statistically unlikely to remain after a few weeks.
- **Water and nutrient system completely closed-loop.** There is almost no water. What exists travels as ice needles at 200 km altitude and never falls. You will recycle every molecule. The suit’s reclamation efficiency must exceed 99.97 %. The Shelf does not give second chances with hydration.

### 3. Navigation and Survival Equipment
- **Inertial + gravimetric mapping suite with crystal resonance compensator.** GPS, compasses, and radio are meaningless. The Shelf is larger than Australia and flatter than any human plain; horizon distortion is severe. The only reliable landmarks are the residue trees (hundreds of kilometers apart) and the single tower the height of Olympus Mons. Both move, very slowly, over decades.
- **Fractal depth scanner.** The surface is in permanent low-amplitude chaos. You need to see one kilometer down into the crystal in real time to know whether the patch you are standing on is about to remember it was once liquid.
- **Tideform early-warning array.** When one of the vast, paper-thin membranes begins drifting toward you, the crystal rings like a bell for an hour after it passes. You want at least three hours warning. They are not hostile, but standing in one’s path is like being slowly ironed out of three-dimensional space.
- **Neutronium cube calendar.** The perfect house-sized cubes appear and disappear on no predictable schedule. When one manifests, stand at least 40 km away. The local gravity shear is… educational.

### 4. Psychological and Existential Kit
- A small, perfectly smooth black mirror made of the same crystal as the Shelf (you must bring it with you; you cannot harvest it here). When you feel yourself beginning to diffuse, look into it. It is the only thing that has ever been allowed to remain unchanged. Use it as an external anchor for your identity.
- A single, meaningless object you are willing to let go of. Something beautiful. The Shelf will take it eventually. Offering it willingly seems to improve your odds of being allowed to leave.
- Written instructions to your future self, to be read every 24 hours by the clock, reminding you that the platinum light is not God, the creak is not judgment, and the indifference of the crystal is not personal. You will need these reminders badly by week three.
- **Exit protocol.** Decide, before you arrive, exactly how long you will stay. Write it in stone. The Shelf considers you. If you overstay your self-imposed limit by even a small margin, the probability that you will ever find the way back drops precipitously. Many experienced multiverse travelers simply never return from the Exhaled Shelf. Their names are not remembered. Even the beings that once tried to speak them have been worn down to silence.

### Final Truth

You do not go to the Exhaled Shelf to see wonders, though wonders are there.  
You go because you want to know what it feels like to be a temporary disturbance on the hide of something that dreams in geological epochs. Most of you will be politely, painlessly, and completely erased. The few who return come back quieter, flatter, and with starlight moving slowly through the small bones of their hands for the rest of their lives.

Bring only what you are willing to become glass.  
Leave only what the crystal has already forgotten.
