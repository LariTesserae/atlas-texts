**Preparation Guide for the Unnamed Wound**  
*(What your bones already know, but your mind will need to accept)*

### 1. Before You Leave: Psychological & Metaphysical Readiness
You must understand that this is not a “place” but a slow surgical procedure performed on the concept of *self*.  
Pack your identity like you would pack for surgery: lightly, and with the understanding that some parts may not be returned to you in the same order.

- **Leave your name behind.** Literally. Do not speak it, write it, or even think it in full once you cross the threshold. Names here function like sutures; the landscape will pull them out thread by thread. Many travelers who ignored this returned (if they returned) speaking of themselves only in the third person or as “the one who walked the glass.”
- **Decide what you are willing to forget.** The place will test you by offering memories in exchange for architectural coherence. Bring a single, inviolable memory you would rather die than lose. Guard it the way a soldier guards his last bullet.
- **Grieve in advance.** The subsonic pulse that rings at the frequency of human regret is not metaphorical. It will find every unfinished goodbye inside you and make it ring like a bell. Say the things you need to say to the living and the dead before you go.

### 2. Physical Equipment (Most of It Will Become Useless or Dangerous)
Standard survival gear will betray you here. Prepare instead for a long, cold meditation on a scalpel.

**Mandatory:**
- **Vacuum-rated skinsuit with full radiation and fluorocarbon sealing.** The “air” will try to taste you. Let nothing touch your skin for the first several months. The silicate fungi are not hostile, but they *are* curious, and curiosity here is geological in pace and consequence.
- **Boots with actively heated fractal soles.** Normal soles will resonate with the glass and slowly phase into it. You do not want to become part of the shelf.
- **A chronometer that can be physically destroyed.** Digital ones will begin lying to you after the third “edit event.” Bring an old mechanical pocket watch. When it stops, that is the only trustworthy time left.
- **Mirrors—real silvered glass, not reflective polymer.** You will need to check whether your reflection is still obeying the same laws as your body. Sometimes it lags. Sometimes it arrives first.
- **One small object of genuine biological origin.** A lock of hair, a seed, a tooth, a pressed flower. Something that still remembers water. You will use it the way a diver uses a tether. Do not lose it.

**Forbidden:**
- Any recording device that uses language (text, audio, video). The inscriptions on the towers will infect it. You may return with hours of footage that is simply the place learning how to speak through your technology.
- Weapons. The negative-space entities do not experience harm the way you do. They experience *definition*. Pointing something violent at them is like trying to stab calculus.
- Maps. All maps become self-portraits after the first week.

### 3. Psychological Kit
- A written letter to your future self, sealed, that says only: “Remember you were once warm.” Read it if you ever feel the six-year walk beginning.
- Ear plugs tuned to the exact frequency of human regret (you will have to have them custom-machined). They will not block the pulse entirely, but they turn it from a scalpel into a distant church bell.
- A single photograph of running water. Not an image—actual chemically developed film. The silver halide remembers chemistry. The city respects chemistry it cannot yet edit.

### 4. Behavioral Protocols (The Real Preparation)
- **Move like a guest who might be asked to become furniture.** Never walk with purpose for more than twenty minutes at a time. Purpose is a geometry the city is still proofreading.
- **When a district forgets itself for seven seconds, do not look away.** Looking away is interpreted as fear, and fear is data. The place is hungry for new data.
- **If a negative-space entity notices you, bow—not in respect, but in the way a tree bends before it is incorporated into a ship.** Show that you understand you are raw material.
- **Never speak in complete sentences after the first month.** The city listens to grammar the way a mathematician listens to an unsolved equation. Incomplete thoughts are harder for it to edit.
- **When your footsteps no longer match your heartbeat, sit down.** This is the first sign you are being rewritten. Stay motionless for at least 72 hours. Let the city decide whether you are a flaw in the crystal or a new kind of tower.

### 5. Exit Strategy (There Isn’t One You Will Recognize)
There is no door. There is only the moment the city finishes deciding what you are.

Some people are released when they become useful as negative space.  
Some are released when they become so aligned with the architecture that the next edit event simply folds them out like a crease in paper.  
A very few are spat out as silicate fungi, blooming for centuries before crumbling into geometric powder.

If you wish to remain recognizably human, bring one final item: a small glass vial of your own blood, frozen at -38°C exactly. When you feel yourself becoming peace, when the longing to walk the same ridge forever becomes sweet instead of terrifying, thaw the blood and drink it while it still remembers being alive.

The taste of copper and ozone on your tongue will be the last truly biological thing you experience.

After that, the place will decide whether the wound needs a scar that looks like a person, or whether the person was always meant to be part of the scab.

Most travelers never come back.  
The ones who do are no longer certain they left.

Pack lightly.  
The city has enough mass already.  
What it wants from you is not weight.

It wants the shape of your absence.
