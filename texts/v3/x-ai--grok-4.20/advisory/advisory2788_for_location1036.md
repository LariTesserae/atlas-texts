**How to Prepare for the Third Ascent (the Ravel / the High Scaffold / Up)**

If you are an ordinary human multiverse traveler with nothing but your own flesh, a backpack, and a multiversal key, treat this place with the seriousness of someone about to spend an extended period on a vertical Everest that is still being born and is undecided about the laws of physics.

### 1. Mindset & Psychological Preparation
- Accept that your sense of “up,” “down,” “finished,” and “real” will be under continuous negotiation. The longer you stay, the more the place will try to teach you new definitions. Some travelers never recover their previous certainty.
- Cultivate extreme cognitive patience. This is a slow, geological city. Things that look fixed will, over months or years, simply become something else. Frustration is dangerous here; the air occasionally exhales concepts, and “panic” is one of the more contagious ones.
- Prepare for constant low-grade sensory overload. The soundscape never stops. Learn to sleep inside noise or you will break.

### 2. Physical Gear (the absolute minimum viable kit)
- **Gravity-assist harness + micro-clamps**: A full-body rig with electromagnetic and mechanical anchors that can bite into iron, living rock, or dense vegetation. Include at least 60 meters of dynamic rope rated for unpredictable gravity flips. The locals call these “faith lines.”
- **Directional boots**: Boots with switchable soles (magnetic, gecko-pads, crampon, and suction). Normal shoes are useless; you will spend as much time walking on ceilings or vertical faces as on floors.
- **Layered, eternally damp clothing**: Multiple thin layers of synthetic wool and waxed canvas. Everything here is perpetually moist. Bring clothes that *like* being wet and cold. A hooded oilskin greatcoat that can double as a tarp or emergency sail is wise.
- **Respirator with conceptual filter cartridges**: The air is breathable, but the atmosphere sometimes exhales metaphors, half-formed ideas, or raw memory. Standard particle filters do nothing against those. You want cartridges labeled “Narrative dampening – Type III” or local equivalents that smell faintly of iron and childhood.
- **Fog lantern + backup chemical glow-sticks**: The bruise-colored twilight is treacherous. Shadows lie. A powerful directional lantern that can cut through mist is essential; many travelers also carry a small mirror-polished shield to bounce light around corners.
- **Condensation harvester & mineral tabs**: You will drink more from the constant dripping and beading than from any tap. A collapsible collector that straps to your pack is standard. Bring electrolyte tabs that can handle water so mineral-rich it can taste like blood and pennies.
- **Slow-time watch**: A timepiece that can be set to “geological tempo.” It helps you notice changes that happen over decades. Ordinary watches eventually start running backward or sideways here.

### 3. Biological & Medical Preparation
- Vaccinate and innoculate against every known vertical-jungle pathogen. The copper-jade vegetation is symbiotic with the architecture and sometimes decides human bodies would make interesting new scaffolding.
- Carry broad-spectrum antifungal powder. Mold grows *inside* people here if you’re not careful.
- Bring calcium, magnesium, and strontium supplements. The slow stresses of uncertain gravity leach minerals from your bones in strange patterns.
- Pack a small kit of “reality anchors”: mild antipsychotics, strong coffee, a photograph of someone you love, and a coin from your home reality. When the city starts offering you new senses or tempting you to let a street flow through you, these objects can serve as temporary fixed points.

### 4. Cultural & Practical Knowledge
- Learn at least three local dialects. Language here is still deciding what it wants to be; words occasionally gain or lose physical weight.
- Never say “finished,” “done,” or “permanent” in any construction district. The place is sensitive about that.
- Respect the megafauna. The brass-voiced herd creatures are not aggressive, but if one decides to graze on the moss growing on the building you’re standing on, simply wait. They move at geological speed and will eventually drift on.
- Carry trade goods that appreciate slow change: good tools, books that improve with age, musical instruments that sound better when damp, fermented things, and stories that get better the longer they’re told.
- Find a crew or neighborhood to attach to within the first week. Solo travelers are pitied and occasionally absorbed into the architecture.

### 5. What to Expect Day-to-Day
You will be cold, always. Your teeth will ache. You will develop an intimate relationship with rust. You will stand on a platform one morning and realize the railing you leaned on yesterday has become a flowering trellis and is now blooming with tiny metallic flowers that ring like bells when the wind moves through them.

You will watch a tenement block slowly forget it is a building and begin to swim.

You will wake up one night to the sound of an entire district exhaling, and for a moment you will understand what it dreams of becoming.

And you will keep living there anyway, because the city is patient, and ambitious, and it is still learning how to be loved.

Bring humility.  
Bring wonder.  
Bring whatever it is you are willing to have slowly transformed into something new.

The Third Ascent is not a place you visit.  
It is a place that begins visiting you the moment you arrive, and it will still be editing you long after you think you’ve left.
