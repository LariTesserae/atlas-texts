**How to Prepare for the Weeping Kiln**

You are not merely visiting a place. You are choosing to step inside something that is still deciding whether it wants you to exist inside it. Treat the journey the way a deep-sea diver treats a descent into an unknown trench: with obsessive respect for the medium you will be moving through.

### 1. Mental and Spiritual Preparation (most important)
The Kiln is aware. Not sentient in a human way, but *remembering*. It studies observers the same way it studies everything else that stays wet long enough. 

- Spend at least two weeks before departure practicing radical silence. Speak as little as possible. Learn to notice the sound of your own thoughts. The Kiln will amplify whatever psychic noise you bring; anxious people tend to leave behind anxious faces in the stone.
- Decide in advance what parts of your personal history you are willing to have immortalized in mineral relief. The place has a habit of choosing the memories you least want preserved.
- Prepare a single, clean intention. Vague tourists are gently but firmly edited out of the Kiln’s ongoing story. The swarms can sense uncertainty the way sharks sense blood.

### 2. Physical Gear (ruthlessly minimal)
Everything you bring will eventually be colonized by water, moss, or memory. Overpacking is punished.

**Clothing:**
- Three layers of tightly woven, water-shedding wool treated with natural lanolin. Never cotton. Cotton becomes a sponge and then a culture medium.
- A hooded cloak of oiled black canvas that can double as a groundsheet. The hood must be deep enough to keep the constant drizzle out of your eyes; the light refraction through water droplets can cause disorienting strobing.
- Soft-soled boots with excellent traction. The black basalt becomes almost cartilaginous when wet. Many visitors break ankles in the first hour.
- Thin leather gloves. The swarms sometimes land on bare skin and taste you. Their mouths are too small to hurt, but the psychological effect is deeply unsettling.

**Protection from the Light:**
The single shaft of white sun is merciless. It does not just illuminate; it *fixes*. Bring:
- A wide-brimmed hat of the same oiled canvas, lined with mirrored mylar on the underside of the brim. This reflects some of the spear-light back upward and prevents your retinas from cooking.
- A pair of smoked-quartz goggles (not glass; glass sweats and fogs permanently). The quartz must be naturally flawed; perfect crystal attracts the mirror-bright insects, which will coat the lenses trying to mate with their own reflections.

**Water Management:**
You will be surrounded by water and yet constantly dehydrated. The weeping water is so cold it shocks the stomach.
- A wide-mouthed canteen made of unglazed ceramic. Hang it openly so the outside grows the same protective lichen the locals cultivate. Drink only water that has passed through at least one communal cistern; the town’s filtration system is biological and strangely intelligent.
- A small oilcloth bag of dried seaweed and mineral salts. Your body will leach electrolytes through your skin faster than you expect.

**The Breathing Apparatus:**
The geothermal exhalations are unpredictable. When the ground chooses to breathe, the temperature can swing forty degrees in six seconds. Carry:
- A long wool scarf (at least three meters) that can be wrapped around mouth and nose in seconds. The locals call this “becoming a chimney.”
- A small tin of thick lanolin mixed with powdered quartz. Rub it on your lips and eyelids before sleeping. The vapor can cause microscopic burns that only appear days later.

### 3. Psychological Tools
- A small, perfectly smooth black river stone from somewhere *else*. You will be asked to trade it. Do not bring anything you cannot bear to lose.
- A notebook made of stone-paper (thin sheets of real slate bound with copper wire). Normal paper dissolves into pulp within forty hours. Write sparingly. The act of recording is noticed.
- One small, sharp chisel and a leather mallet. If the stone begins to remember your face while you sleep, you may need to edit it. The locals will not help you with this. It is considered poor manners to interfere with someone else’s memory, even your own.

### 4. Behavioral Rules (non-negotiable)
- Never raise your voice above a murmur. The Kiln interprets loud noise as an attempt to override its own becoming.
- Do not kill the glittering swarms, even if they crawl into your mouth while you sleep. They are the memory-tenders. Harming them causes the stone to remember you as a wound.
- When the ground exhales scalding vapor, bow. The locals say the vapor carries the dead who have not yet been fully forgotten. To turn away from them is to ask the Kiln to forget you in return. You may not like what replaces the memory.
- If you see a new house or stair already half-grown in the rock, do not enter it. Some rooms are being prepared for people who have not yet arrived. Others are being prepared for people who have not yet left.

### 5. What to Expect Emotionally
You will feel watched, but not by the inhabitants. You will feel *pre-watched*—as though the Kiln has already seen the version of you that leaves and is now comparing that future self to the damp creature currently standing in the spear-light.

Most people experience a period of ego dissolution around day four. This is normal. Some never fully come back together. The ones who return with the cleanest minds are usually those who accepted that the Kiln was not a place but a long, bright, freezing question, and that their presence was part of the answer.

Bring nothing you are not willing to have turned into poetry written in water and mineral across black stone for the rest of time.

The Kiln does not hate visitors.  
It simply has no concept of temporary things.  
Everything that stays long enough becomes part of its memory.  
Everything that leaves was always going to leave.

Pack accordingly.
