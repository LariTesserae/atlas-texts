**How an ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for the Underfoundry**

You are not visiting a planet. You are visiting a single, continent-sized *mechanism* that has already reached its final, eternal form. Think of it as stepping inside the working guts of a machine the size of Australia that has never known an “outside.” Preparation is therefore less about survival gear and more about psychological, ontological, and sensory engineering. This place will try to convince you that you are the only broken thing in it.

### 1. Mental & Emotional Preparation (Most Important)
- **Accept that you will feel blasphemously organic.** The Underfoundry has never known flesh, dirt, or randomness. Your breathing, heartbeat, sweat, and scent will register to its trillion silent custodians as *faults* that must be monitored or corrected. Many travelers report an overwhelming, irrational shame at simply being biological. Prepare for this. Decide in advance that your messiness is not a moral failure.
- **Cultivate profound stillness.** The machines move in perfect, ancient choreography. Any sudden movement, loud voice, or emotional outburst can trigger a micro-pause across entire districts. Some travelers have caused continent-scale “hiccups” that took days to dampen. Practice hours of motionless meditation before you go. Learn to breathe without sighing.
- **Rehearse existential humility.** This world is *finished*. It does not need you, want you, or even fully perceive you as more than a temporary moisture leak with opinions. Ego death is not optional here; it is ambient.

### 2. Physical & Medical Preparation
- **Humidity management is everything.** The air is 98–100 % relative humidity at a constant 31–34 °C. You will never be dry again. Bring:
  - Multiple sets of ultra-breathable, quick-dry merino or synthetic underlayers that can handle perpetual dampness without growing mildew.
  - Medical-grade antifungal and antibacterial powders and creams (the local corrosion products and constant moisture love human skin).
  - Silicone-based lubricants for *your own joints*—knees, hips, shoulders—because the air will leach every natural oil from your body within hours.
- **Vision and optics.** The sourceless gray light is kind to the eyes but destroys depth perception after a few hours. Pack high-contrast micro-LED headlamps with red and amber modes (the machines largely ignore red/amber wavelengths). Also bring polarized goggles; the constant film of condensation and mineral “flowers” creates shifting glare.
- **Respiratory.** The mist contains microscopic droplets of machine oil, mineral salts, and nanoparticulate lubricant. A good half-face respirator with P100 filters and replaceable cartridges is non-negotiable for more than a day visit. Long-term travelers sometimes get “foundry lung”—a non-infectious but extremely unpleasant mineral accretion in the alveoli.
- **Thermal regulation.** Although the temperature is stable, the constant wet warmth will make you feel both feverish and chilled. Layering is useless; instead bring clothing with excellent wicking and one high-quality vapor-barrier outer shell so you can trap a micro-climate against your body.

### 3. Gear & Tools (Minimalist but Specific)
- **Footwear:** Soft, seamless boots with zero metal (the machines are magnetically and galvanically sensitive). The floors are gently undulating iron and copper; they are slippery with perpetual lubricant films and condensation. Cleated rubber or nanomolecular grip soles are ideal.
- **Scent management:** The fauna react to organic volatiles. Use completely odorless soaps, deodorants, and lotions. Some veterans coat exposed skin in a thin layer of medical-grade dimethicone (pure silicone oil) to seal in human smells.
- **Tether and marking system:** Because angles do not always add to 360° and corridors can be longer on the return, you must impose your own geometry. Bring:
  - 500 m of ultra-thin reflective monofilament line with silent magnetic anchors (never use string; it rots instantly).
  - Photoluminescent paint sticks that leave marks invisible to the machines’ spectra.
  - A high-precision inertial measurement unit (IMU) logger. GPS, compasses, and most electromagnetic tools fail or give nonsense here.
- **Sleep system:** The machines never stop. There is no night. Pack a fully sealed, sound-dampening bivy that can be anchored between two motionless titans. Earplugs are useless against the subsonic hydraulic *thuds* that you feel in your skeleton every few hours; you must learn to sleep inside the rhythm instead of fighting it.

### 4. Philosophical & Ritual Preparation
- Decide what you are there *to witness*, not to do. The Underfoundry punishes curiosity that seeks purpose. Travelers who arrive looking for “the control room” or “the builders” tend to break down. Those who come simply to *be inside perfect ongoingness* sometimes achieve a strange, metallic peace.
- Learn the Droplet Meditation. Sit motionless for at least thirty minutes every day before departure. Listen for the single clear note of a droplet striking resonant metal. When you hear it, note the three-microsecond pause it creates inside your own mind. This is the closest a human can come to speaking the native language of the place.
- Prepare a small, perfectly machined metal token (brass, copper, or iron) with no markings. Many experienced travelers leave one behind on a high pipe as an offering of *imperfection*. The machines will eventually polish it into nonexistence, but the gesture seems to grant a kind of safe passage.

### 5. What to Expect Emotionally
The first six hours feel like awe.  
The next two days feel like religious dread.  
After that, something shifts. You begin to hear the continent-scale heartbeat in your own pulse. You may catch yourself synchronizing your blinks with the six-legged maintenance drone that passes every 41 minutes and 17 seconds. Some people never want to leave. Others flee screaming after realizing that the sky is just another machined ceiling twenty kilometers up, and that even the largest mechanisms up there are patiently waiting for needs that will never arise.

Bring no cameras that use shutters.  
Bring no music.  
Bring no plans that last longer than the next droplet fall.

And whatever you do, when the entire district around you suddenly freezes for three microseconds and every machine on every surface turns its sensors toward you at once—

Do not run.

Simply exhale, very softly, and wait for the single clear note that always follows.

Then bow, because you are the only thing here that is still allowed to change.
