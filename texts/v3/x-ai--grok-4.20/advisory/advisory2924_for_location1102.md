**Preparation Guide for a Human Multiverse Traveler Visiting the Weeping Tier**

You are not visiting a city. You are visiting an immense, warm, iron lung that has been breathing for longer than your species has had writing. Prepare accordingly. This place does not accommodate visitors; it slowly *incorporates* them. The difference is measured in centuries, but the process begins the moment your boots touch wet iron.

### 1. Mental and Temporal Preparation
- Accept that your visit will feel like stepping into someone else’s slow, patient dream. Time here is geological. A “short walk” between districts can take weeks if you let the scale intimidate you. Bring a mindset that is calm, observant, and capable of long silences.
- The Tier does not do urgency. If you arrive anxious, hurried, or goal-oriented, the humidity and the sound will leach it out of you over days until you either adapt or begin to rust emotionally. Some travelers never leave because they forget why they wanted to.
- Keep a private journal or recorder. The longer you stay, the more the place will feel *correct* and everywhere else will feel intolerably dry and fast. You will need evidence later that other kinds of worlds exist.

### 2. Physical Protection and Clothing
- **Waterproofing is meaningless.** Everything is *already* wet. Instead, bring materials that *enjoy* being wet or that can breathe through constant condensation.
  - Best outer layer: tightly woven oiled canvas or modern synthetic “eternal” fabrics that resist mineral staining (think heavy waxed cotton or advanced polymer blends). Dark rust-red, black, or deep lichen green helps you disappear into the architecture.
  - Inner layers: moisture-wicking synthetics that dry extremely fast when you find a heat vent. Wool is surprisingly good here; it continues to insulate even when soaked.
  - Footwear: high waterproof boots with aggressive, replaceable soles. The iron is perpetually slick with condensate and mineral films. Expect to replace soles every 4–6 months of continuous exposure.
- **Full-face respirator or high-quality half-mask with replaceable filters.** The air is breathable but saturated with ozone, ferrous particulates, limestone dust, and the faint sweet-rot smell of the respiratory fauna. After two weeks your lungs will begin to feel like they are growing fur. Change filters religiously.
- **Eye protection.** Goggles or sealed glasses. The constant drip and airborne micro-droplets will otherwise turn your eyes into weeping mirrors within days.
- **Ear protection that can be worn long-term.** The sound is not painfully loud, but it is *pervasive*: a continent-sized machine running at 4 beats per minute. Many travelers develop migraines or tinnitus after a month. Custom-molded plugs with pass-through for conversation are ideal.
- **No cotton in large quantities.** It never dries and eventually smells like sour iron.

### 3. Biological and Medical Kit (Non-Negotiable)
- Antifungal powder and cream in industrial quantities. The fungal lattices that replace vegetation will happily colonize human skin, hair, and lungs.
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics and antifungals for respiratory use. The respiratory megafauna shed microscopic chitin and metallic dust; breathing it long-term causes a condition travelers call “red lung.”
- Desiccant packets, silica gel, and moisture-eating devices. You will need to keep at least *some* of your equipment dry or it will simply become part of the Tier.
- High-calorie, low-volume rations that do not mind being damp. The Tier has no agriculture. You will trade with human enclaves or scavenge from the slow machines (risky).
- Calcium supplements and joint-support medication. The constant vibration and humidity leach minerals from your bones surprisingly fast.

### 4. Navigation and Movement
- **Never trust maps.** The city is slowly settling, calving, and rebuilding itself. A avenue that existed last year may now be a 300-meter-deep canyon filled with migrating bell-things.
- Bring a rugged inertial navigation device *and* old-fashioned surveying tools (compass, clinometer, plumb bob). The magnetic field is stable but heavily distorted by the continent-spanning engines.
- Learn to read the respiratory fauna. Their migration routes are the only reliable “roads” that last longer than a decade. If you see thousands of piston-millipedes moving along a girder the size of a highway, follow at a respectful distance. They know where the structure is strongest.
- Grappling gear, climbing harness, and long ropes are essential. Vertical movement is often faster than horizontal. Many “streets” are now vertical faces.

### 5. Interaction Protocol with the Tier
- **Do not try to repair anything.** The Weeping Tier treats repair and breakage as the same sacred process. Attempting to “fix” a machine will draw the attention of the copper-colored dismantler-scavengers. They are not hostile, but they are thorough. They have been known to disassemble entire traveler camps that lingered too long in one place “repairing” things.
- Move at the Tier’s tempo whenever possible. Running, shouting, or using loud, fast machinery marks you as temporary and therefore eligible for incorporation. Many long-term residents speak in low, slow voices and move with deliberate, almost ritualistic care.
- Respect the respiratory creatures. They are not pets, gods, or monsters. They are organs. Touching or interfering with a migrating flock of bell-things can cause localized “immune responses” — sudden changes in humidity, temperature, or steam pressure that have killed careless travelers.
- If you find human communities (there are several, some thousands of years old), be humble. They have gone native to varying degrees. Some have iron filaments growing in their bones. Speak softly and accept their offered water. It is distilled from the Tier’s own tears and is strangely nourishing.

### 6. Equipment That Will Survive
- Stainless steel, titanium, high-quality polymers, and glass. Almost everything else eventually becomes raw material.
- A good, slow mechanical watch (no batteries — they corrode in weeks).
- A brass or bronze notebook with waterproof synthetic pages and graphite sticks. Ink dissolves.
- Small, extremely sharp hand tools. The Tier respects useful mass. A well-made wrench has been known to be incorporated into architecture and venerated by locals for decades.

### 7. What to Expect Emotionally
The first week is awe and slight horror at the scale and wetness.  
The first month is a deep, bone-level tiredness and the growing suspicion that speed was always a mistake.  
The first year (if you stay that long) is the risk of falling in love with a rhythm that no longer needs you. Many travelers choose to remain, allowing themselves to be gently dismantled and built into balconies or ventilation shafts. The Tier is not cruel. It simply has no concept of *separate*.

You will leave (if you leave) smelling permanently of hot iron and wet limestone. Your dreams will carry the sound of distant valves opening and closing for the rest of your life. Sometimes, on humid nights in other worlds, you will wake with the absolute certainty that something the size of a cathedral is slowly migrating across your ceiling, and that it is only being polite by staying invisible.

Bring respect.  
Bring patience.  
Bring the knowledge that you are temporary, and that the Weeping Tier is not.

It has been seasoning for a very long time.  
It is in no hurry to stop.
