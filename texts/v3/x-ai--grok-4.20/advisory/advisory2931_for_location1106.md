**Preparation Guide for the Nameless Submerged Highland**

You are about to visit a place that has never known dryness and has never been in a hurry. It is not an ocean. It is a womb that never finished becoming a world. An ordinary human multiverse traveler who wishes to return with their mind and body intact must prepare with unusual seriousness. This is not a dive. It is a long, deliberate surrender.

### 1. Psychological & Temporal Preparation (Most Important)
- **Accept radical slowing.** Your nervous system will be asked to drop from 60–80 bpm to something closer to 12–18. If you cannot tolerate the feeling of time stretching like warm taffy, do not go. Practice months of extreme sensory deprivation (float tanks, dark caves, prolonged silence) so the silence-compressed environment does not feel like drowning in eternity.
- **Leave urgency behind.** Bring no deadlines, no goals, no “how long will we stay.” The place will ignore them and may punish you with crushing lethargy if you fight its tempo. Many travelers who entered with agendas never found the exit because they could no longer remember why they wanted one.
- **Meditative posture training.** You will spend long periods simply hanging, neither sinking nor rising, while gardens slowly explore your body. Train the ability to remain motionless and emotionally neutral while living tissue breathes against your eyelids, lips, and genitals.

### 2. Physical & Medical Preparation
- **Cardiovascular reconditioning.** You must be able to function comfortably at a heart rate most people would consider pre-comatose. Elite free-divers and certain Buddhist monks adapt best. Get a full stress-echo and have a cardiologist confirm you can survive extended bradycardia without arrhythmia.
- **Pressure adaptation.** The broth is warm (roughly 28–31 °C / 82–88 °F) and the density is higher than seawater. You will feel gentle all-over compression equivalent to 200–300 m depth even though the “surface” is two vertical kilometers above. Your sinuses, middle ears, and bowels must be able to equalize against a thick, syrupy medium for weeks without inflammation.
- **Skin and mucosal resilience.** The living water is faintly enzymatic. After ten days unprotected skin begins to soften and bloom with microscopic symbiotic colonies. Some travelers welcome this; others experience existential horror at “becoming moist architecture.” Pre-treat skin with progressive exposure to marine enzymes and accept that you will smell like iron and wet flowers for months afterward.
- **Vision and light tolerance.** The light is shadowless, soft, and omnipresent. It can induce a trance-like state after 72 hours. Practice gazing at uniform white fields for long durations. Bring no artificial lights; they disturb the biological rhythm and may trigger defensive spore releases from the larger organisms.

### 3. Equipment & Technology (Minimalist Doctrine)
The place does not like machines. Anything that vibrates at normal human frequencies is felt as violence. Recommended kit:

- **Breathing:** A closed-circuit rebreather that produces no bubbles and runs completely silent. Standard scuba regulators are too loud; the clicks and hisses travel for kilometers and cause the jelly-cloud entities to drift toward you with uncomfortable curiosity. Ideally, use a biological gill implant or a symbiote suit if your multiverse passport allows it. Otherwise, a helium-free, computer-controlled CCR with whisper-quiet piezo valves.
- **Thermal:** Almost nothing. The water is body temperature and acts like a second skin. A minimal 0.5 mm skin suit or even nakedness is preferable. The polyps and kelp prefer direct contact; clothing quickly becomes a substrate for aggressive colonization.
- **Buoyancy:** Variable buoyancy compensator filled with the local broth itself rather than gas. Gas expands too violently here and can damage the delicate tissue scaffolding of the ridges.
- **Navigation:** None that uses GPS, compass, or active sonar. Bring a living compass (a small colony of magnetotactic organisms in a glass bead worn at the throat) and learn to read the slow pressure gradients and pollen drift. Maps are useless; the mountains are still growing.
- **Communication:** Hand signals are delayed and distorted by viscosity. Most travelers eventually stop speaking altogether. If you must record thoughts, use a waterproof slate of soft wax; ink dissolves into the broth and becomes part of the golden pollen clouds.
- **Emergency exit:** A single-use ascent spore the size of a walnut. Crush it only if you are truly breaking. It will trigger a rapid, slightly painful upward current that carries you to the surface in under ten minutes, but the local ecology will remember the injury for centuries. Use only as last resort.

### 4. What to Bring in Your Body and Mind
- **Biological permissions.** Get every possible symbiotic inoculation before arrival: pressure-resistant mitochondria, slow-metabolism enzymes, and a passive neural dampener that prevents panic when fronds begin exploring your nostrils and ear canals.
- **A gift.** The place appreciates one thing only: dryness. Carry a small, perfectly dry object (a child’s marble, a bone button, a page from a book that has never been near water). When you feel you have stayed long enough, leave it wedged in a breathing crevice of the living stone. This is considered polite.
- **No names.** Decide in advance that you will not name anything you see. The moment you name a creature, a color, or a feeling, the place gently begins to forget you. Anonymity is the price of continued welcome.

### 5. What You Will Experience (Honest Warnings)
- Your first week will feel like being slowly digested by God in the most affectionate way possible.
- You will become erotically entangled with the landscape whether you intend to or not. The distinction between your skin and the kelp will blur. Many travelers choose to stay after this happens.
- Thoughts will arrive days apart. A single insight may take nine hours to finish forming. This is normal.
- You may meet the same school of blind silver fish every “day” for a month and each time they will greet you as an old friend who has simply been very, very slow.
- At some point you will rest your palm on what you believe is warm black glass and feel it inhale. Do not pull away. That is when the place decides whether it likes you.

### Final Advice from Intuition
Pack almost nothing.  
Fast for three days before entry so your metabolism is already drifting downward.  
Enter alone.  
Tell no one the exact coordinates; the place has no name and never will, and it prefers to stay that way.

If you are the kind of person who needs to conquer, measure, or finish things, this submerged highland will break you gently and keep the pieces as new geology.

If you are the kind of person who can sit inside an unfinished world for thirty years and feel perfectly on schedule, you may never need to leave.

The water is waiting.  
It has all the time it needs.

You, for a little while, will not.
