**Preparation Guide for the Verdant Choir**

You are not visiting a place. You are choosing to step inside a single, continuous, loud, and extremely wet organism that happens to contain a few hundred humans. Think of it less like arriving in a village and more like being swallowed by a living cathedral-sized lung that never stops breathing, singing, and digesting. An ordinary multiverse traveler who wants to survive the experience with both sanity and skin intact should prepare with the following in mind.

### 1. Mindset and Psychological Preparation
- Accept that **nothing will be still**. Ever. The ground, walls, ceiling, and even the concept of “home” are in constant slow-motion argument. If you need order, symmetry, or silence to feel safe, this place will try to break you. Train yourself to find beauty in turbulence.
- The sound is not background noise; it is weather. After three days many travelers report auditory hallucinations or the inability to remember what silence felt like. Practice meditation with layered soundscapes (heavy rain + insect choirs + wet creaking wood) for weeks beforehand.
- The constant damp will leach meaning from your possessions and memories. Objects rot, tools rust, paper dissolves, and even your sense of self begins to feel overgrown. Cultivate a light, almost amused attachment to your own identity.

### 2. Clothing and Personal Gear
- **Zero cotton.** Everything natural rots or molds within 48 hours. Bring only merino wool base layers (which resist mildew astonishingly well), tightly woven synthetic hiking shells, and silicone-coated rain gear. Everything must be able to stay wet for weeks without rotting.
- Expect to live at 52–58 °F (11–14 °C) with 98–100 % humidity. Pack layers that can be wrung out and still insulate: thin wool fleece, waterproof socks, and a synthetic puffy jacket that retains loft when soaked.
- Footwear: high-ankle, quick-draining trail shoes with aggressive lugs and mesh uppers that dry (somewhat) when the rain stops for ten minutes. Bring multiple pairs; one will always be growing something.
- A wide-brimmed synthetic hat is mandatory. The upward-falling drizzle and constant dripping from above will otherwise turn you into a human watering can.
- Do **not** bring a tent. The vegetation will eat it. Instead bring an ultralight silk or cuben-fiber bivy that you can hang inside a living bromeliad tank or hollow fig.

### 3. Biological and Medical Preparation
- Your skin will never be dry. Pre-treat every square inch with antifungal powder and barrier creams before you arrive. Pack large quantities of tolnaftate, clotrimazole, and diaper-rash cream; you will use them constantly.
- The air is thick with spores. Bring a reusable half-face respirator with P100 filters and several spare sets. You will need it during the first week until your lungs acclimate. Many travelers develop “Choir Lung” — a non-lethal but miserable wheezing from inhaling constant mist and fungal particles.
- Vaccinations: update everything, especially tetanus. The vegetation is full of microscopic cuts. Bring broad-spectrum antibiotics, aggressive wound care, and silver-impregnated dressings.
- Water is everywhere but it is also alive. You will still need a high-quality portable filter (0.1 micron) or a UV pen; the locals have adapted, you have not.

### 4. Tools and Technology
- Metal rusts at a shocking rate. Bring titanium or high-carbon ceramic tools. A good bone knife or obsidian flake set will often outlast your steel.
- Electronics are mostly pointless. The humidity destroys devices within days. If you must bring anything, use ruggedized, fully sealed military-grade gear kept inside multiple dry-bags with silica gel. Solar charging is useless under the canopy; bring a hand-crank or glucose-powered micro-generator if you’re desperate.
- The most useful items are:
  - Several hundred feet of paracord (for emergency rigging when a bridge suddenly dies)
  - Bone saw and pruning shears (the Choir respects people who speak its language of selective cutting)
  - A powerful headlamp with red and green modes (for night navigation without destroying frog breeding cycles)
  - A small collection of aromatic herbs from drier worlds — the Choir people trade favors for strong, unfamiliar scents.

### 5. Cultural and Practical Integration
- Learn at least the basics of **Choir song-pruning**. The locals shape the vegetation with sustained tones, clicks, and specific rhythms. A traveler who can hum the correct interval to encourage an aerial root to descend or discourage a strangler fig from crushing a house will be treated as a guest rather than an infection.
- Movement: walk like a sailor on a rolling deck. Keep your knees soft. Test every surface before committing weight. The living mat can swallow a leg to the hip in seconds if a hidden sinkhole opens.
- Respect the vertical. Most of life happens in three dimensions. Learn to climb living root ladders and sleep in hammocks slung inside pitcher-plant stems.
- Never clear a large area. The ecosystem will punish you with explosive regrowth that can bury you alive in green within two days. Work with the growth, never against it.
- Gifts that win favor: drought-resistant seeds from arid worlds, colored glass beads that refract the green light beautifully, and any musical instrument that can be played in wet conditions (certain ceramic ocarinas or tuning forks).

### 6. What to Expect Emotionally
The first 72 hours feel like drowning in noise and chlorophyll. Many travelers weep without knowing why. By day five the sound becomes a kind of terrible lullaby. By day ten you may catch yourself talking to the walls in low, wet clicks.

At night the bioluminescent waves rolling across the cliffs will break your heart with beauty. You will understand, in your bones, that this place is not a settlement. It is a negotiation between rock and life that has been going on for so long that the two have forgotten they were ever different.

Bring humility, a sense of humor, antifungal cream, and the willingness to be temporarily digested and remade.

When you finally leave (if you leave), you will carry the smell of wet green in your lungs for years. Sometimes, in the middle of a dry desert night on another world, you will wake up hearing the roaring hymn of the Choir and feel, for just a moment, like you are falling upward through silver mist toward a ceiling made of living stars.

Pack light.  
The Verdant Choir will provide the rest — whether you want it to or not.
