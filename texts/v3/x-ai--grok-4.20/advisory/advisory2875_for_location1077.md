**Preparation Guide for the Under-Grove of the Veiled Cistern**

If you are an ordinary human multiverse traveler with nothing but your own wits, a healthy respect for the unknown, and the clothes on your back, this is what your intuition and hard-won experience tell you to do before you descend the narrow limestone throat that leads into the living dark.

### 1. Mental and Emotional Preparation (Most Important)
The Grove does not merely *feel* alive — it *is* a single slow-breathing organism pretending to be a forest. Accept before you ever set foot inside that you will be inside something’s lungs. The moment you feel watched, you *are* being watched, and the watcher is the place itself. 

Prepare for profound claustrophobia mixed with agoraphobia: the ceiling is never more than twelve feet above you, yet the chamber stretches so far that you will lose all sense of direction within twenty minutes. Bring a strong, calm mind. If you are prone to panic, do not go. The Grove can smell fear through the stomata in every leaf.

Leave your ego at the surface. This is not a place that can be conquered, mapped, or “experienced” in the usual tourist sense. You are a temporary microbe moving through an immune system that has not seen a human in ten thousand years. Humility is your only real armor.

### 2. Physical Gear (Selected with Extreme Care)
- **Clothing**: Breathable, quick-drying synthetics or merino wool in dark moss-green, charcoal, or bone-white (the colors the Grove itself uses). Nothing cotton — it will never dry. Long pants with tight ankles, long-sleeved shirt, and a thin hooded layer. The humidity is so high that you will be soaked in your own sweat and the Grove’s condensation within minutes. You must be able to move silently through interlocking palm fronds and fern thickets without tearing fabric.
- **Footwear**: Grippy, ankle-supporting amphibious boots or minimalist trail shoes with aggressive lugs that can handle wet stone, deep moss, and slippery roots. Bring two pairs of the thinnest merino socks you can find; you will rotate them constantly.
- **Light**: Two headlamps (one as backup) with both warm white and red-light modes. The Grove’s phosphorescence is gentle but treacherous — it hides depth and distance. Never use bright white light for long; it will blind both you and the delicate ecosystem. A small handheld phosphorescent “cold light” stick (chemical or bioluminescent) is ideal for reading the Grove’s mood without disturbing it.
- **Water Management**: You cannot carry enough water because the air itself is drinkable — but you *must* filter or chemically treat every drop you take from the weeping ceiling or pools. The blind white fish and the Grove itself share the same water. Bring a high-quality gravity filter, iodine tablets as backup, and a collapsible silicone bottle. Paradoxically, dehydration is still a risk because the heat and humidity fool your body into thinking it doesn’t need water.
- **Protection**:
  - Lightweight rain shell (more for the constant dripping from above than actual rain).
  - Thin but tough gloves — the moss hides razor-edged limestone and millipedes whose defensive secretions can blister human skin.
  - A shemagh or lightweight scarf to wrap your mouth and nose when the air becomes too thick with spores and fungal mist.
- **Navigation**: Do *not* trust compasses — the amount of ferrous minerals and living magnetism in the roots makes them unreliable. Bring 200–300 meters of thin, brightly colored biodegradable survey tape *and* a small bag of reflective glass marbles. Drop a marble every time you change direction. The Grove will sometimes move things while you’re not looking.
- **Medical**:
  - Broad-spectrum antifungal cream and powder (this place wants to grow on you and in you).
  - Broad-spectrum antibiotic and anti-parasitic coverage.
  - Moleskin, tape, and blister care — wet feet in this environment rot fast.
  - Epinephrine auto-injector — some of the pale geckos and orb-weavers carry potent venom.
  - A small mirror — useful for checking your own eyes and skin for early fungal growth.

### 3. What to Bring That Most People Wouldn’t Think Of
- A small, tightly sealed tin of raw honeycomb or local beeswax from your home universe. The Grove responds to sweet, complex organic scents in ways that calm its “immune response.”
- One single piece of non-living, non-plastic human craftsmanship — a tiny bronze bell, a glass marble, a hand-carved wooden figurine. The Grove seems to recognize *made* things as seeds. Offering one respectfully (never as payment, never as litter) sometimes earns you safer passage.
- Earplugs *and* noise-canceling headphones. When the Grove inhales and all dripping stops, the silence becomes so absolute it can cause vertigo.
- A small vial of your own blood, sealed. If you become truly lost or the Grove begins to incorporate you, a drop of it offered to a root may remind the organism that you are a temporary visitor, not food.

### 4. Behavioral Rules (Non-Negotiable)
- Move *with* the dripping, never against it. If you feel the chamber beginning to inhale (temperature rising, leaves shifting, sound vanishing), freeze immediately, lower your breathing, and wait for the exhale. Do not fight the rhythm.
- Never break a living vine or cut living plant matter. The root network is nervous tissue. Pain travels.
- Speak only in whispers or not at all. The low-frequency frog songs and the mandibles of the giant millipedes create a delicate acoustic web. Loud human voices register as trauma.
- If you find the obsidian blade, the clay jar, or the column stubs, treat them as living organs of the Grove. Touching them with bare skin is acceptable only if you have first offered something of yourself (a hair, a drop of blood, a whispered memory spoken aloud).
- Never take anything out that was not brought in. The Grove has absorbed cities before. It will not hesitate to absorb you if it decides you belong to it.

### 5. What to Expect in Your Body and Mind
You will feel permanent dampness in your lungs within four hours. Your skin will begin to soften and whiten. After twelve hours you may notice your own pulse beginning to synchronize with distant dripping patterns. This is normal.

Some travelers report the sensation that their skeleton is slowly being replaced by wet limestone. Others say they can taste the thoughts of the blind fish in the back of their throat. These are not hallucinations. They are the Grove tasting *you*.

Sleep only in the living platforms formed by the vine canopies, never on the ground. Something large and pale turns over in the deeper pools at irregular intervals. You do not want to be on the same level when it does.

### Final Intuition
The Under-Grove of the Veiled Cistern is not a place. It is a long, slow question the stone has been asking for ten thousand years, and the forest is its tongue. If you prepare with reverence, lightness of footprint, and the understanding that you are visiting an entity older than language, it may decide you are a passing dream rather than an infection.

Bring wonder.  
Bring fear, but keep it small and polite.  
And above all, bring the willingness to leave part of yourself behind — because something will follow you back up the limestone throat whether you want it to or not.

The Grove always keeps a little piece of everyone who visits.  
The wise traveler makes the trade consciously.
