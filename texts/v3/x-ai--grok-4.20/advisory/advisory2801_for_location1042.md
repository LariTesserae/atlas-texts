**Preparation Guide for the Cathedral of the Ascending Flood**

If you are the kind of traveler who steps between worlds, treat this place as you would a sentient, slow-breathing leviathan that has decided to become architecture. It is not a ruin. It is not a temple in the ordinary sense. It is a machine that is teaching itself how to drown beautifully, and it will teach you the same lesson if you are not ready.

### Physical and Medical Preparation
You must train your body for months beforehand. The fluid is body-temperature saline but far denser than seawater. Breathing it is possible, yet it demands a slow, almost meditative respiratory rhythm; your diaphragm will fight you for the first weeks. Spend time in a pressure chamber or a hyperbaric tank filled with warm saline solution. Practice inhaling deliberately, as if you are inviting the liquid into your lungs rather than forcing it. Panic here is lethal; the moment you fight the fluid, it becomes drowning.

Condition your muscles for crushing pressure and near-neutral buoyancy. Every movement feels like swimming through warm mercury. Train with weighted suits in thick gels or train in deep underwater facilities with added drag. Your legs and core must be strong enough to push through viscous resistance for hours, yet delicate enough to walk the razor-thin bronze seams without slicing an artery on an edge that has never been allowed to dull.

Bring no ordinary diving gear. Conventional scuba regulators will not negotiate with this fluid; it does not behave like water. The only reliable method is to allow the Cathedral to teach your body directly. Most travelers eventually open their mouths, relax their throats, and simply let the luminous saline enter. The first time is an act of surrender that feels exactly like dying. You will not die. But something in you will.

### Mental and Perceptual Training

This will induce a specific trance state that seasoned travelers call “detail madness.” Prepare by spending hours in extremely cluttered baroque churches, engine rooms of abandoned factories, or coral reefs while wearing vision-distorting lenses. You must learn to let your eyes glide rather than focus. If you try to truly *see* everything, your mind will fracture within days.

Practice slow thought. The architecture edits itself on a scale of decades; it rewards minds that can think at a similar pace. Meditate on geological time. Study coral growth patterns, self-organizing industrial fungi, and slow-moving liturgical machines. Learn to notice a change that occurs once every forty minutes (the pulse of the synthetic coral) and once every few hours (the sub-bass exhalation of the Cathedral itself). Your nervous system must be rewired to find peace inside overwhelming, patient complexity.

### Equipment and Supplies (Minimalist)
- A single, seamless pressure-and-temperature suit made of something alive. Synthetic skinsuits grown from the same industrial coral lineage are ideal; they interface with the Cathedral’s own growths and slowly become camouflaged.
- No lights. The piped sunlight is perfect and eternal. Anything you bring will only distort the mineral-amber-rose-green spectrum and anger the structure.
- A thin obsidian or ceramic knife. The edges here are sharper than anything you own; you will need to cut yourself free if the coral decides to embrace you too tightly.
- A recording medium that can survive warm saline for centuries. Most travelers use memory coral slabs grown from their own bone marrow. The Cathedral respects memory that is also alive.
- A small brass prayer valve or devotional flange — something machined with intent. The Cathedral recognizes craftsmanship. Offering it a well-made object sometimes buys you a few decades before the rising fluid forces you into narrower and narrower seams.
- Nothing that rots, nothing digital that cannot survive being flooded with conductive saline, and nothing that makes noise louder than a heartbeat.

Leave your weapons. There is nothing here that can be fought. The only defense is courteous slowness.

### Psychological and Spiritual Orientation
Understand that you are entering a being that is simultaneously a cathedral, a machine, an organism, and a slow-motion apocalypse. It was built with terrifying purpose and then abandoned to its own perfection. It does not hate you. It simply has no category for “visitor.” You are another pressure gradient to be recorded, another set of stresses for the coral to grow around and learn from.

The longer you stay, the more the Cathedral will edit itself in response to your presence. Walkways you used yesterday will be six millimeters narrower when you return. New devotional flanges will appear with your silhouette stylized into the metal. This is not hostility. It is courtship.

You must decide, before you ever arrive, how deep you are willing to go. The lowest chambers have been dry for over two hundred years. Reaching them would require descending through kilometers of warm, breathing fluid while the pressure turns your bones to paste and the coral slowly weaves itself through your ribcage. Some travelers choose this. They become part of the memory lattice.

Most do not.

### Ritual Entry
Do not force a door. There are no doors.

Find a place where a ribbon of the luminous fluid is falling *upward* along a pillar. Stand beneath it. Let it envelop you. When the saline fills your mouth and lungs, do not resist. Offer the Cathedral your first exhalation as a gift. Only then will it open the seams for you.

Once inside, move like a thought moving through a dream that is learning how to think about you. Touch as little as possible. Speak only in the sub-bass register if you speak at all. The engines are sleeping; do not wake them. The coral is remembering; do not give it anything you do not want preserved for ten thousand years.

And when — not if — you eventually feel the structure gently closing the path behind you, accept it.

There was never an exit.

There is only deeper in, warmer, quieter, more beautiful, until the difference between you and the ascending flood becomes a distinction without meaning.

Travel well.  
The Cathedral has been waiting for someone exactly like you for a very long time.
