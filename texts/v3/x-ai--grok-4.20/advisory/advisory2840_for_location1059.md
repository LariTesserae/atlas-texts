**How to Prepare for Visiting the Hollow of Fixed Ash**

Pack as though you are entering a place that has already decided it does not need you. The Hollow is not hostile, but it is profoundly *finished*. It has reached a state of perfect, sterile equilibrium, and your presence is an impertinence it tolerates with glacial indifference. Every preparation should therefore be oriented toward minimum disruption, maximum self-containment, and the quieting of your own nervous system.

### Physical Preparation

**Clothing and Skin**  
Wear loose, breathable layers in matte bone, charcoal, or pale ochre. Nothing bright, nothing that rustles. Natural fibers or high-temperature technical synthetics that can handle constant 50–65 °C dry heat without outgassing. A lightweight hooded robe or kaftan that can be drawn completely over the head is ideal; the Hollow responds better to silhouettes that do not scream “alive.”  

Cover every centimeter of skin. The air is so dry that sweat evaporates before it can form, which means you desiccate without realizing it. Use a heavy barrier balm (lanolin, beeswax, or a high-grade dimethicone cream) on lips, nostrils, eyelids, and any exposed skin. Reapply often. Eyes will feel sandy within minutes; sealed goggles or wraparound glasses with anti-fog treatment are non-negotiable.

**Boots**  
Soft-soled, seamless boots with minimal tread. The glassy grit is razor-sharp in places and transmits every impact straight into the mineral sheet. Loud or aggressive footfalls feel like insults. Many experienced travelers wrap the soles in strips of felt or cloth to muffle sound and reduce abrasion on the stone.

**Hydration and Nutrition**  
You will not feel thirsty until your tongue cracks. Bring a hydration bladder with a drinking tube you can sip from without removing your hood. Electrolyte pastes or salt tablets are essential; the heat is so constant that you lose moisture through respiration even while sitting still.  

Food should be silent and minimal. Soft energy gels, nut butters in squeeze tubes, dried fruit leathers. Anything that crunches or requires chewing with open mouth is inconsiderate. The Hollow does not punish noise, but the silence becomes so absolute that your own chewing begins to feel like vandalism.

### Mental and Sensory Preparation

**The Quiet Discipline**  
Practice silence for at least a week before you go. No music, no podcasts, no casual conversation for several hours a day. Learn to walk, sit, and breathe without unnecessary sound. The Hollow rewards those who can enter its frequency. If you arrive already vibrating with internal chatter, the contrast will be painful.

Meditators, experienced psychonauts, and long-term monastics adapt more easily. If you have never sat motionless for forty minutes without checking your phone, begin training now. The basin’s “breathing” rhythm only becomes perceptible once your own pulse and thoughts have slowed to something approaching geological time.

**Light Discipline**  
The place lives in near-total low light. Train your eyes by spending time in very dim environments. Bring no artificial light sources stronger than a single red LED the size of a lentil. Even a phone screen on lowest brightness feels like a scream here. If you must write notes, do it with a soft graphite pencil on dark paper by feel. Most travelers simply do not record anything while inside.

**Psychological Posture**  
Decide before you arrive that you are a guest who has been permitted to sit inside an ancient meditation that does not include you. Do not expect epiphanies, visions, or messages. The Hollow does not teach. It simply *is*, and that “is-ness” is so complete that it can hollow you out if you fight it.  

Prepare for the peculiar grief that comes when you realize nothing here will ever change because of you. Some people experience a brief but intense existential despair after twenty minutes. Others feel an enormous, bone-deep relief. Both are normal. Have a gentle re-entry plan for when you climb back out of the basin. A quiet companion, warm (but not hot) tea, and soft natural light are helpful.

### Gear Checklist (Minimal and Respectful)

- One change of loose, muted clothing (pre-treated with heat-resistant scentless fabric protector)
- Heavy emollient balm and eye drops (preservative-free)
- Soft-soled boots with optional felt wraps
- Collapsible sit-mat made of neutral-colored wool or high-temp silicone (nothing plastic that crackles)
- Small water bladder (1 liter is usually enough for a 3–4 hour stay)
- Muted red micro-light (only for emergency)
- Earplugs (not to block sound, but to reduce the sound of your own blood and breath if it becomes too loud)
- A small cloth bag of fine ash or powdered ochre from your home world (many leave a pinch as apology for disturbing the balance; the Hollow slowly incorporates it without changing)

Leave behind: cameras, recorders, bright colors, metal that clinks, anything scented, journals with loud pages, expectations of catharsis.

### Final Mindset

You are not here to do anything.  
You are not here to receive anything.  
You are here to be momentarily permitted inside something that has already reached its eternal shape.

If you can sit inside that knowledge without squirming, without narrating your experience to yourself, without trying to “get” something from the place, then the Hollow may allow you to feel its slow, ten-thousand-year lung move once beneath you.

Most people only manage it for a few minutes.  
That is enough.

The basin has been waiting since before your species existed.  
It can wait a little longer while you learn how to be quiet enough to visit.
