**Preparation Guide for a Human Multiverse Traveler Visiting the Mossheart Foundry**

### 1. Mental and Psychological Preparation
Understand first that you are not visiting a *place* so much as entering the interior of a single vast, warm-blooded organism that has learned to digest industry without killing it. The Foundry does not have “weather” or “seasons”; it has *moods*. The closest thing to night is when the phosphorescence dims to a deep emerald and the dripping slows to a meditative tempo. The closest thing to day is when the moss brightens to chartreuse-gold and the flywheels seem to sigh louder.

You must be ready to feel watched by something that is not sentient in the way we are, yet is more aware than any forest. The constant soft roar of water, breath, and creaking metal will press on your nervous system after a few hours. Earplugs will not help; the sound is too low and too omnipresent. Bring something that reminds you of crisp, dry air and sharp boundaries (a small packet of desert sand, a piece of Arctic ice in a vacuum vial, a recording of absolute silence). You will need to touch or listen to it when the Foundry’s oppressive *aliveness* starts to feel like it is slowly licking the inside of your skull.

Expect time dilation. After three subjective days you may discover that only eleven hours have passed on your multiversal chronometer. After two weeks you may feel as though you have lived here for years. The place matures everything it touches, including visitors.

### 2. Physical and Environmental Protection
- **Humidity Management**: The air is 98–100 % saturated at all times and slightly warmer than body temperature. Conventional waterproof clothing will only trap moisture against you until you feel boiled. Instead, wear or carry:
  - Loose, open-weave garments made of synthetic hydrophobic fibers or (better) the local moss-fiber cloth once you can trade for it. The locals’ clothing is spun from dead outer layers of the moss itself and wicks moisture *away* from the body while staying cool.
  - Multiple changes of underclothes in waterproof vacuum bags. You will sweat and condense constantly.
  - A wide-brimmed, completely waterproof hat with a gutter system that channels condensation away from your neck.

- **Skin and Lung Protection**: The moss exudes trace alkaloids and enzymes in its vapor. Most humans develop a faint but permanent greenish tint to their fingernails and a sweet, mossy taste in the back of the throat after a week. Long-term exposure can cause “moss lung” — a benign but uncomfortable thickening of mucus. Carry:
  - A fine-mesh rebreather mask or half-face respirator with replaceable organic filters (activated charcoal will mold instantly here).
  - Barrier cream or oil for your skin. Locals use a distilled moss-wax that smells like warm rain on stone.

- **Footwear**: The moss can be ankle- to waist-deep and is treacherously soft. Ordinary boots will fill with warm water and never dry again. Best options are:
  - Tall, laced gaiters made of oiled canvas or synthetic that seal above the knee, combined with perforated “moss-shoes” the locals make — wooden soles with bronze studs that let water drain while giving grip on wet iron walkways.
  - Spare socks stored in desiccated packets. You will change them multiple times per day.

### 3. Gear and Tools You Must Bring
- **Lighting**: The greenish-gold bioluminescence is beautiful but terrible for fine work or reading. Bring a strong, focused white-light headlamp *and* a set of warm-spectrum oil lanterns that match the locals’ (they become uneasy around harsh white LEDs).
- **Pruning and Maintenance Kit**: A curved bronze sickle (or high-quality steel equivalent that you don’t mind losing to rust) is essential. You will need to cut yourself free of new moss growth every few hours if you stay in one spot.
- **Water Collection and Purification**: Ironically, you should *not* drink the moss-water straight for the first few days. It contains living spores and metabolic byproducts. Bring a compact distiller or at least a high-quality ceramic filter and iodine tablets. After a week your gut biome will have adapted and you can drink the sweet, faintly herbal water the locals tap.
- **Trade Goods**: The Mossheart people value anything that is *dry*, *sharp*, or *dead*. 
  - packets of silica gel
  - obsidian blades
  - vacuum-sealed spices that still smell of desert or pine forest
  - books printed on non-absorbent plastic paper
  - small mechanical clocks (they love things that fight the slow metabolism of their world)
- **Recording Devices**: Conventional electronics will develop condensation inside their housings within hours. Use mechanical cameras, wax-cylinder recorders, or heavily sealed, heated drone units. The moss itself will slowly grow over anything you leave stationary.

### 4. Cultural and Behavioral Protocols
The people are quiet, patient, and damp. They speak in low, unhurried voices that blend with the dripping. Shouting is considered violent and slightly obscene. Move slowly. Pause often. If you stand completely still in the central transept for twenty minutes or more, the moss may begin to grow over your boots. This is not hostile; it is the Foundry trying to incorporate you into its circulatory system. Gently prune yourself free and the locals will nod in approval.

Never damage living moss except with the specific curved sickles or the small bronze “tapping knives” used to collect the medicinal exudate. Cutting with steel or, worse, fire is a serious transgression. The Foundry remembers.

Offer to help maintain the slow flywheels or clear growth from steam vents. Physical labor is the primary social currency. If you prove useful, they will quietly adopt you for as long as you wish to stay.

### 5. Health and Exit Strategy
- **Biological Risk**: The dominant moss is warm-blooded and produces its own immune factors. It is not impossible that it could attempt to “heal” you by incorporating your tissues. Monitor for any unusual velvety green growth on wounds or mucous membranes. Silver nitrate and extreme dryness are the only known countermeasures.
- **Psychological Exit**: After more than a month, many travelers find dry air painful and silence terrifying. Plan a decompression period in an arid or vacuum environment before returning home.
- **Emergency Beacon**: Hide a small, vacuum-sealed emergency beacon or recall device inside a hydrophobic cylinder. If the moss fully claims you, the beacon may still function when activated.

### Final Intuition
Visiting the Mossheart Foundry is like choosing to live for a while inside the warm, wet lungs of an ancient, benevolent titan that has taught itself blacksmithing. It is not hostile, but it is *hungry for equilibrium*. It wants everything — iron, flesh, time, noise, thought — to slow down and become part of its endless, dripping cathedral metabolism.

Go prepared to surrender speed, dryness, and sharp edges. In return you may be given something few other places in the multiverse can offer: the deep, choral peace of a world that has successfully negotiated a treaty between decay and growth, and has been quietly winning that treaty for centuries.

Pack light. Stay damp. Move like moss grows.

And never, ever let yourself become still in the central transept for too long. The Foundry is patient, and it is always, always breathing.
