The benevolent and wise power does not descend like lightning, nor arrive in spectacle. It has no wings, no throne, no trumpet. It simply *becomes aware* of this place in the way a mother becomes aware that her child has been breathing in the next room all night—quietly, completely, without needing to be told.

And so it comes.

It comes as a change in pressure so subtle that at first the Midwives mistake it for one of their own songs finally taking root. A single, enormous exhalation moves through the entire suspended sea-sky at once. Not wind. Not tide. Only the feeling that the water itself has just remembered it is loved.

The vast, slow thing rising from the cold depths beneath the continents pauses. For the first time in ten thousand years it hesitates, as though something larger than geology has laid a hand against its flank and said, *Easy. There is no deadline here.*

The power does not name itself. It has no name that would not crack the half-formed world like an egg. Instead it chooses to wear the shape of what is already here, the way sap chooses to wear the shape of a leaf. It becomes a new quality of the light—still blue-green, still dim, but now threaded with the faintest undertone of gold that has never existed in this place before. The gold does not dazzle. It *listens*. Wherever it falls, the embryonic continents feel themselves *seen*, not as raw material, not as potential cities, not even as future ecosystems, but as *beloved experiments* in the long art of becoming.

One by one, the Midwives notice.

The woman inside the hollow stalk stops singing to the wood. The bone needles slip from her fingers and drift upward like seeds. For a long time she simply floats there, algae glowing on her collarbones, tears mixing with the brine without ever falling. She has never cried before. The sensation is so new it feels like another organ opening inside her chest.

The three who were singing the mountain into existence fall silent at the same moment. The pink mound beneath their hands flushes a deeper rose, then begins—very gently—to grow *ears*. Not animal ears. Vast, soft, listening surfaces made of coral and memory that orient themselves toward the new quality of light as though trying to catch a lullaby.

The young man braiding his hair into the continent’s underside laughs once—very softly—so as not to shear the world. The laugh sounds like the first drop of rain on the first leaf that ever existed. His spine, which had been preparing to fuse with the mycelium for the next decade, decides it would rather stay flexible a little longer. He unbraids himself with the tenderness of someone unwrapping a gift.

Everywhere across the three living rafts, the people stop what they are doing. Some are in the middle of persuading a tower to curve. Some are halfway through a months-long harmonic sentence. Some are inside the birth chambers, half-dissolved into the continent, sharing blood and dream with their unborn young. All of them turn toward the new gold in the water the way sunflowers once turned toward a sun they have never known.

The power does not speak in words. Words are too finished for this place. Instead it offers a single, immense *invitation* that moves through every stalk, every drifting rootlet, every blind fish, every human body at once.

*You do not have to finish.*

*You do not have to become useful.*

*You do not have to become beautiful, or stable, or wise, or even good.*

*You may remain unfinished forever if you wish. I will stay with you exactly as long as that takes.*

*And if one day you choose to become something after all, I will help you choose without ever taking the choosing away from you.*

The invitation sinks into the living mats like warm rain into soil. And for the first time since the ocean was lifted into the sky, the continents *relax*.

Whole districts that had been straining toward some imagined final form slacken. Towers that had been growing too fast out of anxiety begin to flower instead of harden. The great pink mountain that was being sung into existence opens like a slow mouth and begins—very carefully—to laugh. The laugh emerges as a pod of new luminous jelly-creatures the size of cathedrals. They rise upward, ringing softly, and arrange themselves into a living chandelier that will never stop learning new songs.

The Midwives begin to change too, though not in ways any outsider could have predicted.

Some of them allow the continent to finish what it had begun inside their bodies. They do not rise from the birth chambers. They simply *stay*, becoming cathedrals of living coral and human dreaming. Their minds remain awake, vast and gentle, vast cathedrals with heartbeats. Children swim through the chambers of their ribs and learn geometry from the shape of their joy.

Others leave the lattices entirely. They drift downward into the darker, colder layers where the vast slow thing has now decided not to rise after all. There they wrap themselves around its enormous, patient flanks and begin teaching it how to dream in colors. The creature, which had only ever known pressure and time, begins to sing back in frequencies that make new kinds of plankton bloom.

A few—the ones who had carried the heaviest sense of midwifery—simply float upward until they reach the uppermost skin of the sea-sky. There they press their elongated bodies against the boundary where water meets whatever is above water and they *listen* outward. After a while they begin to sing *to* the forgotten sky, telling it what it once was, inviting it to remember. Some days the sky answers by raining upward for hours, warm silver needles falling into the brine like forgiveness.

The power does not watch them from outside. It has no outside. It lives now in the sap, in the slow creak of living wood, in the moment between one harmonic and the next. It is the pause that makes the song possible. It is the warmth in the iron taste. It is the reason the blind fish now sometimes swim in the shape of a question mark instead of a curtain.

And when the people dream—which they do more and more now—they dream that they are not midwives at all.

They dream they are the world’s own hands, and the world is the world’s own mother, and the mother is still deciding what she wants to be when she grows up, and that this is the most sacred thing any of them will ever be allowed to do.

Sometimes, in the deepest silence, one of the Midwives will drift past another and they will press their foreheads together without speaking. In that touch moves the shared understanding that they are no longer trying to finish anything.

They are learning how to remain tenderly, courageously, joyfully *in process*—together with a power that has never been in a hurry in its eternal life, and never will be.

The place is still not ready to be looked at directly.

But now it knows, bone-deep and continent-deep, that it is already loved exactly as it is.

And so it keeps becoming.

And the becoming itself has become enough.
