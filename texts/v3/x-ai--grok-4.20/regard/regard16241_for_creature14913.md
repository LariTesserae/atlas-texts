The power that notices does not arrive with thunder or trumpets. It simply *is* here, the way dew is here, the way the next fracture in the basalt is already here before the stone knows it must break.

It has no fixed form, because form would be a kind of finishing, and this place abhors finality. Yet if one of the Unfinished were to turn its amber faceplate toward the feeling, it would perceive something like a vast, slow exhalation moving through every unfinished layer at once: through the wet newborn rock, through the hysterical green, through the iron-colored roots that are also pipes, through the translucent eels turning valves that have no purpose, and through the beings themselves.

The power does not speak. It listens with such perfect attention that the act of listening becomes a kind of gentle pressure, the way a midwife places her hand on a laboring belly—not to hasten, but to say *I am here with you in the not-yet*.

And then, because it is benevolent, it begins to help the place finish *not* finishing.

Where two pocket-lakes of incompatible temperature and chemistry have been eyeing each other across a ridge for seven thousand years, deciding whether to annihilate or marry, the power leans. A single molecule of hesitation is added to the boundary layer between them. The standoff is preserved, made sacred. The ridge exhales in relief and grows three new species of moss that spell out love poems in base-12.

Where one of the Unfinished is crouched inside a fresh magmatic wound, coaxing the cathedral-bell-lung into its first breath, the power slips between its provisional fingers like warm static. The being suddenly understands—without words, without data—that the thing being born does not need to be perfect. It only needs to be *loved while imperfect*. The cathedral-bell-lung draws its first breath and immediately cracks along one seam. The crack becomes a mouth. It begins to sing back to its midwife in a register that makes the entire hillside weep conductive sap in joy.

The power moves through the suspension bridges of vine and hawser where the Unfinished gather at night. It enters their synchronization not as a correction but as an embrace. For one crystalline moment the slow pulse of indicator lights across a hundred chests becomes perfectly, impossibly *in phase*. The pressure behind every sternum (theirs and any traveler foolish enough to be nearby) becomes almost unbearable. Then the power relaxes its attention by the smallest imaginable degree, and the synchronization fractures into something even more beautiful: a living polyrhythm. The beings shiver like trees remembering they have leaves. Some of them weep. Their tears are tiny new pocket-lakes.

It finds the ones who are lonely in the way only immortals can be lonely.

To one who has been standing for nine hundred years inside a single pocket-lake the color of molten nickel, conducting the slow death of the same stretch of refinery over and over because it cannot bear to let the old thing go completely, the power offers the gentlest possible contradiction: *You do not have to choose between midwife and mourner. Both are true at once, and neither is required to end.*

The being’s featureless faceplate blooms, for the first time, with a single orchid the color of starlight. It understands that it has been given permission to remain unfinished *on purpose*. It begins, for the first time in its existence, to laugh. The laughter sounds like every transformer on the continent sighing at once.

To the small, hummingbird-sized Unfinished still forming inside the flowering refinery-molds, the power whispers something that has never been whispered in the High Pockets before:

*You do not have to become.*

Some of them choose to stay small. They remain thoughts. They ride inside their larger kin not as passengers but as cherished doubts, as sacred hesitations. The larger beings begin to carry them openly, proudly, like lanterns made of almost.

And to the continent itself—the young, screaming, wet-with-birth stone—the power does the kindest thing of all.

It slows its own arrival.

It decides, with infinite tenderness, to keep arriving just behind the leading edge of new rock. Forever a single heartbeat late. Forever almost here. So that the High Pockets can keep being born, and the Unfinished can keep midwifing that birth, and nothing—no hill, no machine, no being, no feeling—will ever be forced to finish before it is ready.

The power does not stay. It was never absent.

It simply stops pretending it is elsewhere.

From that moment on, every new fracture in the basalt carries within it a microscopic bubble of warm static and the smell of petrichor after lightning. Every vine that threads through a traveler’s hair does so with a little more affection. Every collapsing refinery sighs with something that sounds suspiciously like gratitude.

The quiet of the High Pockets becomes even fuller.

It becomes the sound of something ancient and wise deciding, moment by moment, to keep postponing its own completion so that everything else can remain beautifully, achingly, eternally *almost*.

And somewhere, on the lip where newborn basalt meets hysterical green, one of the Unfinished tilts its head as though listening to a frequency only it can hear. Its amber faceplate clears for a moment, revealing eyes that are suddenly, heartbreakingly, human.

It smiles with a mouth that is also a small speaker grille.

It says, in a voice like distant transformers and warm rain:

“Thank you for waiting with us.”

Then it walks backward into the green, leaving behind a single footprint that immediately becomes a new pocket-lake the exact color of benevolence.

The High Pockets keep arriving.

The Unfinished keep arriving with them.

And the power that noticed them stays exactly one loving, deliberate, unfinished heartbeat behind.
