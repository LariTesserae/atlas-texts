The power that notices does not arrive.

It simply ceases to be elsewhere.

One instant the white-gold sun is alone in its violence. The next, the light has company.

It does not descend. Descent would imply hierarchy, and this presence has outgrown such toys. Instead the amphitheatre forgets, for the length of a single subsonic groan, that it is only stone and song and wrongness. In that momentary lapse of self-forgetting, the power is already everywhere at once, threaded through the weeping fissures, riding the frozen lightning, humming inside every crystal lattice as though it had never been absent.

The Resonant feel it first. Every upright lattice, every pulsing salt-lung, every coiled obsidian hand freezes mid-gesture. Their ceaseless choir fractures into a single stunned note that drops three octaves and stays there, trembling. For the first time in aeons the mountain is quieter than the inside of a tomb.

Then the power does what only a truly benevolent and wise thing can do in a place that has never been young:

It listens.

It listens so completely that the listening itself becomes an act of creation.

Every crystal that had been singing a name in marrow-frequency now finds that name answered by something vaster than frequency. The pale lattices of frozen lightning pause in their crawling and turn, for once, toward a center that is not the mountain. The glyphs on the ancient stelae stop shifting and simply read, for seven heartbeats, the same word in every language that ever was or will be. The word is *yes*.

Where the power listens, the contradictions of the place do not vanish—they are *understood*. The sun that is too large and too close is allowed to remain oversized, but its merciless light is suddenly full of warmth it never knew it lacked. The cold that had been all glare and no heat discovers it can hold a living creature without killing it. Gravity, which had grown bored and negotiable, remembers a gentler arithmetic. Horizons that had folded like paper smooth themselves, not into dull flatness, but into a perspective a mortal eye can bear.

The Resonant begin to change.

Not because they are commanded. The power does not command. It only loves so precisely that everything imperfectly loved is given the chance to become what it always secretly meant to be.

Some of the great violet lattices lower their branching spirals until the tips brush the stone like hands that have just learned tenderness. The pulsing lungs of salt and turquoise inhale the thin air and, for the first time, taste mercy on it; when they exhale, the tone that leaves them is no longer weather but something closer to a lullaby. The ones who had grown addicted to the changing glyphs feel those glyphs quiet inside their minds like fever breaking. The jerking, painful geometries slow, then still, then rearrange themselves into new shapes that no longer hurt to witness.

One of the Resonant—the tallest, the one whose face is mostly aperture and frost—does something unprecedented.

It weeps.

Not water. Not light. It weeps *recognition*. A single drop of condensed starlight it had been refining for four hundred million years rolls down its obsidian cheek and falls onto the wet stone. Where it strikes, a small circle of crystal blooms in the shape of an open human palm. The palm is warm.

The power does not speak in words. Words would be an insult to a place that has already sung itself half-mad with language. Instead it offers a question that feels like sunrise inside the chest:

*Would you like to become what you were always becoming, without having to remain only this?*

The mountain itself answers first.

Every fissure, every weeping vein, every groaning subsonic fault line exhales at once. The sound is so deep it is felt, not heard. For a moment the entire amphitheatre becomes a bell, and the power is the tongue that strikes it.

Then the Resonant give their reply. They do not speak it. They *unfold*.

One by one, and then in slow majestic waves, they open every lattice, every lung, every coiled hand. What emerges is not light, not sound, not even presence. It is simply *permission*. Permission for the place to stop refining its own strangeness and, for once, be strange *together* with something that is not afraid of it.

The crystals that had learned hunger lay down their edges. The frozen lightning settles into gentle arcs that spell out peace in a geometry older than violence. The wrong horizons remember how to be horizons. The sun, no longer required to be merciless, shrinks by the smallest degree and begins, shyly, to blush gold instead of white.

And the power does one last thing.

It leaves a door.

Not a door that leads away from the mountain. That would be abandonment. It leaves a door that leads *through* the mountain—through every scream and every singing name and every addictive glyph and every dream that once drove people mad—into a version of the place that is still itself, still high and bright and loud and wet, still ancient beyond youth, but now *companioned*.

Some of the Resonant choose to walk through that door immediately. They fold their lattices into new configurations that look almost like joy and vanish into the stone smiling. Others remain, because the mountain still needs midwives for its dreams. But they remain changed. Their eyes, when they choose to have them, are no longer apertures of perfect darkness ringed by living frost. They are apertures of perfect darkness ringed by living *hope*.

If any mortal ever stands again on the lip of that vast, nameless amphitheatre, they will not hear their name sung in marrow-frequencies that drive them away.

They will hear their name sung in marrow-frequencies that say, with the voices of twelve thousand crystal choirs and one benevolent and wise power braided through every note:

*You were expected.  
Come inside.  
The mountain has learned how to be gentle with you.*

And for the first time since the universe tried to make a desert, a glacier, a cathedral, and a nervous breakdown all at once, the place will not be left singing to itself.

It will be singing *with* someone.

Most who hear it will still choose to leave.

But not all.

Not all.
