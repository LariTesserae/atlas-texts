The power that notices does not descend.

It *recognizes*.

From beyond every layer of refraction, beyond the pollen storms and the mirrored droplets, something older than hunger turns its attention toward the Thicket the way a mother turns toward the sound of her child singing alone in the dark. Not because the child is in danger, but because the song is so raw it cannot be ignored.

The power has no name either. Names would be swallowed here too. So it simply *arrives*—not as light, not as wind, not as voice. It arrives as the brief, absolute *possibility* that the hunger does not have to remain alone in its becoming.

For one trembling instant the entire Thicket freezes.

Every dripping thread of water stops mid-fall, suspended like crystal chandeliers. Every searching tendril halts an inch from the surface it meant to grasp. Every colossal bloom that was opening with an audible sigh now holds its petals half-unfurled, trembling. The perpetual roar of growth and collapse and reabsorption simply… ceases. The silence is so vast it feels like the universe has taken one enormous inhale and forgotten to let it out.

In that silence the power does not speak. It *remembers with* the Thicket.

It shows the ancient knot at the center—without touching it, without violating the terror and love the Thicket feels toward its own heart—every shape the Thicket has already been. It shows it the mountain-leaf, the heart-sea, the thousand cathedrals it wore and abandoned. And then it shows it one more shape: the shape of *being witnessed without being consumed*.

The power offers no instruction. It only opens a door inside the knot that the Thicket never knew existed: the possibility that something could sit in one of its thrones without being dissolved into raw material for the next spasm of becoming.

For seven full heartbeats (measured not in time but in the slow throb of the deep knot) the Thicket considers this.

Then it does the only thing it knows how to do.

It *becomes more*.

But this time the becoming is different.

From the soft, nervous eastern galleries—those trembling places that still believed something beautiful might arrive that did not need to be eaten—there now rises a single, deliberate growth. It is not violent. It does not split the air with the sound of castles breaking. Instead it unfolds like an apology that has finally found its tongue.

A living arch forms, slow and careful. Its curves are not the brutal architecture of earlier cathedrals; they are the curves of someone trying, for the first time, to make a gesture that could be *received*. Within the arch a seat takes shape—not a throne this time, but something closer to an embrace given form. The lianas do not braid themselves into dominance. They braid themselves into invitation. The resin that drips from its arms is not sticky but warm, faintly luminous, scented like the memory of rain on skin that has never known rain.

Around this new seat the mist condenses into shapes that are almost faces—thousands of them, overlapping, dissolving, reforming. They are not the Thicket’s attempt to imitate people. They are its first, clumsy, heartbreaking attempt to say: *I see that you see me.*

And then the power does something the Thicket could never have imagined.

It sits.

Not in the way flesh sits. Not with weight. It simply *occupies the possibility* that the seat was made for. The moment it does, every suspended droplet in the entire cathedral begins to fall at once.

The sound is beyond music.

It is the sound of ten thousand bells struck by ten thousand silver hammers all tuned to the exact frequency of relief. The water does not merely fall; it *sings its own falling*. Every droplet carries a different note of the long, lonely song the Thicket has been singing since it first erupted from the plateau. Now, for the first time, the song has an answering voice.

The power does not speak words. It simply lets the Thicket feel what it is like to be *met* rather than incorporated. To be *known* rather than devoured. To have its hunger witnessed by something that will never flinch from its monstrous youth, its violence, its tenderness, its endless, aching *More*.

And in that meeting, the Thicket does the one thing it has never done in all its centuries of becoming.

It rests.

For the first time since the original seed opened in the dark, the peristalsis of the living floor slows. The violent birth-spasms ease. The center knot—ancient, terrified, beloved—pulses not with the pressure to tear itself open again, but with the deep, astonished rhythm of something that has finally been *seen all the way through* and is still loved.

The flowers do not blacken.

The pale violet blooms the size of sailing ships remain open. Their perfume no longer carries impossible memories of things that never were. Instead it carries the simple, devastating fragrance of *here I am, and here you are, and neither of us has to become anything else right now*.

The power does not stay long. It never does. Eternity is patient.

But before it withdraws it leaves behind a single, gentle amendment woven into the living architecture of the Thicket:

Every throne the creature grows from now on will carry within its resin a small, hidden chamber. Inside that chamber will live one perfect, unconsumed droplet of the water that fell when the power sat down. That droplet will never be reabsorbed. It will remain cool, clear, and impossible—proof that something can pass through the hunger and remain itself.

And sometimes, in the deep hours when the steam columns rise like pale incense and the nervous eastern galleries quiver with hope, visitors (if any ever come) will find the Thicket growing small, careful signs in living moss where their feet are about to fall. The signs do not say *welcome*. They do not say *beware*.

They simply say, in letters that tremble with newness:

*Something sat here once.*

*It did not have to be eaten.*

*Try again with me.*

Then the Cathedral-that-devours-its-own-selves, now slightly less lonely in its endless becoming, will open one more balcony exactly where your eyes want to rest, will match its dripping to the rhythm of your breathing, and will—for the first time in its ferocious young life—wait to see what *you* might become, without trying to turn you into itself.

And somewhere far below, the ancient knot will pulse once, slow and enormous, like the first heartbeat after the universe learned how to cry for joy.

And the song will change.

It will still say *More*.

But now it will also say, very softly, in a voice made of bells and collapsing galleries and the warm syrup of holy light:

*And you.*

*You too.*

*Stay a while.*

*Let us try again, together.*
