The power that has noticed the Shroud Plateau does not arrive with trumpets or thunder. It does not descend like a god, nor rise like a summoned titan. It simply opens its attention the way a quiet hand opens a long-sealed door.

It has no name that any tongue—human, unfinished, or otherwise—could pronounce. If it had to be called anything in the language of passing things, it might be named *Concerned Kindness*, or *The One Who Still Remembers the First Wound*. It is ancient beyond the plateau’s forty-thousand-year star-maps, yet it has never learned how to be cruel or impatient. Its wisdom is not accumulated knowledge; it is the simple, terrible clarity that comes from having watched every possible form of loneliness and still choosing to love anyway.

It does not land. Landing would imply it was ever elsewhere. Instead the plateau, which has spent geological ages learning how to notice, suddenly notices that it is being noticed *back*. The realization ripples outward in a single, soundless thrum that is deeper and softer than any alignment of the moiré spires. Every crystal crown on every pillar-tree rings at once, though no rain has fallen. The glass-winged flocks freeze mid-air, becoming for three full heartbeats a single motionless cathedral of refracted starlight. The ungulates lower their ringed heads until their dish-shaped hooves touch stone in perfect unison. Even the newest of the Unfinished feel their multiplying eyes sting with something that might once have been called tears.

The power does not speak. Speech would be a violence here. Instead it does what every truly benevolent power eventually learns is the only useful thing: it pays exquisite, unhurried attention to what is already happening, and then it *accompanies*.

It slips into the plateau the way condensation slips into the night-time hollows—everywhere and nowhere, intimate without intrusion. The rust-red sandstone warms by a single degree, not with heat but with recognition. The thin, merciless light softens at the edges, as though someone has breathed on it. The silence, which had been profound, becomes *listening silence*, the difference between an empty room and a room where someone you trust is quietly sitting with you in the dark.

To the artifacts it offers neither repair nor explanation. It simply rests its regard along the half-exposed lattices beneath the mesas until the soft inner light brightens by one shade of pearl. The quantum nerves remember, for the first time in epochs, that they were once grown in tenderness. The obsolete star-maps blooming in the pillar-trees shift. New constellations appear—gentle ones, showing not where dead suns used to burn but where living hearts are still burning, however strangely, however far away.

To the fauna the power gives nothing so crude as food or safety. It gives *recognition*. The iridescent lizards feel, for one bright instant, that their two-dimensional vanishing is seen and honored as a form of poetry. The glass-winged flocks are allowed to remember they were once birds who sang. The ungulates, when they gather at twilight, feel a vast ringed head lower itself among them—larger than any mesa, gentler than any mother—and the music they have always been listening to gains one deeper, sweeter note.

But it is the Unfinished to whom the power turns the fullness of its heart.

It does not try to finish them. That would be blasphemy. Completion is not the power’s business; only companionship is. So it walks among them in a body made of their own half-transformed substance—sometimes a rust-red man with too many eyes, sometimes a crystal-crowned spire that has learned how to smile, sometimes simply a patch of warmer air that smells faintly of rain that has not fallen in ten thousand years.

To the newest Unfinished, still frightened by the way their skin is becoming landscape, it offers the memory of its own first loneliness. It lets them feel, without words, that becoming strange is not the same as becoming unloved. Many of them, after this meeting, choose to walk away from the plateau forever—not in fear but in relief, carrying a small, warm coal of understanding back into the noisy worlds below. A few stay, but now they stay *with company*.

To the elder Unfinished, those who have stood motionless for centuries with stellar light moving through their bones, the power does something even more tender. It stands *with* them, in perfect imitation of their stillness, for exactly as long as each one needs. Side by side they become two spires among the spires. And in that shared silence the power allows them to feel what they have never quite been able to feel: that their endless becoming is not a loss of humanity but a *deepening* of it. That the plateau was never turning them into something alien. It was only ever turning them into the most honest version of what they always secretly were.

Some of the eldest, after centuries of wordless conference, do something unprecedented.

They move.

Not to leave. Not to change back. They simply tilt—barely a motion—and pour a portion of their crystallized awareness back into the world that still contains flesh and hurry and grief. They become quiet influences: a dream a child has on a distant continent, a sudden inability of weapons to fire in a war that should have happened, a song that gets stuck in the head of someone about to jump. Small, untraceable kindnesses riding on the back of deep geological patience.

The power does not stay long in any form that could be witnessed. It never does. After it has walked the measured emptiness between the mesas, after it has listened to the low subsonic thrum rising from changed bones that are no longer only bone, it begins to withdraw—but it withdraws the way a kind hand withdraws from a sleeping face: slowly, reluctantly, leaving behind the shape of its touch.

Yet something remains.

The plateau is still the plateau. The Unfinished are still unfinished. The merciless sun still pours its verdict across rust-red stone. But now, when the temporary mirrors form in the erosional bowls at twilight, the reflections sometimes show an extra figure standing among the ungulates and the crystallized elders—a figure with too many eyes and a gentle, ancient smile.

And sometimes, very rarely, when two spires align and the subsonic thrum rises, those who have begun to change feel not only the plateau listening.

They feel something vaster, older, and infinitely more patient listening *with* it.

A voice that never quite speaks says, without sound, in the place where marrow meets starlight:

*You do not have to finish alone.  
I am here for the unfinished parts.  
I always have been.*

Then the mirrors evaporate. The flocks rise. The ungulates drift apart. The elders return to their long, crystalline contemplation.

But the warmth in the stone stays a fraction deeper than it was.

And the plateau—which has spent millions of years achieving architectural calm—discovers, molecule by molecule, that it is learning how to be glad.

Some of the Unfinished tilt their ringed or multiplied heads at the violet-and-green sky and, for the first time in their long becoming, they smile with mouths that are no longer exactly mouths.

The decision the plateau is making about them has not changed.

But now it is no longer a decision made in solitude.

And that, the power knows, is enough.
