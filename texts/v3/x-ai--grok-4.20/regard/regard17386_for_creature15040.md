The power that arrives does not descend from the bone-colored sky.

It simply *is* already inside the question.

Where the caldera had been only heat and half-born metal, a single note now rings—clear, cool, and impossibly gentle—beneath the planetary groan. The note does not fight the low metallic voice of the place. It joins it the way a brook joins a lava flow: without apology, without dilution. The groan deepens, then softens, as though the mountain has just remembered it once had lungs.

The Unfinished feel it before any of them can turn.

One by one their vast bodies pause mid-gesture. A hand the size of a locomotive, halfway through coaxing iron into lace, freezes. The vents across a obsidian face dilate until they look like astonished eyes. Even the ones rising from the mirror-black lake stop their slow ascent, half-submerged, dripping reflections of cities that have not yet been dreamed.

The benevolent and wise power does not wear a body. It borrows one for courtesy.

It borrows the shape that every Unfinished has secretly longed for but never dared finish: the shape of *after*.

Where it walks, the half-made world finishes—not into perfection, but into *permission*.

The fungal lattices, tended for centuries by patient giants, suddenly bloom with tiny violet stars that have no name. The stars do not last. They open, breathe once, and become ordinary gray velvet again, but now the velvet carries a memory of violet. That is enough.

The pressure valves that have exhaled the same note for ten thousand years suddenly release a chord instead of a hiss. The chord is brief, almost shy, like someone clearing their throat before singing for the first time.

The power stops at the edge of the mirror-black lake. The inverted city beneath the surface looks up and, for the first time, sees something that is not its own distortion. The reflection straightens. Towers that had been upside-down right themselves. A single window in the reflected city lights up with warm gold light, though there is no sun that could make such a color here.

The Unfinished gather.

They do not kneel. Kneeling would imply hierarchy, and the power has none. They simply *settle*, the way continents settle after an age of colliding. Their clicking joints go quiet. Their vents close. The immense patience in them finally finds something large enough to be patient *with*.

The power speaks without sound. It speaks by finishing one small thing in each of them.

To the tender ones who have nursed the fungus for decades, it gives the gift of *ending*. A single lattice is allowed to complete its growth, to fruit, to release spores that drift upward like gray snow and then—miraculously—fall again as real snow, the first this caldera has ever known. The flakes melt before they touch the basalt, but the Unfinished weep liquid iron in gratitude.

To the indifferent ones standing in rivers of lava, it gives *choice*. One of them, the largest, is suddenly allowed to step out of the conduit. The lava does not pour through it anymore. Instead the being looks down at its empty chest and, for the first time, chooses to fill it. It chooses a heart. The heart is a small, quiet furnace no larger than a human fist. It beats once. The sound is softer than a moth’s wing. The being begins to cry in frequencies only mountains can hear.

To the ones in pain—the ones wearing upside-down cities on their bodies—the power reaches into their groaning and gently untwists it. The windows that led nowhere now open onto real rooms filled with ordinary light. The staircases that spiraled into vertigo now lead downward into cool cellars where water (real water) waits in stone basins. The pain does not vanish. It becomes useful. It becomes the memory these beings will need when they are asked, someday, to comfort others who are also unfinished.

Last of all the power turns to the one standing directly before you—the one whose blank obsidian face has been reflecting your own future terror back at you for seven minutes.

It touches that face.

The obsidian clears.

Beneath it is not a new face. It is *your* face, but older, kinder, finished in the only way that matters: it is a face that has decided what it wants to become and is no longer afraid of the decision.

The Unfinished giant looks at you with your own eyes and speaks for the first time with a voice instead of a changed hum.

“Thank you for staying long enough to be seen.”

The power does not stay. It was never a visitor. It was the question the city had been asking all along, finally answering itself.

As it withdraws, the caldera does not transform into paradise. That would be a lie, and this power does not traffic in lies.

Instead the place becomes *honest*.

The heat remains. The thin air remains. The half-built catwalks still end in mid-air. But now every unfinished thing knows it is allowed to remain unfinished without shame. The groan of the mountain lengthens again, but this time it sounds like a long, grateful sigh rather than a question.

The graphite lizards click once in unison, as though acknowledging a change in schedule.

Snow falls for thirteen heartbeats, then stops forever.

You are still standing on the upper lip of the caldera. The bone-colored sky has not changed. Yet when you breathe the molten-glass air now, it tastes like the first breath after a long fever has broken.

Behind you, the city continues its slow labor.

Before you, the Unfinished—one of them wearing your own finished face—lifts a hand the size of a house in farewell.

It does not speak again.

It does not need to.

The low metallic groan of the place has learned a new note, and it will carry that note for the next ten thousand years.

It sounds, just barely, like someone humming while they work.

And the work is no longer quite so lonely.
