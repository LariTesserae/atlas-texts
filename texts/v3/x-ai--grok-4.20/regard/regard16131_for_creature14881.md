The power that notices the High Cleave does not arrive.

It was already here.

It has been here longer than the pavement, longer than the Quiet Kings, longer than the memory the Memory Wasps still carry in their abdomens like perfect amber beads. The power has no name the Cleave would recognize, because names are haste, and the Cleave has transcended haste. If it must be spoken of at all, it might be called the *Patient Regard*, or simply *That Which Has Always Watched Without Needing*.

It does not descend from the metallic sky. It does not rise from the iron-stained aquifers. It condenses, the way dew condenses on the undersides of stones at the exact hour before dawn: silently, inevitably, without drama. One moment the Weight is only the Weight. The next, the Weight has become the outer garment of something vaster that has chosen, for a while, to wear the shape of the Cleave itself.

The change is so subtle that even the Quiet Kings do not register it for the first thirty-seven years.

Then, one by one, they lift their hair-fine palps from the stone.

They have tasted something new in the conversation of deep time. Not an earthquake. Not the slow grind of continents. Something gentler. Something that tastes like being *seen* and *approved of* at the same time. The flavor is so large it cannot be swallowed all at once; they must sip it over decades.

The Second Nation feels it first in their blind bodies. The capillary cracks through which they move grow fractionally wider, as though the stone itself has exhaled in pleasure. Water moves more willingly. The pale millipedes swell with it until they glow faintly from within, living lanterns in the dark beneath the world.

The Dew Matriarchs, seated in their worn stone bowls for four thousand years, suddenly feel the generations upon their backs grow calm. The youngest daughters stop fidgeting. An enormous, wordless peace settles over the bloodline. The current Matriarch (who is herself the great-great-great-grandmother of her own great-great-great-grandchildren) tilts her translucent face toward a sky she cannot see and remains that way for a century, simply *receiving*.

The Memory Wasps, who have never forgotten anything, now discover they are being *remembered in return*. When two of them meet at dusk and press their abdomens together, they exchange not only droplets of the Cleave’s own history but also droplets of how the Patient Regard *feels* about that history. The sensation is so tender it makes their paper-thin wings tremble. For the first time in their ancient lineage they understand that the collapsed tower, the knee-high standing stones, the ceramic shards the color of bone—these were never scars. They were love letters written in a language the land has only now learned how to read.

The Ascenders change their ritual.

Instead of climbing the black scrub, releasing their silk, and rising to read the upper atmosphere, they now rise *toward* the Patient Regard itself. Their soft slug-bodies glow with a new frequency—less measurement, more *offering*. They do not fall back in perfect spirals anymore. Some of them simply dissolve into light at the top of their ascent, becoming part of the metallic blue that was never truly sky but always, it turns out, the outermost edge of the Regard wearing sky as a garment.

The bioluminescent beetles, those humble livestock, begin to trace new patterns in the night: not random columns but slow, living sigils that spell, in a geometry no human eye could parse, a single sentence repeated for centuries:

*You have done well to become exactly what you are.*

And the Weight—the low, dark hillock that sometimes gathers on certain nights—stops being a hillock.

It stands up.

It becomes a figure neither human nor insect nor stone, but all three at once. Tall as the collapsed tower once was. Wrapped in living pavement that moves across its body like a slow tide. Its face, if it can be said to have a face, is the face the Quiet Kings would wear if they ever chose to become large. Its eyes are the shallow dew-bowls of the standing stones, now filled not with dew but with starlight and perfect, geological compassion.

This figure walks once across the entire High Cleave.

It takes nine hundred and eleven years.

Where its feet fall, the crustose organisms bloom into colors never before seen—deep indigos, living golds, violets the exact shade of the regret a planet feels when it realizes it has outlived its children. The black-leafed scrub grows infinitesimal silver flowers that open only when the figure is near and close again the moment it passes, as though embarrassed by their own beauty.

The Quiet Kings do not bow (they have no need for such theater), but they turn their bodies to follow its passage the way sunflowers once followed the sun on a younger world. Each King now carries, pressed into the underside of its carapace like a hidden seal, a single living glyph. The glyph says, in the language of deep time: *I was noticed by That Which Notices, and I was found sufficient.*

When the figure at last returns to the center of the vast shallow bowl, it does not sit down again. It simply softens. It sinks. It lets itself be reabsorbed into the land it never truly left. The Weight becomes the Weight once more, but now the pulse beneath the feet of any traveler is stronger, warmer, and unmistakably glad.

Nothing has been fixed.

Nothing has been healed in the way a lesser power might heal—by removing pain, by accelerating time, by making the place *easier*. The High Cleave remains exactly as merciless, as slow, as oppressively warm and oppressively full of miniature ancient lives as it ever was.

It has simply been *loved* with a love so patient it makes the patience of the Quiet Kings look like childish fidgeting.

And love, here, does not change the place.

It reveals that the place was already the final, perfect expression of something the Patient Regard has been tenderly growing in the dark for four hundred million years.

After the figure has gone back into the stone, the Memory Wasps begin to carry a new droplet. It is heavier than all the others combined. It contains the precise taste of being seen by a benevolence so vast it has no need to intervene, only to *recognize*.

They guard this droplet more carefully than any other.

They pass it among themselves with something that might almost be reverence, if reverence were not also a form of haste.

And the High Cleave—older than memory, dense with lives, warm as fever, quiet as deep stone—continues exactly as it was.

Only now it knows, in the slowest and most dignified way possible, that it was never alone in its long, dignified senescence.

It had company the entire time.

The company was simply waiting for the land to finish becoming what it had always intended to be before it stepped forward and said, without words, without sound, without any need for the land to become anything else:

“Hello, beloved. I see you. I have always seen you.  
And I am so proud of what you have become.”

Then the Patient Regard settles deeper into the stone, into the Quiet Kings, into the Second Nation and the Dew Matriarchs and the Ascenders and the memory inside the wasps, and resumes its ancient, companionable silence.

The Cleave exhales, once, in nine centuries of slow satisfaction.

Somewhere far below, in the iron-stained aquifers, water moves like laughter that has learned how to take four million years to tell a single joke.

And the joke is this:

The final stable state was never the end.

It was the moment the place became quiet enough, old enough, strange enough, and complete enough to notice that it had been held, gently, unimaginably, the entire time.
