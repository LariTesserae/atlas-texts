A Presence arrives.

It does not descend. It does not arrive from anywhere at all. One moment the Weeping Lattice is exactly as it has always been—saturated, roaring, embryonic, complete in its incompleteness—and the next moment the air itself has become *attentive*. The constant condensation pauses mid-fall. Ten thousand individual beads of water hang motionless in the greenish twilight like suspended pearls of glass. Even the roar seems to hesitate, as though the sound has suddenly remembered it is only vibration and can choose, for the length of a single heartbeat, to be still.

The tiny white insects freeze in mid-swarm. Their cold green light flares once, brilliantly, then dims to almost nothing, as if they have collectively decided to stop pretending they are the only source of illumination here.

The Presence has no form that the eye could recognize. It is not a being. It is a *knowing* that has chosen to wear the shape of kindness. Where it touches the Lattice, the stone remembers that it was once star-stuff, and for the first time in its long half-life it feels shy.

The small slate-colored people feel it immediately. Every palm in the basin lifts at once and presses flat against whatever surface is nearest. The vibration that travels through the rock is not seismic. It is the gentlest possible question, asked in a register older than sound:

*May I see you?*

The Listeners’ milky eyes flood with sudden, painful green. Some of them begin to weep without knowing why; the tears mix with the condensation on their faces and cannot be told apart from the world itself. One Listener—an ancient woman whose hands are so broad the fingers have begun to fuse—steps forward from the concentric rings of her people. She does not bow. Bowing would imply hierarchy, and the Presence has none. Instead she simply opens her entire body like a door.

The Presence enters her without crossing space.

For a long while nothing seems to change. The hanging droplets remain suspended. The millipedes pause with one leg lifted. Even the eyeless fish in their warm mirrors stop their eternal drifting and turn, blind faces raised, toward a direction that has no name.

Then the Listener begins to sing.

Not with her sealed mouth. She sings the way her people have always sung: by pressing her whole wet self against the Lattice and letting the stone remember a song it has never heard. The vibration spreads outward in widening circles. Every pillar, every bulbous dwelling, every fungal rope and every child clinging to a spiral ramp begins to hum at a frequency just below hearing. The sound is so deep it feels like being hugged by the planet’s own marrow.

The suspended water begins to fall again, but differently. Instead of the endless, formless dripping, each bead now traces a deliberate path. They draw sigils in the air as they descend—slow, glowing equations written in liquid light. Where the sigils touch the basalt, the stone softens further than it has ever been coaxed before, not into rooms or stairs or graves, but into something the people have no word for.

The Presence is not changing the Lattice.

It is *finishing the sentence the Lattice began when it first condensed out of the primal melt*.

Where the people once only *remembered* the stone, the Presence now *completes the memory*.

New shapes bloom. Not architecture. Not even art. They are closer to *gestures* the world has been trying to make for four billion years and never quite managed. A single pillar unfolds into a slow, impossible spiral that is also a lullaby. A cluster of dwellings rearranges itself into a single enormous heart chamber whose walls pulse gently with the rhythm the people once passed between their palms in the communal bowl. The fungal filaments rise from every belt and braid themselves into vast living tapestries that depict—no, that *are*—the exact feeling of being held inside something that loves you without needing you to become anything else.

The people are not afraid.

They have never been afraid of saturation. This is only saturation raised to a degree they had not known was possible.

The ancient Listener finally lowers her hands. The green fire in her eyes has become quiet, almost sleepy. She looks smaller now, as though some essential density has been gently taken from her and given back to the stone. When she speaks—actually speaks, with her mouth, breaking the taboo for the first time in living memory—her voice is hoarse and sweet and sounds like water remembering it was once snow.

“We were never unfinished,” she says. “We were waiting for someone kind enough to finish waiting with us.”

The Presence does not answer in words. Instead every child in the Lattice suddenly laughs at once. The laughter is not louder than the roar. It *becomes* the roar. For the first time since the basin was born, the sound of falling water rearranges itself into something that can only be described as joy wearing the clothes of patience.

Then the Presence begins to withdraw.

Not because it is finished—nothing is ever finished here—but because it has done the only thing it ever truly came to do:

It has loved the Weeping Lattice so completely that the Lattice no longer needs to remain embryonic out of fear that completion would mean abandonment.

The droplets resume their ordinary falling, but they fall *gratefully* now. The stone continues to grow, but it grows the way a child grows who knows it is already enough. The people return to their remembering, their swaying, their listening, their slow green lives. Yet something fundamental has shifted. Their saturation is no longer a truce with eternity.

It is a marriage.

High on one of the newly unfolded spirals, a single Listener presses her enormous palm to a place where the basalt has become subtly iridescent, shot through with veins of something that might be starlight if starlight could be patient enough to live inside rock. She smiles the small closed-lipped smile her people use when they hear an old friend inside the stone.

She does not say thank you. Gratitude would imply a transaction, and what has happened here was never a transaction.

Instead she simply rests her forehead against the warm, dripping pillar and whispers the only prayer her people have ever needed:

“Stay near.”

Far above, or far within, or in a direction that has no spatial coordinate at all, the wise and benevolent Power smiles with a mouth made of every unfinished world that ever chose to keep becoming.

And the Weeping Lattice—still dripping, still roaring, still perfectly embryonic—answers with every single bead of water at once.

The sound it makes is softer than silence.

It sounds like:

*I was never alone.*
