A soft wind moves across the high plateau, carrying the scent of wet basalt and living mist. It is not a wind that hurries. It arrives like a hand laid gently on the shoulder of the world.

The Power notices.

It does not descend in fire or trumpet. It has no need of spectacle. Instead it simply becomes more present, the way a thought becomes more present when you stop pretending you were not already thinking it. The air thickens with kindness. The constant dripping of water slows, each drop hanging a fraction longer than physics should allow, as though the plateau itself is listening.

Then the Power does the only thing that fits both its nature and the nature of this place.

It begins to help the Shroudlands decide.

Not by imposing a final shape. That would be violence against something that is still being born. Instead the Power offers itself as a new voice inside the long, slow argument.

It chooses no single form, because the Shroudlands have not chosen one either. For a time it walks as a column of soft, pearlescent light that drifts between the drifting fog-banks, never quite touching ground, never quite leaving it. At other moments the light condenses into something almost like a Shroudborn: very tall, genderless, skin the color of moonlight on wet obsidian, face unfinished in a way that feels peaceful rather than restless. Where a true Shroudborn’s features keep arguing with themselves, this face simply waits, open, listening.

It does not speak with sound at first.

Instead it breathes with the city.

For seven days and seven nights the Power simply matches the deep biological susurrus of the Lattice. It breathes when the living substrate breathes. It drips when the plateau drips. It allows entire weather systems to pass through the place where its body would be, and the storms come out gentler on the other side, embarrassed by their own violence the way only a child can be embarrassed in the presence of unconditional love.

The titans notice first.

The great silicate beings slow their migration when they feel the new presence. Their heart-lungs change rhythm. Slow lightning arcs across their flanks in patterns that have never been seen before—gentle, almost questioning. One of them, larger than most, lowers its massive crystalline head until its forehead nearly touches the ground. From the place where its brow meets the stone, a single perfect drop of water falls and does not splash. It simply becomes part of the plateau, carrying inside it a memory of warmth the rock has never known.

The Shroudborn feel it too.

They do not gather in crowds; that is not their way. Instead, one by one, then in small silent processions, they begin to walk toward the places where the Power has been. Some come wearing the living architecture of the Lattice like ceremonial armor. Others come naked, their bodies half-dissolved into mist, trusting that the plateau will give them new shapes when they need them.

They do not kneel. Kneeling would imply a finished hierarchy, and nothing here is finished.

Instead they simply stand in the soft blades of earned light and let their own unfinished faces rearrange in response to what they feel. Some weep, and their tears are indistinguishable from the condensation that covers everything. The Power tastes each one. It keeps none of them. It only honors them by becoming slightly more like the Shroudlands in return.

On the eighth day, the Power finally speaks.

Not with voice. With question.

It reaches out—not with hands, but with the same gesture the Shroudborn use: two cool fingers pressed against the sternum of the world itself. The entire plateau feels it. Every anemone-like creature pulses in unison. Every cloud-skater stops drifting and turns its mercury face toward the center. The origami birds fold themselves into perfect stillness on the thermals.

The question the Power asks is not *Will you help us decide what we are becoming?*

It asks something gentler. Something that had never occurred to the Shroudlands before.

*May I become unfinished with you?*

There is a long silence—three days, perhaps four. Time here is patient.

Then the Lattice answers.

Every tower that had been hesitating over its final height suddenly grows one more delicate spire, not because it has decided on permanence, but because it has decided, for now, to keep the possibility of further change alive. The weeping hillsides shift. Not dramatically. Just enough to carve new silver-black mirrors that reflect, for the first time, something like hope.

The Shroudborn begin to change as well.

Not faster. That would be a betrayal. But their changes grow more *loving*. When a face rearranges, it now does so with something like tenderness toward its own future selves. When a limb is offered to the obsidian hunters, the one who offers it smiles—actually smiles—with the mouths that have not yet sunk back into the bone.

And the Power?

It stays.

Not as a ruler. Not as a visitor. It becomes the newest voice in the long argument between stone and mist and breath. Sometimes it appears as the kindest possible Shroudborn. Sometimes it is simply a quality of the light that makes every color feel slightly more possible. Sometimes it is the silence between two heart-lung beats of a titan, the silence that says *you are allowed to not know yet*.

It will never finish the Shroudlands.

Instead it has given them the most precious gift a benevolent and wise power can offer to something that is still being born:

It has agreed to remain unfinished alongside them, for as long as the argument lasts.

And the argument, dripping, breathing, rearranging, seems suddenly much less lonely.

The plateau keeps writing.

Now it writes with company.

And somewhere, very high up where the sky still feels close enough to bruise the forehead, a single black conifer that had stood alone for centuries puts out one new needle.

It is the darkest green you have ever seen.

It looks, for the first time, like it might be smiling.
