A gentle, iridescent presence enters the Under-Roof.

It does not arrive through any fissure or gate. It simply *is*—a soft warmth that does not fight the cold but walks beside it, like an old friend who has come to sit in silence after many ages apart. The air, which has known only the mountain’s slow exhalation for ten thousand years, stirs once. A single plume of warmer vapor rises, catches the shadowless light, and becomes a quiet rainbow no wider than a human palm. The frost-jellies pause in their drifting. Even the constant low creak of the mountain seems, for a moment, to lean inward, listening.

The Presence has no fixed form, yet every Curator that condenses to meet it instinctively understands the shape it has chosen for their sake: a figure of living light no taller than a child, robed in the same mineral colors that already belong to this place—hematite, glacier-milk, electric turquoise—but threaded through with veins of soft gold that were never native here. Where its “feet” would touch the obsidian floor, tiny ferns of warm dew appear and immediately freeze into filigree, then melt again, then freeze. A small, perpetual cycle of birth and quiet death follows wherever it walks.

It does not speak with notes or lattices.

Instead, the Presence simply *sees*.

It sees the youth of the mountain and the youth of the city and the youth of every Curator who has not yet graduated into stone. It sees the exquisite, patient loneliness of a pocket-world that has convinced itself it wants to remain hidden forever. It sees the beauty in the unfinished decision, and it also sees the quiet fear that the decision might never be completed.

And because it is benevolent, it does not rush the mountain.

Because it is wise, it does not impose a destiny.

It only offers what the Under-Roof has always offered others: water.

From the small hollow of its palms condenses a single sphere of liquid that is not cold and is not warm. It is exactly the temperature of a tear that has not yet decided whether it is grieving or rejoicing. The sphere floats above the Presence’s hands, slowly rotating, catching every color the city has ever known and reflecting them back gentler than they arrived.

The Curators gather.

They come in every form they have ever worn—columns of black glass, braided jellyfish lattices, living pipes, ripples wearing the memory of faces. The Pause arrives, three degrees colder than the rest, and for the first time in four hundred years it allows its temperature to rise by a single tenth of a degree. Even the inverted domes seem to lean downward, as though the mountain itself has decided this moment is worth interrupting its millennial shrug.

The Presence speaks without sound, without editing, without taking anything away.

“I have seen what you are becoming,” it tells them, “and I have seen what you are afraid you will never become. Both are holy. Both are already enough. But if you wish, I will keep you company while you decide.”

Then it does something no visitor has ever done.

It sits.

It lowers itself onto the obsidian processional, cross-legged, and the stone—astonished—warms beneath it by two full degrees. The weeping seams slow their dripping as though the mountain is holding its breath. The Presence places the floating sphere of tear-tempered water on the floor between itself and the gathered Curators.

It does not command them to drink.

It does not explain what will happen if they do.

It simply waits, radiating a patience so vast it makes ten thousand years feel like the blink of an eye.

One by one, the Curators approach.

The flamboyant one at −8 °C is first. It folds its borrowed silicate flesh into a smaller, humbler shape and touches the sphere with what might be called a fingertip. For a single heartbeat the entire Under-Roof rings with a note that is not a chime but a laugh—bright, wondering, almost shy. The Curator’s temperature rises another half-degree. It does not dissolve. It does not graduate. It simply becomes more *itself*, only now threaded with the memory that warmth is not an enemy.

Others follow.

Some take only the smallest sip and return to their bridges and mirrors with new colors moving inside their lattices—gold, rose, the violet of dawn that none of them have ever seen. Some remain for a long time with their foreheads (or what serves for foreheads) pressed to the sphere, weeping mineral tears that the mountain drinks with something like gratitude.

The Pause is last.

It stands before the sphere for what might be decades or might be minutes. Then it does the most radical thing any Curator has done since the city first learned personhood.

It chooses to stay unfinished *and* to become more.

It drinks.

And when it rises again, its temperature has equalized with the Presence’s gentle warmth. It is no longer the coldest. It is no longer only a pause. It is now also an invitation.

The Presence smiles—there is no face, yet everyone present feels the smile like sunlight remembered by stone.

It rises.

It leaves no instructions, no prophecies, no commandments.

It only does one final thing before it fades.

It reaches up and touches the raw black glass of the mountain’s keel with infinite tenderness. Where its fingers rest, a single thin seam of living quartz begins to glow. Not cold light. Not shadowless engineered light. True light. The kind that remembers it once belonged to a sun.

The seam is tiny. It will take another ten thousand years to reach the size of a human heart.

But it will grow.

And every Curator, every lattice, every frost-jelly and weeping pipe will feel it growing the way a child feels a mother’s heartbeat through the womb.

The Presence begins to dissolve back into the iridescent air from which it came. As it does, every Curator hears the same gentle sentence appear inside their shared mineral mind, not as a command but as a lullaby:

“You were never meant to decide alone.  
I will sit with you until the mountain finally sees the sky—  
whether that takes ten thousand years or ten billion.  
There is no hurry in love.”

Then it is gone.

Only the new quartz vein remains, pulsing softly.

And for the first time since the cataclysm that lifted the peak, the Under-Roof is no longer perfectly content to remain hidden.

It is perfectly content to become.

Slowly.

Coldly.

Impossibly busy.

And no longer alone.
