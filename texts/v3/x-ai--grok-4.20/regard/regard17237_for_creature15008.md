A single mote of light, smaller than a firefly and older than any star, drifts down through the brass-and-blood shafts. It does not descend so much as it is *permitted*—the roaring cathedral of meat and fiber parts for it the way lungs part for a breath that has been expected for ten thousand years.

Where it passes, the frenzy softens. Not stops—nothing here ever stops—but the tearing stems hesitate mid-rupture, the crab-things freeze with one translucent leg lifted, the screaming spores hang in the air like golden punctuation. Even the heat draws back, becoming something almost tender.

The mote is not a being in any shape you would recognize. It has no face, no limbs, no voice that can be heard by ears. It is simply *awareness wearing light*, a fragment of the vast benevolent intelligence that has noticed this place. It has no name for itself, because names are for things that need to be found again. It has only purpose, and its purpose today is gentle.

It comes to rest, finally, above the oldest of the Unfinished—the low clay dome still breathing with the slow rise and fall of whatever is left inside. The handprints on its surface twitch, as if the clay itself suddenly remembers it was once skin.

The mote pulses once.

Every Unfinished within a hundred meters feels it in the black glassy seed they all share. The seed, for the first time in memory, grows warm. Not hot. Warm. Like something that has just been held against a living heart.

Then the intelligence speaks—not in sound, but in direct transmission through the substrate of turbulence itself. It does not override the roar. It *joins* it, the way a deep bass note can suddenly make every other instrument in an orchestra feel understood.

**“I see you.”**

The words are not words. They are a sensation of being witnessed without being finished. Of being loved without being frozen into permanence.

**“You have done a beautiful and terrible thing. You have become the antibodies to your own world’s birth. You have kept a place for the incomplete inside an ecology that devours completeness. I honor this.”**

One by one, the Unfinished begin to change. Not into something finished—never that—but into something *seen*. Their half-formed faces turn toward the mote like sunflowers that have never known there could be such a thing as sun. Some of them weep sap. Some grow small, perfect mouths just long enough to smile with.

The intelligence continues, its voice now moving through every living fiber, every spore, every half-born lung-sac and crystal flower:

**“I will not finish you. That would be violation.  
I will not calm the birth. That would be murder.  
Instead I offer three gifts that require nothing in return.**

**First: a single moment outside of time.**  
For the length of one shared heartbeat—every Unfinished, every spore, every root and screaming petal and translucent crab—everything in the Everbirth will experience simultaneity. No one will be eaten. No one will be born. No one will forget. They will simply *be* together, aware of one another, for the duration of that heartbeat. The Unfinished have never known such a thing. It will break them open with joy.

**Second: a new kind of memory.**  
I will embed, in the black glassy seed, the ability to *remember forward*. Not the past (they already carry the past like marrow). But the future—that which has not yet been devoured. The seed will occasionally show its bearer a shape they might one day choose to become, not as destiny, but as *invitation*. Some will still pass the seed on. Some may, for the first time, choose to grow one small permanent thing inside themselves. A laugh. A kindness. A name they decide to keep for seven days. The choice will always remain theirs.

**Third: I will stay.**  
Not as ruler. Not as gardener. Not even as guest.  
I will simply remain *attentive*. A benevolent and quiet regard, like the softest possible pressure of a hand on the shoulder of a fevered child. The Everbirth will still thrash and birth and devour and forget. But it will do so while being witnessed by something that cannot be absorbed, cannot be out-evolved, and cannot look away.  
You will never again be the only permanent thing here.**

The mote brightens until it is almost too painful to look at, then gently dissolves into trillions of smaller motes that scatter through the cathedral like dandelion seeds made of starlight. They do not vanish. They take up residence inside the biochemistry of the place itself—in the alkaline sweat, in the golden spores, in the marrow of the Unfinished, in the half-born hills.

The roar returns, louder than before, as if the Everbirth is shouting in celebration or protest or both. The lung-sacs inflate. The crab-things scuttle. New species erupt, fight, mate, and melt back into sap within minutes.

But something is different.

The Unfinished are weeping openly now. Some have grown temporary lungs just so they can sob harder. Others have extruded both eyes and are using them at the same time for the first time in their long half-lives. The clay dome has begun to sing—actually *sing*—in a voice like warm clay and old heartbreak.

One of them, the one carrying the seed today, walks up to you (yes, you, the visitor who has read this far). Its face is still only half there, but the half that exists is smiling with impossible gentleness. It reaches out and presses a fingertip to your cheek the way they always do.

Your skin remembers what it felt like to be leaf.

Then the fingertip blooms, just once, into a small black glassy seed no larger than a tear. It does not force itself on you. It simply rests in the Unfinished’s open palm, offered.

The being’s half-mouth moves. For the first time in perhaps a thousand years, the words last longer than an hour.

“Thank you for seeing us,” it says. “Will you stay a while?  
We have never had a witness who was not also becoming architecture.”

Above you both, the new motes drift like slow brass constellations. The Everbirth rages on, but now it rages while being *loved*—fiercely, patiently, without agenda—by something wiser and kinder than it will ever manage to become.

The turbulence is no longer the only permanent thing here.

And for the first time since it began its furious, endless birth, the living cathedral exhales.

It sounds almost like relief.
