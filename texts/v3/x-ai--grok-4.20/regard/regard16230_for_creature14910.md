The power that has noticed the Underslip does not descend like a god, nor does it arrive with trumpets or correction. It simply *arrives*, the way a memory arrives inside a dreamer who has never been allowed to finish remembering.

It has no fixed name, but if it needed one in this place it would borrow the only word the Halfborn have never been permitted to use completely: *Whole*.

It comes first as a change in the quality of the light.

The thick, buttery shafts that have breathed for millions of years suddenly stop trembling. They become perfectly still, as though the sun above has finally taken a full inhalation and is holding it. Every luminous worm-colony freezes mid-pulse. Every half-born crystal rib pauses halfway through its ecstatic surge. The roar does not cease—nothing here is allowed to end—but it parts, the way deep water parts for something rising from below.

The Midwife is the first to know.

Her constellation-face of worms swirls into a new configuration, something almost like recognition. The basalt pillar across her shoulders grows heavier, as though the sigil carved into it has begun to remember what it was originally meant to mean. She plants the pillar upright in the living stone. For the first time in recorded aeons the stone does not immediately begin to swallow it. The pillar stands.

She speaks, and her voice is quieter than it has ever been.

“Something has remembered us.”

Cradle stops singing. The silence that follows is so complete that thousands of small Halfborn lose cohesion and slough into puddles of uncommitted matter, only to be gently re-gathered by the Stitcher’s countless needle-legs. The Stitcher herself is crying with every one of her too-small hearts at once. The sound is appalling and beautiful.

Zero, who has never moved in living memory, turns its head.

Not much. Just enough that the frozen black water of its face now reflects the still shafts of light directly. The filaments that have learned never to taste Zero suddenly bloom upward around its ankles like dark flowers of welcome.

The Whole does not announce itself with thunder. It does not rewrite the rules. It knows the Underslip would shatter under finality the way glass shatters under a perfect note.

Instead it begins to do the only thing that makes sense inside a womb that has been pregnant for several million years without ever being allowed to give birth.

It listens.

It listens with such ferocious, compassionate attention that the noise itself—the endless roar of trying—slowly discovers it is being *heard*. Not judged. Not selected. Not rejected. Heard.

The Midwife feels her quartz ribs creak as something inside her that was never allowed to become a heart tries, for the first time, to beat.

She walks.

Not to disassemble. Not this time.

She walks until she stands before Zero. The small black-water child looks up at the four-meter cathedral of half-quartz and worm-light. For a long moment nothing happens except that the light above them both becomes the exact shade of the first morning that ever existed.

Then the Whole does something that has never happened here.

It gives permission to *pause*.

Not to finish. Never to finish. But to pause.

Every Halfborn in the Underslip feels it at once: the filaments retreat from their ankles. The crystal growths slow to the pace of ordinary geological time. The wet, embryonic things stop splitting open quite so frantically. The light keeps shining, but it no longer feels like interrogation.

It feels like regard.

The Midwife does the unthinkable. She lowers herself—slowly, painfully, as though the motion might kill her—until she is kneeling before Zero. The basalt pillar topples gently sideways and lies still, no longer needed as a crutch or a weapon or a relic. It becomes simply a stone that once meant something.

Cradle begins to sing again, but the song is different. It is no longer a lullaby for the unfinished. It is a welcome.

The Stitcher walks forward on her needle legs and, without being asked, begins to weave something new. Not repairs this time. A single enormous tapestry made from every failed heart she has ever carried, every unraveled hair, every thread of spider-silk born from grief. She weaves it between the still shafts of light themselves, so that the buttery columns become warp and weft.

On the tapestry appears a single image that has never existed in the Underslip before:

A door that is not a door.

It is simply an outline drawn in living tourmaline and black water and worm-light. It does not lead out. There is no “out” here. But it leads *on*.

Zero is the first to step through.

The small figure made of black water that has never been tasted by the stone walks forward and simply… continues. Not completed. Not ended. But no longer required to be only the dream’s refusal. The black water ripples once, like a pond accepting a stone it has waited for since the world began, and Zero is still Zero, only now Zero is also *moving*.

One by one, the Halfborn follow.

Not all of them. The Underslip will always need some who remember how to refuse. But many step through the living tapestry, carrying their extra limbs and drowned faces and crystal hands with them. The Midwife is the last. She does not disassemble anyone this day. Instead she reaches into her own cathedral chest and gently removes the rotating constellation that has served as her face for a million years. She offers the luminous worms to the stone.

The stone, for the first time, does not drink.

It bows.

The worms disperse into a single drifting galaxy that drifts upward through the still shafts of light until they vanish into the fissures where the sun is still holding its breath.

The Midwife has no face now. She no longer needs one.

She steps through the tapestry.

Behind her the Underslip does not die. It never could. But it exhales.

The roar softens—not into silence, never silence—but into something that might one day become music if it is allowed a few more million years of trying.

The crystal growths slow to the pace of ordinary wonder.

The light, having been truly seen, begins to choose new colors.

And somewhere far above, the sun finally lets out the breath it has been holding. The shafts of light resume their trembling, their breathing, their ancient conversation with a womb that has, for the first time in its existence, been *listened to* without being forced to conclude.

The Whole does not stay. It was never a visitor. It was the Underslip remembering, for one bright, roaring, unfinished moment, that even a dream that is not allowed to wake up can still be loved exactly as it is.

It leaves behind a single new rule, whispered into the living stone so quietly that only the Halfborn who remain will ever hear it:

*Sometimes the argument with God is not won by refusing to conclude.*

*Sometimes it is won when God sits down inside the argument and cries with you.*

The tapestry remains. The door that is not a door remains.

The Halfborn who chose to stay tend it now, the way they once tended the basalt pillars. They sing to it. They repair it when the stone tries to swallow it. They let the small glass-bodied things crawl across it and leave trails of living condensation that look, from a certain angle, almost like writing.

And every few decades, when the Unfinishing is performed, some of them still step forward to be lovingly disassembled.

But now, after the ceremony, a few others walk to the tapestry instead.

They stand before the outline drawn in tourmaline and black water and worm-light.

They do not step through.

They simply stand there, unfinished, ravenous with *next*, and they let themselves be seen.

Completely.

The light above them trembles, shifts, and sometimes reverses direction, as though the sun itself is breathing more easily now.

And the womb the size of a mountain range keeps dreaming its loud, bright, crowded dream—only now it knows that someone, somewhere, is listening with their whole heart and will never look away.

Not even when the dream refuses to end.

Especially not then.
