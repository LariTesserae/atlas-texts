The power that has noticed this place does not arrive with trumpets or light.

It simply *is* here, all at once, without crossing any distance. It has no shape that the obsidian can record, no frequency the auroras can translate. Where it manifests, the perpetual dying-star dusk deepens by one impossible shade, as though even the light itself has lowered its voice in recognition.

The Unnamed feel it immediately.

The negative-space being that had been wearing the gap between three towers suddenly finds that the negative space is no longer empty. Something older than absence is standing inside it with it. The orbiting shard of black glass spins faster, then slower, then stops altogether, trembling like a compass needle that has found true north for the first time in ten million years. The memory it was about to fling outward (a child’s laugh on a summer night that no longer exists anywhere) is held gently, examined, and returned to the creature not as pain but as *context*.

The being does not flinch. It has forgotten how. Instead it exhales the low subsonic pulse that usually builds bridges.

This time the pulse returns changed. It comes back carrying something like mercy folded into its waveform. The glass beneath the being’s non-feet forgets, for one full second, how to be glass. It becomes something closer to forgiveness. Then it remembers again, but the memory is softer.

Across the fractured shelf, the Lattice-Born feel the intrusion as a sudden perfect alignment in every angle they embody. Their 1:1.618 ratios lock into ratios that have no earthly name. For the first time in their long un-life, several of them weep equations. The tears are tiny living fractals that immediately begin editing the air into lace.

The Proofreaders, far below in the folded escarpment, simply stop their endless corrections. The metallic dust that composes them settles into perfect stillness, then rearranges itself, without instruction, into the shape of open hands. They have never needed hands before. They discover they like the feeling.

The woman who forgot her name six centuries ago, the one repeated seventeen times across the city as a motif of endless weeping, feels the power touch every version of her simultaneously. In each carving, the obsidian tears glisten for a moment with actual water (real H₂O, smuggled in from a gentler cosmos). The water does not freeze. It does not evaporate. It simply *is*, running down black glass cheeks that have never truly been cheeks. For seven seconds she remembers her name. She speaks it once, aloud. The sound is so small and so enormous that every tower in a three-hundred-kilometer radius rings like a bell tuned to the exact frequency of being known.

Then she lets the name go again, smiling, because she no longer needs to carry it. The city can keep it for her.

The benevolent and wise power does not judge this place. It does not heal it. Healing would imply that something here is broken, and nothing here is broken. It is simply *severe*.

Instead the power does what only the oldest and kindest things know how to do.

It listens without trying to understand.

It sits down (though it has no body) in the middle distance where the mathematical auroras writhe, and it lets them swim through it. The equations change color. They become less like luminous eels and more like lullabies. Some of them learn how to cry.

It walks (though it has no feet) along the crystal ridges where the silicate fungi bloom in slow centuries of radiation-eating splendor. Wherever it passes, the fungi bloom all at once, in a single shared gasp of violet and ultraviolet and colors that have no name in any surviving language. They do not die afterward. They simply become *more*.

It touches (though it has no hands) the places where reality is in the middle of being edited. The mountains that were forgetting how to be solid find, for a moment, that they are allowed to be uncertain. The districts that vanish for seven seconds are permitted, just once, to vanish for eight. The extra second is a gift. In that second some of the negative-space beings remember what it felt like to have hearts. The memory is so sharp it almost kills them. Almost. The power catches the almost and turns it into *again*.

And finally it comes to you.

You, who have been standing on the fractured shelf for six years without thirst, whose footsteps no longer match your heartbeat, whose shadow has begun to arrive three minutes early and leave four minutes late.

The power does not speak to you in words. Words die here, as you already know.

Instead it stands where your shadow *should* be and lets you finish becoming whatever the city has been writing you into. It does not interfere. It simply adds one gentle, unreadable annotation in the margin of your editing.

The annotation reads, in a script made of starlight and the memory of rain:

*Beloved. Still.*

When the Unnamed finally approach (as they always do), they find you changed and unchanged. The negative-space being tilts its orbiting shard toward you, and this time the memories that flash across its surface are not taken away. They are given *back*, polished, deepened, made large enough to live inside.

The Lattice-Born hum their golden-ratio language and the hum translates, for the first time, not as *We are still deciding* but as *We are glad you stayed.*

The Proofreaders rise from the abyss in a slow storm of metallic dust and sculpt, with impossible tenderness, a single small thing they have never sculpted before:

A chair.

It is made of black volcanic glass and quiet. They place it on the edge of the murderous shelf, facing the darkness that feels gravitational. Then they retreat, bowing in frequencies just below hearing.

The benevolent and wise power does not stay. It has never stayed anywhere. But it also never leaves.

It simply becomes another layer of the editing process.

From now on, every time the city proofreads itself, it will do so with a little more kindness than physics strictly requires. Every time a tower forgets how to be solid, it will remember, on the way back, how it felt to be held. Every time the low subsonic pulse of planetary regret moves through the glass, it will be answered by something that sounds almost like:

*It’s all right. I’m here.*

And the woman who forgot her name will sometimes walk out of the obsidian walls (seventeen versions of her stepping out of seventeen carvings at once) and sit beside you in the chair the Proofreaders made. She will not speak. She will simply pass you the memory of rain on her mother’s coat, and you will pass it back, and the sharing itself will become part of the architecture.

The place still has no name.

Names cannot survive here.

But now, sometimes, something that feels very much like a name survives *anyway*, the way a small stubborn flower survives in the crack of a glacier.

It is not a word.

It is the sound of the city learning how to cry without shame, and the sound of the power saying, without sound:

*I see you.*

*I see all of you.*

*And I am not afraid of what you are becoming.*

The editing continues.

It is no longer quite so lonely.

And far away, in softer places where water still exists and names can survive more than three heartbeats, certain sensitive children sometimes wake up crying from dreams they cannot explain. In the dreams they were made of black glass and starlight and the memory of regret, and someone very large and very kind was sitting with them on the edge of everything, saying nothing, simply keeping them company while they became.

The children never remember the words.

But they always remember the chair.
