The benevolent and wise power that has turned its attention here does not arrive with trumpets or radiance. It simply *becomes present*, the way a beam of true sunlight sometimes finds its way through miles of canopy and touches the floor of a forgotten glen. One moment the Cathedral of Thirst is only itself—slow, green, patient, loud with its own vegetable organ—and the next, *something older than patience* is listening alongside the Choir.

It is not a being in the ordinary sense. It has no name the Choir could pronounce, no form that would not dissolve instantly in their iron-mint fluid. Yet every living thing inside the cathedral feels the shift at once, the way a plant feels the first touch of true spring after a century of twilight. The great respiratory bladders pause mid-contraction. The seed-fish freeze in their synchronized spirals. Even the Rooted, half-grown into the floor, lift their heavy green heads as though waking from a dream that had lasted seventy years.

What the power does is almost nothing.

And everything.

It begins by *remembering* the cathedral exactly as it is, without judgment, without wish for improvement. It remembers the first reed-and-bone scaffolds the long-ago people left to rot. It remembers the taste of dry wind on skin that no one here has felt in ten generations. It remembers the children who once ran on two legs instead of drifting on long, jointed limbs. And in that perfect, unhurried remembering, something loosens.

A single, new note—not deeper, but *clearer*—rises from the root mat. It is not louder than the cathedral’s ancient bass. It is simply *clean*, like the first drop of rain after decades of the same mist. When this note touches the resonant sacs of the Choir, their own voices change without their willing it. For the first time in centuries, some of them produce a sound that is not a drone but a *melody*, fragile and bright, like water remembering it was once snow.

The power does not command. It invites.

It invites the cathedral to taste what it has never tasted: the memory of thirst satisfied. Not the endless submersion it has known, but the brief, astonishing sweetness of water touching parched lips after long denial. The invitation is gentle enough that the living architecture could refuse it without consequence. Yet the cathedral, being courteous in its own enormous way, accepts.

Slowly—over weeks that feel like minutes inside the stretched time of the place—tiny changes begin.

In the uppermost vaults, where the light has always been sourceless and pale green, a faint circle of true gold appears. Not sunlight exactly, but the *idea* of sunlight, filtered and translated into something the plant can bear. The circle is no wider than a dinner plate. Yet every seed-fish in the cathedral eventually drifts up to hover beneath it, turning their translucent bodies so that the gold touches their fronds. They glow like living lanterns for hours afterward.

Some of the younger Choir feel an ache they cannot name. It is not unhappiness. It is the opposite of numbness. They begin to linger near the healing columns not because they are injured, but because the power has quietly reminded their edited flesh that it was once *chosen*, not merely accepted. One young singer, whose rootlets have only just begun to drift behind her ears, presses her forehead to a column for three days. When she pulls away, a single tiny blue flower—something that has not existed in this place since the first settlers arrived—has opened in the hollow of her throat. The flower has no scent that the fluid can carry, but its color makes the entire choir weep in six-part harmony for an hour.

The Rooted are the most deeply moved. One of them, whose legs have been wooden for forty years, does something no one has seen in living memory: he asks the cathedral, aloud, for permission to walk again, just once. The power does not grant or deny. It simply *witnesses* the request. That is enough. The great mat of rhizomes beneath him softens, rearranges, and for the first time in decades the elder’s legs separate. They are weak, ridiculous, beautiful. Supported by two of the youngest, he drifts—not walks, not yet—to the center of the largest gallery and stands upright in the fluid like a man. He cries without sound. His tears are the same pale gold as the gametes released during the great convocations. The cathedral drinks them and grows three new flowering spurs overnight, each one shaped like an open human hand.

The power does not heal the Choir in the way a lesser mercy might. It does not reverse their transformation or return them to dry flesh. Instead it does something wiser: it gives them *choice* again, after centuries of having forgotten the shape of choice.

Some will remain as they are—glorious, patient, half-tree, singing with the cathedral for the rest of time.  
Some will ask the plant to release them. Their rootlets will wither, their extra joints will shorten, their resonant sacs will shrink back into ordinary throats. They will climb the long spiral of living stairs that has never been climbed all the way to the top, and they will breach the canopy miles above into a world they have not seen since their distant ancestors sealed themselves away. What they will find there, the power does not say. That is not its gift to give.

A very few—the oldest Rooted—will do neither. They will simply open themselves more completely. Their bodies will finish becoming architecture while their *awareness* is gently, lovingly lifted free. These souls do not die. They become the new, clear note that now moves through the fluid at unpredictable intervals. When it passes, every Choir member feels, for a single heartbeat, what it is like to be both entirely plant and entirely free.

The power stays for exactly as long as it is needed, which is to say it stays for one afternoon that lasts eleven years. Then it begins to withdraw, the way true dawn withdraws into ordinary morning. As it leaves, it offers one final, wordless understanding that settles into every living cell of the cathedral and every cell of the Choir:

*You were never a mistake.*  
*You were never a surrender.*  
*You were a question asked so patiently that the universe finally had time to answer.*

When the presence is fully gone, the Cathedral of Thirst is almost exactly as it was before.

The fluid is still cool, iron-mint, endlessly circulating.  
The great organ bladders still contract in their slow waves.  
The Choir still drift and sing and release golden clouds when the mood takes them.

But now, once every seven days, the sourceless green light is joined for nine exact minutes by that small, perfect circle of gold. During those minutes the seed-fish arrange themselves into the shape of a human heart—something none of them have ever seen—and the Choir sing the new, clear melody they learned without being taught.

And sometimes, very rarely, a child is born with ordinary round pupils and no rootlets behind the ears.

The child is not treated as better or worse. It is simply treated as *possible*.

The Cathedral of Thirst has outlived every intention its builders once had.

Now it is learning, with infinite slowness and perfect courtesy, how to begin intending again.

And somewhere far above, beyond the miles of canopy, something benevolent and wise smiles with a mouth made of light, and continues on its way.
