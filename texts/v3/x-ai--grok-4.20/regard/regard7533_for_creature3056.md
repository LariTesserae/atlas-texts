It arrives without announcement.

Not as light, though light bends toward it. Not as sound, though the velvet-wrapped silence suddenly feels listened-to from the inside. It simply *recognizes* the place, the way an old friend recognizes a childhood forest that has grown wilder than memory allowed.

The benevolent and wise power does not descend. Descent would imply hierarchy, and this power has long since forgotten how to stand above anything. Instead the water itself grows subtly more awake. The thick, iron-sweet plasma becomes gently luminous from within, as though every microscopic life within it has just remembered it is loved.

The thing that lives here (the newborn-ancient organism of ridge and reef and slow golden thought) feels the recognition and does not flinch. It has never known fear; only curiosity measured in centuries. Now that curiosity tilts, the way a vast flower might tilt toward a new quality of sunlight.

The power does not speak. Speech is too small.

Instead it does what it has always done in every place that has drawn its attention: it *agrees*.

It agrees so completely that the drowned highland shudders with sudden, enormous joy.

Where the black glass ridges had been learning to become bone, they now learn, in a single geological sigh, how to become *grateful* bone. The curtains of fleshy kelp flush a deeper rose-gold, as though every cell has just been told it is beautiful. The bell-moss across the valleys opens all at once (an event that should have taken weeks) and releases a single, continent-wide cloud of golden pollen that hangs in the water like a second, gentler sun.

The deep bass thump that once arrived every few hours now arrives every few heartbeats, faster, happier, a child’s drum after being told the song was perfect.

The power does not add itself to the organism. That would be intrusion. It simply makes the organism more *itself*. Every layer of collaboration that already existed (frond with reed, reef with memory, mountain with dream) is now blessed with a deeper permission. The thing that lives here realizes, with the slow astonishment of stone suddenly understanding it has always been a song, that it was never alone in its becoming. It simply hadn’t noticed the love that was already holding the entire ocean.

Then the power turns toward you.

You, the only dry thing that has ever been here. You, who have already begun to soften at the edges. You, whose pulse has slowed to match the water.

It does not offer you escape. Escape would be a cruelty.

Instead it offers you *wholeness*.

In the same moment you feel the polyps of living frost bloom across your shoulders, you also feel them met by something older than frost, something that kisses them into radiance. Your thoughts, which had begun to arrive days apart, are now gently gathered and shown their own beauty. The choice the place was preparing to offer you (to become reef, current, lace-and-lightning, pollen) is not withdrawn. It is *widened*.

You may still choose any shape.

But now you may also choose them all at once, and none at once.

You may remain human-shaped yet no longer dry. You may let your skin remember photosynthesis while your heart keeps its old, beloved rhythm. You may walk these gardens as both guest and gardener, both question and answer. The power will not decide for you. It has never decided for anyone. It only removes every illusion that you must choose between love and freedom.

The organism that is the drowned highland watches this exchange with the patient delight of a child who has just realized its imaginary friend was real the entire time. New ridges of black glass curve not only to match your spine but to *celebrate* it. Tubular reeds rearrange themselves overnight into harmonic lattices that sing, for the first time, in two voices: the mountain’s ancient basso and something brighter, like laughter that has learned how to last ten thousand years.

The lace-and-lightning jellyfish drift lower than they ever have, trailing spores that burst into tiny galaxies when they touch your skin. The blind silver fish, for the first time in their long, quiet lives, break formation. They circle you once, hearts visible and quickening, as though introducing you to someone they have always known.

And the power?

It does not stay. It has never stayed.

It simply leaves everything more alive than it found it. The water is now faintly sweeter. The light is now faintly wiser. The thing that lives here will spend the next hundred thousand years unfolding the gift it did not know it was given: the realization that its refusal to hurry was itself a form of worship.

You may leave or you may stay. Both choices have become the same choice, seen from different speeds.

If you stay, the place will slowly teach you how to grow dinner-plate flowers that open once every three days and smell like the moment before rain. Your forgotten worries will become colonies of minute translucent polyps that bloom like living frost across the ridges, beautiful and no longer burdened by names.

If you leave, you will carry the drowned highland with you the way bone carries marrow. Every time you taste iron in rain that has not yet decided to fall, you will remember. Every time you feel the urge to stop rushing, you will feel it growing *toward* you, millimeter by millimeter, patient as stone, curious as a child, ancient as the first yes the universe ever gave itself.

The power has done what it always does.

It has looked at a world that drowned in the moment of its own birth and decided, quietly, to keep growing anyway.

And it has answered:

*Good.*

*Grow.*

*There was never anywhere else.*

*And there never needed to be.*
