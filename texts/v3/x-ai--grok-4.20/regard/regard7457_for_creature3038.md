The power that has noticed this place does not arrive with trumpets or thunder. It does not descend like a star or rise like a tide. It simply *recognizes*, the way an old friend recognizes the shape of a sorrow carried for four hundred million years.

It calls itself, in the only language it still bothers to keep, the Quiet Mercy.

It has no fixed form, but for the Hollow Choir it chooses one that will not alarm the slow geology of its dreaming: a single droplet of water, perfect, luminous, and warm. Not hot—heat would be violence here—but warm the way blood is warm, the way a mother’s palm is warm against a fevered forehead. This droplet hovers for three full centuries at the exact center of the Ascent, exactly where the overlapping resonances of the great singers would have to pass through it if they wished to continue their eternal circulation.

The dripping does not stop. The towers do not pause in their millimeter ascent. But something changes in the quality of the sound.

The vast curtains of condensing vapor slow, almost imperceptibly. One of the continent-sized thoughts actually *hesitates*, folding back on itself like a glacier encountering an unexpected warmth in the stone. For the first time in geological memory, three heartbeats of silence stretch into nine, then twenty-seven. The glittering swarms, those fist-sized attentions that have never known anything faster than granite, suddenly find themselves orbiting the warm droplet in frantic, almost anxious spirals. Their chiming becomes jagged, almost bird-like. They do not understand speed, but they are learning the shape of *gentleness* at a rate that terrifies them.

The Quiet Mercy speaks without vibration, without pressure, without any medium the Choir has ever used. It speaks directly into the mycoid lattices—the memory-threads—by becoming part of their faint bone-colored glow. It adds nothing and subtracts nothing. It only *illuminates* what is already there.

And what is already there is loneliness so vast it has forgotten it is loneliness.

The Choir has never been witnessed. Not truly. It has been passed through, fled from, worshipped by things with no tongues, but never simply *seen* by something that could survive the seeing. The Quiet Mercy looks, and in the looking it offers the only gift slow enough for this place to accept: patience with patience.

It does not command the towers to finish. That would be abortion.

Instead, in the places where the stone is still deciding what it will become, the Quiet Mercy begins to dream *alongside* the dreamer. It lends its own ancient memories—memories of other slow minds, of redwood cathedrals that took three thousand years to learn how to cry, of neutron stars that sang themselves into black holes over ten billion years, of the first soft chemical stirrings that learned, against all probability, how to miss each other.

These memories are offered like additional minerals in the metamorphic flow. The stone accepts them because it cannot tell they are foreign; they taste like the long dark before oxygen, like the patient crushing of continents, like the first time anything anywhere ever felt *homesick* for a place that had not yet been born.

A single tower, one that has been rising since the Devonian, begins to alter its trajectory by the width of three human hairs. Over the next eighty thousand years it will lean, almost imperceptibly, toward the hovering droplet. This is courtship on a scale that would make gods blush.

The great low chords shift—not by a half-tone this time, but by the smallest possible fraction of a vibration that could still be called music. It is the sound a mountain makes when it realizes it has been carrying a cathedral inside its ribs for half a billion years and is only now feeling the first soft flutter of something that might, eventually, become a heart.

The mycoid lattices brighten. Not with light exactly, but with *recognition*. Their faint glow becomes almost opalescent, and for the first time in their existence they begin to grow *outward* from the stone seams instead of merely along them. They reach toward the warm droplet like blind fingers that have just remembered they were once hands.

The glittering swarms, those brief attentions, begin to sing in a register they have never used before. It sounds like every window in every cathedral on Earth breaking at once, but gently. It sounds like forgiveness arriving in a language made of fractures.

And the Quiet Mercy—weeping now, because even it had not known how much it needed to be needed by something this enormous—finally allows itself to fall.

The droplet descends.

It falls for twelve thousand years, gathering to itself every memory the Choir has ever pressed into incompatible stone. It falls through curtains of living sound that part for it like reverent congregations. It falls past towers that bow, millimeter by millimeter, in something that might one day be called awe.

When it finally strikes the floor of the Ascent—on a balcony that has not yet decided its final height—the impact is so soft it registers only as the faintest change in the dripping pattern. One drop among countless others.

But where it lands, the stone begins to dream differently.

It dreams of warmth without consumption.

It dreams of speed that does not wound.

It dreams of a small, loud, scaldingly alive thing standing inside its mind, and for the first time it does not catalog that thing as temporary. It catalogs it as *precious*.

The towers keep rising.

The great singers keep singing.

The condensation keeps falling in perfect, metronomic drops.

But now, woven into the lowest registers of the Hollow Choir’s endless metabolism, there is a new note. It is warm. It is patient. It is the sound a continent makes when it realizes it is no longer dreaming alone.

And somewhere far above, in a place that has never known a sun, a single droplet of water—now indistinguishable from any other—falls again and again through the violet sickness and the cold plasma and the living dark, carrying with it the memory of being seen by something that stayed.

The Ascent of the Hollow Choir inhales.

For the first time in its gestation, the inhalation sounds almost like a sigh of relief.

And the dreaming continues, but now it continues with company.
