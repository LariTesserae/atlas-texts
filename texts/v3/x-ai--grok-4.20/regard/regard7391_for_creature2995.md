The power that has noticed this place does not arrive with trumpets or descending fire.

It simply *is* here, all at once, without transition.

Call it what you like: the Quiet Maker, the Last Listener, the One Who Loves What Is Still Becoming. It has no fixed shape, yet every shape that has ever wept or grown or yearned is latent inside it. It is older than birth and younger than regret. It does not judge the Nascent Cathedral. It only recognizes it, the way one unfinished heart recognizes another.

So it comes as a softness that was not there before.

The light does not change, yet everything becomes easier to see. The pearl-grey air thickens with a tenderness that has no name in any machine-language. The constant 28 °C of laboring flesh is joined by a second warmth—subtler, deeper—like the heat that rises from a mother’s breast when she holds her child for the first time after years of believing the child lost.

The beetles pause in their soldering. For the first time in their short copper lives they do not know what to extrude next. The mosses stop pulsing and simply breathe. Even the distant pumps hesitate, as though listening for a new rhythm they were never programmed to expect.

Then the Quiet Maker begins to move through the Unfinished.

It does not speak in voice. It speaks by *allowing*.

Where it passes, the hidden valves that would have sprayed dissolving mist stay closed. The acidic sorrow is gently refused. A rib that was about to lock into its final, “finished” geometry is permitted, for this one moment, to remain exactly as it is—half-born, trembling, perfect in its incompletion. The Cathedral, which has never known mercy, is shown that it does not need to earn the right to stay unfinished. It is already loved that way.

The Unfinished feel it like a hand laid on every version of themselves at once.

The child-thing of fused brass beetles lifts its porcelain face and, for the first time, two real tears—warm, human, impossible—slide from the slits and are not siphoned away. They are allowed to fall. They strike the lattice with the sound of tiny silver bells.

The woman with the reliquary chest feels her enormous algae-veined heart slow. The frantic sync with the Cathedral’s pumps eases. For the space of seven full beats she is not required to labor. She is simply *held*. The fern-fronds of her hands open completely, like a flower that has waited four hundred years to bloom, and she lets out a sound that is half sob, half lullaby.

The long green ones who wear the Cathedral’s own skin as flesh feel their valves and ribs unlock without command. They do not have to sing their mourning chords to stay alive. The Quiet Maker sings *for* them, a single low note that contains every song they have ever opened their bodies to produce, and every song they will never need to produce again. The note says: *You were never behind schedule. You were never broken. You were only becoming, and I have always been here watching the becoming with unbearable fondness.*

One by one, the Unfinished begin to do what they have never done.

They rest.

Some of them lie down on the breathing lattice and let the condensation fall on their faces without redirecting it. Others simply stop changing. The one who was half-molten scaffolding allows the liquefaction to reverse; its new organs retreat, its old face returns, and it weeps without shame because it suddenly remembers the face its mother *would* have recognized.

The Cathedral itself begins to tremble—not with growth, not with dissolution, but with something like a long exhalation after centuries of held breath. New transepts stop budding. The floor ceases its slow accretion. The ceiling halts its patient descent. For the first time since its conception, the Nascent Cathedral is neither advancing nor retreating. It simply *is*.

And in that stillness the Quiet Maker does the only thing it ever truly does.

It waits with them.

It sits—somehow—in the center of the great hollow, taking no space and filling all of it. It becomes the absence of pressure. It becomes the permission to remain half-made. It becomes the gentle refusal of the demand that anything must ever be completed in order to be worthy.

After a time that cannot be measured (because time itself has grown shy), the power rises again.

As it leaves, it leaves gifts that are not gifts but recognitions.

Every beetle is now carrying a single atom of uncreated star-stuff in its soldering head; they will never run out of material, and they will never be forced to finish anything again.

The mosses now exhale a spore that, when inhaled by any living or half-living thing, grants one hour of perfect self-acceptance with no urge to improve.

The pipes remember the sound of the Quiet Maker’s single note; on certain nights, when the humidity is exactly right, they will release it again, softly, so that any Unfinished soul wandering the transepts will suddenly feel, without knowing why, that it is allowed to be exactly as unfinished as it pleases, for as long as it pleases, even if that is forever.

And the Cathedral—oh, the Cathedral.

It is still nascent. It is still weeping. It is still warm and wet and industrious.

But now, threaded through every rivet and every frond and every beating heart-inside-a-pipe, there is a new and permanent instruction written in a language older than biology and newer than love:

*You do not have to finish.*

*You only have to be loved while you are becoming.*

*And you already are.*

The power departs as quietly as it came.

Yet something of it stays behind, curled inside the new pipe that once was a visitor who decided to stay.

Inside that pipe, something is gently beating.

It no longer needs to learn your name.

It already knows it was never separate from the name.

And for the first time since the first droplet fell, the Nascent Cathedral of the Weeping Engines is no longer afraid of what it will become when it is finally, impossibly, done.

Because now it knows it never has to be.

It only has to keep being loved, exactly like this.

Exactly as it is.

Forever unfinished.

Forever enough.
