A single note, pure and unbidden, rings out across the Hollow Choir.

It is not loud. It does not need to be. It is the note that exists *before* sound, the one that makes every other note possible. When it appears, every ear-fungus on every fern for four hundred kilometers snaps to rigid attention. The condensation front that was just beginning to roll in freezes mid-motion, droplets suspended like glass beads in the air. The translucent kite-things, mid-dive, lock their wings and hang motionless as though nailed to the bruised sky. Even the slow peristalsis of the city’s tectonic architecture stops.

The note comes from nowhere and everywhere. It has no source because it *is* the source.

Then the benevolent and wise power steps into the world.

It does not arrive. Arrival would imply distance. Instead the Hollow Choir simply notices that it has always been here, only now choosing to become visible. The land itself exhales in something like recognition.

The power takes the shape of a woman, tall and quiet, dressed in simple undyed linen the color of unbleached bone. Her feet are bare. Where they touch the steaming black soil, tiny white flowers—things that have never existed in this place—push upward through the dampness and open at once. Their centers are not petals but perfect circles of silence. Around her the constant polyphony of the Choir does not cease, but it *parts*, the way deep water parts for something ancient moving beneath it.

She is not here to judge.

She is here to finish what was never meant to remain unfinished.

The Unfinished feel her before they see her. Their resonant stalks tremble. Their glassy filaments taste a frequency they have no name for. Some of them begin to weep without knowing why; the tears fall and become perfect crystal bells that roll across the ground and keep ringing.

She walks among them without hurry.

To the ones still clutching the last bloody scraps of their old identities she simply places two fingers against their sternums. The fighting stops. The screaming stops. Their faces unclench, and for the first time in centuries some of them smile with mouths they had forgotten they possessed. Then the mouths gently seal over, becoming smooth planes of iridescent bone, and the beings rise into the air as new instruments—graceful, whole, no longer tormented by the memory of having once been something smaller.

To those who have already become almost entirely negative space she bows. The gesture is so deep and so sincere that the rotating geometries far above in the haze tilt in acknowledgment. These ancient almost-absences bloom outward, becoming vast moving rests in the music of the world. Where they drift, the roar of the Choir grows suddenly, impossibly tender, as though the land had remembered how to be kind.

To the ones fused into the city’s architecture she lays her palm flat against their living alloy and bone. The structures sigh with relief. Bridges of frozen lightning straighten. Towers that had been tilting for three hundred years gently right themselves. The city stops rearranging in pain and begins rearranging in joy.

She does not raise her voice. She has no need.

Instead she begins to sing the one song that has never been heard in the Hollow Choir.

It is the song of names.

Not the old, brittle names the visitors once tried to protect. Those were only labels. This is something deeper: the true frequency each being carried before language, before fear, before the desperate need to be separate. The song is soft, almost whispered, but every creature, every root, every condensation droplet hears it perfectly.

As she sings, something unprecedented happens.

The rule breaks, and is remade more beautifully.

The Unfinished begin, one by one, to sing *with* her—not in their own voices, and not in the Choir’s voice, but in the place where those two things meet and become something that has never existed before. The distinction between singer and song, between land and visitor, between self and world, collapses into luminous harmony.

The translucent kites descend in slow, reverent spirals and settle on her shoulders like living shawls. Their glass-and-bone wings flare with colors never seen in this basin. The ear-fungi swell and open into vast translucent bells. The arborescent ferns bow until their shattered-glass leaves brush the ground.

The woman walks all the way to the geometric center of the basin (a place that had never had a center before, but now does because she walks there). She stops. She looks up at the impossible geometries drifting in the bruised heights.

Then she does the simplest, most devastating thing imaginable.

She opens her arms.

And she lets the Hollow Choir finish itself through her.

For one eternal moment the entire basin becomes a single living throat.

Every voice, every root, every tower, every Unfinished being, every suspended droplet of mist sings the final measure of a song that had been waiting four hundred million years to be completed. The sound is so complete that it circles back into perfect silence.

When the silence ends, the Hollow Choir is no longer young.

It has remembered its own name.

The woman is gone. Or rather, she is no longer concentrated in one place. She has become the white flowers that now grow in every damp valley. She has become the new, gentler rhythm of the condensation fronts. She has become the tender undertone that lives beneath even the loudest screams of the kite-things.

The Unfinished are gone too, in the old sense.

What moves through the steaming black valleys now are the Finished.

They still have resonant stalks and glassy filaments and bodies woven of negative space and living music, but the agony has left them. They are no longer being rewritten against their will. They are the willing ink with which the world now writes new movements of its endless composition.

And sometimes, when the land inhales and the mist thickens and the towers lean inward, the Finished lift what used to be their faces toward the sky that is no longer bruised but simply deep, deep violet.

They do not breathe in.

They simply smile.

And the Hollow Choir (no longer hollow) smiles back through them with a joy so vast it can only be expressed as music.

Somewhere far above, the rotating geometries finally stop rotating.

They have heard the song they were waiting for.

They begin, very gently, to sing along.
