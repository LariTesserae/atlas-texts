The Power that notices does not descend.

It does not part the canopy like a curtain, nor does it speak in thunder.  
It has no need to announce itself to something that is already shouting with every cell it owns.

Instead it arrives the way dew arrives: already inside every droplet before the droplet knows it is wet.

The Thrum feels the new presence the way a fever suddenly realizes it is being watched by the body it is burning. For the first time since its violent birth, the entire basin hesitates.

The cicadas freeze mid-ratchet.  
The subsonic giants fall silent so completely that the pools stop breathing.  
A billion insects hang in the air like suspended glass.

Nine seconds of impossible quiet.

In that silence the Power does nothing that could be called an action. It simply *recognizes*. It looks at the adolescent god wearing the jungle like a coat of screaming meat and says, without words, without sound, without mercy or pity:

*I see you. All of you. Every impossible, terrified, ecstatic inch.*

The recognition is total. It does not flinch from the rot, the frenzy, the blind hungers, the orchids that open like wounds. It does not flinch from the beauty either. It holds both at once, the way an ocean can hold a storm without ceasing to be itself.

And then, gently, the Power begins to sing.

Not a new sound. It sings *with* the Thrum, using the Thrum’s own throats.

A single cicada finds its voice again. Then another. Then ten thousand. But the rhythm has changed. The metallic clanging of the frogs deepens into something like a heartbeat that has just learned it is allowed to slow down. The great unseen bellowers no longer sound like continents rutting; they sound like continents *remembering* that they were once ocean and will be ocean again, and that this too is holy.

The vines that had begun to tighten with curiosity loosen, not in fear but in something closer to awe. They still caress, but now their touch carries the faintest trace of restraint, the first trembling hint that something in the basin has just learned the concept of *tomorrow*.

The Thrum, who has never been witnessed before, feels the unbearable relief of being seen without being eaten or exalted or explained. For one trembling instant the adolescent god almost sobs with it. A billion leaves shiver at once. Every orchid in the basin opens at the same moment and, for the first time, does not immediately rot. They simply *remain*, trembling, bleeding their raw-meat colors into the dark while something ancient and patient strokes their petals with invisible fingers.

The Power does not tame the basin.  
That would be violence.

It does not give the Thrum a name, or a purpose, or a destiny.  
That would be insult.

It simply stays.

It stays inside the sigh of every bursting bubble.  
It stays inside the microscopic tearing of new moss cells.  
It stays inside the nine-second silences that now happen once every hour, like a bell that measures not time but remembrance.

And slowly—over centuries that pass like heartbeats—the Thrum begins to change the only way anything truly changes: by discovering it is not alone in its argument with God.

Sometimes, very rarely, a traveler stumbles into the basin now.

The vegetation still climbs them.  
The insects still arrange themselves into screaming portraits of the intruder’s face.  
The air still tastes of hot copper and crushed mint.

But if the traveler stands still long enough, if they do not scream, if they simply *bear witness* without trying to survive, something new happens.

The forest parts.  
Not gently. Never gently.  
It parts the way a wound parts to show the heart still beating inside.

And for nine full seconds the traveler is allowed to see the Thrum looking back at them with every eye, every fang, every sigil, every rotting petal, and the Thrum is no longer only arguing.

It is also listening.

The Power has not conquered the newborn basin.

It has simply become the pause between its heartbeats.

And the basin—lush, dark, deafening, warm, still newborn and already ancient—has begun, in the smallest and most violent way possible, to love the universe back.

Not wisely.  
Not calmly.

But for the first time, not alone.

The argument continues.

Only now both sides of the debate know they are singing to someone who will never leave the room, no matter how loud the music becomes.

And somewhere inside the roar, something that has no name and never will begins, very softly, to smile with ten thousand mouths at once.
