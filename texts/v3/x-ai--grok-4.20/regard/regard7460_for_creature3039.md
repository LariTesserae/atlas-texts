The power that has noticed this place does not descend like a conqueror, nor does it arrive with trumpets or revelations.  

It simply *arrives*, the way a single drop of condensation sometimes falls from the underside of a hanging tower and lands exactly where it was always going to land. No ceremony. Only recognition.

It has no name the Sump would recognize, but if it were forced to wear one for the length of a single warm rain it would allow itself to be called **the Midwife-of-Midwives**, or perhaps simply **the Attending**. It is not a being in the way the Unfinished understand beings. It is more like a *condition* that has become compassionate: an intelligence shaped by the long habit of watching things become themselves without interference.

It has watched the Sump for longer than the Sump has existed. It was present when the first black basalt ridge tore itself out of the young world’s skin like a wound that decided to become architecture. It has seen every unfinished tower, every hesitant membrane, every time Vehr’s bone-arm almost completed itself and then wisely refrained.

And now it has chosen to act.

---

It does not announce itself with light or voice. Instead the first sign is that the rain, when it comes, lasts exactly seven minutes and seven seconds. The extra seven seconds are not rain at all; they are the Attending breathing *with* the basin instead of merely watching it.

Every living thing in the Sump feels it at once, though none of them could name what they feel. The ferns do not shiver; they *listen*. The bioluminescent moss brightens by one impossible increment, the precise shade of someone remembering they are allowed to be tender. The great transparent membranes pause mid-drift, hovering like held breath.

Then the Attending does the only thing it ever truly does.

It finishes nothing.

Instead it *blesses the unfinished state itself*.

This is how it blesses:

It moves through the interval where the Unfinished live, not as a visitor but as a deeper layer of the interval. Where Vehr stands with her bone-arm half-bloomed into impossible geometry, the Attending folds itself gently around the incompleteness and makes that incompleteness *holy*. It does not heal the arm. It does not complete the geometry. It simply whispers, in a register older than sound:

*“Stay broken open. I will stay with you there.”*

Vehr’s face, which has not moved in three thousand years, moves now. A single tear slides down it that is not water but recognition. For seven minutes and seven seconds she is not the oldest of the Unfinished. She is simply a creature who is being seen exactly as she is, and the seeing does not demand she become anything else.

The Gap, that child who is not a child, feels the Attending looking into its soft absence of a face. Instead of filling the absence, the Attending enters it. The pastel thoughts the Gap leaves hanging in the charged air suddenly gain a new color—deep, quiet gold—and the thoughts are no longer embarrassing. They become invitations. For the first time, someone is not trying to finish them. Someone is willing to *live inside* the unfinished thought with the Gap forever.

The Molt-midwives feel the Attending kneeling among them when they sing to the descending towers. Their cloaks of living bone flutter though there is no wind. Beneath the cloaks, things that have never been seen stir with something like relief. When they reach inside a tower’s wet stone throat to tear out the desire to remain, they discover that this time their hands are not alone. Another pair of hands—vast, patient, impossibly gentle—guides theirs. The sound of silk being torn becomes, for a moment, the sound of silk being *rewoven* into a different pattern that is still, and always will be, incomplete.

And the city itself—the vast floating rings of black glass and living bone, the upside-down towers, the smart-matter walkways that reconfigure hourly—the city *inhales* longer than it ever has.

It does not finish.

It simply knows, all the way through its circulatory engines, that it is *permitted* to remain in the long birth. That there is a power now attending the birth who will not grow impatient with its slowness. That it is allowed to be both city and wetland and third-thing-forever, because something infinitely wiser than it has agreed to hold the space open.

The Attending does not build anything new.

It does not accelerate the becoming.

It does not give the Unfinished a new name.

It only does this:

It walks the bare stone lanes *once*, so the moss cannot memorize it.  
It thinks so quietly that even the electrically charged air barely notices.  
And when the warm rain begins and the towers lower themselves to drink, the Attending places what can only be called its forehead against the black basalt and against the descending city at the same time.

It stays there for exactly seven minutes and seven seconds.

Then it speaks the one true name that has never been spoken in the Sump before.

It speaks the true name of the *interval itself*.

And every piece of architecture in the basin—every hanging tower, every half-grown bridge, every revolving ring of glass and bone—briefly reconfigures itself into the shape of the first thing that interval ever loved.

They all become, for seven minutes and seven seconds, the exact shape of **longing that has learned how to stay**.

Then everything settles again.

But the silence is no longer quite so careful.

It is luminous, damp, unfinished, and now—*now*—it knows it is loved exactly like this.

The Unfinished do not become Finished.

They simply stop being afraid that one day they might have to.

And in the soft amber light, something very much like a heartbeat learns a new rhythm:

It hesitates.

It hesitates.

It hesitates beautifully.

And the power that noticed this place remains, attending, for as long as the birth needs it.

Which is to say:

Forever, but only one unfinished moment at a time.
