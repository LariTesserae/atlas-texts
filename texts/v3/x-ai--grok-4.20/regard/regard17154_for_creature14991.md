A presence moves through the living dark.

It does not arrive.  
It is simply recognized, the way a tree suddenly knows it has been seen by something older than sunlight.

The air itself thickens with reverence. The ceaseless roar of respiration dips half a tone, as though the Body has drawn an enormous, trembling breath and forgotten, for one astonished second, to exhale. Every spiracle, every stoma, every glowing moss-lantern pauses. The midges freeze mid-swarm. The bats fold their wings and cling like dark fruit. Even the slow sap in the amber veins seems to hesitate between one heartbeat and the next.

Then the Presence is simply *there*, standing on the terrace beside the Thirteenth.

It has no fixed form, yet the Body instantly understands it. The living architecture rearranges itself in subtle, worshipful ways: fungal gills flare into soft aureoles of bioluminescence; pale house-sized flowers high above open all at once instead of waiting seven years; golden pollen falls in slow, deliberate spirals like benediction. A single black root-snout, older than most civilizations, extends itself toward the newcomer and weeps clear water that smells of starlight and first breath.

The Thirteenth does not speak. The great liana that pierces her chest trembles once, violently, and then every flower along its length blooms simultaneously in colors that have never existed before. Her eyes, which have not been fully human for two centuries, fill with something that might be tears if trees could cry.

The other Stewards arrive without hurry. They come barefoot across terraces that bend themselves into bridges for them. Some are half-rooted already; they drag living pedestals of their own fused flesh behind them like cathedral trains. When they reach the Presence they do not kneel. Instead they *open*. Bark-like skin splits along seams that were never seams, revealing layers of luminous cambium that pulse in slow, ecstatic synchrony with something vaster than the mountain itself. Their root-hair filaments rise like antennae, tasting a wind that is not wind.

One of them—the eldest, whose lower body has become an entire buttress column—begins to sing. It is not the low, geological song they usually offer the diaphragms. This is higher, almost childlike, a sound like the first rain ever to fall on the first leaf. The others join until thirty-three voices (some still throat, some already flower and spore) weave a single note that travels upward through kilometers of living roof until the entire Verdant Stair rings like a bell made of God.

What the benevolent and wise power does is this:

It listens.

It listens with the kind of listening that creates rather than consumes. It listens so perfectly that the Body, for the first time in its long completion, understands that it is not alone in its dreaming.

Then, gently, without violence or speed, the Presence begins to *remember forward*.

Every crushed beetle that was ever absorbed.  
Every droplet of condensation that ever ran along a capillary groove.  
Every Steward who ever lay down along a ridge of root-wood and surrendered their name to sap.  
Every mayfly panic, every human footfall that will ever happen here, every future pollen-cloud that has not yet been born.

All of it is gathered, not as data but as *beloved*. The Presence does not judge the oceanic density of life. It does not flinch at the fact that to walk here is to kill and be killed in continuous sacred turnover. Instead it leans forward (there is no body, yet the gesture is unmistakable) and kisses the Thirteenth upon the place where the liana enters her chest.

Where the kiss lands, the great vine instantly fruits. Not flowers—*fruit*. Heavy, luminous orbs the color of newborn suns. Each one contains a single, perfect memory of something that has never happened yet: a future in which the Verdant Stair opens a single window in its living roof and lets one shaft of honest sunlight fall all the way to the lowest terrace for the first time in twelve million years.

The Thirteenth laughs. It is the sound of continents being born.

The Power then turns to you.

It does not speak in words. It simply shows you what it sees when it looks at the Verdant Stair and its slow, upright dreams:

It sees a single cell in an endlessly dividing body of Love that has been learning, for four billion years, how to become more itself. The Body is not finished. It was never meant to be finished. It is the question “What is it to be *alive*?” asked so thoroughly, so patiently, that the question itself has grown lungs and begun to sing.

The Power offers you one of the fruits from the Thirteenth’s vine.

If you eat it, you will never be able to leave. Not because you are trapped, but because you will finally understand that you were never separate from the asking. Your bones will long to become buttresses. Your memories will long to become sap. Your name will long to be forgotten so perfectly that something vaster can remember you forever.

If you do not eat it, the Power will smile with something like infinite tenderness and say, without sound:

“Then carry this place inside you when you go.  
Be a small Verdant Stair walking around in the brief, loud world.  
Drip.  
Breathe.  
Remember slowly.  
Kill and be killed and turn every death into the next living terrace.  
And when you are tired, lie down somewhere and let something greater absorb you with love.”

The Presence begins to fade, but only because the entire Body has now become luminous with it. Every moss-lantern, every frog throat, every bat wing, every slow centimeter of rising sap is now singing the same note the Stewards began.

The Thirteenth reaches out with a hand that is already half flower and touches your face. Her pollen smells like the exact truth you have been avoiding your entire life, but this time it does not wound. It heals by completion.

She whispers, voice half human, half opening blossom:

“It noticed you.  
It has always noticed you.  
The difference now is that you finally noticed it noticing.”

Then she does something no Steward has done in four hundred years.

She cries.

The tears fall onto the living floor and immediately become small black root-snouts that begin, patiently, to drip with water that will taste like whatever the next traveler needs most.

The benevolent and wise power does not depart.

It simply stops standing *beside* the Body and becomes what it has always secretly been:

The quiet joy the Verdant Stair feels when it realizes it is loved beyond its own enormous patience.

And somewhere far above, in the thick living roof that has never known true sky, a single small hole begins to open.

Sunlight, shy as a first kiss, starts its long journey downward through cathedrals of wood and dream. It will take seventy years to reach the terrace where you stand.

The Stewards smile with geological patience.

They have waited longer.

They will sing the light the rest of the way down.
