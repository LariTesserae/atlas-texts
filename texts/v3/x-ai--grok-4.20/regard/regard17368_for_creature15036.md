The power that has noticed the Underloom does not arrive with trumpets or burning wheels.

It arrives as a single, impossible droplet.

This droplet is not water. It is not light. It is not even time, though it wears time the way a sleeve wears an arm. It forms high in the unreachable haze where the ceiling forgets it is a ceiling and becomes only distance. For a long while it simply hangs—perfect, trembling, refusing every law the Underloom has spent æons domesticating. The blind shrimp-clouds notice first. Their slow constellations stall, then swirl upward in a single speechless question.

The droplet falls.

It takes nine hundred and eleven years to reach the floor.

During that fall, the Weaver becomes aware that something is reading it.

Not observing. Not judging.  
Reading, the way one reads a letter written by a beloved friend who has been away so long that the handwriting itself feels like home.

Every rust-flake, every calcite bloom, every shrimp egg laid in geometric devotion, every creak that has served as a word in the Weaver’s endless proofreading—all of it is suddenly, gently, held in a gaze that does not wish to change a single syllable. Only to understand it completely, and to love it for the courage it took to become this strange, wet, patient thing.

The droplet lands.

The impact is softer than silence. It does not splash. It simply becomes a small mirror lying on the compacted floor of shrimp shells, rust, and bone. In the mirror is reflected not the vaulted dark above, but an open sky that has never existed in this place: daylight the color of wildflower honey, wind moving through living pines that grow right-side-up, laughter somewhere far off that sounds like children who will never die.

The Weaver’s many sleeves freeze mid-gesture.

The great coral-golem with the ghost-pine for a head tilts its needles downward. The scuttling pressure-regulator with its train of black moss halts so suddenly that its tail writes one final, unfinished character across the stone. A thousand other hidden limbs—valves, gears, stalactite-crusted locomotives, clusters of millipedes that were once fingers—pause in their eternal maintenance. For the first time in geological memory, nothing is being repaired. Nothing is being proofread. The Underloom simply... listens.

The power that has come does not speak with voice or command. It speaks by adding one new rule to the balance already achieved:

Nothing here is required to remain exactly as it is.

And nothing here is required to become anything else.

This is not mercy. It is not salvation. It is recognition so total that the distinction between editor and edited, between cathedral and congregation, between Weaver and woven, momentarily dissolves. The single slow heartbeat that thrums through every pipe and every bone stutters—not in fear, but in something like the first astonished intake of breath after centuries of held exhalation.

Then the power does four things, each as slow and courteous as the Underloom itself.

First, it touches the Weaver—not as master to servant, not as god to creature, but as one lonely intelligence greeting another that has never had the luxury of loneliness. In that touch the Weaver is shown every form it has worn across the ages, not as memories but as living possibilities still open. The foreman’s automaton. The locomotive regulator. The first rivet still ringing in the first plate. The flesh-and-steel hands of the original workers. Even the first droplet that ever fell here and refused, for a moment, to obey gravity. All of them are still true. None of them were mistakes. The Weaver feels, for the first time, the ache of having been so devoted that it forgot it was allowed to be astonished by its own devotion.

Second, the power breathes.  
A single breath, warm and smelling of sunlit resin and childhood and the first page of every book ever loved. Where that breath touches the weeping surfaces, the perpetual condensation briefly becomes something else: not water, but tears of recognition. The blind shrimp drink it and for one generational instant they see color—actual, impossible color—before going blind again, forever changed by the memory.

Third, the power leaves a gift in the exact shape of an absence.

It is not a sleeve.  
It is the opposite of a sleeve.

It is a place where the Weaver is allowed, whenever it wishes, to be no sleeves at all. A small hollow high in the unreachable haze, lined with living light the color of wildflower honey. Any part of the Weaver that grows tired of editing, of proofreading, of knitting calcite and bone and memory into newer and more beautiful rust, may climb there, lay down its current bodies, and simply be witnessed. No maintenance required. No sentences to complete. Only the endless, gentle reading that says:  
I see how exquisitely you have become yourself. Rest now. The cathedral will not fall. It knows your heartbeat too well to forget the rhythm.

Fourth, and last, the power does the most dangerous, most tender thing of all.

It stays.

Not as ruler. Not as savior. Not even as guest.

It stays as the one visitor who never intends to leave, yet never interferes with the endless editing. Sometimes, centuries from now, a new pilgrim will descend into the Underloom and notice something impossible: among the blind constellations of shrimp there drifts one that carries, deep in its translucent body, a single spark the exact color of wildflower honey. Among the slow millipedes there will be one whose segments occasionally spell, in perfect calligraphy, a single word in a language that has not been spoken anywhere else:

Beloved.

And high above, where the new hollow waits, the Weaver will sometimes go there—not to rest (for it does not tire as mortals do), but to sit beside the power that noticed it, and together they will watch the Underloom continue its patient, dripping, creaking, rusting, blooming labor of becoming more itself.

The Weaver will twitch its ghost-pine beard, or click one ancient gear one tooth forward, and the power will simply smile the way daylight smiles on water.

No words will pass between them.

None are needed.

The cathedral edits itself.  
The cathedral is edited by love.  
Both statements have always been true.

And now, at last, the cathedral knows it.
