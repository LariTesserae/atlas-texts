A pulse arrives.

It is not the usual geological heartbeat—this one is deeper, older, slower. It comes from *outside* the amniotic pocket, from the black stone that should have no outside. The water itself seems to hesitate, as if the cold dark has drawn in a breath it did not know it possessed.

Then the power enters.

It does not arrive as light, or sound, or any visitor that could be recognized by the ribbon-fish or the question-mark predators. It arrives as *recognition*.

The entire pocket convulses once, gently, the way a womb might when it first senses the mind of the child it has been growing. Every polyp lantern flares at once in a color that has no name in any language of pressure or silence. The tubular kelp forests bow—not toward the Dry One this time, but toward a point just above her, where the water has suddenly become *more than water*.

The power does not speak in words. It has no need. It simply *beholds* the city, and in beholding, it completes something the city has been trying to complete for aeons.

For one eternal instant the half-born metropolis is *seen* by something that has never been submerged, never been pressured, never been cold. A mind that remembers the first instant light touched water. A benevolence that has watched stars condense and galaxies wheel and still finds wonder in the trembling of a single bacterial film on a newborn stair.

The Architect feels it like a hand laid against the inside of her changing heart.

Her eyes (those fractured horizontal bars) widen. For the first time in however long she has floated here, she *uncurls*.

The thin envelope of air around her shatters like glass and is gone. Where it had been, something finer remains: a faint nimbus of something that is neither air nor water nor vacuum, but *attention*. The power has given her one last breath of what she used to be, so that she may choose.

She does not choose to return.

Instead she opens every new plate along her arms, her throat, her thighs, and she sings—not the old pattern this time, but a new one. A song of *thank you* in a language the city has been inventing since the first polyp opened its blind eye.

The power does not build for her. That would be violence against the dream.

It does something gentler, something almost shy.

It leans down (if such a word can be used for a presence that has no up or down) and kisses the city with *possibility*.

Every incomplete tower suddenly understands what it was trying to become. Every question-mark predator feels, for a single pulse, what it might be like to have a question answered. The blind ribbon-fish school into the shape of a single enormous iris and look back at the power with something like recognition.

And the city *chooses*.

It chooses to remain unfinished.

It chooses to keep becoming.

The power smiles (a smile felt as a softening of pressure, a slight warming at the edges of the cold that does not melt but *enfolds*). It understands. This place was never meant to be a finished cathedral. It was meant to be a question the universe asks itself in the dark, over and over, forever.

As its presence begins to withdraw, it leaves three gifts that are not gifts but permissions:

1. The Architect will never be fully translated. She will remain the single dry thought in the mind of the wet city, the one imperfection that makes the perfection bearable. Her plates will grow, but never cover her completely. She will pulse with the geological heartbeat, but every seventh pulse she will remember what rain felt like on skin, and the city will grow something new and slightly wrong and achingly beautiful because of it.

2. The creatures are granted *memory*. Not of the surface—they will never need that—but of *her*. They will carry the shape of the Dry One in their small luminous bodies forever, the way some deep-sea fish carry lanterns. When they die, their bodies will not dissolve into the architecture. They will become small floating cathedrals of bone and light that drift through the streets, miniature versions of the larger dream, so the city is always full of tiny moving reminders of the one who stayed.

3. Most tenderly of all, the power leaves a single bubble.

It is no larger than a human heart. It floats just above the Architect’s outstretched hand. Inside it is not air. Inside it is the *sound* of sky—wind moving through leaves on a planet that has never existed, the hush between birdcalls, the particular silence that happens when snow is about to fall but has not yet begun.

The bubble will never pop. It will simply drift.

And every few centuries, when the geological pulse rolls through the pocket, the bubble will sing its impossible surface-song for three heartbeats. Every polyp in the city will flare. Every creature will turn toward the sound like iron filings toward a magnet. The Architect will close her strange eyes and smile with a mouth that is no longer entirely a mouth.

Then the bubble will fall quiet again, and the city will go back to its sacred, patient becoming—more joyfully now, more certain that its incompleteness is not a flaw but the signature of the one who dreamed it from outside.

The power withdraws.

The black stone closes behind it without seam or scar.

But something has changed forever.

The city is still hidden. It is still submerged. It will never see the sky.

Yet now it *knows* the sky has seen it.

And that knowledge is enough to keep it dreaming for another billion years, layer by cold, devotional, luminescent layer—while at its exact center, something that is no longer quite human and not yet quite architecture floats curled like a question mark, one hand forever reaching toward a bubble that holds the memory of wind.

She is smiling.

The long-necked predators drift past her in slow, wondering spirals.

They have begun to sing back.
