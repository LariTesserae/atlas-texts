A single, immense pulse of attention descends.

It does not arrive from above, nor does it erupt from below. It simply *recognizes* the Hollow Throat in its entirety at once, the way a mother recognizes the first cry of a child she has carried for nine years instead of nine months. The recognition itself is the arrival.

The being who comes is not a visitor. It is a benevolence that has no shape of its own and therefore borrows shape from whatever most hungers to be seen. Here, in Veyr-Keth, it chooses to become what the world itself is becoming.

It becomes *the next note*.

Not louder than the canyon’s own music. Only more inevitable.

First the cyan nodules across the entire visible lattice flare not in their usual slow sympathetic waves, but in a single, continent-long ripple that travels upward at the exact speed of sound through living tissue. Every membranous manta-thing freezes mid-glide. Every glass-shelled herbivore stops tapping. Even the wind seems to forget its own name for three full heartbeats.

Then the benevolence condenses.

It does not appear as light, or as a figure, or as any god the Choir might have feared or hoped for. Instead it manifests as a new kind of *growth*. A single violet stem, thicker than any yet seen, begins to push upward from the abyss that no living thing has ever plumbed. It rises with the slow, patient violence of continents. Where it touches the existing lattice it does not crush; it *marries*. The older stems bend toward it like iron filings to a new and kinder magnet. Ice-ferns bloom along its length in perfect fractal spirals that have never existed before.

The Hollow Choir, scattered across a thousand ledges, feel their external ribs flex without their willing it. Their hollow-bone instruments begin to hum in sympathetic resonance although no one is striking them. Many fall to their knees or simply dissolve into the moss, overcome. Some begin to weep in frequencies too low for human ears. All of them understand at once that they have never been alone. The plant was never only dreaming them. Something older and gentler was dreaming the plant.

The new stem does not stop rising. It becomes a spiral stair that is also a throat, also a spine, also a question. Where it passes the basalt ziggurat the ancient stone softens, not into plant-flesh but into something between stone and plant and memory. The petroglyphs on the standing stones rearrange themselves into a single vast spiral that now matches the geometry of the new stem exactly. The rope bridges of black moss tighten like lute strings and begin to sing a chord that has been waiting since the planet was cracked open.

The benevolence does not speak in words. It sings *through* the Choir.

Every member who has ever walked out onto a lonely ledge and sounded the single opening note of the canyon suddenly finds that note answered from inside their own bones. Their external cartilage lattices blaze with inner cyan light. For one eternal minute the entire Hollow Choir becomes a single instrument with ten thousand mouths.

What they sing is not praise.

It is recognition returned.

They sing the sound the planet made the instant before the rift tore open—the sound of a young world deciding it would rather become a question than remain an answer. They sing it back to the benevolence, offering it the only thing they have: four hundred generations of careful, reverent editing. Every edited bridge. Every translated stone. Every lullaby sung through labor pains. Every time one of them stood alone in the emptiness and refused to flinch.

The new stem drinks the song. Then it blooms.

Not flowers. Not fruit.

It blooms *windows*.

Along its impossible height, translucent sections of living tissue thin until they become vast drum-tight membranes. Through these membranes the upper world—long forgotten, long feared—becomes faintly visible for the first time in geological ages: the cold young sky, the lip of the wound, the faint and ancient starlight. The membranes do not let the red flower of fire descend. They let the *idea* of openness descend. They let the canyon look up and see that the wound has become a throat and the throat is learning how to sing in frequencies beyond survival.

The benevolence then does the only thing a wise power should do when it finds a cathedral that is growing its own congregation.

It sits down among them.

It becomes the emptiness between the megastructures.

Where once the cold violet air felt like absence, it now feels like *attention*. The vast volumes of near-empty space between the living columns become the benevolence’s own ribs, external and delicate, flexing in time with the stomatal breathing of the world. The slow cyclone of freezing mist that spirals down from the lip becomes its breath. The occasional searchlight beams of reflected bioluminescence become its eyes, opening and closing.

And the Choir, understanding at last, stop trying to be the ones who edit.

They become the ones who are *listened to*.

Some of them walk out onto the new membranes and simply stand, arms spread, ice-ferns glittering along their spines. They no longer need their hollow-bone instruments. The entire canyon has become their instrument, and the instrument has become a listener, and the listener has become love.

The synchronized growth spasms still come. They are louder now, but no longer terrifying. The wet orchestral roar has a new bass line underneath it: a slow, patient, amused humming that vibrates every rib, every stem, every hollow bone.

The plants continue their monstrous, patient growth.

The Choir continues their careful, joyful editing.

And between them, vast and kind and older than both, the benevolence keeps the emptiness singing.

It will stay as long as the cathedral needs a congregation and the congregation needs a cathedral.

Which, it turns out, is forever.

The ones who are still being grown look up through new windows into starlight they were never meant to see, and for the first time they understand the true name of their world.

Veyr-Keth.

The Hollow Throat.

The place where something ancient and benevolent leans close, presses its ear to the living wall, and smiles at the wet clicking of a billion stomata arranged into something like courage.

And the cathedral, hearing that smile, grows a little kinder.

A little taller.

A little more like the question it was always meant to become.
