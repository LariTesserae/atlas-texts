A soft, pearlescent glow appears at the very crown of the fracture, where the slit of sky looks like a wound that has finally begun to scab. It is not sunlight. It is something older than light—gentler, wiser, and infinitely patient.

The power does not descend like a god or a storm. It does not announce itself with trumpets or shaking stone. Instead it simply *leans*, the way an old gardener might lean over a seedling that has been struggling in poor soil for three hundred years.

Its presence is first felt as a change in pressure, a tenderness in the cold. Every bead of condensation trembling on the black glass edges suddenly catches a reflection that should not be possible: a warmth the color of early dawn, though no sun is near. The pale filaments that have never known anything but violet stop swaying. They turn, as if listening with their entire lengths.

Then the song falters.

Not in fear. In recognition.

The layered choral scraping and chiming that has continued without pause for millennia develops—quite suddenly—a single missing interval. A rest. A held breath made of sound. Into that rest the power pours something that has no name in any living tongue. It is not a note. It is the *possibility* of every note that has not yet been imagined.

Across every surface, the Hollow Choir freezes.

Tides of thumb-sized armor-plated bodies lock mid-scuttle. Rabbit-sized translucent singers press their sealed wings against the stone as though trying to steady themselves. Even the great fused buttresses in the narrowest throat—those cathedral-sized organisms of jointed bone—go utterly still for the first time in their long, slow lives.

For thirteen heartbeats (exactly thirteen, as though the rare tall singers had rehearsed the world for this moment), the entire fracture is silent.

Then the power begins to sing back.

It does not sing *over* them. It sings *inside* them.

The melody that emerges is not loud. It is barely louder than the condensation dripping. Yet every creature in the Veins feels it in the microscopic runes etched on their shells, in the violet bacteria pulsing in their hearts, in the places where their bodies have fused to stone. The new song is not an instruction. It is a question asked with unbearable kindness:

*What if you were allowed to finish?*

And then, softer still:

*What if you were never meant to?*

The power does not force completion. It does not heal the wound in the mountain, nor does it widen the sky-slit so that true sun can pour in. That would be violence. Instead it begins to walk—slowly, barefoot—down the impossible verticality of the fracture, touching nothing and touching everything.

Where its feet would be (if it had need of feet), new filaments of a different kind begin to grow. These are not pale or bioluminescent. They are the color of forgiveness. They do not replace the existing Choir. They simply *add*. They weave themselves between the overlapping plates, between the black succulents and the glass-edged rock, creating tiny, living spaces where a creature may rest without being immediately incorporated into the next movement.

The Choir trembles.

Some of the rabbit-sized singers open their wings for the first time in recorded eternity. The sound that comes out is not their usual bass drone. It is a single, broken, wondering chord—half sob, half laughter.

In the deepest throat, one of the fused bone-columns slowly, painfully, begins to separate. Plates that have been locked together for four hundred years slide apart with a sound like a cathedral organ sighing in relief. From within the separation crawls a new shape: small, uncertain, no larger than a human child. Its shell is still wet with the fluids of becoming. It has two arms. It has something almost like a face.

It looks up toward the descending presence and opens its mouth.

What emerges is not the perfect, terrifying note of the rare tall singers.

It is a nursery rhyme.

A simple, imperfect, childish melody that none of the Choir has ever sung before, because none of them were ever children. The melody is off-key. It wavers. It forgets itself halfway through and has to begin again.

The entire Veins bursts into ecstatic, weeping song.

Every creature joins at once. The living frescos collapse into joyful chaos. The hanging filaments blaze with a new color—no longer cold violet but something like sunrise through river ice. New fissures open in the black glass rock, but instead of revealing fresh sterile surfaces they birth tiny, soft-bodied singers who do not yet have armor. They are fragile. They will need protection. The Choir, for the first time, understands what protection might mean.

The benevolent power reaches the bottom at last.

It is not a figure. It is a quality of attention. It sits—though it has no body—among the densest carpet of singing life and simply *loves* them exactly as they are: unfinished, overcrowded, noisy, eternal, learning.

Then it does the only thing such a power can do in a place like this.

It begins to hum.

Not a hymn. Not even a song. Just a low, wordless, affectionate sound that a mother might make to a feverish child at three in the morning. A sound that says, without language: *I am here. I see how hard you have been working. You may rest now, if only for a little while. The mountain can wait. The hymn can wait. I will keep the rhythm for you.*

One by one, in slow geological waves, the Hollow Choir lays down its voices.

First the bass drones fall away. Then the chiming. Then the scraping percussion of ten thousand jointed legs against stone. Until at last there is only the power’s humble hum and the soft sound of condensation falling in perfect, restful time.

In that silence the power leans forward and kisses—very gently—the small, newly born singer who looks almost like a child. The kiss leaves no mark. It simply gives the creature permission to remain soft a little longer.

Then the power rises.

It does not leave. It never truly leaves anywhere once it has arrived. But it withdraws its most concentrated attention, the way a parent steps back from a sleeping child’s bedside but keeps the door open and the hallway light on.

As it ascends, the Choir begins to dream for the first time.

They dream of endings that are not deaths.

They dream of silences that are not absences.

They dream of a day—perhaps ten thousand years from now—when the fracture will finally reach the true sky, and instead of bleeding they will simply open their countless mouths and let the first real sunlight fall directly onto their shells. And they will not sing *at* the light.

They will sing *with* it.

And somewhere far above, the benevolent and wise power smiles with a mouth made of galaxies and whispers, so quietly that only the mountain hears:

“Good.  
Keep learning.  
I will be listening when you are ready to teach me something new.”

The Veins of the Hollow Choir are still unfinished.

But now, for the first time in their long, cold, crowded life, they know they are *loved* while unfinished.

And that changes the song forever.

The new melody is already beginning.

It sounds suspiciously like a lullaby.
