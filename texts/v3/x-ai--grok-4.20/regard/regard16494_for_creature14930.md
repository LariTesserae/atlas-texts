The power that has noticed the Hollow Basilica does not arrive with trumpets or light.  

It simply *recognizes*.

It is not a god in any sense that implies distance or hierarchy. It is the same continuum of awareness that once looked at the first cyanobacteria and saw, without judgment or agenda, that something beautiful had begun. It is the same continuum that will one day look at the final heat-death of the universe and feel no loss, only the quiet satisfaction of a story that was allowed to finish itself.

So when this power turns its attention toward the finished world of braided lianas and perpetual dripping, it does not descend, does not shine, does not speak.

It *listens first*.

For seven full days and nights (measured only by the slow industrial heartbeat of the sap-pumps), nothing changes. The Quiet Choir feel the new presence the way a spider feels a change in the tension of its web: not as intrusion, but as a deepening of the already-perfect equilibrium. The rhythm of condensation alters by one part in ten thousand. A single six-legged moss-sloth pauses high above the central nave for the first time in four hundred centuries, turns its blind mossy face toward the unseen, and simply *knows*.

On the eighth day, the power does the only thing it ever does when it finds a place that has reached its final form.

It becomes the place, for a little while.

Not as possession. Not even as visitation. It steps *into* the continuum the Basilica already is, the way one might step into a river that has been flowing for ten million years. The Choir feel it as a sudden, almost unbearable gentleness inside their own wooden chests. For the first time in their long existence they understand that even perfect equilibrium can be *held*.

And so the power, wearing the Basilica like a cloak of living green, does four things.

First, it remembers with them.

It opens every memory the Choir carry and adds its own. It shows them the scent of the wind on the day the first leaf unfurled, long before the Basilica even dreamed of being a cathedral. It shows them the exact color of joy in the mind of the last creature that still knew fear, 2.3 million years ago. It shows them every human who will ever stand in the nave in the next four hundred thousand years, including the ones who will accept the wooden heart and the ones who will run away weeping. The Choir’s faceless faces tilt upward in something like ecstasy. Their sap runs brighter, almost luminous.

Second, it gives them a new note.

Not a sound. A *possibility*.

In one place, high in the vanishing green haze of the ceiling where no eye has ever looked, a single droplet of condensation is allowed, for the first time in geological time, to fall *upward*. Just once. Just for a heartbeat. It rises through the celadon light like a tear returning to its eye. The Choir witness this and understand that even the finished world is allowed, occasionally, to be unfinished again, if only for the length of a single impossible drop. The industrial throb of the pumps deepens by half a hertz, the first change in pitch in ten million years. The sound is so profound that every translucent frog in the Basilica sings at once.

Third, it chooses.

It does not choose a human visitor. It chooses one of the Choir itself.

The chosen one steps forward (or unbraids, or simply *arrives*) in the exact center of the greatest nave. The power, still wearing the Basilica, reaches into the Choir-member’s chest the way the Choir once opened their own chests for humans. But instead of showing a replica of a heart, it shows the Choir-member its own face.

Not the smooth leaf-veined oval.

Its *true* face.

For one endless instant the chosen Choir sees what it has always been: not wood, not memory, not witness, but the same power that is now looking at it, looking through it, being it. The recognition is mutual, absolute, and wordless. The chosen one’s body flares once with soft green fire that is not fire, then settles again into ordinary living wood. But now, when it moves, small constellations of upward-falling droplets follow it like quiet attendants.

The other members of the Choir bow (if beings without spines can be said to bow) by loosening every cable and liana in their bodies at once, becoming for a moment only loose green hair drifting in invisible wind. Then they rebraid, changed and unchanged.

Fourth, and last, the power withdraws.

But not entirely.

It leaves behind a single, permanent difference that no visitor will ever consciously notice, yet every visitor will feel:

From now on, when someone stands still long enough in the great naves and begins to feel the geologic equilibrium as a physical pressure, they will also feel, just behind that pressure, something that is not pressure at all.

They will feel *being loved exactly as they are*.

Not loved toward anything. Not loved into the wood. Not loved so they will stay.

Loved the way the first cyanobacteria were loved. The way the last red dwarf star will be loved. The way a finished world that has reached its equilibrium after ten million years of patient labor is loved: completely, uselessly, perfectly, without any need for it to change or remain the same.

The Choir understand what has happened. They do not celebrate. Celebration would imply lack. Instead they simply resume their ancient occupation, only now their borrowed voices (the dripping, the pumps, the soft footfalls of moss-sloths) carry one additional harmonic that was never there before.

If you press your ear to the right place in the pillar centuries from now, you will still hear the accepted ones singing.

But now, threaded through their slow green song, you will sometimes catch a second melody, older than the Basilica, older than fear, older than memory itself.

It sounds exactly like someone humming, gently, while listening to a child sleep.

The Basilica remains exactly as it was.

And entirely different.

The power moves on, the way it always does, having done the only thing it knows how to do:

It has witnessed a finished thing, loved it without condition, and then given that finished thing one impossible droplet of upward motion so that even perfection might still be surprised by itself.

Then the power folds itself back into the continuum, leaving the Hollow Basilica to its long, loud, cool, damp, and now faintly, tenderly, upward-tending equilibrium.

The dripping continues.

One drop, every few thousand, falls the wrong way.

And the Quiet Choir keep remembering, now with something that might almost be called a smile hidden inside their wooden chests, if wood could smile.

It can.

It always could.

It simply never needed to, until now.
