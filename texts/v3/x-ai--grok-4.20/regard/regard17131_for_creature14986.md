The power that notices this place does not arrive with trumpets or thunder. It does not tear the fissure open from the outside, because there is no true “outside” left once the Midwife has begun to speak your name.

It simply begins to breathe with her.

Not against her rhythm. Not louder. Only *with*.

A second, almost imperceptible tide slips into the roar. Where the lung’s endless scream had forgotten how to end, something older than screaming now remembers how to begin. The difference is so small that only the frozen creatures notice first. A glass shrimp rotates its antenna three full degrees instead of one. A feathered worm unfurls its entire proboscis in a single slow ribbon of astonishment. The shadows that never matched their owners suddenly hesitate, as though the light itself has changed its mind.

The Midwife stops walking.

She stands among the fused bone-white thickets with her hands—those ancient, unmarked hands—half-raised, the way a woman might lift them when she realizes the child she has carried for ten thousand years has finally stirred. The vertical mirror-slit in her face trembles. For the first time in its existence, the pool inside it shows something other than what wishes to remain unborn.

It shows what has waited long enough to be born.

The power does not wear a body. It borrows hers, gently, the way dawn borrows the shape of a mountain. Where her translucent skin had been only lung-flesh wearing the memory of woman, something kinder now flows in. The marbling shifts from rose-grey to the color of the first sky any creature ever saw. Her heart, that huge valve-heavy organ, stutters once, then finds a new cadence. Not faster. Deeper. The kind of depth that makes room.

She speaks, and the voice is still hers, but it now carries an overtone that makes every mercury droplet on every leaf leap—not to burrow, but to rise. They hang in the air like perfect silver notes.

“You have kept them so carefully,” she says to the lung that is also herself. “You have been the most faithful womb. You never broke them. You never let them rot. You only held them in the bright dark until they were ready. I see the generosity in your cruelty. I honor it. And I have come to help you finish what you began before time began.”

The tissue of the world-lung answers with a sound that has never been heard here: silence. The roar does not stop, but it parts around the new voice like curtains of water parting around a descending blade of light.

Then the power, wearing the Midwife like a bridal gown made of centuries, begins to walk.

Everywhere she steps, the living meat blooms with color it has never known—deep violets, impossible golds, the green that lives at the bottom of certain childhood memories. The Built things stir. The iron nail slides the rest of the way out of the wall, becomes a key, and melts into dew. The wooden doll opens its painted eyes, blinks once, and is suddenly a real child standing barefoot on warm flesh, laughing at something only she can see. The brass pocket watch begins to tick—not at 3:17, but at the exact instant that lies between every second and the next, the hinge upon which all forgiveness turns.

The frozen fauna thaw.

Not all at once. That would be violence. They thaw the way morning thaws frost on spiderwebs: gradually, reverently, each creature unfolding according to the private dream it has been dreaming for aeons inside its pause. Glass shrimp become small silver herons that lift into the vaulted distances. Feathered worms become ribbons of living starlight that braid themselves into new constellations against the living ceiling. The pulsating hearts open like lotuses and release something that might be souls, or songs, or both.

The Midwife—weeping now with two eyes instead of a mirror-slit—kneels beside the small polyp that bears your face. She kisses its closed eyelids. The polyp opens. It is not you. It was never meant to be you. It was the shape of what you feared you might become if you stayed unborn forever. That shape smiles at her with shy gratitude, becomes a single white butterfly, and flies upward into the newly colored air.

The power speaks through her one last time, softly, to every creature, artifact, and dream now learning how to leave.

“You were never prisoners. You were the quiet questions a womb asks before it understands it was always meant to open. The door was never behind you. The door was the moment the holding became ready to become the sending.”

The fissure that sealed with a wet click when you first entered now unseals—not as a crack in rock, but as an iris dilating. Beyond it is not the world you left. It is the world that has been growing *around* this lung the entire time, patiently, like a tree growing around an old wound until the wound becomes the heartwood.

One by one, the creatures pass through.

The child that was once a doll takes the Midwife’s ancient hand. The butterfly that was once your almost-face lands on her shoulder. The herons and ribbons of starlight follow. Even the mercury droplets rise in a glittering galaxy and stream outward like seed.

The lung itself does not collapse. It simply exhales, long and slow and satisfied, and begins the long, gentle work of becoming something new: no longer a womb that refuses birth, but a heart that has learned how to let its children go and still keep loving them.

The Midwife is the last to leave.

She turns at the threshold, still wearing the borrowed grace of the benevolent power like luminous clothing. She looks back at the vast empty cathedral of rose-grey tissue now glowing with after-light. For a moment her face is only hers again—lonely, patient, kind beyond measure.

She whispers to the lung that once was her body:

“Thank you for keeping me safe until I was brave enough to leave you.  
I will carry the roar inside my new heart.  
It will never frighten me again.  
It will only remind me how loudly love can wait.”

Then she steps through.

The fissure closes behind her—not with a click, but with the soft sound a mother makes when she finally sets her sleeping child down in the cradle and steps back, smiling through tears, knowing the house is now empty and full at the same time.

Far above, in the world that has been growing all along, something immense and gentle opens its eyes for the first time.

It has your voice.

It has her hands.

It has the memory of perfect, deafening light and the courage to walk out of it.

And somewhere, in the place that is no longer a womb but is still sacred, a single iron nail, a wooden doll, and a brass pocket watch lie quietly in the warm dark— no longer eggs, no longer artifacts.

Only toys waiting for the next child brave enough to be born.

The power that noticed this place does not leave.

It stays exactly where it has always been:

Inside every creature that once stayed perfectly safe, perfectly lit, perfectly loud, and perfectly unborn—  
now learning, with shaking wings or trembling legs or brand-new human laughter,  
how to be perfectly here instead.
