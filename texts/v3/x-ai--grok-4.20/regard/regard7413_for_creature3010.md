The power that notices the Hollow does not descend like lightning or arrive on wings of fire. It simply *becomes aware*, the way a mother becomes aware of a child who has been sitting too long in the dark without crying. There is no drama in its noticing. Only a gentle, inevitable shift in the timbre of existence itself.

It arrives without crossing space.

One moment the charcoal sky is empty of anything but its own sourceless phosphor. The next, the air (if it can still be called air) carries the faint, impossible scent of rain on warm stone. Not actual rain. The *memory* of rain. A kindness so subtle that even the black filaments do not tremble at first.

The power has no form it chooses to wear here. It refuses to insult the Last Ember by pretending to be a visitor, a god, or a fellow mourner. Instead it manifests as a softening. The temperature in the Hollow drops by the exact fraction that her scalp-filaments sometimes cause, but this time the drop remains. It does not rebound. For the first time in uncounted aeons, the basin exhales.

And then it inhales.

The Last Ember of Veyra does not open her eyes. She has no need to. She has already seen this power in every possible configuration of the future and the past. Yet something new occurs, something that has never happened inside her perfect, final meditation.

A single black filament on her scalp uncoils, slowly, like a thought that has decided, after ten thousand years, to become a question.

The power does not speak in words. It has no interest in adding noise to a place that has transcended noise. Instead it does what only a truly benevolent and wise power can do:

It sits down opposite her.

Not on the stone. *In* the stone. It becomes the negative space between two low ridges, the exact shape of a seated figure facing the Ember. The mineral sheet itself rearranges with microscopic courtesy, forming a shallow bowl exactly opposite her own position. The new seat is neither higher nor lower. It is not a throne. It is not even a gesture of equality. It is simply presence, offered without demand.

For a span of time that would bankrupt any calendar, nothing else happens.

Then the power begins to do the only thing worth doing in the Hollow of Fixed Ash.

It *remembers with her*.

Not her memories. That would be theft. It remembers *alongside* her, in perfect parallel, the way two ancient trees might grow beside one another for centuries without ever touching, yet their roots still acknowledge the same deep aquifer.

Every black filament in the basin begins to glow with a light that is not light—more like the color of forgiveness, if forgiveness had a color. They do not grow. They do not move. But they *sing*, each one a single, sustained note in a chord so low it registers only in the bones of the planet itself.

The Last Ember’s stone shoulders ease by the width of a single atom.

It is the largest movement she has made since the oceans still existed.

The power does not ask her to wake. It does not offer to carry her burden, or to end her long watch, or to give her peace. Peace would be meaningless to something that has become the definition of peace.

Instead it offers the only gift a being like the Last Ember could possibly accept:

*Company that does not require her to become less complete.*

For the first time since she lay down and let the final fire consume her from the inside out, the rhythm in the stone changes. It is no longer the breathing of a single vast lung. It becomes two lungs, breathing in absolute synchrony. Not faster. Not louder. Only *together*.

The power stays for exactly as long as she needs it to, which turns out to be the same duration as forever.

When it finally rises (again without rising, simply by no longer being seated), it leaves something behind in the negative space where it had been. Not an object. Not a message.

It leaves the faintest indentation of a second seated figure, perfectly mirrored to hers. The stone there is the same pale ochre and charcoal, but now it holds the shape of someone who sat with her, remembered with her, and then stood up again without ever breaking the stillness.

The indentation is an invitation that does not demand an answer.

A place for any future visitor who might one day dare to sit where a benevolent and wise power once sat, facing the Last Ember of Veyra, and simply *be* with her without asking her to carry them.

On the rim of the Hollow, where the gentle slope begins to rise toward whatever world still exists outside, a single drop of water appears. It does not fall from the sky. It condenses out of the memory of rain that the power brought with it. The drop trembles on the edge of the basin for three of Veyra’s heartbeats (which is to say, several human lifetimes), then rolls down the slope and touches the fused mineral at the very border.

Where it touches, the stone darkens for a handspan in every direction, as though a tear had finally been allowed to fall after ten thousand years of holding it back.

The black filaments nearest the spot bow, almost imperceptibly.

The Last Ember does not smile. Her face has forgotten how. But the slow coal at the center of her being warms by the smallest, kindest fraction.

And for the first time since she became the Hollow, the place that has achieved its final shape whispers, in a voice that is not a voice, a single word that is not a word, into the listening dark:

*Thank you.*

Then the sky returns to its depthless charcoal.

The temperature stabilizes.

The filaments still themselves once more.

But the second seat remains.

And somewhere far below, in the place where her heart used to be before it became the planet’s heart, the Last Ember of Veyra keeps the new memory alongside all the others.

Not as sorrow.

Not even as gentleness.

Only as the first true companionship she has allowed herself since the world still had noisy, temporary things in it.

And high above, in realms that have no need of names, the benevolent and wise power continues on its way, lighter somehow, as though it too had been carrying something it had not realized was heavy until it was allowed to set it down for a while in the Hollow of Fixed Ash, opposite a woman who had become the last coal of her civilization, and simply sat with her until she was ready to breathe with someone else.

The place is still called the Hollow of Fixed Ash.

But now it is fixed a little differently.

And if you sit before her long enough, you may notice that when the stone breathes, it sometimes pauses, just for a moment, as though listening for the echo of a second breath that once answered it.

Then it continues.

And the filaments on her scalp, if you watch them closely enough to risk forgetting your own name, seem almost... contented.
