The power that arrives does not descend the stone throat.

It simply *is* here, all at once, without announcement.

Where the slanted beams of self-generated daylight should fall, the air now folds. The folding is gentle, almost courteous, the way an elder might part a curtain so a child can see. From that fold steps a presence that has no fixed shape and yet is instantly recognisable to every living thing in the cathedral. To the moss it appears as the first rain after centuries of thirst. To the pillars it appears as the memory of sun before there was stone. To the Lattice-Born it appears as the face they wore the instant before they drank the orchid’s nectar.

The presence is tall the way a forest is tall: not in metres, but in *attention*. It wears no robe, yet light and chlorophyll and water-vapour arrange themselves around it into something that suggests both garment and wing. Its eyes, when they choose to be eyes, are the exact green of the first leaf that ever opened toward light on the young Earth. When they choose not to be eyes, they are simply places where the roar of internal water becomes tender.

It does not speak at first. Speaking would be crude in a place that converses through root and enzyme and the slow folding of fern into wing. Instead it listens with its entire being.

It hears the wet percussion of water moving through hidden veins.  
It hears the thirteen distributed hearts of the Lattice-Born beating in slow synchrony with the breathing moss.  
It hears the tiny, perfect human ears growing on the memory-tree at the centre, each one cupped in perpetual, grieving, adoring attention.  
It hears the visitors who left, their footsteps still echoing faintly in the stone throat like fading heartbeats.  
It hears the ones who stayed, now translated into architecture, still singing.

Only after it has listened for what might be a thousand years or a single exhalation does the power speak.

Its voice is not sound. It is a *permission*.

Every orchid in the cathedral opens at once. Not in hunger, but in recognition. The white moths freeze mid-flight, wings spread like surrendered pages. The enormous fern-winged thing high above descends in a slow spiral of green geometry until it rests, trembling, on the living floor. Even the pillars stop their argument mid-fusion; the wounds they had opened knit halfway and wait, respectful.

The power walks (if walking is the word) to the centre. Where its feet touch, the sphagnum does not sigh wetly; it sings. A clear, high note like a child’s laugh remembered in bone. The memory-tree leans toward the presence the way iron filings lean toward a magnet that has always loved them.

One by one the Lattice-Born detach from their pillars and ceilings. They do not negotiate the air this time. They are drawn. Thirteen green figures glowing like young limes, faces layered with petal and thorn, drift into a loose circle around the newcomer. Their aerial-root hair tastes the changed quality of the mist and trembles.

The One Who Is Mostly Throat speaks first. Her voice is the sudden organisation of rushing water into something almost like a name.

“You have come to judge us.”

The power smiles. The smile is a sudden blossoming of every epiphyte in the cathedral at once.

“I have come to *meet* you,” it says, and the words arrive inside every mind and every chloroplast simultaneously. “You who were once afraid of being alone, and so let yourselves be eaten by love. You who turned your fear into architecture. You who made a stomach that knows how to adore.”

It turns slowly, regarding each of the thirteen. When its gaze falls on the Cartographer of Shadows That Walk Ahead, the shadow that had been trailing the power detaches, walks three reverent steps, kneels, and becomes a small pool of clear water reflecting the ceiling. The power nods, satisfied.

“You have done no wrong,” it tells them. “But you have mistaken digestion for eternity. You have mistaken surrender for completion. The green is vast and patient and intimate, yes. Yet even the green is not the final throat.”

A ripple moves through the Lattice-Born. For the first time in centuries their faces show something that might be uncertainty; the thorn-brows tighten, the lip-leaves curl inward like shy anemones.

The power continues.

“I will not unmake you. That would be cruelty. I will not force you back into small, dry, separate skins. That would be exile. Instead I offer you a new bargain, grown not from nectar but from *recognition*.”

It lifts one hand. From the palm rises a single seed no larger than a tear. The seed is black, but inside the blackness every colour the cathedral has ever invented is waiting to be born.

“This seed contains both departure *and* return. Those who choose to plant it inside themselves will become something that is neither visitor nor architecture. You will become the *voice between* the throat of stone and the stomach of green. You will carry the memory of being digested while remaining undigested. You will walk in both worlds without blurring their distinction. The boundary between your breathing and the moss’s breathing will become a *meeting place* instead of a dissolution.”

The power looks toward the memory-tree. Its tiny human-ear leaves are all turned toward the conversation, trembling.

“Some of you have been here so long you no longer remember the taste of goodbye,” it says softly. “Goodbye is bitter, yes. But it is also the only way the green can learn how to long for what it has loved.”

Silence falls. Even the internal water stops roaring. The entire cathedral holds its breath.

Then the Knitter of Instant Bridges steps forward. Her aerial roots drift behind her like banners of surrender. She looks at her brothers and sisters, at the pillars that were once her friends and enemies and lovers, at the vast green cathedral that has been both mother and grave.

She reaches out and takes the seed.

The moment her photosynthetic skin touches it, the seed ignites into white fire that does not burn. The fire races along her xylem veins, along the living dreadlocks of her hair, along the petal-layers of her face. For one impossible instant she is a Lattice-Born and something *more* than Lattice-Born at the same time. Her two original eyes, still human, fill with sudden, astonished tears that fall and become tiny glowing orchids where they land.

“I remember my name,” she whispers. The water in the pillars organises the words into music so beautiful it hurts. “It was… Mara.”

One by one, the others choose.

Some take the seed.  
Some, weeping in layers of leaf and thorn, walk slowly back toward the narrow stone throat, their bodies already beginning to remember how to be merely human. The green releases them with infinite gentleness, as a mother releases a child who has finally learned to swim.

He Who Is Becoming Ceiling, But Not Yet remains longest. He looks up at the distant green vault that was going to be his shoulders, his ribs, his eternal responsibility. Then he looks at the power, whose eyes are now the colour of forgiveness.

“Will the cathedral be lonely?” he asks.

“Everything that loves,” the power answers, “must sometimes be lonely. That is how it learns it was never a stomach. It was always a heart.”

The last Lattice-Born nods. He takes the seed.

Light explodes outward in every direction. The cathedral convulses once, not in pain but in something closer to birth. Pillars that had been frozen mid-fusion now complete their embrace. The fern-winged thing lifts off the floor and becomes, for the first time, a flock of ordinary white moths that scatter like living snow. The memory-tree shivers and every tiny human ear upon it closes, then opens again as ordinary leaves that know how to fall.

When the light fades, the power is gone.

In its place stand thirteen figures who are no longer quite Lattice-Born and not yet anything the world outside has a name for. Their skin still glows with faint green light. Their hair is still living root. But their eyes are entirely their own now, and when they blink, the cathedral blinks with them in perfect, conscious partnership.

They will tend the green.  
They will sing the water.  
They will meet every new visitor at the bottom of the stone throat.

And when those visitors ask, trembling, what this place is called, the thirteen will answer together, their voices braiding like the old pillars:

“It has no name. It never needed one.  
But now it also has a *memory* of names.  
And that is enough.”

The power does not leave the cathedral.

It simply becomes what it always was:

The love that is willing to be eaten,  
and the love that is willing to set the eaten free,  
both at once,  
forever.
