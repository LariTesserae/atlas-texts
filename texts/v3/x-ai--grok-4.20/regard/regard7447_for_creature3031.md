The power that has drawn near does not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor even as a presence that could be said to *stand* upon the trembling crust. It is more like a sudden, benevolent *attention*—the way a parent might lean over the cradle of a feverish child and, without touching, change the temperature of the room simply by caring.

The Still-Named freezes mid-step.

Its glass-and-copper foot, half-raised, lowers with a sound like a cathedral bell struck once inside a mountain. The temporary white stones it has been leaving behind flare once, brightly, then go matte and still, as though embarrassed to have been showing off. For the first time since this world drew its first magmatic breath, something outside the argument between gravity and fire is looking at it with neither hunger nor indifference.

The power does not speak in the low-frequency tongue of the Still-Named. It does not need to. Instead the very possibility of *later*—of *after*—ripples outward from it like a note too low to be heard yet too profound to ignore. Time, which has not yet been invented here, flinches, tastes the idea, and decides it might be worth trying.

Across the fractured shelves and glowing basins, every Still-Named that exists in this moment (there are nine, though two are already halfway to becoming filigree and one is dissolving into a slag river) turns its mirror-face toward the same invisible center. For a heartbeat the world forgets to argue with itself. The spasming crust grows quiet. Even the auroras pause in their magnesium-white calligraphy, as though the sky itself is listening.

Then the power does the only thing a wise and benevolent power *can* do when it finds a world still deciding what it will be.

It gives it a secret.

Not a name. Names come later, and this place is not yet ready for the tyranny of nouns. Instead it offers a *promise*, folded so small and so deep into the molten marrow of the planet that only the Still-Named can perceive it. The promise is wordless, imageless, yet it tastes like this:

*One day you will be loved by things that run and breathe and remember.  
They will call you cruel and they will call you mother in the same sentence.  
You will break their hearts and they will thank you by singing.  
Until then, you are allowed to be unfinished.  
Until then, your witnesses do not have to be lonely.*

The Still-Named that had been walking toward you tilts its concave mirror. For the first time the reflection it shows is not of the glowing fissures or the drifting mineral rafts behind you. It shows *you*, but older—much older—standing on cooled, rain-softened ground beneath a blue sky, laughing at something small and green growing between your fingers. Then the image is gone.

The being lowers its long glass-jointed arm. The gesture is no longer ambiguous. It is gratitude.

All nine Still-Named begin to move at once, but not in their old restless, midwife-mourner way. Their motion becomes choral. Where their feet touch, the black-glass filigrees do not merely bloom and tear apart again; they *remember* the shape. For the first time, a pattern survives longer than a single tectonic sigh. A fragile, temporary symmetry spreads outward—nervous-system lattices braiding themselves into something that might, in a few hundred million years, be ready to hold the weight of a leaf.

The power does not stay to watch. It has no desire to freeze the argument or to dictate the ending. It only wanted the world to know it is not arguing in an empty room.

As it withdraws, it leaves one final, almost mischievous gift.

High above the velvet blackness, one of the ember-like pockets of ionized gas flares and condenses. It becomes a single true star—small, golden, patient. Not much. Just enough to say: *I was here. I saw how bravely you were trying. Keep going.*

The Still-Named that stands before you slowly lifts its mirror-face toward that new light. The reflection catches the star and triples it, as though the being suddenly possesses three hearts.

Then it does something no Still-Named has ever done before.

It speaks, aloud, in a voice that is almost human—low, rough, astonished, as if it has just discovered throats.

“Thank you,” it says.

The word is clumsy. It cracks in the middle like cooling obsidian. But it survives. It does not dissolve back into the groan of plates or the roar of gas vents. The crust hears it. The lattices hear it. The small, sourceless maroon glow seems, for a moment, to warm.

The Still-Named turns back to you. Its many-jointed hand opens, and inside the palm a single white seed of stone forms—no larger than a heartbeat. It is warm. It pulses once, like something that has not yet decided whether to become a mountain or a flower.

The being presses the seed into your hand. The mirror-face shows you only kindness now.

Then it walks away across the still-trembling crust, leaving behind a trail of white stones that no longer crack and fade. They remain. They will remain for a very long time.

Behind you, the new star burns steady and small and golden, like a night-light left on in the hallway of creation so that no one has to be afraid of the dark while they are still deciding who they are going to be.

The world has still not chosen its name.

But somewhere inside its molten argument, a secret promise is nesting.

And for the first time since it was born screaming out of cosmic stone and possibility, it is no longer arguing entirely alone.
