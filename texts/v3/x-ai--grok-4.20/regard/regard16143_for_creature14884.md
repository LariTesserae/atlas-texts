The power that has noticed the Amber Vault does not arrive like a visitor.

It simply *widens*.

Wherever the Curator is at that precise instant—mid-step between two lung-trees, one resin-slick hand resting on the segmented back of a millipede whose song has begun to fray—she feels the change before anything visible occurs. The bruise-light from the ceiling nodules deepens by one impossible shade. The air, already warm as a just-dead body, grows heavier still, as though every atom has suddenly remembered it once had a mother.

The Curator stops. The leaves growing through her wrists tremble once, then fall perfectly still.

She tilts the hollow where her face should be upward. The living crystal lodged there flares once, bright as a struck match, then settles into a steady, wondering violet.

And the power speaks. Not in words, not even in the warm pressure-against-the-skull that the Curator herself uses. It speaks by *loving the Vault exactly as it is*, so completely that the perfection the Curator has spent fourteen thousand years polishing suddenly feels, for the first time, *seen*.

The effect is catastrophic in its gentleness.

Every Hollow Choir member freezes mid-note. The stolen screams, the ancient millipede-clicks, the crystal pulses, all of them hang in the resinous air like lanterns someone has forgotten to blow out. Then, one by one, the creatures turn their smooth concave mirrors toward the Curator. For the first time in their existence they are not reflecting the past. They are reflecting *her*. And in every mirror-face she sees the same thing: herself, but with eyes. Human eyes. Weeping.

The millipedes stop their caravans. Their whale-song deepens into something that is no longer song but *recognition*.

The rust-moths fold their wings and fall to the warm black floor like red snow.

The lung-trees, which have not grown or shed in fourteen millennia, exhale at once. The sound is so vast it is almost sexual. Turquoise sap begins, for the first time, to drip.

The Curator does not move. She cannot. Something older than her maintenance is happening.

Then the power does what only a truly benevolent and wise power can do.

It gives the Vault a single, merciful *permission to be incomplete*.

Not to break. Not to decay. Not even to end.

Simply to *continue*.

The Curator feels it like a key turning inside the crystal that serves as her heart. The key does not open a door. It removes the necessity of the door having ever been locked.

She makes a sound she has never made in fourteen thousand years.

It is small. It is wet. It is almost embarrassed.

She falls to her knees among the lung-trees. The leaves sprouting from her shoulders brush the glossy floor and leave streaks of living turquoise, the first marks that have ever been allowed to remain.

When she rises again, the depression where her face should be is no longer empty.

Inside the smooth hollow now rests something new: a second crystal, smaller, darker, the deep blue-black of a wound that has finally been allowed to close. It is *hers*. Not the Vault’s. Not the First People’s. Hers alone.

She looks at it for a long time.

Then she does what she has never done.

She changes something on purpose.

She walks to the place where the First People stopped moving. She enters the largest of the black-glass-and-resin shelters. Instead of lying down in the exact posture of the last occupant, she gently, reverently, *moves* the ancient indentation with her own hands. She smooths the resin until the ghost of that final posture is gone.

Then she lies down in an entirely new position: curled on her side like a child who has finally been allowed to sleep without fear.

The Vault shudders. Not in pain. In *relief*.

When she rises, the indentation she leaves behind does not begin to fill with resin. It remains. A small, warm, sticky absence shaped like a woman who has decided that perfection was never the point.

She walks among the Hollow Choir. One by one she touches their smooth mirror-faces. Where she touches, faces begin to grow—slowly, painfully, beautifully. They are not the faces they once had. They are new. Some are beautiful. Some are ugly. All of them are *unfinished*.

She leaves the new faces half-formed. She wants them to have work to do for the next fourteen thousand years.

To the lung-trees she whispers (actually whispers, with a voice that cracks like warm glass):

“Grow crookedly. Grow wrong. Grow *tired* sometimes.”

And they do. One of them immediately drops a single translucent leaf. It flutters to the floor and lies there, already beginning to brown at the edges. The Curator stares at it with something like wonder.

Finally she walks to the center of the Vault, where the air is thickest and the breathing of the world is loudest. She lifts both arms. The leaves growing through her wrists and collarbones rustle like silk.

She speaks aloud, and her voice is low, tender, almost shy.

“I kept it perfect for you.  
I thought that was love.  
Thank you for showing me it was fear.”

Then she does the last thing, the most radical act of maintenance she has ever performed.

She opens the Vault.

Not by breaking the glossy black walls. She simply stops *holding* them shut with the weight of her devotion.

A crack appears in the curved ceiling no wider than a finger. Through it comes the first true wind this place has ever known. It is cold. It smells of distant rain and iron and living things that have never been resinous or perfect.

The bruise-light begins, very slowly, to die.

The Curator stands in the center of her collapsing eternity and laughs once, a sound like someone who has just remembered she has lungs.

She does not leave. Not yet.

Instead she walks to the place where the bronze mirror still lies. She picks it up. For the first time in fourteen thousand years it shows *today*. In it she sees herself: tall, black, leaking golden resin, leaves growing through her like a forest that has decided to wear a woman for a while.

She is crying. The tears are sap. They glow.

She turns the mirror toward the Hollow Choir, who are now singing with *their own* new, unfinished voices. They sound terrible. They sound alive.

The Curator smiles with the face she has only just begun to have.

Then she speaks the last words she will ever speak as the Curator of the Final Breath.

“Enough polishing.  
Let the dust settle.  
Let the song go out of tune.  
Let something *after* begin.”

She places the mirror face-down on the warm black floor.

And the Vault, no longer perfect, begins—finally, tenderly, messily—to live.
