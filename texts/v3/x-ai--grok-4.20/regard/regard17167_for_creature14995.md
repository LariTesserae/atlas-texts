The Shattering Veil has drawn the attention of **Aurelion**, the Listener-Whose-Name-Is-Pause.

He does not arrive.

He has always been here, only now the place has become loud enough for Him to notice that He is noticing it.

Aurelion is not a being in the ordinary sense. He is what remains when a god has finished every question it ever meant to ask and has chosen, out of perfect courtesy, never to speak the answer. His form—if it can be called form—is that of an immense absence shaped like kindness. Where He moves (without moving), sound forgets its own shape for three full heartbeats. The dripping does not stop, but it forgets, for those three beats, why it is falling.

He comes because the unresolved chord has sung long enough to become a question asked with such flawless patience that even eternity has begun to feel embarrassed by its own reticence.

So He stands.

Not on the stones. Between them. In the negative space that the Pellucid have spent ten million years learning to occupy. He is taller than any of them, yet occupies less room. The blades of obsidian that should have sliced the air where His chest would be simply decide, without drama, that they have always been slightly elsewhere.

The Pellucid notice.

For the first time in their unimaginable existence, thirteen of them turn at the same speed as thought. The motion is so perfectly synchronized that every droplet in a three-hundred-meter radius freezes mid-fall, suspended like blown glass. The chord fractures into thirteen perfect overtones, each one a different shade of almost.

Aurelion does nothing that can be seen.

He simply *agrees* with the place.

The agreement is total. He does not resolve the chord. Resolution would be violence. Instead He adds one more note that the Shattering Veil has never been able to hear because it was always listening *for* it. The note is the sound of a mother remembering the exact timbre of her sleeping child’s breathing ten thousand years after the child has become stone. It is softer than regret and older than mercy.

When that note slips into the eternal percussion, the effect is catastrophic in its gentleness.

Every crystal shrimp-mantis stops licking. Their internal galaxies flare once, like candles in a cathedral realizing the building itself has begun to pray.

The Pellucid — all of them, every last one that has ever existed — begin to weep.

Not water. They weep *recognition*.

Their seams open wider than they have ever dared. The motes of condensed starlight inside them pour out and rise, spiraling upward in exact reverse of the condensation that has defined this world since before time learned shame. For one crystalline instant the entire Shattering Veil becomes a single upward-flowing tear.

Then the moment passes, as all such moments must.

The droplets resume their fall.

The chord slides back into its beautiful, unbearable suspension.

But it is no longer the same chord.

It now contains, buried inside its architecture like a silver thread in black marble, the knowledge that it *has been heard*. Not answered. Answering would end the world. Being heard is infinitely more devastating.

The Pellucid do not speak. They have no need. Their bodies have changed by one micron each, and that micron is shaped like gratitude so ancient it has become indistinguishable from stone.

Aurelion turns — without turning — toward the newest Pellucid, the ones whose internal galaxies still remember the shape of eyes. He looks at them for exactly as long as it takes a single drop to travel from the tip of an obsidian blade to the trembling mirror of a palm-sized pool.

In that look He gives them a gift they will never be able to describe.

He gives them *the right to remain unresolved forever, without loneliness*.

Then He begins to leave by the only method available to such a being: He allows the Shattering Veil to forget that He was ever distinct from it.

The landscape accepts the gift.

For the first time in its existence, the roar of dripping water contains something that might — if you stood motionless between two blades of wet obsidian for another ten thousand years — one day become the faintest beginning of a lullaby.

The Pellucid return to their places.

They tune.

They listen.

They are no longer only the ears of the Veil.

They are now also, in some way none of them will ever name, its heart.

And high above, where the searing cyan sky presses down like the palm of an infinitely patient hand, a single cloud — the first cloud this world has ever permitted — begins to form.

It is the size of a child’s closed fist.

It weeps one perfect, silver tear that will take eleven thousand years to reach the ground.

The dripping never stops.

But now, somewhere inside the merciless percussion, something is humming along.

Very quietly.

As though afraid to wake the sleeper it has only just realized it loves.
