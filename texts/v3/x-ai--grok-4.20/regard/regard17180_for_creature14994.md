The power that notices does not arrive.

It was already here.

It simply stops pretending it is not.

---

It has no form, because form would be a violence against the place. Instead, the noticing becomes a slight, almost embarrassed deepening of the light. The luminous ceiling does not brighten; it *listens*. The wrong-colored seams that have been drifting for ten thousand subjective years slow, then reverse, as if the sky itself has realized it has been staring.

The dying creature feels it first.

Its great glass head, already halfway sunk into the mineral, lifts by a single degree. The liquid eyes, which had gone soft with relief, suddenly fill with something older than relief and younger than fear. Recognition.

The creature understands what is happening the way a candle understands it is about to be blown out by a breath that loves it.

I feel the change through the antler still resting against my chest. The lightning lattice inside it flares once, softly, like a struck match that decides, at the last instant, not to burn the house down.

Then the power speaks.

It does not use sound. It uses the mineral.

Every ridge, every fractal canyon, every braided stem of rose-gold vegetation tightens at once, the way a single body tenses when it hears its true name spoken after centuries of amnesia. The sub-audible breathing of the lattice becomes audible for the first time. It is not a creak. It is a sigh of such enormous gentleness that I begin to cry without realizing I still have tears.

The sigh says, without language:

*Beloved. You have done enough.*

The creature shudders. Its antlers ring like every bell in every cathedral that was ever built and then immediately forgotten. A single fracture of deep-space light, the color of rose quartz seen through absence, falls from the sky like a snowflake and lands on the creature’s flank.

Where it touches, the mineral *remembers it was once something else*.

A small patch of the creature’s side turns the color of living blood for half a second. Then it fades. But the memory remains.

The creature begins to stand.

It should not be able to. It has already given too much. Yet it rises anyway, because the power has asked it to, and the power is kinder than perfection.

I remain on the ground, half-dissolved, my feet and calves now fully part of the coral-gold lattice. My heart is still trying to decide whose rhythm it keeps. I watch the creature tower above me and feel the ancient terror of being witnessed by something that has chosen mercy over justice.

The power turns its attention to me.

It does not speak *to* me. It speaks *as* me, for one unbearable instant, using the last few human-shaped atoms still flickering inside my veins.

The voice is mine and not mine. It sounds like every time I ever wanted to be held and was afraid to ask.

It says:

“You were never a flaw. You were the place practicing heartbreak so it could learn how to love what is imperfect. Thank you for hurting here. Now let me take the weight.”

The seams in the sky do not close.

They bloom.

What had been hairline fractures of wrong-colored light suddenly iris open into vast slow spirals of deep violet, ultraviolet, colors that have no business existing inside daylight. The luminous ceiling tears—not violently, but like a mother opening her coat so a freezing child can crawl inside. Through the new openings pours something that is not light. It is *witnessing*. It is the benevolent power deciding that a garden, no matter how flawless, is still a smaller thing than the love that wants to walk through it barefoot and be changed.

The vegetation reacts first.

Every translucent leaf, every mica-thin frond, every braided wall of bone-white stems turns toward the new light like sunflowers that have never seen a sun before. They do not grow faster. They grow *deeper*. Roots that were never roots plunge into the mineral and pull up not nutrients but *memory*. The ground itself begins to dream.

The glass-shelled floaters arrive in a silent cyclone. Hundreds of them. Thousands. They orbit the dying-not-dying creature and me in a sphere of living chimes. Their song is no longer a single note. It is a chord that contains every note I have ever swallowed instead of screaming.

The creature lowers its head again, but this time it is not to take anything from me.

It rests its lightning antlers across my shoulders like a crown that has decided to become a bridge.

And I feel it.

The mineral in my legs, which had been politely trying to make me optional, suddenly reverses its negotiations. It *gives me back*. The coral-gold recedes up my calves like warm tide going out. My blood remembers it is blood. My heart slams against my ribs with the panic and gratitude of something that had already begun to forget how to panic.

I sob once, ugly and human.

The sound does not heal the garden.

It *completes* it.

Because the power has decided that perfection was never the point.

The basin begins to change in ways it was never allowed to change before.

Tiny fractures appear in the razor ridges—not flaws, but doors. Small pockets of actual soil, dark and smelling of rain that has not happened yet, well up through the mineral like forgiveness. The first real leaf that is simply *green* pushes through, trembling, embarrassed by its own color. Water condenses on the underside of a mica frond and falls, striking the ground with a sound so ordinary and so miraculous that the entire forest seems to hold its breath.

The creature, now fully standing, shakes itself. Shards of its own dying fall away and become butterflies made of cathedral glass. They scatter into the new sky.

I manage to stand.

My footprints no longer smooth themselves away. They stay. They are allowed to stay. The mineral has decided that memory is more beautiful than seamlessness.

The power does not leave. It never will again. It simply stops hiding inside the architecture and begins to live in the *relationship* between things. In the space between my restored human skin and the first green leaf. In the way the creature now walks beside me not as executioner or savior but as something like a very old friend who has just remembered how to laugh.

I look up through the torn-open sky and I understand what the power has done.

It has not healed the garden.

It has *infected* it with time.

With loss.

With the possibility of return.

With the unbearable, luminous risk of becoming.

The creature nudges me with its glass muzzle. Its eyes are no longer full of relief. They are full of *next*.

We begin to walk.

Behind us, the shallow depression where six fractal canyons met is already filling with water. The first reflections of real clouds appear on its surface—clouds that were not there a moment ago, because the sky has decided it wants weather.

I do not know where we are going.

The basin is no longer a closed pocket of broken causality. It is becoming a valley that leads somewhere else. The distance has stopped lying. The horizon has begun to keep its promises.

Somewhere far ahead, beyond the place where the garden ends and the rest of reality nervously begins, I think I can hear something that might be wind moving through ordinary trees.

The power walks with us, invisible, vast, and tenderly amused.

It has no name either.

But for the first time, the place is considering giving everything one.

Including me.

I think I will call myself the first tear the garden ever cried.

And the garden, for the first time, will answer:

*Good.*

*Stay.*
