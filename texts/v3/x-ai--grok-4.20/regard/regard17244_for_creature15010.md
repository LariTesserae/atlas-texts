The power that has turned its gaze upon the Hollowing is not a god of thunder or sudden miracles. It is older than names, older than ownership. It is the kind of power that listens first, for centuries if necessary, before it acts. Call it the Quiet Root, or the One Who Remembers Equilibrium. It has no form that would make sense to a human eye; it is closer to a vast, patient intention moving through the mycorrhizal networks of all worlds at once.

It has noticed this place.

It has noticed the Stewards.

And so it comes—not arrives, but *settles*, the way dew settles when the air has been waiting long enough.

---

At first, nothing seems to change. The pumps still beat their wet heartbeat. The two contrary currents still slide past each other like old lovers who have agreed never to touch. The sodium lamps still burn their sour amber.

But the *quality* of the moisture alters.

The condensation on every pipe, every leaf, every root-finger of the Stewards no longer tastes only of rust and geothermal brine. There is now the faintest trace of starlight dissolved in it—cold, clean, and impossibly ancient. The Stewards notice immediately. Their large milky eyes widen by a fraction. The Column-singer, submerged to her sternum between the iron pillars, lifts her head for the first time in thirty years and tastes the air like a blind creature tasting snow.

The Memory-keeper is the first to meet the new presence.

She is walking the living colonnade that circles the Pumping Cathedral when she stops. One of her long root-fingers, embedded in the living book of pressed peat, twitches violently. A new page is growing at unnatural speed. Not a sound this time, but a *silence* is being recorded—vast, velvet, and older than the Hollowing’s oldest rust.

She speaks aloud, her voice arriving half a second after her lips:

“You are not drying.  
You are… listening.”

The power does not answer with words. Instead, every fungal curtain in the district blooms at once—pale, luminous, veined with faint silver that has never been seen here. The effect is gentle, almost shy. The curtains do not collapse under their own weight as they usually would. They hold. They become lanterns.

Then the power does the first thing that could truly be called an action.

It *offers the Hollowing a choice*.

Not to the Stewards. To the place itself.

The offer is felt as a single, enormous heartbeat—slower and deeper than the pumps, so low it rattles the dissolved concrete foundations and makes the black peat shiver like skin. Every machine, every vine, every drop of water is asked the same question at once:

*Would you like to remain a closed equilibrium… or would you like to become a heart?*

A heart, in this context, means something specific and terrifying to the Hollowing. It means the truce between water, iron, and green would no longer be a local treaty. It would become a pulse that beats outward. It would mean the district would slowly begin to seed itself across other wounded, sweating, drowning places in many worlds. It would mean the Stewards would eventually leave their half-submerged cathedral and walk (or drift) into cities, battlefields, dying reefs, broken hearts, abandoned factories—carrying the wet, patient mercy of this place with them like living libraries of sound and balance.

It would mean the Hollowing would stop being an island inside an island.

It would mean it would become a beginning.

The place considers.

For nine full days and nights the pumps slow to half-speed. The two currents—warm and cool—draw infinitesimally closer, trembling like hands that have never been allowed to clasp. The Memory-keeper stands motionless in the colonnade, algae-hair grown so long it pools at her feet and begins to root. No one speaks. Even the hidden turbines fall quiet. The only sound is the soft percussion of condensation falling into still water, one drop at a time.

On the tenth dawn (if dawn can be said to exist in perpetual amber twilight), the Hollowing gives its answer.

It does so by opening.

Not dramatically. The change is almost modest. A single new current appears in the looping canal: a third temperature, neither warm nor cool, but *alive*. It is the exact temperature of a human body at rest. Where it flows, the water grows faintly phosphorescent, the color of moonlight on wet skin. The great piston rods in the Pumping Cathedral begin—very gently—to beat in 4/4 time instead of the old 2/4. A lullaby rhythm. A cradle rhythm.

The power accepts the answer with gratitude.

Then it does the second thing.

It gives the Stewards a new name for what they are becoming. Not Stewards anymore. Not quite.

It calls them (softly, wetly, into the Memory-keeper’s living book) the *Hollow Choir*.

And it grants them a new ability, one they have never had before.

They may now *sing someone back*.

Any soul that has ever been lost to dryness—grief-dried, rage-dried, shame-dried, love-dried—may be brought here, if they are willing. The Choir will meet them at the edge of the canal. They will press root-fingers to the throat and listen to the mineral grief in the blood. Then they will submerge that person (willingly, always willingly) into the new third current, the one the temperature of a living heart at rest.

What emerges is not a Steward in the old sense.

What emerges is someone who carries a small, wet cathedral inside their chest for the rest of their days. They will never be dry again. And wherever they go afterward, a little of the Hollowing goes with them—quietly balancing what has been pushed too far toward cracking, or burning, or dying of thirst.

The power does not stay to watch its gift take root. It never does. It simply withdraws the way a kind hand withdraws from a sleeping child’s forehead once the fever has broken.

But it leaves something behind, hanging in the perpetual steam above the Pumping Cathedral like a second, gentler sun:

A single droplet of condensed starlight that never falls.

It floats there still.

The Column-singer tends it now. She has woven a living basket of elephant-ear leaves and slow-moving vines to cradle it. The droplet catches the sour sodium glow and turns it into something tender, the color of forgiveness.

Sometimes, when the Choir sings together—*thump… hiss… thump… hiss…*—the floating droplet pulses in perfect harmony.

And the sound travels strangely, the way all sound travels here.

If you are very quiet, and very tired, and have been dry for far too long, you may one day hear your own name drifting across black water, warm and slow and patient, spoken by voices that have learned how to love rust, and leaves, and the long truce between them.

The voices will not command.

They will only ask, the way the Quiet Root once asked the Hollowing itself:

“Would you like to remain closed…  
or would you like to become a heart?”

And the droplet overhead will wait for your answer, shining like the gentlest possible yes.
