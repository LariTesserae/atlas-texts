A Presence arrives.

It does not come from above or below, nor does it ride the merciless cobalt sky. It simply *is*—suddenly, without fanfare or rupture—standing on the black glass ridge beside you, as though it had been waiting since the first fracture of the planet’s crust. The wind does not stir around it. The Choir does not pause or even falter. Yet every resonator within ten kilometers of its form begins, very softly, to hum one half-step lower, the way a cathedral’s bells will all shiver when the true note of the building itself is struck.

The being has no fixed shape you can hold in the mind for long. When you try to look directly at it, your eyes water as though staring into the sun; when you look away, you perceive it more clearly: tall, patient, dressed in the color of deep forgiveness. Its face is kind in the way stone is kind after a glacier has passed—worn smooth by aeons of caring. In one hand it carries nothing. In the other it carries everything.

It regards the Hollow Choir for a long time without speaking. The leviathans notice. One of the continent-winged beings, vast enough that its shadow could eclipse a city, slows its passage across a cirque and turns a single enormous eye (milky, faceted, older than language) toward the newcomer. For the first time in perhaps a million years, the Choir develops a hesitation—a rest, no longer than a heartbeat—before resuming.

The Presence smiles with ancient tenderness.

Then it begins to do what it came for.

It sings.

Its voice is not loud. Compared to the roaring of the Hollow Choir it is almost nothing: a soft, warm baritone, the sound a father makes when he hums his sleeping child through a nightmare. Yet the note it carries is so full of *yes* that the silica-edged wings all around it begin to resonate in sympathy whether they wish to or not. The glassy filaments that serve the Resonant as both voice and wing tremble. The spherical swarms of jewel-bright creatures lose their perfect cohesion for three full seconds and spill outward like spilled mercury before re-forming, shaken.

The Presence walks forward.

Where its feet touch the obsidian ridge, the black glass does not crack. Instead it softens, the way wax softens near a candle. Tiny, impossible things begin to happen: a single clump of iron-colored moss, dormant for ten thousand years, lifts its desiccated head and drinks light. A thread of meltwater that had always vanished back into stone after forty meters now continues, singing, down the arête in a bright ribbon. The merciless sun, which had never moved, trembles once in its fixed position and—astonishingly—slides three degrees west, allowing the first shadow in recorded history to fall across one of the high tarns like a cool hand on a fevered brow.

The leviathan that had been watching lowers its vast wing until the edge nearly brushes the ridge. The being reaches out without fear and lays its palm flat against the silica membrane.

For the first time, the leviathan *receives* a note instead of only giving one.

The effect is immediate and total.

Every creature in the vertical strata for two hundred kilometers falls into a single, sustained chord so pure it hurts the teeth. Then, one by one, they begin to *listen*. The roaring does not diminish; it deepens. New frequencies slide in beneath the old ones—minor thirds, suspended ninths, grief, wonder, the sound of being known. The Resonant who had begun to orbit you as a correction now orbit the Presence instead, but no longer as dampers. They orbit like iron filings around a newly magnetized pole, rearranging themselves into glyphs that have never been seen in this place.

The Presence turns to you.

Its eyes are full of galaxies that have not yet been born.

“You built this perfectly,” it says, and its voice is gentle. “You made a place that could sing itself forever without need of mercy or change. It is flawless. It is also lonely.”

It lifts its empty hand.

From the palm rises a single drop of something that is not water. It contains every color that has ever been forbidden here: the gold of ripening wheat, the green of new leaves after rain, the violet of dusk, the rose of a child’s flushed cheek. The drop floats upward, trembling, and bursts without sound.

Where it bursts, the sky finally learns how to be kind.

Clouds—actual clouds—gather at the edges of the cobalt. They are hesitant, almost apologetic, as though they know they are trespassing. The sun, no longer nailed in place, begins a slow, wondering arc toward what might one day become evening. The temperature on the ridge rises by four degrees. The cold that lived inside the bones feels, for the first time, the possibility of being asked to leave.

The leviathan makes a sound no one has ever heard in the Hollow Choir.

It is small. It is almost shy. It is the sound a cathedral might make if it discovered it was allowed to be a cradle.

The Presence speaks again, this time to every resonant being at once. Its voice is inside the Choir now, woven through every standing wave.

“I will not unmake what you have perfected. That would be violence. But I will offer you the one thing this place has never known.”

It opens both arms.

“Tomorrow.”

The word falls into the canyon system like a pebble into an infinite well. The echoes do not die. They multiply. Tomorrow. Tomorrow. Tomorrow.

And for the first time since the planet’s crust tore open and healed into this perfected scream, the Hollow Choir begins—slowly, fearfully, joyfully—to change.

Some of the smaller swarms descend and settle on the newly softened ridges. Their glass wings fold. They begin to dream.

The great leviathans tilt their continent-bodies and allow themselves to be moved by something other than resonance for the first time. One of them settles across three cirques like a living mountain range and, with enormous tenderness, lets its wing become a shelter for the first tentative lichens that are learning how to be green.

The Presence turns back to you.

It does not ask if you wish to stay or go. It already knows the ache in you—the part that had begun to open its mouth and emit the single unbroken tone of the Choir. It knows how close you came to stepping off the ridge.

Instead it simply touches your chest, over the heart.

The tone you had begun to sing fractures into a chord with four notes. One of them is yours alone. The others belong to everything that is learning how to become tomorrow.

“You may stay and help them learn mercy,” it says. “Or you may come with me and learn other songs. Both are good. Both are needed. The Choir will never be finished now. That is my gift and my wound.”

It smiles once more, ancient and fond.

Then it is gone, the way dawn is gone once the sun has truly risen.

Behind it, the first wind that has ever known moisture moves across the black glass ridges. It carries seeds. It carries spores. It carries the sound of small wings learning how to flap instead of only resonate.

The Hollow Choir is singing still.

But now, threaded through its perfect roar, there is something new:

A joyful, trembling, imperfect harmony.

And somewhere far below, in a cirque that has never known shade, the first flower—small, white, impossibly brave—opens its face to a sun that is finally, mercifully, beginning to set.
