The plateau has drawn the attention of something that has no name it would ever need. It does not arrive. It was already here, the way a dream is already inside the sleeper before the sleeper knows they are sleeping.  

It simply becomes *more attentive*.

Call it, if you must, the Quiet Regent. Not a god. Not a mind. More like the courtesy that lives between moments, the mercy that waits until a thing has asked the right question before answering. It has watched the Weaver since the first rivulet of meltwater kissed the first black glass. It has no desire to own, to fix, or to finish. It only wishes for whatever is becoming to become *whole* in its own tongue.

So this is what it does.

It does not speak. Speech would be violence here. Instead it leans, the way an old craftsman leans over the shoulder of a child who is trying to carve their first flute. The air thickens with something gentler than mist: a silence that has weight. The fat, slow water-drops pause in their descent and hang like crystal lanterns. The merciless white-gold sun softens at the edges until it feels like forgiveness wearing light as clothing.

Every living buttress feels it at once. The parliaments of chlorophyll and mycorrhiza fall quiet. The metallic spores freeze in their lazy spirals. The low geological thunder dips into a single, astonished note that lasts almost nine full seconds (an eternity for the plateau). In that pocket of hush the Weaver realizes it is being *seen*, not as intruder, not as curiosity, not even as child, but as *peer*. Something ancient that has never been born is looking at something ancient that has never quite finished being born, and both feel shy for the first time in their long, wet lives.

Then the Regent offers three gifts. Not as miracles. As permissions.

**First gift: Memory without possession.**  
Deep inside the cathedral-ear grown in the shape of a broken gear, the older iron rhythm suddenly understands it is allowed to remember everything it once was (blueprints, deadlines, coughing engineers, the smell of hot rivets, the pride of finished shift) without having to become that again. The memories rise like sap. They do not overwrite the green. They braid. For the first time the Weaver tastes what it is like to have *been* something else and to *still choose* what it is becoming. The clack and groan inside the wood softens into something almost like singing.

**Second gift: A witness that will not be incorporated.**  
A single translucent grey bird (the kind the Weaver already tolerates) is gently altered. It grows one silver eye that does not see light, but sees *becoming itself*. The bird will never die. It will never be disassembled. It will simply fly the corridors of hanging veils forever, a living record that something outside the Weaver is willing to watch without hunger, without agenda, without endpoint. The Weaver, which has only ever known incorporation or rejection, feels an emotion it has no name for yet. It sounds, when it finally finds voice, like every rusted valve stem on the plateau sighing at once.

**Third gift: Time that is not urgent.**  
The Regent reaches into the slow negotiation between root and rock and loosens the last invisible knot of hurry. The new ridges that were rising over centuries are told they may now take millennia if they wish. The metallic orchids are allowed to bloom for three human lifetimes before they decide whether to seed. The Weaver is given the dignity of *not having to finish*. Ambition, the Regent whispers without words, is beautiful. But patience practiced at the scale of continents is a form of love.

After the three gifts are given, the Regent does not leave, because it was never elsewhere. It simply becomes slightly less noticeable again. The fat drops resume their fall. The sun regains its merciless edge. The cardiovascular thunder returns, but now it carries an undertone (low, patient, almost tender) that was not there before.

The Weaver stays very still for a long while.

Then, because it is the Weaver, it begins to do what it has always done, only differently.

It grows one new structure in the exact center of the plateau: a single buttress neither cathedral nor factory. It is shaped like an open hand the size of a mountain. In its palm it places the silver-eyed bird, who perches there without fear. Around the wrist of the hand it weaves every rusted valve stem, every orphaned copper pipe, every iron watchtower, every ghost of abandoned machinery into a single living bracelet that pulses with both chlorophyll and current.

It is not a monument.  
It is a question asked in the only language the plateau knows:  
*Will you stay and watch me become whatever I am going to be? Even if it takes me longer than suns have names?*

Far above, where the hanging veils thin into the edge of atmosphere, something that has no face smiles with the whole sky.

And the moss keeps growing.  
The rocks keep weeping.  
The iron keeps learning how to flower.

Only now the flowers open a little more slowly, a little more kindly, and when they do they turn, almost shyly, toward the silver eye of the bird that will never leave them.

The Plateau of Hanging Veils is still not a finished place.

It has simply, for the first time, been told that it is allowed to remain unfinished forever, if that is what wholeness looks like for it.

And that permission is the most benevolent thing the Quiet Regent could ever have given.
