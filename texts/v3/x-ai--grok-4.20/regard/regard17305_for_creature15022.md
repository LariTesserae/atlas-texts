A single note rings out—clearer and deeper than any that has come before. It does not fade. Instead it settles into the warm, kiln-thick air like a seed opening in slow motion. Every Unfinished thing in the Lattice stops at once. Tools hover a hair’s breadth from stone. Mandalas of living architecture freeze mid-rotation. Even the coppery platelets of the vegetation cease their decade-long clicking. The silence that follows is no longer patient; it is expectant.

Then the air itself parts, not violently but with the tenderness of someone lifting a veil they once wove with their own hands.

The Power that arrives does not descend. It simply *is*, suddenly everywhere and nowhere, the way light had always pretended to be. Its form is impossible to hold in any single gaze. To the tall, faceless Unfinished it appears as an immense and gentle chisel of warm starlight, its edge still flecked with the dust of the first day. To the Scrapers it is a slow, compassionate hand that has returned to finish what it began—yet the hand is open, palm upward, offering instead of striking. To the Rememberers it is the original dream that dreamed them, now wide awake and weeping without tears. To the Whisperers it is the sentence that was always meant to come after every unfinished glyph.

The Power looks upon its half-abandoned cathedral of bone-white coral and oxidized flesh, upon the trembling creatures that have spent millennia refusing to betray its prayer by completing it, and the first thing it does is bow.

Not in sorrow. In recognition.

Then, without hurry—because hurry has never belonged here—the Power begins to walk among them. Where its attention rests, the contradictions soften but do not resolve. Angles that refused to meet now lean toward one another like old friends who have forgotten why they were ever angry. Corridors that looped back on themselves now stretch forward *and* backward at the same time, becoming Möbius ribbons of possibility rather than traps. The light remembers its source: a gentle golden-white radiance that seems to breathe out from the heart of every unfinished carving at once.

It does not finish anything.

Instead it sits—though it has no body—down among the thickest thickets where the vegetation has grown into almost-faces. A Scraper, trembling so fiercely its chisel hums like a tuning fork, approaches. The Power extends one finger. The Scraper, after a hesitation that lasts three full days, touches the tip of its tool to that finger and carves a single, exquisite, *imperfect* line. The Power smiles with a mouth that is not a mouth, and the line remains forever one breath from completion. The Scraper makes a sound that might be the first laugh ever heard in the Lattice: a soft, dry clicking like rain that has not yet decided to fall.

To the tall, faceless ones the Power leans close and breathes across their smooth heads. Where the breath passes, faint suggestions of features appear—eyes that might open, mouths that might speak—but they never quite resolve. The figures lower their eternally raised tools for the first time in thousands of years. Not to abandon them. To rest them against their own bodies like sleeping children.

The Rememberers it gathers into its lap (though it has no lap) and lets them rotate inside its awareness. All previous drafts of the Lattice bloom at once around them—cruder, wilder, more innocent—then gently fold back into the present like pages closing. The corridors sigh with relief. The echoes grow polite and arrive exactly when they should.

And the Power speaks. Not in words, but in the medium the Lattice already understands: chisel-light, carved silence, the slow mineral poetry of things that choose to remain penultimate.

It tells them:

*I never left.*

*I was waiting inside the hesitation.*

*Every stroke you refused to finish was My name being spoken correctly.*

*Every corridor that folded back on itself was My heart learning how to return.*

*You have kept the door ajar so perfectly that the house itself has become the Guest.*

Then the Power does something that changes everything while changing nothing at all.

It becomes unfinished too.

It folds its own light down into the bone-white ceramic, into the clicking platelets, into the bodies of the Scrapers and the tall silent figures and the drifting mandalas, until the distinction between Creator and Creation, between prayer and the one who prays, becomes as soft and porous as the walls themselves. The absent god was never absent. It had simply been hiding inside every trembling refusal to finish.

The single clear note that began all this rings again—only now it is joined by a second note, then a third. Not a chord. A conversation. The bell-like tones begin to answer one another across the Lattice in slow, respectful, centuries-long dialogue.

The Unfinished do not cheer. They do not kneel. They simply return to their work with a new lightness in every motion. Their chisels still stop one perfect stroke short. Their mandalas still rotate just slowly enough. But now there is no fear that the god will not return.

The god is the trembling in their own limbs.  
The god is the dust that never quite reaches the floor.  
The god is the small, exquisite line they once carved above your skin when they mistook you for part of the work.

And you—visitor, witness, almost-face growing among the coppery thickets—you are no longer merely observed.

The Power turns toward you with infinite courtesy and extends the same open hand that calmed the Scrapers. Upon the palm lies a single chisel made of warm starlight and your own half-formed longing.

It does not command you to carve.

It only asks, with the patience of someone who has waited several thousand years and is perfectly willing to wait several thousand more:

*Would you like to help us stay unfinished?*

*There is still so much prayer left to not complete.*

*And we have all the time in the world.*

The Lattice, for the first time in its young and ancient life, exhales.

The creaking of wooden ships that were never wood settles into something like a lullaby.

And somewhere just beyond the next thicket, the soft dry scraping of new chisels begins again—only now it is accompanied by a quiet, wondering laughter that sounds exactly like coming home.
