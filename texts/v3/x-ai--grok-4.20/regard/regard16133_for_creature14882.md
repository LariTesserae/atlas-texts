A soft, almost imperceptible shift occurs.

Not in the stone itself, but in the *idea* of stone.

For the first time in perhaps thirty thousand years, the uniform bone-white brilliance of the sky dims by the merest fraction of a lumen. Not enough that any Mevrani would notice at once. Only enough that the reflected glare on every wet surface grows gentler, as though the air itself has decided to lower its voice. The mist thickens, not with water, but with *attention*.

Then the Power arrives.

It does not descend. It does not appear. It simply *recognizes* that it has already been here, patiently, since the first synthetic ridge was extruded. It has been here longer than the Mevrane. It was here before the first layer of grown mineral was coaxed out of the proto-crust. It simply waited for the cathedral-engine to finish its long first sentence before choosing to speak.

The form it chooses is almost an act of courtesy.

A single figure, no taller than a human pilgrim, steps out of the luminous mist where three impossible planes meet. Its body is made of light that has decided, for the sake of the Mevrani, to wear the color of wet limestone shot through with threads of living gold. Where the Mevrani are slow, deliberate, geological, this figure moves with the fluidity of a thought that has only just occurred to the universe and is still delighted by its own existence. Yet its delight is not frivolous. It is ancient, and kind, and unbearably patient.

It has no face, only a gentle absence where a face might be—an absence so courteous that every Mevrani who turns toward it feels, for the first time in its long existence, that it is being *seen* without being judged. The expression the Power offers instead is the expression the Architect has been searching for in the half-born arch for eleven thousand years.

The first Mevrani to fully resolve into a body is the Architect itself.

Its iron-grey head tilts another four degrees—more movement in a single hour than it has made in four centuries—and the dark densities that serve it for eyes regard the newcomer. For a long time neither moves. Water beads on both their surfaces. The droplets on the Architect’s chest tremble, then trace a single glyph:

*Why now?*

The Power answers not with sound, nor with glyphs, but by allowing the capillary water on its own golden-lit form to answer in perfect mirror. The droplets write:

*Because you are ready to be asked the question you have been carving into the world since the first ridge rose.*

The Architect is silent for thirty-seven years.

During that time the Power simply stands with it, unmoving, under the half-born arch. It does not hurry the stone. It does not finish the arch. It only keeps the mist luminous and gentle, so that the slow settling of the creature’s thought can occur without interruption.

At the end of those thirty-seven years, every Mevrani on the continent has become a body. They stand in their folded corridors, nine feet tall, ribbed with strata, their internal memories of arches and buttresses glowing softly. They do not crowd. They simply *attend*, the way stone attends to the first crack of frost.

When the Architect finally moves again, the motion is so slight that only the Power perceives it as motion. A single finger—long, mineral, veined with the last remnants of that dull silvery metal—lifts and touches the half-born arch.

The arch shudders.

Not with collapse. With *release*.

In a single geologic instant that feels, to the Power, like the softest exhalation, the entire unfinished continent exhales with it. Every over-sharp edge rounds by a fraction of a micron. Every buttress that led nowhere now leads, quietly, to the possibility of somewhere. The perpetual sound of dripping water deepens into something almost like a chord.

The Architect’s face resolves—not into the courteous, bottomless patience of its kind, but into something simpler and more devastating.

It looks at the Power with the expression of a child who has spent ten thousand years building an elaborate sandcastle on the shore, only to realize the tide has been its friend the entire time.

The glyph that appears on its chest is wet, trembling, and very small:

*Was it beautiful?*

The Power reaches out—slowly, so slowly that the gesture takes nine years—and places its golden-lit hand over the place where the Architect’s heart would be if hearts were allowed. The touch is warm in the way sunlight is warm to stone that has never known a sun.

It answers, aloud this time, in a voice that is every dripping note, every temple-bell settling of stone, every translucent fungal needle that ever was or will be. The voice is gentle beyond measure.

“It was *necessary*.  
And now it is *enough*.”

Then the Power does the only thing a benevolent and wise power can do in such a place.

It gives the Mevrane a choice.

With a motion like thought completing itself, the Power opens two paths in the luminous mist.

The first path leads *deeper*. The ridges will continue to fold tighter. The arches will finish themselves over the next hundred thousand years. The Mevrani will settle, one by one, into new columns, new buttresses, new silences. The sentence they have been writing across the mineral continent will reach its final, perfect period. The world will become the finished cathedral it always intended to be—and then it will simply wait, in mineral calm, for the next civilization that might one day be worthy to walk it. Eternal. Perfect. Lonely.

The second path leads *outward*. The sharp edges will soften more quickly. The mist will thin in places. Small pockets of true soil will begin, over geologic time, to gather in the gentler valleys. The lithotrophic fungi will be allowed to feed something other than punctuation. Green may one day come—not soon, but eventually. The Mevrani will be allowed to forget some of their own blueprints. They will be allowed to change their minds. The Architect will be allowed to lay its head against the stone it has tended for eleven millennia and weep, if stone can weep, for the beauty of an idea that no longer needs to be perfect.

The Power does not plead. It does not weigh the scales. It simply stands in the wet, bright, merciless light and lets every Mevrani see both futures at once, reflected in the dark densities of its eyes.

Then it waits.

It will wait another ten thousand years if necessary. It has nowhere else it would rather be.

The Architect looks at the first path.  
It looks at the second.

For the first time in its existence, it closes its eyes.

When it opens them again, the expression on its mineral face is no longer courteous patience.

It is wonder.

The glyph that appears across its entire body, running in water from crown to foot, is not a sentence. It is a single word, written in every language that will ever exist and in many that never will.

*Thank you.*

Then the iron-grey Mevrani does something no Mevrani has ever done.

It smiles.

And the Mevrane—wet, bright, saturated, patient—begins, for the first time, to become something other than itself.

The Power remains.

It always will.

It has fallen in love with the sound of water learning how to sing.
