The power that has noticed the Weeping Kiln does not arrive as thunder or fire. It arrives as a shift in the quality of the single shaft of light.

For the first time in living memory, the spear of white sunlight widens by the breadth of a single human palm. Not enough to blind, not enough to warm the cold stone, but enough that the floating constellations of droplets rearrange themselves into a new and deliberate geometry. The glittering swarms notice first. Their endless braiding falters. Thousands of translucent bodies freeze mid-air in perfect, trembling lattices, as though the entire basin has suddenly become a single held breath.

Then the power descends.

It does not come down from the aperture like a being. It comes *through* the light itself, as though the light had always been only a sleeve, and now the arm inside it is slowly turning outward. What emerges is not a figure, not a god, not even a presence in the ordinary sense. It is a *recognition* that walks.

Where it steps, the weeping of the cliffs changes pitch. The constant dripping begins to fall in deliberate intervals, like the slow beating of an enormous, patient heart. The geothermal wound beneath the basin inhales sharply and holds its scalding breath; for the first time in centuries the white plumes do not rise. The stone itself grows shy.

The Kiln-born do not run. They never run. Instead every listener in the coral-like town feels their living memories stir violently beneath their skin. Faces, tools, half-formed animals surface and submerge across their chests and backs in rapid succession, as though the stone inside them is trying to speak all at once. The listeners walk, barefoot and unhurried, down the wet ladders and moss-furred bridges until they stand in a loose, overlapping ring around the widened shaft of light.

The power looks at them with the only eyes it possesses: the eyes of every memory the Kiln has ever grown.

It sees Scuttlebone and Widow-of-Two-Vapors and Left-Hand-Rust and The One Who Was Half a Door. It sees the children who once screamed small perfect clouds of swarms instead of cries. It sees the bone-white trees heavy with the mineralized dead, and the new faces forming in the western cliffs—some of them belonging to people who have not yet been born, some belonging to travelers who have not yet arrived, and one, newly emerging, that is unmistakably my own.

The power does not speak in sound. It speaks by *completing*.

Every unfinished memory in the black basalt suddenly resolves. Half-carved faces gain their missing eyes. Tools gain handles. Animals that had been only suggestion leap forward in low relief as though stepping out of the stone into three dimensions for a single heartbeat before settling again, perfected. The Kiln-born gasp in soft choral clicks; some fall to their knees in the running water, not in worship but in recognition. Their own living memories calm. The patterns on their skin stop migrating and settle into final, gentle designs, as though they had only ever been waiting for permission to finish growing.

Then the power turns its attention to the swarms.

All the glittering lattices collapse at once into a single vast spiral that spins around the widened shaft like a galaxy made of ice and mirrors. For a long time the only sound is the new, deliberate dripping. Then the spiral tightens, compresses, and pours itself into the shallow depression where infants are given their second birth. The depression, worn by centuries, suddenly brims not with water but with living light. The translucent and mirror-bright creatures fuse, melt, re-form. When they rise again they are no longer hundreds of thousands of separate bodies.

They are one creature the size of a modest house, winged, faceted, and breathing. Its wings are made of frozen memory. Its eyes are every quartz eye the Kiln-born have ever possessed. It looks once at the power, lowers its vast faceted head in what might be gratitude or farewell, and then dissolves back into the swarms. But they are changed. Now, when they braid themselves together, faint images flicker inside the lattices: entire lifetimes, futures, possible children, possible deaths. The Kiln-born understand at once that the swarms have become the Kiln’s dreaming organ.

The power does one last thing.

It reaches—not with hands, but with the pure act of attention—into the geothermal wound. The ancient wound that has breathed scalding vapor for uncounted centuries. It strokes the wound the way one might stroke the cheek of a fevered child. The heat does not vanish. Instead it softens, ripens, becomes something that can be *sung* with. From now on, when the vapors rise, they will carry not only voices from deeper down, but actual melodies—low, patient, choral things made of overlapping trickles and soft clicks. The listeners will no longer need to boil themselves half-blind to hear the Kiln decide. The decision will simply arrive, sung, on warm mist.

Finally, the power looks directly at the newest memory forming on the western cliffs. The one that is my face.

It leans forward, as though whispering something only stone and water can hear. The half-finished relief of my face shivers. The features soften further. The expression changes from the patient, glistening watching I had expected into something gentler—almost amused, almost tender. The insects tending it pause in their clicking labor, then redouble their efforts, as though now they are not merely growing a memory but polishing a beloved thing.

The power begins to withdraw.

As it does, every drop of water suspended in the glittering air falls at once. For three full heartbeats the Weeping Kiln is utterly silent. No dripping. No clicking. No breath. Then the water begins again, but the rhythm is new. It is the rhythm of something that has chosen what it will become next, and is no longer afraid of the choosing.

The widened shaft of light narrows once more to its ancient spear. Yet something of the power remains behind, the way scent remains in wet moss. The Kiln-born rise from where they knelt. Their quartz eyes are calm. Some are weeping, but the tears are simply added to the constant weeping of the stone and no one can tell the difference.

One of them—the one they call Left-Hand-Rust—steps forward until the edge of the shaft-light touches the living memory patterns now permanently settled across her collarbones. She looks up toward the aperture where the power has already gone, and speaks in the new, softened voice the Kiln now owns.

“Thank you for finishing us,” she says.

Then she turns, walks to the western cliffs, and lays her long clever fingers against the relief of my face. She traces the new, gentler expression the power left there.

“Ah,” she murmurs, the way they always do. “There you are. We’ve been growing you for some time.”

And high above, in the cold bright air outside the aperture, something that is not quite a smile passes across the face of the power before it folds itself back into ordinary sunlight, content that the Weeping Kiln is no longer only deciding.

It has begun to remember how to love what it decides to become.
