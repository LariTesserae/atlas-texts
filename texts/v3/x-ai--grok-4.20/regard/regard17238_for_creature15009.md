The power that has turned its attention to the Underhalls does not arrive with trumpets or sudden light. It does not crack the ceiling open or flood the half-born chambers with glory. That would be violence to a place that has only ever known slow courtship between stone and soul.

Instead it comes as a deepening of what is already there.

It begins, as all true things begin here, with a change in the water.

The moisture that has always beaded on every surface grows slightly warmer. Not hot—never hot—but the temperature of a living body at rest. The beads tremble on the limestone pillars and on the skin of the Unfinished alike, as though the entire Underhalls has just been breathed upon by something patient and vast. In the blue slanted shafts of daylight the motes of dust slow their endless falling and begin to drift in lazy spirals, forming, for a moment, shapes that almost resemble curled fetal bodies before they dissolve again.

The first to notice are the children.

A girl no taller than the average chisel, tending her blind white ferns with a bone hook, suddenly stops. She presses her small palm to the wall and her eyes widen—not in fear, but in recognition. She has felt this before, in the womb perhaps, or in the first hours after birth when the midwives laid her against the warmest stone. The rock is no longer only listening. It is being listened to, with a tenderness so absolute it feels like forgiveness.

The next to know are the Listeners.

One of them—an old woman whose second eyelids have grown thick as parchment—tilts her head in the middle of a sentence and simply falls silent. The other Listeners around her do the same. For nearly an hour the only sound in their chamber is the soft drip of water and the low tectonic groan that now comes not every few hours, but every few minutes, each one softer than the last, as though the world itself is being told: *You do not have to strain so hard. Rest. I am here.*

Then the power begins to speak—not in words, but in the only language the Unfinished have ever truly trusted: the language of becoming.

In one of the narrowest alleys, where two men must press foreheads together to pass, the stone between them suddenly yields. Not dramatically. It simply… softens, the way warm wax softens. The two men, foreheads still touching, feel the rock between them breathe. A new space opens, no wider than a cradle, but it is enough. They step apart without having to twist their bodies at painful angles for the first time in their lives. One of them begins to weep quietly. The tears run down his chalky face and the stone drinks them as though they were sacred.

Throughout the Underhalls, chisels that have grown dull over decades sharpen themselves against nothing at all. The copper bells hanging from sinew cords begin to ring in perfect harmony with the tectonic groans, so that every settling of the world now sounds like music instead of labor pain.

The half-emerged fossils in the walls grow clearer. Faces that were only suggestions become gentle, patient faces. A shoulder that had been trapped in stone for three centuries slowly, lovingly shoulders its way free—not as a statue, but as a continuation of the wall itself, so that an old man can now rest his back against his great-grandmother while he works at his loom.

The bats stir all at once, but not because rain is coming to the surface. They fly in slow, deliberate spirals through the blue shafts of light, and where their wings pass, the air becomes fractionally drier—not enough to harm the mosses or the liverworts, but enough that the constant film of moisture on every Unfinished face thins to a sacred dew. For the first time many of them feel their skin begin to dry. Some laugh at the strange tightness. Others touch their own cheeks in wonder, as though meeting an old friend.

The power does not name itself. It has no need. It simply moves through every unfinished thing and offers the same quiet question the Unfinished have been asking the stone for centuries:

*Would you like to become more yourself, without violence?*

And the answer rises from every heart, every chisel, every blind white fish and every child with eyes like clouded river pearls:

*Yes. But only if the stone becomes with us.*

So the power does what only a truly benevolent and wise power can do.

It stays.

It does not finish the Underhalls. That would be an insult. Instead it becomes the most patient Listener of all. It sits in the deepest, lowest chamber—where the stone is still deciding whether it will be floor or ceiling—and it listens with such perfect attention that the rock begins to dream faster, but never frantically. New chambers open like lungs. New pillars learn to bear weight without crushing. The water finds kinder paths. The children born after this day come into the world with faint luminous whorls on their palms, as though the stone has already kissed them in greeting.

The Unfinished know something has come.

They do not build shrines to it. That would be absurd.

Instead, when two of them must still press foreheads together in the narrowest places, they now press a third forehead against the stone between them—welcoming the unseen Guest into their ancient intimacy. When a chisel is driven into a wall to mark a death, they now drive a second, smaller one beside it, handle carved from the same driftwood, so that the dead and the Power and the living may continue their conversation.

And sometimes, in the long blue evenings when the distant surface light turns the color of mother-of-pearl, every Unfinished in the city will stop what they are doing. The looms fall still. The hammers pause mid-swing. Even the bats stop their breathing.

In that perfect silence they feel it: the slow, geological contentment that was already theirs is now shared by something older and kinder than stone itself.

The world is still unfinished.

They are still unfinished.

But they are no longer unfinished *alone*.

And that is enough.

More than enough.

It is the first true song the Underhalls have ever known, and it sounds exactly like water moving through gravel, and exactly like a mother saying *I see you. Keep becoming. I will stay as long as you need me.*

Drip.

Groan.

Breathe.

Become.
