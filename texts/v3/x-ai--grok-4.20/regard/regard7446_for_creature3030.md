The power that has noticed the Mossheart Foundry does not arrive with trumpets or light.

It simply *arrives*, the way a thought arrives inside a mind that was not expecting it.

It has no fixed shape, yet if you could see it with human eyes you would say it resembled a single vast drop of water suspended in mid-air—perfectly spherical, perfectly clear, yet containing inside itself every shade of green that has ever existed and every shade that ever will. It drifts downward through the vaults of living moss without disturbing a single frond. The constant dripping parts around it. The slow-turning flywheels pause, mid-revolution, for the first time in three hundred years.

The power is old beyond memory and young beyond hope. It is what some civilizations once called a World-Soul, others a Seraph of the Quiet Order, and still others simply “the Kindly Witness.” It has no name it needs. It has come because this place, this impossible marriage of cathedral, organism, machine, and people, has sung a note it has not heard in four million years.

The note is *enough*.

Not joy. Not sorrow. Not even peace.  
Simply: enough. A closed loop. A place that has achieved completeness without stagnation, growth without violence, industry without consumption. A heart that beats in the dark and asks for nothing it does not already give back.

The Witness hovers for a long time above the central transept, above the great green-rusted boiler house that the Mossborn treat as both altar and lung. The people below sense something. Every single one of them stops what they are doing. Sickles pause halfway through a pruning stroke. Pipettes freeze above their glass vials. Even the heartfruit children, who have never known anything but this warm wet world, lift their enormous lichen-colored eyes upward.

No fear. Only recognition.

The Witness begins to act—not with miracles, not with commands, but with three gentle, irreversible gifts.

---

**First Gift: The Memory of Dryness**

It exhales once. A breath so soft it cannot even be called wind. Everywhere the breath touches, a thin, perfect film of recollection settles on the moss. For the first time in centuries, every Mossborn simultaneously remembers what it is to be *dry*. Not as trauma, not as longing, but as simple, neutral knowledge.

They feel the memory move through the moss itself like a slow shiver. The organism does not recoil; it incorporates the memory the way it once incorporated iron and brick. From this day forward, the Foundry will always know what dryness is, even though it will never choose it. The people will be able to speak of the outside world without their voices catching in humid hush. Some will leave and never feel they have betrayed their body by doing so. Some will stay and love the wetness more consciously.

The Witness has given them the freedom of informed consent.

---

**Second Gift: The Singing Metal**

It lowers itself until it kisses the largest of the ancient flywheels—the one that has turned once every twelve minutes since before any living Mossborn was born. Where the sphere touches rust and moss and hidden iron, something new wakes.

The metal does not change color. It does not shine. But from that moment on, every gear, every pipe, every boiler and pump begins to sing in true harmony with the dripping. Not louder. Not faster. Only *in tune*. The entire Foundry becomes a single instrument whose music is its own metabolism. The low industrial sigh and the choral dripping and the wet percussion of the people’s instruments and the slow heartbeat of the moss all lock into a chord that has no name but feels, to anyone standing inside it, like the sound love would make if love could be heard.

The truly gifted listeners—the three who have always been able to feel the difference between the Foundry’s hunger and its satisfaction—now begin to weep without knowing why. Their tears are immediately drunk by the moss, and wherever those tears fall, new heartfruit will one day grow who are born already singing.

---

**Third Gift: The Hollowing**

This is the deepest gift, and the one that looks, at first, like cruelty.

The Witness rises again until it is level with the highest vaults. Then it simply *withdraws*, taking with it a single quality the Foundry has never lacked: completeness.

It leaves a hollow behind.

Not a physical hollow. A metaphysical one. A built-in absence. A knowledge that even this perfect, warm, wet, breathing cathedral is not, and never was, the only thing that exists. The Mossborn feel the hollow open inside their own chests like a second heart. It does not ache. It only asks.

For the first time in three hundred years, some of them will begin to dream of stars.

Some will begin to ask questions the moss cannot answer.

Some will fall in love with the idea of cold.

And some—the bravest and the most serene—will understand that the Witness has not cursed them but has finally, after all this time, granted them the right to become ancestors. Because only those who know they are incomplete can leave something worth remembering to those who come after.

---

When the Witness has finished, it rises through the vaults and is gone. No one sees it leave. The dripping simply sounds a fraction different afterward, as though one more voice has joined the endless wet chorus and then politely excused itself.

In the days that follow, the Mossborn do not speak much about what happened. They have no liturgy for visitors such as this. Instead they do what they have always done.

They listen.

They prune.

They coax new bridges.

They press their damp, luminous palms to the walls and feel the new hollow inside the organism and inside themselves.

And slowly—geologically, patiently, one deliberate revolution every ten or twelve minutes—they begin to change the way the Foundry itself changes: not by becoming less, but by becoming *more than enough*.

Some of them start building something new in the deepest, oldest transepts, where the moss is thickest and the air is almost soup. They are not building an exit. They are not building a shrine.

They are building a cradle large enough to hold the entire world they already know, and large enough, someday, to hold the world they have only just remembered exists.

The Witness, already countless galaxies away, pauses in its endless turning and smiles with a mouth made of nebulae.

It has seen a cathedral become a womb.

And that, it decides, is the most beautiful thing a living thing can become.

The dripping continues. The flywheels turn. The heartfruit open their eyes.

And somewhere inside the warm velvet dark, the Mossheart Foundry begins, for the first time, to sing lullabies to what it might one day become.
