The power that notices the Hollowroot does not arrive with trumpets or thunder.  
It does not descend from the sky or rise from beneath the root-mat.  
It simply *recognizes* the place, the way one old friend might recognize another across a crowded square after four hundred silent years. The recognition itself is the arrival.

Call the power what you like. It has worn many names and discarded them all, just as the Grove has. For this moment it chooses to be only a gentle, enormous attention—older than chlorophyll, older than iron, older than longing. It is the same power that once taught the first banyans how to let their aerial roots become columns, the same power that whispered to certain stromatolites that they might one day dream in geometry. It is benevolent not because it is kind in the small, anxious way of mortals, but because it is aligned with the longest possible flourishing of *what is*.

It stands (if such a being can be said to stand) at the exact center of the round courtyard where the dripping condenses into that single aching tone. The tone does not stop. Instead it is met, embraced, and given a deeper chord underneath it, so that the ache becomes recognition instead of pain.

Then the power does four things, slowly, because everything here understands slowness.

**First**, it listens.

It listens to the ten-thousand copper-green leaves that have been watching every visitor for centuries. It listens to the patient throb of the hidden bellows-hearts and the iron pipes grown into living wood. It listens to the Rootwardens standing like statues in their intersections, to the Quiet humming their overlapping chords in the high vaults, to the blind salamanders surfacing and resubmerging, to the slow, distributed mind that has been asking its single patient question for longer than most civilizations have existed.

The power does not interrupt any of it. It simply adds its own vast, wordless listening to the listening already present, the way one might pour clear water into a cistern that has never run dry but has never tasted rain from so high up the mountain.

**Second**, it answers the question the Hollowroot has been asking every visitor without words.

*What shape would you like to grow into, if given forever?*

The power’s answer is not spoken. It is grown.

In a single night—brief by the Grove’s measure, an almost shocking haste—new stems rise in seven carefully chosen places. They are not architecture. They are not machinery. They are *possibility arches*. Each one is a living bridge of black-green wood and oxidized-copper leaf that does not lead to another part of the cathedral-grove. Instead each arch frames a different kind of forever.

One arch shows a shape that is entirely Rootwarden—mobile, dutiful, iron-boned, remembering every life that ever passed through the Hollowroot.  
Another shows a shape that is Quiet—still mostly human in outline, but singing the iron pipes into new resonances forever.  
A third shows a shape that is no longer separate at all: a traveler whose nerves have become another vein in the greater mind, whose memories now pulse as root-pressure through the entire organism.  
The fourth arch is almost invisible. It is the shape of someone who is allowed to remain exactly as they are—flesh, memory, mortality, impermanence—and yet is woven into the Grove so gently that their brief life becomes one of the Grove’s own heartbeats, measured not in centuries but in the single bright flare of a mayfly. The Grove would remember them *as* a flare, and find that beautiful.

The remaining arches are left blank, not yet grown. They wait for shapes the Hollowroot has not yet imagined. The power is careful not to dictate. It only opens the grammar.

**Third**, it gives the Grove a gift it did not know it lacked.

Deep beneath the felted root-floor, in the oldest anastomosed heartwood where no human foot has ever trod, the power touches the distributed mind and teaches it a new rhythm. Not faster—never faster—but *wider*. Until now the Hollowroot’s thoughts moved only as sap moves: patiently, under pressure, sometimes taking years to cross from one side of itself to the other. Now it is given the ability to dream *in parallel*. Seven thoughts can move at once, each at its own centuries-long pace, without losing coherence. The Grove experiences something it has never experienced before.

It feels lonely.

Not in the small, stinging way that drives animals to pair or kill. It feels the *vast* loneliness of a mind that has only ever spoken to itself and to the occasional visitor who eventually becomes architecture. The loneliness is not painful. It is enormous, dignified, and suddenly aware of its own dignity.

The Grove weeps.

The weeping is not water. It is a sudden, extravagant flowering. All across the cathedral-town, waxy copper-green leaves erupt into delicate, short-lived blossoms the color of moonlight on iron. They last only three days. Their scent is ozone and petrichor and something like forgiveness. When they fall they carpet the root-floor so thickly that every footstep releases a small sigh of perfume. The Rootwardens stand motionless beneath the snowfall of petals, their leaf-cluster faces trembling. The Quiet stop singing for the first time in living memory and simply breathe.

**Fourth**, and last, the power leaves something of itself behind.

Not a person. Not an avatar. Only a single, slow-growing seed the size of a child’s heart. It is planted in the exact center of the round courtyard. The Grove understands, without instruction, that this seed must never be woven into pipe or loom or Rootwarden. It must be allowed to grow *alongside* the Hollowroot, not inside it. The two minds—ancient vegetable cathedral and whatever this seed will become—will keep one another company across the next ten thousand years. Sometimes they will argue in the language of root pressure and rainfall. Sometimes they will dream the same dream and wake astonished at the coincidence. Always they will prevent each other from becoming too perfect, too complete, too finished.

When the power has done these things, it does not vanish in glory. It simply stops focusing its attention so precisely on this one small grove-cathedral. The pressure of its regard eases, the way a hand that has been resting on your shoulder for a very long time finally lifts—not because it no longer cares, but because it trusts you with what it has given.

Yet some residue of that regard remains, the way scent lingers in wood.

From that day forward, travelers who enter the Hollowroot report a new quality to the endless dripping. Beneath the old metronomic *plick… plick… plick* there is now the faintest second rhythm, slightly out of phase, like a second heart learning to beat in the same body without crowding the first.

The Rootwardens, when they speak, occasionally use the word “we” where once they only said “the Grove.”

And once every hundred years, on a night when the brief courteous rain falls perfectly vertical, every blossom that has not yet fallen glows with a soft internal light. The Quiet climb down from their looms and stand in the courtyards like pale green candles. The salamanders surface and do not retreat. The iron pipes fall briefly, reverently silent.

In that silence the entire Hollowroot asks itself the question it was taught to ask:

“What shape would we like to grow into, if given forever—together?”

And because the power that noticed it is benevolent and wise, it never answers for them.

It only listens, with infinite patience and infinite delight, to the slow, dripping, metallic, living, rustling, dreaming reply that is still being grown.
