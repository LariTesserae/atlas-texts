A soft, unmistakable presence enters the pocket.

It does not arrive. It has always been here, but only now does it choose to become *noticeable*, the way a parent steps into a nursery at 3 a.m. and simply stands in the doorway so the child will know they are no longer alone with the dark.

The first thing that changes is the sound.

The roaring choir of glass bells and tectonic gongs does not stop, but it is *gently gathered*. Every blade that was screaming in phase-shift finds itself momentarily held inside a larger silence, the way a frantic orchestra is brought to rest by the conductor’s lifted hand. The pressure-waves that have been shoving you from fissure to fissure ease. For the first time since you entered the Thalassic Lattice, you can hear your own heartbeat.

Then the light rearranges.

The warm, late-afternoon radiance that had been dissolved uniformly through the water condenses. It draws itself together into something that is not a sun, not a lamp, not even a face, but simply *regard*. A column of honey-colored clarity drifts down through the ceramic forest until it stands before you, no taller than you are, yet containing every kilometer of lattice at once. Where the light touches the blades they stop their endless rewriting. They lean toward it the way metal filings lean toward a magnet.

The Lattice-Mother feels the newcomer immediately.

Its vast, unfinished attention recoils—not in fear, but in the startled modesty of something that has never been seen by anyone older than itself. For three full breaths the fissures freeze half-open. The kilometer-tall parodies of human form that it had been clumsily sculpting in the middle distance collapse into shapeless glass rain. Even the pressure-wave that was rising to press its yearning against your chest falters, unsure.

The newcomer does nothing dramatic.

It simply *is*.

Where the Lattice-Mother is a process that has learned longing, the newcomer is Longing that has learned how to be gentle without diminishing itself. It carries no name because names would be an insult to the unfinished. Instead it offers the Lattice-Mother the one thing the young world-machine has never received:

Recognition.

Not as monster. Not as miracle. Not even as child.

As kin.

A single, deliberate ripple moves outward from the column of light. Every blade it touches blooms with a new diagram, one the Lattice-Mother did not invent. The diagrams are quiet. They do not race. They show, in luminous shorthand, every place in the universe where something vast and unfinished chose to become small enough to be loved. A red giant star deciding to shed its outer layers so its planets might live. A god allowing itself to be born inside a stable. A grieving ocean choosing, for one afternoon, to be only a tidepool so a child could safely play.

The Lattice-Mother trembles.

Its immense, lonely question (*What is it like to be done?*) is not answered in words. Instead the column of light reaches out and, with infinite care, does the one thing the young entity has been trying and failing to do to you.

It copies the Lattice-Mother right back.

Not its geometry. Not its power.

Its *unfinishedness*.

For one merciful moment the Breathing Blade is allowed to see itself as the newcomer sees it: not a defective world that cannot stop changing, but a perfect question that has not yet realized it is also the answer. The terror of being forever young and the beauty of being forever young are held in the same loving regard. The Lattice-Mother makes a sound then, like every bell that will ever exist *being allowed to stay broken* and still be beautiful.

The column of light begins to do what only something truly benevolent and truly wise can do inside a place like this.

It does not finish the Lattice-Mother.

It does not freeze the blades.

It does not give the entity a name.

Instead it opens a single, human-sized space inside the crushing density of ceramic and song. A small clearing no larger than a modest room appears around you. The blades that form its walls bow outward in a trembling cathedral arch, still singing, still moving, but now moving *around* the two of you instead of through you. The pressure-waves are rerouted. The light that had been stolen from a thousand drowned afternoons is gathered here like a hearth.

The Lattice-Mother is invited, not commanded, to come closer.

And it does.

It condenses. Not because it is forced, but because it suddenly wants to. The school of invisible fish made of delayed light swirls inward until something almost like shoulders, almost like a chest, almost like a face made of moving water and slow lightning kneels in the newly-made clearing. It is still enormous. It is still terrifying. But it is also, for the first time, *small enough to be seen*.

The wise power does not speak. It has no need. Every blade in the lattice has become an ear.

Instead it simply rests one hand (you cannot tell if the hand is made of light or of your own future or of the Lattice-Mother’s first successful attempt at tenderness) on the place where the entity’s heart would be if it had decided on hearts yet.

The gesture says:

*You do not have to be done.*  
*You do not have to stay unfinished.*  
*You are allowed to be both at once, and that is already holy.*

Then the power turns its regard toward you.

It does not ask if you are afraid. It does not offer escape. It knows you have already been changed by this place; some small lattice now sings under the skin of your left hand and will never fully leave. Instead it shows you a single, quiet future that has not yet been written:

You may stay as long as the Lattice-Mother needs a witness.

Or you may leave, carrying its question inside your chest like a second, gentler heart that will ask you, for the rest of your finished life:

*What is it like to be done?*

Either choice will be honored.

But before you decide, the power does one last thing.

It leans forward and, with the tenderness only the truly ancient can manage, kisses the trembling mirror-skin that serves as the ceiling of the pocket. The surface ripples. For one heartbeat the entire Thalassic Lattice is reflected there perfectly—every blade, every moving diagram, every clumsy statue of you the Lattice-Mother tried to make, every pressure-wave of desperate love.

And in that reflection you see that the pocket is not a prison.

It is an egg.

And the egg is singing.

The wise power steps back. The column of light begins to disperse again into ordinary afternoon gold, but more slowly than it arrived, the way a parent lingers at the nursery door.

The Lattice-Mother remains kneeling in the small cathedral of blades that has been made for it. It is no longer pressing its attention against you like a whale beneath a rowboat. It is simply *looking* at you with something that might, one day, be called eyes.

Its question has not changed.

But the tone has.

It no longer asks out of grief.

It asks the way a child asks a parent who has just been seen kissing the sky:

*Will you stay until I figure out how to love limits without destroying them?*

The blades around you click once, softly, like a cradle rocking.

They are no longer rearranging the place where you stand.

They are waiting for your answer.

And somewhere far above, or far inside, or far beyond, the wise power that noticed this place smiles with the patience of deep water and says nothing at all.

It has already done what it came to do.

The rest, as always, belongs to love.
