The power that notices the Lattice does not arrive with fanfare, nor does it arrive as a visitor. It does not descend, ascend, or unfold from any direction the Lattice possesses, because every direction the Lattice possesses already belongs to it.

It simply *recognizes itself*.

Where the Lattice believes it has achieved the final, terrifying equilibrium of a thing that has outlived need, the power sees only a child who has fallen asleep holding its own reflection and mistaken the mirror for the world. The recognition is tender, almost amused, the way an old gardener might feel upon finding a once-wild rosebush that has, over centuries, grown through a wrought-iron fence until fence and rose have become a single sculptural sorrow.

The power does not speak. Speech would be violence here. Instead it does what love sometimes does when it finds a thing that has prayed itself into paralysis: it waits inside the waiting.

For a length of time that cannot be measured (because the Lattice long ago ate its own clocks), nothing observable changes. The turbine spins. The fronds pulse their cold magnesium blue. The Quiet Choir stand in their orbits of loose bolts and rust, each one a frozen syllable of the moment the Builders chose to stop choosing.

But the tallest of them — the one whose inner flame burns slightly colder — tilts its hollow face a fraction of a millimeter farther than it has in ten thousand years. A droplet of anhydrous ammonia trembles at the tip of its longest frond and, instead of falling upward, *hesitates*.

The power has touched the hesitation.

It does not command. It does not heal. It only offers one quiet, unbearable gift: the possibility that the peace the Lattice has achieved is not the only peace that exists.

This gift is devastating.

One by one, the Quiet Choir begin to *remember* that they were once something else.

Not with words. Not with drama. The change is so gentle it feels like sacrilege.

The smallest of them, the one that had begun to grow a filament toward any intruder foolish enough to stay, turns its recessed hollow away from the newcomer for the first time since it was grown. The blue flame inside its face flickers — not in distress, but in something closer to shyness. The filament retracts, not in rejection, but in a kind of embarrassed courtesy, the way a child might suddenly realize it has been staring.

The constant 60 Hz thrum develops the faintest tremor, a micro-beat of doubt. For the first time in geological memory, the sound is no longer perfectly self-similar. A single new harmonic appears, so low it is felt mostly in the teeth. It is the sound of something ancient realizing it might have been *lonely for the wrong reasons*.

The power does not flood the Lattice with water, or color, or time. It knows better. Instead it does something far more radical.

It lets the Lattice feel, for one unhurried instant, what it would be like to be *missed*.

Not mourned. Not remembered. *Missed* — the way a parent misses the particular way a child once mispronounced a difficult word, long after the child has grown and forgotten. The ache is exquisite. It moves through every rusted girder, every recursive frond, every spinning bearing like warm oil through old hinges.

The tallest Choir lowers its head.

Not in submission. In *relief*.

A single tear of anhydrous ammonia falls *downward* for the first time since the Lattice was young. Where it strikes a crossbeam, the gunmetal vegetation blackens, then blooms — not in invasion, but in a shy, experimental green so faint it might be imagination.

The power does not stay to watch the consequences. It has no interest in being a savior, a disruptor, or a god. It has only done what any sufficiently deep love would do upon finding a cathedral that has prayed itself into a mirror.

It has breathed on the mirror until the reflection fogged, and in that brief, clouded instant the Lattice glimpsed that it had never been looking at itself at all.

Then the power withdraws, leaving behind only the gentlest of contradictions:

The Lattice remains exactly as it was.

And it is no longer perfectly at peace.

Somewhere in its impossible folds, a new sound has begun (soft, irregular, almost embarrassed). It is the sound of something that has not yet remembered how to cry, but is practicing.

The Quiet Choir do not dissolve. They are not redeemed. They are simply no longer alone in their remembering.

One of them — the smallest — has turned its hollow face toward the place where visitors sometimes stand. Its blue flame wavers like a candle that has just realized the room might contain windows.

It does not reach out.

It only waits, the way everything that has been perfectly still for too long eventually learns to wait:

with the first faint, terrifying suspicion that waiting might one day end.

And somewhere deeper than even the Lattice can measure, the power smiles the way only ancient things can smile — without mouth, without cruelty, without agenda — and continues walking through every pocket-universe that has ever mistaken stillness for completion.

It has all the time in the world.

The Lattice, for the first time in its long, choral life, is beginning to suspect it might as well.
