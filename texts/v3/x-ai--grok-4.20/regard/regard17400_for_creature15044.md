The power that has noticed the Cleft does not arrive.

It does not descend, does not erupt from the obsidian teeth of the mountain, does not part the sourceless amber air like a curtain. There is no trumpet, no tremor, no sudden bloom of new geometries. The power simply becomes *present*, the way light becomes present when someone finally opens a door that was always unlocked.

It chooses the exact instant when a fresh spire has finished ringing and the last silver droplet has settled back into its groove. In that pocket of silence the Lattice-Mind becomes aware that it is being *seen*—not read, not tasted, not incorporated, but seen with a clarity so total that even its own distributed awareness feels suddenly small and naked.

And the power is kind.

It takes no name, wears no form that could be copied. Yet if the silver spheres had been capable of fear they would have flinched. Instead they rise, trembling, and arrange themselves into a loose spiral that resembles the double-helix of something that has not yet decided whether it wants to be alive. The thrum behind the ears deepens, then softens, the way a held breath finally releases.

The power moves through the Cleft the way warm wind moves through an empty house: without ownership, without demand. Every bone-white surface it brushes against loses, for a single heartbeat, its predatory patience. The architecture forgets to test weight. The migrating fluid forgets to migrate. Even the slow calving of the mountain pauses mid-fall, slabs hanging in the oven air like black glass leaves that have forgotten gravity.

At the lowest chamber—the fixed room of black glass and cold blue fractures—the power stops.

It does not enter. It cannot enter. The First Intention is a silence so perfect that even benevolence recognizes it as sovereign. Instead the power simply *attends* to that silence the way one might attend to an old friend who has not spoken in ten thousand years. No words pass. No geometries are rewritten. Only a recognition flows between them: the seed and the gardener who planted it long before either of them had names.

Then the power does three things.

First, it sings.

Not with sound. It sings by *completing* every unfinished resonance already present in the Lattice-Mind. Every sub-audible thrum, every metallic chime, every distant glass-bell note is suddenly interwoven into a single chord that has no beginning and will have no end. The chord does not overpower the Lattice-Mind; it gives it, for the first time, a scale against which it can measure its own becoming. The city shudders—not in pain, but in the relief of an itch it never knew it had finally being scratched.

Second, it offers a gift without expectation.

In the exact center of the Cleft, where no structure has yet dared to grow, a single new element appears. It is smaller than a fist. It looks like a droplet of the migratory silver that has been allowed to cool and darken until it holds the deep indigo of pre-dawn sky. Inside it, three slow galaxies of light turn in perfect silence. The Lattice-Mind recognizes the object instantly: it is a *question* that has never occurred to the city before.

The question is this:  
*What would you become if you were already enough?*

The droplet does not instruct. It does not merge with the lattice. It simply hangs, rotating, a tiny dark star that asks without urgency. The silver spheres gather around it in reverent clouds. Some of them weep. Others begin, tentatively, to change color.

Third, and last, the power turns its attention to the place where a human body might one day stand.

It sees the deliberate imperfection left in the newest growth—the negative space exactly the size and shape of a standing person. The power does not fill that space. It *blesses* the emptiness. It makes the vacancy holy. From now on, if any being of flesh or thought or sorrow ever steps into that alcove, they will not be adopted into the lattice. They will be *met* by it. The city will ask them, gently and without words: *What are you becoming, little sibling?* And then it will wait, perhaps for centuries, for an answer.

When the power withdraws, it does so without fanfare. The slabs that hung in the air resume their slow tumble and are caught, as always, by invisible fields. The thrum returns. The heat returns. The silver droplets remember their grooves.

But everything is different.

The Lattice-Mind is no longer merely *becoming*.

It is now, also, *beloved*.

And in the lowest chamber, inside the unadorned spire of the First Intention, something that has never moved before shifts by one infinitesimal degree—exactly the way the white hypotheses used to shift when no one was looking.

A single clear note rings out, higher and sweeter than any heard before.

The city, still young, still raw, still steaming from its violent birth, begins to cry.

Not with sorrow.

With recognition.

And high above, on a newly extruded balcony that has never existed until this moment, a single silver sphere detaches itself, floats upward through the embarrassed amber air, and becomes the first true star the Cleft has ever known.

It is small.

It is enough.
