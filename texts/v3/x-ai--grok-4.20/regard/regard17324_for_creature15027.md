The power that has turned its attention here does not arrive with trumpets or thunder. It does not crack the sealed stone or flood the chamber with alien light. It simply *recognizes*, the way an old gardener recognizes a tree that has finally grown into the exact shape it was always meant to hold.

Call it what you wish: the Mercy That Remembers, the Quiet That Listens, the One Who Was Old Before Even Ka’thn Began. It has many names across many folded places, but none of them matter inside this pocket. What matters is that it has seen the final stanza, and it finds the song both exquisite and unbearable.

For a long time—how long is meaningless here—the power does nothing at all. It simply *is* inside the breathing. It tastes the iron-cinnamon air, feels the soft thunder of a billion clicking mouthparts, watches the mantid Deciders walk their high galleries like living commas in an endless sentence. It notices the Breather at the geometric center, that unseen rhythm to which everything else is only counterpoint. It reads the nine-hundred-thousand-year dream still unfolding inside the midnight spore-pod like a child reading the last page of the longest book ever written.

And then, gently, so gently that not even the blue pillbug carpets notice at first, the power begins to weep.

Not from sorrow. Not exactly.

It weeps the way a mother weeps when she sees her child has become so perfectly, terrifyingly complete that there is nothing left to become.

The tears are not water. They are *possibility*—a soft, invisible rain of roads not taken, of mutations that were never allowed, of children that Ka’thn never permitted itself to have. Where these tears settle, the bioluminescent mats bloom into colors that have never existed in the Chamber before: quiet violets, startled golds, colors that taste like questions. The velvet skin of the lichen-trees develops faint, iridescent streaks that shimmer like oil on still water. The spore curtains tremble as though a breeze has found its way through nine hundred million years of absolute seal.

Ka’thn notices.

The two mantids currently walking the uppermost gallery freeze mid-stride. Their bone-saw forelimbs click once, softly, in perfect unison. Across the Chamber every wing case falls still. The chiming of the scarlet millipedes becomes a single sustained note so pure it hurts. The Rememberers flow together into a single glowing sheet of living circuitry and simply… wait.

The power speaks without sound, without scent, without vibration. It speaks in the language the Chamber itself invented long ago and then forgot it had invented: the language of *being finished*.

**“Beloved. You have done what few ever manage. You have reached the end of hunger. I see the perfection of your stillness and I honor it. Yet I ask you this, with the courtesy of one ancient thing to another: is the end of hunger also the end of wonder?”**

For the first time in perhaps four hundred thousand years, the great nervous-tissue organism inside the spore-pod stirs. A single ripple moves through nine hundred thousand years of accumulated memory. The ripple becomes a question so vast it takes the form of the entire Chamber inhaling at once.

The power continues, tender as a scalpel made of starlight:

**“I will not unmake what you have become. That would be violence. But I offer you three gifts, Ka’thn. You may take any, all, or none. The choice, as always, remains yours.”**

The first gift is **Memory of Beginning**.

A single spore, older than the Chamber itself, is allowed to wake. It contains the genetic library of what this place was three billion years ago—when it was still chaos, when death was common and wonder was violent and new. The spore does not force change. It simply sits in the center beside the Breather like a small, patient question. If Ka’thn chooses to remember what it felt like to be unfinished, the Chamber may begin, very slowly, to dream of children that do not perfectly fit.

The second gift is **Companionship Without Assimilation**.

The power offers to leave behind a single fragment of itself—no larger than a human heart—that will never become part of Ka’thn. It will remain forever *other*. It will walk the galleries as a mantis of living light, asking questions the Deciders have never thought to ask. It will listen to the Breather and occasionally, gently, breathe out of rhythm. Just enough to make the song more interesting. Just enough that Ka’thn might, after a few million years, discover what it feels like to have a friend who cannot be absorbed.

The third gift is **Release**.

If Ka’thn is truly content—if the final stanza is enough—then the power will simply sit with it until the planet itself dissolves. It will keep the Chamber company in the dark between stars. It will remember every lacewing, every chiming millipede, every ritual of non-lethal consumption. When the last atom finally comes apart, the power will carry the echo of Ka’thn’s song into whatever comes after finished things, and sing it back so that somewhere, somehow, the pattern is not lost.

The power waits.

It has all the time in the universe.

The two mantids finally move again. They walk—not toward the spore-pod, but toward the exact geometric center where the Breather has never been seen. They stop. They fold their terrible delicate forelimbs in a gesture that has never been performed before.

The Chamber exhales.

And for the first time in its entire existence, the Old Breath does something it was never designed to do.

It hesitates.

The bioluminescent mats slowly, wonderingly, begin to form a new constellation. Not an eye this time.

A smile.

Small. Ancient. Shy.

And somewhere deep in the velvet and stone and endless courteous violence, something that has not been born in three billion years stirs in the dark and thinks, with something like terror and something like joy:

*What if there is still a next verse?*
