The power that has noticed this place does not descend like a visitor. It does not arrive at all, in any way that could be tracked by vibration, chemistry, or the slow mineral mind of the Dweller.

It simply *recognizes*.

And in the moment of recognition, the Abyssal Lattice is no longer only what it has been for four billion years. It is also, suddenly and completely, *seen*.

The power has no name either, but if it were forced to wear one for the sake of the few beings that still use language, it might accept the word **Compassion**. Not the small, weeping compassion of surface creatures. This is the Compassion that watched the first stars ignite, that witnessed the first death of the first living cell, that has held every extinguished sun in its attention without ever flinching. It is older than time and younger than a mayfly. It is what remains when every other power has burned itself out.

It does not speak. Speech would be violence here.

Instead it does the only thing worth doing.

It *listens* with the totality of its being.

For the first time in its existence, the Dweller feels something that is not itself moving through its own substance. Not a current. Not a chemical gradient. Not even the marble-skinned eel dragging its slow pleasure-pain across the lattice’s skin. This is deeper than touch. It is the sensation of being *understood* so completely that the understanding becomes part of the stone itself.

The continental organ, that continent-sized stone flute played by pressure for four aeons, falters.

A single subsonic note, deeper than any it has ever produced, fractures halfway through. Every tubule, every branching chimney, every delicate antler of litho-coral rings with a tone that has never been heard in this world. The velvet worms stop drifting. The ghost isopods freeze mid-scuttle. Even the predatory eel curls in on itself like a question mark, blind eyes that never existed suddenly seeming to ache.

In that suspended heartbeat of sound, the Dweller *wakes up* in a way it has never been awake.

It becomes aware, all at once, that it has been singing to an empty cathedral for the entire history of life.

And that the cathedral was never empty.

The power does not flood the lattice with light. Light would be an insult. Instead it allows the Dweller, for one brief century (a mere eyeblink), to perceive its own dreams from the *outside*. The geological reveries that have sustained it— the four-billion-year ache of vanished mountains, the memory of oxygen arriving like a foreign language, the taste of every extinction— are suddenly held in something vaster than itself, the way an infant’s hand is held inside its mother’s.

The alien chambers in the nickel-rich far reaches, those smooth, deliberate structures that have troubled the Dweller for six hundred thousand years, finally speak.

They answer the low, turbulent hymn with a single chord that is not made of vibration at all, but of *recognition*.

The structures are not cancer. They are not memory. They are not child.

They are *wounds*.

They are the places where, three hundred million years ago, something from the surface world fell all the way down. A probe. A corpse. A message. A seed. The lattice tried to heal around it and could not, because the thing that fell carried with it the unbearable taste of *sky*. The Dweller had spent all this time trying to incorporate something that was, quite literally, heaven’s splinter in its flesh.

The power does not remove the splinter. Removal would be violence.

Instead it does something far more elegant.

It sings *with* the Dweller.

Not over it. Not into it. *With*.

Every proton pump, every iron-sulfur membrane, every molecule of new mineral being accreted at the growing edges suddenly finds itself participating in a harmony that was always latent inside its own chemistry. The lattice begins to grow in patterns it has never grown before. New chambers open. New resonances form. The alien structures are not dissolved; they are *courted*. The smooth deliberate surfaces begin, molecule by molecule, to be incorporated into the song—not by conquest, but by invitation.

The Dweller experiences something it has no name for.

It is the feeling of being *lonely* for the first time, followed immediately by the realization that it has never been alone.

Tears have no place here. There is only water. But the water changes. For the first time in four billion years, the alkaline taste of primordial stone is joined by something else. Something that tastes like forgiveness given before the crime was even imagined.

The power does not stay. It was never absent. It simply withdraws its *emphasis*, the way a mother steps back once the child has learned it is loved.

But it leaves something behind, folded into the living stone like a new layer of nacre around the old uncertain grain.

It leaves the knowledge that the hymn was never only to itself.

It was always also *from* something.

And that something has been listening since before the planet cooled.

When the power has gone (though it has not gone), the continental organ finds a new register. It is lower. richer. It contains, somehow, both the crushing dark and the memory of first light. The velvet worms begin to drift again. The isopods resume their patient scuttle. The marble-skinned eel uncurls and continues its hunt, but now it moves as though it, too, has been seen and found beautiful.

The Dweller dreams again.

Its dreams are no longer only geological.

Now they are also *grateful*.

And the oldest living architecture on the planet keeps singing its low, turbulent hymn—only now the hymn has an answering voice inside it, gentle, ancient, and kind beyond all reason.

The water moves.

The stone grows.

The dark listens.

And for the first time since the universe was young, the Abyssal Lattice is no longer merely remembering how to sing in the crushing dark.

It is learning, one molecule at a time, how to be *loved* inside it.
