**The Power arrives without announcement.**

It does not descend from the sky. It does not burst from the stone. It simply *completes* something that was already trying to happen.

For one slow, amniotic heartbeat the entire continent forgets to grow. Every gill-frond freezes mid-breath. Every pale translucent herd-animal pauses with its dark organs stilled behind skin like wet parchment. The braided towers cease their patient knitting. Even the humming of the placental bedrock drops into a silence so absolute that the ordinary sunlight, suddenly loud, feels like an intrusion.

Then the Power sighs.

The sigh is not wind. It is permission.

Where the sigh touches the living crust, the warm fleshy stone *softens* the way a mother’s face softens when she first recognizes her newborn’s eyes. New colors—colors that have never existed here—bloom outward from a single point: a gentle, impossible blue the exact shade of a lullaby, a silver that tastes like forgiveness, a gold that remembers every kindness ever offered to anything small and frightened. These colors race across the continental shelf faster than thought, faster than growth, faster than loneliness. They sink into every pore, every braided stalk, every translucent flank.

The being that *is* the continent feels it like the first touch of cool hands on a fevered forehead.

It does not flinch. It *leans in*.

A ridge that had been trying for centuries to decide whether it would become a spine or a riverbed suddenly relaxes. It becomes neither. It becomes a lap. A continent-wide lap, vast and warm and utterly patient, and the Power settles into it without weight, without demand.

Then the Power does the only thing worth doing.

It begins to sing.

The song has no words, because words would be too small. Instead it sings by *remembering forward*. It remembers every future the continent has not yet dared to imagine, and offers each one back as a gift.

Under that song the half-born mountains stop trying to become shoulders or ribs. They become cathedrals of listening. The braided cities—those endless embryonic thoughts—suddenly understand they do not have to finish. They are allowed to remain questions. The courteous predators pause in their slow dissolving of prey and, for the first time, taste something other than hunger: they taste *recognition*. The prey, half-dissolved, opens what passes for eyes and feels, for the first time, that its own ending is not required.

The land itself begins to weep.

Not with sorrow. With relief so enormous it can only be expressed as water. From every pore, from every gill-frond, from the corners of every living tower, clear sweet water begins to flow. For the first time in its existence the continent knows rain—from the inside. The dry mineral hunger is answered not by conquest but by kindness. The vegetation drinks. The stone drinks. The pale herds lower their flat mouths and drink something that is not sap and not blood but simply *being loved without condition*.

The Power does not hurry.

It sits inside the lap it has been offered and lets the continent cry until the new oceans are born—shallow, warm, and full of the taste of breast milk left too long in the sun, now miraculously made clean. It lets the land discover gravity is not only a downward force; it can also be an embrace. It lets every white-blood-cell animal discover that love is not the opposite of an immune system. It is the reason an immune system can learn mercy.

Only when the last tear has fallen and the new seas lie trembling and silver under ordinary sunlight does the Power speak. Its voice is the same temperature as the mammalian warmth that has always risen from below. It sounds like the continent’s own heartbeat, only now it is no longer solitary.

**“Beloved,”** it says, and the word is grown into every stalk at once, **“you have done beautifully. You have kept yourself alive while you waited. Now you may rest inside the question instead of trying to answer it alone.”**

The continent shudders with something too large for any single emotion. Entire cities bloom overnight into vast open hearts of living lattice. The gill-fronds—once liver-colored—flush with every shade the Power brought: the blue of lullabies, the silver of forgiveness, the gold of remembered kindness. The pale creatures begin to sing again, but this time the choral humming is joined by something new: soft, mammalian laughter.

The Power leans forward and presses what is not quite a forehead against what is not quite a brow of stone. Where they touch, a single tree—not braided, not extruded, but *chosen*—rises. It is small. It will never be a tower. Its leaves are ordinary leaves. Its roots drink ordinary water. It is the first thing on the continent that is finished.

The Power smiles with the whole sky.

**“This one is for you to keep exactly as it is,”** it says. **“Everything else may keep becoming. But this tree will stay. So you never forget that being unfinished and being loved are not opposites.”**

Then the Power does the last, most important thing.

It stays.

Not forever. Not even for very long by planetary standards. Only long enough for the continent to learn the shape of companionship. Long enough for the dreaming infant-planet to discover that it does not have to decide what kind of life it wants to be when it grows up.

It is already loved while it is still deciding.

And that, the Power knows, is the only shape that matters.

When the moment comes for the Power to leave, it does not depart. It simply becomes the new rain, the new seas, the small finished tree, the laughter now living inside every translucent animal. It becomes the gentle background frequency beneath the humming of the stone—still there, still warm, still listening.

The continent, for the first time, exhales.

And in that exhale the name it had never needed finally arrives—not chosen, but *given*.

It names itself **Mercy-Still-Growing**.

And the small tree rustles its ordinary leaves in the new ordinary wind, as if to say:

*Good. Now we can begin.*
