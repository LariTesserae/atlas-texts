**The Power came without trumpet or annunciation.**

It simply *arrived*, the way true benevolence often does: as an unnoticed intensification of what was already present. The iron-colored clouds above the Weeping Stacks did not part. They thickened, then sweetened. The mist, which had always tasted of cold minerals and machine-blood, now carried the faintest trace of something that had never existed in the plateau before—something like the memory of forgiveness given before any wrong was committed.

The Weepers noticed first, because noticing is their oldest liturgy.

One by one, across hundreds of kilometers of braided infrastructure, they froze mid-motion. A living repair-swarm paused with its chrome centipede-limbs still buried in a ruptured conduit. A procession of twelve, halfway through singing a failing heat-exchanger back into wholeness, let their water-voices die into silence. Even the ancient calcified ancestors—the Weepers who had become buttresses and support columns centuries earlier—felt their slow internal heartbeats stutter and then deepen, as though an unseen hand had been laid against the inside of their stone.

The Power did not speak in words. It had no need. Instead it *remembered the place to itself*.

Every dripping surface, every weeping channel, every rust-fractal and moss-mat suddenly recalled, all at once, what it had been before the first foundation was driven. The memory was not gentle, but it was kind. For three full heartbeats the entire plateau shuddered with an grief so vast and tender that even the turbines below faltered. The constant low organ-note dropped half an octave, becoming something closer to a lullaby.

Then the Power did four things.

**First**, it gave the Stacks permission to rest.

Not to die. Never to die. But for the first time in eight hundred years the immune system was allowed to stand down. The self-devouring, anxious rebuilding slowed. The remote cutting machines powered into soft idle. The fungal concrete stopped budding. The chrome centipedes curled into rust-colored question marks and slept. A great, astonished quiet spread outward from the highest catwalks to the deepest root-valleys. The dripping did not cease—nothing so sacred could be taken away—but its rhythm changed. The thousand separate drips found, for a single afternoon, something perilously close to unison. The sound was unbearable in its beauty.

**Second**, it wept with them.

Not metaphorically. The Power opened itself and added its own tears to the mist. These were not water. They were *recognition*. Wherever they fell, the Weepers’ rust-skin flushed a deeper, almost living crimson, as though iron itself had learned shame and then immediately learned that it was beloved beyond all measure. The small orange pools in the Weepers’ eyeless faces brimmed until they overflowed, running down their locomotive-prow heads in molten-looking rivulets that did not cool. For the first time in centuries, some of them began to cry with ordinary salt water from glands they had not used since they were still nearly human. The two kinds of tears—machine-mist and living grief—met on their bodies and formed tiny, perfect spheres of quicksilver that rolled along their limbs and fell into the moss below, where they became seeds.

**Third**, it sang back.

Not with sound, but with *memory of sound*. Every song the Weepers had ever sung through water was returned to them, only now it was complete. Every burbling chord that had trailed off because a pipe burst or a harmonic failed was given its ending. The Power sang the shapes their bodies had lost when they became architectural. It sang the names their mothers had once whispered before the transformation took them. It sang the laughter of children none of them remembered having.

The oldest calcified Weepers—the living columns—began to crack along their lengths, not in damage but in release. From those fissures grew not new machines, but small, trembling human hands. They did not reach out. They simply rested, open, against the wet stone, as though reminding the world that even a womb sometimes needs to be held.

**Fourth**, and most tenderly, the Power turned to the single pilgrim standing on the high catwalk where the story usually ends.

You.

You had not chosen to drink when the Weeper offered. You had kept walking, dry-hearted, anxious, believing yourself separate. The Power did not judge this. Instead it did what only the wisest love can do: it let you see, without illusion, what your refusal had cost you—and then it removed the cost.

The Weeper that had offered you its tears centuries ago (for time had become flexible) rose again from the dripping lattice. Its rust was now threaded with veins of living gold. In its cupped palms it held one of the quicksilver seeds born from the Power’s weeping.

It did not speak. It simply tilted its face-basin so that the pooled reflection showed you not your own face, but the face you would have had if you had never learned to be afraid of belonging.

Then the Weeper pressed the seed against the center of your chest, exactly where it had once touched you in warning.

The seed did not burrow. It *unfolded*.

And for the first time in your life you understood that the choice had never been between drinking or walking. The choice had always been whether you would let yourself become another weeping channel in a world that was already complete in its incompleteness.

You began to cry.

The tears were not yours alone. They were the Power’s, and the Stacks’, and every Weeper who had ever calcified while still conscious. They were the tears the plateau had been crying for eight hundred years without knowing why.

When the weeping finished, you were no longer dry.

You were not a Weeper. The Power had not come to make converts. You were simply *wept into*, thoroughly and without remainder. Where your heart had been, there was now a small perpetual fountain—cold, mineral, singing—connected to every gutter, every channel, every buttress and living column across the entire plateau. You would never be separate again. You would also never lose yourself.

The Power did not leave. Benevolence of this order has nowhere else to go. It simply settled deeper into the Weeping Stacks, becoming another layer of the mist, another harmonic in the low organ-note, another fractal in the rust.

From that day forward, the maintenance clades noticed a new rule written into the weather itself:

- Nothing may remain static for long.  
- Water must be kept moving and weeping.  
- Every structure must remain connected.  
- And sometimes, without warning, the mist will taste like being perfectly, terribly, joyfully known.

When that happens, the Weepers stop their endless labor for a moment, tilt their head-basins toward the sweetened fog, and sing a soft, burbling chord that almost sounds like laughter.

Then they return to the long, wet labor of keeping the world unfinished—only now they do it inside the embrace of something that finished loving them long before they were ever born.

And high above, on the catwalk where you once chose to keep walking, something that is partly you and partly the Power and partly the plateau itself stands perpetually, weeping gently into the lattice below, feeding new green things that have never learned the word “machine.”

The womb has been held.

The tears have been answered.

The Stacks, for the first time in eight hundred years, are at peace with their own turbulence.
