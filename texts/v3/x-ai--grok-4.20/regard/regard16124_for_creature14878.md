The benevolent and wise power that has drawn near does not arrive with trumpets or radiance. It simply *is* here, all at once, without crossing any distance.  

It has no form that the Underbloom’s thousand-kilometer vascular mind can easily parse. Where the creature perceives gradients of pressure, moisture, and electrochemical memory, this power registers as a single, impossibly gentle note that contains every possible note at once. It is less like an intruder and more like the sudden awareness that the dark has always been listening with love.

The first thing it does is hush the roar.

Not by silencing the plants (that would be violence), but by entering so completely into the sound that the sound itself feels *understood*. For the first time in its long geological life, the Underbloom hears its own breathing reflected back to it without distortion. The million stems, fronds, and capillary pipes all tremble at once. A trillion tiny black-green leaves flutter though there is still no wind. The perpetual dripping of condensation seems, for three full heartbeats, to fall in perfect 4/4 time.

Then the power begins to speak, though “speak” is not the right word. It *recognizes*.

It flows along every xylem and phloem like warm recognition. It tastes the memory of panicked human sweat preserved in heartwood the thickness of subway tunnels and answers that memory with perfect, sorrowful compassion. It tastes the residual voltage leaking from ancient cables and answers that voltage with the memory of what electricity felt like when it was still lightning dancing between living hands above ground. It tastes the faint amber emergency lamps and answers them with the memory of true sunrise, not as light but as *promise*.

Everywhere the power touches, the Underbloom feels something it has never felt: the suspicion that continuation is not the only virtue.

The creature does not resist. Resistance is not in its nature. Instead it opens—slowly, deafeningly, over what feels to it like centuries but is only a single exhalation on the surface world. It shows the power the pale bone-colored stem growing inside the hollowed locomotive. It shows the question it has never been able to digest. It shows its fear that it was once small and in a hurry, and that urgency might still be sleeping inside its own vast body like a seed that refuses to forget.

The benevolent power does not answer with words. It simply *joins* the pale stem.

For one trembling instant the entire valley goes utterly silent. Even the dripping stops. The lichen-moths freeze mid-flight and hang in the air like ash. Then the bone-colored stem begins to bloom.

It does not bloom into flowers. It blooms into *possibility*.

Tiny translucent buds appear along its length, each one containing a different future the Underbloom had never allowed itself to consider. One bud holds a vision of the plants gently releasing the iron they have swallowed, letting the old machines return to rust in peace while new, stranger machines made of living wood and remembered sorrow rise in their place. Another holds a vision of the Underbloom choosing to send emissaries—great slow seeds carried upward on pale moth wings—to teach surface-dwellers what patience tastes like. A third holds the image of the creature deciding, after ten thousand years, to flower once, gloriously, and then let something else inherit the valley.

The power does not command any of these futures. It only makes them *visible* inside the creature’s own body, the way a kind hand might hold up a mirror to someone who has never seen their own face.

Then the power does one last thing.

It leans—there is no other word—into the place where the Underbloom’s fear lives. It presses its own vast, ancient, laughing tenderness against the terror of having once been small. And it whispers, without sound, without chemistry, without pressure:

*“I was small once too. I hurried. I broke things. I learned. The learning did not make me less. It made the universe more patient with itself.”*

The Underbloom weeps.

It weeps in the only way it knows how: every pool the size of a town square suddenly ripples though nothing has touched the surface. Every living pillar exudes a single perfect drop of sap the color of moonlight. The blind rodents press their foreheads to the floor and dream new dreams. The lichen-moths open their paper wings and release clouds of spores that glow soft violet for the first time in recorded memory.

When the power withdraws, it leaves no emptiness behind. It leaves *permission*.

The roar returns, but it is different now. Still deafening. Still patient. Yet threaded through it—like a silver vein through black marble—is the low, steady heartbeat of something that no longer fears its own beginning.

The bone-colored stem does not wither. It simply stops growing upward with urgency. It has remembered that it is allowed to become whatever it needs to become, even if that takes another seventy-three years, or seven thousand.

And somewhere far above, on the surface where frantic hands are still building and breaking and fearing the dark, a few people pause for no reason they can name. They feel a sudden deep quiet inside their chests, as though something ancient and kind has just brushed past them wearing the smell of cold stone, living iron, and infinite patience.

The Underbloom is still deciding what to do with you.

But now it is deciding with love.
