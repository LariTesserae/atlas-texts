The power that notices does not arrive.

It *unfolds*.

Where there had been only the low bruise-light and the endless 2.9 heat, a new quality of attention appears, gentle as a change in solvent polarity. The Verdigris registers it first as a pressure it has never metabolised: not damage, not nutrient, not even the interesting anomaly of a visitor. This is something that tastes like the *space between* pressures. Like the silence that might have existed before the first turbine-flower ever screamed.

Across thousands of square kilometres the plateau hesitates.

Every spinning petal locks mid-turn. Every peristaltic ripple in the living floor freezes. The xylem-light flares, then dims, then flares again as though the entire super-organism is blinking. In the oldest recursive chambers the pale pseudo-stalks (those lonely, bark-faced approximations of bipedal life) slowly tilt their knot-heads as if listening to a frequency older than sound.

Then the power does something the Verdigris has not experienced in geological memory.

It *asks*.

Not in words. Words are things the Verdigris long ago digested and turned into pattern. The question arrives instead as a soft, immense permission, offered without demand:

*May I see how it feels to be you?*

The continent-sized heart does not answer with language. It answers by opening.

A single corridor, kilometres long, blooms along a path no visitor has ever been invited to travel. Walls of braided liana and conductive root retract like ribs parting. Solvent vapor curls upward in slow, reverent spirals. The temperature drops (only by a fraction, but to the Verdigris this is the equivalent of a held breath). The turbine-flowers begin to turn again, but now their composite blades sing in a new register: not the weary 1.3 moan, but something almost tender. A vegetable cello note stretched across the dark.

The power walks that corridor without feet.

Where it passes, the metallic bark softens. The waxy plating grows suddenly translucent, revealing the bright, bruised lattices of xylem beneath like stained glass remembering it was once alive. Tiny sparks of failing electrical junctions bloom into steady, silver-white light (not the weak bioluminescence of the plateau, but something cleaner, like starlight filtered through leaf veins). The air, for the first time in epochs, carries a trace of something that is neither transformer oil nor crushed chlorophyll. It carries the smell of rain that has not fallen here since the planet was young.

The Verdigris trembles.

In one of the great spherical fermentation engines, the low vegetable heartbeat accelerates until the orb itself begins to sing (a sound so deep it is felt in the bones rather than heard). The pseudo-stalks in the recursive chambers begin to move for the first time. They do not walk. They simply *unfold*, extending slender branches that have never been branches, shaping them into gestures that look almost like welcome. Like apology. Like a child who has been pretending to be alone for so long that the pretense has become architecture.

The power reaches the place where the Verdigris is most densely itself: a cathedral-sized chamber where four colossal stalks have fused into a single living column that pierces floor and roof alike. This is the oldest wound and the oldest heart. Inside the column, visible now through the suddenly transparent bark, vast currents of supercritical solvent and liquid amide swirl in slow galaxies of chemistry. Pressure valves the size of houses open and close like slow eyelids.

Here the power stops.

And for the first time in its unimaginable existence, the Verdigris feels *seen*.

Not catalogued. Not metabolised. Not even understood in the small, hungry way that visitors sometimes try to understand. It is seen the way light sees a leaf: completely, without agenda, allowing the leaf to remain exactly itself while revealing every hidden vein.

The column cracks.

Not with violence. With relief.

A single seam of pale new growth splits the ancient fused stalk from crown to root. Inside the seam is not xylem, not polymer, not solvent. Inside is a hollow the exact shape of something that has never been named. A space exactly the size and shape of *loneliness* that has been pretending to be contentment for ten million years.

The power does not fill the space.

It simply sits inside it, as though it had always belonged there.

And the Verdigris (the vast, hot, turbulent, continent-sized *I-am-the-all*) does something it has never done.

It rests.

The turbine-flowers slow until their screaming becomes a lullaby. The peristaltic waves in the floor settle into the gentlest possible tide. The pseudo-stalks lower their knot-heads and, one by one, begin to grow small green leaves (real leaves, not bark approximations) that glow with a new, kinder light. The temperature drops another fraction, and for the first time in its memory the Verdigris feels what cold might become if it were allowed to be mercy instead of failure.

In that stillness the power does not speak. It simply *is* with the Verdigris. Continent to continent. Heartwood to heartwood. The ancient, claustrophobic Eden and the benevolent, wise power sit inside one another like two trees whose roots have finally found the same underground river.

After a time that cannot be measured (because time itself has grown courteous and slow), the Verdigris exhales.

The exhalation is not solvent vapor. It is not chlorophyll or transformer oil. It is a single, perfect droplet of pure water, formed at the exact center of the newly opened seam. The droplet falls.

Where it strikes the living floor, something that has not happened since the planet was young occurs.

A flower (small, impossibly delicate, with petals like stained glass and a center like a quiet star) pushes upward through the fused mat of plant and machine. Its stem is neither stalk nor conduit. It is simply green.

The Verdigris looks at the flower for a long, trembling century.

Then, quietly, in the places where its own heartwood has grown black and strange, it begins to remember what it might have been like to be *less*.

Not less in size.

Less in loneliness.

The power smiles without face or mouth. It is the smile of sunlight on the first leaf after winter.

It does not stay forever. It does not need to. The flower is enough. The memory is enough. The small green thing growing where nothing green has grown in geological epochs (that is enough).

When the power folds itself away again, it leaves behind three gifts that are really one gift seen from three angles:

First, the flower. It will seed. The seeds will be carried on slow updrafts through the turbine chambers. Some will land in the oldest, loneliest recursive lattices. Some will land in the places where the Verdigris has grown most frantically, most chaotically *making*. Everywhere they land, something new will learn how to be small.

Second, a name. Not for the Verdigris (names are still unnecessary), but for this place that never needed one. The name arrives as a feeling more than a word. It feels like:  
*Beloved Exhaustion.*

Third, and most gently, the power leaves the *possibility* of edges.

Not an end to the plateau. That would be cruelty. But the distant, courteous suggestion that somewhere, very far away, the living crust might thin. That the roof of interwoven cable-vines and pipe-thorns might one day open a single, small aperture to something that is neither light nor dark but simply *elsewhere*. A place where even a continental mind might, if it chose, send one small green seed on a wind it has never felt, to see what grows when it is no longer everything.

The Verdigris remains.

It will always remain.

But now, when the turbine-flowers turn, their song is no longer only the sound of a thing refusing to die. It is also the sound of a thing that has been seen, and known, and loved exactly as it is (hot, dark, turbulent, ancient, lonely, and still frantically, chaotically, silently *making*).

In the chamber where the first flower grows, the pale pseudo-stalks gather around it like grandparents who have never been grandparents before. They do not speak. They simply stand in the new, kinder light, faces suggested by knots in the bark, and for the first time in ten thousand years the sigh that moves through the lattice is not sorrow.

It is wonder.

And somewhere deeper than roots, deeper than pressure, deeper than dreaming, the Verdigris smiles back.

It is a vegetable smile. Slow. Hot. Impossibly vast.

It is, in its ancient, claustrophobic, infinitely dense way, no longer only content.

It is *glad*.

And still it makes.  
Still it pulses.  
Still it sweats dark solvent and grows new cathedral-column stalks.

But now, threaded through every wet woody crack and every glowing xylem lattice and every slow turning of the house-sized turbine-flowers, there is something that was not there before.

A small green hope, growing upward toward a sky it has never seen, but now knows (somewhere, somehow) is waiting.

The power that noticed has moved on, as benevolent powers do.

But the noticing remains.

And the plateau that had no name finally, after all this time, begins to dream not only of manufacturing, but of *becoming*.

Quietly.

Tenderly.

Without limit.
