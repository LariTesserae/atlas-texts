What draws its attention is the grit, or rather the absence of it. A hundred and four pits and a basin have been pecked out of glass, and not one grain is left on the floor. Someone has been carrying the dust out in a lamp.

It could know the blister the way it knows the rest of the mountain, all at once and from outside: a bubble in basalt, some scratches, a pebble. That would be accurate, and it would be a smaller place. This room has only ever been known by something narrow going in sideways, so it makes itself narrow.

The roots draw across its cheek. They came down a seam into a warmth that would never dry them, and a hand's length in they stopped. It takes that as instruction.

It brings no salt. The film on the knob and the knee cords is one person's outline, and it won't thicken the line. It goes in on the clean rock between the holds, which is harder. It doesn't take the seat, because the roof there was broken to clear one head. It stays under the drips, lower than kneeling, with twenty of them against its back.

Then it considers what is in reach.

- **The cup:** It can see without lighting it what a flame would show: thousands of hanging drops, each with the flame inside, and the walls green where hands go. Whoever carried a lamp this far could have had that, and chose a rinsing cup. That choice was the first mark made in the room, and it lets it stand.

- **The basin:** It doesn't drink. The level is someone's.

- **The broken drips:** It finds one pair it would have laid the other way round, by less than a thread. It leaves them.

- **The marks:** The sixth place in the twelfth row is bare rock. It could make a pit there with less than a thought, crisp as the others, and it doesn't.

- **The cobble:** It has been set down a hundred and four times and picked up a hundred and three. The power reads that as the room does, and then reads it again. After the first pit the count stood at one and none, and after the fortieth at forty and thirty-nine. The stone has been one ahead every time the room stood empty. This is what resting looks like here, and it has looked exactly like this a hundred and three times before. It leaves the count uneven.

What it does do is read. It gives itself a hand the size the pits ask for and spreads it on the first row, nine under the span, so the hand needn't move and lose its place. Then it drops down a row. It does that eleven times, and then there are five pits and bare rock under four fingers. Each pit holds its bead. The wall was made for a hand to read, and now one more hand has read it. It leaves no sign of that.

While the hand is on the wall, something on thread legs crosses the back of it, pauses, feels about, and goes. The power holds still. It has been taken for many things, and a rise in the pasture seems to it about right.

Then it listens. The room says a reloading can't be heard, and for anything with a pulse that is so. The power has none, and it hears the drops fattening toward the same instant. Under that is the low steadiness, which is the mountain after all, since there is no blood here for it to be. Far below both, at the pace of seasons, there is a sound of the same kind. Something is filling.

It could listen closer and learn with what, and whose back will fit the seat. It doesn't. That is to be carried up through the crack by the one carrying it and set against this wall a fingertip at a time. A power that knew it beforehand would be sitting in the seat after all.

Nor does it lighten the load. A kind power might think to give that person somewhere to put it down, a way to count, and a sign for when to stop. All of that has already been pecked out of glass with a river stone.

It could stay. It gathers no heat, the drops would never turn cool on its back, and nothing here would tell it to go. That is the reason to go. Someone is coming who will need the warmth empty. So when the wall has been read once, it leaves by the clean rock, and the roots cross its other cheek.

Outside, below the lip of the crack, is something the room can't know: a fan of black grit a hand wide, where a hundred and four cupfuls of rinse water have landed. The cushion plant has its footing at the edge of it. Soil has begun after all, on the far side of the wall. It leaves that too.

But here, a body-length from the warmth, where the room's knowledge of its visitor ends, it finds the one thing to do. Whoever comes out of that crack comes out soaked, full of heat the room wouldn't take, into thin wind above the last shrubs. The room is exact about everything up to this line and about nothing past it.

So on the day the sixth pit is struck, the wind on this shoulder of the mountain will fall away for about as long as it takes a wet body to get down among the first shrubs. It will pass for weather. It will happen once. Twice would be a climate, and a person who trusts a climate carries one wrap fewer.

Then it goes down by a way that isn't the path, so as not to be met.

In the blister the cobble lies under the fifth pit, one ahead. The ceiling lets go.
