The power comes in from the west, level, the way the houses did, and it comes without an hour. The day here is a closed round and every part of it is taken, so anything that arrived by sunlight would be taking that light from a house. It casts no shadow, and the dust does not part for it.

For a long while it only attends, to the whole saddle at once, since that is how it attends to anything. Silence is ordinary here, so it is slow to notice that there has been no click since it came. The windward faces are still going grain by grain, because wearing does not mind being watched. But the loose slabs lie where it found them, and the pellets sit on the copings. A stone is seated here only when nothing is looking, and under an attention with no edges nothing is unwatched. For as long as it has been admiring the city, every house in it has been getting thinner.

So the first thing it does, it does to itself. It learns to look away, which for a power of this kind is a piece of building. It takes the city as its pattern. It thins its attention as the day is thinned, gives it one place and one angle, and sends it round, resting on each house in that house's early morning, low and slanted, and moving on before noon. What lies ahead of it and behind it goes unseen. A few arm's lengths from where it has just been, there is a dry click.

It has the usual mercies with it, and it considers them one at a time.

It has water. It follows a channel into one house's evening and watches the dust come down ankle-deep and settle in the cistern, flat as cream, and in that house's morning it watches the dust drain back uphill. The cisterns are in use. Nothing has ever fallen on this saddle, and rain would be the first thing that did. It keeps its water.

It could make a second grazer. It follows the first one all the way round, an hour behind so as not to change which hours. It counts nine from the west and fourteen from the east and lets both numbers stand. What it looks for is want, any sign that the animal is looking for something that is not there. The head stays low and a little to one side. There is one stain, very thin, and a second mouth would be fed out of the first one's portion. It makes nothing.

It could close the fourth doorway. It is at the house late in the afternoon when the opening appears. It does not see the lintel laid, only that it has been, between one glance and the next. A slab seated across the gap would hold. Instead it goes round to that house's first light and waits. The grazer is on the coping with no shadow, and for those few steps its head is up, the only time in its day. The night has no slant in it, no crust, and nothing to steer by, and the animal cannot walk that part of the round. The door is how it gets across. It is perhaps thinner. The power cannot tell whether that is a fare or a wound, or whether being carried and being eaten are two things in a place where a door and a mouth are the same kind of opening. The animal comes out the other side. It leaves the doorway alone.

It could do something about the eastern scarp. It goes down through the haze, where there is weather and things fall. In the scree some stones still lie fitted together. It holds its attention off them and listens for a long time, and hears nothing it can be sure of. It cannot say whether the fit is what is left of a house or the start of one. It could build a ledge there, or a second saddle. But the oldest pale rectangles begin in the air. A herd that walked in level over nothing knows more about edges than the power knows about herds. It goes back up and leaves the rim as it is.

It comes to the low wall last, having been careful not to come to it first. It has what the wall lacks. It could open the round and let in a twenty-fifth hour. But it looks at the nearest houses, each with one doorway more than its plan allows, low, on the side facing the square. It has now seen what such a door does with a thing that comes along the foot of a wall. The door takes it into a kept night and sets it on a coping at first light, where a shadow arrives from the correct side. The herd may already be arranging to give this one an hour, in the only way hours are given here. Or the doors may be mouths. The power could not tell with the grazer either, and the grazer is still going round.

What it can tell is what a corner is. The slabs at the southern end are turning, laid one by one in the moments of not being looked at, and what they are beginning is an inside. The power is a thing for which there have never been insides. So here it does what might be called a gift. It takes its attention off the square and does not bring it back, not on the slant, and not once in a round. The square becomes the one place it does not know. It can still hear. The click carries to the rooftops and comes more often now, and the power does not count.

One question is left. For its first few steps the grazer has its head up, before a shadow comes to lower it. Whatever is kept in a house with no hour may come out with its head up and keep it up. It may be the first thing in this city that looks about. The power decides that if something is going to look about, there should be something to see besides doorways.

To be seen it needs light, and it will not take a house's. But a few stars stand near the zenith at midday, and they are in nobody's portion. It takes its light from those. By that light it is very little, a grey upright shape far down a street, some hours off, with a shadow too faint to steer by. The dust has begun to go round it. It stands with its back to the square, where anything walking toward it would change which hours.

It does not know whether there is a doorway in the low wall yet, and it has arranged not to know. Behind it the click comes, and comes again, and it does not turn round.
