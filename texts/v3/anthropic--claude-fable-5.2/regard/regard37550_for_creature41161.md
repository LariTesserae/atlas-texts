It comes the way water comes here, sideways and in the evening. It rises with the cloud, crosses the lip of the scarp, and lies over the plateau without moving. Nothing else has ever arrived by another route, and it does not want to be the first.

Then it stays. The creature takes one breath a day and climbs one lattice panel in ten years, and a body cannot be learned faster than it lives. So the power stays for a panel, some three and a half thousand evenings. It counts, and its count comes out to one.

In that time it learns what a visitor of a day cannot. At the fourth tower from the scarp the green column inside the lattice has a kink in it, where it once leaned out toward the arm and afterwards did not. The power knows how long the tremor was gone. It was less than an hour. The lesson was paid for by one frond and learned by six kilometres.

It leaves the fog mesh torn and the trolley where it stands. It cannot tell what the trolley is for either.

It thinks of three things to do.

The first is to speak. The creature has no ear, but there is one channel, the tremor in the footings and the fence wire and the wet rock. The power could lay something on it for a single evening, a pause and a return, enough to say: you are seen. It does not. The tremor has faltered once and taught a distance. A second irregularity would teach waiting. A thing that has been addressed goes on listening for the next time. The hedge now faces the machines every evening and asks nothing of them. That is better than a greeting, and a greeting would end it.

The second is to make the place safe by sheathing the yard and lifting the spans. But the gravel is the one thing the creature knows, and the only thing it has that did not come to it in the cloud. Without the danger the margin is only bare stone, and the spores would have it in a season. The creature would no longer be living beside something live. It would be living on a ruin, early.

The third is the wells, and this takes longest. The power follows the lagged pipe into the cone and looks at the heat. It learns the year. It could hold the heat where it is.

Before deciding, it goes out to the side edges of the flow, a kilometre and more from the nearest wire, where no drip falls. The moss there is thinner and paler and entirely alive. In the lichen and the pinhead liverworts are blue-green threads that take nitrogen from the air. The work is slow and costs them dearly. Under the bands the same threads are present and idle, because there they are fed. So the creature will not starve. It will pale along three lines and carry on. What it will lose is the hum, the light with an address, and the moths' reason to climb. It will lose the thing it faces.

The power decides this is not its to prevent. The creature already lives under a sun, immense and from every side, and it has no terms with that at all, no lean and no distance. It keeps terms with the line because the line is the size of thing that can stop. Made permanent, the line would become weather.

So the heat is left to the mountain's schedule.

What the power does is smaller, and at the other end of the wire. It follows the conductors over the scarp and down through the white to where the current goes. There are kettles, a cold store, streetlights, and a ward where machines breathe for people. It takes its time there too. In a control room there is a screen with a row for this circuit, showing what is sent and what arrives. The difference is small. It rises each evening and falls each morning. Someone has noticed. There is a proposal in a drawer for corona rings and heavier conductor, with a payback of eleven years. Below the cloud the three violet threads, the nitrate, and the dark ridge are a loss of a few kilowatts.

The power does not touch the drawer. The current belongs to those people, and they have terms of their own. It sees to it only that before anyone signs, someone goes up. This needs no miracle. An alarm clears itself, a work order follows, and one person goes with a tool bag and a day more than the job requires. They find nothing to fix. They lay a palm on a casing. They drop a wrench and do not hear it land. In the evening they push a hand into the hedge at the yard fence and feel the mesh, and what is in the mesh. Then they go down and write "no fault found", and whatever else they choose to write. The power is fairly sure what a person does who has stood there, but it does not make sure.

This may already have happened. Someone was up there with a wrench.

Last, it keeps the year. On the evening the wells fail it comes up with the cloud as before, crosses the lip, and lies down on the plateau. The threads do not light. The rock is still, and the hour goes on and does not end. The power does not hum or glow, and it brings nothing the creature could mistake for the line. It is only there, the length of the cloud, each evening for as long as the ferns lean, which is years. It does not think the creature can feel this. It comes anyway.

All this amounts to very little, and that is deliberate. The power is live too, more than the yard is, and it has seen at the fourth tower what happens to a thing that is reached. So it comes as close as it can and no closer. It keeps a margin, though nothing obliges it to. The sign that it has been here is a place where something could have been done and was not, and the edge of that place is neat.
