It notices the curtain first. In the country below, where it has other business, a line of mist runs along the top of the escarpment and is the same in every weather, and nothing else in that country is the same twice. It goes up to see.

It brings nothing. There is not a made thing on the plateau, and it does not make one. It comes in over the rim on the wind that never shifts, goes down through the rain and between the pads, and settles at the foot of one trunk, among the eels. One of them gives off a tick of light, though nothing an eel would count has touched it. That is the whole of its arrival.

Then, for three million strokes of that trunk, it does nothing. Any place looks like a problem to someone who has just arrived, and the power knows this about itself. So it keeps still for a year and learns the interval the way the eggs learn it.

After that it looks for the thing that is wrong, since that is what it is for. It finds death at every trunk, and all of it quick. It finds no hunger and no chase. It finds fish that cannot be startled and birds that sleep when they are tired. It goes along the shrimp file and the valve lips, and out into the empty water between the trunks. It does not find anyone waiting to be let out.

A power that finds nothing to mend starts thinking of gifts, and this is the moment it trusts itself least.

It could give the sea a night. The pads would only have to stay dark when the cloud does. It follows that through. The fleck thins by the first morning, and the fish hold station in a flow that brings them less. The bird, who sleeps when she is tired, would sleep when she was told. The power comes from where there is night, and it misses it. That is the reason for a night, and the reason is its own.

It could let one storm in over the rim. But the plant has gone on refusing weather for longer than the hills below have had their shapes, and it refuses again every eleven seconds, at every trunk. The power lays its attention against the bark the way a hand is laid there, and feels the bore fill and empty. There is no wish in the wood that it can consult. There is only the work, and the work is the answer.

It watches a fish be slow. A quarter of a second is nothing, and it could hand it over. But the place in front of the slit is the one scarce thing in this sea, and a fish that keeps it keeps it from a smaller fish already waiting. The sea holds exactly as much life as the sweetness comes to. The power can choose which life. It cannot choose more without making the plant into something else.

That leaves it with nothing to do, in a place that does not need it. It finds this harder than it expected.

It is some time before it sees that this is how everything else here lives. The plant makes more than it needs and does not ask the surplus to be for anything. The power, too, has more than it needs. So it takes a place, as the fish have the flow and the eels have the rhizome. Its place is what is left over, the one thing the plant has no use for, which is *which*.

Nothing accumulates here, but in the power it does. It knows which fish was slow, and on which stroke. It knows the water in the curtain. It has gone alongside a fish swept into open water, dodging every eleven seconds away from nothing, all the way to the trunk that took it, and that one was hard. From far enough off, a bird is a share of the colony. The power is able to be far off, and it stays near.

It keeps one other thing, which is watch. The wind has never shifted, and the power has known enough winds to understand what "never" comes to. On some day the plant will meet a thing it cannot make the same. Then there may be work here, for the plant or for what lives on it. The power does not yet know what that work will be. It has decided only that it will not be somewhere else.

Meanwhile there is the egg. It spends some days inside the shell, where the stroke is the farther sound, counting forty-four and forty-four. Then the shell stars and opens in its cup between the ribs, and the chick lies there in wet grey down with the patch already white.

Before it can stand, it lifts a wing at the bird above it, who shows white back. It lifts a wing at the chick in the next cup. It lifts a wing at the stack, which goes on with its three notes. Then it turns its head toward the open water, where there is rain and nothing else, and shows white again.

The power has no white patch. Sixty metres down, at the foot of that trunk, an eel that nothing has touched gives off a tick of light, yellowish, the size of a fingernail.

The chick cannot see it through all that water and a leaf the size of a field. Above it the bird shifts her weight, just ahead of the thud, as she always has. She has never noticed that she does. It has been noticed now. The stroke comes.
