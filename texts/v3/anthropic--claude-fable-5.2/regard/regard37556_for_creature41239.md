The visitor comes up the outside of the cone on foot, with a long stick, because that is how the slope is climbed. It reaches the rim in the afternoon and stands there until the next noon, through one turn of the drum.

From the rim everything is close. Seven plants, perhaps eight, stand within reach of the stick. The gap in the shaft is a hand's width. The visitor has a hand's width of anything. It has iron, and grain, and color the eye can keep. Without climbing down it could set the missing length in the shaft, and four legs between the collars and the crescents, bent twice, the way the moss says.

It looks at the moss for a long time. The moss is packed in at every gap, and the torque crosses every gap. The wheels mesh. The weight comes down. Nothing in the room is late.

And nothing in the room was made. A made thing would be the first. After it there would be two kinds of thing on the floor, the mountain's and the visitor's, and the animal would be some of each.

One thing the mountain has not supplied, because the pocket cannot. The animal has never had to make room for anything, and the summit, when it comes, will be open ground.

The visitor lays the stick on the outer slope, pointing downhill, and climbs in.

The room supplies more floor. The visitor could ask it not to. It walks. The nearest vein takes the better part of a morning, and the air thins on the way. The visitor has never been short of anything, and it lets its breath come short.

The daylight gives it a walker's shadow of ordinary size. Look from the shadow to what casts it and there is much more. The visitor keeps to the size of the shadow as well as it can.

It chooses a straight length of vein with no seam, where a step to the side costs nothing, and stands on the copper to wait for its day in the turn. The rock is too hot to rest a hand on. The visitor stands on it. Each day at the driest hour the pollen comes along the vein in its pale thread, passes between the visitor's feet, and goes on.

On the fifth day the ticking comes down the vein in fours, and stops.

Heat comes close to the visitor's face. It looks at the floor. This is harder than the stick. The undecided place has turned toward it, and a thing this visitor looks at does not stay undecided. It would set. It would be a good face. It would also be a face that was seen before it was there, and it would turn toward the visitor for the rest of its life.

So the visitor looks at the shadow, where the head is plain and narrow and the mouth is a little open.

The long breath goes over it. This is the other thing the visitor came down for, to learn whether the gap hurts. Breath is the part of the animal that is finished, and it can be read. It smells of a warm machine shop. There is heat in it, and work. There is no pain in it, and no hurry.

Then the visitor breathes out once, slowly, the thin air it has carried for five days. It is all the visitor gives. Breath came first here, and the animal can be met in it. On the floor the shadow's mouth closes.

The crescents step around, one always on the metal. The visitor does not move its feet until the ticking has gone a long way off.

It is near the center the next noon, at the distance the pocket keeps. The animal stands with its crescents together under the darkest part of the sky. The ribs work and the moss collars shake. It looks like distress. The visitor has thicker air and could put it there. But this is what the chest was finished first for, and it is the one minute of the day the animal stands on all of itself. The visitor watches the shadow go in underneath, and leaves the air as thin as it found it.

Before it goes, it does a sum. It takes the bend of the moss pads, the hand's width a year, and what is left between the peak and the floor. The sum comes out the way it is told from the rim: the legs should be in by then. It could be made to come out better. The visitor leaves the should where it is.

The rim is an arm's length overhead, with ordinary blue sky beyond it. The visitor climbs out. The stick is where it was left, warm from the sun. It looks back once and counts seven plants, perhaps eight. It could know. It goes down the mountain with the count the rim gives.

---

Seven days later the animal comes down that vein again. On the straight length with no seam, the crescents stop. No one is there. One long breath goes out over bare copper. Then it steps around, keeping a foot on the metal, and goes on.

It does this at every turn. What happens here on schedule is kept.

By the end of the season, moss has come in beside the vein at that place. It is a pad the size of a hat, off the copper, with no shape yet. It does not match any part of the animal. The mountain means to have an animal on its summit, and things come to summits. It has started early. When the peak gets here, the floor will be open ground, with others on it. The animal should know by then how to go around.

The drum finishes its turn. The drop falls.
