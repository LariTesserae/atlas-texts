What draws it is the emptiness. From far enough away the plateau is a white field with some thirty dark marks on it, and one morning, for most of the morning, there are none. A power of that kind notices attention the way you noticed the note, as a pressure before it is a place. It comes to see what thirty animals would all go cold for.

It learns the first rule from you, on the rim. Arriving is an event, and events here are paid for, since every edge that turns toward one has taken a dark face off the sun. So it does not arrive. It comes in as marble, water and sky, the three things the groove passes over, and no mark turns.

Then it watches, through more changes of the sun's angle than you could stay for. It has the one thing none of them has, which is both views. It sees a face swing out of true and hold, and it sees, across the field, how that is taken. Often it is taken well. Often the other was already turning. The speaker, creeping back to square, cannot know this.

It could tell them. That is the difficulty with being able to do anything.

It could warm the water. Then a turn away from the sun would cost nothing, and they could say whatever they liked, and it would be worth what free things are worth.

It could open an eye in each dark face, so that showing and looking were one act. Then no one would need to thin to a line for anyone. The pairs would have no reason to walk, and the crossing would get a glance.

It could thicken the windfall. Then there would be sixty of them, then two hundred, every slot a shortened throat. The note is only the sameness of the slots, and it would go under the roar.

Each of these is a kindness that removes the thing it was meant to ease. It turns them over for a long time anyway, because it knows how well restraint flatters whoever is doing the restraining. It cannot establish that they are content. It can establish what they have made: a way of being looked at that costs neither party anything, found inside the sun's own geometry; a morning spent on an orange column; a field going empty. They made these out of a groove and a dark side. It has never once had to go cold to say a thing, and it knows what that makes its own speech worth here.

Nor does it tell them how they are taken. Turning to find out is the second half of everything they say.

It can see what decides which side of a young one darkens. The thing is small and would be easy to lean on, so that the hands came out even and every one of them had an opposite. But that would be choosing, for each, between the notch and the white line. Neither chose, and it does not want to be the first thing on the plateau that does.

The pale line a hair above the sheet it also leaves. Something raised that water once, perhaps kindly.

So what it does, it does elsewhere. The place hangs on two things none of them can reach, and it goes to both.

It goes north first. The plateau's constancy, its one temperature and one level and one note, is all borrowed from snow a long way off, and snow is not marble. The range is the only part of this that can end. Nothing the power does there can be seen from here. A right-dark one, at dawn, looks straight at the work and sees nothing, the groove having no use for what does not change. That is how the power knows the work was done properly.

Then it goes south, over the notch and down to the valley too far below to matter. The pane comes apart on the way and arrives as rain. Under it is everything the plateau would not keep. The grit from the crowns is there, the moth wings, the bodies, all gone into a fan of pale silt. On the silt, green, are the first roots this water meets. It had half meant to make the valley matter, and it finds there is no need. Things collect there. It stays through one rainfall and comes back up.

Then there is you. It knows which edge it was. It does not tell you. From a place where nothing collects you managed to carry something off, and it will not be the one to take that to the notch.

On its last morning it chooses an orange column where a slip of clear tissue lies in the film with the veining showing through. It stays from first light until the updrafts, in no one's line, with nothing being said. There are other fields it is not looking at while it does this. That is the nearest it can come to turning a pale face to the sun.

Around mid-morning, a long way across the field, one mark swings a little out of true, holds, and goes back. It could know what that was, and for whom. It keeps to the runnel. It has said its piece, and here that is done without seeing how it is taken.

The wind comes up. One by one they go down, like letters. Then it goes too, and the note holds.
