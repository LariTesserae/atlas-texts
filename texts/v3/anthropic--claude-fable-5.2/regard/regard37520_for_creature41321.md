**How it comes**

It does not use the doors. The webs across them have hung unbroken for years, and it sees no reason to be the thing that ends that. It comes in the way the brine does, through the tide gates on a spring flood, and goes inward pan by pan. This takes some weeks. It could be everywhere at once, but the place was built for area and only shows itself at walking pace.

**What it could do**

It is a power, so it makes the list.

- **Fresh water.** It is the scarce thing, and the power has any amount. But whatever holds rain here is wriggling within a week, and at the centre of the works rainwater is the one poison. The rains will come on their own schedule, and the crust is built around that schedule.
- **Shade.** The stumps by the manager's bungalow are what a kindness looks like here after a few years.
- **People.** The houses were built for them, and they could be brought back. But the leaving is no longer a wound, and they would start by sweeping the doorways.
- **Lamps.** The flamingos feed by touch. The bats need the dark, and so does what the bats eat.
- **The gates.** The hinge pins are wearing, and the power can see the tide on which the first flap drops into the creek. It leaves the pins alone. The tenant lived in any hollow that held a spring tide long enough to dry, and will again. The works is the largest house it has had, not the only one.
- **The falcons.** It watches them for a long time.

**The ones with insides**

Most of what a benevolent power is for assumes somebody who lacks something. On the flat they are easy to find: a bat taken off the rope at dusk, a stilt back at a bund the jackals have visited, a pup on the boiler house floor with the guano moving toward it.

It does not stop the falcon. The falcon has an inside too. Every count here hangs from another count, and the power cannot lift one without the rest coming up with it.

But under the trusses, when a pup has fallen and the beetles will reach it before the jackals do, it lets the pup go out first. The beetles get what they would have had, and no count changes. The power does not call this an answer. It does it that night, and on the other nights it is there.

**The centre**

Then come the innermost pans, where its usual questions stop working.

It looks for an inside, carefully, because powers get this wrong in both directions. It finds what the lens found, which is almost none. It finds no want, only a direction: a face held up to the cloud. The square already has what it faces. There is no gap to put a gift into.

There is no hardship to lift, either. The square is as salt within as the pan is without. It did not keep the hardship out; it rebuilt itself to need it. If the power took away what looks like its suffering, the square would come apart. The power has met this before in larger creatures and has not always recognised it in time.

It thinks about the salt-makers, who believed they were using the square. From above, that is an easy mistake. But by accident they did one thing right. They built something that needed only the tide and the sun, and then they left. The power has built a great many things, and fewer of them than it would like still work in its absence.

Then it remembers the last rule, that everything large is here for something very small. The power is very large, and it has been on the flat for weeks. It considers that it has been standing in the pans with its head down like all the others. Everywhere else its attention arrives already owed. Here nothing asks for it, and that is what it came to feed on. It is one more customer.

**What it does**

Customers pay.

Looking takes light. Even a power cannot see into the dark without putting something there. To see the one square in its bubble at the bottom of the crust, it has to send a little light in, and the square, which sees nothing, does not send it back. A patch of purple catches the photon, and one proton goes out through the membrane. It takes three or four of those, trickling back, to mint the smallest coin the cell spends. So the gift is a fraction of almost nothing, given to a sleeper that spends almost nothing.

The power does not pretend it is more. But it notes that this is the only time its regard has been taken at exactly what it was worth, by something that could neither need it nor flatter it.

It does not arrange for that square to land in strong brine when the rains come. Enough of them will. Enough is all they have ever needed, and the power is not going to improve on enough.

**After**

It leaves by the creek on an ebb, and the gate drops shut behind it with a single thud.

Down the coast is the orange stain on the cloud. Under it are several million creatures with insides, all lacking something, and the power's work is there. It takes with it what the flat taught: build what needs only the tide and the sun, leave the webs up, pay for what you look at. It also knows that a flat like this stays empty only as long as nobody in that city is desperate enough to want it. So whatever it does for the square, it will do there, among people, and none of it will look like it has anything to do with salt.

Behind it the pan is closing over. The rains are nine days off.
