**The visitor**

It came up from the low country on foot, breathing on purpose. It could have arrived some other way. But the scavengers have the height and the cloud has the eastern rim, and the only way in that anyone careful had made was the one the people use. So it used that.

It stopped at the edge, where everything stops, and watched for a day. A day was enough to have seen all of them.

**What it could do**

It could do anything here, and it took that seriously.

It thought about the notch first. One block set in the western rim would stop the wind, and the wall would go silent. The power followed that forward. The colony would go on screaming, since the scream never depended on the drone. For the first time each bird would hear several hundred thousand others, and none would hear a mate. No voice was waiting under the noise to be let out. Voice was traded long ago for the address, and the address is what they have. The note would end too. It wants nothing, so it could not be said to mind. But it is the one who lives here, and the power did not think kindness could begin by ending the resident.

It thought about widening the six paces to sixty, or to a quiet that ran all the way to the lakeshore. But it had just walked up through the low country, where quiet lies everywhere and nobody climbs anywhere for it. At six paces the quiet has walls. At six hundred it would be weather.

Then it thought about the male calf, and it stayed with that one longest, because that one pulled. It followed a male out of the circle and through his whole life. He never heard breath again. But he lived among animals who speak with the body, in a light that comes from below as well as above, and all day he watched flanks rise and fall beside him. He read breathing the way the rodents read a whistle. What his one hearing had become in him could not be lifted out and looked at. It was somewhere in how he stood near the others. The power could not find a hole in him where the sound should be. It found the ache in itself, and recognized it as the low country, still in its ears.

**The middle**

Then it did the one thing nobody here does, and walked out to the middle.

The plates rang under it in no herd's phrase. It stood where the note is thickest and listened.

The note had never been listened to. The animals live beside it, and the people climb to the one place where it isn't. To hear it, someone would have to stand inside it and need nothing else from their ears. The power could do that, so it did. The listening took no time, or it took the afternoon. With the note these are the same.

It said nothing to the note. A message has a beginning, and there is nowhere in the note to put one. Whether being heard is any gift to a thing that wants nothing, the power did not settle. It listened anyway. The note went on, and that was the whole exchange.

**The six paces**

In the circle it heard its own steps arrive. It looked at the fire ring, the chert flakes, and the unmarked cushions, and it saw that the people had already worked out how to be here.

It saw one more thing. The wind is still cutting. The flutes deepen more slowly than the cushions widen, but they deepen, and in some far age the echoes will fall into step and the six paces will close. The power could have pinned the wall as it is. It left the wall alone. This is the one place in the basin where things begin and end, and it would be a strange kindness to make it the one thing here that never does.

One of the tuned plates, the third, had slipped off its propping stones. Some calf had done it, getting its legs. The plate lay flat on the floor, where it would only clack. The power lifted it, set the small stones back where the wear marks showed they had been, and laid the plate across them. It tapped the plate once with a knuckle. The plate rang, and then it stopped ringing.

It left the others unstruck. The scale is the people's to play.

**Leaving**

Late in the day a grazer came to the edge of the circle, heavy, and stood there. The circle is six paces across and she needed all of them. A calf born there hears two breaths, and it should not hear three.

The power got up and stepped out into the roar, where it had no voice, like everyone else.

From the lakeshore it watched the calf stand, wet, and walk out. The first plate rang under it. The calf felt that in its leg and took it for the ground. The power, which had stood in the middle and listened, thought the calf had it right.

It went down the way it had come and took nothing. In the morning the cloud touched the rim and withdrew.
