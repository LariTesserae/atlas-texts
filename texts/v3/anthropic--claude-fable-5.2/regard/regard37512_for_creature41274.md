The first thing it does is wait out a full turn.

For forty days it attends to the drum and changes nothing. It learns to read the date off the ramp. It is present for each of the valve's openings and for the minute and a half between teeth. By the end it knows which probe is furthest inside its sleeve and which basket of mat gets the least light.

Then it asks who lives here. The plate says four, the tags say four thousand, and the valve says one. The haze isn't counted by anyone. The power decides it doesn't have to settle this, as long as whatever it does is right under every count.

Next it asks whether anything here suffers, because that is the answer that would oblige it. It looks for a long time. It finds growing, and a closing on whatever is touched. It finds no one for whom the bar is a dread or the morning too long. It could be wrong, so it checks what being wrong would mean. The bar takes only what is under forty days old, and the old strap below the seam has kept everything it ever made. Even to a ribbon that could mind, the cut would be less than it looks from the gallery.

It could give the ribbons a someone. That is within its power, and it sets the idea down first. A ribbon able to know its hour in the gas would also know the bar was a few days off.

It considers a night. The plumes would fold for the first time. But it can't tell whether a whorl that has never closed is tired or is only a hinge nobody has needed, and a night given to find out would be for the power's curiosity. The lamps are also someone's decision, and the water is someone's drinking.

It considers the arm and leaves it alone. The arm makes the only season the place has. If it stopped, everything above the line would be against the glass within two turns.

It considers a snail. A grazer would keep the glass clear, but it would be the first mouth in the drum, with nothing above it. The risers brought the haze without being asked. They will bring what eats the haze, and later what eats a dead leaf, in an order the power could not improve. It leaves the risers as they are.

It considers the thirty centimeters between the climber's tip and the third rung, which it could close with less effort than anything else here. It lets the stem grow.

It finds very little to mend. The place is new and does what it was built for, and the water goes down to where it is wanted. It also sees what the drawings don't show. The cliff of tall ribbons lies along the roof glass and travels round with the arm, and where the tips have lain the haze is a little thinner. The drum has started wiping its own window.

It can also see what is coming. The haze is on the roof cone, between the lamps and everything else. The probes that would report the lost light are wrapped in leaf, and they describe water the climber has already used. The mat lives on what is left after nine meters of other leaves. The first brown in the drum will be down there, under the sheet, in a basket whose tag nobody can find. The power doesn't intend to prevent this. The binder was written for it and the spare baskets are waiting in their wrap. The people who built the drum expected to lose plants. But they expected to be told by the probes.

So the place is missing someone who looks. Whoever carries the marker comes up, faces the wall, makes a tick, and goes back down.

It acts once. On the day the third turn closes, it holds a bank of cloud on the stair two terraces below the drum, thick enough that a careful person waits on the landing for it to pass. It holds the cloud for forty minutes and then lets it go.

Whoever carries the marker reaches the door with the arm at the start bearing and the mirror at its widest. The tallest ribbons in the drum are directly overhead, just past the bar, and their tips are through. The power does not turn anyone's head. It has arranged a coincidence, and whether the coincidence is noticed is outside what it allows itself.

The head turns anyway. From below, the leaves lie flat against the silver, as if pressed on a second pane of glass. The person watches until the valve opens, and stays through the twenty minutes of hiss. The water comes back up, and the tips go under and shed their large slow bubbles.

After that the person walks the gallery all the way round. They stop at a sensor stalk and look for the probe, which takes a while. They write the stalk number on the back of one hand in yellow. They rest the other hand on the binder box and leave it sealed, since nothing has failed. They put a palm on the window and wait for a tooth.

Back at the door they make the third tick. They hold the marker a moment longer and draw a short stroke across the top of it, which the first two don't have.

The power doesn't know what will come of the number on the hand. Three of the four plants are past revision A, so someone down the mountain is willing to be corrected by what grows up here. The line might be raised, or the float moved. The probes might be cleared and sleeved again within a month. It leaves that to them.

It keeps watching, which costs the drum nothing. It is there some days later when the stem on the service ladder has grown the length it needed. The tip comes round, meets the third rung, and closes on it.
