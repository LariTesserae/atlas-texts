The power came once, by a way it had not used, and it came as nothing in particular. The forty scrapes had been explanation enough.

It stayed through several shifts without counting them. It walked beside a run of hares. It stood at the rim. It lay along the quartzite rib, a little apart from the eleven hollows, leaving no heat on the stone, and listened to the beat idle at the edge of the rock.

Its first thought was the ordinary one. Here was an animal whose pulse was kept outside it and who stood with one foot lifted every time the note sounded. Here was a power that could mend all of that between one thud and the next. The power stood over this thought, considered it, and walked away. It had seen that done on the flats, and done well.

What it declined:

- **The heart:** It did not move the beat into the fox. The beat belongs to the plateau, so a heart inside the fox would have to be a new one, and a new one would have to come from the power. That would be a part issued from outside, and the fox would be somebody's work again from the ribs back. Nor did it let the beat follow onto the stone. The rock is the one place the moss does not reach, and ten born foxes sleep there on that understanding. The eleventh goes there more often than it needs to. The power did not think it should guess at why and then repair its guess.
- **The note:** It did not tell the fox that the note would never be for it. To make that true, it would have had to take the plateau's work out of the plateau's keeping. As things stand, each time the note sounds and the fox is left standing, the plateau has had a single thing in front of it and has not taken it back. That is the nearest thing to a choice the tableland makes, and it makes it again every shift. A guarantee would have ended it. The power knew what the lifted foot costs. It left it lifted.
- **The hares:** It looked hard, since four thousand is a great many if anyone is home. It found less than four thousand and more than none. There was something like one hare's worth of hare, spread thin along the band, and a little thicker than it used to be. Thickening it was already somebody's job. A second inspector would only give the plateau a second standard, and it is doing well with one.
- **The rim:** It went down through the cloud to the foot of the shoulder, where the peat does not reach and a power may walk as it likes. The flecks land on wet scree. Away from the hum they give up the ear within a day and grow as plain moss, which nobody down there holds against them. Among the stones lie fox bones, clean, each lying differently. None has been kept. It touched nothing and came back up. It did not look to see what would go over if the eleventh ever went. It could have, but on this tableland a print laid down is a foot obliged, and a power's foreknowledge makes a deep print. It left that stretch of moss unmarked.

By then it understood that the post it might have taken was filled. Everything a wise power would have brought to the plateau was already being done there by ten small wet animals on a wage. They ate the right work and refused the wrong. They never gave the moss the same thing twice to copy. They had left a warm hollow alone for generations and stepped round prints that led nowhere. When the cause finally walked in at the back of the following, they neither drove it off nor greeted it. The power could not improve on these manners, so it adopted them.

That left one item, and it was not the fox's.

The plateau delivers out of order. The roar comes and then the gallery, the prints and then the feet. A hollow stays warm for generations, and then there is a sleeper in it. And one pool has held a clear night sky for longer than anyone has lived, and no such night has come. The power had wondered, in passing, what had drawn its attention to a tableland of moss at the edge of nothing. It stood at the starred pool and stopped wondering. It is a strange thing for a power to find its own print ahead of it, filling with water.

It could have stepped round, as the foxes do. But this was the one thing here that only it could do, and it asked nothing of anyone. A sky is not a specification.

So on one night, in the coldest hour, when the hum drops its tone, it held the cloud off.

The cloud base, which sits on the plateau like a lid, went up and kept going. For the first time in living time there was distance overhead, and nobody reissued it. The stars came out as they are, uncountable and unmatched, in no rows, each keeping its place the way the quartzite does. Nothing up there was a run. The cold came down hard and the whole flat ticked with ice. On the sedge tips the violet points burned on, thousands of them, looking for once like a poor copy of something.

Ten foxes raised their heads and looked for about as long as it takes to recognise a thing one has been told of. Then they went back to sleep. They came from somewhere else and have always known there was one. The eleventh did not sleep. It sat upright in its hollow on the rock, with no heartbeat, and watched until the cloud came back. It was put together out of watching. For those hours it had something to watch that the plateau had not made, could not withdraw, and would not be able to stamp out in rows by morning. At the edge of the stone the beat idled. It may have slowed. The power did not check.

Before the light returned, it let the cloud down again. It has not done this since and will not. Once is not a pattern, and the moss cannot work from one.

The next shift ran pools, each a metre across with the same curl of ice, and every one of them reflected cloud, the starred one included. The waders stand in it now, facing northeast. Later that day the eleventh came to the bank by a way it had not used and looked down. There was one fox in the water, thin, carrying its head nearly as the masked one does, careful in the hindquarters, with grey behind it. Nothing in the pool was heavy in the shoulder. There is no copy of this fox anywhere now except the fox. It looked for some time. Then it drank, which a fox may do at any pool once.

The power left by a way it had not come. It still attends, from far enough off that the moss cannot feel itself watched, and it attends only to what is there. It does not know whether the note will one day be for the eleventh fox. It does not know what would go over the lip, or what would be left idling. It arranged not to know. An animal made out of being watched ought to have one stretch of ground ahead of it that nobody has looked at.

When a split shrieks, the eleventh still stops dead with one forefoot lifted. That has not changed and was not meant to. But sometimes now, before the foot comes down, it glances up at the cloud, which the born foxes never do. It has a somewhere else now. It did not come from there, but it knows the direction. Then it puts the foot down in a place it has not put it before.
