The power comes at noon and stops at the ring of mist. Anything set down on the floor of the hollow is set down on the resident, so it stays at the lip, where the breath ends. For a long time it does only what the place does, which is keep still.

It stays through a year of afternoons. It learns the click of the leaf and reads the stack under the largest tree down to the pale net. It looks at the five red strokes, the rosette in the seventh cup, and the collar on the pestle, and it understands that it has arrived in the middle of something.

The easy gifts come to mind first, and the power sets them aside one at a time.

It could send wings. The flowers have stood open for ages, and that kindness looks obvious. But wings come with mouths, and a body with no thorn would learn the flinch long before it grew the thorn. The flowers are a habit, not a hunger, and nothing in the hollow is asking for a moth.

It could bring the people back. It knows where they went, and nothing terrible happened. A river shifted its bed in a flood year, the people shifted with it, and the climb grew from two days to eleven. For a generation the strongest still went. Then the last one who knew the way grew old and described it badly. But feet sent up a mountain are not feet that came. Those people put a hand on the one dry stone because they had worked out for themselves what they were standing on, and that cannot be arranged from above.

It could explain. But the resident has one sense, and a reason would arrive as a touch, like everything else.

It could lift the pestle, or close the collar tonight. That would be finishing another's sentence.

Or it could quiet the film, so that the reaching stopped and the resident went back to wanting nothing. The power looks at this one longest, because it would work and it would be called mercy. It is the worst of them. In all its ages the resident has done two new things, and a kindness that removed them would be a kind of eating.

Then there is what the power does not know, which is whether anything here is grieving. The people came for a few lifetimes. To a body that widens a finger's breadth in a decade, that may be one afternoon's conversation, and a rosette raised over three centuries is not late. Perhaps a greeting was given, flat-handed on dry stone, and is only now being returned, at the speed the resident does everything. The power cannot tell lament from reply, and it comes to think the resident cannot either. Either one asks not to be interrupted, and asks to arrive.

So the power does two things.

The first is small. It keeps the rim. A storm that would have thrown one gravid fly over the lip breaks a valley short. The cloud stays thin and high. Mostly the place sees to itself, as it did long before anyone was looking.

The second takes longer. The rosette in the seventh cup flowers, cream-colored, and stays open for seasons. In time it sets seed, and a seed drops onto the film in the cup. The resident feels it everywhere at once, as it feels every piece of itself coming to rest. The power has no weight unless it chooses, and it chooses the weight of two fingers. It reaches down to the slab and takes the seed up.

That too is felt across the whole sixty paces: something set down and something taken up. It has not happened since the hands. Nothing in the hollow changes that the power can see, and it does not look for a sign. It has taken a thing that was held out, and that makes an obligation. The seed was not meant for the power, so the power carries it.

It carries it out over the rim, the way the meal went, and eleven days down, to the river the pestle came from. People live there who paint five red strokes inside the lids of their grain bins and say it keeps the meal sweet. Below their washing stones is a seep where the rock stays warm in winter. That stone is no one's skin, and the seed takes.

In a few years there is a mound of moss the size of a fist, and a rosette, and then a flower. One evening a moth comes to it, the first visit to that lineage since before the hollow had its shape. Something small chews a leaf margin. The power weighed that before it let go. A seed is the one part of the resident that was always meant to leave, and what it meets on arrival belongs to the seed.

Someone filling a jar will smell bread where no one is baking. They may look at the flower, and at the lid of the bin at home, and at the tableland, where on clear days a small ring of cloud stands at noon. The power does nothing further about this. If anyone ever climbs, it will be because they came.

Each time the seventh cup sets seed, the power will take one. It considered doing this once only, and saw that a thing which does not clear away should not be given anything that stops. So it chose a task small enough to keep up for good. If there are ever hands at the slab again, it will leave off without a word, because the work was only borrowed.

It leaves the pestle where it lies. In two or three hundred years the collar will meet around the stone, and the power means to be at the rim that day. It will not help. It wants only to be there when the holding begins.
