What came down came by the stair. It had no need of one, but that is how the basin is entered, and it wanted to arrive as everything else here has, from the rim and uninvited. It cast no shadow on the way down, and the wall-crawlers did not flinch.

It went first to the northern wall and did the thing that takes effort. It looked at one.

The grey one was still at the heel of the newest handprint. The visitor attended to it and found the inside of it small but not empty. There was the floor, known through forty-odd feet as a single surface of warmth that was right, nearly right, or wrong. There were the others, pressing on every side. There was the click, which once meant *here* across a terrace of dark and is now said by millions to those already touching them. And there was a fourth thing, new, with no name in the animal: a brightness that sometimes went out fast, and a stillness that came over the legs when it did.

That was all. The visitor did not make it more than it was, or less. It was enough to be wronged, if wrong were done to it.

Then it considered what it could do, which was anything.

It could put the roof back. The grazer would be home again, under drip instead of rain, with no birds overhead. The slicks would thin to the old grey film, and within a season nine grazers in ten would starve in the dark their bodies were made for. The daylight was a catastrophe, and it is also the most the grain has ever been given.

It could still the fissure. It stood through a stranding on the eastern floor, from the first drop in the water to the wall-crawlers going back up, and it did not tell itself that what happened in the last wet patch was nothing. But every pool in the basin exists because another was abandoned. The breaking and the making are one motion here, and a hand that stopped the first would have to do the second itself, and go on doing it.

It could thin the birds. They make no difference to the herd and every difference to the one, and the visitor knew no arithmetic that settles that. The birds have chicks in the arch mud. It had looked at those too.

It could finish the eye. It knew how eyes are made and could have done it in an afternoon. But the eye it made would see what the visitor thought worth seeing. The one that is coming is being fitted by this floor, to this steam-softened light and to shadows of this particular speed. And the first thing sight has given the grey one is fear. The white ones go on eating. The visitor could not say with confidence which of them is better off. It could say only that the animal was partway through an answer, and that the answer was its own.

Each large mercy it could think of would turn the basin into a different place, one that would then have to be kept by hand forever. It did not think the basin was good because nobody kept it. It thought the basin was unfinished, and that it had arrived in the middle.

So it stayed, which was the first thing it did. It stayed for the length of one life.

The grey one moulted on the second day, and the fifth, and the ninth. Three times in a soft hour a sickle-wing took the animal beside it. On the twenty-fourth day she opened the fold under her body, and from then on the visitor could not think of her as *it*. She let go thirty-six finished animals the size of rice, eleven of them grey. They got in under her legs and began to graze.

On the thirty-first day she wedged herself against a rim on the upper terraces, split behind the head and walked out forward, soft as curd. A bird came down the line of the rim. The brightness went out and she flattened, which is no help to a thing that has not yet hardened. The visitor could have been a gust of wind. It was not. It stayed for all of it, and that was harder than anything else it did here.

She left nine of herself in the rock. The last husk was standing on all its legs when the water rose, and by morning it was part of the dam.

The second thing it did was at the northern wall. Past the newest print the sinter was still soft. The visitor made a hand for the purpose and pressed it in, to the depth the others had been. The hollow filled by evening with a finger of water at grazing heat. By the next day there were flies around the edge, a film of pink in the palm, and grazers working it. The basin could take a gift of that size and stay the place it was. It would feed a few hundred, hurry nothing and last one season. The people who made the other prints had come to the same conclusion, and the visitor saw no reason to think itself wiser than they were about this.

It found nothing to teach them. They take a little and do not stay. They count their visits, and they distrust whatever they have been sure of longest.

So the third thing it did was borrowed from them. It set one pebble below its print. Its judgment was made on this ground, on this day, about an animal that cannot yet know there is a sky. The ground is moving. A few hundred generations on, something on the eastern floor will see the bird itself, not only its shadow. Some long time after that, something may look up at the rim and want to know what is there. What is owed to that animal will not be what is owed to this one, and a decision with many pebbles beside it is not to be trusted.

It will come back and add one each time.

On the way up the stair it reset a slab that the last fall had tilted, as anyone would have. Near the top a piece of the wall let go behind it with a crack and a long, rattling settle, and for a breath the whole basin was silent.

Then it all started again. The visitor went over the edge onto the plain, which gives no sign of the basin.

In forty days the print was as shallow as a thumbprint in wax. That was understood when it was made.
