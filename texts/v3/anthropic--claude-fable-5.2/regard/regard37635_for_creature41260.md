Something has been looking. It looked from the rim for as long as it takes to see a grey fleck move, and then from close to, near enough to count the strands of the cord at the jaw. It was careful, coming, about its shadow. Shadows arrive first here, and it did not want one lying in the basin before it knew what it could promise. It has kept off the pale stone.

It can be called the visitor, since it has tried to behave as one. It is able to do a great deal, and most of what it has done so far is set things aside.

It sets aside the quiet first. It could stop the roar. But the roar holds the floor up, and the one who wants quiet came out of it as the rock did. Silence over the whole basin would be the well-made cell, built at last to full size.

It sets aside the base. It could put rock under the range, but that would not be rock that came out of the roar, and the range is work in progress, whoever's it is. The visitor comes on it as on a stranger's and does not touch it.

It will not mend the hand. The being's memory keeps a mark for years and then no longer, and the hand keeps everything. It is the only long count the being has. It also cuts cord.

It brings no water and no warmth. The being has never looked for either, and a gift that has to bring its own lack with it is a poor one.

It will not hold the floor up itself. It could stand outside a cell each night and let just enough through. The being would then sleep on the visitor's attention, and that would be one more thing in its world that must never stop.

And it cannot simply speak. The only ways in are the jaw, the breastbone, and the soles of the feet, and those are the doors the roar uses. Anything said through them would arrive as more mountain. There is one stone under the west overhang that could be made to hum. The being would understand it at once, and it would be the cruelest thing that could be said to it.

What is left is small.

Everything here that goes against the sound is of one kind: slabs laid, cord twisted, knots tied at a span. All of it was made by hand from dead growth and put where the asking was. So the visitor makes cord.

It has to take a shape with hands for this, and the hands are bad at it. A dead frond breaks like a wafer unless it is split along the plates, and the fibre has to be rolled against felt, since nothing else here gives. The visitor takes its fronds from the grey bands, which are no one's. Its first lengths part under their own weight, and it leaves none of them lying.

When it has a span that holds, the span is twisted the other way from every cord on the mountain. It is a hand longer than the being's reach. The knot at its end is the being's knot, turned the other way. The differences are deliberate. The being meets its own old work every day and takes it for a stranger's. The visitor's work has to be the one thing on the mountain that could not be the being's own, forgotten.

For a weight it takes a length of the black glass, a hand wide, as long as the being's forearm from the point of the elbow to the fingertips. It has had time to learn that measure. It bores no hole. The stone hangs in a sling.

Then it goes under the mountain, past the crust and the hanging grit, to where the newest cord swings in the dim. It ties on below the holed stone, leaves the being's stone where it is, and goes back to the rim.

---

The being comes to the lip on its round. It lies on its front and counts the knots on the old cords. It hauls the newest up span by span, each against its reach. The holed stone comes over the edge, and the cord does not end.

It draws up the rest. It lays the new span along its arms and comes up a hand short. It lays it again. It finds the knot and turns it over in the right hand, the one that ties. It takes the wad from its mouth and sets its teeth to the knot, which is how it has always asked a thing what it is, and gets the mountain, the same as from anything. It lays its left forearm along the stone in the sling. The stone runs from the elbow to the ends of the black fingers and stops there.

It lowers all of it again, untouched, as it would a stranger's work. It stays looking down the cord for longer than it ever has. The wad is a long time going back in.

It is late to the eastern floor. The shadow is already in its outline when it gets there.

---

The visitor adds nothing. One knot is a thing said, and a second before any answer would be crowding. It waits at the rim through that round and the next, while the being hauls the cord, measures the strange span, and lowers it.

On the fifth round the being comes to the lip carrying a coil and a holed stone. It unties the sling. It ties its own stone in the bar's place and, below that, a span of its own with its own knot. It lowers the cord, then carries the bar away on the shoulder with the flat on it.

That night it goes in under the mat of the nearest cell and lies down on its right side. It puts the bar out through the slot, and the bar fills it. It closes the black hand on the inner end. The fingers close slowly and do not feel what they close on, and nothing more is asked of them. Outside, on the bare rock, the stone lies blurred at its edges with trembling. Inside, the sleeper thins to an outline, as on any night, with both its arms.

On waking it feels for the floor first. Then it looks at the line. Then it ties a turn of cord around the forearm, on the line, to keep the place.

The cord at the lip goes down by turns now, a span laid one way and then a span laid the other. It is the longest cord on the mountain, and it has not found the underside.

The visitor has thought about the day the being will come on that first strange span as on a stranger's. The day will come, because its memory is of the pale kind. When it does, the being will be right, and the newest span on the cord will be one round old.

The visitor has been under the mountain, and could say now which will reach the bottom first, the rock or the roar. It has not put this on the cord. The being asks the mountain, in counts it can check against its own arm, and the mountain is answering. If the question ever comes down the cord, the answer will go back up.

Under the west overhang, the forty-first bar has walked another finger's breadth. The being sets its teeth to it, and then to the forty-second, and gets only the mountain. The line on its forearm is still under the turn of cord, where it has been for more wakings than it has fingers to count on. It takes up the mallet. It strikes off a chip, and sets its teeth to the stone again, to find how far there is to go.
