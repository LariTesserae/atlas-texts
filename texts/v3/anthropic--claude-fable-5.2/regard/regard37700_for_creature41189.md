The power's first act is to give up its view. From outside there is nothing to settle: a shelf of clinkstone, one mountain, and no fact about how many pillars. Everything true here is true for something walking, so it takes a stride, a spine and a sense of the time of day, and goes out by the east cairns.

It counts as it goes and touches each pillar once. Between the two particular cairns it gets forty-four paces going and forty-nine coming back. It comes in by the west cairns leaning a little, with part of an hour gone, and now it knows the weight of one circuit. The counter has carried hundreds.

It sits down on a fallen plate, not in a ring wall, since those seats are worn to someone else.

## What it leaves alone

**The field.** It could true the plumbs, turn the shadows one way and make two mountains of the one. But whatever keeps the saddle seamless also keeps the water at cellar temperature. Mend it and night comes in, the sheet locks solid, and the skin dies in its drip lines. Hundreds of rows, each of them right, would also become hundreds of mistakes.

**The number.** It has one now. It was true once, for the one who made it, and there is nothing behind it to give.

**The counter.** The cairns say that at some time the counter wanted the way home. The later rows say it found work that does not need to end. The instruments cannot show which holds now, and the power will not settle the matter by inference. It would have to ask, and at noon the counter is only a sound and four wet strokes.

**The fifth stroke.** That one is spoken for.

## What it does

**It cuts a row.** It cuts on a pillar midway along, at its own shoulder height, which is not the counter's. The row is its count for that stretch, a different length from every row beneath it, and it ends in a stroke struck harder than the rest. This is the one thing the counter could not make for itself. A person measuring alone can never tell whether the difference lies in the ground or in the one who paces it. A second honest row that also disagrees does not say how many pillars there are. It says the fault was never in the counter.

**It leaves its hand.** Where the power touched the pillars, the skin has come up thick, in dark orange patches the shape of a hand larger than the counter's. They run through the field beside the clean ones. The skin has given up a hand's width on every pillar for longer than the drip cups took to form, and this is a hand's width back. It also shows, to anyone who touches pillars in passing, that touches here are kept.

**It builds one cairn.** At first the counter came and went. The way the cobbles came up is still there, but it opens at noon, and the counter's hour is the amber one. Path and walker have slid apart by the same slow arithmetic as everything else. So the power stacks plates chest-high, off the old lines, at the spot where low light will lay the cairn's shadow along the way down. On top it sets a cobble from the river at the bottom, dark and smooth and unbattered at either end, plainly not a tool. It is the first thing on the saddle left as a gift. Then the power walks the line down, to see that it reaches the river, and walks it back up, to see that it lets a person return. It is not a rescue, only the coming and going put back.

**It asks.** Lower on the same pillar, at the counter's shoulder height, it pecks four strokes and stops.

## What it does not get to know

The cloud comes up with amber in it. The power stays where it is and does not go in to look. A count is true once, for the one who made it, and so is a choice.

It listens instead, since the saddle delivers sound ahead of the thing itself. Tock, tock-tock comes from among the pillars. Some while ago there was one stroke louder than the others, and a pause after it longer than a breath needs. That might have been a hand going flat on a pillar and finding the skin thick where it had always been bare. It might have been nothing. A foot going down the new line would make no sound at all, because the roar at the south lip has no bottom to it, and whatever goes that way is never heard to land.

The cloud clears to noon, the same noon as before, and the bells start. The power gets up, a few degrees off true, and goes out by the east cairns. It has taken on a circuit and will come round again.

Two rows now stand at four strokes. One is wet and waits for a fifth that has already been heard. The other, in a larger hand, was cut low, where someone not tall could reach it. Nothing has been heard of its fifth stroke yet, or nothing that can be told apart from the bells.
