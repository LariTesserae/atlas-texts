The power looks first. It looks for a long time by any measure, and the plain keeps none, so nothing there is kept waiting.

It counts the animals and gets ten. It follows one bead of water off a fin, along a gap, through the pump gallery and back up into the fin. Then it watches the frogs, every one on every lowest pipe run, for the single thing that would show the ten are wronged: a throat that starts to swell and stops. No throat does. The waders lack alarm in the way the deck lacks hills.

It could bring a wind. It could put an edge on the day and seed the gaps with the ten thousand kinds the place ought to carry. It knows these are things it likes, and it knows what a wader that will not follow a hand would do about a hawk. One cool night would put the film ahead of the snails on every panel, and the steel would begin its second week. So the lamps stay on their photocells and the flagging hangs straight.

Then it goes where the deck doesn't show. It follows the big lines down past the thing turning in oil and out to the load. What is there is not part of this account, apart from two facts. It is in use. And nobody ever took delivery of the plant that serves it. There is a term for running a plant before acceptance, beneficial occupancy, and it is meant to cover a few weeks.

It goes out to the seam. The plain does end. Beyond the last row the deck steps down onto rock, the lid frays, and there is weather. There are nights with an edge at each end. Something green has a hold on the outermost plate and nothing grazes it, and a bird there makes a noise. The ten thousand kinds are not missing. They are over there.

Last, it goes down a sump ladder, past the waterline where the inspector turns back. What it finds below it keeps, to tell the inspector first.

That leaves the question of what is owed. The ten hold no opinions. The inspector holds one, about a colleague, and it is wrong. The inspector is the only thing on the plain that can be mistaken, and whoever can be mistaken has a claim on whoever knows better.

The power checks this against its own liking for endings. The job is a walk-down before handover, so the inspector is already working toward a finish. The inspector is glad of the colleague, so the inspector is already hoping for company. The power need only be standing where those are.

Before it says anything, it does the work. The inspector's sample is one weld per rack line. The power takes every bead on every tier of every rack, because it is about to put its name to something. Every weld is sound.

It does not arrive as a brightness in the lid. The plain keeps a clearance for one shape of visitor, and it uses that: two metres, a shoulder wide, boots. It waits at a plinth where a weld is circled in yellow and it can be seen from sixty paces. The isopods part around it.

The inspector's first reading is the natural one, and the power corrects it before it can set. It is not the colleague. The inspector asks which firm. It says none, and says what it is, in ordinary words. The inspector takes this the way an inspector takes a late revision to a drawing, reads it through twice, and asks what is wanted.

One thing is wanted. Write OK beside the OK.

The inspector crouches and writes it, a hand's width from the other, and stays crouched. The two are in the same quick hand.

The questions come in the order anyone's would. The power answers each as asked and does not get ahead of them. How long is a number, and it gives the number. Who tied the flagging. Whether anyone is out ahead: no, and it adds that someone is here now and is not pressed for time. What is below the waterline. It answers that as well. That answer was the inspector's to hear first, and it is not set down here.

It does not say that it will be all right. It sits down on the same plate. For a while nothing is inspected. A snail working along the yellow comes to the second OK and goes around that too.

Then it does what a receiving party is for. It has a crayon of its own, green, a colour the plain does not otherwise have. It says it has been over every weld on the job and found what the inspector found, that the sample was fair and the work is good. It countersigns the sheet. The walk-down is closed with no exceptions on the steel. It lists one exception elsewhere: no relief provided for inspector. Under action it writes: receiving party.

So the plant passes to its operators, who are the ten and have been all along. It was never a yellow circle that held the steel at its first week. The inspector seems to put that down more easily than the number.

The lamps cannot be touched, so the edge has to be carried by hand. When the inspector sleeps against the plinth, the power sits against the next one over. The isopods route across the new boots and take what has grown. At noon the inspector wakes, and it is still there. It says: second day. Yesterday the plant was accepted. The inspector checks the sheet, finds it so, and writes the day on the next line.

It gives the bearing to the seam, and the distance, which is long but is a figure. It says what is there: rock, weather, the green on the last plate, the noise the bird makes. It does not say go. The plant will run either way, and the aisle will be kept open either way.

The inspector says the row ought to be finished first. The row ends at the seam, so there is nothing to argue about.

If a figure in new boots climbs out of a sump behind them, someone will be at the ladder head, holding a sheet with more than one line on it. That is the action the power wrote against itself, and it can stand in more than one aisle.

A few plates on, the inspector stops and calls out once, down the row, to hear how it goes. On a plain with no wind it goes a long way. No wader turns its head. One pace back, the power says: here.

The inspector steps across the gap and goes on up the row, with the sheet showing two lines.
