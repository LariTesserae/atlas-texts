It comes up over the rim before the sun, when the steam is thickest, and steps down into the film. The birds do not lift. The rank opens a few yards ahead and closes a few yards behind, and no head comes up. In other places its arrival is noticed. It takes this as the first thing the plateau has to tell it, and for a day it does nothing but stand.

It gives the place a week, since that is what the town has always given.

It works the way she does, by laying yesterday over today, though it has more yesterdays to lay. It sees the springs wander across ten thousand years and the town go after them plank by plank. It sees the old pipes sink into the sinter and new ones go up on trestles. It sees how much heat is left under the plateau, which is a great deal. It sets down what it has in place of a pole and attends to what comes back. Nearly everywhere, the ground knocks.

A power's habit is to mend, so it goes through what might be mended.

The burn under the jaw comes first. It could thicken the air a little. But then water would boil later, and every band in every channel would slide. The flies would lose their line, and the birds theirs, and two people who had been comfortable at the same distance from the same vent would find they no longer agreed. Everything here stands at its own degree. It leaves the air alone.

The spring coming up behind her hut it considers longest, because by the third day it is fond of her. Turning the heat aside would be a small matter. But she knows her liking the spot has no bearing on it, and the power finds, looking squarely, that its own liking has no bearing either. The town stands on one thing, which is that the ground does not lie. Where the flies thin, something is coming. If once the flies thinned and nothing came, a red stripe would mean a little less, to her first of all. It will not make the birds unreliable for the sake of half a degree. It leaves the spring alone.

Then the hum. It could let her hear it, but a raker has tried that already. She counts everything else she has, and the power decides she can keep one thing uncounted.

Then honour. But the town moves its planks where she paints, within the week, and nobody asks to see the thermometer. There is nothing to add to that.

Then the things she reads and has never mentioned. Here, for the first time, the pole gives nothing back. There are the tongue-marks and the paling of the shrimp, and there is no book. It stands at this spot for most of a day. Then it sees that the town has already painted the stripe. It sent down the escarpment for a boy, from a place where people ask things aloud. Nobody raised up here would have asked. The plateau will teach him to be quiet, as it should, and the power hopes it is slow about it.

So by the sixth day it has done nothing, and is fairly sure that nothing is correct.

On the sixth evening it does one thing. The boy is on the pan-house roof, where he has been set to learn the shutters. The light has gone to brass. The long-necked animals come up over the rim and start along the crust beyond her channel. Between the boy and the animals stands the steam, as always. The power thins it. It does not part it. It thins it by the amount it sometimes thins on its own, for about as long as it takes five animals to work along a crust, stop at a line, lift their heads, and go around.

The boy might have seen this anyway, within the year. The power knows that. It allows itself the one thing, the way she allows her own doorstep the full week.

In the morning he is at the end of the last plank before she is, crouched, looking at where the marks leave off. When she wades up he asks her why they stop there. He starts with his hands, runs out of signs, and finishes aloud. This time she can tell which part he means. Her answer takes a while. He does not ask about the shrimp, because he has not noticed them yet. The power does not wait to find out when he does.

At the end of the week she paints the stripe across her own step. She finds four people and a rope, and the boy is one of the four. The hut goes after the line for the twelfth time.

That night she stands in water that is wrong by half a degree. The power stands in it too, a few yards off, and the birds have closed around them both. It is the only thing it does for her directly, and she does not know of it. She is humming.

Then it goes back over the rim, past the long-necked animals, who do not look up. It has been a week on the planks. Going down the escarpment it carries a low note in whatever it has for a chest, and if anyone told it so, it would say they were mistaken.
