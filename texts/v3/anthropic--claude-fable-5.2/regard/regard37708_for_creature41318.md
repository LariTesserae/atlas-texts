**The Visit**

It came in by the lane, as anyone does, on a morning near the end of the rains. It could have arrived any way it liked, but the lane is narrow enough to touch both walls, and it wanted to touch them.

At the gate it heard the four sounds, and unlike the ear it did not stop counting them. It does not stop counting anything.

It saw Uncle at once, which no visitor had managed before. He also saw it. He hears only what is not one of the four sounds, and attention of that kind was not one of them. He stopped under the frosted pipe with the oil can and looked for about a second, letting the beat go by. Then he made the sign for *come and look*.

**The round**

The round took forty minutes. The power walked behind him at one step to each thud, which it had to learn.

- At the compressor he filled the oil cups.
- At the condensers the power could see through the liverwort to the iron. It knew the thickness of every coil, which joint would go, and in which year. It lifted a hand toward the rack and found fingers resting on its wrist, not hard. It took the hand away.
- At the basin he put his hand in the water, and it put its hand in beside his. They did not disagree.
- At the husk door they both felt for cold. There was none.

It thought about the joint for the rest of the round. Against the joint stood a pair of wet eyes, a tin of tapers kept dry, and sixty years of smoke turning white. New iron would have to be scraped bare to be fitted, and would be trusted by nobody. It left the matter in the hands that already held it.

**What it considered**

- **The sea.** It can see the eleven kilometres of flats, and also the number of wet seasons before the drains run backward for two hours, then six. It could lift the yard or hold the water off. But everything that has lasted here has lasted by being worked around or worked with. A wall against the sea would be the first thing in the yard simply told no, and the yard keeps that word for two places.
- **The bed and the pension.** A decent man had raised these twice. The power raised them a third time anyway, in the engine house, with the signs available. It made *come and look* with the other hand open toward the gate, meaning a dry room, the flats he came from, or anywhere at all. It got tea, and the tea was good.
- **The birds.** It could have brought them to the fig. But the ammonia and the noise are honest, and a kingfisher told to come in would no longer be a kingfisher.
- **The photograph.** It knew what was in the photograph, as it knew the iron. It did not open the tin.
- **His chest.** It listened, since that is where the lane's account begins. Nothing whistled.

**What was already being done**

There is a girl of eight in the lane who carries her grandmother's pots to the leeward side and is slow to leave. She has met the flat hand twice at the belt run and not a third time. Lately she waits about a second before answering her mother, and is scolded for it. Last month Uncle showed her the sign for tea.

The power had its own view of what the works would need in ten years. It found this already under way without it. Had the two disagreed, it would have checked its own view.

**What it did**

It did two things.

The first was for him. The lane leaves it open whether his feet go where the roots are or the roots have grown where his feet go. The power settled the question in favour of the roots. For as long as he climbs, there will be something under each foot. He will not notice, because from his side there always has been.

The second was for the tower. If you asked Uncle who lives at the ice works, he would look at the tower, and the power took his word.

That night he sharpened the machete. In the morning the cutting took ten minutes, and the cuttings went into the canal. It was the top of the tide, and the canal was running backward, as the drains do. One cutting went inland instead of out. It lodged in the brickwork of a disused sluice some way up, where the ground is a little higher, the air is plain, and the only sound is water. The power's part was the timing, a matter of a few minutes.

Anything left on brick here roots. In some years that fig will fruit, and there the figs will be eaten.

This does not save the works. The fastest change the works makes takes two days, and the slowest one being made to it will take a century. The power interfered with neither.

**Four o'clock**

It stayed for the hoist. The first block went down the chute. He knocked a chip off its corner, held it to the bulb, and put it in his cheek. Then he knocked off a second chip, held that to the bulb as well, and held it out.

It was clear right through. The lane's count of ice eaten inside the works is now off by one.

When the crusher stopped he took his tea to the canal step. It was a kingfisher morning, and for most of an hour the three of them faced the same way.

**After**

The power has not exactly left. Its attention stays on the yard at about the level of the mist off the leeward side. By the second day Uncle had stopped hearing it. From him this is not neglect. It is how a thing is admitted to the works.

The engineer measured the difference the next morning and wrote it in the ledger. The number had not changed.

Ask in the lane who lives at the ice works and the first answer is still "nobody." The second, after a moment, is still "well, Uncle." There is no third answer, and the power is content with that.
