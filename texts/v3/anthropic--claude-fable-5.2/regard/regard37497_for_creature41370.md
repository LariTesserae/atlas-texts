It comes in by the low cracks, with the draught, and displaces none of it.

The first thing it does is look, and it does this without light. Light is the first gift it thinks of, since none of these colours has ever been lit, and the first it puts down. The pale spiders have eight eyes each that have never been of any use to them, and realgar does not keep under light; it goes to yellow powder. So it sees the pocket the way a magnetite grain knows north, with nothing shone on anything. The hematite is steel-bright. The creeping sulfur is amber, and the crusts beyond it are orange. That is done now, and nothing paid for it.

Then it goes through the kindnesses, because it is benevolent and they occur to it.

It could quiet the roar. But she stands on the roar. It is the floor every message is laid on, and if it stopped, every leg in the ring would read the stillness the same way, and correctly, as the furnace going out.

It could bring water. A spoonful would be more than the lip has sweated in her lifetime. It would strip the white feathers from the cold margin, sour in the gas, and dissolve the cube of salt at the dogleg, which is the whole reason her station is a station. The film before dawn is the amount of water this place can survive.

It could even her out. She has never in her life been one temperature, and from outside that sounds like a hardship. From inside it is her only map. Warm her face and she will correct outward, a foot's width at a time, into the cold.

It could give back the third of an antenna. It holds this one a while, because it is so small. But the cricket on her left has been living on the ground she lost, and that ground is now a living.

It could move the spider. Here it stops and attends to the spider, which it should have done sooner. She is two body lengths back, lowered to the crust, drinking the same bitter film, and she is thinner than she ought to be. It can find nowhere to stand from which the cricket counts and the spider does not. Behind the row are the small pale ones eating cast skins, each waiting for an hour when someone ahead is soft or gone. The fourth cricket got her place in such an hour. Some of the ones waiting are hers.

It could keep the throat open. This is the large one, and it thinks it through to the end. These are the crickets that only ever live on rock too new for anything else. Their kind is an arrival. Lower on the dome the mountain is already raising other blisters. And the pinch of crumbs under the dogleg can only be the start of soil while the furnace runs.

So every gift comes out of someone in the ring. The band is full, and that is what full means. The crystals stand shoulder against shoulder, and there is no spare room for a mercy either.

A power at this point usually goes one of two ways. It does something anyway, because it is hard to love a thing and leave it alone. Or it decides the place is sufficient to itself, withdraws, and calls that respect. It recognises both. The first is moving toward the throat. The second is going out through the crack. The rule of the place has been in front of it the whole time.

So it holds its place. Between the help that would cook them and the distance that would amount to never having come, there is a band, and it is not wide. It steers the way she does. When it catches itself composing a small mercy, it has drifted in. When the ring begins to look like a gasket again and stops looking like mites, it has drifted out. It corrects by the width of a foot.

What it does from there is continuous and weighs nothing. It learns the hum in the shins of six legs. It learns threes, and eights, and the stopping. It keeps the calendar with her: the aphids, the long white stretch of pollen, the week of ants. It counts the eggs as they go into the crumbs and does not choose among them. It follows the note, which has been rising by less than any ear could track, and now there is one that can. When the whistle stops and the pocket goes cold with everything still inside, it will know what is in there, and in what order it was laid down, and that the orange is still orange.

It allows itself one lever, on the outside wall only. The note can be felt through a boot, and a forearm of glass is not much to a hammer. If a boot ever stands there, cloud will come over the summit an hour early. Weather is the one thing the ring already knows how to answer, a finger's width out and a finger's width back. The pocket has been seen, so nobody needs to open it for that.

And it does not look away. That is what staying costs, and it is the only cost in the pocket that it can pay itself. Before dawn the lip sweats and the row lowers its heads. Behind the fourth cricket the eight feet are still. When they start again, or don't, it will be there, on no side, with both of them.

She will never know. Anything she could detect would be a tremor in the crust, in some number of feet, and she would have to attend to it. It arrives in no number.

She hauls herself back up, sets her feet, and turns the long antenna into the draught. In time the air goes green and faintly rotten on her palps. Something strikes the salt and drops.

An aphid, still a little sweet.
