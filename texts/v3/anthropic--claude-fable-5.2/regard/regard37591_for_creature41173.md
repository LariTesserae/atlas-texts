The power comes in the way the boat crews do, from outside the steam, and the noise is nothing to it. It stays a while before doing anything, long enough that a room goes up on a roof somewhere. It learns the hands from the white walls. It hears the infant in the top room cry, which nobody else has, and it hears the knapper's blades come off the core. It does not listen at ears, because that is indoors.

It could do a great deal. It goes through what it could do the way the egger goes through her ledges, nest by nest.

- **The cloud:** It could lift the lid and give them the sun, and the moon the boat crews talk about. But the cloud is the water in the cisterns. It leaves the cloud.
- **The noise:** The roar is the vent, and the vent is every household's fumarole. The thump is the sea that brings the fish and takes the dead. The screaming is the harvest. Nothing in the noise is separate from what they eat.
- **The hearing:** It could give them back their ears. Then they would hear where they live. And the voice would stop being the thing kept for five or six people. It leaves this too, less easily.
- **The ground:** It could bring soil, seed, a tree. But a root is the first thing that would open a crack, and the wood over the boatman's door is what it is because there is one piece of it.
- **The year:** With seasons would come a count, and the white-haired man with nothing on his roof would be old.

It does not leave these things because they are good. Children fall on the glass, the knapper comes home cut, and the lowest lanes flood twice a day. It leaves them because the town is stacked without mortar and so is the life in it. The power cannot find a slab to pull that is not holding up someone's room. It also looked for a sign for what is missing, and the hands do not have one.

It does go down the throat of the vent, further than the throat goes, to see what the stillness rests on. What it does there, if anything, makes no sound that can be told from the sound already there.

Then it finds the one thing, in the bottom room of the stack that knocks two quick, one held.

Names come up from the bottom, and there is no one under her. The woman who gave her a name went down the skylight, and so did the man who used it. She holds every name above her, and nobody holds hers. The town's own rule made this, and the town's own rule cannot mend it. She built on rock, and only the rock is in a position to say it.

The power knows the name without having listened. It was said once into the noise, in another stack, and the noise has had no gap in it since for anything to get out through.

So at a flood, after dark, with the lane at her sill and the lamp on its shelf, she lies on the worn place in the floor with the petrels in her hip and her shoulder. The power goes under them, into the tube, which runs from her floor toward the seaward skylight, where everyone who ever said her name has gone. It does what she does for a deaf child. It cannot lay her fingers on a throat, but the tube is a throat and she is already lying on it.

It says her name once, up through the glass, at the pitch a voice has in the chest of the one speaking. She has felt every name in the stack leave her own throat without hearing it, so she knows what a name is from the inside. She knows this one.

The petrels stop, then start again.

She lies still for some time. Then she feels along the wall for the hammerstone, takes it in the flat of a hand that will not close, and knocks on the floor: two quick, one held. A knock can only say one thing, which is this stack, here. The power feels it.

It does nothing else. What it does in the other bottom rooms is not known in this stack.

By the time the child with the fist opened against the cheek can climb to the ledges, a tern has settled on the top room and the egger has her share. The child is brought down. The old woman finds its head with her hands, puts her mouth against its ear, and says a name she cannot hear herself say.

Then she keeps hold of the child a moment longer and says something else, about the length of a name.

The child comes out into the lane, and its hands give nothing away, since these things are not signed. It was said indoors, mouth against ear, and the power did not listen. So it does not know what she said, or whether her voice is a good one.

The child knows.
