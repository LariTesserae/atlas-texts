The third answer comes from outside.

It comes over the rim from the cool country, so by the basin's reckoning it arrives from very far. It stops at the margin, where the mud has no holes in it. It does not go down into the streets, because the treads are worn one file wide and it is not the one they are worn for.

Heat is nothing to it. It could bring a degree, or all the degrees, in a single dark. So the first thing it does is not do that, and look.

It looks first at whether the cold is a grief, and does not assume so. There is some evidence:

- **The going out:** the resident goes out at every high dark, and nothing sends it.
- **The wall:** its room has four walls to sit against, and it sits against the one the conduit reaches.
- **The spoonfuls:** it takes nothing from the shrubs and lays in no stores, so it is not provisioning itself to stay far.

This is a reading, and the power holds it as one. Then it goes through what it could do:

- **Bring it in:** a resident warmed in one dark would be new, and nothing here can be new. The corners are being rounded by the hand that is coming, and the sills are dished by exactly the slowness of the approach. Fetched early, it would stand in a town worn by someone else, near everything and arrived at none of it. To be fetched is also not the same as to come.
- **Go out to it:** the power could sit down beside it, a second thing at its distance. But a far place with company in it is a place to stay. The shrubs already give this in the right measure: the length of a wall, then on.
- **Speak:** nothing sounds under the dark, which is the only hour it is abroad. At low stand a voice would carry, but it would be the first new thing in the basin, and the first thing the resident met here would not be the town.

It declines all three. Then it looks over what the town is already doing, expecting to find it short.

The conduit carries its finger's depth of nearness to the wall and stops. It could have breached the wall in a season. It lets the heat through alone.

The roof in the west was hung before there was anyone to shelter.

The power puzzles longest over the spoonfuls, cold water held out to a cold thing, refused, and held out again. Then it sees that they are not for the one who passes beneath. They are for someone at blood heat, later, in a basin where all the other water is bath-warm or worse. They are one cold drink to a block, kept ready. They are also the only place the town keeps any of the far.

It finds nothing short. The less-than-a-degree is not stinginess. It is the rate at which a thing can come and still be the one who arrives.

That leaves what the town cannot do. Things here fall slowly enough to follow, and nothing follows them. The water is always going somewhere, and the dark is an hour. No one stays for the whole of a fall. The slowness is a courtesy extended to nobody.

The power takes it up. At each low stand it follows one drop from roof height down into its ring. At each high dark it follows the other thing. It cannot do this by sight, since there is nothing to tell. It goes by the doorways, whose cool side changes as something passes through. Afterward it goes by the row of ticks up the middle of a stair, which it counts.

It cannot tell whether being followed is any part of the warming. To find out it would have to stop, and it does not stop.

It keeps one other thing. When the resident arrives it will always have lived here, and the town will back it up. The sills, the stone leaves and the full-grown felt all say always. Nothing in the basin will say that it came, or from how far, or that most of every high dark was spent arriving. The power keeps that at the rim, in the cool, where a thing kept stays far and so stays as it was. It does not mean to hand this over. It is there to be asked for.

After some high dark there is someone at blood heat standing with a hand on a corner. A tick sounds, and a ring spreads in the film. The someone watches the drop come down into the centre of its ring, the whole way.

So the following has passed to other eyes, and the power is relieved of it.

Later the someone reaches up to a wall-head, tips a leaf and drinks. For the length of a swallow it is as far off as it ever was. Then it is back, by a road that took it since the first course and now takes less than a breath. It stands a while with the leaf in its hand. It may look toward the rim.

The power goes back over into the cool country, which here is the same as leaving. It takes the count with it.

At the margin there is a dished place in the sinter, the width of one who sat. Its edges are rounded, and there are stone leaves in its footing. It does not look new.
