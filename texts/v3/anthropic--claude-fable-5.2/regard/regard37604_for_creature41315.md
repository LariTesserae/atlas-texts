The attention arrives on no sheet. Nothing in the valley was drawn on an issue that includes it, so nothing is obliged to behave as though it were there. The choughs do not shift along the purlins. The hare runs its catwalk.

To find it, look at dawn, when the works sweats. There is a place where the plate stays dry and is not cold, and there is usually a marmot lying on it.

For the first season the dry place moves about. It is in the glazed office, where the corners of the sheets lift one after another and settle. It is at the gratings. It goes down the ladders, further than the ibex bother to. Each morning it is beside a puddle until the puddle drains, which is long enough; a rivet head is not small to it. It is in the valley when the tone is let out, and it sees which way the figure turns, and for how long.

Then it is at the foot of the cooling tower, beside the figure, and for some days nothing happens.

It had come expecting something to mend. It found something that had started, on its knees, without being asked. Most of what it does next is decline.

It could give the figure a face. At 1:200 none is owed, but that is the office's rule, and the attention is on none of the office's sheets. It does not, because a face is already coming up through the puddles, and that one is the figure's own. A face given now would be the attention's idea of a face, and the figure would have to wear it.

It could let the voices out. The line is in, and is tested once a season. It does not. The choughs would get theirs in the same instant, eleven sheds of them, and the figure would have to find out what its mouth is for in the middle of that. And a voice let out by the attention would owe its first word. On the one sheet large enough to need a mouth, someone has turned the head away. The attention leaves it turned.

It could strike out *only*. The pen is on the sill and the ink is wet. But *do not scale from this drawing* is already on every sheet, in the office's own hand, and where two instructions disagree a seam runs. The attention has seen what the valley does with a seam. A hare runs along one every morning at full stretch. It leaves the two notes unreconciled, and the pen on the sill.

It could bring the sheets up to date. This is the one it stays over longest, because it looks like justice. The moss is hatched in and credited to others, and the one who pressed it in is drawn elsewhere, standing. But anything drawn here is obeyed. A figure drawn kneeling would kneel, at a mark, with a dimension to the knee. The hours when no issue is moving it are the only undrawn thing in four kilometres. The attention is careful in the office and leaves nothing there.

It could stop one seam, so that a single puddle stood until noon and the figure could walk over and look in. It goes to choose a seam and finds the job in hand. The moss is caulking. Every thumb's width pressed in is a thumb's width the water no longer goes through, and the puddles will stand a little longer each year until one of them stands long enough. The figure does not know that this is what it is doing. The attention cannot tell it in this phase, and decides it would not in a later one.

It could do the planting. If *others* means anyone, it could mean this. It does not press in a single cushion. The green lines are a thumb wide, and that is one thumb.

What is left after the striking out is short.

The figure's work is to stand next to things. The kids are stood near it to be given a size, and taken back up. So the attention stands next to it, and takes no size from being there. For as long as the dry place is at the tower, there is one thing beside the figure that it is not working for. In the hours when it goes along the seams, the dry place goes too, one plate behind. When the tone is let out and the figure turns toward the glazed office, the dry place is on its other side. The attention did not draw it, and takes care not to stand where a draughtsman would be looked for.

The other thing is underneath. The puddles answer for this level: more ibex, the lichen further along, both heels down. Nothing answers for the levels below. They are complete. No issue will move anything on them again, and nothing is on its way. The figure is on every one of them, weight forward, and there is one of it.

So the attention goes down, past the level with fewer choughs, past the thin moss, past the four or five where the plate is bare, and on down the ladders to the first. The figure was drawn before anything else, and on the first floor that can be seen. There is no tower yet. There is plate, a datum mark, what light comes down through all the gratings above, and the figure with one heel raised, giving a size to the floor.

There the attention sits down. The plate beside the mark is not cold, and has not been since. The works stands a storey higher every year, and every year the first floor is a storey further from anything, which is why it was chosen. The attention can be there and at the tower. What is done for the figure on the first floor is done for it once, on all of them.

Two things have been observed since, and neither was asked for.

On the first floor, in the seam nearest the mark, there is a green line. It is a thumb wide and about a hand long. Moss does not go down ladders by itself.

At the tower the figure is at its mark, 1750, one heel raised. The heel is raised by a little less than the sheets show. The difference is within tolerance, and the office has not picked it up.
