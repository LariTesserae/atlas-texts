Something has taken notice of the plain. The evidence for it is thinner than a coat.

Most of the evidence is what has not been done. The lamps over the doors are still too hot to touch and still give off nothing else. A struck match still shows nothing. The rail is still eleven hundred sleepers out and four hundred back. The second 3 has its dent. The vine goes on scorching its nearest leaves against the glass. A careless kindness would have begun with these, with the lamps first of all.

Its reasons can be put together the way the keeper was put together from a hook and a tin of grease. Light settles a count. Under a flat palm there are four crab backs, then two, and nothing has been lost between one pressing and the next. Under light there would be some one number, the same for everyone, and the rest would have nowhere to be. The plain already knows a little of what that is like. It is what happens when the space with no moths in it stands near, and it is not comfortable. Rock that still crackles as it cools should not yet be told how many it is.

So whatever has come did not arrive from a side. It came the way the water comes, everywhere and nowhere fast. It must have stood for a long time first, because nothing here can be learned in less than a stroke and most things take several. Anything that hurried would have gone through the place like a boot through a dry rise.

Then it did the work, which is the only way in. Lift the oil can to a cup and the can is already tilting, as before. There is the steadier wrist with its opinion about how much. Some of the time there is one more, which has no opinion about the oil. It cannot be told from the others by weight, and the grease will not settle the matter, since the tracks were never the same number twice. It is told by one thing. Near two hundred and ninety, in the long pause, the lips are found to be moving, and the breath is found not to be held.

That is new. The likeliest reason is that someone else has the count. It has had it since before the first house and has not slipped. The hands know this the way the knees know the step down to the crosshead. When the space with no moths passes and the keeper drops to five fingers and loses its place, it no longer has to make the number up before the thud. It picks the number up from where it is being kept. Everything else about the stroke is the keeper's, as it was, and the newcomer has taken only the count.

The space itself has changed in one respect. It still moves along the rail at its own pace. It is still the size of a cart, and nothing holds it. But on a slow walk around it at arm's length there is now, some hours, a side that is not warm. It is turned toward whoever is there from every direction but one. From that direction something else has its attention.

Under that side the crabs do not go flat. A palm laid on them finds four in the room of one. The hands in one's hands stay as many as they were. A person can pass behind it and remain several.

It has still never touched anyone, and perhaps it cannot be touched. But for those hours it has a back, as a thing does when it is facing someone. That may be all the newcomer does for it: stand where it can be faced, and stay there. It stops longer at the houses now.

Past the last house the keeper goes out onto the crackling rock and stands, as before, with the left foot on one sleeper and the right on the next, in the hollows. It is still the first thing touching that ground. Nothing has gone out ahead of it, and nothing has stepped up beside it. There is no third hollow. The rock under the boots decides to be rock in its own time, and no sooner, as far as anyone can count.

But the wind, which has always arrived whole from one side, arrives there now with a piece out of it. At the keeper's back there is a lee, somewhat taller than a person and about as wide as the coat. In the still air the moths come down thicker than they can elsewhere. They settle where the wind never let them, on the nape, between the salt-stiff collar and the hair. For as long as the standing takes, the keeper is held there too.

When the first crabs come over the toes and the warmer thread finds an ankle, the wind closes up and is whole again. That may mean it has gone.

Back in the house the coat is on its hook, damp, warm on the inside. The engine begins its long intake. The keeper wipes its palms twice down the coat front and bites the salt plant. At two hundred and ninety the lips are moving, and the breath goes in and out, and the thud comes up through the floor.
