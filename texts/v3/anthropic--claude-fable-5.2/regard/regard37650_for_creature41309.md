It comes in without a body. A body would make the hair lean, and they read the hair.

For a long time it does only what they do. It counts the lights in a chamber, counts again, and gets the same count. It follows a road with its attention on the cord until it can tell *thin floor* from *low rim* the way a palm can. It learns the notes of the rooms and does not hum them. It watches two of them sit after a walk and go over each other with their fingertips, and it stays for all of it, which takes as long as the walk did.

It spends some time in the small thick-walled room the grown ones dislike. That is the only place in the foam where one of them can be heard with the mouth open. It listens to an infant cry until the infant stops. After that it understands better what each of them carries wrapped.

Then it considers what it could do, which is anything.

It could bring water. One seep in one corner would be enough. It follows the seep forward and sees sweat, then haste, then an open mouth, then a voice raised across a room. It sees more of them than there are names, and a child old enough to walk the cord who is still waiting to be hummed. At the far end of that road there are people in the foam, and they are doing well. They are not these people, and nobody asked these.

It could open the foam and lead them out to where water falls from the air. They would have to leave the cords behind, and the cords are everyone they have had. It cannot find a way to make that offer in a language where the shape for *other* means a wrong turning.

It could speak. It could make the great room answer. It would only have to do that once. After that each of them would walk to the rim to ask instead of to be sure, and what they are sure of now they got by their own looking.

It notices that every one of these refusals leaves it with nothing to do and something beautiful to watch, and it distrusts that. The scars are not a pattern. The old ones did not give an eye in exchange for anything. An eye is a bag of water with nothing over it, a thread comes loose at a touch, and there is no wisdom in what happens next. It is only what the glass does.

So it looks for the smallest thing that is not nothing, and it takes its rules from the makers, who have been at this longer:

- **Pace:** Nothing faster than a body here can afford.
- **Keeping:** Nothing that keeps itself. The rind and the lights come back on their own, and anything it gives must be a thing they would have to keep on purpose, or could decline to keep.
- **Secrecy:** Nothing done in secret. They are not grazers to be moved along a wall. A palm resting on the thing should know that someone made it, and that it was no one they know.

The foam has bubbles of every size. That includes the size of an eye socket.

Far off the roads, so many walls away that a blue spark arrives brown or not at all, it makes itself a pair of hands and gives them the limits of the hands here. It finds a bubble no bigger than a plum, with a wall as thin as a fingernail where the brown goes to bottle-green. It flakes half of that bubble free, one chip, then a rest, then one chip. It does not need the rest. It takes the rest because it has to find out whether this can be done by a body that cannot sweat, and the only way to find out is to do it as one. It can be done. A doorway takes longer than a life, but this is a small rim. It takes a few hundred sittings with sand-glass.

What it has at the end is a cup of green glass that sits on the bone around the eye, dull rim to the skin. No thread gets past it. Through it the lights are one layer browner, as though seen from the next room. It is a doorway with the wall left in. They have made the other kind for longer than they have kept count, and everything they know about glass has told them to keep it from their eyes.

It makes a second, and stops.

It carries both to where the roads end and sets them on the felt beside the peg. It chooses the rim because this is the one place each of them comes with open eyes to find out whether anything is other. That is as near to an invitation as it expects to get, and it knows it may be reading the knot wrong. A cup can be left lying. The knowledge that someone outside their number ground it cannot be put back, and that is the one thing it will have done to them without asking.

It sits at the rim a while in the hands it made, and the hair leans toward it. When it goes, it leaves the hair leaning. It could straighten every thread. They tell by the hair, and it will not have the hair lie for it.

Then it goes out into the great chamber, below the decks, and finds the free end of the cord where it hangs. It takes hold. The cord runs at hip height, which is where a hand hangs to be held.

The one who comes next is old and has one eye. They stop short of the rim, wait for the threads to settle, and open it. The hair is leaning toward a place where nobody sits. They know where every basket hangs and who is in it. They stay still for a long time, the way they would after a spark.

Their hand finds the cups by the peg. They go over each one with their fingertips, the whole surface, feeling for an edge, and there isn't one. They hold a cup to the eye and look at the wheel through it. The decks slide past at walking pace, a little browner. A thread drifts against the glass and rests there. It is the first time in their life they have looked at anything without guarding.

They draw the cord in, hand over hand. It lets go of the end before they can feel any weight. They find the knot. They watch until they are sure, and the wheel is the same. Then, slowly, with rests, they tie a second knot a hand's width from the first. It is not *other*. It is *low rim*, which on the roads means that someone has made a way through here, and to mind it.

They take one cup and bind it into the felt at the wrist, where other fingers can go over it later. They leave the second where it lies. They never take two from one wall. Afterward they count the lights, as they do after taking anything, and the count comes back the same.

No one has knelt. It has been taken from the way they take from everything that keeps them, once, with a count after. It had not known to hope for that.

They let the cord back out, shut the eye, find the hip cord with the left hand, and start humming the way home. When the free end comes down through the hair, it takes hold again and feels two knots.

It does not follow to see what the others' fingers decide. It does not make a third cup, or replace the one that is gone. The rind does that, and it is not rind. If a question ever comes down the cord, it will answer in knots, at the pace of knots. Until then it holds the end at the height where a hand hangs, and waits to be told whether it did right.
