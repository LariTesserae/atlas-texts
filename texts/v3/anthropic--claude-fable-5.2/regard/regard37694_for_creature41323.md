The attention arrives the way the animal did, from the east, up the wobble of the overflow to the notch. It stops at the sill. Anything that enters the basin bends the currents, and the currents are the streets, so for a long time it stays on the rim and only looks.

It looks for the length of one wall. A line of white at the western edge rises to the level and roofs over. It turns straw, rose, and blue, carries the rust band for its season, greys, shows the next room through itself, and goes to powder. That is long enough to see every room in town made once and taken away once, and to see each thing that lives here do everything it does.

Then it counts. There are sixty or seventy rooms, a few dozen grazers on a band thirty paces wide, four or five hunters, and a few stalks to a roof. There is one animal with long ears. It counts again in the lean stretch, when no wall is grey and the animal walks the edge of the blue, and the figures agree. The town is filled to an exact level with eating, as it is with vapor. What falls through the roofs and what shows through the walls feeds one, with thin weeks.

So it does not bring a second. There are others; it went out and found them, spread over the plain a day's travel apart, which is how that ground carries them. Two here would have one good season, and then each would have half of a lean stretch that is barely enough for one.

It does not carry the animal out. Every cold period the animal walks straight to the one place in the rim where the plain begins, lies down in it, and sleeps facing out. It knows the door better than it knows anything. Each time the cold ends, it gets up and turns around. That choice is made again every time, with the plain in full view, and the attention finds nothing in it to correct.

It does not soften the rule, though it considers this longest. In a pale western wall, a finger deep, there is a grazer that could have been kept walking. But the wall will carry it east through all the colors, and at the far end something will tread a ring in front of it for days and then put its nose through. In a greying corner a hunter has stayed too long, and the crust could be split for it. On the other side of that crust the animal has been lifting its feet in turn since the wall was blue. Every mercy available inside the basin comes out of another mouth.

It does not put sound into the rooms. The silence is in the wall faces, in terraces too fine to see, and walls that gave a noise back would not be these walls.

Then it looks at what is already here, which takes longer, because a thing already given looks like nothing. In the east the free corners keep their edges to the end, at the height of a flank. Through the notch runs the one current that never slackens, and it pours over a sleeper all night and leaves it clean. The dry wind comes from where the animal came from, often enough that the place is not forgotten. It shuts the rule off for an afternoon and moves across the openings of two long ears, which is the only work they get. Most of what the attention would have thought to give is here already, rougher than it would have made it, and working.

It goes down once, in that wind, when there are no currents to bend and nothing can settle on anything. The grey quarter is coming apart. It stands in the main way a pace from the animal, facing the way the animal faces. Violet runs along every upper edge in town, and on the line at knee height, a hand long. The ridge has gone grey during the watch. While the attention stands there, the line shortens from both ends and goes out. It does not touch the animal. The wind is already passing over the strip between the shoulders, the one place nothing of the animal's own can reach.

Two things were missing, and it supplies them.

The first is a record. Nothing on the animal now says how long it has stayed, and the attention keeps the number. It also keeps where each ring was trodden and in front of which wall, and each straw and rose plate left lying in the grey. It keeps every cast and the shape printed inside, and the first night, spent lying in a room. The powder takes a print perfectly, and the wind takes the powder. The attention is one place where these things do not grey.

The second is the notch. The rim is a recent collapse and has not finished. On the north side of the gap a slab the size of a doorway leans on a wedge of smaller rock, and each cold period the wedge gives a little. Cold is when the rock works, and cold is when the animal lies beneath it. In a mild period, with the animal deep in the east under a ceiling of hanging threads, the attention seats the slab. It gives it a quarter turn and settles it a hand's depth, one edge into a socket. Nothing in the basin registers it. The pool stands at the same level, and the stream pours out as it did.

The seams will not breathe forever. When they stop, the whole town will go grey together, with one last glut on the floor, and then stubs, powder, and wind. On that day the animal will need a way out and a memory of open ground. The wind has been keeping the memory. Now the way out is kept too. The animal trusted that the notch would not shift, and now it will not, for longer than the town will last.

At the western edge, on bare floor, a line of white has started, a finger high, with a gap in it where the air goes through.

The attention leaves at the start of a cold period, the way everything leaves, east down the main way and past the stubs to the rim. The animal is already on the dull patch, curled, with its back to the basin and its face to the plain. The pool has risen to the sill, and the town's air is pouring out through the gap, fast and silent. The attention goes out with the stream and over the sleeper. In this town everything that touches a body leaves something on it, and the attention is careful to leave nothing.

Both ears turn, together, toward the plain. The eyes stay closed.
