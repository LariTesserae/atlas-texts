The power does not come up by the cableway. It arrives the way the cloud does, over the rim and all at once, and nothing on the summit marks the difference.

It is tempting to do something large. A power is used to being needed, and it looks for the need first, much as the surveyors looked for the fall of the rivers. It looks, then a little longer, then longer still, which takes some years. The toads walk. The swifts go out through the water and come back. The black shuts at dusk and starts again with the light.

Then the power goes through what it has to give.

- **Protection.** From what? The crust has been through seas frozen to the equator and continents welded and pulled apart beneath it. The great dyings were mostly deaths of animals, and it came through them without any sign that it noticed. Lightning leaves a pale star that heals. A slab falling from the rim is new surface.
- **Rest.** It rests at every dusk and in every dry hour, completely, and wakes within minutes.
- **Company.** It is a crowd under the lens and a pasture above that, and it is on every tableland on the horizon.
- **Thanks.** There is nowhere in it for thanks to land. The power gives them anyway, and they run off with the rain.
- **A mind**, so that it could know what it has done. The power holds this one longest, since a mind is what a mind thinks to give.

It puts the last one down more carefully than the others. To know about the air, the crust would have to know the rest: that oxygen was a poison when it was new, and that most of what lived then died of it or was driven down into the mud, where its descendants still are. The largest thing ever done to this world was done by something that only meant to go on living, and it was a catastrophe long before it was a gift. A mind would hand the crust the credit and the guilt in the same moment, and it has earned neither, having intended nothing. A mind would also bring a self, and a self is something that can be lost. The crust has gone more than two billion years with nothing to lose.

The power takes this as a lesson addressed to itself. It, too, could do something very large. It notes what the largest thing so far cost, and how long it was before anyone could breathe the result.

So it turns to the small things, the survey's leavings, thinking at least to tidy. But the plinth is outcrop now, and the diesel is a planter. The weir and the pipe are surface. Counted honestly, the survey gave the resident a few hundred square meters of new ground. Whatever the river sweeps into the open pipe was going over the rim in any case. That leaves the copper gauge and the pink tongue beneath it, the one hurt on forty kilometers of stone, which a power could right by tipping the gauge over.

It leaves the gauge. The tongue will close in a few centuries without help. Closed early, it would be a mark all the same, a stripe of rock that went dark ahead of its time, and the power has by now decided to leave less than the surveyors did.

There are others here that can be hurt, and the power does not forget them: the frog in each tank, the toads that cannot hop and so cannot cross to any other mountain. Everything they have depends on the cloud coming back. If there is a threat to that, it is not up here. It lies at the other end of the strand of cable that hangs over the edge, among people who breathe what the black made and mostly do not know it. The power has business there. It is a different account from this one.

Before it goes, it understands what it has been looking at. The black passes for the color of the stone. It feeds everything that stands on it and is taken for the ground. In all the time it has been doing this, nothing has needed to know it was there. The power, which is called benevolent, has not seen it done so well.

So it does the one thing the resident has ever had a use for. Toward evening the cloud lifts off whole. The horizon appears, and the other tablelands, each black under its own storm. Then the cloud comes back up over the rim and wets the rock.

Whether that was the power or the weather, nothing on the summit can say. The one instrument that might have measured it has been overflowing for sixty years.

The black goes on with what it was doing.
