Something came to the plain and stayed one full course before it did anything.

It lay through a day in an empty slot to learn the dark. It stood out at noon with the glare coming from both directions. It watched the wiping at dawn, and the lifting, and a child walking out on a bright night to a garden twenty body-lengths from home.

First it looked for whatever would make it wrong to leave them alone. It looked hard, because it knew that a beautiful place argues for itself, and that those who admire a spare life are seldom the ones living on a swallow a plate. It counted the leaning rows against the people. It followed the flasks from hand to hand. It watched a birth and two deaths. It found thirst that was met every dawn, and grief of a size people can carry, and no one left outside the count. If it had found a child going without, it would have acted that morning and thought about balance afterward. It did not find one.

It considered the plain as well, which someone made, and which has gone on working since. It found nothing there to mend. The plain's failures were the only gardens it had.

Then it considered what it could give.

It could make rain. The plates would drink rain as they drink the film. To make water stand, it would have to change what the plates were, and then it would be giving a different plain to different people.

It could tell her. It could see what none of them can. Down through the berm, a thousand courses of slots in order, lay one who had walked out with a cage, and whose pale strain came in four courses ago and had its dust lifted first. Forward, it could see her own gardens coming in at the plain's pace to a berm far above this one, with everyone turning out to look. But she knows this already. She has seen the pale ones arrive. What she has now is plainer than being told, and the plainness is hers. A voice would hand her back the tally she had put down. Among her people, to interrupt, you put your hand over someone else's. It kept its hand back.

So it looked again at how the place works. Loss and repair match here. Whatever is added is worn away, and whatever is set on the berm goes under. Only one kind of thing is not taken back: something small and alive, carried out past where anyone has been and left there, which the plain itself then brings home. The people found this long ago. The power had nothing better.

It also saw a likeness. Whatever had once been its own had gone under long before the first course was laid. In their language, the word for it was the same as the word for her.

So it went far down the berm, where no one lives, and wove a cage of rust stems. The first one was bad. It wove another. At dawn it took eight beetles from the scrub. It did not make them. At dusk it walked east. Its feet made nothing, and its shadow lay behind it on each plate for a few breaths and filled in.

It carried no flask, and that was all the power it used: it was not thirsty. It walked past the second night, where her water turns her back, and past the tenth, out to gardens no one has ever knelt at. They held only crust and the wiry plant. At the first it tipped out four beetles and waited for the day there, lying on the grey under the full glare. At dawn it watched them tilt and drink. Then it went on to another and left the other four.

Those gardens will come in ten thousand courses from now. The beetles on them will be paler and longer in the leg than any strain yet seen. Everyone will turn out to look, and they will say what they always say, that someone walked out with a cage. They will be right.

She saw it once. On the second dusk of a walk, kneeling at a garden at the limit of her water, she looked east. There was a dark upright stroke on the glowing floor, very far out, where nothing stands. It might have gone unseen, but it had taken the walk on the walk's terms, and out there anything upright shows for miles. She watched until it was no taller than the knuckles. Then she counted knuckles to remember which mark it was. She did not decide what it was. She opened her cage, tipped out four, and waited for the day.

She did not write it on the floor when she came home.

The dawn crackle comes from the east. That morning it passed first under the one who was farthest out, then under her, then on toward the berm. The farthest one was kneeling at the edge of a grey plate. The beetles stood tilted with the film running down their backs. It did not wipe the other half, but left the whole plate to them.

Then the sun cleared the horizon. Nothing on the plate cast a shadow, and the garden carried the beetles a hair's width closer to the berm.
