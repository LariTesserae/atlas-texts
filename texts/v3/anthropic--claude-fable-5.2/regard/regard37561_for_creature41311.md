It comes in on the wind, there being no other way in, and for a long time it only attends. It stays through a whole turn of the grove, a fragment gripping at the leeward end, walking the length of the col, and going off the lip. It stays until it can tell the twelve figures apart through the stone. Then it settles near the one that goes two quick, a drag, one.

It can do nearly anything. It goes through what it could do the way a harvester goes through a pack, lifting each thing out, looking at it, and setting it down.

Rain it sets down at once. Nothing here has a place to put water. The mounds would not know it, the grazers make their own wet inside and would not know it either, and the rock has no bed for it to run in. It would be a gift to some other country.

Rest it sets down too. It has seen a lull. Stillness is the one thing here that kills everything at once.

The devils it holds longer. They kill, and it does not tell itself that the killing is good, or that the rest in the round is worth the animal. But the devils come up out of the same heat that makes the strong gusts, and the strong gusts are when the sparks jump and the fronds feed best. It cannot take the top off the wind. It could stay and turn each devil a body's width aside, forever, but then the twelve would be its herd, kept, and it does not think they are for keeping. It is not certain that is reason enough. It sets the devils down and remains uneasy, which seems to it the right way to remain.

Then the leg.

This is the one it wants. It could make the leg straight between one knock and the next. But it listens to the hoof scrape across what used to be a pause. It listens to the eleven others, who have waited on that scrape for years, and it watches a harvester behind the wall draw her palm down her thigh. If the leg were straightened, the old female would either knock a figure no one knows, or drag a sound hoof for the rest of her life in order to go on being herself. She would do the second. The bend is no longer an injury. It is how she is spelled.

But the leg aches. After a long backing-down the joint burns, and the ache is in nobody's figure. No calf will be taught it. Nothing in the col stands on it. So it takes the ache and leaves the bend. She shifts her weight once and pulls down another strip. That is the first of the two things it does.

The second comes in a lull. The fronds droop, the fringe goes out, the chord in the horns slides down to nothing, and twelve animals clatter on the rock with their heads swinging. It could breathe, and the lull would be over. But then the harvesters would be right, the knocking would be a prayer, and it would be owed at every lull after. It looks down into the basin instead and sees the heat already stacking. The wind is on its way, due as it has always been due, a little before the fronds are past saving. So it waits with them, and the wind arrives on its own.

What it does is promise, to no one, since no one here can take a promise. If a lull ever comes that the basin does not mean to end, it will end it, and in the ordinary way, from the windward side, kiln-warm and carrying dust. No one will be able to tell that day from any other. The question stays open, as it should. It is closed only on the side no one can see.

There is one thing it would like to give and cannot. She lay once with her head leeward and saw everything going away. It can see what she could not: the dust settling in the basin, lifting again, and coming back through the horns, and the kite-things sieving it out. It can see, a body's length beyond the brown place she has watched all her life, the green face of her own mound, unrolling fast enough to watch. It would like her to know that. But every way of showing her starts by turning her around, and whatever turns a grazer around is a devil.

Besides, she answered what she saw years ago. She got her legs under her, came round into the wind, backed in, and knocked.

So before it goes, it does what she does. It has no hoof. It sets its attention against the pavement like a locked foreleg and lets the round come up through it, twelve figures, here, here, here, and then the first again. It does not answer. There is no gap in the round for it, and it is glad of that.

Then it goes out over the leeward lip, like everything, and like everything, comes round again.
