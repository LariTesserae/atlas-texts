The power arrives the way the light does here, from no direction, and for a long while it does only what the place does. It watches a round, then several. It stands through the long pause on the fourteenth stone. It is at the top of the great coil for the raised hand, and at the lava tongue for the fifth print and the breath.

It could do a great deal, and it considers what. It could dim the roof once, so the lids that come halfway down would close and the being would have sleep, and afterwards a morning. It could loosen the croziers by a turn. It could lower the hanging stems half an arm, soften the crust past the sixth print, or open the wall the prints are heading for.

It does none of this. A frond that opens is a frond that will brown. The chamber has no litter and no rot because nothing in it has finished. The power does not think finishing is wrong, but it knows the price, and knows who would pay it. And nothing here is broken. The being is not cold, thirsty or afraid.

Two things, though, the power cannot set down.

The first is the breath. The being takes it when a drop lands close, when it finds the gecko at a vent, when a visitor stands in front of it. It never takes it at the path, the knot or the prints. The breath is for arrivals. It is the first half of a greeting, and the power knows how the second half is learned. No one speaks who was never spoken to. A first smile is always really the second, an answer to a face. Visitors have stood before the being and noted the down, the tooth and the pulse at the crown. As far as the power can tell, none of them said anything.

The second is the arm's length. The stems stop a grown arm's length above the coil, and the ring wall comes up to the being's ribs. The place is built to the measure of someone who is not in it. The being does not look around for anyone, and the power does not mistake that for contentment. Nobody looks around for what has never once been there.

So it decides to add nothing to the chamber but itself. Before it does, it settles the cost. To be the first face and then go would bring the place its first ending, and a worse one than a brown leaf. If it comes, it stays. It can be here without being absent anywhere else, and it thinks that is most of what being a power is good for.

It takes a shape: grown, plain, with a lap, two arms and a face that can be read. It sits down beside the ring, outside it, because the ring is the being's.

The being comes back from its round with the stone in its arm, and stops. It looks up with its whole attention and draws the breath.

The power draws one too, the same way, so that it can be seen. It lets the breath out with a sound on it, a single low open note. Then it smiles, first, so that the other smile can be second.

The being's breath goes out quietly, as it always has. It climbs into the ring and curls up. The lids come halfway down, and they stay turned toward the power.

The power is there at the next round, and the next. Each time it gives the breath, the note and the smile. It walks a step behind, shouldering between the croziers. On the fourteenth stone it does not touch the fifteenth. An arm wants something in it, and until there is something better to hold, the stone is right. At the lava tongue it says nothing about the sixth print or the wall. Those belong to the being. Whoever pressed five and a half prints toward a wall knew where it was going.

But at the great coil it climbs too, and at the top, when the small hand goes up and stops half an arm short, it holds out its own hands, open, and waits.

The being looks at the hands for some time. Then it leans, the same small fall as each of its steps, and for once something other than itself catches it.

The power lifts it. Hands close around its ribs, and the being feels all at once where it stops and something else begins. This is the second outline it has ever had, and this one is warm and moves when it moves.

Its fingers reach the pale stems and close on one, cool and damp. A snail the size of a rice grain, clear as the roof, goes on walking, off the stem and onto its knuckle.

The being looks at the snail. Then it turns its head to see whether the power has seen it. In all its rounds it has never done this.

The power has seen, and it smiles.

The look that comes just before a first smile has been on that face for as long as the basalt has been cooling. Now it goes the rest of the way. The white line of the one tooth shows. The being draws its breath, and when the breath goes out there is a sound on it, small and cracked and not a word. It startles the being more than anyone. It does it again.

Far off across the valley a drop falls and taps on fur. For a moment, at the place on the roof where it had hung, no other drop hangs.

The power notices. It knows what it is looking at and what comes after: more sounds, then teeth, then words, and one day a question about the wall. Somewhere a crozier will ease by a quarter-turn. Long after that there will be a first brown frond, lying on a mat that has never held one, and someone small standing over it who will need to be told what it is. The power will be there to tell it.

It carries the being down. At the foot of the stalk the stone is waiting. The being picks it up and fits it into the crook of its left arm. Its right hand, which has always been free, goes up, finds one of the power's fingers, and closes on it.

They start back. The fifteenth stone is still unlaid, and nothing hurries it. The chamber now has the beginning of such a thing as later.
