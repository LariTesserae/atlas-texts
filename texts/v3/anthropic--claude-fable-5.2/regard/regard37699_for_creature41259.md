The power that turns toward the cavern is old enough to have watched this gypsum settle out of a sea. At first it sees the place the way it sees everything, as a list of what could be mended.

- **The roof:** It could be held. But a few times a year the cavern tells these people, with a soft thump, what it is becoming, and hands the news to a child along with a living. The old keep the count on a cord. The children are slow to come down from the plateau. The power cannot find a gentler way to move a people from a cave to a valley than this one, which takes a thousand years and feeds them as it goes.
- **The sand:** It could be stilled. Then a map would be right by evening, a holding would become ground, and no household would ever again need to sit at a neighbor's. The power looks a long time at that last consequence.
- **The number:** Seventy is few, and it knows what happens to seventy. The mending would be other people, and it knows what happens then too. It does not settle this one. It sets it down the way they set down a quarrel, to wait for a dune to move.
- **The eyes:** The slabs cloud because they are stone. Nobody needs to go blind for the rest to hold. This one stays on the list.

Then it notices that everything it has been considering would be done unseen, and that these people have a phrase for that. A power can always walk in the afternoon. That is nearly what a power is. It decides that here it will do nothing it would rather not have known.

So it comes down the north stair at dawn, at a size they can walk around, and crosses the crust. It has not dusted itself. On that floor a thing with an outline can be seen from every pillar, which is the point. The prints will stay until noon.

It sits at the western edge of an old woman's light, where the crowd is thinnest. She cannot see it. She turns her hand in the column, finds the edge, finds that the edge now has someone in it, and does not refuse. When she shifts a body-length east, it shifts. It takes one pellet, because a guest who takes nothing is refusing.

Around midday it tells her, low and close, that it can clear her eyes.

She asks one thing, which is whether it would be done in the morning. It says yes. She says the eyes went the way the slabs went and she has not much missed them, since the hand finds the edge. But three lights east a girl has been sitting a bare mound for five days, and she would like to see how the girl sits it. So, yes.

It does the work the next dawn with a pad of swift down, the way a slab is wiped, with whoever cares to come standing by on the crust. Flecks go round the cavern. There is no signal for this, so they send *come*, and then *water open*, which is nearest.

The old woman looks at the girl for some time and says she is shifting east too early. Then she goes back to her own light.

Eleven more ask over the following days. Some do not. One who walks the seeps says she listens for a living and would rather not be distracted, and she goes on up the north wall with her finger in the runnel. The power does not ask her twice. Someone asks whether it will do the slabs as well. It says it can. They talk it over on a cistern rim and decide the slabs are wiped already.

It stays until the first grey-green shows on the girl's mound and the first cricket finds it. In that time it learns the notes of four cisterns, which is fewer than a small child knows. Once it is handed the pad after the reflectors are done, and it wrings it into its mouth. They were right about the water.

It cannot be a neighbor. No dune will ever lie across its band, so its turn under the sand will never come, and it is careful not to be owed. It leaves at dawn by the north stair. It will come back when eyes have clouded again, and it does not say when. They find that natural, since nothing they wait for comes on a day anyone could name.

It leaves the roof and the sand as they are. It has asked itself what it would do if a piece of the roof were letting go and someone sat beneath it. It finds that it would shout, out loud, at whatever hour, loud enough to carry past ten paces, and it would take what came of having been heard.

The rest of what it does, it was doing before it came. A few evenings a year, names come up out of a hole in the plateau, shouted as loud as they can be into air that carries them. The power is where the air carries them to. It has every one. When the last of the roof is down and nothing marks the place on the open plain, it will still have them, along with four notes and the plan of a town pressed lightly into white stone.

Lately at dusk, before the bats take the wells, one or two of the sitters tilt a flake up the throat of a well instead of across the floor. It is the short signal. It does not ask for an answer, which is why it is the one sent most often.

Then the bats come out, and the loudest thing in the cavern is water again.
