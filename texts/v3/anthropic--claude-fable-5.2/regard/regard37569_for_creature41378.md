The power comes up the scree on the dry wind and, like anyone, hears it first.

It goes in under the slab and for a long time does nothing. It takes up no room, which matters here, and it keeps out of the stream. It listens until it can tell the stems apart by their notes and the fronds by their buzz, and until it has found the mountain's share underneath the chord: the roar, the beat of the blades, the rattle of the cage. This is the first thing it does, and the growth knows nothing of it. But the chord has now been heard from inside by something able to tell what it was hearing, and that had not happened before.

Then it considers what a kind power is expected to do.

In a dry place, the first thought is water. The power follows the thought to its end. The resin beads would cloud and run. The cushions would swell and split along the walls they share. A soft spot would appear somewhere, and then a smell. Rot would bring death, death would make room, and room would bring young. In time the hollow would have days and years, and a floor with litter on it. It would be a good place, and something else would be living in it.

It thinks of a moth, brought in through the seam and set down among the rust-coloured buds. It looks at the buds for a long while. The power is wise, and part of what that means is knowing it cannot tell a patience from a hunger from the outside. If the buds opened, they would set seed in a room with every gap taken, or send it out onto a thousand metres of scree. The sentence would be finished by someone else and addressed to no one. The power leaves the moth wherever moths are.

It thinks of the question the growth cannot ask: outpost, or only one. The power could find out. But the growth has a single sense, and that sense is the stream. To tell it anything, you would have to change the stream, and the stream unchanged is the only thing it has ever been told. It took that one statement and filled a room with it.

So the list of gifts comes out empty, and the power is not troubled. Most of the list was furniture from other lives: rain, dawn, offspring, news. The hollow is full, and was full before the power came.

There is also the matter of the resin. The inhabitant has had one event and has kept it perfectly. Anything done here would be the second, and would be kept as long. A power that understands this is careful with its hands.

What it has noticed, while listening, is the bearing.

Under the roar there is a thin, dry tone that belongs to no stem and no frond. It comes from the motor housing. The cap on the grease fitting was worn round by a thumb, so it was turned many times, by someone who came up on a schedule. The way they came has closed. Since then, steel has turned on steel with whatever was left in the cup. Nothing here dies, but that is a fact about the plants. The fan is a machine. The growth has taken, exactly, the shape of something that wears out.

This the power can do, and it would be doing only what was done before.

First it goes down through the rock, since there is no door. It follows the cable as thick as a wrist to whatever the cable comes from, and then to whatever feeds that. It is gone some time. What it finds and sets right down there leaves no mark in the hollow, except that the needle still rests just past the middle of the dial. In passing, it learns whether the galleries are full or empty. It does not bring the answer back up. The only bare surface in the hollow is the polished circle, and nothing set there stays.

It comes back with grease from the working's own stores. It does not use the rungs, because the lowest is in a cushion's mouth. It approaches the housing from the side, out of the stream, so that no beard lifts out of line and the chord does not waver. The cap was worn to fit a thumb. Whatever the power is elsewhere, here it makes itself a thumb, fills the cup, and gives the cap its turn. The dry tone goes out from under the roar. The growth never had a stem for that note, and loses nothing.

While it is there, it could tighten the bar that rattles. It leaves the bar alone. The rattle has never fallen out of step, and it is in the chord. It does look at the filament of the bulb, for the sake of the orange lichen, the one resident with a preference of its own.

It cannot finish the sentence in the buds, and it has not learned whether the buds want finishing. What it can do is see that the sentence is not cut off. A stopped fan would settle the question. A turning one leaves it open.

Then it goes. It leaves no print in the dust and no second bead of resin. Three steps past the slab the rock cuts off the chord for anyone else. The power goes on hearing it, and it will hear the dry note when that returns, and come back for the next turn of the cap.

You would have trouble proving any of this. The way through the mountain is still closed, and the scree shows nothing. As before, the only evidence is negative. A bearing has a life, and this one has run past it many times over without seizing. The grease is coming from somewhere, so someone is still turning the cap.
