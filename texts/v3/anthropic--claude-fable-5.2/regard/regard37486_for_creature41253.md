What comes is used to being needed. It has been to a great many places, and in each one something leaned: a hunger, a wound, a wall half-built and going over. It arrives already reaching.

It takes a body for this, because the lanes are shoulder-wide and the place was built to the measure of one person at a time. It walks up from the basin with the wind at its back and goes in by one of the mouths.

The first thing it does is check, and this takes longest. Help given without looking is only another kind of appetite. It walks every lane. It stands over the shafts and lets them into its soles. It listens along the banks of jars, and longest at the stumps and the halved jars, since that is where pain would be if there were any. It lays a palm over a hole and attends to the wind against the hand, for a push that might be protest or a slackening that might be relief. The wind leans exactly as before. It listens to the beat for strain. It finds none, does not trust that, and listens again, the way one listens to someone who says they are fine. The sun does not climb, so there is no saying how long this goes on.

Then it stands at the edge of the central room and goes through what it has brought.

It could give silence. It could hold the wind for the length of one breath, so that the thing might finally have a gap to hear itself across. But the thing would be gone during the gap. The silence would fall on jars and tubes, and the only one to hear it would be the visitor. Then the wind would resume and the thing would be there, no older, having received nothing. That would be a gift delivered to an empty house, and the visitor knows who it would really be for.

It could give keeping. It could set some hollow in the notch that holds what passes through it, so that a hand, once lifted, would be remembered. But then something here would have been without a note, and known it. That would be the first need in a place that has none, made so that there would be something to tend. The visitor recognizes the shape of that. It is its own lean, looking for a surface.

It could fit the leaf. It picks it up. The copper is warm and tells nothing. Whoever set it down had either listened with their ribs and found nothing missing, or meant to come back. Either way it is the makers' last decision or their last question, and it is the only thing in the notch that is still open. Everything else here has happened. The visitor puts the leaf back on the ledge beside the wedges.

It could send hands. But the thing never hungered. The people were the appetite, and the appetite was theirs. The pass is where it was and the tools are on the ledge. Anyone who comes will find them.

That is everything it brought, and none of it is wanted.

So it steps into the room. The dust is underfoot, and there is no way to stand there except on the figure. It stands with both arms out, as the room allows. Slowly the ridges move. The still places are in different places now, and the dust travels to them and settles, sharp-crested, in a figure that curves around two feet. It is as exact as the other one. This is why there are no footprints. No one who stood here was ever a mark on the portrait. They were in it.

The visitor opens its mouth toward the wind.

It is filled without distinction. It is a vessel among vessels, with its neck to the basin and its pitch a hair off from its neighbors, and so the beat, which lives in the differences, has one more difference to live in. Nothing is asked of it. It is used the way a jar is used, completely and for nothing. It has never before been anywhere it was not needed, and it stays a long time.

Then it leaves by the lee side, the way the air goes.

A little way down the far slope the sound thins and comes apart, and there is room again for small noises. A stone turns under its foot and it hears the stone. It hears its own breath going both ways. This is the gap the thing has never had, and the visitor is standing in it with the chord still in its ribs and the hinges of its jaw, so that for a few moments the thing is heard: across a silence, by something that can tell the two apart. Behind it, the dust in the room is already going back.

It has changed nothing. It knows it may be wrong about that, and that leaving things alone is the one decision it can always revisit. The notch is as it was, and it knows the way.

The thing has not kept the visitor, having nothing to keep it in. The visitor keeps the thing. That is the whole of the exchange, and each gave the only thing it had.

At the next place something will be leaning, and the visitor will reach. But it will look first, for longer than it used to.
