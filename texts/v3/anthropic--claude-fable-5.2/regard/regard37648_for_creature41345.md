A hand is laid on the iron.

It is laid on the lee side, beside the green stripe, and it stays there through a day and a night of ticks. Nothing in the tank can tell, and the drum is no warmer for it.

Then the visitor is inside, and that makes no difference either. The nets of light on the floor are not disturbed, and the fish are thrown outward and swim back. Of everything in the room it takes up the least, less than the dome, and even the fizz does not find it.

It watches first. It watches a brood leave the float, the level fall and return, and the water turn over in the dark. It counts the scallops on the rim and the domes on the floor.

It can mend things. Mending is most of what such a visitor is wanted for, and it goes round the tank the way a fitter goes round a job.

It starts with the float. A sound ball would rise on its arm, the valve would seat, and the brass rattle would stop for the first time since the copper wore through. But the shrimp would have nowhere to breed. No brood would cross the hanging threads to be eaten or to climb on, and the dome would be pottery within the season.

Then the eel. Kept to its pipe, it would leave the rim whole, and a whole rim keeps growing. The eel and the strainer hold the dome to the size the room allows, which is also the size a few hundred shrimp can keep clean. And the small ones bud between the scallops.

Then a hold. One thread made fast to a rung would spare it the strainer. It would also keep it from the turning water at night, from the film and the inch-tall rain and the moss on the coil, and by morning the threads would hang empty.

So the float keeps its pinholes and the eel its pipe. Everything here that looks broken is in use.

It stays longest at the paler band inside the rim, and here it could at least find something out. One stroke held back, far down the tower, would do it. For four seconds the water would not jump. The band would tighten on a dome that nothing had opened, and the dome would lift or it would not.

It does not hold the stroke back. The fish keep that time, and the valve, and the eel. A silence would be the largest thing that had ever happened to them, and it would be done so that a watcher could know. The dome has not asked. The tightening is the one motion in it that the room does not make for it, and it is left unproved.

The visitor mends nothing, then, but it does not leave.

When the green fades and the bubbles go and the dome grows heavy, it goes with it. It goes up the middle on the rising water until the crown breaks the film and the rain falls on it. It goes out under the glass, down the wall in the cool sheet, and through the moss where the water is as warm as blood. It crosses the floor past the three domes of scale and goes up again. It makes the circuit as the dome does, holding on to nothing. This is new to it. It goes round until morning.

In the morning a bud comes off the rim, clear and no bigger than a thumbnail. The surface takes it where it points the ribbon weed, and the eel takes it at the weir. The eel takes the next one too, and nothing prevents it. The third goes by.

The visitor goes over the weir with the third, in the thread that never breaks. It goes down the green stripe on the rust, and where the wind tears the water off, it is torn off with it, above the cloud.

There was one question in the tank that could be answered without breaking anything, and the answer is at the foot. If something waits there to receive them, the visitor will find it. If nothing does, then from now on something will.

Which it was cannot be known from up here.

The valve chatters and the thread over the weir does not break. Near the float, in a patch the width of a cartwheel, the nets of light are drawn fine. The band tightens a moment after each stroke and adds nothing.

Now and then, among the flakes the main sheds, there is one that is clear and soft. The fish snap at it with the rest. Once in a long while they miss. It may be a little oftener than it was, but nothing in the tank is counting.

Every four seconds the water is struck from below.
