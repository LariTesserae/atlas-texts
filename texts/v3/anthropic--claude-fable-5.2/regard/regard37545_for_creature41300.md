What draws it is the pale stone. From high enough the plateau is one grey, the thumbprint grain running out to cloud on every side, and in it are a few dry specks where something was set back today. The power does what anyone does who wants to find a person here. It follows the palest one down.

It sees everything at once, which is its nature and here its first difficulty. The people go round the slumped quarters with their eyes on their own kept way, because a fallen stone belongs to whoever finds it, and a single glance takes in more than a life could put back. The power has no kept way to look at. In its first moment over the plateau it has found every fallen stone there is. Under the rule it owes them all, before dark.

It could pay, and that is the second difficulty. It could have every slab back in its course before the fog rises, each on the first try. It could do more:

- tilt the bedrock by a hair, so the water ran and the lanes dried
- bring the willow up to the knee, then the shoulder, and with it fires a person could stand in front of
- thicken the sedge root and double the eggs in the hearting

It follows each of these forward. The water runs and makes a noise, the moss dries in the joints, and the frost gets into the hearting twice as fast. The fires grow, and a household no longer sits with knees touching. There are more children, then more hands than fallen stones, and somebody is the first to own a hammer. On the morning after the mending, a girl who is still bare walks her lanes looking for a stone to set and finds none down anywhere, and every wall in her mother's keeping is in a hand she cannot read. None of these cities is a bad one. Some are kinder to the body than this. But none of them has these people in it. It would not have helped them. It would have swapped them for others who needed less.

It does not let itself off with that, because cold is cold. It goes down under the fog and sits, in the way it can sit, at the fire the size of two cupped hands, and listens for what these five lack. Not expecting a thing is different from not wanting it, and it knows the difference. It looks hardest for what it would not stay still for: a child kept hungry while another eats, someone walled out, a cruelty under the quiet. If it found that, it would not be still. It finds thin food shared evenly, a hare taken only when old, and the dead given a room. The wants it finds are small and near: for the stone to go in, to know which wall that was, for a certain footfall to come home ahead of the fog. Most of what they want they are already able to give each other. They have not asked for anything else. It will go on listening in case they do.

Then it goes back over the afternoon, because something in it was instruction.

The girl squatted with the slab across her knees. The power could see the fit from the start: grey face inward, the thick end to the left, a quarter turn. She tried it wrong eight times. One lane over, a bare foot came down on wet stone and stopped. Whoever stood there has set ten thousand stones and could have had this one in at a touch. Nobody came round the corner. The foot was still there when the low sound came.

The power understands that it has been shown how help is given here. One comes near and stops. One lets it be known, by a single footfall, that someone is a lane over. The next day one lays a palm flat on the stone and pushes once, and says nothing, and goes on.

So that is most of what it does. It stays one lane over.

There is still the rule. It has found what it has found. The people's way of not looking is honest for them and would be a lie for it. So before dark it goes to a slumped quarter many days' walk from any keeping, where the walls have the low, rounded shape of something that has finished falling. It takes hands. It makes them thick and ignorant, hands that have never turned a cobble, because any other kind would leave no hand in the stone. It picks up the first slab. It looks at the stone and not at the gap, which is a child's mistake. It makes the mistake on purpose and then, after a while, not on purpose. The slab goes in near the end of the light on something like the fortieth try, weathered face inward, the clean underside out.

One stone, before dark. Tomorrow, one more. That is the rate at which it has decided to pay, the rate of a household of one. At that rate the debt will never be cleared, which puts it in the same position as everyone else here.

For what it is they would have no number, only the gesture, the hand opening and moving away from the body. But its stone is bare, and by the only count the plateau keeps, so is it. In ten or twelve years there will be a speck.

Its patch will show pale from the wall-tops to anyone who climbs the through-stones at evening. Once in a long while someone may follow it in, usually young, walking on purpose. They will find no one in the doorway, or what looks like a piece of wall. They will find a few mended courses and the next stone lying in the water. If they pick it up, the power will leave off, and its work will pass the way all walls here pass, from one hand into another. Long after, a woman walking that lane will be able to say where its courses stop and the next begin. She will not know whose they were. She will know the habits as she knows a neighbour's: here was one who tried every stone too many times, and set each as though it were the first.

Tonight the fog is up to the wall-tops. Near the middle, a slab clicks somewhere to the north. The power knows the wall and the course, the year it will come down, and whose hands will find it lying in the water. It does not go out to it. It has not fallen yet.

In the cell the girl's hands are turning the cobble. Days off, under the same white floor, one stone sits in a slumped wall with its underside outward, dry and pale and not yet weathered, set by the youngest thing on the plateau.
