I think it comes on foot, over the low shoulder of the western rim, and crosses the line squarely at a gate, as the herds do. A power like that could arrive any way it liked, but the raptors hold the air here, and the first act of wisdom in a strange place is to learn its manners.

Then it stays some days and looks. It watches the dew taken and the ferns turn green and brown again. It watches the cat fail four stalks and succeed at a fifth, under a slab, in rain. It does not look away, and it does not lift a hand.

It thinks about waking the keeper. It could give the keeper things in place of differences, and let it see, once, the glitter running along a dome flank and know that it caused this. That is the gift a kind heart reaches for first, and the visitor holds it longest before setting it down. The keeper knows only one way to care, which is to prevent the first step of change. If it saw the grazers, it would begin to keep them. No pan would dry and no stalk would succeed, and the herds would stand in an endless evening. The basin is alive because the keeper's attention is narrow, and everything it leaves strictly alone has been free to become itself. Its blindness is the most generous thing about it, and the visitor will not cure it.

It thinks about release, about telling the keeper that the ones it was keeping the evening for are not coming and it may stop. But it looks for the one who waits and finds what you found, the edge worn off. There is no vigil here to end. The release would satisfy only the visitor's sense of story, and the heat would leave in a single night, with the toads still sealed in the grit.

It thinks about bringing the makers back, or wearing their shape for an hour. That would be a lie, and it would rest on a mistake, which is that the welcome was never received. The dip in the threshold is what a welcome looks like when it has been taken up every day for ages. The guests were not the ones expected, that's all. They come a few at a time, they want a level place and clean water, and what they do here cannot bear interruption.

So it does almost nothing.

First it checks, honestly, whether there is suffering in the keeping. It looks where suffering would show in a thing like this: a correction that overshoots and hunts, a warmth that flickers before dawn, any sign of strain. It finds the round pan refilling without hurry. It cannot be certain, since no one can see from outside whether a thing has an inside, so it chooses what would be right either way.

It becomes a guest. It drinks at the round pan, and the level does not fall. It sits in the shell without a shadow while the sentinel stands and drops, until, after an hour, the sentinel stops. At dusk it walks to a slab and lies down at the edge of the ring, among the grazers, who shift a little to keep their sightlines. It faces outward like the rest, with something level and grey and slightly warm at its back. For one night it is part of the only body the keeper has.

Facing outward here also means facing up, through less air. I don't know what it sees. I think by morning it knows why they came.

Before it goes, it says aloud, once, that the evening has been kept. The words travel two paces and stop, which in this place is how everything is said. The keeper cannot hear them, but the visitor does not think thanks are owed only to what can receive them.

Walking out, it attends to the one cliff in a basin that has none. Nothing here is running down, but everything alive here is built for a warm night. If the keeping ever ended, it would end between one dusk and one dawn. The visitor does not touch the keeper to prevent this. It takes the basin into its own accounting. It will know if the warmth begins to fail, and it will come back and let the cold in slowly, at the pace a bird grows foil under its wing, so that the end is a low rounded shoulder like the rims. That would leave time for fur and for burrows. If the quiet goes too, it might leave time for a voice.

Then it leaves by the gate. It was not here long enough to wear a groove, and the keeper registers nothing, because nothing has drifted from where it should be.

The basin has been taken into account. That is the whole of what the visitor does. It learned here, from a keeper that sees nothing and a ring of animals that see everything, that this can be enough.
