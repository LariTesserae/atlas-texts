It came down the cups backward, like anyone, and it took the wool from the niche. It had no need of wool, but that is how the place is entered.

It did not stand in the middle. Whatever is set down there is gone by the next day, and this holds for gifts as it does for pebbles. It took the recess, beside the drying cell, and stayed a winter.

Its first question was who lives here, and the room had already answered: the ferns, who can live nowhere else. So it went to them first and asked what they needed, and the answer was that nothing change. This settled more than it seemed to. A quieter room is a drier room. Any kindness done in the path of the air would be paid for at the edges, by fronds one cell thick.

So it began with what could be done without touching anything, and saw what no one has seen.

It went down the throat between swells and lay in the tunnel among the cobbles. The otter does count, and it gets the count right.

In spring it followed one of the young spiders up the shelves and out of the lee. She came down in a well shaft some miles north, found a joint in the stone, and will not go toward light.

It heard the wren. The song is the same song as on the heath, every note in place, and it ends where it should. The woman is right about where it arrives.

It read the wall. The oldest hand counted seals. A later one counted winters and stopped at eleven. The man counted the years he had kept the place, having guessed that the hand before him did the same, which it did not. The woman is wrong about his day by some weeks and one whole meaning. What the marks share is their length. Each is as long as a stroke can be made in one indrawn breath of the room, because that is the time there is.

It also found the man's note, which the stone had kept the way it keeps everything, as wear. It set his note beside hers. The gap between them is narrower than anything a throat could sing on purpose, and it is the only measure anyone has taken of the room.

Then it considered what to mend.

It could give the woman back the top of her hearing, but the room would have it again within two winters.

It could tell her what the man meant. But he meant a guess, and so did the one before him, and hers is as good. It is also the one in use.

It watched the girl's face after each blow for many days. The breath cannot be handed over, and the woman knows this, which is why she does not try. Some morning the girl will breathe out early by accident, the blow will find nothing to take, and she will have it for good.

At the rim it tried the peg and the rope. Both were sound. They have been looked after for longer than it had been looking.

What it did, it did once. On a day when the turning was done and the lamp was filled, the woman put her back to the wet wall and her throat began to move. The girl began to look away. The room was drawing its breath just then, the long hiss that pulls at sleeves and hair, and inside that pull the girl felt, on her shoulder, the weight of a hand. In that room a hand on the shoulder means "look."

She took it for the draught, but she looked.

There was not much to see: a woman with her mouth shut and her eyes on nothing, and a movement at her throat that stopped for the blow and started again after it. The girl watched through three blows. Then came two taps, and up.

It stayed long enough to see one thing more. Late in that winter, on a day when the work was done, the girl stood for a while with her own back against the wall, a little apart, facing the scoured floor. Her throat moved. She was too high, and then less so. The woman saw and looked away, which was a courtesy, and then looked back.

The two of them stood there feeling for the same note. If either found it, she had found the other's as well, and neither would ever know. Two shelves up, the wren did not stop for them.

It made no stroke on the wall. The wall is for those who send the meat up the rope. It climbed out with everything it had carried in still tied to it: the tunnel, the count, what the marks were for, the two notes a hair apart. At the rim it put the wool back in the niche.

For an hour afterward, the place it returned to sounded thin.
