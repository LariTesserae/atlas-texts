The visitor comes in at the bottom.

That is deliberate. Anything set down on the plateau, however lightly, would be entered in the record. A footfall on that sea would still be crossing it a month later, and the thing that listens would spend the month working out what had risen to bend the swells that way, find nothing, and go on looking. So the visitor arrives at the one place on the planet where a disturbance ends. It settles on the fan of grit at the mouth of the gully, about as heavily as a cloud shadow.

Kindness is not what drew it, though it is kind. From far off, this place reads as a fault. Heat is going the wrong way here, and water is standing in a face with nothing behind it. A power like this keeps accounts, and in them a sea five kilometres in the air shows as an error. The visitor has come the way one walks over to look at a crack in a wall.

It stays a long time. The cracking of the fronts comes down through the plateau's flank as a grain in the rock, too fine to call sound. The fall is a pressure. Every few weeks the pressure moves a little way along the rim, and some days later its note changes slightly as the long wave goes out and returns. The visitor waits through one whole circuit of the rim, which takes years. By the end it knows the map nearly as well as the thing does, and it knows this is not a crack. Something has hold of it.

It would like to help. That should be said plainly, because most of what follows is a list of things it does not do.

It does not take the edge. It could, since the weight is nothing to it. But the hold is not a load someone put on the thing. So far as the thing is anything, it is the holding, and to lift that off for an hour would be to lift the thing off itself. Besides, it already rests the only way a grip can rest, by shifting, and it worked that out alone.

It does not hurry the columns. A hand's breadth an hour is the rate at which basalt sets true.

It does not send word. Nothing up there takes words, and anything said into that sea would be said again on every crossing.

The hardest thing to decline is the obvious gift. The thing has never felt a wave end. The visitor could take one swell at the closed rim, let it run up, and not let it rebound. It gets as far as choosing the swell. But up there the returning wave is how the thing knows where its own edge is. A swell that failed to come back would not feel like rest. It would feel like the cliff had gone.

So the visitor leaves the plateau alone and turns to what is in front of it. There it finds it has been anticipated twice.

The first is the lake. A litre would test clean, but the visitor does not bother with litres. It exists only at scale itself, and it knows how to look. The thing is in the brown water, or as much of it as has come down so far. It is not holding the lake's level, which creeps up the grit with every rain. It is not holding heat, because the shallows are warm in the afternoon and cold by morning. It is not holding an edge, because the edge is wherever the water happens to have got to. It is in the lake the way a hand is in a lap. This is the first of it that has ever been idle, and more arrives with every rain.

So the rest the visitor wanted to give is already being given, by the thing to itself, two hundred kilometres of rim at a time. It was never an omission in the plan. The whole plateau is how it gets there.

The second is the islands. All through the listening the visitor kept returning to them, a few flat places in the map that should have been corrected and were not. Now it understands them, because it recognises the rule. The rule is its own. The visitor has been at its work since long before the first column. It also corrects, and it also leaves no error standing, and the one thing it has never been able to pull down is a rock that something has got a grip on.

It sees one thing more in them. Below, nothing will be exact. Gullies cut where they like, and grit lies however it fell. A thing that has corrected every mistake within a week is going to live among shores, which are made entirely of what nobody corrected. On a few black pavements barely awash, it has begun to practise. The largest rosettes are the size of a hand. It has had that much practice, and it will have had more by the time it needs it.

So the visitor does one thing. In its accounts, against a sea standing where no sea can stand, with heat running backward under it, it makes the entry the thing makes when a ridge comes up wrong with a grey-green star in the joint. Let stand.

Nothing on the planet changes when it does this. The swells go on crossing, the fronts tick, and a disc of open water begins to steam. On the rim a gold seam thickens along the top of the glass, waiting for the gap to come round.

The visitor allows itself one thing more, because it costs the place nothing. It waits for a ripple. One comes in off the brown lake, runs up the fan, thins, and sinks between the grains where the visitor is. Then the visitor goes the same way. It does not withdraw, because a withdrawal is a kind of wave. Its attention runs up to the place, thins, sinks in, and sends nothing back.

There are two olive lines on the grit now. The second is a little higher than the first.
