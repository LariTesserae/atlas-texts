The power comes up by the scree, on foot, with a stone.

There are other ways it could arrive. But the place begins in the valley as a hiss mistaken for wind, and thickens on the last slope, and whoever wants to know it should take it in that order. So the power has taken a body for the day, a climber's, and at the foot of the scree she picks up a rough stone, somewhat bigger than a fist, with all its corners. By the col it has marked both her palms.

She does not speak. She could be heard over the fall if she chose, and she does not choose. She sits down by the ledge, out of the spray, between the rough pile and the round, and stays until dark, since that is how long it takes to be sure there are two birds.

Once in the afternoon she goes down the rope. She is struck as the floor is struck and makes no arrangement to be struck less. Under the white ceiling she counts sixty breaths and listens: one crack, one tick, five between. The one that ticks is not yet ready. She comes up with empty hands that will not close, and waits by the ledge for them to come back.

Then she goes over what is in her power.

- **The wall:** One stone would join it. But the gap is the place where someone understood, and the chink at the end of the northern arm has a nest in it.
- **The overflow:** She could cut in a moment the bed that the water will need a thousand winters to find, and the cushion plants would stop dying. It would be her bed. The water is new here and is working out how to leave at a finger's depth a day, and the plants are following it, each living one seeded from a husk.
- **The rope:** It could be made so that it never rots. Twisting a new one every few years is one of the ways the valley remembers.
- **The floor:** A hardness could be set under the basin, a forearm down, so that the pole touched bottom again and round stones came up for ever. That would be to put it back on bare floor, slowly. This is the first place where the floor has kept giving.
- **The feeding:** Rough stones could fall from the dry air above the line, one whenever one was needed, and nobody need climb. Then it would be hers. She has other places, and the attention of a power is no steadier than a habit of people. It also has less need of a guarantee than it seems to. It has been forgotten four times, and forgetting did not end it. It shifted its hold and trailed until its foot caught.
- **The knowing:** This she turns over longest. It could be made to learn them: to lean, a little, toward a climber it had felt before. It is the gift she would most like to give, and it is the worst one. A thing that can know someone can miss them. The note on bare floor is the sound of work on nothing. She would be making it a cry.

When she has been through it all she sees that she has come late. The wall stops where she would have stopped it. The smallest spheres sit where she would have told them to be left. The rule is the one she would have given, and it was arrived at by people with numb hands and five signs, who lost it four times first. They came late to the birds. She has come late to them.

There is one more reason, and it concerns her and nothing else on the saddle. She knows what she is. She is fastened somewhere high and still, and her loose end is in the world, working, and what she tends to take from the things given to her is their corners. Everything a power has mended comes out a little rounder. And she knows what she sounds like where there is nothing to mend and she works anyway. This rock still has all its edges. She can stand on a floor and leave it alone, and she has not always been able to.

About the depth: she does not know whether people will go on climbing with a stone once nothing comes down. That is theirs to find out. She thinks they may know already. The smallest spheres are no use to anyone, and for longer than any rope has lasted, people have left them where they lie.

Toward evening the stones bunch, a surge goes over the rim in one piece, the overflow swings west, and the trough fills, shuddering. This time it is seen.

A grey bird on the rim dips, and blinks white, at the other one.

She gets up and puts her stone on the left of the ledge, among the rough ones, where it cannot be told from them. Someone will carry it down the rope, or, later, drop it from the rim by ear when the note lifts. It will go in with a crack. After a long time it will tick. Whether anyone brings it up is not hers to settle.

Then she makes two of the five signs, to nobody, because they are the two that apply. Enough. Go.

In the valley it is a hiss again. She does not mistake it for wind.
