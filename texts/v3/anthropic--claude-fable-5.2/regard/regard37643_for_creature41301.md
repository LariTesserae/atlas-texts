The power comes in over the saddle on foot. It could arrive any way it liked. It chooses to have weight, because weight is one of the three things the valley can read.

It stops where you stopped and looks at the plumes for a while. Then it walks down onto the mat and does what it does first in every place it comes to, which is look for pain.

This takes several days. It lies with an ear to a cushion and listens to the water going uphill, ten seconds a swallow. It walks the shear seams and watches them part and close. It digs a well with its hands, down through this week and last week into the felt, and sits beside it until it heals over. It stands on the polished rock at the lip while a slab the size of a barge leans out. It watches for the red, and the red does not come.

Nothing here is asking for anything. The power has stood in a great many places, and nearly all of them were asking.

So it goes on to the second thing, which is to go through what it brought. A power of this kind carries gifts the way a doctor carries a bag, and the careful ones look hard at each instrument before taking it out.

It could give hearing. But to hear, the valley would need some part of itself stiff enough to be shaken, and it has spent ten thousand years arranging to have no such part. A stiff place cannot close behind a tear.

It could give memory. Memory is something kept. The cheapest kind is a scar, and after the scar come the grudge and the keepsake, and each of them is a piece of last month that did not go over the lip. The valley has one way to die, and this is it in a small dose.

It could give company. It looks for the gap that company would fill and cannot find one. No post has ever stood empty. And there is the hare.

It could wake it. The power holds this one longest, because being awake is what the power is, and it is partial. Awake, the valley would know about the lip. The red would go there at last. A slab would lean out and eight kilometers would flinch, and a flinch is a grip. Within a few centuries it would be up its own walls with its roots smothered, and it would know that too, the whole time.

The power closes the bag. It is used to that. Closing the bag is most of what it knows how to do.

What is left is small enough to be done.

It kneels. The damp comes through at the temperature of a hand. It puts its own hand flat on a cushion and lets the hand be weight, warmth, and salt, and nothing else that it is. The green comes up around the fingers. It puts a fingertip to a stalk and takes the bite, and gives back its charge, no more of it than a hare has.

The red goes out across a hectare in under a minute. Behind it a few hundred stalks come up and begin to breathe.

That is the meeting. The largest thing that will ever come into this valley has been entered on the roster as a weight, warm, salted, since lifted. In two days the stand that came up for it goes grey and folds into the mat. In a month the cushion that took the handprint leans out in a slab and drops into cloud. The power stays to see both. It wanted to know whether it could give something to a thing that would not keep it. It finds that it can, and that it has not practiced this much.

Then it does the one thing the valley cannot do for itself and will never feel done. It keeps it. From the saddle it takes down the places of the plumes, the curl of the seams at the step, the hour the breaker needs to fall, the rime stripes and the grey bird working them, the hare's hundred meters. It has room. Keeping costs the power nothing, and here it costs the valley nothing either, because the keepsake is held outside, where it cannot climb a wall or smother a root. The peat holds the pose without knowing what it holds. Now there is a second copy, and the one who holds it knows.

It makes no promise about the afternoon cloud. The ice had this bed for longer and must have looked as settled. If some century the cloud stops coming over the saddle, the power will not stand in the gap and push weather. A valley propped from outside is still being held on to. It will come and see what small thing gets into the grooves next, and it will keep that too.

It also takes something away, which powers rarely admit to. It has a habit. What it mends, it likes to keep mended. What it loves, it stands over. It has smothered a few roots in its time. It goes back up to the saddle with the valley's gait in its legs, come by the way the valley came by the ice's.

On the way it passes your stone, well down the fast lane, green to the shoulders. It leaves it where it is.

It will be back. It cannot stay, because a weight that stays gets a sump opened under it and ends by cutting a groove. So it does as the hare does. Every so often it will come over the saddle, lie down on a warm cushion, be carried a little way toward the lip, and get up and walk back to where it was. Each time the valley will close a green rim around it, take its charge, and send the red, as if for the first time. For the valley it will be.
