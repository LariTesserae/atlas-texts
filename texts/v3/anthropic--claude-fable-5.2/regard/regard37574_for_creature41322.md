The power comes the way starlight comes into the foils, without weight. It moves no air, because a breath would shift leaf-fall that has never been shifted. It carries no charge and it keeps off the seams, so the field over each crossing stays as it was and every blade keeps its line.

It comes facing the way it always faces, which is toward whatever is wrong. It looks for the hurt thing, the hungry thing, the thing waiting to be found. It goes through every grove from the green rim to the straw at the center, and along every wire, and finds none of that. It finds a creature that has never lost anything.

The power has gifts with it, and it considers them one at a time.

It considers rain. Rain would lie on the copper under the sweet gas and raise a green that is not alive. It would move the foils. Something would fall from the cliffs at last, and after that the edges would begin to round.

It considers company, something small with legs to leave a track in the grit and lift off the pipes at evening. But anything with legs has a mouth, and the oil has no bitterness in it. The power also notices who the company would be for. The grove has never looked for anyone. The looking was done by the eye that came here, and the power's eye is one of those.

It considers a mind. With a mind the creature would know itself, and the first thing it would know is the distances. As it is, the thing is forty kilometers across and nowhere far from itself. Given a mind, the bead would be nine kilometers from home on bare rock.

It considers haste. But the creature's only clock is its own width, and to hurry it would only bring nearer the day when the seams are all worked. So the power looks at that day. It looks down through the rock to where the warmth and the current rise, and they have longer to run than the seams have. It looks ahead to where the rings widen until they meet one another, and the green between them narrows to nothing, and the plateau stands finished in the light with every stem it ever raised still in place. The power could prevent this. But that day is the thing the creature is making, and preventing it would mean preventing the creature.

So it puts the gifts away, and that leaves the bead.

The crossing ahead of the bead is three kilometers off, and the bead makes a finger's width in a day. That comes to about a hundred and seventy thousand days. The power goes over the seam between them first and finds it bright, flush and unbroken the whole way, with nothing on it to clear. Then it goes to the bare X, settles on the black rock beside it, and waits. It does not shorten the distance by a finger's width.

For a hundred and seventy thousand days, each day is the same day. The sun crosses and the groves glare in sequence as the angle comes right. At night each grove holds the stars at its own angle. At long intervals, kilometers off, a foil lets go with a tick. The power is not used to days that ask nothing of it. It had a facing when it came. Long before the bead arrives, it has lain down along another.

All that time the bare crossing makes no sound. The power notes this.

The bead arrives. It stops and thickens and puts up a stem no wider than a straw. The first leaf opens and lies down along the line of the new field, which is not the line the parent holds. This is the only moment in the life of the plateau when something turns, and this once it is seen.

Then the note begins. It is very faint, and it is the same note as at every other crossing. It was not in the seam, and it did not travel in with the wire. It starts when the one is laid on the other. For the moment there is something here that can say whose note it is, though there is no one to say it to. The power takes the answer with it when it goes. That is all it takes, not a foil and not a leaf.

It leaves as it came, and there is no mark in the grit where it waited. Behind it the stem stands on the crossing with its one leaf, and it will be the center of everything that grows there. The next day is the same day. By the end of it the leaf has a foil on its underside, thin as breath on glass.
