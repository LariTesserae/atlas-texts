It was the knock that brought it. On a still night the sound came up through the sandstone, one clear note like a struck bottle, and the power, which had been attending to larger things, attended to that.

It came in by the slot and the passage, bending twice like anyone. It brought no light. It took a body, since the chamber knows nothing else, and took it at the temperature of the air. The tadpoles bumped its shin. The soft-shelled thing walked over its foot. A cricket's feeler crossed its wrist, and the cricket stepped on and stepped off. Nothing in the chamber could have said that anything had arrived.

It walked the place once round, by hand. Then it followed the knock and found that it did not come from the chorus. It came from a slack ring, where the ferns were down to their crowns and the vine was bare, and one frog the size of a thumb joint sat on a leafless stem, calling, with almost nothing to eat.

It sat down in the moss beside it and stayed twenty-six days.

In that time it considered what it could do.

It could have learned the colour of the leaves. But every leaf within reach of a light would have started turning toward it and needed a week to turn back. The colour is still not known.

It laid a hand on each post, and each was at the temperature of its hand, which was the temperature of the chamber, so it learned no more from that than anyone does. It judged the posts good work. Whoever set them gave the place one thing and left nothing else, and the power took that for judgment rather than neglect. It did not set a fourth. It thought through running all three at once: no slack ring, nothing waiting in a coil, and no reason for the chorus to reach the water at one time more than another. The chamber would lose its year. It left the turn alone.

It stood in the open stretch where the hand finds nothing, and the clickers went by on both sides, pushing air against its face. That air is their road, and the drops need it too. It put nothing there.

It did not reach for the whistler. It could have closed a hand on it. One animal in the chamber had decided to get out of the way, and the power let the decision stand. It left without knowing the size.

It thought longest about the frogs that do not jump. It did the sum the visitors have done, and got the same two buckets. It could have taught them to fear a hand. That was the one thing it could have given them that could never be taken back, and it did not give it.

On the eleventh day a nestling came down, some paces off, heavier than it should have been. The power got up, found it in the moss, and put it back where it had fallen from, which no visitor could do. The soft-shelled things that had started up the sand went back to the water without it. This was not fair to them, and the power did not make a rule of it. The next nestling to fall, after it had gone, stayed where it landed.

For the rest it sat in the slack ring. It had a face, and the few insects still drifting there found the corners of its eyes, as they find anyone's. It let its eyes water a little. It did not brush the insects off. The frog climbed from the stem to its finger, gripped with the pads of its toes, and went on calling, and the knock could be felt in the palm. It ate what came to the face. Some days that was five or six, like a pinch of chaff.

On the twenty-third day the coils on the fern crowns had loosened. On the twenty-sixth the first rasp came in off the open sand, a few lengths at a time, and the tapping against the face thickened. The power set the frog on a new leaf, cool and rubbery and not yet as big as a palm, and went out. The chorus dropped to a murmur after the first bend and was nearly gone after the second.

What it did that was large, it did outside. Everything green in the chamber came up the cliff as a stone inside a bird, and the birds go down each dusk to trees that anybody could cut. It went over the rim the way they go. The work there is long, and none of it can be felt from inside, unless it is this: the stones still fall, smooth and slick, the largest the size of a thumbnail.

It left the slot open and the passage as it was. The people who have come this far have put the frogs back. But it has not forgotten the buckets, and on still nights it keeps an ear to the rock.

It did not add up to much: three posts touched, one nestling returned, and one frog fed for twenty-six days. Most of what it did was not done.

The knock is still there. Whether it is the same frog cannot be told through that much stone, and it has not gone down to find out.
