Something comes into the hollow that the plant did not hand down. It does not arrive through the leaves like the light or through the floor like the cracks. It casts no shadow and puts no weight on the floor, because the floor is how the nymph is told things, and the visitor has nothing yet to tell it.

For six lights it does nothing. It stands by the fringe of root tips and learns the room. It watches a drop let go over the centre and counts to twenty-six before the tick. It hears rain that is not falling. In the dark it hears the chop overhead, and far out on the floor it sees the speck draw in its legs.

It could close the hollow as a hand closes. But the room would close on the one walking in it, and the wound is the plant's to finish. The place is green, and it will season in its own time.

It could steady the days. But the count is already kept. The pads are thick, and what is folded in them is wet and veined and cannot be folded smaller.

It could carry the nymph to a wall. The walls are splinters, and they draw back a hand's width every noon. There is no seam left to put it in.

It could lay a thumb over it. This it considers longest. The next time the forelegs lifted they would find a roof. But a thumb has to be taken away, and the nymph has had one wall leave it already.

It will not soften the chop either. The nymph takes each thing once, when it happens, and is right about all of it. The visitor leaves the floor honest.

Then it looks at the stick, and goes up through the closed ceiling the way the light comes down. Above there is leaf litter, and a pit in it as deep as an arm, and a person kneeling over the pit. The stick in their hands is as long as a leg. Its point is black from the fire and its cord is pale and tight. The visitor looks at the flat places the stone left. They are the same flat places.

So the stick below is this stick, later. The room gets things at the wrong hour, the hiss after the rain and the faint crack ahead of the loud one, and it has been sent the stick ahead of the hole. The hole is coming, then, and the visitor does not have to make one. The thing the nymph fears most in the dark is cutting the way out it does not yet want. There is no telling it so. The visitor leaves the digger kneeling. The digger is thin, and the ground is food, and the arms keep working.

Below, it picks up the stick. This is the first thing it has touched. The last of the cord comes off as dust.

It walks to the middle the long way, which is the only way. It could have the floor be four paces, and it lets the floor be what it is. Lights swing over. It passes the old skin standing on its hooks. The root-threads point where it is going. It passes the nymph wide, during a drink, and goes on along the nymph's line, and stops some lights' walking ahead.

It waits for the dark. When the thud comes from overhead, it drives the charred point into the floor in the same instant, low and slanting, the grip end raised back toward the nymph. The flesh takes the point the way the point was made to be taken. Milky sap wells around it. This is the one hurt the visitor does here, and it does it once. Out on the floor the nymph already has its legs drawn in. The visitor adds no fear to the dark that the dark did not have.

The stick holds. It stands knee-high at the grip and runs down to nothing where the point goes in. The plant does not grow onto it. It casts its shadow correctly.

The nymph waits a little longer than the thuds, then goes on. Some lights later it walks under the high end of the stick without knowing, as it has walked under everything. It stops, lifts the forelegs, finds air, and puts them down. It walks. The wood comes lower over it with every length. It stops again and lifts the forelegs, and they touch.

What they touch is dry and checked and tastes of nothing. It is not plant. The nymph holds still with its forelegs up. Then it goes forward, since forward is the way it goes, into the narrowing, until the hump of its back meets the wood. It pushes once more. It stops walking.

At amber it puts its beak down and drinks.

The chop still comes in the dark, and the nymph still draws in its legs, under the thing that makes the sound. The days are still short. The count still climbs too fast, and the root-threads still fall. The visitor has changed none of that. When the count is full, the nymph will back out of the angle and find that its roof goes up. It will climb to where there is only air beneath it, and hook on, and split.

The visitor has seen to that much. It has not seen to whether the hole will have come by then, or whether anything will answer what the ribbed plates make. It has not seen to what the digger will think, lying on the litter with empty hands, looking down at their own stick gone grey in the middle of a room too wide for its walls, with an amber skin hooked to it, split down the back.

It goes the way it came, and nothing comes through the floor.

The light swings to its certain slant. The kneeling shadow joins the leaf-shadows, arms working, and the shadow of the stick comes down and meets, on the floor, the shadow of the stick. The nymph gets none of this. Its back is touching something.
