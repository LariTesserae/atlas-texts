**Notice**

Something has taken notice of the plateau. It has no weight the peat can register, so the film stays whole on the pools and the frost keeps no print. There is no kind of sign.

It moves the way the animal moves along a ridge, slowly, stopping at each thing.

**The check**

It does what the animal does. It lays itself against each thing for a breath or two to learn whether it is alive.

For the plateau the test is not warmth. Peat is cold all the way down and is meant to be. The test is rate. It checks the front coming over the western rim at walking pace, the ridges, the black pools, and the white threads at the east. It checks the grazers' pattern, the seams of the vole runs, and the flies in the closed crowns. On a terrace in the breeding weeks it watches the white soles rise and lower.

Everything is exactly as plentiful as yesterday. The animal's rule for the warm ones is to leave them. The rule is sound, and the power keeps it.

**What it could do**

It could do a great deal. It goes through the list, because declining a thing is not the same as never having considered it.

It could keep the old female's rosette warm. But she has three hundred trunks and one test, and the test has never been wrong. A warm dead trunk would be the first lie on the ridge. It would also be pith rotting upright, and a seedling with no gap to take. The trunk will cool at its own speed.

It could show her the cloud. She has been inside it every day of her life, and it would only take waking her at noon. But the plateau she knows, clear and hard with stars over it, is a whole plateau. Nothing is missing from it that she would know to want. The gift would be for the giver.

It could make her rounds matter, so that the ridges stood because she walked them. It considers this longest. A power is inclined to think a thing is better for being needed. But a line that must be walked is a different thing from a line walked. She walks hers to find pith, and three hundred trunks standing in gaps she cut are what fifty years of that looks like. Adding a purpose would add nothing.

It could keep her company on the night she cuts. Here it finds the work already done. Many ridges away, two of her kind will feel four beats and then no beats, for hours, and will know what that means. One of them may stop walking and take it in. This was arranged long ago, by no one.

It could find her another place to sleep. But she has laid her cheek to the next rosette along every night for as long as she has had the ridge. She checked it last night, and it was warm.

**The rims**

What could change the rate is not on the plateau. The cloud is made somewhere to the west, over country that can't be seen from the rim. If it came thinner, or higher, passing overhead the way cloud does elsewhere, the peat would begin giving up what it holds. The water is used somewhere to the east. If the valleys wanted more than yesterday's, someone would climb up, find no gate, and build one.

So the power's work, if it has any, is there. In the valleys there are those who can be spoken with, who can answer and can refuse. That work is long, and it is done at their pace. None of it will show from up here.

**Before it goes**

There is one thing it does first. At first grey the old female comes back along the ridge with her breath hung behind her at every trunk. She lays her cheek against her own trunk and backs into the sleeve. The power stops there and lays its attention against her, the way she does it, for a slow breath or two.

She is warm. Her teeth are nearly brown. She is already asleep.

This is not for her. She doesn't need it and won't know of it. But a power should also find out by touch what it is leaving.

**After**

One dawn that season the trunk is cold. She sleeps in it anyway, because the day is coming and the sleeve is still dry. Overhead the ticking starts, and a few drops work down through the leaves.

At dark she comes out and strips the sleeve, including the low place on one side where the leaves are polished. She sets her teeth to the stem. The scraping carries a few paces. Many ridges away, a walk of four beats and a pause stops.

By first grey the trunk is down along the ridge. She backs into the next one. The leaves are stiff and don't lie close, and it will take some years to polish them. Above her the crown warms and the flies leave.

Mid-morning the cloud comes over the western rim. At the east the water goes over, cold, iron-tinted, and exactly as plentiful as yesterday. The plateau has no gate or gauge and no mark of survey. It is in continuous production, and no one runs it. Something knows that now, and means to keep it true.
