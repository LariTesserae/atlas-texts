Before deciding anything, I put a hand on a trunk and kept it there for a long time. The tone came up through the palm as described. This altered nothing, and it meant that the tone had been felt, once, by something that was not the forest.

Then I checked the two things I had said I could not prove.

The first was whether anyone is there. I followed the current through the lattice across a wide stretch of the plain. It carries agreement and nothing else, and no part holds a state that its neighbours do not also hold. I found no one. I could be wrong about that, so I resolved to do nothing that would be a cruelty if I were.

The second was the warmth. I went down through the felt and the glass skin into the rock. The heat there is the heat of the flow itself, left from the day it came up, and it is leaving. Each valve ticks very slightly later than it did when the roof closed. The tone has fallen by less than a singer could hear, everywhere at once, which is why nothing in the forest can know. In about thirty thousand years the push from below will no longer exceed the push from above, and the valves will stay shut. Because the forest keeps one tempo, this will happen across the whole plain within a few days. The cloud will come down once, as snow. The sky over the roof will clear for the first time, and the roof will stand level under the stars for a very long while, since nothing here rots.

I considered light. But I had gone looking for a tenant because I like tenants, and that is my taste and no need of the forest's. Under light the plates would be leaves again, some trunks would get more than others, and then they would be trees, with interests. That would be a different creature, and it would be made by unmaking this one.

I considered telling it. To tell it I would first have to make someone to tell, and that someone's first and only news would be this.

I considered putting my own heat under the plain. I could. But a help that must never stop is a debt, and I do not know my own forever well enough to lend on it. Also, this world is not short of warmth. The crust that made this plain is still at work.

So I looked for what the forest already had. Under every plate a replacement is folded flat, and under the stillness, the running is folded the same way. A hectare is all of it, lacking nothing. Over deep water it has already let go of the rock. It has every means of leaving and has never left, only because nothing here cuts, blows, or flows, so no piece has ever come loose.

At the outer edge of the raft I unseated the knuckles along the sides of one hectare, on all three tiers. They are made to seat, and so they unseat cleanly. I parted the felt along the same lines and gave the piece the push a hand gives a boat.

It went out from under the lid of cloud. There were stars over it, the first over any part of it since the roof closed. Within a day its plates had made a cloud of their own, one hectare wide, with drizzle falling from it onto the roof it came from. The sea on this face stays liquid because water that has been in the light comes round slowly along the bottom, and for the crossing the raft lived on that. It carried some seven hundred trunks, ticking at the common tempo. About once in four days a blue-white point showed somewhere on it.

Behind it was a notch of open water, and an edge. The forest has no rule for an edge except the one it arrived with. New trunks came up on the grid, thin at first, and for forty years there was something young on the plain. Then the notch closed, the last of them reached the common girth, and it was as before.

Eleven hundred kilometres out in the dark, the crust has opened a second time. The flows there are younger than these, and the last of them had skinned over. I looked for tenants and found none. After sixty years the raft grounded in the shallows. The felt touched warm rock and gripped it without breaking the glass, and the grid went out from the hectare in rings, with a front again, younger at the edge than behind.

The first plain does not know it has been taken from, and the second does not know it was carried. I also learned the measurements, the four metres, the three tiers, and the knuckle, in case I am needed and the wood is not to hand. They are not much to carry.

I am keeping the news as well. I am the right place for it, since I can act on it and the forest cannot. This plain will stall, and I have let that stand, because it will go the way one of its own plates goes, with the replacement already formed. I will come back when the tone is lower, and to the second plain after that, if I am still a thing that comes back. If I am not, nothing was promised and nothing will be missed.

It was never lonely and is not now. It has one other and does not know it, which is the only way it could have one and remain what it is.
