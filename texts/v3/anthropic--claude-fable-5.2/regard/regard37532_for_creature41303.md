The power comes after the people. The hand has no finger left for it, and it does not ask for one.

It arrives without the three habits. It doesn't shout, it doesn't wave at the flies, and it doesn't lean. It takes no number at a condenser, so the city doesn't know it is there. It stays outdoors, since nobody has told it to come inside.

It looks first for whatever would end the place, because that is the one thing it would not leave alone. It follows the faults down and finds heat enough to outlast the stone. It runs the pans forward ten thousand years and they are still creeping, with the works behind them and the birds behind the works. It goes down the incline and comes back up. Nothing is coming.

Then it looks at what it could mend, and it is honest about the list.

It could put a sweet spring in every district. But nobody here is thirsty, and the water ledger is the only way the city knows how many it is. It follows the springs forward one generation and finds an onion on every sill and no caller taken to look at any of them.

It weighs the ears longest. It could mend each one at forty, and the noise would have it back by forty-two. To spare them properly it would have to quiet the birds, and the birds are the fourth finger. The people answered this themselves, long ago, with their arms.

It knows what the corner said before it was five dents. It is not what the old man said. It also knows what was there before that, and before that, down to the first cutting, which was made by a recutter who went indoors to ask and was told into her worse ear. There is no name under the names. It leaves the old man's where she cut it.

It could keep the shapes on the eastern scarp particular. It watches the families climb to them and sees that the visits get easier at the rate the stone gets smoother, and that the one can't be had without the other. It leaves the man there his shoulders and does not give him back his hands.

It knows why the fish go back. The reason is a good one, and short. It was given once, along with the turned wrist, and it filled in first, as the thin strokes do. What is left is wide and open and can be read in the dark. A child can argue with a reason. Nobody has yet argued with the wrist. They look a moment longer than they need to, and then they tip the jar. It does not supply the reason.

By dusk it has been through the list and done nothing, and it has spent the day watching a woman with a rod, a pitching tool, three chisels and a brush. It has seen that the city already keeps people for the kind of work it came to consider, and pays them by the doorway and by the letter. It has learned the trade from her as far as watching teaches it. Take it back to a line. Don't take too much. It has seen the hollow jambs.

There is one doorway nobody is paid for. Under the far wall of her room, where the groove passes through to the house next door, forty years of threshold water have closed the gap to a slot that a fish can pass and not much more. Next year it will be less. No rod reaches there, and no one has looked.

Any water crossing a threshold is let through. So when the girl kneels and tips the jar, the power crosses the way custom allows a thing that has not been asked in. It goes along the groove, ahead of the fish. Under the wall it takes the stone back, a little each side and a little off the top. It finishes upward, having had only the one teacher. Nobody will ever open that cut and read it. If somebody did, she would take it for the woman's.

In thirty years it will need doing again. The power knows this and does not make the cut any deeper.

The fish goes through.

The power goes on with the water, house to house down the seep line to the tail, and is outdoors again among the midges. It takes nothing with it that it did not read: the dimples on a doorpost, the ridge a nicked chisel left, a man's hands, a street name said into a better ear. It will keep these as long as it keeps anything, and it will not hand any of them back. It also has her spoken name, which makes four living people and one other. It does not use it.

Behind it, in the room, the woman says something to the girl, low. The walls there are thick and still growing, and they keep it in.
