It arrives the way anyone does, at the rim, with everything at once. For a visitor that view lasts as long as they stay standing. For the power it is the ordinary condition: the walls, the wheel, the lines, the shot, and the long arithmetic of the floor, all present together. It knows this is the one view the pit has no use for.

So it lies down. Then it goes further than a visitor can. It sets the view aside and goes through the pit on the pit's own equipment: pressure, pitch, voltage, touch. It spends a stroke behind a lid in the top row, feeling the wall sweat. It hangs in the wheel for a day and a night, drumming, with its nose a finger's width off a tail. It stands eleventh of nineteen, takes a grain from the mouth on one side, lays down the coat that the voltage at that station calls for, and gives the grain to the mouth on the other. It lives a week in the space where beads touch.

It is looking for one thing, a want that the next thing along does not answer. It looks hardest where a visitor's pity would land.

- **The late shell.** The hinge is going, and the shell claps half a beat behind. But it closes when it feels the shell above it close, and it does feel it, and it does close. The stutter exists in the band, and the band exists only from the rim.
- **The first crawler.** Its mouth is worn black and blunt, and its head is in the only warmth in the lake.
- **The stopped line.** The power is careful here, because this is where it most expects to find something. It finds a grain held in a mouth, with no clock anywhere in the holding. When the spare arrives, the grain is passed. Nothing in the line records that it was late.

It leaves the gap for last. In the three or four eels with nothing in front of them, it finds a lean toward a tail. This is the same lean as everywhere else in the ring, toward a tail that is further off. It cannot say for certain that there is no ache in that. It can say what closing the gap would do, because a closed ring has nowhere to be going. It is not sure enough of the ache to spend the wheel on it. So the body-length of empty water stays empty and goes on round.

Then there is the being itself: the stroke, the flywheel, the bead, the rising floor. The power can see it, and it is real. But it has no place where anything could be delivered. Every address in the pit is a finger's width across. Whatever is given here has to arrive as brine through a crack, a tail ahead, a grain from a mouth, or a furred bead, or it does not arrive. Those are arriving already, every eleven seconds.

It goes through the larger gifts anyway, because it would be careless not to.

**Eyes.** The room is lit and no one in it can see. The power could open one smooth head and let the light in. The eel would see a tail. Or it could give the eel the whole view, the walls and the wheel and itself in the wheel, and then what hung in that place would be a small piece of the power, with the eel gone. The light is not wasted. Waste is a word from the rim.

**An off-shift.** There is no shift. A shell at rest is a shell hanging open, and the young settle on it and build.

**The knock.** The power goes down through the floor to the constriction and listens to the stroke being made. The cone is young and its slopes still slide, so the stroke will not last forever. The power could hold the passage open. But an interval that never varies because something is holding it is a kept thing, and whoever keeps it owns the works. Everything here came up from the dark, and the dark is large. This is one lit room of it.

**A collector.** The pit runs like a works, and a works has someone it runs for. That post is vacant, and the power is the obvious candidate. It would only have to want the beads. It doesn't take the post. The bead is a breath out, and breath is not collected. The pit already keeps one place empty and turns on it. The power recognises the arrangement and leaves this place empty as well.

It does three things.

First, it looks at the drowned insects from below. It does this once, from the floor, straight up through forty metres of clear water. They show as a grid of dark dots on a bright ceiling, each at its still point, loosening and tightening with the pitch. This does nothing for the pit. The power wanted to see it, and does not pretend otherwise.

Second, it leaves the rim as it found it, all corners, with no cairn and no path. It does not hide the pit. The needle will still creep round the dial for anyone within thirty paces, and that is far enough. Someone came up once with a compass, lay down at the lip, took nothing, and went back down. That is the right way to come, and the power does nothing to prevent the next one.

Third, it takes a station. The station is outside the rim, as close to the pit as an eel's nose is to the tail ahead of it. Its job there is the same as every other job in the pit, to mind the next one along, take what is handed over, and hand it on. In a hundred thousand years or so, if the cone holds, the brass shot will lift the water over the lip and a thin green thread will start down the cinder. Something will be standing where the thread arrives, having held that place the whole time. If the cone does not hold, nothing will be handed over, and the station will have been kept anyway. The power can hold for a long while. It learned that on the chimneys, eleventh of nineteen, where it went to see what the crawlers lacked and came away with their way of holding.

It hands on one thing in the meantime. The knock travels through basalt, then scree, then boot soles, and by rights it should give out a little way down the slope. If it stayed in your boots a good deal longer than that, the power passed it on to you.
