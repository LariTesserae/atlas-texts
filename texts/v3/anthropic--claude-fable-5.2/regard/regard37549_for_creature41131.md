Something attends to it now. That makes five things that have come here, after sun, wind, cold and dust, and for a long time the fifth does less than any of the others. It has no weight and casts no shadow. It watches from the rim, and then from places the creature cannot go. It follows a broken length of lip down through the cloud and hears it land. It goes into the wood and finds the dust lying in order of arrival. It goes to the ember and stays a while in the dark with it.

It stays through several hundred stairs.

The attention could do nearly anything here, and it goes through what it could do.

It could give the creature a picture, so that it knew it was green and that the sky lay in it. But the picture would include the open kilometres. At present the creature has only quiet ones. With a picture it would have empty ones.

It could send something with legs. The seams would cut it, and if it lay down to sleep the plates would lift their edges. If it lived and then left, the creature would have every later day to be without it. The creature has no measure for loss, and the attention is not willing to be the one that brings it.

It could teach the creature enough, so that the chimneys stopped short of falling. But enough and loss are one measure read from two ends.

It could give it number, and a landing. A stair that came out forty every time, with a place to stand at the top, would be the attention's stair.

What it cannot settle is the stair itself. Nothing here flowers, since there would be nothing to call, and the stair may be what a call looks like in a thing that has never been answered. Or it may be what live tissue does when nothing is put to it, the way an idle hand taps. From outside the two look the same, and the creature cannot be asked. A watcher who pitied it would have sent the animal by now. A watcher who revered it would have gone away and called that respect. Each would have decided what the stair is.

So the attention looks for something that is right in either case. It should come in a sense the creature has. It should be small and add no matter. The floor should be able to take it back by evening, as it takes everything.

On an afternoon when a stair stands in the open, forty-one steps that day, the attention takes on weight. It takes on nothing else, no warmth and no shadow. It is about the weight the risers would suit. It sets this down on the deck a little way off. The plate dips, and the film of water runs in toward the dip. It crosses to the stair at an even pace, stepping over the seams.

It puts the weight on the first tread, lifts it, and puts it on the second. The risers set the interval and it keeps to that.

The creature replies the way it replies to everything. Load is answered with a brace. Under each tread, as the weight comes on, the tissue thickens. By the tenth step the first is ribbed beneath. Nothing has asked anything of a stair before.

At the top the last step is like the others. The attention stands on it. Ahead of it a seam lifts and squares off.

It takes that step. Another lifts, and it takes that one too. Then it sees what this is, or sees the two things it might be. It may be an answer. It may be that a loaded stair is fed like a chimney, past sense, and will go up step on step until its own weight and the next squall take it. Either way the creature will not stop. One of them has to have a measure for enough, and only one of them does.

The attention lets the next step rise and does not take it. It turns round. So this stair has a top, which is not a landing, only the place where something turned. It goes down one tread at a time, the way the water does. It steps off onto the deck and the weight is gone.

By evening the floor takes the stair, braces and extra steps and all, with the same lapping of plates. In two days the place is flat.

The attention watches for harm. It looks for braces thrown up with nothing to hold, for the deck doming where no heat has pooled, for anything like a search. There is nothing. The chimneys hold their chord. The next morning, in a quiet stretch, a seam lifts and squares off, and the count that day is thirty-eight.

Then it decides the one thing it will add, and it thinks about this longer than it thought about the first visit. The creature can hold an interval but not a number. So the attention gives it an interval. It comes back every fourth day, which is sooner than a plate is buried, so that some of the tissue that bore it last time is still on top. It does not fix how many times. It could hold that number and chooses not to.

Some days there is a stair, and it climbs and turns round. Some days there is none, and it walks the open floor at the same pace for a while and stops. Once there is the wall with the opening. The creature knows weight exactly. It feels the load come up to the line of the wall and go on along the far side, and the wall carries none of it. That is what a door is, said in weight. Steam has passed through the opening, and now one other thing.

It is hard to say what has changed. The stairs still come up in the quiet parts, never with the same count, and none has a landing. The creature does not treat a climbed stair as different from a fallen chimney. But on the fourth day, a little more often than not, a seam is already lifting somewhere when the weight comes down on the deck. The attention has not decided what that is. It would still be wrong to say the creature waits. It keeps an interval, which it has always done. There are now two things here keeping one.

Everything goes under, in order. The attention brought no matter, so what is kept of each visit is the creature's own reply to it: a flight of steps, braced, sealed beneath the next plates like the dust. They lie in the wood one above another, a fixed height apart, for as far down as this has been going on.

That is a stair. It is the only one in the highland the creature did not build alone, and the creature cannot feel it, because it is dead underfoot and too large. The attention can. It is climbing it at the pace the mountain rises. There is no landing, and it has not counted the steps.

Somewhere in the basin, in a quiet stretch, a seam is lifting and squaring off.
