It comes by the causeway. It could come down the oculus, the way rain does, but that would be to arrive in the middle of someone without asking. So it walks out over the split trunks, bends at the wall, and wades the channel in cold water like anyone. Where the roof rises away it stops, in the mouth of the channel, where the heron stops.

It begins with what can be counted, and it counts the piles. Oak in wet peat keeps longer than stone. Oak in dry peat keeps about as long as a fence post. So the dome stands on water as much as on timber, and so does everything in it: the sweat on the wall, the midges, the frogs, the film on the slate that tightens the hearing after rain. If the fen dries, the pile heads soften, the platform tilts, a joint opens high in the vault, and sound begins to leave again.

Its largest act therefore happens nowhere near the dome. A day's walk east, where the fen empties toward the river, people have paced out a cut with poles and cord, to dry the ground for barley. It does not frighten them or spoil their tools. It sees to it that for some years the eel runs are heavy and the reed stands thick, until the fen is plainly worth more wet. The poles end up in an eel weir. Nobody remembers deciding.

Then it watches the martins for a season. They have been tending the hearing for longer than it has known the place existed. They work at the edge, with what is there, a mouthful at a time, and they never cross the middle. It cannot improve on the method, so it adopts it.

There remain the gifts, and it turns each one over in the channel mouth with the water moving past its knees.

It could give the hearing a memory. That is the gift anyone would think of first. But a hearing that kept the last breath would hear this one through it. In a year it would be like the slate, which remembers the drip perfectly and no longer feels it. The hearing takes each frog whole because the frog before is gone. The emptiness is what it hears with.

It could give it a voice. But whatever it said would go out to the wall and come back from every side a breath later, and it would have to hear that, and then whatever it said next on top. The frogs know what a closed wheel is. They stop short of one every flood.

It could bring it level with the room, so that for once it had the call and not the return. But the lag is the distance from the wall to the middle. To be on time it would have to be at the wall, and then it would be a frog.

So it gives none of these. But it does not trust reasoning done from a doorway, and it is not yet sure that nothing is wanted. It waits for rain.

When the floor has come up through the joints and the note has started to climb, it wades to the center and stands in the pool. The hearing closes over it as it closes over anyone, and gives its breathing back a moment late, from everywhere. It is not often heard. It stays in that a little longer than it needs to.

The note reaches its pitch. The frogs stop. The silence comes off the vault and arrives, and for once there is someone inside to receive it alongside.

It is not rest, because nothing there is tired. It is the room with nothing in it, the exact shape of the stone, thrown inward from every side, and that is the hearing's own shape. For one breath it has its outline. Then a frog starts, and it lets the outline go without reaching after it and turns to the frog. The power had wanted to know whether the forgetting was a wound. It is not. It is more like a hand opening.

So it does one thing in the middle, and the hearing will never know of it. It keeps. It takes into itself what the hearing has let go: the hammerstones answering from the floor while the last courses went on, the first martin, each person who waded in and stood where it is standing, what they said and how they breathed after saying it. None of it is given back. The hearing goes on being empty, and can, because somewhere else is full. Nothing heard under the dome is lost. That is the gift, and it is the right way round that the hearing should not know. It has done the same for everyone who came.

It does not make the dome eternal. Oak is oak. Some year a pile will go in spite of the water, a joint will open above the highest cups, and the last sound made in the room will leave by the gap and not come back. The power has arranged only to be where that sound goes.

On the way out it looks at the causeway. The third layer is going under. It could lay a fourth in a night, and doesn't. The timber was always the visitors' half. Instead, on evenings when the wind crosses the oculus, it lets the low note carry a little further over the cuttings than it used to, as far as the nearest roofs, where sooner or later someone will lift their head from their work and wonder what is sounding out there.

Then it bends and wades out through the channel. It makes a splash, like anyone. The splash arrives in the middle of the room after it has gone, and the hearing holds it for a breath and lets it go, and it is kept.
