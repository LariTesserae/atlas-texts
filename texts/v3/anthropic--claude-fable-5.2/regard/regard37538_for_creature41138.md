It comes up the red road like everyone else, and stops where the scoria stops.

That is the first thing it does, and for a power it takes some doing. Attention has weight, and it has seen what one person's weight did here in one second, forty or fifty years ago. It also knows about the sheep. So it stays on the gravel and looks, which the ground can bear.

Then it does what powers do, and goes looking for what is wrong.

The roar is the obvious thing. It could close the wells in an afternoon. It asks who the quiet would be for. The water bear has never been reached. The ravens have made a language out of bowing on domes and would not thank it. The steam is light in the kitchens below the rim, in the same towns where the springs come out. So it leaves the roar.

The green tongues take longer, because from the road they look like harm: lives that should last years, run through in a summer. But it counts the way the residents count, in wet hours, and the sum comes out the same. A summer without gaps and ten years full of them hold the same few months of climbing and drinking. The gaps were never life taken from anyone, since there is no one in them. Nobody in the tongue is short.

The ravens find some of the eggs. The cups older than the birds sitting in them say that enough are missed.

The bootprints it studies longest. It knows who walked out from the third dome, and why, and it keeps that to itself. What holds it is the result. The kindest thing anyone has done for the residents was done by someone breaking the one rule, with no kindness in mind, and it worked because it happened once. Done on purpose, done again, it is a trampled field. The power takes this as information about its own hands. It will not fill the prints, or sink new ones, or hurry the stems leaning in from the rims.

That leaves the water bear. The power sends its attention where it will not send its weight, down the line of dark holes to the farthest one, and looks for the want. It finds a leaf, a film of water, a cell to pierce, and the next leaf. It could keep the film from drying, but water is how the months get spent, and an extra hour comes out of the same account. It could give the animal something larger, some sense of the sky or the column or the size of the field, and that would be sheep again. The world down there is complete at the width of a leaf. The power had expected benevolence to have something to supply. It finds this harder than closing wells.

The plover is the one it cannot get past. The line learned to stand high and be seen, but it never unlearned the call. Every day the beak opens and something is sent that arrives nowhere. Of everything on the plateau, that alone looks to the power like a thing still owed.

So it does one thing. On a still morning in June, at changeover, the well being bled has already been shut in when the valve on the next one sticks. It sticks for twenty minutes. A fitter swears at it, hears himself, and stops.

The crew stand on the road with their defenders round their necks. For a while there is nothing at all, because the moss gives nothing back. Then, from a dome a little way out, comes one thin falling note, and the same note again. Two hundred metres off, another bird lifts its head and answers.

Somebody says, out loud, "Is that what they sound like," and is startled when the others turn.

Then the valve frees, the column goes up white, and everyone covers their ears. The plover goes on standing on its dome with its beak working. But the crew will look at that beak tomorrow and know what is in it. The power has left its mark in them, the only material up here that can take one without being crushed.

Under the road's edge, through all of it, the film on the top leaves went on thinning on schedule.

At four the power is back at the farthest print, as small as it can make itself. A leaf's worth of water becomes half a leaf's worth. The legs come in. The segments close into one another. After that, the power is the only thing at that end of the plateau still keeping the time.

It keeps it until the cloud comes over the rim and the field goes green. The water bear unfolds and reaches for the next leaf. Nothing has happened. The power, which knows otherwise, leaves by the road.
