It counts first, which it has in common with her. Its count comes out one higher than hers every time, and that is where it starts.

For a long while it does nothing else. Nothing in the room is getting worse, which is the argument for leaving. It goes through what it could do the way you go through the tubes, sideways.

It could finish the job: order the pour, cut the drain, tap the two bosses and fit the governor the casting was made for. Then the room would be what the drawings wanted, and the drawings never wanted a chough. A governor also corrects a drift after it happens, and she corrects before. It would be replacing her with something worse.

It could shut the penstock. The lamps would go out, the stripes would break up, and frost would come through the sleeve by dark. That is the release read as widely as she has read the hold, the same mistake in the other direction.

It could leave. But her fingers don't heal, and her weight is on the front foot. Her thumb is under the catch, and someone who holds one thing for her own sake has a sake. And she looks at hands before faces. She has not asked for the dam to be finished or to be let go. She has asked for a paper, because she was never told how far the word reached. That can be brought down nine rungs without breaking anything.

So it comes down in a shape that fits between the tubes, over the rung that is always wet. It does not go to her. It makes the round: bracket, sill, mug, sleeve. It keeps a palm on the bracket until the tick arrives. It leaves the level alone. It watches the mug long enough to get the seepage, and at the sleeve it counts flakes. It steps over the stripes. The choughs shuffle aside and shuffle back.

At the shuttering it works her row of figures, and they come out. Her check mark is through the face ply and needs nothing more. It makes its own beside hers, once, lightly. The figures have never before been checked by anyone who didn't write them.

Then it meets her in the tubes, her back to the pipe run and its back to the cable tray. She looks at its hands, and there is a paper.

*HOLD: east wall pour. Nothing else.*
*Figures checked. Agree.*
*Relief at spear valve. Break 10 min.*
*Will trim for snow.*

There is no name on it she would know. She reads it chest to chest, with fog beading on the pencil. Then she finishes her round, because she does nothing the quick way, and at the shuttering she holds the paper against the plywood, under the two marks. She stands there longer than at anything else on the round.

At the wheel she takes its wrist and lays its palm on the bare steel of the rim. It finds the sixteenth and takes it up. She watches the floor, the mesh of the lattice and the edges of the stripes, for a good deal longer than ten minutes. The mesh holds. She lets go of its wrist. Then, separately, she takes her other hand off the wheel.

What it could not have known from outside is that the wheel takes everything. The air in the sleeve is cooling and the speed has to fall a hair ahead of it. It has no attention left over to watch her with, and it decides that this is correct. A break that somebody watches is not a break.

So it knows her break the way she knows the speed, from the room. The whistle stops, and that has never happened. Then come wings. The steel empties tube by tube, and the midge sheet tears and mends, tears and mends. Somewhere behind its shoulder a wire catch is up.

When her hand comes back onto the rim beside its own, her mouth is closed. The tin on the flange sits lighter. The paper did not say she had to come back. She has come because the quiet is where the salamanders lie and she is the one who can keep it there. Her weight is on both feet.

She does not go straight to work. She takes its wrist once more, leads it to the cable tray above the exciter, and lays its palm on the sheathing under a nest. She watches its face. The tick that arrives is slower than the splice and not regular. When it nods, she lets go.

The rest it leaves, because the rest follows. The west wall begins to pale from the top, and the lime thins out of the air. The bare rim will rust now unless it is oiled, and she finds a rag. Her fingers ache that night, and the aching is the splits starting to close. There is a shell under the tray. Later a midge lies on the water, the first dead thing, until the grey lattice closes over it. Her count comes out different. She says it twice, then writes a second row of figures under the first and checks it once.

The east wall stays tied, shuttered and unpoured, with the bats along its top edge. The hold was right about the wall.

It leaves the two bosses untapped. She is near the end of her time, and that job is hers. It leaves the hatch, which never sealed. The first fledged chough will find the gap without help.

What it does is come back. It comes down nine rungs at every break, makes the round, checks the new row, and takes ten minutes at the wheel with the slack taken up. This is small work for a power. It has looked for something larger that would not be worse and has not found it.

By the lime it is getting on for afternoon. By her it is ten minutes past the break.
