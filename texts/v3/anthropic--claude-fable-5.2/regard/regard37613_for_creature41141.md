The attention, when it comes, has no plume. By eye there are still nine.

It does nothing at first. It stays beside her through one loaf, from the first anvil to the rim, and it can do what a walker cannot, which is turn back along the groove and still find her. So it learns where the chip came from, and the hitch in the third leg. It leaves them. The scuffs are already in the rut ahead of her, all the way to the rim, and the new plate would only grow the chip back in.

Then it goes through what it has to give.

It has rain. But the water in her has been in every one of them there has been, and rain has been in no one. She would be filled a second time, by a stranger.

It could give her her own blow. Being what it is, it can hear the stroke where it is struck, which you cannot, and it listens to a great many. Each is a short flat knock, like a plank dropped on a floor. One hammer is not much. What she has instead is herself a season back and a season on, at full volume, and the space between them that she lets each stroke go into. Given the knock, she would have a noise. What she has now is a place to put it.

It could turn the lenses outward. Then she would have the rim to look at, and the loaf going over it. As things are, she has never lost one.

It watches her stand the day with her jaw low over the edge, takes this for grief, and is ready to act on it. Then the bead falls. It looks under a shell at the millimetre of orange. She is making a delivery. It may be the other thing as well. The power is not shown which, and notices how much it wanted to be.

By the end of the loaf it has looked everywhere for something missing and found only things that are absent. Those are different, and the tableland stands on the difference.

One thing can be checked, so it checks. It goes out past the shells, where the roar shuts like a door, and down through the haze to where the loaves come in. Sound is ordinary down there. It weakens with distance and is heard best by whoever stands nearest. The power stands near. A loaf arrives. It lands the way anything lands, with one sound that has a source, and the sound fades. The loaf is not left where it falls. That is as much as belongs in this account, and more than she has asked.

So nothing needs mending, and a wise power would go. This one finds it cannot go without doing anything. That part of it is benevolent and not wise, and it can tell the two apart.

It allows itself a sound. From beyond the rim, twice a year, which is as often as it trusts itself, it sends one long note in over the shells. The note says nothing. What the power would like to say is that the loaf landed, and she has no use for that. The note only has a direction. On a plain where every sound comes from everywhere, it comes from somewhere, and so for the length of a breath there is a somewhere.

Every hammer stops. The shiver along her spine stops, which it does at no other time. For one breath she is not listening to herself anywhere. She is at one anvil. The calves turn their heads, each a different way. She does not. The power had hoped she would, and then sees she is right. The rim is on every side. There is no way she could face that is not toward it.

The first time it sounds the note, it learns it is not the first time. The hammers have been stopping twice a year for as long as the plateau has been this height. The breath was already worked in, like the chip in the new plate, like the scuffs in the rut ahead. And the note is not left where it falls either. When the roar comes back, each beast is one anvil on with no step taken, which the power never intended. Nothing that arrives here stays a gift. It is taken up into the work.

Before it went down, it did the things any walker can do. It put a hand on her flank, hot as a pan handle, and kept it there until the swell of the water had passed rearward under the palm and come back along the lower plate. It stood behind her in the dark while she raised the boss and held it. When the blow came, the two calves lifted their flat heads with hers and brought them down an inch short of the stone.

It could have closed the inch. It left it to them.
