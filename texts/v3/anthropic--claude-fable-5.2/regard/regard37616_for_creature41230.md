**Approach**

It does not arrive all at once. Anything that large, coming suddenly, is a shout, and it has understood from the ridge that nobody shouts here. It comes up from the valley on the afternoon wind with the midges and the winged ants, and sifts down through the gratings at about the speed of the bucket chain.

Before anything else it touches brass. It had come carrying a charge, four or five things it already meant to do, and it lets them go to earth along the rail. There is a small dry snap, which nobody remarks on.

**Three shifts**

It stays through all three shifts before forming an opinion, since that is the only way to see the whole town awake. It learns the knocks. It sits on the transformer ledge, where a chough goes through it, finds nothing, and tries again the next day. It reads the glazier's ledger back to the first page. It watches a cell slid a metre west with two sleepers and a cat inside, and is more moved than it expected to be.

During the eleven minutes it could hear what the mouths are saying under the drills. Nobody asks, and it does not either.

It goes to the east end and looks at the creased door. Nothing is explained. It takes this to be how the town teaches, and decides to keep to it.

**What it knows**

It reads the rock, which no one else can. The fracture will not finish in the lifetime of the vein. The rock would not bear much more drilling than it gets. It would bear a speaking voice, a dropped spanner, laughter, and probably a song. The town is right about the drills and wrong about the laughter, by a wide margin.

It considers telling them and decides against it. The figure is true this month, and the rock is still moving. People who know a limit work up to it. The town's margin has carried it for thirty-one years through things no figure would have covered, and what lives here, the single habit, lives in that margin. It keeps the number to itself.

**What it sets down**

- **Holding the walls.** It could, with a palm on the stone. It asks itself plainly whether a habit is worth a life, and does not think so. But the bucket goes down every day, nearly always empty of people. The eldest of the born rode it down, saw water run unweighed, and came back up with the ice. Everyone here remakes that decision daily, and each child at eight is shown what is being weighed. It will not overrule them. If the walls were stilled, the slips would stop breaking, and within a year someone would cough without reaching for felt. The four hundred would remain, and the thing it came to see would be gone.
- **Water.** Falling water would freeze on the gratings and foul the separators. A spilled cup is watched the way it is because it is the only one.
- **Red.** The same reasoning applies to red.
- **A voice.** Here every knock is a person, and the knocks of the dead are never given again. A message on the rail from a knock nobody gave would be a haunting.

**The loose bolt**

For the time being it lives here, and so under the town's one law: a loose bolt belongs to whoever sees it.

It has seen one. Behind the transformer casings, where the off-shift sit with their backs to the warmth, a hairline has opened in the south wall. No glass crosses it. This is the one stretch of rock nobody watches, because it is where they go to stop watching, and the heat works at it every day.

It will not hold the hairline shut. Instead it moves a moth. A moth that would have died on a bulb dies at the foot of the south wall, and a spider comes into the hairline for it. The next day there is another moth, and after nine days the line is thick with spiders. On the tenth, during the eleven minutes, with every face turned up, a wallcreeper leaves the north face, crosses the gap, and clings above the ledge, flicking its wings.

It is the only red in the place and it is on the wrong wall. Four hundred people look, and the glazier looks longest. By the next shift she is behind the casings with cement and a slip, and the ledger has a new line, measured from the east cell.

Nothing is explained.

**The tick**

Nineteen days later the slip breaks with a single clear tick.

The wedge-turner's palm stays on the stone, the brush stays raised, the cat stays flat, and the choughs stand fixed along the trays. It stops too. This is the first thing it has done here that it did not decide to do. It does not count aloud, and it arrives at thirty within a breath of the rest.

A knuckle on the rail says *held*.

That week the riggers shift the transformers and the ledge four metres west. The off-shift find the warm place moved and do not remark on it. The month after, a plate of gneiss the size of a cell door lets go where the casings stood and drops into the nets. The nets are emptied on the hour.

**After**

It draws no ice, so the clerk's count stays right. It keeps the retired knocks, since no one else is permitted to.

Then it goes down in an empty bucket, with a chough riding the rim.

In the valley, water runs along the ground. In a kitchen, someone drops a pan and the talk goes on. Near the door, something has gone still. At thirty it finds the nearest metal, a tap, and puts a knuckle to it.
