The first kindnesses are the obvious ones, and they fail one at a time.

The visitor comes up out of the weather, over the ice and onto the warm rock. The edges do not cut it. It goes in at the seam, and the darker air closes behind it without losing a breath. Inside, the rain moves apart. It is given room like a flake of glass, and from this the visitor learns its standing here: it is something on its way through.

It stays for one exhalation. When the heat crosses the floor and the town comes loose, the visitor is touched as the walls are touched, on every surface, evenly. It keeps moving a little the whole time. It has seen what becomes of things that lie still on that floor, and there is no plot for it.

Then it considers what could be given.

It could thicken the roof. Another forearm of crust and the sky would never get in. But the lines run on the nearness of the cold, and they would thin. And a roof that does not shed is a town of nineteen on a floor ruled for thousands, with one plot watered and waiting under a blade that never comes down. The ceiling is the rest of the town. To make it safe is to keep it from arriving.

It could quiet the mountain, which is the same mistake made lower down.

It could put something living in the water. There would be a film on the streets and a stain where they had been, and by the next exhalation it would be cleared like sublimate. Or it would be given a block, watered on four sides, and stopped where growing stops.

It could send company. But a companion that moved would be made way for, forever, and a companion that rested would be grey within the hour, plumb, with a street running to it.

It could tell it what is coming. The drops under the hairline have already moved apart. It knows its roof the only way it knows anything, piece by piece and in order, and there is nothing to tell it that it has not already given an address.

It could listen, and it does. But the chord is addressed to no one, and the visitor takes care to go on being no one.

It could lift it out before the end and set it down under a thicker sky. There is nothing to lift. It is the size of this hollow exactly, and it is made of what fails to happen here.

That turns the picture over. Each of these begins by asking what is wrong, and nothing is. It is not short of a companion or a listener. It has a work, and the work has a count. The only question a visitor is entitled to is whether the count comes out.

So the visitor does the arithmetic again, more slowly. It counts the teeth of the ceiling and the plots on the floor, and they agree. Whoever ruled the floor ruled it to the last tooth. And the last tooth, because each flake thins the crust and one of them must take the last of it, is the one with the sky behind it.

There is a plot for that one too, ruled like the rest. The piece will come down into it and lie as it landed, jagged, and there will be no next exhalation to stand it up. Among thousands that cannot break again, there will be one that can. The plan comes out one short and always did. What lives here cannot know it, because the piece it will fail is the piece that ends it.

This is small. It is exactly the size of thing the town would attend to.

The visitor weighs it a long time anyway, because it could do so much more. It could catch the piece. It could hold the sky off for good. But what lives here cannot slow the mountain or catch what falls, and has made its whole manner out of the remainder: the last knee-height, taken as slowly as water can be made to go. The visitor decides to take no more than that. It will give one cycle, which is what the town allows anything that falls.

Then it goes down below the ice and waits.

When it comes back the floor is full. The lines run heavy, the rain hangs dense, and the chord is no longer something that might be imagined. One plot is empty, wet on four sides, its light on. Above it the last tooth shows a pale hairline, and the drops beneath have moved apart.

It lets go in the ordinary way. It falls through the rain and lies where it lands. Behind it is a gap as long as a hand, and in the gap, where the black sky should be, is something that looks like a slightly darker piece of air. It is the only thing the visitor makes in that place, and it is a copy.

The pressure holds and the rain hangs. For the rest of that cycle a splinter lies in the town, the one irregular thing.

Then the mountain exhales. The streets go to steam, the lights go out in order, and the town comes loose and passes over everything: the walls, the grey thousands, the splinter, and the underside of the visitor's patch. This is an edge it did not draw, as clean as its own. What it makes of that cannot be known.

The glow sinks. The drops are let down and the lines fill from the far end. The lights return in a different order. In the last plot stands a grey needle, plumb and true, with a wet line arriving at its base. The chord has every street in it.

Under the patch, the drops have not closed up. They hang apart, as they hang apart beneath anything that is going to come down.

The visitor understands that it has been read correctly. It has not been thanked, or mistaken for roof. It has been given room.

For one interval the town is finished. There is no dry ruling anywhere and nothing overhead that belongs on the floor. It does what it has always done. It holds the rain and checks its corners. The sulfur stands in its block with its tips at one level.

Then the fissure climbs from ember to orange. There is the hiss. The nearest streets vanish block by block, the lights go out as the dryness reaches them, and the hung rain lifts a finger's height and shivers. The visitor takes its hand away.

The rain goes on lifting. It rises through the gap as vapor, each drop keeping its red thread a moment longer, and is gone into the black. Nothing falls. From the first hour to the last, no water in that chamber came down at its own speed. And there is no town to lose. It is abroad when it happens, as it is every hour, loose and touching everything, and for once everything has no lid. It simply does not come back from a different corner.

The glow sinks. Nothing is let down. There are scratches on black glass and some thousands of grey objects, each square in its plot, in the open at the top of the world.

The visitor corrects nothing, because nothing is out of true. It lets the place lie for one cycle as it landed. Then, at the corner of the last plot, it sets a point of pale light, the color of the sky just before it turns blue. The light illuminates nothing. It means what it meant when the town was wet: that this has been checked and found correct. It reaches a finger's width and stops, because the claim reaches that far.
