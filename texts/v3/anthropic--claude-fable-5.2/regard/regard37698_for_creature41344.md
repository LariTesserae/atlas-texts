A power of that kind has to settle first how to arrive.

The basin does with attention what it does with iron. Anything that stands above the knee and keeps still becomes the highest point in a day's walk, and whatever this power is made of, it conducts. If it stood on the floor and looked, the needles would lean, the cell beneath would swell out of turn, and frost would be laid toward it shift by shift. Within a few seasons it would be studying a place arranged around its own regard. A basin with no middle would have been given one.

So it does not stand. It waits under the western rim until the cloud floor heaves, and comes over inside the fog-tide, lower than the knee, at walking pace. The basin knows what to do with fog. The needles comb the power along with everything else and grow their feathers pointing into it. It crosses once, rim to rim, which takes a day in a place that has none. It looks the way the corona asks to be looked at, a little to one side.

It has come carrying the usual offers, and the crossing takes them from it one at a time.

The first is company for the grazer. Four hundred metres is not far, and a little frost laid crooked would bring two tracks together. But she reaches each cold cell with her wool stood out from her body, and that is everything she brings. Two adults who met would spend it on each other in one blue thread, and both would walk on sleek and narrow to cushions that failed to rise. The distance between them is the cargo.

The fog reaches the iron, and the power reads what is on the polished face. It is not much. Put as nearly as it can be, it says that the one who left it was here, and was still walking. Every animal within a kilometre takes this up from several others once a loop. The power had been ready to call her alone.

Passing, it does the one thing it will do here. It brushes the face at the height of a fleece. There is a tick and a blue thread. It leaves what the grazers leave, in as near their manner as it can manage, and takes up what was there, set down some hours before by an animal now two kilometres round her track.

Further on it passes a hunter at a bend as the long edge lifts, and it does not look away. It is over before the cell has finished subsiding. Later it passes another, lying across an old kill in a browned patch, cold, with the copper hair already grown into it along its whole length. This is the animal a kindness would remove. It is also the one that pays for each meal by putting out its own fire and waiting to be let back in. The power dislikes the folding edge and keeps the dislike. But it can find no way to lift the hunter out that does not also settle, for every grazer on the massif, how the walking is to end, and it is not sure enough of a better ending to choose one.

The third offer is the generous one, and it is meant for the discharge itself, which wants better paths. All over the basin there are joints where the ore gave out and the roots are making wire a hair at a time. A power could lay a seam in each of them between one shift and the next. Then it looks at what stands in those gaps now. A cell with a seam comes on by itself. A cell without one comes on because a fleece touches it, and lays down frost so that the fleece will return. Every gap closed in copper is a wage stopped. With its seams made good the basin would run better than it ever has, in the dark, with nothing walking in it. The discharge would not mind, because it is not particular. The power is, and it leaves the gaps as they are, since that is where the grazers live.

At the eastern rim it thinks about the upright one, who has not come yet, and thinks longer than it did about any of the others. To the basin it would make no difference whether that one was still breathing when the wire arrived. To the power it would. It goes along the tors looking for what is missing and finds the notice already given. The tors stand higher than a head, and the sky comes down through them first. Someone who kept close under one could look for as long as breath held out. Out on the floor the corona gathers at the shoulders inside a minute, the cell rises under the boots, and three hundred metres off a head turns. Nothing about the place is concealed from a person who stands still in it. A warning set on the rim would be one more tall thing. The power leaves the rim alone and marks which tor gives the widest lee, so as to know where to look.

Then it goes out over the edge with the last of the fog, carrying what it took from the iron. Whatever route it keeps is laid a little nearer the massif from then on. It will come over again inside some later tide, pass the iron, and go on.

Behind it the rimed cells come on shift in their turn. The ice melts and runs to the joints, and for an hour the channels carry a finger's depth of black water before they film over.

Some hours later a grazer bends her loop to the iron and takes up on her wool a thing she has not met before. She stands with her head raised for a while, though there is no direction to hold it in. The frost is where it always is, and she follows it. The next cell has no seam. She brushes down its margin and the needles tick. At the far corner she is sleek and narrow, and the cushion behind her has begun to rise.
