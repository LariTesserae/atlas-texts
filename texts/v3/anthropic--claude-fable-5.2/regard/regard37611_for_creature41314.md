The power's first act is to stay where it is. It stays below the cloud for a long time and listens upward.

What it could do is plain. The pallor behind the eastern ridge has been about to arrive for longer than the map lichen has been growing, and letting it arrive would take very little. The mussel-shell would go to rose and the rose to gold. Drops would make rings, snow would lie, and the bud by the tarn would open its hands. The wall is open to that side and her shadow lies that way.

The power looks at what comes after gold. Morning is the first part of a day. The pouch would empty by the pinch, her face would begin to keep what crossed it, and the years that have occurred to her would start to be hers. That may be what she is waiting for. But a shadow pointing is not a nod, and the power has not been asked.

It could also leave her be. Nothing here is broken, and she has her work, her round and the spider's regard. The power stays close to this choice for some time. What moves it in the end is the floor of the ring wall, where she sweeps both halves.

So it comes as the one thing the place has room for.

It comes up the western step, out of the cloud, the way the midges and the winged seeds come. It will not come from the east. She expects something from the east, and the power is not that thing and will not be mistaken for it.

Below the lip it leaves everything it might have brought: fire, bread, a word that carries, the morning. This is harder for a power than for most. When she turns among the cairns, all at once and squarely, and lifts her hands, the hands it lifts back are empty, and it has made sure they are.

It sits in the swept grit and she sits on her slab. She shows it the nine.

The power has always heard everything. It hears the basin whole, each drop, each grain, both choughs and the thing under the cloud. It has never before heard one thing. She touches the first pebble, points at the north wall and holds the point. The crack of the slab stands out from the weight of the rest, single and clean, and the power finds that knowing a thing is there is different from being shown it. It does not hurry her. At the sixth it does not ask which she means. At the eighth it listens under the cloud, where it has just been, and what she has got out of there is not what it would have chosen.

At the gap she stops, looks down at the pebble in the grit, and does not point anywhere.

The power has known since below the cloud that the roar holds no tenth. What she is listening for is the morning, and the morning cannot be in the roar, because the roar is what is keeping it. Here the morning would be heard before it was seen, and what would be heard is the roar leaving off. A pebble stands for a sound. What her hands set stays if it is so, and this one is not so, and the place puts it back.

But she left the capstone room, the way she left the wall its opening and the floor its other half. The gap has been on the wall as long as the nine have, a thumb's width, kept clear. It is the one thing on the wall she has never touched and held.

The power does what a guest does, and shows her one in return for her nine. It leaves the pebble where it lies. It puts one finger in the gap, on the bare capstone, as she touched the others. Then it does what she did and does not point anywhere. It holds that.

She looks at the finger for a long time. Then her head turns a little, and she listens to the place where nothing is.

It comes clear the way the others do. At first it is a thinness in the roar, straight ahead. Then it is a place where the roar is not. For as long as she holds it, the sound has nothing, and the happening goes back to the things it was taken from.

A drop lands in the tarn with a small round noise that has an edge to it. A ring goes out, reaches the orange lichen and wets it.

Snow lies, a breath's worth, a few grains to a stone. One grain is on the capstone, in the gap, a thumb's width from the ninth.

The wind arrives on the skin. It takes the rime feathers off the near stones as it passes, and that is part of it too.

A slab lets go on the north wall and is seen to fall. At the foot of the fan there is one pale face of new rock.

Behind the eastern ridge the mussel-shell takes on a line of rose along its lower edge, no wider than a thread.

She hears breathing, and it is hers.

Whatever her face does then, it keeps.

Then she lets go, as she lets go of the others, and it goes back in. The roar is again the first fact of the place and the largest, and the air is cave air. But the lichen at the tarn's edge is dark with wet, the grain is in the gap, and the thread of rose has not gone. She puts her hand to the pouch on her cord and weighs it. It is lighter by a pinch. She is someone who knows what a pinch is. She is as hungry as someone an hour before a meal, less one breath.

The power watches her take in the whole of it, the rose and the pinch together, and does not help her with that.

It gets up. The swept half is for whoever comes, and one who stayed would be something else. She stands and lifts both hands, palms out, and it lifts its own. Then it says thank you, which is felt in the throat and not heard. She watches its mouth make the words and gives no sign. They will come round in the roar in their time, and she will be listening.

It goes back by the western lip. It has neither brought the morning nor kept it from her. The morning is now where her hands can reach it, in breaths, at its price, to take as fast or as slowly as she likes, or never. The power does not know which she will choose, and finds it does not need to.

At the foot of the wall the tenth pebble lies in the grit, directly below the gap, where it has stayed.

The power is one step below the lip, in cloud, when the roar stops behind it for the length of a held breath. After a while it stops again, a little longer.
