It stops a long way off. This is the first thing the power does, and most of what it does.

It is large, and it can see the rule here: whatever is large enough to shade gets lifted. If it leaned over the saddle, the ice beneath its attention would stop wasting while everything around sank. In a few seasons the keeper would have raised it on a stalk and handed it back to the sky. It has been handed back to the sky by a great many places. It knows what a plinth feels like from on top.

So it stays at the distance from which it can look without casting a shadow, and not a finger further. It recognizes that distance. The keeper holds the same one, for the opposite reason.

Then it looks for a long time, and finds that nothing here is asking. That is rarer than it sounds. Benevolence is used to being needed. It goes through what it could do anyway, because it is thorough:

- explain the sea
- bring the platform down
- let the thought carry over from one day to the next
- hold the nights cold forever

It could explain the sea. But then the keeper would have an answer where it had a bead, and it never wanted an answer. It wanted the bead, whole, at the right depth, and it has that.

It could lower the platform and deliver the gift at last. But the power heard what those people said when they laid the stones, and it is not certain the ice was who they were talking to. The sky may have been the right address all along. Besides, the keeper has their pin.

It could join the days, so the thought that stops mid-thread at dusk would resume at sunrise. Then the keeper would begin to conclude things. It would carry the grey tongue inside it instead of hanging it over the cliff.

The last one it considers longest. The balance is not guaranteed. It is an agreement between the height of the pass and the temper of the air, and airs change. A season may come when the day spends more than the night gave, and then another. The power could put a thumb on the ledger. But a kept balance is not a balance, and the keeper lets its runnels stop mid-thread without minding. The power decides only this much: if that season comes, nothing held here will be lost. It has a second floor of its own.

So it does none of them. I want to say that plainly, because it is the truest part. What it does instead is small, and there are two of them.

First, it listens. The keeper's whole speech takes a day, and no one has stayed for the end of the sentence since the wood by the fire ring was still brown. So the power makes itself middle-sized, which for it is like holding a breath. It comes up the rock rib before dawn and sits down inside the cupped hand of the windbreak. It does not burn the wood. It hears the first tick, then the others. It hears the hush under the cloud and the graupel hissing on the blades. It hears the quarter hour of creak and ping, and then the stop. It does not answer, since nothing was asked. But the thing has been heard once all the way through, by someone who knew it was speech.

Second, it loses something. This is harder for a power than it sounds. Whatever it lets go of, it lets go of on purpose, and a thing let go on purpose is an offering, and it has seen where offerings end up here. The keeper could not tell the difference. The power can, and wants it done properly. So in the morning, when it gets up stiff from the gravel and goes back down the rib, it arranges not to know what it is carrying loosely. It does not check, and it does not look back. For something that sees everything, this takes more effort than moving the horns would.

Something falls. I can't tell you what. If I knew, it would know.

By the next noon there is a new well at the near end of the old lane, where the blades are short. A forearm down, something small and dark hangs in clear water, slightly enlarged. The keeper looks up through it at the indigo sky, learns nothing about the power, and keeps it perfectly.

And the power, back at its distance, is looked at. It is not asked, thanked, or lifted. It is looked at, through a thing it lost, by something that wants nothing from it. It had not come for that. It returns for it most noons.

At dusk the wells skin over, that one with the rest. The power does not mind the interruption. It learned that here.
