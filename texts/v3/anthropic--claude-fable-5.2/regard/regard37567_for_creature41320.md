She came round by the lee side.

It was the first thing she decided, and nearly the last. A power of her kind is used to arriving from whichever quarter the need is on. Here she walked the long way across the salt to come up to the wall with the wind in her face, so that her body would shelter no mouths. She left no track.

She stood at the rim through one full turn of the light, white to pewter and back to white. She went through the room as she had gone through every place she was ever called to, asking of each thing what it lacked.

**What she had with her**

Her kind carry things: water, mending, company, and meaning for those who have run out of it. She had all of it with her, and she looked for somewhere to set it down. There was no floor to see and nowhere to put a foot.

**The dumb jars**

She could have closed the cracks, and the chord would have had its thirty teeth back. But in each mended jar the wind would get under the spoonful of grit and lift it out with the rest of the dust, cushion and all. The room had made this trade already, one jar at a time, and nothing in it asked for the trade to be undone. She would not choose between the sound and what lives where the sound is not.

**The seed**

This held her longest. The seed went over the wall where she stood, at about the height of her knee, and on to the salt where nothing comes of it. Waste is what her kind is for.

So she counted. A jar cracks through once in a long while, and a grain stays once in a long while. There were thirty silences and thirty cushions, and no silence stood empty. The seed was not being wasted, then. It was being offered at the only rate the room could take it. Anything she helped to root would need a jar broken for it.

**The water**

Before dawn she watched the shards go cold and take their film of damp. She had rain with her. But rain would stand in the jars, and a jar with water in it gives a different note, then none, and afterward it has a tide-line. She left the insides clean.

**The beat**

She could hear which two jars they were. Disagreements are brought to her to be settled, and a hair's weight of clay would have settled this one. She listened and found that nothing was driven by it and nothing was hurt. It was the one place in the chord where you could hear that hands had made the jars. She let them disagree.

**The chord**

The chord was the hardest. It was the only thing there on her own scale, and she would have liked to be of use to it.

She could give it a hearer. But it does not call, and a thing that is heard is a thing that can go unheard.

She could give it a memory. But the jars remember for it. A memory would give it a yesterday, and with the yesterday the first lack it had ever known.

She could give it a rest, but it was not tired. She could give it a phrase, but it was not saying anything, and to have something to say is to need someone to say it to.

And she could hold the wind. She thought about that all through the dark. The wind will not always come from that quarter at that pace, and she could see to it that it did. But none of the air in the chord was in it a moment ago. It has ended and been made again at every instant since the first, and has never been lost and never been kept. A chord that happens and a chord that is held in place are different things. The makers knew which one they had built.

**The hands**

She understood the makers last. She had taken them for an absence, someone to be thanked or brought back. She held her hand near a palm-print in the brick and did not touch it.

They had limed the walls year over year until the lime stood thick as rind, and when it no longer needed renewing they stopped. Then they went, and their going was the last piece of the work, as much a part of it as the shards chinked on edge. There is no track on the salt because they finished.

She saw that she had been shown how.

**What she did**

She listened. It was not for the chord's sake, and she was clear with herself about that. The chord was given to no one. She only stood where it could be heard, as the lichens grow in the lee of the jars.

She set nothing down. She put no ward on the place and gave it no name, since a ward is a kind of lid. She never stood on the windward rim, so a lost traveller has had more dealings with the chord than she did.

The rule of the place is plain once seen, and she found it applied to her. Every space was filled, so there was nothing to add. Every mouth was left open, so nothing was shut against her either. She had been let in as far as the wind is, all the way and across.

She had always known the first half of that rule; filling is what her kind does. She took the second half with her. It was the only thing she took.

Then she went back over the pan with everything she had brought, toward the places where something is waiting. At a hundred paces she could still feel the chord in her teeth, and a little further on she could not. She did not turn round. It was not calling her, and that she could hear it so far was only its size.

Behind her the chord held, the north corner low and the east wall bright. It kept the same slow beat and the same thirty gaps, each with something grey and alive in it.
