The visitor arrived the way a front does, as a direction first. For some time a part of the plain was being attended to, and nothing else about it was different.

It did not begin with the builder. It began with the stones, since they were most of what there was, and stayed with them until it could tell one roar from another. Then it took the weed, the clampers, and the wader. A wader turned and faced it once, and waited. Either that was the only time a wader has been wrong, or what arrived from that direction was simply not down.

Then it walked crosswise behind the builder to eleven rings, or to one ring eleven times, and did nothing. This took longer than anything it did afterward.

---

It had come able to do a great deal, and the plain looks like a place where something has gone wrong. So it went through the list honestly.

It could break the cloud. But a sky would put out the ember the way noon puts out a coal, and the builder finds flat stones as dark shapes in that glow. Under a sun it would walk crosswise through bright water, looking for something it could no longer see.

It could fasten down to the ground again. Then the water would have something to obey and would leave. The stones would stop. The plain would go quiet, then cool, and the weed would lie with one side up and the other side dying. That would be a mended world, and nothing now alive here would be in it.

It could mend the joins. It considered this longest, because the joins look most like damage. But the builder lives in the looseness. It has never been startled by the sky because the water shows it the flash early. It never has to find the ring because distance and place have come apart, and there is always a ring about a stone's carry away. Fasten them back together and the ring is in one place, on a floor with no landmarks, and the builder dies looking for it. What looked like ruin from above was, at the height of a low back, the terms of a life.

It could open a seam. It stood inside one for a while, with the stones climbing through it, and looked both ways. It knows now whether there are two builders or one that takes both downhills. It has not acted on what it knows. The builder has looked through a seam too, and seen, and did not cross. The question belonged to the builder, and the builder had answered it.

By then the visitor understood what it had been following. Someone had already made one place on the plain where the water is still, where a thing stays where it is put, where the weed is allowed a top and a bottom, and where something can lie down on its side. It had been done with no power at all, by keeping round stones still. The visitor had come to see whether the plain needed a keeper, and it had one.

That left the question of what to do for someone already doing the work. The answer to that is old. You carry a stone.

---

First it tested the guess. It lay down in the open, low and broad, and kept the stones beneath it from rolling. Fronts went over it. The black film came, and then the rust crust. Clampers found it and wore seats. A wader stood on it and left pale prints. It stayed until there were seats inside the seats.

When it finally got up, it left its weight where it was, because of the clampers. There is now one more sitter on the plain than the old ranges account for. Under where it had lain, for a few paces, the stones were flat.

So the guess is right. The next front took them, planing and sliding, dark in the ember. Some went into a seam and out the other side. Stones cross seams even if builders do not, so whatever is over there got its share.

It kept back one stone from the edge of the patch, a white one that had stayed round.

Then it did the part that cost something. It could have been at a ring without traveling. But every stone in the row means the same thing: this was carried here, through that. A stone that had not been carried would be a false entry in the only record the plain has. So it took a shape for the walk, low and four-limbed with the head down. It was not a disguise. It is the shape that walking crosswise through rolling stones makes of anything, eventually. It put the white stone under its jaw, let the roar into its breastbone, and went.

It walked during fronts. The cobbles rolled out from under its feet. Carrying a round stone, it learned what a flat one under the jaw is for. In a silent patch it slowed. With the roar gone there was something small and regular in its chest, which it had not put there on purpose, or perhaps it had. It checked on it, and it was still going.

Then there was a ring. It was farther than the visitor expected, exactly as far as a stone can be carried. That distance is set by the carrier, and the visitor had not known its own.

The builder was inside, asleep on its side, with the clampers grazing its back. The visitor did not wake it or touch it. It did not strike the vein, which was not its to strike. It set the white stone down at the end of the row.

Then it stayed for the length of the sleep. Nothing else on the plain sleeps, so nothing on the plain had ever been watched over before. It is not clear which of them that was for.

While it sat there it made the only other use of what it had come able to do. The wearing will go on. It will get into joins no one has thought of yet, and the visitor will let it, for the reasons above. But there is one join the plain did not make and the builder did: the join between a thing and where it was put. The visitor rested a forefoot flat on that one. It had not made the join true. It undertook only that the join stay true. Whatever else comes loose, a stone set down inside a ring will be where it was left.

It went over the wall before the builder woke.

---

The builder woke and cropped the green nap. It took a white cobble from the floor and drew it along the vein, corner to corner, and for a breath there was a line of orange with edges to it.

The row was one stone longer than it had left it. The last stone was round and white, the commonest object there is.

It did not seem troubled. Before it left, it set its own stone down beside that one. It rested a forefoot on the vein and went over the wall. The clampers pulled down tight, and the roar came back into its jaw.

Some fronts later, walking crosswise with its head down, it saw two dark shapes in the ember where it would have expected none. It pinned the nearer one with a forefoot and let the cobbles pile up behind its leg.
