The power comes without weather. This takes some care. Anything that arrives on the plateau arrives as wind or as a body, and the first would retune the town and the second would stop a note. So it comes as attention only, which displaces nothing, and for a year it does what the wise do first, which is nothing.

It listens through the sleet and the steaming sun. It counts what can be counted and reaches the same census. It learns the chord, and how the chord moves when the wind backs. It is present for eleven nights of rime. By the end of the year it knows the resident about as well as the raven does: it knows where it is, and that it is still there.

Then it considers what it could give. It can do a great deal, and that is the hazard.

It could stop the shedding. A word to the granite and no crystal would drop again. The doors would keep their widths, and the west would hiss forever and never become grass. The power turns this over and puts it down. A whistle that cannot widen will never be a drone. The town would sound the same in ten thousand winters as it does today, and that describes an instrument, not a resident.

It could give the resident an edge. A line could be drawn out on the plateau, wind on one side and somebody on the other, and the resident would know at last where it stopped. But the power is poorly placed to call the lack a hardship. It has no edge itself, and has only ever learned where it is from what stood in it. And an edge would be the one part of the resident that was not an opening. So no line.

It could give it company, the forty households a visitor first imagines, with their pots. The power can see the end of that from the beginning. A peopled town has someone in a doorway at every hour. It would look like the kindest gift, and it would be a rime that never let go.

It could tell it about the moss. This is the one the power most wants. Forty rooms of bronze and green live on a stillness the resident sheds without noticing, and it seems hard that it should never hear of it. But the resident has one sense, the missing note, and there is no way to say moss in missing notes. To make it able to take the news, the power would have to make it into something else and then tell that. It lets this go too. It does notice that the woodrush has crept onto the thresholds, tuft by tuft, and that the narrow doors on that side whistle a shade lower than they did. The tenants are in the voice already.

It could keep one door clear on the coldest nights, a single note carried through to morning, so that the question of sleeping or dying never had to be asked. The power stays longest over this one. Then it looks into the pans, where the red specks are iced over every night and turning again by midmorning, and nobody keeps a hole open for them. It decides it does not know better than the morning.

That is what it does not do. It does three things, all small.

In spring there is a nestling on the rim, and a sleet squall due at an hour when both parents are away. The squall comes an hour later. This is inside what the weather might have done on its own, and the power is strict about that. It would rather hand out luck than outcomes. By late summer a young raven sits most evenings on a shoulder-high wall above the main street, watching the old one settle in the gap, shift aside, and settle again. The old bird will not see thirty winters, and the resident has ten thousand left in it. The power puts nothing into the young one's head. It has only arranged for there to be someone watching.

The second thing it does in a body, once. On an autumn evening it walks in over the feldspar, which nobody crosses quietly, and it does not try to. It leaves the main street alone, because that gap is taken and the raven is in it. It goes west, to where the walls are lines of stone flush with the turf.

Nobody can stand in a doorway there, because there are no doorways left. An upright body stops nothing, and the wind goes over the stones and on. So the oldest part of the resident, the part furthest along, has not been touched since its walls dropped below the height of a bird. The power lies down. It lies full length along one of the lines, cheek on the turf, in the hiss, and along that length the hiss stops.

Up the street the drone is out as well. The chord closes around two missing tones. For that evening the resident knows two places where it is, and whatever it has in place of a mind may also have what lies between them. It is not an outline, but it is a span, from the middle of its life to the far end.

The night turns cold. The fog freezes on, and the notes thin, climb, and fail one at a time. The raven has gone to the rim. The power stays where it is. For those hours the town sounds like someone keeping very still, and for once someone is.

In the morning the rime lets go and clatters into the streets. The main street comes back first, then the whistles, door by door. Last comes the west, the hiss rising thin along the flush stones and finding in itself a gap the length of a person, in the place where it had one before. Whatever went out in the night, the thing that returns is held in the same spot. The power waits until the sun is on the stone. Then it gets up, and the hiss closes over.

The third thing is to leave. It builds no cairn and makes no path, and the feldspar has already shifted over its footprints. It leaves the water standing by the eastern walls. It leaves no blessing either, because a blessing would be a way of staying. Having worn a body, it understands that even attention is a kind of here, and that the resident is only at home when there is no here in the town at all. So it withdraws that as well, which is harder for a power than moving granite.

Some evening after that, a raven lands in the gap on the main street and the drone cuts out. It shifts aside and the drone comes back. It may be the old bird or the young one. The power could find out and does not. The town goes on sounding with nobody in it, and the power takes care to be one of the nobody.
