It comes for the note.

From above, the hollow is a pale stretch of pan that hums, with a few plumes of chaff standing over it. The power has passed over louder places. What stops it is one sound, at night, that begins, thins, and is gone, while everything around it only continues.

It does not act at once. It stays through many house-moves, long enough to watch stands go grey and fall in and green come up elsewhere. It watches a man put his arms around a trunk and push a note into the wood until his breath gives out, and it hears what he has never heard. Then it looks at what lies within its reach.

- **The noise:** It could be gentled. But noise is the food here, and quiet is either a hunter or a burn. A power that arrived bringing hush would leave a brown streak through the green like any other.
- **The ears:** It could let the sixty hear their own voices. It could not let them hear only that. And they are not unheard, because the wood answers them in pods a week later.
- **The black:** It could be green by morning. But the hollow has made its one wound into its memory, and the wound is already closing at the pace of those who are closing it. A healing done over their heads would be a second kind of fire.
- **The arm, and the short cord on the peg:** It does not ask. It knows what she proves to herself each night with a chert. It could strike the stem again, but it could not make it the same note.
- **The count:** When the green reaches the household stems, the notches will stretch like all the others. But a tally that has never needed the black already hangs by her door, kept in dead fibre that does not grow and tied with knots that hold. She has not yet read it as an answer. Children are walked in along the cord to be measured, they have never seen a knot hold, and they ask with their hands. The power leaves that with them.

So most of what it does is decline. Two things remain.

The first is that it listens. Each night when the roof goes blue and she takes the wax from her ears, it is there, and it attends to the note for as long as there is a note. It does not answer. An answer given once would turn every later night into a refusal, and she is not asking for anything. She is keeping something true, and it keeps it with her until the sound is gone.

The second is that it works. It cannot stand in the green and simply attend, because a stillness there is a hole in the noise. If it is to be present, it must be present as something loud. So among the beetles at the margin of the black there is now one more, fist-sized, no different from the others, taking its chances with the roof-hangers like the rest. From before the light whitens until after it blues, it drags its wing-cases along the glaze.

That is the whole of the intervention. It is the only help she has ever shown that she wants, since it is what she does herself.

One beetle is not much. But over the next years, the offcuts by her door run a little longer than the ones above them in the bundle, by a finger's breadth, sometimes two. She holds the new against the old. She measures the distance from the edge to her house again. It is still more than she has years for, but the gap has narrowed further.

She puts it down to the beetles, and she is right.

In the morning she walks the cord out. Where the floor begins to hum she stops her ears and kneels at the margin, and there is a beetle to her left. They scrape. Behind them, a hand's width at a time, comes the green.
