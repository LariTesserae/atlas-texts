What notices the lake comes first to the rim, and it arrives the way the cloud tears. For an hour the sky is nearly black and the cold comes down on wet shoulders. The adults look up. A child looks down at the dark shape on its feet and begins to scrape.

So the first thing it learns is that its looking has weight. It draws back, the cloud closes, and after that it attends the way the light does here, from no one direction, so that nothing it regards casts a shadow.

It does nothing for a year. It learns the mallets by their notes and which doors are slow. It goes down past the floors that have names to where there is only stone with cord in it, and comes up quiet. When it asks itself how deep, for a long time the answer is *lash*. By the end of the year it is *chest*.

It could do a great deal. It could thin the water until the water let go of them. It could hold the cloud open and give them a sun, or fire that stays lit, or metal. It could cut the notch down to the first rock, so the lake stopped climbing over its dead.

It goes through these one at a time and finds the same cord in each. Take the stone from the water and no one's lashes need a thumb. The houses stop, the pillars stop, and the morning tapping stops, and with it the way they count each other. It does not tell itself the grit is good for them. A widow's heels are not improved by it. But what is hard here and what is tender are tied in one knot, in a hand it does not know, and it will not cut what it could not retie.

It asks whether they are shut in, since only the dead leave. But below the notch the terraces go down the cold flank, basin under basin, and it sees that they are a stair. The lake is building its own way out, a finger's width a year. The doorway exists. It is not the visitor's place to carry anyone through it, or to hurry the steps.

That leaves the center.

It has seen lakes like this elsewhere. The belch is the mountain knocking its own threshold. The vents keep moving, and so they are not kept. But the water there is as loaded as anywhere, and some year a vent will hold still one night too long. Then the gas will stay down, more each season, until it comes up all at once. It will roll heavily east along the shelf, in a place with no line between what is breathed and what is drunk. The embers under their hoods would go first. In the morning there would be no tapping. In a year there would be no doorways, and in ten no windows, and the town would stand like the reedbeds of the northern shelf, every blade intact.

It does not weigh this one long. There is no tenderness tied into it, and no comb or paddle of theirs can reach it.

Whether to tell them is harder, and it is less sure here than anywhere. They already keep away from the center. They already hear that sound among the others. A warning would hand them a fear with no handle on it. But a thing kept from people is also a kind of crust. It leaves the question open and returns to it each year.

Meanwhile it does what a neighbor does. It listens for the mountain's note with the rest. The year the note is late, with the brightness overhead and the center silent, it goes down with what serves it for a mallet and knocks the rind from the vent. The sound comes up thick and single, like a jar being unstoppered. In the lanes, people who had stopped paddling without knowing why begin again. Nothing is said about it afterward.

It expects to do this for a long time. It will not be thanked, because what it makes cannot be seen. It is only a lake that is still a lake.

There is one more thing, and it is not certain this one is for them.

It could look ahead and learn whether anyone ever sleeps in the frame at the southern edge. It does not look. No one else who lifts that mallet knows either.

One morning before the first tapping it comes by, as small as it knows how to be, and takes the mallet down. The handle is dark and fits a palm. It strikes too hard, then too soft, and a flake of the sill comes away with the frost. It is clumsy at it, and it is allowed. The note goes out flat through the wet air, and three lanes over someone hears it and counts it with the others.

It hangs the mallet back. It has never come two mornings running.

A little later the woman with the left-handed knots passes on her round. The threshold is clear. She does not slow.
