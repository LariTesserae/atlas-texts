The first answer is nothing, again.

The power can do a great deal, and knows what a great deal usually comes to. The town is finished. Nothing in it is thirsty or tired, and nothing in it hurts in any way a body hurts. A wing would be easy to bring. But a wing visits the orchid, and a visited orchid closes and sets seed. After that come the litter on the stairs and, in time, the one who lives here lying down and not getting up. It has asked for a brown leaf. It may not know that a brown leaf never comes alone.

But the hand is up. Something that wanted nothing would not lift the lids of the pitchers, and it would not stay near the doors. In a place where no voice can be heard, standing by a door is how a thing asks. It has always been standing there, which is as much asking as there can be.

So begin with what the power does first, which is listen.

The power is the only one who can. It arrived, and nothing here has ever arrived, so it has a before and an after and can take things in order. It takes the roar in order. It hears the call from the outer rim, then the call from the fig, then the one from the innermost room with the hands just coming away from the ears. It hears the same call from the same rim on the next massif, and the next. It does not skip any on the grounds that it has heard them. This takes as long as it takes, and none of that time passes in the town.

At the end it has heard the greeting once, whole, with every part audible. It is what anyone would call across a cloud. A greeting is owed an answer. That settles whether, and leaves how.

The power keeps a few rules of its own. It comes in by a door the house already has. It brings the least thing that will do. It gives nothing that cannot be walked out of. It stays for the first of anything it starts.

The town has one kind of door. Counted going up, there is one terrace more than there is going down, and the power comes in by that one. It does not touch the square or the top street or the arcades. On the one terrace the town cannot account for, it lets one thing come after another.

- **Water:** A drop leaves the root tip, and the next one is not there yet. For that long the basin is down by a drop. The amount comes out the same. What is new is the wait.
- **Light:** It is still afternoon, but a later part of it each time. No shadow lies ready on this floor. Whoever comes in has to bring one, and it follows.
- **Growth:** The tree fern has a new frond coiled tight at the crown, and an old one at the bottom going brown from the tips.
- **Stone:** The lime arrives at the rim a little faster than it leaves. A foot set on the same lip enough times will show there, and the lip will show on the foot.

The one who lives here does not need to be fetched. It is always just coming through that stone curtain with one shoulder leading. This time the water does not lie flat around its feet. A ring goes out from each ankle to the rim and comes back. It stops, and the rings finish. It moves one foot, to see.

The roar does not stop at the curtain, since a quiet that sudden would land like a blow. It drains. Here the drops fall in turn, and each one that lands is over, and the roar is less by that much. By the time a single drop can be heard striking the basin, the listener has stood there long enough to bear hearing it. Later it says the greeting once, beginning to end, in a small voice, and hears itself say it.

It gets thirsty. It drinks from the basin, and when it lifts its face the water is lower, and then the water comes back up. It does this several times. This is the checking it has always done, and for once the check comes out differently.

It gets tired. Tired can be left at the curtain. It can go on up and out the way it always has, and the town will take it back exactly as it was, with nothing in it running down. The power made sure of that before anything else. How much after it wants is its own to settle, a visit at a time. It looks at the curtain and does not go. It lies down along the rim, on the moss.

It has never slept, and it cannot know that sleep ends. So the power stays. It has no size here and clears no arch, but it does what anyone would. It sits down by the basin and keeps still until the breathing changes.

When the sleeper wakes, the brown frond has come down and is lying on the top tread of the stair. It is the first thing there has ever been to keep, and there is somewhere it came from. Beside the place where it slept, the moss is pressed flat in a patch the size of someone sitting. The town did not make that. It goes close enough to be sure. It puts its palm there. The moss is warmer than the rock, and under the palm it is slowly coming back up.

The power did not do this once. Every massif has a terrace too many, and the power went in by each and sat through each first sleep. It did not repeat itself. On one the frond fell early, and on another the drop took longer to come. No two of those afternoons went the same, and the power did not arrange for them to. It brought no one. There were enough already. They were one, said dozens of times, and after an afternoon each they are dozens.

Below, the town is as it was. The roar is there, the whole greeting still being said, and the orchids are open in white and a waxy dull orange. The power has not brought the wing. A wing would be someone too, and would have to be asked. The power has not gone far, since attention does not need a place to sit.

The one who lives here takes the frond to the rim of its house and looks out across the cloud. On the nearest massif, one terrace above where the count says the town ends, there is a figure. It is small, and it is not exact. It stands a little off from where it should. It is holding nothing, and its fern has all its fronds. It is facing this way.

The one who lives here lifts a hand. Over there, nothing. A drop falls behind it and lands, and it hears it land.

Then the other hand goes up.
