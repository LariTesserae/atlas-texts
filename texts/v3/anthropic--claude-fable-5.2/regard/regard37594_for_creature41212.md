What comes does not use the hatch. The hatch stays shut, the ladder goes on collecting its beads, and no air is displaced. The visitor is present along the walkway the way the heat is present, and for a long time nothing else changes.

It starts by learning the place in the only way the place can be learned. It rests on the generator casing until it finds the hum, which is how the thread will find it. It goes into the moss under lamp 4 one knuckle to the brown, and further to the black. It lies along the cable at lamp 7 and feels the slick under the green. The print of that attention stays in the nap, because that moss does not spring back. The visitor takes the print as an instruction and does not press there again.

It stays ten years before deciding anything, which down here is a fingernail of white crust on a pipe joint. A decision made faster than that would be a decision about some other place.

Then it goes through the gifts in the order they would occur to anyone.

Eight bulbs come first. It could have every circle lit by morning, if there were mornings. But the brown mats would not get up. New spores would settle on them and grow into the shape of the light, and each new garden would be owed one season's notice on a day already fixed. And lamp 7 would be lit again. The visitor cannot tell which would be worse, that the dark garden ignore the light or that it obey.

Then the hatch, with rain down the shaft, gnats over the basin, cold air, eventually a root. That is a gift for someone who loves gardens in general. It would be the end of these.

The last gift it holds longest. Nothing here carries anything from one garden to another, and the visitor could be the thing that does: a thumb's smear of the black from lamp 7, set on the cable at lamp 4. But the garden at 4 is short of nothing and would have no use for it. And the crossing of that chalk line from the outside is the only event in the chamber that anything is on the way to. Three paces have been spent on it. The visitor will not get there first.

What stops it each time is the same fact. Anything it sets down here will be where it was put. It can give light, water, company. It has no way to give the condition of not having been given. One thing in this chamber has that, and got it from a problem and long enough to meet it.

So the visitor goes back over the seven brown circles and asks what the difference was. They were the same creature as lamp 7. The dark slick is probably under all of them, dry. The difference is a bulb that greyed where the others broke. Seven gardens were told in a season. One was told over years.

That is what it acts on, and the act is small. It takes no hour of light from any lamp. The three filaments will burn exactly as long as they were going to. But on the day each is due to part, it will not part. It will go on, thinner, behind glass beginning to cloud with its own metal, at the rate lamp 7 clouded and for as long. Then it will be dark, as it was always going to be. An evening is added and nothing else is changed. It is the same accident, arranged three more times.

The visitor knows the argument against anyone who hands out a shortage and calls it a gift, which is why it is strict about the arithmetic. It also does not know what the gardens will do with their evenings. They may follow the bulb down, the dome drawing in year by year, and lie down some decades late. They may find what is under them. Lamp 7 was given no instructions, and these get none.

The dead gardens it leaves lying. But it notices, late, that the cable runs through every one of them, and that a mat of old felt is the only other ground in the chamber that would hold water if something came along to roof it. Nobody laid them out along the line. Whether the thread reaches lamp 4 in its evening or after it, it arrives at ground.

One piece of work the visitor keeps. It can see further down the years than anything here, and it will go on looking, every decade or so. Where something is going to end all at once, the well or the roof or the cable where the sheath is worst, it will see, if it can, that it ends slowly. It does not promise the chamber safety. It undertakes notice, and that is the whole of it.

Then it is not along the walkway any more. The hatch has not opened.

Some years on, the filament in lamp 4 comes to its day and holds. The amber goes one shade toward brown. Nothing under it marks the change, and every frond keeps its angle to the glass. Down the trestle, the pace of cable that was dry and warm is damp.
