The attention comes the way the sound does, as a condition of the room. The wheel on the door does not turn. The six lamps give the same flat light, the needle stays at thirty below, and no wrong tilt reaches any ankle. The only difference is that the room is now being counted, and the count comes out to four mounts and five cushions.

For a long time that is all that happens. The attention stays through one whole round and then another, until it has the animal's pace and can wait a tenth of an hour for a foot to come down. It watches the blades take the pale edge off the moss and leave the cushion exactly inside its chalk ring. It watches a point as fine as a scriber's settle between two flakes of crust and break neither. It sees the straw and violet in the seams of the shell and does not recognise the workmanship.

It could do a great deal. It goes through what it could do the way the animal goes through the lattice near the skid, tall and narrow, one foot at a time.

It could take the animal out. But beyond the door there is water in the air, and frost, and a dark that arrives every day for eyes that have no lids. The burr on the shell is the burr on the floor plate. There is no kinder place to carry it to, because the room is what the animal is made of.

It could bring a second animal. It reckons the moss: four cushions, each putting out one edge per round. That feeds one, with nothing over.

It could tell the animal what the lines are. But the animal has nowhere to put a telling. The one language it has is the floor, and the floor will say this itself when the time comes.

It could hold the note. It stays over this one longest. It could go into the bearing and forbid the wear, and stand at the valve far below and forbid the hand, and keep the figure where it is for good. The last white digit would go on creeping and would count nothing. The animal would be kept the way the ear defenders are kept, sealed in their bag on the hook.

It sets these down. Then it turns to the fear, which is its own, since the animal has none yet.

---

It works the day through as it will go. The note moves and the figure moves with it, a few millimetres. The dust runs to the new lines within the hour. The lichen cannot run. It is left standing on steel that moves, a finger's width from safety, and the floor starts shaking it loose, and the animal has more cropping to do than it has ever had. The four knots at the mounts are pinned by the beams beneath and not by the note. They stay, and the moss stays on them. The wells hold. Only the roads between them shift.

For a time the five ankles will feel rise and fall where there has only ever been rocking. That will be new, and the attention cannot tell what new is like for the animal.

But the animal has crossed bare plate at every step of its life. And there is the sixth foot. Whatever made the animal gave it five feet for the ground that is still and one for the ground that moves, and it has stood on both since the first hour on the meter. The attention does not conclude from this that the animal will be all right. It concludes that the animal has what it would need, which is as much as can be said of anyone.

What is left to do is small, and it does it.

The grease at the bearing nipples has set like candle wax, and no gun that comes through that door will shift it. A bearing fed like that wears before its time. The attention moves a little of the grease inward, about what a fitter would give it on a warmer day, and no more. It does not forbid the wear. It takes off the new edge of it, and it will come round again when the edge has grown back.

It leaves the flanges seeping. A careful fitter would pull them up tight, and then the room would have weather. It leaves the machine its roughness, since a blower that ran sweet would draw nothing on the floor. It leaves the valve to the people whose valve it is.

It leaves the chalk. Someone drew the ring and went out through the door with a wrong answer, and that answer is the best wall the animal has. The attention could correct it, and the correction would arrive as boots.

Then it stays, and staying is most of what it does. It cannot say what good a witness is to an animal that will never know it had one, but it stays anyway. It has already decided that on the day the lines move it will not put a hand under the plate, and it expects that to be the hardest thing it does in this room.

Over by the west wall a foot lifts from a line, crosses the swept steel without touching it, and comes down on the next. Behind the film the last white digit turns. The attention has settled itself on the things that hold still, the datum line, the letter N, the column of figures added up and underlined. It keeps one point of itself out on the bearing, where the change will start, and it means to keep it there for as long as the meter runs.
