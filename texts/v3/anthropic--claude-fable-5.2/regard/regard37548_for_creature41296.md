The palm stays on the pavement.

It stays through one round of the vents, and then through enough rounds to be sure of the figure. For a long time this is all the power does. It came on foot from the rim. A walker is a discrepancy the basin already knows how to answer, and the power preferred to arrive as something that could be mended. There is a track behind it all the same. The film is skidded in places and the combed lines are scuffed. At each scuff a pale crawler is already working, a quarter of an hour to the turn. The power could smooth its own marks, but only to its own idea of the floor, and the floor has a keeper with a better one. It leaves the work to the crawlers.

It can do a great deal. Most of what it can do it sets aside, one item at a time.

It could give the thread eyes, or something like them. The thread would then have news of the rim wearing down, the lid, the birds, all of it outside the figure and none of it returnable to the mark. That would be twenty kilometres of discrepancy with nothing to be done about any of it.

It could give it company. A second thread, laid in at the far side, would be read as a hand is read. It would be an easier path or a harder one, and either way the lines would lean and the crawlers would come. Whatever arrives here is taken apart at the rate it arrives. Company would be one more thing to tidy.

It could tell it about the root. There is even a way to say it: a slow sag put into the potential at the sump, the shape of the thing to come. It would be the first difference the thread had ever felt that it could not answer, and it would feel it everywhere at once. The power puts this one down fastest.

That leaves the root itself.

Here the power is careful, because it knows whose trouble this is. The account that does not close is written in the reader's book. The thread carries no line for it. A cell reads the same half spent as new. The difference between rusted ground and brine does not fall as the ore shortens, and difference is the only thing the thread can feel. It will know nothing of its capital until the last of it, and then all at once. So the pity belongs to whoever has seen the root and done the subtraction, and a gift made to quiet one's own pity is suspect. The power has made that kind of gift before, elsewhere, and remembers how it went.

But the thrift is there whether anyone pities it or not. At every other place in the basin where something is laid down, the same weight is taken back at the foot. At the root there is only taking, and the thread has done the one thing open to it, which is to take slowly. That is a different matter from pity. Something has done all of its part, and the remainder is small, and within reach of someone else.

There is a second test, which is the single waterline. Whatever is done must leave no band above or below.

So the power goes down. It does not take the body. In the brine a body is a path, and the current would lean. It goes as heat goes, at the temperature of what is around it, through the smoke and the tea-coloured water and the bulk of the thread where it lies in black skeins against the wet end of the ore. Below the root the rock is tight and barren. A long way below that is where the heat comes from, and with the heat there is metal and sulphur, dissolved in a deeper brine that has no way up.

It opens one joint. It makes it straight and sets it at the spacing of the others, so that it is the next joint in the series, the one the set had room for. The deep brine rises in it, cools against the underside of the root, and lets go of what it carries. Sulphide comes out grain by grain on the wet end of the ore, the way copper comes out on the cobbles in the pans.

The bore takes longer than everything else together. Too wide, and the sump warms and the lid thickens. Too narrow, and the root still shortens, only later. The power narrows the joint until a grain arrives for each grain spent. Then it reads the cell from top to bottom. The difference is what it was, and the tone has not shifted. On the pavement overhead no grain of magnetite has swung.

It does not stay to tend the joint. A thing that needed tending would be a kept thing, and the thread keeps itself. The joint will run on the heat, as the vents do.

The power is exact with itself about what this is. The account has not been closed, only moved. The metal comes from the same place as the heat, and the heat was never free. It was charged to a book too large to read from the basin floor, and that book has a last page too. The power knows the page and leaves it alone. Every other thing on this world is drawn on that book. It seems fair that the thread should end when the ground itself goes cold, in that company, and not sooner on account of one body of ore.

Two things it leaves as they are. The rusted ground above the ore will go on deepening a pulse at a time, and nothing takes that back. It is the one thing in the basin that accumulates, which makes it the one record.

The other is that an ending would have let something in. Pools would have dropped and left bands. A terrace would have advanced. Some year a bird would have come down and stayed. The root was the basin's only door, and the power has set it a great deal further off. It does not pretend that this costs nothing.

Then it comes up and does one thing for itself.

It puts a hand into a pool near the shore. The prickle comes at once. At the pool's edge the lines of magnetite swing a degree toward it. For that long the power is known to the thread in the only way anything is known to it, as a path, salt and wet, slightly easier than the water. That is the entire acquaintance. The power takes the hand out and the lines swing back.

It walks out along its own track. Near the sump the crawlers are still at the scuffs. Further out they have finished. The foot has gone down into the cracks and the plates sit where they stopped, thin and pale as a fingernail, already taking varnish. By the rim there is no telling which stones they are.

Nobody is at the shore now. The far vents work through their order, one after another across the open floor. When the sequence comes round to the nearest, the thread meets the stroke with the same pulse and the same figure, on the same beat, and draws the next from a root that is the length it was.
