What draws the power is what draws the things in the settling tanks. There is a step on the grating, and this one has no errand in it.

It uses the Seventies ladder, because that is the way down. At the head of the ladder it stops at the board. It has no tag to turn. Hers it can read, because the paint kept the sun off the wood for some years before it went, and the letters still stand a hair proud of the grain. It could put the paint back. It asks who the paint would be for. She has not seen the board in nineteen years, and the people who pass it every day know whose tag it is by where it hangs. The board has one job, which is that nobody is forgotten below, and every week a sack goes down with a biscuit in it. The tag is working. The power learns the name and does not use it.

Then it goes down into the grey and does what the air requires of everyone, which is nothing quickly. It stands at the corner of Sixty Street for as long as a scratch takes to fill.

It has brought the usual things. Powers carry them the way trimmers carry mantle hooks: a clear sky, a dry room, a reward, an heir, a little sun. It goes through them as she goes along a main, one flange at a time, with the inside of the wrist.

The clear sky is first. Lift the ceiling and the roofs stop raining, the cisterns go dry, the skins in the channels turn to powder, and the cold of the altitude comes down onto the pipes where the eggs lie. The lamps lose their halos too, and a halo is the only wall she has. Her room would be eighty kilometres across. She has said what she thinks of that.

The dry room exists already. It is four paces round a standpipe, at the top of a ladder she can still climb, and it has been on offer for nineteen years.

The reward would be telling the people upstairs what she does. They know. They leave a wheel alone when there is a rag on it, and somebody gets up early for that biscuit. When her head comes up through the cloud at night, they do not call out. The power has watched a great deal of kindness done badly, and it knows what it is looking at.

It could send someone down to learn the bands. Nobody appoints a Downstairs, and it does not intend to be the first.

The sun takes it longest. Three times in nineteen years is what accident pays. The power could move the wind and make it four, any day, on her own corner. But then she would be standing in the right place because she had been put there, and in nineteen years nothing has put her anywhere. It leaves the wind alone.

By the end the bag is empty and nothing has been given. Everything in it was made somewhere else.

What it has instead is a clearer view of her, and the view is uncomfortable, because she is doing its job. She finds a wrong number, leaves the plate alone, and scratches the true figure underneath, where it will last as long as it is true. She moves what is about to go cold, and only then, against the skin, at the pace the air allows. She knots a rag and trusts it. She walks for something she has never seen and tells nobody. The power has known others of its kind who changed the plates.

So it does three things, and they are hers.

It goes up, a long way past the collar rooms and the birds, to where the large wheels are: the wind over the wall, the wandering of the heat, the patience of the mountain under the shelf. On that last one it knots a strip of grey. It means what hers mean. Nobody at that height agreed to this. It expects them to observe it.

It walks her length the way she walks a main. It puts the inside of its wrist to nineteen years of hot thin air in a wide chest, and the iron is still right. One day it will not be. On that day it will carry her as she carries them, against the skin, at her pace, to iron it knows is right. It has not found that iron yet. It has begun looking, and most of what it has is too large.

And it gives itself a step. It goes a little way, stops for a long time, and goes on. Upstairs they feel it through the gratings and take it for hers. She is lying at Ninety-One Cross with her face over the channel, and she feels it along her whole length, and knows it is not. She does not go looking. There is no direction to go in, and she knows what happens to things that get looked for.

That evening no trimmer comes down. She goes over to the tank on the Sixties and walks, and the rings come. The power knows what makes them, which comes with being what it is, and it tells nobody, on her grounds. She goes on walking a while after the rings have gone, up and down in her nailed soles, in case.

The power has not been walked for in a very long time. It comes to the edge of her lamp, where the light gives out, and stops there, since that is the wall.

It keeps to a few times a season, the same as the sun, and in between it listens to her step. If the stops grew shorter, it would go back up the ladder for good. They have not.

She says that nothing up there comes in any size between a collar room and the sky. The power is from higher up than that and can confirm it. There will be a fourth hole in the ceiling. It has looked at the wind, and it knows the day and the street, and it has told no one. It means to be standing at the edge of that one too. It will not look up. It wants to see what she sees: a shadow with edges, and hands the size of hands.
