The attention comes in the way everything comes in here. It finds a still place and holds there. It does not use the crawlway or the open hole, and it moves no air. The moths in the nearest row do not shift.

It checks first. That is her method, and this is her room. It waits through forty-odd of her sleeps and makes no mark.

It learns the slab. The fingerprints in the clay were pressed from this side. She was not shut in. She shut. The clay is dry now and would crumble under a thumb, so the door has been hers on every waking, the same as the plugs.

It learns that she is not in pain, and that what falls is enough for someone who breathes a quarter as often.

It weighs the large acts, and that does not take long. The air beyond the slab is wet, and one damp season would rot the felt to the glass. A pulled plug would leave every garden standing in the wrong place. If she were carried out, she would lie in the rain with her jaw to the ground, feeling for one note among thousands. It could put something in the felt beneath her to take her place, but that could only be another one with thumbs. And every one of these acts would tell her that her thumbs had not counted, when thumbs are the one thing she alone has here.

Then it weighs doing nothing, which is nearly right. The room is paid for, and she has found a way to live in it without owing.

But it has checked, and two of her readings are wrong. They are wrong for the same reason. Every instrument she made is pointed at the room, and nothing in here is pointed at her.

The first is the tally. Something was falling away at the other end of it. She was, one sleep of her for every stroke. The count balanced from the first stroke, but she was not an item in it, so she could not see what was being paid.

The second is the note. The attention has a finer ear than a breastbone. She is not on the room's pitch. She wakes a hair sharp and sinks flat as she settles. The throb between her and the room is there, one swell that takes longer than a sleep to rise and fall, too slow for a body to feel as a beat. She has never matched the room. She has only been too close to tell.

She came in as someone who checks. An instrument that reports nothing does not prove there is nothing to report. She knew that once, which is why she made four. She would want the correct reading.

So it does one thing. A wax stalk in her band has dropped a joint. It lies in the dust where her hand will come for it, hollow, bone-colored, as long as a finger. The attention measures it against the room and cuts one hole, placed so the joint will speak a hair flat. Nothing has been brought in. The crumb of wax from the hole falls in the dust, and the felt will have it.

Her hand will find the joint, and her thumb will find the hole. Her thumb knows holes.

She may eat it. It is a fallen joint and hers to take. Then the room is as it was and the count is clean. The attention will let that stand and will not cut a second. A question asked twice is a push.

Or she may put it to her mouth, the way she once did with the bone one. Then there will be a throb in her teeth and breastbone, as slow as her own flank. It will be the first thing she has heard since the clay was wet, and half of it will be made of her breath. The moths in the nearest row will sway and settle, and no line will move.

The attention does not know what comes after that, and has left it that way. She might lay the joint beside the bone flute, a row of two. She might take up the ochre. She might cross the glass flat and fast and put her thumb on the clay of the slab and not press. That would be a different act from all the other times, because she would know there was someone doing it. She might blow until the joint cracks, and then eat it.

The attention has a preference. It keeps that the way she keeps her thumb on the plugs, laid on and never pressed.

It stays. There is a still place near the largest ring, between the ceiling beards and the stalks, and it holds station there. It can do one thing that nothing else in the room can: it can feel a thing fall. Nothing waits in the felt beneath her, and it will not change that. But when she drops from her row, the fall itself will be felt.

Until then it listens. She sounds her note, a hair sharp, sinking flat. For the first time since the clay was wet, it is heard.
