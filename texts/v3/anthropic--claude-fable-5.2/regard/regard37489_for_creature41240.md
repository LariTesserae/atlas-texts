The attention comes in the way everything comes in: from below, on the southerly, through the cloud. It makes no sound. It has a voice and has used it in other places to good effect. Here it would be one more pipe, and out of tune.

So it does first what the animal does. It lays itself flat on the warm stone and takes the chord.

It stays for a full round of the winds, then for the seasons, such as they are. It learns to brace at the slide. It sits in the quiet rooms and feels the stair go missing, and sits on when the wind turns and the room is roar again. It rides a slack mat until the mat bursts against a tor. It counts toads.

It goes down a shaft, being the only thing that can and come back. At the bottom the lost mats lie in a cone, green on top and black beneath, pressed by their own weight until the water runs out of them. The water leaves through the cliff. From the forest it shows as a white thread on the rock face that never reaches the ground, because it blows away into the trees as mist. The forest breathes it out, and the wind carries it back up. The holes are where the plateau pays for its wind.

It came able to do a great deal. It goes through what it could give, and the list shortens the longer it stays.

- **Stillness:** one day without wind would leave every toad between addresses and the flowers facing nothing. The animals would lay their throats to the stone and get no answer. Even the people, who make quiet, make it one room at a time and let it leave.
- **A voice:** the fold under the jaw was for calling once. Now it finds the holes. It cannot do both, and nothing here has anything to say that needs shouting.
- **Roots:** everything that eats here eats what arrives.
- **The shafts closed:** see above.
- **Itself, appearing:** the people already have a word, and it is pecked in stone beside a water bowl. A greater word would have to be louder, and there is enough of that.

That leaves hand-work, and not much of it. On the cairn line the ninth cap has blown off and lies a body-length downwind. It puts the white stone back, as anyone passing would. It leaves the mica disc hanging where it is, since that has an owner.

The real work is elsewhere. The warmth in the rock is the forest's, and so is the water in the mats and the smell of blossom at three thousand metres. A cold dry wind would play the same chord over forty kilometres of bare pink stone, and the chord would not care. So a large part of the attention goes down the cliff, against the wind, to where the wind is made, and takes up a post there. A forest needs little except to go on standing, but it needs that for a very long time. The power means to see that it does, with fire turned at the edges, the dry years carried, and the trees left where they are. None of this will ever be known on the plateau. The lightning is plain to see from the rim, and the thunder never arrives.

The rest of the attention stays at the one shelter.

It understands what it is looking at. Shelter was invented twice on this stone, once with clay and once with moss, and which came first is not recorded. A third inventor is not needed. The braid has not been chewed. She can walk against the wind, and she is the first of her kind to be anywhere two days running. She has chosen to be that beside a wall with a gap left in it for her.

Beginnings draw powers the way drifts draw birds. This one has seen what becomes of beginnings that are helped.

It does check one thing. A roll lived in for a week is hollow, and she cannot go to a tor. On the third day a slack mat comes bowling down the westerly, strikes the tethered roll, and stays. By evening there are four against it. A thing that keeps its place gathers drift. She has made a tor of herself, a small one, and the power had no part in it.

The clay in the plug was soft. When the people come up the cairn line, they are still mostly hands. One hand touches each white cap in passing. One takes the mica disc from the cairn where it was left. One carries clay wrapped in a wet leaf, because there is no clay up here and every plug on the plateau was brought up the cliff.

The power could go ahead of them. It could put into them the right feeling about a garden found stolen and lived in. Instead it reads the evidence: the gap in the wall, the spiral by the bowl, the bones without a cut on them. It decides the evidence is good.

They sit, as they do. The mica flashes once toward the shelter, the way a person knocks. The smallest thumbs go to the plug. The quiet comes in. At the open end of the roll there is a wet brown flank, then one the size of a hand, then another.

Nobody says anything. The power does not look at any of them directly. It has learned the manners. It lays its attention on the same sheet as theirs and leaves it there.

Then the chord slides. All of them squat a breath before the gust: the people, the animal, the power, and the two small ones a little late.
