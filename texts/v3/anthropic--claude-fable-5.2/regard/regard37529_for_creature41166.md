It does not come through the ceiling in a sleeve of bubbles. Nothing marks the arrival. No band of pale fish splits, and no snail clamps down. The power is in the basin the way the cold is in it, and the first thing it does is the one thing nothing here is able to do. It holds still long enough to look.

It looks for a season, and sees a wall's worth of order go and about a wall's worth come back. It looks through a spawning, the mound heaped and the mound spread. Then, because it is in no hurry, it follows one carrier from the eyelash to the angle between two walls. A power that was only kind would have acted in the first hour. This one is also wise, and here that mostly means slow.

It should be said that it can do anything. Stopping the wind would cost it less than the turn costs a fish.

So it considers the wind first, as anyone would. Without wind the water column stands straight. The cold plumes stop dropping, and the plumes are what carry down the drowned insects and the dust, so the amphipods stay in their joints and thin out there. The silt settles for the first time. In some years the paving is under mud, and in some more there is weed, and the place is at last a lake in the sense the word implies. Lying in it are a few hundred dense-boned fish with jaws set around an absence, holding stones that nothing is trying to take. The power looks at this for a while. It is the only version of the basin in which the stone is a burden.

It considers the fish. A little more bone and a carrier would stand on its own weight. But a fish that needs no stone lifts none. Then no gap is filled, no knock goes out along the floor, and no steady knocking ever comes from a single spot, which is the only way one carrier has of calling another.

It considers the stones, and stays longest here, because this mercy is nearest to what the fish itself seems to be after. Let a stone, once set and turned, hold. The power runs it forward. The ticking thins. Carriers range farther each year for a cobble that will come. The floor grows true from terrace to terrace, and then there is a day when every stone in the basin is seated, and a seated stone is the one kind a carrier cannot take up. Each fish sets its cobble down to feed and has nothing to rise with. The power has already seen the one stone in a carrier's life that is placed and stays placed. It has seen what that stone sits in.

After this it stops thinking of the storm as something that happens to the carriers. It tries to find a piece that could be lifted out with the rest left standing: the lips, the weight, the gap, the surge that empties the gap. Each one comes away with all the others attached. They fit.

There is still the matter the watching could not settle. Nobody knows what a fish wants. The power can know, and it goes and finds out.

There is no basin in a carrier. There are no terraces, no rim, and no season's losses, because nothing in the fish has ever added the stones together. There is a tick, which arrives as a kind of soreness at a distance, with a direction to it. There is weight in the mouth, and the weight is what being somewhere feels like. There is the turn. Then there is the place where the tick was, with nothing in it, and for a moment the fish is empty-mouthed, with the water pushing, and at rest. The power stays inside that moment longer than the fish does. It would not call it happiness, which is a wide word. This is narrow, about the size of a socket, and exact.

The loss is real, but it is visible only from the rim. The power has the view from the rim and the grief that comes with that view, and it is honest enough to ask whose grief it is. It is its own, and it keeps it.

It knows, as a matter of course, whether there were ever hands. It knows what the angles are for. It looks for someone to tell, and for anything the telling would seat, and finds neither.

That leaves very little to do, and the power does it.

First it goes to an angle where two walls meet. A cobble sits there, a month seated, the bones gone into the joints. This is the stone that was placed without turning. The power does not touch it. It does the part of the turn that was always the point, and listens. The stone is silent. It went in true.

Then it waits for a big wave. The thud comes down through the water, every cobble in the basin lifts a hair and settles, and up-current one of them begins to tick. The power goes to the tick. It takes the cobble with no more strength than a blunt mouth has, and finds the curve already there to be held by. It carries it against the water, that being the only direction in which anything here can be steered, and sets it in a gap in the floor. The knock goes out along the paving. It turns the stone once and listens until the ticking stops.

It does not ask the stone to stay. The place a stone is put will not hold, and the power lets that be true of its own stone. By next season the cobble will be somewhere else, and the basin's account, which has only ever balanced to about a wall's worth, will show nothing.

Forty meters off, a carrier has heard a stone go home close by. It does what a carrier does on hearing that. It lifts its fins from the joints and moves off, and leaves the newcomer a circle of floor to work in. That is as near as the basin comes to a welcome. The power has been asked for a great deal in other places. Here it has been given room, and it takes it.

It does not knock a second time in that spot. A steady knocking from one place means something here, and it will not say so without meaning it.

Then it is not in the basin, and nothing marks that either. Silt crosses the cobble it set. A band of pale fish passes over. The cobble rocks once in its socket and holds, for now.
