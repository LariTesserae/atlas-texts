It comes the way the counter comes, along the narrow-gauge track to the bent end, and it stops there. It does not go down.

A hand put in spills a hand's worth of woodlice over the far lip, and the visitor is a good deal more than a hand. So it takes a place on the fringe, between a ground beetle and the small crab, facing inward. It weighs nothing on the rail. The wasps on the chain do not stir.

On the morning tide the gap rides up to within a row of the rim, and the visitor leans in to see it better. A woodlouse shifts first. The gap goes back down. The visitor sits back, and is glad.

It stays a day and a night, through two tides, the pours, and one rain. It watches a full round. Because it is a power, it goes through the things a power does.

It could fill the place. That is what the beetles are waiting to do, and it would end the only one here who has been everywhere.

It could give it room by cracking the slag or clearing a row. But a new cell is a sibling and belongs to the beetle, and a cleared row is thirty absences with nothing to tell one from another. The gap is someone at exactly one.

It could keep the heap warm. That would mean cars tipping on the far side forever, and the gap kept forever at the size of a snail so that it need not end. The visitor does not think a life is improved by taking away its evening.

It could send it, once, into a toad's cup. It thinks about this longest, then leaves the three cups alone. Something that has been everywhere else should keep a place it has not been.

So for most of the day it does what the toads do. But the toads attend to what crosses the gap, and the visitor attends to the gap. By afternoon it disagrees with one thing that could be said of it, that it is a guest handed down a table.

It was the whole room first. Every snail in the lower wall is housed in a piece of it. The one piece left is the piece the rest live by, because a full hollow with no empty place is a hollow where no snail can step, no moth can lift a wing, and no row can rise when the tide comes under it. Every advance any creature here has ever made was made into this. The toads have the direction right. It is the only thing in the hollow that happens.

The visitor does not say so. A word needs somewhere to land, and anything that lands in the gap only moves it on.

Late in the afternoon rain falls on the upper heap and hisses, and the hollow fills with white. In the steam nobody can see the gap. That is when the visitor goes down.

It goes in the way its attention has been going in all day, which pushes nothing out. The gap is among the moths at that hour. For as long as the white lasts, there is a slate-shaped patch of bare slag on the shaded wall with someone in it, and it is still bare. The empty place has been a room just vacated ten thousand times a day since the first snail came down the channel. It has never before been a room someone came to.

The visitor takes nothing, and it leaves no slime or wing dust.

Then the steam clears. The three toads are facing three different ways, and one after another they correct. The visitor is back on the rail.

Before it goes, it counts. Its count comes to one more than the chalk. It does not write this on the plate. The counter has been one short every time and, the visitor thinks, knows it, which is why they keep coming back. The number is theirs to reach. But in a cell on the rim there is a stub of chalk worn down to the size of a thumbnail, and the visitor lays a whole stick beside it. That is all the matter it moves.

It promises one thing, without saying it. It will not keep the heap warm. But on the evening the warmth has gone, when cells stand open by the dozen and there is nothing left for a toad to face, it will come back along the track, sit at the bent end, and face it. It will face all of it, kitchen-sized again, in the shape the gas left. It cannot keep the gap someone. It can see to it that a thing watched all its life is not unwatched while it is getting large.

The fig will be over the rim by then, with a root in every crack. A fig knows how to grow a closed room with everything in it facing inward and one door the width of a wasp. The visitor has looked at the four leaves. It is not worried about the place.

Toward evening the gap comes down the wall to the waterline, and a single fish turns. The visitor gets up from the rail-end and goes back along the track.

Where it sat there is now a place the shape of whoever has just left. It is outside the hollow, where nothing is waiting, so nothing comes to fill it. The wagtail cocks its head at it once. It will still be there when the counter next walks out along the rail. They will stand in it without knowing, look down, and start to count.
