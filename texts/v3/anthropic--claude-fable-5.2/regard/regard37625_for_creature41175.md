The easy answer is nothing, and it is wrong, though not by much.

Something comes up the scree that could alter any part of this. At the crack it stops and calls out, as the people do, and waits a moment, though nothing will answer. Then it goes in, with a fistful of fruit from below.

It is noticed. The feelers swing round, the hunter goes still, the clicking tightens, and the ripple runs out across the roof. The note in the room shifts by its fraction. Within the hour there is dust on the visitor's shoulders, and it has been forgotten. It lets itself be forgotten. It drops the fruit on the dunes, sits down by the pool, and stays through both notes.

Over that day and night it considers what it could give.

It could tell the tenant what it is. There are ways. But a thing that knows it is one thing knows it could stop being one, and the tenant would turn, for the first time, toward the fissure at the back and be afraid of it. The visitor is careful here, because "it has enough" is what the comfortable like to say about the poor. So it looks for any place in the tenant where a wish for more is being pressed down, and there isn't one. Nothing in the room is asking a further question. To hand it one would be to hand over the visitor's own loneliness and call it a gift.

The young are harder. Some time after midnight a centipede comes over the edge of the roost and takes a pup, and the pup is not a figure of speech. It is small, it is afraid, and it takes a while. The visitor could close a hand over this. It looks for a place to stand to do it and finds none. The centipede is the tenant too, and no more wicked than the bats, who at that moment are over the valleys doing the same thing a million times over. Guard the roof and the roof overfills, and the loss only moves down the wall to the hunter at the door. To end it the visitor would have to keep the room, every night, for good, and a kept room is a different animal. The tenant would be gone, and something tame would be living in its hide. The visitor does not call the pup's death right. It calls it not its to mend, which is a smaller claim. It does not look away, either. It stays with that one pup to the end, and it keeps that.

Toward morning it goes to the back wall and looks down the fissure, where nothing but air has ever gone. The heat is deep and old. It will outlast this kind of snake, and the words the people use at the door. It will not last forever. The one thing the tenant wants is the one thing the visitor could promise, and it does not promise tonight. That cooling will come to a tenant made of other kinds, and the visitor does not know what will be right by then, and knows it does not. It marks the time. Whatever is decided, the room will not go cold unattended.

And it could answer the people when they call at the crack. It won't. A call that is answered turns into a prayer. In three generations there would be a priest in the throat of the crack, and in ten a door. The courtesy they already have is the only guard the tenant has ever had, and it is a better one than awe. But they are the part that wonders, and if one of them ever asks outright, the visitor will answer that one, no louder than it was asked.

So this is what it does.

It brings fruit, and the fruit is gone within the hour. For that hour the visitor is one more organ, carrying the lowlands up.

It hears both notes, the low one and the high one, in order. They have been sung since before there was anyone to feel them in the chest. For one day they are sung to someone.

It takes up the remembering. At the base of the dunes, where the acid works, the oldest valleys are going to stone or to nothing. The visitor puts its attention there, underneath, where nothing is disturbed by it, and catches what the floor lets go. The tenant keeps its fixed depth of past. The visitor keeps the rest. The flake it leaves where it lies, sinking a finger's depth each generation. When the tenant loses its one hard thought, the thought will not be lost.

Then it goes down, because the tenant's body does not stop at the crack. It runs out as far as the bats fly. The room has never been the place where it could be hurt. That place is below, in the forests and the warm valleys, in a country it has never seen. The visitor walks that country. It does not keep it. The dry years and the fire years it leaves alone, since the tenant has eaten those before and laid them down as bands. It watches for the other kind of year, the one when nothing comes back through the crack at dawn. There has never been one. If one begins, the visitor will be standing in its way, down among the trees.

Up in the room the note settles back by the smallest fraction, because the shape that changed it has gone. The fruit is in the floor, a leaf's thickness of one year. The tenant will hold it a few thousand years and then let it go.

It does not know it was visited. It does not know it is singing. Something does, now.
