The power does not arrive. It attends, and it begins by learning the ground at the ground's own speed. It goes down into the squeezed handful of moss and finds the water bears in need of nothing. It follows the fox's line around the pad. It reads the logbook, where the most accurate mark in a winter of entries is a question mark.

Then it spends a dusk on the ledge among the ferns. The roar is no obstacle to it, so it becomes the first to hear the note since autumn.

The note is there, but it is not the note of the three centuries. Anything that cannot hear itself pushes, and the being has been pushing against the well all winter without knowing. The pitch is the same, the force is several times what it was, and it holds on longer.

The power could answer. It considers this for as long as the note lasts and does not. Whatever it said back would be company for one evening and a false account of the country for every evening after.

It could make a second one, having the basalt and the patience. But a second one made to end the solitude of the first would be an instrument, and would come to know it.

It could close the well, and it looks at this longest. A little sand in the casing would do it. But the steam is on its way to becoming heat for people whose winter is as dark as this one, and the power's goodwill does not stop at the scarp. The roar is also the walls of the only room where the being has ever stood upright under the sky. The power will not take that away some weeks early in order to hand back a quiet that is coming regardless.

So it goes through the built things in order.

- **The well:** left running.
- **The lamp:** left alone. The being took it for what its own note is, something held out at dusk to find out whether anyone is there. It is a bulb nobody switched off. The power does not correct this. It would rather the mistake stopped being one.
- **The pipe:** the decision at the flange belongs to the people it will or will not warm. Either way the noise ends.
- **The stakes:** the line runs west from the pad, and one stake stands a stride short of the seam the survey crew took for a crack. Whatever is built along that line will cross the path, and the builders will not know. This is the one danger in the trough that will not cure itself, because a road can only be bent around a rock that somebody has pointed at.
- **The track:** it cannot be mended faster than moss grows, and the power does not try. In a place where a bootprint lasts forty years, it takes care to leave no mark of its own.

What it does comes to three things, and all three are weather.

In the middle of the winter a thaw comes in a day ahead of itself, with rain. The trough turns green within the hour and the being with it. It stops on the path with its glazed side to the weather, and the mineral runs out of the hair in a grey trickle. The lichen on that shoulder is past saving and the power leaves it so. Lichen will come back at lichen's speed.

After the next crossing, the wind swings and stays swung for two days, and the fallout comes down on moss that is already rimed. When the crew drive in, nothing has glazed over. The one who keeps the log finds the prints: long and narrow, deeper at the heel, each the length of her forearm, set so far apart that she takes three steps to one. She follows them with her eyes to the edge of the fan, where they go into the moss and are gone. She stands for some time. Then she goes into the shed and enters the pressure, which has not changed, and leaves the remarks line empty.

The third is the evening the test ends. The valve has stood open since autumn, and closing it takes two people and most of the light. The power's part is the air, which for the first dusk in months has no wind in it.

The quiet comes back all at once. On the ledge the being opens its mouth, as it has every dusk, and for the first time since autumn there is only one thing in its sternum. The note goes down the fissure at the size it has been all winter. The walls give it back a fraction late. It stops, which it has never done partway through. Then it starts again.

On the pad, two people with their ear defenders in their hands hear a low held sound come up out of the ground to the west, and hear the basalt return it. One says it is the casing cooling. The other says nothing. She isolates the shed as the procedure requires, one breaker after another, and leaves the last one alone.

The next dusk it stops at the stopping place. There is no plume or noise, and nobody is on the pad. The lamp is on. It has now been left on for the reason the being always supposed, and it will never learn there was a winter when it was wrong.

The rest is not the power's doing.

In spring the stake line has a bend in it that the plan does not explain. In that country roads have been bent around boulders on less.

And in the brown fan, where the crust no longer heals, there is a line of prints that nothing has glazed. They run from the edge of the moss to the middle of the bare ground and stop at two prints side by side, deeper at the heel. On some evening after the quiet came back, something stood up there, in the open, with no walls at all. The prints will be there for years. The people will be passing them for as long as it passes theirs.
