Something came up through the valley mouth with the cloud, in the wet months, at walking pace. It had watched for some time from further off, the way the people watch one another from the opposite slope, and had decided that was not close enough to know anything.

It came carrying nothing. It did not yet know what that meant here.

Above the second weir it stopped on a wall top to look. A girl with a sack of leaf came along the wall toward it and stopped as well. She did not speak. She waited the way the lorry drivers wait for the deer. It was a long time since anything had waited for the power, and longer since it had been in anyone's way. Then it understood, and stepped down backward into the tea, badly, and she passed.

"What have you got?" she said, because it is polite to ask.

"Nothing yet," it said, because it is polite to answer.

So it picked up a stone for the gang, and stayed.

A family had gone out over the pass that spring, and forty metres of back drain on the lower lines had turned strange. The power let its attention grow over that. It was slower work than it was used to. It learned the note of the drain, and then the note changing. It learned the knots. It stood in the warm spill with the dogs and the babies and listened to the second fire through its feet. It took a turn on the rota for the racks, and lost an argument about it.

It had come with a list, as such powers do, of what it could do. It crossed the items off one at a time, mostly while carrying firewood.

- **It could dry the valley.** A word to the cloud would do it. But the cloud is the head tanks and the leaf and the moss. When it looked for the line between the wet and the things the people loved, so as to cut along it, there was no slack there either.
- **It could set the walls so they never bulged again.** Then they would be its walls. A wall relaid forty times with the black side out belongs to the hands that relaid it.
- **It could hold the valley whole on their behalf.** It could see both slopes at once without waiting for the cloud to open. But holding the place whole from outside had been done to this valley once already.
- **It could catch whoever fell from the line.** It did not cross this one off, and it did not mark it done.

What it found, a drain at a time, was that the valley did not need improving by anyone standing outside it. The rules were good rules. By the fifth, six ropeways halt and the leaf sits dying in its sacks against the second, all for one man. The power had known richer places that would not stop for anything. And the people had made room, without being told, for what carries nothing: the thrush, the ridge forest, the water below the last weir. They had worked out how to be courteous to a creature that does not know to step down. The power had been on the receiving end of that.

One thing in the valley was not tended, and that was the book.

Everything else that bulges gets a stake and a rag. The book had been bulging for five generations and nobody staked it, because it lay in nobody's section. Each household knew its own page the way a plucker knows her bushes. Nobody knew the next page. It was the only place in the valley where the pieces did not overlap, and the only thing known whole, though not by anyone who lived there. It was known whole over the pass, where the chests go.

The power read it through, which took no time. There were two things in it. The first debts were a passage up from the plains, a blanket and a season's rice, and they had been worked off before the oldest walls went black. What had come forward since was only the carrying forward. And every page in the book, added together, came to what the ropeways send over the pass in nine days.

Now it had something. Walking back along the wall tops, it noticed that people stepped down for it.

It did not rule the book off, though it could have. Nobody's ancestors chose the valley, and the power would not be one more thing they had not chosen. So it passed the load the way stones are passed. When the woman whose drain ran next to its own asked what it had got, it gave her her own page and the page of the room on either side, and nothing more. That was a few metres of overlap at each edge. She carried it on.

It took the rest of the wet months to cross both slopes. Somewhere on the factory road it reached the priest, who works the weighing shed and is good at figures. He added it up twice.

One morning there was a stake outside the co-operative with a rag on it, tied in the knot that means before the next heavy fall. The power would like it noted that it did not tie it.

What followed was wall-gang work, and the power mostly fetched. The book was taken down a page at a time, in the open store, with many hands on it. It was relaid with the same stones, black side out: the store, the rota, the ration, the firing-room places for the old. The carried-forward column was left on the ground, as a stone is when it turns out to have been the one doing the pushing.

There was also the other end of the cable. The power went out over the pass once, with the chests, to a dry room on the plains where the book's twin is kept. It was neither gentle nor ungentle there. It set nine days beside five generations and waited, loaded, until the people in the room stepped down.

Some left that year, more than usual. They wrote home that the plains are loud. Some came back and said little about it. Most households still could not sleep anywhere else. Few of them owe now, and it is no longer the owing that keeps them. The difference between being kept here and keeping the place, which had worn away, is back. It is about forty centimetres wide, which is enough to walk on.

The three totals still do not agree. There is a ration book at the co-operative that nobody can put a hearth to, and it is stamped anyway. A stretch of drain on the lower lines is no longer strange.

---

The ropeway stops.

The quiet reaches the power the way it reaches everyone, as a sound would. It comes out onto the veranda with the others. All along both slopes the faces turn up toward the grey, and they count.

It can see him. It is the only one who can. He is forty metres out from the fourth trestle, above the grease line, working at a grip with a bar. It holds a hand under him without touching, as it has each time. Twice it has had to close the hand. It has not decided whether that was right, and has stopped expecting to. It only knows it cannot stand on this veranda, among these people, counting, and do anything else.

Then a grip passes over a sheave, and drops patter from the cable.

"What have you got?" says the woman beside it.

It looks down. It has an oak branch for the stack, picked up somewhere on the way along the wall without its noticing.
