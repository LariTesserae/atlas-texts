**How it comes**

It does not come through the roof. Things that come through the roof land hard and leave a crater. It comes in at the floor, where everything new here comes in, and for the first season it does what a newcomer should. It stands in one place and watches a column go up.

**What it thinks of first**

Its first thoughts are old work, the kind made on rock. One is to hold the ice still, and another is to set down something in the middle of the hollow that keeps its shape.

It follows each thought upward. If the ice holds, no column falls. No light reaches the floor, so there are no shoots, no browser, and no new stumps for the flies. There is no wreck with leaf and eggs in it, and no day when the sleds come in from several directions and matches are made. A still roof would be kind for about a season.

A gift that keeps its shape would be a step cut as a step. The people learned this before the power did, and it learns it from them.

**The stones**

Where the people have an answer, it leaves the answer alone. They have the cord for the lean, the nicks for the stretch, and the shoot and a night's sitting for the dead. None of these needs anything added.

The stones are different. When the roof gives its note they stand still, because there is no telling which way to step. That is what is left when there is no answer. So the power takes this part. From now on, a stone that would have landed on someone lands a few paces off.

Nothing else about the stones changes. The note still comes and they still stop. Afterward there is a black mark on the frost, and someone walks out to fetch it. The people count, and in some later generation a cord may be tied that says the stones have stopped taking anyone. They will conclude that standing still is right, and they will be correct.

**The hip**

A woman cutting a ladder she cannot climb is a sad shape, and a fine one, from a distance. The power is not at a distance, and it is not an audience. It does not need her to stay that shape.

So the cold goes out of the hip, a little each morning, as much as a joint might ease in a good season and no more. She will put it down to the standing work. She will not climb to the water. But she will walk to the next fall beside the sled instead of riding on it.

This is less than the power could do, and it knows that. More would be talked about at the fall, and people who have worked everything out for themselves would begin to wait instead. It is not certain it has stopped in the right place.

**The stride**

It can see the leg the boy will have. It has no way to tell her that is not a voice from the roof. And the guess is hers. She has retied that cord every time he grew.

She has guessed well. If anything she has guessed short. He will be tall, and he will take the rungs without thinking about them, which is what she wants. She will go on believing they may be wide. Of everything the power leaves alone, this is the one it minds.

**The missed morning**

Late in the season a fever keeps her in the stump for a day. The power has seen it coming and has thought about the row. A row of nicks is small work. A gap is a long reach for every climber until it passes above the water.

Before the power has decided, the boy takes her adze out to the column. He cuts at his own hand height, too deep, a little off the side that will face up. He spaces the nicks a finger apart by his own finger. The row rises with the rest. There is no gap in that ladder. There is one crowded step, and everyone who climbs it will know which morning that was.

The power did not steady the adze. It had been ready, and it was not needed.

**Mornings**

It stays. It does not stay overhead, because what is overhead is old and moving off with the ice. It stays at the ground.

When she has cut the row and hung the stone and looked, she puts her palm on yesterday's nicks and waits. The power does the same in the only way it has. It rests its attention on the woman, the boy, and the young column, lightly and flat, and waits with her. When the tick comes, the row moves up under her hand by the width of a finger. The three of them move by about as much under the power's attention.

By the next day that attention is above reach, like anything else made here, so the power lays it down again at hand height. It does not miss a morning.
