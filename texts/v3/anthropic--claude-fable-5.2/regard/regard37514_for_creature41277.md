It came in through the open, where nothing is told, and it let nothing be told to it. For a while it hung past the last feathers, short of the walker, among the things that are waiting. It has been obeyed in a great many places. It wanted to hear what the wood would say before it said anything itself.

Then it entered a sleeve the way a drop does, from outside, and was rained on from all sides. The wood told it where down was, and it took that as given. It was asked to weigh something and settled on what a frog weighs.

It lay along the newest span a mile back from the tip, put one side of itself to the bark, and kept still.

It heard hooves a day's walk off. It heard the load come round and learned the order in which the knots take it. It heard a dozen adzes, and after a while could tell each from the others by the stroke. It heard the long animal take the last walker in a file. It heard heart-rot working in the old knots. Each evening it heard her forty-odd turns go slack toward the knee. It kept still long enough that moss took it, and then fern.

It could do a number of things. It went over them one at a time, the way a trunk is gone over for the place it will split true.

It could tell the open which way is down. Then four hundred and some crouched people, each with an arm out, would be told it in the same instant, above a cloud that has never opened.

It could put rock under the lowest spans. The weight would go down and stay. But the country stands by handing its weight on, and so do the people, and they sleep on the sound of it.

It could tell the open *come* and have every one of them on the wood by morning. They would spend what they have left all at once, side by side, and in thirty years there would be no adze going anywhere. There are a few hundred of them, and the country takes more lives than that. The stopping is how they make them reach.

It could hurry the root. A span that arrives early arrives thin.

Then there were smaller things. It could roll a pass of cord for her in the evenings. But a twist is read the way a face is read, and there has never been a stranger here. It could put her raised palm into the grain of light the old eye holds, so that the palm would be the first thing the face was told. Then one of them would stand up believing the stopped can see, and would go out the next time believing it. It was not willing to be how a thing that isn't so got into the country.

It could move the frog. She had not, and she has been here longer.

Its mistake was that it had taken her palm for asking, and it needed many evenings on the bark to find that out. She holds the palm up as long as a lashing takes and lowers it. She does not wait to see what comes back. The hand out there is not waiting for anything to come back either. *Here* is not a question. And it is being answered anyway, in wood, at about an arm's length a day.

It had come a long way, able to do nearly anything, to a place with two signs. It had no right to the knock, since nothing here needed to hold still for it. The other sign it could learn.

It went out by the tip, past the frog, straight. It went to each of them, up and down as well as across. It did not stand in front of them. In front of each is the line a root is taking or will take, and it would not be between a hand and its tip. It stood to the side, made what it has in the way of a palm, and held that flat toward them for as long as a lashing takes. Nothing told them. Then it went on to the next.

The farthest was last. It put nothing in the open eyes. It held up the palm from the side, and the old hand went on saying *here* past it, to the newest tip, where at that hour she was asleep on her ear.

It came back in by the sleeve. It left no tool and no band. A mile back from the tip there is a burl beside the groove, mossed, with fern across it and one side flat to the bark. It has no hands. It sleeps on that side, as they do, and it will wake if the creak changes. It has not decided what it would do then, only that it would be awake.

When the dark has gone over the tip and off the end, she kneels in the trough and lifts her palm toward the open. A mile behind her, something is held up toward her back for the same length of time. She is not told.

She lowers her arm and picks up the cord. It has no cord. It puts its ear back down.
