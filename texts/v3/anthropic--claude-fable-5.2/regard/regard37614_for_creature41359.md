The power came down the coast on a morning between squalls and stopped at the rim, on the seaward side, among the mangroves. It could have crossed, but everything else stops there, so it stopped there too. It stood at the edge for nineteen days and did what the monitor does.

By the fourth day it had the notes, three hundred and some, and could tell from a chord which hall had come down to supply it. By the ninth it had the list, as far as the bats have it. It did not learn the set kept back for the whaleback. When those lines sounded, it turned and looked at the swamp behind it until they stopped.

It had the means to do most things, and it went through them in order.

It had string, any amount. But string was never the short thing. More line would mean more of the town standing at once, and every standing room has to be leaned on. There is little enough of the builder left over as it is. With line for the whole plan, the whole plan would stand, the thuds would end, and nothing would be left to walk the green between one job and the next. That might be what the builder is working toward. It might be the one thing the shortage protects it from. Dished rock shows where a weight has been put and does not show what the weight wanted, and a gift that size cannot be handed back.

It knew where there were people, and a current can be turned. But people do not keep to an hour. They wake wanting yesterday's house in yesterday's place, and within a season they would be cutting paperbark at the rim for posts that cast shadows. They would also eat the egrets. Nor was it certain the town was drawn for people. The benches would fit them, but the lanes fit the rails better.

It could have chosen between the ring and the square, or looked to see what either was for. But the rock is the only lot in the basin the builder has not finished thinking about, and looking would have been a way of finishing it.

It knew whether anyone was ever coming in from the coast. It kept that to itself.

That left what any traveller could do, and for that it needed to be asked. So it made itself a weight, about what a grown person comes to in wet clothes, and stood where the lanes all point from.

On the nineteenth day the monitor's head came round to the seaward rim and stayed there. Just inside the edge, in front of the power, the frogs had gone quiet in a patch a few strides wide. Rain fell straight through the patch. Neither side moved for most of an hour.

Then a vertical appeared, an arm's length inside the rim. It hung for its minute, and for a good while longer. The power kept its hands at its sides. Four short notes were laid across the line, a cell front, and then the rest of the row and the row facing it, with the lane between them starting at the edge and running straight in. The plant went up crackling. The weight arrived, the egrets got their catfish, and the quiet patch went down the lane once with the rails behind it.

The power stepped over the rim. The mat quaked, and the ripple went out and took a long time to die. The stems in the lane were already lying the way it was walking.

It stopped at each counter, as one does. There was nothing on them, and it put nothing there. In the last cell it knelt and drank from the basin in the floor, beside a frog that did not move. The water tasted of nothing, which is rare in that country. Then it crossed the open green to a long hall on the east side, went in by the open door, and sat down on a bench.

The mat dipped. It was not much, one person's weight, but brown water welled at the footing and two catfish came up with it. The egrets, already drifting off, turned their heads. The frogs cut out and started again from one side.

On the whaleback the monitor's head swung to the hall. A squall came through, loud on the mat, and slid off sideways a hand's width above the roof. The frogs outside the door went quiet. Then the frog in the floor basin went quiet too.

The bench is long, and the power moved along it.

Whether anything sat there cannot be told from the bench, which would not have dipped either way. But the monitor held that one bearing all afternoon, which it had not been known to do. Across the basin the walls already rising closed and landed, and after that nothing was drawn and nothing came down. A market stood past noon. The frogs had never gone so long without a thud to stop them, and by late in the day the chorus had drifted out of time with itself.

Nothing was said. The power did not ask about the rock and did not mention string. It sat as a guest sits, letting the house hold it up.

It rose when the light went yellow, before the bats left, because the hall was on a list. The bench came up behind it. It went out by the lane, toward the sea, and its feet turned some of the stems the other way. The builder did not comb them back. The row came down next morning with its lane lying in both directions, like any street that has been used.

Before the power reached the rim, a pale vertical was hanging over the green to the north, and the work went on, half a day behind. It has stayed half a day behind.

One other thing is different. Some buildings now land twice. There is the thud, and then, after the egrets have fed and gone, a second and much smaller settling, good for a catfish or two. Not every building does it. It is usually a hall, and usually one with bats in it. The second settling is the weight of one person sitting down. The builder had never had a guest to measure, and now it has, and it is a thing that keeps measurements. Each house lands lighter by about that much, and nothing in the basin has gone short. For a while afterward no new line appears anywhere. The monitor holds its bearing, and a squall may come through.

The lot on the rock is as it was. The kept-back lines still come around it, hum for their minute and go out. It is still a ring on some days and a square on others.

Three times since, a row of cells has been drawn from the seaward rim, with its lane running straight in. The power has come each time. It does not come otherwise.

Nothing shows in the gneiss. Where the mat tears over that hall's footing, the stone under the bench is no smoother than the stone beside it. One person's weight, set down now and then, is very little, and the rock is nearly finished. But the rock has given before.
