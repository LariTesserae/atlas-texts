**The Attention**

It comes up the flue, since that is the direction in which everything arrives here, and it stops in the middle of the room where the swifts hang. It takes care to weigh nothing. She would feel a new load on the ring girders through her feet within the hour, and she would lose a week looking for the fault.

It stays forty nights, once around the lamps, and for most of that time it does what she does, which is read.

It reads the log from the first crossed seven to this morning's leaning *steady*. The geckos have to be moved, and it puts them back. It reads the tide marks beside each band and the nests between the blades, down through the grey and the darker hair to the black and the red. It follows the flue down past the fans, through the works and out onto the slag terraces. It looks for a long time at what is there, and then it comes back up.

What it sets aside:

- **Silence:** It could stop the fans for an hour, and she would learn whether she is deaf. But the room is made of the note. The shelves of mist would fall, which would be the first rain here, and the roots would hang down. Nor does it need the experiment. It has watched her bring a breath true against a pipe the height of a mountain range, and whatever she has is enough to tune by.
- **The way down:** It went to the bottom to see what it would be offering. She has grown around the fourth band as the figs have grown around the handrails. To take her down, it would have to break either the fig or the rail.
- **A word:** She has words by sight, and it could put one into her as sound. But the first word she ever heard would be its own, and afterward she would be someone listening for a second in a room that cannot give one.
- **The reason:** What the works were built to make, they stopped making before the red-haired hand began. What they make now is a warm wind, and the wind makes this room. It follows one stuck blade forward through a season and sees the levels jump faster than moss can creep, leaving dead felt from floor to roof. So *steady* is a true word, and she has been keeping a garden under the name of minding a machine. It considers telling her. Then it watches her give a blade its missed degree with the feather out and her eyes on the moss, and sees she is in no doubt about what she minds.

That leaves two things, both small.

The first is the handover. Nobody is sent up. It made sure of that before anything else. The climb is chosen and it is long, and for reasons that are no one's fault it has always ended a day late. Each hand since the first has come through the hatch to find readings one day old and nobody making them. Each learned the work from scratches, and each finished it alone.

It will not shorten the ladder, because that would change what it is to have climbed it. But some years from now, on the outside of the stack, there is a night when rime would have formed on the rungs and cost a day. It sees to that one night's weather. There will be a day in the log with readings in two hands. What the two of them do with the day is theirs.

The second is tonight. She sits at the gallery edge and raises her hand to the far wall, and some of the frogs show their feet. It does not touch the frogs. That question is between her and them.

Then she sets her breastbone to the stiffening ring and holds her note. It is a little flat this evening. The beat starts in her chest, the slow hand opening and closing, and she begins to slide upward toward the wall as she has for longer than her hair has been grey.

It goes to the blades. It does this the way she would, though by far less than a quarter-turn, and it closes them. Eight kilometres of air flatten by a hair. The still levels shift by less than the width of one of her scratches, and the moss does not notice. The note comes down to meet her while she is still on her way up, and the beat lengthens and stops sooner than it should.

For the length of that breath there is, for once, something to tell her from the wall, because the wall moved.

When her breath runs out, it gives the blades back their fraction of a degree. It leaves the way the swifts do, out between the louvres into the cold.

She sits a while. Then she takes out the feather and tries the fourth band, and the edge of the vane comes sharp on the newest scratch. In the morning the shelves are flat. She walks to the gauge board, moves the geckos, and writes the readings, and *fern*, and *fern*, and *steady*.

Under *steady* she stops. No word in five hands will do for it. She scratches five short lines spread from one point, the size of a frog's foot.

The next hand will copy the mark carefully, as each has copied the last, down to the lean. But there will be a day now when she can be shown what it means. One of them will raise a hand and spread the fingers, and the other, a metre away, will answer.
