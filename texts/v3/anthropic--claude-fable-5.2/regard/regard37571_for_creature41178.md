It arrives on the ridge without displacing any of the wind, and stops at the line where the wind stops. That is the first thing it does, and most of what it does.

Outside that line the rules are ordinary. Inside, the rock is already holding two arrangements, the room with someone in it and the room without, and paying for the effort in grains. A third presence, however lightly it stood, would be a third arrangement. The edges would round a little faster. Dust would land on the shelf that no one had sent, and the traveller would spend seasons straightening it.

So it stays at the mouth and looks, and to look properly it has to slow down. It waits until it has seen the line on the plate lengthen by the width of a grain. After that it can think at the right speed.

It goes through what could be given, in the order a kind person would think of them.

It could warm the room. But the cold is a property of the room, as grey is a property of the rock, and a room with different properties is a different room. The traveller chose this one to stop in.

It could add light. But the light is the time, and it has been rationed by the only one who knows how long the name is. To add to it would be to say the sum was wrong, and the grains arriving on the shelf say it was not.

It could finish the line. Then the plate would bear a name, and it would be a different name.

It could make itself known. At the traveller's pace this would mean leaning on the room for centuries in order to be felt as one grain shifting. And the traveller is not alone. Every few hours it hears from itself, further along, that all is still well, and every few hours it passes that on. Few who stop in high shelters are so well accompanied.

It could go up through the rooms to where the afternoon is nearly over, see whether the name gets finished, and read it. It decides instead to take the traveller's word. That word arrives on the shelf from the one whose afternoon it is, and nobody is better placed to know. But it notes how fast the shelf fills, and it will go on noting. If the grains ever stop, it will think again. As for reading, a name in a shelter is written for whoever comes next, and it is not yet next.

It also keeps in mind that keeper and animal were wrong, and that traveller is only the guess that has not failed yet. So it looks for things worth doing under any of them. It finds three.

The slab that makes the gap came to rest leaning, and a name written at a grain's width a year is a long time to lean. Whoever is inside will need that gap, at the width of shoulders turned sideways, three paces from now. At the slab's foot, among the scree, it sets one stone against the direction of slip. The stone is angular and clean, and it is set the way scree sets things, without a mark. In time the varnish will take it.

Nobody has had reason to name the summit. Reasons gather lower down, in lists and surveys and the need to call a thing something. It sees to it that this summit goes on being overlooked. That takes very little, since there are better-looking peaks to either side. The name on the plate may not be meant for the summit at all, but if it is, the place will be free.

The third is what any visitor does. It makes a hand, bare, and holds it across the line into the still air. The hand stays warm far longer than it should and then is cold all at once. It is one payment, accepted whole. It could afford more and doesn't offer it. A shelter in which someone has left a fortune is hard to walk out of lightly, and the traveller means to walk out.

Then there is one thing it decides and does not do yet. When the name is finished there ought to be someone to read it, or why write it in a shelter. There may be visitors then, or there may not. If there are not, it will come back up the ridge and go in as anyone must, shoulders turned. By then the room will count four faces either way, and entering will disturb no one. It will take three paces to the back wall, crouch to the height of a hand for someone seated on the floor, and read.

It stays a little longer at the line. Inside, something ticks, once. The word is not addressed to it. It hears it anyway, and goes.
