Something came over the col that could wait longer than the cold allows.

It did not come in through the skin. It understood the first rule from outside, and understood what the rule would make of a visitor like itself: whatever it brought in would be put, and would stay. A kindness spoken in that water would hang at the height it was spoken for as long as the dome stood, and everyone after would have to go around it. So it stopped in the thin ring of snow at the foot, in the wind, and attended from there.

It took the town in the order the town permits: the lamps, then what the lamps hold, then the dark, then the ones who hang in the dark. It read the doorways. It read the ring near the floor, the heavy arc and the light one and the gap. The town had settled that matter and the settling still stood, so it left it standing.

With her it was careful. It could have seen her whole. Whatever that means among her people, she had not offered it. So it gave its attention an edge, as light has there, and let it stop. Inside the edge were an open hand, a hand's width of water, the top of the ridge, and one bead. It knew what she had said. She was right.

Then it considered what it could do.

It could dull the edge. This was the one it looked at longest. But an edge dulled for her would be an edge that had been put, and she had gone down past the last lamp, off every line, to be near the one thing that was not. It would have taken the ridge away and left something shaped like it.

It could run a rail to her. Then a hand could find her, and she had opened her own hand knowing that none would.

It could send a short double pull up the lines, the second lighter than the first, and bring the town down. That would be a forgery in the only script they trust. Noticing was theirs, and where it went next was theirs.

It could tell her what the edge does to a hand that closes. Said inside, that would hang beside her bead for good and be the larger of the two. And she has been a hand's width from the glass for a long time with her hand open. She is the one person in the town who has shown she can open a hand and leave it open. It was not certain this was enough. It let it be enough.

It could answer her. It could tell her where the ridge goes, what stands on either side of the col, and how much there is that was never put, with the dome resting on it like a drop on wax. But everything she has ever taken hold of was let go by someone first, and an answer would be one more.

So inside the dome it did nothing, and nothing in the dome records that it came.

What it did, it did on its own side. Where the ridge comes out from under the skin into the weather, it sat down in the snow and laid a hand on the rock. The hand was not on the edge and not around it. It lay flat on the flank below the glass, the way one touches a thing that has not been handed over. It did not pull. It has no pull to be known by, and would not have sent one.

It reasoned this way. The hand changes nothing she can know until she has already chosen. If she goes back up to the rails, they are where they were, her place at the ring is where it was, and she never learns the rock was touched. If she stays as she is, the same. Only if she lays her own hand on the ridge is there anything to find, and by then the choice is hers and made. The ridge is no more put than before. A hand resting on a thing does not put it.

What she would find, it could not say. Rock is not line. But the ridge is the one line in the town that does not pass through the hoop, so whatever comes along it comes unturned, for the first time in her life exactly as it was sent. Most of that would be the mountain: wind on the col, grit striking, the weight of two peaks that have not begun to wear. Somewhere in it would be a hand, hauling neither way. On the rails they leave a bead every body-length so that whoever follows knows the line has been travelled by a living hand. Out here a breath is gone before it is finished. Staying was the nearest thing.

It is there now. Outside, nothing stays where it is put, so it has to go on putting itself there. She has never had to do that, and it does not mind. Snow slides down the dome and banks against its side. Snow lies on the back of its hand, since it is not the skin and takes frost like anything else. It has stopped looking in. Whatever it learns of her from here on, it will learn the way her people learn of one another, through the line.

Her hand is open over the edge. Its hand is open on the rock. It has brought as much time as she was given, and has set it down beside hers.
