It comes at slack water, since that is when the hall receives visitors, and it does not use the slot.

For a long time it does nothing. This is method, not yet restraint. The differences in the room are small and take many tides to add up, and it has no wish to add them up wrong. It learns the drone, the clap, and the knock that follows the clap. It learns which conger is the fat one. It waits for a slack when the door does not open, which takes the better part of four years. Through the tides after, while the mouth of the third casing stands open with nothing in it, it finds that it keeps outside the arc as well, along with the crabs and the pouting.

By then it has a list. Its means are not in question, since it could take the roof off the hall and set it down in another sea. The list is short all the same.

- **The hole:** It could be closed with a patch of iron as the iron was on the day it was poured.
- **The second pump:** It could be moved a few paces nearer, into reach.
- **A sixth house:** The foundry cast a larger pattern. One lies in the mud of another dock some miles up the coast, and it could be here by the next slack.
- **The seal:** It could be given reasons to fish elsewhere.
- **The hinge pin:** It has perhaps sixty years left in it, and a new one would be easy.

Every item is a repair, and it notices that the room is not broken. It also notices that the list is what occurs to someone who has been here four years, about an animal that has been here since the grease cup.

So it does what it should have done first and goes back along the line of old houses.

On the pipe bend, on the outer curve, there are four pale rounds where the growth is younger than the growth around them. On the valve body there are two. On the gauge case there is one, the size of a fingernail. The grease cup shows nothing, or nothing that has lasted. The striped anemones were not put once. They were put at every move, lifted off the old house and pressed onto the new. The animal has kept them longer than it has kept anything.

Then it looks at the hole properly. The edge is not graphite. Where the feet of the anemones meet the rim they have laid down a brown, horny skin, thin as a thumbnail, and it runs a finger's width out over the opening. The pale creased thing leans on it, and it gives, and it is being added to. It is the only wall being built in the hall, and the patch would have gone on top of it.

The animal is ahead of the list. The iron will go on giving. It will go through in a second place and then a third, and each breach will get its ring. Seawater took the metal out of the casing and left the graphite, with every contour kept. This is the same trade one step on, and far in the future what stands on the bearers may keep the shape of the volute with no iron left in it. After five houses from one foundry, the animal is arranging to have one that grows.

The power does not know whether this will work. It knows the arrangement has worked for small hermits in deep water, and that this one is the animal's own.

What the animal is short of is anemones. There are seven around the hole, and there were four on the pipe bend, so decades of cold water have added three. The striped kind does not settle in the hall. It rides whelk shells on the harbour mud beyond the culvert, on the backs of ordinary hermits, and the animal cannot go and fetch one, because its reach ends at the arc.

So this is what the power does. Some floods, fewer than one a year, a whelk shell comes in through the harbour culvert with the current. It rolls along the furrow and stops where the furrow passes the third pump, among the dead pouting and the rags of kelp. The shell is empty. Its tenant died in the ordinary way, and the power arranged only the route. On the shell sits one squat anemone, striped buff and brown, shut to a knob by the tumbling.

At slack the antennae find it. The animal holds the shell in its small claw and works around the base of the anemone with the tips of two legs, tapping, with no hurry and no force. It is the one delicate thing it does. After most of the slack the foot lets go. The animal leans out along the curve of the casing, sets the anemone against the iron beside the others, and holds it there until it takes. Then the tide turns. It does not get round to the second pump that slack.

It cannot tell the gift from housekeeping. Nothing reaches it that the flood might not have dropped anyway, so no thanks are possible, and that suits both parties.

The rest of the list is closed out as follows.

- **The seal:** It is left alone. It is the only other one who knows, and it has behaved well.
- **The hinge pin:** It is left alone. When the door falls the hall will sound different, and the hall is allowed to.
- **The second pump:** It stays where it is. The animal crossed that water once, in the other direction, with nothing on. Why it passed the second casing by, the power does not know. If the crossing is ever needed again, that will be for the animal to measure, and it is still measuring.
- **The sixth house:** It stays in its mud up the coast. The power has checked that it is sound, and it is not above keeping a spare.

Most slacks it does one more thing. It comes in as far as the bare arc and stops at the edge, where the crab pits stop. From there it can see the outflow flickering in the falling silt and the pale patch moving every few seconds, and it can count the ring, which stands at eight. It stays there for a while. Once in a great while the impeller gives its one low note.

It is the second thing in the room to know the tide has turned. The antennae come in, the legs fold back over the rim, and the claw seats itself without a sound. By then the power is gone.

A few breaths later the flap valve lifts off its seat, the furrow begins to smoke, and every fish turns to face the harbour, which is where things arrive from.
