**The Visit**

Nothing on the saddle marks its arrival. The shadows go on leaning toward the masts. The frogs, which stop for the rattle and move off for the cat, do neither. The cat looks for some time at a place just above the bench where nothing is, but she does that most days.

On the first day it only keeps the schedule. It is there for the steam and the goats, then the cloud, the midges, the rain. It watches her go up at her own speed and come down as if lowered on a string, for a martin that isn't there, and sees nothing in this to improve. At dusk it goes with the rattle from mast to mast. It is on the frogs' side too, which complicates dusk. It lets dusk stand.

That night it reads the post. The record is good: a fingernail a day, as the keeper had it. Only in the newest strokes is there a drift. They top out a little lower than the floor's rise would put them, because she doesn't reach quite as high as she did. The post is reading the floor, and it is also reading her. The second reading can't be corrected. The power reads it through to the top.

There are things it could do, and it goes through them.

- **Take her down.** There are valleys with hearths in them and floors that stay where they are put. It could carry her there asleep. But she was brought here without being asked, and doing it again in the other direction would not mend that. She'd wake where noon is bright and the prey sits still, with nothing she had marked anywhere.

- **Stop the masts.** The masts aren't its work. And the floor would cool in a season. The frost would cross the line, and everything that came here for the warmth would have a day's climb in every direction and nothing at the end of it.

- **Proof her against the stone.** It could give her a coat the stone slides off. Then she would leave no parings, no reading, no ovals, and no pad. Most of what she does in a day, she does because the stone takes hold. The power isn't sure what would be left.

- **Raise the bench.** A bench that stayed up would have to be kept up, and the power doesn't mean to stay.

- **Fetch the keeper.** The keeper lives two valleys down and still measures small things in fingernails. The power could put the climb into the keeper's head. But the keeper would arrive with a box.

It leaves the line in the dusk alone as well. It knows what is past the line. She has judged that correctly without help.

What it keeps returning to is the pad. The pad is the only thing on the saddle that gives. It is also the thing that takes hold of her, for the same reason: it is built from what it pulls out of her.

One other thing here gives a little, and that is the liverwort. It keeps its living surface a layer ahead of the stone, so whatever rests on it rests a layer ahead too. A frog can sit on a liverwort patch all night. But it spreads by small green discs, no bigger than a midge's wing, and a disc that lands on bare floor is under by morning. To take, it needs something soft, with hair in it, that the sheet water goes around. The saddle has one such place, and the nearest patch is by the hut's downhill corner, forty paces away.

The goat she allows has been licking the floor beside that patch all morning, and there are discs on its chin. It comes to the hut and licks the salt from her flank, and one disc is left among the stone beads on her left hock. This has happened before, most weeks. The disc always comes off when she shakes herself, or in the wash, or in the rain.

This once, it is still there at night. That is all the power does.

She walks the bench, the stakes, and the post. She goes to the pad, turns around twice, and lies down. When she gets up she leaves some coat in the day's layer, as always, and the disc with it.

Nothing shows for nine days. Then there is a green fleck the size of a frog's eye. She notices it the way she notices anything new, by sitting down beside it facing the other way. By the time the bench backrest goes under, the pad is green from edge to edge and stands on a pedestal of its own, hair below and liverwort above. It is damp, which she would rather it wasn't. It doesn't take hold.

For some weeks she goes on getting up from it in the night, as she always has, and leaves her line of ovals across the floor. Then one night she doesn't. In the morning the goats are at the frost margin, the roof is in sun, and she is still on the pad, asleep, with the one goat standing over her, waiting.

It doesn't make her young. The strokes on the post will go on falling a little short, and some night she will lie down on the pad and not get up. That will be age, and the power leaves age alone. But nothing will be holding her.

Until then, with your eyes shut, you can hear what was done. The frogs call until dawn all over the floor, except in one place just outside the dusk of the nearest mast. The silence there is two cat-lengths wide. It used to get up and move on every little while. Now it stays where it is until morning.
