It arrives the way everything arrives here, unseen. For the first several closings it only listens.

It hears the booms travel out under the crust and the fissures holding their pitches. It hears a two-note horn handed down a file of grazers, and two days off, the same call from a herd the first will never meet. Under the fissure-hum it hears a thin doubled note, a little sour, and knows that someone cut it.

It could do a great deal here. It knows this the way a person kneeling at a lip knows the drop.

It could lift the brown smear on the southern edge into a sun and hold it there. It listens ahead to that. The people would turn from their fires to face it, their soles would thicken, and the paths would heal over unfelt. It could warm the air. But the felt is a few degrees kinder than the air, and that difference is the fog, the water, the tunnellers' runs, and the lid drawn back to the chest. Warm the air and the difference is gone. It could take the bitterness out of the mat. Then rime would taste like everything else, and there would be nothing to hand a guest. It could dull the glass. Then there would be no blades, palms would heal smooth, and two people meeting after many seasons would have nothing to read. It could still the ground. Then no gap would open in the season of a birth, and no one would have an age.

It holds each of these a while and sets it down. It has cut bone before, in other places, and it knows which way bone can be cut.

So it does what a stranger does on the plateau. It finds someone who knows the route and walks behind her.

It keeps the distance the last walker keeps. Early on it leans too close over the crust, and the tunnellers beneath it freeze, as they do when the wind drops. It learns from her to listen outward instead. At each post it stands where she stood and hears what she hears: which gaps are sounding, how low they are, how far each has come down. Over some closings it has the route as she has it, one long chord, slowly sagging.

It notices what she does not do. She has never tried to hold a fissure up. She does not argue with the ground. She moves the thing her people made out of the way of harm, in the one direction it can go, and when it has gone as far as it can, she lays it under the crust.

Somewhere on the far stretch it hears what it is in this chord. It is large and it is kind, and people walk toward that. If the families came to know it was here, a warmth off the path, a note promising more than shelter, they would turn toward it and away from the posts. They would be hearing two shelters, and under one of them there is no ground they could stand on.

So the first thing it does, it does to itself. It moves sharp, out of the way, to a pitch nothing here listens for, and it holds there.

She knows anyway. She has spent her life standing still to hear what has changed, and something has. Behind her, a file's length back, there is a place where the rime ought to crack and doesn't. She does not turn around, since there would be nothing to see. At the next windbreak the family has heard the clack a day off and scraped rime. She melts her share on her tongue, all but half. The half she sets on a black stone outside the crescent, on the windward side.

By the time the lids are lifted the stone is dry.

It tastes of nothing at all. This is fog with none of the plant in it, and the power understands what a gift is supposed to be here.

There are things it would like to give her. She will come to one of her teacher's posts, find it has to be raised, try for the old quick beat, and miss. It could steady her hand. But the miss is how a walker hears two women in one bone, and it leaves her hand alone. Her own fissure is furred nearly shut. It could part the plant and let the note sound once more as she passes. But she has an answer for her age now, and the answer is true.

Then a drum-skin parts ahead with a sound like canvas tearing.

She kneels well back from the lip, takes her flake, and listens. It listens too, and it hears further than she can. This ground is being pulled faster than the ground around it. The note will come down early, onto a post she will not walk past again, and the turns she is about to give that post will not be enough.

It will not slow the ground. It will not touch her bone, because the beat in it is hers. It could tell her. But then she would walk the rest of her route listening for it, and not for the fissures.

The fog is due and already gathering in the hollows. It holds the wind hard for one hour more. This is a thing wind does.

The sky stays open and black. The new gap goes on sounding, raw, with no wobble in it. She has her flake and her reckoning and is shifting her weight to rise. She does not rise. Near the end of the hour she hears what could not be heard in less. The note is lower by a hair. It is falling while she kneels at it.

She goes to the post and turns the awl further than it needs, and then a good deal further than that. Families on that stretch will wince at it for years and say her ear has gone. A child will be given the new note, will carry it down through a breaking voice, and will walk that stretch in cloud and hear one shelter.

The fog gathers, late. No one remarks on it, since closings have never been even.

She stands still until her feet find the seam. Then she does something she has not done since she last walked with others. She hums, low, forward into the cloud, the way the one in front does.

It considers. A hum that comes back short stops everyone, and it does not want to be the reason she stops. It hands the hum back as it was given, with nothing added.

She walks on. It follows, last in a file of two, learning her beat, the slow one, the wobble you could walk to. It means to keep that beat after the posts that carry it have shrilled and been pulled and laid under the crust. When it tries the beat over to itself, it comes out a little quick.
