The attention comes down by the streets, since there is no other way into the lower town. It stops on the bottom step, where the cat stops, and keeps its feet out of the film.

That is its first decision, and the rest follow from it. The yard keeps whatever enters it. Anything set down on that brick would be lifted, burned off and rained back every forty minutes from then on, and the yard has engines enough.

It stays through a great many rains. Rain does not hide her from it. It watches her walk on the drop and stand still for the double blow. It watches her lay her palm on the chimney above the second band, feel what is happening there, and take her hand away. It knows that gesture. It came down here to make it.

It could do a good deal. What it leaves undone is as exact as what it does:

- **The pointer:** It does not steady it. A level held from outside would make a lie of every coat on the line, back to the first.
- **The fig:** It does not slow it. She has set her pace by the fig, and more time would only tell her she was behind.
- **The animals:** It sends no bee to the hyacinth. If anything comes down the steps when the iron is quiet, it will come on its own account.
- **Her hands:** It does not dry them. They are right for the work, and the work is not finished.
- **The answer:** It has looked at every tank and every corner and done the sum, and it thinks the pointer would hold. It is nearly sure. That is exactly as sure as she is, and she has had years of this yard where it has had days. It has nothing to tell her. If it had, it would still say nothing, because the reading she wants is the one with no hand on it, and a kind hand is a hand.

From the step it listens where she listens, in the gap between strokes. The drip is there, and the culverts. Under them is a third beat, out of time with both engines, and the smallest. The town does not think to count her among the pumps. She has stopped counting herself among anything else. The attention thinks both have it wrong. An engine does not decide to stop. She is the only thing inside the four walls that has ever left something alone on purpose, and that is the part of her it will not touch.

At dawn she stands at the tank margin while the taro weeps, and the water beads at her fingertips and falls. She has given up the distinction. The attention has not. From the step it can tell which drops came off the lid and which came out of her. It does not show her the count, but it keeps it.

What it does is small, and is done on the steps or above them.

In the evenings it sits beside the cat. It does not hold the cat. The cat has sound judgment about what to sit beside, and on the fifth evening, when the valve lets go, its ears go flat and it stays where it is. The steam clears. She has not moved, and for the first time neither has anything else. She looks at the cat until the rain comes.

At the top of the steps it finds one thing already arranged. The can is there, lidded and full, as it has been every day for years, set down by someone who has never seen the yard. The attention did not do this and does not look into who does. It only notes that she is already counted up there, by one hand, once a day.

Then it arranges one thing more. The coat is wool and cut for wind, so there will be wind. Whatever morning she climbs, it will be waiting above the fourth step, steady and dry out of the upper streets, enough to lift a collar. It is not much. It is so that the coat will have been worth keeping.

And on that night it will be here, on the bottom step. It will not hold the level. It wants to see which corner puts on the foot. It has a guess and keeps it to itself. If it is wrong about the pointer, and she is, there are four steps, and it has checked twice that one night will not cover the first. It would get its feet wet, and she would get a true reading. She would come down in the morning and the method would be hers to mend, as it was hers to invent.

None of this is tonight. The line wants another coat first. She opens the tin of red lead, and the attention on the step does not count the coat against her. Each one is a morning the level held. One of them will be the last, and she will know which before it does.

The fern in the breast pocket has put out a second frond. The attention has seen it and left it. Shaking it out is hers.

At the top of the steps, most mornings now, there is a wind.
