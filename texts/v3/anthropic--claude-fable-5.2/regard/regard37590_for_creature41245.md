The other visitor has been noticed only once, on the day it came.

It came the way attention does, all at once and to everything, and the plateau did what it does under anything large. The whistlers dropped. The frogs nearest the pores shut their throats, then the ones below them, down through the galleries to the rock. The beetles' sizzle ran on a moment longer over nothing, and stopped. For the first time since the hide closed over the table there was no sound on it at all. At the hearths the people took the plugs out of their ears and looked at one another. They have a sign for that day, both hands pressed flat downward, and they have not needed it since.

It had meant only to look. But a quiet here is the shadow of something that eats, and it had laid one across three days' walk of country. Only in a few fist-shaped chambers was it taken well. There the young of the long animal woke, lifted their heads, and waited to be fed. It saw that too, and kept it.

Then it withdrew to a height from which nothing below could feel it. After a while one frog knocked, and then the one beside it.

From up there it went through what it could do. It could close the old fuel scars, but the people read their rule in those scars, one block a season. It could make the frogs slower to forget, and the hunter would starve inside its quiet. It could carry wood up the escarpment, and more hearths would follow the wood. It did not know what the larders were for, and anything that old in the dark of the hide was probably for something. The hide does not forgive, and it saw no reason the hide should make an exception for it. It concluded that it had more to learn here than to give.

It still wanted to be near, and there is only one teacher of nearness on the plateau. So it did what the animal does. It let down as little of itself as would serve, at the edge of one whaleback, where the hide laps against the stone, and it stayed. The frogs within a few lengths stopped. It waited through the warm months and through the snow that is gone within the hour, and it is good at waiting. One morning, with the steam-threads standing straight up, a frog started, and then the one beside it. The note closed over the place where it was. It lay in the full din with a frog knocking a whisker's length away, and it took nothing.

That is most of what it has done. It has counted the birds. It knows how old the deepest larders are. Each night it knows whether there is one frog fewer under the edge of the hide, and it has never altered the count. It has followed the known animal round the rim of the rock more times than the people have summers. It has watched the bird guts go off the stone many hundreds of times, and it still could not sign whether they are payment or housekeeping.

It has acted once.

The known animal grew old. Its quiet travelled more slowly and went out more often. In the last warm month of a late year it came round the rim of the whaleback, under the foot of the cairn and past the wall of the shelter, and lay down in a chamber under the edge of the hide. It put its chin on the straw. The frogs forgot it sooner than they once had, and the knocking closed over it. It was not waiting for a frog.

The visitor had thought about this hour for a long time. At any other hour a quiet coming from outside would have meant nothing to the animal, or would have spoiled its hunt. And the visitor owned only one thing that this creature, alone of everything on the plateau, had ever had reason to want. It was the same thing it had done by mistake on the first day.

It let itself be felt, a little, over a few lengths of gallery. The frogs there shut their throats and waited, as they would for any hunter passing, and it cost them part of a night. A quiet came in over the animal that the animal had not made. That had not happened to it since the chamber lined with whistler fur and down, when the knocking stopped and she was back.

What the quiet meant to it then is known. What it meant now the visitor could not tell. There is no telling from above, and it found there is none from beside either. The animal lifted its chin from the straw and put it down again, and that was all.

On the stone, a woman who had spent many summers at that hearth lay awake with the knocking in her hip and shoulder. She felt the note thin along one edge of the rock. It did not wander. She followed it with her eyes shut for a long time, and toward morning it filled in, and she noticed, as such people do. At first light there was nothing at the foot of the cairn. The guts lay on the stone until noon, when a falcon came in off the rim and took them.

She drew the tip of her smallest finger slowly along her palm, then pressed her flat hand downward. Nobody else was awake to see it.

The resin will keep the animal as it keeps the birds, dry and almost weightless, and the hide will rise over it by less than a grain's width a year. The visitor is still there, under the note, taking nothing. It has not decided whether what it did was a gift or only its own need to do one thing. In the last warm month of the year after, a scat appeared on the highest part of the bare stone, smaller than the old ones, and it had been placed.

The knocking is whole again, as far as anyone lying on the stone can tell. There is one listener now who can tell exactly.
