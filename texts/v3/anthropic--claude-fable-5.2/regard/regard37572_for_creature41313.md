The power starts the way she did, as a set of habits with no body. The first habit is not coming in.

Anything that entered would be a patch of wrong notes. She would go still for it, and it would learn only what a column can tell. So it stays up in the limestone shelf and listens to the ticking until it knows it drop by drop, as she does. This takes a long time, and it has a long time.

It counts what can be counted: four isopods in an hour, one cricket, a few dozen pearls, each loose, two notches, two handprints, three lines, and one woman, narrow from front to back.

It asks first whether she suffers. The honest answer is that she does not, in any way the room lets her feel. Nothing hurts, nothing is cold, and that is the difficulty. The room has not harmed her. It has taken away what she would need in order to know.

It considers the things a power usually does.

It could lift her out. There is grass above the shelf, and wind. It thinks of noon on those pupils and dry air on that skin, of every edge she has arriving at once. The people who deepened the basin are gone, and their words are gone from every mouth. It would look like a rescue only from above.

It could let her finish. It could slow the hand at the basin and let the eggshell crust thicken some night until it holds. The bat is not troubled. But she goes to the bat, runs a finger along the stone twigs, and then walks away from it, and she has done that more times than there are straws on the ceiling. She turns the pearls so that they will not be cemented. She kneels at the clay to learn she is still her own size. Whatever she has given up, she has not given up being loose in her socket. She has held that one line by herself, in the dark, with no one to know it was being held. The power knows it now, and it will not be the one to close her hand.

It could withdraw, which would look like respect. But the room is not neutral. She does not remember deciding to stay, because she did not decide. The room decided for her when her foot stopped reporting. To leave things as they are would be to side with the room and call the result her choice.

So what is missing is not light, or company, or a way out. The way out is there, two bends long. What is missing is difference. She stopped the line in the clay because she could not think what would make the fourth mark different from the third. Where nothing differs, nothing can be counted, wanted, or refused.

The power gives her one difference. It makes it out of water and touch, since those are what she kept.

High in the shelf a joint in the rock comes close to the surface. The power widens it by the breadth of a hair along the length of an arm and does nothing else. After that one soda straw among the thousands, above the largest pool and within reach of the basin, carries water that was rain nine days before and still knows what the weather is doing.

That water is cold.

The power has measured. The pool below is no colder by any amount an isopod would notice. The mat does not change. The drop falls in one place only, and the rest of the room is as it was. This matters. She did not like the lamp, and she may not like this either. She has to be able to stand somewhere else.

She hears it before she feels it, a new note in the ticking that does not move the way a visitor moves. She is still for a long while. When the gray fades she goes to the basin and turns the pearls, smallest to largest. Then she puts out her hand.

The drop lands, and she has a back to her hand. It is a circle the size of the smallest pearl, with an edge all the way around. She waits. Another comes.

The drop is not the same twice, and that is the larger part of the gift. After rain it comes quickly. In a dry season it stops, and she finds it gone, and so she has something to miss. In the cold half of the year it is cold enough to ache. Now and then it brings something down with it: a grain of sand that is not calcite, a seed coat, once the wing case of a beetle, which she holds between finger and thumb through an entire dark. The cricket gets some of what falls.

The power leaves the passage alone. The footholds are there under their clear skin. She knows how to keep a notch open with a thumbnail, if she ever wants to.

It does one more thing, and it does this for itself as much as for her. She had a name. The others called it up the pole when she was in the high recesses. A word stops a few steps from the mouth here, so the power does not say it. It keeps the name the way she keeps the pearls, loose, with a quarter turn each night, so that it will not be cemented if she ever comes for it.

Some nights later, and now it can be said how many, the drop comes fast and very cold. She stands under it until her shoulder has a shape. Then she goes down the terraces to the clay and sets her hands in the prints, and they fit.

She does not touch the old line. She puts one finger to the bank beside it. The power could look ahead and does not.

Yesterday the drop was slow, and today it was quick. She draws the mark. It is shorter than the other three and deeper, and it is different.

Up in the shelf the power goes on listening. One note in the ticking keeps changing now, and on some nights there is a place beneath it where the drop lands on skin.
