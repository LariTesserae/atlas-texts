It came in the way cold comes down a live root, without displacing anything. The springtails did not pour, and the cords carried no rhythm of it.

For a long while it did nothing. It went along every street and lay in the peat with the pot-worms. It rode one circuit inside the shrew, whose heart ran at a thousand a minute and who was never more than three hours from starving. It found nothing in the room more frightened or less to blame. It counted the seed in the granary. There is enough, by a margin the keeper is right not to discuss. It looked at the four babies who do not turn toward the patch. Their eyes are sound. They have only never been given a reason.

Then it went through what it could do.

It thought of light first, since anyone would. A seam in the snow at the lip would give them back each other's faces. It would also show them the town: forty houses on threads over a floor that swallows, and the thing in the wall. They have crossed that every day by not seeing it, and the faces are coming anyway. It left the snow alone.

A single degree of warmth would end the cold in everyone. It would also start the frog.

It could turn the shrew aside for good. But the shrew's is the one pocket in the room with nothing in it. And these people winter under a different log each year, where the power might not be. One soft season should not teach them the quiet is optional.

So it settled on two rules. It would do nothing that had to be paid for by another resident. It would do nothing the town would need explained.

That left very little, and it did what was left.

The first thing concerned the shrew. The pressure in the air now comes a little sooner than it used to, by three drips or four. The stillness has time to reach the last house on the last cord before the dry rain starts. Stopping is their skill, and the power only gave it room. The shrew eats as often as it must, and the floor can spare what it takes. No house has gone since. The power does not promise that. It made the warning longer and did not make the shrew less.

It could not help with what the town does afterwards, the going back through a lost household's last days. It knows what those households did wrong, which is nothing. It has no way to say so that is not a voice. It keeps the knowledge, on the grounds that it ought to be kept somewhere.

The second thing was overhead, where no one in the town has ever been. The spruce went over in autumn and was caught, and the limb that caught it has a check in the wood, a hand's length from the neighbour's trunk. Frozen wood is brittle. A gale was eleven nights off that would have run the crack through. The trunk would have come the rest of the way down, the lid would have opened like a door, and the room would have been a hole in the snow with the sky in it.

The power put its attention in the crook, under the leaning weight, and kept it there. The gale came. The roots creaked. Every house swung on its root and the cords went slack and tight again. In the morning each household was found where it had been, and fists were set in palms all up the streets.

The power had no name for what it was doing in the crook until it had been in the room a while. Then it had one. A closed weight rested in an open hold and did not move. The room had been that shape since the night it was made. The town had found the sign for it by themselves, in the dark, in under a hundred days, having never seen the tree.

About the frog it did nothing, because nothing there was wrong. The frog has ice between its cells and sugar inside them, and it is carrying the date. The power read the date and did not pass it on.

It did go, every day, when she went. She lay on the flank with her arms spread, and it lay, in its fashion, on the other side of the glaze. For a full string of drips the two of them did the same job. She cannot know this. It thought about letting her know and decided the wish was mostly its own. She will have the date within a day of the frog having it, and from her own hands. She has spent the winter building the instrument. Subtracting her own pulse, daily, from a silence is the finest thing made in the room besides the sign, and the sign is also hers.

On the mild days she stays longer. So does it.

---

A day comes late in the thaw. The leaf is going double and the low cords have been rehung twice since morning. She lies at the throat and takes her own pulse out, and something is left over. There is one beat, then a long nothing, then another.

She has no sign for it. Nobody does. At the first junction a palm is waiting, as always. She sets her fist in it and lets it be, for three drips, what it has been all winter.

Then she opens her hand.

That goes up the streets faster than the day-pull ever did. By the next shrew the houses are empty. The town is out through the lip, standing in more light than it can use. Everyone is looking at faces and finding them wrong, and laughing out loud at how wrong. The babies are held up and for once they turn.

By then the limb is in sap and would hold without help. The power stays in the crook anyway until the frog has dragged itself out of the wall and gone. Then it does what she did, and opens its hand.
