The power comes in the way she did, by the water underneath. The crosshatch on the pool does not part for it, and no fern nods.

**First, it stays**

It stays a full year, though a year is hard to find here. It watches the frost come up the flat and stop at its line. It does the count and gets the same error in the same places. It lies an afternoon in the silt beside her, where the shaking of the mat cancels itself, and learns what that quiet is like from inside.

**What it sets aside**

Several kindnesses suggest themselves, and it weighs each one.

- **Pity:** Waiting requires knowing, and she does not know. She is fed and warm, and she has outlived every eel that went on past the weir that spring. They silvered decades ago and swam to the weed-choked sea, and none was built to come back.
- **Reverence:** The room is so finished that it is tempting to count her as part of it, like the stump. But the room holds her pause open by trimming, and what gets trimmed is hers.
- **Carrying:** It could set her down in the estuary tonight. But the silver animal is not a place she can be taken to. It is an answer her body gives to a question, and the question has to actually arrive.
- **Silencing the note:** It could let the frost cross the forty metres. By morning the guppies would be dead, and so would the frogs that never learned to dig. A kindness that spends a whole room on one tenant has not done its sums. And the hum is light in somebody's kitchen.

**What it notices**

It attends most closely to the damp nights, when she lies a third of her length out on the mat. It is careful not to call this longing. But it lies where she lies, and finds that the kingfisher gap is the one place where the outside leaks in. The air there is cooler by less than a degree, and the roar beyond it is the river. She is putting as much of herself as she can where news would come from, if any came.

So she has not been asked and answered no. She has never been asked.

It notices two more things. Of the three keys, one was never missing: two metres down in the root it has been moonless for sixty years. And in an office beyond the levee there is a date for the station, not near and not far. On that night the cold will cross the flat before morning. That would be an asking too, but a shouted one, put to a very old body in a room dying around her.

The power has no view on what she should answer. It finds it does have a view on whether the question should reach her.

**What it does**

It chooses one root.

When the crack willow fell, it left a root from its old life running east under the reed wall to the bank, where the river passes before the station has touched it. The root has been dead since the fall. The power lets the warm rot that trims the pool have this root too, and in a season it is a pipe the bore of a finger. It opens at the end of her least-used gallery.

In summer the thread of water it carries is nothing. In October it is a seam of cold falling through the warm. After the first spate it runs brown and tastes of ploughland and leaf-rot. The warmth takes it apart within an arm's length, so the room does not change by any amount the guppies could detect. This is not a trick. It is exactly as much October as there is, let in at a volume she can decline.

**What she does**

She finds it within a day, as a flaw in the note. She backs away and does not use that gallery again that year. The power counts this as an answer.

The second autumn is the same. In the third she lies an arm's length off, where the cold has already been taken apart, in what is left of the taste.

The power discovers it has a preference after all, and keeps it to itself.

In the sixth autumn she lies with her head in the thread for the whole of October. She stops taking the largest tadpoles, and the count begins to come out differently. The yellow goes from her belly. Her eyes, which were small, are not.

Then a damp night comes with the lines sizzling and the river brown beyond the reeds. She comes up through the pool further than she needs to, and then further, until all of her is on the mat. The frogs stop. She goes out through the kingfisher gap, which turns out to be the size of an eel too. The groove she pressed stands full of water for a minute before the mat drinks it back.

The power does not go with her. Downriver there are weirs and nets, and after them three thousand miles of ocean. It could smooth all of that, and then it would be the one who went.

**After**

The count comes out right now, which for this room is wrong. More tadpoles reach the largest size. The snails increase, and the drift on the north rim gets smaller. One morning a guppy goes under the lip of the mat and comes out again. The galleries begin to close, slowly, from the narrow ends.

The thread still runs every October into the empty gallery, and the power leaves it there. Some spring, something the length of a finger and hardly yet coloured may turn aside under the mat. There are fewer of them than there were. The power does not arrange it.

It drops a leaf on the pool and watches the pattern break and reassemble. The leaf turns slowly anticlockwise to the north rim. Then the power leaves by the water underneath, and the floor gives slightly and gives back.
