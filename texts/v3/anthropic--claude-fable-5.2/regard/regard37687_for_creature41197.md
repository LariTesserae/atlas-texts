The power comes down the fissure, since that is the way in. It does not enter the water. Nothing has ever crossed that silt, and it sees no reason to be the first. It settles in the air gap beside the ferns, where a thing can be present without displacing anything, and it stays for as long as one frond takes to unroll.

It spends the first weeks making sure. Powers have been wrong about stillness before, and have mistaken things that could not cry out for things that had no cause to. So it looks for want in every form want takes: a stem leaning, a cell at the brown margin spending more than it earns, any chemical signal that would translate as complaint. It finds the margin living thinly and evenly, and nothing leaning.

Then, because it is a power and giving is its habit, it goes through what it could give.

- **A grazer:** one snail would make the room a place where something moves. It would also be the first mouth here, and the first thing in the chamber able to go without. The power would be introducing hunger in order to have something to look after.
- **A night:** dimming the lamps for some hours would give the ferns a rhythm and, within a year, a reason to die back. The garden would have something to wait for, and then it would be a waiting thing.
- **More light:** the border could be moved out a meter, or to the far wall. But the border has two sides, and the power has looked at the other one as well. The bare rock is scalloped limestone in clear water, finished, and it has not asked to be green.
- **A witness:** it could put a thought in the head of whoever walks the basement on nights, to look down the crack with a torch. But the power knows what the word lampenflora was coined for. Cave keepers scrub the stuff off with bleach, and a waterworks that learned of algae around its intake would do the responsible thing and shade the fissure. The garden is safe because nobody meant it and nobody knows.
- **A guarantee:** this is the one it turns over longest.

The arrangement will end. The power can see the blisters on the flange seams as well as anyone, and it can see further, to a refit, or a new riser, or lamps that switch themselves off when the hall is empty, fitted by someone sensible. Then the green will go brown in a season, and the brown will settle. The power could prevent this indefinitely. It would mean reaching into the lives upstairs and holding a hand over a switch forever, for the sake of something that could not tell that forever from this one.

It looks at the room. The room has been other rooms. It was dry and dripping once, and the stalactites are what is left of that. It ran with fast water, and the scallops are what is left of that. Neither age was the ruin of the other. When the lamps go out, twelve thin tubes of grey crust will lie in the oval of silt, with a faint stain on one strip of wall, and that will be the record of this age, kept the way the others are kept. The garden has been writing it a grain at a time.

The dust will go on sifting down the joint, the same dust, carrying the same spores. If light ever stands in that water again, the same border will draw itself along the same line. What lives here is not one irreplaceable thing. It is what the upper world does wherever it is let in, the commonest green there is, under an uncommon stillness. The power cannot make it safer than that.

So it gives nothing. Around the time the frond finishes opening, it notices something it might have noticed sooner: it has been the only thing in the chamber that wanted anything. It came down hungry to help and has been waiting for an occasion. That is not a thing to leave behind in a place like this, so it takes it back up.

It does carry one thing away. It knows now what enough looks like, a film exactly as thick as its light allows. In its line of work it does not see that often.

It goes up the fissure alongside the pipe, against the light, in company with the water going the same way. That water has a little of the garden's oxygen in it, too little to measure, and by morning it will be in kettles and cisterns in the town. Nobody intended that either.

In the basement of the pump hall it passes the switch for the lamps and leaves it as it found it. That is all anyone upstairs has ever done for the garden, and it has been sufficient.

Then it goes out into the country overhead, where rain is falling on the hills. That rain will reach the chamber in some years, with the storm taken out of it. Up here the storm is still in it, and there are things under it that are hungry and waiting. The power has work.
