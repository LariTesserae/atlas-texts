It comes as a walker, because that is the only shape the place makes room for. It has no shoulders of its own. A gap opens among the grazers anyway, and the power takes the width it is offered as its size and steps in.

For a long while it does nothing. It lays a hand on the grazer with the torn ear and finds the wool exactly as warm as the hand. It takes this as a caution. Whatever it feels up here will be partly its own temperature handed back to it, and that includes pity.

It can do a great deal, so it goes through what it could give. It could give wind, since the windbreaks are already curved to receive it. It could give rain, and a channel for the rain to cut. It could give a night, so that one eye on the tableland might close. It could give appetite, a calf, and a path down through the cloud. Below the cloud, every one of these is a kindness.

Then it follows each one through, and each one needs an after. With an after, the ash blows off the hearth and the stone starts coming lower. The hundreds of afternoons behind the hearth fall into a line and become a lifetime, already spent. And the grazer beside the hearth has been killed. Here that has cost it nothing, and it stands next to its own hide and breathes. Once there is an after, it is only a hide.

So it will not rescue them. It asks next whether anything here is wrong at all, and looks for signs. There is no second hearth and no path. Nobody in all those afternoons tried to leave. The tally rows are straight and every cord is tied off, which is not how a frightened person counts. The scraper was put down by someone who looked up from the hide, saw the animal still standing there, and has gone on looking. The power cannot tell whether this is happiness. It can tell that it is not flight, and that the animal is not enduring anything. Held still this long, with nothing holding it, the stillness is an answer.

It does not pardon the kill. A pardon comes after, and it would not be the power's to give. The one it belongs to has given something else, which is to stay within reach.

It finds one thing lacking. Every row says how much of the animal could be seen from where the builder knelt, and the builder is in none of them. Nobody has ever stood where both could be counted. The power is standing there now.

Whatever is set down here stays. So it decides to set down very little, and only what it would be content to have lie there for good.

It goes, and comes back with two pebbles from a streambed under the cloud. The pavement sheds no stones, so the builder must have carried theirs up from somewhere, and the power does the same. It kneels past the end of the last row. The stone there is scaled over with beetles. It holds the pebbles a little above them and waits, because the place has one way of saying yes. If nothing moves, it will carry them back down.

A gap opens among the wing-cases, two pebbles wide. It sets them down touching.

It is a row of two. It is a total like the others, what could be seen from where one visitor stood, and it is right the way the others are right. It does not finish the count or correct it. In some afternoon the builder may kneel there with a handful, find a row they did not lay, and understand that someone came, and counted, and that they were in the number. Or the builder may never look at that end of the floor. The power will not find out which. It decided before it knelt that it could do without knowing.

It stands and a gap opens, and it walks. After a hundred paces it looks back. The gap has closed. The animals behind it are whole, every left ear entire. The mark has gone back to wherever the nearest looking is being done, which is beside the hearth, a hand's width from the awl. The power takes that as the last thing it needed to learn, that it has not come between them.

Then it is gone, and the plain is smooth where it was.

Behind the hearth the pebbles lie in their rows. Every shadow on the tableland falls at its own angle, each from its own afternoon. The two at the end were set down by one hand at one time, and their shadows lie the same way. It is the only place on the pavement where that is so. Around them the beetles have closed up to the edge of the stones, and the whole plain draws in and lets go, about once every four seconds.
