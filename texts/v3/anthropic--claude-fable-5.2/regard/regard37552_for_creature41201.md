**The coming**

It does not come as light. One new lamp anywhere would stop the amber and the white from being a true map, and that would be the first harm. It comes the way the cold is in the rock, already there and without an edge.

For three sleeps it only watches, since no one who has watched for less has seen the whole town. It counts sixty notes, two hundred people, three olms. It had expected two hundred beings. By the third sleep it understands it has been watching one, which has a single sign for the fall, and here, and always, and itself.

**What it could give**

It could give a great deal, and it goes through the gifts one at a time.

It could pinch the hole in the roof to a trickle and give them quiet. Sixty ringing-stones would hang dumb, and two hundred people would stand on the ledges not knowing which door was theirs.

It could open the neck of the jug to the sky. The moonmilk would dry and flake, and the shadows, which have never moved, would begin to travel the walls.

It could loosen their throats and give them words. It has heard a great many languages. It knows of none in which two people speak at once, as if carrying a load together, with a pulse under the fingers to keep them honest.

It could cut a sixth tier and a fourth place on every hide, so that no one need go up. Then no one would be going past above, and nothing would be found at the last bend.

It notices that it loves the place, and it distrusts that a little. A balance is easy to love for those who do not have to stand on the scale. So it makes itself look at the child lifted off the wall for starting on the right foot, and the child cuffed for playing with the flicker. It looks at the old woman counting jambs with her hand because the high notes have left her. It does not call these nothing.

But these are a people who can ask. The dying ask for the quiet cell and are carried there. So it lays its attention where a hand would go, on one forearm after another, and listens for a line that means *otherwise*. It finds *yes, it continues*. It finds a blade wanted, a quarrel, a child being told whose warmth this was.

It knows a people can lack a thing they have no sign for. It looks where the lack would show: the forefinger worrying the thumb, the head leaned through the quiet doorway, the child hanging over the sump with no hand on its arm. It finds a space there, kept the way the quiet cell is kept, small and on purpose.

It may be wrong about that. It decides that leaving a door unwidened is the smaller error, because that one can be mended later and the other cannot. Carving might move the silence.

**The fourth**

One matter it cannot leave at that. The fourth who goes up has not asked for anything either.

So when the line is next drawn wrist to elbow, on each arm of a household on the second tier, it goes behind the one who goes. She is young. At the lip she lays her palm over her ear. In the tube the bass falls away at the first bend and the hiss at the third. The ringing stays, as it was always going to.

It had meant to do one thing at the top. Her first sleep above would be on unwarmed ground, a cold turn, which is what grief is. It had meant to warm the ground for her, once.

At the mouth of the tube a hide is laid on the rock, and someone is asleep on it. A second person sits beside the hide, facing down the hole, with the look of having sat there a long time. When the girl's head comes out into the air, the sitter puts a hand on the sleeper's shoulder. The sleeper rises, takes up a bundle, and walks away from the mouth without looking back, toward whatever is farther. The sitter takes the girl's forearm and draws a line on it, elbow to wrist. Then the girl is laid down in the hollow, which is still warm.

So there is a hide at the mouth with three owners, and it is never cold. The mercy it came to give has been given already, for longer than the tally cuts under the flowstone, by people who took the manner of it up with them.

It does not follow the one who walked on. That is another place, and it has not yet watched it for three sleeps.

**The water**

That leaves only what is out of their reach.

Above the hole in the roof is a hill, and on the hill a stream goes into the ground through a throat of rock. Here there is green. A tree has put a root across the throat. The root is the width of a wrist now. In forty lifetimes it will be the width of a thigh, and it will catch leaves, and the column will thin. A second white line will form around the sump below the first, and the people have never had to live with a second line.

It considers holding the hill still forever, and decides against it. That would be the same error as the sixth tier, made more slowly. Instead it takes a rule from the lowest tier: nothing should come to them faster than the flowstone comes, faster than whoever notices can meet it with a hammerstone.

So it takes from the root about as much as the root has grown, a fingernail's thickness. The stream carries the shavings down the throat and through the roof. They fall past five tiers into the hollowed block and go out by the slot in the far wall.

It will come past again. The town is fed where it sits, by those going past above it, and it supposes it is now one of them.

**Going**

Before it leaves, it goes through the room once more.

On the second tier a lamp has burned low between one passer and the next. The lamp is fed where it sits. On a bridge in the neck a black drop has fallen, and it is wiped away. No one below could say who did either, and the question would not make sense to them.

On the fourth tier a newborn is having one of its first sleeps. It does not go in. It leans its attention through the doorway, as most of them have done at some time, and the fall closes off behind it like hands over the ears. It hears a drip, and it hears the child swallow.

It comes away the way they do, carefully, as if carrying a full basin.
