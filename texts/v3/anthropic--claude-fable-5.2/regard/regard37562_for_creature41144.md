The power's first act is to do nothing for a year. It is there for the dusk wheel, the Thursday crew, and the hour nobody hears. It learns the crèche the way a mother does, one voice at a time, except that it learns all of them. It counts the mites, since at last someone can. It also reads the paper: the contract, the dispute, and the line that pays for the pumps.

Because it is benevolent, the list of things it wants to do is long. Its wisdom mostly takes the form of crossing things off.

Finishing the station goes first. Trains would be a kindness to the city and the end of everything under the crown. Whether the line resumes is a question that belongs to the people above, and the power does not answer it for them.

It could make every bulb burn forever. Then the ferns and geckos would depend on a miracle instead of a filament, and it would have to mean forever. It doesn't start what it isn't prepared to continue.

It leaves the snake alone. She misses most of them.

It stays longest over the drifts under the crèche. It could catch the pups. Each weighs three grams and falls for under two seconds. But they fall because the mesh is crowded, and the crowding is how the colony makes the heat that keeps the other thousands alive. A pup put back takes a place, and the shoving that follows loosens another. A wider crèche would fill, and the drift would get the same number a season later. The fall is what the warmth costs. The power can't pay that from outside without ending up holding every bat on the crown in place by hand, and then the animals would no longer live here so much as be kept.

So it doesn't catch them. What it does is the only thing it does in the Box that the Box could not have done for itself. A pup on the drift calls in the one voice its mother knows. She is over the city, and nothing else down here has any use for the sound. The power cannot find anyone those minutes are for. From that season on, a pup that loses its grip is asleep before it lands. The roaches get what they got before, and the beetles' accounts are unchanged. The power knows there is a moth in a gecko's mouth to which nearly the same reasoning applies, and it has not finished thinking about that. It doesn't wait to finish.

Then there is the sump. The pond is knee-deep there because three pumps hold it so. The power follows the water back through the joints into the rock and finds the level it is trying to reach, which is well above the foot of the shaft. If the pumps stop in the wet season, the only door the colony knows closes in eleven days.

By weight and heat the Box belongs to the bats. By this measure it belongs to three float switches. The power came to consider the being who lives here, and it finds that the one being the place cannot do without doesn't live here at all. It visits for two hours a week in ear defenders, clears the strainers, and goes home.

The power could make the pumps unfailing, but that goes the way of the bulbs. The pumps are paid for by a dispute, and the dispute will end. Someone in an office will resume the works or abandon them, and before signing will ask whoever knows the site whether there is anything down there. At present the honest answer is: bats, and it stinks.

So it does one more thing. In the second wet season, during a storm it did not send, it takes a breaker that would have held and lets it go, at half past three, having checked the margin. The alarm rings the phone of the man who invented the sign for the snake. He comes down the scaffold stair alone with a torch. The transformer is silent, and so are the fan and the pumps. With nothing running there is no reason for the ear defenders, and they hang round his neck.

He is on the upper landing when the bats come home, falling past him down the shaft like thrown stones. Then the calling starts. He turns the torch off, because the light makes it worse, and stands in the heat for most of an hour. He doesn't know what he is hearing. He knows it isn't static.

He resets the breaker, the pumps cough, and he puts the ear defenders back on.

On Thursday he tries to tell the other two and has to make up a sign for it, a hand cupped behind the ear. They take it to mean a bearing going in the fan. By the end of the month they've understood, and before the season is out all three come down early once, on their own time.

This guarantees nothing, and the power knows it. It judges that a place guarded only by something no one can see is exactly as safe as that thing's attention, and that the Box has less need of a miracle than of a witness who can say what is down there when asked.

It isn't responsible for the rest. When the bulb over the ferns fails, the man brings one from home in his shirt pocket. Nobody asked him to. The fig roots are eleven metres short of the floor.

The station still has no name. The power, which knows several thousand, does not offer one. Each of those names is now known by two.
