It comes down the ramp at walking pace. A power that can be anywhere can still choose to arrive, and this town has wide streets and a lamp every ten paces, which is someone's idea of how a guest should come in.

It makes no sound of its own. That is settled before the ramp levels out. Every street here is a sum, and a sixty-eighth pitch, however kindly meant, would change every chord in town.

Then for a long time it does nothing else. It sits on the fourth step of that one stair, in the still place, where the town cancels itself and a visitor is least in anyone's earshot. It stays through the whole swing of the interval, eleven minutes out to fourteen and back, and then through several more, because a place that settled this long ago should not be judged on one day.

It counts sixty-six and finds the double step. It also learns that the late one is not slow. On the long days the answer arrives at the very end of the lull, when the rest of the town has already begun to fill back in. The late one has not. On those days it is the only one still listening when the answer comes.

After that, the visitor goes over the things that look like needs.

- **The lamps:** Three in four are dead. It could relight them, and in a few years the town would be greener. It checks instead whether the live ones are failing, and they are not. Lighting the rest would only make the town more to the visitor's liking. The ferns have bent themselves to the light there is.
- **The animals:** They live on spill and are not short of it. There are twelve fish, and there have been twelve for a long time.
- **The sixth house:** It goes in, climbs the stair, and stands against the side wall. It does not step into the place a pace back from the window. It could put a note there, but not the note.
- **The absence:** This is the one that looks like a wound. But a call answered every eleven minutes for this long is either a rescue that has never succeeded or an arrangement that has never failed, and from the windows the two sound the same.

So it goes to find out. It walks into the rock beside a conduit, not in it, because the conduit is carrying their call. It follows all twelve. None has a break or a cold stretch. The way is open in both directions.

The one who is away is a single plain pitch in solid rock, about as loud as breathing, and it is moving. It goes out and comes back in a long slow loop that takes many hours. The visitor keeps pace for several circuits. At the far point of the loop, the answer reaches home just inside the late one's extra breath. The one away never goes farther than that. Nothing holds it. It simply goes as far as it can and still be heard, and the town's patience sets that distance.

The visitor does not ask why it went. A guest who has checked that no one is hurt, held, or lost has run out of questions it is entitled to.

But it notices what arrives out here. Through this much rock, the call is one blunt knock every eleven minutes or so, with the town worn off it. The one away knows it is called. It cannot hear by whom. It has never heard the lull, because the lull is the town being quiet, and quiet does not travel. It left before the stairs had basins, and it may not know that it rains now.

The visitor thinks about the fish before it does anything. If the one away turned for home, there might be no more calling. The ceiling would keep its water and only drip, the straws would grow past a hand, and the crickets would lose their minute. A whole small world lives on the fact that someone is missed. The visitor weighs this honestly and finds it is not a reason to keep a letter from the one it is addressed to. What the one away does after reading it is still its own to decide.

It waits until an answer has gone and is well on its way, so as not to touch it. Then, close by and very quietly, it gives the one away a single lull.

It gives all sixty-six at half strength, each where it lives. The low one is under the floor and has to be felt. The pair come with their slow swell, still a hair apart, and the high one is thin as a finger on glass. The third street steps down house by house, with the double step left open. Water drips from the eaves and runs in the channels. A gecko gives three clipped notes and is answered from streets away, and a cricket makes one dry roll on the pavement. Then the town fills back in, and the late one comes last, by a breath.

It leaves nothing out and adds nothing. It takes about a minute. The visitor still has made no sound of its own. This one is theirs.

The one away had been moving, and now it is not.

The next knock comes through the rock. The one away answers it. It is the same note, as it has to be, since that note is the missing step of the third street.

The visitor goes back the way it came and stands in the corner of a doorway through one more venting. It hears the crack, the roar, and the warm rain on the roofs. In the lull the gutters run, the geckos start up, and the thin note arrives from no direction. Then it climbs the ramp.

It does not stay to learn whether the answer begins, over the years, to come a little sooner. If it does, the late one will no longer need to wait the extra breath. It probably will anyway.

Behind the visitor, at the top of the slope, the high house holds level and then begins to climb.
