Something turns its attention to the chamber. It is old, it means well, and it can do nearly anything. It comes down the sinkhole at midday with the light and keeps going where the light gives out. It has no body to bring. It moves no water, and no tower closes as it passes up the avenue.

It settles over the plaza and stays for eleven tides. It learns the fish the way the fish learns the plaza, by what moves. Then, like anything that is able to help, it counts what it could do.

It could bring light. But the grey specks would report nothing, and the green film would follow the light inward and grow over the fans.

It could remove the eels. It follows one for a day along the avenue, inside the shut band. The eel is thin. It has struck four times since the last storm and missed three. The attention finds it cannot care for the fish without caring for the eel. It finds also that the lids, the narrow doors, and the shiver the fish listens for were made by eels as much as by anyone.

It could stop the sinking. It goes down the shaft past his courses, past the snail whorls and the plain pebbles, to the pale stone. There it understands that the well is not being lost. It is being handed down, and the settling is how. With the foundation fixed, the towers would also grow the last hand's width into fresh water and die there.

It could save the young. At the release it counts them as they go out on the turning water, a little over six hundred. Then it counts the wells in the plaza. There are nine, and each has a fish in it.

So it does none of these. It does do one small thing, which it would have known better than to do if it had watched longer. It searches the foot of every wall in the basin and finds a chip of shell, flat, whole at the edges, and smooth on one face. By every measure it has, this is the best chip in the city. At slack water it lays the chip where he noses along the terrace.

He takes it up and turns it over with his tongue. He spits it out and takes the one beside it, which he sets in the rim, removes, turns a quarter, and sets again.

The attention examines both chips for a long time and cannot find the difference. It concludes that he knows something about the well that it does not, and it does not try again.

After that it stops looking for what the fish lacks and looks for what the city cannot answer. The city answers a great deal. It has lids for the eel and the lean of the towers for the tide. Even the storms that kill the tower tops bring the fat weeks. Whatever has come at the city slowly, through the water, the city has built against.

What it has no answer for is above. The roof is a few meters of rock. The lens the hanging city lives in is rain, and it is thin. The sinkhole is the only door the chamber has, and it has no lid. Beyond the forest there are people. They are no worse than eels, but they are faster than anything the worms have had time to build against. Sooner or later one of them will want the water, and another will want the stone.

Here the attention acts, because here a small push costs no one much. A test bore sunk at the forest's edge comes up brackish, which is common this near the coast, and the drillers move inland, where the water is better anyway. A track that was wearing toward the sinkhole is lost in a wet season and is not recut. The trees whose roots hang in the grey-blue column stand through the next storm. No one above is robbed, and no one above learns there was anything below to spare. The attention lies over that ground the way the clam valve lies over the shaft. It expects to be there a long time. The notch in his door was not worn by one fish.

It keeps one part of itself in the plaza, and that part does nothing.

In the fat weeks she crosses. He takes the clutch into his mouth, and the door lies where it was left. For those days the attention stays at the rim. It does not pull the door across. On the ninth day the hiss along the avenue stops out of turn, and the quiet spreads from tower to tower. The shut band reaches the plaza. The eel comes within a length of the open well. Nothing in the well is moving, and the eel goes on toward the wall. The attention has done nothing. It is spared finding out whether it would have.

At the slack before the ebb he rises, and the young go out, and he tastes the rim.

When he calls again from the bottom of the shaft, he is answered from across the plaza, and then from farther off. The attention knows all nine notes now. It would know within a tide if one stopped. This changes nothing for the fish, who does not know he is known.

The chip it chose still lies at the foot of the wall. He has passed it over many times since. It may suit some later fish. There was one, once, who favored snail whorls.
