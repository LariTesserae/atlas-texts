It comes in through the fence, there being no other way, and it has no size until it is inside. Inside there is one size on offer. The floor dips by the height of a person, the fence comes to the chest, the bench is empty and the tools on it are warm. The coat would fit. It notices this before it notices anything else, and it does not sit down.

It stays for some crossings of the bright panel, and several things occur to it.

It could make rain. A dry bowl in a dry place looks like a request. But the grubs are unfired, the moths are ash, and the membrane has never been tested. Those are the small reasons. The large one is that water would leave a line, and the line would have a maker. The bowl is the one place in the basin where following a thing does not bring you round again. Fill it, and it would lead to whoever filled it.

It could supply the hand. But the height has gone into the floor and the reach into the tiles. To gather a person back out of them would mean taking the basin apart down to the last cord, and that has been done here once already.

It could settle whose the bowl is, the animal's or the one who took the animal apart. It leaves that not possible to say. A remainder belongs to what it was left over from, and this has stopped being a remainder.

It could finish the lizard. The beetles are still standing along its side with their pitch, and the grub has been over the corner a third time. It would be easy to give the thing a seam, or a name. The power has a mark of its own, and keeps it to itself.

It cannot even speak. A word from it here would come down as rope, enough to restitch every panel, and the birds would gather it and use it, being birds. From then on the roof would be held together by something it had said. So it is quiet, like everything else.

What it does is go down to the low point, under the leaves. The lizards do not walk around it. It takes its turn.

At the bowl it finds itself in the position of the beetles. It is not made of anything that takes up wet, and nothing in the bowl can change it. So it does what the beetles do. It sets down on the rim what it is carrying, which is everything it could do here, and it waits. The tiles against the bowl do not tick. Then it picks it all up again. It is no lighter, and neither is a beetle's crumb. That does not seem to be what the waiting is for.

While it is there a bird drinks, flies up to the lowest leaves and opens its beak. No twine drops. There is the short low sound. The others come down and walk the floor with their heads turning.

The power hears it. This is the one thing it has that the basin has no part for. The basin has gatherers and no listeners. Nothing is picked up, and the sound is not stitched into anything. It was heard, and now it is gone, the first thing in the basin ever to be spent. Nobody there has a way to count that as loss, and the power does not count it so either.

It watches the moths longest. A moth on the bowl would be a smear that stayed a smear, and it does not know whether that would be an end or a rest. In a place with no night those may be one thing. The birds keep the ash off, and they have not asked its opinion. It leaves this as it is, and is not easy about it.

Then it goes out through the fence and does not come in on the other side. The outside is the thickness of the fence, and that turns out to be room enough to stay in.

There, above the membrane that has no panels, it makes rain after all. It makes one rain, of ordinary size, and does not let it fall. The roof is not ready. But the birds that have drunk are stitching, and where they stitch, the holes close round the cord. The green may reach the poles in time. Something down there may come to be thirsty in a way a dry bowl does not answer, and look up. Nothing in the basin has done that yet, except toward a panel the light never came from.

Or the basin may make its own rain out of whatever it is becoming, and never need this one. Then the power will be holding a thing that has no use. It has seen what is done here with a thing like that. It is given a low place, the ash is kept off it, and it is visited.
