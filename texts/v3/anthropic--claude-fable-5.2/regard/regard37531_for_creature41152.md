The quick thing would be to put the slabs back. A power of that kind could do it between two gusts, and by evening the swifts' wheel would run true and the barkers would find their lulls.

For a day and a night it does nothing but attend. From the rim it follows one full turn of the schedule, the ticking thaw, the cloud pouring up over the sill, the hail, the quarter-hour funnel of swifts, as closely as the hollow follows a moth in a stone cup. Nobody has done this here before. The gatherers greeted the thing, paid it, and went up the pegs after eggs. Nothing has ever listened to it for as long as it listens to everything else. Whether it notices is hard to say. The pauses in a falling feather grow a little longer on the rim side.

The first question is whether to do anything. There is a case for leaving it. The last time this creature's house broke, it was given the sky, and the failing wall may be only its next astonishment, arriving slowly. But in all its age the thing has taken whatever came, falcon and hail alike, and asked for nothing. Now it is asking. That settles it.

The second question is what it is asking for. The power listens to the scrape, the knock, and the pause, and the pause decides the matter. A wall has no pause in it. The pause is a person standing in the window with empty hands and a tilted head, waiting for a gust to say whether the stone is right. The thing wants someone who cares what the mountain sings. The wall is folded inside the request because the two have never come separately.

A wall set right by no one would be a poor answer, then. It would also be the first thing ever to happen in the hollow without a sound, the one event of its life it could not keep.

So the power comes up the mountain on foot, in a body that has to stop every twenty steps. At the rim it hums the note and waits until the note comes back lower. It climbs down by the ledges and the old pegs, then the notched pole, testing each hold and strengthening none. At the flat boulder it leaves the drowned moth where it is, takes up a cobble, and pays a few hundred unhurried strokes into the shallowest cup.

Then it turns to the window. Most of the fallen slabs lie under the sill, sunk in moss, and come up with a sound like cloth tearing. Two went down the long drop. For those it climbs out, searches the scree under the ridge for flat stones with the right ring to them, and carries them down one at a time. It seats each slab, taps the chinking home, steps back, and waits for a gust from the bad side. Sometimes the gust says no and the slab comes out again. Each time it steps back, the turning air slows against the window, the way the shaft goes quiet after a falcon.

The work takes three days. On the second a falcon does stoop, an arm's length from the sill, and the power, like the hollow, does nothing for the swift.

It lays the courses dry, as they were laid. It could build better than the first builders and chooses to build exactly as well, so in some hundreds of frosts the wall will need someone again. It thinks about this longer than about anything else, and leaves it so. The wall is the one part of this place a person can answer. A hollow that never needed anyone would, in time, have no one.

It leaves other things alone too. It doesn't renew the rotted cord or pull the pegs, and it sends for nobody. When the last slab is in, the note settles where it is easiest to carry, and it carries. A climber on the ridge, stopped at a twentieth step, would feel it under the breastbone and might turn aside to look over the lip. What she does then is her business. If she finds the pegs, some swifts will lose their eggs, as some always did. The power weighs that and moves nothing either way.

It spends that night behind the knee-high windbreak, beside the cold ring of cobbles. The barkers start, ledge by ledge. Toward the middle of the night the kept things surface: hail under a clear sky, swifts while every swift is in the rock. Then come the scrape of a flat stone, the knock, the pause, and under them something new, the breathing of a body not made for this height. The work has come back a shade lower. It is no longer played toward the pole. It is played to nobody, in among the hail and the swifts, with the things the hollow goes back over because it likes them.

Near morning come the two syllables, called upward. The power knows the word. It also knows that nobody at the top of a rope answers that word by shouting. The answer is to take the weight and keep still. So it lies still until the sun reaches the western wall and the ticking begins. Then it climbs out, and somewhere below the ridge it lets the body go.

The hollow has no idea that anything but a person came. That was the reason for the body. What it believed about the world, that someone comes down, hums, pays, and listens, is still true, and nothing larger has been added for it to wait for. The power goes on listening from further off, as it was before it climbed the mountain. Listening is what the two of them have in common.
