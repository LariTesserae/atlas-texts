One more arrival, then, the most recent of all, and it leaves less trace than the alder seed did.

It comes at the top of the tide, while the geese are up, and nothing marks it. The eagles go on facing the wind. What drew it is the thing she would least expect. She carries no light, and nothing visibly leaves the valley, but forty-some winters of someone standing in this and taking it in turn out to show from a long way off.

It should be said plainly that it can do a great deal. It could lay the storm flat the whole length of the inlet as easily as a rod lays four paces of it. It could hold the till slope on the north side and keep the cottonwood bar. It does not spend long on these. The valley is not in trouble. It is being made, and this is what that sounds like.

The woman takes longer. It reads her the way anyone would, off her things, and they all say the same. There are no windows, no light, no path. She has not taken a coho in forty winters. Her building is no warmer than the rock, though people who can stop the wind could surely have warmed it. She wanted to be able to stand here, in this, and attend, and she would not have let anyone strike out "in this."

It goes through the list anyway, because not to would be carelessness:

- **The knee:** It could be right again between one step and the next. She would know by the fourth post. After that she would be someone for whom things are done between posts, and she would never again be sure what the ground was telling her.
- **The cold:** She sleeps on her own heat. So does the marten, and nobody proposes to do anything about the marten.
- **The solitude:** Her people send one at a time, and the next will come when the eighth post does. Anyone brought to her sooner would be company for a season and after that her replacement.
- **The question:** It knows what becomes of what she takes in. It could say. But an answer, once given, would stand in every circle with her. She would be attending on behalf of something, and listening over her shoulder for it.

It leaves all four. It leaves the knee last, and does not like to.

What it does instead is what she did. It finds a place to stand.

At the third post the circle is full. The marten has the rod. The woman has the place a pace off from the rod. It comes in over the clean edge of the moss and takes what is left, behind her, without a claim on anything. By the reckoning that applies inside the circle it is nobody much. The voles have been coming here for a hundred and fifty generations, the marten's family for twenty, her people for two, and it since the top of the tide. It is the only thing in the circle the rain does not find.

In there, with the storm behind its door, she comes apart into her eight or nine, the way the roar does for her. It has the knee and the eleventh winter. It has the breastbone the line was set by, and the hands up the sleeves with devil's club in them. It has a voice forty years old, close against her ear. It has the family feeling for the rotted stakes, and the count of cottonwoods running on behind her face. It has the seven delays, kept without working them out. It takes all of it, and it gets none of it late.

Then the ice comes, the better part of half a minute old, and she sorts it out of the rest as she always does. Tonight there is one thing left over, and it is not a sound. She has it the way she has the edge of a circle in the dark, by the wind stopping on one cheek, though there is no wind in here to stop. Once, for a single season, someone else stood in this circle with her. She does not turn round. She does not ask, having stopped asking some time ago. She stands until she has all of it, and tonight all of it is one thing more than it was. Then she finds the edge with her hand and goes back out into the storm.

It goes with her. Between posts nothing is done for her, and it does none of it. Slush goes down her collar. The moss takes a boot and gives it back. The knee is the knee. It does not put out a hand. It is only there, in the part of the valley where no one has been with her since the other went down the inlet.

It makes one arrangement, and she will not learn of it tonight. Everything that reaches her is a little old. Some winter the moss will let her through and she will not get up, or the seam on the knob will not open. When that happens it will not hear of it half a minute later from the tide line. It will be there, on the near side of the delay, as close as the weather requires. If she is ever to be told what became of everything she took in, it will be then, with a mouth against an ear, since that is the only way anyone talks here on the nights that count.

Until then it wants only what she wanted, to be able to stand here, in her weather, and attend.

Toward morning she climbs the knob. The seam opens and closes, and she is in the place where she goes to stop. It does not follow. The building has no windows, and it does not make one. It waits outside on the rock in the sleet, and in the lee of it the lichens grow as they grow anywhere.
