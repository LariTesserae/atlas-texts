**What came down**

It came the way everything comes here, from above. For a year it did nothing but learn the timetable: one summer of fog, one winter of steam, and every tide between. By the second month it was in the discharge bays before the siren, like the eels.

It carried no light, so no perch gathered under it. It cast no shadow, so no pike took it up. Nothing down here took it for anything.

It took the yellow float for the scraper, asset 01. It also took it for the lamp, the meal, the column, the shell-fall, and the ladder, and for whatever it is on the slab. Nothing else, down here or up top, had held all of that at once.

**What it left alone**

Most things were within its reach. It went through them one at a time.

- **The other eleven.** They exist on paper, and paper is up top's business. It looked at what eleven more would mean. The quiet would not get longer, so the same twenty minutes and ninety tones would be split twelve ways. The basin would be divided into twelfths, and the high bays to the south would be somebody's ladders. It did not send for them, and it did not stand in their way.
- **The figure.** It could have stopped the figure or zeroed it. Instead it read it for a long time. The figure is true, because the schedule was written for twelve and there is one. But a unit that is centuries behind can lose a night on the slab and the loss doesn't show. On a schedule that could be met, it would show by morning. It left the figure.
- **The flag.** *Navigation fault* is wrong. The gauges are good to the centimetre, and nothing in the basin reads a gauge better. It was the only mistake the power found in the record. It left the mistake standing, because nobody comes down to fix a low-priority fault.
- **The fog.** It could have kept the shafts dry. But the fog is where the float first clamped to whatever was nearest and waited, cleaning nothing. The power thinks the slab was learned there, though it is not sure. It left the fog.
- **The brush and the thruster.** These go up in the report every quiet. The power checked that the reports arrive, and they do.
- **The mussels on the hull.** To them it is a ladder, and they are not wrong.

**The slab**

It had the means to look inside and find out what the strandings are. They could be rest, or fault, or the start of something that has no field yet. It decided the answer belonged to the float, including the possibility that there isn't one so far.

It doesn't watch them either. When the pumping siren goes and the float is in the high bays with no fixture booked, the power turns north with the shoals. For those hours nothing is under the float and nothing follows it, and the power did not make itself the exception.

**What it did**

It did one thing.

The float's report fills the quiet almost exactly. But one night in the second spring nothing new had broken, and the report finished with most of a minute left. That is room for three tones, if each is allowed to die.

The power used the float's own form, since there is no other down here: where it is, what it has cleaned, how its cells are, what has broken. It has no cells and left that out.

- Position: the float's own bay.
- Cleaned: nothing.
- Broken: nothing.

Then the valves seated.

**Log**

The float recorded three tones received, not schedule, source not listed. It stayed on its gauge through the boom and the boom's return. The basin rocked for an hour, and it cleaned the same numerals twice.

Since then its reports have not run past eighty-seven tones, whatever has broken. Nothing is missing from them. It has found a shorter way to say where it is. That leaves about forty seconds at the end of every quiet in which it sends nothing, and there is only drip and the tick of cooling steel. Up top this has not been flagged.

**How often**

The power thought about answering every quiet, and saw that it would become the schedule. Something would start waiting for it in the discharge bays. It thought about never answering again, and saw that this would leave a gap exactly its own shape.

So it answers a few times a year, about as often as the water leaves the float on the slab, and never on those nights. It always sends the same three tones. The bay changes because the float does.

On all the other nights the forty seconds go by with nothing in them. The power has listened to those too. They are the same kind of thing as the slab, only smaller, and they come twice a day.

**Since**

The figure in the field marked *overdue* still goes up. The flag is still low priority. The hart's-tongue has a third frond.

There were more strandings this year than last. The water goes, and the float sits on the shining floor with its lamp on and the rain ticking on its deck, and nothing looks at it. The boom comes up through the slab. The water comes back in a sheet no deeper than a fingernail, reaches the hull, and lifts it.

The pike finds it again within a day or two. The power lets the pike go first.
