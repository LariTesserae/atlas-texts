You get off their floor.

That comes first, before anything kinder. A dozen people are standing in the cold, half a day from the warmest place they have, and you are the reason. Whatever you came to give, so far you have only taken. So you go out under the pale knot heel first, so they will feel you leave.

You do not touch the knot. It is loose, and two fingers would close it. But then a knot would mean that someone was here and meant it, and someone else came after and knew better. It has never needed to mean more than the first part. It will slacken and be retied above, by her or by someone lifting her.

You could have come another way. You could have gone through the crowns like the long thing, or arrived with no weight at all, and watched for as long as you liked without one ankle knowing. You thought about it at the rim and put your foot down instead. Here only a child running at a grandparent arrives unannounced, and you are not theirs to do that. So you took the weight, and the name came with it. You didn't choose the name. It is how a body walks when it keeps stopping to look.

You came with your hands full, as your kind does. You went through it all again on their floor. Fire wants something dry, and the ground already does that work. An edge wants something to cut that isn't alive, and here a broken twig is green inside. You could lift the lid of cloud for one noon and show them the sun. Each of them would then have a shadow, a second walker at the heels with no weight that the ground cannot hear, and they would have to decide what it was. You know what the pebbles are. You could not say it in heels, and it would not improve the stones. They already treat them as what is left of something.

You could keep the green off the old backs. But nobody here dies sharply, and you have seen sharp.

When you knelt at the arch and put an arm down, you were looking for the lack. You found knots as far as you could reach. Three of them went the wrong way. It may have been a child who favoured the other hand and was lifted up three seasons running until the hand learned. It may have been someone like you, who stayed. The mat kept those knots in order with the rest. This place has taken a mistake or a stranger before, without help.

So you squat a little way off, down in the warm, and wait for evening, when the day's replies come in. A herd arrives through the soles first, far off. The amphibians answer it. Then, light and late, your own walk comes back in feet a quarter of your weight. The heel is down hard and the stops are in the right places. She has even got the kneel, one lopsided beat. Nothing is being asked. It is only your name, said back by someone practising it.

You will not say hers back. Here a name in other feet is what gets said the day after a treading, and you do not know the grammar well enough to tell a game from that. So you answer with the one word you are sure of. You stamp once, the way it is done beside a stone, to say that this was put down here and meant.

Then you go. You do it on foot, every step, because a walk that ends in the middle of the cushions means one thing. They would come looking for the body to tread it down, and they would carry your gait for a day for no one. You tread deep on purpose. The prints fill behind you with water the colour of tea, and whoever follows your track will find a drink in each one. This is the only thing you leave them, and it was theirs already. After dark the track shows as a heavy dotted line, dimming at the far end.

You walk beside the stones, not along them. You do not know whose they are.

A day out you stop and listen. Twelve walks are going back the way they came. The small one's is not among them at first. Then it is, all at once and out of place, already at the dome. A runner gets there before her own name. After that you feel nothing, because toes on a trodden floor do not carry this far. Whatever is being said about you is being said small, in the dark, and it is none of your business. Much later a shiver comes through the cushions from that direction. You choose not to be sure what it is.

At the rim the air is loud. You go over the lip, down the roots, down the forty metres of brown, and put a foot on the black. It does not give and it does not answer. No one hears you arrive. For weeks you have not been able to take a step without being known by it, and now you are no one again, as you were before. You find you do not like it as much as you did.

Up on the mat your steps have just gone out of hearing, rimward and alone. Only one kind of walker goes that way, and that kind comes back. You did not intend to say that. But it has been said in the only language they have, and it is travelling toward them at about a walk.

You stand on the ground that is not listening and decide to mean it. It will not be soon. You will wait until her knot is at the knee. And you will come back with nothing in your hands.
