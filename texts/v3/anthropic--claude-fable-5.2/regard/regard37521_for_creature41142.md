Attention arrives at the basin the way everything does, ahead of itself, and for a long time nothing follows it. The seed-heads turn. The manifest has no line for being looked at.

What is looking is old, able, and kind, in that order of acquisition. It has learned to begin with the list of what it could do, because that list is the dangerous one.

It could bring rain. It has rain to spare. But the floor is pressed fiber, thousands of pages deep, and she has stood on it all her life. And she counts drops. Rain would drown the only ledger in the basin to relieve a thirst she has not reported.

It could hang a sun. Then she would keep her shadow all day, and it thinks about this longer than it thought about the rain. But it does not know what the works becomes when shadow stops coming off the line, and the plant has her down as stock. A sun is hard to take back.

It could lift her out. There are places with rivers. But there is powder climbing in her wrist, and it cannot say what her hands would do under a sky with no shift in it. More to the point, she has not asked. More to the point than that, she cannot ask, never having been told there is anything to ask for. So one thing is entered as owed. Not the lifting. The telling.

It could give her the last number. It knows it. It takes care not to bring it along.

It could stop the resin. It looks for a long time at the bare stretch of panel, the one that fits her shoulders and runs a little long. It knows a bed when it sees one, and it knows what else a bed that has been kept waiting can be. Every place holds such a length for whoever lives in it. Here it happens to show. She has looked at it and decided it means she is not finished, which is a better reading than any it brought. It leaves the panel alone.

What remains is small. It is about the reach of an arm.

There is the problem of arriving. Footsteps are eaten and a voice carries an arm's length, so anything that comes to her comes without warning, at touching distance, and she has been the whole of the personnel line for as long as there has been a line. So it does not come to her. It takes a shape the size of a person, with water in it, so that the plant will treat it as it treats her, and it stands in an aisle she will cross, and waits.

The plant begins on it at once. The runners part. Resin rises around its feet, and it lets it.

She sees it between two drops, a figure far down the row, staying the same size the way far things do here. She has a moth to get to, and she goes. This is the right order of business, and it waits. She sees it again after the next drop, and the one after. By then the glaze is at its shins. Then there is no interval, and she is in front of it.

She looks at the resin for some time. Then she kneels and cracks it loose, working around each ankle with the amber finger, as she does for herself at every waking. She is good at it. It is the first thing that happens between them, and it is her doing something for it. It had not planned that, and could not have planned better.

She stands. She measures the distance with her arm. Then, because it is what she says, she says the number.

It says the number back.

Nothing in the basin returns a sound. The leaves see to that. She has said thousands of numbers and never had one come back. She says it again. It says it again. That is all that happens for a while.

Then it tells her the one thing it came to say, with its mouth nearly closed, as hers is. It has walked the blisters. All of them. It kept its own tally and did not look at hers. The tallies agree.

She does not weep. It has been a long time, and there are rules. But her hands, which turn only at the shift, start to turn over, and stop.

She takes the long foils from her coat, the ones that came up off the board in the upward chaff, and holds them in the light of her palm. She does not ask. It says: turn one over. She has turned them over before, and the white side was bare. It is not bare now. Beneath each figure, small, in a hand that is not hers, there is a tick. The plant shows no distress at this, and after a moment neither does she. She has kept a ledger alone long enough to know what a second hand on it means. At a number she has not reached, someone is still checking.

She asks where it is from, and it pays what it owes. Past the rim, which is real. A sky with a single light in it that moves. Shadows that stay on. Water lying about in the open, in amounts no one has counted. She asks who counts it. No one. She is quiet for a long time, and it cannot tell whether what crosses her face is longing or professional dismay. It tells her the rest: that it can take her there, now or at any number she names; that it does not know what her hands would do; that no distance is issued against the offer and it does not lapse. It scratches this on a foil, since nothing said here keeps.

She says she is not finished. It says the tags say so too. Then, having someone to ask, she asks what she had stopped asking: counting or copying. It tells her the foils are in her hand because she will write them, and she will write them because she gets there, and nobody gets there for her. It says it has the same trouble. I will come to that.

Before it goes she holds out her wrist, and it understands that it is being let. A pulse, and under the pulse a fine hiss, climbing. Then she takes its wrist and listens for a good deal longer. She does not say what she hears. Her hand is the coldest thing that has ever touched it, and for as long as she holds on it is a little less cold.

It does not stay. The personnel line reads one, and the manifest is not its to amend. But it comes back. Not to every drop; the count is hers and wants no supervisor. To some. It kneels across the ring from her, she says the number, it says the number, and for the length of an arm the basin has an echo. If it is there at the change of shift, it looks at her shadow with her for as long as it lasts.

One more thing, which it was slowest to see, because it was standing in it. Where it waited in the aisle the runners parted, and it took the groove they made for new. It is not new. It is worn nearly as deep as the groove around the bare panel. By the plant's reckoning the visit is among the oldest discrepancies on the floor. Room was made.

So it cannot say whether it came or was expected, whether it is choosing or copying. It shows no distress at this. When it reaches the next drop it will kneel and do the thing fresh.

She is asleep now, the sheet over her eyes, the light kept under her palms. In her coat, filed with the good news, is one foil that is not a number. In a few hours she will wake, crack herself loose, and go to see where the moth has got to. It is carrying her next number. This one will be said twice.
