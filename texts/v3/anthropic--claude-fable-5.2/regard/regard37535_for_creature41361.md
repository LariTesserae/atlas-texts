What came is harder to find than either. It left no gap in the liverwort and rubbed no metal clean. The best evidence is the grazers. For some days one herd carried a thing on its outside edge, where the later copies go. Then they let it into the middle, and after that they stopped placing it at all. The hunter made one run at it, and it broke neither left nor right. The hunter trotted east and found nothing there to set up for.

So there was one of it. Anything able to do what it did later could have stood along a whole run at once, but it walked the hours like everything else, with the film beading on it and its hair lifted toward the bands.

For a long time it only looked. It walked her old run east to the end and watched a stretch go down. Then it turned and walked west, past the place where looking starts to ache and past the place where the moths turn back.

There is a first one. The band comes up out of the rock there and has no west to it. The animal lies on it with the chip in the jaw pad and the young with the pale forefoot, and its eyes are open. It is looking at the ridge. Nothing is telling it anything. It is an animal on a rock, attending to the rock, and when the ridge is about to let go it knows, and gets up. Every animal east of it lies with its eyes shut in order to hear an animal whose eyes are open.

Nothing was wrong there, and the visitor changed nothing. It sat through a few strokes of the nearest columns and came back east.

Some of what it could have done can be made out. Early on, one piston field stood still for half a stroke. The herd on it was caught at thirty heights with nothing coming level, and the stamping went through the rock for kilometres. The field started again, and no field has stopped since.

It could have held the runs true. But the error that turns the hock out is the same error that lifts the head. A run without drift is forty animals with their eyes shut, permanently, and none that ever walks off.

It could have put the soft ground into the copper at the head, so that the warning came down with the rockfalls. It would have come down as one more thing told. By the twentieth animal it would be a warning about a floor that is sound, and by the fortieth a hum. And any animal that walked off because the copper said so would still have its jaw on the copper.

What it did instead was go to the far end of a run and lie down beside the last animal, the one pressing a warped pad to the metal as hard as it could. It put a hand on the ribs. The drone was in there, faithfully, with no stops left in it, going down through the jaw to an east where no one lay. That may be the only time one of those has been heard. It stayed there while the floor went, and the columns closed over all of it.

Next cycle something else came up on that ground, and the visitor was among what came up. There is no knowing what it met in the orange. The only report is what it did after.

The ground under a failed run still softens. But where it once went from soft to open in the time a grazer takes to drink, it now stays soft through the rest of that cycle and most of the next. That is the whole change. The slabs stack as before and the runs drift as before. It added no warning, no voice, and no mark. Time was the one thing it could give that is not a thing told, and so cannot arrive worn.

It is not much. Most of them still press the jaw down and listen until the seam opens. But a floor that gives for a day and a half is a long thing to lie on, and where now and then one used to get to her feet, some do.

It found her at a pool. She let it inside the length a sound holds, and it put a hand on her ribs and heard what she tells the young one, so now two have heard her. It followed her to a strange band and was touching the copper when she tilted her head and sent. So it knows what she puts in. It did not add to it, or correct it, or carry it farther than the metal did.

It looked at the hock for a long time. She rides the columns badly because of that leg. The leg is also in every step of the way she walked. It left the leg. It walked an hour east to check, and she was not there again, and it left that too. The hunter has not made a second run at her. That may be the hunter.

It is still on the plateau, as far as anyone can tell. It works the tails of the runs alone, the way the hunter works the piston fields, lying down where the floor has started to give.

From a ridge crest in the dark the lines still stammer blue-green from west to east, each run telling itself. But here and there now, on bands she has never touched, a short figure shows that did not come from the west.

She comes to a strange band and gets one edge of the pad onto the metal. Something comes in from the east, where nothing has ever come from. Her head comes up, and it stays up.

Later, at a pool, she puts her left forefoot in the water and holds it there. The young one walks beside her now instead of under. It has watched this all its life. It puts in the right.
