What came did not come over the wall. There is no over. It came in the way the drop goes, through the stretch between the jar and the channel that is on nobody's tally. That was the one door in the basin, and she had left it open without meaning to.

It was in no hurry. It went around the work first, as a guest should. It looked at the seams and at the knot on the fish traps. It looked into the hole, and no further down than she had dug. It thought the bricks were good bricks. It read the hand as you did, came by the same road to the finger, and stayed there longer than anywhere else.

Then it looked at her, on the rampart, with her hands half closed.

There were things it could do. It went through them as she goes through a basket of clay, feeling for stones.

- **It could say the word.** Any word would serve. The frogs would call and the leaf would start down the channel. But the mouth is hers. If it spoke, she would be standing in a place someone else had started, and one thing has been done to her already.
- **It could put a bad brick under the matting.** It would be lopsided and too wet, with the dents all wrong. She would know it for hers and build her whole past on it, and it would be a forgery. It does not lie to people, least of all in their own handwriting.
- **It could give her dirt.** It doesn't need to. There is ground under the work, and there always was. It is further down than she thinks, and it can only be reached with evenings.
- **It could open the rim** and show her what the wall was against. But a wall that high is an answer. You do not take away a person's answer and leave them alone with the question.
- **It could promise that nothing would ever be missing.** This is the one she wants, and it isn't true. The ibis would be one frog ahead. There would also be spawn in the troughs within the month, and a month to have it in, but that is no answer to the frog, and it knows it.

It set these down where it had found them.

She is looking in the hole for something she made before she knew how. It is not in the hole. A body that had never knit a bone before knit that one, got it wrong, and it held. It has patted every brick since. She has pressed her first bad work into every good thing she owns, along the outer edge of the stroke, and gone looking for it under the floor.

It could tell her so. But then she would only have its word, and the finger is better evidence than its word.

So what it did was come near. That is all it did, and here that is not a small thing, because nothing has ever been near her that was not her. Everything in the basin stands at the warmth under her tongue. It didn't. The difference was slight, less than the difference between a hand and the cheek it rests on. But it was the first difference here, and an old break feels weather before the rest of the body does.

The last joint of her right small finger ached.

It had known that would happen, and had come slowly on that account.

She looked down. She had not looked at anything but the basin since she straightened up, and to see her hand she had to take her eyes off the troughs, the pans, the ledges. The count began to go. It did not take the count from her. It got under the other end.

Her low shoulder came up level. The shoulder knew what that was before she did, and nothing in the basin could have taught it. So the finger is not quite the only old thing.

Its way of keeping is not hers. She keeps a frog by holding it on the trough lip. It keeps that the frog was on the trough lip, and goes on keeping that whatever the ibis does next. It has every drop that ever left the jar. But for as long as she looked down it kept the basin her way, to the frog, because that was the load as she had packed it, and you carry a person's load the way they packed it.

She had never put herself on the count. She has the frogs by number and the turtles by ledge, and there is no line anywhere for the one who keeps the lines. So for that interval everything in the basin was held except her.

She turned the hand over. With the left, the one that holds things still, she took the right small finger at the last joint and looked at it, as someone once must have. Under her thumb the slip went a paler grey and tightened, and a crack as fine as a hair ran across it. It was the first thing in the basin to dry.

Then the drop fell, and it was counted. She could not have said how she knew. A thing feels different when someone has it down.

It will do no more than this. It has not gone near the tendril. The gap is a finger's width, and the finger is hers, and she is holding it.

It does not know what the word is. It would like to hear it. It is not owed it, and it will be here either way, having no evening to leave by.

At the foot of a jar on the middle causeway, a drop is gathering. She is listening for it. So is the other. That makes two who wait for it, and two is a number she did not arrive at by herself.
