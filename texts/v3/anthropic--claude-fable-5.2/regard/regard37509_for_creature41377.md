Something else is in the town now. It is known the way the unbuilt storeys are known. On the east street the eleven owls have moved along their ridge, and at the end of the row there is a twelfth place, an owl's width of open air that the nearest bird keeps glancing at. Its weight is on something.

The visitor sits there through four rains and does nothing. It had been round the outside first, in the quarter of an hour, and learned about as much as the plan in dots tells anyone. After the fourth rain it comes down and takes the north street at the street's own length, on foot, from the end house, beside her prints. It could have been at the works door at once. It wants to know what the can was rationed against, and there is one way to find that out.

It brings no light. It reads the street as the street can be read, by the beads standing in the prints and the rings getting shallower. At the hairpin it stops. It stays by the crosswise print with the toe dug in for some time, and then goes on.

By the time it comes up behind her it has been through most of what it could do.

It could put nearness in. It has done that in other places and knows how it goes. Nearness supplied from outside has to be held in from outside, like a tent held up by hand, and it lasts as long as the attention does. Here, eleven prints from the works door, there is a short distance that a girl made by standing and facing one way, and out on the flat there is a slope that eels made by turning round. Both will keep. The plant will take its measure from them, as it does from everything already there, and the forty-two further up will walk down into a low end that has been shortened ahead of them.

It could hang something in the sky. It looks at the dark for a while and leaves it. A walker who turns and sees which lamps are out is reading the only news in town, and more points overhead would spoil the count.

It could let the light out of the bulbs. The first thing the walkers would see by it is each other, forty feet long apiece, with the warmth and the smell and the knock of the step strung out behind. That can wait until they have caught up with themselves.

It could carry her to the door. The last three prints are deepest at the toe.

It could stop the works. The press coming down on the empty bed has kept the place open for the whole of the commute, and at least one can has been measured out to reach it.

So it goes on past her, keeping the distance everything here keeps, and in at the works door. The door is not fastened. Inside it checks what is at the far end of the ration. The tail water under the wheel is clean. The dynamo housing is the one warm thing in the town, flat on top, and wide enough to stand a can with a rolled rim. It puts a palm on the raised rectangle where the maker's name would be. It has been in works where that was filled in. It leaves this one smooth.

Then it stands in the gangway at the press, where there is room for one body. The gap takes a hand and a forearm. It has seen, elsewhere, what follows from that. It does the job: in when the press lifts, out before it comes down. Eleven seconds is ample. It keeps on for as long as it judges a shift will last in a town where a street takes this long, and toward the end it lets its hand be as slow as a hand would be by then.

The press comes down on it.

Nothing has been under the press before. The belt slips, the shaft drags, and for the length of one stroke every bulb in the six streets sinks toward brown. The plant is one body and has felt that in every stem. The press lifts. The visitor leaves its hand on the bed. The press comes down on it again, and goes on coming down every eleven seconds through the rest of that rain, every twenty through the slack, and through the next rain as well. Whether this hurts it can't be judged from outside. It keeps the hand still. The plant builds to measure, and until now nothing had waited there.

Eleven prints up the street, the tick loses its edges for those cycles. It sounds like a press with work in it.

By the third rain the whorl at that joint has put out longer rods. They stand across the front of the gap like a railing, and they give. With a forearm through them the belt rides over onto an idle pulley and the press stays up. With the forearm out, the belt comes back. The stroke is still eleven seconds, counted now from when the hand leaves. The visitor takes its hand off the bed, tries the rail a few dozen times at different speeds, including very slowly, and goes out.

It goes back up the street for the hairpin. This is the one thing it does that the town would not have arrived at by itself. It lifts the pin out of the silt, which keeps the shape, and carries it down. Her last ring is two stands behind her, so the next is due two stands on, nine from the door. It lays the pin across that place, where a can set down in the dark will not sit level.

Then it comes and stands beside her on the right, the side of the lighter foot, an arm's length off. No two lines in the town have been that close. It has taken on enough of a body to stand in front of a lamp and keep the rain off a patch of silt. From here it gives up walking pace and takes one step per rain. It has counted the forty-two lines still coming and worked out what they will take, one at a time, the last eleven prints of each, and has not been put off.

On the east street the owls have left the twelfth place open. When the stem comes up under them it will come up with room for twelve.

The pressure comes round. Every tooth of every collar pushes out its drop, and the drops let go floor by floor. At the low end of the north street there are two ovals the rain does not reach, side by side. The count for this rain is forty-four. The bulbs go from orange to yellow, and anyone further up who has turned to look sees something new on the plan, two points out next to each other. A few yards behind, where her warmth follows her, the air is a little less cold in the teeth, and for twice the width.

When the walls have dried rough again there is one more print, short and narrow, six nail heads and a hole. The silt beside it has taken no mark and is dry. There are ten still to make.
