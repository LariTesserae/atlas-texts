The power arrives without crossing anything. It carries no day inside it and has no need to lie down, so nothing plucks at it. It understands that it can be a guest here only because it cannot be eaten.

At first it does nothing. The unit of this place is the hour, and it stays long enough to learn whether the hour tells the truth. It sits at the cupped pan through four hundred pairs of frogs. It follows a frond from fiddlehead to crab to droppings to fiddlehead. It listens until the fabric comes apart into its fixed points, and then until each point comes apart into frogs.

It is looking for a lack, because that is what benevolence knows how to answer. It finds only small ones, an hour long: the third and fourth frog at a pan for two, the rail that has never shut both eyes. These belong to the frogs and the rail, not to the basin, and they are the arithmetic of every forest. Elsewhere soil lies over the arithmetic. Here it shows.

Then it considers what it could give.

It could give a night. It could part the lid for one evening and let dark and cold come down. Everything would stop at once, both eyes shut, the first full rest the basin ever had. But some would wake before others, and what does not move is food. A night is a gift only to things that can survive lying still.

It could give ground. It could hold the crabs off one fallen frond in one trench until the frond blackened into a handful of earth. The basin would begin to have an age. But a forest with ground under it is another forest, and this one would be spent to make it.

It could wake it. Wakefulness crosses the bats like a hand passing over fur, and the power could pass that hand over all ten kilometers at once, so that for one moment the inhabitant knew itself. The power wants this more than the others, and so trusts it least. What the inhabitant would see in that moment is the rim, the lagoon, and a heat that comes from somewhere it is not. That would be its first lack, handed to it by a visitor. The power sees that the wish is its own, a wish to be seen back, and it takes some time to set that wish down.

It could lift the two extra frogs out. But every pan is full. A pan made for them would be another basin, and within the hour it would hold four.

So inside the rim it does nothing. Anything given here is a thing laid down. Either it is gone by the next hour, or it stays and the basin is gone. The place cannot receive. The power finds that it loves the place anyway, and that loving it gives it nothing to do.

It does three things, all of them outside.

The first is to keep the count. Which two frogs makes no difference to the pan, but it makes a difference to the power. One of them had a catch in its call that the hollow never quite corrected. One always waited half a beat before answering. The basin sounds its note and remembers nothing, and it is right to. But memory is something laid down, and the power has room. It lays the basin down in itself, frog by frog and midge by midge, and it does not ask the basin to know. If the place has an age anywhere, it is there.

The second is at the rim, on the outer side, where the wind reaches and the nights are real. A little below the crest it makes a dry ledge under an overhang, with a seep of cold water at the back. It is the last good place before the top, and anything climbing toward the basin with a day inside it will reach it tired. It will lie down there, all at once, the way things from outside do, and nothing will shift at the edge of its vision. It will come to the crest in the morning with the whole length of its waking ahead of it. From there it will hear a sound that never draws breath, and it can make of that what it is able to. It can cross, or look and turn back. The power builds no fence and leaves no sign, because the basin is not a crime and the visitor is not a child. It only moves the visitor's sleep to the other side of the line.

The third is a decision about the end. The stone has never cooled, as far as an hour can measure. The power measures farther. Rock like this cools, and the lagoon lies only a few meters below the lowest fern. The power could hold the heat where it is forever. It decides not to. To do that would be to become the stone, and the inhabitant would then be something the power was doing.

It decides to be present instead. A time will come when the threads of steam thin and the lid tears. Light with edges will come through, and there will be shadows, and then an evening. In the cold the chorus will thin for the first time. The crabs will slow. A frond will fall and lie where it fell until morning, the first thing ever laid down here. The basin will begin to have an age on the night it stops being what it was. The power will not call that a death or a birth. It will be there, and it will be the only thing anywhere that knows what the cupped pan's note used to be, and which frogs sounded it.

That is not yet. For now the power goes back to the smallest pan. One frog calls while the other sits, and then they trade. The power does what nothing else here can do. It holds entirely still, and it attends. A crab walks through the place where it is and does not stop.
