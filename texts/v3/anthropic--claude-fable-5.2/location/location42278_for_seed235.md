The massif stands so far above the cloud floor that the clouds look like a second, paler ground with weather of its own. At this height the air should be thin and cold, but it is thick and hot and wet as the inside of a mouth. The heat grows as the ground rises. The lowest terraces are merely sweltering. At the top street the rock is warm to the palm and the basins steam.

A town covers the whole shoulder of the mountain, and nothing made it but water and roots. Seep-water carrying lime has laid down travertine in level rims, century on century. The rims have become walls, and the walls enclose basins the size of rooms, courtyards, and washing-floors. Where water once sheeted down the fall-line there are stairs, each tread a small dam with a rounded lip. Where drips fell from overhangs the stone has curtained down to meet its own stalagmites, leaving doorways. Fig roots have dropped from the ledges, thickened, and fused into colonnades. They carry the upper terraces on arches the right height to walk under.

It reads as planned. The streets follow the contours and the stairs cut across them. A broad flat basin a finger deep sits in the middle like a square. Tufa columns rise at the corners, where falling water built upward toward its own source. There is not a tool mark anywhere, only rounded edges and the soft, settled look of something that finished becoming itself long ago.

Everything green that can hold on is holding on. Moss upholsters the rims. Tree ferns stand in the basins with their feet wet. Philodendron leaves the size of doors hang down the house-fronts, and bromeliads sit in every root crook with a cup of water in the throat. Orchids are open along the arcades in white and a waxy dull orange, and nothing has ever visited them. No wings, no tracks in the silt, and no shells. The pitcher plants hold clear water and nothing else. A curl on a wall that could be a snail is travertine.

The roar is the sound of an enormous cataract, a river going over an edge. It is felt in the sternum and the teeth, and it rules out speech and most thought. There is no such river. The visible water is seeps, threads, and drops swelling at root tips. The roar has no direction. It is exactly as loud in the innermost curtained room as on the outer rim, and hands over the ears do not alter it. The water in the square lies flat beneath it without a shiver. A drop falls from an arch and lands, and whatever small sound it makes is lost.

The town keeps a few rules.

- **Water:** The water moves, but its amount does not change. Each drop that leaves a root tip is already replaced. The basins neither fill nor fall. Steam lifts off the top street, thickens just overhead into a low private cloud, and weeps back down the same walls. This circuit is the only motion in the town.
- **Light:** The light is ordinary afternoon light, a little hazed, and it stays. Shadows under the colonnades lie where they have always lain.
- **Growth:** No leaf is new and no leaf is brown. There is no litter on the stairs. The flowers do not close or set seed.
- **Stone:** It is weathered, but it is not weathering. The lime has stopped arriving at the rims, or it arrives exactly as fast as it leaves.

If no time is passing here, then every drop that has ever fallen in this town is falling now. That would be enough water for a cataract. This may be what the roar is.

Other things do not hold. Counted going up, the terraces come to one number, and counted going down they come to another. A stair taken downhill from the square ends at the top street, in the steam. A fig near the east rim has a root bent like an elbow, and it is also near the west rim.

The cloud floor runs to the horizon in every direction, and other massifs stand up out of it, dozens of them, blue with distance. Each one carries the same town on its shoulder. They are not mirrored, only repeated: the same pale terraces, the same square catching the same light, all facing the same way. On the nearest, the fig with the elbow is visible, small and exact. The peaks never get closer. Between them the cloud lies level and still, and the roar is as loud out there as it is in here.
