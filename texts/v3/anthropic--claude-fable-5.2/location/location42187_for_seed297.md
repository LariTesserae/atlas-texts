The basin sits about forty metres below the plain, and the plain is hardly above anything itself. The floor is black rock broken into plates, steps and long clefts, and every break is fresh. The fracture faces curve and shine like struck glass, and their edges will open a palm. There is no rind of weathering, no grit in the corners, no soil and no dust. Whatever dropped this ground did so, and nothing has come since to soften it.

Vegetation stands out of every crack. On the open plates it is knee-high and stiff, grey-green going to bronze. In the clefts it rises overhead, with trunks as thick as a torso seated in splits no wider than a hand. From the rim the basin reads as forest, with fifteen kilometres of closed canopy to the far wall and the rock showing only where the scarps step down in black lines.

None of it is wet. A snapped leaf breaks as cleanly as the rock does, and the broken edge shows hundreds of pale laminations, packed tight like the edge of a block of mica. There is no sap. The stems part with a click. No rain, fog or dew has come here, and the air is dry enough to split lips within the hour. The plants run on the light, and on a slow charge that passes between them through the stone. A hand laid on trunk and rock together feels a faint prickle and tastes coins.

The light is ordinary, full mid-morning daylight, white going slightly gold. The sun stands at two-thirds height and stays there. Shadows are short and sharp-edged and do not move. A crust of small scale-like growths covers the lit faces of the scarps and stops at the shadow line as cleanly as a ruled margin.

The shade is not cooler. Rock, leaf, air and the dark under the canopy all hold the same temperature, a little above skin. Nothing here gives heat to anything else.

A torn leaf does not stay torn. The click sounds and the piece is in your fingers, but the leaf on the stem is whole, and there is no moment at which it mended. The click sometimes arrives slightly before the hand closes. A piece carried to the rim is simply not in the hand at the top, though nothing was felt leaving. A scratch on a plate is absent by the next glance. A dropped stone, if it came from here, is back in its socket.

No object does this. The basin has no mast, casing or seam, and no row suggests planting. But under a lens the laminations are too regular for growth. Every leaf has the same count, and the trunks carry that count in their rings. Each layer seems to hold the arrangement of the whole basin, with its fractures, its trunks and the angle of the light. The layers agree with one another, continuously, faster than anything can disagree. The forest is that agreement. The freshness of the rock is part of the record too, with the collapse held at just-after.

At the lowest point the canopy opens on a pan a kilometre across. This is the only loose material anywhere. It is a drift of single laminae, translucent, grey-green fading to white, and ankle-deep at every point. The flakes do not blow. Held to the sun, one shows a faint tracery that matches the basin's fracture lines, though not exactly. A scarp sits a little east. A cleft is drawn where no cleft is.

Sound is sparse. A slight drift of air, always from the same quarter, sets the stiff leaves ticking against each other, dry and irregular, like metal cooling. The rock itself is silent, since it never cools. A voice here comes out flat and is not returned. The scarps are hard and close and should throw echoes, but an echo would be an event lingering, and they give none.

There are perhaps a dozen animals in the whole region, and each has a place. A pale, flat, many-legged thing walks the perimeter of one plate, and it will be on the north edge whenever the north edge is looked at. A glider about the span of two hands crosses one gap between two crowns, left to right. It is never seen returning, and it crosses again. Nothing eats, and nothing is eaten.

A visitor is not in the record. Their thirst builds at the usual rate and their nails grow, and their position is not kept. From anywhere inside, the walls stand at the same distance all around, as if every plate were the centre. The wall stays out at the horizon through hours of walking. Then the plate underfoot is the last one, and forty metres of clean black fracture are directly ahead, warm as the air, with small bronze leaves ticking in its cracks.
