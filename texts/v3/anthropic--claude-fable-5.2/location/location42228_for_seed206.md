Down on the river flat, below the levee and a few hundred metres from where a power station lets its cooling water back into the world, there is a room that nobody made.

It lies inside a reed bed so old that it has stopped being a bed and become a mass. The room is a hollow in that mass, perhaps six paces across, with a roof low enough to touch at the edges. The only ways in are a kingfisher-sized gap on the east side and the water underneath.

**The room**

- **Floor:** There is no ground, only a raft of pennywort, reed rhizome and willow root, woven and rewoven until it bears weight. A pole pushed down would go through two metres of root, then warm water, then silt as fine as flour. The surface gives slightly underfoot and gives back.
- **Walls:** Giant reed stands cane against cane, the gaps stitched shut with bindweed.
- **Roof:** A crack willow fell across the hollow decades ago and kept living. Its trunk is the ridge beam, and its shoots rise straight up through a thatch of wild grape.
- **Light:** Daylight arrives filtered green, even and unremarkable, like the light in a shaded yard. At night the station's lamps soak through as a dull amber, so the room dims but never goes fully dark.
- **Contents:** The room is mostly empty. There is one pool in the middle, the size of a tabletop and black. There is one stump furred in liverwort. A few ferns are spaced as if they had agreed on distances. The rest is warm, unoccupied air.

**Water and warmth**

Water is everywhere without ever quite being rain. It beads on the undersides of the grape leaves and drops at long intervals. It stands in every heel-print for a minute before the mat drinks it back.

The pool is the only open water. It is open because the outflow seeps in warm from below and rots the roots as fast as they reach across. The reeds advance and the warmth trims them, and the rim has held the same rounded lip for longer than the frogs have had generations to count.

The water is the temperature of a bath someone forgot. The air is a little cooler and always the same. Outside, in January, frost comes up the flat and stops at a line about forty metres off. Nothing in the room knows about that.

**Sound**

The room is loud. Three sounds lie under everything else:

- **The turbine hall:** A steady, low, pitched drone comes through the mat itself as much as through the air.
- **The outfall weir:** A broad white roar comes from the east.
- **The transmission lines:** A dry electrical sizzle hangs somewhere above the roof on damp days, and every day here is damp.

On top of that are the animals, which have had sixty years to arrange themselves around it. The tree frogs call a notch above the drone and the big green frogs grunt just beneath it, so the engine note sits between them like a floorboard between storeys. The crickets fill what is left. They never stop, because the cold that would stop them never comes.

A person would have to raise their voice to be heard across six paces. The reed warblers do.

**Animals**

The room looks empty and sounds full. The animals are in the walls and under the floor.

- **In the pool:** Feral guppies, descended from someone's tipped-out aquarium, hang in the pool with tails like sparks. Pale tropical snails graze the lip. Tadpoles of every size swim there at once, because there is no breeding season, only breeding.
- **Under the mat:** Now and then a terrapin shoulders past below, and one fern nods.
- **In the canes:** A grass snake lives somewhere in the reed wall and is seen as a rearrangement of shadows.
- **In the air:** Dragonflies come in through the kingfisher gap, quarter the open air once, and leave. The kingfisher itself arrives, takes a guppy, and is gone before the ripples register.

But the ripples are odd, and they are the one thing a visitor would stop and stare at. The pool's surface is never smooth. It carries a fine, fixed crosshatch of standing wavelets, like the skin of milk on a vibrating table. This is the station's hum made visible.

Drop a leaf on it and the pattern breaks, then reassembles around the leaf. The leaf then turns slowly anticlockwise to the same spot on the north rim where every leaf goes. A small brown drift of them rests there, and it never gets larger. The snails see to that.

**How it holds**

Everything here has finished arriving. The willow has finished falling. The silt below has found its slope. The mat has thickened to exactly the weight the water will carry.

The station runs base load, day and night and all year, so the warmth, the hum and the seep never vary. The room has shaped itself to a constant the way other places shape themselves to seasons. Leaves are shed one at a time, on no schedule. Something is always in flower, though never much.

Nothing made by hands is in here. No bottle, no float and no cut stem ever found its way in. The industry enters only as heat, as tremor, and as one long note. The room has grown around all three as if they were weather that had always been there.
