The mountain has been worn down to its floor. What remains is a tableland of pale granite, high enough that the air tastes thin and metallic, and level enough that rain hesitates before choosing a direction to run. Nothing here is steep anymore. The peaks left long ago as gravel, and the gravel left as sand.

On the flattest part of the summit stand the walls. They cover a patch you could cross in five minutes: perhaps forty rooms, six streets, and one wider lane running through the middle. The granite split along its own joints, at right angles, and frost has opened the cracks into corridors. Some walls reach the shoulder and most reach the knee. At the western edge they are only lines of stone flush with the turf, a floor plan and nothing more. There are no tool marks, no mortar, and no roofs. Every corner is rounded like used soap. The streets are paved with loose feldspar crystals shed from the walls, coarse as rock salt, which crunch and shift underfoot.

It looks like a town, and it has what a town has: thresholds, a square, and a deep weathering pan in the square that holds water the way a well would. The gaps between rooms line up in rows. Nothing made it.

The rooms are nearly empty. Each one holds a floor, a little water, and a great deal of moving air. But the floors are thick with growth. Out on the open plateau the wind permits only a skin of lichen and some bristling sedge, while inside the walls it is a hand's width warmer and stiller, and that is enough. Moss fills the rooms in deep cushions, bronze and green, soaked like sponge. Dwarf willow creeps flat along the base of the stone, its catkins standing up like small candles in the short summer. Crowberry mats the corners. On the lee side of the taller walls, ferns come up out of the cracks, and woodrush grows in tufts along the thresholds. No two rooms are quite alike. One is all moss, and another, with a lower wall, is half lichen and half bare grit.

The water mostly arrives sideways. Cloud crosses the plateau several times a day, and the walls comb it. Droplets gather on the windward faces, run down, and collect in the shallow pans that pit the tops and floors of the stone. Each room has at least one pan, almost always on its eastern side. They freeze over at night and are open again by midmorning. Tiny red crustaceans live in a few of them, turning in the meltwater.

The sound is the first thing a visitor notices and the last thing they stop noticing. The wind never drops below a hard, steady push, and every gap in the walls gives it a pitch. Narrow doorways whistle. The main street carries a low drone that is felt in the breastbone, and the shallow western ruins hiss. Together it makes a loud, shifting chord, loud enough that speech has to be shouted, and it changes whenever the wind backs a few degrees. A street will fall quiet while another starts up, as though something were moving from one to the other.

From inside any room, the noise in the next room does not sound like wind. It sounds like occupancy: a murmur, a scrape, something set down on stone. Step through the gap and the room is moss and a pan of water, and the murmur has moved one room on.

The rule here is simple. A body in a doorway stops its note. Stand in a threshold and that street goes dumb behind you, and the whole town's chord shifts to close around the missing tone. Step out and it returns.

Weather comes through in quick succession. Bright ordinary sunlight throws sharp shadows from the low walls, then cloud is in the streets at walking pace, then sleet rattles on the stone, then sun again, steaming. On the coldest nights the fog freezes onto every windward edge in white feathers, and the town goes muffled and strange. When the morning warms, the rime lets go all at once and falls clattering into the streets.

Almost nothing lives here that moves. A hare keeps to the eastern rooms, and the only sign of it is clipped willow. Voles tunnel under the moss. A single pair of ravens nests on the plateau rim and comes in most evenings. One of them lands in the same gap on the main street and sits there, and the drone cuts out. It shifts aside and the drone comes back. It does this for a long time, then leaves, and the town goes on sounding with nobody in it.
