Below the surface the rock is a froth. Some enormous melt took gas all the way through itself and then set before a single bubble could rise or burst, and what remains is glass foam at the scale of a continent. Chambers the size of a fist sit beside chambers the size of a harbor, sharing walls that are fingernail-thin in places and hill-thick in others. The glass is deep brown, going bottle-green where it thins. Nothing has blunted it. Every fracture is still a clean shell-curve and every broken rim would still take the hair off an arm. It looks as if it cooled yesterday, and it has looked that way for longer than anything here keeps count.

It cooled to blood heat and stopped. The walls, the air, and the animals are all the same temperature, so touch loses one of its senses. A hand cannot find a wall by its coolness, and the glass feels like nothing at all until it cuts. The air is dry enough that lips split. There is no drip, no seep, no damp corner. All the water in this place is inside something, and it moves only when one body takes it from another.

Where the bubbles pulled apart in setting they drew threads between them. These are filaments of gold-brown glass, a few as thick as a finger and most finer than hair, strung across every chamber in the thousands, wall to wall and floor to ceiling. They crowd close enough that moving through a room means parting them with the back of a wrist. In the dry air they carry a charge, and they lean slightly toward anything that passes.

On the gas side of the thinner membranes grows the only thing here that could be mistaken for a crop. It is a grey-violet rind, hard as eggshell, fed by whatever still comes through the glass from the sealed bubbles behind. It is not alive in any way that can be demonstrated. It thickens exactly as fast as it is scraped away, and no faster.

Everything else stands on that rind, and there is a great deal of everything else. Flat pale grazers the width of two palms cover the walls like scales, their feet soled in felt and their mouthparts working with a rasp softer than a fingernail on cloth. Smaller things live in the grazers' moult. Long many-jointed hunters sit where the threads converge, a limb resting on each of a dozen filaments, reading the chamber the way a hand reads a pulse. In the wider spaces, gas-bellied drifters hang at fixed heights and trail feelers down into the hair.

Nearly all of them carry a little light, a dull green-white no brighter than a watch dial. Because the walls are glass, the lights of the next chamber show through, and the ones beyond that, each layer browner and dimmer. The dark is deep and speckled in every direction, including down. The number of lights does not vary. What is eaten is replaced, and a chamber counted twice gives the same count.

It is quiet for a reason. Every bubble has a note. A loud sound sets a membrane ringing, and a ringing membrane sheds its hair in a fall of needles and small cold sparks, since this glass gives a pinprick of blue wherever it breaks. Nothing here calls or stamps. The loudest common sound is a single thread letting go somewhere, a tick and a point of light, and after that the rasping again.

There are marks of hands, though not many. Felt roads cross the worst floors, strips of beaten grazer-moult a forearm wide, laid edge over edge and patched in places with newer felt of the same grey. Sinew cords run alongside at hip height, tied off to bone pegs wedged in fractures and knotted in patterns that fingers can read: turn here, low rim, thin floor. Doorways have been pressure-flaked through membranes one chip at a time and then ground with sand-glass and patience until the rims are dull. Those rims are the only rounded edges in the entire foam, and a palm resting on one knows at once that someone made it.

Knapped blades with gut-wrapped handles lie cached in small bubbles beside coils of spare cord and a drifter's bladder stoppered with bone. The bladder holds a few mouthfuls of what the drifter held. Sleeping baskets of felt and rib hang from the thicker threads. It is impossible to tell whether one has just been left, because everything here is as warm as a body.

The makers find their way by voice, kept very low. A hum at the right pitch, barely more than breath, makes the chamber answer with its own note. The next chamber's note leaks through the doorway a little flat or a little sharp, and a route is a melody remembered. The notes have not drifted, and the cords are knotted to the same tune.

Far in, the roads end at a ground rim opening onto a bubble so large that its far wall is only a theory. Here the foam has weather. The drifters fill the chamber in decks, tens of thousands to a layer, and the whole population turns in one slow wheel around the chamber's axis, driven only by their own faint sculling. From the rim it looks like dim green cloud banks sliding past at walking pace, with darker lanes between where the hunters' threads run. The wheel has never been seen to speed, slow, or reverse.

Someone has tied a cord to a peg at the rim here, let it out into the hair, and knotted the free end once. The knot means *same*.
