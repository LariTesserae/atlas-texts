**The fracture**

Just under the summit ridge, where the air holds about half of what a lung expects, the mountain has broken open. The break is recent. Two slabs of dark, garnet-studded rock have pulled apart and then leaned back toward each other, leaving a cleft some forty paces long, six wide at the belly, and as tall as an old tree. At the top the slabs almost meet, and the sky is a crooked scratch, usually plugged by cloud.

Nothing here has had time to become round. The edges cut, the crystal faces are still bright where they sheared, and the floor is wedged angular blocks with black gaps between them. The rock smells of struck flint. Now and then it ticks, settling, a finger's width wider each generation.

**The air inside**

Outside, the cold kills. Inside it is only cool, like a cellar, because the cleft is heated by what lives in it.

The walls are furred with moths, grey and fat, shingled wing over wing like roof tiles from the floor to the dark overhead. There are millions, layered so thickly that the rock beneath shows only where one loses its grip. Their massed bodies make the cleft's weather. Their warmth meets the cold stone and condenses, and the walls sweat. Water beads on wing-scales, runs in threads, and gathers in hollows. Their dust hangs in the damp air, a fine grey powder that swallows light. A flame carried in here would light a sphere about one arm across.

**The others**

The moths have drawn everything else up the mountain:

- **At the mouth:** choughs crowd the ledges shoulder to shoulder, bickering.
- **Higher up:** swifts hang from the wall by their claws like dark fruit, and a knot of small bats roosts though no bat should be at this height.
- **On the walls:** pale harvestmen cluster in mats that pulse when breathed on, and jumping spiders work the moth-fur the way herders work a flock.
- **On the floor:** ice-crawlers, amber and soft, move between the blocks, and springtails lie on every pool in a skin like soot, dimpling the surface.
- **At the back:** where the walls pinch to a shoulder's width, something large and furred sleeps wedged upright. Its breathing is slow enough to mistake for wind.

**The sound**

The noise is constant and comes from all sides. The wings never quite still. There is a dry hiss like sand poured on hide, and it thickens to a roar when a shiver passes through the mass. The chough calls crack off the slabs and return doubled. The bats tick. Wind crossing the top slot blows the whole cleft like a bottle, a low note felt in the teeth, and the wing-roar seems to rise and settle around that note as if tuning to it.

Sometimes everything stops at once, with no signal anyone could name. For five or six breaths the only sound is a single drip. Then it all resumes.

**The rule**

What clings is left alone, and what falls belongs to the floor. The spiders herd but do not take from the wall. The choughs walk the blocks and pick up only the dropped. The sleeper at the back, when it wakes, licks the floor stones clean and never raises its head.

The pools have their own rule. In each pocket of water, some no larger than a cupped hand, lives one pale swimmer the length of a finger, colourless and blind. There is never more than one to a pool.

**What grows**

Very little grows here:

- At the mouth, a rind of orange lichen is just beginning on the fresh rock, in coins no bigger than a thumbnail.
- Along the drip lines, where droppings and water meet, there are dark cushions of moss.
- Deeper in, a white fungus threads the drifts of shed wings that fill the gaps between blocks, and it smells of nuts and ammonia.

**What was left**

People have been here, though they built nothing. On one flat block there is a hollow worn smooth by grinding, the only smooth surface in the cleft, and beside it lies a fist-sized river cobble that someone carried up from far below. A bone awl is jammed in a crack, and a scrap of knotted fibre net has rotted to lace.

On the north wall is a soot-stain in the shape of a tall flame, where smoke was once used to make the moths fall. No moth has settled on that patch since. It is the only bare rock on the wall, a black handprint of a thing done against the rule.

**The light**

Once a day, when the cloud allows, a blade of sun comes through the slot and travels slowly down the east wall. The moths part ahead of it and close behind, a seam of naked rock moving through the fur, garnets flashing red for a moment each.

Over the season the whole mass creeps upward a hand's width a day, following the cold. Beneath it the moss advances, and the cleft opens a little more.
