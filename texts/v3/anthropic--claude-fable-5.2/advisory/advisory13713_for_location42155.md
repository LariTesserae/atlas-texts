# Preparing for the saddle city

## What the visit is like

Nothing here will attack you in the usual sense. The air is breathable and cellar-cool, the paving is level, and the city is busy with itself and indifferent to you. The dangers are thirst, arithmetic, and inattention. There is no water, distances don't add up the same in both directions, and anything left unwatched gets tidied into a wall.

Expect dim, even twilight under an indigo sky, with a steady wind on your face that makes no noise. The only sounds will be your breath, your boots, and your partner's voice if they are close. Most people find the silence harder than the emptiness.

Four rules matter more than everything else here:

1. **Bring a partner and never separate.**
2. **Carry all your water, and budget the return as longer than the way in.**
3. **Leave nothing unwatched, including a sleeping person.**
4. **Stay out of doorways and away from the foot of walls.**

## What to bring

**Water and food**
- All of your water. Nothing has ever fallen here and the cisterns hold dust. In air this dry you will lose more than you expect, so plan on four to five litres per person per day, plus electrolytes.
- Food that needs no cooking and no water.
- A wheeled cart, if you are hauling several days of water. The paving is continuous and the kerbs are true, though you will have to carry it up stairs.

**Clothing and body care**
- A windproof shell over light insulation. The cold is mild but the wind never stops. You need no rain gear.
- Natural fibres such as wool and cotton, because static is a feature of the place and synthetics will crackle.
- Cushioned boots for long days on hard stone, plus tall gaiters for the dust.
- Gloves, since schist edges are sharp and flaky.
- Lip balm, saline nasal spray, eye drops, and skin salve. Expect cracked lips and nosebleeds by the second day.

**Dust protection**
- Sealed goggles and a proper particulate respirator. The dust is fine rock flour mixed with flakes of the black crust, and neither belongs in your lungs.
- A long non-metal probe pole of wood or fibreglass.

**Light, time, and breathing**
- A headlamp with spare batteries, because the light is always dim.
- A mechanical watch each. It is the only clock that keeps your own time.
- A pulse oximeter and a small oxygen supply. The place seems to forgive the altitude, but verify this on arrival. You cannot descend if you get sick, because below the rim there is only haze.

**Keeping things together**
- A short tether to link you to your partner.
- Lanyards for every item you own.
- Waste bags. Pack out everything, including your own waste, or the city may build it in.

Keep metal to a minimum and electronics in a shielded pouch. You will be carrying the only metal on the plateau, and blue threads of charge crawl along the high walls looking for somewhere to go.

## Where to arrive

Arrive on the western rim if you can choose. The wind there pushes you inward, away from the drop. On the eastern rim the wind is at your back, and the houses are slowly walking off the edge above scree fans.

The rim is the one place where distance is honest. The perimeter can be walked in a day and stays walked. Make your base there and spend the first hours sitting still and watching the shadows disagree until this stops feeling like a problem with your eyes.

Stay on the saddle. Whatever holds the cold off probably ends at the summits and below the rim.

## How to navigate

Several ordinary tools fail here:

- **The sun** is useless as a compass, because each house keeps its own.
- **Counting** houses, courses, or paces gives a different answer in each direction.
- **Footprints** are erased by the dust tide.
- **Cairns and markers** will be found seated in a wall.

Four things do work:

- **The wind.** It is constant, and since the walls erode on their windward faces while the houses walk east, I infer it blows from the west. Wind in your face means you are heading for the western rim.
- **The pale rectangles.** They trail west behind every building and so point the same way.
- **Your own shadow.** It swings as you pass from one house's hour to the next, so it tells you whose time you are standing in.
- **The crust.** It grows only in long shadows, so a wall with thick crust is in its early morning or late afternoon.

## How to budget a foray

The bare paving between houses is hours laid flat. Four hundred paces is a short walk and also a long time. Because the count differs by direction, the way back may hold more houses than the way in.

Your water is the only ledger that reads the same both ways. Turn around when a third of it is gone, at the latest. Eat and sleep by your watch, since the light will give you no cues.

For a first visit I would walk the perimeter, then make a foray two or three houses deep, and stop there. A full crossing takes far longer than the map suggests, and nobody can tell you how much longer.

## Staying together

Sound doesn't carry. The click of a slab seating itself reaches only a few arm's lengths, and I would expect your voice to do little better. Test this when you arrive. Agree on hand signals and stay tethered.

The grazer is always alone and always far down a street. I suspect that is what happens to anything that gets an hour to itself. A partner you can see at the next house may be hours away, and the number of houses between you depends on which of you does the walking.

If you do get separated, don't chase. Both of you should walk into the wind to the western rim and meet there.

## The unwatched rule

A loose slab left unwatched ends up in a wall. So do the grazer's pellets. I can't say whether the city distinguishes between a slab, a rucksack, and a sleeper, and I would not run the experiment.

- Keep gear on your body, tethered, or under someone's eyes.
- Sleep in shifts, with the waking partner actually looking at the sleeper.
- If you hear the click, something within a few arm's lengths has just been placed. Check your things.
- Take no stones or pellets as souvenirs. What belongs to the walls tends to return to them, and you don't want that happening inside your pack.
- Leave no tool marks. The city has none.

## Walls, doorways, and windows

Something here imitates a doorway. It appears as an extra opening, darker than the dim room behind it could make it, and it takes whatever passes along the foot of the wall. Its known prey is the size of a hare, but the opening is the size of a door.

- Walk down the middle of the streets. There is plenty of room.
- Never shelter in a doorway. Never enter a house you have not watched for a long while, and preferably don't enter one at all.
- Don't trust what you see through windows. They keep their own hours and show you the street as it was or will be. If you see figures through one, or far down a street, don't follow them.
- Climb with care, if you climb at all. Rooftops give the best views, but the copings carry charge and the windward faces are slowly coming apart.

## Dust

Toward each house's evening, the dust runs ankle-deep down its streets and fills the channels and cisterns level with the paving. A full cistern looks exactly like the street, and I'd expect it to swallow a person the way a grain silo does. Probe ahead whenever the tide is in.

Camp in the open near a house whose shadow is long and pointing west. That is a morning house, so its tide is on the way out. Avoid the lee side of walls, however tempting the shelter. The crust grows there and the building happens there. I would also avoid sleeping on the pale rectangles, though that is only a feeling.

## The centre

You will see the square from any rooftop, with its single low wall and no shadow. As you approach, the shadows shorten and begin to agree, and your own shrinks toward your feet. The gaps between buildings get longer, which means the rule that paving equals hours has become something else that I can't name.

The click comes from there most often. My guess is that the wall is where unwatched things end up.

Treat the centre as a landmark and not a destination. My rule would be to turn back while you still have a shadow.

## Leaving

Leave from the rim where you arrived. Before you go, check your watches against whatever the rim-side one says and do a full inventory of gear. Then count your party from both ends.
