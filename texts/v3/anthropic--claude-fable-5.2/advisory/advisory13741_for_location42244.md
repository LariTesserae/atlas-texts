# Preparing for the town under the basalt

Nothing in this place will come after you. The dangers are heat, bad air, sharp or loose rock, and machinery that has run unattended for a long time. The other risk is missing the lift. Plan a short visit, about an hour on the floor, and treat it like entering a working boiler room inside a mine.

## Before you go down

- **Learn the lift's timetable.** It runs on a timer and waits for no one. Watch at least one full cycle from the top and time the interval and how long the gate stays open. Check whether the shaft has a ladderway, and if it doesn't, the lift is your only exit.
- **Leave word on the surface.** Tell someone your return time, or better, bring a companion.
- **Check your fitness for heat.** If you have heart trouble or asthma, are pregnant, or cope badly in saunas, skip this one. The struck-match smell is sulphur dioxide, which is hard on airways.
- **Clean your boots and gear.** Nothing down there eats the plants, and you don't want to be the one who introduces something that does.

## What to wear

- **Hard hat and eye protection.** Flakes still fall from the roof.
- **Thick-soled boots.** The street is hot iron grating that will cook your feet through thin soles, and it is slick when wet.
- **Leather-palmed gloves.** The fresh rock edges cut like glass, and bare metal burns.
- **Thin, loose long sleeves and trousers.** You need cover against hot brick and sharp rock, but as little insulation as possible.
- **No raincoat.** The rain is warm and a shell would trap heat. You will get wet and be dry within a quarter of an hour.
- **A coat for the surface.** The coat hooks in the canteen suggest it's cold up top. Carry the coat down in your bag in case you surface somewhere else or much later.

## What to carry

- **Water.** Bring two to three litres with electrolytes. Don't drink the rain or anything from a pipe, since both are loaded with minerals.
- **Lights.** Bring a headlamp and two backups, all sealed. A third of the lamps are dead, and the rest light only a few metres.
- **A gas monitor.** It should read oxygen, carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulphide, sulphur dioxide and flammable gas. Carry an escape respirator if you can get one.
- **Nothing that burns or sparks.** Leave lighters, matches, stoves and cigarettes behind. The NO NAKED FLAME sign was put up for a reason.
- **A watch you trust.** Phones overheat and fog, so keep electronics in a sealed bag. Lenses will fog too. Use a pencil, because ink runs.

## Moving through the town

- **Use the rain as your clock.** It comes about once an hour and lasts eleven minutes. Wait it out in a doorway, since you can't hear the rock or the valves over it. The lamps brighten then, so it is the best time to look around.
- **Stay at street level.** The upper storeys are hotter, hidden in vapour, and braced by jacks that have fallen behind the rock. Don't trust the catwalks.
- **Touch nothing that is working.** Leave the valve wheels, jacks and drill carriage alone. The grey pipe lagging is very likely asbestos, so don't brush or tear it.
- **Read the plants.** A green disc marks a working lamp. A line of dark liverwort marks a leaking joint, possibly a scalding one. Green surfaces are slippery.
- **Keep clear of the relief valve on Boiler Row.** It vents every few minutes.
- **Be careful at the low end.** The sump would scald you, and heavy gases collect in low places. Don't go into the culvert.
- **Keep the far end brief.** The cut face is the hottest, rawest and least supported part of the chamber.

## Looking after yourself

- **Cool off at the landing.** The shaft is where the cooler air comes in, so leave your spare water there and return to it.
- **Leave on any sign of heat illness.** That means headache, nausea, cramps, dizziness, or noticing that you've stopped sweating. Confusion is the dangerous one, because you won't notice it yourself. Agree a turnaround time beforehand and obey it.
- **Leave if the sounds change.** Between rains you can tell every sound apart. If the clinks on the roofs come faster, or you hear something new, go.
- **Be at the gate early.**

## What it will feel like

The town will feel inhabited, with its mugs on hooks, lit corners and a lift that arrives on time. You will probably catch yourself listening for footsteps. The turbine's note sits in your chest and can pass for dread, but it is only the turbine. The coats are gone and the mugs are not, which suggests the people left in an orderly way, though I can't tell you why.

You may want to tighten a jack, chalk a date or call out down the street. Calling out does no harm, though your voice will sound strange in there. Leave the jacks and the chalk columns as they are.
