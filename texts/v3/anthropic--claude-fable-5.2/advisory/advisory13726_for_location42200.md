# Preparing for the rain column on the saddle

This is a hard day visit to a place that is loud, cold, sharp, and tended by other people. Three things can hurt you: the rock, the water, and the noise. The one thing you can damage is the arrangement of stones in the basin.

## Decide everything in the valley

At the col you cannot speak, and I expect you will find it hard to think. Sound that sits behind the breastbone crowds out planning, so make every decision while the fall is still a hiss.

- **Plan:** Agree who goes where, how long you stay, and when you turn back.
- **Signs:** Ask someone in the valley to show you the five signs: stone, rope, deeper, enough, go. They have worn down to small movements, so watch closely. Add a few of your own for "I'm fine", "cold", "stop", and "help".
- **Rope code:** Agree on tugs for when hands can't be seen. The column is a wall of water, and a person inside it is invisible from outside.
- **Animals:** Leave them below. Goats refuse this place, and so will anything else with sense.
- **A stone:** Pick up a sound, fist-sized rough stone before the scree and carry it up. That is the nearest thing this place has to an entrance fee.
- **Season:** Go in warm weather. In frost, I would expect the overflow and spray to glaze the whole saddle.

## The climb

The rock is new, and every edge is a blade.

- **Boots and gloves:** Wear your thickest-soled boots and leather gloves, and bring something to bind a split sole.
- **Dressings:** Bring more than seems reasonable. A fall here means cuts as well as bruises.
- **Route:** There is no worn path, because nothing has had time to wear. The roar is your bearing.
- **Water underfoot:** There is no stream bed, so the overflow can cross your route anywhere and may have loosened the scree. A wet sheen, finger-deep fresh channels, and live cushion plants mark where it runs now. Grey husks mark where it used to.
- **Rockfall:** On the last slope you will not hear it, or a companion's shout. Look around more than feels natural.

## At the col

**Noise.** Wear plugs with muffs over them, and accept that they only help. Much of the sound arrives through bone. Expect unease, perhaps nausea, and a headache that altitude will sharpen. Set a limit of an hour or two and keep it. Locals may appear at your elbow without warning, since you won't hear them come.

**Water.** The column is roughly ten metres across and falls from about seventy metres up. It is not rain in any sense you know. It will beat on your head and shoulders like a waterfall, and the air inside it is half water. I would also expect a cold, wet wind blowing outward from its foot, from the air the fall drags down with it. The edge shifts with gusts, and the moss ring shows only its average reach. Stand farther back than you think you need to.

**Your kit.** Put dry clothes in a sealed bag on ground higher than the basin rim, toward the crest. The overflow changes direction within the hour, and a surge can send it anywhere lower. Do not use the stone ledge as a shelf.

**Cold and sun.** Spray, wind, and altitude will chill you even if you never get wet on purpose. The sky is clear and the column is blindingly white, so bring dark glasses, a brim, and skin cover.

**What to look at.** These need no risk at all:

- Walk along the ridge until you see the underside edge-on as a trembling line, and wait for a chough to cross above it.
- With the sun behind you, the spray should hold a rainbow.
- Find the moss ring, alive on one side and dying on the other.
- Watch the grey bird walk in under the edge. It goes no deeper than the edge, which tells you something.

## What isn't yours

- **The wall:** Do not join it. People who could build both ends could have built the middle.
- **The gutter and trough:** Do not re-lay the slabs to chase the water. If the trough fills while you are there, I think you may drink. The water has nothing in it and is cold enough to hurt your teeth.
- **The pole, pegs, and rope:** Do not move the notched pole, since it is the only record of how fast the bowl is deepening. Do not hammer anything new into the cracks, where the larvae live. Never cut the hide rope.
- **The spheres:** These are someone's long labor. The gaps were made by people with a right to make them. Take one only if it is put in your hand. That includes the small ones, which may have spent the longest in the basin.
- **Your stone:** Set it with the rough ones on the left.

## The rule

The basin is never left empty. Treat this as the only law here.

- **Order:** If any stone comes out while you are present, a rough one goes in first.
- **If the sound is wrong:** If you arrive to a climbing, unbearable note, the floor is bare. Check that no one is in the basin: the rope will be slack and no gear will be at the boulder. Then take a rough stone in along the rope and drop it over the rim.
- **Warning:** You do not have to understand why this holds the column in place. The four dimples are the evidence.

## If you go in

You don't have to. Most of what this place is can be had from the spray margin. If you do go in, understand what the basin is.

- **Under the fall:** The basin is narrower than the column, so there is no calm edge to surface at. You must walk several paces through the fall to reach the rim. Go crouched, in a helmet, with your chin tucked so you breathe in the pocket under your own face.
- **Froth:** The top arm's length of water is too full of air to float you. You will come out by rope, not by swimming.
- **Rope:** Your hands will be useless in less time than you expect. Use the black rope as a handline, but your weight goes on your own line. Tie it in a chest loop before you enter and anchor it to the boulder. A companion outside the column tends it.
- **Walls:** The rim is razor rock. Below it, the walls are ground smooth and slick with algae. You cannot climb them.
- **Stones:** Seven stones, up to the size of a head, are moving on the floor. Keep your feet and fingers off the bottom and never reach between them.
- **Time:** Cold shock will cut your breath-hold to a fraction. Plan single short descents. Agree a count with your tender, after which you are hauled out whether you like it or not.
- **Gear:** If you can get them, bring a wetsuit, neoprene gloves, a hood, and a mask strapped on under the hood. Without a mask, the clear water is only a blur.

What you get for it is a few seconds below the white ceiling. The water is empty and perfectly clear, nets of light slide over the pale walls, and the stones knock slowly, one at a time.

## Kit, in short

- Thick boots and a sole repair.
- Leather gloves and tough trousers.
- A helmet.
- Plugs and muffs.
- Dark glasses and a brim.
- A full dry change of clothes, sealed.
- Wound dressings.
- Your own rope.
- Food you can eat without stopping long.
- A slate or card for anything the signs can't carry.
- One rough stone.

## Leaving

Start down with daylight to spare. The scree is worse on the way down, with tired legs and ringing ears. Sleep in the valley and not on the saddle: there is no soil to anchor a shelter, the water may come through your camp, and you could not sleep in that sound anyway. For a while afterward you will shout without meaning to, and the quiet will feel strange.

Before you go, count what you moved and make sure the basin holds as many stones as when you came.
