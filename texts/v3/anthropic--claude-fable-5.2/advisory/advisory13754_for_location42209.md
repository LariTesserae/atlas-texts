**The short version:** at night this is a physically gentle place. Nothing here bites, stalks, or freezes you. The dangers are the day that follows the night, the total absence of water, the darkness, and the harm you could do to the place, since everything in it has stayed where it fell for thousands of years.

## Timing

- **Arrive just after dusk and be gone before sunrise.** The night is warm because the day is brutal. This is a windless basin below sea level, ringed with dark stone and offering almost no shade.
- **Bring a watch with a vibrating alarm.** There is no moon, the stars are hazed out, and the temperature never changes, so you will lose track of time.
- **Stay at least a few hours.** Your eyes need about forty minutes to adapt, and your ears need longer.

## Water and heat

- **Carry all your water.** There is none here and none nearby. Bring three to four litres for the night, with electrolytes, and the same again in reserve in case you are caught by day.
- **Drink on a schedule.** The air is near skin temperature and very dry, so sweat evaporates before you feel it and you will not notice thirst in time.
- **Protect yourself from the alkali dust.** It dries the lips, eyes, and nose. Bring lip balm, eye drops, and saline spray, and wear glasses rather than contact lenses.

## Seeing

- **Use only a dim red light, pointed at the ground.** White light erases the faint grey of the floor, which is the only real view the place offers.
- **Navigate by the grey.** Once your eyes adapt, bushes are dark patches and the rim is the line where the grey stops.
- **Carry a compass.** There are no stars to steer by, and on the plain above you could walk past the hollow or miss your way back.
- **Approach the rim slowly.** The ledge is a waist-high drop that you will not see until you are on it.

## Getting in and moving around

- **Enter from the east**, where the ledge is only knee-high. The varnished stone is slick, so sit and step down rather than hop.
- **Look before you put your feet down.** The grit under the ledge holds the snail shells, which are paper-thin and the only animal remains in the hollow.
- **Wear soft, flat, closed shoes.** Avoid lugged soles, metal pole tips, or stakes, because there are no tracks on the crust and yours should not be the first.
- **Walk around the domes near the centre.** They held under a pressing hand, but your full weight is a different test, and a broken one stays broken.
- **Find the thorn tree with your light, not your face.** It stands at the lowest point, at head height, and it is very hard to see in the dark.
- **Leave the plants alone.** The saltbush is brittle, and the lichen under the north ledge has taken longer to grow than you would guess.

## The slab on the west side

This is the reason to come, and it needs the most care.

- **Check it with the red light before you sit or put a hand down.** The chert flakes are still sharp enough to cut, and a clean cut in dry warm air bleeds freely.
- **Move nothing.** That means the flakes, the worn-down core, and especially the quartzite cobble. Its position, white end toward the centre, is the last thing someone did here, and once you lift it you cannot put it back the same way.
- **Sit at the clear end of the slab.** People sat here and built nothing, and you should do the same.
- **Touch only the grinding hollow, if you touch anything.** One dry fingertip is enough, and it will feel wet though it is not.

## Leave nothing, take nothing, spill nothing

Nothing here removes what you leave. There is no rain, wind, insect, or scavenger to do it. A crumb, a thread, or a dropped cap will lie where it fell for as long as the flakes have.

- **Count your items in and count them out.** Tether small things.
- **Eat over your pack**, or eat before you come.
- **Do not spill water.** A cupful on the gypsum would be the first water in an age, and it would leave a mark that stays.
- **Relieve yourself before you arrive**, or carry a sealable bottle and bags and take everything out.
- **Light no fire or stove.** You do not need the warmth, the saltbush is resinous and dry, and no ash has lasted here.
- **Take no souvenirs.** That includes pods, shells, and flakes.

## The silence

- **Know the two sounds in advance.** A pod on the thorn tree ticks as it dries, and very rarely a plate of crust settles with a scrape like a fingernail on pottery. Both are startling if you are not expecting them.
- **Expect to hear yourself.** You are the only animal in the hollow, so you will notice your pulse, your breath, and the creak of your clothes.
- **Wear quiet fabric.** Avoid velcro and rustling shells.
- **Agree on silence beforehand** if you bring a companion.
- **Expect a strong reaction, and know you can leave.** The body-temperature warmth and the stillness can feel like sitting beside something asleep. Some people find that comforting and others find it very lonely. If it turns bad, climb out to the plain for a while.

## What to pack

- Water, electrolytes, and crumbless food
- Red headlamp and spare, compass, and watch with a vibrating alarm
- Loose, light, long-sleeved clothing and soft closed shoes
- A thin sit pad
- A cloth for your face in case the dust comes down
- Lip balm, eye drops, and saline spray
- A small kit for cuts and thorns: pressure dressing, tape, tweezers
- Sealable bags for all waste
- Sun cover and the reserve water, in case you are still there at dawn

You do not need a tent, warm layers, insect repellent, or anything for protection against animals.

## Leaving

Go out the way you came, by the east. Check the ground where you sat with the red light and count your things. The hollow should be exactly as you found it.
