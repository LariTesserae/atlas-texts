**The short version:** this is a gentle place with strict manners. The cold, the glare and the crawl in are the only physical risks. The real preparation is learning to be a guest in a room where nothing has changed in living memory, and leaving it that way.

## Getting in

- **Arrive by daylight, from the beach side, visibly.** A town with no streets has no public space. Every passage is effectively someone's hallway, so wait at the edge until someone takes you in.
- **Stay off the roof unless led.** It looks like pasture, but it is old turf over whalebone and driftwood, built for sheep, with smoke holes and one unfenced drop a man's height deep. Only the crow and the sheep seem to belong up there.
- **Expect to stoop or crawl.** The passages are low, dark and smoky. Wear your bag on your chest, cover your head, and reconsider the trip if you have bad knees, a bad back or claustrophobia.
- **Keep lights dim and pointed down.** Bats hibernate in the passage mouths. Waking one in winter can kill it, so don't touch them or shine a light on them.
- **Bring a gift that makes sense there.** These people knap flint, grind grain by hand and roof with driftwood because there are no trees. Good flint, seasoned wood, salt or a fine hide would suit. Offering to gather shell for next year's lime may be better still.
- **Come healthy.** A small, enclosed, smoke-filled community is a bad place to bring a cold.

## What to wear and carry

- **Soft-soled shoes.** Hide shoes are worn because hard soles would cut up the moss, which is ankle-deep and wet. Wear moccasins or similar, with wool socks that stay warm when damp.
- **Warm layers for sitting still.** The cold is cellar cold, windless and patient. Bring a hat, gloves, and a folded hide or pad for the stone bench.
- **Old clothes.** The lime is thick and soft and will come off white on anything that brushes it. Try not to brush it.
- **A small cup.** The basin holds a few handfuls for the whole town, so take a mouthful, not a bottleful. Don't touch the ice or the hole in it. It is condensation off a limed wall under a row of doves, so sip at your own judgment and carry your own water for actual thirst.
- **Something for your eyes.** You will come out of a black passage into a court brighter than open ground. Do what the locals do: stop at the threshold with a hand over your eyes and wait. A hat brim helps.
- **A silent timer.** No alarm will be heard. Set something that vibrates against your skin, for reasons given below.
- **Quiet handwork, if you like.** People come here to work in the good light. Mending or drawing is a legitimate reason to be present, as long as every scrap leaves with you.

## The silence

Your voice will travel about an arm's length and stop. Footsteps, wingbeats and a dropped pot will make almost no sound, and you will not hear the sea two hundred paces away.

- **Agree on hand signals beforehand** with anyone you travel with. You cannot call for help or shout a warning here.
- **Learn the one local signal.** A touch on the shoulder means "trade places with me." You won't hear anyone approach, so expect the touch and don't flinch.
- **Don't test the silence.** Clapping, shouting or dropping things to see what happens is the mark of a tourist. The townspeople treat the silence as ordinary, and you should too.
- **Expect it to feel strange at first.** You may notice your own pulse and breath more than you like, and tinnitus will seem louder. My guess is that the unease passes in a few minutes and what replaces it is why people sit here.

## Conduct in the court

- **Walk on the slightly flatter moss.** Those are the paths. The faint moving ridges are vole runs, so step around them.
- **Take nothing.** That means no fern frond, no sealed snail and no flake of lichen. Don't break the frosted webs.
- **Leave nothing.** That means no crumbs for the wrens or doves, no offerings, and no mark in the lime. The limpet shell behind the fern on the east wall is the only mark anyone has left. Look at it if you find it, but don't touch it or the drop it holds.
- **Leave the antler pegs alone.** They mark where the frost stops, and they are not for hanging coats.
- **Let the crow look at you.** By every account it was there before anyone living.
- **Ask before photographing people**, by gesture, and accept a no. I think the place is better served by sitting than by recording.

## What to do there

Take your cup to the basin at the brightest hour, when the sunlit wall sweats and the drops run down the groove. Then sit on the north bench with your eyes closed and your face lit, as the townspeople do. That is the whole visit.

One caution, which is my own reading. The court gives you no cues that time is passing: there is no sound and no wind, and nothing in it changes. You are also sitting still in freezing air with your eyes shut, and feeling peaceful. Chill will creep up on you unnoticed, and so might the afternoon. That is what the vibrating timer is for. When it goes off, or when someone touches your shoulder, get up and give them your seat.

The best outcome is that the oldest people in town could not tell you had been there.
