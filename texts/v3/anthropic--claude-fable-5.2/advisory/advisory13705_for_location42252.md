# Preparing for the Box

The animals are not the danger here. The bats, roaches, and snake will mostly ignore you. The three things that can hurt you are the heat, the air, and an abandoned construction site in the dark. Prepare for those in that order.

## What it will feel like

The entrance is the worst part. The scaffold stair starts just under the roost, so your first minute is the hottest, most ammonia-laden air in the vault. It is easier at platform level, though never comfortable.

The noise is constant and directionless, and you will not hear a companion shout. Within thirty paces of the floodlights you lose detail, and beyond that you lose everything but shapes. The guano drifts move. Warm drops land on you, some of them groundwater and some not. What wears people down is the combination of heat that won't lift, a mask on the face, noise, and no horizon.

## Timing

- **Stay off the stair and out of the shafts during the dusk exodus and the pre-dawn return.** Find out the local dusk and dawn times before you go.
- **The best first visit is at night.** Watch the forty-minute emergence from the surface, then descend into the emptied vault. It is slightly cooler and far quieter, and you disturb almost nothing. Be out well before dawn.
- **For a daytime visit to see the roost occupied, go on a Thursday.** Speak to the pump crew, since they know where the trackbed drops and which cables are live, and copy what they wear.
- **Keep it short.** Sixty to ninety minutes is plenty.

## Heat

Sweat does not evaporate down there, so your body cannot cool itself and drinking water will not fix that. Only leaving does.

- **Before you go down:** arrive rested, hydrated, and fed, with electrolytes waiting at the surface.
- **Clothing:** wear thin long sleeves and trousers. Do not wear an impermeable coverall, which trades a modest hygiene gain for a real risk of heat collapse.
- **When to leave:** go at the first headache, nausea, cramp, or clumsiness. The way out is a thirty-metre climb through the worst air, so start it while you still have reserves.
- **The climb:** go steadily and do not rest on the top flights.
- **Practice:** climb stairs in your respirator and ear protection somewhere hot beforehand. Finding out you can't tolerate the mask should happen there.

## Air

- **Respirator:** wear a half-face respirator with combined ammonia and P100 cartridges. A dust mask does nothing for ammonia, and a gas cartridge alone does nothing for spores.
- **Guano:** treat it as infectious. In warm, damp, enclosed roosts it commonly carries fungal spores that cause lung infection weeks later. Don't kick or dig the drifts, and don't sit down.
- **Dead ends:** stay out of the blind west heading and don't wander up the east tunnels. Nobody is moving air in there. A rented gas monitor is a reasonable extra.
- **Power failure:** if the power fails, the lights, fan, and pumps all stop together. Leave immediately on your own light.

## The site itself

- **Light:** bring a headlamp and two backups. Keep beams low and off the crown, use red if you have it, and never use flash. A flickering lamp alone makes the roost shriek.
- **Water:** it is opaque, strongly alkaline, shared with rats, and has submersible pumps running in it on old wet wiring. Stay on the cast platform where you can. If you must cross the trough, do it at the shallow west end in knee-high rubber boots, probing ahead with a pole. Keep away from the sump. Cover any cuts with waterproof dressings and rinse splashed skin afterward.
- **Rebar:** the unfinished side is a field of starter bars with brittle caps. A fall there is an impalement, so admire it from across the trough.
- **Electrics:** touch nothing electrical, including the cable trays, the bulb string, and the transformer. It has all been wet for years and gnawed by rats.
- **Pumps:** leave the float switches alone. The pumps are the only reason the Box is a pond and not a lake.
- **Stair:** it is wet, greasy steel. Keep a gloved hand on the rail and go one person per flight.
- **Things to stay off:** don't climb the drill rig, don't poke at the blasted rock face, and don't use the ladder in the vent shaft. It belongs to the snake and the fig.

## Animals

- **Bats:** get a pre-exposure rabies course before the trip and never handle one, even if it is grounded. If you are scratched or bitten, wash the wound for fifteen minutes and get treatment the same day.
- **Protection from above:** wear a hard hat or brimmed hood and eye protection against the constant fall of droppings.
- **Walls:** don't lean on them. The centipedes are fast and can bite.
- **Insects:** tuck trousers into boots and close your cuffs. Keep your bag zipped and on your back, or roaches will travel home in it.
- **Everything else:** the rat snake, toads, and geckos are harmless if left alone.

## Kit

- Respirator with spare cartridges
- Ear defenders
- Three lights
- Hard hat
- Wraparound eye protection with anti-fog
- Work gloves
- Rubber boots
- Thin, disposable-grade long clothing
- Waterproof dressings
- A probe pole
- A dry bag for anything electronic
- A waterproof notebook and pencil, since touchscreens fail with wet hands
- At the surface: water, electrolytes, soap, a change of clothes, and heavy bin bags

Expect lenses and glasses to fog instantly. Let cameras warm to ambient temperature before you open them.

## People

- **Don't go alone.** Agree hand signals at the surface for *stop*, *look*, *I'm not well*, and *out now*.
- **Leave a return time** with someone above. There will be no signal below.

## Coming out

- **Clothes:** strip the outer layer at the shaft head, bag it, and wash it hot.
- **Boots and hard gear:** scrub and disinfect them.
- **Yourself:** shower before you eat.
- **Before your next world:** clean everything, and arrive here with clean gear too. The roost has no defences against fungi from elsewhere, and other worlds have none against this one's.
- **Afterward:** fever, cough, chest pain, or severe muscle aches in the following month need a doctor. Say "bat guano and rat water" in your first sentence.

## Courtesy

The lime straws on the crown are about a finger long and grow a centimetre a year, so the Box has been left alone for most of a decade. In that time it has become something's home. Take nothing, leave nothing, and step around the green halos under the lamps.
