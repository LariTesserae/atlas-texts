# Preparing for the Flow and Its Edge

This place is not very dangerous, but it is hard on feet, ears, and nerves. You will cross a mile of dry, sharp black rock, then step over a line into a wet, loud, stinking, working green. Most of what follows is inference from the description.

## The crossing

- **Use the rail line.** The spur is the only made surface across the clinker. Ride in if anything runs. If not, walk the track bed, and look behind you often, because near the Works you won't hear a train.
- **Wear sacrificial footwear.** The rock ruins a resident's boots in a season. Bring thick-soled boots you don't love, plus spare laces and something to patch a sole.
- **Dress for falling.** Wear leather gloves, tough trousers, and gaiters, and carry a stick. A stumble on glassy clinker means lacerations, so pack real wound dressings and have a current tetanus shot.
- **Carry all your water.** The flow has no water at all. Take two litres each way.
- **Navigate by the plume.** The white vapour shows from miles off. The green trails northeast of the Works, so from the southwest you reach bare rock and survey stakes first.

## The green

- **It drips constantly under a clear sky.** Nothing dries outdoors. Bring a hooded shell, a brimmed hat, wool layers, and rubber boots or a well-greased second pair.
- **Protect anything that fogs or soaks.** Bag paper, optics, and electronics. Things will probably dry indoors, since a place with free steam likely has warm buildings.
- **Stay on the cinder streets.** The soil is a finger deep and took forty years to make, so a boot heel can tear it. The overgrown lots hide rusted stakes.
- **Ask before you drink.** The only standing water is the sumps, which hold frogs and bird-wash. Residents must draw clean condensate somewhere, so find out where, and bring a filter anyway.
- **Be careful around the plant.** Assume any pipe is hot until you see ferns under it. A steam leak is invisible near its source, so don't trail a hand along pipework. Leave the pipe lagging alone, because in a plant of this era it may be asbestos. Moss over the buried mains may be warm, and wellhead lids are.
- **Find Well Six in your first hour.** After that, don't loiter near its stack.

## The noise

- **Bring hearing protection.** Take plugs and muffs. The valve shrieks about 130 times a day, on top of the drone, the hiss, and thousands of seabirds.
- **Expect to feel off.** The drone sits in your teeth and may bring a headache or unease for a day or two. For relief, walk out onto the upwind clinker.
- **Sleep will be poor at first.** The valve blows all night and the frogs call under it. Keep your plugs in.
- **You can't hear danger.** Shouts, footsteps, and carts are all lost. Keep your eyes up, look before crossing the spur, and approach people from the front.
- **You will flinch at the starlings.** Don't judge the valve by ear. The real one presses on your eardrums and puts the birds up in a white sheet.

## The birds

- **Cover your head from April to August.** Nesting terns dive at heads and draw blood. Wear a hat and hold a stick above it, since they strike the highest point.
- **Expect to be hit by droppings.** Wear washable outer layers and eye protection.
- **Keep clean.** Wash your hands before eating, and don't handle dead birds.
- **Brace for the smell.** It is ammonia, wet moss, and hot oil, and you will stop noticing it in a day.

## The people

Sixty people live in a town surveyed for three hundred, so there is likely a spare bunk. It is also a company town where everyone will know you are a stranger.

- **Present yourself to the manager first.** The house with the glazed porch is the one to find.
- **Learn a dozen signs before anything else.** Start with stop, danger, hot, yes, no, where, wait, eat, and thanks. Ask a child to teach you, since children here sign before they read.
- **Use the slate and the flag.** Read the canteen slate daily, and don't write on it uninvited. Dinner is signalled by flag, so learn where it flies.
- **Carry a way to write.** A pocket slate or waterproof notebook with a soft pencil will do.
- **Respect the gap.** The half-minute after Six blows is when the real talking happens, and people save things up for it. Have one sentence ready, stand at arm's length, and don't spend someone's gap on pleasantries. If a resident uses theirs on you, listen.
- **Bring useful gifts.** Boot leather, laces, chalk, and fresh food will mean more than curiosities.

## What not to bring

- **No cat or dog.** The colony exists because this ledge has no cats, and the soil exists because of the colony.
- **No stray seeds or mud.** Clean your boots and pack before arrival. Everything living here arrived by accident, so don't add to it.
- **No disturbance at the windward border.** Leave the survey stakes alone and step over the last moss cushion. Someone measures it every year.

## Season and frame of mind

Summer has long light but the most noise and smell. In winter the birds are gone, but the drip probably freezes onto everything, and the streets will be slick. If you can choose, I would go in early September.

You may find the contrast stranger than the noise. The cinder streets run bright and empty under green taller than the roofs. A fat owl sits in daylight, and seals sound faintly under everything when the wind turns. The whole place is a temporary arrangement around borrowed heat, moving a few paces a decade.
