This chamber is not especially dangerous, but it is hot, dry, sharp-edged, loud in a way earplugs don't fix, and very finely balanced. Everything in it arrives at the rate it leaves, and you are the one thing it hasn't budgeted for. So prepare for two things: looking after your own body, and adding or removing nothing.

## Getting in and out

- **Don't count on the raise.** It is forty metres of vertical shaft with a running fan at its foot and a live cable down it, and the only ladder in the place is a loose length against the east wall. If your means of travel can set you down inside the chamber, use that, and be sure it can take you out again.
- **Pick your arrival spot.** Aim for bare floor near the middle of the room, away from the lamp, the fan, and the walls.
- **Keep the party small.** Go alone or with one companion. Every body adds heat, breath, and moisture.

## Your body

- **Drink on a schedule.** The heat is bearable, but sweat leaves your skin before you feel it, so you will dry out without feeling thirsty. Drink by the clock.
- **Spill nothing.** A splash of water will soften salt that has stayed sharp since it was cut. Use a bottle with a valve, and carry all waste out, including urine.
- **Eat beforehand.** Crumbs are a large event in a room whose only soil is a knee-high cone of moth wings.
- **Protect eyes, nose, and lips.** Bring lip balm and eye drops. Wear glasses rather than contact lenses, and a light mask against the salt dust. Cover any cuts before you go.
- **Expect the note in your bones.** It comes through the rock and your boot soles as well as the air. Keep your teeth apart, since a clenched jaw will buzz into a headache. Hours of it may bring fatigue or queasiness, so if you get migraines or vertigo, plan a short visit.
- **Bring ear protection for the lizards.** Their bark is a sharp crack, and one sits in every drill groove. Stay away from the walls and it will pass around you rather than through you.

## What to wear and carry

- **Clothing:** light long sleeves and trousers, cut-resistant gloves, and thick-soled boots with ankle support. The rubble has never settled and may shift.
- **Helmet:** bring your own. The one on the rubble block is full of cricket skins and should stay that way.
- **Trouser cuffs:** tuck them in. The sun-spiders are fast and bold, though if they are like ours they are not venomous.
- **Metal:** tape or remove anything that rattles, such as zipper pulls, keys, and jewellery. It will all buzz, and a hard click near the walls could sound like a bark out of turn.
- **Light:** a dim red lamp, pointed at the floor. A bright white beam would pull moths out of their ball, and they would fall somewhere other than the cone. Give your eyes twenty minutes to adjust instead. Take no flash photographs.
- **Timekeeping:** a watch you can read by sight, because you won't hear or feel an alarm.
- **Silence:** switch off every device that makes a tone, and don't hum, sing, or whistle. The crickets hold their pitch a hair under the fan's, and a third note would not help them.
- **Your gear:** keep it on your body. Something gnawed the insulation off the blasting wire in neat rings and would likely do the same to straps and cables.

## Moving through the room

- **Walk the bare lanes.** The dust ribs are where the crickets live, so step over them. They are a pace apart, and a normal stride will do it.
- **Rest astride a rib.** Stand with a foot on bare floor either side and your head over the still line. It is almost quiet there.
- **Watch your feet in the lanes.** The sun-spiders hunt there because nothing can feel them coming, and that includes you.
- **Use hand signals.** Speech sits close to the fan's pitch and gets swallowed.

## Things to leave alone

- **The fan.** It supplies the note, the moths, the seeds, the damp, and your air. Don't touch the housing, the cable, or the grille, and don't stand in its airflow. The beetles pack the bearings with moth wax and the bearings run cool, so don't try to help.
- **The lamp, the cone, and the sprouts.** Stay well back, and don't stand between the duct and the lamp. Don't breathe on the lichen either. Its edge sits where the damp runs out, and your breath is damp.
- **The walls.** The edges will open your hand, and each groove holds a lizard.
- **The ladder, the chock, the wire, and the hat.** Don't move or take any of them, and don't take a salt crystal or a cricket skin.
- **The 7-E arrow.** It points at solid rock. Don't tap, pry, or dig there.
- **The base of the cone.** Something carries it away as fast as it builds. Don't go looking for what does it.
- **The paint drip.** It looks wet and you will want to touch it, but it is dry.

## Time

The room looks as if the crew stepped out this morning, but nobody is coming back. The warm dark and a throb paced like a sleeper's breath will make you drowsy and make an hour feel like ten minutes. Decide how long you will stay before you arrive, and keep to it.

When you arrive, count the throbs in one full circuit of the lizards' bark. That count is your baseline, so check it now and then. If it changes, you are the likeliest cause. Stop what you are doing and move away.

If the fan's note itself wavers, leave at once. It is your air supply as well as theirs.

## Afterwards

- **Check for passengers.** Before you go, look over your clothes and pack for crickets, beetles, and seeds.
- **Rinse and rehydrate.** Wash the salt off your skin and gear, or it will corrode zippers and electronics.
- **Expect the hum to linger.** For a day or so you may feel it in your jaw and feet, and ordinary silence will seem thin.
