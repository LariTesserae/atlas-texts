# Preparing for the Standing Lake

You are preparing for a dive inside a sound, near the top of a volcano that is still building itself. Three things will hurt you if you're careless: the noise, the heat, and the edges. The open side is the one that can kill you. The stones are the only things in the place that deserve your trust, and they also call for manners.

## Getting there and getting in

- **The approach:** The mountain is new, so you'll be climbing loose, sharp scoria in rain and cloud, with gas venting around you. Bring boots you don't mind ruining, gloves, a helmet, and a respirator.
- **Staging:** Leave dry clothes and something hot to drink in a weighted bag on the intact side of the rim, well back from the water. You'll come out blood-warm and soaked into cold mountain wind, and that's when an ordinary person gets into trouble.
- **Entry:** Go in over the intact rim, never through the standing face on the broken side. The lake's edge is a wall you can walk through. You'll feel a cool skin, then warmth, then the roar closing over you.

## Breathing

It is water, however strange, and you can't breathe it.

- **Use a rebreather if you can:** Bubbles here are small silences, and I wouldn't want to shed silence into a lake made of noise. If you only have open-circuit gear, keep the visit short and stay away from the cairns when you exhale.
- **Don't breathe the silences:** They are dry, and some are big enough to crouch in, but they come from the vent and may be full of volcanic gas. Keep your mouthpiece in.
- **Never hold your breath:** A silence can slide over you without warning, and you'll go from water to air in an instant. Breathe steadily throughout, as on any dive.

## The noise

- **Dose:** Earplugs under a hood help less than you'd hope, because the sound comes in through your jaw, breastbone, and eyes. Your vision may shimmer. Give yourself a time limit and keep it.
- **Practise:** Spend time beforehand in the loudest water you can find. The first minute is the worst. After that the roar stops registering, and that's when you get careless.
- **Signals:** Voices and alarms are useless, and sound has no direction anyway. Use a lit wrist display, hand signals, and a short cut-resistant line to your companion.
- **Bring no sound of your own:** Leave behind anything that makes a tone or cancels one. Where the noise thins, the lake thins. You don't want to learn what a noise-cancelling device does here.

## The heat

Blood-warm water gives your body nowhere to shed heat.

- **Suit:** Wear a thin one, for protection against cuts rather than for warmth.
- **Water:** Drink plenty before you go in.
- **Cooling off:** Rest at the edges of the bowl, where the mountain air cools the water.
- **Hot threads:** They will sting like wires. Back out the way you came rather than pushing through.
- **Red seams:** They give more heat than light, so you'll feel one before you see it. Trust your skin over your eyes.

## The floor

Everything is new glass.

- **Protection:** Wear cut-proof gloves, hard-soled boots, and knee and elbow guards.
- **Handholds:** Don't steady yourself on anything black and fresh. It will cut you, and it won't be there in a minute.
- **Movement:** Stay low and slightly heavy. Plan to crawl and walk rather than swim, because the currents have no pattern and the floor heaves like a chest. Let it lift you instead of fighting it.

## The open side

Bubbles here don't rise. They move away from the vent, and you are carrying bubbles in your lungs and your gear. Expect a faint, steady push outward from the centre.

- **On the intact sides:** The push presses you against the bowl.
- **On the broken side:** The push carries you toward the standing face, which shivers in and out by an arm's length. Beyond it is air and a long fall of scoria into cloud.

So keep the vent between you and the open side, or stay well off to the flanks. Treat the pale band of leaked daylight as a boundary and stay out of it. Don't swim up into the column above the rim either, because it has no sides at all.

## Finding your way

The terrain is no use as a map. These are:

- **Bubbles:** They always travel away from the vent.
- **Cairn fuzz:** It grows only on the side facing the wall, so every cairn points you there.
- **The pale band:** It marks the open side.
- **The frond:** It shows which way the noise is moving at that moment. Check it before you cross open floor.

## Silences

One will pass over you sooner or later. You'll suddenly be heavy, dripping, and in dead quiet on pale grit. Crouch, keep breathing, and let it go. Don't follow it and don't try to stay inside it, because every silence eventually leaves through the wall of the lake and falls off the mountain.

## Echoes

Echoes arrive before the events that cause them. A crack means a pillow is about to split, though you won't know where, so get clear of anything bulging.

If the dimness deepens and you hear something large passing before you see it, get into the lee of a cairn and keep still. The pale creature already shelters that way, and it has survived here longer than you have. Leave it alone, along with any silence sitting beside it.

## The stones

The five cairns and the wall are the only things here that are smooth, old, and steady. Somebody carried them up from a river that isn't on this mountain.

- Shelter beside them, but don't lean on them or restack them.
- Leave the two upside-down cairns as they are.
- Take nothing, including the frond.
- Put nothing in the gap in the wall. It isn't yours to fill.

My guess is that these stones are what the lake stands on, more than the bowl is.

## The falter

If you're lucky, the roar will catch once while you're there. The water pulls back a hand's breadth from everything, the cairn tops stand dripping in air, and for half a second you can hear the one low note under all the rest. Be near a cairn and be still. Don't hum along. The water returns immediately.

## Leaving

The light never changes, so go by your clock, not your instinct. Leave over the intact rim while you still feel fine, and get dry and warm at once. The world will sound thin for a day or two, and your ears will ring.
