# Preparing for the sounding court

Nothing here will hunt you, trick you, or notice you. The dangers are a sound that never stops, light that comes from every direction, air that dries you without your feeling it, a drop with no warning, and the damage you could do to the place.

## Ears

This is the main preparation. If speech at arm's length is lost, the rim is as loud as heavy machinery, and unprotected ears have minutes there, not hours. The complete absence of animals, even flies, is a warning.

- **Wear double protection.** Use fitted earplugs with over-ear defenders on top. Put them in when you first feel the chord in your teeth, about a hundred paces out.
- **Expect the bass to get through anyway.** The great jars sound in your chest, and plugs do nothing about that. Some people feel pressure behind the breastbone, queasiness, or unease with no cause. If that grows, walk away until it fades.
- **Take breaks.** Every half hour or so, withdraw until the chord is only a hum. The place will never tell you when you have had enough.
- **Consider not going** if you have tinnitus, migraines, or balance trouble. Do not bring an animal.

## Eyes and skin

There are almost no shadows, which means there is no shade. The pan and the limed walls throw the light upward as well as down.

- Bring wraparound dark glasses or glacier goggles with side shields. They also keep blown salt out of your eyes, and contact lenses will dry out in this wind.
- Cover yourself where sunlight never normally reaches: under the chin, the nostrils, the undersides of your ears.
- Bring lip balm and something greasy for your hands. Salt and dry wind crack skin within a day, and cracked skin stings in salt.

## Water, wind, cold

The wind is cool, so you will not feel yourself sweating, but it dries you all the same. There is no water anywhere, since the only moisture is a pre-dawn dampness that supports thirty fist-sized plants.

- Carry all your water, at least four litres a day, and drink on a schedule.
- Wear a windproof shell over warm layers. A steady wind chills you slowly, and the pan will be much colder after dark.
- If you stay overnight, camp well off to one side, never upwind of the court. Pitch low, nose to the wind, and sleep in earplugs.

## Crossing the pan

- Wear sturdy boots, because salt crust is abrasive and can lie thin over brine mud. Rinse or brush everything afterward, since salt eats zips, buckles, and electronics.
- There are no landmarks. Mark your arrival point with something tall and dark, and note the wind's bearing when you set out. The wind never changes direction, so it is the one reliable compass.
- **Watch for the edge.** The pan stops at a straight line with no parapet and no shadow to show the drop, and the description does not say how far down it goes. Let the sound tell you that you are close and slow down when it does. Do not approach for the first time at dusk.

## At the rim

- **Do not go down.** There is no floor. The top tier is fired clay worn thin on its windward lips, over an unknown number of tiers. If you fall you will be cut and may not get out, and every jar you break is a note removed for good.
- **Do your long looking from the leeward rim**, facing into the wind. There the wind pushes you back from the edge, and anything you fumble blows away from the court. On the windward rim the wind is at your back and anything you drop goes in.
- **Mind your wind shadow.** I suspect a body standing on the windward lip casts a lee, and that you would hear the jars below you falter. Crouch or stand back on that side.
- **Tether everything**, including hat, glasses, gloves, and lens caps. A glove lying across three mouths silences them, and you cannot retrieve it. Leave notebooks in the bag unless they are clipped down.
- **Walk the whole circuit.** The chord only changes when you move, with more bass at the north corner and a reed-tone along the east wall.

## Talking and keeping time

- Agree on hand signals beforehand. You need at least "look," "back off," "I'm not well," and "leaving now." A slate and chalk help.
- Do not rely on audible alarms. Use a watch that vibrates.
- If you are roped or travelling in a pair, keep each other in sight. A shout will not carry.

## What it does to your head

- For the first few minutes the sound feels like an assault. Then it stops registering as sound and becomes more like weather. This is when people take their ear protection off, and you should keep yours on.
- With nothing changing in the sound or the light, your sense of time will slip. Decide before you arrive when you will leave, and keep to the watch.
- A mind given a steady roar will start to supply voices, tunes, or your name. This is ordinary pattern-hunting, and it comes from you.
- You will probably hum. Finding a note that locks with a jar until you cannot tell which of you is sounding it is harmless.
- Do not expect a climax or a revelation. The place is not building toward anything, so your visit will have no natural ending.

## Take nothing, add nothing

- Do not take a shard or a thumb-pot. Each is either a voice or a wedge holding others in place.
- Do not put anything in a jar, whether a coin, a flower, or a note. The jars have never held anything but wind. An offering would be the first foreign object in them and would turn a voice into a container.
- Do not touch the lichens or the cushion plants. They are in exact balance with the wind and will not recover from a thumb.
- Leave no crumbs, peel, or spilled water. Relieve yourself far out on the pan, downwind of the court. A place with no droppings and no husks has no means of absorbing yours.
- Your footprints at the rim will be the only tracks there, so keep them few.

## Leaving

The chord will follow you a long way across the pan. After it finally drops below hearing, your ears will keep producing it for hours, perhaps for a day or two. Ordinary quiet will feel thin for a while, and the first voice you hear will seem oddly full of starts and stops.

Check that you still have everything you tethered, rinse the salt off your gear, and drink more water than you want.
