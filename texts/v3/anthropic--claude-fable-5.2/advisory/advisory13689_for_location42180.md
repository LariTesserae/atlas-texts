# Preparing for the fig-tree chamber

Nothing in this room is hunting you. The dangers are quieter: the heat, the air, the climb, and the dust. The other half of the picture is that you are the biggest risk to the place itself. You will be the loudest, driest, sharpest, and brightest thing to enter it in a very long time, so prepare to be soft, slow, dim, and brief.

## The country above

- **Water:** Carry all of it, at least four or five litres per person for the day, plus electrolytes. The scrub has none, and the basins below belong to the frogs and crabs. They are also laced with sulphur and guano.
- **Sun and ground:** Bring a hat and sturdy boots for the walk in. The surface limestone is as sharp as the room below is smooth.
- **The roof:** Near the fig you are walking on the roof of a dome that has already fallen in once. Approach on foot, lightly, and camp well back.
- **Dry clothes:** Leave a set at the surface with a warm layer. You will come out soaked, and dry night air will chill you fast.

## Timing

The room runs on a schedule, and you should fit inside it.

1. **Dawn:** Stay out. The bats pour in through the root-throat, the only door, and for an hour droppings rain down. Watch from a distance.
2. **Mid-morning:** Go down. The snake is looped and waiting for evening, and the air is at its coolest.
3. **Noon:** This hour is the reason to come. It is the only time you will see the ferns, the gold gnats, the geckos following the edge of the light, and the red hands properly.
4. **Early afternoon:** Start up while you still have strength. The heat thickens, and the climb is the hardest thing you will do all day.
5. **Dusk:** Be out and away from the hole. Thousands of bats will funnel through the roots while a snake strikes among them.

The night, with its warm soundless rain and the blue lights brightening, is the heart of the place. I would not stay for it on a first visit, because it means twenty hours of wet heat and doubtful air with the only exit closed at both ends by bats. If you do stay, sit up on the flank of the mound, well above the fissure, and doze rather than sleep.

## The way down

The people who polished those handholds climbed all their lives, and their children did too. You have not, so bring help.

- **Rope and ascenders:** Bring a rope at least twice the height of the tree, a harness, and mechanical ascenders or a light cable ladder. Coming up a wet root hand over hand after hours in the heat is how an ordinary person falls.
- **Anchor:** Tie off to the fig's trunk with a wide padded sling, not to the limestone lip. The tree is the room's lid and its light filter, so don't cut or break anything.
- **Line placement:** Hang the rope beside the thick root, not on it. The polished patches are an artefact, and the snails are grazing there.
- **Pack:** The hole is the width of a well, so lower your pack separately and keep it small.
- **The snake:** You will pass within arm's reach of it. It is almost certainly a bat-eater and not interested in you, but look before you place each hand, move slowly, and give it the side of the throat it has chosen.
- **Company:** Bring one companion and no more. Make sure each of you can get out alone.

## The air and the heat

The boiled-egg smell is hydrogen sulphide. Frogs and crabs live happily on the floor, which suggests the air is usually fine. But this is a bell with one hole at the top, and heavy gases pool low.

- **Monitor:** Clip a small gas monitor (hydrogen sulphide, carbon dioxide, oxygen) to your collar if you can get one.
- **The smell:** At dangerous concentrations hydrogen sulphide deadens the nose. If the smell suddenly vanishes while you are near the fissure, that is the warning. Move up the mound.
- **The fissure:** Don't crouch or lie by it, and don't put your face to it. The steam can scald, and the yellow crust is sulphur and is not for touching.
- **Symptoms:** Headache, dizziness, or nausea means go up and out, immediately and without argument.

The heat is the slower danger. The air is saturated, so sweat won't cool you. Drink steadily, move at the room's pace, and treat the climb out as the main event to save yourself for. It gets cooler as you ascend, which helps.

## The dust and the animals

- **Guano:** Warm, wet bat guano with fungus growing on it is where lung infections like histoplasmosis come from. Wear a well-fitted particulate respirator (N95 or better), especially near the walls. Add eye protection and a brimmed hat. Don't kick up the floor, and cover any cuts.
- **Bats:** Never handle one, even a fallen one. The beetles will see to it.
- **Whip-spiders:** They look dreadful and are harmless.
- **Millipedes:** Some can leave irritants on your skin, so keep your hands away from your eyes.
- **Being walked on:** Every surface here is occupied, and once you sit still you become a surface. Crickets will read you with their antennae. If you can't bear that, this is not your destination.

## Clothing and gear

- **Clothes:** Wear thin long sleeves and trousers that you are willing to throw away afterwards.
- **Footwear:** Soft, grippy soles. The flowstone is slick with algae.
- **Nothing sharp:** Leave behind pole tips, studded boots, and anything that scrapes.
- **Clean gear:** Scrub and disinfect everything before you come, boots above all. You could carry in a fungus that kills frogs or bats, and you are arriving from another world entirely.
- **Skin:** Wear no repellent, sunscreen, or scent. Frogs breathe through their skin, and everything here drinks the drip.
- **Tethers:** Tie everything to you. A dropped object lands without a sound, so you won't know it has gone.
- **Electronics and metal:** Keep electronics in dry bags with desiccant. Lenses and glasses will fog. Leave silver at home, because the sulphur blackens it.

## Light and sound

Use a headlamp with a dim red setting, pointed at your feet. Never shine it at the ceiling, since a disturbed roost of thousands is a disaster for the bats and for you. Keep it off the fissure too, or the glow-worms will go dark. Don't use a flash.

Give your eyes half an hour to adjust. The brown-green dusk holds more than you would expect, and the frogs' white feet are meant to be seen in exactly that light.

Sound dies in the moss, and your companion's voice will not carry as you expect. Agree on hand signals beforehand. After a while you will stop wanting to speak anyway.

## Conduct

- **Where to sit:** Choose the bare flowstone or the algae zone, and stay off the fern crown and the deep moss. A footprint in that moss may last for years.
- **The hearth:** It is a frog nursery now. Light no fire, anywhere.
- **The stack:** It holds gecko eggs. Don't touch it and don't add a stone.
- **The hands:** Don't touch them, and don't breathe close to them. You will want to lay your palm over one, especially one from the lower row. Hold your hand in the air in front of it instead, and add nothing of your own.
- **Waste and food:** Carry out everything, including bodily waste. Eat on the surface.

## What it will feel like

Your body will notice the wrongness before you do. The warmth increases as you descend, the back wall feels like an animal's flank, and the fissure breathes. The floor moves slightly at the edge of vision. The silence is that of a full room, and some people find it unnerving. Most of the unease passes if you sit still for twenty minutes and let the room carry on around you, and it will, because nothing here needs you.

## Afterwards

- **Stowaways:** Before you climb, check your pack, boots, and collar. Something will have moved in.
- **Decontamination:** Clean your gear again before you take it anywhere else.
- **Recovery:** Rehydrate, and expect to smell of sulphur for days.
- **Illness:** If you develop a fever or cough in the next three weeks, tell a doctor you were in a bat cave.
- **Discretion:** Tell very few people where the green fig is.
