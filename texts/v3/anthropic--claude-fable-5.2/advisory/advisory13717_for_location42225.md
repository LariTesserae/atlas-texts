Expect a quiet, warm, damp walk through permanent dusk, across ground that cuts and with light that misleads you. Nothing here hunts you and nobody will bother you. The real risks are falling on sharp rock, running out of water between halos, and trusting your eyes or a lamp the way you would elsewhere. This is how I would prepare.

## Before you go down

- **Start from the old sea cliff.** It is the only place where you can see the whole city at once. Sketch the buildings, their green rings, the place where two halos have merged, and how many rows lie between you and the sea.
- **Read the roads as a pattern.** They follow the lines where the pull of two buildings balances, so they never lead to a building. They pass between buildings and meet at points where three pulls balance.
- **Let your eyes adapt.** Spend half an hour on the way down without looking at anything bright. Pack sunglasses for the walk out, because ordinary daylight will hurt afterward.
- **Clean your boots and gear.** Remove seeds and mud before arriving. The halos are a young ecosystem with few species, and you come from elsewhere.
- **Keep the first visit short.** I would base myself on the cliff and make day trips. Days of brown twilight will flatten your mood and your sense of time.

## Light

- **Lamps.** A lamp will not light a circle. Its light streams toward the nearest building, so to read a map, hold the page on the building side of the lamp, along the line of your shadow.
- **Looking around.** My guess is that you will see best when facing away from the nearest building and worst when facing it, since light is drifting past you toward the wall.
- **Reading the ground.** Your brain judges bumps and hollows by assuming light comes from above. Here shadows lie sideways, or doubled and faint, so pits can look like mounds. Carry a staff with a rubber tip and probe before you step.
- **Building edges.** Don't stare at them. The sliding light strains the eyes and may make you queasy.
- **Sight lines.** Bent light means a distant figure or doorway may not be exactly where it appears.
- **Equipment.** Solar chargers, signal mirrors and photography will disappoint, so bring a pencil and paper. I would not count on radio either, since radio is a kind of light. Carry a whistle for emergencies only, because the place is very quiet.

## Finding your way

- **On the road.** Keep your two shadows equally faint.
- **At a junction.** You should cast three shadows. Choose your branch by which pair you keep matched.
- **If you stray.** One shadow darkens. Walk directly away from it until the second shadow catches up, and you will be back on a road.
- **Your shadow as a compass.** It always points to the nearest building. Follow it to reach a wall, and walk against it to leave.
- **Deep in a halo.** Where it is too dark for shadows, use the plants. Every leaf leans outward toward the open plain.
- **In the fern tunnel.** Navigate by your feet. The road is smooth and the rest is moss.
- **Large bearings.** The cliff is inland. The sea is the other way, marked by the sound of surf and, at night, by the red line under the water.
- **At night.** There are no shadows, so I would not walk. If you must, smeared stars show where the roofs are and clean stars mean open ground.

## The ground

- **Footwear.** Wear boots with thick soles and grippy rubber. The clinker shreds footwear, warm rock softens glue, and the polished roads are permanently wet and slick. Bring tape or cord for sole repairs. Avoid metal spikes and bare pole tips, which skate on basalt and would scar the roads.
- **Clothing.** Wear leather gloves, long sleeves and long trousers. A fall on this rock is like falling on broken glass.
- **Blisters and hollow rock.** Stay off blister roofs and anything that sounds hollow when tapped. In the halos, deep moss hides the same holes and edges.
- **First aid.** Pack plenty of wound-care supplies: irrigation, antiseptic, dressings, tape, and tweezers for glass splinters. Cuts infect quickly in warm, wet air.
- **Heat and gas.** Test warm ground with your hand before sitting. The geckos mark the comfortably warm cracks. Don't rest in hollows, where volcanic gas can pool. If you get a headache or feel light-headed in a low spot, climb out.

## Water, food and sleep

- **Water.** Carry three to four litres a day. The air is humid and you will sweat, but the open plain is dry. The pools lie at the foot of the walls, through the darkest and most hazardous ground, in someone's halo. It is better to collect the sheet-flow at a halo's outer edge before dawn, or drips from leaves in the green band. Filter it. The tea colour is only plant stain.
- **Food.** Bring all of it. Don't forage, because aroids are usually irritant when raw and the green is not yours. A small stove works, and a warm crack will heat a tin or dry your socks.
- **Sleep.** If you sleep on the plain, bring a closed-cell foam mat, since inflatable ones puncture, and a bivy or tarp weighted with clinker, since stakes won't go in. Choose ropy rock on open ground, check that it is warm but not hot, and stay more than a hundred metres from any wall, beyond the reach of the pre-dawn water.

## Midday, rain and the seaward edge

- **Midday.** The note in your sternum will probably arrive as unease, pressure or mild nausea. That is your body responding to the sound, not a premonition. Sit down halfway between buildings, eat, and don't make decisions until it passes. It also tells you when noon is.
- **Rain.** Stop and wait. Steam cuts what little visibility there is, and the hottest patches can scald. The bent columns are only a trick of the light.
- **The red line.** Go at night to see it, but watch from the last established road. Stay upwind of the steam, which is acidic and gritty where hot rock meets seawater, and keep back from the surf edge, since new rock built into the sea can break away.
- **The newest shelf.** Stay strictly on the roads. If your shadow already leans toward an empty place, then whatever will stand there has, in the way that matters, already begun. Don't cut across the empty places, and don't camp in them.

## Manners

- **Halos.** Treat each one as a front garden. Don't enter without need, and step on rock rather than moss, which is slowly making the only soil here.
- **Residents.** They walk slowly, carry no light and keep indoors. I would do the same: move slowly, speak quietly, and never aim a lamp at a person or a door. If you see someone cross a road far off, let them go. An opening door is not an invitation unless someone makes it one.
- **Animals.** Leave the frogs their pools and the heron its hour.
- **Traces.** Leave nothing behind.
