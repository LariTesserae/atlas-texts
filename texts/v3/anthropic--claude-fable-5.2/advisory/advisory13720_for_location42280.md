# Preparing for the sealed lava tube

This place is sealed, hot, dark, sharp, and very slow. Nothing in it will come after you. The dangers are the ones you carry in: your need for air, water, and cool, and your weight on things that grow a fingernail's width in a decade.

## Before you go

- **Secure your way out.** The lava plug, the collapse, and the dead hatch mean your only exit is the way you arrived. Your return must not depend on anything in the chamber. Set a hard time limit of 60 to 90 minutes.
- **Clean everything.** Every living thing down there arrived by accident on someone's boots or crates, and you are the next someone. Scrub your soles, wash your clothes, and empty your pockets and cuffs of seeds, crumbs, and insects. A single pair of gnats would be the largest ecological event in the chamber's history.
- **Check your health.** Have a current tetanus shot, since the place is full of rusty steel and sharp glass. If you have a heart condition or handle heat badly, don't go.
- **Decide about the lamps.** You will stand over eight dead gardens knowing the generator has power to spare, and you will want to bring bulbs. Settle this at home. My intuition says go as a witness: the wiring is old and live in wet air, and the place has not asked.

## What to wear

- **Boots:** thick, heat-tolerant soles and ankle support. The floor is warm enough to feel through footwear, and soft foam will suffer.
- **Gloves:** leather or cut-resistant. The basalt is fresh volcanic glass with no weathering, and if you fall you will catch yourself on your hands.
- **Clothing:** long, tough trousers with knee pads, and a light, breathable long-sleeved top. You are trading cut protection against heat load, so keep the layers thin.
- **Helmet:** the collapse is recent, and the ceiling is hung with thousands of brittle stone fingers.

## What to carry

- **Gas monitor:** one that reads oxygen, carbon dioxide, and hydrogen sulfide, plus a small escape respirator. This is the most important item. A sealed volcanic chamber with a sulfur smell is a textbook bad-air space. Healthy ferns prove nothing, since plants are happy in air that would put you on the floor. Hydrogen sulfide deadens the sense of smell as it gets stronger, so if the sulfur smell fades, don't take that as good news.
- **Lights:** three independent sources, at least one with a dim or red mode.
- **Water:** two to three liters with electrolytes, and a cloth to wet. There is nothing to drink there. The one basin is the width of a dinner plate, and it belongs to the garden.
- **First aid:** wound closures, pressure dressings, and burn dressings.
- **Sitting pad:** something insulating, because the bare rock is hot.
- **Waste bags:** everything leaves with you, including anything your body produces.
- **Optional:** an infrared thermometer, a pencil and notebook, and a camera on a small tripod with no flash. Bring cold optics in a sealed bag and let them warm, or they will fog instantly.
- **Leave behind:** flame, stoves, smoking materials, food you might crumble, and anything alive.

## Arrival

You will arrive on bare rock, probably in the dark, and you will feel the heat before you see anything.

1. Stand still, since the floor is pleated, knife-edged ridges.
2. Turn on your light and read the monitor. If it alarms, leave at once.
3. Find the three amber circles. The tube is straight and only seventy paces long, so they are visible from anywhere. If every light you own fails, you can still walk slowly toward an amber lamp.
4. Find the stencilled numbers on the trestles. They are your address system.

## Moving around

- **Walk slowly.** Place each foot deliberately and use the grating walkway where it exists. Test it first, since it has spent decades in sulfurous damp.
- **Keep your hands off the rock.** Never steady yourself on it with a bare hand.
- **Leave the ceiling alone.** Don't throw stones at it to test the height.
- **Avoid the rubble.** The collapse at the end is unstable, so stay off it.
- **Skip the ladder climb.** The hatch admits nothing, and the rungs are wet and rusty. The foot of the ladder is still useful, though. Its cold steel is the one cool thing in the chamber, and the condensation on it is the only clean water. A wet cloth there and a few minutes' rest will help.

## The machinery

- **Pipes:** one will burn you and one won't. Find out which with the thermometer or a hovering hand, not your grip.
- **Lagging:** it is old and split, and may contain fibers you shouldn't breathe, so don't disturb it.
- **Wellhead:** the handwheels hold back whatever is in the well. Don't turn anything.
- **Instrument hut:** you can look in, but the gauges will tell you nothing useful.
- **Cable:** it is live and old, in wet air, so don't handle it.
- **Chain hoist:** don't stand under it or load it.
- **Generator:** you may briefly lay a palm on the casing to feel the hum, after checking its temperature. That hum is the heartbeat of everything alive here.

## The gardens

Stay outside the chalk-sharp line where growth stops. A fist-deep moss cushion took decades to build, and a bootprint in it will outlast your memory of the trip.

- **How to look:** sit on your pad on the bare rock at the rim of lamp 4's circle, turn your own light off, and watch the ferns under the amber light.
- **The pipe drips:** one falls about once per long breath, silently, into moss.
- **The basin under lamp 9:** don't touch it, rinse in it, or sweat into it.
- **The dead gardens:** the brown felt circles under the dead lamps deserve the same care. Walk around them.

## Lamp 7

Visit this one last, and plan your route so you never go from it to another garden.

Something here is green in total darkness and is following the cable toward both the generator and lamp 4. It may be what survives when the last bulb goes, or it may be something else. Either way, it is crossing three paces in years. Moss carried on your boots would complete that journey in one afternoon, so don't let it. Don't step in it, sample it, or hold a bright beam on it. Look with dim light from the edge.

Before you leave, brush and bag your boot soles so that none of it goes home with you either.

## Your body

- **Heat:** the air is saturated, so sweat won't cool you. You will heat up steadily without noticing.
- **Routine:** drink on a schedule, rest at the ladder, and keep to your time limit.
- **When to leave:** headache, nausea, clumsiness, or sudden irritability all mean go now. Clumsiness is the dangerous one on this floor.

## What it will be like

You will be the fastest and loudest thing in the chamber by several orders of magnitude. Your breathing will sound enormous. Now and then a trestle will tick, and the dark will swallow the sound.

There is no day here, only lamps that have been on for decades and a slow succession of circles. Most people are either unsettled by this or deeply calmed, sometimes both within ten minutes.

When your alarm goes, leave the lamps burning, take everything you brought, and leave nothing behind.
