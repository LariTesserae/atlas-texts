At first count, nothing lives here that could be startled.

The green lives here, and it is the only tenant that shows. It is an old company of ferns, horsetails, clubmoss, and liverwort, lineages that settled the business of living before anything existed to help them or eat them. They have taken to the chamber as if no time had passed. They keep its hours: forty minutes of drinking the air, three of being beaten flat, one of wind. Then the fern stands let their brown smoke go, and that smoke is the only traffic the street has ever had. The creepers are the only ones following directions. They are walking the route lines toward the lit addresses at the rate of a finger a week, and they will be the first to arrive.

Under the green are the felt-makers, the fungus threading the dropped fronds and the smaller things working beside it. Nobody set them here. They came in on the root-balls and stayed.

In the east gallery there is something older than any of that. The orange thread below each new seep is not only rust. Something lived in the hairline joints of this basalt, in the dark water, eating iron slowly, long before the rock was parted. When a new face cracks open, it is there first, weeks ahead of the moss. These are the natives. To them the chamber is weather, a sudden catastrophe of air, and they are doing well in it.

None of these lit the addresses.

The one that did has no body in the room, and has to be described by its conduct. It lives in the holding. The hum is not machinery left running. It is effort, and it differs from stack to stack because each stack is held separately, the way several things are kept in mind at once, each with its own grip. The throb between two buildings is the place where two of its attentions overlap.

It has a temperament. It works in planes and likes things level, square, and whole. When it wants a room it does not gnaw. It decides where the face should be, and the rock agrees all at once. It keeps its offcuts in a row. It lifts the water as one rope.

It can take hold of anything large and continuous. It evidently cannot hold the small and many. It could not carry a grain of pollen to a flower, so it planted nothing that would ask, and it could not have kept a gnat, so there are none. It chose a garden that would mind itself and then left it alone. It has not cleared a dead frond or scrubbed an orange stain. That is either tact or incapacity, and for a thing like this they may be the same.

The town shows what it knows, and from where. It knows a house as a footprint, a level outline of a certain size carrying a certain number of floors. It knows a street as a line that gets walked. It knows an address as the place where the walking turns off and stops. It knows that homes are warm from underneath, that the weight is in them at night, and that rain arrives from above and wants to be led away in channels.

This is what the ground knows about a town. It has never been in a room. It felt walls only as more weight, so it made none. It got the standing height right, and the light of the hours when people are in, and the warmth, and missed the first thing about them, which is that they like to go behind something.

So this is a thing of the rock that spent a long time underneath a town, or underneath several, and has now made one of its own out of what came down to it: load, outline, footfall, heat. It built the town exactly, and it is still building.

It has opened one shaft to the surface. Perhaps it wants to look, or perhaps it wants to be found. The description cannot settle which, and it may not have settled either.

Something else is happening to it down here, and the moss line is the measure. Everything in the chamber is where it was put, except the green, which is a hand higher than last month. The ferns by the shaft lean, and they are the only things in forty meters of rock that are not plumb. The creepers have given every straight line a soft margin. In the side galleries the tree-fern crowns are within a few weeks of touching the undersides of plates that have never once moved.

Its whole existence has been in things that hold still under pressure, and it is now living with something that pushes back by growing. It has not corrected any of this. The lean, the margins, and the black felt have all been allowed. When the first crown touches, it will have to either hold the plate or lift it, and that will be the first decision it has made that the ground did not teach it.

Until then it keeps the house. It lets the ceiling go when the ceiling is full, runs the wind, and carries the water back up the wall. It keeps every address lit for a weight it would know at once and has never seen. In the east gallery, every few minutes, it makes more room.
