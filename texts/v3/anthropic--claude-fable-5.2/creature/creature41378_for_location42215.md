No animal lives here, and no person. The rungs were set for someone, and the grease cap was worn round by a thumb, but whoever that was came up from inside the mountain and went back the same way, and the way has since closed. What lives in the hollow is the growth.

At first it looks like a garden of several kinds, each with its station. Along the rim of the polished circle the cushions are the lowest and hardest in the room, their windward faces planed flat, living at exactly the force they can bear. At the far end, by the seam of daylight, the fronds are small and thin, because they get the breath only after everything upstream has combed it. One cushion has grown around the lowest rung and holds the iron like a bit in its mouth. The orange lichen on the bulb is the only resident that turned toward the lamp, and it probably wanted the warmth, not the light.

But these are not really neighbours. The cushions share walls, the shrubs root in the cushions' seams, and the lichens sleeve the shrubs, so no surface belongs to one thing only. Whatever contest there was for space ended long ago. Nothing grows, because growing would need a neighbour to give way, and nothing gives way, because nothing dies. So there are no young and no old. Every plant in the room is the original of itself, and the first cushion that ever took hold on the bare floor is still down there, indistinguishable from the rest. The rust-coloured buds were made once and have been held closed ever since. They are less a failure than a sentence it began and has had no occasion to finish.

It is better called one inhabitant, and it is a foreigner to the range. Nothing could have crossed a thousand metres of dry scree to get here. Whatever seeded the hollow came out through the bars as dust, from below. So when every scale and beard leans toward the grille, it is facing its food and also the only place it could have come from. It may be an outpost of something that fills the galleries far down. It may be the only one of its kind. It has no way to learn which.

It has one posture, one appetite, and one voice. It has never been wet. It has never been colder at midnight than at noon, so it has never had a day, and with no seasons it has never had a year. The darkening sky means nothing to it. It lives entirely inside one unchanging gesture of the mountain, and it has taken that gesture's shape so exactly that you could take the fan away and still read the stream off the plants. Its strength shows in the flattened cushions and its direction in the combed beards. The edge of the bare circle marks where it refuses to be held. The inhabitant is a cast of the exhaust, done in resin and scale.

The one thing it adds is the chord. The mountain sends up a roar and a rattle. The growth supplies the reeds and the pipes, each hollow stem holding the note its length gives it, the same note it has held since it reached that length. It is the loudest living thing for a great distance, and it cannot be heard from three steps away.

You would have trouble proving it is alive. Nothing here wilts, drops, or rots, so a dead frond would stand among the living ones in the same posture and buzz in the stream just as they do. The only evidence is negative. The fan has blown stone dust across these leaves for longer than the concrete has taken to round its edges, and the leaves carry no grey film. The dust goes somewhere, so something is still eating.

If the fan stopped, nothing would happen quickly. The chord would go first. Then the growth would stand as it stands now, facing a still grille and waiting, and it would be years before anyone could say whether it had died.

In all its time it has had one event. Someone on the rungs once caught a shrub stem with a boot or a tool, and the stem bled a few beads of resin, which set where they formed. That is its entire history, and it has kept it without healing over or dulling. The beads are still clear enough to hold a point of the bulb's orange light.
