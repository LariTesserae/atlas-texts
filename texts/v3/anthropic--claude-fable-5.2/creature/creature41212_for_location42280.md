The whole census is plants, and under the plants the small rot that came down with them. There are perhaps four mosses, two liverworts, and one fern. Nothing eats them and nothing carries anything from one garden to another.

Each lit garden is less a community than a single long sitting. The ferns under lamp 4 are, as near as matters, the same ferns that first stood up there, renewed from the same creeping stems, each year's fronds laid down on the last. Push a hand into the moss and the green is one knuckle deep. Below that it is brown, then black, then a wet felt of its own dead. What soil the gardens have, they made out of themselves.

They are obedient. Every frond under lamp 4 is angled at the bulb, and the dome is the lamp's shape, not the fern's. The cushions are deepest under the drip joints. Each garden is the exact outline of the place where light and water overlap, and none has ever had to choose between anything. The only travelling is done by the ferns' sperm, which swim a finger's width through the film of water in the moss, and by whatever makes the skin on the basin.

The seven dead gardens are the same creature after the lamp. Each had one season's notice.

Lamp 7 is different, and the difference has to be found by hand. The moss there is not in cushions. It is short and dense, like the nap of a carpet, slightly slick, and it does not spring back when pressed. The ferns are knee-high, with small leathery leaflets, and they make no dome. They lean, all of them the same way, toward the trestle. The nearest have laid their stems along the cable, and the liverworts have left the trestle legs and closed around the cable in an overlapping sleeve.

The grey bulb is warm. It did not fail the way the others did, with a filament parting and the dark arriving complete. It still burns inside glass it has spent years blackening with its own metal. Each year it gave a little less light, so this garden alone was short of light for a long time before it was without it.

What it did with that time is underneath. Against the cable, where the sheath has gone chalky and cracked, lies a dark slick the other gardens do not have. It is rust-black and smells strongly of the wet iron the whole chamber smells of faintly. It was probably always here, in the rock or the pipe sweat, living on warmth and iron and sulfur and a trickle of leaked current. It never needed light. It needed something to keep it wet.

The moss roots go down into that layer and stay there. The moss gives it a roof and water, and it gives the moss whatever the lamp used to. Whether one is feeding on the other has stopped being a useful question. The green may be only habit now, kept because nothing has required giving it up.

The undersides of these fronds are bare. This garden has stopped putting spores into the still air to be let down on hot rock, and it goes forward only by contact, never letting go of itself. That makes it one body, a circle with two threads, about six paces long.

It keeps the circle because the circle is where its dead are, the only ground that holds water. The thread is the first shape it has made that the lamp did not draw. At each tip the green is not in front. A hand's length ahead of the moss, the cable carries a damp, dark smear, and the moss follows, roofing it over at perhaps a hand's width a year.

It does not know lamp 4 is there. Its habitat is a line, where every other habitat in the chamber is a disc, and a line leads somewhere. In one direction it will reach the pale edge of a garden that has never met anything, and it will cross that chalk line from the outside. In the other it will reach the generator, whose hum can only be found by touch, and touch is how this thing lives.

So the chamber holds three gardens waiting on their bulbs, seven that have finished, and one that is no longer quite a garden. It has no eyes and has never moved faster than the mineral crust. It is the only thing down here that is not where it was put. A pace beyond the tip, the cable is dry and warm, and in a few years it will be damp.
