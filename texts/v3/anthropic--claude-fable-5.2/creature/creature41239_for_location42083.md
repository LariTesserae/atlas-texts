From the rim it is the eighth plant, the one the count was unsure of. It has the squat shape of the leather crowns and the same dark gloss. But come back in an hour and it stands a hand's width from where it stood. Watch it for a quarter of that hour and its leaves draw together and open again, once.

Climb down and it goes off into the distance with everything else. What you have instead is the ticking. Most of it is the walls. Some of it comes in fours, evenly, from far out on the floor. It stops for long periods and starts again somewhere else.

The shadow is the first part of it you see clearly, because the daylight is complete and gives it the whole animal. A deep chest stands on four long legs set close together, the way legs are set on things that live on ledges. The back slopes to narrow hips. The neck is short, the head plain and narrow, carried low with the mouth a little open. Look from the shadow to what casts it and there is much less.

There is the chest. It is a barrel of broad flat ribs under a hide like stamped leather, dark with oil, carried at the height of your hip. At its four corners, where legs should leave it, are collars of moss. Below, on the floor, are four crescents of dull iron, each the front edge of a hoof with no hoof behind it. Between collar and crescent there is nothing. The crescents step in order anyway, the chest rides above them in time, and the weight comes down through the gap. The tick is the weight arriving.

Ahead of the chest is a place the mountain has not decided. It has weight. It has heat, more than the air around it. It turns toward things. The eye cannot keep hold of it. If you want its face, look at the floor.

It was not born, and nothing made it. It is setting, as the drum set, in order of use. Breath came first because of where it lives, then footing. The rest is on schedule.

It lives on the copper. Whatever will run from head to legs has not formed in it yet, and the current in the veins stands in. So it walks only where the veins go, one crescent always on metal. Where a vein crosses a seam, it lowers the undecided place to the rock, and afterward the seam is dry for a hand's length. It takes the veins in turn. Each one ends at a plant, and it gets there near dark.

At night there is no shadow, so there is least of it: a chest lying across the vein on the hot rock, breathing its quarter hours. The moss has come in at these places. Beside each plant is a bed in the shape of the rest of the animal. There are four long pads, each bent twice, and a small round one set a little forward with a dent in it. The finished parts lie on bare stone. The legs, when they come, will fold the way the moss says.

It has never had to make room for anything. Stand on open floor and it passes at the distance the pocket keeps, hours off. Stand on a vein and you are on its road, and on your day in the turn it arrives. The crescents stop. Heat comes close to your face. One long breath goes over you, smelling of a warm machine shop, and on the floor the shadow's mouth closes. Then it steps around you, keeping a foot on the copper, and goes on. Anyone counting from the rim just then would get seven plants, perhaps nine.

By noon it is always back at the center. Here the breathing changes. At the lowest point, where the air is thinnest, it breathes like an animal, fast, with the ribs working and the moss collars shaking. This is what the chest was finished first for. It stands with its four crescents together on a space a hat would cover, under the darkest part of the sky. Its shadow, which has lain beside it all morning, goes in underneath. For that minute it stands on all of itself.

It is the highest living thing on the mountain, and that is its whole work. The mountain means to have an animal on its summit, and it has started early, in the place where the summit will be, like moss packed in ahead of a part. The peak outside comes up a hand's width a year. When it gets here the floor will be open ground, and the legs should be in by then.

The drum finishes its turn. The drop falls. The animal puts one crescent on the next vein.
