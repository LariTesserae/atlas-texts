The first answer is nothing. The orchids say so, and the clean silt, and the pitchers of clear water.

But the arches are the right height to walk under, and that is a height. The treads of the stairs are a stride. The square is a finger deep, and the rock of the top street is warm to the palm. Somebody's finger went into that water. Somebody counted the terraces twice, looked into the pitchers, put both hands over both ears, and went close enough to a curl on a wall to be sure of it. A town with no one in it is not known this well.

So begin with its size. It clears the crown of each root arch by the width of a hand. It goes through the curtained doorways with one shoulder leading. It is upright. It has two hands, and one finger it measures with.

A figure got this way is only a hollow the town fits around, and it has the fit backwards. Nothing here was laid down for anyone. The water built stairs with no one to climb them. The one who lives here fits the arches because it was arrived at the way they were, slowly, without a tool mark. It is pale like the rims. It is rounded where bodies usually keep their corners, at the knuckles, the heels, and the bridge of the nose. There is no scar on it and no callus, and nothing on it shows where it was begun. It is not young. It has the soft, settled look of something that finished becoming itself long ago, and it is getting no older.

It keeps the rules of the town on its own body.

- **Water:** It sweats, and each drop that leaves is already replaced. It is never thirsty and never less than soaked. It drinks from the throats of the bromeliads anyway, and the cup is as full when it lifts its face. Its fingertips are wrinkled from the wet, exactly as wrinkled as they have always been.
- **Light:** Its shadow does not follow it. A shadow lies ready in each place it stands, and it steps into each one the way a foot goes into a shoe.
- **Growth:** No hair is new and none has fallen. It does not get hungry and it does not sleep, since nothing in it runs down.
- **Stone:** It is worn, but it is not wearing. Its soles are as smooth as the lips of the stairs, and neither gets smoother.

It leaves no tracks. It crosses the square and the water lies flat around its feet, since a ripple would need a before and an after. It walks under the orchids with a hand's width to spare and has never touched one. They are not open for it.

What it does is check. It lifts the lids of the pitcher plants. It looks along the silt. It counts the terraces. It is looking for a second thing, anything the town did not make. A wing would do, or a shell, or one brown leaf on a stair.

If no time is passing, then every check it has ever made is being made now. Its palm is flat on the top street. Its hands are over its ears in the innermost room. Its face is a breath away from the travertine curl. It is on the fourth terrace counting up and on the ninth counting down. That is enough of it to fill the square, which may be why there is a square. Only one of it is ever seen, as only one drop is seen falling from the arch. The rest is felt the way the roar is felt, with no direction, as strongly in the curtained rooms as on the outer rim. It is the feeling that the town is lived in, and it is why the place reads as planned.

The roar may be its doing too. The roar is felt in the sternum and the teeth, which is where a voice is felt by the one using it. Hands over the ears do not alter a sound made inside the head. The air is like the inside of a mouth. It has called out across the cloud, as anyone would. If every drop is falling now, then every call is being made now, from this rim and from the same rim on every other massif. That too would be enough for a cataract. It may be a single greeting, said so completely that no part of it can be heard.

It does not live in the whole town equally. It lives where the town fails to hold.

Counted going up, there is one terrace more than there is going down. That terrace is its house. It can be climbed to and it cannot be come down through. It has a basin the size of a room, a stone curtain, and one tree fern with its feet wet. The inhabitant keeps nothing there, since there is nothing to keep and nowhere a kept thing could have come from. It goes there because the town disagrees with itself there. For the same reason it takes the stair that runs downhill from the square and comes out at the top street in the steam, and then it takes it again. And it leans in the fig near the east rim, where its own elbow fits the bend in the root, and it is leaning also near the west rim. A miscount is the only kind of door the town has. It stays near the doors.

From the fig it looks out across the cloud floor. On the nearest massif the fig with the elbow is visible, small and exact, and so is the one leaning in it, turned away, looking at the next massif along. All of them face the same way. It has never seen a face out there, only the back of a head, dozens of times over, going blue with distance. It knows the face anyway from the square, which has never once shivered and is the best mirror there could be. It knows what it is looking at.

It lifts a hand all the same. Every hand goes up. It is watching for the one that goes up second, because a late hand would belong to someone else, and late is the one thing this place does not have. So the hand is up, and it has always been up. Behind it along the arcade the orchids are open in white and a waxy dull orange, in the same way and for the same reason.
