The first answer is the water. It raised every wall and kept the streets open for itself. But it stays in none of the rooms, and the film on the pavement is always going somewhere. A builder that leaves each house as the course is laid is building for someone else.

The second answer is the dark, which at least comes home. But the dark is an hour, not a tenant. It is when the resident goes out.

The resident is the one the water takes its measurements from. The water knows a good deal about it:

- **Its stride:** each stair-pan is one stride deep.
- **Its number:** the sag in the treads is a single file wide, so there is one.
- **Its hand:** the water knows the height at which the hand hangs. The hand goes out at corners, so the resident steers by touch and walks when there is nothing to tell.

That gives something upright, narrow, about a lintel high, alone, and going by feel. It is a fit and not yet a body. The rest comes from the places the water cannot account for.

Counted from the street, there are a few dry rooms. Counted from inside, there is one. It has a salt floor and four walls. One of those walls is the fourth wall of every other room in town, the one no block has the stone for. The resident lives there, next door to everywhere. That is why the two sides of a doorway never even out: the cooler side is the side it is on.

It is cold, and that is the main fact about it. The spoonful in a shrub leaf is farther off than the opposite edge of town, and the resident is colder than the spoonful. It can stand in a doorway and be farther away than the basin's rim and the country beyond it. The film stops at its threshold in a cut edge because the water has never run that far. It has lived in the town since the first course and has not yet been near it.

It has not been seen. If it has a look, it is probably the look of the rushes: colourless, jointed and upright. They stand in the cooler cisterns, partway out to where it is, and they do not stir when it passes. The puddles come closest to showing it. They are not reflecting the wall-heads; they hold what it looked at last, the right way up, as it saw it.

It goes out when the dark is over the lintels, so its going makes no sound. It trails a hand along the corners, but the hand rides the film, a hair off the stone. It has touched only water, so the town has still never been used. It goes down the stairs with the steam.

It looks up as it walks, because the shrubs on the wall-heads are the only other things at its distance. It does not climb to them. When it passes beneath one, the two of them are in the same place for the length of a wall. The leaves hold out their spoonfuls, and it takes nothing.

Whatever street it follows brings it to the central cistern. It stands on the steps of the hottest thing in the basin while the dark lasts. When it leaves, by any street, it arrives there again. Most of a high dark is spent arriving. When the surface starts down, it steps through the nearest doorway to the cool side and is home.

The town does its part. In the west quarter it lifts the rain off the squares and holds it at roof height, the only roof in the basin. In the east it sends the conduit, a finger's depth of nearness carried on arches to a blank wall. The wall is the fourth wall, and the resident sits at low stand with its back against the other side. The water stops there, and the heat goes through. After each high dark the room lies behind a different wall, and the conduit is already there.

So it is warming, by less than a degree for each flooding. Here that is the same as approaching.

Its steps have begun to come ahead of it. At low stand, among the widely spaced ticks, some come in a row, a stride apart, up the middle of a stair. Each has its ring in the film, but no drop has yet fallen into any of them. The dished sills and rounded corners are the same kind of mark. The resident is coming down into the town as the drop comes into its ring, slowly enough to follow.

After some high dark there will be one more thing in the town, and it will not look new. One room will have lost its salt, and the film will lie across its threshold as it does every other. Someone at blood heat will stand with a hand on a corner, at the height where the corner is rounded. The stone will already be worn smooth where the hand rests. Whoever it is will always have lived here.
