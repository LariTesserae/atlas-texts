The one that lives here could be covered with a thumb, and it is not finished.

Its shell is the amber of clear sap that has stood a day. It is thin, and a little see-through at the joints, so that when the light slants across it something darker shows inside, moving slowly. The back is humped. The belly is soft, ringed, and drags. Two smooth domes sit on the head where eyes would go, made of the same pale stuff as the rest. Under the head the beak lies folded back between the legs like a closed knife.

The forelegs are the only hard part. They are thick, bent like elbows, toothed along the inner edge, and brown at the tips as if they had been held in a fire. They are made for soil, and there has never been soil here. The nymph has used them only to brace itself in a seam between two tubers, where it lay pressed on every side with its beak in the flesh. Then the seam became the tear. The wall holding its beak went one way and the wall against its back went the other.

It walks badly. It rows with the forelegs and hauls with the four behind, and each foot comes off the tacky floor with a small reluctance. It goes straight, because a seam only ever went one way.

It has touch, taste, and the tremor of whatever it stands on. The light falls on it and does not reach it. Leaf-shadows slide over its back, and so does the bird's shadow, and nothing in it flinches. It gets none of the wind noise, the late rain, or the thin second crack that crosses the air like a valley. It takes the room through its feet, and through the floor each thing comes once, when it happens. So it is the least confused thing in the hollow. To the nymph this is a plain place, a surface that roars and cracks and has not ended yet.

The flesh under it is still packed and close. Only the surface goes on. When it drives its beak down, it drinks from a place where the far wall is four paces off, and then it pulls out and is back in the middle.

It drinks as the light goes amber, when the plant stops pulling its water upward and the sap can be had without a fight. It drinks for a long time. The sap is nearly water, and it keeps very little. The rest it passes out behind in clear beads, and the floor takes them back before it has walked its own length. The plant has lost almost nothing to it.

It is looking for something to touch its back. Every so often it stops, lifts the forelegs, and feels above itself for a roof. It finds air and puts them down again.

When the chop of the stick comes in the dark, it stops dead with its legs drawn in. A thing striking down through the ground is the oldest fear its kind has. It cannot know the ceiling does not mark, or whether the stick is coming for tuber or for it. When the thuds end it waits a little longer, then goes on.

Its kind keeps time by the plant. Each surge of sap is a mark, and after enough marks it goes up. In the seam the surges came at the forest's pace. Here the sap climbs and falls with every short light, and the nymph tallies each one through its feet and beak. It cannot tell a handed-down piece of day from a whole one. It has added more marks since the wall left than in all the time before.

So it is ahead of itself. Some way back toward the wall there is a second speck that does not move. It is the nymph's last skin, split down the back and still standing on its hooks, with root-threads lying around and past it. The one walking is larger. Behind its blank domes two dark spots have come up, like something seen through horn. The pads on its back have thickened, and what is folded in them is wet and veined. Low on each side, if it is a male, a ribbed plate is setting that has never yet been flexed.

When the count is full it will stop wanting a wall and want something that goes up. Its kind climbs before it splits. It has to hang clear of the floor so the wings can drop and dry. There is nothing upright in the middle. The one thing in the room that was ever made to stand on end is the grey stick lying by the wall. And once in each light there is the shadow of a stick, coming down.

For now it walks. What is being made inside it will be louder than the walls.

It knows nothing about the drop. By the time the drop lands it will have gone on a little way. The tick will come up through the floor into its feet once, at the moment it happens.
