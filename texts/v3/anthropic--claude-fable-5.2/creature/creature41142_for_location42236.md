The first sign of her is handwriting.

The plant marks its bales with the same enamel scars it uses for the rows. But here and there on a rick of shadow you find a second tag, a square of leaf-foil tucked under the binding with the silver side out and figures scratched into the silver. They are not row numbers. They are larger than any number the plant uses, and each one you find is larger than the last.

For a while you could take this for the work of a crew. The tags turn up in every sector, and nothing else in the basin is done by one of anything. But the hand is the same throughout, small and upright, with a crossed seven. The manifest has a line for personnel, and the figure on that line is one.

She is the color the place has made her. Her skin has the finish of the board. Chaff has sifted into her hair for so long that the hair has given up being any other color. She wears a coat of pressed husk, gone soft at the elbows, and the leaves eat her footsteps as they eat everyone's. The first finger of her right hand ends in amber. I will come to that.

By the standards of the basin she is mostly contamination. She knows it and conducts herself accordingly. She keeps her mouth closed. She does not spit, and it is a long time since she wept. When she rests, the plant begins on her. Resin rises from the board along her outline, a glaze no thicker than a fingernail, and on waking she has to crack herself loose. Her heels carry a permanent varnish. The runners part around her wherever she stands, as they part around the blisters, without slowing and without interest.

That would make her a spill, something the works is in the slow course of sealing. It is not the whole account.

She eats the powder. She nicks a cane at the joint and takes what hisses out onto her tongue, and it tastes like a coin held in the mouth. Years of this show. Her palms, her soles and the insides of her forearms have gone chalk-white, and they give off a little light, enough to read a tag by. They are the coldest parts of her. If you held her wrist to your ear, and she let you, you would hear a pulse, and under it a fine hiss, climbing.

At the change of shift, when every leaf in the basin turns over, her hands turn over. She does not decide it. Wherever she is and whatever she is carrying, her palms come up white side to the sky. For that interval the light falls into them and they are warm. Then they turn back. The plant has her down twice, once as a spill and once as stock, and it routes around the discrepancy.

For the same interval she has a shadow. Light that falls from one direction will do that. It lies on the board at her feet, the only shadow in the basin still attached to what cast it, and she looks at it for as long as it lasts. When the leaves turn back it comes off her like any other waste, a soft dark sheet, and a runner carries it away to be baled. Those are the bales she tags.

She sleeps in shadow because there is nothing else to sleep in. She draws one sheet from a rick, folds it over her eyes, and lies down under the full glare, colder than she was and in the dark. The runner comes for the sheet when she wakes. The bale it came from was one sheet short before she took it.

Her voice reaches as far as her arm, like anyone's, and she uses it anyway. To hear her you would have to stand where she could touch you, and what you would hear is a number.

The number is drops. You might see the moth once in four days. She sees it more often, because she walks toward it. She knows the notch. When it falls she is there if she can be, kneeling on the board while the black ring spreads to a hand's width and stops. It is her hand's width, because she has checked. Once, early on, she put a finger to the wet. The plant treated the finger as it treats the ring, and since then she has had something to write with.

She says the count aloud, scratches it into foil, and files it with the next bale of her own shadow, which is the only thing on the line that came from her. She has tried counting other things. Shifts would not hold. Rows do not add. Days are not issued. But a drop is sealed where it fell, and it stays. It is the only kind of event here that stays where it happened, and so hers is the only running total in the basin.

The plant does not object. Her tags arrive like everything else, ahead of themselves. She will find one in her own hand on a rick that has not been stacked yet, bearing a number she has not reached. She leaves it where it is. When she gets to that number she writes it out fresh, and she has stopped asking whether she is counting or copying. Now and then a foil comes up off the board in the upward chaff from the far side, with a figure on it longer than any she has needed. She keeps these in her coat. She has decided they are good news.

There is one more thing, and it took me the longest to see, because there is nothing there. In an aisle she can always reach, since no distance is issued against it, there is a stretch of board the length of a person lying down, and the runners part around it. It has no blister and no stain. It is bare panel, and the groove around it is worn deeper than the groove around any blister in the basin. By the plant's reckoning that makes it the oldest discrepancy on the floor, older than the first drop. Room was made before there was anything to make room for.

She has lain down in it. It fits her across the shoulders, and it is a little too long. She shows no distress at this. She has decided it means she is not finished.

She sleeps there most shifts now, with the sheet of shadow over her eyes and her white palms turned to the board so the light stays under her. The glaze starts at her edges. In a few hours she will wake, break it, give the sheet back to the runner, and go to see where the moth has got to. It is carrying her next number.
