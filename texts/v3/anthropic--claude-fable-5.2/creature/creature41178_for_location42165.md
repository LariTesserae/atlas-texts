By every test that works lower down, nothing lives here. There is no water to be made of, nothing to eat, and nothing that has been eaten. Counted anticlockwise, the hollow has four faces and no occupant, and that count is not a mistake.

Counted the other way, the room is in use.

The first sign is the band of dust along the east wall. Dust only heaps at one end like that where a shelf meets something, so there is a shelf; it just isn't offered to a visitor's hand. A groove drawn through the dust closes in a season, in a room with no draught. Something puts it right, and a season is how long that takes.

So the first guess is a keeper, something that minds the place and has no other business. The accounts look kept: light spent at a fixed rate, wear balanced against removal. This guess is wrong, but it gets the speed right. Whatever is here moves at the pace of the line on the plate, a grain's width a year. At that pace a visitor barely happens. A boot on the floor reaches it as one grain shifting, and a whole visit is shorter to it than the tick is to the visitor. Only warmth gets through, in the one form it can take hold of. The bare hand stays warm because nothing has been taken yet. Then it is cold all at once, because the warmth has been accepted whole, like a coin.

The second guess is that the hollow is the animal, with the ring for a mouth, the filament for a nerve, and the plate as a slow growth record. This is closer, and the wear undoes it. A body doesn't erode where it is most itself. The edges beside the fifth wall are rounded like the arms of a chair one person has used for a lifetime. Here it is not many sittings but one, never interrupted. The room is worn where it holds something.

So it is there, against the narrow face with the quartz vein, in the arrangement where that face exists. The wall may exist because the occupant needs something at its back. It sits low. The plate at a visitor's knee is at the height of a hand for something seated on the floor. The shelf at a visitor's chest is just above its eye level. A hand laid on the quartz is as near as anyone gets.

That makes sense of the filament. It is something long, stood upright by the west wall, seen from a side on which it has no width. It could be a staff or a pole, some carried thing set down. A finger passed through it goes around it and comes out a little to the left.

And that changes the guess again, because nobody sets a staff down where they live. They set it down where they have stopped.

It is not a keeper or a resident. It came in through the gap as anyone must, shoulders turned, out of that wind. It took three paces to the back wall, put down what it carried, sat, and began to write. By its own reckoning that was a few moments ago. The light is not stored. It is simply the time, in here: still the afternoon it arrived. The dimming the instruments argue about is that afternoon getting on.

What it writes is what travellers write in high shelters. A stroke with the beginning of a turn is how a great many names start. Whether the name is its own, or one for the summit nobody has had reason to name, can't be told from this much of a letter.

When the name is done, it will take up the thing by the west wall and walk three paces to the mouth. The light has been rationed for the writing and for those three paces. The return takes longer because it is the part still to come, and the room is holding the time open. A visitor leaving wades through a little of what is being saved.

Below, in the ring, it has only just come in. The plate there is nearly bare, and it may still feel the line where the wind stops, one ear in the hiss and one in the quiet. It is the same traveller, as the two pebbles are one pebble. Somewhere above is a hollow in whose floor this one sits the size of a forearm. There it is later, the light is lower, and the name is further along.

The shelf is the only thing it keeps. The dust comes off rock that has to hold two arrangements at once: the room with someone in it and the room without. Every grain is made by its being here. A grain that lands on the shelf fell from the later room. It says only that the room up there is still worn, so the traveller is still there, and the afternoon has not run out before the name did. It straightens these grains, slowly, as people do with things they've been sent.

And every few hours it sends the same word down, to the one with the whole afternoon still ahead. That is the tick.
