The first answers are the obvious ones, and they fail one at a time.

It is not the nineteen. The split one shows there is no inside to be in. They are not houses but the housed: each is a thing that fell and was taken in, and if the town has citizens, these are them. They do nothing.

It is not the lights, though there are thousands and they go out and come back like windows. They come back in a different order, and lamps would be relit in sequence. This is something finding its place again, starting each time from a different corner. A light is on where an intersection has been checked and found correct. It reaches a finger's width because the claim reaches that far.

The water is nearer, but the water is only what it uses. And the water gives something away. While the streets are wet, a fallen splinter stays a splinter and a new crack stays unbridged. The corrections are there after the exhalation. So whatever handles things handles them in the minutes when there is no town.

That turns the picture over. The town is its composure. Its working state is the other one: loose in the chamber as heat and vapor, touching every scallop of the wall, the inside of the new crack, the whole surface of the splinter. It touches only then. When the glow sinks it withdraws into its lines.

So there is one of it, the size of the hollow exactly. It has no body except what fails to happen. Water does not fall at its own speed. Light does not spread. Pressure does not leave through the seam. A thread of water does not boil on a floor that would take skin. Wherever something in the chamber ends more cleanly than it should, that is a surface of it. It is made of edges, as other things are made of flesh.

It lives on the difference between the fissure and the sky, as a mill lives on a stream. Below it is ice, above it nothing. For a very long way in any direction, it is the only place where water can lie down as water. It is the one temperate thing on the mountain, and its whole idea of temperance is the level, the plumb and the thread.

It does not make. Nothing in the chamber was made by it. It receives, and what it receives it corrects toward the nearest true shape: a splinter becomes a needle, a flake a slab. The ring on edge was the rim of a bubble. The grey is the same all the way through because glass has a skin and a core and strain between them, and the grey has none. It has turned each fallen thing into something that cannot break again. That is the nearest thing to tenderness that can be proved of it.

It lets each piece lie for one cycle as it landed. Perhaps it can only work in the heat. Or perhaps it allows the thing an hour of being what it was.

The park is what it has instead of curiosity. Sulfur comes out of the vapor already straight. Nothing else arrives in the chamber true without being corrected. It clears the needles from its streets because they are in the way, but it gave them a block and watered all four sides and put a light at each corner. It keeps checking. Their tips stand at one level, and it is not possible to say whether it trims them or whether they simply stop where it has decided growing stops.

It may not be the planner. The scratches survive the exhalation and it does not, quite. Each hour it returns to a ruling already there and takes it up. It may have drawn the lines once. It may have found them as anyone would find them, and it has no way left to tell. But the plan runs up the end wall, so it was never fitted to the floor. It was fitted to a count.

The count is the ceiling. Every tooth the rain hangs from will let go in its turn, and there is a plot for each. The ceiling is the rest of the town, not yet come down. The mountain does the breaking. What lives here only knows its roof piece by piece, in order, and has the address ready.

The arithmetic follows. The crust is a forearm thick, and each flake thins it. As it thins, the cold comes nearer the ember, the difference steepens, the ceiling sweats harder, and the lines run fuller. It will be strongest just before the end. Then a piece will fall and leave sky behind it. The darker air in the seam will have nothing to hold. The rain will fall once at its own speed and be gone before it lands. What remains will be scratches on black glass and some thousands of grey objects, plumb, each square in its plot, in the open at the top of the world.

Nothing in it suggests reluctance. Beneath the blade with the hairline, the drops have already moved apart. It makes way for what is coming down and always has. Making way is its manner with everything: a hand passed through the rain would be given room like a flake of glass. Things in motion are not its concern. It attends to what has come to rest.

If it has a feeling, the feeling is in the speed. It cannot slow the mountain or catch what falls. It governs only the last knee-height of each drop's descent, and it takes that distance as slowly as water can be made to go.

Once an hour, when the lines fill, it sounds. The chord is every street running level at once, at the edge of what the air will carry. It is addressed to no one. It is the sound of being back, found again from a different corner, with nineteen in place and one plot wet on four sides, its light already on.
