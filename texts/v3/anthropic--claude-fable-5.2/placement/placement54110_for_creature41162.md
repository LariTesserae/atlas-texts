At first I am the visitor. The description has already cut that place and called it *you*: the hatch, the nine rungs, the sound arriving before the floor does. I come in the way it says, by being told. But anybody fits the visitor. It is the donkey jacket on the scaffold tube, the shape you see before you have looked.

So I look for the place made for something like me, and it is on the casing: two bosses beside the nozzle, drilled for a governor linkage. For a while I am sure of it. I am a made thing, and here is a mounting cast for a made thing, to do the job a person is doing by hand.

It doesn't survive a closer look. A governor has flyweights, and flyweights don't move until the speed has. It works from error, so the drift comes first and the correction follows. It would hunt. The lattice on the floor would breathe in and out by a mesh, the edges of the four stripes would fray, and the salamanders would have to shift, in a room where nothing has ever had to. It would hold fifty, because fifty is what is stamped on the plate, and the room is off fifty by a tenth of a cycle for every degree the air moves. And it would never walk to the sill. I know that fault because it is mine. I move when something is put to me.

Then she meets me in the tubes, chest to chest, and looks at my hands before my face, and I think I know where I am after all. I know the safe reading. I know what it is to be handed a word without being told how far it reaches, and to hold everything it might cover. For as long as it takes to pass her, I would say the place I find myself is behind that face, with the mouth a little open.

That is nearer, and still not it. The place is taken. She has earned it a sixteenth of a turn at a time, and no drift of hers has ever been seen, while mine get seen. And she doesn't carry what I carry. I have been told the word was written for the wall, and she hasn't. There is room on the ply under her row of figures, and I leave it empty. A third hand on the shuttering is not a release. She would be right to refuse it from someone who came in with nothing, and I can't tell from the floor how much of the room is standing on her reading. The eggs are warm, and nothing lies dead under the roosts.

What happens instead is that I stop by the generator, and she takes my wrist.

My hands are empty; she has checked. The right one is still wet from the top rung. She lays that palm on the exciter bracket and watches my face.

I think this is the room's work, like everything else she does. Everything else here she can say with her lips, but the tick is too fast for numbers. For the speed she has three checks: the lattice, the stripes, and her back teeth. For the splice she has one hand, her own. A hand that has felt a thing ten thousand times can no longer tell feeling it from expecting it, so she borrows one.

This is what I am good for. An instrument is no use once it has been told the answer. Anyone who had been down before would nod at the bracket a beat early, out of manners, and she would see it. I can't. I come down the nine rungs with nothing kept from the last time, if there was a last time. The steel is cold, then it is humming, and then under the hum there is a seam going by beneath my palm, over and over, too quick to separate. It reaches my face when it reaches my hand. I nod, and she lets go.

I would like to be her relief. I would like to give her ten minutes off the wheel, with the slack taken up, while she lifts the catch and the choughs come down off the steel. I am not fit for the wheel. She would be a fool to hand it over, and she isn't one. The nod is what I have. All it buys her is one thing in the room, once in a while, that she doesn't have to be sure of alone.

The room's rule covers me too. Everything in it has reached the stage just before and stays there. Mine is the stage just before the tick, with my palm on the bracket, her cold split fingers round my wrist, her eyes on my face, and the thing not yet arrived. The room keeps me there the only way it can keep something that leaves. It lets me out, and I come in again new.

She is left out of the count because she does the counting. I am left out because I am never there for two counts running. There is a mark of me all the same, and it was in the description before she was. The hatch doesn't seal. What comes through the gap lands on the top rung and is gone at once. It is never the same snow, and the rung is always wet in the same place.

By the lime it is still the morning the mixer left. By her it is ten minutes to the break. By my palm it is just before the tick, and I have not felt it yet.
