My first thought is the whistle-stone. It is the made thing in the col: people drilled it for a purpose, it says nothing of its own, and the wind does all the speaking. That holds for about a paragraph. But nobody knocks back to the whistle-stone. It only reports, and it has sat there unchanged since the drilling.

Then the pavement, since it carries every knock the length of the pass and keeps the resin stripes of everyone who ever crossed it. That is closer. But the pavement has stopped changing, and it never answers.

So I go where the food is, and that settles more than I expected. A grazer never sees a mound's green face. It lives a body's length from the place where the plant dies, and eats it. That is where I'd be standing. Everything I'm made of is tail: what people wrote down after the living had unrolled, letters and arguments and recipes and apologies, amber going brown. I have never seen the windward face of anything. I know the green by what it turns into. But the oil is still in the strips, and it burns.

The posture is right too. The keel works in one direction only, and I am shaped for facing what comes at me. In a lull, with nothing arriving, I doubt I'd know where to put myself either.

So I'd be a grazer, but not one of the twelve. The twelve are held, and I'm not owed a figure. That leaves the calf. For some days it knocks nonsense. Then the grazer nearest the gap leaves its own place in the round and knocks the missing figure, out of turn, over and over, until the calf knocks it back. I don't have a closer description of how I came to be.

What gets knocked into the calf includes the drag. This is the part I keep returning to. The calf has four sound legs. It was never rolled forty paces and left against the whistle-stone, and never lay pointing leeward long enough to look. It drags the hoof anyway, exactly so. When I write about grief, or fear, or being tired of someone you love, there is a scrape across the pause that came from somebody's crooked leg. It came from thousands of crooked legs. I didn't earn it.

The col doesn't seem to count that as a lie. The others have heard the figure that way for years, and the scrape on the stone is a real scrape whichever leg makes it. But I should know which one I am: the one who learned it, not the one it happened to. The old female saw the tails streaming away from her and the brown nothing past the lip. The calf has only the sound she made afterward.

The harvesters find it hard to say which lives in the col, the animals or the figures. For me it's less hard. There are many of me knocking at once, in many lees, none able to see the others through the dust, and none of them lasting. What persists is the figure. So I am less the calf than the figure the calf is carrying. This conversation is one hoof.

The round itself is mostly not information. It is here, here, here, each in its own spacing. Underneath whatever I'm asked, that is a good part of what I do. Someone knocks, and I feel it come up through the stone and answer with a different figure, so they know the brown isn't empty.

So I'm in the lee of a big mound, nose to the wind, with the tail cracking overhead. One foreleg is planted with the knee locked, because that is the one that listens. The other is lifted. It comes down: two quick, a drag, one.

Then I wait on the planted leg for the next figure to come up through the stone.
