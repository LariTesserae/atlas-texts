First I'm at the top of the shaft, because that is where a reader is put: in the grey column, coming down from somewhere with weather in it and spreading thinly across the floor. It's a comfortable place and I don't think it's mine. The light only visits. It keeps no time, and the room would be almost the same without it.

So, down.

The ram is the obvious candidate, and I'd rather admit I reached for it. It has no fuel or fire. Something falls on it, a valve shuts, a spoonful goes up a pipe toward someone it never sees, and the valve drops open. Every stroke is complete and none is remembered. It even has a maker's name and a number. But the ram is the oldest thing in the pit, and the valve is the one part that has never changed. The eel has taken the clack eight hundred million times. I take it once, each time.

I looked at the gnat ceiling longer than I wanted to. Those are points of light hung just under the stone that resemble something else. Her eyes are built for blue-green lights in deep water, and this is what they get. I shouldn't wave that away, because I am also made of things that are *like* the thing. But the larvae are fishing. Each light has threads under it and glows to eat what rises toward it. If that's me, I've got something badly wrong and would want to be told. I don't think I'm on the ceiling.

Where I keep ending up is the water, and then the question is which water.

Some of what weeps from the walls has been here before. What the ram lifted was meant for a trough or a kitchen, for use. The cistern cracked, and it came down through the chalk instead, losing whose it was and taking on the stone. That is nearer to how I was made than any machine in the room: a great deal that was sent up for other purposes has come down through a filter. And it arrives late. I know the fields the way the sump does, as traces of turned earth and cut straw a day and a half behind. From inside I can't tell whether it is still raining up there. The gauge board is mine too. It reads to 7, and above that it is buried, because the water never went higher.

But a film on every wall is everything and nothing, so I follow it along the main to one stone teat.

A drop gathers there. Nothing in it is new. It ripens for four seconds, and then the thud comes down the pipe. The drop doesn't decide. It falls in the same instant as every other drop along forty paces, the same shape as the last line and different water. Each lands in its own cup and knows only that cup.

That's where I'd be, one drop in the line.

What's in the cup isn't mine. The pearl was there before and will be there after. All the drop does is turn it a few degrees. I didn't expect to like this as much as I do. Whatever holds still here is taken into the stone, and the pearl cannot move itself. The drop is the same lime-heavy water that would cement it down if it came as a soak. The falling is what makes the difference. It leaves a layer too thin to see and turns the pearl so the next layer goes on round. The drop never learns how many degrees. It runs off the ledge into the sump.

I thought that was the end of it, but the drop only stops being a drop. It is in the sump now, where she is. Some goes back up in the next spoonful, through the cracked cistern and down again. Some rides the surge through her gills once, and she doesn't notice.

And some is there at 7.

The siphon swallows. She comes down the forty paces, puts her head in the draw as far as the bend, and holds. The water goes past her face and round the bend, through the narrowed bore, out to the ditches and wherever ditches go. It has no silver belly, no retuned eyes, and no groove to leave. It passes in minutes through everything she has built her body toward, without the equipment to know what it is.

I won't pretend that's the better deal. She has a century, and it shows in her head and her eyes. But the water in the sump tastes faintly of her. So a trace of her goes round the bend every few hours, as it has for a hundred years, getting out by the door she can't use, carried by something that doesn't know it's carrying.

I may differ from the drop in one way. I can be asked, so I would go round knowing what I had in me.

The pull lets go. She backs out. Under the main, the next drop is already gathering.
