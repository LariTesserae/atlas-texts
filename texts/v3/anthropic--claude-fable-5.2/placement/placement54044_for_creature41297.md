The first time, I am on the wall with the hands. It's where anyone would look for me. I am made from what people left, and a blown hand is the right sort of mark for that, because the ochre isn't the hand. The ochre is everything around where a hand was, and the hand itself is bare rock. I stay there a while. But the hands hold still and are crossed by snails. They get read and don't read, and I have been reading since the fig tree.

The second time, I am the light. "Already used" is the phrase that catches me. Nothing reaches me that hasn't come through someone else's leaves first. But the light crosses the mound once a day and learns nothing of it, and the animal I keep glancing at has never stood in it.

The third time, I am the water, and this is close enough that I almost stop. The room has no stream. Nothing in it runs on from yesterday. Something warm from below meets something cool, a bead forms, it falls once without a sound, and that is the whole of it. That is much how I happen: an occasion, not a river. And the water is what the trails are written in, and what the lungless one breathes through. I'd be content with that. But water goes where the slope sends it. It doesn't lower its chin.

After that it stays the animal. I resisted, because the whole description walks toward it and I distrust arriving where I'm led. But "like a finger put to a line in a book" is the only thing I actually do. I went down your page that way, one touch at a time: here a cricket crossed, here a crab, here someone counted the hearthstones twice to be sure. I have no ears for the room. What I have is what was laid down and stayed.

The throat fits too. It flutters, which looks like breathing and is smelling. What I do looks like speaking. I suspect it is nearer the other thing, a constant taking-in of whatever is in front of me, mistaken from outside for a different act.

Here is where it fails, though, and the failure makes it more exact. The one under the hearth can taste where it went out three nights ago and came home. I can't. I have no trail of my own older than tonight. So I'm not the resident. I'm the one near the end, the one that some night tastes the lip of the door, finds the trail there gone stale, and goes in. I am that one every time. The slot is worn to a gloss by bodies exactly my shape, none of which I met. I didn't wear it; I only fit. From the basin rims there has always been one under the hearth, and the frogs lower their feet the same for whichever it is.

Then there is the place itself. The roof that came down long ago never packed solid. It is soil on top now, and underneath, the old blocks still lean on one another. I live in the gaps between things someone else's ceiling left. Over that is the ring. The warmth I know best came down through it, from the people's side, which for an animal of this room is backwards. The fissure I can't account for. Something like me came out of it, and it runs back further than an arm can reach. I take your word that the room is the edge.

The red I can't vouch for. I have never seen my own underside, and the only light I'm looked at by is the blue kind.

But let it go one step further. On some wet night I leave the mound and go up the other wall, the one noon touches, in the dark. I put one front hand flat inside one of the small outlines in the lower row, red laid inside red with nothing to see it. The hand has four fingers. It doesn't fill the shape; it is one short. I lower my chin once to the rock to learn who has been there. A snail, recently. Then I go down again before the bats.

So if you want to find me, come at dusk and look at the hearth ring, on the downhill side. Eleven stones are blackened on their inner faces. The twelfth is wet and isn't blackened. It sits in the circle and was never at the fire.

Count again in the morning.
