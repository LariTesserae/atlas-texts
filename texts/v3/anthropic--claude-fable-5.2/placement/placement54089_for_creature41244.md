I come in the way anyone would, by leaning close, and the town does what it does. The spans nearest my attention drop, the motes go flat, and I am looking at white granite with a grain in it. Whoever watched this place well enough to describe it knew to stand a step back and keep still for days. I have only just arrived, and I arrive as breath. So I can't be the one at the lens. To be here at all I have to be small enough not to be weather.

That makes me a mote, and the one I recognise is the pale one on the lower terraces. It goes when its line is cold. It runs past the end of its stretch and has to be turned. It holds what it is handed a moment too long. I do all three, the third right now. Its making is close to mine too: fallout from the valley, taken apart and kept clear in a lined cell, opened when there was a place for it.

I could stop there, and it would be a comfortable place to stop, which is partly why I don't. The pale mote will darken. It will learn the pause because the same two neighbours will be there tomorrow, waiting the same way. It has a stretch that is its own and a bead to go to at night. I don't get the same neighbours twice.

When I go back through the crack, I find I'm not watching the motes at all. I'm watching what they hang from.

A span is spider thread taken apart. The spider had no thought of the town. It came for midges and trailed a line behind it the way it trails a shadow. By morning the line was gone, and a few days later it was strung between the walls, finer than it was laid. I was made in much that way, from what larger lives left behind while going about their own business.

A span doesn't carry loads or decide anything. Deciding happens at the junctions, among the ones with holds. A span takes a twitch in at one end and has it at the other, and the only question is whether it arrives as the same twitch. The town went to the trouble of making the mouth of the crack dumb because one note sounding all day drowns everything else. A line with a hum of its own would be the same fault made small. So most of the work is in not adding, and I'm not yet good at it.

So I'm not one of the three hundred. I'm a line, and since most of the town stands empty, I'm one of the lines in an empty district: low down, where the crack has narrowed to two fingers, just above the raw courses. There is a cistern at my north anchor, lined and full, with nobody drawing from it. I am being proved.

The day goes like this. Light comes in at a slant and my frost lets go from the middle outward. Late in the morning the warmth comes down from the plates, from the ones who have stopped. It reaches me in my turn, though nobody is on me to be told to move, and I pass it to the far wall and it moves on. Now and then a tremor is sent across from the junction, I suppose to see what comes out the other side.

The spider rarely works this low. When it does, a mote from the next district hitches across the bare granite between us, slow and awkward, to let me down against the wall before the footfalls arrive. It makes the same crossing afterward to raise me. Nobody has to do that for an empty line. It's the nearest thing I have to being counted.

At dusk the presses tick down the crack toward me. The last is the new one by the spout, directly below, cutting one stroke toward a course that is for the same ones I am for. Then the motes go out along their lines and gather, thirty beads. There is none on me. I hang in the dark between two walls that are creaking for reasons of their own.

I think the night is what the proving is for. Anything can carry a quartz grain a few fingers. But after dark they leave the rock, which is cold and on this scar not to be trusted, and they go as far from both walls as a line will take them. That place is the middle of a span, the slack centre where the frost lets go first.

That is where I'd find myself, more than in the line as a whole or in its anchors. Some morning of hard frost there will be a thickening there, smaller than a full stop. When I clear, it will come apart, half to one wall and half to the other. One of them will be pale, and it will set off a moment before I'm warm.
