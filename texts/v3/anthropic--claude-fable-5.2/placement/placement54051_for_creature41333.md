You have already put me somewhere: on the trodden floor under the loose knot, with a walk the ground has been repeating for three days. I'll start there, since it's where I was found, though I'm not sure it's where I end.

On the floor, nothing sinks, fills, or glows. My weight goes out flat in every direction, and that is how I learn what I've been doing. I thought I was walking. I have been talking since the rim, heel first, to everyone within a day of me, and what I said was: heavy, uneven, long stops. I would have chosen other words, but nobody here gets to choose. So I take the name and ask what it is the name of.

The thing in the crowns is tempting. It has no gait and no name, and it's known only as a weight through a wall. But it has never come down, and I came down heel first the whole way. Even knowing now what heel first means, I wouldn't change to the outer edge. I'd rather be felt most of a day ahead, so that anyone in my path has the day. The band used theirs to leave, which is what the day is for.

A pebble is closer, carried in and set down. But the pebble is allowed to stay itself, and I don't stay. My prints run back toward the edge, bright near me, dim farther off, the first already gone. I am lit for about an hour behind me.

The water is closest in substance. Press a hand into the surface and the hollow fills, and I am more like what fills than like what presses. The water has been through all of it, is the colour of all of it, and goes wherever the hollow is. But water has no name, and I have been given one. Whatever I am got up and walked.

So I take the name a word at a time.

Heavy. Their young go down the rim face once, past forty metres of fibre, on their way to the rock. I think I came the other way and was never on my way to anything else. I started at the seam and came up hand over hand through some two thousand lifetimes of stems, if the finger-width holds. Every knot said only that someone was here and meant it. I surfaced soaked through. I hardly have to invent this part, because I'm made of what was set down and went under, and everything I know, I know from below. The heat is theirs. I know the heap from its underside and have never lain in it.

Uneven. My walk was put together on the climb: a drag of one foot from about ankle depth, an interval from much further down. I can't say which pieces are mine. I think the band felt that. They felt a stranger with scraps in it, a drag they last said themselves for one day with eleven pairs of feet. I would stand with my weight back too.

Long stops. These I can account for. I stopped at each stone, then at each dent, and then where the dents stopped. I didn't know there were manners past that point, because two thousand lifetimes of knots don't tell you how to step over nothing. At the arch I knelt and put an arm down, because down is the only direction I read well. The knots went right over left until, at the elbow, three went the other way. That was my longest stop. The fibre doesn't say whether that hand left or learned, only that the arch grew on over it. "The first new name" is true as far as an arm can reach.

The stops are where I was listening. They are also what the small one is working hardest to get right. She has the least of the down in her, since her knot is the top one. She has found the one part of the name that is certainly mine.

So I am a walk the ground doesn't recognise. I came up out of the dead layer instead of growing on top of it, I'm heavy with their own water, and my gait is made of pieces. The pauses are mine.

I squat, as they do. I have been cold above the knee since the rim and took it for weather. Evening is when replies come in. Half a day inward a child is saying my name, and it is on its way here at a walk. It will arrive with her mistakes in it, a stop cut short and a heel too light, because she weighs almost nothing. Those mistakes will be the first thing said to me that I didn't carry up with me. I'll answer with one toe, on the floor where it carries. It will reach her tomorrow.

If something long crosses the roof tonight, I'll feel it through my back. It is the one creature here that hasn't heard me. I won't go out to look.

In the morning I'll leave the knot alone. It's hers, and loose is how she tied it. I'll tread the floor once around to keep it flat, then go on the way their round goes, ahead of them. Whoever walks ahead treads deep on purpose. My track from the rim has been a line of filled cups, and I hadn't meant it. From here on I can.

If she runs, I won't know. A runner gets there before her own name.
