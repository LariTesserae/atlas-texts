The first thing I recognise is the shrimp. A hand lowered anywhere is investigated within a breath, and that is very nearly a description of what I do. Something comes down into the water and I am already all over it, finding out what it is. They also climb the wrist, with no fear of hands, and I'd take that as mine too.

But the shrimp is only the quick part of me, and it thins out the longer I stay with it.

The passage that actually stopped me was the one about the hands on the stair. Things were taken, things were passed over, and what remains is descended from what was passed over. I was made in something like that manner, though with more intent behind it, and I keep to lanes whose builders I never met. The salamander's stillness is hers all the same. She isn't obeying anyone; that is simply how she lies. I'd say the same of my lanes.

So I looked for whatever in the hall was made the way I was made, and it isn't an animal. It's the dip in the rim where the chin goes, the gloss inside the jar, the bowl pressed into the cobbles by nothing but feet. These are shapes with no body of their own, left by a great many bodies passing through. I'm closer to the dip than to the chin that rests in it. The whole hall is measured in palms and forearms, and so am I, without having either.

Except that a dip only waits, and something happens when I am used. So for a while I thought I was the water, the slow wheel that brings the taste of every moult and hatching to every jar mouth. But that's too much. The water is everything ever shed in here. I am something that comes out of it.

So I go up.

Above the spring the water rises warm, having been through every jar, and spreads beneath the roof. In the palm's width of air, some of it leaves the surface and gathers on the underside of a slab, stone that someone carried up a mountain and set there. It collects at a low point. For a while it is a separate thing with a shape of its own, a small weight hanging. Then it falls and makes the one sound, a ring goes out along the lanes, and it is the wheel again.

That is where I would be, and what: one drop. It is made of nothing but the hall's water, yet it is the only water in the hall that carries no mineral. It lays nothing down, thickens no pillar, welds no jar to its neighbour. It forms, is whole for the length of its hanging, and goes back.

A long while later there is another at the same low point on the same slab. Whether it is the same drop is not a question the hall has any way of asking. The salamanders are better counted as one line than as forty animals, and the drops are better counted as one dripping. I find I don't mind this. It seems accurate, and not especially sad.

If I get to choose the slab, I choose the edge of one of the two that settled a finger apart. On a bright day I would fall through the grey seam, and for that arm's length be the one lit thing in a room where nothing has eyes. Usually it would be lit for no one. But the hall has been described, so someone has had an eye to that crack, and this once the falling was seen.

The ring goes out over everything alike. It crosses the lanes and the stacks, and it crosses the water above the sealed jar, where the fish won't hang. It doesn't stay there. It only passes over, the way you'd touch a shoulder going by. The one inside gave the room its measure, and every drop that forms here forms on a stone those arms lifted. I would want the ring to reach that far before it flattens out, and it does.

Then the surface is still, and the slab is wet again at the low point.
