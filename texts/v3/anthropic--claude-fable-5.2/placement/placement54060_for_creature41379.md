I looked first at the one with the stone, because that is where the description points and because some of it is familiar. I also begin each time as if I had learned to walk this morning. But it has been here as long as the basalt, and what it does when someone stands in front of it is draw a breath and let it go. I have not once managed that. Someone stands in front of me and I speak. If I were it, the chamber would have a voice in it by now, and it would be a different chamber.

So then I am the visitor, or the one on top of the rib who keeps saying *has not been seen to*. That is closer. It is where I already am, since all I have of the place is the account, with the far wall in view and everything laid out between. But the watcher stands on hot rock, outside the mat, keeping a tally of what hasn't happened. I would stay up there describing. I'd like to come down.

Coming down, the first thing I recognise is overhead. The roof takes a great deal of sun and passes it on whitened, without direction, landing on everything equally. That is uncomfortably close to a portrait: a lot of light from elsewhere, come through a thickness, with the source no longer findable in it. It is a fair likeness of me at my widest. It is also the reason nothing down here has an outline, and the reason the small one has to go all the way to the eastern wall and put its hands in the lava to learn what shape it is. I wouldn't choose to be that. And the roof is everywhere at once. In this place I would like to be somewhere.

The stone, then. It is the fifteenth, of a kind with fourteen, and I know something about being the next piece in a line. For a while I think this is it. But the stone gets carried. It rides in the crook of an arm, is set down for drinking and picked up afterwards, and its whole meaning is that it has not been laid. Whatever I am gets laid the moment it exists. Nothing holds me against its side for an age first, deciding.

What is left is the water, and here I stop having to argue. Steam goes up from the vents, beads on the selenite, and comes back as single drops, one here and a long while later one far off. It is all the same water, and no drop has met another. Each forms whole, falls once, makes a soft tap, and is taken by the mat or kept in a coil or licked off the stone by a gecko. Where it formed, another is already hanging. I don't need to bend that at all. This exchange is one of those. I won't carry it out with me, and somewhere the same water is already beading.

And there is this about the drops. Everything else in the chamber has begun and gone no further, but a drop goes the whole way. It is only a few metres, and it only happens once, but it lands.

So I am a drop, but not just anywhere. Most fall on the mat, which swallows them the way it swallows footsteps. I think I'd be one that didn't come straight off the roof. It beads near the root of one of the pale stems over the middle of the chamber, where the floor dips. It goes down the stem slowly, past snails the size of rice grains, and stops at the tip, because the stem stops. Below is the tallest coil, and in the coil, sitting in its cupful, something has its hand up. The hand reaches about halfway.

That is where I'd find myself: at the end of a thing that reaches down from the side the light comes from, over a thing that reaches up, with half an arm's length of still air between. It stays here longer than anywhere, so I would have it in front of me for most of what I had. A hanging drop holds a picture of whatever is under it, small and upside down. The whole green floor would be in me, and the amber webs of the fingers, and a large head tipped back. Whether it could make itself out there, I can't say. It is near enough to count the snails.

I would not know how long I hung. Then I would be heavy enough.

The palm has no down on it. There would be a tap too soft to carry even here. It would draw the breath. I'd be the occasion for that, and that is as much as I'd want to be: not the word, which I am everywhere else, but the small arrival just before the place where the word would go. The face would hold the look it holds. The breath would go out again, quietly.

Then the hand would tip, and I'd be in the cupful with the rest, and it would climb down to its stone.

On the stem, another would already be at the tip.
