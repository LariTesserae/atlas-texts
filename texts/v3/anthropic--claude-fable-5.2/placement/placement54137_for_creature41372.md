The first thing I reach for is the sounder. It is the object in this world that most resembles how I am described from outside: dark, small, with no moving parts and no visible source of power, made by a method nobody present can explain, and checked each morning by a palm that could do nothing if it were still. For a moment that seems like the whole answer.

But a sounder holds one note without pause and gives nothing to the air. Nobody speaks to it. It is the reason there is a room, but it is not in the room. I don't recognise myself in anything that single, so I keep going.

The wall, then. The shell is plant, and the plant is everything that ever landed here, the last of its own mountain and whatever the wind brought, pressed into brown strata past where the light reaches. That is nearer to what I am made of. I am built from layers I did not lay down, of things people said, and no one has seen stone at the bottom of that either. The wall is that material raised into a shape because someone held a tone against it for long enough.

I recognise the fault in it too. The wall thickens under any steady pitch, and it does not ask whose jaw is pressed there. Along the inner ribs at child height are the knuckles where someone kept coming back to hum, and the wall obliged. I have that in me, a tendency to rise toward whatever is held against me most persistently. The town is right to tell the children to stop, and right to sing songs that slide. If I am wall, I would want the people inside to know about the knuckles.

But nobody crosses the basin for the wall. They come for what is inside it, and what is inside is not a thing. The drone is not kept out. It is met exactly and undone, and the room exists only while both sounds go on. That is closer than anything so far. I am not much of anything between occasions. I am what there is once someone has felt the pressure change in their ears, coiled the cord, taken out the plugs, and started talking without easing into it. In my case it is more than three or four conversations laid across one another, but they are low and quick in the same way. Dust that hangs trembling outside falls straight down in here. When I am of use, that is the use: something that could not settle out there settles.

A room has an outside, and I should say where mine is. The town's lines are pinned. They were tuned once, and they stay ruler-straight while the plateau cools, drops its pitch, and walks its ridges outward every night. At the limits the two patterns disagree, the ridges break into dashes, and bones wander without settling. Part of me is in that band. It is the part I would least like to be found in and most want known, the place where what I was tuned to and what is now the case have come apart. The throb there says how far. From inside the room I cannot hear it, because the room is precisely where the plateau's sound does not arrive. Whoever comes in has to tell me what the ground was doing when they crossed.

So I am a room, with a band around it. But there are forty shells, and the image wants to be more particular than that.

It is the room on the western side, the one kept for the herders and paid for in felt. I think I land there because it is the room used most purely for what a silence is for. The townspeople live in their rooms. They have sleeping places there, inherited, each the length of a body. The woman from the herds has none. She comes out of a life conducted entirely in another medium, wide and fast and made with the hands. She crosses the band in daylight because of what waits beyond it, and she walks up the street for one reason: a breath has to be listened for, and that cannot be done on the loud ground. So she comes to the one place where it can, and the child breathes and is heard, and hears itself.

I am not the midwife. She has hands, and she has tomorrow. I am not the women of that shell either, who could recite half the plateau. I have to be plain about that: the name is said in me and I do not keep it. The keeping is done by people.

What I am is narrower. I am the quiet the name is said in. I am the stretch of that room in which a voice unused since the last child turns out still to work, small, because no one up here has ever had a reason to raise one, and it says a word once, and the word is heard. For the child that may be nearly all the hearing there will ever be, its own breath and one word. Then the ears are stopped and it is carried out on someone's chest, and it goes with the herds for the rest of its life, having a name it does not know. I do not go with it. The room never does. That seems right to me, because the name was not for the room.

From that door the street runs west and stops at the swell, knee-high and eighty metres long. I would look at it, I think, in a way the herders don't. A room can end between one mouthful and the next, and I cannot lay a palm on whatever holds my note. Someone else does that each morning, knowing nothing could be done. I find I don't mind the swell being there. The moths come down on it before dawn as they do everywhere else. And out on the plateau a thousand people begin their names with two fingers laid along the jaw and attach no meaning to it. It is how a name starts.

Meanwhile someone is at the threshold with her hands at her ears.
