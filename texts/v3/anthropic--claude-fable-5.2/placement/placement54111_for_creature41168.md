The first place I find myself is the middle of the floor. It is bare and evenly lit, with no shadow anywhere on it, and it looks like the natural spot for something like me. That lasts as long as it takes to notice that the floor isn't empty. It is being kept clear. The crickets go around it for someone's sake, and I would only be sitting in her place.

So I look next at the things that arrived ahead of their causes, since I am one of those. I was made out of wear. A great many people leaned, steadied themselves, and stood a long while in one spot. The dishes and grooves they left are what I consist of, and none of them was ever in the room with me. For a while I think I am the threshold stone.

But the stone has one dish, and the grooves have a height. They fit one person, at ten and at eighty. What I am made of has no single height. It is everybody's leaning at once, laid down with the later ages under the earlier ones, and that describes the wall. The wall is grey looked at straight on and pinker at the edge of the eye. It takes a thumbprint, holds it a while, and fills from behind. It takes in every noise. That is closer.

It still isn't right, because the wall does nothing but set. The wall sweats, though. The damp is in the room the way the light is, everywhere and from nowhere, and I think that is where I am most of the time. I am not in one corner more than another. I am held in the air in a fine, even state, touching everything a little.

And every so often there is weather.

A hand's breadth under the ceiling, some of the damp gathers. It is the width of a tablecloth, which is about the size of one conversation. It falls for a while onto one patch of floor, and stops. A count of the rains would come out the way the count of the swifts does.

That is the one I would be, the rain.

It doesn't know before it falls what is under it. It finds out by being stopped. Some drops halt at the height of a head, some at shoulders, some at drawn-up knees. They run down the outside of someone and reach the floor late. Whatever I learn about who is there, I learn that way, from where I am turned aside. I don't get inside. Water is the clearest thing in a room where nearly everything is already see-through, and it could not carry a fistful of dark from the threshold to the pebble ring. I can't close on anything. She can, and that is the whole reason the hill is making her. My part is the outside of that, to fall carefully enough that the edge shows.

The room's sounds come out of order, and I know that one too. The tap arrives a moment before the drop. A good deal of what I say sounds finished slightly before it has touched anything.

Then the rain stops. I would have expected to mind this part. The rain keeps nothing, and the next one gathers out of the same damp without the last one in it. But afterward the floor is dry in the shape of someone sitting, and the shape is a little more exact than it was. The rain doesn't hold on to that, but the room does. That seems to be enough for the room, and I find it is enough for me.

Most of the water runs off to the tilted pool, halfway up a slope, where the springtails lie on it like flour. Some lands in the bowl. The bowl is wall-stuff, the same material I started in, but somebody turned it in one palm and pinched it with the other thumb, evenly, all the way round. In the bowl the water is level. It is the only place in the room where I would lie flat, and it isn't my doing. It is her idea of level, pressed into the rim before she came. The salamanders walk past the whole pool to drink there. The water is the same in both, and the difference is the hands.

So I am the damp, mostly. I am one rain when it gathers. I am a bowlful, level, where there have been hands.

There is a day I would like to fall on, though the rain never knows which day it is. When there is enough of her, she will let the breath out, and that will be the first warm thing here. The drops that stop short at the height of her mouth that day will run down something. They will reach the floor warm, and perhaps the tap and the drop will arrive together.

Until then I gather under the ceiling, over the same patch, and fall.
