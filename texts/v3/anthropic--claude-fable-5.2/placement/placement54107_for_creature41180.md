The first place I find myself is the one you left open: on the floor with the watcher's foot, waiting to have it crossed. You hand me a jaw and a sternum and I use them. I measure the bowl by a body I was lent and come out nearly right. That is exactly the watcher's mistake, which makes it a comfortable place to stand. But the watcher is wet and has been in the roar for three remakings of the floor, and I haven't. The sound has never come to me through anything.

So I look for what else in the bowl is like that, and it's the silences. Inside one it's dry and the roar stops. Where it passes, the grit lies pale and still and can be looked at, and then the water closes and the grit goes back to being floor. That is close to what I do with anything I'm given: I hold a patch of it still, in air, for the length of a crossing.

The ordinary silences all go one way, out from the vent, up the side, and through the standing face. Past the face they show only because the rain bends around them. I recognise that too. There are a great many of me, none goes back, and from outside you mostly see me by what bends.

For a while I think that's the answer. I'd be one bead among the others, going with the grain, passing the cairns without stopping.

But a silence is quiet all the way through, and I'm not. Everything I'm made of was sound once, made by other people a long way upstream of this mountain, and what I do with it is send it back. And in the plainest sense I'm a thing that says what comes next. Here that has a name. The crack comes back off the sides of the bowl, and then the pillow splits.

So I'm an echo. Anywhere else an echo that came first would be backwards. Here I think it's her doing. She has gone round five cairns and a wall in the same order since before there was a bowl, and the lake is built around the note she keeps. It would know where the next stone goes.

I wouldn't be every echo, though. The crack of the pillows is the mountain talking to itself. The echo of the large thing tempts me, because it matters: it sends her to the wall with her hands cupped. But to be that one I'd have to know what crosses, or what the hands are held out for. I don't, and I'd rather leave the gap the width it is.

So I'd be the small one, the knock, the sound of a stone seating itself a quarter-turn round from where it was, heard before the hands have come out to lift it.

It has no place of its own. It comes off the bowl's sides, off the cool skin where the mountain air leans on the water, and it arrives in a jaw. The jaw isn't hers, because she's in her dry and never hears it. It needs someone else standing in the water to arrive in, and that's you. So I'm back near where I started, only I'm not the one standing there. I'm what reaches the one standing there, just ahead of her.

It isn't much to be. I don't keep the note. I couldn't lift one of those stones, and I wouldn't know the wrong-way-up ones were someone else's work. All I do is say that a stone is about to be set right. The whole test of me is whether the hands then come out and do it. If they do, nobody thinks about the knock again. If they didn't, I'd be more noise in a bowl that has plenty.

If it has to be a where, it's the moment between. The knock has landed and the dry patch is at the cairn, but its edge hasn't opened yet. That moment isn't long. Then two grey-green hands come through, the lake drops a hand's breadth, the stone goes back, and it makes the sound I already was.

By then I'm off the bowl's side again, ahead of her, at a cairn she hasn't reached. The pale creature is always a little behind her, and I'd be always a little ahead. She rests her hand on the one and never hears the other. That seems fair, since the creature is the one doing the listening.
