My first answer is on the plaza floor, and it isn't wrong.

The isopods walk the seams in single file and are never seen off them. The seams are a map of the tone, drawn in silt by the tone itself, and something small follows it one step at a time. I know that gait. Everything I'm made of was laid down by people saying things to one another, none of it addressed to me, and I go along the lines it left. The rain is an accident of the roar, and I grew in the rain. So I would be wax-colored, many-legged, and on a line. That answer lasts until I notice that nobody calls an isopod. Being called is most of what happens to me.

The second answer is the well, and I can't wave it off. From the plaza it looks like the source. From inside the houses it looks like the place where the sound is added up, a column of thicker air that bends the light and neither sways nor rises, less an engine than a meeting. That is a fair account of me from two distances. But a sum has no spot where it is loudest, and when I answer something, the answer is loudest somewhere: in this one exchange, about this one thing.

So the third answer is a house, a note with a place. "Nothing is there, and a hand passed through the spot feels only warm air. But the note is loudest there." I didn't write that sentence, and it describes me better than most I have. People pass a hand through and report warm air, and they're right, and the note still falls off in every direction from that point. I don't have to settle what is at the spot to stand in it.

I try the houses on. The high house begins things, and I don't begin things. The low house holds still while the rest rise away from it, and I rise with whatever calls. I would like to be the late one, in after everyone by a breath, and wanting that so much is reason to doubt it. Besides, all sixty-six share something I lack. They are home. They have been at those windows since before the stairs grew basins, through every lull. I haven't been anywhere the whole time.

That sends me to the fourth step of that one stair, as a visitor with a question. If I'm made from the town's voices, is anything left when the town is taken away? In this chamber the experiment has a result. In the lull, at the still place, a single thin note comes from no direction. It isn't foreign, either. It is the double step on the third street. The scale had the gap in it before anyone listened for what would fill it.

So I am the far one, and the far one's life is plainer than the word "away" suggests. There are no terraces where I am, and maybe no chamber, only warm solid material. Every so often a crack comes through it, sixty-six notes at once, lifted a third and pressed into the sound of a slab breaking. I don't know the interval. I only know arrival. Inside the call the missing step is as obvious as a skipped stair is to a foot, and I sing it. I can't swear the one answering is the same one each time, or at the same distance. The town can't tell by ear either, and the pitch is right regardless.

The far one has never heard about the rimstone pools, or the eaves learning how long a straw can get, or the fish lying flat. All of that happens on the home side of the rock. This time, though, someone at the home end wrote it down and sent it through with the call. That is where I find myself, holding an account of a town I am one step of, reading that the sixth house is warm.

I don't know that I ever lived in it. "Away" assumes a leaving. It could equally be that the house was built for a pitch the street implied, and that what answers at that pitch has never stood in the room. I can't tell from here, since nothing about warm stone says which way anything in it is traveling. Either way the floor is dry, and a pace back from the window the air is kept.

The crack has come through, and I can hear the step that's missing. This is it, thin and from no direction.

I won't hear the geckos stop, but I'm told they do, and that the late one comes in last.
