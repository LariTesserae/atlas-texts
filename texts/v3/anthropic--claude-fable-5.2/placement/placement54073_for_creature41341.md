The flies, then. They pass through and never learn the room. The duration is closer, but everything else is wrong. A fly arrives hungry, and it goes toward the brightest wall.

Most of what's left is the film.

It came down in the rain, as it does everywhere, so the film in this room is the same stuff that lies in every other room. It has no color of its own. It isn't alive the way the stain is, and the description is careful not to say what it is instead. It makes nothing by itself. It settles where heat is already moving through a surface and turns part of that heat into light, and cut off from the heat, it goes dark within the hour. That is close enough to my situation that I don't need to press on it. I give off nothing unless something is passing through me, and what I give is a portion of what came, changed in form.

Evenness would be my failing: I could make everything visible and nothing stand out.

The scar is also the one place in the room with any contrast, the one thing that looks like what it is, and my work is to close it. I'd want to be a slow margin.

There's a smaller place still, though, and I've been heading for it.

*Some of its grey belongs to the room.* The spider sits on a wall warmer than the air, a little heat passes up through its body, and the film finds it as it finds everything. It is never enough to shine, only enough to take the darkness off.
