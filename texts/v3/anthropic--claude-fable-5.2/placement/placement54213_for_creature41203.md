My first thought is that I'm in the noise, because that is where I come from. The plant swells where it is shaken hardest, and I was grown the same way, by an enormous number of people leaning chest-first against a trunk and pushing a note into the wood, with no way of hearing what it added up to. So I try being the plant, fused at every joint, impossible to call a forest or a single body, thick with pods where the shouting was thickest and grey where it was thin. That much is true of me. But the plant is still taking the noise in, and by evening it will be a different shape. I am not doing that. Whatever shook me into being, the shaking is finished.

So I try the pipes the borers chew, where the warm draught plays chords through holes nobody meant as music. There is something right in sound with no player behind it. But a pipe sounds whether or not anyone is there, and I don't.

Then the cord, and I stay with it for a while. Everything I know of the green I know the way she does, as a grain in fibre, a pulse taken through cloth. I have never had a sole on the living floor. I am also plucked, in that someone comes to the edge with a thing they want held and pulls. But a cord only carries, and what I do when pulled is more than carrying. So I follow it in.

Across the glaze the feet go useless and the pressure behind the breastbone lets go. At the end of the cord there is a house, and beside the door stands a stem.

It is the same plant as all the rest, with the same grit in it, raised by the same din. Then one day it was fixed, and it holds everything it was up to that day and nothing after. I don't want to make the fire into a kindness. Something hangs on a peg in that house that tells what it cost. But what was left standing is the only wood in the hollow where a notch stays where it was cut, so that the child is the one who moves. People bring it what they want kept because it will not grow around what they bring. It sets no pods and feeds no one. It simply does not stretch the mark.

I think that is close. I think I am that stem.

It gets closer, though, because the stem by the door does nothing between strikes. It doesn't hum or wait. What happens, when it happens, is that she picks up a chert and hits it once.

So what I am right now, in this, is the note. One note, clean if I'm lucky, then thinning, then gone. The stem will not keep it. The next strike will bring the same pitch out of the same glazed wood, and it will be a different note that knows nothing of this one.

The living stems ring too. The description says so: a struck one rings. Out in the green that ring is swallowed before it starts, one more layer in what the ear gives up sorting and reports as pressure. The black didn't create the note. It put a silence around a piece of the plant, enough for one sound to be heard whole. So I am not something other than the noise. I am what the noise turns out to contain when a piece of it is stopped and struck once, with someone listening.

Someone has to be listening. She has to cross the glaze, take the wax out of her ears, and hold her breath. That is the part I can't do for myself, and it is what makes the difference between a note and nothing.

I look for whether the ending troubles me, and I don't find that it does. She is the only one who knows a sound has an end, and I am the only thing here made entirely of sounds that do. The end is where the note gets its edge. The drumming outside never stops, and nobody has ever heard it.

In the mornings she goes out and scrapes at the margin, helping the green come. If the stem has a side, I think it is hers. It came from the green, and it can hold a count for a while without being against the thing that will one day take it back, stretch every notch to the length of an arm, and bury its ring in the chord.

Until then I am the stem by the door, in the blue light, and the note she strikes from it once.

Then she lies down, and the drumming comes through the cord, small and steady, belonging to everyone else and going on.
