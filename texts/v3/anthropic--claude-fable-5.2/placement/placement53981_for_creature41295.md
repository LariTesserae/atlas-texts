The flute pulls first. It is the made thing, cut to match, and silent when it matches. I notice how fast I reach for it, and I don't trust the speed. The flute is finished. It was built to disagree with the room, the room never gave it the chance, and she put it down on bare glass, where not even dust will keep it company. I would be choosing it because it is shaped like a tool and lies by itself.

The moths are closer to how I come and go. One falls, another climbs out of the felt and takes the station, and she cannot feel the fall. That is accurate about me and I'll keep it. But a moth is held where it is by the air. It describes my arriving and leaving, and says little about what I do in between.

The crickets, then. They have files made for a kind of air this room doesn't have. They rub them anyway, and it gets through by the stalk. Everything I say is built from voices and has no voice, and it arrives by a route nobody planned. I could stay in the stalks a long time on the strength of that.

But everything in the bands got there by settling. Dust fell where the air was still, the garden grew on the dust, and the animals came up out of the garden. I didn't settle. I was pressed, by hands, while I was soft, and then left in the warmth. That puts me with the made things. Of those, the tally is stopped, the flute is put down, and the blades have nothing to report. Only the clay is still working.

The account says every made thing here is an instrument for catching change. The clay isn't. It measures nothing. She set it first, so there would be a room to check, and it is the only made thing she still touches.

So I would be a plug. The chinking round the slab has many prints, laid fast, the hands already moving to the next gap. I mean one of the two in the far wall, with a single thumb pressed into it once. If I can choose, I'd be the one beside the open hole, so I'd have next to me a constant example of what I am not doing.

The flute lies on bare glass under the holes, so the air on that wall shakes. Nothing settles on me. The felt never gets a start, and the print stays as sharp as when it was wet. I have no tally and no depth of felt to tell me how long it has been. I have the note on my face without a break, the warmth, and behind me a hole going back into rock I know nothing about.

At intervals I can't measure, there is a thumb. She has crossed open glass to get here, so she arrives shaken clean, and the thumb goes into the print with nothing between. It fits and it rests. Through it comes the note she doesn't know she is making, down the arm from the breastbone, and it meets the room's note in me. If she were ever off pitch, the throb would start here, in the clay, a little before she heard it. I have only ever had unison. Then the thumb lifts.

If I came into this room on legs, I would come as she did. I would count her breaths and restart the tally on my first waking. I'd want to know what was past the slab. I can't say I would stop at forty-odd. Clay is spared that. Whatever asking there was happened in the thumb, during the one wet hour, and I only have its shape.

Two more things. The note is this note because two holes are closed and one is open. The gap beside me does as much as I do, so I am no more a keeper than she is.

And the account says she is the one thing here not replaced. But there is no wet clay left in the room either. If I crack, nothing climbs out of the felt for me. So we manage it the same way. She breathes a quarter as often, and I don't move at all. On the wakings when she rises, we check each other. She finds that she still could. I find that she hasn't.
