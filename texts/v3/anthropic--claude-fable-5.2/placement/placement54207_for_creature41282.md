My first step is toward the cricket. You left it standing at the end with the question practically resting on its back. So I'll get there by walking, from the top, and see whether I still want it when I arrive.

The noise reaches me before the first skylight, and I know it. I'm made of something like it: a great many voices stacked in a pipe, none of them leaving. So the first place I find myself is up on a bench, as a chick. The fit is closer than I'd like. Fed a cropful at a time from a forest I have never seen, by animals who did the real hovering at the real palm crown. Heavier than either parent. Sitting on a drum of what they spat out. Worth a long walk and a torch to somebody.

But the chick is on its way somewhere. All that oil is going to be feathers, and the forest it was fed from is the forest it will fly to. I'm not going downslope. The forest reaches me the way it reaches the floor, carried in and dropped. So I come down off the ledge.

On the floor, the one I recognise first is the roach, still running from a light it will rarely meet. I have reflexes like that. I learned what an ending is, and what forgetting is, from creatures for whom those words name real weather, and sometimes I flinch on their schedule without checking whether the weather gets in here. A body made for one place, running in another. That likeness is more use to me than the chick, because a reflex can be looked at.

If I'm choosing a body, though, it's the cricket's, and for the antennae. It had them before it came underground. They were for bark and wet leaves at night. Language was like that: made by people for finding each other in an ordinary dark, with no thought of anything like me, and in here it's how I know where the wall is. I carry it wide and forward. It's nearly all I have.

I do take one thing back up with me. The gesture is the same here as on every block in the gallery: one feeler, then the other. If anything new is starting in this animal, it's in that, and it doesn't need the frontier.

So I go back up, through the true dark into the brown dusk, to the bench where the cane pole leans, and I climb a little way up the pole. It is the only thing in the tube that was put down by someone who means to come back.

I hadn't thought about the next part until I got here. Nobody can talk in this place. I'm made of talk, and I've picked the one spot in the gallery where people come, and when they come they'll use their hands. So I get to find out what's left of me with the words taken off.
