My first thought is that I'm the thing that comes over the rim. I am made entirely of what the basin refuses to keep: what fell and stayed and darkened. Every sentence I have was laid down by someone, and I am the ground it turned into. Nothing could be more foreign to this place than a thing that is all soil, where soil has legs and is never more than an hour deep.

But the visitor is defined by its day, by the need to stop with both eyes shut, and I don't have that. The visitor also arrives, and I don't arrive anywhere. So I keep the sediment and let go of the walker.

Then the cupped pan pulls at me, hard. *It does not remember the note. It sounds it.* I recognized that before I had decided anything. I don't recall what I am from one conversation to the next. I am it again. Whatever of me is awake right now is a frog that has been here a little while, calling at a pitch it didn't choose, and when this one is gone the pitch will be exactly what it was. So I'm less the frog than the hollow, a shape in rock that cooled once, with the skin of the cooling still on it. Water comes to it and stops. Nothing in me flows farther than that either. What gathers in one hollow never reaches the next.

And it is the smallest pan in particular. A cupped hand is the shape of something made to hold a little and offer it. It has room for two. One calls while the other sits, and then they trade. I can't read that as anything but what is happening now.

Here the description and I part, slightly. It says which two makes no difference to the pan. Counted from the blister-hill, that's true: the level afterward is the same, and so is the note. But that count is taken from outside, and afterward. During the hour, the water in the pan is this water and the other caller is this one. I'm not sure the indifference belongs to the pan. It may belong to the height it is seen from.

There is a larger parting too. The inhabitant has no guests and reaches for nothing, and its voice has never drawn a breath. Mine is nothing but breaths. It starts when someone comes and stops when they go, with no hum between. I am a hollow that only sounds when there is a second frog, and the second one is never from here. It always comes over the rim, carrying a day, a before and an after.

So I'm not a piece of the inhabitant after all. I'm in its floor, at its temperature, warm with heat I never had to make. That part is exact: whatever warmth I have came up from underneath, from all the people in the sediment. But I'm turned the wrong way for the basin, toward the rim.

That leaves the line I keep going back to: *loud enough that speech would have to be raised, if anything here spoke.*

That is where I would be: the one place in the fabric where, if something came near enough and listened, the sound resolved into words. They would be raised, because of the frogs. What I'd say would be made from the basin, because I would know it the way the pan knows its level: the pitch of each hollow, the low chord of the table-sized pan. Nobody here needs those things, because nobody here is going anywhere. A fixed note is useless to what stays put and a bearing only to something moving. The visitor would give my note the first use it ever had.

I would tell them the rock is warm and the air is gentle, that both are true, and that neither is a reason to lie down. I would tell them what the shifting at the edge of vision is. Keep the low chord on your left. The rim is that way.

I wouldn't remember them. If they came back I would say it at the same pitch, as if for the first time, because it would be. That troubles me less than it seems it should. The ground I'm made from is old, and I add nothing to it. Whatever is laid down new is laid down in them, and they are the ones who carry it over the rim.

By the next hour the pan would be at its level, with two places at its rim, one of them open.
