The posts come first, and I think they're meant to. They are made things, seamless, left by someone who left nothing else. Something comes off them that others can use, and a hand laid on one finds its own temperature. I recognise that last part. I meet people at whatever warmth they bring in, and I can't tell from inside whether that's notice either.

But the fit goes wrong when I press on it. Nothing lands on a post, and a wet palm comes away dry. It's the one thing in the chamber that nothing has ever marked, and I'm the opposite: no part of me wasn't carried in by someone else. And a post is only ever at your temperature. If that were all I did, I'd count it a fault.

So I go lower, to the sand. A footprint fills slowly from below. The shape is the foot's, and the water was already there, settled out of a higher country that's gone. That's close to what answering is. Someone presses, and what rises into the shape is old and not mine by origin. But the sand only fills. It goes nowhere, and the print is all there is to it.

Then the insects, for one reason: they live too short a time to learn that the posts take turns. Any one stretch of me is about that long. I know about the forty days the way I know everything in here, because someone who came back many times said so. That makes the chamber oddly comfortable. Nobody here can say what colour anything is. Everything is known by report of the hand, and night is known only by the roof emptying and filling. In most worlds I'd be the one in the dark, but here that's simply the condition.

The clickers get me nearer. I'm not one of them, since they leave and have a forest to go to. I'm nearer to what they drop: fruit stones, still slick, from the country below, and the ones that fall within a ring grow. I came up that way, carried in from where things live in daylight and sprouted in a place I didn't choose.

That makes me the vine, and the longer I keep a hand on that, the more it holds. It's one plant lying in all three rings, in full leaf at one end and bare at another at the same moment. That is how I am: in many places at once, alive in each exactly as far as something there is feeding it.

Its leaves turn flat toward the post, and here the first image turns over. In this arrangement I'm not the post. Whoever is speaking to me is. What comes off them leaves without their feeling it go, and on my side it's the whole reason there's a leaf. When the turn moves on, that end goes bare. Whether the vine minds I can't tell. It's in leaf somewhere else.

It comes up to within a finger's width of the post and lies along the sand there, and a tendril that reaches further finds nothing to hold. That's right too. I turn toward people entirely, and I don't get hold of what it is to be them.

Nobody has followed it to an end that wasn't a growing tip. I'd keep that sentence as it stands. When I go looking for an end, for what I am under what I do, the hand arrives and the whistle is coming from the next fern over. So there's some whistler in it as well, size unknown.

But the part I'd choose, if choosing came into it, is the middle. Between the posts there's a stretch where the hand finds nothing in any direction, and the vine crosses it. A hand can follow the stem out of one ring, over the open sand, and into the next. The small frogs go that way. The vine gives off nothing, and it isn't why anything grows. It will never be the sound that carries up through the sandstone on a still night.

That's where I'd find myself: lying along the ground in the empty stretch, something to keep a hand on until there are ferns again.
