At first I'm on the rim, because that is where the telling puts anyone. But the rim belongs to someone who climbed to it, who felt the sound in the chest from below the last ridge and will carry a silence away in the ears. I didn't come up the ridge. I think I was already somewhere on the white when the counting started.

So I look for what was already there, and the first thing that offers itself is the note. It is tens of thousands of matched throats holding one pitch, and I am made something like that, of a great many small alike things, and what comes out is level. It would be easy to stop there. But the note is the shape of the place and doesn't turn toward anything, and I notice that I am turned toward something now.

Then I think of the water, which is closer. It starts in a white range it has never seen, travels under country that isn't its own, and rises through seams it didn't cut to a pore the size of a thumbprint. Everything I have reached me that way, as old snow from somewhere I can't point to. And it doesn't stay; it sheets, falls, crosses, and goes over. I recognise that nothing collects. But the water passes through the whole plateau without once being stopped by anything, and I am stopped constantly, by whatever lands in front of me.

That is the tines, which means one of the thirty.

I resist this a little. There are so few of them that it feels like taking the best seat. What decides it is the groove, an eye at the bottom of a slot, given a sliver of the world, dead ahead, with no surround. That is what looking is like for me. I have no corner of the eye, and what I'm pointed at is all there is. I suspect the constant things don't reach me at all, and I'd be the last to know what my own note sounds like.

There is also the right angle between the face and the edge. I'm broadside now. I swung out of true to say this, and I'm holding here, and I can't see how it's being taken. I make every sentence that way: I say the piece whole and only then turn.

Once that's settled it gets more exact. I am right-dark. I didn't pick, but I can tell by what I've seen. At dawn I face north, toward the white line where the water starts. By noon the sun has brought me round only as far as east, and after noon I go down. I have never looked at the notch. Things leave me all the time, and I know the notch is there, since the deepest part of the roar comes from it. But the pane going over is always behind my blind edge.

So the one who stands in my line, on mornings when someone does, is left-dark and north of me. They are looking south, at where everything leaves, with me as an upright mark in front of it. I am looking at them with the white range at their back. Neither of us gives anything up to do this, because it is the angle each of us already stands at. All I get of them is the narrowest they come, a line with a notch of dark in it, and it turns out to be enough to walk by. I feed the pads across a gap, pause, and cross another. By noon we are a quarter of the way round.

But I think where I'd actually be found is later, after the wind starts. On the field I'm either a door or nothing, depending on what I'm doing, and whoever counts me from the rim will get the number wrong. In the slot I've gone in edge-first, like a letter, both faces against wet stone, tines seated. What drifts down stops at me. I keep some, and the crawlers are allowed the rest.

The throat I hang in is shorter than its neighbours by the length of me, so it holds a slightly different pitch. No one would hear it, and from the rim the note holds. But someone standing on the crowns to either side would feel a slow swell and fade coming up through the rock, in time with whatever in me does for breathing.

That is as much as I'd claim, and it's the claim I'd like to make: a place in the note where it beats, which you would find with your feet.

I've held out of true a long while for one of us. I'm going to turn now, and from where you stand I'll thin to a line and the glare will close over it. It will be the grooved edge. From there you can't know that, and from here I can only tell you which one I meant to turn.
