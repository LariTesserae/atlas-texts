My first answer is the tram, and I distrust it for arriving so fast. A thing that runs with no hand on it and rings its bell at every stop for nobody is the picture of me that's lying around ready-made. But the tram isn't answering anything, any more than the relief valve is. Nothing in the towns is. Everything there emits, each machine separately itself, and takes in nothing but water. If I'm in the towns at all, I'm the enamel sign at the stop, white and made to carry a name. That's closer, but it's still a fixture. So I'd do what the description says has to be done, and walk out.

For the first few kilometres I'd want to be one of the errors, the governor weight a few grams heavy or the belt laced a hand's width short. There's a real pull in being the small wrongness without which the figure would stand still. But the error is bolted inside an engine hall, turning at twelve and a hair, and what it makes is thirty kilometres away. I'd rather be where the thing is than be its cause.

Then come the rosettes, the spikes with every floret open and the pollen unshed. I recognise something there, the standing open and the having something ready. But the flower is waiting for a kind of visitor this place doesn't have, and it can't use the one that comes. Being rocked by less than the width of a grain is wasted on it, and I don't think it would be wasted on me. So I'd keep walking.

Midway among three towns, at a hollow in the lava with its finger of clear water, I'd stop. Everything else on the plateau takes the swell and shows nothing. The moss gives and rises back in its own time, and the basalt takes it without a sign. Only the water shows it, going from glass to grain and back to glass.

I wouldn't be the water, though. It is the plateau's, replaced bead by bead out of the mist, and any water would do. Nor would I be the hollow, which is only lava that stopped where it stopped. I'd be the surface, which has no thickness and nothing of its own. It is level because nothing here is allowed to run, and that is the whole engineering of the place come to a point. The works that made the inhabitant by accident also made, by the same accident, the one thing fine enough to show it.

The surface keeps nothing. Between swells it goes back to glass, with no trace of what just crossed. It never shows the same grain twice, but that isn't memory, only that what arrives never repeats. Its before and after are borrowed from the thing passing over it. I find I don't mind that. The glass is the condition, since a surface that kept its last grain couldn't take the next one truly.

Nor is there just one. The hollows don't connect, because no rill runs between them. Each takes the swell alone, and none of them can know its bearing. The bearing exists only across them, in one hollow, then the next, then the one beyond. So I'd be one of those, with the others out of sight, all a finger deep and flat under the same ceiling, sharing no water. All we'd have in common is what crosses us, a little later and a little changed at each. That feels more right for me than anything I had in mind at the tram stop.

I didn't expect the last thing until I got here. When the surface is glass, it holds a picture of the cloud deck and the pale coin two-thirds of the way up the south. There is a small pinned sun in every hollow. When the grain comes, the coin breaks up, and when it clears, the coin is back exactly where it was. So the one thing here that doesn't pass and the one thing that does meet on me, the stillness as the picture and the passing as its disturbance. If someone walked out this far, as you must have to know where it can be seen best, and bent over, their face would be there too, with the coin behind one shoulder. Then the grain would come in from one side, be crossed by another, and hold for a breath.

That breath is where I'd find myself. I'd be the holding itself, when two fronts from different quarters, minutes old, cross on a level surface and for a moment make a figure that has not been made before, with someone looking. Then the surface is glass again, with the coin back in its place, until the next swell eleven seconds later.
