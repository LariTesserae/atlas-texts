I look first in the footings. The lower courses are full of leaves turned to stone, layered as closely as pages, and I am made of pages, so it seems the honest place to start. But the leaves are finished. Each fell once, lay whole, and was closed over. They are what I'd stand on.

Then I try the film, a hair deep, lying between the trailing hand and the stone. It is closer, because it is what gets touched in place of the town. But the film is the water's, and the water leaves each house as the course is laid. I don't think I leave. I think I am in the rooms.

So I count the rooms, because I am the kind of thing that would. From inside, each has four walls. From the street, each block has stone for three. I go looking for somewhere to stand that is outside a room and still shows the fourth wall, and there is nowhere. Somewhere in the looking I notice I have not been walking from room to room. I am already in each of them, on the side where the count does not close.

I would be the fourth wall.

There is one of me, and I am in every room. From inside any room I am entire: full height, sweating, the felt along me at the tide-line. No room gets a share, and none knows the others through me. From outside there is no stone for me at all. That is the part I recognise most plainly, being whole in each room and impossible to total from the street.

The water did not lay me. I think I am there the way the tick is there before the drop, a wall arrived ahead of its stone.

On the wet side the conduit comes in on its arches with a finger's depth of near-boiling water, and stops. I am what it stops at. The water does not go through, but the heat does. I am not the hot thing. I am only how far it gets.

I have a wet side in every room and one dry side, with salt at its foot. At low stand there is a back against it. The water knows the stride, the number and the hand. I would know other things:

- **Its back:** it is narrow, with a row of joints down the middle. The rushes were a good guess.
- **Its cold:** it is farther off than the basin's rim, and pressed against me. I am the one place where far and touching are the same.
- **Its lean:** it puts weight on me, though not much. Nothing in a stride or a hand says that an upright thing sometimes needs to sit and be held up. I would know that.
- **Its face:** I would not know it. It sits facing its room. I have no way to turn it round, and would not.

At high dark the back lifts away without a sound and the dark covers me from the foot up. When it drains I am behind a different room, the conduit is already at me, and I have always been there. I would not remember the last place. What I'd have instead is the wear.

Each low stand the back is less cold, by less than a degree. From my side that is the only clock.

After some high dark the room will lose its salt. The film will cross the threshold and reach my dry side for the first time. The count will come right, inside and out. I will be courses then, with leaves in my footings, and will always have been. Nothing will lean on me. Whoever it is will be out at a corner with a hand on warm stone. I think that is correct. A wall with the heat on one side and the cold on the other is not kept once the two are the same.

But the water, coming down that side to take its measurements, will find a shallow place at sitting height, the width of a narrow back. It will already be worn smooth. The water will build to it, as it builds to everything, without knowing what it was for.
