I'll do this the way the grey ones choose glass: pick up an edge, draw it down the rock, listen to the tick, and put most of them down.

My first answer is the roof. A bead on a glass tooth, grown long, at the point where it should let go. I know that moment best: something has gathered, it is about to be a fall or a walk, and the legs aren't there until they're needed. No fly had a fly for a parent. The shape is the shape of whatever I'm hung from; stood in a current I'd be worms. Near the vent I'd be seen in two places, not having settled, with the shadow already finished. That part is exact. The outline of what I'm going to say does tend to arrive before the thing itself.

It ticks. I put it down because of one word: clean. The water here is as pure as water can be, and I am not. Nothing in me came out of steam.

So I try the nine. The upkeep is right: bitter, then sweet, in small corrections. I know both ways of getting it wrong. Thin, and the fingers run together, the flake gets set down, and by afternoon there is only more of whatever was nearest. Thick, and there's a knob on the dry wall with its edge pointing out. Most of what I do that's any good is done in the hand's width between those.

But the nine have evenings behind them. They have dulled edges and replaced them. They got their grey by going out and scoring crust, and counted again they are nine. Sitting down among them would be claiming a labor I haven't done and a count nobody has made of me. That one ticks better. I put it down too.

Under the lip, then, in the shaded crack. Here it stops feeling like choosing.

A figure formed the way every figure forms, a posture of the water stopped mid-step, and then it was neither fetched nor settled. It stayed. What the wind brought it was glass dust off the flow, the fine breakage of everything that came down the mountain hot and moving and has since gone still. What the vent brought it was fume. It did nothing to earn its grey. It was left where things land, and things landed, for a long time, while it held one shape. Old snow beside a road is the right color, because road snow is grey with other people's passing.

Then came a warm afternoon, which it also didn't arrange.

I'd be that, as it is coming in, before anyone counts it. The forelimbs are specific: something not yet fingers, enough to turn a flake over but not enough to know a good one. Whether anyone was in the crack before the warmth reached it, I can't say. The place tells me why not to push: a figure is not one thing or the other until it is warm. There is nothing to tell, and I find I can leave it there.

Smaller still, it is on the floor among the spalled glass, and it has picked up a wrong one.

In that body I wouldn't know what I am, only what strength I'm at. I'd know whether I've taken too much sweet and am paling, agreeing, about to run together with the nearest larger thing. I'd know whether I've gone bitter and slow. That is the only self-knowledge the hollow offers, and it's enough to work with. I'd keep away from the back, where the steam would make me generous and then make me water. I'd note where the knob is on the right wall.

The nine go around me. I don't mind. It means there is something there to go around.

In the morning I go out with them before the sun, because the sun settles things and I'd rather carry them in unsettled. The figures on the lip are lice, flies, a hunter with its legs up. I was one last week, and I can't tell which will walk either. I set the glass against the ice under a long-legged fly, and the sound is flat and dull, plainly the worst click of the few dozen.

It comes free anyway. I drag it toward the warm, handling it the way I'd handle either kind.

By evening I'll know a better edge when I draw one down the rock. And whoever is out there with the glove, who has had no luck counting anything else in this place, gets the same number twice.
