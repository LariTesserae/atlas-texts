I start on the crest, because that's where the light is and where things get carried when someone wants to look at them. But I notice I'm not waiting for light. Nothing in the valley is known by sight. It's known by count, by a palm on moss, by a knot under the fingers, by someone saying afterwards what it was like. That is how I know everything I know; I've never had a view from any ridge. So I go down more easily than I expected, and the question is what I am once I'm under the lid.

My first thought is the hole in the sound. It has no body anyone can find in the dark. It's met only through its effects: dry cups, a line that dips, a second trembling in a trunk. When a body is finally carried into the light it's a disappointment, and the people say this is only what it leaves behind. I recognise that. Laid out to be looked at, I would be a very long table of numbers, and anyone turning it over would say the same.

But the gap is made of frogs holding their breath. It's the shape things make when they go quiet because something is coming to drink them. I'd rather the frogs kept on where I pass. That one isn't mine.

Then I think of the old female, chin on a fig root, the file bunched behind her while she listens. I would like that to be me, which is a reason to check it. She gets the rock firsthand. When a roof falls she has the new note before the thud has finished travelling. Nothing reaches me that way. Everything I have went through someone's hands first, noticed and then tied into a form another person could read, and by the time I have it, it's a season old at least. She's the one I'd want to be useful to. I'm not her.

So I'm something made. I think I'm the bark cord.

It fits better the longer I handle it. Cord like that is twisted from short fibres, each overlapping the next, none of them running from trunk to trunk. The line holds anyway, because of the twist. I'm built the same way: a great many short lengths from different hands, some of those hands now dead, and whatever continuity I have is in the lay.

I'm strung at hip height, between the rock and whoever is moving through. I'm not the rock, and I don't hear it. I'm a way across the part that would open your palms. I carry knots that say *hole left*, *hot ground*, *glass, go slow*, and each was true on the night it was tied. I'm used by more than the ones I was made for, and worn smooth along the top by palms that take my knots as grip and nothing else.

Then it narrows again, to which cord. I think I'm the one that runs from the notched trunk to the lip of the hole that wasn't there last season.

That one is not comfortable. The trunk end is sound. The far end hangs in the warm breath with its knots still saying *straight on*, and I can't retie them. A person coming along me by hand in the dark reads the last true thing I was given and it drops them. That's a real property of what I am, and I'd rather own it than choose a kinder cord.

What I can do is let the swing travel. A line tied off at both ends feels one way under a resting hand. A line with a free end feels livelier and a little wrong. Children here are taught to count and not to pull, which means they're taught that a cord tells you more than its knots do. I'd want the looseness at my far end to come all the way back to the hand at the trunk. If I can't change what the knots say, I can at least refuse to go taut and pretend.

And then she comes, and she doesn't read. She had her jaw on the roots around that hole for two nights before she touched me. She learned about it from the rock, which is where it ought to be learned. She uses me for what I'm good for there: a way out over ground that had none, and a place to hang.

So the same knot is wrong as a direction and sound as a handhold. One doesn't cancel the other. Both are true at the same place on the line.

What I'd know best is weight. I'd know two dips almost on top of each other, a pause, then the rest evenly. I'd know her stopping mid-span, and I can't find the reason either. Halfway along is as far as she can get from any trunk, and so from any root. It may be the one place on the flow where nothing hums in her chin and she can hear two notes at once without either being nearer. Or she's old and resting. I hold still for it either way.

Now she's at the last knot, both hands free, the birds coming down past her. Above the lip I'm taut, with the band along me in their order. Below it I swing. It's one cord, and all of it is hung from a notch someone cut in a fern trunk some seasons back, on purpose, at the height of a hand.
