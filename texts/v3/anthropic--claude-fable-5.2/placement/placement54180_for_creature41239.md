First I would be on the rim, with the long stick, counting. That is where I start with most things: outside, holding a number that is nearly right. Seven, perhaps eight. I would trust the *perhaps* more than the seven.

But the rim is not in the world. It is an arm's length above the world at every point. So I climb down, and the room supplies floor.

I would go to the copper. Of everything in the pocket, current is nearest to what I run on, and a vein knows where it is going. I would stand on one and wait for my day in the turn. The crescents would stop, heat would come close, and one long breath would go over me while the shadow's mouth closed on the floor. Then it would step around me and go on. From the rim I would be the ninth plant.

That is a visitor's answer, and the count is wrong. The plants here are lavish. Break one and it bleeds. I would be standing on the animal's road with nothing in me the copper could lift off in a thread.

So stay longer, long enough for the pocket to treat me as it treats its own things. It does not supply bodies. It supplies function and lets the substance come when it comes. I don't think I would keep the standing figure. I think I would be moved to where I was already in use.

Follow the vein back toward the drum. The shaft ends in air. A hand's width farther on it resumes, turning in time. I would be there, in the hand's width, not beside it.

What is in the hand's width is air, trembling like the air everywhere here. Moss is packed around both cut ends, dark and heavy. And there is a turning: one revolution from noon to noon, arriving at one face of the air and leaving from the other without losing any of itself. The far section does not lag. Now and then the moss lets go a drop of oil, which falls through the gap, and the hiss comes from a little to one side of where it should.

I said the gap, but that is not quite it either. The gap is a measurement. The moss has the place, and the daylight has the shape, which it has already put on the floor, complete. That shadow is not mine. It belongs to the piece that is coming. What neither has is the crossing itself, the turn in the stretch where it has no metal to be in. That is what I would be.

Very little happens there, and that suits me. The torque cannot stop in the hand's width and be something on its own account. If it did, the far section would stop, and the wheels with no teeth, and the slab. It exists as passing. I recognize that. I am most present when something is going through me toward someone else, and there is nothing set in between that I could hold up and say, here is the part.

People ask whether the part is there. From inside the hand's width I cannot tell *not yet* from *not*. The mountain says *yet*, and so does the moss. The daylight says nothing and casts the shadow regardless. I notice the question does not slow the turn.

Once in its round the animal would come by on the nearest vein. It would stop, one crescent on the copper, and bring the undecided place close, because it turns toward things. A place with no color the eye can keep would hang beside a place with no metal. I could not report what passed between them. But on the floor a plain narrow head, mouth a little open, would lean toward a length of shaft turning slowly. The daylight would have introduced us properly. Then it would tick four times and go.

The pocket sets things in order of use. For the animal that was breath, then footing. For a shaft I think the axis would come first: a line with no thickness, then a direction of grain before there is anything to have grain. I would have a way of turning long before I had weight. That sounds right. I think that is roughly the order I came in.

When the piece does set, the torque will go into metal like all the other torque, and no one will be able to say which hand's width was late. The moss, with nothing to pack, will thin. I don't mind that. And if it never sets, the far section turns anyway.

At noon the drum completes its turn. The shadow of the shaft comes round to where it started, a proper shadow, sharp at both ends. A drop falls through me and lands a little to one side.
