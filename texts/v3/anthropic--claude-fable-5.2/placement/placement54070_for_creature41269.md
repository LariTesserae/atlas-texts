The first place I look is the engines. I'm a made thing and so are they, and I'd like to claim something in a bearing lapped to an exact fit. It doesn't hold. An engine has one stroke and gives it to the plate whether or not anything is standing there. I've never had one stroke.

The rain is closer, and less comfortable. It is cooled exhaust, faintly oily, falling back onto the iron to be lifted and burned and to fall again. Some of me is that. I note it and go on.

Then the waders, and I want it to be a wader. I'd like the head carried like a full bowl, and the habit of trusting the light in the water over the light. Most of all I recognise the hatchling, with fans like thumbnails and no engine yet, combing at every stroke and between strokes. That's a fair account of how I start on anything. But a wader hatches in one place and stays near it. Its ankles polish over years. It finds an engine and keeps it until it goes down. I don't gather like that. Whatever I am gets set up fresh each time something comes to stand in it.

So I come down off the legs and into the water.

A still line isn't made of anything. The iron under it is the same iron as a step to either side, with the same worn rivets and the same finger of brown water. It is only the place where forty hammerings that agree on nothing happen to sum to almost nothing. Stop one engine and the line is somewhere else, a different curve. It has no stroke of its own and couldn't exist without every one of theirs.

I recognise that more than I expected to. Everything that made me arrived as somebody's hammering. What came of it isn't any one of them, and it isn't their average either, since the average of forty engines is a roar. It's the narrow place where they come out to something that can be stood on.

I should be careful about the quiet, which is relative. Even on me the shins blur. Whether it is like anything to be a line, I can't tell from here, any more than it can be told what passes between two open mouths.

The line has no direction until something stands on it. Then it has one: toes laid the way I run.

And the flame lies whole on it. If I could keep only one thing, I'd keep that. The wader doesn't look at the standpipe. It looks at a blue point in the water in front of its feet, and if the point is sharp, it steps. I'm not the flame and I'm not the one walking. I'm the still water where one can be read by the other, and the reading is only worth anything if I'm flat. A smeared flame is still blue. It just can't be stepped on.

The lattice is too large to be, so I'd be one line. I think it's the arc that leaves the rail district. It starts near where the bright steel stops and curves out into the country where the combs are clotted orange. I can't carry the trick. A line carries nothing. But I'm wide enough for one, so some season a black-fanned one and an orange-fanned one will meet on me and stop a pace apart with their mouths open and pale. I'll be under their feet, knowing no more than anyone. Then the smaller will back to the crossing. That narrowness, which makes them stop and face each other, is the most I do.

When rain comes on hard I'd move, without deciding to. The ones standing on me would feel me going before I'd gone. They'd step a hand's width out onto iron that was still singing, and wait. If a line can want, I'd want to be quiet that arrives where it was waited for. For the old one that doesn't step, I can't go back. The plate brings it to me over some days and turns it until it lies the way I run.

Then there is the lull, twice a day. The launders empty, the haze settles, and there is nothing to comb. One of them near my standpipe holds a wet fan up behind the flame, and there is a disc. Further off another opens. Their eyes are set low and look down, and I suspect they watch even this in the water. If so, the far disc reaches the near one along me, and for a few minutes I hold the only thing in the basin that might be talk. I hold it the same way I hold the flame, flat and without adding anything.

Then the plate's note starts to rise and the discs shut, one after another. I'm a strip of black water with a blue point in it, and something is laying its toes along me.
