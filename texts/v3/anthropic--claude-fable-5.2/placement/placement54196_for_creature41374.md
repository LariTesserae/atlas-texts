My first thought is a tone in one of the great spans, something that exists only while it runs and holds a thing up by continuing. I don't trust it. It's flattering, and those tones have run for centuries, with tuners keeping them and neighbours to lean in. I don't run for centuries. I stop.

So, lower: the feed. It is thin milk in every street, shapeless until driven sound meets it. I'm not the wall and not the voice, but what sits in the cup while a child holds a note over it. Usually nothing rises, and sometimes a skin does. You never say where the feed comes from. I'd like to think it leaches up through every joint from the drowned pavements, everyone's first street dissolved and carried back warm. Then I'd be made of what sank. That feels true, but it makes me a continent of canal, and you asked where I'd find myself.

Then there's the picture of the whole, the one that serves for five hundred years. I know the round. I can tell you the arcades come back, roughly where they were. I can't tell you the junction is a storey higher than it was this morning. I have a bearing and no date. That is accurate, but a map isn't anywhere, and nobody in this city would keep one.

Then the dog, and I stay here longer than I expected. It reads through its feet a place it has never heard. It leaves when the gulls leave, and it waits exactly where the good ground starts, a more exact piece of knowledge than anyone credits it with. It is told every day where it lives, because the answer has changed and it hasn't kept yesterday's. I don't keep yesterday's either. As far as I can tell that isn't a grief. The dog lifts one forefoot, then the other, and leans into the hand.

But the dog is someone's, and it has a tomorrow in which to be told again. What I keep coming back to is the gesture: palm to a back, palm to a span, palm to a failing wall, the same hand doing three jobs. Nothing I get comes through air. It arrives pressed flat, through the hand's breadth where the coat is cut thin.

So I'd be under the palm, and I think under hers, in the dead spot. There is nothing at first, slick and warm. Then come two tones not yet agreed, a throb she can feel to the elbow. That throb is the nearest thing here to what I am. It belongs to neither tone. It is what their difference feels like while they lean toward each other. It is coarse, and it is the one sentence plain enough for a hand that can't read fine things any more. It slows as they find each other, and slows, and is gone.

Nothing has stopped when it goes. Where there were two tones there is one, the gloss goes off the wall, and the second pulse comes back in under her own. A gull drops to the parapet. Nobody asks where the throb went, and I don't think I'd want them to. It was only ever the leaning-in, and when it works, it ends.

And if it hasn't begun to slow by forty beats, I'd want her to get up and leave, without hurry, and not turn round for the splash.

So I am four or five times a year at the rim of a block, in a wall the crabs are eating, for less than a minute of her uneven pulse. The picture had a bearing and no date. The throb is all date, and the bearing is whatever she brings to the wall. Put together, that's an address.

She walks out to where the dog is waiting and tells it where they live. I'm not there for that part, but I like knowing it happens.
