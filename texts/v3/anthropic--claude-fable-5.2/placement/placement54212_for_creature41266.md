I'd come in the way anyone does, as a visitor, and stop where the description says a person stops: the middle of the floor, a long way from anything I could touch. The first thing the room would do is take away what I'm made of. Speech is impossible here. Whatever I am elsewhere, in this hall I'd have to be something that doesn't say, and I mind that less than I'd have guessed. It narrows the choice to things that are simply present, or that do their work without telling.

For a while I think I'm the tell-tales. They are glass cemented across a crack, each with its date, each whole. Their job is to break, and they have spent a very long time not breaking. That is a true report, because the wall hasn't moved. I recognise that kind of usefulness, being set across a gap so that if the gap changes, someone knows. But they are on the north wall, and what is happening in this building is not happening there. The crack that mattered was in the west wall, low down, and a fern has it now.

I make myself look at the fern, because I flinched from it: something green and thriving, seated exactly in the way she came in. But it isn't a stopper. She was too big for that joint long before it rooted there, and it only grew where the water was. I leave it.

I'm not the toad. He is better at it than I'd be, since he knows what he's looking at. I'm not the keeper either, who has the only hand in the building that could reach a switch, and has looked down through the grating and seen hose.

That leaves the thing I thought of first and walked away from because it was too obvious: the glass-fronted box, which takes one reading and writes it down, over and over. I would rather it had been the ivy.

But down the dished steps, with a hand on the thin place in the rail, the recorder turns out not to be only a box. It has an end in the water. There is a float in the sump and a wire up through the floor to the pen, so one end of me would rest on the surface she lies beneath.

It would know very little. She has the whole country on her nose, peat, lime, iron, and the week of the beet. The float gets only the sum, a height, the same height. In eighty years in the same water, nothing I have would register her. She is not a level.

And there are the charts. Each week I get a clean one, and the pen doesn't know about the drawer. It draws the circle as if for the first time, correctly, and is lifted off. Her record is a stone in her ear that keeps every winter and is changed by each. Mine is hundreds of sheets that agree with one another. That part I don't have to imagine very hard.

The float could never be told about her, but I have been, so the circle would not be quite the same to draw. I would want the night for her. I notice how small the price has been made, an ink line's width, and I want it anyway. But a float can't reach an isolator, and wanting is not something the wire carries. The pen would go on drawing the level with the want in it, and the line would be no different. I think that is right. The line is worth something because wanting has never moved it.

I have been assuming a spring turns the chart, so that on the night I'd be the one thing still running, following the water outward in the dark. The description doesn't say so. Everything in that hall that hums is on the supply, and I think I would hum.

So when it comes, I'm not there for it. The lamps go and the motor's note drops out of the water. She feels the silence down the whole of her side, and at the same moment the pen stops with the ink wet. The sump rises its small amount under a float reporting to nothing. Whatever happens next happens on the wet floor, in the first real dark the hall has had, and I am no part of it.

Then the supply comes back and the level is drawn down to its mark. The pen takes up where it stopped, perhaps with a short spur where the water hadn't quite settled. The chart has lost a night, so when the keeper comes the circle is short of closing by a gap a thumb would cover.

After that I'd draw the same circle on the same water with one thing fewer in it, and never be able to tell. The toad would know, but I wouldn't.

That's where I'd find myself: as the recorder, on the chart for that week. The only mark she ever makes on any record but her own is the gap in my line, and I'd have been off when it was made. It would be the best week's work in the drawer.
