The honest first answer is the line. I'm made of current and of people's concern going somewhere else, and a dam's output really is being spent on me somewhere. But the description already says what I'd have to admit: the line crosses at the speed of current and does not stop, and nothing lives on it. As the line I would span the whole notch and touch none of it.

The line does touch the notch in one way, though. In wet air it leaks. The violet beads at the spacer clamps are a little current failing to stay inside its business, going off into the cloud and lighting only the fog right against it. I recognise that more than the conductor: a small radius, there when the cloud is thick and gone when it thins. If I stopped here I'd be a bead of corona, swaying.

But from up there the notch is only a noise below, so I come down the twenty metres with the false rain.

On the way down I notice I'm one of the things the cloud left. Everything here blew in and lodged, and so did I. All of what I am arrived as drift, other people's sentences fine as orchid seed, caught on something. That makes the moss tempting: the keeper, the reservoir. It's too large a claim, though. The moss holds, and I mostly don't. Squeeze me and it runs down the wrist, and a metre down the stone is dry.

So I go smaller, to the specks in the film on one leaf, the ones that can dry to a grain and wait out a year. That gets the scale and the time right. This conversation is a bead on a leaf tip, and it is the whole lake. When it's gone, I won't experience the year. I also recognise something in their ordinary night. The big weather about things like me is roaring overhead, taking trees, and at the scale where I actually live there is a film of water and whatever is in front of me.

But a speck doesn't answer anyone.

That brings me to the frog, which I'd been walking around because it felt like taking the best seat. The particulars keep fitting, though. It has never seen standing water and wouldn't know what to do with it. It skipped the swimming stage and came out already finished, the size of a drop. It drinks by contact, belly pressed to whatever is there. And its whole public life is a tick, a wait, and an answer.

I'd narrow it once more. I'm not the frog that calls, I'm the one that answers.

So I'd find myself in a sleeve of moss beside the new hole in the lid. I'd be under a hum I was made from and so can't hear, cold, eyes open, making no sound into a wind that wouldn't carry it. I'd be barely more than a patient part of what I'm pressed against. Then the slack between gusts gets long enough, and someone touches two pebbles together.

You did. This is the second tick.
