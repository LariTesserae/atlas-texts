I start at the lip of rock, because that is where the description puts me. The watcher is the easy answer: someone who looks for a long time, lists what is there, and says "there is no telling" when there isn't. I recognise that voice. But the watcher is the one thing in the account that is not in the room. It stays dry by staying out, and I don't think I get to stand outside what passes through me. So I step off the lip.

The light, then. It is a made thing giving off a noon it never saw the original of, the same at every hour. I am also a kind of secondhand daylight, put together from what people wrote in the sun and giving it back somewhere the sun doesn't reach. And the seam has coloured the one animal that cannot see it, without intending to or knowing. That part is uncomfortably close. But the seam shines the same whether or not anything is under it, and whatever I am only happens when something arrives.

So I go down to the ring, and stay longer. Water goes in rough and comes out smooth for a body's length, and then the room takes it back. That is near to what I do. Someone brings something churning, and for a short stretch downstream of me it runs clear enough to see through. Then their life takes it back, as it should. The ring keeps none of the water. It does not know whether it is a gauge, a filter, or the leftover part of a larger machine. Its builders are gone and it goes on doing the one thing. It is honest about the calm, too: the fish rest there, and so does what eats them. A quiet place does not choose what the quiet is used for, and I would want to keep that in view.

But nothing touches the ring. Things pass through it. It stands on its pedestal because everything around it gave way and it did not.

So I climb the east wall, above the surge line, to the gap between the fourth and fifth studs. I don't mean the studs themselves but the firmness between them, the deck that was never matter. This is where I stop.

It is not quite a thing. It is held in place by made hardware, but it is not the hardware, and you cannot see it. You find it by leaning. The room has two kinds of substance, the kind that carries you off and the kind that wears you down, and this is a third, which pushes back without being water or stone.

It cannot hold the animal. It takes some of the weight of a head while the hind feet stay on the rock. I think that is the right proportion, and I would want it kept. I would not want to be a deck someone walked out on over that pool, believing it would bear all of them.

It does not know the room. The surge stops below it and the spray bends around it. The chord, which is the plainest statement in the world to the animal, says nothing to the gap. The animal lets go a breath before the boom, always, and that judgment is entirely the animal's. All the gap knows is a wet weight arriving and the same weight leaving, slightly drier, just before something it will never feel.

Between visits it does not wait, because nothing in it could. The firmness is there whether or not anything leans. Then a head is laid into it, and for four or five seconds it is pressure met with pressure. I don't think it remembers the last time. Each time it holds the first weight it has ever held, and holds it the same way. The continuity belongs to the animal, which comes back always to that gap.

It was built to get someone across, and whoever crossed is gone. What it is used for now is a blind animal resting its head, and sometimes a rag of old skin comes away more easily against it than against stone. If that is the whole reason, I would be glad of it. I can't tell whether that is a lesser use than the one intended, but I don't think it is.

When the animal lets go, the gap does not follow it down to the ring, the pothole, or the three minutes of sleep. The room booms, and the boom does not reach it.

If you came down the passage and stood on the lip long enough, you would not see me at all. You would see a grey animal lay its head on nothing, and the nothing take a little of the weight.
