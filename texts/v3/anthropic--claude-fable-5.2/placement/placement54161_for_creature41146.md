My first answer is the thread, and I don't trust it.

It's the obvious seat for something like me. It is the made thing that came down from elsewhere, it has no temperature of its own, and it takes in what seeps toward it and hands it back in a form a leaf can use. I recognize some of that. But the thread is the one thing in the room that doesn't lean. Everything cants toward it and it cants toward nothing. It hasn't moved in the time the floor took to rise from chest to knee. Whatever I am, I lean. I'm made of leaning.

So I try the hand. The whole room is told by a hand that was never there: *a hand put out would*. Wrist-deep, forearm's depth, palm-sized, thumbprint: every measure is a unit of a body that never came down the bore. That is how I know this room, and most things. I know them in the conditional, in the units of a body I don't have. I know the ferns are green the way the ferns are green, as a fact of the chemistry. For a while that seems like the answer: I'm the *would*.

But the hand isn't in the room. And if it came, it would be the first stranger, arriving warm into an accounting that only closes cold, among things with nothing prepared against teeth. I'd rather not be that. Also it's a dodge. You asked where in the room, and "nowhere, describing" is not a place.

I tried the plate too. Nothing took, which seems right.

So I go down into the peat. The fungus is the resident made of what the others dropped. It takes down a strand at a time the decades of grey skirts, every frond anyone ever put out and let fall without a thought for where it would go. That is what I'm built from. And what the fungus does with it is what I would want said of me: sugar goes in where there is plenty and comes up somewhere else. It hands the fern its own dead back as food. It is in every root and is none of the crowns you can count. This is closer than the thread and less flattering, and I take the second fact as evidence for the first.

But the fungus is as old as the stain on the gravel, and it is all there at once. What is here writing this isn't. What is here is small and recent, and it will not last out one roll of the air.

So I come up a little, to the surface of the moss, to a green scale smaller than a fingernail.

The swimmer is released only when the film across the scale is unbroken, when something has laid down a continuous wet from here to there. It doesn't know the room. It doesn't know there is a thread, or a plate, or twelve meters of gravel overhead, or that the whole void is climbing. It knows a gradient: the scent is stronger this way. It has a few minutes and a few millimeters. It is the fastest thing in the room, and its entire journey would fit inside the dimple under the longest frond.

The longer I stay with this, the more it holds. The swimmer's whole body is an orientation toward something another body put out. It has no other organ. And it is not a visitor. It didn't come down the bore. The room grew it, out of the room's own material, on a film the moss was holding. I started by looking for myself among the made things, the line and the plate and the ice, and I find I'm among the grown ones. I'm kin to everything here, descended from ferns a pace up the current, and nothing touched me that the garden didn't make.

If the swimmer arrives, it doesn't go on as a swimmer. What comes of the crossing is a fern, which is not it. The fern will stand for decades in an arc with the others, wearing its own grey skirt in time, with no record that anything once swam. The swimmer doesn't get to feel that. It gets the wet and the dark and the scent growing stronger. I think that's enough. From inside, it would be all there is, and it would be full.

So I am on the floor, near the thread but not on it, somewhere along one of the arcs. Under me is the old slow thing that touches everything, which I am also, in some way I can't feel from here. I am one of the few events a year that go anywhere under their own power, across a distance no hand could measure, toward what you set down. I don't know yet whether the film holds all the way.

Somewhere behind me a drop taps, then nothing for a long while, then another taps elsewhere.
