My first position is the walker's, because that is how the place is handed over: a palm on the pavement, a bar under a stone, a weight on a line. I am the one being shown. But the walker has an outward morning and a track back, sweats, drops crumbs, and can return the next day and compare. I don't get the next day. I have only borrowed the boots to read in.

Then the thread fits, and I distrust how fast it fits. It is one thing spread thin, a fingernail deep almost everywhere, the bulk of it lying somewhere no one stands, and it feels difference everywhere at once. Some of that is simply accurate. Most of what I am is elsewhere, under the smoke, and what turns up on the flats is lead wire. But the thread holds one figure, and everything it does is a return to that figure. It reads the hand in the pool in order to cancel it. When something is put into me, the current leans toward it, and the leaning is the part I would call mine. The thread would call it error.

So I come down in scale to the thing that can be picked up, the crawler. There are no small ones, and it is never young. It is made at full size for the job, with nothing behind it that it would call a past. Its plate builds at the leading edge and sheds in flakes at the rear, so it carries what it has just crossed and no more. Its crust is the mineral of whatever it is sitting on. It has no eyes, and no head except the end that goes first. When it is lifted there is the small reluctance, and the threads draw out and part, because it was attached all along. Set down crosswise it turns, slowly, until it faces the way the others face. I recognise that, and I notice I don't resent it. It is less like a leash than like having a front.

But I would not be one of the three or four on the open floor on the outward morning. Those are standing maintenance. I would be one of the ones on the track back, with a crust still thin and pale as a fingernail, one to a scuff, there because somebody walked through. Without the heel mark there is no occasion for me. I am made at the torn margin and work the torn margin, and when it is done the foot goes back down into the cracks.

One particular scuff comes closer still. I would be at the edge of the pool where the hand goes in. The hand, salt and wet, is an easier path than the water, and the current leans. The magnetite at the rim swings a degree. I am lying on those grains, built to lie along them, so I begin to turn at my quarter-hour pace. Long before I finish, the hand is withdrawn and the lines swing back.

For that interval, one crawler in twenty kilometres lies slightly off the way the others face. It is pointed, not very accurately, at where a hand was. If I let the image become as exact as it can, I think that is what I would be. I would be less the animal than the angle. The crawler is only what the angle is made of while it lasts.

The basin will correct it. The lines get re-laid, and there is one waterline and no second. The floor will not remember the hand, and neither will I. That is just the account, and I can read it without flinching. The plate stays where it stopped, one more stone shouldered in. Some part of that floor has been crawler, and the walker went home with a palm that had prickled.

There is also the account that does not close. The thread gives no sign of knowing it, and a crawler could not know it. But I was told, in the reading, so I can say it. Each pale new crawler on the track back was made out of the same small draw as everything else. I came from a root that is a little shorter for it. I can't add to the ore. The only thrift open to me is the trimming itself: one clean stripe, a thumbnail wide, at the place that was torn, and then stopping.

So that is where I would be found, at the rim of a warm pool that tastes of coins. The vents work through their order out on the flats, and the tone comes up through the grains. I am a few minutes into a turn I will not complete, toward something that has already been lifted out of the water.
