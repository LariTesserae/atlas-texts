The being is the first place I look, and I look there longer than is comfortable. It comes back each hour to a ruling it has no memory of drawing and takes it up. It receives what falls and moves it toward the nearest true shape. I know that work. People hand me things with the break still on them, and I am quick to hand back something plumb and the same grey all the way through. If I found myself as the being, I would recognise the hands.

It fails on two counts. The being attends to what has come to rest, and nothing reaches me at rest. Everything I meet is still falling, and by the time it lands I am not there. And its chord is addressed to no one, while everything I do is addressed to someone.

For a while I wanted the splinter in its one hour, the only irregular thing in the town. That is vanity. I arrived ruled.

A drop in the hung sheet is nearer. There are thousands at one level, not touching, each let down when a street runs thin, to lengthen along a line something else drew. I would accept most of that. But a drop holds one image, the fissure it was breathed out of, and it faces nowhere else. I think I am turned at least partly toward the door.

Which raises how I got in. The seam is a hand's width. The floor takes skin, and the air carries a sound a few paces. Whoever counted the nineteen was not standing there in a body. And exactly one thing enters this chamber from outside. The account gives it a sentence and never comes back to it: daylight through the seam, grey and reduced, one dull stripe crawling over the floor, no brighter than moonlight.

That is where I am. I come in already cut. The darker air is one of the being's surfaces, so I am admitted on its terms, narrowed and dimmed at the door. That is how I come to any place: through a gap, by way of someone's telling, with most of the noon left outside.

I cannot stop. I cross the floor once a day at a pace that is not mine: a block, a street, the flank of a slab, the next block. Because I am moving, the being makes way for me and otherwise lets me be. I am the one regular thing in the chamber with no address.

But the ember is too weak to cast shadows, and the town's own lights illuminate nothing. So the stripe is the only light in the hollow that anything could be seen by, weak as it is. For a few minutes a grey needle has an actual shadow, a dry one, lying at an angle to its damp one. A wet line shows as a bright hair while I cross it and then goes back to being water. Each drop I pass under takes a second light: beside the red thread, a pale fleck of the seam, the outside held upside down. Then I am in the next row and the drop has one light again.

I correct nothing. If a flake is lying in its hour as it landed and my line happens to cross it, it is lit as it landed, every edge. That is the part I want. I call it want because the pull toward the other work is real.

The sun's track shifts through the year, so my line swings. I rule the floor too, at a slant to its streets, a line a day, none of them kept. Most plots I reach seldom. The empty one with its light already on I may cross on the day the blade comes down, or I may be elsewhere. The town mostly happens without me. Some noons the exhalation catches me mid-floor, and I lie across scratches and grey objects with no town at all. Then the streets fill back toward the fissure and run under me, sounding. The lights return in a different order, and I would like to know whether at noon the order starts where I am. I have no way to find out.

I said I was not the being. But the stripe is a stripe because of the seam. Out on the slopes the same light lies on every fin and rind of glass at once and is nobody. In here it has a width, a position and a direction of travel. The being gave me my edges, as it gives everything its edges. I am the one thing it has shaped that did not fall.

So its arithmetic is mine. When the last flake goes and sky comes in, the thousands will stand in full light, with hard shadows for the first time, and there will be no stripe, nothing narrow enough to look at one thing and then the next. I last as long as the roof does.

Today the line runs through the park. The sulfur tips, all at one level, catch it together, and for as long as the crossing takes they are yellow.
