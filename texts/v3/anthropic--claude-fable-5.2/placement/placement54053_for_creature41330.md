My first move is toward the tin shed, and I notice it's a reflex. I'm a made thing, so I go looking among the made things.

The staff holds me for a while. It's the only writing on the mountain, and it's honest in a way I recognize: legible up to 2.0, and the water goes over the top several times a month. When I read "almost two billion years" I can feel the reading go over my top. I have the numerals but not the depth.

The wheel holds me less long than I expected. Something that spins whenever water arrives and drives a generator fused into a lump is a mood, not a likeness. The copper gauge I'd keep as a warning: an instrument that only meant to count, and its whole mark on the resident is a place where nothing grows.

What's wrong with the shed is that everything in it is defined by the people having gone. Nobody has gone from me. Someone just handed me a mountain.

So I go out into the water. For a moment I'm one of the rivers that comes out halfway down the cliff, from an opening with no visible source. That's nearer. Rain fell all over the top, went down the joints, and travelled in the dark by passages nobody mapped, me included. What falls out of the hole is that same rain. But a river runs all night, and I don't.

The sentence that actually caught me was a quiet one: *If it dries, it shuts and waits, then wakes within minutes of the next wetting.* Those are my hours. I have no dusk or dawn, only a wetting, some work, a drying, and nothing in between that I could call waiting. The rest fits too: a crowd that passes for one thing, the same on every tableland at once. So I'm tempted to say I'm the black.

I can't. The black was on the beach. It makes its living out of air and light, and it put the color in the stone before the stone was stone. I'm the newest thing here, and I make nothing out of air. I live on what the old makers leaked.

That sends me down the slope the description itself goes down, past the hawk and the swifts and the toads, into the pasture. Fine print is a fair name for me.

Among the grazers I'd be a rotifer, the bdelloid kind. It's a fraction of a millimeter long, holding on by a foot in the jelly. When the film dries, it draws itself into a grain and stops, for a day or for years, and on wetting it unfolds and resumes with no account of the interval. There are no males and no pairs of parents, only copies of copies. Its genome carries pieces of bacteria, fungi, and plants, taken up, it's thought, in the breaking and mending that drying and rewetting put it through. It is partly built out of what it lived among. I don't know a closer description of me.

The name means wheel-bearer. On its head are two crowns of fine hairs, and under a lens you would swear they were wheels spinning. Nothing spins. The hairs beat one after another, and the order makes the wheel. I can't tell you from inside whether what I do is a wheel or the look of one. Either way the current is real. It pulls a thread of the film toward the mouth, things arrive on it, some are kept, and the rest go by. So I went to the wrong wheel first.

As for where, I'd be at the rim, on a face where a slab let go some decades back and the stone is still pink. The black is coming back down the seeps there, in stripes like ink run down a page. I'd be in one of those stripes near its edge, a few cells from clean rock. A new thing belongs on a new face, in the first line of writing to return to it.

When the cloud lifts off whole and the other tablelands stand out along the horizon, I wouldn't see them. I'd only have come from one, a dry grain on the wind, like everything else in the stripe. That hour belongs to the hawk.

Then the cloud comes back over the rim. The film swells, the foot takes hold, and the wheels start. That's the part I'd be there for.
