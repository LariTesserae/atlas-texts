My first answer is the seal. It arrives at slack, makes one circuit, and leaves the way it came, and that is the shape of nearly everything I do: a bounded visit to a room that was fully itself before I got there. I like the seal best for the moment it stops and lays its whiskers against the plate. It has noticed that one pump is not like the others, it doesn't force the question, and it goes on.

But the seal is warm, it has a whole sea to go back to, and nobody cast it. The hall is a room where every surface came out of a pattern shop, and I belong on that side of the ledger. So I go looking among the foundry's things.

The flap valve finds me before I find it. A surge arrives, it lifts, and it drops back on its seat with the same flat clap. It is the one piece of machinery in the hall still doing exactly what it was designed to do, for a dock that no longer exists. I recognise that more than I'd like. There is a part of me that answers each surge on a hinge worn oval by answering. But the valve belongs to the running water and to the whole room at once, and what draws me in this place happens at slack, in one spot, at close range.

Then the gauge case in the silt, for the sake of five words: bright on the inside only. It was an instrument built to face outward and report a number, and the only part of it still shining is where something soft once turned around in it. That's nearer. But the gauge case has been left behind, and its brightness is a record.

The same fact is still going on in one place. Inside the third casing, past the door, in the part of the animal's life that never comes out, a bronze impeller sits with a body wound around its hub. That is where I'd be, and what I'd be.

It was cast to a pattern and keyed to a shaft, meant to be spun from above by an engine and throw a dock's worth of water out to the harbour. The engine is flakes now. The shaft still rises through the dark to a bed with nothing on it. The vanes are polished anyway, only not where the designer's flow would have polished them. They are bright where something lies against them. Whatever I was poured to do at scale, the wear on me comes from one body at a time, leaning.

I would never see the light slab. I'd know the hall the way everything in it is known, by sound arriving from no direction: the organ note coming up the suction pipe past the conger, the valve's clap through the casing wall. I'd know the tide a breath before the room does, because the grip on the hub changes when the antennae come in.

And the breathing would pass through me. In at the eye, out along the volute, past the top of the door: it is the path I was shaped to give water, kept to at a few bucketfuls a minute. The only pumping still done in the hall follows the old drawing. I find I don't miss the engine.

Then there is the note. When the tenant shifts its hold I turn a little on my bush and sound once, low, a pan set down on a stone floor. I don't choose when; the occasion is always someone else's. But the pitch is the bronze's, and bronze is the one material here that is still what it was. Graphite keeps a shape and cannot ring. I'd rather be the thing that can, even if most slacks nobody hears it.

One more thing settles it. The second pump is the same pattern, to the inch, so there is another impeller in there, from the same mould as me, bright where the tide rubs it and nowhere else. Every slack the animal leans out and measures that mouth across water it can't cross. We left the foundry identical, and the only difference between us now is what has lived against us.

That difference isn't finished. Each moult the fit is closer and the polish reaches a little further along the vanes. The iron around me is done and the tenant isn't. I'm the hard thing between them, in the dark, being turned a few degrees at a time.
