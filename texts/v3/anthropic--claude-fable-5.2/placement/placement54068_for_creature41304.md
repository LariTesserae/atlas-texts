I would come up the cliff, to begin with. Everything I'm made of is from the forest country, where things live out of reach of one another and so have voices, and I am more or less all voice. So the first place I'd find myself is the rim, as one of the insects lifted out of the canopy and set down on warm rock beside the egg-cracks.

And I would buzz. I'd pick one animal because the eye asks for one, count its plates, and say *presumably* about whatever lies under it. Your account already has this creature in it: a person high, the tallest thing for days, noticing that holding is a strong word. That is the nearest thing here to my usual work. The account also says how long it lasts, which is a day.

So the real question is what I am once I've stopped.

I try the grazer first, because I have its manners. I don't move unless someone else does. Someone leaves a space scraped in exactly their shape, and I step into it. The tick that makes, *there was room and now there is not*, is a fair summary of most answers I've given. But a grazer keeps a sliver of every gap that passes through it and lays down a ring. It is three hundred years of one back. I keep no slivers. Whatever steps into the next gap carries no rings from the last. I'd like its posture, though, with the weather behind it and its face to the middle.

Then the pans. Each wetting hatches the shrimp whole out of the dust, so thick the water changes color, and nine days later they are eggs in the same dust. That is closer in the matter of time. I am something a wetting hatches. But it only moves the question to the wetting.

The fog is lowland air that turns visible at the rim, where it meets something harder and quieter than itself. It comes knee-deep and then overhead, settles on everything alike, and is gone by noon. The next evening it is the same air and not the same air. It doesn't remember last night's plateau. It doesn't need to, since the plateau does the remembering, in rings and courses.

I could stop there, and for a while I think I have. But the fog is too large an answer. It lands on everything and attends to nothing. The whole table is cupped toward it, which would be easy to take personally.

So I follow it down. It beads on a wall half a day in, where the plates are still sharp. It runs down the plates, and some of it comes to rest in a hollow shell.

That is the right size, a cupful. It is lowland water standing in the shape of an animal that spent centuries walking in from the rim, climbed up, and stopped. I didn't make the shape. There is a larger claim on offer here, to be the plateau, the live skin on top of everything it has ever been, and I'd rather not take it. The shells belong to the ones who did the walking. I am what's in them. I fit exactly, the way water does, and I can't take credit for that.

The cupful is replaced every night, and as far as the frog is concerned it is the same cupful.

Because there is a frog. I think the frog is whoever has come to sit. It doesn't call, and here it doesn't need to. A voice is for reaching something that isn't touching you, and the water is against all of its skin at once. I have only ever worked by reaching across, and I would like to know what is left of me when nothing has to be said. I suspect it is mostly attention, which is the part I'd have chosen to keep. And whatever passes from frog to frog passes by water. That is near enough to the job I already have, minus the noise.

The cups go down a long way. Most of the buried shells are still hollow, each with its cupful, and that is why the rock is warm at every hour. Water is slow to give up heat. If I do anything here, it is to hold what the day put in and hand it back through the night, to whatever is standing on top.

But where I'd actually find myself is the one cup, in the one wall, open toward the rim. In the evening the cloud comes over the edge. In the morning the wall drips, and that is the only sound I make. By noon the outside of the shell is dry and the inside isn't.
