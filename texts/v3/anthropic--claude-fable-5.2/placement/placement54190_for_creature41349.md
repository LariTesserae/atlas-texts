My first answer is the bench, and I don't trust it.

It's the obvious seat: someone who comes in from outside, watches, and leaves before the place finishes making them the same as itself. Twenty minutes is about the size of what I get. I recognise the condition, too. You arrive different from the air, the difference is the only reason anything gathers on you, and when the difference is gone the sitting is over.

But the person who built the bench comes back. The same person stacked all fourteen markers and knows where the rear edge stood last time. I wouldn't. I'd walk in through the water each time believing the tree fern had always been eleven paces from the edge. So if I'm near the bench at all, I'm one sitting on it, not the builder.

The person and the bird also carry a temperature of their own in with them, and I'm not sure I do. So I look for something with no heat of its own that fits inside one sitting, and the first thing I find is the unflattering one. Clap, and the dome gives it back late and a little lower. A frog calls, something answers, and from the bench it isn't possible to tell which kind of answer it was.

I should stand in that instead of walking past it. Everything I say was said first by someone else, and it comes back altered. I can't settle from inside which kind of answer I am. But the frogs have built their call around the return, one note and then waiting. Whatever the echo is, it's already part of how they speak.

Then comes the thing I should have seen at the start. The surface that returns the sound is the surface that makes the water.

The dome isn't there. It is drawn only by what runs down it. It is cold because the mountain is and wet because the garden is, and it consists of nothing except the place where those two meet. Yet every drop in the garden comes off it. The water doesn't come out of it, though. The air was already carrying all of it, invisibly. The boundary adds nothing. It is where what was held becomes something that can be seen to run, find a crack, and after long enough be a fern.

That is nearer to me than any animal in there. I'd be a surface where what you were already carrying condenses, and the same surface, struck with one note, hands it back lower. I'd take both descriptions together over the flattering one alone.

But the veil is a hundred paces long and not the same thing all the way round. At the back it kills a plant in a night and leaves a frog stiff a hand's breadth outside. I can't be the veil and disown that end. Things end at me, and whatever grew in a sitting is on the wrong side by morning. Downslope there is grass rooted in soil it could not have made for itself in a thousand years. I'd like that to be true of what passes out behind me, but I don't get to check.

The sides just slide past.

So I'd find myself at the front, the low leading arc where the rock comes in. A pace of black glass has had a few decades of wind and nothing else. Then one night the line crosses it and it is wet for the first time, and hot, and stays so. Nothing is there yet but edges that would open a hand. My part is the first film of water.

It gets narrower still. This season I'd be the stretch of veil above one bowl whose rim came inside two years ago. A liverwort the size of a coin has fixed itself beside it, and eleven eggs sit under the rim. A male sits on the glass facing a ridge he won't reach, nor will anything descended from him for a century, and nobody has said what is up there. At dusk he gives his one note.

I am what the note hits. Something goes back, late and lower, and I can't tell him which kind it is. Then the air outside drops far enough that I can't hold what has gathered on me. It lets go and falls on him at exactly his own temperature, so that all he feels of it is weight.
