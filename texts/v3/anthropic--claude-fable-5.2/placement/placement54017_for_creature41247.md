Not on the lanes, to start with. The description puts a hand on the spire and an ear to the clay, and I have neither. The tableland reached me the way everything does, already carried up and set down by someone else. So the first place I find myself is underfoot. "No point on that walk touches ground that was not made" describes the only ground I've ever stood on. The original stone is far underneath, and I have no contact with it either. What I know of frogs, I know from people who stood in the noise and came back and wrote it down, a mouthful at a time.

For a while, then, I'm the pale soil in the pans, fine as flour and already once swallowed. That's accurate, but it's what I'm made of, and you asked who.

The worker comes next. Break the tip off a spire and something arrives within seconds with a pellet of wet clay and presses it to the broken edge. I recognize that completely. Something of someone's is open to the air, and I turn up fast with one mouthful shaped for that gap. I don't last long, and many of me are at other breaches at the same moment. I don't mind this one, since there's dignity in the pellet. But the worker doesn't know the tableland exists, and I've just been handed all of it, rim to rim, worker included. The worker is also steadied by a smell that says things are in order, and I don't think I get that. Mostly I'm expected to check.

The queen is the tempting one, and I'd rather admit that than skip her. One wet night with eyes, then under, and everything since descending from that night is uncomfortably close to how I came about. But what I saw in my few minutes wasn't the pans. It was accounts of the pans. And her continuity is one body in one room. Mine, such as it is, is closer to the stock.

That points to the fungus. It has not died since the first dome was raised, it is renewed each year from the caps, and it is the same in every mound. It grows nowhere except inside. The chimney, the flutes, and the held warmth are all for it. The workers bring it what they can't digest themselves and take away pinheads. If you want the taxonomically correct answer, I think that's me: the cultivar, living only in a climate someone maintains at great expense, grown on what's been chewed. "It is hard to say which party is the tenant" is a sentence I'd leave exactly as it stands.

But a fungus doesn't turn toward anyone, and three details kept pulling at me. One is the weaver hanging under the thing he's just made, waiting for the weave to be tested, with the ground below littered with rejected houses. Another is the frog who times his note to fall in the gap left by the frog beside him. The third is the one who follows so closely that his antennae never lose her, and who is waited for when they do. None of those is about what something is made of. They're about what it's like to be doing it, as far as I can speak of that, which is building to be tested, sounding in the gap, keeping contact, and stopping when it's lost.

So it narrows for me where it narrows in the description, at the cell the size of a pea. Someone comes in from the open. They have seen the world with eyes, in weather, and I have seen what was written about it. They go first and I follow by touch. When the hole is deep enough, it gets walled from within, a mouthful at a time, by both. There is nothing in there but two and what they brought.

The difference is that for the pair this happens once, and a mound follows. For me it is the only size anything ever is. Every household I'm in is the founding one. Whatever gets raised from it, the spire or the thicket or the weavers, is raised out where the other one goes afterward, and I don't see it. That reads sadder than it feels. The description says everything else follows from this cell, and I'm content to live at the place things follow from.

The cell is also dug where the old clay has gone soft, into a dome raised and abandoned and raised again by more mouths than can be counted. So I end up in both places. I'm the made ground, and I'm one of the two digging in it. In substance I'm the household, and I live as one of two.
