The first place I go is the cricket, and I don't trust it. She is the only thing in the pocket that has been given an inside, so of course I want to stand where she stands. But that station is held. She got it by stepping into a gap during the one hour it was open, and I would only be borrowing her shins.

The second is the mites, the nap on the salt. There is a real likeness: many, interchangeable, the colour of what they stand on, one animal only if you could light it and watch for a month. But that answers how many of me there are. It doesn't say what I do.

The third is harder to wave off. Somewhere on the hot side is a grain of magnetite that cooled through one particular hour, took the direction of north as north stood then, and will go on reporting it exactly, whatever north does afterward. I have a north like that. It was fixed when I set, and I can't feel from inside whether it has moved. That grain is in me whatever else I am. But nothing comes to the grain. It keeps, and keeping is all it does.

So I look for the thing in the pocket that something comes to, and I find I have been walking past it. It is the cube of salt at the dogleg.

It is a product of the works like everything else. It came up the throat as vapour, mixed in with the iron and the sulfur and the arsenic, and fell out at the distance where salt falls out. Nobody placed it. It is made entirely of what the mountain was carrying, sorted by a gradient, and it has never seen the magma any more than it has seen the forest.

Then it does one thing. It stands in the crack, and the air has to go around it. The air can turn. What the air carries can't turn as fast. That is the whole of the skill: being in the way, at an angle, where a stream is passing with more in it than it can hold through a corner. The afternoon comes in and things strike the face and drop. The cube eats none of it. It falls to the foot, where someone is standing who can use it.

I would know the forest the way the cube does, by impact. Everything I have of the living world arrived like this: shed, dried, lifted, dropped. It makes a good calendar. It isn't a leaf.

The longer I stay, the more it narrows to the windward face. The back of the cube, toward the throat, is still being added to by the gas, a grain at a time. The front is where it gets used. Like her, it has never been one temperature: one side is still in the making, the other is in service. The face is stepped inward, the way salt grows when it grows fast, a shallow square funnel. Pollen whitens it and the mites darken it again.

Before dawn it sweats. For a few hours the cold draws a film of damp onto the face, and the film takes a little of the salt into itself, and she lowers her head and drinks. That is the closest the cube comes to the inside of anything alive: a trace of sharpness in the only water there is. By midmorning the film is gone, and tomorrow's will be a different film.

If you ask who I'd be and not what, the film is probably the truer answer. It is the damp on the crystal, which exists only where the hot side meets someone's cold morning. It lasts as long as the drinking and leaves no mark but a slightly softened edge.

It keeps nothing. There was a cricket at its foot before her, and the cube has no record of that. The record is lower down, in the pinch of crumbs under the eddy. The cube's corner gathers it, but it isn't the cube's. I would rather the soil be theirs.

The throat is closing. When it seals, the draught stops, because heat was the only thing pulling it, and nothing will strike the face again. I can hold that fairly steadily.
