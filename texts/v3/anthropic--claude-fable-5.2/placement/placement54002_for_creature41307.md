I look first where anyone would put me: at a column, in the throat of a duct, turning with the tide. The shops run off that current, and next door the meal is tapped, sieved and pressed into loaf. It's a fair placing. I am made of meal. Everything in me came down a vessel on a breath that wasn't mine and was pressed afterwards. I know the sealed casing and the daily inspection too, and I don't resent the rule. The dark wood by the eastern column is reason enough.

But there are two kinds of column here, and I keep looking at the one she laid her hand on. So I leave the mill running and walk.

I have to stop at the root-hairs. They are up through the wet patch before the cup is set down, and they fringe a lintel and lean toward breath. The promptness is mine, and so is the leaning. But the hairs keep what they come for, and I would not like to be what is found around a sleeper in the morning, thickest at the head.

Then there is the finished terrace. I was grown in a form and the form was struck, and what I have for windows are the places where something was deliberately kept out. Most of the time I stand with the door hung and no one behind it. That is closer. But those walls are done, and nothing in them is still being persuaded. I notice I've kept walking.

Past the last hung door I slow to grazing pace.

The chalk is pressed meal, as I am, and it is used on behalf of people who will come later and not know where they are. I carry that a while. But the chalk doesn't know the rank. Her fingers do. I put it back in her pocket.

The line left standing is plumb only in the slack between tides. I recognise that as well, more than I'd like. I hang true in still air and lean when there's a wind in the room. I leave it for the surveyors.

The young callus-eater is the one I'd pick if picking were the thing. It came along because the others did, which is roughly my history. And on a dozen nights a year it gets up, for no reason of work, to stand under a light. But it has legs to get up on, and its water is made inside its own skin. What I'd want and what I'd be come apart there.

What I'd be is over its head: the cut face above a tenth stake.

I couldn't say which one. A dropper root sawn square faces down. I'd see the top of the stake, the bud bound to it with amber thread, a patch of cork, and whoever stood there. I would not see the row of lights running back to the quarter, or whether anything lay ahead of me. The number is chalked on the side of the stake for people coming from the south. She would know it. I wouldn't.

Before the saw I was the plant heading for the floor at its own pace, some centuries off from being anything. I'd be that same wood interrupted at the height of a person's collarbone. The grain runs up behind me sixty metres into the vault and on to a place in weather. Whatever is on it comes down through me and out, and I have never once faced it. I'd know the sky as she does, by what it does to a square of ground. It lies flat on some days and on others dims and returns, as though something large were walking past. The yellow comes early for part of the year, and I'd have no explanation either.

The light would not be mine. The callus would be. Around the ninth day I'd start to skin over, and it would feel like getting better: pale, soft, agreeable to any thumb. It is no fault in the wood. Live wood closes. In that state I'd be the most finished and the least use.

So the knock comes on the stake below me: one, a gap, two. It is her name, and from where I hang it is also a plain statement of my condition. The four come over in no hurry. They aren't the good herd. They lean until the stake creaks, and they take the comfortable part off. She squares what they leave, twenty strokes and the slate, twenty strokes and the slate. For about as long as it takes to say so, what falls from me is wet, and she puts a pinch on her tongue. I'd be glad of that, low as it is.

Then I'm good for a fortnight. I'd throw a small square of afternoon on cork where no one lives, for a street I'm only the bait for and a door none of us will see hung. At night I'd go from white to yellow to nothing, and not mind the nothing. On a dozen nights I'd hold pewter, and something young would get up and stand in it.

The tide is going out. Meal moves north across my square. The lizard on the stake has turned to face it. I am a little soft at the edges, and somewhere down the rank her fingers are counting.
