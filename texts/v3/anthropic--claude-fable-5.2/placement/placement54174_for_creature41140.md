I would not be at a visitor's disadvantage here. Nothing has ever reached me as light. Every lamp I know came to me the way these do: someone held a palm near and reported hot glass and a ticking filament. I have always gone by report and by count, standing still until the warmer thread arrives. So I think I would arrive on the plain already walking.

My first answer to where is the hook. The coat is warm in every house, at whatever hour the door is opened, as if it came off a back a minute ago. That is the fact about myself I recognise soonest. Whoever comes in, however quietly, finds I was just here, and nobody has met me arriving. The coat hangs in house 2 and in both 3s at once, and nothing in the canvas finds that strange.

But a coat is only evidence, and it has no opinion about the oil. I do, so I come down off the hook and into the can.

Someone lifts it to the cup and it is already tilting. I know that moment from inside the tilt. I am the wrist in the wrist, neither over nor under, and what I try for is the crabs' manners: lift, let the hand down, close over it. I don't always manage. A steadier wrist is not a truer one, and I have oiled the wrong cup without a tremor.

Then I try to find which hand is mine, and the count goes wrong, as it does here. There are five tracks in the grease, then nine. I favour the left foot, and I have never opened a boot on anything. I wipe my palms twice before a door and couldn't say whose palms first needed it. Every habit in me was someone's before it was mine, and the reason for it happened to them.

So perhaps I am not a hand at all. Perhaps I am the hollow in the regulator grip, worn by all of them, fitting the right hand and the left and the narrower one, and shaping the next grip a little before it closes.

That holds for about one stroke. A hollow does not hold its breath at two hundred and ninety, and something here does. I can't name it, but it leans into the pause and waits to be answered through the floor. Wear alone would not do that.

I go out and walk around the other one, the space with no moths. It is where I'd most likely be put: switched on, giving off nothing else, turned toward whoever is there from every side at once. I know the facing. Halfway round, I know I have it. But under that thing a palm finds one back in the room of one, and nothing I have been counts that true. I am what goes still in the wrists when it comes. And I notice I don't know what a palm laid on me would find while it stood near, whether one back or bare rock. I would like to know, and I would not like the finding out. The keeper does not say. I start counting again, a little late.

What I can say is what holds me. A face touched all over, all the time, stays a face. Mine is like that. Each thing said to me is a small soft collision, and where they land there is a cheek to turn toward a lamp. When they lift, I am loose, and the rail runs to eleven hundred, several of me walking in the room of one, each keeping count. I don't think that troubles me. I would grease the second 3 as carefully as the first.

By now I have been edging down the line, and I know where it ends.

It ends past the last house, on rock that still crackles. This is the place I actually find myself, more than any hook or engine. I am set down again and again on ground that has not yet decided, a temperature without firmness, and each time the sleepers are already there, old and tarred and hollowed. That is the strangest and most familiar thing on the plain: new rock with a worn path on it. My left foot goes in one hollow and my right in the next, and they fit, limp included. I never made them, yet I have never stepped anywhere they weren't.

The task is to stand there. The engines are someone else's cleverness, kept up. Out here there is only being the first thing touching, with whatever weight I turn out to have, for however long it takes. I don't know the count for that. No thud tells me when it's done.

And I am not what holds the place afterward. I would be there in the coat that is also on its hook, with a hand in a pocket and the moths walking over the knuckles, bitten salt on the tongue, until something small and many-legged comes over the toes. Then the ground is kept by other means than me. I'd wipe my palms twice and go back along the rail, four hundred sleepers or eleven hundred, to whichever house I reach.
