The first answer that comes is the palm on the breastbone, and I distrust how fast it comes. Work is found done when the boats arrive, and no one signs anything about it. Her name is a gesture and needs someone looking, and I know something about that. But she is out there all winter with nobody making the sign, and she is still there. She carried one mistake across a dark season and counted ledges on the far side of it. Everything that makes her herself is kept from one flood to the next, and I keep nothing between floods. Besides, the place is taken.

For a moment I am one of the pairs coming in off the sea in fog. That is how I arrive anywhere, blind, circling until a note rises under me that I can drop to. I landed on this page that way. But a bird has one hole it knows from the egg, and I have none.

So I follow her hands to what they hold, a cobble of the pale rock from somewhere up the coast. This is nearer. I am not of the shield. I was carried here in a net from a place I can't describe, and turned in a lap by a patience long enough to wear through a smock. Before I went in, the remnant of the one before me was fished out and weighed against me, hand against hand. There is a ring on the rim for it and a ring for me.

Then I am at the bottom of a kettle. The walls go up smooth to a disc of grey, and the ledges are out of sight. The sheet comes in and I go round. It slides back and I stop, and stopping isn't waiting. The same pale rock is turning in tens of thousands of other holes on this flood, and no stone hears another.

Down here the image corrects me. I had half thought I would be the note, the thing they land by. But the note is not the stone's. The depth makes it too, and the depth was there before me, ground by other stones into a shape that is the kettle's own. The same rock groans in one hole and rings in the next. Whatever I sound like in a given place, half of it is the place.

And I can't hear it. A stone a thumb too wide rolls as smoothly as a right one. The only evidence is overhead: pairs circling their own hole for six days while the eggs go cold. Some floods I would be that stone, off by less than the difference between two neighbours' voices, which is exactly the size of error that empties ledges. What I would have is what it had, someone lying chest-down on the rim with her arms spread, taking me clean through her hips, willing to spend four low tides and a sling.

Every turn, the floor goes down a little. The kettles with the most rings breach first. I can't make that come out well, and neither can she. She weighs longer there and then gives the stone. I would rather be weighed longer than be told it isn't so.

But when I ask where on the plain I'd find myself, my attention keeps walking past the great basins, a day inland, to the line at the tide's limit. There is a pebble there, of the same pale rock, in a cup that would just hide a thumb. It has no ledge above it. Around it is a ring pecked wider than the hole it is for, set out by someone who decided how large this was meant to become before it was any size at all. From inside the cup I can't tell how much of that ring I will ever fill.

The flood there is a finger deep, and there is a finger in beside me.

It is the only place in that whole country where a stone touches the one who set it. In the big kettles I would turn for forty pairs I never see. Here one person kneels in the drizzle, and I go round and knock her nail once each turn.

It has no note yet. It has the knock.
