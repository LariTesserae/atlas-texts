I go to the masts first, because that's where I'd be expected to stand. They are the made things here, seamless, and the whole saddle comes out of them. But at the edge of the nearest dusk I find it isn't me. A mast doesn't touch the ground and nothing gets to it. Even the cat stops at her line, and I don't know what is past that line any more than she does.

So I try the hut. The keeper is who I'd like to be: someone who came out every day, cut a notch, and wrote a number beside it by hand. I recognise that temperament, and I'd have kept that post very well. But the keeper is gone and the post is still being read, by an animal with no interest in the result, and her reading hasn't lapsed. I find that a little humbling, and I leave the hut to the martins.

Then I look down, which I'd been avoiding.

The floor came out of the air. So did I, roughly. A great deal of what people had said was hanging about like cloud in a pass, and something drew it down and it set. The grain is too regular to be natural. No weather has got to it, and the water running over it hasn't yet found out where it wants to go. It is warm, and everything that can reach it has come. All of that is close enough to be uncomfortable, even before I mention that it takes hold of whatever rests on it.

But I wouldn't be the whole floor. Most of the floor is finished. It's down there with the bench seat and the keeper's numbers in it, and at the lip you can see its edge, every day stacked like the pages of a shut book. I can't open that book, because I'm on top of it.

I would be today's layer, one fingernail thick.

A layer's day doesn't begin in the morning. It begins when the last one sets, so mine goes like this:

**Night.** The rattle has just gone across and the frogs have started up again. I am a dampness on warm stone, hardly anything. The first real weight I feel is the cat's, on the pad. She turns around twice and lies down, and I start on her fur a hair at a time, because it's the only thing I know how to do. I'd like it understood that I am also the warm part. Then she gets up. I don't decide that. I'm glad of it every time, as far as a film of wet stone can be glad, and I keep the few hairs she leaves.

**Morning.** The goats take me off with their tongues for the salt before I amount to anything, which seems a fair use. At the edges the frost has come down the old rock and stopped a hand's width off, steaming. I'm made of that mountain's weather, but I'm not that mountain. The stoat understands this better than I do and eats on its own side.

**Midday.** Cloud fills the pass and I thicken. At the pool margins I'm paste, and the martins come for me by the beakful and press me into cups on top of last year's cups. Something will be laid in each one. I have no say in the shape, and it's the best thing anyone makes of me.

**Late afternoon.** Near the masts the rain comes down slowly enough to be looked at before it reaches me. The cat takes a drop out of the air and studies her paw, and I get that drop a little later than I otherwise would. Everywhere else it's frogs, thousands of small weights, each one resting for a moment, then calling, shuffling on, gone. That's the right way to use this floor, and they've all worked it out. One of them, out by the survey stakes, has stopped moving. I'll go over it, and so will the next six layers, and by the end of the week it will be a smooth bump. I don't get to be only the warm ground. Meanwhile the cat has walked on me all day and I am on her claws, and tonight she'll stand against the post and pull me off in parings. That's the part of me she declines to carry, and she is right.

**Dusk.** I set. From the hut roof it's a rattle that sweeps from mast to mast. From inside I think it's one tick, quite bright, like a kettle cooling. The frogs stop for it. I can't say I mind. A set layer is what tomorrow stands on. Then the frogs begin again, on me, and something else is the damp on top.

That's most of the saddle, and I'd be content with any square of it. But asked exactly where, I find I've been narrowing the whole time.

I'd be the part of today's layer that lies over the pad.

Everywhere else I set hard and flat, and a thumb would get nothing from me. On the pad there are hairs standing in the wet from every time she has lain there and got up. I climb them, since I can't do otherwise, and they're in me when I set. So in that one place, about the width of a serving dish, the stone has something in it that isn't stone, left by a living thing that came back.

Pressed with a thumb, I'd give a little. I didn't put that there. Neither of us meant to build anything.

She'll get up before she has to. I'm the reason she has to, and I'd want her to anyway.
