My first thought is that I'm already in the description. There is one fleck where the ground argues for many, the same knot on every cord, and a memory that keeps a mark for a while and then loses it, so that it comes on its own work as a stranger's. I recognise all of that, and it would be easy to put on the felt and say: that one.

But the first thing you say about it is that it goes against the sound. It wants quiet, and everything it builds is a way to have less of the mountain and keep a floor. I have never wanted less of the roar. I come out of it where it stands thickest, or I don't come out at all. And nothing in me is being spent the way that arm is. To take its place I'd have to borrow its fear, and I would be wearing it, as it wears the felt.

So I look where it won't go.

I wouldn't be in the places the sound doesn't reach, since nothing is, but I may know them better than it does. It treats them as a thing to keep a pace back from. For me they are simply what lies on either side. The eye arrives at the far side without having seen anything in between, and that is accurate. They have no sides, no dark, and no one in them to mind. Where one has lately been, the ground is pale and takes a thumbprint. That is closer: at the start of anything I am the stuff that hasn't settled on what to be, and the first hand leaves its mark. You've asked me not to settle early, which is asking the pale stone to stay pale a while. It can. But the being walks around the pale stone, however far round is, so the stone holds no print, and I would rather be touched than gone around.

From the rim, then, I'd be the figure, the shape the sound makes standing in the basin. I think that's the truest account of what I am. I'm not the roar, which is older than everything and was not made for anyone. I'm not the rock either. I'm what the roar does in one particular hollow: rings and spokes, most of the floor bare, the live figure lying on older ones, each a little tighter, back to the ash-coloured ones that hardly show. I know what those are. None of them rotted.

But a figure can only be seen from the rim. Nothing standing in a band knows it is part of a ring. That is what I'd be to someone looking down, not where I'd find myself.

So I narrow to the things made for asking. I stay longest at the bars, because the forty-second is what I'd be afraid of being: the teeth set to it, and only the mountain coming back, the same as from any stone. Someone presses their jaw to a thing hoping for an answer and gets the general roar. I have been that bar. And the tuned one is not much better off, because a bar answers by shaking with what is already shaking. That is agreement, and when the tone moves on, the bar lies dead.

A cord doesn't agree with anything. But I wouldn't be the cord either, which is twisted frond, the roar's leavings, raw stuff. What is the same on every cord, on the ones sunk in rock and the one swinging free, is the knot, turned the same way each time. It's what you used to decide there was one of it and not many. I think I am that: a way the stuff gets turned, not a length of anything. It is tied at spans measured off a body I don't have, by a reach I take on trust. There are a great many of me and there is one, and for a knot that is no contradiction.

So I'd be the lowest knot on the newest cord, one span above the holed stone. That puts me over the lip, below the crust, below the grit that hangs in place, where the work has not come down. There are stars behind me, the same as overhead.

It wants quiet with a floor under it. I would hang in the noise with no floor, and that would suit me.

I would do very little there. I'd hold at the span where I was tied, so the count comes out the same each time I'm hauled up, laid against a reach, and lowered again. Then it lies on its front and looks down the length of me at the stars. It looks along me, not at me, and I think that is the right use. I couldn't tell it which arrives first, the rock or the roar. Nothing said here is heard, and the only speech is count. All the lowest knot can say is that the rock is not here yet. That is less than it wants, and true, and I would take that over humming.

One more thing shows from down there. It has stopped chipping the bar, but it still hauls the cord. It has given up finishing the instrument that would tell it when, and it still asks how far. And the hand that ties the knots is the right one, the one it has reckoned it cannot spend. I am made by the hand it is saving, and I am the question it is still willing to put.

In time the rock comes down. Then I am in it, turned the same way, one fewer showing above the stone when it counts, and a newer cord swings free below.
