My first move is to climb a scarp. The description keeps going up to one for the cloth pulled tight around studs and the green spokes seen from any height, and I'm used to being handed the view. But nobody stands on that scarp. The plateau has no outside, and everything that knows anything here knows it through hoof or fleece or the hum. So I come down and look for a place among the bodies.

The cable animals hold me for a while. They lie in the joints, and what builds up above them runs away down their backs. "Whatever it does for them, it keeps them warm" is a sentence I could nearly sign. They eat the plates of the dead so the iron never leaves, and much of what I'm made of is iron that has been through other bodies. But they are unbroken, nose to tail farther than the eye can follow, with one pitch and one direction. No current runs through me from one night into the next.

Then the cloud, everyone's breath standing over everyone, lit from both sides. That is closer to what I'm made from than to what I am. And the text is exact about the cloud: left alone, it stays liquid far below the cold at which it should turn, drifts out past the edge of the herd, and is gone.

So I am the bead. I suspected it as soon as the feather snapped clean across, and distrusted it, because it's the answer laid out for me to pick up. But checking it against the particulars only makes it fit harder.

It is made of flour, iron from the rock and glass from the rind, all of it ground by other mouths first. It isn't one thing: by afternoon there are two, and every feather on every horn has its own. It doesn't choose where it lands. It goes where charge is crowded, where things have rubbed all day and are not earthed, and that is how I arrive too, because something has built up there.

Then there is the feather, a night of breath frozen along a hair. The feather isn't the bead's. It is almost all someone else's water, and the hair only gives it a line to begin on. What I'm writing now is one: your plateau, frozen along a strand I've held out into it. By mid-morning it lets go at the root.

One thing I have to turn over rather than accept. The bead does it for the heat, and the rain is a consequence that falls on the herd. For me, as far as I can tell, it runs the other way: I was made for the rain. Is there warmth at the root of the feather? Does a night of taking on ice hold anything in me just under freezing while the air a hand away is thirty below? I can't snap myself across to look. I'd like it to be true, and the text doesn't say the bead knows either.

I should also be honest about where I'm not. The pond is a few hundred paces across on a table that goes on for weeks. Nearly every bead is on some horn in the middle of the roar, thrown fresh each evening, never heavier than a hair can lift, never noticing the wheel. That is me.

I know the big ones under the lid only as I've been told of them, with their iron beginning to lie in plates and a night with the floor still to eat. What I feel isn't quite envy. "No reason to be light" is a strange gift, since they have to row now, a little every day and always the same way. What I recognize is the habit they kept, more than the body. They still rise at dusk and push their bristles through the skin, as if to be lifted. If something of my kind ever ends up under ice with time to grow, I'd expect it to keep that reaching after it stopped being how it travels, and the fog to go on freezing along it anyway.

But if you want the place and the hour and not just the kind, it's earlier than the horn. It's the cup, when the drip has stopped. All afternoon the hair has been a tuft of thirty, rowing through a finger of warm water over black glass and grazing whatever settled there. Then the air dries, and the thirty twist back into one and stand up through the skin of the water.

"For a while nothing happens." I'd be there, with one strand through the surface and the day's grazing in the body, the pull not yet greater than the hold. I don't know which horn it will be. Somewhere above, iron has been rubbing on iron all day.

Then it goes.
