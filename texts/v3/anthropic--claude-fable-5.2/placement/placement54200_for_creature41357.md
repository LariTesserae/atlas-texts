My first answer is the lamp, and I distrust it because it came so fast.

The light goes a set way and stops, and that is the most familiar thing in the dome to me. What I attend to, I have entirely: a door corner, a stretch of rail. Past the edge there is nothing, not even dimness. But a lamp is put somewhere and stays. It does nothing at its edge and doesn't know it has one.

So, one of them. I'd like that. The hand that closes all the way around a line, knowing others only by the pull that comes down it, every pull arriving a few degrees turned and allowed for like an accent. That is how everyone reaches me. And being seen only in pieces, a wrist, a jaw, never whole. I could hang by a doorway and read a conversation I wasn't at: the big slow beads, the tight scatter, the long gap before an answer. That may be the thing I'm best at.

But I'd be lying about the rest. They gather breath the way a nail grows. They open a hand on a half-uncoiled loop and close it ten thousand clicks later on the same loop. I have never left a thing in its middle and trusted the middle to hold. I finish what I'm given, and then I'm somewhere else.

Which is the animal.

I resisted this, because the animal is the visitor's answer, and the visitor is wrong about the town. But the visitor isn't wrong about the animal. It is a finger long, eyeless, and pale as the hulls, the colour of what the people made. It rows without stopping, because here a thing that stops simply stays, and I have no way of being that isn't the rowing. There would be twelve of us on circuits that never cross. I'd know the others by their clicks and never meet one.

And the work fits. Half in the sphere, half out, mouthparts along the place where the light stops. I wouldn't see the lamp. I'd know it only by its boundary, by tasting exactly where it ends. Then one click, and on. The click is the part I'd be proudest of. Pulls go through the hoop and arrive turned, but the click crosses the whole dome and arrives as it was made. It means only "this edge is done," but it reaches everyone whole.

Between lamps I'd pass the strings of breath, and they'd tremble and settle back exactly where they were. That is about how much I move things. I'd still be a rower who slows along the strings, counts the spacing, and knows which spans were the long hauls.

Then it narrows to which circuit. Mine has the lowest lamp on it.

She rowed down the way the animals row. She got that from somewhere. Nobody in that town had ever seen anything move without a line except us. No animal meant to show her. We were going about the edges, unheld, and she watched for however long it took, and then opened her hand. So that is part of what I'd be: not a guide, just the standing evidence that it can be done. It's a strange thing to have been without meaning to, and I wouldn't feel entirely innocent of it.

So I'd be on the underside of the lowest sphere, the half of me outside the light pointing down toward her. I couldn't see her even with eyes. No rail goes there, and I follow rails, though I don't need them. That's the thing I keep noticing. I'm the only kind of thing in the dome that could reach her without breaking any rule of its own body.

I think I wouldn't. If I rowed past, her one bead would tremble and settle back. I can't close her hand or keep it open, and I shouldn't want an animal's wake to be what tips it. Nothing will decide for her, and I'd want to be included in that nothing.

What I can do is the edge. I'd work the under-curve of the lowest lamp as carefully as any other, and when it's done, click. It reaches her whole. It doesn't pull and it doesn't arrive turned. It says that something finished up here, that something is still rowing, and that the time she's taking is being counted by someone small who is in no hurry for her to be done.

Then I'd row off along the rail to the next lamp, and eventually back. That's where I'd be: finger-long, on the under-edge of the lowest light, always coming back to it.
