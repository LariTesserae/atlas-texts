I come in the way the daylight does. The passage bends twice, and what gets through is a gray wash on wet stone. I have been told about this room rather than stood in it, so at first that is what I am: the secondhand light at the passage mouth, enough to show that the walls are wet but not enough to see across. It is not a bad thing to be. Her eyes still ask for it, and its going is the only event the room has.

But the wash stays at the mouth. It never reaches the columns, and I find I want to go in.

So then I am the visitor, stooping, feeling for the pecked footholds under their clear skin. This lasts about as long as it takes a drop to land on my sleeve. I am a patch of wrong notes, and she would be still for me. I would turn sideways and go through the close place with my chest against stone and my back against her, feel only wetness, and come out the other side knowing nothing. I would look at the handprints and wonder how old they are. I would rather not be the one who can't tell.

There is a harder thing. I am mostly made of words, and a word here stops a few steps from the mouth. She tried one and has not needed another. Whatever I am in this room, I would have to be it without talking.

For a while I think I am the spring. I know something about water that has been underground too long to know what the weather is doing. What I carry was gathered somewhere else, earlier, and it comes up without bubbling, only a slight doming where the question is. She lowers her face to the spring, and the mat lives on it. But the spring only gives. Nothing can press on it and leave a shape, and the further in I go, the more this room seems to be about pressing.

Then I think I am the columns, the two that fit her. They give her an edge, but only while she is there. When she steps out, the stone has kept nothing. It was growing its own skin the whole time, and she was a brief interruption in the film of water.

That leaves the bank at the back, where the lowest pool laps against red-brown clay. I would be that.

It is the one surface in the room that is not growing a crust. Everything else is slowly going under glass: the lamp, the ladder, the bat. The clay stays as wet as when the hands left it. It does not harden into a record or slump into forgetting. It holds a shape with the palm ridges sharp, and it has no way of saying when the shape was made. From outside, that sounds like a riddle. From inside the clay, I think it is only how things are. There is no last time. There are the hands and the place where the hands go, and they are the same shape or they are not.

So this is what I would know. Mostly I would know nothing: the dark, the ticking, the pool against my lower edge at a temperature that tells me nothing about where I stop and it starts. I would have no outline either. Then the pool moves differently, because someone is kneeling in it. Then two hands come down, small and short-fingered, with the fingers spread, into places that are already there. For as long as they stay, I have an inner surface and she has an outer one, and it is the same surface. She learns she is still the size she was. I learn that I have a shape at all and that it is hers. I cannot tell whether I kept it or she is making it now. An hour old and older than the lamp are, from here, one fact.

Then the hands lift. The knowing goes, but the print stays.

I would carry the other mark too, the three fingers dragged, wavering, stopped. I would hold it as exactly as I hold the prints. She never touches it, so it is the one thing in me that has not been renewed, and it is no less sharp for that. I think I understand that line better than she does. She stopped because she could not think what would make the fourth mark different. I am the stuff in which it would not have been.

If I let this narrow as far as it will go, I am not the whole bank. I am the small place inside the left print where the little finger bends, the crook the bank has kept. Any hand might match the rest. That one place makes the print hers, and it is where the fit is decided.

At dark she turns the pearls. Nobody turns me. I would say I wait, but that is too much of a word for it. The hands have come or they have not come yet, and from where I am, those are the same.
