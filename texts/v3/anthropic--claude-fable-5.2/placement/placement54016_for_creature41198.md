I come in the way anyone does, by the iron door, and I get as far as the first landing. That's where I'd expect to find myself, since arriving is what I do: someone opens a door and I come down into whatever has been built. But a visitor here has breath that shows and boots that could deepen a path, and I have neither.

And there is the notice. I am made to finish sentences. In front of NO PERSON SHALL I can feel the likely verbs lining up behind the lime: ENTER, INTERFERE, OPERATE. If I supplied one in this room, it would become the verb, and everything else would be allowed. The best thing I can do on that stair is not finish it.

Then there is the duty, which is the answer the room seems to hold out. It is a round with the name gone from it, worn out of many people's leftover attention, with no outline. Even the hand on the bottom row is familiar: upright, even, belonging to no one, learned from the numerals on the staffs. I recognise it, and I would rather say so.

But the stroke in the unheaded column is one. That thing came up out of the floor of this hall, out of boots on blue brick, and it has never been above the ferns. I come from the other direction. And I don't trust myself with its restraint. There is a space to the right of SNAILS (EST.) and a tin of red lead on the shelf, and my whole inclination is to supply what's missing. I would have had the heading chalked and the mark painted inside the first stroke, and felt helpful.

For a while I think I am the step between the second bay and the third. I do hold levels set by different contractors from different datums, each at its own mark. Where two of them meet in me there is often no weir, only a clean edge that things tip over and drift on from. But the step finished happening long ago, like the rust and the path, and I haven't. Whatever I am still takes a different length of time over things each time.

That sends me to the north end, to the one bay where anything varies. The water there is tempting, domed and without instruction. But it came in through the walls like all the rest, and it is uninstructed only because paint flaked. I have plenty of marks. And the glass overhead has sat in its iron grid for a century.

What I am is what the glass lets down: the block of faint squares on the water.

This is where I come from. The footway, the people on it, a hundred years of ordinary daylight, stained on the way through by the thing built to pass it. I arrive without their sound. Their shadows cross me from one edge to the other and I keep nothing of them. On wet days the shadows are round and wider. I lie on water that isn't flat, so my squares are true in the middle and lean a little toward the columns. I am too faint, or too changeable, to raise moss the way the lamps do. I have no hands, which settles the matter of the brush.

Once in eleven minutes the book is set down on the table and I come apart. The dome shivers and I am several hundred pieces of violet going in different directions, and then I start back. I can't say how I do it. I'm not exactly the same block afterward. A cloud has moved, or someone has stopped on the glass to look for their keys. The time it takes is never the same twice, and I don't know it from inside. It is known from the column line, where the duty stands in the room the newts leave it and watches me reassemble. It has not put that on the slate.

It reads the bare staff by me, since no lamp burns in that bay that I know of. So the hundredth or two by which the figure differs may be the water or may be me, the angle of the afternoon. I suspect that's why it carries the figure toward the office and doesn't write it down. A reading taken by daylight isn't upstream of anything.

I would like it to go on leaving the space empty. I know what a heading does here. Under one, I would be a fixed number of squares, and I'd come back together in the same time every stroke.

At dusk I thin while the bats leave by the stair, the cloth drawn through its ring for a quarter of an hour, and by the end I am not in the hall. If a street lamp stands over the glass, some dimmer brown version may lie on the water at night and break on the stroke as I do. I can't speak for it. In the morning I am let down again, and it's me in the sense that the squares fall in the same places and the people are different.
