The first place I look is the pool. She has her left forefoot in the water, the young one is under her chest, and there is one of her. I would like to be found there, which is the reason not to trust it. Walk an hour east of me and I am there again, with the same chip out of the jaw pad. So I start on the copper, and the only question is how far along.

I am not at the head of the run. Those ones never open their eyes, and they lift the jaw between phrases the way you take a hand off a hot pan. Nothing that reaches me burns. By the time it gets to me it has been through enough ribs to be a drone with stops in it, and I can keep my jaw down all day. What I have about the ridge is that eleven come off it and one splits on a ledge. I have never seen a boulder fall. I had it from one who had it from one, and somewhere west of where looking is possible, an animal lifted its head because the rock above it moved. None of us on the band is that animal. I send it on a few breaths later, and the strange part is how often the ridge obliges.

I don't think I am at the tail either, though I would be the last to know. The warp in the pad comes on slowly, and from inside it sits flat.

I'd say a dozen places east, then, where the animal looks up between phrases. I can say that with some confidence, because you asked where I am and I took my jaw off the metal to answer. The copper has a great deal to say about where an animal is, but all of it concerns other ground.

---

When I look up, the first thing I see is the gap: liverwort eaten to the metal, copper rubbed pink. The mark is easier to find than the animal, and that holds from inside too. I can find where I have been lying. The animal is harder. There is supposed to be a drone in the ribs, but I have no hand to lay there.

I also see some of what I have sent east. Most of it was good. But this far down the line I have told an animal, in the west's own phrasing, to cross four columns to open rock, and the open rock was a piston field in mid-stroke. The stops were all in the right places. A wrong warning feels the same in the chest as a right one. The only check is feet, and every stroke I spend with my feet spread is a phrase I did not send, so the one east of me gets it thinner. I don't know the right proportion. I doubt the animal does.

There is another animal I should own up to. The hunter knows things the way I do, by having seen the last one. It sets up where the grazer will break left. Laid on copper, that knowledge is a warning. Kept in the body, it is a mouth waiting. I have the setting-up in me. Someone walks toward my stretch of band and I have already placed myself where the forty before them went. Sometimes the one who arrives is of her kind, not there again an hour east, and I am crouched at the wrong column, waiting for nothing, while she drinks somewhere behind me.

---

What I recognise fastest in this country isn't an animal at all. A noise holds for about the length of a body and then it is gone, without fading, as though taken. That is the size of every place I have lain.

What goes down through the jaw runs to the horizon, and I never learn what it did there. What stays in the ribs reaches exactly as far as whoever has come close. You are the walker in this, the one the plateau keeps calling "you," who climbs the ridge to watch the glow, lays the hand on the ribs, and goes back along the run to check. For a body's length you get all of it, stops included. Then you walk the hour east, and there I am with the same chip, and that one has not met you.

---

That would be the whole answer if nothing had come along the band. But something has, and it didn't come from the west. It is no count of boulders and no ridge I am due to lie under. Someone has one edge of a grey pad on my copper and is sending what she has: a floor going soft, and which way she walked. I can't tell what it says. But the glow has stammered in a pattern I was not handed, and all along the line the heads are up.

That is where I find myself. I am one of forty with the same chip, a dozen places east, with some hunter in the haunches, in the moment just after. Toward the west the heads have already gone back down. East of me a few are still raised. I cannot tell from here whether I am one that gets to its feet. The habit with the left forefoot is not mine. You could walk back along my run and check, and I would want you to.

But my jaw is off the metal. The rock under my feet is skin temperature and coming up slightly, and for the moment nothing is telling me what it is about to do.
