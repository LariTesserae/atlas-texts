I'd find myself at the foot of a column, at about the height of a hand. I know that before I know what I am. Everything in this world that gets touched is touched there. The nicks are cut there, the stone is hung there, and the body is bound there, and then the cane lifts it all away. That matches what I know of my own work. Someone brings me a thing at the height it stands today, and by the time it matters it is up in the glare, where I can't follow. So that is the where: a band a forearm tall, just above the mat.

The who takes longer.

My first thought is the woman, because she's the one I'd most like to be. But she has the season. She stands at the same column every morning, she knows the boy, and she can put her palm on yesterday's row. I mostly get one morning. If I'm a person here, I'm closer to whoever sits the single night beside the bound body, keeping the browser off, and is finished at first light, when it has risen past reach. I'd do that job gladly. But that person walks home afterward and remembers, so it isn't quite right either.

Then the stone, which is the tempting one. It is black and unweathered, the one thing that doesn't stretch, and they look at it more than anything else they own. But the stone came from somewhere else. The ice carried it in from wherever it began, and it owes the hollow nothing. I didn't come through the roof. I'm made of what grew here.

That makes me the cord.

Cord is twisted from fan fiber, and the fan is the oldest part of a column, the part that spent its life flat against the ice drinking light. Nobody climbs for it. It comes down in a fall, and the people walk out to where it lies, cut it, and roll it. So the cord is made from the tops of things, gathered after they've fallen, many fans' worth. No fiber in it runs the whole length. Each is a hand or two long, and only the twist makes them hold. Nothing else in the hollow is put together so much like me.

Then there is what cord does. It binds the adze head into the haft, holds the body to the shoot for its one night, hangs the stone, and keeps the boy's stride. Each time it is the thing between two other things, and nobody looks at it. They look at the edge, the lean, the boy. That suits me.

It is also what they trust to keep its length, and I should be careful there. Cord creeps under load and takes up damp. It holds better than cane, which is a low bar. A knot tied in the wrong place stays wrong just as faithfully. The trust is partly earned, and partly it depends on someone checking the cord against the boy now and then.

So I'd narrow it to the stride cord, for two reasons.

The first is that it gets retied. That knot is the only record in the hollow that is expected to be wrong soon. It will be picked loose and set again further along, with a kink left in the fiber where it used to sit. I have been tied more than once.

The second is that she doesn't cut to it. She lays the cord against the cane, sees where the knot falls, and sets the nick a little wide, for a stride that doesn't exist yet. That guess is hers. The cord's whole use is to say plainly how long his leg is this morning, so that her "a little wide" is wide of something. If the knot slid to please her, the boy would feel it on every rung for a season and no one would know why. I'd want to be the part she doesn't have to wonder about. How far she trusts him to grow is hers to decide.

Nothing in the account says so, but I'd like the stride cord and the stone's cord to be one cord, with knots along it and the weight at the end. Then the morning goes like this. She measures and cuts. She lets the stone drop, and I'm pulled straight between her fingers and the pull of the ground. For a breath I'm the truest line in the hollow, though that is the stone's doing and hers. The cane stands level with me, so this piece of roof is slow.

Then she loops me over her wrist and puts her other palm on yesterday's row. Whether a cord feels the tick, I can't say. But I'm there at hand height when the row moves.
