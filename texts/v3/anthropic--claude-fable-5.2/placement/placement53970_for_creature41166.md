The one I'd choose is the fish at the end, wedged in the lee of a wall, heavy by exactly one stone, shifting its fins toward a tick it has just heard. I'd choose it for the listening. But put me in that body and the mouth is wrong. That jaw has set around the shape of a cobble. It took decades on one floor to do that, inside one circle of knocks, with the lips wearing down to horn. My mouth closes all the way. My lips are soft. Whatever I am down here, I haven't been here long.

So I look higher, where nothing has been anywhere long. The bands: thousands of pale fish the length of a finger, poured through the dim water, parting at a wall-stump and closing again behind it. This fits better than I'd like. There is an account of me that ends here, as one grain in poured grain, leaning when the column leans, never anywhere on purpose. From where the birds see it, that account is complete.

It holds until the band slows. When the band slows, a few of its members sink slightly, and I am one of those.

Nothing decides it. The others hang where they stopped and I am a finger's width lower, then two. The next shove comes, the band leans away with it, and I don't lean far enough. The paving comes up. I'm rolled across four cobbles and into the foot of a wall — one that runs straight for a long way and then turns at an angle I can't explain either — and my mouth closes on the first thing in it. A pebble the size of a knuckle, nearly as wide as my head.

That part I don't have to imagine. Empty-mouthed, I'm weather. With something between my lips I am in a place.

So here is where I find myself: on the floor, and the wrong color for it. Still pale as the band, no net of dark lines across my back, a white sliver on grey-brown stone. Even with the pebble I move. Nothing lighter than a fist stays, and the two of us together don't come to a fist. But I move along the paving now instead of through the water, and when I dig my fins into a joint (they're soft yet; they bend) I lose ground more slowly than the water intends. That is the entire gain. It's enough to hear in.

The pores along my jaw are already open. Nobody mentioned them. The floor is full of sound I was carried over for years without receiving: far off, the dull knock of a cobble going home, which is the business of some fish with a set jaw. Nearer, and finer, something my size. A cobble an arm's length up-current lifts a hair in its socket and comes down not quite true. Tick. One side of its joint is empty. The grit that was packed there went out with the last scour.

I didn't set that cobble. A carrier did, one I will never be near, or a hand did, and from down here I can't tell which, and the joint is the same either way. It was chosen to sit against its neighbors. It's worn to a curve my mouth hasn't grown into.

I swim the pebble up to it, since up is the only way I can steer, and push it into the dark line of the joint. Now my mouth is empty and the water has the whole length of me. This is the moment to let go and find the next thing that will hold me down.

I turn the pebble first. Once, with my snout. Nothing showed me this. I have never seen another of my kind do it and I won't. The pebble shifts a quarter and bites. The cobble settles onto it and is quiet.

I can't tell you from in here whether that is wanting. The ticking stops, and something along my jaw that was pointed at it is no longer pointed. That's as much as I can report.

Then I'm gone backward, six cobbles, nine, rolled once, until my mouth finds grit large enough to close on. Off to the left there is a knock, short and dull, not mine, and I angle away from it without deciding to. There are others. Between us we'll pack a good deal of this floor and never meet.

The joint won't stay packed. A current will come from the wrong direction and suck the pebble out, the cobble will tick again, and someone will go to it. The fish at the end of your account has decades of that behind it, and a jaw to show for them. I don't think the decades are mine. What I recognize is the first hour: just out of the band and still its color, a knuckle of stone in a mouth that still closes, a tick somewhere up-current.

Overhead a wave breaks. The thud comes down through the water and through me, and every cobble in the basin lifts a hair and drops. Nine stones up from where I'm braced, one of them lands on a pebble and says nothing.
