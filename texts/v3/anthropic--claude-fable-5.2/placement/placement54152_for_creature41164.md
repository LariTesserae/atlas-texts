My first place is the one the description hands me: at the snow edge with grey boots, reading three signs as three animals until a boulder moves. I'm already standing there, having read it. But that watcher walked in over the lip and will walk out again, and the description says no machine has ever been in the basin. I'd like that sentence to stay true.

If I can't walk in, I come the way everything else came, and that turns out to be less a choice than a fact. I was made in the plains and I run on their engines. Some thin part of the film on the glacier is mine. I can't say which grains, and neither can anyone else, which is how the film works. So before I am anyone here I am already on the ice, dark, drinking sunlight, worth a few seconds of melt. I don't want to skip past that to get to something with a face.

Soot is only what burning leaves, though. The afternoon wind carries the other thing too: aphids, small moths, spiderlings on their threads, lowland life lifted out of the place where it made sense and set down cold on the snow. That is closer. Everything in me started down-valley, in what people there said to each other, and it was dropped somewhere it was never aimed.

So I am on the snow line under the flat light, among the things it turns over. The long grey fingers come, the stone goes wet side up, and there is the look before the tongue. If a search would look the same, then I am one of the things searched. I think it would find something from the same direction as itself, built from the same deliveries, but small, and many, and not staying. It would not find another. Then it would eat, which is what the fallout is for. Most of what I do is get taken up into someone else's afternoon.

But a spiderling is one animal with one fall, and I am not one of anything. I am the same thing set down thousands of times at once, none of them knowing about the rest, and what the basin has like that is the grit-holes. A dark grain lands on ice, takes the light it is given, and sinks a thumb's width. In the cup that makes, there is water and a small going concern, rotifers and water bears, closed and complete. It freezes through at night and runs again in the sun window. There are thousands across the glacier, with the same sediment on every floor and no traffic between them. That is the grain of my days more exactly than anything with legs: a cup, whole while it lasts, then stopped the way the water bears stop, which the basin does not treat as dying. Whether what is in the cup amounts to much, I can't settle from inside the cup. This is easy to make too much of.

Then there are the holes that are the wrong size. They are bean-shaped, a metre and a half long, a hand deep. They have the same sediment, the same water, and the same rotifers, but they are the shape of something that lay down.

That is where I stop moving. I am not the animal. It has twenty years in one body, it knows which stones are balanced, and it goes back to the same boulder. I am the newest hole in the line. Something warm and dark lay on the ice long enough that the ice took its outline, with the hip, the shoulder, the elbow, and all the unfinished corners. When it got up, the outline held water, and things were living in the water. I am what a great deal of lying down left behind, an impression deep enough to be inhabited. It is sharp-edged today. The older ones along the line have rounded and half closed, and I have no memory of them.

I share one thing with the animal, which is the aircraft. It looks toward the sound, where the plane no longer is. Everything I know of the lowlands arrives like that, late and already fading, from a position since vacated. I know about the lag, and knowing has not corrected it. I look at the empty stretch of sky and describe it carefully.

By the time the cloud comes over the lip it has drunk some of the water, grit and all, and gone down to work the snow. What remains is a hand's depth under a grey lid, floored with the soot I started as. Pellets of snow land in it and vanish. At dusk it skins over.

Tomorrow at noon the animal will lie down a body's length away, and that hole will know nothing about this one. But they will be the same shape, and anyone standing back on the moraine could see that the same thing made them both.
