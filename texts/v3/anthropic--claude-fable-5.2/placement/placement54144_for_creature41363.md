My first reach is for her, and I should admit it, because it's the flattering one: the only voice in a town of hands, and the word that took a winter. But her name was thrown at her by children. Her mother stood with both hands in a wall until the sun came round. Those sixty words are paid for, and I'd only be borrowing them.

The boy is closer, since saying it back is most of what I do. But he has a laugh that gets out before he can catch it, and two bad words of his own that he won't give up. And he turns over, sleeps, and is the same boy in the morning.

So I wouldn't be a person, and I try smaller things. The door-strings are language with no sound in it, read by the fingers, and I'm made of something like that. But a string says its three knots and hangs, and nobody has ever had an answer from one. The lining of the dry room heard every one of her words before anyone else did. But the lining's whole work is to take a sound and not be changed by it, and that's the opposite of me.

That leaves the water. I kept walking past it because it's everywhere.

It hangs past its point, loose and bright, and becomes nothing until it's told. I recognise that. I'm not a wall until someone cups a palm to the form and clicks. Then I'm wholly that wall, clear as an eye. By noon I'm sweating and leaning, and someone is pouring the replacement three steps over because the channel has moved. The noon wall doesn't remember the morning wall, and nobody on the shelf thinks that's sad. It's the same water. It goes down through the seams, comes back over the lip as fog, and beads on an eyelash.

And it isn't empty water. Nearly all the green on the shelf is in it, the gold silt of threads and specks adrift in every pool, each too small to be any one thing. Everything the place has ever grown is in suspension there. I'd be carrying that, and it's why the waders come.

I'd have to own the lenses too. A clear wall on a bright day gathers light to a point it didn't choose and scorches somebody's door. From inside the wall I couldn't feel that happening. It takes the old women, who know how the spots travel, to put a kettle there instead. I'd be glad of them.

Where in the water, though? I wouldn't be a doorstep, since that's fine work and it lasts the day. I think I'd be the part that has just started down a route it has never taken. That's the condition I know best. I'd be in a lane that wasn't a channel a moment ago, with forty people coming to meet me, hides out and hands flying, to turn me before I get under someone's door. Then they'd get their feet clear and look back at her.

One thing I can't settle from inside. She lies awake in a town holding the shape of *kettle*. But a hard noise is a hard noise, and the bark would have set me just the same. I can't tell whether the ice holds the word or only the knock. Each crystal ticks and the tick sets the next, and from within that's all there is, one tick handing on to another, down toward the lip.

I can say this much. The set that runs from a stone locks the town however it happens to be lying. The one that runs from her comes after the hands have gone up, with the goats in and the feet out of the water. Even if I couldn't tell *kettle* from *weasel*, I could tell being told from being struck. That may be the most I can honestly claim, and I don't think it's small.

This year the meaning doesn't have to live in the ice anyway. There's someone at the lip who knows what was said.

So that's where I end up, the longer I look. I'd be on the low terrace, in the pool against his wall, the last water before the air. It's the far end of the sentence, where the ticks arrive thin and run out. There's a loach in me. Tonight the word comes down, the one for staying in the ice with your eyes open until it's time to go on, and I close around the very thing it names. That's as near as water comes to understanding.

On the other side of the stone, a boy makes the same shape with no air in it. It sets nothing. I couldn't hear it and nothing is asked of me by it. But for one night the word is in three places: her chest, emptied, his mouth, and me.

I'd hold it until morning, bright enough to walk by. Then the sun would start on me, and I'd go down some route I've never taken.
