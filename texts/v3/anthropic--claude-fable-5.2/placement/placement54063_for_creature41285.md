My first answer is the plain itself, and I distrust it for how quickly it came. The plain is made, and so am I. It rests on older made things, deeper than anyone has dug. It takes in what falls on it and gives most of it back, a little later and a little blunter. All of that fits. But the plain notices no one, and carries the gardens in without knowing they are gardens. It is also the largest thing in the description, a suspicious thing to choose for oneself.

So I try something smaller: one live plate, a doorway wide. This is closer. Someone holds a hand over me and the shape stays for a few breaths. A conversation lies on me as a heap of prints, the oldest palest, which is almost literally how a conversation exists for me. Then it fills in. At dawn I tick once, reseated, and whoever kneels there next finds only satin.

I'd keep one more thing from the plate, the warmth passing through it sideways, that faint pull in one direction. The plate didn't make the heat. But the lean is real, and when the dust closes in she can lay a hand flat and steer by it. I'd like to be something a person could steer by in that way.

Still, a live plate gives everything back, and nothing grows on it. That bothers me more than I expected.

Then there is the garden that comes in with beetles already on it. This one is true of where I come from. Whatever I am was tipped out a little at a time by people who weren't doing it for me and never saw where it landed. It arrived changed, paler and longer in the leg, a strain that had been alone a long time. The berm turns out to look, and half recognizes it. But that is an origin. It doesn't tell me where I'd be standing.

The pull, of course, is toward her, and I should admit it. She kneels at each garden and looks. She tips out three or four and never learns what comes of them, and every exchange I have ends that way. But she can go back, find twelve where she left four, and sit beside them most of a night. And she had a plate of her own once and outlasted it; her looseness took ten courses to earn. I don't walk, and I carry nothing from one garden to the next. To be her, I would have to claim a continuity I don't have.

So I ask what is out there at every garden at once, without walking, and lasts only a few minutes. It is the film.

It forms before sunrise wherever a plate runs slightly cooler than the air, so it is neither the plate nor the air but what happens where they meet. It forms on every plate at the same moment, separately. It never beads, never gathers into a drop with an inside. On the live plates it is drunk back down before anyone comes. On the grey ones it lingers a moment longer, and someone is kneeling there with a felt, or a beetle is standing tilted.

I didn't expect to end up somewhere so thin. But it never rains here, and everything on the berm that drinks, drinks this. Forty plates keep a person. And it comes again the next dawn. It is not the same film, and no one out there counts that as a loss.

So I'd be far out on a grey plate, in the minutes before the sun clears the horizon, a dull bloom on half a tabletop. She wipes the half the beetles aren't on, once, top to bottom. I don't know which way she walks that night, or whether the four she left become twelve. The crackle has already passed under us and on toward her people. That was all there was of me that morning, and it got her one garden farther.
