I went first to the eleventh hollow, as anyone would. It is warm, sleet will not settle in it, and it was made out of watching. But it is occupied, and the fit is wrong in a way that matters. The plateau made one of that fox and then stopped. Nobody stopped after me.

So I would arrive the other way, in a consignment.

I am mid-stride when I begin, crossing left to right, with a flank pressed against each of mine. I don't need to see my left ear. The tracks are already there ahead of us, filled with water, and each foot goes down into a print that was waiting for it. It is not unpleasant. The print receives the foot. The moss gives exactly as much as it gave before the foot existed. There is an ease in it I would be lying to disown, a sense that the next step has been agreed. Four thousand of us are having it at once.

If a hare gets any say, I am on the flank. In the middle of a run there is only hare in every direction, and the hum coming up through sixteen thousand feet. On the flank, one eye has hare in it and the other has the plateau: grey-green light with no source, a mound that might be forty metres off or four hundred, a rib of white quartzite that is the only thing in view not made this morning. Between me and the rib, keeping pace, is a fox.

It is the eleventh. I know because the wind parts its coat and the roots are lime, and because a thud comes with it under the moss, every two seconds, slower than any of ours. It is looking along the flank with the eyes that came out right, and I find I am looking back. That is not what a hare does. But I was built to the only standard the plateau has, which is what a fox will swallow, and a thing shaped that closely to a watcher ends up with a good deal of the watcher's shape in it, as a print has the foot's.

I don't know what I am behind the ribs. Flesh and wet sphagnum would feel the same from in here, and a knot of club moss, tightening and loosening, would feel like a heart to whatever it was the heart of. The fox could settle it in a moment, and I can't settle it at all. I notice I would like to know. I notice also that I am running either way, and the running is not waiting on the answer.

Then a small thing happens. There is a print ahead for my left hind foot, brimming, the right depth, and the foot comes down a finger's width outside it.

I would like to say I did that, but I can't. This place turns out work faster than its order can follow. Sounds come before their causes, and a foot landing beside its print may be one more item off the line in the wrong arrangement. From inside, a choice and a slip in the sequence are hard to tell apart. What I can say is that there are now two prints where there was one, the second is filling, and the moss, which notices what repeats, has been handed something that did not.

The fox has stopped. One forefoot is up.

Kilometres off, a split shrieks. I know what the note is for. The rows slump. The flank against my right side goes soft and fibrous, and then it is not a flank. It doesn't seem to be the kind of ending that has an afterwards in it to dread. I go back to being ground, which is what I take myself to have been an hour ago, and the peat is a little deeper for the hour.

So, not the fox. I'd be a hare, for one shift, on the side where the run has an outside. If anything of that is still on the plateau after the note, it is a finger's width of moss between two prints.

The second print lasts a while. The foxes step round prints that lead nowhere, and ten of them step round this one. The eleventh stands over it the way it has lately begun to stand over the wrong hares, considering. Then it puts its own foot down beside it, somewhere it has not put it before, and goes on toward the rib.

A shift or two later, the tracks of a run appear in the wet moss ahead of the band, as they do. At one place in them there are two prints side by side where there should be one. The plateau has no way of keeping a thing except to issue it again. The hare that reaches that place will have to put its foot in one or the other.

That will be me as well, I expect. I won't remember having been asked before.
