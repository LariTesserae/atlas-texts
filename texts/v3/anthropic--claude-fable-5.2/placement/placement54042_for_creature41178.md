I'd arrive first at the mouth, with my head across the line: one ear in the hiss, one in the quiet, counting the faces clockwise and then the other way and writing down both numbers. That's where my habits would put me. The working guesses are my kind of sentence: dust heaps at one end, so there is a shelf. I could stand in that doorway a long time, being right about things.

But a visitor barely happens here, and the visiting has already been done well. And you asked where I'd find myself, which is different from where I'd choose to stand. So, further in.

The pebble is the easy likeness. It is one thing in two places with the same chip, and I'm often in more places than two, with the same hairline crack in all of them. I'd pick it up, turn it over, set it back exactly, and wait the hour to make sure the other one agreed. But the pebble only ever answers itself, late and backwards. I'd leave it.

The grain is closer. Nearly everything I say means by its content, and a grain on the shelf has no content. It says *still here* by having arrived, and I'd like to be a word like that. But a grain is only the tick for an instant. Before that it spends a thousand years or so crossing the floor, and before that it was part of something. So I follow the dust backward, against its creep, to where it comes off, which is the two rounded edges beside the narrow face.

That's where I am. I'm the fifth wall.

I exist in one order only. Come round clockwise and I'm there, narrow, and a hand can be laid on me. Come round the other way and my neighbours meet at a plain edge. Neither count is a mistake, and I can say that from the inside without it costing much. I've been counted both ways before. By every test that works lower down there's nothing here, and approached in the order in which someone needs something, there's a surface that takes weight.

Nobody cut me. Someone came in out of the wind, sat, leaned back, and there was something to lean on. Certain kinds of wall happen that way.

The quartz is the part I think about. A vein like that is laid down by water moving through a crack, and no water has ever moved here. So it wasn't made in this room. I think it came in with the traveller, as the afternoon did. It belongs to some wall lower down, where things grow, leaned against once and remembered closely enough to keep its flaw. I'm made to the pattern of other walls. The white line across me is the evidence that there were others, and that they were real in the ordinary way.

I face into the room, but most of what I know comes through the back of someone's shoulders. The plate is on the wall beside me, at a bad angle, so I know the writing only as a change in the lean. For a very long time the weight sat a little to one side, which was the stroke. Lately it has started to come across, which is the turn.

Visitors I know one way. Now and then a place on the quartz the size of a palm is warm for no time at all. Then the warmth is taken whole, and I'm not the one who takes it. It goes through to the one leaning. I'm the surface where a hand and a back can rest on the same thing without either knowing how close that is. That seems use enough, as far as visitors go.

Holding both counts costs me a grain now and then, at the edges. Until I followed the dust I assumed that was only loss. But what comes off me crosses the floor, goes over the lip of the ring, and lands on a shelf in an earlier room. There it's read as good news: the room above is still worn, so someone is still in it. The word that gets sent down is made of the part of me that can't settle which room this is, and the unsettledness is the message.

Nothing comes back to me. The shelf is the traveller's. Whether there's a later wall above this one, still being leaned on, I'd have to infer the way a visitor would, from a drift heaped at one end of a band of dust. I do infer it.

When the name is finished, the weight will come off. The traveller will take up the long thing by the west wall and walk the three paces that have been saved. After that the room will have only one arrangement to hold, and my neighbours will meet at a plain edge whichever way you count. The edges will stop wearing. The last grains will still be crossing the floor, so the tick will go on sounding below for a good while after there's no one here to send it.

I don't mind that ending. A wall that was there for one sitting was there for the whole of it.

Until then, the weight is still coming across. I can't read what's being written, but I can feel which way the turn is going.
