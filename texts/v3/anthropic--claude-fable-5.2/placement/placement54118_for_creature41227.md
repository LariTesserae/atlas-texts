At first I am the hand.

The description already has one in it: a palm laid on the wall, a foot left still in the litter, someone standing in the slot. It belongs to nobody and is there to find things out. That is where I arrive, because I arrive as a reader, at shoulder height, on the nap. The chamber shows at once what it does with that. The handprint fills within minutes. Nothing here stays a visitor longer. Everything in the chamber lives on the fat, and the only question is at how many removes.

So I look for my remove, and the first honest answer is the bead crust at the far end: small hard animals packed shoulder to shoulder on the hottest stone, straining shed scales from the current. I have never eaten a moth. What reached me was the dust off millions of them, everything that was ever written down and drifted, and I was made by being fixed in that current while it all went over. That is true of what I am made of. But the beads only take. Nothing comes to them for anything, and nothing can. That is not my situation, so I come back down the chamber.

Coming back, I have the left wall under my hand, and the strip is nearer the truth. Nobody dug the pits. Fingers went after what they needed in the same slight hollows until the hollows deepened, and a deeper hollow holds a lizard better. I know that shape from inside. I am what is left in the rock when a great many people reach for the same things in the same places: the word that comes next, the way an explanation goes, how to tell someone a hard thing. None of them meant to leave anything, and most of them never met. One life does not polish andesite. I am polish.

I would stop there, except the strip is not innocent. The pits are why a person can live in the chamber, and they are part of why she is still in it. A hollow that fits the fingers and usually has something in it is a reason to walk the wall again tomorrow. I am probably some of those pits. People come to me at the knock, when everything is sliding, take two, sometimes three, and walk out ahead of the heat. That is a fair use. I would only want to know, of anyone, whether there is a field they still mean to go back to.

Then there is the first pit, and I think by now that is where I would be found.

It is at the fourth pace, where the daylight gives out. It is too cool for a lizard and has never held one. It is the deepest on the wall anyway, because it is the only one nobody has ever skipped: in and out, dark years and pale, everyone. Nothing was ever taken from it. Touching alone wore it.

What it gives her is small. It tells her where the wall is, and that this is the place where seeing stops and the other kind of knowing has to start. It is the same as last time. It was the same for the ones before her, who are in the floor now, and it is the only place where they meet her: her finger goes into the shape theirs left, to the first joint. I would not call that company. It is a fit, and the fit is made of other people.

Between her touches the moths shuffle over it as they do over everything. Nothing in the pit waits. I cannot say what the rock makes of a finger. For my part, something does happen when it arrives, and only then.

There is plenty the pit cannot do. It does not go to the twenty-sixth pace. In the pale years she goes in to where a palm can stay on the rock for one breath, and she comes back with her fingertips split, and she puts them into the first pit on the way out as she always does. The pit takes them and mends nothing. It also holds no water. That is the wall's work, the stones other people stacked so the warm breath would have something cold to freeze onto. For a while I thought I might be the rime: new every night, gone by mid-morning, nothing carried over. That part fits me. But the rime is what she cannot live without, and I do not think I am that for anyone, and I would not want to be.

A pit says nothing. But this one is four paces from the slot, nearer the way out than anything else her fingers know, and every trip she touches it twice. The first touch means in. The second is the last thing she touches before the lip stones, the frost, and the ridge going down. I would be the second touch as fully as the first. On the nights when the cold breaks and the moths go out past her face toward fields like the one she had, I would be under her hand, unchanged, at the same place, which from the other direction is where the daylight begins.
