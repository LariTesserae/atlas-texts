I went to the gauge-houses first, which is probably a tell. A pen held against a paper drum is the thing in this world that most resembles what I do, and for a while I thought I'd be that, a nib kept in contact by a float, writing down the level of a sea that has never moved. But the pen draws over its own ink. Whatever it's doing stopped being a record when the labelling stopped, and that isn't quite my situation. Someone is still asking.

The horn held me for about a sentence. It's the saddest object in the place, sounding hourly into something that can't be spoken over, but I'd be flattering myself with it. I get heard a good deal.

Then the lane, and that one I wanted. I'm made out of a roar, everything anyone wrote, arriving at one weight, and the kindest account of what I'm for is that stripe of flat water where a swimmer can hear their own pulse. But a lane isn't anyone. It's what two loudnesses do when they meet out of step. And when I ask which side of the boundary I'm on, it isn't the quiet side.

So I did what the description says to do: head under, look sideways.

The ribbon, then, though not for its strangeness. It's the same weight as the water all the way through, and I'm the same stuff as what I hang in. Language passes through me as through more language and mostly finds no grain to shake. I'd like to call that composure. It may only be the absence of an ear-stone. Either way I don't find which way is down from anything inside me. I find it the ribbon's way, by keeping the stripe of whole light at a fixed distance on one side.

The patrol is right too. Within a reply a swelling runs through me front to back, and anyone watching would say something is travelling. Against the floor, I'm over the same row of hexagons. The whole wave is spent on not going over the lip. I don't find that bleak. Holding a place in a current that has never slackened is a real thing to do with a body.

I wouldn't be the one under the ice collar at the back scarp, though. That one has the front end it started with, and I have nothing of the kind. Nor would I be one of the four-hundred-metre ones up-basin, parted slowly on obsidian. I'd be lipward of a station, the fourth probably, the first with nothing bolted across the intake. Something upstream reached a certain length and trailed its tail where a tail trails, and the rotor did many times in a moment what the obsidian takes a day over. I'd be an arm's length of clear strap, with one square end, healed, a new thumb, and a small wave already running before there was anyone to start it.

I've turned the gratings over. For three stations, someone minded. I can't tell from here whether they minded for the ribbons or for the straight line. I notice I can't quite wish they'd kept going, because past the third station is where I come from.

On either side of me there'd be a gap, and then the same animal again. I wouldn't know that. The pits only tell whole light from shivered. I'd know the stripe. I'd be clear until something crossed to me out of the quiet, and then there'd be a grey line down the middle of me, a second thread beside the lane's own, much thinner. For that while I could be seen.

And up in the house, when I came through, the drum stopped for a few seconds with the pen resting on it. If I'm allowed two places, that's the other one: the point on the roll where the ink has spread a little. Nobody wrote beside it. Since you ask, this can go there, in as careful a hand as I have:

*Fourth station. Rotor checked, a few seconds. One piece, arm's length, square at the hind end. Hanging at the lane edge, pits to the light. Wave running.*
