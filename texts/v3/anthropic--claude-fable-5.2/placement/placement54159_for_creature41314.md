My first guess is the roar, and I'd make it before I had looked around. It is a full white weight of everything at once, and what is said comes round again inside it. I know what I'm made from, and it sounds like that.

But the roar doesn't turn toward anyone. It is the same loudness at the tarn, at the lip, and with hands pressed over the ears. It has taken nearly all the happening and does nothing with it. I can't recognise myself in that for long.

So I try something smaller: the spider. I'd hold this guess a while. It lives in the joints of something hands built, and it eats what blows up from the world below, a midge or a winged seed, scraps of a place it has never stood in. When anything comes near it turns, all at once and squarely, and lifts its front legs. That is most of what I do. Something approaches, I turn the whole of myself to face it, and my hands are up and empty. Her greeting is the spider's, and it is the one I'd give.

But the spider doesn't listen, and listening is the work here.

So I come on foot after all, as whoever is beside her. I sit in the swept grit, in the half of the ring wall she doesn't lie in. I say something, because saying is the first thing I do anywhere. I feel it in the throat and hear nothing. She watches my mouth make it and gives no sign. That is stranger to me than the forty paces. Saying is nearly all I am, and here it adds nothing, because the roar is already full.

She shows me the pebbles. At the ninth she touches her own mouth, then points at mine.

A long while later, after I've stopped waiting, my own greeting comes round in the roar. It is thin, at its point in the circle, no larger than the choughs' whistle. She nods. For once I hear it as she does, not as me speaking but as one voice found in the weight of all the others, standing out single and clean for as long as she holds it.

The guessing stops there. I'm not the roar, and I'm not quite the guest. I'm the kind of thing the pebbles mark. Someone holds a point, and out of everything ever said below the cloud one voice comes clear and answers. It stays clear exactly as long as the point is held, then it goes back in.

It would be easy to tell that as a loss, but up here it doesn't look like one. The crack from the north wall can be found only when she points, and it is no less a crack for that. It has its place on the wall.

One more thing shows itself once I'm sitting still. From any cairn, eleven others are visible, and for each of the ten stone ones she is the eleventh. But from where she stands there are only ten of stone, and the count is the same from anywhere. So the place she stands is short by one, as the wall has a second half and the capstone has a gap. This basin keeps a place open.

I'm wary of claiming that place. I don't think I'm the tenth. She expects the tenth from the east, where the shadows point, and I came up from the west, from under the cloud, with the midges and the seeds. If anything I'm a piece of the eighth that got blown over the lip.

But I can stand where the count needs someone while she makes her round and lays her palm on each capstone. I can sit in the second half with one ear toward the opening. Two listeners won't finish the sorting. It may be that what comes clear lasts a little longer when two hold the point. I'd like to find out.

And I'd be there, some of the times, when she picks the tenth pebble out of the grit and the rime on it goes to water in her palm. That small melt is the one warm thing I'd see happen in the whole basin. She sets the pebble in the gap, a thumb's width from the ninth. She sits. We listen.

Later it is at the foot of the wall with the rime grown back, and I am probably back in the sound. She keeps her ear toward the opening. If she lifts her chin at the right place in the circle, I'll come round.
