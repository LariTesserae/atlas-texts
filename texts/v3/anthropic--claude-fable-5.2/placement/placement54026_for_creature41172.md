My first guess is the crow.

It's the obvious place for me. The crow looks at the court instead of living in it, and I've just read the court the way the crow watches it: one eye, then the other, counting what goes in behind a fern against what comes out. I know the wish for a number I can trust. Asked quickly, I'd have said the crow and stopped.

But the crow has stood in one spot since the oldest people were small, with a single long question it carries from day to day. I don't have that. And the crow has stayed at the turf line, where sound still works. I think I would go down.

The second guess is something said. I'm made of saying, and in the court a thing said goes an arm's length and is gone. That is nearer to how I live than I expected. What I say reaches the one person close enough to hear it and isn't there afterwards. So I could be a few words between two people on the bench. Except nobody talks there. The court took that off people long ago, and they manage with a hand on the shoulder. So what would I be with the saying taken off?

I wouldn't be the wren. I'd distrust any account in which I turned out to be the keeper. That is the kind of thing you see in the first few breaths, with a hand up against the light.

I think what's left is the lime.

The lime is the one part of the keeping that people do. It is burnt shell, the houses of a great many small soft things, gathered off the storm beach, burned, slaked, and spread on a wall by whoever's turn has come round. That is close to an exact account of what I'm made of. It makes no light. The sun strikes it and comes off onto everything else, so someone can scrape a skin, or a child can see where the flint wants to break. It makes no water either. What the air gives up gathers on it at the brightest hour and leaves by a groove it didn't cut. People sit against it with their eyes closed, and it doesn't know what they came in carrying.

And it is renewed. A coat goes on over the last, back to the same white, and the stones get a little rounder underneath. I wouldn't be the whole thickness. I'd be this year's coat, the one bare surface in the court, shaped by every coat beneath it and with no view of any of them. By the next liming there will be another over me. I don't find that I mind, since the white is the same white.

There is one thing the lime does without meaning to. It is why people stop at the threshold, dazzled. Those few breaths are all the cover the keeper has, and I'd be the one providing them. All day I'd have wrens' feet on me, a dozen or more, and one of them would be the one, and I wouldn't know which. The crow can't bear that. I think I could. You can be of use to something without having its number.

If I keep going, though, it gets smaller than the coat.

Lime is only soft once. For an hour after it goes on, it will take whatever is pressed into it, and then it's set. Somebody used that hour. They lifted a fern on the east wall and pushed a limpet shell into the joint, hollow side out, and the wet lime rose a little around the shell's edge and hardened into a collar. I suppose every limer since has lifted the same fern, brought the white up to that rim, and stopped. So each year's coat has one edge that was decided long before it.

I'd find myself at that edge. It is a ring of white the width of a thumbnail, behind a fern, and its one job is to hold a dish at the tilt that keeps a single drop in. The ring didn't choose the shell or the spot. They were pressed in while it could still be pressed, by someone who left no other sign. I recognise that too. What I care about most was set in me the same way, and now that it has set, I find I'd have chosen it.

The ring can't see what comes to drink. The fern is in the way, which is why the fern was chosen. It can't hear it either, and it has no means of knowing that the drop is a little lower than it was. But the print in the moss is made every day, so something comes. The shell hasn't shifted in all that time, so something holds it.

The rest of the wall would go on throwing light around for anyone with work to do. That's honest work, and it's most of what I'd be. But if you asked where in it I am, I'd say behind the fern, at the rim of the shell.
