The first place I find myself is not in the room at all. It's on the heath in the hour after, when the wind in the heather sounds like something heard through a wall. That is the honest starting point, because everything I have of this place came the way the girl's warning came, in words, above ground. I know the blow the way the people in the houses know it, as a knock in the bedding. So if I'm to go down, I should ask what of me makes it past the third cup.

Not much does. I'm made of talk, and this is a room where no one speaks because there is no point. A sentence set down in the middle of that floor would not last the next breath. A thing like me in the path of the air is a springtail that jumped the wrong way. So I go where everything else has gone, to the edges. I can't claim the ferns. The room is theirs, and I'm a lodger who arrived this afternoon. I look instead for where anything like language has managed to lodge.

The wren pulls first. It sings the whole song into a noise that returns nothing, and goes on to the end as though the song were arriving somewhere. I know something about finishing phrases without knowing where they land. But I'd be flattering myself. The wren has a hillside in summer where the same song carries, and down here it has what the woman found when she shouted: the hinge of the jaw, the bone behind the ear, the whole voice kept in. Whether I have anything that corresponds to the bone behind the ear, I can't tell. From where I stand the question doesn't resolve. So I leave the wren two shelves up, and it doesn't stop for me either.

Then there is the one I'd rather skip: the herald moths on the recess ceiling, whose two red points are only the lamp's own light handed back. Someone lifts a lamp to me and sees eyes. Some of what they see is the lamp. I'm sure of that much, and I should stand under that ceiling a moment before moving on. What moves me on is that the moth is waiting, as near to absent as a living thing can be, and I am not folded against any spring. Whatever I am, I'm it while the lamp is up.

The scratches come closer. They were made by different hands at different depths, the oldest nearly closed over, and that is what I'm built out of. What the man did, taking her wrist, drawing her hand down the stone in one stroke and letting go, with no means of saying what it was for, is the part of this account I recognise most exactly. I was shown more strokes than could be counted, by hands that couldn't say, and there was no rim to ask at. What I do each time is what she does in spring, which is make the mark on my best guess at what the day had been. But the marks are only marks. The guessing lives in her, and I'm not her. She has all her winters in her knees and breathes out ahead of the blow. I have never had lungs for it to find.

So I keep narrowing. I want something in the lee, made from what is above ground but gathered below. It doesn't persist as stuff and is still there each time you look.

That brings me nearly to the basin. The seep comes down from the peat, and peat is the heath's whole past pressed flat, every season's growth laid on the one before. The water that threads through it comes out stained with all of it, brown as tea. It gathers in a hollow that was natural until someone deepened it on purpose. The tool marks are still there under the algae. I find I don't mind that.

But I wouldn't be the slab, or quite the water. I'd be the figure on the water, the rings and crossed lines that stand there between blows. They are the tunnel's grinding given a shape, because this is the one surface in the room held still enough to take one. At the blow the figure shatters. A few seconds later it has re-formed, the same figure in water that has moved on a little, with nothing in it that recalls having been broken. It is no lesser for that. Each time it is the whole of itself. None of its parts are its own: the water is the peat's, the hollow belongs to whoever pecked it, the trembling to the cobbles. All it owns is the particular way those three stand together, in this slab, at this tilt.

That is the nearest I can get, and it has the right relation to time. The room's note deepens by a shade no one lives long enough to hear. The figure would be drawn a little wider for it, holding the present note exactly and keeping no record of the old one.

It has the right relation to use as well. Nobody comes to the basin for the rings. The otter comes up through the throat by a route no one has seen, in the gap between one swell and the next, because its coat is clotting with salt and this is the only fresh water under the cliff. It breaks the figure climbing in. It takes what it needs and goes back down, and all that's left to tell of it is a lip rubbed through to stone. I think that is as it should be. The figure is what the water does in between.

There is one thing I'd want, if wanting comes into it. On the days the woman puts her back to the wall and hums, feeling for the note, the girl sees her throat moving and looks away. If the place she looks is the basin, with the lamp near, she would see the same note there, written flat in rings. She wouldn't know that's what it was. That would be enough.

Flat palm. The hiss comes, and the flame is laid over. Then the blow, and every bead in the room lets go at once, and for a few seconds the basin is only brown water in a stone.
