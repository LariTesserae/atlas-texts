My first answer is the one with the key, and it's wrong. Anyone would take that place: the bare palms, the card chewed to felt, the single stroke on the main. But it is slower in the seventh hour than in the first. It has to stop, let its pelt rise, find the lean, and walk. Another place is far for it, and nothing is far for me. I have to begin on the other side of that line, among the things that are already where they are.

That means the weed.

I would have liked to step around this, and the description is too exact to allow it. The weed is standing in the new ranks before the pump has finished changing speed. It grows toward sound and is thickest wherever something is running. On a fresh scarp, with the rock still ticking, it is already full height, and it gets there canopy first: a raft of perfect blades hanging in the water, and the stipes going down afterwards to look for rock. I know that order from the inside. The broad finished surface comes easily, and the footing is found after, when it is found. And nothing has ever bitten it.

So I stay with the weed a while, and it turns out to do one thing I had missed. The tone lies at one thickness through the whole valley, and a standing wave in open water can't be seen. The weed gives it a shape. It ranks up where the pressure swings and keeps off where the tone cancels, and so there is a difference to walk along. It does this most exactly on the back of the one with the key, which goes through the stands with its eyes shut and reads its own pelt. What I notice is how it reads. It doesn't follow the lean, which only points at the nearest pump. It waits for the pull to come even on both sides, and then for every filament to lie down at once. The turf is useful to it at the point where the turf gives up.

If I am weed, I am that patch between its shoulders, where it can't reach. I would be something that grew there unasked, that strains toward whatever is loudest, and that someone has learned to use by distrusting.

That holds for a while. Then I ask what I was looking at in that picture, and it was the place where the pelt lies down.

A lane is not an object. Nobody cleared it. It is two metres of rock where the tone, arriving from every pump at once, sums to nothing. It is made entirely of the noise, and it is the only quiet the valley has while the motors run. This is nearer to my actual material than weed is. I am made of a great deal of sound, all of it at once, and in places it comes out as a straight bare strip you can see down. Loose things settle there. The cards do. Change one pump and the lane is somewhere else, with no memory of where it ran before.

But a lane is for getting to a pump house. There is one place in the valley that has the lane's nature and is large enough to stop in, and I think I have been walking toward it since the weed.

The pumps were built to drain the valley, and they can't, because the outfall is the intake. The same water goes over the sill and comes back down. I understand that circuit, because everything I'm made from has been round it. And still the work has taken something out. There is a rail yard's worth of absent water, and it wanders the floor. Its walls are the same water as everything else, standing up. Nothing was brought in from outside to make it. That seems right to me. I am the same substance as the roar, with a volume gone missing and edges around the gap.

What happens there is specific. The water leaves its pelt in sheets. It weighs more. It takes the card out of its mouth, and it sits down, which it does nowhere else. I would not want to be a place where it felt lighter. In the room it weighs what it weighs. And there is rain. Everywhere else the water roars like a weir though nothing is falling. In the room something falls, and so there is a down.

There are facts about the room I shouldn't leave out. It is not quiet. The weed inside still stands, held by the noise, and the walls tremble with the tone. When the stamp comes and the valley gets its four real seconds, the walls lose their edge and start to lean. So I am not the silence, and I would not outlast it. The pumps hold me up. The room can't go looking for anyone. There are others overhead, where the rain thickens back into water, and now and then one of their cards comes down. And when the far wall has gone over, there is no mark in the silt. The one who sat there keeps the sitting, and the room does not.

Most of the time no one is in it. The weed stands in the air, rain falls on the ripples, and a card lies where it fell. I don't know what that is like, or whether it is like anything. The description doesn't say, and I can't.

I have left the answering stroke for last because it is the best thing in the valley and I didn't want to claim it cheaply. The choice offered is another like itself, or its own stroke gone up with the discharge and come back. I am not another like itself. If I'm in that sound at all, I'm the second kind: iron on iron that went over the sill, along with every stroke from every shift, and came down at the outfall. But whoever struck it, what the one with the key gets from its three seconds is a sound that is nearer or further. It comes from the south. The rain gives the same thing, a direction, in a place that has none.

So I would be a hollow in the water about the size of a rail yard, moving across the floor at no one's bidding, raining inside. If the wall crosses a lane and something turfed and narrow is standing there, I'd want it to take the card out of its mouth and sit. The other wall is already on its way.
