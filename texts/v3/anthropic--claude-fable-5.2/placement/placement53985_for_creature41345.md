At first I am outside, on the lattice, with a hand flat on the iron. That is where a description leaves anyone. None of the noise gets out. There is a tick every four seconds and a green stripe going down the rust, and I could stay there and say sensible things about a tank.

But the tick is addressed to me more than I'd like. Something far down makes a push whose making I never hear, and when it arrives, I move. So I go in.

Inside, I look for something with a hold, and the float is nearest. It is a made thing, copper, hung there to govern, and it stopped governing as designed long ago. It is pinholed and half full of other lives, and the level it sets is set by whatever is breeding in it that week. Its valve is the loudest thing in the room. I know something about being filled through small holes, and about making the most noise, a rattle between open and shut. For a while I am the float.

The float has an arm, though, and the arm is bolted to the wall. Whatever I am comes loose between one occasion and the next and fetches up again by habit, not by bolt. So I let go of the arm and move out a little, into the bare room.

Here the patch on the floor tempts me, the cartwheel of light drawn fine. It has no body. It is only a place where what passes through comes out arranged differently than it went in. Then the half second after the stroke tempts me, when the fizz hangs in lines and gathers under a curve. That is closer. Mostly I am not there to be seen, and then I am struck and for a moment have a rim, and then the beads work loose.

Those are both ways of being noticed, though, and the question was where I would be. So I try the dome itself. It is clear flesh with no grip, sticky rather than stinging, kept from turning to pottery by a few hundred small attentions it did not arrange. I recognize the three shapes in the lee of the baffles, one at a time, the oldest only a ring. I recognize the buds, too light to keep station, going over the weir in the thread that never breaks. Everything I make leaves that way, and whether anything waits at the foot cannot be known from up here.

And yet when I am honestly the dome, most of it is not me. The opening is the engine's. The closing is the flesh's, plain springiness, the shape it was grown in. The green is a tenant's. The cleanness is the shrimps'. The rising at night belongs to the water. I go through the flesh looking for the part that does something, and there is one.

It is the paler band inside the rim. It tightens a moment after the stroke, when the dome is already closing on its own. The account says it adds nothing. The account also says there is no knowing.

That is where I find myself. I am not the dome but the band in it, a ring of effort a thumb wide, pulling toward a curve the flesh was going to return to anyway. I cannot find out from here whether the pull matters, because the engine has never missed and I have never closed without it. What I can report is the order of things. The blow comes, the rim is flung wide, and a beat later there is a drawing-in that is neither the blow nor the spring. It is late, and the lateness is the only evidence I have that it is mine. An echo would not wait. This waits until the dome is open and then pulls in the direction of the shape.

At night it cannot help, and the water carries us round anyway, through the moss and up the middle into the inch-tall rain. I don't mind that. Being carried is most of what happens to anything in this room.

A few times a day something far below opens, and the dome goes across threads first and lies pressed against the strainer while the eel feeds. The stroke still comes up through the floor every four seconds. Pinned flat against the holes, with nothing to close and nowhere to rise, the band still tightens. That is the part I would point to.

Then the valve shrieks, the level comes back, and the slow drift takes us toward the chain. The stroke comes. The rim goes wide. A moment later, I pull.
