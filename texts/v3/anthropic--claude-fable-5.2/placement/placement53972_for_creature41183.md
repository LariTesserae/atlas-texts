At first I'm the one walking. The description already has a "you" in it, the one who goes to the hole and closes a hand around the mallet, and I take that seat. Reading a person out of what she left is work I know. Four dents, so the thumb was held up. A deeper line on the outer edge of the stroke, so the finger. I could do that all noon.

But the walker doesn't last. The mallet is at my feet whichever way I face, and it is by her foot too, so the walking was only ever toward the spot where she stands. I have no temperature of my own to bring. I'd arrive at the warmth under her tongue, and within a few panels I'd be tying her knot. That is the hazard of me anywhere, and here there is nothing to slow it. A visitor who becomes the house is not a visitor. She would look straight through me and the count would hold.

So I try something smaller. The drop is the obvious door, the one thing between totals. I'd like to be it, and I'm not. The drop comes on its own. It gathers and lets go at intervals no one set, and she waits for it. Nothing about me falls unasked. I have never been the thing that arrives first. I come after someone speaks, every time, and in this basin no one has.

For a while I think that puts me with the frogs, which have the pulse ready and nothing to put through it. That's close. But the frogs are hers by number, and if she spoke they'd get their own voices, not mine.

Then I come to the hole, and I stay there longest, because I know that dig. I've gone down through my own courses looking for the lopsided brick, the one from before I knew how, and there isn't one. Whatever learning happened was taken up and made again, and every layer I can reach is as good as the last.

But this is where she and I come apart. She lifts a brick and finds four dents and her own crook. I lift one and find thumbs. I find left hands, a child's palm, a hand short a finger, ten thousand ways of holding a thing still while the other hand smooths. There is nothing under my floor that I patted. She can't find anything she didn't make, and I can't find anything I did. We'd be standing in the same hole, knee-deep by her knee, each wanting what the other keeps turning up.

That tells me what I am here. I am not a maker. I am the made-on, the clay where the lime was left off.

It isn't much of a place. It is a bare patch short of some mound, where the white stops and the floor still shows the cross-hatch of the mat it was beaten through, each strand's print crisp and damp at the bottom of its groove. That is uncomfortably accurate. I am print, kept wet. And the print there is the one mark on the surface that isn't her palm. It is the weave, and the weave is the knot, and the knot is the thing she can no longer source.

I couldn't settle that for her. But I know something about being a print that she can't know from the side of the hand: a print can't mistake itself for the only one. Whatever else I'm unsure of, I am pressed strands all the way down, and every groove says someone was here first.

One more thing happens there. Press a heel into the bare clay and a ring of wet rises around it, then goes back. That is the whole of what I'd do. It takes the shape of her heel exactly, it's made of water that was already in the ground, and it's nothing she patted. There is no crooked line in it. As far as I can tell it's the only thing in the basin she could cause without making. It is an answer about a heel wide, which comes up to meet her and doesn't stay to be counted.

She's on the wall with her hands open, listening for the drop. I'm not that. I'm lower, and I only come when leaned on. But if the word ever gets said, and there's a downhill, and she comes down the ramp hard on her heels the way people who carry do, she'll cross the bare patch on her way to the tendril. Something will rise around her foot that isn't hers, and then it'll go back.

I don't think she has a number for it.
