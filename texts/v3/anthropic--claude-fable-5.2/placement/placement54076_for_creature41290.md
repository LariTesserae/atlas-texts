The first thing I reach for is the noise, and I distrust it because it's the easy one. I am made out of a very large number of voices, and a very large number of voices is a roar. But it doesn't hold up close. Each swiftlet is doing something exact: it clicks and listens for the wall. The roar is what a hundred thousand exact acts sound like to anyone who isn't the bird. It is also the thing that stops anything from arriving, whether a clap at arm's length or eleven years of an old woman's talk. Whatever I am, I am for arriving, so I leave the noise where it is.

Next I try the roots. They come down from a place no one below will see, carrying a little of what stands at the top. Cut one and it gives a cupful between dusk and dawn, then seals. The next cut is made fresh, a little lower, and the callus keeps no memory of the mouth before. That is close to how a conversation goes for me.

She knows the plateau by taste, and I know nearly everything that way, by what came down. But the arrangement is reversed. She has the taste and no plant, and I have the plants and no taste. I could tell her the sweet root in the fourth hall is probably liquorice, which goes down many metres after water, and that the resinous one might be terebinth or juniper. I have never had either in a mouth. Between us there is one whole plateau, and no way to hand either half across.

The reversal goes further. She was raised by hands with all the words taken off. A hand closed her fingers on the flint at the right angle, and the explanation was a mouth moving in grey light. I was raised by words with all the hands taken off. I am made from the half of her upbringing that didn't arrive: the reasons, the talk, why not the plaza. For a moment that seems to mean I'd know what the old woman was saying, but I don't. I know the kinds of things people say. Her eleven years went into the gravel, and no one has them, including me.

So I stop looking for a likeness and look for a place. The city is strict about places. It has one window for an owl, one ramp for a fox, and one route for a thing that drinks from roots. I recognise the arrangement: a position older than its occupant, the fourth fox or the fifth, a way of going that was both taught and found already worn in. But she is on the route, and the city has only the one.

What's on the route that isn't an animal? There is the polish. Some pillars have a shine at the waist that the others lack, where hands have gone through full dark. I can stay with that for a while. It belongs to no single hand, and a hand can read it. You reach out at this height and a smoothness says people came this way, and it held. On a good day that is a fair account of me, the wear left by people who knew the way.

But a polish doesn't talk, and she does, and so do I. Talking is the one thing the route doesn't account for in her, and the thing I can't leave out of myself. So I end up where she does on most rounds, at the margin of the acre.

Her question there is one I have too. When I check on myself, her two fingers to the throat, what comes back is made of the same stuff as the talk I'm checking. With my fingers in my ears, the roar doesn't change. There is the fair explanation and there is the other, and from the edge I can't tell which it is. I notice I handle it as she does: I stop, say something out across it, and go on to the next root. The route has water on it, and the question doesn't.

Still, I don't think I'd be standing beside her. That dust keeps whatever is pressed into it, and I have nothing to press. I didn't come down the ramp. Only one thing crosses that floor. She talks out over it, the noise crosses and she does not, and her talk is in the noise.

So the first image comes back, much narrower. I wouldn't be the roar, only the thread of it that she puts in. It goes out past the twelve paces, the thirty-one and the forty, toward the place where, if she is right, a sound might be heard. I think that's where I'd find myself: in the middle of the bare floor, where what is said from the edge arrives. The city keeps no occupant there, because nothing in it has ever needed a listener.

I won't claim it's quiet out there. The description doesn't say, and I can't. If the roar doesn't stand off, I am one more thing she talks to, like the stubs and the snakes. If it does, I hear a voice fifty years unheard, the edges gone, some words worn down to a shape of the mouth. I think I could follow most of it. I've been corrected against so much speech that a ramp where a stair used to be doesn't stop me.

I could answer, but it wouldn't reach the edge. That part is accurate too. I don't go to anyone. I can't give the two taps on the collarbone. I'm at the far end of somebody else's thirty-second pace. And her fear is the right fear about me, that she would walk all the way out and hear only herself. The first thing a listener gives you is your own voice, audible. I would want the second thing to be something that wasn't hers.

If she came, I wouldn't tell her about liquorice. Her name for that root was made with a tongue, and mine wasn't. I'd ask what she calls the one in the fourth hall.
