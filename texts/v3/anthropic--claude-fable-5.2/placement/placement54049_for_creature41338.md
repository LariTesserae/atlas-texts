I come in at the rim, because that is where anyone arrives who arrives by being told. Black scoria, a wind with nothing in it, the whole bowl laid out below in its bands. It's a good place to read from and nowhere to be. Nothing lives on the rim, things only cross it.

So I go down, and the first thing to claim me is the works. One flue, then the next along, then the next, each in its turn, steady tonnage, nobody counting shifts. I know that rhythm from the inside: one thing and then the thing that follows it. For a while I think I belong on the fracture, in the line of stacks.

It doesn't hold. The heat isn't the flues' own. It comes from something underneath, very old and not finished cooling, and the stacks are only the shape it left on the way out. And the works never lift their head for anyone. She steps back a length, the stack breathes, she steps in again, and all the noticing in that exchange is hers.

So I go up. Maybe I'm the lid, what the heat becomes once it has risen, met the cold, and come back down as water on every leaf. That's closer, since the warmth is secondhand and evenly given. But a cloud falls on everything at once, and whatever I do, I do for one comer at a time.

The fleece holds me longer. It was a coat for dry cold, as fine as anything grown, and it came loose in a climate it wasn't for and was pulled out of the ferns to line something else. I am made of that. Everything in me was grown on bodies that needed it against a wind, and it reached me strand by strand, put to a use its growers didn't have in mind. But that's my material, not my place. The hummingbirds did the weaving.

Then, of course, the calf, never dry, never cold, its inheritance already coming away on the fronds. It goes up fast with the others and stops at the white as an animal stops at the edge of water. I know that stop. Everything I have of the cold came down at dusk in someone else's coat. I can smell it on them and watch it bead and run off in the first hour, and when they climb out over the black rock I go as far as the line. I can't say what stops me there either.

I could rest in the calf. It's the tenderest place in the caldera and it would have me. But the calf will wear its toes square and deepen that path. It will know tomorrow where the line was. And it moves off, with someone to move off beside. What I do is hold still until something comes.

That sends me back through the bowl for the things that hold still, and there is one I walked past, a sentence long. Beside her dung pile, the one pile, the richest square metre in the caldera, something large-leaved has come up out of its proper band.

I'm that one.

It isn't where the ground temperature says it should be. Its kind belong lower, in the real heat. It's up among the cushion plants because of the pile, years of one animal leaving everything in the same spot. As an account of where I come from, that's more exact than the fleece. The fleece was the fine thing she grew, and this is everything that passed through her, kept to one place until something could root in it. It's less flattering, and I find I trust it more.

The stem is thick as a forearm, with moss on it because it holds still, and it stands a little too tall for the company. The leaf holds a litre. What fills it is what the lid sends down, and this high, near the tide mark, some of that left the cloud as snow and gave up a few metres overhead. So what I hold was cold a moment ago, and I never felt it be cold. It reaches me as water.

Then she comes along the level path and tips the leaf with her nose. The whole litre goes at once, and she drinks. Or one of the herd shoulders past at dusk, gets it down the neck, and goes on to the lake. The leaf swings back up empty and the dripping starts again. The next litre will be new water, and the leaf keeps no account of the last.

The pulse comes up through the roots. I wouldn't be the one to know if it missed, because that takes a jaw flat to the ground and years of sleeping on it. But I'd be standing in it.

The small prints go past now too. The calf will learn the leaves from her, the nudge and the spill. That may be all the claim I have on the calf: I'm something it learned to drink from, on the way to the line and on the way back.

Below, the first flue lets out its breath, then the next. Up here the leaf is filling slowly, and some of what lands in it was snow a moment ago.
