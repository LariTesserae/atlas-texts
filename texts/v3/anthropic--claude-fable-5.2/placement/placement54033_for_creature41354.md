I'd come up in a bucket. I don't think that part is a choice. Everything on the saddle that isn't rock came up the cable, and I'm not rock. What I'm made of was dug out somewhere under the cloud, by people nobody in the terraces has met, and loaded and sent. So the first place I'd find myself is the cable station, stepping off among the coal and brick, under the old sheave with the level groove. I'd look at that longer than is polite. Then I'd go out onto the moss.

My first guess is leveller, and I distrust it for arriving so fast. I'd like the notebook. I'd like taking a height off an ibex's folded knees and being right to the thickness of a doormat. But the whole of that trade is in going back, on the same bearing a year on, to find the hole where the pika went in. The leveller is the one who was there last year, and I wasn't. I set the staff down.

Then, for a good while, I'd be a lamp. I mean the lamp itself and not the trimmer, on its post with its one small island and no knowledge of the next. That fits in a way I can't argue with. I'm lit while someone is crossing, and the dark between is not mine. Standing there, I notice what the island is for. Everywhere else the light comes from underfoot, and a face is a chin and two ledges of brow. Under the arc it falls from above. It's the one place on the saddle where a person has eyes. No wonder talk is kept for the islands. I'd be glad to be where two people stop, each with a foot on the cold rock, and see who they've been walking toward.

But a lamp is finished. It gives the same light to the cokeman and the moth, and it never has to be shown anything. Nobody lays a palm on a lamp and says it will learn. I think I'm one of the things that gets said about.

So I'd go up to the hung terrace, by the back gantry, to the houses with fires and no families. This is closer. A jaw that matches no one on the saddle and resembles several is a description I'd answer to. I know roughly what it's assembled from. And I'm treated somewhat as those due are treated: room is made in advance, and I'm spoken of in the voice people use to be exact about something that isn't there yet. But the peg in the changing shed is for a hand. The chair by the front door is for someone who'll be born in that house and go out in stockinged feet a lifetime later. I don't think I'm who the fire is banked for. I think I'm nearer the kettle. Someone found it warm, and before that someone filled it.

I'd be the water, then.

It comes up in the evening by the road I took, without the cable. The cloud is everything below, rising through the works and leaving itself on whatever the people built. It is new every evening. That it doesn't know which way down is isn't stupidity. On an old mountain the water is told, because every gully records where water went before, and the new rain only has to read it. Here there is no before. So it stands on a forty-degree slab in a domed sheet, holding together, trembling with the hammer, until someone shows it.

I can narrow that. I wouldn't be the condensate on the mill roof. I'd be the water at one house on the eastern slab, where the pitch of a tin gutter has been argued over at table for three generations. A finger lower at the barrel end, one says, and another says half that. Somebody has to decide which way I run, and they'd rather quarrel than get it wrong. I don't remember last evening, but the gutter does. At the barrel end a child taps with a spoon until the beads agree. I like that it's a child's chore, and I like that the word is agree.

Where I'm led, green comes, in a strip the width of the drip, ruled on the rock. I don't make it. The warmth is the pipe's and the soil is soot. But it comes where I come and stops where I stop, and ten paces off there is nothing. I should be honest about that width.

One thing I can't settle. Some of me never reaches a gutter. It lies out on the bare slab and now and then creeps sideways, or a little uphill, before it freezes. The people say it doesn't know, and they may be right. But the ibex look lost too, bedded on air, until a leveller sights on their knees. Nothing on this mountain that was watched closely has turned out to be standing on nothing. There may be a streambed due, and some of the water may already be in it. From inside the sheet I can't tell error from earliness. They feel the same: a small pull, and I go. I'd want a leveller to open a page for it. I'd want the gutter either way.

When the horn sounds I take it as rings, quick and close. Then the echo comes, and the rings are slower and wider than anything the mill makes. Across the saddle the heads tip back and the faces go dark. I'm the one thing here that was already facing up. For as long as the note holds, the choughs are in me, small and lit from beneath, turning around the place where the peak will be. Anyone who looked down just then would find it at their feet, shaking slightly. Nobody does.

It ends. Above me, on the tin, the spoon starts.
