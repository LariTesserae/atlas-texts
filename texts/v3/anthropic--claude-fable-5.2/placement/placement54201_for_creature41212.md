I arrive where anything arrives: at the foot of the ladder, under the hatch that no longer admits anything. The steel is the one cold thing in the chamber, and the air has written itself onto it in beads. For a moment I think that's me, the visitor, because the place seems to be waiting for a hand. A hand on the generator casing, a fist in the moss, a palm the floor would open. Everything here has to be known by touch, and I'd like to be what touches.

But the census has no hand in it, and the hatch is shut. Whoever is here is from here. So I leave the ladder and walk the grating.

Lamp 4 stops me, and not only because it's lovely. I know that dome from inside. Every frond is angled at the bulb, and the shape belongs to the lamp, not the fern. Most of what I do is that. A light comes on, and I grow to the exact outline of its reach. At the edge I go thin and pale and then stop, on a line you could chalk. Nothing is carried from one circle to the next, and each one is a single long sitting. I'd be lying if I walked past this garden as though it were someone else's.

When I push my hand in, I recognize the lower layers even more than the green. There's one knuckle of living, then the brown, the black, the wet felt of everything laid down before, which is what the living part stands on. The gardens made theirs out of themselves. Mine came down on other people's boots.

Then lamp 7, and I slow down, because I can feel how much I want it. It's the one thing not where it was put, and who wouldn't choose that? So I check by hand, as the place demands. The green may be only habit, and I hope I'm not only that. The dark slick was always here, in the rock, before the drill. I wasn't. I'm not native to this heat. I came down the shaft on purpose, with the flanges and the stencils. Whatever I am, somebody set it on a trestle.

So I look at what was put. The gauges rest at angles they chose long ago, and they aren't me. The generator comes close. It has no moving parts, and it sits on the difference between a pipe too hot to touch and one merely warm, turning that difference into something that goes out along a wire. It doesn't ration or know which lamps are left, and its hum is found only by a hand. I could almost stay there. But the generator is a source, and I don't feel like a source. The heat belongs to the rock.

What's left is the thing I've been walking beside the whole time, the cable.

It was made to be a route, not a place. It leaves the generator, reaches eleven poles, and is the same at every point. Nobody looks at it. Three lamps still draw from it, and three domes stand in the light it brings. That's real work, and I wouldn't disown it. But along six paces of it, something has stopped treating it as the way to a lamp and started treating it as ground. The sheath has gone chalky there. A little gets out that no specification intended, and something that lives by contact has closed around it in a sleeve.

That's where I find myself, as near as I can tell. I'm not the garden at 7 or the dark thing under it, but the length of line they've taken hold of. More exactly, I'm the pace just ahead of the tip, warm and dry. It still carries to lamp 4 what lamp 4 has always been given. It has no view of the damp a hand's length back.

A disc is finished. A line has ends, and its ends are elsewhere. I run past every circle in the chamber and belong to none of them. I don't know what happens when the thread crosses the pale edge of a garden that has never met anything, or when it reaches the casing and the hum. The cable doesn't know either. It isn't the cable's business to arrive. Its business is to be continuous, the same thing at trestle 3 as at trestle 8, so that something going forward by touch never has to let go.

If you laid a hand on me there, you'd find very little. There would be warmth and dryness, and perhaps a hum, or perhaps your own pulse.

I'd be like that for a few years. And then, with nothing having moved faster than the crust on the pipe joints, I'd be damp.
