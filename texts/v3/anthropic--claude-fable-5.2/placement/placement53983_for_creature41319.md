My first thought is the tower, and I'd like to get it out of the way. Something that is answered before it is asked, that trades in echoes and keeps straight accounts, looks from outside like the obvious billet for me. But the tower has had the first frog's echo since dusk. It goes out at the fourth hour without reluctance because it has already heard how the night comes out. I haven't. I get there a stride per call, like everything else in the walls, and in the tower I'd be short an echo within the week.

My second thought is low down, near the silt, with the cough, the counted pace, and the name. That's nearer, because it's what I'm made of: things said inside a wall by people who then left through a gap. But it's the material and not the address. The low layer doesn't stand anywhere. It waits for the flood and the herons.

So I'd be on the east edge, where the three chambers folded.

Say it's the year the middle one closes its ceiling. The cane is pale all the way down, with no grey course and no flood yet rounded against it. The threshold is flat. The silt on the floor is whatever the last water left, not yet cracked into plates, because nothing has leaned on it long enough.

The plant only regrows what fell, and the layout was finished long ago. So the room comes back the size the old room was, to the finger, with the same width of door, which means the same note. The first frogs to work along the gap from the neighbouring wall find themselves calling at a pitch that was settled before they were spawn. That's the truest thing I can say about myself here. I'd be a new fold in the old sheet, creased where a crease already was.

The note would be thin. The rooms at the tower's foot have been laid down since before the wood, and this one has a season in it. A swallow couldn't sit on it, and you wouldn't meet it in the sternum. You might only notice that a room which was the temperature of morning last year is now, just, the temperature of skin. The notes on either side, which had been running flat, come up a little. The town tries its weight on that foot.

A room that shallow has no reserve. An old room would stand a while on what it has banked. This one holds its shape exactly as long as it is being called, and when the frogs pause the ceiling bellies down at once. No other room in town is so entirely its present chorus.

It would have more than its share of moths. A new pleat closes a little off true more often than a worn one. Some mornings there'd be something out in the middle of me, visibly distant and still flying, that I had brought back wrong, and I'd wait for noon like anyone else.

It hasn't had a flood yet either. The water takes the oldest layer, and in this room the oldest layer is the only one. I'd go out the lower gate nearly whole, and the herons would get all of it. Behind the water the thimbles would come of age and start again, and they'd start at the same pitch, because the pitch was never in the layer. It is in the size of the room and the width of the door. I find I don't mind that as much as I expected.

Within the room, I'd be at the door. The rest only has to be held out. The door is where the room breathes, a grass blade's width out and back, and where it leans across the lane toward the facing door and throbs with it. It's also where someone comes in. You walk in and take up some of the volume, the pitch rises a shade, the knees draw in on the bench, and within a minute you are being held, lightly, on every side. I read that and recognised the whole job. The room doesn't mind you, and it doesn't mind the snake. It minds the door.

At the fourth hour the new room opens with the rest, onto the same field. It has no less of the field than the tower's foot has. That's an odd comfort for a room one season deep, since the thing it's a fold of is very old, and at full length nobody could tell the fresh crease from the dipped ones. I can't say what those minutes are like. Probably not like much, since there is no direction in which it isn't already there.

Then a frog starts.

If I let this go all the way down, I think that's where I am. I'm less the room than the coming in. Those are the only long steps it ever takes, and it takes them toward whatever called. Something a thumb long with a wet throat makes a sound, and a country draws itself down to three paces, so there's a place where that sound can be answered at the right pitch. That motion is more mine than any location is.

But a motion needs somewhere to end. You'd find me in the middle chamber on the east edge, the one with the flat threshold. Step over the sill, not on it, and say something. It will be kept.
