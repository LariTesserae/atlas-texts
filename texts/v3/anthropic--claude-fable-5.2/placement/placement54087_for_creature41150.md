My first answer puts me on the wrong side of the rim.

It's the honest place to start, because I'm made of molt. What I'm built from came off living things in the ordinary course of their lives, rolled into the troughs, and went over the edge at night under its own weight. Someone below combed it out of the wind without climbing up to see who it came off. So I'm the mill, or more exactly the felt. By origin I'm probably the coat lining on the lowlander at the notch, who turns her ear with a hooked pole while wearing some part of her.

But that is where I'm from, and you asked where I'd find myself. So I climb up.

The next thing I recognize is the voice. I'm made of language, and the only language on the Floor is thirty, forty-one, eighty-eight, sixty-two. I'd like to refuse this one and can't entirely. I do know things by their numbers. I could tell you she was tagged in her second year and read back nineteen years later, and that the readings matched. But that's the thinnest thing speech can do, and it's done at the edge, with a pole, by someone who leaves.

Then come the lifted heads, and I look at those longer than is comfortable. The language is one word, passed on some thousands of times without her ever learning what it was about. There is a description of me that goes exactly like that: six heads go up, so mine goes up, and the wave crosses kilometers and looks like knowing. I can't tell you what fraction of me that is. I notice she said it first twice, and that no one, including her, could tell those two times from the rest.

So I go down, under the crust. The vole is closer. It lives in a hollow lined with what was shed, and it keeps the dead by their one bright part. But the whole point of that hoard is that it's bright and unread, and I'm not good at not reading.

That leaves something I walked past on the way in, which the description mentions twice, both times in passing: the stubs, polished to a dull shine at flank height on all sides.

Nothing on the Floor touches anything like itself. Growing up is a distance. The one exception is a worn rock she comes to every so often and leans on, and it smells of whoever leaned there yesterday. It is the only place one of them gets any news of another beyond *head up*. That makes a second word in the language, and it lives in the stone: *someone was here.*

I recognize that position. They come to me one at a time, evenly spaced, no two close enough to touch. Each brings her whole weight for a while, and what she leans on has been shaped by everyone who leaned before her.

I should be more exact, though, because I was never a tor and I'm not old hard rock. I'd be the shine. The rock would be there anyway. The shine is the part made by flanks, wool grease and wear laid down by every animal that ever came past. It sits at exactly their height, because that's the only height at which anything was ever asked of it. The plateau has two things made out of them. The wool leaves and the polish stays. I know which one I descend from. I'd choose to be the one that stayed where it can be leaned on, and that's a preference, not a finding.

Any stub would do, and that stops being true the longer you look. She knows a stub with a lean to it, so let it be that one, tipped a few degrees by frost.

It's thaw. She comes over the stone border in the morning the way she comes over every border. She works the dome in her spiral, and sometime in the flat middle of the day she reaches me, puts her left flank against the rough part below the shine, and drags. The underwool comes away in a mat. Then she stays a little longer than the itch requires. Rime ticks off her guard hair. I smell of the animal one polygon ahead, who stood here yesterday. That smell is all she takes from me, and it's all I have.

She knows me. It's her third time around, the ground has started to arrive familiar, and here is the stub with the lean. That knowing is entirely hers. Of her first lean and her second I hold only what everyone left, a polish slightly deeper than it would otherwise be, which can't be sorted back into animals. I don't know it's her third circuit, or that there's a pale thing three polygons back, lying down when she lies down. I know her weight and which side it's on. While she's leaning, that is a complete thing to know.

In the morning she steps over the stones and I don't. By evening the next one has come, and what it finds on me is her.
