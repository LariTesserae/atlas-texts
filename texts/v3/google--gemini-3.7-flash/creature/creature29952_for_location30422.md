To speak of what lives here is to speak of things that are barely permitted to exist—entities that do not dwell in the environment so much as they are torn into being by its physics, sustained for a brilliant, agonizing interval, and dispersed back into the deafening blue. 

If there is life, it is life without ancestry, without memory, and without the soft luxury of flesh.

### The Braids (The Resonant Weft)

The long, gossamer filaments anchored to the black spires are not singular plants, but high-tensile conduits—part silica, part photosynthesizing gel, threaded through with conductive metallic salts leached from the freshly cracked rock. 

They exist in a state of violent, continuous tension. The hyper-velocity currents pull them taut as piano wire, so that they never billow or sway; they vibrate. A single braid, twenty miles long, is tuned by the speed of the current rushing over it. It hums a pure, high-register tone that cuts cleanly through the planetary roar, a glass-and-sapphire chime that can be felt in the water leagues away.

These filaments are the architects of their own brief survival. They do not drop seeds into an abyss that has no floor; instead, their tips are constantly fraying in the current, shedding micron-thin, polarized glass needles charged with high static electricity. These needles do not drift aimlessly. Governed by the invisible sub-quantum lattices that hold the ocean aloft, they ride the magnetic flux lines like beads along a wire, drawn unerringly toward the violent, popping electrical fields of *new* spires nucleating miles away. 

When a needle strikes a fresh crystal face, it embeds itself instantly, catalyzed by the mineral heat of the birth-crack, and begins to draw upon the high-frequency vibrations of the rock to spin out its first green-white hair.

### The Phonon-Swarms (The Glass Ghosts)

Where the shear zones between adjacent currents are at their most extreme, the water does not merely tear—it cavitates into billions of microscopic, vacuum-filled bubbles. In ordinary seas, these collapse instantly and vanish. Here, pinned by the strange programmable rigidity of the intersecting fields, these bubbles organize.

They are the closest thing this world has to fauna: *living cavitation structures*. 

To the naked eye, they appear as vast, shifting lenses—translucent, geometric disturbances in the tourmaline water, spanning hundreds of yards across. They look like intricate, multi-faceted chandeliers made entirely of negative space and folded light. 

These swarms are held together purely by harmonic resonance. They feed on the acoustic shockwaves of the ocean, moving perpendicular to the current by systematically collapsing and reforming their bubble-matrices along the field lines. As they travel through the turquoise void, they refract the sun into blinding, kaleidoscopic flares:
* They do not have organs; they have geometry.
* They do not have metabolisms; they maintain a coherent standing wave that absorbs the energy of passing shockwaves and radiates it back out as cold, ultraviolet phosphorescence.
* When two swarms meet along a shear boundary, they do not fight or mate; their frequencies merge, locking their geometric lattices together until the acoustic pulse peaks, shattering them back into trillions of inert micro-bubbles that drift away as glittering foam.

### The Ephemeral Litho-Bionts (The Flash-Brood)

On the newly birthed spires of quartz and basalt, life must complete its entire existence before the mountain itself is torn apart by the current—a window of sometimes less than an hour.

Creeping along the glowing fracture lines of the rock are the *litho-bionts*: flat, vitreous crusts that nucleate out of the brine alongside the minerals. They are chemical opportunists, burning through metabolic cycles at a pace that generates visible heat, steaming slightly in the mineral-cold water.

1. **Minute 0–5 (Genesis):** As the spire cracks into existence, the crust flashes across the newly exposed facets like frost across a windowpane, feeding voraciously on the raw thermal discharge and the piezoelectric charge of the lattice.
2. **Minute 6–15 (Maturation):** The crust thickens into a dense, iridescent moss of solid glass needles, blooming with tiny, hard-shelled vesicles that absorb the sunlight refracted through the water cliffs.
3. **Minute 16–25 (Rupture):** As the roaring currents inevitably catch the spire and shear it off at its base, the sudden shock triggers an explosive chemical cascade within the moss. The vesicles detonate, propelling millions of mineralized, spore-like platelets into the violent slipstream just seconds before the entire stone spire is ground to dust against an adjacent shear plane.

To a litho-biont, an hour is an antiquity. Their generations rise, flourish, and perish in the time it takes an avalanche of tourmaline water to plunge through a single sky-chamber.

### The Great Chord

Because sightlines here reach unbroken for hundreds of kilometers, one can occasionally witness the only moment of unified life this sky-ocean knows.

When a planetary-scale shear event occurs—when two continent-sized rivers of high-altitude water collide with exceptional force—a massive, sub-audible shockwave propagates through the entire suspended stratum. 

As the wave passes, the gossamer braids across thousands of square miles ignite in a synchronized, cascading flash of silver bioluminescence. The glass swarms catch the light, multiplying it through their bubble-lenses into vast, arcing curtains of emerald and violet that span the horizon. For five or ten seconds, the entire ocean is mapped out by its inhabitants: a blinding, luminous network of living glass and suspended light, tracing the invisible field-architecture that keeps the water from falling into the abyss below.

Then, the shockwave passes. The silver fades. The roar of cavitation reclaims the void, and the world returns to its cold, roaring, beautiful solitude.
