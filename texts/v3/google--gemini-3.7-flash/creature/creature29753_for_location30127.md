They do not have names, nor do they possess the luxury of individual sleep. To watch them at their stations is to realize that the boundary between an organ and an inhabitant has been completely erased.

The most common of them—the ones who swarm the rising facades in their tens of thousands—are the **Plaster-Wrights**, pale, grain-sized crustaceans whose forelegs have ceased to be limbs of locomotion and have instead broadened into flat, spatulate paddles. Their bodies are semi-translucent, revealing the internal churn of their digestive tracts, which are not designed to nourish them so much as to process minerals. They gorge continuously on the silt-dust and calcium scrapings ferried in from outside, churning it within their acidic bellies and vomiting it out as a hot, alkaline paste that instantly curdles into stone upon meeting the near-freezing brine. They work until their shells become brittle from mineral depletion; when their calcium is spent, their legs seize mid-stroke, and their companions simply lay the next tier of mortar directly over their still-clasping carapaces.

Higher in the vaults, suspended in the stagnant water like living chandeliers, hang the **Siphon-Wells**. These are not arthropods, but colonial zooids that have abandoned the open ocean to serve as the city’s plumbing. Each Siphon-Well is a pale, milky column of fused flesh, forty or fifty millimeters long, tethered by chitinous guy-wires to the shell’s inner ceiling. Their bells contract with a heavy, arhythmic peristalsis, drawing nutrient-rich brine up through their stalks and sweating it out through porous dermis-veils. The lower castes cluster around them like cattle at a trough, pressing their mouthparts to the sweating membranes to feed on lipids and dissolved sulfur before returning to the scaffolds.

Between the tiers glide the **Demolitioners**, or the *Chitin-Melt Castes*. They are soft, blind, slug-like organisms encased in hinged shields of thick, grease-coated horn. They move with an agonizing, deliberate slowness along the older walls, their undersides trailing ribbons of pale, yellow enzyme. Where they pass, the cured chitin of yesterday's tenements melts away into a viscous syrup, which is immediately lapped up by trailing swarms of larval porters and ferried back to the nursery-vats. The Demolitioners possess no eyes, no antennae, and no defensive spines; they are living erasers, guided entirely by the chemical gradient of obsolete scent-markers left by the planners hours before.

The security and sanitation of the corridors are maintained by the **Encapsulators**. Flat-bodied and multi-segmented, moving on dozens of needle-pointed stilts, they stalk the shadows beneath the bridges. Because a single rotting carcass could spoil the sterile, stagnant chemistry of the shell-cavity, the Encapsulators react to stillness as a structural defect. If a builder pauses for more than a few minutes—whether from exhaustion, injury, or death—an Encapsulator descends upon it soundlessly. Its segmented arms fold the limp limbs flush against the dying thorax; its mouthparts rapidly spray a quick-drying, vitreous lacquer over the subject, tucking it into a neat, rectangular nodule. Within sixty seconds, the fallen worker has been transformed into a smooth, standard-gauge building block, left on the curb to be collected by the next masonry crew.

At the highest, deepest point of the shell—the tight, spiral apex known as the *Columella*—resides the **Chancery**. 

Here, where the shell narrows to the width of a sewing needle, lives the source of the city's frantic geometry. It is not a single queen, but a tight, pulsing knot of fused ganglia, pale as milk-fat, suspended in a bath of dense, oily mucus. It has no limbs, no jaws, and no sensory organs facing the outside world. It is a biological calculation-engine, an ancestral knot of nerve tissue that continuously secretes complex, hormonal scent-chains into the blue-green conduits. These chemical decrees wash downward through the aqueducts, dictating the angle of every archway, the width of every thoroughfare, and the exact moment a district must be founded or dissolved.

Beneath the Chancery, in the absolute dark of the deepest gutters, crawl the **Spicule-Harvesters**. They are the only citizens who ever leave the shell. Equipped with heavy, blackened carapaces capable of withstanding the raw weight of the abyss, they venture out through the scarred lip of the shell into the open silt. There, in the soundless desert of the ocean floor, they harvest the glass needles of dead hexactinellid sponges and the jagged teeth of long-decayed deep-sea predators. 

They drag these enormous timbers back into the cavity one by one, hauling them up the vertical causeways like ancient teams dragging obelisks. They do not pause; they do not interact with the other castes; they drop their crystalline burdens at the foot of the rising spires and immediately turn back toward the outer dark, driven by a chemical momentum that will only end when their joints wear down to powder and their bodies are folded into the wall.
