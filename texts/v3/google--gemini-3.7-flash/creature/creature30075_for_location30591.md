To live here is to understand that gravity is not a law, but an appetite. 

Everything that moves, feeds, or breeds in the spans between the stone needles is designed to refuse the mouth of the lower sky. Nothing possesses mass it cannot justify; nothing surrenders a grip it cannot immediately re-anchor. Life does not spread across a surface; it clutches, strings, or drifts, suspended in an eternal, wind-blown vertigo.

### The Wood-Eaters and the Marrow-Siphoners

The titan-roots are not merely habitat; they are pressurized vascular systems carrying thousands of gallons of boiling, mineral-thick sap through the open air. Because of this, the wood itself is a hunted thing.

Living deep within the moss-pelt are the **gout-ticks** and **chisel-borers**—crustaceans the size of a man’s forearm, their shells matte-gray and pitted like the stone needles they mimic. They possess counter-threaded mandibles made of biological tungsten, capable of drilling through eight inches of petrified thorn-bark in a single morning. When a borer breaches a sap-canal, the sap does not bleed; it erupts in a frothing, scalding plume that smells of toasted sugar and sulfur. Around these breaches gather dense, jostling communities of **blind skinks** and **leaf-tongued moths**, their wings fused into hard, scoop-like paddles to catch the spray before it hardens into clear, amber glass in the dry wind.

The roots fight back. Where a borer digs, the marrow-tree produces *reactive galls*—tumorous, hollow bulbs that swell to the size of cottages. These galls become micro-climates. Inside, where the air is trapped and wet, lives the **needle-ant**, an entirely blind, velvet-bodied creature that farms phosphorescent mold on the tree’s internal scar tissue. The ants do not tolerate intruders; if a borer breaches their gall, tens of thousands of them will flood the hole, biting the invader and immediately vomiting a fast-setting, rubbery resin that encases both the pest and themselves in an airtight tomb, healing the root-wall from within.

### The Pelagic Sky

The miles of blue air between the roots are treated by its fauna not as empty space, but as a dense, navigable ocean of heat and acoustic waves. 

The **drift-polyps** are the whales of this medium. They are colonial organisms, neither jellyfish nor plant, grown around large, porous spheres of bone-foam filled with warm methane produced by internal rot. A single polyp may measure forty feet across, its sun-facing dome armored in overlapping plates of chalky, calcified parchment that clatter like dry leaves in the thermals. From their undercarriages trail hundred-foot curtains of stinging, golden gossamer. These lines do not drift aimlessly; they are weighted at the tips with heavy, smooth pebbles—stones swallowed from the needles centuries ago and passed down through generations of budding colonies. As the polyp rises on the morning plumes, its curtains comb the air for swarms of **spore-midges** and loose feather-lice. 

Hunting the polyps are the **skiff-vultures**—creatures that are almost entirely wingspan and jaw. With wings of translucent, sun-taut skin spanning thirty paces, they have no legs at all, only four heavy, spring-loaded grapples tucked beneath the tail. A skiff-vulture does not flap; it banks across the thermal boundaries, silent as a shadow, until it locates a polyp. Diving from above, it tears away whole plates of the polyp’s armor with its serrated beak, gorging on the warm fat-sponge beneath before the polyp can vent its gas and sink out of reach. 

Higher still, near the blinding apex of the sky where the kite-wasp swarms drift like glittering oil slicks, hang the **anchored web-casters**. These are pale, soft-bodied arachnids that never leave the undersides of the root-bridges, but drop single lines of weighted silk three miles long into the void. The wind bows these lines into massive, horizontal crescents that catch gliders, falling swifts, and wind-thrown wasps. When a silk-strand shivers with a strike, the web-caster reels the line up using a muscular, rotating organ on its thorax, winding in the prey through miles of sunlight like an angler hauling a catch from the deep sea.

### The Underside Folk

The humans who inhabit this world are long-limbed, narrow-chested, and barrel-ribbed, their lungs enlarged to draw sustenance from the thin, warm wind. Their skin is deeply tanned, mapped with thick calluses along the ribs, shins, and the outer edges of the feet from a lifetime of pressing against rough cedar bark. 

They do not walk; they *lace*. 

A hunter or gatherer moves along the underside of a root-span inverted, suspended in a harness of woven sinew and insect-silk attached to three independent bone carabiners. They slide these clips along permanent guide-cords—*mother-lines*—that have been anchored into the iron-wood by their ancestors over dozens of generations. To unclip entirely is a madness reserved for the dying.

Because the acoustic roar of the wasps and the wind is constant and deafening, the people have largely abandoned spoken language for daily use. Their ears are small, hooded, and lined with dense, oily fur that baffles high frequencies. Between themselves, they speak in **tap-sign**—a rhythmic, percussive code beaten into the living wood using stone plectrums worn on the thumbs. The vibrations travel through the pressurized sap-lines of the marrow-trees for miles; a mother resting in a blister-hut can press her cheek to the wood and feel the pulse of her partner hunting three root-spans to the west, recognizing his rhythm by the weight of his strike.

When they do use their voices, it is a piercing, whistling falsetto that mimics the cry of the sickle-beaked swift, pitched precisely to cut through the drone of the kite-wasps without scattering the swarms.

#### The Architecture of the Blister

Their settlements are called **nodules**. To an observer from above, they are invisible; to a glider below, they look like clusters of dark, pendulous fruit clinging to the belly of the wood. 

A nodule is constructed by carefully shaving back the outer dead bark of a marrow-tree until the living, flexible inner fibers are exposed. The people train these living fibers to grow downward, weaving them with dried polyp-sinew and river-stone counterweights into teardrop-shaped cages. The floor of a home is not solid stone or timber, but a flexible, open-mesh web of boiled vine; dust, waste, and food scraps simply fall through the floor into the three-mile abyss below, leaving the living quarters scrupulously clean, dry, and scentless so as not to attract the skiff-vultures.

Water is life, and it is gathered at dawn. When the valley breath crawls up the stone needles, the women go out onto the upper curves of the spans carrying long sponges carved from the pith of dried drift-polyps. They wipe the night-dew from the moss, wringing the water into bladder-skins slung around their bellies. The water is yellow, bitter with tannin, and tastes of ancient rock dust, but it is pure.

#### The Hunt and the Drop

The hunters are trappers of the sky. They use no bows—arrows are useless in the unpredictable, shearing thermals. Instead, they use **spring-harpoons** mounted to the outer nodes of the root-spans. 

When a cloud of green-winged flippers or a lone drift-polyp rides a thermal near the bridge, the hunter launches a heavy, four-fluked bolt carved from thorn-wood, trailing a line of braided spider-silk. The bolt is designed not to kill, but to snag. Once the prey is anchored to the colossal mass of the root-tree, it exhausts itself fighting the tree's immovable weight, tumbling in circles until the hunters can pull it in with bone-toothed winches.

Every part of the prey is used:
*   The **horn-sheaths** of aerial beetles are split to make roofing shingles that deflect the corrosive droppings of the swift-colonies.
*   The **visceral fat** of the drift-polyps is rendered into a clear, smokeless oil used to coat the people's skin, protecting them from the sun-scorch and the biting moss-lice.
*   The **hollow leg-bones** of the vultures become flutes, signal-horns, and needles for repairing the mother-lines.

#### The Philosophy of the Suspended

To these people, the concept of a "ground" is a terrifying myth. In their cosmology, the universe consists of two things: the **Hold** (the trees, the needles, the bone-spans) and the **Hunger** (the blue void below). 

They do not believe in burial, nor do they burn their dead—wood is too precious to waste on fuel, and smoke brings the predatory swarms down upon the blisters. When a person reaches the end of their life, or when an accident unclips a worker from the mother-line, there is no mourning song. 

Instead, the community gathers at the lowest lip of the blister-weave. The body is stripped of its silk and bone so nothing of value is lost to the Hold. The wrists are bound to the ribs, the knees to the chin, making the body small and clean. 

They do not drop the dead; they wait for the great midday thermal—the plume that smells of hot stone and old sap. When the up-wind is strong enough to lift the hair from their shoulders, they launch the body out into the middle of the void. 

For several minutes, the deceased does not fall. The warm, roaring breath of the abyss catches the light, dried frame, carrying it upward into the golden sky, rising through the swirling sheets of kite-wasps, higher and higher, until it is a tiny, dark speck vanishing into the blinding light of the upper sun—taken by the wind, belonging at last to nothing at all.
