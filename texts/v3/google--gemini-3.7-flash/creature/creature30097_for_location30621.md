To inhabit a world of perpetual structural violence and boiling hydraulics, life cannot be soft, nor can it afford the fragile mechanics of legs, wings, or open lungs. What lives here is forged from the same uncompromising materials as the towers: dense heartwood, vulcanized resin, high-tensile fiber, and pressurized sap. 

The boundary between the city and the things that move through it is almost non-existent; the fauna are less like independent animals and more like the detached, mobile organs of the architecture itself—beings secreted by the wood to manage, exploit, and ride the kinetic frenzy of its growth.

***

### The Sluice-Planes
Along the sheer, vertical faces of the spires, where hot sap and boiling water sheet downward in continuous glass-like films, move the *Sluice-Planes*. 

They are broad, flat, and hard-edged—resembling hydrofoils carved from single slabs of cured, iron-dark heartwood. They have no distinct heads or limbs; their undersides are lined with thousands of microscopic, capillary grooves that draw the boiling moisture inward, creating a frictionless hydraulic cushion upon which they glide. They do not crawl; they fall in controlled trajectories, surfing the cascades down vertical drops of fifteen hundred feet, adjusting their course with razor-thin lateral fins that bite into the wet grain.

The Sluice-Planes feed entirely on the kinetic rush and the dissolved tannins of the water. As they plummet, their internal chambers act as miniature turbines, compressing the scalding fluid to extract minerals and heat. To return to the summits, they do not climb; they seek the high-velocity updrafts of geothermal steam bursting from the balustrade vents. Catching a plume on their wide, concave dorsal plates, they are launched back into the mist in screaming, ballistic arcs, trailing long wakes of vapor as they bank across the two-mile voids between spires to latch onto the next descending cataract.

### The Turnbuckles (Keystone Knots)
Where the titanic horizontal beams groan under catastrophic loads, a very different kind of life congregates. These are the *Turnbuckles*—dense, bulbous masses of living, cross-grained muscle-wood, some as large as the hull of a coastal barge. 

A Turnbuckle is essentially a living chock or dynamic brace. Attuned entirely to the acoustic frequencies of structural fatigue, it senses the microscopic fractures that precede a beam’s failure. It moves with agonizing slowness, hauling its immense weight along the underside of arches by contracting deep, fibrous cords that drive themselves directly into the wood like iron rivets.

When a Turnbuckle reaches a point of maximum shear, it wedges its dense, calcified body directly into the widening seam. As the beam tries to split, the creature does not resist the pressure—it drinks it. The immense crushing force stimulates the creature’s core, triggering an explosive metabolic surge. It secretes a rapid-curing, amber-colored resin that solders the splintered wood together, while simultaneously sending out braided tendrils that wrap around the joint like wet hawser ropes, drying taut within seconds. 

For weeks or decades, a Turnbuckle becomes a living part of the bridge, gorging on the high-pressure sap that bleeds from the wound it holds shut. If the beam eventually shears completely, the creature is torn apart with it—its core violently shattered into thousands of pressurized, spore-like seeds that drift on the steam, seeking the damp cracks of newly grown colonnades.

### The Cavitation Needles
Deep inside the hollow spires, within the colossal, vertical conduits that siphon boiling water upward from the depths, live the *Cavitation Needles*. 

These are long, slender organisms, rigid as obsidian and shaped like double-ended lances. They live entirely in the dark, suspended within the violent, vertical torrents of the taproots. They possess no eyes, navigating solely by the brutal pressure differentials of the ascending columns. 

The Needles are predatory, but not upon meat; they prey upon the fluid dynamics of the towers. By aligning their fluted, hydrodynamic bodies against the inner conduit walls, they induce deliberate cavitation bubbles—pockets of vacuum that collapse with hammer-like force against the wood. When the conduit wall is cracked by these controlled micro-explosions, the high-pressure cambium bleeds rich, concentrated sugars directly into the Needles’ open, siphon-like mouths. 

They are perpetually in motion, swimming against a current that would tear flesh to ribbons, their polished, green-black bodies vibrating at a pitch that resonates through the spire’s outer walls, adding a thin, singing treble to the deep, groaning bass of the expanding timber.

### The Pneuma-Vessels
High above the plazas, drifting through the pale glare of the open voids, are the *Pneuma-Vessels*. 

They are the only things that truly inhabit the misty gulfs between the monuments. Formed of thin, papery membranes of woven bast fibers sealed with natural latex, they are hollow spheres and ribbed cylinders, buoyant as bladders. They have no internal heat source of their own; instead, they anchor themselves over the whistling balustrade vents, tethered by long, coiled tap-lines.

As the vents roar, the Pneuma-Vessels inflate with superheated geothermal steam, swelling until their skins are drum-tight and translucent. Once fully charged, they sever their tethers with a sharp, scissor-like snap of their basal valves and rise slowly into the thin air. 

Drifting between the spires, they function as airborne condensation engines. As the thin, high-altitude air cools the steam trapped within their envelopes, the water condenses on their internal ribs and drains into a heavy, suspended ballast pod at their base. When the pod is full and heavy, the vessel drops rapidly through the mist, deliberately colliding with the lower root-thatched decks. 

The impact is explosive: the pod shatters, drenching the dry, newly formed timber beneath in a concentrated burst of mineral-rich water, while the torn bast envelope rapidly shrivels, roots into the impact crater, and begins growing the anchor-point for a brand-new pillar.

***

In this realm, nothing rests. The creatures do not sleep, nor do they seek shelter, for there is no shelter from a world that grows and collapses by the hour. They are the friction, the ballast, the valves, and the splints of a monstrous, self-generating engine—born of the wood, fed by its boiling pulse, and crushed back into its grain to make way for the next spire climbing into the light.
