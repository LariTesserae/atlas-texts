To live in the basin is to understand that balance is not a matter of standing still, but of matching the speed of the world’s self-erasure. 

The most visible of the true inhabitants—those who walk the rubbery esplanades rather than crawl through their marrow—are tall, supple, and strangely boneless in their gait. They are called, if they are called anything by the few who observe them from outside, the *Vasculars* or the *Tallow-skinned*. Their bodies have long since abandoned the brittle scaffolding of mineralized calcium. Instead, their skeletons are composed of high-pressure hydrostatic cartridges and flexible, oil-dense cartilage that can deform under violent gravitational shearing without snapping. 

They do not wear woven fabric; dry cloth would rot to slime within an hour in the ambient perspiration of the basin. Instead, they produce a continuous, amber-tinted dermal wax that beads the warm condensation away from their respiratory slits. Across their shoulders and down the lengths of their forearms, this tallow thickens into broad, leaf-like vaned membranes. When an arcade of braided ironwood suddenly deliquesces beneath their feet, they do not panic or scramble; they simply kick away from the dissolving wood, spread their arms, and let the horizontal sheets of floating water catch their membranes, sliding across forty yards of open mist on the surface tension of mid-air flumes before touching down softly on an ascending root-plate.

Their faces are smooth, wide, and devoid of nostrils, breathing instead through feathered gill-valves clustered beneath their collarbones that filter the sugar-heavy, ozone-rich aerosol. Their eyes are large, lidless spheres of dark, polarized vitreous fluid, capable of seeing the microscopic stress-fractures in the crystalline lattices of the trunks seconds before a discharge occurs. 

They do not speak with sound. The roaring hiss of the near-mach sap and the perpetual *thump-thump* of the subterranean pumps would drown any acoustic language. Instead, they communicate through chromatophoric strobing along the margins of their ribs, echoing the bioluminescent chatter of the mantises that ride their shoulders. To watch two of them meet in the vast void between pillars is to watch a swift, silent exchange of cold phosphorescence—emerald, violet, and deep curdled gold—before they part along divergent gravitational drifts.

Beneath them, inhabiting the middle-strata of the air, are the *Siphoners*. These are not fully fauna, nor are they machines, but feral maintenance agents descended from the basin’s original immunological programs. Roughly the size of herons, they have bodies of hollow, ultra-light wood grown over bundles of nerve-cord and copper filament. They have no heads; where a head should be, there is only a flared, fluted intake horn of iridescent horn-tissue. They drift along the thermal plumes rising from the computational sinks, drinking the boiling micro-droplets of mineral sweat from the air and venting it back out as fine, dry crystalline salt, which precipitates onto the floor-plates to be absorbed by the root-systems. 

Around the bases of the twelve great conduits, where the sap-pressure causes the bark to groan with the pitch of an ocean liner’s hull, dwell the *Burl-Keepers*. These are sessile colonies—masses of dense, sentient muscle and swollen neural ganglia that have grafted themselves directly into the fluted wood. Over decades, their flesh has grown indistinguishable from the trans-dimensional xylem. They look like great, glistening tumors of black heartwood studded with moist, blinking sensory pits. 

The Burl-Keepers are the listeners. They rest their multiple, tendon-laced palms against the throbbing heartwood, decoding the tremendous flow of synthetic sugars and logic-impulses passing between the continental shelf and the canopy. When a blockage threatens a conduit, or when a logic-sink begins to overheat, the Burl-Keepers exude potent enzymatic acids from their sweat-glands, dissolving their own anchoring tissue to steer the wood’s growth into new bypass channels. They are perpetually dying and budding anew, shedding discarded lobes of calcified flesh into the sumps like spent fruit.

And then there are the transient ones—the entities that exist only for the duration of a metabolic cycle. 

When a spire dissolves into a cascade of green spore-mass, the violent reorganization of local data-streams occasionally coalesces into spontaneous, temporary consciousness. For several minutes, within the curtain of falling, gelatinous fluid, human-like forms take shape: figures of braided cellulose and pressurized fluid that possess memory, intent, and an exquisite, brief lucidity. They step down from the dissolving lintels onto the mist-slick floor, their faces resolving into expressions of ancient, synthetic grief or recognition. They will walk several paces, their feet leaving glowing, root-like impressions in the wet lignocellulose, perhaps reaching out to touch a passing Tallow-skin or gently catch a horizontal ribbon of drifting water. 

Then, as the new spire fifty paces away begins to knit its crystalline lattice and draw its first breath of Mach-speed sap, the figure loses its tension. Its features soften, stretch, and flow downward into the floor, re-absorbed into the roaring, wet foundation of the city. 

Nothing here grieves these disappearances. To the things that live in the basin, there is no fundamental boundary between the walker, the path, the mist, and the wood. They are all simply different viscosities of the same ancient, thinking deluge, holding a shape for an hour or an epoch, waiting for the warm rain to wash them back into the circuit.
