Because there is almost nothing to land upon, life here has relinquished the habit of standing.

The dominant mode of existence is suspended tension. Organisms here are either permanently buoyant, anchored by tensile tethers, or specialized in the physics of perpetual falling.

### The Great Chord-Gliders (*Vitreovanes*)

The broad, ten-yard gliders that slice through the slate-colored haze are not avian, nor are they truly bats; they are cartilaginous rafts, nearly two-dimensional in cross-section, with flesh the consistency of salted tendon. 

Their bodies are built around acoustic resonance. They have no eyes—photons are useless across leagues of indigo dimness—but their dorsal ribs are exposed, protruding through skin like the bleached struts of an umbrella. Each rib is calibrated to a fractionally different frequency of the hollow’s ambient hum. As they drift through the void, the sound of the structure vibrates through these bone tines, transmitting an exact topological map of the surrounding spars, thermal columns, and hanging cities directly into their spinal cords.

They never land. They cannot; their limbs have withered into tiny, recurved hooks used only for copulation or latching onto the vertical underbelly of a passing basalt city during the violent octave-surge. 

They feed on *drift-slurry*: microscopic fungal spores, shed plant-matter, and the high-altitude beetle-fry that are constantly shaken from the pylons by the structural roar. A Vitreovane feeds simply by yawning. Its mouth is an open horizontal slit spanning nearly its entire front edge, lined with fine, comb-like baleen made of flexible keratin-glass. 

When one dies, it does not drop immediately. Its wide, low-density wing-surface catches the continuous thermal updrafts rising from the foundational radiators. A dead glider will spiral slowly through the middle dark for days, picked clean in mid-air by swarming aerial scavengers, until only its dry, singing ribcage remains to plunge into the heat-shimmer of the deep foundation.

### The Chime-Weavers and Cable-Lice

Along the thirty-mile needle pylons, life is compressed into ribbons only inches wide. Here, where the knife-clean bevels of synthetic glass drop sheer into the black, the *cable-lice* graze.

These are flattened, disc-shaped arthropods with carapaces of polished, translucent chitin that mimic the sheen of the carbon-glass they walk on. To stay anchored against the roaring draft, their legs end not in claws, but in high-pressure hydraulic suckers that seat into the microscopic pores of the manufactured surfaces. They move in mechanical, clicking unison, scraping away the copper-colored fronds and processing the metallic nitrates within the plant tissue.

Trailing just behind these herds are the *chime-weavers*. 

These are pale, long-bodied arachnomorphs with legs like glass needles. They do not spin webs to catch prey that flies into them; instead, they cast long, gossamer guide-lines across the gaps between parallel spars—sometimes shooting a single silken anchor half a mile through the empty air to catch a neighboring conduit. 

Once secured, the chime-weaver tunes its silk by tightening it with its rear pedipalps until the line hums at the exact frequency of the megastructure's baseline drone. When a beetle-louse loses its footing or is dislodged by the octave-surge, it falls through the dark until it strikes one of these invisible, tensioned webs. The web does not just catch the falling body; the sudden dampening of the string's harmonic tone alerts the weaver miles away, which slides down the singing filament like a bead of condensation.

### The Inhabitants of the Hexagonal Cities

Within the hanging basalt sectors—the ghost-grids locked in place by quantum pinning—the atmosphere is different. Here, out of the open wind, the air is thick with the warm, conditioned breath of the ancient machinery: a stale, dry breeze smelling of ozone and copper dust.

These empty terraces and hexagonal corridors have been occupied by the *Pulp-Tongues* (or *Graft-Marrows*).

They are soft-bodied, pale quadrupeds roughly the size of a wolf, but with elongated, prehensile digits tipped with a dense, spongy resin. This resin adheres instantly to the smooth basalt walls and ceilings, allowing them to gallop along the vertical faces of towers with absolute silence. 

The Pulp-Tongues are deaf in the conventional sense; their ears are closed slits. Instead, they have developed broad, fat-padded bellies that drag lightly along the stone as they run. Through this contact, they read the sub-bass vibrations of the quantum-pinning nodes. They know which room is structurally stable, which corridor is undergoing a harmonic shift, and where another creature has stepped three blocks away.

They live in the deep interior of the towers, nesting in the dry air ducts and maintenance shafts where the synthetic climate controls blow steady, body-warm air. They are entirely blind, possessing instead long, whip-like antennae that taste the air currents for the faint, damp rot of the external moss beds. 

At the edge of the civic blocks, where the basalt cliffs drop off into the void, the Pulp-Tongues sit in rows along the ledges. They lean out over the thousands of feet of empty air, waiting for the 20-minute surge.

### The Twenty-Minute Pulse: The Ecstatic Arrest

The life of the hollow is ruled by the clock. The regular purge-pulse that re-aligns the magnetic suspension is the central organizing event for every nervous system in the void.

Three minutes before the surge, the hum deepens, dropping below human hearing into a visceral shaking of tissue. 

* The Vitreovanes tilt their bodies vertically, aligning their thin wing-profiles parallel to the magnetic field lines so the induction current does not cook their blood.
* The chime-weavers gather their legs beneath their bellies and lock themselves flat against the bevels of the pylons.
* The copper fronds begin their reflexive curl, wrapping their moisture-rich veins inside a tough, fibrous outer sheath.

When the pulse strikes and the acoustic drone snaps upward into a screaming octave:

All falling things stop falling. 

For nearly six seconds, the localized magnetic inversion creates a brief micro-gravity plateau. The sheets of falling mist break into billions of stationary, spherical pearls that float fixed in the air like an endless field of cold glass beads.

In this frozen interval, a specialized class of animal awakens: the *surge-skaters*.

Tiny, feather-light creatures resembling ribbon-thin mantids, they spend the nineteen minutes between pulses dormant in the cracks of the basalt. But during the six seconds of acoustic arrest, when the air is still and the droplets hang weightless, they launch themselves out into the void. 

They do not fly; they literally walk across the field of suspended water drops, using the surface tension of the floating spheres like stepping stones. In a frantic, six-second blur of speed, they mate, snatch floating spore-clusters, and dive back toward the nearest spar just as the octave drops back to the baseline.

The pulse dies. 

Gravity reclaims the mist, which collapses downward in a sudden, crashing cataract. The fronds unfurl with a wet, leathery sigh. The great gliders bank into the renewed updrafts, and the endless, flat roar settles back into the bones of everything that clings to the wire-world.
