To live in the Sub-Plaza of the Seventh Siphon is to be entirely continuous with its physics. Nothing here has soft, indiscriminate skin; nothing here possesses the clumsy, tearing vulnerabilities of surface fauna. An organism composed of unprotected meat would be pared to ribbons against the self-aligning edges of the bridges, or boiled and swept into the sumps by the violence of the three-minute purge cycles.

Instead, life in the chamber has taken the form of things that can shear, slide, tension, and drink the current.

***

Along the underbellies of the cantilevers, deep in the blind zones where the guide-strips cast no amber, live the **Tension-Striders**. 

They are neither entirely mechanical nor purely biological, but resemble long, spindly armatures of dark vitreous tendon, narrow as reeds and twice the height of a man. They possess no central torso to speak of—only a node of dense, oily gray tissue housed within a shell of black, hydrophobic chitin that matches the texture of the monoliths so perfectly that, when still, they vanish against the architecture. 

The Striders do not walk so much as pinch. Their four limbs terminate not in pads or claws, but in opposed, microscopic ceramic vices. They move exclusively along the razor-sharp perimeters of the sky-bridges, slotting their limbs directly over the micron-thin edges and gliding along them as if on monorail tracks. They are deaf to the roar of the water, possessing no tympanic membranes, but their limbs are hollow acoustic resonators tuned to the gigawatt hum of the deep field-conduits. They graze upon the hexagonal moss, stripping it from the undersides of the walkways with downward-facing rasps made of synthetic diamond. 

When the purge cycle strikes—when the air reverses and the power-lattice shifts an octave higher—the Striders do not flee. They drop into a flat, diamond-shaped squat, locking their vices into the sheer edges of the metamaterial with an audible *snick*, allowing the hot, ionized gale to scour their slick carapaces clean of accumulated salt and mineral film.

***

In the gulfs between the suspended towers, where the sheet-water breaks from the cantilevers and falls through hundreds of meters of empty air, the cataracts are inhabited from within.

These are the **Lumen-Veils**, translucent, ribbon-like colonial entities that live exclusively inside the thickness of the falling water. They are ten, sometimes twenty meters in length, but scarcely thicker than a wet sheet of parchment. Composed of flexible, glass-clear cellulose interwoven with conductive nerve-fiber, they anchor their root-horns inside the micro-perforated conduit rings high on the rock ceiling and let the torrent carry their long bodies down into the void.

The Veils do not resist the fall; they surf the internal shear of the cascade. As the deionized water rushes over their slick, ribbed membranes, they harvest kinetic friction, converting the sheer weight of the drop into internal bioelectric heat. They are the true cultivators of the glowing algae: their skins are lined with micro-grooves that trap and agitate the dinoflagellates, milking them for luminescence. 

To look up into a falling cascade is to see the Veils undulating within the white, phosphorescent column like pale, thrashing spines—arranging themselves into complex, nested geometries to funnel the water’s pressure away from the modular towers. When a Veil dies or snaps under the hydraulic stress, it does not rot; it dissolves instantly in the deionized flood, breaking down into pure sucrose and colloidal silica that feeds the mosses below.

***

Deeper inside the architecture, within the narrow spaces between the modular towers and the rock floor far below the spray-line, exist the **Sump-Loomers**.

Where the Striders are brittle and light, the Loomers are massive, sessile mounds of fibrous, rubbery muscle that anchor the drainage basins. They appear almost like black, water-logged leather cushions, meters across, wedged precisely into the structural expansion joints where the monoliths meet the unhewn basalt. They are the district’s metabolic dampers.

The Loomers have no eyes, no limbs, and no face. Their upper surfaces are cratered with wide, porous siphons that ingest the turbulent water after it hits the floor. Internally, their bulk is threaded with labyrinthine capillary matrices that separate the ozone and the suspended algae from the runoff, packing the biological waste into dense, dry briquettes of inert matter which they extrude into the sub-crust fissures. 

They breathe in sync with the city’s three-minute pulse. In the moments before the purge, when the water flow hesitates and reverses, the Loomers vent enormous, shuddering plumes of cooled vapor from their peripheral vents with a sound like a distant foghorn. The fronds overhead twist and unfurl to catch this vented breath; the entire botanical layer relies on the Loomers to shed the heat that would otherwise bake the roots against the power-radiating walls.

***

And then there is the presence inside the modules themselves.

The civic clusters—those vast, pristine complexes of stacked apartments, administrative mezzanines, and seamless terraces—are not empty in the way an abandoned house is empty. They are sealed, held at negative pressure, their interiors dry, sterile, and dark. 

Behind the razor-edged, hydrophobic windows, moving through the cold indigo illumination of the guide-strips, are the **Curators**. 

They are small, segmented creatures constructed from overlapping plates of the same matte-black composite as the walls, scarcely larger than a human hand, moving on dozens of tiny, lubricated rollers instead of legs. They do not enter the humid, plant-choked open air of the vault; the moisture would ruin the pristine vacuum of the living spaces they tend. 

Through the glass, they can be seen ceaselessly buffing the internal floors, realigning the modular partitions, and testing the atmospheric seals of rooms meant to hold thousands of breathing lungs. They have no awareness of the colossal, roaring cataract only inches away on the other side of the pane, nor of the four-meter translucent leaves that slap rhythmically against the glass in the updrafts. They prepare the interior for an arrival that was scheduled before the crust above was sealed, moving through their geometric routines with the quiet, furious precision of a clock ticking inside an unopened tomb.

***

Over all of it sits the vault itself—not an inanimate room, but a closed, self-devouring circuit. The plants do not belong to the wild; they are the siphon’s cooling fins. The creatures do not belong to nature; they are the moving parts of a filtration unit that has learned to reproduce, twitching and grazing in the warm, roaring dark, perfectly content in the thunder of clean water and the eternal, electric noon of the deep earth.
