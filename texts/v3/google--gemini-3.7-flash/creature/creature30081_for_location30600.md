To find the inhabitants of this place, one does not look for signs of life in the biological sense—there are no discarded scraps, no stray hairs caught on the razor-cornered sills, no warm breath pluming against the dry, sub-zero mist. Instead, one looks for where the city’s excessive resolution has concentrated itself into a point of intent.

They are known, if they can be named at all, as the **Tenantry**, though they do not rent or build. They arrive into the district the same way the towers do: fully articulated, without infancy, cast directly into the geometry as though they were dropped from a coordinate above the sky.

### The Walkers of the Seams

In the narrow, finger-width alleys where two buildings interpenetrate, you will occasionally see one standing. They are human-shaped only in the way a spear is tool-shaped—narrow, elongated, and stripped of extraneous curvature. Their height is inconsistent, varying between seven and nine feet depending on which of the two ambient shadows you use to measure them.

Their skin has the dry, matte texture of unbaked porcelain, but beneath the surface there is no blood; instead, there is a faintly luminescent, slate-colored grain that shifts like stirred oil whenever they turn their heads. They do not have faces in the expressive sense. Where eyes should be, there are two perfectly flush, inset lenses of polished obsidian that do not blink. These lenses do not track movement; they remain fixed upon the vanishing point of whatever street they occupy, registering both the physical dead-end thirty paces ahead and the infinite, pale avenue visible through the nearest threshold.

Their garments are integral to their bodies—heavy, seamless robes of a dense, non-woven textile resembling compressed shale dust. The hems do not sway as they walk; they have no inertia. When a Walker moves, they do not push off the flagstones. They simply cease to coincide with their previous position and coincide with the next, progressing in smooth, dead-silent increments that leave the spherical, mercury-bright beads of condensation on the pavement entirely undisturbed.

They communicate without sound, though to stand within three paces of a Walker is to feel a sharp, cold ache in the molars—the same high-frequency vibration that hums within the black slate walls. It is a language of pure spatial adjustment: a micro-tilt of the jaw, an imperceptible realigning of their dual shadows, which other Walkers register instantly across the entire basin.

### The Avenued Millions

The true population does not inhabit the streets; the streets are merely the vestibules. 

If you stop before one of the atom-sharp doorways—where the interior opens into a sun-bleached, pale boulevard extending miles past the six-inch depth of the building—you will see them. In the far distance of these compressed vistas, crowds are moving. They are small as pencil points, yet their clarity is infinite. They walk in dense, organized currents through the pale daylight of those interior plains, dressed in colors that do not exist under the overcast exterior sky: shades of scorched zinc, cold cadmium, and deep, non-spectral violet.

These are the **Dwellers of the Metric**. They exist in an intense, high-bandwidth state of civilization within volumes of space that have been folded down to the thickness of a leaf. Occasionally, one of them will walk toward the threshold from miles within, growing larger and more distinct with every stride, until their face is six inches from the opening. They will pause there, looking out into the freezing, narrow canyon of the sunken district. 

From the outside, their face appears framed by the doorway like a portrait on a mantle. You can see the minute, hexagonal pores of their skin, the slow drift of their cold pupils, the absolute stillness of their lips. They will watch the exterior mist gather on the shale walls for hours without blinking, studying the slower world outside with the patient, clinical regard of a diver inspecting coral through a thick pane of glass, before turning and walking back into the pale miles of the interior.

### The Latency Feeders and Metric Fauna

Around the edges of this populace, non-human entities maintain the district’s strange, synthetic metabolism:

*   **The Lag-Moths:** Clustered thickly around the frames of the vacuum windows are small, paper-flat organisms with triangular wings of crystallized graphite. They do not eat matter. They anchor themselves to the perimeter of the frames where the daylight is delayed by half a second, feeding entirely on the temporal shear—the tiny, energetic friction created when the past and present overlap on the glass. When startled, they scatter with the dry, rustling sound of shuffled cards, leaving behind small, rectangular voids in the air that take several minutes to heal.
*   **The Discontinuous Swifts:** The swift-like creatures that dart between the cantilevered rooftops are not birds; they are single-point observers. They possess no organs, only a polished, teardrop-shaped shell of cold copper. They exist to measure the district. Each of their instantaneous, discontinuous leaps is a calculation of distance. Where they land, the air smells sharply of ozone, because for a fraction of a millisecond, they have displaced space rather than moved through it.
*   **The Bismuth Turners:** In the drainage gullies, the segmented entities with shells like stepped pyramids of bismuth continue their figure-eights. They are the ball bearings of the district’s shifting geography. Wherever they trace their loops, the stone beneath them remains fluid at an atomic level, allowing the stairwells to tilt and the walls to rotate without cracking or generating friction. If you pick one up—its weight is shockingly immense, like lifting a lead anchor the size of a beetle—the pavement beneath it immediately hardens into absolute, unyielding shale, and the faint, spatial sigh of the surrounding buildings rises to a sharp, stressed whine until it is replaced.

### The Presence in the Stone

Deepest of all are the entities that do not walk, but are *co-present* with the district's construction.

Where the copper-grained lintels pass through the matte-white walls, you can sometimes see human-like silhouettes embedded entirely within the solid substance. They are not trapped corpses; their eyes are open, tracking the slow, multi-hour drift of the street orientations. They are the **Anchors**—beings whose consciousness is distributed throughout the load-bearing conduits and the fiber-optic bundles beneath the pewter shrubs. 

When the local metric of space shifts, and a vertical flight of steps settles into a horizontal floor, it is the Anchors who absorb the torque of the universe twisting around them. Their fingers, faintly visible within the translucent layers of the post-physical slate, twitch in complex, algorithmic sequences, holding the sunken basin steady against the crushing weight of the ordinary, un-rendered world outside the rim.

They are waiting. The entire population—from the silent watchers at the door-frames to the millions walking the compressed avenues—exists in a state of suspended readiness. They are not asleep; they are simply calibrated to an arrival. When the raw shale perimeter finally weathers, or when the laws of the surrounding earth finally decay enough to match the flawless, freezing mathematics of their city, they will step out of the doorways all at once, and the narrow basin will unfold to cover the world.
