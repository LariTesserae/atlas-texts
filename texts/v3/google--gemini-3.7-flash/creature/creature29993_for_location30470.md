To survive in the Great Chasm is to have made an absolute peace with the absence of water. The life that moves through this dry, phosphor-lit expanse does not bleed, does not sweat, and possesses none of the wet vulnerabilities of the surface world. Everything here is constructed from dry plates, taut fibers, and sealed oils—a biological mirror to the cold foundries bolted to the stipes.

### The Grazers of the Turf: The *Steelyards*

The chalk-white creatures moving across the fibrous floor have the gait of long-legged stags, but their bodies are built entirely of jointed, porcelain-hard plates. The workers call them *steelyards* or *flint-marrows*. 

Their legs are triple-jointed and extraordinarily thin, terminating not in soft hooves, but in broad, self-sharpening chisel-pads made of dense mineralized keratin. When they walk across the razor-edged shelves of fallen xylem, there is no sound of clicking—the sound-eating turf swallows their steps entirely. They move like puppets drawn on invisible strings, silent and weightless despite weighing several hundredweight.

A steelyard does not graze by tearing; it planes. Its mouthparts consist of four horizontally opposed scissor-plates of amber-colored horn, powered by compact, dry-muscle bundles. It leans against a vertical spire of reed-timber and walks forward, neatly shaving off ribbons of fresh, mineral-rich bark with the crisp, continuous sound of an automated wood plane. Inside its barrel-like torso, there is no churning stomach of wet acid. Instead, it houses a dry, gizzard-like mill filled with ingested flint chips that grind the wood to an impalpable talc. From this dust, its body extracts aerosolized copper, silica, and dry starches, while its wastes are expelled as dense, compressed briquettes of dry fuel—eagerly gathered by the rail-crews to fire the pneumatic booster boilers.

Their eyes are not wet globes, but polished, multi-faceted lenses of clear, unblinking calcite. Behind these lenses, a cold, deliberate intelligence operates. They have no fear of the roaring foundries or the sliding cable cars; they treat the dark-lacquered steel complexes as merely another variety of shelf-fungus, often rubbing their itchy molting-crests against the vibration-damped pylons of the rail bridges.

### The Drifters: *Pellicle-Wings*

High above, cruising the thermals that spill from the refinery cooling banks, drift the gliders. Up close, they bear little resemblance to birds or bats. They are *pellicle-wings*—creatures that are essentially living sheets of tensioned vellum.

Their bodies are as flat and light as a yard of linen, built around a flexible keel of hollow, silica-ribbed reed-bone. Their wing surfaces are translucent, dry membranes made of tightly woven cellular skin that feels like oiled tracing paper. They have no flapping flight; they are creatures of pure buoyancy and thermal balance. When one wishes to rise, it alters the tension along its trailing edge by pulling on internal mineral-tendons, banking sharply into the invisible plume of dry heat rising from an ozone dynamo.

The pellicle-wings live on the aerial fallout of the Chasm. Their undersides are carpeted in an ultra-fine, velvety pile that carries a permanent electrostatic charge. As they sweep through the vast, clear voids between stipe-pillars, this velvet combs the air, collecting the microscopic drifts of dry resin-spores, zinc flakes, and shed cellulose that circulate on the ventilation winds. 

Periodically, a glider will bank toward one of the lacquered housing tiers, landing softly against the vertical wood with a dry *flump*. Clinging with hook-like wing-tips, it uses a specialized, prehensile rasp-tongue to groom the accumulated dust from its chest-velvet, swallowing the nutrient-rich paste before launching itself back into the phosphor glare.

### The Grain-Creepers: *Lacquers and Spindle-Mites*

Deep within the microscopic cellular tubes of the stipes themselves exists a frantic, micro-scale ecology. The most vital of these are the *lacquer-mites*—flat, silver-bodied creatures no larger than a coin, possessing eight pairs of brush-tipped limbs.

Where the great stipes groan and micro-fracture under the weight of the steel foundries bolted to their flanks, the lacquer-mites swarm. Drawn by the scent of freshly ruptured xylem, they harvest the slow-seeping, hyper-dense amber from the core and mix it with their own dry, alkaline secretions. Working with frantic, synchronized precision, they pack this mixture into the cracks, where it cures in the arid air within seconds to a glassy, steel-hard seal. 

Without the mites, the stipes would split under the localized torque of the industrial elevators. The factory masters know this; the vents of the refineries are deliberately seeded with dried starch-meal to feed the mite-colonies, maintaining an unspoken, industrial symbiosis.

### The Inhabitants: The *Vapour-Frugal*

The people who work the drop-forges and tend the sifting mills are not pale troglodytes; they are a population hardened and perfected by the absolute aridity of the rift. They call themselves the *Lignarii*, though the surface-traders who meet them at the upper pneumatic shafts simply call them the *Dry-Lungs*.

To a stranger, their appearance is startlingly sculptural. Their skin has the matte, non-reflective finish of fine suede, ranging in color from ash-grey to deep walnut. They do not sweat—an evolutionary or engineered adaptation that prevents catastrophic dehydration. Instead, their circulatory system shunts excess heat through broad, vascularized cooling flanges woven into the heavy, lacquered-silk tunics they wear, or directly into the chilled, circulating copper handrails installed along every urban catwalk.

Their eyes are shielded behind dual lids: an outer, clear nictitating membrane that sweeps away the ubiquitous talc dust, and an inner, amber-tinted lens that filters the unrelenting noon-glare of the overhead phosphor lattices. 

Because the air is devoid of moisture, human voices do not carry well; the porous wood-bedrock and fibrous air drink high frequencies instantly. Consequently, the Lignarii have developed a culture of profound quiet. They communicate over distances using a complex, rhythmic vocabulary of knuckle-taps on the hollow reed-balustrades, or via low, chest-resonant humming that travels through the dense grain of the wood far better than spoken words. When they do speak, their language is clipped, dry, and sibilant—consonant-heavy words spoken without opening the lips wide, conserving the tiny film of moisture that coats their throats.

Their lives are bound to the breathing cycle of the Great Chasm. When the overhead phosphor lattices dim for the "Tide-Slack," and the colossal stipes begin their deep, subterranean groaning, the city alters its posture. 

Foundry shifts do not end with horns or sirens. Instead, the masters release the tension brakes on the cable rails, allowing the long trains of amber and mineral powder to glide home on the natural tilt of the flexing wood. Families gather in the suspended steel pods that hang beneath the stipes, listening to the vast, wooden heartbeat beneath their floorboards. In the cool, talc-dusted dimness, while the chalk-white grazers settle into the fibrous turf below and the gliders hang like folded paper from the eaves, the people rest in the absolute, absorbent silence of an immortal, dry forest.
