In a world where there is no rot and no slack, whatever lives must possess the tolerance of a ground bearing. Nothing here is soft in the manner of grubs or moss; nothing leaves a trail of sloughed skin or shed scales that could foul the laminar troughs. The life that occupies this pitch-black, pressurized engine is not an infestation upon the lattice—it is the dynamic tooling of the basin itself, living components cast in dense cartilage, burnished horn, and oil-bathed tendon.

### The Sheet-Gliders (The Planar Shunts)

Where the warm, distilled water sheets down the knife-edged flutes of the vertical trunks, life takes the form of tension. 

These are organisms as thin as gold leaf and broad as banquet tables, consisting of a continuous, semi-rigid membrane of horn-like keratin. They have no eyes, no appendages, and no recognizable anterior or posterior ends. Instead, their undersides are micro-grooved with microscopic capillary channels that mirror the math of the bark beneath them. 

They do not crawl; they are hydroplaning foils. Clinging to the vertical surfaces by pure surface tension, they ride the falling water in absolute silence, sliding down miles of octagonal wood. Their bodies act as dampening plates. Wherever the sheer volume of descending condensation threatens to bead, bubble, or break into an audible splash, a Glider is drawn to the turbulence by fluid dynamic pressure, settling over the disruption to iron it flat against the fluting. 

They feed without chewing or grazing. As they skate, their porous bellies absorb the microscopic esters and electrical charges carried in the weeping distillation, while their dorsal surfaces—smooth as polished obsidian—repel the vapor, keeping the laminar boundaries mathematically shear.

### The Sleeve-Clamps (The Facet-Walkers)

Around the octagonal girth of the great machine-trunks cling creatures shaped like split collars or heavy, segmented rings. 

Constructed from an interlocking armor of dark, enamel-coated bone, their inner geometry is a precise, eight-sided negative of the columns they inhabit. They possess no legs with joints that could buckle. Instead, their inner faces are lined with broad, hydraulic suction-pads fed by their own closed vascular systems, filled with heavy, lipid-based blood.

These Sleeve-Clamps move by micro-peristalsis—a slow, rhythmic shifting of internal mass that allows them to climb or descend the perfectly vertical shafts without ever breaking their airtight seal. They are the galvanic bridgers. From their outer armor project long, rigid spines of copper-tinted keratin that touch neighboring columns, spanning the dark gaps between vertical beams. 

Through these living bus-bars, the Clamps bleed static electricity from one sector of the canopy to another, balancing the ambient current across leagues of pitch darkness. When one passes near, there is no sound, only the sudden, faint smell of hot zinc and the feeling of the hair rising on one’s arms as a circuit of living tissue calibrates the air.

### The Gasket-Beasts (Piston Symbionts)

Deep within the structural floor, where the iron-hard rhizomes meet the hydraulic root-pistons, the pressures rise to hundreds of atmospheres. Here, in the unyielding joints between massive wooden beams, reside the dense, spherical organisms that serve as living seals.

They are completely blind, spherical masses of compressed, fibrous muscle, possessing the consistency of vulcanized rubber. They have no skeleton; their rigidity is maintained entirely by internal hydrostatic pressure that rivals the plant-pistons themselves. 

These creatures wedge themselves permanently into the junctions and expansion-seams of the continental floor. As the plant-fiber rams slowly stroke back and forth to maintain the tension of the basin, the Gasket-Beasts compress and expand in perfect counterpoint. Their thick, slick skins secrete a perpetual film of heavy, amber-colored wax that lubricates the wooden machinery, preventing the friction of raw lignum on lignum.

They do not sleep, nor do they seek mates; they reproduce by slow, axial cleavage over centuries, splitting only when the gap they occupy widens enough to require a twin bearing. They draw their sustenance directly through their skins from the galvanic saps that leak under pressure from the root-tubes, filtering the heavy metallic salts and converting them into lubricating lipids.

### The Thread-Loomers (The Arterial Fauna)

Along the floor’s low-lying arterial troughs, where the distilled runoff flows back toward the capillary roots in glassy, uninterrupted ribbons, live the longest and most elusive of the basin’s denizens.

These are the Loomers: organisms consisting of a central, iron-dense nodule no larger than a fist, from which trail dozens of hair-thin, tendonous whips up to a quarter of a mile in length. The central node anchors itself to the lip of a trough, perfectly streamlined to present zero drag to the water, while its long filaments drift down the current.

The filaments are alive with piezoelectric sensitivity. They are the basin's central nervous system, though no brain governs them. Every micro-vibration, every subtle alteration in fluid viscosity, temperature, or chemical balance across the entire league-wide terrace is registered along these taut, submerged strings. 

If a mineral block forms or a hydraulic piston begins to lose pressure miles away, the change in flow ripples down the filament. The Loomer responds not with thought, but with chemistry—releasing precise micro-droplets of enzymes from its trailing tips that dissolve obstructions upstream or adjust the surface tension of the trough, keeping the fluid cycle at its absolute, invariant speed.

### The Quality of Consciousness

To speak of "minds" or "behavior" in this expanse is to misunderstand its ecology. Nothing here hunts; nothing flees. There is no hunger, because energy is supplied continuously through the galvanic moisture and ambient heat; there is no fear, because there are no predators, no falls, no collapses, and no decay.

The consciousness of these things is not narrative or reactive. It is the consciousness of a balanced equation. 

Their awareness is purely sensory and purely corrective: an uninterrupted, millivolt-scale dream of alignment, resistance, and flow. They know the world only as an intricate, pitch-black architecture of vector forces, surface tensions, and hydraulic thresholds. 

If an alien object—a foreign body, a human hand—were placed upon the wet, octagonal wood, the inhabitants would not attack it or inspect it with curiosity. The Gliders would smoothly glide over it, using the warmth of their bellies to compress it into the wood; the Sleeve-Clamps would read its electrical resistance and shift their spines to ground it; the Loomers would adjust the fluid chemistry to compensate for its displacement. 

They are the caretakers of an unlit perfection, beings who have surrendered all the messy liberties of life to become the moving parts of an immortal, self-operating world.
