To live here is to abandon the concept of the floor. There is only *the Face*, *the Void*, and *the Drift*.

Every creature that clings to the spires or floats between them is shaped by the peculiar generosity of the air—an atmosphere so dense and buoyant that falling takes a long time, and breathing is almost effortless, like drinking warm broth.

### The Face-Clingers

The sandstone belongs to the **Gutturals**—the bladder-throated quadrupeds whose calls vibrate the wet stone. Up close, they are longer and flatter than they sound from across the abyss: five-meter slabs of leathery, slate-grey muscle covered in a dense pelt of water-repellent bristles. Their six broad limbs terminate in wide, muscular disks rimmed with fine, hooked claws that bite into the sandstone’s iron-rich grain. 

They do not walk so much as flow over the vertical surfaces, moving with a deliberate, hydraulic slowness. Their skin is cold-blooded, drawing all its heat from the geothermal vents that weep steam from the vertical fissures. A Guttural lives its three-century life entirely at a ninety-degree angle, grazing on the blue-veined lichens and scraping the calcium-rich secretions of the containment rings. 

Their vast, translucent throat-sacks, patterned like veined marble, do more than sing. When inflated, they act as balance-ballasts, shifting their center of mass away from the stone to let them pivot effortlessly over overhangs. When two Gutturals meet on a narrow fluting, they do not fight; they press their warm throats together, harmonizing at frequencies so low that the moss around them shivers and sheds its moisture in fine, buzzing halos.

Deeper in the vertical root-tangles dwell the **Needle-Weavers**. Neither spider nor monkey, these are long-limbed, dry-skinned primates whose forelimbs have merged into singular, chitin-tipped probes. They live in the hanging hollows of the ironwoods, harvesting the silk of giant bag-moths to spin complex, tension-balanced suspension bridges between root-clusters. They never touch the rock directly; they navigate the vertical canopies on their taut guy-lines, hunting the pale, blind crustaceans that crawl out of the bromeliad cisterns when the sun sinks low. Their eyes are vast, glassy spheres of pure amber, tuned to pick out the faint blue bioluminescence of the lichen in the deep crevices where sunlight never penetrates.

### The Drift-Fauna

The open chasms—the vast, warm spaces between the spires—are not empty air; they are an aerial ocean. 

The **Marrow-Rays** rule the thermals. Their bodies are wide, ribbed sheets of vascular, parchment-thin skin, spanning forty feet from wingtip to wingtip, supported not by bone, but by pressurized lymphatic tubes that stiffen and flex with hydraulic pulses. They do not flap; they bank against the rising heat of the deep steam-floor, tilting gracefully to scoop up the horizontal curtains of pea-sized water droplets in wide, baleen-lined under-jaws. 

A Marrow-Ray is an entire floating biome. On its flat, sun-baked dorsal surface grows a miniature meadow of golden moss and miniature pitcher plants, nourished by the ray’s sweat and the constant mist. Small, iridescent insects—**Gilders**—live exclusively on the backs of these flying islands, never knowing stone or bark, feeding on the moss and cleaning the ray’s spiracles in return.

Lower down, where the containment field begins to warp and the air grows thicker still, float the **Censer-Jellies**. These are semi-buoyant colonial siphonophores that drift in loose constellations through the steam. They maintain their altitude through chambers filled with heated methane, generated by the slow fermentation of the organic soup trapped in their hanging stomachs. Their tentacles—pale, beaded filaments thirty yards long—drift lazily through the void, gathering the pollen drifts and fallen ironwood leaves that tumble endlessly from the upper heights. 

### The Ring-Keepers

Around the great alloy toruses—the silent, ancient machinery that holds the sky in place—lives the closest thing the Upper Sump has to people.

They call themselves the **Tethered**, or the *Sump-Weavers*. Whether they are the degenerate offspring of the engineers who forged the compression fields, or castaways who climbed up from the forgotten lowlands generations ago, no one remembers. 

Their bodies have adapted entirely to the three-dimensional verticality of the peaks. They are tall, excessively slender, and light-boned, with wide, barrel-shaped ribcages built to inhale the thick, perfumed air. Their feet are hand-like, with opposable, prehensile outer toes and thick, leathery calluses on their insteps. Their skin is pale olive, permanently stained with the verdigris and iron-rouge of the lichen pastes they use to protect themselves from the intense high-altitude ultraviolet light.

The Tethered do not build houses; they weave **Cradles**—elliptical, basket-like pods woven from ironwood roots and ray-sinew, lashed directly to the ancient hulls that float motionless in the void, or anchored under the colossal overhangs of the containment rings. 

Their entire culture is governed by the laws of tension and ballast:
*   **The Cord:** No Tethered is ever unclipped. From childhood, they wear harness-corsets of braided silk, linked by smooth bronze carabin-rings to static lines that run along the spire-faces. To be untethered is the ultimate exile—not because of the fall, which is so slow in the thick air that one might starve before reaching the bottom, but because of the Drift, which carries the unmoored out into the dead air between spires where no thermal can bring them back.
*   **The Silent Trade:** They harvest the dormant buoyancy nodes from damaged observation craft, using the cold, gravity-nullifying metal to craft floating gardens—rafts of soil and rotting mulch that hover beside their cradles, where they grow sweet root-tubers and broadleaf tea.
*   **Acoustic Memory:** Because sound travels with terrifying clarity through the hyper-dense air, the Tethered do not write. They speak in a dense, clicked register, and communicate across the mile-wide chasms using hollow wooden resonators tuned to match the singing frequencies of the stone itself. A message tapped on the north face of a massif can be felt through the soles of a hunter’s feet five miles away.

They revere the machinery above them, not as gods, but as the quiet bedrock of life. Once every generation, a crew of climbers will make the pilgrimage to the highest tips of the spires, where the air finally thins and the violet blackness of space presses down against the compression lens. There, hanging upside down over the endless golden steam of the world below, they scrape the creeping lichen from the smooth, gunmetal toruses, ensuring the seamless metal remains clean, cool, and running forever.
