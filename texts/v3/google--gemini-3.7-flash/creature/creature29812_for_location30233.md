In the upper rafters of the district, where the timber girders are thickest and wet with fresh cambium, the **Caliper-Beasts** move with the deliberate, shivering precision of surveyor’s tools. They are eyeless, their bodies little more than a pair of four-jointed legs of dense, porcelain-hard cuticle joined by a central, pulsating yolk of soft, translucent flesh. They straddle the girders, extending their tips to measure the span, tapping the heartwood with sharp, rhythmic clicks. The sound is an assessment: they listen through their limbs to the hydraulic pitch of the xylem beneath the bark. Where a beam is under-pressurized or sagging under the cold load of the void, three or four of them will gather, locking their limbs together in a rigid, self-tightening triangle that splints the wood until the architecture itself thickens to compensate.

Beneath them, inhabiting the drafty clearance between hanging tenements, are the **Gasket-Ticks**. These are flat, disc-shaped organisms the color of boiled lard, roughly the circumference of a barrel lid. They possess no true limbs, only a circumferential skirt of muscular cilia that allows them to glide along the slick faces of the wood-iron viaducts. They are drawn to leakage. When a mortise-and-tenon joint weeps too much un-oxidized sap, or a vine-piston vents a hiss of celery-scented vapor, the Gasket-Ticks slide over the seam and clamp down with enormous hydrostatic suction. Their bodies swell as they drink the excess fluids, their pale backs stretching until they are drum-tight and blue-veined, effectively sealing the mechanical rupture with their own living bulk for days at a time.

Inside the half-formed domestic facades—behind the bay windows glazed not with glass, but with taut sheets of clear, gelatinous cellulose—the interiors are not empty, but occupied by **The Squatters of the Pith**. 

These are pale, vaguely hominid figures, though they lack the internal scaffolding of bone. Their flesh is composed of the same fine, white, compressible fiber as the core of an elderberry stem. They do not walk so much as they are displaced; they grow directly from the floorboards of the embryonic parlors, their feet trailing off into rootlets that knit directly into the building’s vascular grid. They sit on the unyielding, freshly secreted wooden furniture, perfectly motionless, their faces smooth and unfeatured save for deep, vertical slits that open and close in sync with the distant vine-pistons. 

They are the city’s sensory nodes. If an outsider were to strike a wall three blocks away, a ripple would pass through a dozen of these fiber-men: their slits would flutter, a brief sigh of vapor escaping their chests, registering the stress vector and passing it into the floorboards like an organic telegraph. When an apartment is mature and its walls have fully lignified, these figures wither, turning to dry, fragrant shavings that the wind from the lower void sweeps out through the floor-drains.

In the zones where gravity turns upon an edge, life has adapted to the sheer geometry:

*   **The Ledger-Crabs**: In the rooms where gravity shifts sideways toward the pod-walls, small, flat creatures with dozens of needle-like appendages scuttle along the vertical faces. They do not feed on organic matter in the usual sense; they feed on the heat generated by the ticking seed-pods. Their life cycle is entirely computational. As they scuttle across the pods, their sharp feet trigger or damp the vibrations of the seeds, altering the rhythm of the mechanical tally. To watch them is to watch a living calculation: a swarm of pale, frantic hands constantly re-indexing the flow of sap through the precinct's lower plumbing.
*   **The Plumb-Gnats**: Microscopic, bioluminescent clouds that hover in perfect, razor-straight vertical columns across the black gulfs. They are utterly sensitive to the local gravity field. When a parcel of space shifts its orientation, the cloud snaps instantly to the new angle, a flash of cold green light outlining the invisible seam where "down" has rotated ninety degrees. 

Deeper down, in the massive, hollow spaces where the condensation falls in eternal, rhythmic droplets, drift the **Draft-Vanes**. These are solitary, silent things with wingspans of thirty or forty feet, constructed of an ultra-light, parchment-like membrane supported by ribbed struts of hollow cane. They have no beating wings; they are purely passive gliders that ride the rising plumes of cool, moist air produced by the piston-exhausts. 

From their underbellies trail miles of gossamer-fine, sticky thread. As a Draft-Vane circles slowly in the dark clearance, its trailing threads brush against the turning bark flywheels and the high-tension catwalks. Over hours, these threads wind around axles, creating delicate, tensile linkages across gulfs of empty air where no timber has yet grown. They are the riggers of the void, tying distant towers together with web-lines that will eventually serve as the scaffolding for the next generation of horizontal viaducts.

And beneath everything, clinging to the lowest taproots where the city roots itself into the unseen bedrock of the chamber, are the **Cambium-Eels**. Thick as a man’s torso and blind, they slither through the internal xylem channels of the master pylons. They move against the pressurized rush of sap, their rasping, circular mouths scraping away calcifications and fungal blooms before they can clog the city’s lifeblood. Their skin is so cold that condensation freezes into a fine, white frost upon them whenever they briefly breach the wood surface to cross an open joint, leaving a trail of rime that evaporates almost instantly in the dark, cellar-wet air.

Nothing here sleeps, because the city is not yet finished. Every creature is an organ, an enzyme, or a counterweight, bound to an embryonic architecture that is rushing, cell by dividing cell, to fulfill an architectural plan that has no architect.
