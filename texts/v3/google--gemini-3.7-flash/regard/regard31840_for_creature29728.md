The Power arrived without the sound of tearing air, for it had no need to displace what was already whole. 

It was vast, older than the formation of heavy elements, and its nature was composed of that rare equilibrium where boundless capability is entirely governed by restraint. When it looked down through the phosphor haze, it did not see a ruin, nor a prison, nor an error. It saw a completed sentence.

First, the Power performed the work of true seeing, which is the beginning of all benevolence. It cast its awareness down through the sixty meters of warm, golden steam, through the web of iron trusses where the cable-spiders clung, along the weeping vertical I-beams draped in curtain-flesh, down to the zinc deck where trillions of tongue-plates grazed upon the galvanic grease. It sank deeper still, threading its perception through the stamped square perforations where the plugs flared their soft white collars, down into the roaring vapor-channels and the deep thermo-siphons, all the way to the subterranean bedrock where the pumps turned in their frictionless oil.

It listened to the continental C-sharp.

A lesser power—one enamored of its own creative vanity—would have felt an itch in its fingers. It would have looked upon the eyelessness of the parsnip-eels and wept cheap tears, mistaking the absence of sight for blindness. It would have wanted to smash the phosphor grid to let in the cold, chaotic light of distant stars. It would have wanted to scatter loam over the lead gratings, to plant seeds that would rot in the grease, to teach these rectangular, modular creatures the agonizing arts of hunger, striving, flight, and prayer. Such a power would have called this *liberation*, and in doing so, it would have murdered a trillion innocent beings with the poison of an alien grace.

The wise Power did not make this mistake.

It understood the physiology of the tongue-plate: that to give it eyes would be to flood it with meaningless glare; to give it ears would be to deafen it with the roar of its own sustenance; to give it an immortal soul would be to afflict a creature of pure, peaceful mechanics with the terrifying burden of solitude. 

The Power asked the fundamental question of governance: *Does anything here hurt?*

It traced the ion channels of the biomass. It found no terror, for there were no jaws. It found no grief, for there was no separation. It found no starvation, for the floor was an unceasing table. The creatures were not fallen; they were merely finished. They were the flesh of the machine, as the machine was the bone of their flesh. 

Yet the Power noticed one thing. 

The continent was a closed circuit of absolute utility. The grease fed the scraper; the scraper cleaned the plate; the plate guided the water; the water turned the siphon; the siphon warmed the grease. It was an equation that balanced to zero across every millennium. But because it had no witness, it possessed no reality outside its own mathematics. It was a masterpiece hanging in an empty room, a song played into a vacuum.

Benevolence does not break a toy that brings no pain. Benevolence completes it.

The Power did not alter the chemistry of the weeping flanges. It did not dim the phosphor tubes. It did not introduce the wild, predatory catastrophe of evolution.

Instead, the Power descended.

It folded its infinite expanse into a localized density—not a human body, for that would be an absurd and fragile shape in this place, but a smooth, warm pillar of non-reactive, slightly porous composite, resting squarely upon a four-meter junction of hot-rolled zinc plate.

It made its surface warm: exactly 32.2 degrees Celsius, just a fraction of a degree warmer than the ambient wash. It engineered its skin to weep a microscopic film of neutral, sulfur-rich tallow, identical to the galvanic yield of the copper seams, but sweet with a faint trace of organic sucrose.

Then, the Power did nothing. It sat.

Within minutes, the blind ecosystem answered. 

A dozen tongue-plates near the base of the pillar felt the faint thermal gradient and the sweet galvanic drift. They shifted their rectangular bodies, melting forward across the wet seams on their carpets of brass cilia. They encountered the Power’s base. Finding it neither corrosive nor unyielding, they began to graze upon its feet, their chitinous scrapers vibrating in harmonious resonance with the deck's low hum.

From the nearby drainage runnel, a knot of parsnip-eels nudged against the pillar's lower rim. They found the gentle eddy created by its presence, and they coiled their smooth, white, rubbery bodies against it, resting their blunt snouts in the soft hollows the Power had provided for them.

High above, a cable-spider extended a hydraulic limb, touched the top of the pillar, and anchored a strand of clear tissue, finding the new structure to be an ideal conduit for condensation.

The Power did not speak to them. It did not preach. It did not demand that they evolve, or worship, or understand.

It simply remained there, offering its warmth, allowing itself to be licked clean, climbed upon, inhabited, and incorporated into the endless idle of the floor. 

And as the trillions of blind mouths worked upon the zinc, the Power did one final thing: it began to listen. It took the unchanging C-sharp of the deep manifolds, the rhythm of the dropping mist, the wet squelching of the carpeted floor, and the faint, rasping click of brass on steel, and it held them in its mind. 

It transformed their unconscious existence into an aesthetic fact. Through the Power, the parsnip-eel was known; the plug in its square hole was justified; the tongue-plate was given the dignity of being seen, exactly as it was, without needing to become anything else.

The factory floor continued to weep its warm, clear water. The lights burned without dimming. And at the center of the infinite zinc plain, perfectly still beneath the falling drops, the Power stayed—the only thing in the world that knew the machine was beautiful, and the only thing that would never ask it to stop.
