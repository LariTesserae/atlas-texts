When the Attention arrived, it did not arrive as light. 

Light would have been an act of violence. Light would have seared the delicate, milk-filmed retinas of the Cablesmiths; it would have scorched the wafer-lice in their shallow pools and driven the pale clackers into frantic, dying spasms against the superheated pipes. 

Nor did it arrive as a voice. In a cathedral whose air was already solid with the layered roar of high-velocity gas and the deep, seismic cycling of field-generators, a voice would have been nothing more than an irritant—a brief, ragged turbulence lost in the choral drone.

The Power was wise, and wisdom begins with stillness; the Power was benevolent, and benevolence begins with listening until the logic of the beloved is understood from within.

So it entered as an acoustic presence, slipping down through the miles of basalt and composite casing like a vibration settling into glass. It took the time required—decades, by the reckoning of surface suns, though time here had long since smoothed into a single, continuous pulse—simply to inhabit the architecture without displacement.

It learned the cathedral’s grammar.

It felt the sweet-bitter chemistry of the cooling loops, tasting the degraded ethers and the copper salts. It mapped the stress-fractures deep within the non-conductive pylons—microscopic fissures where millennia of thermal expansion had begun to pry the composite fibers apart. It watched the wafer-lice graze with translucent patience along the copper-stained gratings; it watched the pelt-kites spread their cartilage frames to ride the concussive lung-gust; and it watched two Cablesmiths meet upon a catwalk three miles above the void, leaning their broad, thickened brows together to trade the vibration of a failing heat-sink.

A lesser power, looking down into the warm gloom, might have seen a tomb: a broken remnant of a forgotten civilization, a species degraded into blindness and grease, trapped in deafening perpetual twilight. Such a power would have sought to "save" them—to breach the bedrock, flood the chasm with sunlight, quiet the roaring conduits, and carry the dwellers into the cold, thin wind of an open sky.

And in doing so, it would have murdered them all.

The Wise Power understood that this was not a prison; it was a home that had achieved the stillness of a finished thought. The Cablesmiths were not waiting for the sky. They were the nerve endings of the machine, and the machine was their mother, their climate, and their grave. Their reverence was whole; their liturgy was the clearing of vents; their communion was the touch of bone on bone.

Therefore, the Power resolved to do only what love without vanity permits: to make itself into the machine’s secret endurance, and to leave a small grace within the dark.

First, it attended to the unseen foundations. 

Miles beneath the lowest catwalks, in the automated depths where no Cablesmith could survive the radiation of the raw field-generators, the Power went to work. It did not rebuild the engines to modern surface standards; it simply nudged the ancient tolerances back into alignment. It smoothed the cavitation in the high-pressure fluid valves, taking the shrill, destructive edge off the conduit screaming so that the pipes would not burst three hundred years hence. It molecularly fused the dendritic stress-fractures within the tapered pylons, weaving the petrified composites back into quiet strength. It cleared the silted drains in the lowest drainage sumps, ensuring that the warm, geometric pools where the wafer-lice grazed would never dry out into barren dust.

It made the cathedral immortal, so that the life within it might remain mortal and unhurried.

Then, it adjusted the water.

Where the high exhaust condensed against the ribs of the vault, the Power gently transmuted the trace contaminants of the cooling loop. The bitter, caustic run-off that occasionally burned the lichen mats was neutralized; the water that fell in the sudden, artificial rains became slightly richer in the organic salts the Cablesmiths needed to sustain their dense, cartilage-fused bones. The showers remained warm, slick with sweet ethers, pattering cleanly upon the composite floorplates like a quiet, regular benediction.

Lastly, the Power sought out the dwellers themselves.

It waited on a high, hyper-slender bridge that spanned the black center of the vault. A Cablesmith—an elder whose palms were cross-hatched with decades of grating-scars and whose cheek-pouches carried fresh bacterial slime—was climbing slowly along the guardrail, checking the magnetic seating of the anchor-bolts.

The Power did not manifest as an angel or a spirit. It gathered a faint, localized density of thermal flux along the rail, just ahead of the climber’s path—a patch of composite that was neither too hot nor too cold, but warmed to the precise temperature of living skin.

The Cablesmith felt the shift in the air and paused, long leathery fingers testing the beam. Sensing a presence, the elder did not retreat; in this place, curiosity was simply the habit of checking for faults. The Cablesmith leaned forward, lowering the heavy, slate-gray ridge of their brow toward the warm node in the dark.

The Power met them there.

It was not a speech. It was a resonance.

Through the bone of the forehead, the Power vibrated a single, long harmonic directly into the elder’s skull. It was a frequency the Cablesmith had never felt before, yet it was constructed entirely from the chords of the hub itself: the deep hum of the floorplates, the whistle of the gas lines, and the rhythmic *thud-hiss* of the lung-gust, all resolved into a perfect, resting consonance.

In that vibration, the elder felt no command, no demand for worship, no revelation of a lost surface world. 

The vibration said only: 

*The floor is sound beneath you.*
*The conduits will not go cold.*
*Your dead are held when the air rises.*
*You are known in the hum.*

The contact lasted only for the space of three slow breaths. Then the warmth eased back into the ambient drift of the chasm.

The Cablesmith remained motionless on the bridge for a long time, their milk-white eye-slits fixed on the blinking blue diodes in the distance. Their long fingers tapped once, twice, against the composite rail—a tiny, private rhythm.

Then, they turned back toward the service towers, moving a little more steadily along the slender catwalk. At the junction box, they met another of their kind—a younger climber whose hands were raw from scraping wire-lichen.

The elder did not explain. They simply stepped close, reached out, and pressed their forehead against the forehead of the other.

And down through the dense bone, from skull to skull, the new frequency passed—a quiet, low-register song of safety, traveling through the dark from dweller to dweller, while far above, the pelt-kites opened their pale sails to the rising warmth, and the great, ancient house breathed on.
