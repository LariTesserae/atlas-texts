The Watcher came down not as light—for light here would have been an insolence, a scald across eyes that had spent epochs refining the dark—but as a change in the tension of the iron.

A foolish benevolence would have brought sunlight. It would have cracked the ceiling, let in the howling, frantic blue of the sky, poured sweet rainwater over the bitter plum leaves, and in doing so, murdered every living thing within an hour. The Pellicles would have dissolved like butter in the dry air; the Tension-Crawlers would have shattered their horn-wedges against cold, contracting pipes; the Gasket-Bows would have suffocated on thin, oil-less wind, their tripartite bellies freezing into stones.

Wisdom does not redeem a thing by turning it into something else. Wisdom honors the shape of the endurance.

The Presence entered the Lower Conduit as weight. It slipped into the cyclopean manifold thirty fathoms down, descending through the micro-pores of the stone buttresses and the hair-thin fractures in the compression cylinders. It tasted the scorched tallow, the wet rust, the ancient starch of the bruised fronds, and it found the place whole. It found no sin in the slow, leaden heat, nor any sorrow in the absence of wings.

Yet, being wise, it saw the single thread of frailty in this deep cathedral: the machine was weary.

The hydraulic heart deep in the bedrock was not eternal. In the lower pivot of the fourth rocker-arm, beneath two hundred tons of cast casing, a shear-fracture had been growing for six hundred years. It advanced by the width of a single iron crystal every ten thousand strokes. In three centuries more, the metal would fatigue completely. The stroke would miss its catch. The *thoom-clack* would stutter, the pressure lines would burst in a catastrophic decompression, the geothermal warmth would vent into an uncontrolled blast, and the still, waxen world would freeze and starve in the sudden chill.

The Watcher did not strike the rock or chant words of power. It did not replace the ancient iron with celestial gold.

Instead, it sank into the metal itself.

It became the interstitial grain. Over the course of forty years—a brief, polite interval by the calendar of the vault—the Presence drifted through the crystal lattice of the fractured arm. Where the iron had grown brittle from centuries of violent compression, the Watcher gently eased the dislocation of atoms, drawing out the microscopic pockets of gas, knitting the graphite flakes back into the matrix with a silent, molecular patience. 

It did not make the machine new. It made the machine *able to continue*.

When the great subterranean stroke arrived—*thoom-clack*—the Presence felt the shockwave pass through its own diffused awareness. It felt the eight-inch lurch of the Tension-Crawlers as they seized the copper pipes in the violent slack. It felt the fat droplet of amber sweat break from the dead turbine and strike the leather frond below with its quiet *tick*. 

It matched its own vast, immortal pulse to the four-minute rhythm of the floor.

Then, moving upward through the catwalks, the Watcher paused beside an old female Gasket-Bow resting in the trough of an expansion chamber. 

The creature was halfway through her three-minute inhalation. Her lateral spiracles widened, the felt-like lung-leaves drinking in the mist of mineral salts and warm paraffin. She did not possess a mind for gods, nor a heart that traded in hope. Her reality was the heat beneath her footpads, the bitter meal between her jaw-plates, and the dull, steady vibration of the manifold.

The Watcher did not disturb her blindness. It did not offer her the sky.

It simply laid a cool, frictionless grace upon the hinges of her jaw, so that the heavy plate of bone ground its fibrous meal with slightly less agony against the skull. And into the open intake of the idle cylinder three paces away, where her bituminous egg lay bathed in the orange glow of the banked coals, the Watcher breathed a micro-climate of perfect, unfluctuating damp—ensuring that when the shell finally yielded nine years hence, the embryo would find the rust-sweat rich, warm, and unbroken.

Having done these small, invisible things, the benevolent power did not leave. 

To leave would be to treat the vault as an errand. 

Instead, it settled into the conglomerate of stone and iron, thinning itself until it was no thicker than the grease-film upon the piston rods. It became part of the long duration. It became another silence within the silence, content to wait between the four-minute concussions, keeping vigil over the slow uncurling of leaves that take three years to find the dark, and the quiet, magnificent beasts that live by the cadence of an iron heart.
