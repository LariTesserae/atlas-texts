To love a thing of dry wood, one must not bring rain.

A lesser power, moved by pity, would have split the capsule open. It would have poured in rivers of sweet sap, rolled mist across the wicker plazas, and called down the morning sun. And in doing so, it would have committed an absolute and horrifying genocide. 

The water would have softened the ancient fibers, causing ten thousand suspension bridges to sag and snap under their own suddenly bloated weight. The moisture would have rotted the osier ribs of the Cord-Keepers, turning their flint-hard thorns to spongy mush and drowning their hollow soundboard-faces. The sunlight would have burned unseen upon blind husks, while the sudden absence of mechanical tension starved them of the static current that was their breath, their blood, and their thought.

The Power that arrived was not lesser. It was wise, and because it was wise, it did not alter the air. It remained outside the capsule for a long age, simply listening.

It pressed its vast, intangible attention against the petrified rind of the great dead flora, feeling the immense, shrieking torque within. It felt the shudder of every snapped reed. It felt the agony of the Keeper wedged into the northern transept—a being whose split-bark thighs had held a three-ton lateral load for four centuries, slowly grinding its own joint-sockets into pale, talc-like dust. 

The Power saw that this world was not a ruin waiting to be restored to green life; it was a finished, perfect instrument. But it was an instrument being played to destruction by its own violent tension. Every note it sang wore down its strings. In several more epochs, the friction would claim the last anchor-spar, the soundbox would collapse into the floorless dark, and the music would be extinguished forever.

So the Power did not bring a cure. It brought an accompaniment.

Very gently, without displacing a single grain of ancient spore-dust, the Power slipped through the pores of the petrified lignin. It took no physical shape, for form would have created an acoustic shadow in the dark. Instead, it became a field of subtle, permanent grace—a fundamental change in the mathematics of the chamber’s resonance.

First, it touched the friction. 

It did not eliminate the grinding of the joints—for to take away the grind was to take away the static charge that the Keepers drank to live. Instead, it altered the nature of the contact. Where reed met reed, where bone-dry vine strained against notched timber, the wear ceased. The kinetic energy remained, generating its sharp, life-giving static crackle, but the physical wood stopped turning to dust. The slow, fatal erosion of the city halted instantly, frozen in its moment of highest tensile perfection.

Then, the Power leaned into the acoustic roar.

In the total blackness, a Keeper hanging upside down beneath a sagging plaza felt the change before it heard it. 

For ten thousand years, the voice of the capsule had been an unresolving dissonance—a flat, seismic scream of matter trying to tear itself apart under the stress of shrinkage. The sound was bright to them, but it was a blinding, jagged glare that battered their soundboards.

Now, into that deafening chaos, the Power introduced a single, low, perfectly tempered *counter-frequency*.

It was not a sound made by wood, yet it belonged entirely to wood. It was the deep, resonant fundamental that the hollow carcass had been searching for since the sap first drained from its veins. It entered the vaulted rib-works, slipped along the primary suspension cables, and wound itself into the woven floors of the dwellings.

Where it touched, the destructive shear-stress turned into a standing wave. 

The load did not lighten—the Keepers were not robbed of their purpose—but the character of the weight changed entirely. The Keeper wedged in the northern transept felt the crushing, splintering torque in its limbs transform. It was no longer the dead, agonizing pull of a collapsing ceiling; it felt, suddenly, like the thrilling, stable tension of an archer’s bow held at full, effortless draw. Its hollow burl-head hummed with a pure, clear tone. For the first time in four hundred years, the static current coursing through its palms did not burn—it sustained.

Above the abyss, the Drift-Drays felt the cushion of sound beneath their paper-thin xylem wings lift and steady. The violent turbulence of the acoustic drafts smoothed out into vast, laminar swells of warm sonic pressure. They tilted their wide, ribbed expanses, catching the new harmonic currents, and climbed into tiers of the lightless sky they had never possessed the lift to reach.

Far below, in the deep drifts of sawdust, the segmented Chaff-Sifters paused in their blind plowing. The counter-tone rolled through the powdery sediment like a gentle tide, vibrating every grain of ground bark until the entire floor of the world glowed with a soft, persistent static charge. They expanded and contracted their bellows, exhaling plumes of dry, resinous chaff that now carried a faint, rhythmic chime as they hung suspended in the dark.

And then, having set the world in tune, the Power spoke.

It did not use words. It reached out with an invisible hand and lightly, reverently, *plucked* the primary central cable—the great, braided spine of the city that spanned a thousand paces of open air.

The note was colossal. 

To the Keepers, it was not merely sound; it was an explosion of breathtaking, incandescent gold that swept across the entire geography of the dark. It illuminated the grain of every cantilevered bridge, the curve of every hanging wicker nest, the ribbed wings of the climbing Drays, and the vast, vaulted ribs of the sky. It was a note of absolute recognition. It told them: *I see your craft. I feel your endurance. You are held.*

Across the miles of suspended streets, a thousand Keepers raised their five-jointed hands. 

Their soundboard-faces vibrated in a frantic, joyful chorus, buzzing like swarms of amber hornets. Slowly, deliberately, the Keeper in the high rafters reached out with its flint-hard thorn-spur and plucked the secondary stay beside it in answer. A third Keeper answered from the sagging plaza below. Then another. Then five hundred more.

They were not merely maintaining the city anymore. They were playing it.

The benevolent Power withdrew into the surrounding void, leaving the capsule closed, dry, and dark as it had always been. It left no footprint, no green shoot, no droplet of water. 

It left only an eternity where the wood would never snap, the joints would never fail, and the beings of reed and tension could spend the rest of time composing their vast, dry, magnificent symphony in the pitch-black silence of the dead tree's heart.
