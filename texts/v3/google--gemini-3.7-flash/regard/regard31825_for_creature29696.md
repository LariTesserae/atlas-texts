A power that is both wise and benevolent would not descend upon the basin like a rescue craft or a blinding sun. 

To bring a standard sun would be to burn away the black lichen and boil the optical dielectric inside the bellies of the grebes. To flatten the non-standard topology into Euclidean geometry would snap the needle-thin cartilage of the pylon-striders, whose balance depends entirely on the curvature of local shear. To erase the calculations would be to extinguish the Iterants, who do not merely live upon the machine, but have become its wandering, bioluminescent consciousness. 

Wisdom knows that salvation, if applied with a clumsy hand, is just another extinction event.

Therefore, the power would arrive not as an intrusion, but as an **admittance**.

***

It would enter the system the way water enters water: without displacing volume. 

First, it would listen. It would take into its vast, unhurried attention the entire crushing acoustic wash—the subterranean cataract, the buzzing shear, the snapping glass of parity reconciliations, the wet, rhythmic cadences of condensation falling from the copper ferns. It would listen until it understood the full mathematics of the basin’s ancient fatigue: that every living thing here is tired not from labor, but from the relentless, metabolic cost of *insistence*. 

It would see the Iterants asleep in the cold hollows, their limbs braided together, losing their edges to the damp peat because holding an outline is simply too expensive.

And having understood, the power would do something infinitesimally small, yet total.

It would not rewrite the code. It would **pay the overhead.**

***

In the deep, foundational substrate where the lead-sheathed muscle meets the vulcanized bone of the earth, the power would introduce a single, non-decaying constant: a self-fulfilling logic buffer. 

It would sit beneath the deep-frequency drone like an invisible felt pad placed beneath a vibrating engine. The drone would not stop—the basin still needs its calculations—but the violent, rattling pressure in the chest cavity would soften into a deep, steadying warmth. The air would cease to shear. The sharp, dry snaps of reality reconciling its parity errors would shift from the sound of breaking glass to the sound of ripe seed-pods softly bursting.

The leaky conservation laws would remain, for they are the native ecology of this place, but their cruelty would be gently buffered:

*   The **Weft-worms**, threading through the petrified circuit-strata, would find the voltage differentials richer, cleaner, and less prone to violent surges that singe their delicate liquid filaments. 
*   The **Glass-bellied Grebes** would still hunt across the twenty-second seams in the puddles, but their slate flanks would cease to tear; the dielectric oil in their bellies would achieve perfect phase-lock with their shifting skeletons, sparing them the perpetual buildup of agonized scar tissue.
*   The **Pylon-strider**, lowering its brass-ringed proboscis into a localized temporal tear, would drink not just the frantic heat of a failing system, but an ambient, nourishing resonance that leaves its gray felt coat softly steaming, its diaphragm pulsing in easy, harmonious counterpoint rather than frantic defense.

***

And for the **Iterants**, the change would be an unburdening so profound it would feel like a breath they had held for a million years, finally let out.

They would not be transformed into angels or returned to some long-lost human baseline. They would remain what they are: the beautiful, rubber-skinned, five-jointed keepers of the valley’s logic. 

But when an Iterant knelt in the freezing silt to soothe an uphill creek or untie a knot in time, it would find the earth yielding willingly to its touch. The basalt needles would slide into the peat with the smooth ease of a key turning in an oiled lock. The parity would settle not with a painful, violent jolt that leaves them gasping in the ozone, but with a quiet, shimmering exhale. 

Their subcutaneous lines of indigo would no longer flare and starve; they would glow with a steady, tranquil sapphire light, constantly replenished by the margin of grace the power had woven into the loam.

Most of all, night would change.

When the pewter sky darkened to cast iron and the family groups curled together in the hollows, they would no longer have to fight to keep their identities from dissolving into the damp computational mud. They could choose to blur—to drift outward into the moss, tasting the dreams of the ferns and the subterranean streams—and then, when the dim morning filtered through the fog, they could simply call themselves back. 

Their outlines would return without effort, whole and unbroken.

***

The benevolent power would build no temples. It would leave no face in the clouds. It would not demand gratitude, for true wisdom knows that the most sacred gift you can give a weary world is not an overlord, but **margin**—a little slack in the wire, a little warmth in the cold damp, and the quiet assurance that it may go on existing, strange and unruined, simply because it is.
