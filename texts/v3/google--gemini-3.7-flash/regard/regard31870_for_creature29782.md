The power that arrived did not arrive with light, for light here was only an amber sickness that lived in the gel, and an intrusion of clean brilliance would have blinded the creature’s fat-shielded eyes and boiled the delicate, dough-skinned isopods in their tracks.

Nor did it arrive with rescue. 

Wisdom is, before all things, the refusal to commit the cruelty of unwanted salvation. A foolish benevolence would have reached down with great golden tongs, plucked the knuckle from the stratosphere, and set it down in an inland meadow of clover and clean rain. And in doing so, it would have murdered the thing instantly: the creature’s lungs, built to sift boiling mint and wood-ash through combs of stiff copper, would have collapsed in the thin, dry chill of a terrestrial breeze; its flat, un-nailed pads would have found no wet clay to seal; its heart, calibrated to the screaming centrifugal fling of the apex, would have torn itself from its moorings in the stillness of a flat earth.

The visitor understood the sanctity of the task. It understood that this being was not a prisoner waiting for an open door, but an organ—a beating ventricle hung between nothing and everything, keeping the world’s upper margin wet and soft enough that the sky did not shatter like cold bottle-glass.

So the power did three things, and each was done in the grammar of hide, torque, and silt.

***

First, it addressed the exterior roar.

It did not silence the shear-winds—the creature lived by the clock of their violence, measuring the health of the wall by the pitch of the screaming bamboo. But outside the woven hull, where the indigo vacuum whipped past at planetary speed, the power laid a skin of absolute, frictionless grace. It did not stop the wind; it simply greased the boundary between the wicker and the void. 

Instantly, the frantic, destructive shear smoothed into a long, low, cello-like hum. The bamboo ceased its dry *thwip-thwip* of impending failure and settled into a deep, working flexion—a sustainable strain that would never snap the willow ribs, no matter how many centuries the knuckle swung through the dark.

Inside, the creature paused. Its breathing slits snapped shut with a wet click. Its blunt, tallow-colored spade of a hand remained pressed against a seam where the mud was thinning. It waited, its thick neck tensed, its buried amber eyes feeling the vibration travel through the rawhide lashings into its breastbone. 

The vibration was steady now. It had become a rhythm a body could lean into without the panic of the final minute. The creature let out a long, whistling plume of steam, relaxed its heifer-like shoulders by half an inch, and went back to work.

***

Second, the power reached into the jar.

The suspended earthenware vessel was the knuckle’s furnace, but the creature had been buying its heat with its own life, pressing its blistering, marrow-hot breast against the rough clay until its skin was scoured raw and its fat was burned down to the meat. 

The power did not fill the jar with sweet nectar or celestial water. Instead, it placed a single, round, dense grain of river-basalt into the dark moisture at the jar’s bottom. 

The stone was not hot, but it was *endless*. It held within its microscopic pores the deep, tectonic memory of the earth’s iron core—a slow, patient simmering that never rose to a scald and never dropped toward frost. 

Slowly, the jar stopped shivering with the violence of near-collapse. The sweat that beaded through its unglazed red flank turned steady, warm, and rich as fresh colostrum. 

When the creature next circled the perimeter on its sliding pads and climbed the ropes to take the vessel into its arms, it did not find the bitter, desperate chill that usually drove it to exhaust its own lungs. It pressed its chest against the clay and felt, for the first time since the first coil of mud was laid, a warmth that pushed *back*. 

It lingered there for a long time. It did not possess the anatomy for weeping, nor the mind for prayer, but it rested its snout-less, smooth brow against the neck of the jar, its breathing valves fluttering in the rich, minty vapor, while the dough-colored isopods crawled between its ribs in peaceful, undisturbed circuits.

***

Third, and finally, the power left a companion for the mud.

It did not make another lunged beast, for the four paces of space could not feed two appetites, and the pale cabbage-vines could only yield so much sugary gel. 

Instead, the power knelt within the wet mortar itself.

On the far side of the sphere, directly opposite where the creature lay wedged between the willow ribs, a new shape formed in the fresh, un-setting slip. It was a handprint. Not a human print, but a wide, blunt cushion with thick thumb-whorls, pressed deeply into the grey river-silt. 

From that moment on, whenever the shear-winds surged and a hair-line fracture threatened to open in the high corner where the outward gravity pulled hardest, the creature would scramble around the curve to knead it shut—and find that the slip there was already warm, already worked, smoothed over by a broad heel that matched its own.

It was not alone at the seam.

The birth was still unfinished. The knuckle still hung furious and wet at the edge of the void. The mud never set; the willow never hardened into dry bone; the cabbage-vines continued their slow, sweet creak against the wicker. 

Everything remained as it had to be: four paces of boiling genesis held together by thumb-pressure and rawhide. 

Only now, the spire had an anchor, the jar had a hearth, and in the perpetual first minute of the world, the beast had someone working the other side of the wall.
