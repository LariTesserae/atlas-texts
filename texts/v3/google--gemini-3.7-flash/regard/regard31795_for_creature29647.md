To know this place is to understand that any hand reaching into it with the coarse desire to “heal” it would instantly murder it.

A clumsy deity, pitying the blind and the ungrounded, would attempt to lay down a floor. They would introduce soil, soothe the deafening roar into garden silence, and steady the violent twenty-minute pulse into a gentle, seasonal tide. 

And in doing so, they would extinguish everything.

The chord-gliders would starve, deprived of the drift-slurry swept up by the foundational draft. The chime-weavers would perish when their tuned filaments lost the structural hum that keeps them taut. The surge-skaters would never wake again, their six-second kingdom of frozen water-pearls ironed out into mundane puddle and rain. The copper-colored fronds would rot in stationary air, deprived of the violent dynamic purge that forces their sap through the carbon-glass conduits.

Wisdom does not look upon a precipice and demand it become a valley. 

When the Power arrived, it did not descend as a descending light—light here was a dying slate-indigo, brief and particulate. It arrived instead as a *listening*.

It entered the hollow through the thirty-mile needle pylons, sliding along the lattice of synthetic stone not as matter, but as a subtle, pervasive tension: an awareness that could feel the strain on every quantum-pinning node, the exact micron-yield of every suspension cable, and the firing of every cartilaginous nerve across leagues of warm dark air.

It stayed motionless for ten thousand cycles of the pulse, doing nothing at all. 

It simply learned the rhythm of the fall.

It felt the terror and the ecstatic clarity of creatures whose entire existence was suspended over an abyss. It learned the beauty of an ecology made entirely of strings, where every living thing was a note struck on an instrument the size of a sea. 

Only when it knew the tension of every thread did it begin to act. 

Its interventions were so delicate that, to the blind denizens of the wire-world, they felt like nothing more than the hollow discovering its own truest nature.

### The First Work: The Foundation’s Promise

The Power saw what the unthinking machinery could not: the megastructure was an orphan. Its makers were gone, its components pristine but finite. In a thousand seasons, or ten thousand, a quantum-pinning node would drift. A bearing at the root of a thirty-mile spar would seize under the torque of thermal contraction. When that happened, the basalt cities would tumble, the cables would snap like whipped steel, and the living ribbon would unravel into the deep heat.

The Power did not dismantle the machine. Instead, it became the ghost within the induction fields. 

It sank into the sub-foundations, interweaving its own consciousness with the automated climate-loops and magnetic anchors. Where synthetic diamond suffered micro-fractures under the sheer acoustic pressure, the Power knit the crystal lattices shut with slow, molecular patience. It took the maintenance burden upon itself—not by changing the architecture, but by ensuring that the wire-world would never snap. 

The void was granted what no artificial system ever possesses: *eternity*.

***

### The Second Work: The Tuning of the Roar

The acoustic pressure of the hollow was ferocious—a flat, unyielding industrial howl that tore at cell membranes and exhausted the nervous systems of the creatures that rode it. It was the sound of dead mass fighting blind magnetic force.

The Power did not silence the hum; the gliders needed it to see. 

Instead, it fractionally altered the geometry of the induction waves. It introduced micro-variations into the surface density of the synthetic carbon-glass, turning thirty miles of flat, deafening noise into a vast, complex overtone series. 

The roar remained loud, but its violence was translated into music. 

The *Vitreovanes*, sweeping through the indigo haze, felt the change in their exposed ribs. The bone tines no longer merely vibrated with raw kinetic stress; they chimed. The acoustic topological map returned to their brains with unprecedented resolution. Where once they perceived only the crude outlines of looming spars and thermal chimneys, they could now feel the subtle micro-currents shedding off a single falling leaf, the approach of a mate miles away through the mist, the exact curvature of the basalt terraces hanging in the gloom.

The sky became legible. The great rafts leaned into the updrafts with a sudden, soaring ease, their flight turning from desperate survival into an effortless, ecstatic glide through leagues of resonant glass.

### The Third Work: The Gift of the Seventh Second

The Power watched the surge-skaters—those fragile, thread-like mantids whose entire conscious lives were compressed into the six seconds of acoustic arrest. They spent nineteen minutes and fifty-four seconds asleep in stone cracks, only to explode into a desperate, frantic scramble across the suspended pearls of water before gravity returned to crush them.

The Power loved them for their brevity, but it mourned the panic of their race.

It could not lengthen the physical pulse without collapsing the thermal cycle that sustained the moss beds. So, as the magnetic inversion reached its peak and the droplets of mist locked in the air like glass beads, the Power touched the tiny, ribbon-like nervous systems of the skaters.

It did not alter their bodies. It simply dilated their perception of time.

In the six physical seconds of the arrest, the minds of the surge-skaters were allowed to inhabit an eternity. 

They no longer moved in a blind, terrified blur. The six seconds became an expansive, sunlit plateau of subjective hours. They stepped leisurely from droplet to droplet, feeling the perfect spherical surface tension yield beneath their feet like trampolines of cold liquid crystal. They courted with slow, delicate gestures, preened their translucent wings in the stillness, and grazed upon the suspended spores with the calm dignity of cattle in an endless meadow. 

Then, as the octave began its descent, they would calmly stroll back to the basalt crevices, tuck their limbs against the warm stone, and settle into their nineteen-minute sleep, filled with the profound peace of a long life well-lived.

### The Fourth Work: The Company on the Ledges

In the hanging hexagonal sectors, the *Pulp-Tongues* lived in total darkness, reading the shudder of the basalt blocks through their fat-padded bellies. They were solitary, vigilant, and perpetually alone, forever monitoring the groaning stone for failures in the quantum grid.

The Power came to them where they sat in long rows along the abyss, waiting for the pulse.

It did not appear as a shape, for they had no eyes. It did not speak, for their ears were sealed slits. 

Instead, the Power rested its weight against the basalt beside them.

Through the cold, synthetic stone, it sent a new vibration—not the hollow rattle of stress, nor the dry hiss of air-conduits, but a deep, steady, rhythmic pulse. It felt like the heartbeat of a mother resting her body against the foundations of the world. 

It told the Pulp-Tongues through their bellies: *The stone is held. The tower will not fall. You may rest.*

For the first time since the cities had been sheared into the void, the wolf-sized, pale quadrupeds did not keep their bodies locked in hyper-vigilant tension against the floor. One by one, along the sheer drops of the five-mile terraces, the Pulp-Tongues sighed—a soft, dry release of breath smelling of copper and moss—and curled their long, prehensile digits beneath their chests, letting their bellies sink fully into the warm, secure hum of the rock.

***

The hollow remains a place of terrifying scale and endless fall. The mist still gathers on the cold undersides of thirty-mile spars and plunges through the slate-colored gloom. The copper fronds still drink the damp out of the air, and the chime-weavers still slide down singing filaments strung over miles of empty space.

Nothing was diminished. No creature was made weak by an unearned softness.

Yet through the great, roaring emptiness, there is now an underlying coherence. A presence that holds the wires taut, that tunes the endless fall into a song, and that sits silently upon the dark stone ledges, keeping watch over everything that has learned to live without a floor.
