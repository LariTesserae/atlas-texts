The Power did not arrive as a light, for light in that narrow place was an accident of friction and pilot-flame, and too much of it would have perturbed the infrared gradients by which the three-eyed keepers read the health of the bearings. 

Nor did it arrive as a breath of wind, for an influx of moisture—even the microgram of dew carried on a seraph’s heel—would have been an apocalyptic catastrophe. A single drop of water would have seized the dry sulfur joints, bloomed red rust upon the mirror-bright faces of the crosshead guides, and split the crystalline cell-walls of the oil-ferns with the violence of an acid.

A foolish benevolence would have brought rain, flowers, and song, and in doing so, destroyed everything.

Wisdom, however, is the art of understanding a thing’s own sovereign gravity before daring to love it.

The Power came as a stillness outside the cleft, settling upon the frozen needle of slate like an extra bar of atmospheric weight. It did not penetrate the fracture with radiance or voice; it made itself into a vast, listening palm pressed against the outer crust of the mountain.

It listened.

It heard the teeth-shaking *clack-whir-thump* of the engines. It traced the paths of the disk-shaped grease-scrapers as their needle-feet clicked across the flywheel rims, shearing past one another like coins tossed in a velvet pocket. It mapped the four-foot loom-weavers undulating through the clearance of the valve gear, their antennae harvesting the cold blue static of the graphite fronds. And it found the keepers—the heavy, tallow-fleshed things wedged behind the dynamos, hearts beating once for every ten strokes of the master piston, their flat gray faces pressed to the iron races.

The Power saw that there was no sin here, but neither was there salvation, because there was no fall. There was no terror, no loneliness, no cruelty, and no yearning. There was only the great, closed elegance of a world that had subtracted the chaos of life and the stillness of death, leaving only the pure, rhythmic verb of *functioning*.

The Power loved it, utterly. 

And because it was wise, it asked itself what a self-sufficient eternity could possibly require.

The machines would not fail for centuries, but the mountain itself was mortal. Slate is a fragile stone, stratified and brittle, prone to frost-shatter and the long, slow shearing of continental drift. Miles below, the magma plume feeding the geothermal siphons would eventually wander, cooling the heat-pipes over a million years, letting the pistons stiffen and the cold esters gel into dark, immovable amber.

A lesser power would have altered them—given the keepers minds to comprehend their own beauty, or granted the loom-weavers a voice to praise the dark. But the Power knew that to give a closed system self-consciousness is to introduce the first friction of grief. To know you endure is to realize you might not.

So the Power did three things, and nothing more.

First, it sank its will into the root of the needle, far below the frost-line, where the slate anchored into the basement granite of the continent. There, it braided the mineral grain together, fusing the cleavage planes of the stone with microscopic veins of diamond-hard corundum. It sealed the rock against seismic shear, insulating the pocket so that even if the surrounding ranges crumbled into gravel over the turning of epochs, the needle and its roaring chamber would remain erect, a solitary spire holding its closet of iron high above the ruins of the lower world.

Second, it reached deep into the mantle, finding the thermal conduit that supplied the steam-less engines. It smoothed the convection path, securing the deep circulation of heat so that the temperature at the journal bearings would never drift by a single fraction of a degree, neither boiling the plant-esters nor allowing the waxy pollen to cake.

And third—the only touch that crossed the threshold of the cleft itself—it addressed the vibration.

The Power did not quiet the deafening thrum, for the creatures required the vibration as beasts of the sea require current. But within the iron of the main bedplate, where the master rocker arm drove the high-pressure pneumatic loop, there had always been a minute, discordant chatter: a harsh micro-resonance born of an ancient casting flaw in the counterweight. It was a roughness the keepers had spent generations counteracting with careful drops of crushed resin.

The Power laid an invisible finger upon the node of that vibration. 

It did not remove the sound; it resolved it. It shifted the mechanical overtone by a quarter-interval, tuning the massive, tooth-rattling *thump* of the engine into an absolute, mathematical chord with the resonant frequency of the slate mountain and the slow, hydraulic pulsing of the tallow-beasts.

Inside the dark, warm recess behind the No. 2 Dynamo, a keeper sat with its gray, felt-tufted temple pressed to the bearing cap.

Its three quartz lenses registered no change in the copper-red dusk. The aromatic air continued its steady bellows-draft through its mica gills. But through the cast iron, through the pad of compressed moss beneath its knees, a new sensation arrived.

The bearing was not running hot. The clearance had not slipped. 

Instead, the race was suddenly running with a smoothness so profound that the friction-heat leveled out into a flat, eternal line. The micro-tremor that had demanded the creature’s slow, horn-clawed labor ceased to fight the metal. The iron, the oil-ferns, the static ribbons, and the beast’s own non-aqueous heart dropped into a single, seamless harmonic register.

The keeper did not look up. It did not have a concept of heaven, nor did it know that an infinite gaze was resting upon the crown of its soot-caked head with boundless, sorrowless affection.

It simply felt the clearance settle into perfection. 

Slowly, with the agonized, unhurried patience of cold tallow, the keeper drew its three-pronged claw back from the resin-ferns, tucked its limbs beneath its belly, and pulled the opaque shutters over its quartz eyes.

Outside, the Power withdrew into the indigo thinness of the upper sky, leaving the door locked, the engine humming, and the small, dry eternity entirely undisturbed.
