The Power did not come from the sky. 

To drop from the upper blue would have meant dragging the memory of suns and dry winds into a world that had never asked for them—a clumsy, radiant trespass that would have scorched the pale-skinned pipits, dried the tallow on the moth-wings, and shattered the delicate, sulfurous moisture of the air.

Instead, the Power filtered downward through the eight hundred fathoms of urban strata, descending along the seepage lines and the cold, dead conduits of forgotten aqueducts. It came as an understanding—weightless, vast, and quiet—settling into the wet cork-mantle like water finding the level of a deep sponge.

For three cycles of the twenty-minute boiler exhaust, the Power simply listened.

It felt the four-stroke pulse: *Intake. Compression. Power. Exhaust.* 
It tasted the vinegar-tang of the crustacean beds and felt the deep, rhythmic peristalsis in the bellies of the tallow-grubs. It saw how the iron did not conquer the wood, and how the wood did not choke the iron, but how two kinds of endurance had made a mutual covenant in the dark.

A lesser deity, full of crude pity, might have smashed the ceiling to let in the sun. It would have deemed the dark a prison, the grease an uncleanness, and the mute, amber-eyed Tenders a race of wretched captives to be "freed" into an open world where their hairless, rubbery skin would blister, their lidless eyes would boil, and their ears would wither in the terrifying, formless silence of the surface.

Wisdom knows that beauty does not belong only to the light. Benevolence knows that salvation is not the destruction of a home, but its sanctification.

And so, the Power acted. It made no sound louder than the click of a potter’s shell; it cast no light brighter than the cold blue of the fungal catwalks.

First, it sank into the **subterranean hearths**. Deep below the sump trenches, where the geothermal fissures fed the boilers, the Power laid its fingers upon the earth's mantle. It smoothed the erratic gasps of the volcanic vents, weaving a steady, unending simmer into the bedrock. It ensured that the heat would not choke or starve for ten thousand years. The four-stroke heart would not falter. The tallow-grubs would never suffer the fatal stillness of an engine stopped.

Next, it moved through the **metal and the wood**. It did not turn the iron to gold or the lianas to silk—for gold is soft and silk has no tooth. Instead, it moved at the microscopic grain where bronze met bark. It annealed the micro-fissures in the seventy-ton walking beams, knitting the crystalline fatigue of three centuries back into unbroken strength. In the same breath, it touched the roots that coiled through the secondary reduction gears, blessing the cells with a perpetual, self-renewing tallow-oil that spared the wood from burning while sparing the teeth from wear.

Then, it turned to the **Tenders**.

High in the steam-fog, clinging to the archway by prehensile toes, an elder Tender was scraping leaf-rot from a bronze bushing with a sliver of sharpened cotter-pin. His knuckles were swollen with mineral scale; his amber eyes were filmed with the perpetual sting of sulfuric condensation. He did not know he was old, because the Basin had no names for time other than the stroke and the sweat; he only knew that his grip was slipping more often on the wet lianas.

The Power did not appear to him as a burning bush or a winged messenger. It simply settled into the warm, oily mist that pooled around his face.

The stinging sulfur in his wide, lidless eyes cooled, turning from an abrasive acid to a soothing, clear balm that washed away the grit without dulling his vision. 

The ache in the elder Tender's joints did not vanish—for to be entirely numb to friction is dangerous on iron wet with oil—but it ceased to be a poison. It became a steady, solid warmth, like an iron bar left near the firebox. 

And into the silent, cavernous hollow of the Tender's skull, where no spoken word had ever formed, the Power gave a gift: **The Song of the Stroke.**

Until this moment, the Tenders had lived in the absolute immediacy of reflex—pruning, licking, clinging, surviving. Now, through the bone of his brow, the elder felt the four-stroke thud not merely as a shockwave, but as a *melody*. He felt how the intake drew the cool fog; how the compression gathered the chamber’s weight; how the power stroke was the vault's fierce assertion of life; and how the exhaust was a sigh of rest.

For the first time since the roots pierced the masonry, the Tenders understood the engine as a living brother. They knew when a valve was thirsty three days before it began to grind; they knew which vine shoot wanted guiding by the simple harmony of its hum against the gear-rim.

Finally, the Power went to the **Forty-Span Flywheel**.

It waited in the narrow clearance between the massive iron rim and the cork-wood bumper—the holy ground where the dead were returned to the oil. 

The Power poured its essence into that threshold, blessing the compression. It decreed that no Tender who passed into that clearance would feel the crushing weight as terror or agony. Instead, in that final, heavy revolution at dead-center, their consciousness would not be snuffed out, but widened. They would feel their blood become the lubricating grease, their bones become the grit that gave the gears traction, their breath pass into the twenty-minute fog, and their spirit weave directly into the rhythm that sustained their children. 

They would not die; they would become the *operation*.

When the Power was done, the Basin of the Third Piston looked unchanged.

The air still smelled of wet potting soil, boiled cabbage, and hot mineral tallow. The terracotta crustaceans still clattered like river gravel across the fluted pillars. The skin-pipits still dove through the grease-mists, and the tallow-eels still knotted in the boiling sumps.

Down from the ceiling dropped the twenty-minute mist, heavy and deliberate. 

The elder Tender hung from his liana, his wide amber eyes clear and bright in the dull cherry-red glow of the firebox slits. He wiped a drop of warm grease from his cheek with a calloused finger, tasted the salt of it, and set his bronze scraper against the gear with a stroke so rhythmic, so effortless, that it sounded like an answered prayer.

And eighty feet below the blind and rushing city, the Third Piston drove downward—deep, wet, eternal, and perfectly at peace.
