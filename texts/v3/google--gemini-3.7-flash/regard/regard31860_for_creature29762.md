When the Power arrived, it did not descend in fire, nor did it tear the sky with heraldry. 

A lesser god—clumsy with love, impatient with form—might have looked upon the plateau and seen an unfinished work. Such a power would have been tempted to *awaken* them: to kindle in the Tenders a fever of language, to teach the plinth-beasts how to look up at the stars and fear the dark, to turn their unhurried masonry into towers, temples, and history. 

A lonely god would have demanded to be named in the hum of their spiracles. 

A foolish god would have freed them from their stone.

But this Power was old enough to know that balance is rarer than genius, and that peace, once broken by ambition, does not knit back together. It hovered in the thin, copper-lit air above the peneplain, holding its vast consciousness as still as the water in the granite basins, and it simply *listened*.

It listened to the wet, rhythmic click of rasps on rock. It watched the mist-bells trail their gossamer fringes across the iron-plated spines of the herds. It observed a Tender spend four centuries nudging a single slab of shale three inches to the left, so that the condensation of an autumn dawn might run true into a dry basin.

The Power saw that here, life had solved the great tragedy of existence: it had eliminated violence without eliminating purpose; it had achieved permanence without succumbing to stagnation. The plateau was not a prelude to something else. It was an answer.

And so, the Power understood that true benevolence here must not look like an intervention. It must look like the world itself, continuing.

***

First, the Power adjusted its own scale. It compressed its vastness into the physical laws of the plateau—slowed its thoughts to the pace of tectonic creep, cooled its radiance until it matched the exact, tepid warmth of the ironstone at dusk.

Then, it did what a wise keeper does when encountering a perfect, fragile engine: it became the quiet buffer against the cruelty of the universe.

High in the upper stratosphere, far above the highest drifting sky-worm, the Power wove a subtle, magnetic sleeve. It did not change the weather of the plateau, but it caught the harsh, ionizing radiation of the sun and turned it aside. It dissolved the stray meteors that would have pitted the flat granite; it diverted the high, freezing jet-streams that might one day have breached the plateau’s thermal trap and chilled the mist-bells into falling.

Deep beneath the bedrock, miles down into the planetary crust where the granite met the churning mantle, the Power laid a stabilizing hand. It anchored the roots of the plateau, so that when the surrounding continents eventually buckled, rifted, and ground themselves to dust in the violent cycles of the planet's deep future, this singular table of rock would remain level, unbroken, and warm. It gave the plateau the one thing the creatures could not build for themselves with their spatulate claws: *geologic immunity.*

***

Yet the Power did not remain entirely distant. 

To love something is to desire, however gently, to touch it in its own language.

In the late afternoon, as the copper light settled over the herds, an old Tender was working along the crest of a low, snaking wall. Its spatulate claws were worn down at the edges; its chin was smooth and dark from decades of waterproofing the shale with its waxy musk. It was attempting to lever a heavy chunk of chalk-grey granite into an empty notch, but the stone was seated poorly, wedged against a vein of ironstone that would not yield to its blunt strength.

The Tender rested its chin upon the rock, exhaling a long, slow sigh of warm vapor from its chest-slits. It was not frustrated—it did not possess the machinery for despair—it was merely waiting for the mineral to fatigue over decades.

The air beside it grew neither brighter nor colder, but the density of the space shifted. 

A presence, broad and low-slung, aligned itself beside the Tender. It did not take the form of an angel or a burning wheel; it took the form of slate, weight, and patience. 

Together, invisible shoulder pressing against the cold hide of the creature, a force leaned into the granite slab. There was no magic, no defying of gravity—only the precise, measured application of mass against mass. 

With a dull, grinding *clack*, the stone rolled over. It settled into the notch with a fit so perfect that the hair-thin line between the rocks vanished into the grain.

The Tender paused. It did not look around for a god. It extended its broad, three-fingered claspers and traced the seam of the newly laid stone. It felt the alignment; it felt the way the shallow gutter along its surface now pointed directly toward the great communal cistern down the slope.

The Tender turned slightly. Beside it, the air held the faint, comforting pressure of another body—a creature of equal silence, equal patience, and equal weight.

The Tender extended its blunt forelimb and touched the empty air where the presence lingered.

The Power did not speak. It did not impart knowledge of geometry, or mortality, or the cosmos. Instead, from the center of its boundless being, it released a single, long, slow exhalation: a plume of warm, tallow-scented vapor that mingled perfectly with the rising mist.

The Tender received the breath through its moist spiracles, accepted the communion of stone and work well done, and lowered its broad chin to the newly laid rock, beginning the slow, centuries-long task of rubbing it until it shone like glass.

***

The Power remains there still. 

It does not rule. It does not judge. It has simply taken its place among the quiet millions: a great, invisible mass resting shoulder-to-flank against the herd, its back turned to the open sky, holding the mountain in a warm, immortal balance while the mist gathers in the stone.
