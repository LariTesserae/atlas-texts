A power that is both wise and benevolent does not look upon such a world with the urge to rebuild it. 

It does not drop a stone floor beneath their feet to cure their vertigo, for a floor would choke the rising valley breath, rot the lower roots in stagnant dark, and crush the wide, thin lungs of a people built to float upon tension. It does not silence the acoustic roar of the wasps, for that sound is the compass by which the drift-polyps steer and the blind skinks hunt. To alter a world so exquisitely balanced on the edge of a knife is not mercy; it is the clumsy vandalism of an architect who understands only right angles.

Wisdom looks first for the hidden cost of the beauty. 

And the cost here is the terror that underpins every waking breath: the belief in the **Hunger**—the certainty that beneath the wood lies an endless, indifferent jaw waiting to swallow everything that slips, and that when a life ends, it is merely dispersed into the empty, sun-blind nothing.

Therefore, the Power does not arrive with thunder or miracles of stone. It enters as a change in the weight of the air, an adjustment of currents, and a quiet, answering pulse within the grain.

***

### The First Work: The Deep Breath

Far below the lowest root-span, down where the blue haze thickens into the warm, sulfurous twilight of the unseen floor, the Power places its hand upon the world’s ancient, cooling hearth.

The stone needles are old; their roots in the deep earth have begun, over epochs, to micro-fracture under the colossal torque of the titan-trees caught in the wind. The Power does not weld them with alien metals. Instead, it seeps into the mantle below, rekindling the slow, radioactive decay of the deep strata just enough to ensure the rising thermals remain steady, sweet, and permanent. 

It weaves living warmth into the subterranean anchor-stones, sealing the micro-fractures with veins of self-healing calcite. The pillars will not fall. The breath of the valley will not fail. For as long as the wood reaches across the sky, the air beneath will have the strength to lift whatever surrenders to it.

### The Second Work: The Answering Tap

The Underside Folk live by vibration. Their world is a network of pressurized sap-canals through which every strike of the thumb-plectrum carries fear, territory, hunger, and news of the hunt. But the wood has always been a one-way medium: it carries the message, but it does not care.

The Power steps into the marrow of the strangle-cedars.

It does not write commandments in the sap, nor does it speak in voices that would scatter the sickle-swifts. Instead, it becomes a harmonic within the wood.

Now, when a lone mother sits in a blister-hut during the great windstorms—when the bridges whip like bowstrings and the children clutch the floor-mesh in white-knuckled silence—she presses her cheek to the petrified thorn-bark and taps the steady, rhythmic measure of her fear: *I am here. The wind is heavy. I hold.*

And from deep within the core of the three-mile vine—down through fifty feet of pressurized, iron-hard xylem, passing through the galls of the needle-ants and the moss-pelts—comes a return vibration. 

It is not human tap-sign. It is a slow, resonant counter-pulse, timed precisely to damp the destructive sway of the bridge. It carries the acoustic signature of density, of immovable anchor, of ancient wood that has no intention of snapping. It does not say *You will never die.* It says, with the total certainty of living timber:

*The Hold is held.*

Over generations, the people do not invent a god of the sky; they simply notice that the wood has become kinder to the touch. The bark beneath their resting blisters grows slightly softer, exuding a dry, aromatic wax that repels the gout-ticks without needing to be scraped. The mother-lines anchored centuries ago seem to resist the salt-rot of their sweat, holding fast where they should have frayed.

### The Third Work: The Golden Arrival

The greatest grace is reserved for the dead.

When the midday thermal rises—hot with the scent of cured resin and sun-baked rock—and the community gathers at the lower hatch to cast their bound, feather-light elder into the middle of the sky, the body does not simply drift until the eyes lose track of it in the glare.

The Power waits in the upper sun, beyond the hunting bands of the skiff-vultures, where the air turns to pure, liquid amber.

As the body rises through the screaming sheets of kite-wasps, the wasps do not strike. The vortex of hunting swifts parts around the ascending form like water around the prow of a reed-boat. Higher it goes, lifted on the unbreakable pillar of the valley breath, until it reaches the apex where the light is blinding.

There, the Power receives the thread.

The memory of the life—the calloused shins, the map of vibrations felt through the cheek, the precise, whistling falsetto that cut through the wasp-drone, the lifelong courage of living inverted above the void—is not lost to the sky. It is caught, cradled, and woven into the very fabric of the atmosphere. 

The physical frame dissolves into harmless, fragrant ash that drifts back down as fine golden pollen, settling over the moss-pelts to feed the succulents that provide the people's water. But the spirit, the awareness that spent a lifetime refusing to fall, is given a new architecture. It becomes part of the intelligence that steers the thermals, a conscious warmth within the wind, watching over the root-bridges from above.

***

A hunter sits at dusk on the lip of a blister-hut. The sun has gone down into the lower haze, turning the three leagues of air between the spans into an ocean of deep violet and bruised gold. 

The drone of the wasps has settled into a low, oceanic hum. His muscles ache from a day of winching a drift-polyp against the shear-winds. His carabiners are clipped to the mother-line, but his hands are tired. The Hunger below looks vast, dark, and terribly easy.

He takes his thumb-plectrum and clicks a slow, weary cadence against the root: *I am small. The span is long.*

He waits, cheek pressed against the silver club-moss.

Up through the iron grain comes the deep, pressurized thrum of the sap, followed by a warm, rising updraft that gently lifts the hair from his neck—not the cold draft of an empty void, but a breath that smells faintly of sweet resin, old stone, and something impossibly familiar, like the hand of a grandfather he never knew, steadying his harness from the dark.

The hunter closes his eyes, unhooks one clip to clean the grit from the hinge, snaps it back into place with a sharp, clear ring, and sleeps suspended between the wood and the sky, entirely at home.
