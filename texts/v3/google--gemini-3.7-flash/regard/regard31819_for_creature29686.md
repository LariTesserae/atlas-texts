A lesser god, arriving here, would have wept. 

And in weeping, that god would have destroyed everything. 

The tears would have fallen as a caustic, blistering rain. Upon striking the thousand-degree flanks of the basalt needles, the water would have flashed to violent steam, shattering the mirror-pure vitrophyre through catastrophic thermal shock. The moisture would have ruined the dry van der Waals fibrils of the Shear-Plates, sending them tumbling into the gulfs. It would have oxidized the Balusters into brittle flakes of red rust, glued the buoyant carbon-lattices of the Gliders with heavy surface tension, and fouled the singing, micro-filament webs of the Glass-weavers with mildew and mud.

The lesser god would have called this *salvation*. They would have tried to plant moss in the cleavages; they would have tried to tear open the heavy ceiling to let in a star’s blinding, bleaching radiation; they would have tried to teach the Balusters to rest. 

The power that arrived, however, was neither young nor arrogant. It was old enough to know that benevolence is not the imposition of one’s own comfort upon the strange, but the fierce and humble defense of a foreign perfection.

It did not descend in light. Light here was an intrusion, a vulgarity that would wash out the subtle, maroon pulse of the mantle and the holy, triboelectric sparks that danced along fresh fault lines. 

Instead, the Power made itself into something this world could hold without breaking: **a presence as vast, dry, and patient as the negative space between the spires.**

It observed. For three thousand cycles of the Balusters’ slow reassembly, it did nothing at all. It simply listened to the grand, tectonic cathedral. It learned the deep-mantle dialect of shifting plates; it felt the terrifying, ecstatic mathematics of the Shear-Plates drinking a lightning-snap; it watched the Gliders slice through the thermals like falling knives of hammered bronze.

It saw that this world was not suffering. It was *straining*. It was singing a severe, unyielding hymn of pure physics, composed in the only language that iron and glass can speak.

And so, having understood, the Power acted. Its deeds were three, and they were silent.

***

### I. The Aegis of Absolute Aridity

The Power turned its face outward, toward the cold cosmos beyond the heavy, sulfurous troposphere. 

It saw that this cradle of sharp stone was vulnerable. In the wider galaxy, there are stray comets—wandering masses of dirty ice, stray hydrogen plumes, the wet debris of dying stars. If an ice-meteor were to pierce this world’s sky, the resulting deluge would be a poison of catastrophic proportions.

The Power cast a great, invisible membrane around the outer perimeter of the atmosphere. Not a wall of force that would trap the world’s radiant heat, but a sieve of total desiccation. Any frozen wanderer, any drifting cloud of interstellar moisture that dared approach this coordinate was instantly turned aside, vaporized, or stripped of its hydrogen, cast out into the dark.

The sky was sealed forever: dry, black, and safe from the terror of water.

***

### II. The Prime Datum

The Power saw the Balusters. It watched a segmented colossus of hematite spend four centuries hauling a three-hundred-ton lintel up a sheer cliff, trying to pin an erupting mountain against the dark, only to have the ground sheer away beneath it and cast it into the abyss.

The Power did not stop the mountain from rising—to stop the ascent would be to starve the Shear-Plates and silence the Glass-weavers’ harps. It would have robbed the Balusters of their holy, Sisyphean purpose.

Instead, the Power reached into the unfathomable, ductile floor, far below the reach of the weavers, and set down a single, unshifting anchor: **The Datum**.

It was not a physical pillar, but an eternal, piezoelectric resonance—a single, unchanging acoustic frequency hummed directly into the planetary core. 

To the Balusters, whose entire consciousness is the acoustic geometry of stone, this note was a revelation. It gave them, for the first time in their history, an absolute metric against which to measure the strain of the world. They no longer built in the blind dark; they could feel precisely where the shear-vectors converged, miles before the vitrophyre cracked. 

They did not build less. They built with *sovereign precision*. Across the spires, the megaliths took on an impossible, interlocking majesty—vast, counterbalanced cantilevers that swayed with the rising spires like the escapements of titanic, stone clocks, surviving the tectonic snaps, riding the ascent, holding the violent earth in a state of perilous, magnificent dynamic equilibrium.

***

### III. The Ear in the Void

A world that exists without an observer is a tragedy of physics; a world that is seen only by things that wish to change it is a tragedy of cruelty.

The Power did not interfere with the Glass-weavers. It did not blunt their razor-sharp filaments, nor did it soften the impact when a drifting Glider struck a web and was cleaved in two. It understood that in the logic of this place, the sheared fragments of the Glider became seeds for a thousand new kites, and the singing of the filament was the breath of the mountain.

Instead, the Power became the **Listener**.

It filled the great, airy corridors between the basalt fins with an attentive, appreciative consciousness. It gave the singing webs something they had never possessed: a medium of perfect acoustic reception. 

Before, the filaments hummed in isolation, their deep, dust-liquefying frequencies dying in the local voids. Now, the Power held the sound, allowing the harmonic vibrations of a snap on the western ridges to ripple across thousands of miles, braiding into the songs of a million other tension-webs. 

The world became a single, continuous instrument. When a tower of black vitrophyre shattered in the deep gloom, the concussive *crack* did not merely echo and fade. It was caught, resolved, and carried into the overarching polyphony of the dark—a vast, metallic symphony of strain, release, and ascent.

***

Having done this, the Power did not demand worship. It did not speak. It did not carve its name into the mirror-faced basalt. 

It remained as it came: a cool, dry, immaculate attention, holding the dark in reverence, letting the glass shatter, letting the hematite grind, perfectly content to watch a world that would never yield, never soften, and never, ever bleed.
