When the gaze of the Power first fell upon the terrace, it did not descend with light or wind. It arrived as a vast, deliberate deceleration.

A lesser power, driven by the clumsy urgency of compassion, might have spoken. It might have poured down rains of ambrosia, accelerated the blooming of the great jade cups, or granted minds and voices to the pale mantles folded in the dark water. It would have filled the quiet with song and populated the empty spaces between the plants, mistaking stillness for loneliness and simplicity for lack.

Wisdom, however, knows that to touch a balance this ancient is almost always to break it.

The Power understood immediately that the terrace was not an unfinished thing awaiting a miracle; it was an altar that had achieved perfection by having shed every unnecessary part. The plants did not dream of moving; the silk-creatures did not dream of the sea; the polisher upon the stone did not dream of an end to its circuit. They were completely and flawlessly adequate to their existence. 

And so, the Power’s first act was to make itself completely weightless.

It drew its consciousness down through the sky, thinning itself until it was no heavier than the sunlight already warming the travertine. It entered the greenhouse-warm air without displacing a single molecule of steam, hovering across the parabolic swells of the floor like a second, invisible layer of atmosphere. 

To perceive the terrace properly, the Power slowed its own reckoning of time. It set its heartbeat to the cadence of the travertine's growth—one millimeter every three hundred years. 

At that speed, the terrace transformed. The absolute stillness gave way to a magnificent, low-frequency music. The Power watched the glass-thin discs drift across the carp-scale basins, sliding along lines of mineral tension like planets in micro-orbits. It watched the silk-creature deep in the jade cup billow its pale folds, drawing the sky’s dust into the dark vascular mouth of the succulent in a rhythm as stately and certain as the tides of an ocean. It watched the polisher creep over the ancient grooves, its broad muscular foot dissolving and glazing the rock, leaving behind a wake of wet, shining pearl.

It looked at the ancient basalt adze resting in the basin. It saw the ghost of the human hands that had shaped it—hands that had understood, thousands of years ago, the slope of the water and the necessity of the channels. The human had not stayed. They had shaped the rock, laid down the tool, and walked away, allowing the mountain to inhabit the form they had cut. The Power recognized that the polisher and the human were part of the same sentence, spoken across millennia.

Then, the Power looked downward, into the stone beneath the water.

There, five miles into the dark, cold bone of the mountain, it saw the flaw. 

The thermal fissure that fed the terrace was shifting. A slow, tectonic migration was pinching the basalt throat that carried the warm alkaline water upward from the earth’s mantle. In less than four human generations—the blink of a leaf-tip—the flow would thin to a weep, the basins would dry, the jade cups would split in the arid mountain wind, and the silk-creatures would desiccate into their final, permanent cysts, waiting for an aquifer that would never return.

Here was the only labor worthy of a wise benevolence: not to alter the life above, but to secure the foundation that held it.

The Power sank into the stone. 

It did not split the rock or cause the mountain to tremble. It worked in the deep, silent dark, pressing its will into the subterranean strata. With patient, immoveable strength, it braced the limestone shear-lines against the deep faults of the earth. It gently widened the geothermal channel, easing the basalt back, and carved an arch of impervious granite around the thermal wellspring so that no shift of the continent could ever pinch it shut again. It calibrated the pressure with exquisite precision, ensuring that the water rising to the terrace would never surge into a violent geyser, nor dwindle beneath the thickness of a fingernail, but remain forever a smooth, glass-thin skin sliding over the rock.

Having secured the water, the Power rose once more through the stone and returned to the sunlight.

It did not leave a sign of its presence. It did not carve a symbol upon the cliff or cause a flower to bloom out of season. But as its consciousness began to lift away, returning to the wide spaces between the stars, it allowed the faintest fraction of its warmth to settle over the western curve of the shelf.

The polisher was just then making its circuit across the basin. Its leathery skirt flowed slowly over the dark river basalt of the ancient adze, cleaning the stray sediment from the worn grip. 

For a single, suspended second, the creature felt the stone beneath it grow infinitesimally sweeter—a brief, microscopic abundance of minerals dissolved in the warm wash, perfectly suited to the chemistry of its foot. It did not know it was being thanked. It simply drew the nourishment into its pale flesh, laid down a ribbon of flawless, iridescent glaze across the cut travertine, and continued its slow, blind journey toward the edge of the sky.

The Power withdrew, leaving the terrace exactly as it had found it: wet, warm, utterly vacant, and now, enduring forever.
