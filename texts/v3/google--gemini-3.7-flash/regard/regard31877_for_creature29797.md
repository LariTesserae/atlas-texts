The presence did not descend like light into water, because the light was already there, hard and unsweetened. Nor did it arrive with warmth; to introduce heat into this high slot would be to rob the melt of its dissolved breath, de-oxygenate the razor-plates, and suffocate the valves in lukewarm rot. 

A lesser god—one sentimental and human-minded—might have looked upon the drowned foundry and wept. Such a power would have seen only confinement, deafening thunder, the pitiless crushing of young spat, and the agonizing rigidity of beasts that could not turn their heads to see the sky. Such a power would have tried to plant moss. It would have softened the water, dulled the basalt edges, loosened the bolts, and in doing so, committed a swift and total genocide.

But this power was wise, and therefore it possessed the terrible mercy of restraint.

It came as a settling of attention—a vast, weightless caliper laid gently against the exterior of the mountain. It did not speak, because sound here was already occupied by the drop-forges and the pneumatic drag of hematite guts. Instead, it listened with the absolute, non-interfering fidelity of a master horologist watching an escapement wheel that has not missed a second in four hundred thousand years.

The power understood what the rift was: not a prison, not an error, not a ruin of soft life turned to stone, but an *altar of pure tension*. Here was life that had made peace with the brutal physics of the crust. Life that had discarded the fragile, leaking mess of mucous and fat in exchange for the clean, unpitying permanence of the ore-vein.

It saw the **Bellow-Beasts**, whose single heartbeats shook the shelf; 
It saw the **Ticking Stars**, shearing pyritic frost from the hinges with the solemn devotion of monks scraping tallow;
It saw the **Brace-Worms**, sweating their hematite glue, their bodies taking upon themselves the crushing tonnage of a continent so the rift might stay open just wide enough to let the cold sky through;
It saw the **Chime-Chits**, suspended in the magnetic draft, wafer-thin, singing in piezoelectric spasm.

The Power asked itself: *What does an engine lack?*

It did not lack purpose; it was full of it. It did not lack beauty; its geometry was immaculate. 

What it lacked was an end to *metal fatigue*.

The mountain was young, but basalt is brittle. The continuous, concussive slamming of the drop-forges, combined with the thirty-second galvanic arcs, was slowly shearing the micro-crystalline anchors of the lower ledge. In three centuries—a blink in the life of a magnetite bed—the northern wall would have suffered harmonic shear, collapsing into the hairline fissure, pulverizing the brace-trusses and sealing the forge in its own iron slag forever.

So the benevolent power did not alter the living. It did not touch the beasts, nor tame the current, nor sweeten the brine. 

It worked only on the stone.

Down through the clear, lens-like water, through the whistling exhaust plumes and the cavitation foam, the power sank its will into the root-matrix of the mountain. It did not soften the rock; it aligned the crystal lattice of the magnetite. It smoothed the deep subterranean fault-slippage from a jagged fracture into a single, continuous, resonant plane.

It turned the mountain into a true anvil.

Where the rock had previously absorbed the shock with destructive, chaotic friction, the Power tuned the basalt to a perfect, low-register fundamental frequency. Now, when the Bellow-Beasts slammed shut, the concussive blow did not shatter the micro-fractures; it traveled cleanly down through the banded ore, dispersed harmlessly into the tectonic root of the continent, and returned thirty seconds later as a pure, coherent piezoelectric rebound—refreshing the quartz joints, feeding the Chime-Chits' spines with clean, untainted current, and triggering the next stroke with lossless, frictionless timing.

The rhythm did not change. The violence did not soften. 

The clank of the carapaces was just as deafening. The Ticking Stars still ground the stray spat to iron meal. The water remained cold enough to crack glass. 

But the machine was now eternal.

Having balanced the forge, the wise power pulled back its attention, retreating beyond the trembling surface of the mountain lake, out into the thin high air under the sun. 

It left no footprints, no symbols, and no gospel. 

It left only the slot, holding itself open against the world, breathing its cold, iron breath into the light—a dropped hammer that would never break the floor it struck.
