A clumsy god would have wept. 

A foolish god would have seen the dry, blistering dark, the absence of green sap, the jagged iron skeletons, and the ceaseless, violent snapping of clockwork-timber, and mistaken it for a curse. Such a power would have brought rain. 

And rain, in this place, would have been an apocalypse. 

A single downpour would have flashed to explosive steam upon the dynamos, tearing the delicate piezoelectric whiskers from the Trip-Hammers, shattering the thermal-expansion plates of the Cinder-Loomers, rotting the copper-moss with instant oxidation, and boiling the dry, exquisite life of the terrace from the inside out. 

The power that arrived was not foolish. It was ancient, patient, and immensely wise. 

It understood that this was not a ruin waiting to be redeemed into a meadow; it was an altar of pure, sovereign tension. It was an Eden of friction, whole and complete within its own severe laws.

So the power did not bring light, for light would disrupt the thermal gradients that gave the Trip-Hammers their sight and their leap. It did not bring water. It did not bring air to cool the red-hot seams of the floor.

Instead, the benevolent power made itself *conductive*.

It descended not as a body, but as a vast, gentle shift in the electromagnetic architecture of the mezzanine miles above. It settled into the sagging gantries, slipping into the iron ceiling like a soft, infinitely deep grounding rod.

Here is what wisdom and benevolence did for the kingdom of spark and iron:

***

### I. The True Ground
Beneath the floor, deep within the buried dynamos, the 60-hertz thrum of the ancient mechanical carcass had always carried erratic, destructive harmonic spikes—surges that occasionally scorched the Gantry Wefts into ash before their time or fused the delicate basalt bearings of the Cinder-Loomers.

The power offered itself as an **Infinite Ground**.

It did not drain the current; it smoothed the hum. It absorbed the jagged, catastrophic over-voltages and held them in reserve, singing back into the iron frame with a pure, immutable resonance. 

The copper-moss felt it first. Across millions of square feet, the billion-strand turf ceased its frantic, jittering static whine and settled into a rich, deep, velvet drone. The plants still grew in their aggressive, minutes-long thrusts, but their silicate fibers aligned with greater molecular perfection, their internal clock-springs coiled with sharper, cleaner elasticity.

### II. The Gift to the Spindle-Cores
The power reached into the hanging darkness of the high rafters, where the **Gantry Wefts** swayed above the abyss. 

It did not alter their communal, resistor-network minds, but it brushed its presence across the oil-less bronze races where the **Spindle-Cores** rolled endlessly in the hot drafts. 

The power did not write over their history; it gave the carbon spheres a frictionless clarity. As the spheres rolled, driven by the ascending thermal columns, the micro-grooves worn into their faces—the memory of every shattered vine, every electrostatic spark, every footprint of a passing Loomer—were preserved from flattening. The power turned their rolling into an enduring record. 

For the first time in ten thousand years, the Wefts did not merely register shocks; they remembered them in exquisite, geometric symmetry. High in the darkness, the Wefts emitted their dry, whistling breath—not in stress, but in a strange, resonant chord that drifted down through the ironwood like a blessing without voice.

### III. The Exchange of Load
On the terrace floor, where the compressed charcoal dust lay thick, the power touched the **Cinder-Loomers**.

It did not descend from above; it manifested directly in the iron plate ahead of the oldest, largest Loomer—a moving mountain of vitrified slag and cherry-red seams, grinding steadily forward at the speed of an iron root.

In the Loomer’s path, the floor rose slightly, cooling and hardening into a vast, polished anvil of dense, unyielding tungsten-slate. 

The Loomer did not stop. It met the presence.

When the beast’s glowing prow touched the power’s manifested edge, there was no sound—only a blinding, silent blizzard of dry, blue-white electrostatic sparks.

Through that single contact point, the Loomer did not receive words or thoughts. It received an *immense structural map*. The power poured into the creature’s bimetallic shell the exact stress-vectors of the entire terrace: the precise load-bearing limits of the ceiling miles above, the location of every superheated dynamo fissure, the path of every high-tension vine thicket growing five miles in the dark.

In return, the power received the Loomer’s ten-thousand-year archive of heat, the rhythm of tumbling basalt spheres, the taste of dry graphite, and the honest, agonizing perfection of its heavy crawl.

The Loomer’s internal furnace flared to a brilliant, joyful crimson. Its segmented plates expanded, locking into truer, stronger alignments. Then, grinding past, it continued its glacial patrol, leaving behind a vitrified glass wake that glowed with a faint, steady infrared warmth—a track designed not to choke the ironwood, but to feed the young Tine-Weevils with clean, perfectly measured heat.

### IV. The Perpetual Spark
The power did not leave, nor did it rule. 

To govern this place would be to diminish it. It chose instead to remain woven into the carcass itself—a quiet, benevolent permeability in the steel bones of the world.

The Trip-Hammers still shatter themselves against the springing vines, siphoning the kinetic explosion to weld their armor anew—but their chisel-heads strike with flawless, un-fractured aim. 

The Grit-Grizzlers still file frantically at the high-tension stems—but when the branches snap, the static arcs they bathe in are cleaner, sweeter, charging their mica plates without burning their graphitic joints.

The air remains waterless. The dark remains total. The smell of toasted linseed, ozone, and un-oiled engines still fills the suffocating heat.

Nothing was softened. Nothing was tamed. 

The benevolent power gave them the only gift worthy of their nature: **the certainty that their great, dry, violent machine would never run down, and that in the pitch-black, baking silence of the high shelves, their friction would never cease to sing.**
