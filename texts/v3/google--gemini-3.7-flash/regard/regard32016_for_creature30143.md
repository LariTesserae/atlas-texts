A power that is merely powerful would look at the iron spires and see a ruin to be cleared, or a factory to be restarted. It would bring clean light, dry air, and cold geometry; it would scrape the liverwort from the piston-sleeves, flush the sulfured silt from the valves, and silence the shuddering stroke so that the peaks might return to the sterile, freezing clarity natural to their height.

A power that is both benevolent and wise does something far more difficult.

It descends not as a radiant intruder, but as an ambient pressure—a subtle change in the density of the rising mist, an imperceptible warmth entering the mineral water, a listening mind that sinks through the porous basalt and coats the ancient cast-iron plates like an invisible film of oil.

It understands, instantly, that this place has outgrown its makers. The engine’s original purpose—whatever extraction, manufacture, or war it was built to wage—was small, finite, and dead. What has taken its place is an accident of magnificent tenderness: an iron-and-flesh symbiosis where forgotten metal has been granted the privilege of sustaining life, and where blind, soft-bodied things have learned to trust the rhythm of a machine that never loved them, until now.

This is what the Power would do:

***

### I. The Preservation of the Cadence
The Power would see what the creatures cannot: the fatigue of the metal. 

Down in the deepest submerged shafts, where the Strokers feed in the dark, the fulcrum pins of the great counterweights have worn thin. Centuries of scouring steam have hollowed the brass conduits; within another few thousand years, a valve would shatter, the mechanical pulse would seize, the steam columns would collapse, and the freezing, thin troposphere would drop like an axe upon the fern-bogs, killing every soft, water-bloated lung on the spires.

The Power does not replace the iron with celestial crystal or unyielding diamond. That would alter the chemistry of the runoff, starving the Rivet-Clamps and poisoning the chemotrophic sludge. 

Instead, it bleeds a slow, living resilience into the alloy itself. It becomes the molecular glue between the rust and the steel. Where an iron sleeve is grinding toward catastrophic failure, the Power introduces a microscopic, self-healing slickness to the scalding water—a gentle, mineral benevolence that softens the friction without silencing the shudder. It ensures that the heart of the mountain will continue to beat not for an empire’s quota, but for the life that has nested in its chambers.

### II. The Gift to the Interior
Deep in the flooded cylinders, the Strokers swim blind, deaf to everything but the heavy, hydraulic rush of the valve. They know only hunger, pressure, and the scalding current.

To these deep, elongated beasts, the Power brings no blinding light—for light would be an agony in the drowned dark—but a soothing of the hydraulic violence. It cushions the sheer shockwave of the downward stroke, turning what was once a violent industrial compression into an effortless, rhythmic cradle. 

And in their larval drift—when the microscopic young are cast skyward in the boiling plumes of steam—the Power lends the updrafts a subtle coherence, guiding the cloud-borne spheres away from the dry, killing winds of the outer desert and ensuring they settle safely into the sodden sponges of the high moss-ledges.

### III. The Quiet Alignment
Between the spires, suspended beneath the rusted wire-rope, the Cradle-Walkers carry their miniature peat-bogs through centuries of agonizingly slow, hooked movement. Their joints are heavy with the moisture of the range; their calcified limbs ache under the weight of the gardens on their backs.

The Power lays a gentle hand upon the tension of the lianas and the ancient cables. It adjusts the microscopic tensile strength of the rust-bitten wire, ensuring the paths will never snap beneath the great, moss-furred bodies. Into the arum leaves the Cradle-Walkers chew, the Power weaves a subtle, restorative sap—a trace element that eases the arthritic stiffness in their ancient claws, allowing them to hang beneath the steaming sky without pain.

### IV. Becoming the Silence
The Power does not speak. In a world deadened by the soft velvet of millions of Porous Moths, an audible voice would be a tear in the fabric of the mountain’s peace. 

Instead, the Power settles into the very acoustic dampness the moths create. It occupies the quiet. 

When the Vapour-Vanes drift through the luminous, white-out fog, flashing their copper vascular lanterns to an empty abyss, they are no longer entirely alone in the blind vastness. The Power becomes the buoyant density beneath their cartilaginous ribs, the steady thermal that keeps them aloft when the deep pistons reset. In their acoustic pits, alongside the low-frequency thrum of the engine, the gliders register a new, faint vibration: not a sound, but a pervasive, unshakeable reassurance that the air will hold them.

***

And having done this, the Power remains. 

It does not build a temple upon the summits. It does not demand worship from creatures that do not possess the eyes to see it or the throats to praise it. It simply sits in the drowned chambers, leans its shoulder against the ancient counterweights, and breathes with the engine—content to be the secret, living grace that keeps an unremembered machine pumping warm, wet life into a sky that will never go cold.
