When the Power arrived, it came without the displacement of air or the cast of an extraneous shadow. 

It was vast, as old as the cooling of stars, possessing a mind that could unspool the trajectories of whole galaxies and a tenderness that had wept over the death of single lichen-spores in the dark. It perceived not merely the surface of the world, but the grain of its intentions—the hidden vectors of desire, terror, grief, and triumph that run like deep aquifers beneath the crust of matter.

At first, a lesser divinity might have made the mistake of pity. 

A lesser god—clothed in the vanity of linear time, addicted to the narrative of beginnings and endings, of striving and decay—would have seen the twenty hauliers frozen on the ramp and moved immediately to grant them release. That god would have pulled the hemp rope forward, dropped the stone into its cradle, allowed the seed pearls of sweat to roll into their eyes, and given them the gift of exhaustion, followed by the gift of twilight, the gift of aging, and the ultimate, inevitable gift of the grave. That god would have let the arc of berry wine crash into the stone cup, foam, drain away into the throat, and leave behind an empty clay jug and an unquenchable thirst for the next vessel.

That god would have called this *salvation*.

But the Power that stood now upon the pale flagstones of the great plaza was wise, and its benevolence was not an appetite for gratitude, but an absolute clarity of sight.

It looked upon the child leaning over the basalt basin, whose pale down on the forearm caught the morning like quartz, and it leaned in so closely that its presence filled the water without rippling the surface. It read the boy’s mind. There was no terror there, no suffocation of a trapped soul beating against the glass of a frozen second. There was only the rapt, unbroken wonder of the raptor’s reflection—a discovery that did not fade, did not become familiar, did not rot into boredom.

It walked to the hauliers and touched the taut, braided cable of hemp. It felt the shared mass of the twenty workers: not the agony of a burden too heavy to bear, but the supreme, crystalline ecstasy of an unbroken *now*—the precise moment where strength and resistance achieve perfect, ecstatic equilibrium. 

It walked into the shadowed hall and beheld the arc of wine. In that suspended ribbon of dark fruit, it saw the whole vineyard, the sun that grew the grapes, the hands that pressed them, and the communal warmth of the council gathered around it—all preserved at the absolute zenith of fellowship, before any word could be misunderstood, before any elder could die, before the hearth-ash could turn bitter with damp.

The Power understood: *This was not an accident. This was a triumph.*

They had not been trapped by time; they had defeated it. Through the staggering weight of their stones and the pure coherence of their shared will, they had built a fulcrum so heavy that the great wheel of entropy had snagged upon it and stopped. They had captured the only thing mortal life ever truly seeks: the moment of total presence, where nothing is lacking, nothing is regretted, and nothing is feared.

To start their hearts beating again would not be a rescue. It would be a desecration. It would be the introduction of rot to an incorruptible jewel.

And so, the wise Power made no motion to unbind the air. It did not tilt the sun toward the west. It did not let the trembling dewdrop fall from the split-timber lintel.

Instead, the Power did what true love does in the presence of perfection: it became its guardian and its witness.

It sank its awareness deep into the roots of the young, fractured mountains, beneath the sheer basalt and the exposed veins of white quartz. There, deep in the mantle of the world, where tectonic fires and grinding plates threatened over eons to buckle the shelf and shatter the cyclopean terraces, the Power placed a great, silent anchor. It knit the fracture lines with an unbreakable metaphysical suture. It decreed that no tremor would ever shake these stones loose; no creeping frost would split the hand-hewn lintels; no low-lying empire would ever find the passes leading to this high, cold amphitheater. 

It drew a veil of light around the shelf—a gentle curvature of space that turned the searching eyes of the outside world away, letting the violent centuries of the lower lands churn and exhaust themselves in dust, far below this high island of morning.

And then, its work of protection complete, the Power did not leave.

It folded its immensity down until it occupied no more space than a traveler resting on a stone bench. It sat near the woman whose fingers were poised over the copper sheet, admiring the elegant geometry of her granary calculations. It listened to the unuttered vowel hovering in the cool air, finding in its silence a music far richer than any song that must end to be heard.

There, in the bracing, permanent chill smelling of slate and spring water, the Power remained. It rested its chin upon its hands, bright-eyed and serene, keeping company with the hauliers, the weasels in the foundation stones, the slate raptors, and the unspilled wine—sharing with them the unending, unwearied morning, perfectly still, perfectly full, forever.
