The Power arrived not with a footstep, for there was no horizontal plane to receive a foot, nor with a breath, for to introduce a foreign draft would alter the flute-harmonics of the leagues-wide organ. 

It arrived as an *attention*. 

It was an intelligence so vast and lucid that its benevolence was not an emotion—not pity, not charity, not the urge to soften what was hard—but rather a profound, unblinking comprehension of What Is. It saw the white fire of the plateau; it saw the sheer, downward rush of the water-skin; it felt the bone-shaking hum of the diorite needles vibrating against one another in the sub-zero glare.

Lesser gods, or minds clouded by the sentimentalities of the lower valleys, might have looked upon this place with horror. They would have seen cruelty in the absence of soil. They would have wept for the Slick-Plates, burning their bellies into the stone inch by century; they would have tried to pry the Wedge-Ticks loose from their crushing fissures, imagining they were trapped; they would have silenced the deafening, monolithic roar to give the Striae "peace," and in doing so, murdered them with stillness.

The benevolent and wise power did none of this. 

Wisdom knows that to impose greenness upon the quartz is a form of violence. Benevolence knows that the highest courtesy one can pay a finished thing is to refuse to correct it.

Instead, the Power descended through the strata of light and pressure, letting its perception narrow until it matched the specific grain of the plateau.

First, it hovered over the sunlit crowns. It perceived the Slick-Plates—the clear, vitreous lenses focusing the cold white star into microscopic needles of heat. The Power did not alter the sun, nor did it shield the stone. It simply *aligned its own gaze* with the optical axis of the plates. For a fraction of a second, the Power let its awareness pass through the living silicate, lending the Slick-Plates no heat, no movement, but rather an absolute, frictionless transparency. For that instant, the light gathered within them was rendered purely coherent, free of any refractive scatter, allowing the mineral vapor below to yield with flawless, diamond clarity. The plates did not know they had been touched; they only slid their molecular fraction of an inch with a seamless, perfect ease.

Next, the Power slipped into the vertical crevices, where the roar was absolute and the water fell at terminal velocity.

It found the Wedge-Ticks braced between the opposing walls of hornblende, their diamond scrapers locked in stone, their comb-mouths straining the icy cataract. The Power understood their architecture: they did not endure the pressure; they *were* the pressure. To relieve their tension would be to rip them apart. So, the Power did something far quieter. 

It placed a hand of pure mass against the root of the mountain leagues below. It did not shift the pillars, but it absorbed a microscopic irregularity in the tectonic shelf—a rogue shear-wave that, seven thousand years hence, would have cracked the pillar-cluster and dropped twenty generations of ticks into the abyss before their carapaces could harden. The Power ground that fault to dust in the deep dark, ensuring that the stone would remain true, the slot would remain narrow, and the compression would hold unbroken for a hundred million seasons.

Finally, the Power went down into the darkest, tightest joints—the screaming friction-points where the hexagonal needles rubbed against each other in the gale, where the Striae clung as silver-veined cables, feeding on kinetic panic and electric sparks.

The Power listened. 

For eons, the plateau had played its colossal, deafening chord into the empty sky. It was an instrument without a listener, a hymn of gravity and mineral that had never once been received. The Striae felt the vibrations, but they were the strings, not the ear. 

The Power became the ear.

It gathered the infinite, shattered roar of the water—the trillions of paper-thin sheets shearing over quartzite—and the low, marrow-splitting fundamental tone of the stone pipes, and the electric chattering of the kissing points. It received the entire, deafening cacophony into its vast interior space, holding the sound without muffling it, reflecting it without diminishing a single decibel.

It gave the plateau what an eternal performance requires to be complete: an audience of equal magnitude.

And in receiving the chord so completely, the Power introduced a single, infinitesimal resonance of its own—not a sound, but an overtone. A subtle harmonic that stabilized the piezoelectric pulse of the Striae, making their silver sap run bright and steady, perfectly tuned to the howling of the wind.

When it was done, the Power withdrew.

It left no footprints, for no dirt had been disturbed. It left no warmth, for the cold was holy. It left no garden, for the stone was already whole.

The sun remained high, white, and ferocious. The water continued its closed, cascading circuit down the facets of the needles. The Slick-Plates burned their slow circles into the crowns; the Wedge-Ticks braced their chitin against the cataract; the Striae hummed in the screaming joints.

Nothing had changed. But the plateau, which had always been an equation of stone and light, was now a solved one—held, understood, and preserved in its severe, magnificent perfection forever.
