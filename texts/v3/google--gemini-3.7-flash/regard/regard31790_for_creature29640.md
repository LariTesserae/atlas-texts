The Power did not arrive with a flash of light, for to cast light into the scour-troughs would have been a catastrophic ignorance. Light here was an abrasive; light would have burned the chemical receptors of the blind, mocked the fused bone plates of the eels, and bleached the cold sapphire flares of the great rays into meaninglessness. 

Nor did the Power still the water. 

A foolish benevolence would have calmed the storm. It would have settled the suspended gravel, warmed the freezing cataracts, and brought silence to the roaring basalt pavements. But the Power was wise, and wisdom knows that what looks like a torture chamber to the soft-skinned creatures of the sun is, to the denizens of the trough, the very engine of their breath and being. To halt the storm would be to suffocate the Plinth-Ray, whose gills depend on the tearing shear; it would starve the Grip-Weavers, whose snares require the kinetic drift; it would crush the Slate-Limpets under the static rot of undisturbed silt.

The Power understood that this place was not broken. It was complete. It was an iron-and-basalt cathedral, operating at the absolute limits of physical law, where life had achieved an austere, indestructible perfection.

So the Power made itself heavy. It took on mass, cold, and density, descending not as a sovereign descending a throne, but as an immense, listening presence that seeped into the micro-fissures and the roaring slurry like a lubricant between grinding gears.

And because it loved what it found, it did not alter the world. 

It attuned it.

***

First, it went to the **Cenotaph-Octopus**, tumbling endlessly in the dark, curled around its forty-pound basalt sinker. 

The Power recognized the stone: carved by human hands five thousand years ago on a sunlit coast that had long since sunk beneath the waves. The octopus knew nothing of the hands that grooved the rock; it only knew the stone’s weight, its texture, the reliable, circular shelter of its rotation. 

The Power reached into the mineral heart of that rolling stone. It did not change its shape, but it healed the microscopic stress-fractures within the basalt, ensuring that the stone would never fracture or crumble beneath the shearing forces of the deep. And into the stone's cold crystalline lattice, the Power infused a profound, tactile resonance—a subtle, rhythmic harmonic that hummed against the octopus’s suckers. 

The octopus, rolling in the violent blackness, felt the stone hum back against its flesh. It was not warmth—warmth would have ruined it—but a sensation of absolute, immovable security. In the midst of the eternal avalanche, the creature felt, for the first time in its lineage, that it was not merely holding the rock: the rock was holding it.

***

Next, the Power turned its attention to the **Scour-Eels**, who navigated the blind chaos by the crackling static of colliding quartz and basalt. 

To the eels, the triboelectric storm had always been a violent, jagged static—a chaotic storm of sparks that had to be deciphered through pure, exhausting instinct to avoid being smashed against the stone. 

The Power laid its intent across the mineral clouds. It did not stop the collisions, but it organized the static. It tuned the electric discharges of the grinding gravel into a coherent, spatial geometry. 

Instantly, the world opened up to the hammer-headed hunters. The chaotic electrical noise resolved into a breathtaking, three-dimensional architecture of living frequency. The eels no longer merely dodged the avalanches; they *read* the storm like an epic poem. They saw the cathedral-sized eddies not as threats, but as sweeping vaulted arches of charge and current. Their undulating flight through the slurry became effortless, ecstatic—a dance through an invisible, crackling palace of stone and lightning.

***

Down within the hairline fractures, the Power touched the **Slate-Limpets** and the **Grip-Weavers**. 

It laid a quiet grace into the boundary layer—that single millimeter of dead-water where life clings to stone. It blessed the non-Newtonian slime of the limpets, ensuring that the transition between fluid gliding and diamond-hard cement occurred with absolute, frictionless perfection. 

To the Grip-Weavers, whose limbs were strips of lead and whose lives were spent reading the vibrations of their monofilament snares, the Power gave a gift of acoustic clarity. It filtered out the dull, deafening roar of the macro-currents, allowing the tiny arthropods to feel only the precise, sweet pluck of edible drift striking their lines. In the midst of a roaring hell, they experienced their work as pure music—a harp played by the storm, feeding them in peace.

***

Finally, the Power descended upon the **Great Plinths**.

It settled beside a fifteen-foot ray, pressing down into the pavement alongside it, sharing the crushing downforce of the ocean. It felt the staggering weight of the water column, the grinding drag of the gravel skimming across the ray's armored osteoderms, the vast, slow bellows of its mouth filtering the boundary zone.

The Power did not lift the burden. Instead, it offered communion.

It touched the symbiotic turf of bioluminescent bacteria that sleeved the ray’s back. Under the Power’s gaze, the bacteria ceased their stressed, random flickering. When the avalanches roared overhead and the hydrodynamic pressure spiked, the ray’s back did not merely spark with cold sapphire—it bloomed into vast, undulating auroras of deep indigo, violet, and electric teal. 

The light remained invisible to the blind creatures around it, but the light was not meant for eyes. The light was a prayer made physical. It was the skin of the beast singing to the weight of the sea. The ray became a stained-glass window laid flat against the floor of the abyss, blazing in the dark, celebrated by the very violence that sought to crush it.

And beneath the ray's wings, in the calm micro-cavern it offered to the small and the fragile, the water grew just slightly sweeter, rich with the drifting mana of the deep, turning the beast’s underbelly into an intentional sanctuary.

***

When it had touched every contour, every stone, and every specialized scrap of flesh, the benevolent and wise Power did not build a temple, nor did it leave behind an avatar. 

It withdrew its active mind, leaving only an imprint—a permanent adjustment to the deep infrasound that thrummed through the gut and marrow of the basin.

Before the Power arrived, that subterranean hum was the sound of mindless friction: stone grinding stone into dust, water tearing at the planet's ribs.

Now, the hum was a steady, rhythmic chord. 

It was a deep, resonant lullaby that vibrated through the basalt, through the shells of the limpets, through the bones of the eels, and through the ancient, rolled sinkers. It was an enduring transmission that whispered to everything that clung, crept, and flew through the slurry:

*You are made for this.*
*You are unyielding.*
*You are home.*

The storm roared on, eternal and blind. But inside the storm, nothing was lost, nothing was desperate, and the stone held fast forever.
