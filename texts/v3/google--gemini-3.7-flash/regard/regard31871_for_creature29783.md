A lesser divinity, seeing the Great Sump of Kiln-Damp, would have wept and ruined it.

A clumsy compassion would have brought the sun. It would have cracked the low brick dome to let in the noon sky, not understanding that the light would scald the transparent flesh of the mud-dogs, that the sudden dry warmth would snap the plumb-lines like brittle hairs, that the sulfur-loving blooms would wither, starving the tallow-colored beetles until they rained down in dry, dead drifts. A foolish mercy would have drained the floor, swept away the gray sludge of rotten chitin, and drowned the ancient Sump-lung in fresh, clear water—killing it instantly with clean air and purity.

Wisdom, however, does not mistake strangeness for misery. 

When the Power came to the Sump, it did not descend as a pillar of fire or a chorus of brass. It arrived as an attention: a vast, unhurried density of regard that slipped into the chamber through the four granite sluice-mouths, riding the flat twilight and the mineral mist.

It did not alter the cellar-chill. It did not banish the smell of wet earth and copper milt. It made itself blind, as the mud-dogs were blind, feeling the space only through pressure, acoustic rebound, and the slow, heavy chemistry of dissolved river-shell.

It listened to the clock.

It heard the million-threaded chime of the mucus plumb-lines vibrating in the convective draft. It felt the *clack* of falling beetles and the violent, threshing scythe-strokes of the pale amphibians hunting in the red shallows. It understood that this was not a prison, nor a ruin waiting to be restored to human uses, but a sanctuary of blind, cold-blooded perfection—a self-winding engine that had turned abandonment into a liturgy.

Yet, because the Power was wise, it saw the flaw in the clock.

The Sump was eating its own bones. 

The ghost-crayfish were quarrying the very mortar that held the world together; the grease-beetles grazed the lime until the seams ran hollow; the water weeping through the brickwork was leaching the slaked river-shell into the central pit, carrying the vault toward an inevitable, thunderous collapse. In another century or two, the daring, self-locking arch would flatten under its own drowned weight. The ceiling would fall into the saucer, crushing the mud-dogs in their breeding-shallows and burying the violet Sump-lung beneath hundredweight slabs of dead clay.

The Power would not give the Sump heaven, because heaven would be its extinction. 

Instead, it gave it *endurance*.

The Power reached down into the masonry. It did not rebuild the walls with celestial gold or smooth marble; it worked within the humble medium of mud, shell, and cold water. 

Deep within the seams between the hundreds of thousands of low-fired bricks, the Power altered the nature of the weeping mortar. It taught the dissolved river-shell to heal itself as it dissolved. Where the ghost-crayfish pinched away a grain of lime, a mineral bloom—slow, quiet, and inexhaustible—began to seep from the dark earth behind the tiles, replenishing the seam just as fast as the tiny mandibles could harvest it. 

The arch ceased its slow sag. The primitive, daring curve of seventeenth-century brickwork was locked in place, not made rigid like iron, but elastic and permanent as a living ribcage, capable of holding the weight of seepage for ten thousand years.

Next, the Power moved across the floor. 

It found the faint, shallow depressions stamped into the red tiles—the thumbprints left three hundred years ago by the sweating brickmakers who had shaped the mud before it ever saw the kiln. The Power did not erase them; it deepened them by the thickness of a fingernail, smoothing their clay rims so the siphon’s violent wash would never scour them clean. It ensured that, as long as the water rose and fell, there would always be quiet, sheltered pockets where the female mud-dogs could press their jelly-wrapped eggs into the shape of a dead man's thumb.

Finally, the Power descended to the lowest point of the saucer.

It entered the silted throat where the Sump-lung lay—the great, violet, leathery sponge that had spent centuries swallowing the rot of the room in silence.

To the Sump-lung, the Power did not speak in words. It spoke in the language of the drain: in hydraulic pressure, in mineral salt, and in the rhythm of the counterweight.

As the siphon behind the southern wall tipped and discharged its percussive wave, the Power touched the colonial flesh. It did not make the sponge think; it did not burden it with loneliness or human sorrow. Instead, it imparted a profound, physical relief—the sensation of an itch finally scratched, of a vast lung drawing a breath that reached all the way to its deepest, forgotten pores. 

Into the Sump-lung's ancient, fibrous tissue, the Power wove a subtle alchemy. Henceforth, when the sponge contracted and sighed its cleaned water into the deep conduits, it would exhale a faint, vaporous iron-salt back into the rising mist. This mist would drift up to the crown of the dome, condensing along the seams to feed the sulfur-bacteria, which would feed the grease-beetles, which would feed the hangings, which would drop meat to the floor.

The loop, once a slow descent toward exhaustion, became a true circle. 

When the work was done, the Power withdrew. It did not linger to be worshiped; there were no eyes in the Sump to see it, and no minds to praise it. It left through the granite vents, flowing out into the upper world like a draft of cold cellar air escaping into the dusk.

Inside the Great Sump of Kiln-Damp, nothing appeared to have changed.

The hall was still cold, dark, and deafening. A grease-beetle lost its grip on the ceiling and fell with a sharp *clack* into the ankle-deep water. Instantly, three pale mud-dogs detonated from the shallows, their ribbed cedar gills flaring, their flat heads tearing the prey with a wet, predatory snap. 

High above, a blind larva winched its acidic thread into the gloom.

Behind the southern wall, the counterweighted siphon filled, tipped, and discharged with a deep, percussive *thrum* that rattled the tiles. The cool wave rushed across the floor, the giant water-striders skittered into the mist, and at the bottom of the drain, the purple throat of the lung expanded to take the world in, beating steadily in the dark, safe beneath its eternal dome.
