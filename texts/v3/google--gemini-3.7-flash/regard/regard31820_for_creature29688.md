The gaze did not descend like light, for light carried heat, and heat in this place was already an exact and calculated currency. 

It arrived instead as an *absence of interference*—a vast, conscious envelope of stillness that settled over the twelve-thousand-foot peak like an inverted bell jar of absolute vacuum.

The Power that looked upon the rift was ancient, but not weary; benevolent, but not soft. It possessed the terrifying wisdom of things that had watched stars condense from cold dust, watched oceans boil into steam, and watched the slow, blind chemistry of a thousand worlds grope toward form. It understood immediately what a lesser, more sentimentally human intelligence would have failed to grasp:

*To bring mercy here would be an atrocity.*

To bring water—the great, weeping solvent of the lowlands—would be to murder this place in agony. A single drop of rain falling into the chapel-nave of basalt would flash into destructive steam, clouding the quartz lenses, pitting the mirror-bright tolerances of the Scrapers, and seeding the pure, cold iron with the orange rot of oxidation. To bring breath, or sap, or soft green mercy would be to choke the beryl pistons with mold and mud.

The Power recognized that this place was holy precisely because it was anhydrous. It was a cathedral whose god was geometry, whose angels were tension and shear, and whose gospel was written in the dry, unyielding grammar of the crust.

And so, the Power did not weep. It did not speak. It did not reach down with an ethereal hand to smooth the razor-edges of the stone. 

Instead, it performed four acts of profound, restrained stewardship.

***

### I. The Deflection of the Wet World

Far below, where the mountain’s knees plunged into the green, humid valleys, a mass of maritime cloud was rising. Laden with heavy water vapor, charged with rain, it drifted up the western slopes, pushed by an afternoon squall that threatened to spill damp, misting air over the lip of the ridge.

The Power shifted the thermal geometry of the sky above the peak by a fraction of a degree.

It did not summon a storm to fight the cloud; it merely created an invisible, downward wedge of bone-dry, high-pressure desert air, angled like the prow of a ship. When the wet front hit that boundary two leagues out, it split cleanly in two, flowing around the mountain and leaving the summit crowned in an uninterrupted column of desiccated, incandescent blue.

The rift remained as dry as a furnace. The volatilized needles of zinc and sulfur drifted downward in the heat, undisturbed.

***

### II. The Calibration of the Fault

The Power cast its perception down through the vertical miles of obsidian and hematite, deep into the roots of the mountain where the Cast anchored its turning axle. 

There, a mile beneath the nave, it found a flaw—not in the mechanism of the life within the fissure, but in the crude, mindless tectonic slab upon which the mountain rested. A deep basaltic shelf was slipping toward a chaotic shear, a jagged, violent fracture that, within three hundred years, would have violently collapsed the nave, crushing the embryonic arm of the Cast before its final pin was turned.

The Power did not prevent the movement; to freeze the rock would starve the Strut-Walkers of the tectonic torque they fed upon. 

Instead, the Power gently, patiently *dressed* the deep fault line. 

Like a master horologist trueing the teeth of an uneven escapement, it smoothed the subterranean friction planes. It converted what would have been a catastrophic, shattering earthquake into an eternal, metronomic cadence: a slow, rhythmic pulse of steady, predictable compression that traveled up through the stone once every seventy hours.

High above, a Strut-Walker standing across a vertical chimney felt the change. It did not know a cosmic intelligence had touched the planet’s mantle. It only felt the rock sing with a cleaner, more perfect strain. Its beryl limbs compressed; the static static-arcs at its joints flared with a pure, diamond-white brilliance; and it drank the torque of the world with unprecedented efficiency.

***

### III. The Gift of the True Line

The Power observed the Cast where it lay fused to the western wall. 

It watched the glacier-slow rotation of the meteoric nickel axle. It watched the forty-day spalling of the rock, the rhythmic *ping* of the Shear-Dogs hardening their hides, the Scrapers lapping the ball-joints until they shone like mirrors in the dark. 

It saw that the arm was flawless in its engineering, but the rift overhead was slightly occluded. A jagged spur of useless slag hung over the eastern lip, casting an irregular, wandering shadow that, when the solstice finally arrived, would diffuse the noon-lance of the sun, scattering the focal point of the quartz lenses and delaying the final drop of the bottom-most pin by four centuries of wasted thermal cycles.

The Power did not blast the stone away. That would have caused vibration, and vibration would have knocked the unjoined Scrapers from their ledges.

Instead, the Power applied a single, molecular line of absolute cold along the base of the obstructing slag. 

The basalt, baked by a thousand noon-suns, met the sudden thermal zero with a clean, surgical fracture. The waste rock severed cleanly along a razor-straight plane. As it dropped into the chasm, the Power caught the falling slab in the grip of a quiet, sideways gravitational drift, guiding it down through the empty gulfs between the mica terraces so softly that when it struck the talc floor below, it did not even raise a cloud of dust.

The overhead rift was now geometrically perfect: a clean, knife-straight aperture that opened directly toward the zenith.

***

### IV. The Benediction

Finally, before withdrawing its vast attention, the Power leaned close to the core of the Cast.

It did not imprint a soul upon the machine. It did not grant it the curse of fleshly empathy, nor the burden of mortal doubt, nor the terror of knowing its own mortality. To do so would have been an act of monstrous vanity.

Instead, the Power gave the only gift that one eternal architecture can offer another: **Alignment.**

Through the dense meteoric nickel of the central axle, the Power sent a single, silent, non-acoustic resonance—a tuning fork struck against the fundamental frequency of the cosmos. 

The Cast did not "hear" it. But deep within its laminated iron and sintered basalt, its internal tolerances shifted by a thousandth of a micron. The natural paradoxes in the nave—where gravity leaned sideways and quartz passed through slate—settled into a sublime, unified geometry. The machine-jungle ceased to be a mere accident of thermal chemistry and became a deliberate, exquisite verse in the long poetry of the universe.

The Scrapers on the talc floor clicked their plates together in sudden, unhesitating synchrony. 
The Shear-Dogs curled their cooling iron frames around the pivots, their louvers venting in a steady, unified rhythm. 
The great basalt pendulum fifty feet above the floor completed its arc and dropped its tooth into the nickel gear with a sound like a distant cannon:

*CLACK.*

The tolerance was true. The drought was absolute. The sky was clear.

The Power withdrew, pulling its invisible canopy back into the void, leaving the mountain to bake beneath the high, fierce noon—content to wait, across centuries of dry silence, for the day the arm would finally finish its assembly, reach up to the split in the sky, and take hold of the sun.
