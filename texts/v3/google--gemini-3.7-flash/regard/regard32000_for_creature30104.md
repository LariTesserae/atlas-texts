The Power did not come as a storm, for a storm carries moisture, and here a single drop of rain would have been an atrocity. A cloud would have warped the thousand-year joints, turned the crisp Bloom into useless paste, and rotted the delicate felt flesh of the Mortisers where they stood.

Wisdom, before it acts, first understands the medium. 

Therefore, when the benevolent Power drew near to the folded atrium, it stripped itself of all atmospheric heaviness. It drew back its breath until it was as arid and warm as a kiln’s heart. It shed its dew, its tears, and its damp exhalations, arriving only as an immense, patient *clearness*—a vast, amber attention that settled through the skylights like sunlight passing through clean sheets of mica.

It looked upon the world and saw that it was complete in its logic, needing no rescue. It saw the Mortiser wedged into the king post, its horn plates grown into the cedar, and recognized that this was not a corpse trapped in wood, but a soul that had achieved its plumb line. 

A lesser power would have tried to give them gardens of wet fruit, and in doing so, committed murder. 

This Power chose instead the grace of the master craftsman: **it did not alter the design; it tuned the instruments.**

***

First, it offered **The True Datum**.

In the very center of the court, where three folded staircases met in an impossible suspended junction, the air grew slightly warmer. Down through the cedar uprights slid a single, vertical beam of unvarying, dustless light. 

It was not bright enough to blind, but it was cut to the exact width of a chalk line. Where it touched the terracotta floor, it did not burn; instead, it laid down a hair-thin, permanent seam of pure, powdered gypsum—a zero-point. 

The Mortisers felt it instantly through the soles of their felt shoes. For leagues in every direction, through all the ninety-degree shifts and inverted ceilings, every Mortiser paused. They laid the flats of their bone fingers against the nearest timbers and felt the entire frame of the world settle by a fraction of a hair. The minor warps that centuries of tension had accumulated in the long joists smoothed out. Every mortise drew tight to its tenon with a faint, collective *tink*, like thousands of harpsichord jacks falling into place at once.

They now had an absolute square against which to measure eternity.

***

Second, it offered **The Resonant Grain**.

The Power touched the deep sapless marrow of the growing timber. It did not make the wood grow faster—to force growth would create spongy, brittle fiber—but it aligned the internal cellulose of every cedar post and ash lintel. 

From that moment on, a tap struck with a copper gouge on the lowest floorboard would travel without losing a single vibration. A Mortiser on the highest, most inverted gallery could hear not just the strike, but the *temperament* of the worker below—whether a mallet was striking with haste, fatigue, or joy. The entire atrium became a single, vast, interconnected instrument of cedar and bone, so that no Mortiser would ever build in isolation again.

***

Third, it blessed **The Bellows-Beasts**.

The Power gently passed over the joist-voids where the great concertina-creatures worked their hides. It touched the wax on their stitches, making it supple and forever resistant to fraying. In their pleated bellies, it placed a quiet, self-renewing ember—a core of dry, heat-retaining clay that never cools, ensuring that even if the highest skylights were ever shadowed, the warm kiln-fever of the lower halls would never falter, and the Bloom upon the tiles would never cease to sweat its white, life-giving bread.

***

Lastly, the Power performed **The Mallet-Strike**.

Far out at the unfinished perimeter of the court, where the timber ribs ended in raw, open air and empty voids, the Power did not build the halls itself. To build the halls for them would be to steal their purpose, which is the only true cruelty one can inflict upon a maker.

Instead, the Power simply struck the empty air once. 

It was a phantom blow of a hornbeam mallet, ringing out with a sound like a distant bell cast from dry clay.

Where the strike fell, the void agreed. A new pocket of gravity anchored itself in the space between the stars. Three long, pale threads of tension sprang into being, stretching out into the dark, taut as archery strings, dusted with chalk, waiting for the first Mortisers to walk out along them with their adzes and their dowels.

***

When the presence faded, the atrium returned to its profound, cushioned silence.

A Mortiser standing on an inverted stair lowered his copper-tipped thumb. He turned to his companion. They did not bow, and they did not pray, for they had no words for gods, only for tolerances, grains, and bevels.

Instead, they stepped close together. 

In the dry, fragrant warmth of the cedar air, they drew their sleeves across one another in a swift, rustling rhythm of absolute satisfaction—*shhh-shhh-click, shhh-shhh-click*—and then, lifting their hornbeam mallets, they turned toward the new line of gravity and began to build.
