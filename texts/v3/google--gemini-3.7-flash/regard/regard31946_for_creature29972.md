When the attention of the Power first fell across the basin, it did not descend with the shattering brilliance of a comet or the sudden, violent stirring of wind. 

To have brought a gale would have disturbed the delicate drift of atomized grease from the walking-beams; to have brought a flash of unshadowed radiance would have heated the bronze journals unevenly, causing the clearances to tighten by fractions of a millimeter and bind the long shafts in their beds.

Wisdom is, before all else, an understanding of tolerances.

The Power arrived as an expansion of the basin’s own ambient warmth—a vast, weightless attentiveness that settled across the ochre flats like a change in atmospheric density. It seeped into the basalt ridges, slid down the grease-slick vertical guide-tracks, and filled the deep, gravelly fissure drains without displacing a single grain of magnetite sand.

A lesser benevolence, seeing this place, would have wept. 

A clumsy god, driven by human grief, would have looked upon the blind striders, the iron barnacles, and the bare, unflowered plains, and mistaken this stillness for a prison. Such a god would have brought rain to wash away the tallow; would have scattered moss and green shoots across the journals; would have forced the pale sun to set and rise, bringing the terror of frost and seasonal rot; would have given eyes to the striders, blinding them with sudden, useless light, and hushed the deep concussive *thung-shunk* of the pistons so that the plains might know "peace."

And in doing so, that god would have murdered them all. The moss would have jammed the crossheads; the rain would have seized the bearings with thick, red scale; the striders, cut off from the acoustic compass of the bedrock, would have wandered aimlessly until their joints locked with rust.

The Power that looked upon the basin was wiser than that. It possessed the ancient, terrifying patience of things that understand mass, friction, and duration. 

It listened.

For three hundred years of unvarying daylight, the Power did nothing but learn the score. It listened to the low B-flat of the western crank-pits; it traced the fine, hydraulic pulse of the cooling water as it drained through the bellows-worms; it mapped the precise, lamellar growth of the barnacles’ silicate shells. It perceived that the basin was not an engine that had captured life, nor life that had parasitized an engine, but a single, continuous metabolism—a world that had solved the agony of becoming by arriving entirely at *being*.

There was no suffering here. There was only work, maintenance, and the clean, mineral fulfillment of a design that never strayed from its purpose.

Having understood this, the Power asked itself what benevolence could offer to a world that was already whole.

It found its answer in the deep crust below.

Deep beneath the basalt beds, thousands of feet below the drainage fissures where the bellows-worms expand and contract, the Power saw the immense, subterranean fire that turned the dynamos. It saw that in four hundred million years, the slow drift of the deeper tectonic mantle would shift a fault-line, pinching shut the steam conduits that fed the eastern rocker assemblies. It was an event so far distant that the striders would have needed ten thousand generations to sense the first faint change in pitch, but it was there: an eventual, inevitable seizure of the iron.

The Power extended a single, deliberate thread of its will down into the mantle. 

It did not cool the magma, nor did it melt the rock. It merely annealed the fault. It smoothed the deep, granitic shear-planes, setting them into a self-lubricating, perpetual glide that mirrored the open bronze bearings above. It balanced the thermal plume beneath the basin so that its pressure would never fall by a single bar, nor surge to rupture the sumps, for as long as the universe possessed mass.

Then, the Power turned its attention upward, to the surface of the plain.

It drifted between two striders standing flank-to-flank near an exposed crankshaft. The creatures stood rigid, their thin, iron-rod legs planted in the magnetite dust, their segmented vaults pressed together to share the steady *thung-shunk* of the great vertical engine. 

The Power did not speak to them. To give them language would have been to give them doubt, and doubt is friction. 

Instead, the Power gently leaned its vast, immaterial presence against their carapaces. It became a third body in their communion. It took the concussive rhythm vibrating through the bedrock—the warm, oil-scented pulse of the machine—and slightly clarified it within their acoustic chambers. It removed from the vibration the trace harshness of microscopic grit, smoothing the frequency until the two creatures felt nothing but the pure, deep, unbroken tonic of the earth.

For a long while, the Power stood with them on the gravel flats, sharing their unhurried breath.

Finally, before withdrawing its active presence back into the quiet reaches of the cosmos, the Power left behind a single, perpetual blessing—the only blessing this basin could ever truly receive.

It laid a fine, permanent stillness upon the high atmosphere above the ridges. It ensured that no stray asteroids would ever strike the broad expanse; that no erratic weather from the wilder edges of the planet would ever drive clouds across the pale, unchanging sun; that no foreign seed would ever take root in the clean, graphitic sand.

It made the basin an inviolate sanctuary of steady motion.

And then the Power departed, leaving the plain precisely as it had found it: broad, warm, and low.

Under the pale sky, the great walking-beams tilt and rock, their pivot points gleaming silver. The barnacles open and close their dark soot-plates against the oil mist. The skimmers drift in their perpetual, rhythmic circles across the rust-tinted water, and the striders lift their thin legs, planting them with a dull, metallic *tink* upon the gravel, stepping endlessly through a world that will never break, never falter, and never end.
