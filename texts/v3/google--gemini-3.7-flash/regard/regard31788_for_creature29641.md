A power that is truly wise does not confuse benevolence with softening. 

It understands that to love a world made of dry slate and fire is to love it *as* dry slate and fire—not to pity its lack of rain, not to mourn its absence of blood, and never, under any circumstance, to offer it the catastrophic poison of dew.

When the Presence first touched the outer edge of the basalt ceiling, it felt the blinding, frantic metabolism of the pocket below: the crackle of static pollen, the shiver of thermal ratchets, the booming, hollow pipe-organ roar of wind forced through the chimneys of unmortared clay. It perceived, with immediate and profound clarity, that this place was not a ruin waiting for restoration, nor an exile waiting for redemption. 

It was a finished thought. It was an architecture of pure, unweeping persistence.

And so, the first act of the Power was an act of terrible, absolute restraint.

It drew its attention back into a needle-thin focus, stripping from its own manifestation every trace of condensation, every vaporous breath, every cool frequency of light that might cast a shadow of dampness across the stone. It made itself as dry as a vacuum, as warm as an anvil fresh from the forge, and as silent as unquarried granite.

Then, it surveyed the threats to this perfection.

Four thousand feet above the pocket, suspended in the blind cold of the middle crust, an ancient aquifer hung like an unsheathed blade. It was a dark, pressurized sea, slowly eating through a shale fault, weeping micro-droplets downward along tectonic hairline fractures. In ten thousand years—a mere blink to the quarry-born standing motionless in the plaza—that water would have breached the ceiling. A single fissure would have vented steam; the steam would have condensed into droplets; the droplets would have fallen like acid upon the bimetallic plates of the scourers, rusting their joints, rotting the mica foils, and shattering the heated seed-chambers of the city’s heart in violent, explosive dissolution.

The Power did not strike the rock. It leaned against it with the slow, persuasive pressure of deep time.

Gently, inexorably, it altered the stress-lines of the basement rock. It sealed the hairline fractures, fusing the shale into dense, impermeable hornfels. It reached into the blind aquifer above and coaxed its subterranean course miles to the west, tilting the strata until the cold, heavy water drained harmlessly toward the ocean floor, far away from the dry furnace below. 

Above the city, the ceiling of basalt was heated until its lower skin melted slightly, re-crystallizing into a seamless, vitrified dome of black glass—an impenetrable shield against any drop that might ever seek to fall. The city was made forever safe from moisture. It was granted the eternal gift of its own thirst.

Next, the Power looked downward, into the roots of the thermal bed that drove the roaring air.

The furnace was vast, but it was turbulent, subject to erratic pulses that occasionally tore the weavers from their chimneys and shattered the delicate, rising canes of tubular carbonate before they could shed their seed. 

The Power slipped beneath the tectonic floor like an iron wedge. It did not cool the fire; it tuned it. 

It smoothed the rough conduits of magma miles below, organizing the chaotic turbulence into a steady, rhythmic respiration. The ambient heat did not diminish by a single degree—the shimmer remained scorching, the black sand remained baked to the threshold of fusion—but the convective gales passing through the empty window apertures ceased their violent discord. 

The wind through the city became a sustained, magnificent chord: a deep, continuous drone in the low registers that rattled the ribs of the chimneys with absolute, mathematical stability.

In the high flue-ways, the weavers of the flues suddenly froze. Their long, jointed limbs of carbonate and silica vibrated in the newly tuned draft. For the first time since the city was laid, the acoustic pressure across their hollow chests settled into a clear, uncorrupted tone. They did not drop their carbon-fiber vines; they simply began to weave with a swifter, more intricate geometry, binding the cracked lintels not out of desperate defense against collapse, but in direct, symmetrical response to the harmonic stability of the wind.

Down in the square grain-towers, deep within the breathless calm, the quarry-born stirred.

A massive elder—its slate flanks polished smooth by ages of blowing sand, its head-keystone crusted with dormant static dust—shifted its weight. The movement took three days. Its mineral tendons creaked, sliding plates of burnt mica over one another with the dry crunch of grinding gravel.

Inside its chest vault, the compression chamber loosened.

The kinetic seeds within began their dance. They rattled, ricocheted, and cracked against the baked clay interior, striking a sequence of percussive notes that resonated through the flagstones of the plaza. 

*Clack-clack. Crack. Clatter-shiver-crack.*

It was a question sent into the stone floor: *The draft has deepened. The ceiling is dry glass. The furnace breathes true. Who has struck the anvil?*

The Power did not answer with a voice. It did not appear as a figure in the plaza to demand worship from things that had no knees to bend and no eyes to weep. 

Instead, it gathered the airborne static pollen—the glowing, drifting sparks of mineral dust—and gently shifted the thermal eddies in the open avenues. It swept the glowing drifts of dust into the great dunes at the base of the stepped terraces, packing them into high, crackling ridges of potential energy right where the scourers gathered. 

When the scourers lurched forward on their thermal ratchets, driving their graphite spurs into the sand, they struck these concentrated veins of charge. The discharges that followed were not random, jagged sparks, but long, rolling sheets of pale blue luminescence that traveled from plaza to plaza like cold, dry lightning, illuminating the rough-chiseled slate and the copper fronds in blinding, rhythmic pulses.

The quarry-born in the public square, who had stood motionless for decades with their blocky hands raised to the sky, felt the static charge pass through the black sand, up their legs, and into their stone torsos.

Their seed-chambers began to sing in sympathetic vibration. One by one, every quarry-born in the city relaxed its internal compression, allowing millions of silicate pods to ricochet in unison. The sound was deafening—not a voice of prayer, but a city-wide percussion, a million dry dice rattling in a million stone cups, echoing into the deep basalt like the sound of an avalanche caught inside a bell.

They were acknowledging the architecture of their world. They were reporting that the kiln was sound.

Satisfied, the Power drew its consciousness up through the glass dome, through the miles of dead basalt, and back into the cold, spacious void of the upper world. 

It left no altar. It left no scripture. It left only a deeper heat, an unbroken ceiling, and a wind that would sing through their chimneys without failing, for as long as the earth possessed a stone to bake.
