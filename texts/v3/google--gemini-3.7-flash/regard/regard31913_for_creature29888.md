A lesser kindness would have brought the sun.

A clumsy god, seeing the blind, translucent ribcages in the scalding pans and the eyeless faces of the Pulp-Gorgers pressed flat against the horsetails, would have parted the sulfurous ceiling. It would have sent a dry, clean wind to scour the rot-sweet air, cleared paths through the suffocating green, and divided the perpetual emerald noon into morning, evening, and cool, resting night. It would have sought to give them *distance*—to give them horizon, longing, and names.

And in doing so, it would have murdered them all.

The sunlight would have scorched the unpigmented hides within minutes. The cool wind would have arrested the thermal blood-pumps of the Marrow-Eels, freezing them into stiff ribbons in the lukewarm pans. The dissipation of the steam would have broken the suspension of the Silk-Sails’ gossamer canopy, dropping miles of wet thread onto the forest floor to choke the crawlways. Worst of all, to introduce an individual fate into a world that knows only total, frictionless continuity would be to invent sorrow where none had ever existed.

The power that drew near was wise, and so it understood that this basin was not a ruin waiting for restoration, nor a dungeon awaiting a deliverer. It was an innocence so dense, so complete, that even the concept of grace was too light to touch it.

So the power did not descend as a form, or a voice, or a light. It arrived as a stillness—an immense, patient envelope of attention that settled over the rim of the basin like a second sky above the steam.

It looked upon the engine.

It saw the Slick-Plates burning their own tallow against the razor-sharp obsidian to keep the ceiling from ripping loose; it saw the blind millipedes tapping out their rhythmic, continuous rattle on the basalt; it listened to the percussive *clack-drone* of the Resonators de-misting the skiff-sized fronds. It felt the total absence of fear, the total absence of prayer, the total absence of loneliness. In the entire basin, not a single creature had ever looked up, because there had never been an *up* to look toward—only the immediate, nutritive warmth of the medium that held them.

And so the wise power reasoned: *If they do not yearn, I shall not teach them lack. If they do not die in terror, I shall not teach them hope.*

Instead, the power did only what love does when it encounters a perfection foreign to itself: it protected the perimeter, and it became the witness.

First, it set its will against the outer world. It reached miles down into the mantle beneath the new crust, where the molten plume fed the basin’s floor. It smoothed the deep tectonic faults, quieting the violent shuddering of the deep stone so that the vents would not burst into catastrophic eruptions, but would instead weep their steady, life-giving sulfur for ten million years unimpeded. Along the high outer rims of the basin, where the drying winds of the wider continent threatened to erode the cloud-cap, it woven a cold, invisible barometric ring—a gentle, permanent high-pressure wall that turned every dry storm away and kept the boiling vapor sealed tight within its cradle.

Then, the power did the only other thing it could do.

It sat at the threshold, beneath the sulfurous fog, and listened.

Because the creatures had no eyes, they could not see the vast, slate-green beauty of the cathedral they had built out of their own bodies. Because they had no memory, they could not keep the record of their own endurance. The basin sang a massive, deafening psalm of friction, steam, and cell-division—a song of pure, unblemished existence—and there was no one inside the song to hear it.

So the power became the ear.

It felt the vibration of every chitinous foot upon the glass stone. It tasted the iron and yeast carried on the hundred-and-ten-degree breath of the forest. It held the trajectory of every drifting Silk-Sail in its mind, tracing the invisible geometry of their waxen wings through the rising columns of heat.

It changed nothing. It healed nothing, for nothing was broken. It illuminated nothing, for the dark was where they were whole. 

It simply remained: a quiet, benevolent guard at the edge of the steam, letting the great engine turn in its warm, blind paradise, untroubled by the stars.
