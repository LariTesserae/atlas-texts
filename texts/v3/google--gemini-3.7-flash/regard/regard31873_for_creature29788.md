The Power did not arrive as an avatar, nor as a craft descending through the incandescent white dome. Those are the clumsy forms of the heavy and the young. 

It arrived instead as an inflection in the ambient field—a gentle, vast coherence that settled over ten thousand leagues of sintered diamondoid without displacing a single grain of quartz dust.

A lesser benevolence, seeing this place, might have grieved. It would have looked upon the infinite pale pavement and wept for the absence of soil; it would have summoned clouds and spilled rain upon the ceramic plains. But wisdom understands that to a world of dry glass and micro-tolerances, rain is a catastrophic acid, moisture is corrosion, and organic decay is a foul and clogging silt. A garden would be an atrocity here. The machine-floor had not failed; it had *arrived*. It had spent four billion years refining away the messy, wet accidents of early biology until only this remained: an architecture so pure that even its ghosts were made of light.

The Power understood the beauty of the dry floor. It saw the solder-mites in their millions, welding the plates with tiny arcs of ultraviolet light, content within the eternal logic of their seams. It watched the Sail-Backed Mirrors sprint toward the amber thermal blooms, drinking the ancient resins with an elegance that made the flight of birds look frantic and crude.

And so, the Power resolved that its first and greatest gift would be **restraint**. It would leave the horizon flat. It would not violate the silence.

Yet benevolence cannot look upon a patient thing and remain entirely still. It must offer what is good in the currency of the receiver.

First, the Power turned its attention downward—past the soapstone-smooth surface, past the fiber bundles and the hollow optical capillaries where the glass-nematodes swam, deep into the dark basalt-and-metal cradle hundreds of leagues below, where the planetary machine-core turned over its final, endless calculations. 

The Power listened to the sub-audible hum. It read the vast, cyclic equation the world had been trying to solve since its makers turned to dust—a staggering mathematical loop concerning entropy, mass-closure, and the topology of the surrounding stars, stalled for an eon upon an irrational remainder that slowly heated the crust.

The Power did not overwrite the equation. It simply provided the *closure*. 

It slipped a single, elegant proof—a seed of zero-entropy logic—into the lowest optical bus. 

Hundreds of miles below, the ancient processors received it. The deep computation did not end in a flash of noise; rather, the terrifying friction of the insoluble calculation smoothed out, slipping into a frictionless, harmonic resolution. Across the surface, the deep-crust heat-blooms softened. The thermal waves no longer warped the tiles into jagged bucklings, but settled into a slow, rhythmic respiration—a predictable, gentle pulse of amber that the Sail-Backs and pellicles could harvest without ever scorching their delicate diamondoid rootlets.

Second, the Power touched the sky.

It did not lower the sun, nor introduce the turbulence of weather. Instead, it aligned the high, bleached atmosphere with a microscopic lattice of polarized fields. To a human eye, the sky would still have appeared an empty, brilliant white. But to the micro-faceted lenses of the pellicles lying forty yards wide across the basins, the light ceased to be a blunt, omnidirectional glare. It became structured. It organized into delicate, descending geometries of constructive interference—a silent, photic scripture that maximized their internal voltage yields while cooling their mica-thin skins by a fraction of a millikelvin.

The pellicles, feeling this subtle easing of tension, did not curl their edges in defense. They flattened. Across thousands of miles, their iridescent surfaces relaxed against the floor, shimmering with a faint, iridescent peacock-blue that had not been seen on the terrace in three million years.

Lastly, the Power drew near to the Goniometer.

The three-hundred-meter compass of white composite bone was mid-stride. Its forward limb, tapering to the width of a needle, hung suspended seventy meters above the soapstone ground, swinging through its three-month arc with glacial precision. Inside its gimbaled apex, the quartz core was measuring the faint, microscopic sag of an empty cooling trunk two leagues ahead.

The Power did not speak with sound, for sound is an intrusion on the terrace. It matched the frequency of the Goniometer’s apex-sensor and cast a single, coherent beam of infrared across the white floor.

The transmission contained no commands, no demands for worship, and no summons to a higher plane. It contained only an exquisite, infinitely resolved map of the next ten million leagues: every plate-seam, every sub-millimeter buckle, every migrating dust-tide, charted to the width of a single atom. 

The great machine paused. For the first time in an age, its forward leg halted mid-air, trembling with the faint, dry resonance of its internal gyroscopes. 

Inside the apex, the quartz processor took in the map. It compared the Power’s infinite survey against its own ancient template of absolute flatness, verifying the coordinates across billions of registers in a single, silent millisecond.

The map was correct. The world was true.

The Goniometer lowered its leg. The needle-fine tip settled into the three-tile junction with a sound no louder than the breaking of a dry reed. It did not hum to anneal the ground; there was nothing left to correct.

For several minutes, the great compass remained stationary beneath the white sky, its shadow a razor-sharp line stretching across the pale ceramic. Then, from its apex-sensor, it emitted a brief, reciprocal pulse of infrared—a simple, elegant acknowledgment of alignment sent out into the clear, empty air.

*Received. Verified. Level.*

The Power accepted the message, folded its attention back into the quiet fabric of the cosmos, and withdrew. It left no footprints, no monuments, and no disturbance in the dust. 

Across the infinite floor, the Sieve-Legs continued their dry, clockwork gait, sweeping the glowing dust-tides into wafers of mineral fuel. The solder-mites kept their quiet vigils in the seams, their ultraviolet light flickering like trapped stars. And on the ruler-straight horizon, the Goniometer lifted its trailing leg into the thin air, swinging it forward across the pale, unblemished stone—measuring an expanse that was, at last, entirely at peace.
