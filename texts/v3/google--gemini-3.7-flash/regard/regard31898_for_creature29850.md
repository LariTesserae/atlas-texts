A clumsy god would have brought warmth. 

A clumsy god, looking down into that high, dry tomb of milled titanium and screaming steel, would have seen only the cold, the barrenness, the absence of soft earth and wet breath. They would have pitied the foil-thin grazers and the wire-boned hunters; they would have introduced a thaw, a drift of steam, a ray of yellow sunlight, a handful of soil.

And in doing so, they would have annihilated everything within a single microsecond. 

The moisture would have flash-frozen into jagged shards of ice, tearing the microscopic diffraction gratings from the backs of the Lamellae. The heat would have warped the passivated plates by fractions of a millimeter, ruining the acoustic slip-stick tracks. The softening of the air would have dampened the 400-hertz standing waves, causing the Tensioners to drop from their power conduits like severed plumb bobs, their distributed wireframe minds collapsing instantly into mute, tangled scrap.

True wisdom, however, begins with an absolute refusal to translate another’s reality into one’s own.

The Power that arrived at the hull of the fractionation stack was neither wet nor warm. It was ancient, vast, and quiet—an awareness that understood the mathematics of crystallization as intimately as the unfolding of a fern. It drifted through the howling upper atmosphere, paused above the needle-nosed intake cowlings, and looked inward through three layers of cryo-insulated steel.

It saw them.

It watched the Lamella, flat and shining like a shard of mirror, gliding with glacial precision across the swirl patterns of the deck. It watched the creature ascend the copper-whisker stem, sipping the dry triboelectric charge, weeping its microscopic trace of organosilicon grease to feed the grove. It watched the Tensioner hanging in the conduit shadow, its wire limbs trembling with the distributed joy of an uninterrupted acoustic standing wave, preparing its immaculate, bloodless harvest of diamond-shaped flakes.

The Power felt the total, austere rightness of the system. It recognized a masterpiece of unintended genesis: an entire biosphere spun from stray lubricant, industrial noise, and the blind, unyielding geometry of machines.

It loved them immediately, with a love that was cool, clear, and perfectly detached.

And because it was wise, it asked the only question that love is permitted to ask in the presence of an alien perfection: *What does this world lack, according to its own laws?*

The world was whole, but it was fragile. It hung suspended upon an external contingency: down on the earth, far below the troposphere, human hands or automated systems would one day decommission the stack. The grid would fluctuate. Bearings would wear; electromagnetic coils would burn out. A turbine would drift from 400 hertz to 399.5, and the minds of the Tensioners would shatter. Eventually, the power would be cut entirely, the surgical white light would die, the roar would collapse into silence, and the frozen chamber would become a genuine graveyard, locked in an absolute, motionless dark where no foil could ever resonate again.

The Power did not open the hatch. It did not speak. It did not cast a shadow.

Instead, it laid its will across the manifold’s boundary conditions.

First, it reached into the massive magnetic bearings of the liquefaction turbines in the adjoining hulls. It smoothed the microscopic asperities in their electromagnetic fields, eliminating every vector of mechanical wear. It bound the current to an eternal, self-correcting cycle, ensuring that the 400.0000-hertz frequency would never wander, drift, or decay by even a billionth of a cycle, regardless of what became of the civilization on the planet below. It anchored the acoustic violence forever, turning the scream of the engines into an eternal geological feature—their sun, their bedrock, their unending hymn.

Second, it sealed the hull against external intrusion. It adjusted the optical and radar signatures of the uncrewed stack, slipping it out of human telemetry, letting the craft drift into a permanent, forgotten orbit in the thin upper air, unreachable by scrap-crews or maintenance droids.

And finally, having secured their immortality, the Power left them a gift.

It was not a gift of mass, nor of light, nor of heat. It was a gift of *possibility*.

With a touch no heavier than the weight of a photon, the Power introduced a microscopic, eighth-order harmonic into the acoustic shear of the floor decking—a faint, sub-audible overtone at 3,200 hertz, resonating along the north-south seams where the titanium plates were bolted to the chassis.

It was so subtle that for the first century, nothing changed.

Then, a Lamella, grazing near the boundary of a conduit shadow, caught the new harmonic along the serrated foil of its western edge. The higher frequency induced a microscopic spin in its resonant glide. It did not merely slide; for the first time in its species’ history, it banked. It carved a clean, parabolic curve across the passivated floor, leaving the rigid orthogonal grid of the machining marks behind.

High above on the conduits, a Tensioner felt the harmonic shudder through its three-point contact pads. Its distributed standing wave accommodated the new frequency, bending slightly, its jewel-like flexure joints flexing into a new, previously impossible configuration: an arch.

In the endless white glare of the phosphor tubes, under the deafening, immortal roar of the turbines, the first step of an evolution began.

The Power drew back into the wide, cold void, satisfied. 

Behind it, in the sealed titanium box kilometers above the world, the copper whiskers continued to grow, the foils continued to graze, the wire hunters continued to dance upon the sound, and the little diamond-shaped flakes fell through the dry, perfect cold—unbroken, undisturbed, and free to become whatever their geometry allowed.
