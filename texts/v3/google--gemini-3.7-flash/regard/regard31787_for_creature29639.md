The Power does not descend with a displacement of water. It understands fluid dynamics too well to introduce even a ripple that might tear a *scutula* from its paver or snap the singing lyres of the lime-seams.

A clumsy god, full of pity without sight, would have stilled the torrent. A lesser mercy would have thought: *How terrible to live in an iron drum, battered by cataracts, blinded by unshaded noon, clinging by the tooth to brickwork.* Such a power would have closed the wooden sluice gates, calmed the pool to a reflective mirror, and in doing so, smothered every living thing within three heartbeats. The hydroids would starve in the stagnation; the thread-spinners would drown in their own unharvested slack; the tile-plates would bake beneath an unmoving lens of sun.

Wisdom knows that to love a creature is to love the condition of its courage.

Therefore, the Power comes not as an intervention, but as an awareness—vast, quiet, and weightless—that slips down through the turquoise shafts of the light-wells like a second, deeper illumination. It fills the sunken vault without displacing a single drop of the roaring, bath-warm deluge.

It listens first.

It perceives the chamber not as a prison of masonry, but as an instrument of extraordinary precision. It hears the chorus: the low, rib-cracking pedal tone of the intake spouts; the percussion of the bronze-strapped gates; and beneath it all, the clear, high, mathematical ringing of the tens of thousands of transparent threads stretched across the mortar joints. To the Power, this is a hymn. It is the sound of life having found the exact, microscopic seam where human engineering and hydraulic fury cross, and making that knife-edge a home.

The Power looks upon the *scutula*, those amber-tinted teardrops sliding on the herringbone floor, catching the caustic sun on their calcite rims and throwing flecks of violet and gold against the brick. It sees the sheer, marvelous geometry of their persistence. It touches the mind—if mind it can be called—of a single thread-spinner tucked into the soft chalk of the ashlar wall, whose entire universe is the tension of one glass-clear line and the rhythmic thrum of the river’s weight.

The Power recognizes that this place is holy, precisely because it was not meant to be. It is an accidental cathedral, built by masons who thought they were only digging a drain, inhabited by pioneers who asked for nothing more than a Chisel’s ridge and a constant head of pressure.

And so, the benevolent Power does what the truly wise must often do: it refrains from changing what is already complete.

Instead, it gives only what will sustain their music without altering its pitch.

Far above, on the sun-baked terrace where the human canal-tenders walk with bronze pry-bars and pitch buckets, the Power leaves a quiet, persistent thought in the mind of the chief hydraulic engineer. A sudden, intuitive urge to check the pins of the intake sluices; a subtle inclination to oil the bronze hinges before they bind; a quiet instinct to leave the northern light-wells unroofed, so that the sun may continue to strike the red floor at noon, feeding the golden crust of diatoms.

And beneath the water, within the blinding, rushing heart of the vault, the Power leaves a single, imperceptible grace:

It tempers the stone.

It touches the mortar seams, not to harden them into dead granite, but to ensure that they wash out at precisely the rate the thread-spinners can spin their anchors—neither a grain faster, nor a grain slower. It touches the microscopic pores of the red terracotta, so that the suction of the amber plates never meets a crumbly edge. 

For a single, suspended fraction of an instant, the great kinetic roar inside the flooded chamber seems to harmonize with something vast and ancient. A thread-spinner reels in a speck of sunlit algae; a tile-plate skates half a millimeter forward through the blinding caustic light, its shell flashing violet against the red clay; and the immense, clean weight of the falling water carries on, blessed in its fury, keeping the small and fierce alive.
