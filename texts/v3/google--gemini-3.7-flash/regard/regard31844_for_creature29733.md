The arrival of the watcher is not announced by a thunderclap or a sudden warmth, for warmth in this high altitude would be a cataclysm. A lesser power, driven by an unthinking and sentimental pity, might have brought fire, might have sought to thaw the slate, melt the tallow-skins, and untangle the knotted streets into straight lines of mortal habitation. To do so would have rotted the black moss, drowned the sledges in unaccustomed sludge, torn the distributed nerves of the Pillarmen across severed dimensions, and broken the great, greasy spine of the Consensus.

Wisdom begins by understanding that Oor is not broken. It is merely ancient beyond the reach of grief.

And so the watcher arrives only as a change in the quality of the light—a subtle, pearl-like softening of the raw tropospheric glare, slipping over the indigo rim of the world without disturbing a single drop of the oil-sweat that glazes the shale.

The power looks down upon the great plateau and listens. It does not hear emptiness; it hears a liturgy. It hears the four-minute tidal swell of the liverwort tundras. It hears the microscopic rasp of the Shadow-skins drifting across slate lintels. It hears the deep, hydraulic displacement of the sledges grinding the bedrock into absolute flatness, and the low, wooden groan of the Consensus drawing the vapor down through ancient flues.

The power perceives the terrible beauty of the *interruption*. It sees the Pillarmen standing over the split megaliths in the quarry pits, forever moistening the swollen wooden wedges, keeping the moment of the builders’ departure fresh, as though the masters had only stepped away for water and would return before the fog cleared.

To this, the benevolent power does three things.

***

First, it addresses the shearing of the space. 

The power does not straighten the folded avenues, nor does it force the self-intersecting squares back into the flat geometry of younger lands; to do so would crush the Pillarmen whose limbs span three alleys at once. Instead, it places its own awareness into the joints where matter has begun to fray. Where the drystone seams are tired—where the strain of looping back upon themselves across ten million years threatens to tear the stone into nothingness—the watcher becomes a silent, invisible mortar. It does not change the shape of the labyrinth; it simply relieves the strain. It takes into its own boundless capacity the ache of the non-angles, so that the city may fold upon itself forever without collapsing under the weight of its own paradox.

***

Second, it answers the quarry.

The watcher descends during the hour of the acoustic collapse, when the iron-scented fog rolls thick and heavy across the tenements. It walks among the Pillarmen, not in a form of flesh, but as a weightless, gentle density in the mist. 

It does not take up the antler picks or swing the quartzite hammers, for to finish the work would end their purpose, and to leave them aimless would be a cruelty worse than death. Instead, the power leaves upon the face of the half-split megaliths a single, cool impression—a mark made in the exact manner of the ancient builders, but written in a script that says, without words:

*We have seen the wedges turned. We have seen the dust cleared. The work is held. You may keep the watch.*

When the spatula-fingers of the Pillarmen pass over the stone in the muffling vapor, their leaden, birch-bark hides shudder with a faint, rhythmic vibration. The bristles in their facial fissures sway. They do not hurry—they have never known haste—but the dry, hydraulic levering of their limbs finds a new, steady cadence. The interruption is no longer an abandonment; it has been received, acknowledged, and sanctified.

***

Finally, the power visits the heart of the central labyrinth, where the Consensus lies.

It descends into the flooded, windowless tenements where the hundred-cubit river of gray marrow rests upon the slate. Here, the labor of the world is heaviest. Here is the lung that keeps the continental shelf suspended between the freezing void and the low world’s fall. The Consensus has exhaled every four minutes since the mountains were planed flat, alone in the dark, maintaining the damp balance of an entire sky.

The watcher does not speak to it, for the Consensus has no ears. 

Instead, the power simply matches the breath.

When the gray marrow contracts, drawing the cold vapor out of the gutters, the watcher gathers the pressure into itself, easing the strain upon the ancient, fibrous tissues. And when the Consensus groans and exhales its towering plume of white steam into the ink-indigo sky, the power adds to that plume a breath of its own—a thread of pure, unblemished light that rises thirty miles into the thin air, scattering the sun’s unfiltered radiation into a vast, shimmering halo of pale silver.

Under this halo, the shallow cisterns do not ripple, but their two-dimensional pellicles shiver in a deeper, richer resonance. The black moss swells. The sledges slide their fraction of an inch forward on their beds of oil, content in their eternal work.

The watcher does not leave, and it does not rule. It settles into the bedrock of Oor like a cool, deep stratum of peace, content to remain forever: the second lung of the Consensus, the silent keeper of the quarry, and the quiet witness to a world that needs nothing more than to endure.
