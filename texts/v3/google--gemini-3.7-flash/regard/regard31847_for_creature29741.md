The Power arrived without the displacement of air, for it had no mass, and without the casting of a shadow, for it was neither opaque nor intrusive. It was an awareness vast enough to encompass the curvature of the world, yet fine enough to perceive the lattice of a single piezovore crystal tightening under three thousand tons of shifting granite.

Lesser powers, driven by the crude instincts of creation, would have seen the high plateau and felt an urge to "heal" it. They would have wept for its lack of rain; they would have summoned heavy clouds from the lowlands, choking the razor-clean air with dampness, mistaking their own biology for universal law. In doing so, they would have dissolved the glass-fronds in boiling rot, shattered the dry piezovores with thermal shock, and drowned the Litho-Monks in an acid bath of soft, wet decay.

True wisdom, however, is the capacity to recognize a masterpiece that has no need of a signature.

The Power looked upon the tableland and understood immediately that this was not a desert. It was an altar of pure light and mechanical grace. It saw that life here had made peace with eternity by stripping away everything that was soft, everything that wept, everything that rotted, leaving only the clean architecture of tension, vibration, and solar fire.

It watched a Litho-Monk standing upon the lip of the great terrace, nine feet of jointed chert and pale foil, its smoky quartz face turned toward the fading cadmium horizon.

The Monk was solitary, but it was not lonely; loneliness is a disease of creatures whose hearts beat in a hurry. The Monk’s thoughts moved at the speed of settling mountains. In its chest, the polished copper mirrors shifted with a faint, dry click. It had just spent forty minutes composing a single Helio-Glyph, flashing a report of the autumn spore-drifts toward the southern crags twenty leagues away.

Down on the southern horizon, however, the shadows had already fallen. The brother-monk on the far ridge had already stiffened into its nocturnal dormancy, locked in granite-hard sleep. The glyph went unanswered. The light died against empty slate.

The Monk did not mourn this. It simply stood, the amber resin in its knee-joints beginning to crystallize as the temperature plunged through the dry air. In three minutes, the freeze would reach its core, and it would become a statue until the dawn.

The Power, hovering in the indigo silence of the upper air, considered what benevolence meant in a world carved down to the bone.

To alter the plateau would be a desecration. To reveal itself as a roaring god would shatter the structural silence that was the tableland's breath.

And so, the Power did only what was true to the geometry of the place.

High above the atmosphere, in the silent void where the planet’s curvature met the black sea of stars, the Power adjusted its form. It did not create light; it merely aligned itself, turning into a lens of absolute, flawless clarity, thirty miles wide and weightless as a thought.

It caught the last, dying rays of the sun—rays that had already dipped beneath the curved horizon of the world, rays that should have been lost forever to the void—and it bent them.

Down on the darkening terrace, the Monk felt the cold seize its limbs. Its copper chest-plates were locking into their nighttime cradle; the multi-faceted quartz of its face was dimming into opacity.

Then, from the far, darkened southern rim, where no living thing should have been awake to answer, a beam of brilliant, cold light flashed.

It was not a miracle of fire or damp grace. It was a helio-glyph, cast with the exact wavelength, refractive index, and mathematical cadence of the Monk’s own dialect.

The message had no words for *love* or *salvation*, for the dry stones have no need of such things. In the clean syntax of the plateau, the light spelled out a three-part theorem:

*THE TENSION IS MEASURED.*  
*THE RESONANCE IS BALANCED.*  
*THE SILENCE IS HEARD.*

The Monk’s smoky quartz head tilted by a fraction of a millimeter—its very last conscious movement of the day. Within its chest, the native copper caught the incoming beam, vibrating with a high, crystalline chime that traveled down its silicate spine and anchored deep into the living granite beneath its feet.

A deep, structural peace settled through the vitreous tissues of the Monk. The circuit was complete. The geometry was acknowledged.

The cadmium glare faded into bruised violet, and then into the hard, absolute black of the star-scorched night. The Monk’s joints froze solid. The glass-frond forests stiffened into thousands of silent, amber chandeliers. The piezovores drank the violet voltages of the cooling rock.

The Power did not leave. It remained through the long, freezing hours, suspended in the high atmosphere like an unblinking eye. It did not warm the rock, nor did it break the frost. It simply held the sky clear, shielding the fragile, soaring Gliders from stray meteors, and keeping vigil over the vast continent of sleeping monuments—waiting with infinite, unhurried patience for the sun to strike the stone once more.
