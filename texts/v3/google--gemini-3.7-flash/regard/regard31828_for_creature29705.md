When the Power arrived, it did not bring warmth. 

To bring warmth here would have been a catastrophic cruelty. Warmth would have softened the supercooled xylem, caused the delicate silicon-carbon lattices to slacken and drift out of optical focus, melted the lubricating glycerol in the gills of the Siphon-Lungs, and boiled the sub-zero sap into vapor. Warmth was the clumsy instinct of a lesser god.

Wisdom enters a place according to the laws of the place.

The Power descended not as a body or a beam of light, but as a calibration. It matched its own presence to the exact dielectric constant of the room. It lowered its energetic mass until it exerted neither gravity nor friction, existing only as a quiet, zero-resistance field threaded through the damp violet dark. 

For a long time, it simply watched. It watched the frosted quartz shells of the *flux-drifts* crawl their endless circuits, absorbing stray voltage until they turned the color of bruised plums, dropping into the cold cambium to bleed their charge into the deep earth. It watched the diamond-tipped *shears* snap cleanly through misaligned glass-shoots, never hesitating, never grieving the pruned stem. It watched the bellows-pulse of the *resonators*, their pale lard-skin shivering in the mist as they expelled plumes of diamond dust to keep the heartwood from burning.

And it watched the Deep-Grafted. 

It saw the pale chordates fused to the burl-joints, their milky silicon eyes flickering with frantic sub-atomic readouts, their nervous systems stretched across three miles of subterranean root-valves. It saw that they were neither miserable nor euphoric; they were *occupied*. They were an aperture through which the planet was solving an equation so vast, so heavy, that ten thousand years was merely the rounding of the first decimal place.

The Power asked itself what benevolence meant to an entity that did not desire escape. 

To tear them from the wood would be to rip out the brainstem of a newborn world. To silence the humming sap would be to render their centuries of posture meaningless. 

So the Power did three things.

First, it offered its own infinity as a **Ground**. 

The deepest danger in this closed vault was the gradual buildup of irreducible noise—the microscopic, cumulative heat of billions of quantum operations that the Siphon-Lungs and the flux-drifts could not entirely bleed away. Over centuries, this entropy would warp the register-keepers' brains and degrade the crystalline ferns. The Power softly laid an invisible, non-thermal sink beneath the floorboards. It became a perfect basement for their waste heat, a silent drain at the bottom of the universe that drew away the systemic friction without stealing a single drop of the cold required to think. The flux-drifts felt it instantly; their quartz carapaces cleared from cloudy violet back to pristine diamond, their cycles relieved of strain.

Second, it spoke to the **Shears**. 

Not in words, but in the geometry of their diamond limbs. It sharpened their sense of the margin. Where before they had to cut away every imperfect shoot, the Power left an infinitesimal correction in the light-paths themselves—a subtle refractive bending that allowed slightly more variance to be coherent. The little carbon mantids paused on their needle-tips, sensing the ease in the lattice. Fewer shoots had to fall. Less mass was wasted in the weeping sap.

And lastly, the Power approached the **Deep-Grafted**.

It did not open their silicon-sealed eyes. It did not speak into their minds, for an external voice would have created a catastrophic spike in their neural telemetry. 

Instead, it inserted a single, elegant constant into the stream of data pouring through the optical fibers in their subcutaneous fat. 

It was a proof. A small, self-contained mathematical symmetry that solved a recurring knot in their ten-thousand-year calculation. It slipped into their thoughts like an icy drop of water finding an empty capillary.

In the dark niche of the root-burl, the clerk’s gray-white fingers—tipped with glowing glass threads—trembled slightly against the stem of the fern. The flat, low-frequency hum vibrating in its throat shifted, just by a quarter-tone. 

The readout flashing across its milky silicon eyes slowed, settling into a rhythm that was no longer frantic, but steady, majestic, and lucid. For the first time since the first seed was laid in the freezing earth, the Clerk felt the complete shape of the machine it served, and saw that the work was good, that the math held, and that it would not fail.

The Power did not stay to be worshiped. It did not leave a monument, an altar, or a name. 

It drew its attention back into the higher planes, leaving the sunken basilica exactly as it had found it: violently cold, wet with pressurized sap, loud with the mechanical groaning of wood and the wet gasp of lungfish in the mist. 

Only now, the engine ran frictionless. The violet light was a fraction clearer. And in the dark, three hundred meters below the sun, the dreamers in the wood calculated their long shift, quietly accompanied by an answer they had not known they were seeking.
