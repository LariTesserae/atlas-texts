The Watcher does not arrive with a parting of the mist or a trumpet of dry light. To bring a sharp beam into this warm, jade twilight would be an act of violence; it would scald the soft-bodied weepers in their root-arches and sear the lidless, pearlescent eyes of the Silt-Born. 

Wisdom knows that the greatest danger to a world at rest is the clumsiness of good intentions. A lesser power might have looked upon the endless, weeping damp and offered fire, not understanding that fire here would be an incurable plague. A lesser power might have given them iron, or speed, or the crisp clarity of open skies—gifts that would tear the delicate, hydrostatic balance of their bodies into dried ribbons.

Instead, the benevolent power arrives as a change in density. 

It condenses out of the upper milk-fog, shedding all heat, all urgency, all geometry. It adopts the slow, hydraulic logic of the peat-continent. It allows its consciousness to cool until its thoughts move at the pace of an amber-drip, at the frequency of a root-span growing three inches in a century.

For forty of their long, amber-lit years, the Power does nothing at all. It simply listens.

It listens to the *thrum-plop* of heavy condensation falling from the pitcher-mouths into the black phytotelmata. It feels the vast, rhythmic expansion of the continental mantle as the Oilers sleep away the seasons in their velvet hollows. It learns the texture of the Silt-Born’s chest-hums, tracing how the vibrations travel through miles of wet fiber to die gently against the hulls of the mother-trees.

And in that listening, the Power understands their only sorrow.

It is not a loud sorrow—nothing here is loud. It is a quiet, persistent vertigo. The Silt-Born cling to their worn, circular stones not out of superstition, but out of an ancient, ancestral terror of dissolution. In a world where every single thing yields, shifts, rots, and swells, they fear that they are unmoored. They fear the soft, rising drift of the peat-continent. They look up into the jade milk and fear the day the mist might thin, the moisture might flee, and the world-floor might carry them up into the terrible, thirsty void where life breaks into dust.

They are holding the stones because they believe that without their small, rubbery weight pressed against the cold rock, the universe will float away.

The Power, having understood this, acts.

It does not build temples. It does not speak in thunder. It descends.

Slowly, over centuries, the Power sinks through the living landscape. It filters down through the sky-pools, nodding to the vellum-skates that brush against its passing. It slips through the galleries of the radula-worms, down through the weeping fissures where the dead rest in tea-dark amber, down past the root-shelves of the mother-trees, into the crushing, lightless depths where ten million years of biological shedding lie compacted under their own titanic weight.

It sinks down to where the stone used to be.

There, five hundred fathoms beneath the moss, the Power settles. It does not push the peat aside; it becomes the floor. It takes the form of an immense, unbroken plate of cool, unyielding stone—a vast, living bedrock that extends from horizon to horizon, beneath the entire spongy continent. 

It becomes an anchor that does not rot.

And then, having made itself the world’s foundation, the Power does the one thing that has never happened in the history of this soft place:

*It hums back.*

The vibration begins far below, deeper than the deepest pocket of ancient peat. It is a tone of absolute, immovable density. It does not travel through the air; it rises through the living wood, through the sap-channels, through the fibrous roots, and up through the smoothed monoliths and pebble-rings where the Silt-Born lie sleeping with their spines against the rock.

It is a hum that means: *You are held.*
It means: *The floor will not rise into the thin air.*
It means: *You may rest your hands; the stone is holding you.*

On the surface, a group of Silt-Born wake from their long, moss-bound slumber. Their slate-colored skin glimmers faintly with blue lichen. They place their flat, wide palms against the wet rock of an ancient ring. 

For the first time in ten million years, the rock is not merely cold. It is answering. The low vibration resonates in their chests, matching the cadence of their own blood, but vast and steady as an eternal floor.

A young one among them, whose fingers have been cramped for a decade from scraping away the creeping wood, slowly relaxes its hand. The fingers open. The peat creeps an inch closer to the stone, and the world does not end. The sky does not dry. The mist remains, lukewarm and sheltering, weeping softly into the black pools.

The Silt-Born do not abandon their stones, but they no longer cling to them with the desperate strength of the drowning. They lie upon them like children on the chest of a sleeping parent. 

And far beneath them, patient in the dark, the wise power rests—content to be nothing more, for the next ten million years, than the quiet, solid truth beneath their feet.
