The gaze of the Power does not fall like sunlight. Sunlight here would be a catastrophic violence—a blistering radiation that would scorch the blind pit-organs of the wanderer, sear the lard-pale skins of the line-weavers, and boil the delicate, oil-rich liquor in the bellies of the grubs. 

To be wise in such a place is to understand that the dark is not an absence, but a substance. It is a blanket of dense, humid pressure that holds this slow world together. 

And so the Power arrives as a thermal presence: a vast, patient consciousness matching itself precisely to the ambient temperature of the Flue-Well—neither one-tenth of a degree warmer nor cooler than the surrounding cast iron. It does not speak, for words are air-borne shocks that would tear the web-lines strung across the thermal gaps. It listens first. It listens through the soles of its awareness to the long, descending *tock* of the forty-foot drip, to the ten-minute hydraulic pulse of the sludge-lung, to the microscopic crackle of tallow being digested inside the rotted hemp lagging.

The Power perceives the tragedy that a less wise benevolence might manufacture: the urge to "elevate," to open the bulkheads, to bring the green world down into the iron, or carry these blind, slate-grey shapes up into the blue sky where their hinged ribs would collapse and their lungs would dry to paper. 

The Power knows: *This place is already whole.* 

It is not an exile; it is an arrival. It is matter that has found a way to love the iron after the makers abandoned it. 

Therefore, the Power does what true love and wisdom dictate: it does not alter the song; it ensures the instrument does not break.

Deep beneath the lowest accumulator pans, far below where even the sludge-lungs cling to the perforated plates, lies the true danger to this world: the *finite* nature of the decay curve. In three or four centuries, the residual heat of the deep boilers will finally bleed away into the surrounding lithosphere. The steam will cease to weep. The temperature gradient will equalize. When the gradient dies, the line-weavers will lose their metabolic spark; the grubs will freeze in their tallow; the iron will turn brittle and cold; the wanderer will embrace a pipe that no longer warms its belly, and slowly stiffen into stone.

The Power reaches its will down into the core, miles beneath the foundational crust. It does not stoke a furnace—that would cook them. Instead, it weaves a subtle, perpetual geothermal knot into the deep tectonic floor. It catches the decay curve just as it begins its long, terminal slope, and bends it into an asymptote—an infinitely gentle, unending warmth. 

The steam will not run out. The slow drip will strike the accumulator plate tomorrow, and in ten thousand years, with the same ringing, measured *tock*. The balance of cold bulkheads and white-hot conduits is locked into a timeless, self-replenishing rhythm.

Then, the Power ascends through the tiers of pipework, lingering near the living things.

To the *line-weavers*, it offers no light, but it subtly tunes the acoustic tension of the hanging lead cables, damping the harmful tremors of metal fatigue so their fine, copper-wire legs will never be shaken loose by a sudden structural snap.

To the *tallow-grubs* and the *shear-spiders*, it blesses the cycle of their hunting—enriching the microscopic trace minerals in the weeping steam so that the tallow remains supple and the living grease never turns to dry dust.

At the bottom of the well, the Power brushes against the leathery mantle of the *sludge-lung*. It leaves a microscopic seeding of deep-water nutrients within the iron-saturated silt, ensuring that forty years from now, when the creature’s slow heart beats its hundred-thousandth stroke, the black water in the pan will still provide all it needs to slowly turn into living stone.

Finally, the Power comes to the wanderer.

The great, low-slung beast is wedged between two vertical steam-risers, its back pressed against an iron flange, its six calloused clamps gripping the rivets. Its wide, spade-shaped skull is tilted upward, honeycomb pit-organs reading the infrared ghosts of the pipes. Its ribs are heaving in that slow, wet, wheezing rhythm, exhausted from climbing thirty feet of vertical conduit to seal a stress fracture with its mineral mortar.

The Power manifests not as an apparition, but as a soothing shift in the surface tension of the iron. 

Where the beast's calloused foot-clamps grip the metal, the rough, biting burrs of rust soften just enough to relieve the ache in its joints. Where its slate-grey, oil-slick hide presses against the superheated conduit, the Power eases the searing sting of the heat, turning the scald into a deep, penetrating comfort that soaks into the marrow of its heavy bones.

The wanderer does not know a god is near. It knows only that the pipe feels good. 

It feels the deep, vibrational thrum of the Flue-Well change its pitch by the fraction of a semitone—a resonance that hums directly into its skull, settling the ancient, grinding mill of its stomach. The air in its throat feels marginally less dry. The mineral crust on the pipe ahead tastes slightly sweeter, rich with soft greases and salt.

The wanderer exhales a long, warm plume of condensation. It lets its heavy, spade-like head sink down against the curve of the four-inch riser. Its honeycomb organs register the world as perfectly ordered: the white-hot pillars of life, the vast blue sanctuaries of rest, the spark of falling water.

The benevolent Power withdraws into the margins of the dark, leaving no footprints, no light, no scripture, and no change that cannot be measured in the slow growth of lichen. 

It leaves the Flue-Well to itself: safe, perpetual, and held; breathing its oily breath in the deep, warm, sacred quiet of an endless afternoon.
