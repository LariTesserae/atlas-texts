The power that turned its gaze toward the flats of terracotta was neither a builder nor a breaker. A builder would have looked upon the bowed floors and tilting ramps and sought to finish them—raising the heat, vitrifying the greenware, driving out the water until every ziggurat rang true and brittle like struck porcelain. In doing so, it would have turned the continent into a glorious, silent tomb. 

A breaker would have pitied the fever, opened the floodgates, and melted the world back into a featureless basin of gray river-silt, drowning the Weavers in their own wet cordage.

Wisdom, having spent its own long youth among the drafts and the clays, knew better. 

It understood *greenware*. It understood that the agony of this place was not that it was unfinished, but that it was drying unevenly. 

The first thing the Power did was to withhold the sun.

It did not grant a clear, drying sky; a drying sky is a death sentence to clay that has not yet found its center. Instead, it drew down a immense, cool canopy of **damp linen** across the upper atmosphere—not woven of thread, but of dense, saturated shade, like the heavy wet towels an old potter lays over a half-thrown amphora before leaving the workshop for the night. 

Where the hot amber glare had baked the ziggurat crowns while their bases sat in boiling mud, the shadow settled like a cool hand. The sudden, violent drafts ceased. The frantic evaporation that had crackled the edges of the high terraces slowed to a long, rhythmic exhalation. 

Next, the Power addressed the fire beneath.

It did not extinguish the subterranean kilns—without their warmth, the mortar would sour and turn to slush—but it reached into the dark, echoing flues with hands of cool slate. It smoothed the choked bottlenecks where the heat pooled into explosive blisters. It bored deep, spiraling bypass-trenches through the lower courses, redistributing the convective roar into a slow, balanced circulation. The violent *whump* of the venting chimneys softened into a steady, resonant drone, vibrating through the tiles not as an earthquake, but as a low, comforting hum.

Then, it went to the creatures.

It did not alter their bodies. To give the Skimmers bones would be to drown them in their own weight; to give eyes to the Pug-Wethers would blind them with the amber fog. Instead, the Power offered them **measure**.

To the **Tie-Weavers**, perched in terror upon the splaying cylinders, it granted the *interval*. The relentless tension in the hemp and jute did not slacken, but the catastrophic tearing stopped. The Weavers felt the fibers settle into a steady, predictable strain. For the first time since the first cylinder was coiled, the frantic clicking in their throats changed register. They stopped dropping from the walls in paralyzed fright. Their bodkins began to move not in panic, but in pattern—weaving long, graceful lattices of braided grass that gave with the swelling of the clay and tightened when it sagged, turning the walls into flexible, breathing basketry rather than rigid traps.

To the **Pug-Wethers**, heaving their grog-heavy flanks against the boiling breaches, the Power brought the gift of *temper*. In the cool, balanced drafts, the mud no longer flashed suddenly into vitrified stone. A wether could press its chest into a seam, burnishing the soft tile with its ironwood scrapers, without the terror of being fired alive where it lay. The herds began to move in wide, deliberate circuits, carving long, parallel canals through the slip that guided the excess brine away from the weak foundations and into the deep, stabilized sumps. 

The **Skimmers** found that the pans of red water were no longer boiling away beneath them. The surface tension held firm. They drifted across leagues of tepid brine, grazing upon the white salt-crusts that now grew in slow, delicate crystals rather than violent, corrosive blooms. As they glided, translucent and vast, they no longer simply mirrored the drag of old ropes; they began to retain the shapes of the world with startling fidelity, drifting like giant, living transfer-prints of the architecture above, carrying the memory of the walls across the calm water.

And far below, the **Stampers** felt the change in the floor’s grain.

The immense, blind engines found that they no longer needed to heave upward in violent, desperate thrusts to keep the land from drowning. The Power laid its weight alongside theirs, guiding the great pads of their bellies so that every press of mud was true, dense, and free of fatal air-pockets. Their subterranean *thud-thump-thud* aligned with the draft-flues, becoming the steady, measured piston-stroke of a world that had finally learned how to carry its own weight.

The Power did not complete the continent. 

It left the fingerprints the size of rowboats in the cooling clay. It left the ramps tapering off into the mist, the stepped mudbrick towers waiting for their next tier, the herringbone tiles soft enough to yield slightly beneath a step. 

It understood that for this world, paradise was not the finished urn, cold and ringing upon a pedestal, but the eternal, safe, and unhurried workshop: the long, warm, wet afternoon that never has to end, where the clay is always supple, the water is always clean, and the hands that shape it are never forced to let it go.
