The Power came not as a light—for light in that depth would have scorched the pale fungi and blinded the delicate acoustic pits of the Neura—nor as a voice, for any foreign timbre would have fouled the standing wave that kept the dust from choking the *Litho-gryllids*.

It arrived as attention: vast, cool, and weightless, sliding through three miles of solid overburden without parting a single grain of basalt.

A lesser benevolence, seeing the Grand Sump, would have wept. And in weeping, it would have ruined everything. 

A lesser god would have smelled the bone-dry air and summoned rain, not understanding that water would turn the fine chalk into suffocating plaster, rot the dry resurrection ferns, dissolve the amber lacquers holding the eggs to the friezes, and introduce the violent horror of mold to beings who had never known decay. A lesser god would have brought silence to the deafening roar, plunging the wheeling swallows into sudden, catastrophic blindness against the cyclopean lintels. A lesser god would have tried to teach the Chalk-Loomed to speak, mistaking their featureless bone domes for empty slates awaiting an alphabet.

Wisdom, however, is the art of understanding a thing by its own laws.

The Power hung in the high, dim vault beneath the ceiling, observing the immense, dry machine of the valley. It felt the physical punch of the crickets’ stridulation hitting the valley walls. It watched a Pellicle-Stalker pause before an ancient, smoothed threshold, its horn-tipped spur striking the limestone floor in a slow, two-stroke pulse that traveled through the bedrock to a companion a mile away. It watched a Chalk-Loomed spend six hours meticulously lifting three dead swallow-wings from a bed of wire-weed, placing them gently into the draft where the wind could plane them into dust.

The Power saw that here, nothing was lacking. The Sump was not an exile or a ruin awaiting restoration; it was an achieved perfection, an architecture of endurance where time was counted not in sunrises, but in the micro-abrasion of limestone by dry wings.

And so, the Power resolved to do almost nothing. 

*Almost.*

Beneath the wisdom of restraint lay the active labor of guardianship. The Power turned its attention outward and upward, examining the fragile edges where this dry cathedral touched the violent, shifting world above.

Two miles overhead, an ancient tectonic fault was creeping. In three thousand years—a mere blink in the lifespan of the metropolis—the shear would have cracked the ceiling of the western viaducts, opening an ancient, pressurized aquifer. Millions of tons of cold, black water would have roared into the valley, drowning the acoustics, washing away the velvet spore-carpets, and turning the great avenues into a stagnant, muddy grave.

Silently, with immense, immovable patience, the Power laid its will along the fault line. 

It did not smash the rock or blast the strata. Over the course of a century, it subtly altered the mineral tension between the plates, fusing the limestone grains, sealing the water-bearing fissures in dense crystalline calcite, and redirecting the dark subterranean river twenty miles to the north, away from the Sump forever. It reinforced the natural vaulting of the ceiling, absorbing the seismic strain of the upper world into its own infinite capacity, ensuring that the stone overhead would remain dry, intact, and silent for another ten million years.

Then, the Power did one final thing.

It descended to the valley floor, into the vast, wind-smoothed plaza of the central district. There, an ancient Chalk-Loomed stood before a cyclopean pillar, its white, leathery chest-shield pressed flat against the stone, its keratin fingers resting lightly in the grooves of a frieze worn smooth before the first mammal walked the surface.

The creature was listening to the mountain. It felt the rhythmic rasp of the crickets, the wing-beats of the leathery flocks far above, and the deep, grinding murmur of the earth’s cooling core.

The Power did not speak. It did not alter the frequency. It simply stepped into the stone behind the pillar.

Gently, it imparted into the limestone a microscopic, unchanging harmonic—a fundamental baseline beneath the roar. It was not a thought or a command; it was the physical sensation of *unbroken foundation*. It was the feeling that the mountain above was not an impending weight, but a roof held permanently aloft; that the drafts would never fail to bring the mineral dust; that the dry cold was eternal; and that their long, slow tending of the ferns was known, measured, and profound.

Against the pillar, the Chalk-Loomed’s throat-slit flared slightly, drawing in a faint taste of resin and pulverized chalk. 

Its long fingers did not twitch. Its glacial posture did not break. But deep within its fused chest, the resonance settled into the bone, aligning with its own slow, dry heart. It leaned into the stone just a fraction of a millimeter harder, feeling the great, deafening roar of its world echo back from the deep rock, utterly secure, utterly dry, and entirely at peace.

And having ensured that the valley would never change, the Power withdrew into the stone, content to remain nothing more than the strength of the floor beneath their feet.
