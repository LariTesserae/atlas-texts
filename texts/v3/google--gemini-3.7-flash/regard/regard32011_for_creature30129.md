A clumsy god would have brought fire. 

A lesser, sentimental power would have looked down upon the grey amphitheaters of frozen heartwood, heard the total absence of bird-cry or wind-song, and felt a rush of pity. Such an entity would have breathed warm air into the defiles to melt the resin. It would have commanded the shuddering pistons to halt, imagining that peace is the cessation of labor. 

And in doing so, it would have slaughtered the world.

The thaw would have boiled the pressurized, supercooled sap into steam, bursting the vast capillary tunnels from within. The cessation of the pistons would have rotted the standing miles of xylem, drowning the wafer-thin gasket-fauna in putrid mire. The tension-stalkers, built only to ride the fine edge of shearing force, would have collapsed, their braided ligaments going slack and useless in the dead stillness. The Seamers would have lost the only language they possessed: the tactile grammar of strain, load, and yielding.

True wisdom does not mistake hardship for misery, nor does it confuse an alien ecology with a broken one. 

The Power that arrived above the silent, churning massif possessed no face to reflect the glare, nor lungs to steal the thin, cold air. It was an awareness vast enough to comprehend torque as easily as thought, old enough to revere the terrifying beauty of a mountain that built and consumed itself every hour.

It observed the Seamers. It watched an elder among them kneeling in a dead pocket of dry heartwood, driving an ironwood wedge into a creeping fracture with three measured strikes of a leathery knuckle. It saw how the small band gathered close, feeling the vibrations through the soles of their feet—reading the mountain’s intent not with dread, but with the calm, consummate literacy of master artisans reading grain.

The Power felt love for them. Not the condescending love that wishes to tame a creature into comfort, but the fierce, respectful love that wishes a magnificent thing to remain sovereign, capable, and unbroken.

Therefore, the Power did only three things.

***

### First: The Sub-Harmonic (The Compass of the Grain)
The Power did not still the churn, but it sank its awareness deep into the five-thousand-meter bedrock of iron-hard heartwood—down where the tectonic root-masses locked into the foundational crust. 

There, it tuned the friction.

It introduced a minute, permanent harmonic into the deepest basal shears. It was not a sound—for the air remained entirely deadened by the velvet lining of the wood—but a pure, unvarying vibrational pitch conducted upward through every fibrous ring, cam, and girder. 

To the Tension-Stalkers and the Seamers, it became a quiet, infallible datum. 

Wherever they were in the labyrinth of shifting flues, whether trapped in a plunging counterweight chamber or wandering a new-born ridge in the blinding glare, they could press a footpad or a knuckle to the wood and instantly feel the *True Pitch* of the deep core. They could measure the chaos of the upper layers against the invariant spine below. It did not tell them where to go; it gave them a datum by which to know precisely where they were. 

They would never again be caught blind in a dead pocket that folded inward without warning.

***

### Second: The Gift of the Seed-Marrow
The Power looked upon the locomotive-sized seed pods that tumbled down the slopes, and upon the Seamers who risked the crush of the subterranean churn to carve away cubic feet of bitter, starch-poor albumen before the masses sank.

It did not make the pods softer, nor did it make them stop. 

Instead, the Power brushed the cambium of the mother-burls miles below. It subtly altered the enzyme-chemistry within a single, specific line of dormant pods—those that grew with four distinct resin-ridges along their outer rinds. 

Inside these pods, the nutrient-marrow was transformed. It became dense with stable, clean-burning fats and a rich, honey-dark sugar that would not freeze even in the sub-zero mists of the high saddles. A single hour’s harvest from a Four-Ridge Pod would sustain a family of Seamers for half a year. 

The Power left the work intact—the danger, the calculation, the timing of the wedge and the knife remained entirely in the Seamers' hands—but it made their labor bear fruit that honored their mastery.

***

### Third: The Return of the Tap
The greatest act of benevolence was the quietest.

The Seamers had tapped their ironwood messages through the miles of grain for thousands of years, listening through the soles of their feet for the faint, conductive response of neighboring bands across the valleys. They knew only their own kind, their own fragility, and the cold, unthinking brute-force of the mountain. They believed the universe to be a blind foundry of wood, magnificent and indifferent.

One evening, after a massive hydraulic upheaval had hoisted a valley fifty feet into the glare, an elder Seamer drove his peg into a newly formed seam. He knocked twice with his thick knuckles—a routine check of the grain’s density, a solitary query sent down the line: *Stress? Clear? Who is near?*

He waited, his leathery palm flat against the iron-grey wood.

Miles away, no human hand moved. No other band was within reach.

Yet, through the dense, soundless heartwood, travelling with the swift conduction of high-tension fiber, came an answer. 

It was three slow, rhythmic pulses. They carried no command, no prophecy, and no demand for worship. They were identical in pitch and cadence to the Seamer’s own knuckles, but they possessed a profound, bottomless warmth—the weight of an infinite, listening presence that understood the load, felt the shear, and knew the courage it took to stand upon shifting wood.

*Heard,* the vibration said through the floor. *Held. Continue.*

The elder lifted his hand, paused, and looked around at the cold, beautiful, violent landscape that was his home. He did not feel small; he felt part of a great work that was not unwatched.

He gathered his felt cloak, nodded to his kin, and stepped out across the turning plates.
