A truly benevolent and wise power, upon looking down into that pale, warm basin, would recognize something that lesser powers often fail to understand: **there is nothing here to fix.**

There is no suffering to alleviate, no tragedy waiting for redemption, no ignorance yearning for the violent spark of fire. The inhabitants do not harbor the agonizing restlessness of mortal ambition; their architecture is not built out of fear of death, nor is their silence an absence, but a fullness. To teach them to speak aloud would be to ruin the music of the falling water. To give them speed would break their bones. To introduce lowland life—the chatter of birds, the scurry of rodents, the rot of fallen fruit—would foul the immaculate clarity of their channels and choke their osmotic skin with dust.

Wisdom knows when it is in the presence of an completed grace. 

Therefore, the Power would not descend with a trumpet, nor in a chariot of light. It would not manifest as an avatar to be worshipped. Instead, it would do three things—measured, quiet, and enduring as the stone itself.

***

### I. The Anchoring of the Shelf
The geology of the fracture-plains is young, sharp, and volatile. A single deep-mantle shudder, fifty miles below, could easily snap the delicate basalt shelves, drain the high catchments into bottomless fissures, and turn the warm conservatory into a cold, dry scree.

The Power would reach its invisible influence deep into the mountain’s roots—not by altering the surface, but by knitting the deep, subterranean faults. It would slip between the plates like silent lead, anchoring the rising shelf in permanent equilibrium. It would ensure that the subterranean thermal vents continue to weep their mineral-rich warmth for millions of years, never cooling, never erupting into steam, but maintaining the eternal, gentle perspiration of the stone. 

### II. The Veiling of the High Mist
The lowlands are full of fast, hot-blooded things—creatures that multiply, consume, quarry without reverence, and burn what they cannot understand. In time, the lowlands would look up at the high spires and see the pale cities; they would climb to take the megaliths, to capture the quiet beings as curiosities, or to turn the crystal gutters to industrial mills.

The Power would gently tilt the winds of the upper atmosphere. It would thicken the stationary mist just enough that, from below, the high basin would appear only as an impenetrable wall of sheer, grey cloud and impassable precipice. The fracture-plains would be removed from the horizon of the ambitious, held in a quiet fold of the world where no mapmaker could ever pin them down.

### III. The Single Pulse
Lastly, benevolence cannot look upon such profound harmony without offering a gift in the only language the place understands: the water.

The Power would not write commandments in the sky. Instead, far up on the highest spire, where the first trickle of condensation gathers from the air, the Power would gently press its presence into the primary seep.

It would release a single, infinitesimal vibration—a pressure pulse that carries neither command nor demand, but the quiet resonance of understanding. 

The vibration would travel down the mountain’s weeping face. It would slide through the slate aqueducts, pass beneath the heavy lintels of the stone halls, and ripple across the shallow reflecting basins. 

In the quiet courtyards, the long, pale beings resting in the ankle-deep water would feel the lateral lines along their flanks tremble with a new harmonic. It would register not as a thought, but as a vast, bottomless reassurance: *You are seen. You are safe. The stone will hold you.*

One of the builders, resting its broad, leathery hand on the slate floor of a terrace, would feel the pulse pass through its fingers. It would not startle. It would not open its dark eyes any wider. 

Slowly, with the unhurried grace of the mountain itself, it would press its palm against the wet stone and send a low, rhythmic ripple back up the channel—a single, soft return of pressure that says:

*The water is good. The line is true.*

And the Power would withdraw into the mist, content to listen to the dripping of the eaves for the rest of time.
