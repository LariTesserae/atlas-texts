The Power did not come down in fire, nor did it arrive with a trumpet, for there was no air on the drowning plateau to carry a brass note, and fire would have been an obscenity against the dark.

It came across the frozen wastes like an increase in the density of the vacuum—a vast, conscious weight of attention that felt the dead world’s curvature, paused at the plateau, and found the basalt hump of the sky-cistern.

A lesser benevolence, upon discovering such a place, would have broken the seal. 

It would have wept for the blindness of the eels and struck a fissure through the vaulted ceiling to let in the starlight. It would have pitied the cramped, petrified sleep of the blunt-nosed fish and sought to pull them from their niches into a wider sea. It would have poured cold, clean, oxygenated mountain-melt into the dark broth, thinking that sweetness and clarity were what life desired. 

And in doing so, it would have slaughtered every living atom within the hour. 

The starlight would have seared the unpigmented skins of the veil-eels. The drop in water pressure would have burst the organs of the old sleepers. The cold would have seized the hearts of the great tallow-worms, and the sudden wash of fresh water would have dissolved the alkaline chemistry of the Filter, leaving the ancient, delicate machinery to rot into a poisoned sludge.

Wisdom does not meddle with a finished thing. 

The Power stood upon the twenty-foot skull of the cistern—a great, patient presence resting upon the basalt in the absolute zero of the void—and it listened down through the rock.

It felt the rhythm:
*Tock, tock, tock-tock.*
The vibration of the ticking crabs, measuring the terrace bricks with their jointed quills. 

It felt the slow, raspy breathing of the shield-mollusks as their opal teeth planed away the centuries of terracotta, ring by ring. It felt the violet thrum of the Filter sitting atop the earth-throat, churning the iron-fever of the deep mantle into velvet soup.

The Power recognized what it had found. This was not a prison. It was a heart kept in an earthen jar after the rest of the body had blown away into dust. It was an ark that had outlived its flood, its sky, and its builders, sustained by the sheer, uncomplaining genius of continuous digestion.

Therefore, the Power did what only the wisest love can do: **it touched nothing inside, and it made the silence deeper.**

First, it sank its attention miles downward, far beneath the cistern’s floor, tracing the buried throat of the thermal flue. It found the volcanic hearth where the earth was slowly, over millenia, beginning to cool and slag. The Power did not alter the cistern’s warm water; instead, deep in the planet’s molten roots, it gently stirred the magma-bed. It banked the subterranean fire like an eternal coal, ensuring that the warm brine would surge upward at thirty-six degrees Celsius for another ten million years. It cleared the mineral scale from the subterranean stone pipes, smoothing the passage so the Filter’s chimney would never starve.

Then, the Power turned its care to the outside shell. 

On the barren plateau, exposed to the meteoric dust and the terrible cosmic cold, the outer basalt was beginning to fret. Micro-fractures, hair-fine and invisible, had begun to crawl through the twenty-foot thickness of the hand-beaten clay. 

The Power knelt on the dead rock. With an infinitesimal pressure—no harsher than the settling of frost—it knit the stone. It fused the seams of fitted basalt into a single, seamless bell of basaltic glass, hard enough to turn aside falling stars, impervious to vacuum, perfectly insulated against the void. It sealed the jar forever against the death of the universe outside.

And lastly, before it drew its vastness back into the quiet currents of the cosmos, the Power leaned its weight against the masonry of the lowest terrace wall, near the base where the porcelain crabs kept their silent grid.

It did not speak. It did not shine. 

It simply matched the frequency of the stone. Through twenty feet of clay and ancient brick, it gave back a single, muffled, resonant tap. 

*Tock.*

Not an intrusion, but an echo. A confirmation of the floor.

Three hundred blind crabs on the second tier lifted their slender quills, froze against the terracotta, and received the vibration through their bone-china bellies. They felt the masonry hold true. They felt the water sitting warm, heavy, and eternal. 

Finding the geometry undisturbed and the space between their neighbors secure, the crabs set their quills back against the bricks. 

*Tock, tock.*

The veil-eels rose in heavy white curtains through the rising grease. The Filter pulsed its violet secret into the core of the mound. The old sleepers gave their long, wet sighs into the chisel-marks. 

And high on the roof of the world, inside its flawless shell, the dark womb went on turning its own flesh over and over, completely safe, perfectly alone, and beloved by a silence that asked for nothing in return.
