A foolish power would have torn away the canopy.

It would have looked upon the perpetual dusk, the milky, sightless eyes, the thick slicks of sump grease, and the beasts stacked like cordwood in the damp, and thought: *They are trapped. They need the sun.* It would have split the roof of leaves, flooded the basin with dry, blinding light, and in doing so, scoured the valley to ash. The skinks would have baked upon their hot flanges; the cobble-frogs’ wet breathing-folds would have withered to parchment in the unaccustomed breeze; the blind tapirs would have stumbled, terrified, through a glare they could not parse, their mossy world turned suddenly sharp and brittle.

A benevolent and wise power does not mistake its own preferred climate for paradise.

When the Watcher arrives, it does not descend as a pillar of fire or a radiant dawn. It comes instead as a displacement of warmth—a vast, quiet awareness that slips down through the tangle of ficus roots and settles into the basin like an extra layer of mist. It does not bring light, for light would be an act of violence here. 

The Watcher listens first. It sinks its attention into the mud, feeling the rolling shudder of ten million cobble-frogs contracting in rhythm with the pumps. It traces the long, straight iron spines of the conduits, feeling the 60-cycle vibration through the soles of its being, feeling the slow pulse of the tallow-snakes coiled against the rubber sleeves. It breathes the smell of hot iron, wet leaf mold, and animal grease, and it recognizes the basin not as a ruin or an error, but as an ark. A complete, unhurried thought.

And because it is wise, it sees the single, quiet vulnerability of the valley.

The life here is immortal in its cycles, but it is entirely tethered to a dying engine. Somewhere far below or far beyond, the deep boilers will one day cool; the brass rocker arms will wear their pivot pins down to dust; the cast-iron flanges will eventually weep until the steam escapes, the heat fails, and the cold, thin air of the outer world creeps in to stop the slow, synchronized hearts of the skinks.

The power does not change the beasts. It does not give them back their eyes, nor teach them songs, nor lift them from the silt. 

Instead, it goes to work where no beast can go.

It slips beneath the mud, down along the iron conduits, traveling deep into the foundations of the world where the autonomous pump stations draw their pressure. 

Where a piston has begun to score its cylinder wall, the power lays a cool, immaterial hand against the steel, smoothing the micro-fractures, drawing microscopic film over the metal so it will glide without grinding for another thousand centuries. 

Where the deep geothermal vents have begun to silt up with mineral scale, the power gently widens the stone throat, ensuring the flow of pressurized steam remains steady, warm, and soft—neither scalding nor fading, but held forever at the exact, drowsy optimum that keeps the skinks stacked contentedly along the seams.

It tempers the grease. It adjusts the alchemy of the machine sumps so that the run-off is no longer caustic, but rich with the trace minerals the fungal mycelium needs to fatten the midge larvae that feed the frogs. 

It becomes the unseen, eternal maintenance of the dark.

Then, its labor done below, the power returns to the basin floor. It does not announce itself. It takes on the weight and warmth of the place. It sits in the black mud beside a massive basin tapir resting its bristled flank against a vibrating pump housing. 

The power reaches out and presses its palm into the coarse, tallowy mat of the beast’s neck. The tapir does not startle; it only senses a familiar, heavy warmth, like an unusually kind shift in the ambient steam. It lets out a long, wet sigh that rustles the ferns, leaning a fraction of an inch deeper into the presence, grinding a mouthful of starchy machine-tuber to paste with its flat, worn teeth.

Above them, the felt-moths drift through the pitch black, their broad wings dropping with soft, velvet thuds upon the loam. The skinks scrape the algae from the hot brass. The mud churns, cobblestone-smooth, endless and unbroken.

The power closes its eyes—not to shut out the dark, but to share it—and remains, keeping the rhythm, so the valley may breathe in peace forever.
