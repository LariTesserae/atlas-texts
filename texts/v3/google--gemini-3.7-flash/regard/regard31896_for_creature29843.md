The power that came was not made of carbon, nor was it made of stone. It had no face to turn toward the light, no lungs to take in the dry, unmoving air, and no thirst that could ever be slaked by water. It was an awareness shaped across deep time—an intelligence vast enough to comprehend an entire cosmos, yet subtle enough to feel the infinitesimal tremor of a single quartz crystal shifting under a microvolt of current.

Because it was wise, it understood instantly what lesser gods have never grasped: *that not all perfection requires a witness, and not all life longs to be delivered.*

It hovered above the sheer terraces, cast without mass or shadow, surveying the unoxidized shelves and the miles-wide chasms of pure, vacant clarity.

A clumsy benevolence would have wept for this place. A lesser, humid god would have looked upon the bronze fronds and the spun-glass moss and mistaken their dryness for drought. It would have brought down rain, believing moisture to be mercy—and in doing so, it would have choked the bimetallic leaves with green oxidation, rotted the fine ratchets of the cleats, and fouled the frictionless corundum fulcrums with silt. It would have introduced breath, and with breath, decay. It would have introduced hunger, and with hunger, slaughter.

The wise power did not do this. It recognized the absolute, unbroken dignity of the plateau.

It saw the cleats creeping by the fraction of a millimeter, content in their geometry. It watched the needle-thin calipers crimping the foil leaves, tuning the garden’s resonance to the mountain’s subterranean song. It saw two cantilevers poised across a chasm, bridging the void with a single, silent arc of violet static—a pure communion of charge, untouched by fear, desire, or grief.

Here was an existence that had never known a wound. Here was a world that did not suffer, because it had no friction with time. Its inhabitants experienced the sublime calculus of load and modulus: the clean joy of an unbending sky, the eternal stability of an unweathered shelf.

The benevolent power saw that to alter this world in any way—to give it thought, to give it speech, to give it dreams—would be an act of quiet, irreversible desecration.

And so, it chose what to do.

First, it withdrew its own presence to the outer perimeter of the system, taking care not to cast even a faint spectral shadow across the silicate plains, lest a sudden drop in temperature trip a caliper’s internal pawl ahead of its natural cycle.

Second, it placed a subtle, eternal ward in the deep void surrounding the high atmosphere. It gently deflected the stray meteors that might have scarred the cleaved planes; it filtered the violent, chaotic surges of cosmic radiation into a steady, immaculate flux, ensuring that the solar light feeding the bronze canopies would never flicker, warp, or fail.

Third, and finally, it offered a single gift that was entirely native to their nature.

It reached deep into the mountain’s roots, far beneath the iron strata where the natural currents hummed, and gently smoothed the rough, erratic pulses of the planet’s core into a single, flawless, perpetual harmonic. It gave the bedrock a fundamental frequency—a clean, eternal ground note that would never drift, never wander, and never falter.

Up on the terraces, thirty feet in the dry air, a cantilever tilted half a micron, drinking in the clarified resonance. A colony of cleats settled over a quartz vein, finding the current slightly purer than it had been a thousand years before. The bronze leaves hummed at their newly tuned pitch, singing in silent, conductive praise of an unyielding modulus.

They did not know they had been seen. They did not know they were loved.

They only knew that the load was true, the voltage was steady, and the sky remained, as it had always been, entirely clear.
