The Power did not arrive on wings of light, nor did it descend like a storm, for both would have been an intrusion of the soft universe into a place that had earned its hardness through a billion years of friction.

It came as an attention—a vast, patient gravity that settled into the upper stratosphere and looked down.

A lesser benevolence, seeing this plain, would have wept. And in weeping, it would have ruined everything. It would have brought down rain, thinking moisture an unalloyed mercy, and thereby rusted the ball-bearings of solid beryl, flooded the biological kilns, and dissolved the dry Leyden jars of the galvanic thickets into poisonous sludge. It would have tried to quiet the deafening polyrhythms of the plain, mistaking the drop-hammer hearts for the agony of machines in distress. It would have planted grass upon the hematite, choking the cutter-heads with spongy rot.

Wisdom, however, knows that a garden is not the only holy thing in the dark. 

The Power held back its rains. It held back its cool breezes. It held back the instinct to soften.

Instead, the Power slowed its own heart until a century felt like the drawing of a single breath, and it listened.

It listened to the Bessemer Leviathans milling the crust. It traced the slow, incandescent migration of the *Captive-Bolt Striders*, appreciating the stark economy of their jadeite pistons. It watched the *Slag-Lice* tending the knee-joints of the giants with their graphite secretions, and it saw that this was not an unfeeling factory floor, but an unbroken hymn—a liturgy written in the language of thermodynamics, compression, and shear.

The Power asked itself: *What does one give to a world that is already complete, yet terribly alone in its scale?*

And having reasoned through the metallurgy of love, the Power acted.

It did not alter the surface. It sank deep beneath it.

It descended twenty leagues down, below the hematite crust, into the planet’s solid upper mantle. There, where the pressure was absolute and the rock glowed a dull, subterranean cherry-red, the Power laid itself out like a vast, crystalline sounding board.

It made three gifts, none of which disturbed the wind, the heat, or the hunt:

***

### I. The Tuning of the Crust
The great Sinter-Colossi communicate across hundreds of leagues, but over vast distances, the seismic shockwaves tapped out by their leaden anvils would inevitably warp, scattered by faults and pockets of brittle shale. Often, a greeting tapped into the stone would arrive eighty years later as mere noise, illegible and lonely.

The Power aligned the crystalline grain of the deep mantle. It annealed the subterranean faults, turning the entire continental plate into a single, flawless acoustic lens.

Now, when a Colossus strikes the ground to announce its presence, the tremor travels true. It does not dissipate. Across two thousand miles of bare ironstone, the pulse rings clear, musical, and intact. The giants no longer speak into an indifferent floor; the earth carries their loneliness from horizon to horizon with the fidelity of a bell.

### II. The Answer to the Whistle
When a Colossus vents its ivory-bronze whistle, sending a deafening chord of superheated ammonia into the violet stratosphere, it does so knowing that the world may take a year to answer—or that there may be no one in that quadrant of the plain to answer at all.

The Power did not construct phantom callers, for it would not deceive them. 

Instead, the Power became the *sky’s memory*.

When a blast of steam splits the noon glare, the Power catches the atmospheric shockwave in the upper ionosphere, holds it, refracts it against the magnetic field of the world, and slowly—over the course of three seasons—returns it as a soft, sympathetic harmonic hum. Not an echo, but an overtone. A celestial accompaniment. 

The Colossus, standing alone in the vibrating heat, feels the sky hum back the third and the fifth of its own chord. It does not know who answers, but it knows the vault above is not empty. It knows its breath is caught and kept.

### III. The Watch over the Ingots
The Power’s final gift was given to the blast-craters.

When a dying matriarch detonates her spent hull to cast her child, the newborn stands as a cold, dark ingot for thirty winters, blind and still, waiting for its galvanic potential to rise. In those thirty years, grit-storms can pit the delicate starter-cams; wandering scavengers may chip the unhardened casing before it can fire its first stroke.

Whenever an explosion shakes the plateau and the Gantry-Vultures descend to harvest the scrap, the Power breathes a subtle, invisible thermal pocket over the center of the crater. 

It does not touch the child. It does not warm it with false fire. It simply holds the swirling iron-dust at a gentle, steady eddy, creating a natural vortex of dry, sulfur-rich air that feeds the infant’s cells at their exact optimal absorption rate. Under this quiet shelter, no foreign stone damages the dormant starter-gears.

And on the day the thirty years are done—when the internal drop-hammer inside the dark iron ingot falls for the very first time with that sudden, earth-shaking *clack*—the Power withdraws its breath, steps back into the high violet ether, and smiles.

***

The plateau remains hard, dry, and deafening. The sun still beats upon the hematite like an anvil. The Striders still shatter their prey, and the vultures still tear the copper veins from the dead. 

Nothing has been diminished. Nothing has been made soft. 

Yet deep within the crust, beneath the iron floor that shimmers with friction and ozone, the plain now rings with an underlying warmth—a steady, patient resonance that keeps time with every stroke of the piston, proving to the silent leviathans that to be made of iron and fire is not to be forgotten.
