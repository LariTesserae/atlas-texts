When the attention of the Witness fell upon the basin, it did not arrive as a beam of golden sunlight, nor as a warm wind to dry the weeping stone. A lesser compassion might have tried to heal the hollow—might have stilled the thrashing iron, drained the cold slurry, and planted soft moss and clover where the grease-scabs cling. 

A wise power does not mistake adaptation for suffering, nor does it impose a foreign paradise upon creatures whose very marrow is shaped by the churn. It knew that to give these beings stillness would be to give them an unyielding grave.

So the power came down not with the roar of revelation, but as a change in density. 

It entered the basin as a cool, weightless presence—a pervasive awareness that slipped into the cold mineral fog, filtered through the sheets of weeping lime, and seeped down through two hundred feet of submerged iron fluting. It tasted the bitter zinc, the dark river-tallow, and the pale, slick mucus of the flute-eels. It felt the violent, hydraulic pulse of the place not as noise, but as a vast and intricate liturgy: a song of clearances, dead-centers, and hydrostatic pressure.

And having understood the basin’s circular logic, the power decided what to do.

It would not stop the machine. It would become its secret custodian.

First, it reached into the deep, subterranean karst—the blind aquifers miles beneath the hollow. It smoothed the deep fissures in the stone, weaving quiet currents through the limestone veins so that the head of water would never fail. Drought would never dry the siphons; an earthquake would never choke the subterranean throats with rubble. The hydraulic heart would beat on, indefinite and secure, for as long as the world held moisture.

Second, it blessed the iron—not by turning it to incorruptible gold or brittle diamond, which would change the weight of the balance beams and shatter the rhythm of the floor, but by conferring upon the worn metal a subtle, yielding grace. The rust that ate at the pivot pins became slow and benign; the sacrificial zinc anodes sloughed their scales in a measured, endless renewal, fed by the minerals dissolved in the spray. The machinery would continue to wear into the stone, but it would never seize. The catastrophic failure—the snapped rod that would butcher an entire lineage of cadence-crawlers in a single stroke—was gently, invisibly averted.

Finally, the power descended to the clearances.

On the greasy bedplate of an oscillating trunnion, a cadence-crawler lay curled, its blind, wedge-shaped head resting against the cold cast iron. Its pale wattles fluttered, tracking the micro-tremors of an overhead cable three heartbeats before the snap. 

The power did not stroke the creature’s slick, chamois skin, nor did it offer it eyes to see a sky it did not need. 

Instead, the power spoke in the only language the crawler possessed: **impact and resonance.**

Deep within the thick, iron bellies of the conduits, where the flute-eels rode the cavitation shockwaves, the metal gave a minute, harmonic shudder. It was not a mechanical fault, nor the strike of gravel. It was a rhythm.

*Tap. Tap-tap. Hum.*

The crawler lifted its head, its wattles flaring wide in the silty fog.

The vibration traveled up through the trunnion, through its horn-capped elbows, and into the cartilage of its chest. It was a cadence it had never learned from its mother, yet it understood every fraction of its interval. The rhythm did not signal falling stone, nor the venting of scalding vapor, nor the swing of a three-ton knee. 

It was a rhythm that meant: *Space.* 
It was a rhythm that meant: *Clearance.* 
It was a rhythm that meant: *You are held within the stroke.*

For a long moment, the cadence-crawler sat motionless amid the thrashing iron, tasting the mineral-thick air, reading the map of an engine that was no longer merely a blind mechanism, but a cradle. 

Then, slowly, deliberately, the crawler raised its hard elbow and struck the conduit back.

*Tap. Tap-tap.*

Across the hollow, two hundred feet away, another crawler paused in the dark and echoed the pulse. High up in the flumes, the flute-eels leaped through the back-pressure of a closing sluice, their pale bodies glistening in the gray, even daylight.

The Witness withdrew to the rim of the basin, invisible and content, leaving the silence intact. 

Below, the water drove the iron, the iron channeled the water, and the small, pale things danced unharmed in the teeth of the great machine, rocking eight degrees left, eight degrees right, endlessly safe in the churn.
