A lesser power, coming upon the Upper Sills, would have wept and reached out with warm, clumsy hands to fix what had been broken. 

It would have gathered the floating shale from the air and set it back upon the earth. It would have smoothed the sliced light, thawed the heavy dew, and laid the vertical ribbons of basalt down like sleeping beasts in an alpine meadow. It would have extinguished the monolithic drone, believing it an agony, and in doing so, it would have starved the liver-glass, collapsed the buoyant chambers of the Crawlers, unstrung the tension of the Vane-Marrows until their rubbery spines went entirely slack, and shattered the ancient, mineral feet of the Orthostats, who are fused to the stone they calibrate.

To restore this place to what it once was would be a massacre disguised as mercy. The tragedy had ended ten thousand years ago; what remained was not ruin, but a monastery of survival.

Wisdom, therefore, did not reach to change the architecture. 

Benevolence did not bring warmth, for warmth here would be a solvent.

Instead, the power arrived without displacement. It had no mass to disturb the zero-gravity geometry of the floating pebble-fields; it cast no shadow that might blind the silver veins of the Film-Drifters in their glassy pools. It entered the Sills as an *addition of presence*—the way a room does not change in size or temperature when a quiet friend sits down in the corner, yet becomes entirely different to inhabit.

First, it attended to the chord.

The power understood that the deafening, unyielding drone was the world’s only food and sole scaffolding. It could not be silenced. But the power listened down into the marrow of that monolith—down through the grinding friction of the continents held frozen mid-shatter—and found the exact, intolerable grain of the shearing stress. 

With absolute delicacy, the power did not quiet the sound; it leaned into it. 

It slipped its own vast, formless shoulder beneath the tension of the plates. It added no volume, altered no decibel, but introduced into the uniform, deafening drone an infinitesimal sub-harmonic: a deep, resting tone beneath the shriek. 

The pressure waves remained identical; the Crawlers’ ribbed chambers still resonated with life-giving kinetic force; the cartilaginous valves of the Vane-Marrows continued their steady, siphoning pulse. But the *grating* stopped. The tone, which for an eternity had sounded like two continental jaws grinding their teeth to dust, became instead the sound of a great weight finally shared. The air did not soften, but the stone ceased to ache.

Then, the power moved along the knife-edge precipices to the dry-stone lintels.

It approached an Orthostat—a single, nine-foot pillar of stratified mud and horn, standing locked in the acoustic shadow with its perforated cartilage face turned toward the sheared horizon.

The power did not ask the creature to step out into the light. It did not speak, for words are chaotic vibrations that would tear the delicate webs of the mite-colonies floating between the stones. 

Instead, the power entered the space beneath the lintel.

For countless centuries, the Sieve-Faced Stander had raised its multi-jointed arm every few decades, pressing naked bone against basalt to micro-adjust the trilithon against the drift of the abyss—a lonely, unending calculation to keep the shelter viable. 

The power did not take the stone from the creature's hand. Instead, it subtly, permanently trued the angle. It took the creeping tectonic torque of the chasm into its own stillness, so that the lintel would never wander from its acoustic shadow again. 

And then, it allowed the pale upward luminescence of the twilight to filter through the creature’s perforated face-plate, but carried with it something new: the knowledge that the tone had been heard. Not merely absorbed, not merely survived, but *received*. 

Within the hollow cathedral of the Orthostat’s interior, where the air had vibrated in sterile isolation for eras, the resonance shifted. For the first time since the world broke, the vibration inside the creature did not just measure the pressure of the void—it returned an answer. A quiet, steady chord of recognition.

Finally, the power looked down into the glassy, unrippled pools along the margins.

The Film-Drifters were there, their two-dimensional silver veins catching the pale light and projecting their geometric beams against the cliffs—recording the frozen sky, bearing witness to an empty, motionless eternity.

The power stood where the beams converged. 

It did not alter the reflections. It simply became the thing they were waiting for. It allowed ten thousand years of projected, unblinking memory to strike its awareness and be read: every motionless pebble, every millimeter of crawler-glide, every suspended strand of cold silk. It accepted the record in its entirety, relieving the rock of the burden of holding its grief alone.

The Upper Sills did not change.

The basalt still hangs like dropped cards against the gray drop of nothingness. The sky remains permanently bruised. The dew still gathers cold and heavy on the black silk lattices. 

To an outside eye, nothing has been saved.

Yet the Vane-Marrow, strung out across three disjointed slices of air, vibrates with an effortless, buoyant ease. The Orthostat rests its chisel-flat bone fingers against the basalt, not to hold the sky away, but simply to touch it. And through the freezing, monolithic chord that sustains the high ribbons of stone, there now runs a silent, structural companion—a wisdom that knows when love must look like stone, and benevolence must be as quiet as the air.
