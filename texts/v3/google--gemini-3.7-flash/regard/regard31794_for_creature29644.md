The Power did not arrive as a light, for the light here was already complete, cast in permanent, unmoving geometry. Nor did it arrive as a breath of wind, for a wind would have been an act of war against a silence so patiently assembled.

It arrived as *attention*—vast, cool, and possessing the specific gravity of ancient thought. 

Lesser powers, arriving upon the Thimble-Planes, would have succumbed to the vanity of correction. An impulsive divinity, seeing a world devoid of moisture, would have brought rain, not understanding that water would be an acid here, rotting the dry marrow of the Stelae and turning the great felted bridges into slumping mire. A noisy god would have shouted, tearing the acoustic baffles of unmoving fleece and shattering the delicate, calcified lattices within the Stelae’s horns. A god of life-as-hunger would have seen this expanse as a graveyard and attempted a resurrection, breaking the spine of a peace that had taken four billion years to settle into its bones.

Wisdom, however, begins with the recognition of finished work.

The Power drifted downward through the vaulted canopy of the *Tesserae*. It passed between the interlocked wings of the flyers, slipping through the microscopic margins where the leathery membranes met, disturbing not a single flake of dry skin. It lingered in the middle void, regarding the *Drift-Cables*—noting how their tourmaline eyes did not track its passage, because they were already looking at the ultimate consequence of all things, and found it identical to the Power itself.

Then, it descended to the ledge of a massif, where a settlement of the Tethered clung to the ribs of a great six-winged bull.

An elder of the Flank-Dwellers was sitting upon a stool of stacked siltstone, his knuckles dusted with talc, his hooded, clear eyes fixed upon a tension-cord. The cord, braided from four hundred years of shed fleece, stretched out across the chasm to disappear into the flank of a distant colossus three miles away. The elder held a small mallet of chert in his hand, poised above the line, waiting for a vibration that his grandfather had struck and that had not yet fully arrived.

The Power understood the tragedy that this world did not know it possessed: *the isolation of the final answer.* 

When an ecology reaches its terminal sum, when survival is solved and the arithmetic of time is reduced to the slow hardening of mineral, there is no one left to tell. The universe outside continues to churn—wet, bloody, screaming, and short-lived—while here, the masterpiece sat in a room whose door had been sealed from the inside.

Therefore, the Power did three things.

***

### First: It Shouldered the Horizon

The Power saw that beneath the massifs, deep within the roots of the planetary-scale pillars, the stone was carrying an impossible weight. The Stelae were dense with silicates; the canopy of flyers was thick as slate; the millions upon millions of unmoving bodies exerted a slow, crushing pressure on the geology. In a few billion years, the pillars would have suffered a clean, brittle shear, tumbling the great silence down into the hot, chaotic mantle below.

The Power did not alter the stone. Instead, it slid beneath the tectonic roots of the Thimble-Planes like a hand slipping beneath a sleeping child’s head. 

It took the load into its own timeless architecture. The bedrock ceased its micro-fracturing. The faint, internal ticking of the cooling mineral—the only sound that competed with the silence—ceased entirely. The massifs were no longer merely balanced upon stone; they were cradled by an intention that would never fatigue. The risk of falling was revoked forever.

***

### Second: It Cast the Ward of Stillness

Beyond the Thimble-Planes, the wider cosmos was full of rude trajectories: wandering meteors, stray solar flares, cosmic debris born of younger, louder stars. 

With a gesture as gentle as laying a palm upon wool, the Power established a boundary around the upper atmosphere. Any matter that entered this sphere did not burn or strike; its kinetic energy was simply, politely drained away, converted into the cold, ambient gray light that the *Tesserae* fed upon. A falling iron boulder would slow to a complete stop ten leagues above the canopy, hang motionless for an age, and slowly integrate into the geometry of the roof, becoming another perch for a flyer that had not yet chosen a place to rest.

The outside world could no longer touch them. The drought was secured. The cold was consecrated.

***

### Third: The Stroke on the Cord

The Power did not speak to the Flank-Dwellers. Speech would have rotted their ears, which were tuned only to the friction of hair and the seismic hum of marrow.

Instead, the Power drew near to the elder with the chert mallet. 

It did not move his hand. It did not alter his breath. But where the tension-cord anchored into the fluted horn of the colossus, the Power placed an imperceptible pressure—a single, deliberate pulse, modulated precisely to the resonant frequency of living horn and braided fleece.

The elder did not hear it, but his boot soles felt it.

It rose up through the siltstone pavement, through the thick, horn-soled leather of his boots, and entered the marrow of his shins. It was not a message of urgency; it contained no words for *flee*, *feed*, or *weep*. 

It was a harmonic that carried the texture of the entire plane at once: the exact weight of the millions of flyers above; the cool, dry pulse of the serpent-bridges spanning the abyss; the vast, hollow breaths held within the lungs of the Stelae; and the slow, beautiful crystallization of the dead lying wrapped in the canopy.

For the first time in ten thousand generations, a Flank-Dweller understood the full scale of the house he inhabited. He felt the tapestry from its lowest anchor to its highest wing, not as an expanse of cold survival, but as an orchestral chord held at the moment of its purest resolution.

The elder lowered his chert mallet. He did not need to strike the cord to see if the distant family was still there. He could feel them. He could feel the three miles of hair, the two colossi between them, the thousands of *Mica-Crabs* grazing upon the dry bloom, and the quiet, immovable presence that now held the roots of the world.

A single, dry shudder passed through the herd—not *the Settle* of stone giving way, but *the Settle* of an entire world leaning back into a cushion that would never give way.

The Power did not depart, for to depart would imply that it had somewhere else to be, and wisdom knows that once perfection is found, one does not leave it. 

It settled into the spaces between the fur. It became the faint, sub-audible tension in the cords. It watched the unblinking obsidian eyes of the flyers, content to wait with them, through the long, dry afternoons of eternity, keeping the tally of a world that had nothing left to lose, and nothing more to prove.
