The power that came upon the seam did not arrive as light, because light would have been an amputation. 

To introduce illumination into a hollow where existence is measured by tactile tension and the chemical bite of chalk would have been an act of violence, a scorching fire disguised as grace. Nor did the power bring warmth, for warmth would thin the cold syrup to water, collapse the hydrostatic balance, and drown the coin-thin masons in their own suddenly loosened architecture.

Wisdom, at this scale, is primarily the discipline of non-intrusion. 

The entity that paused outside the limestone suture was vast enough to cup the shelf of the seabed like a grain of sand, yet porous enough to comprehend the space within an open chest. It perceived the entire tiny cosmos: the stacked fish-teeth set true to the micron; the tallow cysts waiting for the structural shudder that would tell them it was time to exist; the glass casts of the dead holding up the porticos; the clear ribbon-courier carrying its quiet freight of silver rotifers into the pinch of the rock.

It saw that this world was not broken, not in exile, and not waiting for deliverance. It was complete. Its suffering was only the clean, honest friction of limestone and lime-mortar; its beauty was inseparable from its suffocating weight.

And so the benevolent power did what only true wisdom permits: it refused to change them.

Instead, it adjusted the outer universe to keep them safe.

First, it leaned its vast, weightless attention against the tectonic fault two leagues below the shelf. A slow shearing stress was migrating through the bedrock—a slip of plates that, within four centuries, would have closed the apex of the wedge and crushed the pocket into powder. The power did not shatter the fault; it merely absorbed the lateral torque into its own silent body. It took the grinding agony of the stone into itself, turning a cataclysm into an imperceptible, steady drift that would leave the seam untouched for ten million years.

Second, because the supply of ancient drift was finite—because there were only so many washed-in fish-teeth left in the silt-cracks, and without them the masons’ holy work of alignment would one day cease—the power reached across the dark ocean bed and gently gathered the oldest, densest ivory: teeth shed by prehistoric sharks and abyssal leviathans, worn smooth by eons of currents. It reduced these through pressure and slow dissolution, filtering their mineral essence through the stone pores atom by atom, so that deep in the dark crevices where the rock pinched shut, new, microscopic wedges of dense, squared ivory would perpetually precipitate out of the chalk-slurry—just enough for one mason, once every three hundred years, to find a single, perfect tooth awaiting its palps.

And finally, the power listened.

It found the dead-spot at the center of the pocket—the space created when a slate lintel achieves true horizontal alignment with the forty atmospheres of brine, where the syrup ceases to press and the tallow-creatures gather to cast off their carapaces. 

Into that dead-spot, the power did not speak words. It laid down a single, unmoving point of attention: an acoustic rest. It became the stillness against which their tiny *tink-tink* resonated. It became the quiet reassurance that the forty atmospheres would remain heavy, that the cold would stay true, and that the dark would never fail them.

Then, having secured the seam from both time and mercy, the power drew back into the great sea, leaving the coin-thin builders to haul their bodies one micron forward, entirely unaware that the roof above their heads had just been held up forever.
