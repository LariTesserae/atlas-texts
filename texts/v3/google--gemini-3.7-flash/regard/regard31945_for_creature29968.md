The Power arrived not with the rending of air or the blazing of an alien fire, but as a vast, gentle settling—like the first cooling shade that falls across a sunlit wall, or the imperceptible shift in atmospheric pressure before a calm tide turns.

It possessed the wisdom that knows when *nothing is broken*, and the benevolence that understands that love does not always demand an overhaul.

For seven hundred cycles of the great sun-glow, the Power did not act. It did not speak, for there were no ears here shaped for language; it did not touch the bark, for the cambium was already whole; it did not alter the flow of a single fluted pylon, for the hydraulic equations of the shelf were already without error.

Instead, it *listened*.

It followed the long, blind migration of the Laminates as their hundreds of tube-feet planed the causeways to a flawless sheen. It felt the violent, ecstatic rush of the Needle-Eels through the high-pressure dark of the xylem channels, where water moved like thrown steel. It rode the warm updrafts beside the Hydra-Kites, watching the thin cartilage of their bellies flush with starch-amber against the roaring spray. 

The Power saw that lesser minds might look upon this shelf and call it cold, or mechanical, or empty of spirit. A lesser power might have been tempted to grant these creatures eyes, not realizing that sight would only bring the terror of the void. A lesser power might have dropped seeds of flowering earth into the troughs, not understanding that soil is the mother of rot, and that here, rot would be a catastrophic profanation. A lesser power would have sought to turn a singing clock into a tragic stage.

Wise benevolence knows the holiness of an unbroken circle.

Yet, because it loved what it saw, the Power chose to leave an offering—one so light, so native to the grain of the place, that it could never be felt as an intrusion.

It descended into the deep, foundational heart-wells far beneath the causeways: the silent, primary chambers where the lukewarm water gathered before being thrust upward into the great pylons. There, in the dark where the biological pumps beat with the rhythm of mountains, the Power dipped its presence into the reservoir.

It did not change the chemistry of the water. It did not alter the starch, nor the viscosity, nor the pressure.

It gave the water **Memory**.

Before the touch, the water was merely volume and velocity—a blind medium of force, driven from conduit to vent and back again in an amnesiac loop. 

With the touch, an infinitesimally fine harmonic was woven into the liquid’s surface tension. 

Now, when a torpid Hydra-Kite broke its vacuum-seal with that sharp, wet *thwack* and peeled away into the sunlit chasm, the water caught the resonance of its release. When the Laminates vibrated in quiet chorus to ease a swollen joint miles away, the frequency rippled into the sap and did not dissipate; it was carried into the heartwood, through the sphincter-vents, and out into the white curtains of mist.

The architecture began, very faintly, to hum with the history of its own living parts.

The shelf did not change its shape by a single millimeter. The timber remained pale, hard as kiln-fired tile, and smooth as melon rind. But through its vascular veins, the roaring plumbing took on the character of an endless, subtle song—a resonant archive of every flight, every glide, every surge, and every resting seal.

And at the end, when an old creature’s water finally leaked away and its dried cartilage was drawn into the living cambium, it was no longer merely engulfed by indifferent wood. As the amber was subsumed into the column, the tree’s memory met the creature’s shape, holding the quiet imprint of its life in the grain forever.

Having given the place its own voice to keep, the Power drew back into the wide, cavernous sky. 

It did not depart; it remained as the vast, warm expanse between the mega-structures—a watchful, unmoving presence that asked for nothing, judged nothing, and demanded no worship, content forever to listen to the grand, vascular hymn of a world that had never fallen.
