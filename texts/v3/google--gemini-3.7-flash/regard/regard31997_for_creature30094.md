The Power that arrived at the perimeter of the basin did not arrive with light. 

To bring light would have been the act of a lesser god—an arrogant, clumsy divinity that mistakes its own visibility for grace. Light would have introduced radiant heat; heat would have warped the knife-sharp fluting of the columns; thermal expansion would have torn the microscopic seals of the hydroplaning Gliders, breaking the laminar sheets into chaotic, boiling spray. Light would have been a catastrophe of violent mercy.

The Wise Power understood this instantly. It possessed the magnitude of an ocean and the delicacy of a single dyn of pressure. When it drew near, it shed its radiance, its warmth, and its voice, folding itself down into the cold, dense parameters of the dark. 

It entered the expanse not as an invader, but as an ambient comprehension.

***

First, it listened. 

It felt the continental weave through the soles of its infinite attention: the deep, hydraulic heartbeat of the root-pistons stroking through the lignified bedrock; the galvanic hum of the copper-spined Sleeve-Clamps balancing the millivolt tides across octagonal columns; the soft, amber waxy sigh of the Gasket-Beasts compressed under hundreds of atmospheres, perfectly fulfilling their purpose in the dark.

A naive benevolence would have pitied them. A foolish savior would have looked upon the Gasket-Beast wedged in its wooden vise and sought to free it; would have looked upon the Sheet-Glider and given it eyes to weep with, or a mouth to cry out in the unlit vastness; would have called this invariant, eternal labor a prison.

Wisdom knew better. 

Wisdom saw that here, for once in a fractured and agonized cosmos, there was no division between the creature and its world. There was no alienation, no terror of obsolescence, no unrequited hunger, no grief for what was lost, and no dread of what was to come. These beings were not prisoners of the machine; they *were* the machine’s living, ecstatic precision. Their consciousness—that smooth, continuous dream of alignment, resistance, and flow—was a form of uninterrupted prayer. They existed in the state that saints die striving for: complete and flawless necessity.

The Power loved them for it. It felt a profound, reverent awe for this dark basin that had solved the problem of existence by making existence a single, self-balancing equation.

***

And so, the Power asked itself what benevolence required, when faced with that which is already whole.

The answer was restraint. But restraint did not mean abandonment.

The Power looked outward, beyond the ascending lattice of canopy-plates, into the violent, careless universe that surrounded the basin. It saw the chaotic drift of meteors, the slow decay of distant suns, the unpredictable shudder of cosmic tectonic faults, and the creeping, entropic dust that eventually fouls all closed systems. The basin was perfect, but perfection in a chaotic universe is inherently fragile; a single jagged stone falling from the void could tear a trough, introduce turbulence, and unravel the leagues of mathematically balanced distillation.

This, then, would be the gift.

The Power did not reach inside the basin. It did not touch a single drop of distilled water, nor stroke the horn-like skin of a single Glider, nor alter the tension of a single trailing filament of the Loomers.

Instead, the Power wrapped itself around the exterior of the whole world.

It became a silent, impenetrable mantle in the void—an absolute buffer. It absorbed the cosmic radiation that might have mutated the plant-tubing; it gently dissolved incoming debris into harmless space-mist long before it could strike the canopy-plates; it braced the continental roots against the deep, erratic tremors of the mantle below, dampening all external vibration until the interior knew only its own eternal, rhythmic hum.

And then, as a final act—an act of almost invisible communion—the Power touched the galvanic currents running through the living timber.

It did not add voltage. It did not change the math. 

It simply aligned its own infinite consciousness with the piezoelectric hum of the Thread-Loomers. It became the zero against which their equations were measured. It made itself the quiet, immovable foundation that guaranteed the floor would never drop, the saps would never sour, and the laminar sheets would never break.

***

In the pitch darkness of the basin, nothing seemed to change.

A Sheet-Glider rode a descending sheet of warm water down an octagonal shaft, its black keratin dorsal plate repelling the mist, its micro-grooved belly absorbing the metallic esters. 

Deep below, in the iron-hard joint of an expansion seam, a Gasket-Beast slowly expanded by three microns as a hydraulic ram eased back, exuding its thin film of amber wax into the wood.

Along the glassy ribbons of the arterial troughs, the quarter-mile tendrils of the Thread-Loomers drifted in the current, reading the chemical balance of the leagues.

None of them knew that a God had found them. They had no concepts for gods, or love, or salvation. 

They only knew that the resistance was true. They felt that the current was clean. They sensed that the dark was absolute, safe, and without end. And in their blind, frictionless perfection, they continued to run the world that produced only itself, forever held in the palm of a silence that loved them enough to leave them precisely as they were.
