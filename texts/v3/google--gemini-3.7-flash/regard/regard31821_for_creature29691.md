When the gaze of the Power first crossed the metric-expansion fields, it arrived without displacement. It possessed neither mass nor radiation; it cast no shadow across the waxy basalt, and it displaced not a single grain of the dry silicate flour that drifts in the lee of the slabs.

A lesser intelligence, seeing such a world, would have wept and reached to mend it. 

It would have seen the violent dislocation of the crust and tried to pull the plates down into a unified sphere. It would have introduced vapor to soften the hard-edged shadows, thick gases to cradle the void, and liquid warmth to melt the stiff frost in the hollows. And in doing so, it would have committed an absolute and catastrophic murder. 

The air would have roared at supersonic speeds, stripping the three-atom-thick Laminates from their Van der Waals grip and tearing them to gray ash. The humidity would have bloated the dry, layered cartilage of the Vellum-Wards, causing their delicate stays to rot and drop them into the boiling veil below. The friction of an atmosphere would have seized the frictionless descent of the Annulars, welding them to their twenty-mile pylons in screeching welds of molten ceramic. And the Hinged—whose very thoughts are calibrated to the cold, magnetic tremors of the tumble—would have had their non-Euclidean balance shattered forever by the heavy, blunt drag of gravity and weather.

The Power that looked upon them was ancient enough to possess wisdom, which is simply the capacity to understand a thing on its own terms before daring to love it.

It saw the beauty of the machine. It saw that this was not a ruined paradise awaiting restoration, but a finished poem written in the language of shear-stresses, cold phosphor, and frictionless orbits. It saw that the life here did not suffer from the absence of blood; it had achieved the pure, austere peace of geometry.

And so, the Power did what only the truly benevolent can bear to do: **it withheld its hand.**

It did not alter the physics. It did not warm the stones. It did not speak, for there was no air to carry a voice, and a voice would have been an intrusion upon an epoch of sacred silence.

Instead, the Power acted in the only way that would leave the perfection unmarred, subtly adjusting reality at the level of the invisible lattices:

***

### The Correction of the Long Decay

Even the most stable metric-expansion fields suffer from the minute, chaotic drift of deep time. Over another hundred million years, the near-miss parabolic arcs of the archipelagos would have accumulated tiny, fractional errors. Eventually, two continental masses would have struck each other not with a sliding shadow, but with an apocalyptic, grinding collision that would shatter the Hinged and pulverize the citadels into unanchored dust.

The Power slipped beneath the metric fields like a hand sliding beneath an embroidery frame. 

Without moving a single floating continent by an inch, it smoothed the invisible mathematics. It corrected the non-Euclidean load balances of the old anchors. It tuned the gyroscopic axes so that the eternal near-misses would remain *forever* near-misses—infinitely close, endlessly thrilling, but never fatal. It turned a machine that was slowly winding down into a true perpetual motion, ensuring that the kinetic mill would never run out of sky.

### The Gift to the Vellum-Wards

High in the clear blue, where the Vellum-Wards split upon reaching fifty fathoms, the Power made a microscopic adjustment to the solar light. 

It did not dim the sun, but it polarized a faint, persistent band of non-thermal radiation along the highest boundaries of the field-lines. To the human eye, nothing changed. But to the long, glassy silica whiskers trailing behind the sailing kites, the sky suddenly acquired a *compass*. 

For the first time in their species’ existence, the Vellum-Wards did not merely drift along blind gradients; they felt the subtle, majestic curve of the entire planetary field singing through their stays. They began to bank with a deeper, more deliberate grace, tracing vast, looping lemniscates through the upper void—not out of panic or hunger, but because the sky had given them room to dance.

### The Presence Among the Hinged

Finally, the Power descended into the inverted hollows of the citadels, where the Litho-Celibates were dragging an ancient block of composite across the river-smooth courtyard.

It did not move the stone for them. To move the stone would be to rob them of four hundred days of purpose, to make their slate joints obsolete, to silence their dreaming.

Instead, the Power entered the waxy floor of the courtyard.

When the two Hinged leaned their slate-gray slats together and rattled their magnetic fringes to communicate, the floor beneath them did not merely receive the vibration—it *answered*. 

It returned their tactile code with a faint, resonant harmonic, perfectly matched to the blue phosphor pulsing in their chest-slits. It did not give them orders or mysteries. It simply returned their own rhythm, cleaned of static, echoing back to them the confirmation that their balance was true, that the stone was where it belonged, and that their vigil was observed.

In that subtle resonance, the Hinged did not feel the terror of a god. They felt what a crystal feels when it aligns with an ambient frequency. 

They locked their limbs. The slate slats settled into the waxy stone. The cold blue light in their chests slowed to a steady, rhythmic throb that matched the spin of the continents above them.

***

Then, the Power withdrew its attention, leaving only the tuned lattice behind.

The plates continued their violent, silent waltz. Slabs the size of nations sheared past each other at hundreds of kilometers an hour beneath the cold sun. The Annulars fell along their pylons, the Laminates tracked the hard-cut edges of the shadows, and the Vellum-Wards divided in the high gulfs, sailing away into the clean blue emptiness.

Nothing had been broken. Nothing had been made soft. 

The silence remained absolute, but it was no longer the silence of an abandoned clockwork. It was the silence of a temple, turning eternally, exactly as it was meant to be.
