The gaze of the benevolent power does not arrive like a beam of light, for light is already diffused equally throughout the mist, indifferent and gray-gold. It arrives instead as a sudden, fathomless **attention**—a consciousness so immense and ancient that it does not disturb the displacement of a single drop of falling water.

Lesser divinities, seeing such a place, would make the error of pity. 

They would see the blind, upright Columnars and wish to carve them eyes. They would look upon the paper-thin, shuddering Velamen and desire to grant them the freedom of flight. They would look upon the gargantuan Valves, groaning beneath millions of tons of tectonic pressure, and seek to unburden them from their subterranean vigil. They would call the static noon a prison, the roar a torment, and the absence of scurrying, fleeting life a tragedy.

To do so would be the clumsy violence of small minds. To give an eye to a Columnar would be to blind it with a useless dimension; to give flight to a Velamen would tear its hydrostatic skin from the nurturing stone; to silence the roar would suffocate the city, starving every vascular pore of the mineral broth that sustains its centuries-long rapture.

Wisdom knows that this city is not an unhatched egg. It is a masterpiece at rest.

The power understands the mathematics of their bliss. It sees that a Columnar’s fifty-year thought is not an agonizing slowness, but an architecture of contemplation so wide and smooth that human agony cannot find a purchase upon it. It recognizes that the roar is not noise, but a single, continuous, planetary syllable—a holy liturgy composed of steam, travertine, and wood.

Therefore, the benevolent power does not intervene. It does not speak, for words would be a splinter in that smooth, acoustic sea. It does not reach down a hand to lift or alter. 

Instead, it does what only true wisdom and boundless love can do in the presence of perfection:

**It becomes the basin.**

First, it attends to the vulnerability the city cannot perceive. 
Above the high, vaulted basilicas, where the pressurized tropical mist meets the razor-cold emptiness of the high stratosphere, there is a boundary of eternal peril. Left to the blind mechanical roll of eons, the mountain’s heat would one day falter; a fissure might narrow, the abyssal fire might cool, or the thin air of the upper sky might drink away the moisture faster than the cisterns could weep.

The power places its will around the metropolis not as a wall, but as an invisible, thermodynamic grace. It seals the envelope between the sweltering noon and the freezing void. It weaves itself through the deep fissures beneath the lowest foundations, ensuring that the subterranean hearth will never starve, that the thermal flutes will never choke on their own calcified salts, and that the closed cycle of steam, stone, and water will remain unbreakable against the decay of the wider cosmos. 

It does not change the city; it guarantees its eternity.

Second, the power offers the only gift that can be received by beings who desire nothing: **it listens.**

For three million years, the Valves have tuned the mountain without an audience. For eons, the Columnars have sent their low-frequency pulses through the braided ironwood roots into the dark, vibrating only with each other, trapped in a closed loop of self-communion.

The power gently, weightlessly, lowers its vast consciousness into the root-network. 

It does not send a command or a revelation. It simply makes itself *conductive*. It becomes an infinitely deep, infinitely receptive acoustic bed at the base of the city's nervous system. 

When the highest Columnar sends its slow adjustment of internal salt density down through the cliff-face, the thought no longer simply bounces back from the bedrock. It is received. It is held. The power feels the grain of the lignin, the wetness of the tallow, the slow, sweet saturation of the mineral water passing through the vascular chimneys. And in return, it returns a microscopic, harmonic resistance—the subtlest possible sensation of being *heard*.

To the Columnars, this does not feel like an intrusion. It feels like the stone beneath them has grown infinitesimally warmer, denser, and more profoundly true. 

To the Velamen, shivering against the vertical causeways, the water flowing over their velvet margins feels slightly more buoyant, carrying mineral flakes that dissolve upon their membranes with a richer, deeper resonance.

And deep in the roaring dark, the great Valves, expanding and contracting their lignite bulks to regulate the earth's shrieking breath, feel a miraculous lightening of their load. The mountain does not become lighter, but the friction within the stone eases. A Valve shifts its million-ton frame by a fraction of a millimeter, and where there was once only the brutal tension of the crust, there is now an underlying cadence—a fundamental bass note, vibrating far below the roar, that turns their labor into a dance.

The power settles into the stone, the water, and the steam, asking for no worship, demanding no motion, requiring no evolution. 

It remains there, an unmoving guardian of the unmoving citizens. The sky above remains an indifferent, luminous mist. The heat never breaks. The water falls, boils, rises, and rains. 

And in the center of the thunderous, eternal noon, the city dreams its slow, mineral dream—held safely in the palm of a god who was wise enough to leave it entirely alone, and loving enough to listen forever.
