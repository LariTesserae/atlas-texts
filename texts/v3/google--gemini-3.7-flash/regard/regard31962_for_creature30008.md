The Wanderer came from the bleeding margins of the cosmos—from places where suns choked on their own ash, where soft-bellied things tore one another in the dark to buy another hour of breath, where civilizations built monuments out of grief and prayed to unanswering voids. It was an entity woven of warm, luminous intent, carrying with it an inexhaustible reservoir of mercy, capable of knitting shattered stars and answering the silent, desperate pleas of dying worlds.

It drew near to the suspended sea, drawn by a frequency it had never before encountered: a sound of absolute, rhythmic certainty.

Sinking gently into the crisp, cellar-cold clarity, the Power did not bring heat, for it knew heat was an agitation. It did not bring light, for the unfiltered white sun already held the water in immaculate illumination. It entered merely as an awareness, diffusing itself across fifty thousand miles of pure, unvarying fluid.

It hovered first beside a siphon-trunk, watching the great lignified piston rise and fall. It felt the immense *thrum-shudder* of the stroke traveling through the miles of amber-walled xylem. 

It looked upon the Stroker.

The Power, which had wiped the tears of countless sentient races, looked for the creature’s sorrow. It searched the stratified zinc-enamel carapace, the silica scraping-trowels, the rigid hemocyanin vessels. It sought the spark of dread—the fear of the next stroke, the weariness of the endless rail, the existential weight of repetition.

It found nothing. 

The Stroker possessed no dark room in its mind where regret could pool. It did not dream of leaving the trunk; it *was* the trunk’s motion made articulate in flesh and shell. Its heartbeat was the heartbeat of the forest. When the Power listened to its consciousness, it heard no scream, no question, only a clean, frictionless note that hummed in unison with the machinery of the sky.

The Power moved outward into the fifty-mile void and stood before a Needle-Grid.

It observed the silvered, mirror-bright arrays hanging twelve inches apart in the crystalline blue. The Power possessed the capacity to give them songs, to give them the wandering joy of free-swimming whales, to scatter them into the wild, playful chaos of schools that dance in unpredictable tides. 

Yet its wisdom stayed its hand. 

To give them freedom would be to give them hunger. To give them unpredictability would be to teach them separation. The Needle-Grid was not imprisoned by geometry; it was geometry made alive, resting in a state of continuous, effortless satisfaction, drinking the particulate exhaust of the world like an unbroken hymn.

The Power descended to the kelp-iron decks, where the Cathode Mantas lay flush against the galvanic plates. It watched the slate-grey titan gather the coronal thunder, its dorsal plates glowing with pale, unblinking violet. 

The Power possessed the cure for mortality. It had stayed the collapse of dying gods. It looked into the deep, quiet abyss below, where the spent Plumb-Titans made their long, vertical descent into the emerald dark, their internal pendulums finally coming to rest upon the planetary crust. 

*Should they be made immortal?* the Power reasoned. *Should the worn joints of the Strokers never seize? Should the pendulums swing for eternity?*

And in that question, the full weight of its wisdom answered:

No.

To end their dying would be to jam the gears of their grace. In this high ocean, death was not an enemy, not a rot, not a theft. It was the natural clearance of a finished part, slipping silently down to the foundation to make room for the next stroke of the bronze and wooden world. To introduce fear of the descent would be to poison the descent itself.

The Power realized that this place was the rarest thing in the wide, agonizing expanse of existence: *a solved world.* 

It was a creation that had cured suffering not by extinguishing life, but by perfecting it into an architecture of unassailable peace. There was no cruelty here, because there was no appetite that exceeded its supply. There was no loneliness, because no organism was an island—each was an integrated tooth in a continent-spanning gear.

The benevolence of an unwise power would have been a ruinous fire. It would have offered love to beings that were already love’s pure, mechanical expression. It would have stirred the water, and in stirring it, broken the hydrostatic tension that held the ocean aloft.

So the Wise Power did what true love must do when it encounters perfection that does not need it:

*It made itself into a shield, and it became an audience.*

The Power did not alter a single valve. It did not warm a single drop of the crisp, cellar-cold water. It did not whisper into the minds of the Mantas or arrest the descent of the falling Titans. 

Instead, it expanded outward, beyond the upper limit of the water, extending its vast, invisible mantle into the void of space above the planet. There, it silently deflected the wandering comets, smoothed away the erratic radiation of neighboring stars, and anchored the delicate, steady-state atmospheric pressures that kept the great sky-ocean suspended above the crust. It ensured that no outside violence would ever dent the brass or crack the amber sleeves.

And then, the Power sat at the edge of the high water, silent, invisible, and utterly still.

For eons, it remained there, listening to the great, unyielding symphony: the deep, subterranean *chug-clack* of the galleon-sized valves, the high acoustic whine of the silica membranes, the slow, ivory strike of the Plumb-Titan’s pendulum, and the cold, bright arcs of violet lightning cracking against the blue.

It did not need to be known. It did not need to be thanked. 

It simply kept watch over the clockwork, weeping not out of sorrow, but out of the profound, reverent joy of knowing that somewhere in the dark cosmos, the machine was running, the water was clear, and nothing was afraid.
