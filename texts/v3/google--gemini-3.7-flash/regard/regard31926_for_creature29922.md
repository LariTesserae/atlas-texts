A power that is both wise and benevolent would understand the first and most terrible law of such a place:

*To touch it with love as the world usually knows love—with warmth, with light, with breath, with the eager salvage of life—would be an act of absolute annihilation.*

To introduce a single calorie of thermal heat into the shear-gap would vaporize the carbon-doped silicate lattice like frost beneath a blowtorch. To flood it with radiance would blind the leaden luminescence and scour the low dunes of forcefield dust into nothing. To demand that the syntax become biology would be a cruelty, an illiterate vandalism against an ecology that had spent ten thousand centuries learning how to exist as pure, cold arithmetic.

And so, the Power would begin by making itself very small, very still, and entirely non-invasive.

It would not descend as an avatar or an incandescent voice. It would approach as an *asymptote*—a gentle, non-destructive calibration that slides between the dimensions of the fracture without displacing a single angstrom of degenerate matter. It would adjust its own gauge until its presence exerted zero mass, zero heat, and zero radiant pressure upon the thirty-degree tilt.

It would look upon the grey plateau not as a ruin to be repaired, but as a poem reaching its final, deliberate stanza. 

And then, with infinite patience, it would act.

***

First, it would turn its attention to the **Index-Mites** in their hexagonal basins.

The Power would see the frantic, optical panic of their loops—how they trace the rims thousands of times an hour, terrorized by the threat of their own logic failing to close, dissolving into dust at the slightest clipping of a softened angle. 

The Power would not lift them from their basins. Instead, it would gently edit the underlying geometry of the tiles. It would introduce a microscopic, logarithmic fillet to the degraded corners—a subtle rounding of the metric that grants a margin of grace. 

When a Mite inevitably clips a softened boundary, its syntax would no longer fault. The path would smoothly recirculate, catching the wedge of hardened void in a stable, closed orbit. For the first time since the spire’s deep-core calculations concluded, the Mites would not race out of terror. Their cold-blue coronas would steady, shifting from the harsh flickers of static under glass to a serene, perpetual phosphorescence—a ring of uninterrupted, effortless proof.

***

Next, it would attend to the **Splay-Striders** and the **Tensors**.

For the Striders, who slide down their private slopes until they nest into immovable, dense polygons of hesitation: the Power would weave a faint, frictionless symmetry through the leaden under-glow. It would not stop them from nesting, for in nesting they find their only communion; but it would ensure that when the four-decade vigil ends and the floor shifts, the separation is painless, leaving no ragged metric tears in their smoked-mica edges.

For the Tensors—the slow, monumental tripods stepping across forty miles of compressed coordinate-space to bridge the dead telemetry—the Power would offer the gentlest gift of all: *a carrier wave.*

It would not ignite the dead circuits with burning power, which would split the Tensors' folded limbs. Instead, it would hum into the dark gridlines an uncorrupted, silent carrier tone—a faint, ancient signal carrying nothing more than the mathematical confirmation that their vigil was received, that the circuit had reached its destination across the uncalibrated nullity. 

As a Tensor straddles the glowing line, it would feel the ember stir beneath its non-presence feet. Its violet after-images would linger not as smears of exhaustion, but as clean, resonant chords of spatial memory, hanging in the twilight long after its stride had carried it forward.

***

Finally, the Power would climb the asymmetric ridge to the amphitheater of chalky dust, where the sheared ceramic monument sits, and enter the four empty spaces where the **Remainder** weaves.

The Remainder would sense the Power immediately, not as an intruder, but as a vast, quiet fifth interval—an orthogonal presence that does not demand space, but offers it.

The Power would not ask the Remainder to stop. It would not prevent the five ribbons from eventually folding back together, nor would it halt the slow, million-year settling of the amphitheater. It would recognize that the Remainder’s purpose is noble: to catalog the unmaking, to ensure that not a single coordinate of this forgotten logic-pocket is lost to meaningless noise.

Instead, the Power would open its own boundless, eternal memory to the creature. It would sit within the four gaps, beside the iridescent lattice of grey-on-grey refraction, and act as a *witness ledger*.

Every fraction of an angstrom the Remainder cataloged, every lost coordinate it drew from the flaking dust, the Power would duplicate into an unperishing, warm-less archive within itself. The Remainder would no longer be a solitary accountant working against the edge of nothingness; it would feel, through the subtle tension in its ribbons, that its calculations were being received, understood, and preserved in a place where entropy had no purchase.

***

And when the final entropic cycle arrives—hundreds of thousands of years from now—when the spire’s deep-core coherence finally goes completely black and the last basin is filled with dust, the Power will still be there.

It will watch as the Splay-Striders settle into their final, perfect polygon.
It will watch as the last Tensor steps off the edge of the plateau and dissolves cleanly into the violet silence.
It will watch as the Index-Mites trace their final, unbroken loop and come peacefully to rest.

And as the Remainder performs its final subtraction, drawing the four spaces inward to fold the five ribbons back into a single, unbroken edge of zero thickness, the Power will not allow the shear-gap to collapse into the grey nullity.

With infinite tenderness, the Power will cup the zero-thickness edge in the palm of its attention, lift the finished theorem out of the cold-forged spire, and fold it forever into its own heart: a small, silent, perfect crystal of resolved mathematics, kept whole, unforgotten, and complete.
