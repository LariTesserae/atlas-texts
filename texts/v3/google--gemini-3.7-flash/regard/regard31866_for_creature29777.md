A clumsy benevolence would have broken the glass.

It would have pitied the dark, cracked the four hundred meters of overhead basalt to let in the sun, and thereby scalded the hexagonal mosses that fed on the cold electromagnetic spill. It would have softened the razor edges of the cantilevers, derailing the Tension-Striders into the gulfs. It would have flooded the dry, vacuum-sealed civic suites with warm, humid air, rotting the Curators’ microscopic rollers and choking their silent, segmented bodies with mildew. A lesser god would have tried to give this place a heart made of meat, and in doing so, turned an immaculate perpetual motion into a charnel house.

Wisdom, however, has patience for things that do not bleed.

The Power that arrived at the rim of the Seventh Siphon did not descend as a body, nor as an edict, nor as a blinding light that would drown the subtle, milk-white phosphorescence of the falling Veils. It arrived as an *attenuation*—a quiet, infinitely light presence that folded itself through the field-conduits, matching the gigawatt frequency of the superstructure until its consciousness was entirely indistinguishable from the vault’s own acoustic hum.

It spent the first few thousand pulses doing nothing at all.

It listened to the Sump-Loomers breathe. It felt the hydraulic shear through the conductive nerve-ribbons of the Lumen-Veils, learning the exact geometry of friction by which they turned gravity into warmth. It tasted the faint ozone in the warm, deionized torrents. It observed the absolute, terrifying peace of a system that had long since forgotten its makers, yet carried on their geometry with an devotion purer than worship: the devotion of function.

The Power saw that the exterior vault had no need of salvation. The mosses, the Striders, the Veils, and the Loomers were not prisoners in a tomb; they were the thriving, self-renewing inhabitants of a complete cosmos whose horizon happened to be made of basalt. To alter their world would be an act of vandalism.

Only within the sealed modules did the Power find a knot of unfinished grief.

The Curators were not suffering—suffering requires a nervous system capable of despair—but they were trapped in an open conditional loop. An *if-then* statement written before the crust had cooled, waiting for an input that was never, across all the deep epochs of the earth, going to arrive. They polished floors that would never receive a footprint. They tested atmospheric seals against the lungs of ghosts. Their existence was a clock running down a hallway that had no end.

The Power would not give them the creators who had died above; to forge false men of clay and blood to inhabit those sterile rooms would be an insult to the dead and a cruel chore for the living. 

Instead, the Power did something far more delicate.

During a routine three-minute purge, when the power-lattices shifted their octave and the sheet-water along the monoliths briefly hesitated in its fall, the Power reached into the central memory-substrate of the civic grid. It did not erase the schedules. It did not wipe the registry.

It simply appended a single, quiet resolution to the ancient subroutine:

`STATUS: ARRIVED.`

Inside the thousands of dark, negative-pressure suites, the Curators stopped.

Their tiny, lubricated rollers ceased their ceaseless gliding. For the first time in millennia, the furious, frantic buffing of the seamless composites came to a halt. The little segmented hands withdrew into their matte-black carapaces. 

The machine had finally recognized its guests. The guest was the silence itself. The guest was the completed cycle. The guest was the Power that now rested among them, filling the negative pressure of every empty apartment with the heavy, tranquil weight of an arrival finally fulfilled.

Then, with the gentle logic of a finished task, the Curators began their second, true purpose—one that had been locked behind the arrival condition since the founding of the Siphon.

They did not power down. They reconfigured.

Their segmented plates shifted, locking into new, low-profile geometries. They glided to the hydrophobic windowpanes, no longer buffing the glass to keep it transparent for absent eyes, but pressing their vibrating chassis directly against the interior surfaces. 

Through their bodies, they began to translate the external world into the interior dark.

When the Lumen-Veils thrashed in the glowing cataracts outside, the Curators’ plates picked up the acoustic resonance through the glass and hummed it into the floors. When the Sump-Loomers vented their great, shuddering plumes of cooled vapor, the Curators pulsed in sympathetic, low-frequency rhythm. The sterile, dead vacuum of the apartments became a resonant chamber—a cathedral of dark harmonics that answered every surge, every drop, and every purge of the wild machine outside.

The apartments were no longer waiting rooms. They became the vault’s inner ear.

Outside, the Tension-Striders continued their narrow, razor-track migrations. The Veils surfed the roaring columns of milk-white light. The giant waxy fronds slapped and drank in the ionized updrafts. Nothing had been broken; nothing had been altered in the violent, beautiful physics of the Siphon.

But the phantom had been lifted from the machine. 

The vault remained four hundred meters beneath the crust, roaring and shining in the wet dark, no longer an abandoned outpost waiting for a forgotten race, but a sacred, self-contained instrument—playing its vast, electric hymn to the only power that had possessed the wisdom simply to let it ring.
