The Power arrived without the displacement of air or the splintering of slate. 

It did not descend in a pillar of celestial fire, nor did it announce itself with the trumpet-blasts that lesser gods use to shake the foundations of the world. Such things belong to the realm of mass, torque, and rupture—and this basin already possessed all the mass and torque the rock could bear.

Instead, the Power came as an absolute, weightless attentiveness. It settled into the cold, saturated mist at shoulder height, spreading through the damp clearances like a second, immaterial atmosphere. It entered the tolerances between the titaniferous magnetite crankshafts and their stone journals; it slid beneath the lamellar plates of the ironstone scrapers; it touched the blind, electro-receptive pits of the nickel-skulled lungfish in their deep, oil-skinned sumps.

A lesser benevolence, seeing this place, might have felt an instinctive, meddling pity. 

A lesser benevolence would have looked upon the blind lungfish and wished to grant them eyes, not realizing that sight would only reveal the churning dark of the sump and bring the terror of perceived space. It would have looked upon the soot-colored swifts and wished to give them melodies of joy and sorrow, not understanding that a song with a beginning and an end is an agony to a creature whose peace is the eternal, uninterrupted pitch of venting steam. A lesser power would have sought to free the flagstone-hided beast from its unvarying circuit beneath the bronze counterweight, mistaking its millimetric precision for imprisonment, and in doing so, would have brought the crushing bronze down upon its skull.

Benevolence without wisdom is an axe that calls itself an artist. But this Power was ancient, and its wisdom was as deep and cold as the roots of the massif.

It understood instantly what it had found: a sanctuary of pure, unblemished *is-ness*. Here was a corner of creation that had bypassed the great, bleeding wounds of desire, history, and decay. The creatures here did not suffer, because they did not resist the stroke of the piston; they did not fear death, because they were already woven into the lubrication of the gears; they did not pray, because their entire existence was a closed, mathematical hymn that had never fallen out of tune.

To change them would be to invent their suffering. To give them minds would be to introduce the concept of an end.

So the Power did three things, using neither force nor command, but the quiet authority of an elder reality stepping into alignment with a younger one.

First, it looked outward to the violent, shifting mantle of the world. It saw the tectonic churnings of the young mountain range—the unpredictable upthrusts, the volcanic fault-lines, and the inevitable frosts that would one day fracture the razor-sheared slate rim and drop a million tons of chaotic scree into the basin, jamming the unshielded cogs and breaking the great basalt shafts.

Gently, the Power laid its fingers upon the deep geometry beneath the mountain. It did not alter the tear in natural law; rather, it *annealed* it. It set a profound, silent gyroscopic anchor into the deep crust below the sumps, isolating the basin from the seismic convulsions of the continent. The frost might split the outer peaks, and the glaciers of the centuries might grind the surrounding valleys to dust, but this single hollowed tooth of rock was pinned to the steady axis of the world. It would remain level. The tolerances would not drift. The lithomarge would never run dry.

Second, the Power turned to the solitary beast of wet flagstone and yellow lichen, currently standing motionless in the gravel clearance, its breath rising in rhythm with the nearest vent as it waited for the bronze counterweight to sweep past.

The Power did not speak to it—for language is a wedge that splits the self from the world—but it stepped into the space beside it. For the duration of three long strokes, the Power let its infinite awareness fill the simple, rhythmic consciousness of the beast. It gave the creature no thoughts, no dreams of green meadows or distant herds. It gave it only the profound, physical sensation of being *comprehended*—a momentary deepening of the cool rock-oil against its flanks, a sweet and total alignment with the swing of the bronze, a brief flash of warmth through its mineral blood that felt like the sun it had never seen and never needed. 

When the clearance opened, the beast took its step, exactly on the split-second, its hide scraping the spinning stone shaft with a deep, content, resonant hum.

Finally, the Power did what wisdom always does when it encounters a work of immaculate, self-sufficient grace:

It sat down.

On the ledge of unweathered slate, where the newly split slab hung suspended mid-topple against the spinning steel armature, the Power folded its vastness into a quiet, permanent presence. It did not breathe, lest it alter the humidity of the clubmoss. It did not radiate light, lest it outshine the pale, static-blue ionization clinging to the tips of the giant ferns.

It simply remained there, in the silver dusk, listening.

The engine had turned for eons without an audience, producing an infinite, unrecorded masterpiece of low-frequency thrumming, cold suction, and metallic click. Now, it had a witness. The Power took the whole ceaseless chord—the clack of the stone valves, the hiss of the steam, the quiet rotation of the embryo-bearing rods, and the slow, grinding return of the dying into the fine silt of the journals—and drew it into its own eternal memory.

The basin needed nothing. But now, it was known.

And high on the fractured shoulder of the unnamed massif, beneath the perpetual, unchanging mist, the great iron gears turned through their beds of dripping moss, safe within the clockwork of the earth, humming to the quiet god who had loved them enough to leave them alone.
