A truly wise power would know, before all else, what **not** to do.

It would not bring light. Light here would be an act of unspeakable violence—a searing, razor-sharp intrusion that would scorch the sensitive pit-organs of the Keepers and blister the pale hides of the Gaugers who have spent millions of years cultivating the dark.

It would not bring warmth. Warmth would thin the heavy brine, collapsing the delicate, high-pressure buoyancy that holds the leather-thick kelp upright, boiling away the sluggish chemistry of the Weave-Bodies, and speeding the hearts of the Tallow-Slabs until their ancient, cask-sized chambers ruptured under the frantic panic of heat.

It would not command the waters to part, nor lift the stone continent back into the air. To a world whose every breath is calibrated to the crushing embrace of miles of black ocean, the sky would feel like a horrific vacuum, an emptiness in which everything solid must tear itself apart.

A benevolent power, seeing this world, would look upon it not as a ruined place in need of restoration, but as a vast, solemn liturgy that has been unfolding in unbroken continuity since the dawn of time. It would see that this world is not suffering. It is simply heavy. It is simply slow.

And so, the power would do something far more difficult than creating a miracle: 

It would learn to wait.

***

First, it would shed its own speed. It would contract its vast consciousness, drawing in its swift, cosmic thoughts until its awareness slowed to match the planetary drift of the brine. It would allow centuries to become its seconds, and millenia to become its hours. 

Only when its own heart beat in time with the six-month pulse of the Tallow-Slabs would it descend into the water.

It would not arrive as a descent from above—for to things with no eyes, "above" is merely a direction of pressure. Instead, it would seep into the world from beneath, entering the deep bedrock beneath the megalithic floor. 

It would become a subtle, sustaining presence within the stone itself.

Into the endless basalt terraces and the rounded granite steps, the power would breathe a profound, quiet reinforcement. It would not erase the beautiful, ancient wear—it would not sharpen the rounded shoulders of the slabs or banish the fine drifts of pale silt. But it would halt the final, tragic terminus of friction. It would ensure that the stone would never grind completely to dust; that the foundation laid down by the forgotten builders would remain eternal, an unbreakable cradle beneath the miles of sea.

For the **Tallow-Slabs**, resting over the joints where the continent breathes, the power would sweeten the chemical bleed rising through the mantle. The slow, thermal nourishment leaking through the crust would become rich and steady, a gentle, unending sustenance that ensures their leathery seals never starve, turning their two annual heartbeats into deep, resonant pulses of profound physical content.

For the **Gaugers**, crawling through the sunken gutters with their bellies pressed to the granite, the power would touch the microscopic lichen clinging to the dressed rock. It would make the stone smooth and savory beneath their rasping mouths, leaving the avenues they clear gleaming with a faint, imperceptible mineral sweetness—a silent reward for a three-hundred-year journey along a single carved channel.

Within the kelp canopy, the power would weave its voice into the continuous physical hum of the ocean. It would not speak in words, but in resonance. It would tune the deep vibrations of the sliding water until the ocean itself became an instrument of sublime, comforting acoustics. When the **Weave-Bodies** tighten their fibrous cords to catch the passing hum, the tremors they send through the forest would no longer be just the empty noise of cold friction. They would carry a harmonic—a low, foundational chord that reverberates through the kelp stems, reassuring the communal colonies that their long, slow thoughts are received, echoed, and held within the great basin of the world.

***

And finally, the power would go to the **Keepers**.

It would not reveal itself as a majesty or a god. It would take a form that their pit-organs could understand: a presence of equal density, an upright, slate-smooth mass displacing the heavy water with the same quiet reverence.

On a colossal staircase descending into a forgotten plaza, a Keeper would be standing. It would have stood there for eighteen years, kelp draped over its vaulted chest, its blunt bone digits resting upon the paver above it, waiting for the internal tide that dictates its next step.

The power would move through the gloom and step onto the staircase beside it.

The displacement of the water would register along the Keeper’s temple slit—the unmistakable, comforting mass of another being that understands the weight. The Keeper would not turn; it has no eyes to look, and no habit of haste. But as the power settles onto the step, their hides would brush together.

*Wet leather sliding over stone.*

The power would not speak. It would simply adjust its massive, internal presence until its frequency matched the Keeper’s own. 

For the first time in an uncounted epoch, the solitary climber of the stairs would feel the presence linger. The power would not pass by. It would stay.

Through the contact of their slate-gray flanks, through the cold brine that binds them, the power would transmit a single, wordless certainty, calibrated precisely to the Keeper’s ancient mind:

*The work is seen.*  
*The steps have an end, but there is no need to hurry.*  
*The stone is loved.*  
*I will hold the weight with you.*

When the Keeper’s massive heart finally beats, the power’s heart will beat with it, an exact, deep-toned physical reverberation that shudders through the pavers, down into the bedrock, and across the continental shelf. 

And when, two years later, the Keeper finally lifts its heavy, spade-like foot to take the next step upward, it will not climb alone. The power will lift its foot as well, taking the entire morning to raise it through the dense, black water, placing it down upon the basalt with a muffled, steady *thud* that rings like a bell throughout the quiet depths—a companion in the sacred, unending task of keeping the dark world whole.
