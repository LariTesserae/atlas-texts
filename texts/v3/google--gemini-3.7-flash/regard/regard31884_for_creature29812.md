The power did not arrive from above, because in that place "above" was merely an unanchored ceiling where wrought-fiber gaslamps hung upside down into the dark. Nor did it arrive with light; it knew that a sudden radiance would blind the Caliper-Beasts, scorch the gelatinous cellulose of the bay windows, and curdle the pressurized sap within the virgin xylem.

Instead, the power arrived as an *accordance*.

It was an ancient, patient consciousness—one that understood that when living tissue mimics the forms of cities, it is not because it wishes to manufacture iron and commerce, but because the living world has only ever had two ways to hold back the cold: to grow a shell, or to build a house. 

This chamber was an orphan’s nervous system, frantically framing a home for an occupant it had never seen, driven by a panic encoded in its very cambium.

The power did not tear down a single beam. It did not level the shifting gravity or command the vine-pistons to cease. True wisdom does not interrupt an embryonic dream; it enters the gestation quietly, ensuring the creature does not break its own bones in the dark.

***

### I. The Tempered Sap

First, the power touched the lowest taproots, where the blind Cambium-Eels slithered through the ice-slick channels.

It did not kindle fire—fire was anathema to this moist, breathing timber—but it introduced a gentle, subterranean respiration: a deep, catalytic warmth that smelled not of soot, but of malt, rain-soaked loess, and old compost. 

As this warmth entered the vascular grid, the viscosity of the xylem eased. The high-tension creak of structural timber relaxed by a fraction of a millimeter. The Cambium-Eels felt the rime melt from their hide; their frantic scraping slowed into a long, rhythmic glide. 

Where the viaducts sweated, the falling droplets no longer struck the void with the sharp, anxious *tock* of a mechanical deadline. The rhythm settled, broadening into the steady, reassuring cadence of an ancient water-clock.

### II. The Sleep of the Pith

Next, the power drifted into the half-formed parlors where the Squatters of the Pith sat anchored to the floorboards.

It saw the tragedy of these elderberry-flesh men: spent like fuses, shuddering at every stress-vector, registering every blow across six city blocks only to wither into forgotten shavings when the walls grew hard.

The power sat beside one of them on a freshly secreted sofa. It laid an immaterial hand against the pale, slit-faced cheek.

It did not sever the creature’s roots—to do so would blind the city’s self-repair—but it gave the nervous system a *memory*. 

When the distant vine-pistons fired and the shockwave traveled through the floorboards, the power filtered the shudder. The vertical slits on the Squatters' faces fluttered, but no vapor of panic escaped their chests. For the first time, the stress was translated not as an alarm, but as an impression: the sound of a roof holding against the night; the feeling of a doorway remaining true; the sensation of a room keeping a promise.

And when an apartment matured and the white fiber-man began to dry, the power ensured he did not simply crumble into refuse to be flushed through a drain. As the elderberry core shrank, it left behind a fine, aromatic resin in the grain of the floor—an amber residue of lived peace. The house was no longer merely lignified; it was *inhabited by memory*.

### III. The Ledger’s Sum

In the vertical rooms, where the Ledger-Crabs danced upon the ticking seed-pods, the calculation was an endless, open loop. The crabs scuttled until their needle-legs ground to stumps, trying to tally an infinite debt of sap and clearance.

The power reached into the seed-pod wall. It did not stop the ticking; it introduced an *answer*.

It whispered a prime ratio into the pods—a harmonic number where the intake of the taproots and the expenditure of the Draft-Vanes met in absolute equilibrium. 

One by one, the Ledger-Crabs paused. Their needle-feet hovered over the casing. The frantically vibrating seeds settled into a deep, purring resonance, like the sound of a thousand bees asleep in a hollow trunk. The crabs did not starve; they nested in the crevices between the pods, basking in the steady, rhythmic hum of a ledger that had finally balanced its account.

### IV. The Center in the Void

Finally, the power gathered itself into the vast, hollow clearance—the cold, empty hundreds of thousands of cubic feet where the Draft-Vanes circled in perpetual drift.

It saw the five-story clock towers swinging on their unanchored tethers, their hands frozen, waiting for a time that had not yet begun.

The power settled into the highest, loneliest belfry. 

It did not ring the bells. It became an *axis*.

Around this belfry, the discrete parcels of shifting gravity gently aligned. "Down" ceased to be a series of abrupt, ninety-degree chasms; the invisible seams softened into a slow, cradling curve. The Plumb-Gnats, sensing the true center, swept across the dark clearance not in sudden, frantic angles, but in long, swirling auroras of cold green phosphorescence.

The Draft-Vanes felt the draft change. The rising celery-scented steam carried a new loft, buoyant and vast. They no longer had to weave frantic miles of thread to prevent the towers from drifting apart; they banked their thirty-foot parchment wings on the rising current, trailing lines of gossamer that caught the green light of the gnats, turning the empty gulf into a cathedral of shimmering, hanging constellations.

***

The city did not stop growing. The fresh industrial gantries still clicked their way into the dark, dividing cell by dividing cell. The Gasket-Ticks still clamped their lard-pale bodies to leaking seams, and the Caliper-Beasts still locked their limbs to splint the under-pressurized spans.

The architecture remained an ecosystem that built factories instead of forests.

But the desperate, frantic rush was gone. The orphan mechanism had discovered that someone was inside the house. 

In the velvety darkness, between the wet *shlick* of the valves and the slow turn of the bark flywheels, the city continued to secrete its streets—no longer as a barrier against an empty universe, but as a vast, living cradle, perfectly plumb, perfectly level, and quietly loved.
