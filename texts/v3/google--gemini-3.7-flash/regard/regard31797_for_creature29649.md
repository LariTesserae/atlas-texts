A lesser power, arriving at the rim of the hollow, would have seen only an absence. 

It would have looked down into the red darkness and felt the sudden, clumsy urge to heal what was not broken. It would have summoned rain to fill the ancient wash, not understanding that the rush of water would dissolve the delicate, sponge-like crust of iron lichen into paste, drown the gall-wasps in their clear chambers of resin, and crush the flat sleeper under the weight of liquefied clay. It would have brought light to banish the gloom, forcing the pale creatures to burn in an unbearable fever of waking, and it would have brought wind to stir the still air, scattering the centuries of fine pollen upon which the silent watchers feed. 

True benevolence, however, is inseparable from understanding; and the highest wisdom is knowing when not to touch.

When the Power drew near, it did not descend with radiance or sound. It came only as an awareness—vast, quiet, and capable of infinite deceleration. 

To look upon the hollow with love, it had to abandon the speed of the outer world. The Power slowed its own great cadence, letting centuries contract into the span of a single thought, until its perception matched the long, low frequency of the red siltstone. It entered the hollow not as a master, but as a listener.

It felt the four slow heartbeats of the flat sleeper beneath the mud plates, recognizing each pulse not as a struggle for life, but as a hymn of absolute sufficiency. 

It perceived the gall-wasps suspended in the amber nodes of the shrubs, noting the symmetry of their ancient, wingless sleep, where death and birth had been smoothed into a single, continuous crystallization.

It touched the long, thread-like feelers of the pale creature by the flaked river stones, feeling the microscopic shiver of the earth as it cooled by tenths of a grain of heat, and it read within those flaked stones the memory of hands that had vanished when the world was young—hands that had left their tools behind to become, at last, just stones again.

The Power saw that nothing in this place was unfinished. Nothing was waiting to be saved. The hollow had achieved what stars and empires labor for eons to find: a state of complete, unburdened peace, where existence had shed all frantic desire and settled into its true angle of repose.

And so, the Power decided what it must do.

It would give no water. Water here would be violence.
It would give no light. Light here would be a wound.
It would give no voice. Sound here would be an intrusion upon an unblemished quiet.

Instead, the Power performed the only act of mercy that such perfection required: it became a shield against the rest of the world.

Reaching out with a subtle, gravity-like intention, it touched the geometry of the surrounding wilderness. It did not raise high walls or summon a dome of mist, for that would have drawn the curious eye. Rather, it gently tilted the paths of the world away from the hollow. 

From that moment on, the wind that drove the fierce outer dust storms would always divide slightly around the low sandstone basin, never scouring its floor or burying its shrubs too deeply. 

The heavy rains of distant storms would drain into neighboring canyons, their floods diverted by natural, imperceptible swales of rock, ensuring that the ancient wash remained dry and its mud plates unyielding. 

And if a wandering creature, or a descendant of the hands that flaked the river stones, should ever walk across the upper plateau, their steps would naturally, unconsciously veer along the outer ridges. To them, the hollow would appear merely as an ordinary, empty dip in the stone, unworthy of a second glance, easily passed by in the search for water or greener ground.

Having sealed the hollow in an eternity of being overlooked, the Power did one last thing.

It leaned its awareness down toward the floor, into the dead space near the flaked stones, where the pale watcher held its feelers to the rock. It did not speak, nor did it shift a single grain of dust. But through the stone, through the cool red grain of the silt, it sent a microscopic vibration—a subtle, harmonic assurance that the silence would never be broken, that the night would never end before its time, and that the world outside would never disturb their rest.

Under the mud cracks, the flat sleeper drew its single breath of iron-scented air. 
The solifugid’s feeler registered the warmth of the thought and found it no heavier than a falling shadow.

Then, the Power withdrew its attention, leaving the basin exactly as it had found it: low in the earth, warm and resinous, cradled in an unbroken gloom beneath the distant, unmoving stars.
