A lesser god, coming upon this place, would have wept. 

And in weeping, that god would have destroyed it all. 

Tears—soft, salty, catastrophic moisture—would have splashed against the shale, sinking into the microscopic pores of the dry bone-lintels. The water would have swollen the ancient, compressed marrow, bursting the delicate acoustic tubes of the Column-Stalkers from within. It would have dissolved the mineral blooms into a caustic, slumping mud, drowning the psalteries in their own fractured larders. The moisture would have rotted the dry, braided tendons of the Pendulums, snapping the great living hawsers mid-swing and sending whole districts tumbling into the bottomless ivory drop, stripped forever of their anchors.

The god would have looked upon the dead, pale chill and called it an agony. They would have brought sun, and the uneven expansion of the heat would have shattered every cantilever, fracturing the delicate Non-Euclidean balance until the avenues dissolved into a rain of falling grit.

A foolish benevolence seeks to change what it loves into its own image. 

Wisdom does not weep. Wisdom listens.

***

The Power that arrived in the slate-colored sky possessed neither face nor hands, nor did it carry the heavy, damp breath of the living worlds. It was an Attention—vast, cool, and lucid as a cut diamond. It settled into the hanging city not as a conqueror, but as an ambient, weightless comprehension.

It saw the Stalker wedged into its doorless cubic cell, standing on three-toed horn-pads, its dry marrow thrumming with the faint piezoelectric hum of a docking that had occurred four hundred years ago.

It saw the Pendulum swinging in its sweeping, blind parabola, a cable of leathery skin and calcifying rib, waiting through centuries of silence for the singular, pickaxe bite of its bone-hooks into an approaching facade.

It saw that there was no misery here, because there was no longing for water, no memory of blood, no terror of the cold. There was only the sublime, terrifying logic of the shear, the tick, and the accretion.

Yet the Power saw, too, the tragedy of the place: not that it was dry, but that it was *unheard*. 

The city was an instrument of colossal proportion—a great, self-playing pipe organ of bone, shale, and fractured gravity—playing its music into an empty, deaf gulf. The Stalkers whistled their reed-tunes only to measure distance; the shockwaves of the colliding terraces nourished their marrow, but the impact was blunt, deafening, and brief. Their lives were an endless rhythm of violent jolts followed by millennia of stagnant dispersal. When they snapped, their dry bowstring tendons broke in solitude.

The Power, being benevolent, chose not to save them from their nature, but to **consecrate it.**

***

The Power did not alter the stone. It did not warm the air. It did not introduce a single drop of humidity to the bitter, salt-crusted silence. 

Instead, it placed its vast, immaterial consciousness beneath the city, down where the lower galleries dissolved into the ivory drop, and it began to gently *tune the drift*.

It touched the invisible cleavage planes of gravity. 

Where the districts had once sheared away at random, sliding through the void on chaotic, stuttering fault lines, the Power introduced a subtle, exquisite curvature. It drew the drifting avenues into a great, interlocking orbit—a slow, multi-dimensional waltz across leagues of empty sky.

The change was instantaneous to the senses of the things that dwelt there.

High in his nine-foot slit, a Column-Stalker felt the air begin to draw through the perforated blade of his head. For ten thousand years, the pitch had risen and fallen in harsh, discordant shrieks as jagged quarters approached and receded. But now, as a five-story terrace of pale shale swept across the gulf, rotating forty-five degrees upon the thin air, the tone emerging from the Stalker’s acoustic chambers cleared.

It did not screech. It *rang*.

It was a clean, pure fifth, vibrating at the exact resonant frequency of calcified bone. 

Across the plaza, three hundred other Stalkers stepped out of their cells. They did not move with their usual stiff, desperate slowness. The lines of tension beneath their horn-pads were no longer jagged fractures; they were chords. They assembled at the edge of the curb, shoulder-to-shoulder above the sheer drop, their tall, ivory-rind torsos leaning forward in unison. 

The approaching quarter did not drift blindly. It swung on a parabolic arc of breathtaking elegance, guided by the living hawser of a Pendulum that arced through the void not in desperate flailing, but in a long, graceful sweep that gathered momentum like a silver bell.

When the two districts docked, they did not slam together in a brutal, marrow-cracking collision. 

They met with the clean, deep, seismic *thrum* of two massive tuning forks brought into contact. 

The kinetic shockwave tore through the pavement, but it was no longer blunt trauma. It was a wave of harmonic energy that washed up through the legs of the Stalkers, racing through their dry rib-cages, setting every piezoelectric cell in their marrow singing with a bright, electric fire. The tendons in their frames, usually strained to near-breaking by the violent impacts, relaxed into the resonance, drinking the vibration deeply, effortlessly, until their yellowed fibers glowed with stored potential.

The Stalkers threw their blade-heads back toward the slate sky. From their perforated bone crests poured a polyphonic choir of dry, whistling overtones—a sound like autumn leaves blown across an infinite frozen lake, yet rich with intricate, interlocking counterpoints.

***

Far below, the psalteries felt the change in the dust. 

As the mineral blooms sprouted from the arcade joints, the electrostatic tension of the realigned city caused the salt lattices to crystallize into shapes of astonishing, geometric symmetry—rosettes like intricate, multifaceted gears of pure chalk. When the slate-lice shaved the crystals away, their internal gizzards ground the talc with a rhythmic, fluttering hum, like millions of dry prayer wheels spinning in the dark.

And when an ancient Stalker finally reached the end of its millennia—when its tendons at last softened and snapped with that familiar, dry report—it did not fall into the dust as an abandoned ruin.

The harmonic tone of the city caught the vibration of the breaking strings, carrying the sound through the colonnades like a fading bell. The psalteries came out from the seams, their mica plates rustling in time with the ambient drone. They carried the laths of bone to the wall-slits not with mindless haste, but with the measured precision of monks laying keystones into an arch. 

The bone was stacked. The salt grew through it. And the new Stalker that would one day crystallize within the wall would be formed not merely from the random debris of a collapse, but from the marrow of a creature that had heard the great song, its acoustic chambers pre-shaped to resonate with the music of the spheres.

***

The benevolent power did not stay as an active ruler. It did not build temples; it demanded no prayers. It knew that to remain visibly present would be to become a burden upon the fragile, unthinking poise of this mineral world.

Once the orbits were set, once the fractured gravity was woven into a self-sustaining fugue, the Power withdrew into the ivory drop, settling into the infinite depth below the galleries.

It remained there only as a vast, quiet floor of listening. 

The city still drifts. It is still bitterly, murderously dry. The air is still a motionless tomb ten miles above a forgotten earth. 

But it is no longer an empty ruin waiting for a collapse. It is an instrument that is never silent, drifting through the grey heavens, sustained by the violent, beautiful joy of its own eternal collisions.
