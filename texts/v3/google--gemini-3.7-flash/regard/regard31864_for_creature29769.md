To love a thing rightly, one must love it within the laws of its own being. 

A lesser or more impatient divinity might have looked upon the drowned cordillera and seen only misery: the blind dark, the brutal hydraulic vise, the eternal freeze, the absence of songbird and flower. Such a god would have brought sunlight down into the abyss, and in doing so would have boiled the siphon-weeds, cracked the granite baffles with sudden thermal shock, and blinded the pressure-pits of the tenders with agonizing, useless radiation. A foolish god would have lifted the weight of the sea, bursting their hydraulic chambers and reducing their ancient, mineralized carapaces to slack and ruined gristle.

Wisdom knows that to these beings, four hundred fathoms of brine is not a prison; it is the atmosphere of their grace. The crushing weight is the very bone of their architecture. The darkness is the clear medium through which their music travels.

Therefore, when the benevolent power turned its attention to the range, it did not descend as an incandescent flame or a sudden warmth. It descended as a presence that made itself dense, cold, and slow. It took on the specific gravity of the deep faults. It entered the medium as an answering mass—a vast, conscious weight that slid quietly beneath the tectonic plate, resting where the granite roots meet the mantle of the earth.

It did three things.

***

**First, it became the foundation of the work.**

The greatest sorrow of the cordillera was not the cold or the dark, but the catastrophic slip. Once every few centuries, a blind seismic spasm along the deep trenches would heave the saddles, shattering the unmortared breakwaters, splitting the hollow ballast-bulbs before their time, and scattering the slow, century-long labor of the tenders into the silt of the abyss. 

The Power did not stop the ocean weather; it did not quiet the shear-currents that the siphon-weeds required to cast their seed. Instead, it placed its vast, invisible palm beneath the fractured joints of the range. It absorbed the violent, jagged shears of the crust, translating them into long, harmonic tides. 

Where the granite had once sheared unpredictably, it now broke cleanly along predictable seams, offering the tenders perfect, tabular slabs of basalt for their chisels. The stone did not stop being heavy, nor did the work become effortless—for in their labor was their purpose—but the stone was made true. The wedges of gristle found purchase that held; the unmortared walls stood not for centuries, but for eras.

***

**Second, it tuned the deep.**

In the small stone shelters beneath the basalt lintels, where the tenders gathered to press their reed-tubes together in the lime-rich drifts, the acoustics had always been bounded by the thickness of the rock. Their low, pneumatic humming could travel only a valley or two before the violent percussion of the kelp-forest drowned it out. An elder dying on an isolated col died into local silence.

The Power infused the bedrock of the entire cordillera with an acoustic permeability. It tuned the granite until the whole mountain chain acted as a single, sympathetic sounding board.

When the dwellers in the northernmost saddle cycled their pressurized brine through their reed-bundles, the vibration did not fade in the silt. It entered the floor, carried through the basalt ribs of the peaks, and emerged hundreds of miles away in the southern troughs as a clear, steady drone. 

For the first time, the tenders knew the full breadth of their own kind. They felt the rhythm of distant breakwaters rising; they registered the synchronized harvest of seed-pods across four mountain chains; they felt the collective thrum of their civilization holding back the sea. They were not alone in the dark. The cordillera had become a single instrument, and they were all playing the same slow, patient hymn.

***

**Third, and most gently, it answered.**

A tender’s life ends not in rot, but in petrifaction. Over centuries, their hydraulic vents silt over with iron and lime; their gristle stiffens into flint; their plates fuse permanently with the granite they have spent their lives levering. In their final days, as the brine ceases to circulate through their chambers, they experience a narrowing—a gradual settling of silence as their internal senses lock into the stone.

The Power did not prevent this death, for to live forever as stone is the natural completion of their lifecycle; it is how the breakwaters are renewed. 

Instead, the Power met them at the threshold.

When a tender locks its broad, keeled back into the angle of a wall for the last time, and its internal vents release their final, faint plume of brine into the current, it feels a sudden, profound resonance rising from the granite beneath it. 

It is not an alien voice. It is the sound of the ocean’s weight made loving—a deep, infrasonic tone that matches the precise frequency of its stiffening shell. The Power takes the memory of every stone that tender ever lifted, every seed-pod it ever sheltered from the shear, every reed-note it ever blew into the dark, and absorbs it into the mountain’s permanent memory. 

The dying tender does not feel its consciousness extinguish in the cold; it feels itself expand into the ridge. It feels the entire mountain range take up the burden of its weight. It ceases to be a tender of the wall; it becomes the wall itself, held safely within the living awareness of the deep.

***

Above, on the surface of the sea, the world of air and sunlight goes on—ships pass, storms whip the waves to foam, and the sun rises and sets over an ocean that appears empty and flat. 

Four hundred fathoms down, in the absolute and freezing black, nothing has changed that an unwise eye would notice. The violent currents still roar across the cols; the iron-ribbed kelp still clatters like snapping bone; the dense hydraulic mass leans with crushing indifference against the peaks. 

Yet the garden flourishes. The unmortared walls curve gracefully across the saddles, catching the drifting silt. The monumental seed-pods swell and burst on schedule, sending their soft shockwaves through the valleys. And through the soles of every hydraulic dweller, through every reed-pipe and basalt slab, there hums a low, eternal, third note in the chord: 

*You are seen. You are sufficient. Endure.*
