I am not in the middle of the sky where the broad rafts drift, and I am not deep in the warm, dry ozone of the hexagonal air shafts. 

I am wedged into the shear-edge—the absolute knife-line where the underside of a floating basalt terrace meets forty miles of open drop.

To exist here is to be an instrument played by the air.

My body does not have the luxury of bulk. I am long, narrow, and jointed in seven places, built of ribbed cartilage and translucent, oil-sheened skin that feels like wet horn. I do not have front or back in the way earth-bound things do; I have an *anchor-side* and an *open-side*.

My anchor-side is lined with three dozen soft, saucer-shaped pads that sweat a thick, clear resin. They do not merely stick to the carbon-glass and synthetic basalt; they sink into the microscopic pores of the structure, fusing my flesh to the foundation until the vibrations of the quantum-pinning nodes pass directly through my breastplate and into the fluid of my inner cavities. I don't listen to the world with ears. My entire ventral plate is a thin, dry drumhead. When the basalt thrums with its low, bone-grinding baseline, my stomach aches with the exact frequency of the city’s mass.

My open-side belongs to the void.

From my dorsal ridge, four long, needle-fine antennae arch outward into the slate-colored haze, extending twelve feet into nothingness. They are strung with microscopic, hair-like cilia that catch the drift: the copper-spore dust, the microscopic grease shed by the cable-lice grazing three pylons away, the thermal slipstreams curling off the deep foundation radiators. 

I live in the overlap of two kingdoms. I am too heavy to walk on water droplets like the surge-skaters, and too long-limbed to graze the moss like the disc-beetles. Instead, I am a harvester of tension.

Below me, a chime-weaver has anchored three long, singing cables between my terrace and a needle-pylon two miles to the south. The filaments pass within inches of my resting crook. I keep two of my elongated, hook-tipped forelegs resting lightly upon the top string—not to break it, but to steal its news.

Through the silk, I feel the whole hollow breathe.

A Vitreovane has just banked three leagues out; its wake sends a faint, rhythmic shudder down the wire, like a finger drawn across a wet glass rim. Half a mile down the line, a cluster of cable-lice have lost their hydraulic grip in a gust of thermal condensation; their tiny, tumbling impacts tap against the filament like hail: *tick... tick-tick... tick.* 

I do not drop after them. To let go of the stone is death; my body has no wings, only the geometry to resist the wind. Instead, I extend my mouth-whip—a jointed, ribbon-like tongue coated in sticky digestive enzymes—and let it trail down the vibrating wire, scooping up the sheared fragments of moss and pulverized chitin that slide down the thread like beads on an abacus. It tastes of cold copper, wet nitrogen, and the faint, bitter tang of synthetic glass.

And then, the countdown begins in my blood.

It is not a thought; it is an electro-chemical pressure that builds behind my eyes. 

Seventeen minutes since the last surge. The ambient hum begins to swell, its texture thickening from a dry rattle into an oily, pervasive roar that makes my anchoring pads tingle and burn.

Eighteen minutes. The chime-weaver’s thread stops singing its light, erratic gossip. The weaver itself is flattening into a crevice; the cable-lice have seated their hydraulic skirts with a wet, vacuum snap. 

Nineteen minutes. The air temperature spikes three degrees as the foundation radiators dump their thermal waste. The warm, bruised indigo of the structural joints flickers, turning pale and hard. The copper fronds along my basalt ledge begin their frantic, curling retreat, their dry leaves rasping against my flank like parchment. 

My ventral drumhead goes dead. The sound has dropped below acoustic vibration into pure, tectonic displacement. The marrow in my long bones feels suddenly heavy, like mercury.

I pull my antennae tight against my spine. I dig my resin-pads into the basalt until the suction turns white against the black stone. 

*The snap.*

The world shears upward. The drone screams into the higher octave—a piercing, crystalline whistle that strips the moisture from the air in a single heartbeat.

Gravity unhooks its claws.

My belly lifts from the basalt. For six seconds, the stone stops pulling; the forty-mile abyss below me ceases to be a pit and becomes merely a room. 

All around me, the falling sheets of condensation shatter into trillions of perfect, stationary spheres. They hang in the indigo dimness like an infinite chandelier, perfectly spaced, trembling with the screaming high-octave field. 

In this six-second eternity, I do not hunt. I do not eat. 

I extend my body to its fullest length, arching off the stone into the weightless gulf, anchored only by the two rearmost pads on the tip of my tail. 

I reach out with my forelegs and touch the nearest suspended pearl of water. It does not break; the magnetic tension holds it rigid as a marble. Reflected in its curved, indigo surface is the vast, impossible architecture of the hollow: the thirty-mile needle-spars glowing like heated wire, the ghost-silhouettes of the hanging cities, the pale, frozen shape of a glider caught mid-bank in the slate-colored haze.

For six seconds, I am not clinging to the edge of a void. I am resting in the center of a crystal.

Then the octave breaks.

The high scream drops with the violence of a guillotine back into the guttural, planetary roar. 

Gravity slams downward like an iron slab. The trillions of glass pearls collapse in an instantaneous, roaring deluge, a vertical river that smashes against my carapace and washes over the basalt in a warm, drenching wave. 

The wind tears at my flanks, howling with renewed fury. 

My tail contracts, snapping me back against the soaking, resin-slick basalt. My anchoring pads bite deep into the stone, sealing tight against the torrent. My dorsal antennae whip outward once more, slicing into the wet, warm draft, drinking the electric aftertaste of the pulse.

Below me, the chime-weaver’s lines begin to hum again, settling back into their low, obedient drone. 

I settle my drumhead against the vibrating rock, taste the copper-rich runoff dripping down my face, and wait for the nineteen minutes to begin their slow, beautiful crawl through my bones.
