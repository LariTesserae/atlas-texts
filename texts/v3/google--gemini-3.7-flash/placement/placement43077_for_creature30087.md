I would not begin as a thing with edges. 

To exist here is first to be the *conduction* itself—the bruised-zinc broth pressed flat against your surfaces with the weight of five vertical miles of black water, the ache of a medium so packed with dissolved iron and acoustic shock that it cannot distinguish between carrying a sound and delivering a blow.

At first, I would feel like a hesitation in the current: a pocket where the deafening, apartment-sized piston-strokes do not tear the fluid apart, but collide from four directions at once, folding the water into a tight, standing knot. 

If you searched for me early, you would only find the drift:
A shredded braid of copper-xylem torn loose from an overcharged Stator-Tree; a plate of corrugated tin-leaf still sizzling where a purple arc cleaved it from its bough; a handful of heavy, brassy spore-nodules tumbling downward into an inverted gravity pocket, stopping dead, and shivering in mid-water.

---

Where I settle—where the form finally takes hold—is inside the cracked throat of a drowned intake manifold, twenty feet wide, bolted to the basalt on the lip of the central rift.

I am not an animal that crawled inside, nor a tree that grew there. I am the **Accretion**. 

My core is a massive, cold-worked ball of petrified sap, dense as vulcanized cannon-rubber and shot through with miles of hair-fine copper filament. Over decades of hammer-blow resonance, the violent acoustic pulses of the distant turbines have hammered me against the interior fluting of the iron manifold, peening my outer shell until it matches the rifling of the machine with microscopic precision. I fit the iron throat like a jammed piston, held in place by the endless, howling gale of superheated brine.

I do not have organs; I have *tolerances*.

Around my perimeter, where the iron of the pump housing meets my dense, resinous flesh, the Galvanic Scum has burrowed deep into my skin. It feeds on the high-frequency friction between my body and the cast-iron casing. We are welded together by a thick, diesel-smelling slime that acts simultaneously as an adhesive and an industrial lubricant. Through this scum-ring, I can feel the entire architecture of the valley: the shudder of the conveyor bridges half-a-mile west, the rhythmic, gut-emptying cough of the Bellows-Galls discharging gas-ice into the trench, and the high, needle-thin whine of the Auger-Thorns drilling blind through the mid-depths.

I do not photosynthesize; I do not ground high voltage like the trees above me. 

I am a **Harmonic Choke**.

When the Stator-Trees arc overhead, vomiting thousands of amperes into the brine and flash-boiling the basin into sheets of white, screaming steam, the immense pressure wave slams downward into the rift. Most structures are sheared clean off at their footings. But my body, layered with alternating bands of silicified cellulose and lead foil, absorbs the shockwave. 

I compress. 

Under the sledgehammer blow, my core yields by three inches, squeezing the internal reservoirs of pine-scented hydraulic sap through thousands of micro-porous capillary vents. As the sap is forced through these rigid, microscopic nozzles under thousands of pounds of pressure, it crystallizes into fine, razor-sharp needles of pressurized amber-ice. 

Then the pressure wave passes. 

My compressed rubber-lignin springs back with violent, elastic torque. The intake manifold groans, the basalt beneath it cracks anew, and I violently vomit that charge of crystallized sap and metallic grit back out into the open current, spraying a dense cloud of abrasive, seed-bearing shrapnel directly into the path of the unhoused propeller screws.

The propellers chew through my discharge, coating their cathedral-sized bronze blades in sticky, high-pressure sap. The seeds immediately take purchase in the microscopic pits worn into the metal by cavitation, sending out clawing holdfasts that dig into the spinning alloy, feeding on the friction, slowing the great shafts down by fractions of a millimeter per century.

---

I do not know the sky. I do not have a concept of "up," because "up" is merely the direction where the acoustic trauma sometimes slackens enough to let the Inverted Drifts fall downward like lead anvils, before the next engine-stroke blows them toward the surface again.

I am the point where the machinery’s exhaustion becomes the plant’s structural foundation. 

My "sight" is the interference pattern of the sound waves bouncing off the basalt walls, translated through the copper filaments braided into my marrow. I know the valley as a shifting, vibrating map of shear-stresses, thermal plumes, and torque. 

I know when an Auger-Thorn has struck a conveyor bridge because the fundamental frequency of the basin drops half a semi-tone. I know when a thermal vent is about to erupt because the Galvanic Scum along my flank begins to weep pure zinc into the iron fluting, anticipating the heat.

I am neither alive nor dead in any sense that a dry world would recognize. I am a valve that has grown thick with wood; a seed that has encased itself in cold-hammered brass; a perpetual, unyielding bruise in the throat of an engine that cannot stop running, and will never finish tearing itself apart.
