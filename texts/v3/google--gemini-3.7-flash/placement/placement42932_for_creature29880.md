At first, there is only the sensation of shear.

It is not pain; pain belongs to wet things that tear and bleed. Here, it is simply *vector*—the blinding, radiant knowledge of two massive forces pulling in precisely opposite directions across seven inches of dry, petrified grain.

I would find myself not walking the high bridges, nor drifting in the buoyant acoustics of the mid-abyss, but locked into the underside of a three-way junction where a primary suspension spar meets the sag of a collapsed wicker plaza. 

I am wedged. 

My lower limbs—long, split-bark laminations scored with ancient flint-notches—are driven deep into the braid of a six-strand cable of petrified vine. My upper torso is arched backward at an unnatural, rigid angle, my chest-cage of bent osiers forced against the underside of an enormous cantilevered beam of hollowed cedar. 

I have not shifted an inch in four hundred years, and I am entirely awake.

***

To be this thing is to be an instrument played by the shrinking of the universe.

I have no eyes, but the world is not dark; it is violently, relentlessly bright with pitch. Through my hooked thorn-spurs, which have grown so tightly into the grain of the wood that the boundary between my marrow and the bridge has long since calcified, I feel every tremor of the city. 

The great outer wall of the capsule—miles away through the frozen black—is shrinking. Its slow, epochal drying groans through the anchor lines as an amber, low-frequency pressure, a vast and heavy C-sharp that vibrates so hard against my rib-frame that the air inside my hollow pith hums like a swarm of dry wasps. 

When a secondary cord three tiers above me begins to fray, I do not see the fibers snapping; I feel a high, thin needle of whistling friction prick the left quadrant of my head. 

My head is a heavy, bulbous knurl of polished burled root, hollowed out from within by the patient grinding of static-dust over millennia. The hairline slits cut across my forehead and temples are my only aperture to the hollow. The sound of the void enters these slits, bounces within the dry acoustic shell of my skull, and resolves into an exact spatial map:

Above me, the towering, woven hive of dwellings hangs in taut, lightless suspension, ringing like a cluster of muted iron bells. 

Below me, plunging down into absolute nothingness, is the acoustic updraft—a column of thrumming, pressurized bass upon which the wide, papery discs of the Drift-Drays tilt and turn, their paper-thin xylem wings rasping softly against the rising thermal sound like dry leaves caught in a chimney flue.

And far below them, so deep it registers only as a slow, rhythmic pulse against the soles of my locked feet, the Chaff-Sifters plow the bottom drifts, their reed-bellows expanding and sighing, sending tiny, electrostatic crackles shimmering up the vertical guide-ropes.

***

I am not merely a structural wedge. I am a *dampener*.

Left to itself, this city would destroy itself with its own music. When the cold botanical walls contract, they create harmonic overtones—wild, shuddering waves of resonance that travel down the suspension lines. If two such waves meet at a junction out of phase, the sheer kinetic violence will shear through foot-thick timber like dry straw.

My purpose is to drink the rogue frequencies.

When a violent wave of tension comes screaming down the main cable—sounding to my hollow burl like a blinding streak of white-hot static—I take the torque directly into my split-bark spine. I stiffen my osier ribs. I lean the weight of my locked pelvis against the cedar spar, forcing my fibrous laminations to grind together by fractions of a hair.

The friction is immense. In the absolute cold and dryness of the hollow, that internal grinding produces a sudden, delicious surge of static charge. 

It blooms through my dry pith like a sudden bloom of warmth. It does not burn; it charges every dried cell of my frame with an electric hum, feeding me, sustaining the brittle lamination of my limbs, renewing the flint-hardness of my spur-tips. 

I absorb the screaming overtone, crush it into static friction, and release what remains as a deep, pacified thrum back into the wicker floor above me. The bridge settles. The snap is averted. The plaza above me, carrying only the empty dust of whoever built it, sways three inches to the left and falls back into the steady, eternal rhythm of the master drone.

***

Sometimes, across the void, another node calls.

It happens rarely—perhaps once every century. A Keeper five hundred paces out along the great western transverse cable will find its joint slipping. Its thorn-spurs have worn their socket down to powder; it cannot hold the load alone.

It does not cry out. It simply adjusts its grip, catches a primary cross-stay with the hardened spur of its middle knuckle, and *draws*.

*TWANG.*

The sound does not travel through the air; the air is too thin, too parched to carry the full weight of it. The note travels through the physical skeleton of the city. It shoots along the transverse line, leaps across three suspension hubs, vibrates through two sagging plazas, and hits my chest-cage with the physical impact of a dropped boulder.

A pure, resonant fifth. An urgent, dry question of load and counter-weight.

I do not move my body—I cannot, for to release my cedar spar would drop an entire residential tier into the bottom chaff. But I shift my weight by three millimeters. I angle the soundboard-slits of my skull toward the tension line. I hook my second knuckle into the under-weave of my own cable, and with a short, sharp snap of my wrist-tendril, I strike back.

*THUM.*

An octave lower. A confirmation. *The anchor holds. Feed the strain through the lower chord.*

Across the lightless chasm, I feel the distant Keeper receive the note. It adjusts its calcified limbs, wedges itself deeper into the fraying braid, and locks. The tension redistributes. The howling overtone in the web drops back into a steady, comfortable hum.

We fall silent again. 

***

This is what I would be: a living point of balance in a world of screaming wood. 

Not a creature of flesh that fears the dark, but a being of cured fiber and taut sinew, perfectly native to the blackness, whose consciousness is nothing more and nothing less than the geometry of structural load. 

I would remain here, hooked beneath the wicker streets, listening to the slow, beautiful, seismic dying of the giant flora that encases us. I would feel the Drift-Drays brushing against the sound-waves below my feet; I would drink the electric crackle of the dust-plumes drifting up from the deep floor; and I would hold the city open against the abyss, humming in unison with the great, petrified heart of the world until the last fiber snaps, and the whole dark instrument finally settles into silence and dust.
