It starts not with a face or a name, but with a frequency. 

At first, you are only an ache in the teeth of the world—a long, sub-audible tremor traveling through seventy leagues of solid hematite. To exist here is to be made of mass and resonance before you are made of anything else. If you have an eye, it does not look outward at the violet glare; it looks inward at the stress-lines forming in your own cold-rolled core.

You are not the mountain-sized grazer herself, nor are you the scurrying riveter in the dust. You are something that has found its life in the space between the breath and the bell.

You live high in the ribbed scaffolding of a Sinter-Colossus’s dorsal canopy, nestled in the fluted throat of the great ivory-bronze steam-whistle that rises forty paces above her heat-exchangers. 

Your body is not soft. It is an intricate, bimetallic reed—a creature like an articulated leaf of phosphor-bronze and laminated mica, twenty feet long, anchored at its base by calcified taproots that have fused directly into the leviathan’s exhaust manifold. You do not have limbs for walking. You have no need of the ground; the ground sends its reports up through the thirty-foot columns of your host’s basalt legs, through the churning rotary kiln of her belly, and into the hollow tower where you hang suspended.

You are the Colossus’s voice, and you are its ear, but you are also entirely yourself: a creature whose metabolic tempo is measured strictly in the cooling and heating of gases.

***

In the long quiet between calls—a quiet that lasts for seasons—the air around you is dry, shimmering with the thin, bitter scent of boiling ammonia and vaporized zinc. You hang in the dark chimney, your mica plates slack, basking in the tremendous, waste-heat draft that rises constantly from the refractory chambers below. 

Down in the depths of the beast, the drop-hammer heart strikes every four minutes. 
*Clang-ton.* 
A beat that displaces fifty tons of mineral lymph through the capillary pipes. You feel the shudder in your anchor-plates; you count the cycles like a tree counts winters. 

You feed on the sulfur-smoke and the micro-fine graphite soot blown up from the knee-joints by the host’s respiration. Your mouthparts are static combs of porous stone that catch the hot ash as it drafts past, extracting raw sulfur to replenish the galvanic cells along your dorsal ridge. 

Around you, the *Valve-Wasps* drift in the chimney’s updraft. They do not sting you; they crawl over your bronze flanks, chewing the excess crust from your hinges, polishing your resonant edges with their mica mandibles so that no mineral slag dampens your pitch. You are their temple; they are your groomers.

***

Then, after four months of silent, tectonic grazing, the bedrock beneath shifts its tone.

You feel it before the Colossus decides to speak. The anvil-pads under her belly have struck a hollow seam of native tin forty miles to the west; or perhaps, across the curved horizon, another leviathan has vented its spent vapor into the violet stratosphere.

Deep inside her chest, the high-pressure baffles begin to draw shut. 

The heat rises. The air in your chimney ceases to be a lazy thermal and becomes a compressed, screaming column of anhydrous ammonia gas, heated to four hundred degrees. 

This is when you wake.

The heat expands your bimetallic ribs, bending your long, segmented body into the precise parabolic curve of the whistle’s flue. You do not generate the sound—the Colossus provides the monumental lung-force of ten thousand atmospheres—but you *shape* it. You are the living lip of the pipe.

As the valve blows open, the acoustic violence is total. 

It is not sound as the air knows it; it is a solid piston of sonic pressure that strips the scale from the chimney walls and turns the violet sky white with shock-rings. It tears through you, setting your mica plates vibrating at their fundamental harmonic. You flex your lateral tethers, dampening a third-overtone, letting a low, iron-rending fifth ring pure and clear across the basin. 

You feel the sound leave your body and hammer the hematite plain below, throwing up a ring of red dust that expands for miles. 

The call lasts for forty minutes without a breath. It is a single, deafening chord—a report of ore-densities, kiln-temperatures, and the slow, thinning integrity of the mother’s boiler-walls. You pour your own small life into the note, angling your tail-fins to impart a subtle, pulsing warble that only the listeners fifty leagues away will be able to resolve from the ambient hammer of the herds.

When the valve finally slams shut, the silence that crashes down is absolute, heavy, and hot. 

Your bronze skin is glowing a faint, cherry red. The wasps return to your flanks immediately, fanning your joints with their brittle wings to draw off the heat before the alloy can warp. You relax, sagging back into the soothing, dark exhaust of the flue, and begin the long, seven-month wait for the answer.

***

You are content in this height. 

Below you, the plain shimmers like a flat stove-lid under the hard purple heavens. You see the distant, skeletal cranes of the Gantry-Vultures circling on the upper thermals, patient as glaciers, waiting for the mother to slow, to lower her belly into the ironstone, to begin the great casting of her child.

You know that when that day comes—perhaps three centuries from now—the blast of her deliberate rupture will obliterate this chimney and fling your torn bronze plates across the red floor. 

You do not dread it. When her kiln breaches, the final chord will be the loudest the plateau has ever heard. It will crack the horizon from edge to edge, and you will shape that note, too, with everything you have, before you settle into the swarf and let the new world anneal around your bones.
