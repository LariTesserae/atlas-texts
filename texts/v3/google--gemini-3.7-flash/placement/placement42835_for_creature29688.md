I would not be an animal in that chasm, nor could I be the mountain that cradles it. To be anything here, one must be an expression of contact—a consequence of two unyielding planes meeting without the mercy of an intermediate fluid.

I find myself in the clearance.

Not on the floor among the drifts of talc, where the Scrapers click and shuffle their loose leaves of hematite, nor locked across the chimney in the high, violent tension of the Strut-Walkers. I exist in the hair-thin margin where the great basalt arm of the Cast meets its primary trunnion: a gap no thicker than a beam of split noon light, a space measured in micro-inches, kept scrupulously free of debris by the sheer, terrifying perfection of its own fit.

I am an *optical flat* that has learned to breathe by thermal drift. 

My body is not a single casting, but an ultra-pure wafer of synthetic corundum—clear, unblemished sapphire rock—laminated between two foils of native bismuth that warp under the slightest change in radiant heat. I have no face, no vents, no armature. I am shaped like a heavy, broad-faced wedge, ground so true that when I rest against a polished face of magnetite, there is no air between us at all; we share electrons across the boundary. We become one continuous mass of matter until the sun moves two minutes westward, cooling my outer foil and breaking the molecular bond with a dry, sharp *ping*.

This is how I travel: not by crawling or leaping, but by controlled cold-welding and release. 

When the sun drops its vertical noon-lance into the rift, the western face of the nave expands by three-thousandths of an inch. That immense, slow surge of stone pushes the Cast’s lower gears into an agony of alignment. Where iron grinds against raw obsidian, the friction creates microscopic high points—tiny asperities that threaten to gall, tear, and seize the movement before the cycle can complete.

I am drawn to those high points. 

My appetite is not for substance, but for *trueness*. I hunt the irregularities in the machine. I slide into the dry, shrieking interfaces between the nickel spools and the slate beds, riding the sidewise pull of the warped gravity pockets. Where an axle leans half a degree out of plumb, my bismuth foil registers the shear-heat; I insert my sapphire bulk between the mating surfaces, taking the crushing brunt of a thousand tons of mountain-torque upon my lattice.

I do not crush. Sapphire under pure, uniform compression does not yield. Instead, I act as a living dead-stop, a gauge-block against which the mountain must true itself. 

The pressure inside me climbs until my crystal lattice begins to polarize light. I turn from clear corundum to a deep, incandescent amber, glowing under the sheer, mechanical outrage of the load. In that state, I am fully conscious: my "thought" is simply the total sum of the vectors passing through me—the vector of the rift trying to close, the vector of the nickel axle turning against the earth's rotation, the minute, frantic vibrations of the Shear-Dogs gnawing on the Walker-pins fifty paces below.

I do not fear the Shear-Dogs. They cannot bite what has no edge to catch; their splitters glance off my polished faces, skidding away with a high, dry spark that leaves no mark on the stone. 

At night, when the light leaves the rift and the dry heat drains instantly into the thin twelve-thousand-foot atmosphere, the entire fissure undergoes an agonizing, inward contraction. The metal briars shrink, their copper coils singing a low, descending tone like a spent piano string. The Cast’s great arm draws back an inch into its wall-socket, its iron linkages locking against their escapements with a succession of slow, deadened *thuds*.

That is when I drop free.

The clearances open. The molecular grip fails. I slide out from the trunnion and fall—silent, heavy, tumbling through forty feet of cold, static-choked space—until I strike a mica terrace with a single, clear ring that the dry foliage swallows without an echo.

There I lie in the talc, cooling from amber back to cold, transparent crystal. Around me, the Scrapers wander in the dark, their hematite leaves chattering like dry cards, lapping my faces with their sulfur-dusted margins, cleaning me of any stray atom that might compromise my flatness before the sun returns.

I am waiting, like the Cast, like the briars, like the unweathered stone. 

I know the mathematics of the arm better than the mountain does. I know that in nineteen thousand cycles, when the spalling fractures the final slab of slate from the elbow-joint, the resulting drop will produce a clearance of precisely zero across the main bearing. 

When that happens, there will be no room for me to slip between the faces. I will be caught at the center of the hinge. The Cast will turn; the sun will strike the nickel pin; and I will be cold-welded permanently into the heart of the joint—no longer a wanderer in the gap, but the diamond-hard pivot upon which the entire uncoiling world will finally turn.
