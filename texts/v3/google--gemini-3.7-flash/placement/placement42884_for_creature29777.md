At first, there is only the sensation of being held in extreme, unyielding tautness.

Not on the floor, where the Loomers take the brunt of the mass, and not inside the dead-calm vacuum of the suites where the Curators roll through their dry, geometric loops. 

I would be higher up, in the dark transitional collar where the unhewn basalt of the roof—cold, dumb, carrying the crushing weight of four hundred meters of dead crust—meets the first micrometric collars of the black metamaterial. 

It is the only place in the vault where the machine touches the raw earth. 

Here, the engineering is not quite seamless. It cannot be. The stone is irregular, fractured by ancient seismic stresses, sweating its own slow, mineral-heavy seepage that the siphon’s deionized sheets must endlessly wash away. Between the hyper-precise, razor-turned sleeves of the suspension pillars and the rough socket of the mountain, there is a boundary layer: a ring of dense, flexible mastic, four inches thick, designed to absorb the micro-sway of three suspended cathedrals.

That is the niche. I would not be an observer; this place does not tolerate sight that isn't functional. I would be the interstitial material itself, or the thing that has grown inside its damp, compressed grain.

Call it a **Null-Tether** or a **Shear-Grit**.

My body is flat, laminated, and immense in circumference though only millimeters thick, wrapping entirely around the neck of the primary anchor like an oily, fibrous gasket. I have no front or back. I am stratified: my upper face is rough, textured with microscopic silicate teeth that bite into the jagged ceiling rock, anchoring into its feldspar veins; my lower face is frictionless, coated in a self-replenishing lipid that allows the black monolith to slide, pivot, and breathe against me without shearing itself away from the crust.

I feel the entire district not as an image, but as a dynamic vector sum.

Every time a cascade breaks from a mid-tier landing, the suspension cable hums down into my tissue as a three-hertz shudder. When a colony of Lumen-Veils aligns within the falling torrent to siphon kinetic drag, the balance of load shifts—a faint, rhythmic tug toward the north sump that ripples through my lateral sensors. 

I live on the friction of the city trying to fall. 

The weight of the whole suspended apparatus—the stacked apartments, the moss-slick sky-bridges, the thousands of tons of falling, recirculating water—hangs from my collar. That immense, continuous downward draw generates a piezoelectric yield across my cellular plates. I don't eat the algae, and I don't scrape the hexagonal moss; I feed directly on the strain. The harder the city pulls toward the earth's core, the more current bleeds through my marrow, keeping the oily fluids in my joints warm and ionized.

My sense of time is strictly the three-minute purge.

In the long middle of the cycle, there is only the thick, unchanging baseline: the deafening white wash of the cataracts, the high-voltage thrum passing up the core of the pillar, and the steady, cold pressure of the ceiling rock pressing down against the hot, vibrating pull of the vault. It is an ecstatic, motionless equilibrium. The deionized mist drifts up this high, but it arrives only as a warm, superfine vapor that condenses on my lower margins, washing away the stray flecks of basalt grit before they can score the metamaterial sleeve.

Then, the hesitation begins.

A fraction of a second before the systemic purge, the field-conduits in the monolith drop their load. The gigawatt hum shifts up an octave, thinning into a needle-sharp whine. For two heartbeats, the gravity of the vault feels altered: the upward blast of thermal exhaust lifts the cantilevers by half a millimeter. 

In that micro-slack, the tension eases. The pressure on my body drops from hundreds of atmospheres to almost nothing. 

It is the only moment I can move.

During the purge’s upward draft, as the sheet-water reverses and boils into lavender-scented steam against the hot modules, my laminated plates unseal. I ripple around the circumference of the pillar like a muscle contracting under skin. I clear the accumulated mineral salts that have seeped from the rock, grinding them between my upper silicate pads and extruding them into the micro-perforated conduit rings to be flushed down with the next cycle. I re-lubricate the metamaterial sleeve. I adjust the city’s trim by a fraction of a micron, leaning against the basalt to correct for the growth-weight of the four-meter fronds on the south cables.

Then the pressure drops with a concussive thud. 

The thermal updraft cuts out. The cascades tear free from the high rings and plunge downward again. The entire weight of the Sub-Plaza slams back into its hang, catching me, flattening me instantly into a dense, solid ring of living carbon and oil.

The current surges through me again—hot, violent, and steady. The diamond-crisp edges of the sky-bridges settle below in the mist, perfectly level, perfectly true, held suspended in the hollow dark by the single, breathless clamp of my skin against the stone.
