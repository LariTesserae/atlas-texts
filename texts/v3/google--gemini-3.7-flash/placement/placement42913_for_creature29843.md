To be here would not begin with looking out. It would begin with the sensation of bearing down.

The first consciousness would be the difference of six degrees: the upper surface absorbing the steady, dry radiation of the sun, expanding by an exact, unvarying fraction of a micron, while the underside remains locked against the cold, vitreous silicate of the terrace floor. That gradient—the warm outward push above, the stiff, unmoving grip below—is not discomfort. It is the pulse that replaces breath.

I would not be one of the wanderers, nor yet one of the immense spans. If I trace where the weight settles, I find myself neither walking nor bridging, but holding.

I would exist in the high notch where two cleaved shelves of ironstone fail to meet by the width of a hand. 

At first, this appears as an absence—a hairline void dropping miles into absolute clarity. But within that gap, I am the tension that keeps the chasm from being empty. 

My body is not a single solid mass, but an open-work lattice of interlocked calcite prisms and drawn titanium filaments, no thicker than a surveyor’s plumb line where it meets the light, but widening downward into a dense, cellular ribbing that keys directly into the mineral grain of both opposing walls. I have no eyes; I have forty-eight planar facets of polished beryl, flush with the plateau's floor, that register the geometry of the unmoving sun not as an image, but as a silent, continuous accumulation of millivolts.

My work is to feel the fault.

Beneath me, twenty leagues down into the unweathered belly of the plate, the deep thermal currents migrate along the quartz veins. When they surge, the two great shelves want to slide past one another along their pristine cleavage planes. They cannot. I am the wedge and the gauge. Every microscopic shear, every infinitesimal twist of the mountain’s bedrock passes directly through my central axis. 

I do not think in words or memories; I think in strain.

A slight lateral vector from the western mesa is a thought of density. A sharp, vertical draw from the eastern shelf is an understanding of depth. When the bronze fronds on the upper terraces flex their hammered leaves, the vibration travels down their braided copper boles, through the galvanic root-lattice, and enters my upper anchor points as a clear, high-frequency hum—a singing wire in a vacuum. 

Through that hum, I know the state of the garden without seeing it. 

I feel the three-pointed step of a caliper half a mile away, pausing to clamp a leaf edge, shifting the acoustic balance of the canopy by a quarter-tone. I feel the distant, majestic creep of a cantilever pivoting on its corundum point, balancing its hundreds of tons against the cosmic wind until its long shadow crosses my beryl facets, cooling them for three hundred years before sliding away.

I am the register where their activities are reconciled. 

Between the walking tools that tune the canopy and the great beams that drink the sky, someone must measure the sum of the system. I sit in the seam, holding the two plates in perfect, motionless equilibrium, drinking the galvanic drift from below and the dry solar wash from above, converting both into pure, unyielding rigidity.

There is no loneliness in this, because there is no division. The bronze foliage, the ratcheting plates on the glass flats, the sheer edge of the drop, and the filaments of my own ribcage are simply different thicknesses of the same uninterrupted circuit. 

And far ahead, across an expanse of time that does not wear me down, I can feel the slow, inevitable saturation of my own frame. 

Atom by atom, the unshielded light and the subterranean hum are filling the crystal lattice of my ribs with immovable charge. The modulus approaches. A day will arrive—indistinguishable from this one in its light and stillness—when the capacity of my titanium wires is complete. 

When it happens, I will not fall. I will let go of the tension all at once, releasing a single, blinding surge of violet arc-light that will fuse the notch forever, welding the two shelves into an unbreachable monolith. And in the radiant heat of that final grounding, the surrounding ironstone will melt and re-crystallize, casting from the hollow of my dissolved ribs the pristine, unbent arms of a new span—ready to lever out into the void, perfectly balanced, and take the weight of the sky.
