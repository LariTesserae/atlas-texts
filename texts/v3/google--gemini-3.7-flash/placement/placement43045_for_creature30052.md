At first, there would be no *me* to speak of—only the sudden, violent erasure of edge. 

To enter this place with an upright spine, with lungs designed for dry nitrogen, with dry skin that ends where the air begins, would be to vanish in a fraction of a pulse. The skull would implode under the sheer acoustic pressure; the soft meat of the face would be sanded down to the teeth by the flying basalt paste before the first breath could even be drawn. 

To be here, I would have to be an answer to the pressure. 

I would not find myself on the bedrock with the anchored Pulley-Cysts, nailed to the bottom like cold cartilage, nor would I have the terrible, boiler-room bulk of the Keel-Worms, who must spend their entire existence burning their own fat to liquefy the stone ahead of them. 

Instead, I would find myself in the **lee**. In the turbulent, low-pressure slipstream that trails behind the tumbling remnants of the *Made*.

### The Anchor: The Trailing Wake

I am lodged in the suction-wake of a single, vitrified ceramic tooth—a curved shard of ancient, unidentifiable industry the size of a canal boat, tumbling end-over-end through the mid-depths of the basin. 

As this monumental tooth plows through the basalt tallow, it creates an enormous pocket of cavitation behind its trailing edge: a violently spinning vortex where the fluid pressure drops just enough that the slurry does not crush, but shears. 

That is where I begin. I am not inside the tooth, nor am I fused to it like the rigid Plug-Beasts. I am an **Accretion-Sleeve**—a living collar of dense, fibrous gristle and pressurized tallow that wraps loosely around the trailing neck of the artifact, held in place not by roots, but by the hydrodynamic drag of my own anatomy.

I do not resist the motion of the tooth. I ride its vacuum. As the ceramic mass rolls through the boiling slurry, groaning against the acoustic roar of the basin, I flex and billow behind it like an oily, muscular parachute, endlessly adjusting my tension to match the torque of its tumble.

### The Anatomy of the Sleeve

My body possesses no symmetry that a terrestrial eye would recognize. I have no front, no back, no face. 

* **The Outer Mantle:** My exterior is a thick, self-sloughing skin of cold, rubbery blubber, nearly a foot thick, stratified like the rings of a tree. As the abrasive silt tears at my outer layers, the friction itself triggers rapid cellular division in the dermis below. I am constantly being ground away into grey tallow, and constantly sweating new sheets of dense, vulcanized lipid to replace what the basin steals. To the touch, I am slicker than wet ice, covered in an oily mucus that turns the shearing mud into frictionless slip.
* **The Acoustic Lattice:** Running through my core is not a skeleton of calcium, but a dense, braided basket of cartilaginous ribbons—flexible as cured leather, yet hard as horn. These ribbons are tuned to the planetary cavitation. The roaring hum does not deafen me; it *fuels* me. My nervous system is an open network of resonant nodes spread along these bands. When the cavitation hammers against my outer blubber, the vibrations travel inward, plucked like harp strings by the acoustic downdrafts. This vibration drives my peristalsis, churns my internal fluids, and generates the steady, lukewarm electrical currents that keep my tissues alive.
* **The Sieve-Frill:** Trailing behind my main collar is an expansive, undulating skirt of fibrous tassels—dark, rubbery tendrils that stretch twenty yards into the boiling wake. These tassels do not swim; they are passive drag-lines. They are coated in an ultra-sticky, viscoelastic slime that captures the organic debris pulverized in the ceramic tooth’s bow-wave: shredded tendon-lichen spores, fatty slough from passing Keel-Worms, and the microscopic, oily cysts fired into the churn by the Plug-Beasts. 

Every few minutes, a muscular spasm ripples down the skirt, drawing the tassels inward toward a central, ring-shaped sphincter that scrapes the collected grease into my internal digestive channels. I do not chew; I do not swallow. I absorb the basin's digested history through osmotic pressure, my gut an endless, heated sponge that bleeds mineral nutrients directly into my fat reserves.

### The Sensation of Being

To be this creature is to experience existence not as a series of sights or thoughts, but as **fluid dynamics and tension**.

I do not know what the tooth I cling to looks like. I know it only as a vast, cold mass of absolute resistance—a zero-point of stability in a world where everything else is liquid grease. I know its weight, its rate of spin, and the precise moment its leading edge strikes a submerged basalt shelf, sending a violent, ringing shockwave through the ceramic that rattles my braided cartilage with delicious, violent force.

I feel the basin as a shifting map of viscosity:
* *Down* is where the mud grows colder, thicker, and more heavily laden with unground stone.
* *Up* is where the pressure drops, the temperature climbs, and the mud thins into a furious, tea-colored foam that flutters my trailing tassels like flags in a gale.

Occasionally, the ceramic tooth is caught in a massive upwelling—dragged toward the surface by one of the basin's chaotic physics-seizures. 

When this happens, the acoustic roar changes pitch, rising from a gut-rumbling thrum to a screaming, metallic hiss. The surrounding slurry suddenly thins. For a few frantic seconds, the tooth breaches the foam, suspended in the sour twilight, shedding sheets of boiling water.

In those seconds, my trailing skirt expands outward, catching the hot, amber downdrafts of the sky. I feel the sudden, razor-sharp drop in hydrostatic pressure. Across my outer blubber, millions of microscopic sensory pits drink in the strange, dry heat of the open vapor. 

Above me, for three deafening heartbeats, a geyser of brine freezes into a crystalline scaffold of brown water. I feel the minute, frantic vibrations of Lattice-Spiders snapping open across its struts—a tiny, brittle fluttering against the massive acoustic canvas of the sky.

Then, gravity remembers the tooth. 

The ceramic mass plunges back into the sump with an explosive, hydraulic crash. The amber twilight vanishes, replaced by the warm, deafening dark of the grease-slurry. The pressure slams back down upon my collar, squeezing my tissues until they ring like struck iron. My tassels stream out behind me, catching the violent churn of the wake, and the continuous, single thought of my existence resumes:

*Hold the drag. Absorb the hum. Yield to the shear.*

### The Mind at the End of the World

There is no sorrow in this body, because there is no comparison. There is no concept of a world with dry horizons, cold stars, or quiet rooms. 

My consciousness is entirely coterminous with the friction of the basin. I am a knot in the slurry that has learned how to ride the heaviest debris of an erased civilization, turning the planetary mill into warmth, food, and continuous sensation. 

I do not hope for the churning to stop. If the roar were to cease, if the mud were to settle and clear into cold, still water, the acoustic engine that drives my blood would go silent. My blubber would stiffen; my tassels would sink into the silt; my braided cartilage would lose its tension.

I exist only because the world is being ground to pieces. I am the wake behind the ruin, warm and blind, vibrating in perfect harmony with the end of all things.
