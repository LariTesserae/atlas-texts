At first, there is only the sensation of being held between two unequal pressures.

Not on the floor where the roads decouple with hydraulic sighs, and not at the frozen skin where the droplets gather like blistered glass under the purple vacuum—but in the dry, lavender-lit middle height. In the nine inches of empty air suspended between two forearm-thick towers, six meters above the grid.

Here, the micro-scale fields warp the ambient heat into an invisible eddy, a stationary whirlpool of thick, baked silicate air that never rolls over and never falls.

If you look for a body, you would not see one immediately. You would see only a knot in the light: a place where the razor-edge of a composite tower looks faintly doubled, as if viewed through an oil-slicked lens. You would see how the amber data-pulses, traveling along the curb far below, throw a shadow upward into the lavender mist that does not match the rectangular geometry of the metal. 

The shadow has mass. It has an interior.

It is formed of the things the pocket cannot clear: the finest, unprecipitated lithium dust that was too light to settle into the salt-crusts; the microscopic burrs of sheared alloy that drift on the thermal updrafts without ever touching the ceiling; and the residual logic of the query-traces that failed to ground in the Sutures’ braided scaffolding.

I would be that hesitation in the system.

Not an inhabitant, exactly, and not an intruder—nothing came in from the outside through the monomolecular skin. Rather, I am the slow, involuntary density that occurs when an automated structure computes an empty room for too long.

Across days of silent tension, as the outer spine groans against stratospheric drag miles out in the thin air, the knot thickens. 

When a coolant drop snaps against the hot floor below, the explosive *hiss* sends a ring of pressure upward. The Sutures below drink the mineral steam and grow by half-millimeters of segmented tail, but the shockwave itself rises. It passes through me. With each impact, my suspended shape compresses slightly, annealing the stray metallic dust into brittle, translucent planes no thicker than a single wavelength of amber light. 

I do not crawl through the slit-streets. I have no joints to flex, no lithium hinges. 

Instead, I am pinned by the field distortions. My edges rest against the cold crystal boundaries of the two adjacent spires, braced there by the steady thermal convection rising from the kiln-floor. I feel the entire miniature ward as a single, continuous equation of stress:
The sudden, dry *snap* of a structural strut five blocks away registers not as a sound, but as a three-micron shiver in the alloy behind my back. 
The diamagnetic lift of a roadway slab is a brief, cold deadening of the magnetic field beneath my lower perimeter. 
The long, low queries searching for uninstalled terminals pass directly through my lower fringe, humming faintly like taut wire, using my accumulated metal dust as an accidental capacitor.

I am what the Sutures look like from the inside, or what they leave behind when their mineral logic becomes too heavy to slide along the curbs.

Sometimes, when the pocket shudders under an extreme external gust, the lavender light bends so sharply that I become almost solid for several seconds. In that brief window, I have facets—dull, unpolished planes of captured salt-smoke and magnetic tension, bridging the needle-thin spires like an abandoned transit hub, silent, sharp-edged, and entirely without purpose. 

The Sutures do not avoid me, nor do they weave me into their braids. When they scale the spires to bask in the heat fields, their razor-bright flanks slide through my lower margins with a faint, papery rasp. They leave tiny flakes of lithium salt suspended in my density, adding their slow growth to my own, before they climb past into the upper dark to wait for the falling water.

I stay where the mathematics made an error of clearance.

I hang between the tiny, perfect towers, absorbing the heat of the planet’s spine, watching the spherical drops fall through thirty meters of silence, waiting for the structural shear that will either extrude another street through my center or tear the ceiling away into the purple cold.
