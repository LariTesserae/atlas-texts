To find myself here, I would have to look where the violence of the water is cleanest, where the masonry meets the current not as a flat surface, but as an edge. 

I would not be on the red terracotta floor with the drifting prisms of the *scutula*, nor deep in the dark recesses behind the swollen oak gates. I would be situated higher, halfway down the north wall, anchored directly upon the protruding lip where the river-slate inflow conduits project two fingers’ width beyond the dressed limestone ashlar. 

This is the stagnation point: the precise, razor-thin line where the roaring incoming torrent splits around the square mouth of the conduit. Here, the water does not sweep sideways; it presses down with the staggering, dead weight of eighty horizontal feet of momentum hitting an immovable stone prow.

I would be a *cuneus*—a wedge-builder, a living shim.

***

At first, I might be mistaken for a drop of molten solder or a fleck of lead sealant left behind by the pipe-fitters, long since oxidized and smoothed by the river. My body is barely the size of a thumb-joint, completely flush, possessing neither top nor sides in the animal sense, only a low-sloping bevel that mirrors the exact angle of the mason’s bevel on the slate.

I have no interior hollows. To contain an empty pocket would mean to be crushed into paste by the static head of the vault. I am solid, dense as tooth enamel, formed of successive laminations of calcium carbonate stripped from the leaching mortar, cross-woven with a dense, flexible collagen that I lay down in microscopic tiers. My dorsal shell is glassy, semi-translucent, and faintly opalescent, tinted with the pale celadon hue of copper salts washing off the nearby bronze straps.

I do not crawl. I do not glide. Movement for me is a matter of slow, hydraulic growth and chemical erosion. My underside does not possess suction cups; instead, it exudes a mild, controlled acid that micro-etches the limestone beneath me, sinking my lower profile a fraction of a millimeter into the grain of the stone itself, while my leading edge secretes a fast-setting, mineralized paste that seals me to the rock so tightly that the stone would shear before the bond would fail.

I am an extension of the masonry's tolerance. Where the builder’s chisel left a microscopic gap between slate and lime, I fill it. 

***

What is it to be this?

It is, first, to exist in an agony of light that carries no heat of its own, because the cool, heavy rush of the thermal water constantly carries the warmth away. Through my dorsal shell, the caustic grids of sunlight do not merely illuminate; they focus. The crystal structure of my back acts as a compound cylindrical lens, focusing the blinding white glare of the noon sky down into my core, where specialized symbiotic chlorophytes—so tiny they are little more than naked green chloroplasts caught in a mineral matrix—convert that searing noon brightness into sugars. 

It is, second, to be entirely deafened into a strange, motionless clarity. The sound here does not travel through ears; it passes directly through the calcite of my shell and the limestone of the wall as a single, uninterrupted vibration. I know the state of the entire aqueduct system through my footing. If a wooden pin loosens in a sluice gate forty paces away, the pitch of the vibration shifts by a quarter-tone. If the mountain rains miles above swell the intake, the compressive load against my leading bevel rises, pressing me down with a fierce, reassuring weight that anchors every crystal of my body more securely into the slate.

My mouth is not a jaw, but a porous intake plate oriented upstream, flush with the bevel. The water is forced into it not by my own effort, but by the kinetic ram-pressure of the inflow itself. Millions of gallons of bath-warm, sun-saturated water are shoved through my internal micro-channels every hour, filtered through stiff, mineralized sieves that extract the dissolved lime, the pulverized silica, and the torn fragments of silk-algae that have been hammered into microscopic pulp by the falls. 

I do not sleep, because sleep is a loosening, and to loosen is to vanish into the south drain in a single, blind millisecond. 

Instead, there is only the rhythm of the sun traversing the open light-well above. When the noon light is vertical, my interior is an incandescent furnace of turquoise radiance and chemical production, building shell, laying down stone, hardening the seal against the rock. As the sun angles away and the light-well dims toward dusk, the caustic nets on the terracotta floor stretch and fade, and my metabolism slows to a dense, stony vigilance—holding the line of the conduit, braced against the dark, roaring volume of water that never grows cold, never slows, and never ends.
