I do not begin with an outline or a face. In this place, to have a fixed silhouette would mean being planed off by the first passing bough or welded into the flank of an expanding terrace before the third breath.

I begin, instead, as a sensation of intolerable torque.

I am wedged between two colliding plates of green heartwood, where a seven-story viaduct of braided wicker is shearing sideways into the crown of an ascending spire. The noise here is not sound as the ear knows it; it is a violent, cellular friction that travels directly through my core—a white-hot frequency of cell walls buckling under four hundred atmospheres of turgor pressure.

At first, I think I am merely the knot. A dense, iron-hard gall of compressed tension-wood, swollen with amber resin, trapped in the fault-line. 

Then the sap hits me.

It does not pool; it detonates through my interior. A high-pressure conduit ruptures below, driving a needle-thin jet of warm, calcium-rich fluid straight into my central ring. The chemical surge snaps my valves open. I am not a static flaw in the timber; I am a *Shear-Anchor*, an organism that exists only in the moments when two colossal geometries attempt to occupy the same space.

My body is small compared to the roaring towers—no larger than a timber-beam, perhaps six feet long—but it is built entirely of counter-grain. My skin is a dense, spiraled sheath of dark, flinty bark that does not stretch; it splits into hundreds of razor-barbed splinters that bite backward, locking into the opposing surfaces of the colliding wood. 

I do not have limbs that swing. I have three nested segments of hydraulic heartwood, telescoped within one another like the stages of a piston. Inside my core, a pocket of trapped ethylene gas cooks under the heat of the city’s violent friction. 

I feel the viaduct groaning above me, its wicker ribs screaming as they twist toward the light. If it falls now, the avenue below will choke on unripened pulp, drowning the root-plates before they can anchor. 

My task is not to save it—nothing here is saved—but to meter the catastrophe.

I fire.

The catch-valve in my rear segment releases with a crack that reverberates through the wood like a cannon shot. My central piston drives forward four inches, burying its chiseled, lignified snout directly into the oncoming grain of the spire. Instantly, my barb-sheaths flare. I become a cross-brace, a living iron-wood bolt jammed into the jaws of the fracture.

For ninety seconds, the entire crushing momentum of the viaduct passes directly through my marrow. 

The sensation is ecstatic, blinding pressure. Through my vascular capillaries, I read the whole basin at once: the boiling upward surge of the *Solar Cavitators* miles above, their reeds singing at the edge of vapor-lock; the soft, churning heat of the *Pith-Weavers* miles below, liquefying the city's dead footings into boiling white mash; the rhythmic, percussive *clack-clack-clack* of the Drivers running the lower ramps, their hollow thrumming passing through the pavement like an electric pulse. 

I taste the sunlight filtering through the green wicker: dry, flat, and furious. It cooks my outer bark until my resin boils, foaming out of my joints in thick, bright yellow beads that harden into amber glass under the midday glare. 

The viaduct reaches its summit. Its crown flowers—huge, melon-sized pods of guttation water—rupture with wet, tearing thuds, dumping tons of sweet moisture onto the moss-ways below to fuel the next wave of shoots. Its purpose is complete.

The base of the spire buckles. The shear-force triples in an instant.

I do not fight it. My outer rings splinter, shearing cleanly along the annual lines. As the two mountain-masses of green timber grind together and collapse into the lower thoroughfare, the immense pressure crushes my outer casing to pulp—and in doing so, triggers my final valve.

The compressed core within me, hard as obsidian and slick with pressurized oil, is squeezed out of the wreckage like a wet seed pinched between two giant thumb-pads. 

I shoot into the hot, sweet air, airborne above the boiling canopy. 

For two seconds of breathtaking silence, the roar drops to a hiss. Below me, the city is a writhing, emerald ocean of thrusting needles, foaming white bridges, and falling spires, devouring its own ruins in a blur of hyper-kinetic growth. The dry sun warms my naked heartwood.

Then gravity and velocity reassert themselves. I arc downward, plunging toward a newly erupting colonnade of boxwood that is tearing its way out of an alley, already tilting, already groaning under its own unsustainable weight.

My snout strikes the green grain. The barbs bite. The valves fill. 

The pressure catches me again, and I lock in, holding the sky open for another sixty seconds.
