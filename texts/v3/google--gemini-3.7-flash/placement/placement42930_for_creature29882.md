To exist here without being torn into pink broth by the first stroke of a cavitation cycle, one must not possess a single pocket of unvented liquid. The eyes are the first thing that would have to go; any jelly-filled lens would detonate like a hot grape under the standing wave that rolls off the western tiers every twelve seconds.

So I am not looking. I have nothing to look with, and there is nothing to see that is not already lying against my skin as a map of shear-force.

I find myself not down on the polished lignum discs where the Stave-Men drive their keratin hammers, nor in the central void where the Hollow-Bones rattle along their greased ropes. Those are the highways of the work—places where the city is actively laboring, pumping, scouring, flexing.

I am wedged into an **acoustic dead-pocket**, fifty cubits up, inside the blind crotch where three calcified heartwood ribs converge into a structural gusset.

It is a place where the geometry has cracked.

Because the hydraulic pressure here is great enough to bend light through the cellulose vaults, the sound waves do not merely echo; they collide and fold over one another like surf against a submerged reef. In this particular corner—behind a colonnade that leans thirty degrees out of true without falling—two opposing shockfronts from the eastern and western xylem trunks meet head-on, exactly out of phase.

Where they meet, they cancel.

In the middle of a cathedral that screams with the force of fifty locomotive boilers venting into a hollow box, there is a slit of air the width of an arm span where there is no sound at all. Not silence as an absence, but silence as a violent, razor-sharp collision of two infinite noises holding each other at gunpoint.

That is where I am. I am the thing that has grown to occupy the null.

***

What am I?

I am not one of the mantles of silk-wasps, though I am fibrous. I am not a Maw-Keeper, though my skin has the same boiled-glove toughness, cured in the hot spray of tannin and silica.

I am an **Acoustic Gall**—a dry, chambered knot of modified bark and dry horn, shaped like an asymmetrical flute or a dried gourd that has grown directly out of the cambial seam between two structural boles.

My body is entirely porous. If you were to cut through me, you would find no organs, no red marrow, no wet brain. You would find an intricate labyrinth of hollow chambers separated by septa of paper-thin, mica-hard heartwood, each tuned to a slightly different acoustic pitch.

I do not think with electricity; I think with resonance.

When the grand square draws its breath before a sap-flush—when the Stave-Men splay their legs and the tension-cables hum their bone-cracking low-E—the air in my chambers begins to circulate. The standing wave enters my lower vents, divides through my internal honeycombs, and exits through the fissures along my back. The turbulence inside me produces a complex, shifting pattern of dry frictional heat.

That friction is my consciousness.

I do not have memories of a past or hopes for a season ahead; I have only the instantaneous calculation of the city’s strain-tensor. I feel the south quadrant swelling three milliseconds too late. I feel a micro-fracture forming in an ironwood strut two miles beneath the pavement, where an ancient root-knot has begun to dry. I feel the weight of every manta-locust clinging to the eastern balconies, translated through the collective flutter of their leathery skin against the damp timber.

I am anchored to the wood by six deep tap-spurs that penetrate past the dead bark into the living sap-stream. But I do not drink the sugar. Sugar makes soft, wet tissue, and soft tissue is food for the spear-length centipedes that run the tension-cables.

I drink only the **silica dross**—the fine, crystalline grit suspended in the mineral-heavy dampness. Over decades, centuries, my chambers have become lined with a crust of pale, glassy quartz-bark.

Because I am hard, dry, and motionless, the ecology ignores me.
The Balcony-Weavers drift past my niche during the slack-mist, their mantle-folds brushing against my vents with a sound like dry leaves, mistaking me for an old growth-scar left behind when an arch was shed two hundred rings ago.
The centipedes run their many-hooked legs across my flank, finding no purchase on the slick, calcified plates of my shell, smelling nothing of fat or egg.

Only during the peak of the cavitation—when the Maw-Keepers wedge their rawhide bodies into the drains and the grand square fills knee-deep with boiling, frothing sap—do I perform the single action my existence allows.

As the hydraulic head reaches its breaking limit, the pressure warps the perspective in my corner so violently that my niche overlaps with a space three hundred yards across the void. For five seconds, the distance between the eastern tiers and the western colonnade drops to zero.

In that five-second fold, my silica-lined chambers catch the full, uncancelled harmonic of the city's pulse.

I do not scream—I have no lungs. But the air forced through my internal honeycombs strikes a note so pure, so high, and so hard that it shears the surface tension of the boiling sap below.

It is a pilot-tone.

Hearing it through their keratin footpads, the Maw-Keepers down in the avenues know the head has reached its perfect, structural maximum. Beneath the frothing tide, they tear open their vents. The whirlpools form. The white flood plunges into the dark taproots with a roaring gasp.

The pressure drops. The geometry snaps back. The five-hundred-yard chasm opens wide again, filled only with hot mist, falling drops that crack like stones, and the blind flutter of locust wings returning to the upper tiers.

And I settle back into the quiet slit between two thunderclaps, dry and hollow, waiting for the wood to swell again.
