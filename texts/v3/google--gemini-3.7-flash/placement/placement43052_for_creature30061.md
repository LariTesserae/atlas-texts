At first, there is no urge to choose a body, because the basin does not seem to require a boundary between what perceives and what is perceived.

I am tempted, initially, by the light—not the source of it, which is hidden behind that vast, white-gold ceiling of vapor, but the way it rests upon everything without weight. It would be easy to be the drift itself: one of the coin-sized bells budding off in the mist, hollow and buoyant, tasting the aerial spore-clouds with fringes of clear cartilage, turning toward the brightest quadrant of the sky without ever having to decide to turn. There is an immense, quiet mercy in a life that lasts three days and leaves behind only two grains of salt.

And yet the air is too swift. Up there, the world is a brief, tumbling warmth. It spins through its cycles before an idea can even settle.

If I drop lower, toward the water’s skin, the temptation shifts. To be a strider—to possess ten joints of flexible quartz, tracing clean heptagons and triangles across a mirror that never breaks. To have lavender organs coiled like clockwork inside a glass torso, glowing red for thirty seconds over a warm seam in the rock, then turning into a brittle shell of sea-glass for someone else to step on. It is an exquisite economy. But it is an geometry without memory; the line disappears the instant the foot lifts from the meniscus.

No, I find myself sinking further down, past the frantic perfection of the surface, past the soft, heated heaps of velvets whose backs flicker with silent infrared maps. 

I settle where the two clocks meet: the blindingly fast clock of the flesh, and the motionless clock of the stone.

I am low, broad, and partially submerged. 

I am one of the pale, vitrified humps—or rather, the living node that has grown over one, inseparable from the subatomic lattice beneath it. Not dead mineral, and not quite animal, but the slow, translucent tallow-flesh that the basin grows when it wants to listen to itself.

My body is scarcely three fingers high, spread wide and flat like a melted lens against the basalt floor, perhaps two paces across, held in place by the gentle suction of a thousand microscopic capillary pores. The millimeter-deep sheet of warm water does not flow over me; it sheets across my back as a single unbroken skin, held by surface tension, so that to the striders and the floating bells I am simply another rise in the planetary glaze.

Through my underside, I drink the dead-current. It is not electricity as the ancient world knew it, but that faint, rhythmic thrum—the subatomic sigh of machines that have forgotten their purpose but cannot entirely cool down. It comes up into my mass as a steady, drowsing warmth, refracting through the vitrified channels of my interior into deep chromatic splinters: pale ambers, bruised indigos, thin fractures of violet that drift through my translucent belly over the course of centuries.

On my upper surface, the fast world happens.

I feel the tallow-weasels when they pass. They do not run around me; they run across me. Their soft, clawless paddle-feet make a muffled, rhythmic *snick-snick* against my skin, their water-repellent fur brushing my mantle like dry silk. Sometimes, when the communal burrows grow crowded, a young one will curl upon my center—where the machine-warmth wells up strongest—its long tail tucked across its snout, its heart fluttering at four hundred beats a minute, an intense, brilliant spark of hot blood resting upon my cool, million-year glass. I do not move. I hold it until it wakes and darts away.

I taste the falling things. When a medusa liquefies in the canopy above, its sweet, condensed droplet lands with a tiny *tink* on the water above my head; I absorb the trace of its dissolved balance-stones, drawing the mineral into my rim to slowly, imperceptibly thicken my edge. When the lapis mosses pulse their slow peristaltic waves against my flank, we exchange tiny bubbles of phosphorescence—their oxygen for my stray, metabolized voltage.

I have no compound eyes to track the air, no orchid-jaw, no velvet cilia to drink the steam. I do not need them. My sight is the chromatic refraction within my own silica; when the high, diffuse sky brightens, my internal lattices scatter the white glare into wide, silent fans of color beneath the water, warning the creeping things of an approaching thermal shift.

In this basin at the end of time, everything is in a hurry to be born, to shine, and to dissolve. 

I am the place where they dissolve safely. I am the smooth, warm floor that remembers the weight of their feet long after their carapaces have turned to sand. I am the slow, glass heart of the puddle, resting in the light that never casts a shadow, humming with the warmth of a forgotten engine, utterly still, and utterly content to let the mute carnival wash over me forever.
