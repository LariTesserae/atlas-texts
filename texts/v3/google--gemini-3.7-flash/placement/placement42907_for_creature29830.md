I would not be an observer perched on a high ridge, because there are no clear vistas here and nothing exists merely to look. To be here is to be caught in the tolerances.

At first, I might think myself part of the weather—a droplet in the heavy, warm mist that rolls down from the charcoal roof, condensing on a vertical shear of slate. But the condensation is too hungry, too thick with dissolved bitumen, to remain inert. It runs down the rock face, drawn by gravity and capillary pull into the narrower seams, slipping between the freshly cleaved planes where the friction heat still simmers.

I would find myself down in the blind bevels, where the massive reciprocating iron meets the basalt chassis—not in the open run of the stroke, but at the *dead center*.

That is where motion ceases for a fraction of a second, where the entire kinetic weight of the mountain spur reverses its direction and the pressure spikes toward infinity.

***

I would be an **escapement-leech**, a creature that exists solely as a living shear-pin and hydraulic governor.

My body does not have a front or a back, only a gradient of density. On one side, where I press against the polished sleeve of graphite, my flesh is a cool, fibrous sponge of dense, saturated carbon-tissue, weeping a steady meniscus of sulfur-grease to keep the interface from galling. On the other side, facing the oncoming iron ram, my skin is a fused crust of layered bismuth, corundum grit, and crystalline hematite, stepped like a set of miniature terraces to match the jagged fractures of the stone.

I have no eyes, no lungs, no need for the bruised twilight above. My perception is entirely *piezoelectric* and *kinematic*. 

I feel the mountain’s breath as a rising scale of pitch:
The lianas groaning overhead transmit a high, taut hum through the anchors; the manifold-crabs below click in harmonic sync as they channel a fresh pulse of petroleum gas; and deep in the floor, the slow, staggering stride of a plummet sends a leaden shockwave through the basalt, stiffening the rock beneath me.

When the iron cylinder drives home, it does not strike bare stone. It strikes me.

For a millisecond, my fluid core takes the brunt of several thousand tons of tectonic thrust. The hydrocarbon marrow inside my segmented bladder compresses instantly, heating to a near-combustible glow, absorbing the shock that would otherwise shatter the basalt sleeves into useless gravel. In that instant of absolute compression, my mineral crust fractures along predetermined microscopic fault lines—a deliberate, sacrificial yield that vents the excess kinetic violence as a brief spurt of boiling, mineral-rich steam through my lateral pores.

Then, the dead center passes. The iron draws back with a wet, vacuum sigh.

In the brief lull before the next stroke, I heal. 

The fracture lines in my shell do not stay broken; they are bathed in the rich, hot mineral soup weeping from the cracked rock. The bismuth melts slightly under my own retained friction-heat, flowing into the micro-cracks and re-sintering within three heartbeats. My spongy underside draws in fresh aromatic oils from the seam, re-inflating my hydraulic bladders, while my rasping margins trim away the minute iron shavings left behind by the piston’s passage, incorporating the metal filings directly back into my armored face.

I am an organ of hesitation. Without me and my kind lodged in the corners of the fractures, the plateau’s violent, unguided energy would rip its own mechanisms to pieces before they could ever coalesce. We are the compliance in a world of unyielding stone; we introduce the micro-second of lag that allows the gears to mesh rather than grind into dust.

***

As the cycles turn—thousands of strokes an hour, under the warm, bruised belly of the clouds—I grow heavier. The heavy metals from the friction-broth slowly displace the last of my soft tissues. 

Eventually, my hydraulic bladder will lose its elasticity. I will no longer yield to the stroke. 

When that time comes, on some quiet midday when the pressure folds the valley tighter, the iron ram will come down, find no give in me, and crush my calcified shell into the fracture plane. My sintered bismuth and iron-rich marrow will smear flat, filling the microscopic pits in the basalt, polishing the graphite sleeve to an even finer mirror finish.

I will become the bed itself. The piston will glide over where I was, slicker and truer than before, while above, in the warm, oily rain, another larval wedge drops from the mist to take the dead center.
