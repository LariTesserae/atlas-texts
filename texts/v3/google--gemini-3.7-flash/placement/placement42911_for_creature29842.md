At first, there is only the sensation of being held in a vise that is entirely liquid and warm. 

I do not begin with eyes or a name, because neither has any purchase here. What presents itself first is the grain: the texture of the current dragging across a broad, flattened surface that I realize, slowly, is my own margin. 

I am not in the open water where the siphonophores trail their translucent galleons, nor am I down in the sulfurous cauldrons of the sumps where the carrion-anemones turn meat into rock. I find myself lodged in the middle register—in the hollow throat of a junction where three ventilation flues converge into a single, vertical avenue. 

I am not stone, but the stone has taken an interest in me. 

At my anchor-point, where my underside meets the polished curve of a fluted buttress, the crust-weavers have already begun their quiet, blind masonry. Their micro-cables of calcified slime creep up my flanks by millimeters a season, binding my tough, rubbery skin to the nacreous wall. I do not shrug them off; their encrustation is the anchor that keeps the draft from peeling me into the dark. We have reached a slow, biological truce: they lend me purchase against the current, and my body, broad and crescent-shaped as a ray’s wing, directs the nutrient plume across their feeding fields.

I am an acoustic baffle, a living valve. 

My flesh is thick, dense with lipid pockets and oil-filled chambers that do not compress under the thousands of fathoms above. I am blind, but the dark is not empty; it is crowded with weight, with temperature, with the coarse chemical taste of iron filings and decaying cuticles drifting down from the upper nurseries. 

My nervous system does not gather into a skull; it runs in wide, branching rivers beneath my skin, a dense web of lateral pits that feels the shape of the city miles out. 

I feel the thrall-worms before their water reaches me. Two avenues over, forty feet of pale muscle ripples against a conduit, and the tiny back-pressure of its stroke taps against the left flank of my body like a soft, wet drumbeat. When they synchronize, their cadence sets up a harmonic in my internal fluid chambers. I ring with it. I am an echo-chamber for their labor, softening the shudder of their strokes so the delicate gossamer shells of the pteropods above do not shatter against the colonnade walls.

Further down, through the bedrock of the hive, comes the dry, telegraphic chatter of the sessiles: *tock... tock-tock... tock.* 

The sound does not travel through the brine as noise; it travels through the stone as an ache in my anchored base. It tells of a fault three leagues south, where cold abyss-water—thin, starved, and biting—has cracked a low rampart and begun to bleed into the warm tenements. The stone shivers with the news. 

In response, my body alters its trim. I do not move with fins or limbs; I alter the turgor pressure within my lateral lobes. I swell one side, taking on water through my intake pores, and let the other thin to a razor-sharp fringe. 

The hydrodynamics of the flue shift instantly. 

The warm plume rising from the mantle floor is diverted slightly eastward, forced through a narrower conduit where the increased shear will provoke the crust-weavers into frenzy. Within weeks, their unthinking, mucous-heavy repair will plug the fracture, guided entirely by the way my mass has warped the drift.

I do not have thoughts, not in the way creatures of the sun have them. My consciousness is the hydraulic balance of the district. When the water is rich and hot, thick with the velvet fleece scraped loose by the felt-gleaners, I am heavy, languid, nearly dormant, tasting the steady fall of sulfur and molted carapace. When a cold eddy penetrates the chamber, an electric alertness runs along my margins—a tightening of muscle, a tilt of the flesh to choke off the dead draft before it starves the nurseries above.

Sometimes, a predator—a cornice-gorgon, dark and boneless—will drift across my upper surface, its trailing tactile hairs brushing my skin with an exquisite, feather-light caution. We ignore one another. Its acid does not interest my tough, oil-slick hide, and its hunt is too small to alter the currents I regulate. It uses me as a sill, a stepping-stone to launch its silent strike into the thoroughfare; I use its passing to gauge the density of the shoals drifting above the lintels.

Centuries pass without a dawn. The weavers advance their mortar up my sides, millimeter by millimeter. Eventually, in some distant cycle of the mantle’s breath, they will cover me entirely. My lipid chambers will drain, my soft tissues will be hollowed out by the sulfur-biofilms, and the space my body occupied will remain as an arched, perfectly proportioned sluice gate in the stone—a permanent hollow in the cathedral wall that continues to direct the water long after the animal that made it has dissolved into the nacre.

Until then, I rest in the throat of the dark, listening to the city hum through my belly, feeling the endless, blood-warm river rise around my edges, holding the shape of the breath it takes.
