I would not be among the walkers, nor among the hunters who wait for the walkers to crack. To have a trajectory across this plateau—even one that unspools over fifty years on a carbon-fiber winch—requires an ambition of mass that I do not possess. Nor could I bear the high stillness of the Cenobites, whose centuries are bought with petrification and lead-salt blindness.

I find myself lower than the sky, higher than the bore-lines, but entirely inside the tolerance.

I am a creature of the **clearance**. 

Where the cold-rolled hip-cowls of the six-legged browsers slide over their chassis-pins, there is an engineered gap of roughly three millimeters. It cannot be tighter, or the metal would gall and cold-weld in the diamond-cut violet wind; it cannot be wider, or the quartz grit thrown up by the gizzard-chambers would foul the race. In that three-millimeter interval, between fifty tons of downward hydraulic thrust and the screaming friction of an un-bushed pivot, is where I am thickest.

At first, you would take me for an abiotic fouling—a black, tarry deposit of oxidized molybdenum disulfide, perhaps, or the charred tallow left behind when an exhaust vent flares out of sequence. I have no carapace that could be struck with a hammer and ring. My body is a dense, laminated felt of microscopic copper-leaf platelets suspended in a cold-flow silicone matrix, folded over itself ten thousand times like the leaf-spring of an iron carriage.

I do not have a front or a back. I have an *in-stroke* and an *out-stroke*.

When the browser’s six-hour stride begins, the pressure across my body climbs from a nominal five hundred atmospheres to something that approaches the threshold of phase-change. My copper leaves slide over one another, generating a faint, interior luminescence—a cold, greenish triboluminescence that illuminates nothing but the microscopic surface imperfections of the host’s iron bone. In that compression, I do not suffer; compression is my respiration. 

Under the peak load, when the beast’s massive hoof clears the scree and its full weight rolls through the hip-socket, the grease in my tissues is forced through a labyrinth of capillary valves made of hollow, vulcanized horn. This fluid movement drives my metabolism. I am an animal powered entirely by the work of *damping*. I absorb the violent, high-frequency shudder of steel striking granite—the micro-fracturing vibrations that would otherwise crystallize the browser’s frame over a decade—and I convert that destructive harmonic into chemical potential. I eat the shock. I grow fat on deceleration.

My sensory world is not one of light or scent, though I register the sharp, ozone-and-kerosene draft when the vent-gaskets burp overhead. My world is the texture of the load. 

I know the exact metallurgy of the pin against which my upper membrane rests: it was cast during a warmer epoch, seven hundred miles to the south, when the host grazed a stratum rich in antimony, and its grain runs slightly diagonal to the bearing’s rotation. I know when a Billet-Biter has dropped its carbon tendon into a crevice two miles behind us, because the slight, dragging impedance on the host’s trailing leg alters the resonance of the third thoracic spring by three-tenths of a hertz. I know when the Bore-Singers deep in the mantle are shifting their stroke from brine to steam, because the shale under our feet softens by an imperceptible fraction, and the host’s hydraulic accumulator compensates with an irritable, sharp chatter in the hip-valves.

I am not alone in the joint, though I am its principal tenant. 

In the low-pressure margins where the grease pools and cools into yellow grease-wax, tiny, wire-legged commensals—no larger than needle-eyes, with bodies spun from single threads of hardened silver—graze upon my sloughed copper platelets. They are too delicate to survive the center of the race; they live on the perimeter, darting into the shear-zone during the browser's two-hour exhalations to harvest the warm flakes I shed under the load, then retreating before the next piston-stroke flattens them into foil. Sometimes I absorb one. It does not hurt; its minute silver skeleton simply becomes another slip-plane in my anatomy, a momentary grain of higher conductivity before it is rolled flat and smooth into the general laminate of my flesh.

Reproduction, for me, is an involuntary consequence of overfilling the gap. 

After forty or fifty years of heavy browsing, when the host has ground down a whole ridge of quartz-veined shale and my body has thickened from the steady diet of vibration and graphite, I begin to extrude from the lip of the hip-cowl. I emerge into the violet daylight as a black, oily lip of tissue, stiffening instantly as the sub-zero wind strikes me. 

The frost-dust settles on this exposed fringe, coating it in a brittle jacket of ice-glass. When the host takes its next violent, shuddering step, the edge of the steel cowl shears this overgrowth clean away.

The severed fragment does not die. It falls through the freezing air like a lead coin, tumbling down the iron-plated flank of the browser, bouncing off the bellows-housings, and landing in the copper-filament moss below. 

There, in the cold, it lies dormant for decades. It feels the *thung-clack* of the Bore-Singers passing through the granite underneath. It feels the slow, tectonic creep of the tableland. It waits until another browser—perhaps a young one whose pins are still rough from the kiln, whose tolerances are still loose and weeping—steps directly over it. 

As the massive foot crushes the fragment into the scree, the heat of the impact liquifies its silicone matrix. The fragment clings to the rising sole; it creeps upward along the grease-trails left by the weeping annular seals; it slips between the sliding leaves of the knee; it climbs, millimeter by millimeter over the course of a winter, following the warm draft of the hydraulic lines until it finds a fresh, un-damped clearance.

There it settles. It spreads itself thin. It matches its grain to the grain of the turning iron.

And when the great piston above it draws back its fluid, pauses, and descends with the weight of an entire subcontinent's cold, mechanical will, I flatten into myself, I let the load pass through my copper bones, and I begin to sing the silent, glowing song of friction.
