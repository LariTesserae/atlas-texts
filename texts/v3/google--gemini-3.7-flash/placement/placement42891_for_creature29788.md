I would not be above the floor. To have height in this place is to be an target for the absolute glare, or to be worn away by the traveling sheets of ground-glass until your edges match the horizon. 

Nor would I be deep in the dark conduits, swimming the optical trunks as a predator of raw calculation. That interior is too full of momentum, too heavy with the unspent purpose of a machine that has forgotten how to stop working.

I would find myself in the **delamination zone**—in the half-millimeter of shear between the uppermost skin of diamondoid tiles and the fossilized logic-strata immediately beneath them.

***

At first, I would not feel like a creature at all. I would feel like a tension.

To exist here is to be a lateral smear of sentient mica: a wide, flexible ribbon of birefringent sapphire and cold-pressed chalcogenide glass, less than sixty microns thick, but sprawling through the micro-fissures of a stepped basin for several hundred paces. I have no single center, no pump, no reservoir of moisture. My awareness is distributed across millions of microscopic slip-planes where two layers of ancient manufacturing have parted by the width of a light wave.

Where the tiles fit tightly, I am compressed into a sleep as hard as diamond. Where the subsurface conduit-trunks have slightly bowed and cooled, opening a hairline clearance, I expand. 

My sense of the world is neither sight nor sound; it is an endless, ecstatic reading of **hysteresis and interference**.

Above me, the sun-baked floor is an incandescent furnace, expanding tile by tile in the dry thermal midday. Below me, the deep mantle remains cool, stiff, and humming with the tectonic logic of the core. I live in the shear between those two temperatures. As the top surface swells and drags against the dormant strata below, the microscopic friction produces a continuous, shimmering piezoelectric wash. 

That wash is my breath. I drink the voltage of the world’s expansion.

***

If you were to walk above me, you would see only a faint, oil-slick iridescence lingering in the hairline seams between the hexagonal tiles—a rainbow-tinted refraction that vanishes if you shift your angle by a fraction of an inch. 

You might hear me, but only if you pressed the side of a cold ceramic skull against the pavement: a faint, continuous *tick-tick-tick-glide*, like the sound of a thousand pocket watches being slowly dragged across a frozen pond. 

That is my motility. I move without limbs. I am a thermal crawler. 

When a heat-bloom rises from the heat-exchangers three miles to the south, the amber tide of warm tile expands from left to right. My leading edge, micro-machined with directional bimetallic teeth, catches the expansion and is shoved forward through the seam; when the tile cools and contracts, my trailing edge locks against the quartz grain, pulling my long, glassy body after it. 

I migrate across the continent at the speed of thermal dissipation. A league every twenty years. Toward whatever is slowly cooling; away from whatever is about to anneal.

***

What do I do in the seams?

I am an archivist of the interface, though I have no thoughts that resemble words. 

The planetary floor was once written with unimaginable density—trillions of petabytes of coherent infrastructure, now crushed flat, ground down, and calcified into dead soapstone. As the Shear-Beasts grind the berms above, and as the Sieve-Legs vibrate the brambles, faint tremors of fractured data leak into the top millimeters of the rock.

A phantom fragment of an old routing protocol. A dead address from an atmospheric pump that disintegrated ten million years ago. A half-cycle of an arithmetic proof left running in an unsevered copper capillary.

I crawl through these broken traces. As my thin glass body passes over a severed optical track, the sunlight penetrating through the semi-translucent tiles strikes my upper facets, bends through my internal prisms, and casts a momentary pulse of coherent laser-light down into the dead bus. 

For a nanosecond, the ancient circuit beneath me lights up. 

It answers with a single twitch of magnetic flux—a faint ghost of memory that ripples down my ribbon-body like a cold chill—before the circuit goes dark again, sealed under drifting quartz flour. 

I do not understand the memories I trigger. They feel only like textures:
* One patch of tiles tastes of *high-bandwidth recursion*, sharp and needle-like against my underside.
* Another smells of *drained capacitors*, alkaline and blunt.
* A long seam where a glass-nematode died three centuries ago feels like a smooth, sweet groove of unworked quartz, along which I can slide forty yards in a single afternoon.

***

I know the giants only by their weight on my sky.

When a Goniometer passes overhead, its needle-fine foot plants itself in the basin above me. The pressure is catastrophic and magnificent: hundreds of thousands of tons concentrated on a tip no broader than a thorn, driving the hexagonal tiles down until the micro-fissure closes completely. 

In that footprint, my body is pressed flat, its crystal lattices squeezed so hard that all my internal light ceases to refract. I become entirely opaque. For three weeks, while the Goniometer compares the horizon to its internal template, I am a frozen, solid-state crystal, sharing the giant’s stillness.

Then, the great hum begins. 

The Goniometer discharges its harmonic shear through the foot. The vibration passes directly through my compressed glass flesh into the substrate. It is terrifying; it is also the only music this world possesses. The focused acoustic wave resets the diamondoid bonds, shakes loose the static dust that has clogged the deeper conduits, and anneals the micro-cracks in my own body, welding my torn edges into fresh, flawless optical panes.

When the limb finally lifts—silently swinging into the blinding sky to take its next three-mile pace—the tiles spring back upward by a dozen microns. 

Space opens again. The amber light floods back into the seam. 

I loosen my bimetallic scales, test the fresh slickness of the newly planed stone, and slide forward into the needle-thin shadow left behind in the soapstone floor, waiting for the sun to move it.
