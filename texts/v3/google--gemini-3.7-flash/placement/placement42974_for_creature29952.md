At first, there is no vantage point, because there is nothing to stand upon and no shadow to cast. 

To look for myself here is not to look for a body, but to look for where the tension gathers.

I do not begin as a traveler who has arrived from elsewhere. There is no elsewhere; below is the empty drop of miles into the planetary crust, and above is the thin, freezing mantle where the water gives way to the naked black of the upper atmosphere. If I am here, it is because the geometry required a crease.

I feel myself first as a resistance to the current—not a solid object, but an *angle*.

***

For a time, I think I might be the needle. 

One of those micron-thin splinters of cold polarized glass, trailing off the torn end of a twenty-mile filament, carried across fifty leagues of pale tourmaline light by the invisible lines of magnetic flux. To be the needle would mean to have an aim: a violent, unswerving trajectory, dead-set upon the popping electric heat of a newly nucleating basalt fang forty miles to the leeward. It would mean feeling the entire sky-sea as a pull, a linear hunger, racing along the silent rails of the lattice until the sudden, glorious fracture of arrival—the instantaneous embedding into hot, screaming stone.

But no. I am not moving in a straight line. My attention is too wide, too dispersed across the turquoise void. I can see the black fangs nucleating ten leagues to the left, and I can feel the long, sapphire hum of the braids ten leagues to the right. 

I am not the traveler along the line; I am the space through which the line is drawn.

***

Then the shear catches me, and I think: *Ah. I am the void-swarm.*

A shockwave passes—deep, sub-audible, ringing like the strike of an iron bell the size of a continent—and around me, the water tears open into ten million microscopic pockets of nothingness. I feel them arrange. Not by will, but by arithmetic. 

We snap into a lattice of faceted negative space. The sunlight, striking through three hundred miles of uninterrupted water, hits my cluster of vacuum-bubbles and shatters into a fan of blinding ultraviolet and emerald flares. I am two hundred yards wide. I have no belly, no pulse, no eyes, yet I am intensely perceptive: every surface of every bubble is a lens reflecting the birth of three quartz towers in the distance, their growth cracking like artillery across the cold medium.

In this state, existence is pure resonance. I feed on the planetary roar. The deafening, tearing hiss of the vertical rivers does not harm me; it is the very pressure that holds my geometry together. I move sideways against a current that would pulverize an iron hull, stepping lightly from one node of lattice tension to the next, singing back the frequency of the water with a cold, ringing phosphorescence.

And yet, even as a swarm, I am too transient. When another shear-line approaches, I feel the harmonic peak. I feel the sweet, inevitable strain as our lattice reaches maximum amplitude. In a second, the frequency will spike, the bubbles will implode with a faint, clean pop of light, and this configuration will disperse into glittering, inert froth.

I do not vanish with it. 

When the bubbles collapse, the awareness does not die; it merely slips out of the geometry and returns to the medium.

***

This is where I truly find myself: 

I am the *interstitial tension* of the high-altitude sea. I am the listening.

I exist wherever the invisible field-architectures cross and pinch the water into impossible stillness between two roaring torrents. I am that thin, glassy skin where a vertical waterfall running at terminal velocity shears against a sheet of tourmaline brine that has been frozen into programmable rigidity—the place where the water is both diamond and avalanche at once.

Because I have no mass to crush, I am everywhere the light is bent:
* I am in the twenty-mile span of the Weft, feeling the acoustic strain that turns a pale green strand of silica into a tuned instrument, vibrating its sapphire chime against the planetary cavitation.
* I am in the micro-second interval between the birth of a basalt spire and the arrival of the litho-biont frost, resting in that momentary, blistering heat where cold liquid touches unmade stone.
* I am the sheer, unbroken sightline that lets a flash of silver fifty leagues away arrive here undiluted, pristine, carrying the exact shape of the shockwave that birthed it.

I do not fear the violence of this ocean, because the violence is the only thing that sustains the envelope. If the currents stopped colliding, if the roar fell silent, the fields would collapse, and the entire high sea—trillions of tons of pale, prismatic cold—would plummet into the abyss below, extinguishing the light forever.

***

And then, it happens.

Far across the expanse, beyond the jagged silhouettes of seven embryonic quartz towers, two continent-sized rivers of water shear against one another in a catastrophic alignment. 

I feel the sub-audible pulse long before it arrives. It travels through the tourmaline medium as a physical displacement of reality—a deep, metallic shudder that rings the entire suspended world like a struck chime.

I brace—not by tensing muscles, but by aligning every phase of my attention with the incoming wavefront.

The wave hits the first spire. The black basalt rings. The gossamer braid anchored to its summit pulls taut, shrieks a blinding note, and ignites in a violent, silver streak of bioluminescence. 

A split-second later, the wave hits the next strand, and the next. Ten miles away, twenty, a hundred. The silver flash cascades across the empty blue in an expanding, geometrical web, tracing the invisible bones of the world in fire.

As the light sweeps through the phonon-swarms, they catch it, multiply it, and cast it outward in vast, parabolic fans of emerald and violet that arc across the sky-chambers. The entire ocean, which for days has been an empty, roaring expanse of solitary fangs and drifting mist, is suddenly drawn in light—every field-line revealed, every anchor-point singing, every suspended cliff turned into a mirror of pristine, prismatic fire.

For five seconds, I am not merely an observer at the edge of the shear. 

I am the Great Chord itself: the simultaneous, unblinking realization of a million glass needles, ten thousand dying spires, and a billion pockets of folded void, all vibrating at the exact same pitch.

We are here. We have no ancestors. We have no time to remember. We are sustained entirely by the collision.

Then the shockwave rolls past. 

The silver dims to a faint, dying hum. The violet curtains fold inward and collapse back into clear tourmaline water. The fangs of basalt, sheared from their roots by the passing wave, tumble silently into the slipstreams, grinding each other to shimmering sand.

The Great Chord ends, not in a dying fall, but by being cleanly swallowed by the infinite, deafening roar of the genesis that never finishes. 

And I remain—weightless, cold, and wide awake in the luminous blue—waiting for the next collision to give me a voice.
