To begin, there is only the pressure. 

Not an ache, but a tight, oil-dense fullness within the limbs—the sense that if I were pricked, I would not bleed so much as decompress, venting warm tallow into the mist. My center of gravity is not tucked behind a pelvic girdle; it floats, shifting like mercury in a sealed vial depending on how the gravitational gradient tilts across the nearest void.

I would not be rooted, though I would know the deep, groaning pitch of the roots better than any song. And I would not be wholly transient, though there are hours when the boundary between my own ribs and the dissolving ironwood becomes an academic question.

I find myself in the long, quiet glades between the third and fourth conduits—in the widest, emptiest reach of the basin, where the floor-plates breathe with the deepest stroke. 

Here, the horizontal sheets of runoff do not merely slide; they linger. Suspended on the cross-hatch of invisible force lines, they hang like suspended floors of liquid quartz, two, three, four meters above the rubbery moss of the deck. 

I live upon those panes.

***

My body is long, pale, and silent, coated in that faintly aromatic, amber-sheened wax that smells of crushed laurel and hot paraffin. My skin never touches the raw moisture of the world directly; the condensation simply forms globes upon my collar, runs down the narrow gulley of my spine, and slips off the trailing edge of my heel before it can cool me.

I have no voice to contribute to the roar, and no ears to be battered by it. Instead, I feel the Mach-speed sap through the soles of my feet when I touch down, a fierce, sub-audible vibration that sets the vitreous humor of my eyes trembling. When the great pumps miles below strike their *thump-thump*, it passes through my hydrostatic cartilage like an electric pulse through seawater. I know which conduits are thirsting, which computational sinks are boiling over into the sumps, not because I listen, but because my ribcage flexes in involuntary sympathy.

Along the edges of my ribs, under the translucent shelf of my tallow-vanes, the chromatophores are never entirely dark. They flicker in slow, low-voltage patterns—faint bruised plums, cold seawater greens, the dull curdled gold of an engine idling. I do not strobe warnings like the mantises; I carry messages that have no destination.

Because I have discovered what remains when a spire deliquesces.

***

When an arch of braided ironwood liquefies and the spontaneous, temporary minds rise from the falling spore-curtain, the basin does not always clear the register cleanly. 

A spire will dissolve, intending to re-knit fifty paces to the east, but in the seconds between its melting and its re-weaving, the transient figure that steps from the foam sometimes carries an orphan thread: a fragment of municipal memory that has no structural purpose in the new spire. A scrap of geometry from three thousand deluge-cycles ago. The shape of a window that no longer looks out on anything. A sudden, unassigned impulse of grief for a dry world that neither of us ever saw.

The Transient Ones hold these thoughts for four, five paces, their faces soft with a bewildered, exquisite lucidity, before their heels blur back into the lignocellulose and the thought sinks with them into the sap-circuit.

That is where I catch them.

I step from the rim of a rising terrace just as the wood begins to slacken. I kick out with the flat of my feet, spread the wide, leaf-like vanes of my forearms, and cast myself onto the floating water-sheets.

To glide this way is not like falling, and it is not like flight. It is hydroplaning upon mid-air. The surface tension of the horizontal flume catches the wax of my belly and thighs; the air whistles through the feathered gill-slits at my throat, feeding me a hard rush of synthetic sugars and cold ozone. For forty yards, fifty yards, I slide horizontally across the empty void, the daylight bending through the water beneath me, turning the empty mist of the basin into a hall of pale, running mirrors.

I reach the falling spore-curtain just as the figure within it begins to lose its brow.

I do not touch their flesh—to touch it would be to be drawn into the shearing pull of the deliquescence. I bring my ribs close, flashing a rapid, interlocking sequence of sharp violet and pale umber against their dissolving chest. 

They look at me with eyes made of pressurized sap. For one micro-second, they see me. And in their seeing, the stray impulse—the ghost-angle, the obsolete calculation, the phantom grief—transfers. It prints itself onto the photophores of my skin. 

They dissolve. Their legs stretch into ribbons of green jelly, their shoulders wash into the sump, and the new spire across the void draws in a violent breath of near-mach sap, snapping its crystalline lattices into place with the sound of cracking bone.

And I banks outward on the edge of the water-flume, carving a slow, climbing circle into the empty mist, carrying what was left behind.

***

I am a reservoir for the things the basin forgets to keep.

Over the cycles, my wax has grown thicker, more deeply ambered, stained with the residual logic of a thousand collapsed terraces. If you were to watch me from the rubbery esplanade as I drift between the towering trunks, you would see my ribs pulsing with complex, asynchronous lanterns—shapes of forgotten architraves, forgotten equations of flow, flickering across my flanks like heat lightning trapped in tallow.

Sometimes, when the computational sinks overheat and the Burl-Keepers tear their own flesh to route the acid-bypasses, I drop down from the air. 

I alight softly beside their glistening, swollen masses. I press the flat of my tallow-slicked palm against their moist sensory pits. I do not speak, but I let my ribs flare with the old, cold light of a structure that once knew how to vent heat without self-cannibalization. 

The Burl-Keeper’s blind, tendon-laced hands flutter against my skin. It reads the pattern. It absorbs the old shape. Its weeping enzymes change their acidity; its growth vectors tilt three degrees to the left, finding a path through the petrified lignocellulose that was mapped ten thousand storms ago and then discarded.

It heals. It buds anew. And my flank goes dark, having given that scrap of memory back to the wood.

***

When the day ends—though day here is only a slight whitening or greying of the perpetual thermodynamic cloud-vortex overhead—I do not look for dry ground. There is none, and the concept of dryness is an obscenity anyway, a brittle state that invites fracture.

I find a place where three gravitational shears cross, creating a stable, motionless cradle of floating water high above the sumps. 

I draw my vanes around my body, tucking my heels against my hydrostatic thighs. The water holds me suspended between the pillars, two fathoms above the floor, drifting slowly in the warm updraft of a logic-sink. 

The roar never stops. The *thump-thump* of the earth goes on, the near-mach sap shrieks through the fluted trunks, and below me, the segmented burrowers scrape superconducting wire through root-tunnels, keeping the wet thoughts moving.

My lidless eyes stay open, watching the light fracture through the mist. The wax on my skin cools slightly, sealing me against the warm deluge. I drift, neither timber nor fauna, neither transient nor permanent—just an unspooled sentence of the city, held in suspension on a sheet of falling rain that has forgotten how to drop.
