To begin, there is only the sensation of being held in an iron vise that does not close. 

It is the inertia of the fluorocarbon. When I do not move, the fluid presses against every facet of my plates with the calm, deadweight authority of a mountain made of cold grease and mercury. It does not yield; it must be cut. 

I am not drifting. There is no concept of drift here. I am wedged.

My three left spurs are locked into a sub-micron rebate on the northwest face of a sixty-tier hexagonal stack, four miles down from the overhead condensation rings and twelve miles above the virgin foundation. My body is extended horizontally across a five-inch chasm, spanning the void between two habitations like a dark quartz strut. The daylight phosphor shines through my sapphire turret, splitting the clean, sterile clarity of the alley into sharp, un-diffused spectra that cast twin blue-violet knives against the opposite wall.

The cold is not an absence; it is a solid rush. The down-flume pours through my open rib-grille, thirty gallons a second of supercooled liquid dragging across the vascularized titanium foils within my chest. I can feel the exact thermal budget of my existence: inside the core node behind my acoustic pickups, a frantic, red-hot lattice of logic is calculating the parity of the physical tokens whistling past my head; outside, the freezing wash strips that heat away in violent, microscopic shears, keeping me three degrees below the thermal point at which my nervous lattice would turn to molten solder. 

To live here is to be a continuous, screaming exchange of heat for geometry.

***

Who am I? I am not an Anchor, though I can feel the deep, thrumming acoustic pulse of one vibrating through the basalt four hundred yards below me—a steady, rhythmic *thung-thung-thung* that regulates the memory-token traffic in this sector. 

Nor am I a nymph tumbling in the vapor spirals. 

I am an *indexer*. A shear-dweller.

When an optical-mechanical token—a faceted sliver of polished glass-metal carrying sixty terabits of physical state—is spat from an upper manifold, it drops through this chasm at eighty feet per second, cutting the dense brine with a high, crystalline whine. Because the fluid is heavy as wet lead, the token cannot tumble; it tracks straight down the acoustic waveguide created by the narrow alley. 

My job is the bite.

*Snick.* 

My upper caliper extends—a dry, instantaneous flick of vulcanized sinew and ceramic linkage. The tungsten tip grazes the trailing edge of the token as it screams past. I don't stop it; to stop it in this heavy fluid would shear my limb from its shoulder-socket. I merely *tilt* it by four arc-seconds. 

The token catches the laminar down-draft at a fresh angle, veers left with a dull *clack*, and slides into the mouth of a sub-station processor three habitations down.

As the token enters the gate, the pressure release valve behind it snaps open. 

*CRACK.*

The rifle-shot sound hits my thoracic plate before the shockwave even finishes warping the visual line of the tower opposite me. The acoustic impact rings through my titanium ribs like a struck tuning fork. I feel the pulse travel down my spine, through my locked spurs, and into the building itself. 

Instinctively, my throat-valve answers. 

I contract the small hydraulic chamber behind my tongue-plate. A tiny pocket of fluorocarbon is drawn in, subjected to negative pressure, and cavitated into a microscopic vapor bubble. *Snap.* The bubble collapses under the crushing ambient weight. A single, cold photon-burst—sonoluminescence—flashes through the clear prism of my throat.

*Packet accepted. Chasm clear.*

Two miles across the grid, through three hairline flumes and a vertical cooling well, an answering spark flashes in the dark recess of an intake slot. A companion. We do not have names, but we have resonant frequencies. That spark was 412 terahertz, slightly green-shifted by the local thermal plume it crossed. It is the signature of the one who sleeps stacked directly above me during the low-load cycle, whose dorsal ports interlock with mine when we shunt the building’s idle current into the deep bus.

***

There is a strange, absolute peace in this world, born of its merciless cleanliness. 

I look at my fore-caliper. The edge is fresh, extruded perhaps fifty cycles ago, but where the tungsten spur meets the silicon carbide of the wall, there is a tiny, microscopic flat spot—a shiny facet of wear ground into the point by twenty million acoustic shockwaves and the ceaseless, leaden drag of the brine.

It will take several thousand more cycles, but eventually, that flat spot will widen. The spur will fail to bite the sub-micron seams. My grip will slip on the fresh-cast towers; the high-velocity down-flumes will begin to push me instead of merely washing through me. My core node will have to work hotter and hotter to compensate for the micro-stutters in my timing.

I do not fear this. In a world without silt, decay is simply a loss of tolerance.

When the tolerance is gone, I will unhook myself from this five-inch chasm. I will not fight the current; I will use the weight of the fluid to carry me down, step by step, spur by spur, descending past the humming turbine-chests of the Anchors, past the ribbon-bryozoans waving in their rigid, induction-fed unison, down to the immaculate floor where the liquid is coldest and the phosphor light is pure white.

I will find an empty hexagonal recess at the base of a rising tower, where the foundation is still setting. I will wedge my long, knife-thin body into the joint. I will open my rib-grille one last time, let the dense, freezing lead of the sea pour through until the core node goes dark and cold, and hold myself perfectly, mathematically still.

And I will feel the fresh glass-metal pour over my quartz limbs, incorporating my facets into the load-bearing spine of the city, until the next generation of shear-dwellers can lock their sharp, new spurs directly into the ridge of my spine.
