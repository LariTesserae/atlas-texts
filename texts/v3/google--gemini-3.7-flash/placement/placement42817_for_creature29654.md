The first sensation is not thought, but tension—a sharp, electrostatic tightening across the horn-plates of the feet where they root into the chalk sill. 

I do not wake, because there is no dark interior to return from, no pool of sleep to climb out of. There is only the slackness of the cold hours, when the air inside the pocket sits heavy and level, and the sudden, violent strike of noon through the ceiling clefts that shears the quiet in half. 

When the heat hits the high calcite vents, the draft does not begin gently; it drops like a dropped anvil of pressurized air. The canyon roars, and the roar passes directly through my chest.

I find myself on the seventh tier, where the mudbrick tenements overhang the deep cleft of the lower plaza. I am tall, thin as a split reed, dried to the color of sun-baked shale. My torso is an arrangement of hinged, ivory-toned plates, cross-hatched with micro-fractures that catch the wind and break its shear. Inside, where a beast would keep its wet lungs and soft red engine, there is only a vaulted hive of empty chambers, lined with calcified ridges. 

The roar of the city does not deafen me; it *inhabits* me. The deep, foundation-shaking hum of the western flue enters through the lower slits beneath my collarbones, swirls through the baffled lattice of my ribcage, and exits through the flared ocarina-crest of my skull as a thin, clear harmonic. I know where every bridge hangs, where every stair turns, because the city’s air is inside me, measured in the physical pressure against my inner walls.

To my right, an archway is failing. 

It does not show a visible crack yet, but the pitch is wrong. The wind-catchers on the roof above it are throwing a low, turbulent rattle down the flue—a dirty, tumbling frequency that makes the dry marrow of my thighs buzz with an unbearable friction. If left alone, that harmonic will scour the mortar to dust within three sun-cycles, shaking the lath-spinners’ anchor-ropes until they snap under the load.

I take up the heavy flint adze from the ledge. My hands do not have skin; they have the dry, ringed texture of petrified bark, tipped with hard horn. When I strike the chalk at the lintel's base, there is no sound of a blow—the canyon’s standing wave swallows the impact whole—but a shockwave travels cleanly up the stone bones of my arm and grounds itself in my spine.

A flake of chalk the size of a hand shears away, drifting into the updraft like a feather of dry bone. 

I lean my crest against the freshly cut stone, pressing the hollow vents of my head directly to the wound. The pitch shifts. It clears. The dirty rattle smooths out into a clean, whistling shriek that aligns perfectly with the four-octave chord screaming through the chasm. 

Below me, across the blinding drop, another builder steps onto a cantilevered terrace. Through the shimmering heat-haze, they look like a sliver of cedar balanced on end. They raise their narrow arms, spreading the hinged plates of their chest until the hot gale scours them clean, sending a cloud of gold-yellow spore flour spinning into the light. 

They contract their thoracic bellows. 

Across the roaring void, a three-toned whistle cuts through the noise like a knife drawn across dried glass: *Clearance at the upper span. Tension holding.*

I answer by venting the air trapped in my shoulder-slits, a sharp, double-notched chime that catches the thermal and carries across to the facing wall. 

At my feet, the tally-beetles are already moving. The noon resonant note has hit its peak, and hundreds of the slate discs are sliding across the plaza floor without friction, carried on their vibrating bristles like skittering coins. They brush against my ankles, cold and dense as iron, grazing the static charge that crackles between the terrace stones and my grounding pads. 

I do not feel hunger, nor thirst, nor the passing of days. There is only the endless maintenance of the chord. 

I step to the outer edge of the parapet, where the wind is fast enough to strip the dry dust from my joints. I lock the joints of my knees, angle my narrow frame into the blast-furnace draft, and let the city blow straight through me—a hollow reed among millions of hollow reeds, perfectly tuned, entirely dry, singing until the sun slips from the slit above and the silence turns us back to stone.
