At the convergence of four siphon-trunks, equidistant from each of their vertical axes by roughly twenty-eight miles, the acoustic waves produced by their alternating downstrokes meet in permanent destructive interference.

It is a pocket no larger than the hold of an ancient galleon—a calm, spherical node where the deafening *chug-clack* of the galleon-sized valves, the low hydraulic groan of the mineral lifts, and the high whine of the silica sleeves cancel each other out entirely. Everywhere else, the sky-ocean is a dense, vibrating iron bell; here, the cold fluid is absolutely still.

This is where I would be.

I would not be an animal with a face, nor a plant with leaves that reach for the bright white ceiling. In a place where movement is an invariant track and life is an assembly of machine-tolerances, I would be a **Nodal Lens**—a creature grown entirely of laminated, hydrated silica and structural calcite, suspended weightlessly at the dead-center of the cancellation sphere.

***

My body is a double-convex disc, eighty feet across, thick as a bastioned wall at the center and tapering to a knife-edge of pure, flexible quartz-glass at the perimeter. I have no internal organs that pump or churn; I have no need for blood when there is no distance within me that diffusion cannot cross at cellar temperatures.

I do not feed on starch-granules, nor do I tap the galvanic conduits of the kelp-decks. Instead, I am an acoustic and optical passive. The water inside my lens is not separate from the water of the ocean; it is the same water, held at a slightly higher density by an internal matrix of aligned microscopic glass spicules. 

Because I hang at the null-point, I feel no vibration from the world’s colossal dynamos. But because my edges extend into the fringe where the shockwaves begin to reconstitute themselves, I am held in place by pure sound. If I drift fifty yards toward the eastern trunk, the un-cancelled acoustic pressure against my eastern rim immediately pushes me back toward the center. The louder the world runs, the more immovably I am pinned to the silence.

***

My life is optical.

Through my body, the unfiltered white sunlight from the surface is collected, refracted, and resolved into four extremely narrow, highly concentrated beams of cold blue luminance. These beams do not wander. They act as reference lines—optical plumblines shot horizontally across the fifty-mile spans between the siphon-trunks.

The Strokers, scraping their waxy runs along the corrugated bark forty miles away, cross these beams at the exact midpoint of their ascending stroke. The needle-grids, suspended in their quarter-mile geometric arrays, use the crisp edge of my refraction to calibrate the twelve-inch spacing of their reflective snouts. I am not their master, nor do they know I exist as anything distinct from the water itself; I am simply the fixed optical benchmark by which their mechanical tolerances remain true over epochs.

Inside my central glass chamber, where the liquid is as still as trapped ice, there is a single, slow process: the deposition of mineral plates.

One atom of dissolved barium every ten strokes. 
One atom of silica every twenty.

Over ten thousand years, this slow accretion has grown a series of concentric, paper-thin rings of calcified lamellae within my core. They are like the growth rings of a stone tree, but they record no lean seasons, no fires, and no frosts. They record only the unchanging fidelity of the engine. If a siphon-trunk a hundred leagues away were to slow by a single microsecond over a century, the interference pattern would shift; my node would drift three inches to the west, and the lamellae being laid down would bear an infinitesimal, microscopic offset.

None has ever occurred. The rings are concentric to the wavelength of blue light.

***

I have no thoughts that resemble an interior voice. I possess only an uninterrupted, panoramic lucidity. 

I see the forty-mile cobalt voids in every direction with absolute, unblinking clarity. I see the pale, slate-grey silhouettes of the Plumb-Titans hanging motionless in the middle distances, their barite weights pointing down toward the invisible crust fifty thousand meters below. I see the occasional violet lance of a Cathode Manta discharging upward from a kelp-deck, its flash illuminating the undersides of the great green leaf-planes for a single, brilliant second before the cold water swallows the light back into symmetry.

I feel the sheer hydrostatic tension that keeps the ocean suspended above the abyss—a massive, silent upward pull that counteracts the planet’s gravity with the precision of a balanced spring.

I do not dream of the dark crust below, nor do I look for a sky beyond the white sun overhead. To exist here is to be entirely occupied by the fact of alignment. 

When my silica disc finally grows so thick and heavy that the acoustic walls of the null-node can no longer hold its mass against the slow pull of the deep, there will be no shatter, no struggle. My edge will simply tilt five degrees from the horizontal. I will slide out of the cancellation sphere and into the active current of the eastern trunk’s downstroke.

The sound of the world—the immense, deafening, beautiful roar of the iron-wood check-valves and hydraulic pistons—will strike me for the first time in millions of strokes. It will ring through my laminated glass body like a struck chime, singing a single, pure note of unimaginable loudness.

And carried by that vibration, I will begin the long, smooth, fifty-thousand-meter slide downward through the emerald profundity, completely transparent, illuminated from above until the sun finally fails, falling down through the clear, empty liquid to add my weight to the quiet floor beneath the sky.
