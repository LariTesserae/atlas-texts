The light at this elevation does not diffuse; it cuts. The sky is an unyielding, hard cerulean, so pale and thin that the sun casts razor-sharp shadows across the naked rock. Below the inhabited ledges, thousands of yards down, lies a continuous floor of cloud that rolls against the cliffs like an ocean against a continent, but up here, across a mountain massif that stretches beyond every horizon, the air is clean, freezing, and perpetually still.

This is a young landscape, not yet softened by centuries of rain or deep glacial carving. The mountains are immense blocks of pale slate, fractured basalt, and granite, sheared off along vertical joint planes with crisp, untumbled edges. The rock is brittle and sharp enough to cut leather. Because of the extreme altitude, the temperature rarely rises above freezing, yet there is moisture here—not in rivers or deep snowdrifts, but as a subtle, pervasive dampness held in the shadows. Nightly, frost grows in delicate needle-crystals across the rock; during the bright hours, the sun melts this rime into thin, glistening films that trickle down vertical crevices, feeding small cushions of black moss and sulfur-colored lichens before vanishing back into the freezing dry air.

Clinging to these jagged terraces and fault lines is an immense city, dense and vertically stacked, sprawling across leagues of cliffside. 

It is a metropolis built entirely out of the mountain itself with the simplest of tools. There are no iron arches or complex engineering feats, only the staggering, cumulative labor of stone laid upon stone. Millions of dry-masonry walls step up the inclines in infinite tiers. The houses are small, thick-walled cells of rough-split slate, chinked with stiff clay and dried lichen, roofed with overlapping stone flags weighted down with heavy river cobbles hauled up from ancient glacial beds. The dwellings are built wall-to-wall, roof-to-terrace, forming vast, continuous honeycombs that follow the natural contours of the rock fractures. Stepped lanes, barely wide enough for two laden beasts to pass, climb the cliffs in relentless zigzags, worn smooth by millions of soft-shod footsteps.

Despite the bitter cold and the barrenness of the terrain, the city hums with quiet, heavy density. Life here is sustained by a profound abundance of high-altitude fauna. Everywhere along the stepped ramps and sunken courtyards, there are animals. Great, broad-chested ruminants with shaggy, grease-heavy coats of gray and ochre wool stand tethered to stone posts, their slow breath rising in thick plumes of steam. Smaller mountain goats with spiral horns clatter along the retaining walls, picking at dried scrub brought up from lower elevations. In the eaves and nesting niches left deliberately in the dry-stone masonry, tens of thousands of black choughs and white-breasted swifts roost, their wings occasionally snapping the stillness as they wheel out over the empty gulfs below.

Human movement through this space is deliberate, slow, and largely silent. People move bundled in heavy, multi-layered garments of dense felt, oiled leather, and uncured hides, their faces greased against the sun-glare and the windless, biting frost. Because wood is precious—harvested only from the stunted, twisted juniper that manages a foothold in the sheltered couloirs—fires are small and carefully tended. Low, flat-bottomed braziers burn cakes of dried beast dung and peat, sending faint, sweetish threads of white smoke out of smoke-holes that drift lazily across the stone tiers before dispersing into the thin air.

Sound carries strangely in this vast, quiet world. The high density of stone and bodies absorbs echoes locally, reducing the city's voice to a muted, rhythmic thrum: the dull tap of a stone mallet shaping slate, the dry click of hooves on cold rock, the low, guttural calls of herders, and the perpetual, faint whistling of air shifting through the millions of chinks in the masonry. 

Everything here exists in a state of long, patient endurance. The mountains are too vast to be conquered, so the city simply nests within their fractures, growing outward stone by stone, generation by generation, held fast by gravity and cold against the sheer vertical face of the world.
