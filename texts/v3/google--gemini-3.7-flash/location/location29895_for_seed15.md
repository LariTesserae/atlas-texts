The chamber is entirely made of fitted limestone and kiln-baked terracotta tiles, sunken eight fathoms beneath the surface of a sun-baked terrace. It is small—scarcely forty paces across—a hidden retention vault built to hold and channel the overflow of a vast, hand-dug thermal aqueduct system. 

Every surface is constructed. There is no raw bedrock here; the floor is a herringbone weave of dense red pavers, and the walls rise in clean, vertical courses of dressed white stone, their edges still crisp from the mason’s chisel, the mortar seams pale and chalky where the water has only begun to smooth them. 

The space is entirely flooded, filled to the brim with clear, warm water that hovers at the temperature of a fresh bath. Above, the vault is pierced by wide, unglazed light-wells that open directly to a cloudless noon sky. Because of this, the light beneath the surface is blinding. Beams of raw, white sunlight plunge down through the water column, cutting through the turquoise depth to strike the red-tiled floor in searing, shifting grids of caustic light. The brightness reflects off the pale stone walls, filling the submerged room with an intense, shadowless radiance.

The water is in constant, violent motion. Heavy wooden sluice gates, bound with hand-hammered bronze straps and held by wooden pins, funnel massive volumes of water through the chamber’s lower perimeter. Inflow spouts—simple square conduits lined with smooth river slate—pour torrents into the pool from the north wall, while outflow grates swallow it on the south. The sound inside the water is immense: a heavy, resonant roar that vibrates through the teeth and ribs, the percussive thudding of colliding currents, and the high, rushing hiss of millions of tiny air bubbles sheared apart against the stone lips of the intakes.

There are no fish, no snails, no drifting nymphs. The system is too new and the current too fierce for animal life to take hold. Life here is sparse and strictly vegetal: along the sunny margins of the upper tiers, where the flow slackens against the corners, fine emerald silk-algae clings to the mortar lines. It flutters violently in the draft of the sluices, bright green threads stretched taut against the terracotta, waving like combed hair. In the deeper recesses, a thin, velvet crust of golden diatom film has begun to dust the flat faces of the bricks, soft and fragile, easily dislodged by a stray eddy.

Floating debris is rare and quickly evacuated by the draw of the lower drains, leaving the water crystalline. Small wooden guide-pegs and lost dowels occasionally spin through the shafts of sunlight, turning over and over in the bright turbulence before being pulled down into the dark throats of the drain conduits. 

The heat of the water radiates through the stone, keeping the chamber in a steady, humid equilibrium. It is an entirely manufactured pocket of light and pressure, an engine of masonry and hydraulic weight, humming with the clean, fierce kinetic force of water moving from one man-made level to the next.
