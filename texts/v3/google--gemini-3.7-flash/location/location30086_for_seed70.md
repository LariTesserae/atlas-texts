The water begins without a shore. Held aloft in a massive high-altitude saddle between newly thrust peaks, this elevated sea rests thousands of meters above the lowlands, cupped within a jagged cradle of unweathered stone. The water is deep, extraordinarily clear, and perpetually warm, heated from below by the lingering vitality of the young crust that broke these mountains into the sky.

Looking across the expanse, the scale is vast and unobstructed. Beneath a bright, sun-flooded surface, the underwater terrain drops and climbs in razor-sharp relief. Because the stone is young and has known neither the grinding of glaciers nor the dulling friction of surface storms, the topography consists of sheer fault-scarps, needles of dark basalt, and fractured granite shelves that look as though they were snapped apart only yesterday. Their edges remain knife-clean, cutting upward through the water column toward the sunlit ceiling.

Despite the sharpness of the rock, the basin is filled to near-saturation with life. The warmth and constant light have fostered an immense, dense forest of aquatic flora that clings to every facet of the young stone. These are not oceanic kelps, but broad-leafed, vascular water-plants with leaves the size of skiffs and stems thick as ancient timber, anchored directly into the fresh fractures of the rock. They rise in dense, overlapping tiers: deep down, in the shadowy crevasses between spires, grow thick mats of liver-colored moss and velvet-textured liverworts; higher up, along the vertical walls, vast vertical gardens of fern-like fronds and amber-veined ribbons reach upward, their tips floating just beneath the surface in the bright, warm wash of daylight. 

Every cubic meter is occupied. Stems cross stems, broad pads shadow narrow tendrils, and pale, translucent filaments weave the vertical forests together into a contiguous, breathing mass. Yet there is almost nothing moving through it. Animal life is exceedingly sparse. Occasionally, a single translucent organism—resembling a slow-pulsing lung or a plate of paper-thin cartilage—drifts through an open corridor between fronds, grazing without haste on the film of micro-algae coating the rock. There are no shoals of fish, no sudden darts of predation, no quick shadows.

The sound within the shelf is a deep, warm silence. The only acoustic presence is the dull, heavy pressure of the liquid itself and the microscopic, continuous whisper of photosynthesis: billions of tiny, pearl-bright oxygen bubbles releasing from the underside of leaves, ticking faintly against the rock as they detach and begin their slow, unhurried ascent to the sky.

The dynamic of this world is immensely patient. The currents are weak, driven only by gentle convective loops where warm water wells up from the dark fissures between the peaks and cools slightly near the surface. Fronds twenty meters long sway over the course of hours, leaning infinitesimally toward one cliff face, then easing back toward the other. The light shifts from brilliant, crystalline gold in the morning to a warm, refractive amber in the late afternoon, cutting clean angled paths through the submerged canyons, illuminating a jungle that grows upward into the sun, perfectly preserved within its high, still basin.
