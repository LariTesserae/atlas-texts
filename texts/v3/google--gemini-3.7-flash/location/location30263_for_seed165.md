At this altitude, the air should be too thin to carry weight, yet it carries an immensity of sound. 

It is not wind—the atmosphere does not stir. The sound is a deafening, monolithic chord, uniform and unvarying, like the friction of two continents ground together and then held perfectly still at the moment of peak tension. It has no rhythm, no pulse, and no source. It exists equally across leagues of gray distance, vibrating through the marrow of the stone and through the damp, cold film that covers every exposed surface.

The land consists of the Upper Sills: an expanse of extreme elevation where the crust of the earth fractured into vertical ribbons, and then ceased to obey gravity’s singular pull. Slabs of dense, slate-colored basalt—miles long, centuries thick—stand upright, angled like dropped cards, frozen mid-topple. Between these towering slats lie gulfs of sheer nothingness, dizzying abysses that drop away into gray vapor, leaving the ground density close to zero. You do not walk across a plain here; you move along knife-edge margins suspended in the stratosphere, separated from the next nearest surface by hundreds of yards of empty air.

Yet, spatial continuity here has failed in a permanent, settled manner. 

Looking across the chasms, one sees that distance does not diminish objects in the ordinary way; rather, distant stone ridges appear fully clear and immediate, but sheared into repeating horizontal slices, like a painting cut into strips and displaced by an inch. If you drop a pebble into the void between the slabs, it does not accelerate downward; it drifts horizontally at a steady, fixed speed, passes into a pocket of reversed refraction, and simply remains there, pinned against the dim sky, joining thousands of other suspended chips of shale that have hung motionless for millennia.

The light is a permanent, bruised twilight. The sun never rises above the horizon, nor does it set; a pale, cold luminescence bleeds upward from beneath the lowest gaps of the rock, casting long, upward-reaching shadows toward the apex of the sky.

Everything is wet, though it never rains. Moisture condenses directly out of the stillness, a heavy, frigid dew that coats the vertical rock faces in a sheen of black gloss. In the small cups and natural indentations along the stone ridges, this water gathers into shallow, glassy pools. These pools never evaporate and never freeze, despite the bitter, biting cold; they sit in absolute equilibrium, holding the reflections of fractured sky without a single ripple to disturb them.

Vegetation is scarce, almost mineral in its habits. In the narrow crevices where the slabs join, patches of *liver-glass* cling to the damp basalt. It is a lichen-like organism with the texture of cold tallow, dark indigo and translucent, feeding entirely on the constant vibration of the sound. It does not grow, sprout, or decay; individual clusters have occupied the exact same square inches of stone for eras, sustained in the motionless chill.

Built elements are few, ancient, and reduced to essentials. At rare intervals along the spine of the precipices stand dry-stone lintels—two rough-hewn pillars of slate capped by a third, erected without mortar, tools, or decorative intent. They frame nothing but the void. Whoever set them worked with bare hands and immense, pre-human scale, hauling slabs into alignments that match the sheared geometry of the horizon. Inside some of these lintels, the air is subtly thicker, and the deafening ambient drone drops by a fraction of a tone, providing the only shelter this realm offers: an acoustic shadow.

Fauna exists, though it scarcely behaves as living matter. 

High up on the vertical shears, where the rock face hangs inverted over the abyss, dwell the *Bell-lunged Crawlers*. They are pale, six-limbed things with shells resembling wet river stone, moving so slowly that a change in their position can only be measured over months. They possess no eyes or mouths in the conventional sense; their undersides are dominated by a cavernous, rib-lined chamber lined with damp velvet skin. This chamber captures the omnipresent noise, resonating with it, deriving energy from the sheer kinetic force of the sound. When one occasionally detaches from the cliff, it does not plummet—it drifts outward, buoyed by the acoustics of the gorge, settling onto a parallel wall miles away without a sound of impact.

There are no seasons here. There is no weather. The moisture does not turn to snow; the cold does not deepen; the drone does not fade. The entire region is a monument to an equilibrium achieved after some ancient, violent shattering of natural law. It has decayed as far as it can decay, broken as deeply as it can break, and now sits locked in its own cold, damp, ringing majesty, suspended miles above a world that has forgotten it.
