At this altitude, the atmosphere should be thin and freezing, but instead the air is thick, warm, and slick with an oily, mineral condensation. The plateau does not roll; it shears upward in fresh, razor-edged steps of unweathered slate, basalt, and ironstone, so newly cleaved that the fracture planes still smell of friction and ruptured silicates. There is no sand here, no rounded pebble—only geometry with its burrs still on. 

It is immense, spanning thousands of leagues across the upper troposphere, vast enough that it generates its own low, bruised pressure systems. Yet despite the scale, the sky is never truly open. A low roof of charcoal and umber mist hangs just overhead, trapping a humid, greenhouse warmth that rises from the stone itself. The light is a perpetual bruised twilight, filtered through leagues of suspended petroleum-vapor and hanging mosses.

What looks at first like an untouched wilderness of hyper-dense, rubbery vegetation is governed by a strange, unbuilt mechanization. There are no chimneys, no bolts, no brickwork, no signs of hands having ever touched the stone, yet the landscape behaves like an unmetered, self-assembling factory floor. 

Deep within the fissures of the young crags, massive cylindrical plugs of native iron oscillate vertically, sliding smoothly against sleeves of polished graphite with the slow, muffled stroke of low-frequency engine pistons. They are not constructed; they have crystallized this way, grown from the hot mineral broth of the plateau’s violent birth. Overhead, thick braided lianas—dense with black, spade-shaped leaves that drip warm, lubricating fluid—twist around each other in continuous, ratcheting rotations, acting as organic drive belts that transfer kinetic stress from one shifting mountain spur to the next.

The sound of all this industry is strangely smothered. The damp, dense air and the carpet of felt-like liverworts swallow vibration, reducing what should be a deafening industrial roar to a faint, rhythmic thrum felt primarily in the soles of the feet: a soft *chug-hiss-click* that travels through the rock like a suppressed pulse.

Every surface is packed. Ferns with serrated, manganese-heavy fronds sprout directly out of raw copper seams, their roots secreting acid to harvest charge from the tectonic currents running through the shelf. Intermittent bursts of warm, oily rain sweep across the ridges in sudden squalls, leaving puddles in the sharp depressions of the stone that shimmer with rainbow sheens of biological grease. In these shallow pools, larval things with segmented, stamped-tin shells twitch and feed on falling soot.

Reality here is loose at the seams, refusing to settle into permanent categories. Matter shifts its phase states under minor thermal fluctuations. You can watch a vertical spine of black glass slowly soften, develop the fibrous grain of hardwood, sprout a canopy of oily red lichen, and then, within an hour, stiffen into a cold, counter-rotating cluster of basalt gears that turn once, jam against a slab of native lead, and shear back into motionless gravel. The terrain is in a state of rapid, violent improvisation, constantly testing new configurations of mechanical ecology.

Fauna fills every crevice in swarming, quiet abundance. Soft-bodied, multi-jointed organisms glide along the reciprocating stone columns, scraping off the grease and sulfur-crusts with hardened keratin rasps. Some mimic the surrounding industrial forms—blind, beetle-like things the size of shields that link head-to-tail over rushing thermal vents, forming temporary, flexing pipes that direct boiling steam toward colonies of pale, blind grubs nestled in the rock. Above them, long-tailed creatures with skin like vulcanized rubber drift between the towering moss-strangled spires, their wings undulating with the silent, measured rhythm of a loom’s shuttle.

Nothing remains static for long. A ridge that was a sheer cliff at dawn may buckle by mid-day under subterranean hydraulic pressure, folding like a compressed bellows to form a series of tight, warm valleys choked with fast-growing, resinous scrub. The sheer kinetic energy of the plateau is staggering, yet it spends itself in this continuous, claustrophobic self-churning.

In the dimness, the landscape continues its quiet, massive work: pumping, condensing, growing, and fracturing. It is a world caught in the immediate aftermath of an impossible creation, frantically manufacturing itself out of its own raw, mineral flesh, entirely indifferent to the absence of an engineer.
