The sector occupies a shallow, sunken depression no wider than four or five city blocks—an intimate, sunken borough enclosed by a perimeter of raw, un-weathered shale that looks as though it were sheared open an hour ago. The air is sharply, bitingly cold, hovering just above the threshold where breath turns to glass, yet it holds a quiet, pervasive dampness. A fine, sub-zero mist does not fall so much as hang suspended between surfaces, condensing upon every cold plane in perfectly spherical, mercury-bright beads that never quite freeze and never run.

It is an urban district that is simultaneously embryonic and unfathomably old in its design. The architecture is impossibly dense: slender towers, cantilevers, cubic habitations, and braided conduits are packed together like teeth in a jaw, leaving only narrow, canyon-like alleys barely a stride wide. Yet there is no wear here. The edges of the buildings are atom-sharp, mathematically flawless, untouched by wind, scuff, or soot. It is a city that has just arrived, or is constantly being re-rendered in real time, cast down into the cold rock without the intermediate step of construction.

The materials are post-physical. What appear to be walls of black slate are warm to the touch from within, vibrating with a high-frequency, soundless hum that can only be felt in the dental nerves. Window frames do not hold glass; they hold sheets of stabilized vacuum that reflect the daylight at a half-second delay. If you look into a doorway, the interior does not correspond to the building's exterior volume; you are looking down an avenue that stretches for miles into a pale, neutral daylight, though you can reach through and touch the frosted back wall of a utility closet six inches in.

Light here is flat and even—an overcast afternoon's luminescence that seems to emanate from the ambient air rather than a sun. Shadows fall in two directions at once, faint and gray, cast by a logic that accommodates multiple simultaneous viewpoints. Where two buildings lean toward each other, their upper stories don't merely touch; they interpenetrate, sharing the same coordinates without displacement, so that the copper-colored grain of a lintel passes through the matte white surface of an adjacent mezzanine without disturbing either.

Vegetation is minimal, almost accidental. In the damp seams where the un-eroded bedrock meets the base of the super-dense infrastructure, a pale, fibrous lichen grows. It does not spread like ordinary mold; its fronds are geometric, expanding in strict hexagonal arrays across the damp stones, drinking the condensation. Occasionally, a single, stunted shrub with leaves like polished pewter will push through a joint in the pavement, stiff and motionless in the chill, its root system visibly entangled with exposed fiber-optic bundles that pulse with faint, pale-blue Cherenkov luminescence.

There is very little sound. The cold suppresses resonance, swallowing footsteps before they can echo off the sheer, immaculate walls. The only noises are small, patient, and discrete: the faint, rhythmic ticking of thermal expansion in the conduits, the soft *clink* of a supercooled droplet detaching from an overhead truss to shatter like beadwork on the cobbles, and the occasional low, spatial sigh—a pressure differential equalizing between two incompatible pockets of geometry.

Fauna is extremely rare, and what exists does not belong to standard biology. Now and then, a creature the size of a swift will dart between the narrow rooflines. It does not flap wings; it moves along predetermined vectors in instantaneous, discontinuous leaps, disappearing from one point and reappearing six feet ahead, leaving behind a faint smell of ozone and wet stone. On the lower levels, near the sluggish drainage gullies where condensation collects in shallow, dark pools, small, segmented entities with shells like polished bismuth drift slowly over the wet pavement, cleaning nothing, eating nothing, tracing endless, intricate figures-of-eight across the flagstones.

The city operates on an extremely slow, deliberate cycle. Every few hours, the orientation of a street will shift by a few degrees—not by mechanical rotation, but through a slow, imperceptible drift in the local metric of space. A stairwell that led downward toward the cold basin floor gradually becomes horizontal, then subtly tilts upward, its steps remaining level to the feet of anyone standing on them.

Everything feels paused on the lip of an event that may take centuries to occur. It is a district built for millions, dense enough to house an empire, yet scaled to the footprint of a village, utterly immaculate, freezing, and waiting for the laws of physics to finish settling into place.
