The scale of the place is that of a grand municipal square, perhaps four city blocks across and vaulted beneath a dome of fused ironwood boughs, but within those boundaries the volume is overwhelmingly hollow. 

It is an urban district grown rather than built, yet constructed with the strict, geometric intent of an ancient capital. Colonnades of pale, calcified heartwood rise sixty cubits into the warm air before branching into interlocking ribbed vaults. Streets—wide, paved in interlocking discs of dense, polished lignum vitae—run straight and true, flanked by multi-tiered facades punctured by thousands of empty window-cells and arched balconies. Yet everything here is a single, continuous organism. The mortar is living cambium; the lintels are pressurized root-struts; the gutters are open vascular channels carrying a thin, steady flow of bitter, mineral-heavy dampness.

Despite the vastness of the vegetative mass that bounds the space, the center is a massive void. Between the eastern tiers and the western colonnades lies a yawning chasm of warm, empty air, hundreds of yards across, interrupted only by tension-cables of braided vine thick as ship masts, stretching taut across the chasm to keep the opposing districts from collapsing into one another. 

The air is warm—the temperature of an animal’s mouth—and persistently damp. Moisture does not fall as rain, but condenses out of the stagnant atmosphere onto the cold upper ribs of the ceiling, falling in heavy, rhythmic drops that strike the wooden pavements with the crack of small stones. 

The sound is not an ambient background; it is an absolute, bone-grinding roar. 

At this scale, the internal plumbing of the city is deafening. Behind the five-story facades, millions of microscopic stomata snap open and closed in coordinated tides, producing a continuous, mechanical hiss like steam locomotives venting under high pressure. Above, within the massive structural boles that arch across the ceiling, billion-gallon xylem channels cavitate under catastrophic tension, groaning and booming like an iron suspension bridge in a gale. The acoustics of the empty central volume catch this hydraulic thunder and multiply it, sending standing waves of pressurized sound bouncing from facade to facade until the air itself vibrates violently enough to blur the edges of the architecture.

There are no tools here, no iron fittings, no glass, no soot. It is a city that bypassed the machine by having its tissues differentiate into the necessary shapes: gutters, aqueducts, cisterns, staircases grown in precise, spiraling helical steps.

Yet the geometry does not hold. 

Where the sound reaches its peak intensity, reality warps under the strain of the plant’s impossible biological pressure. Perspectives within the empty square do not converge at a normal vanishing point. A boulevard that appears to run flat and level for two hundred paces is found, upon walking it, to incline at an impossible seventy-degree angle, though gravity continues to pull the body squarely down against the bark-tiles. In certain corners, the shadows cast by the midday-bright light—which filters down through massive, translucent panes of living cellulose in the roof—do not correspond to the arches above them, but to arches that were shed three centuries ago during an earlier growth ring. Looking across the central emptiness, the far wall seems simultaneously a mile distant and close enough to touch; sound arrives before the motion that caused it is seen, shattered and reordered by pockets of differential atmospheric density.

Through this roaring, warped cathedral of timber moves an intense, frantic ecology. 

Because the architecture is entirely cellular, it sheds a continuous dross of fibrous scales, dried sap crystals, and shedding bark. Feeding on this rain of debris are clouds of blind, membranous fauna—creatures resembling soft-shelled manta rays crossed with locusts, with wings of thin, leathery skin that beat against the air in furious synchrony. They nest by the tens of thousands in the empty window-cells of the facades, launching into the central void in spiraling plumes that look like smoke. Predatory centipedes, each the length of a spear and armored in pale, wax-coated chitin, run with sickening speed along the underside of the tension-vines, snatching the fliers from the air with hooked forelegs. 

The dynamics of the place are rapid, violent, and biological. Every few hours, a sudden contraction runs through the city’s heartwood: the walls groan, the tension-vines hum at a pitch that makes teeth ache, and the damp gutters surge with a violent, foaming flush of pale sap that scours the pavements clean before draining down dark, spiral maw-holes in the center of the avenues. The air grows instantly hotter, wetter, and the deafening hydraulic roar shifts up an octave into a sustained, screaming whistle.

Then the valve clears. The water recedes into the pores of the floor. The roar settles back into its familiar, thunderous cavitation. The vast, empty room—a city without citizens, built entirely of living wood, suspended in a permanent state of hydraulic tension—resumes its blind, roaring growth in the warm, green-filtered light.
