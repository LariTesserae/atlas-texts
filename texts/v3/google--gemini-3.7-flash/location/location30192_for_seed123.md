Inside the lower casing of a copper-jacketed turbine housing, beneath a round quartz inspection port, a territory exists that is scarcely larger than a steamer trunk, yet it is full to bursting. 

The floor is a series of stepped, newly milled brass ribs and cold-rolled steel troughs. There is almost no wear on the metal; the lathe marks are still razor-sharp, silver-bright, and unblunted by age or rust. Everything here is fresh, installed recently enough that the blue temper-tint on the bolt heads still gleams cleanly in the light that beams downward through the thick glass disc above. The illumination is clear, steady, and warm, casting distinct, tiny shadows across an intensely crowded floor.

Between the brass ribs, there is no empty space. The cavity is utterly saturated with bodies. 

They are small, segmented organisms—chitinous, amber-shelled creatures the size of knuckles, interspersed with pale, flat-bodied crawlers whose jointed limbs interlock like the bristles of two brushes pressed together. They do not pile into loose heaps; they tessellate. Every square centimeter of the warm metal surface is occupied by a shell, a set of twitching maxillary palps, or the translucent, oiled abdomen of a brooding female. Where the metal steps down into the drainage troughs, the living mass simply thickens, layer upon layer of them shifting in place with a dry, frictionless glide.

Water here is not a body or a current, but an intermittent, mechanical pulse. Every few minutes, a micro-valve somewhere above vents a short, warm breath of steam. It does not fog the air for long; instead, it condenses immediately against the cooler bronze walls, forming heavy, perfect beads that roll down the vertical fluting in fast, erratic lines. The fauna drink it on its descent. Long, thread-like tongues whip out from between overlapping shell plates to catch the droplets before they strike the trough bottom. By the time the water reaches the floor, it is almost entirely harvested, leaving only a dark, greasy dampness that keeps the underbelly of the swarm slick and cool.

Vegetation is virtually absent, save for a microscopic, chalk-white velvet that frosts the edges of the unpainted steel screws—a fungal skin so low and thin it resembles powdered sugar more than plant life. It offers little sustenance; the ecosystem runs instead on the fine, aerosolized tallow injected into the steam line, and on a vicious, quiet cycle of cannibalism and shedding. 

The atmosphere is heavy with the smell of hot mineral oil, distilled moisture, and the faint, sweet musk of living chitin. It is noticeably warm—a steady, incubation heat thrown off by the heavy mechanical core just behind the bulkhead. 

Despite the sheer kinetic energy of the chamber, it is remarkably quiet. The great machine to which this pocket belongs must be turning somewhere distant, for the metal beneath vibrates at a frequency felt rather than heard—a deep, settling hum. The only audible sounds within the enclosure are the soft, collective clicking of thousands of clawed feet against brass, the tiny, sharp *snick* of an occasional valve shifting its seat, and the rhythmic, muffled *thump-hiss* of the condensation cycle. 

Movement within the swarm is constant, driven by a tight, frantic metabolism. When one of the larger, iron-jawed burrowers shifts its weight to scrape at a bead of grease, a ripple passes through hundreds of adjacent bodies, a shiver of shell and leg that travels across the entire shelf before settling. Dying individuals are not left to rot; they are dismantled within minutes by their neighbors, their fresh, unhardened shell pieces passed mouth-to-mouth and incorporated into living tissue with mechanical efficiency.

Overhead, an array of tiny, exposed brass linkages moves on a slow, three-minute sweep. As the arm swings past, its shadow wipes across the packed field of creatures, causing thousands of pale antennae to sweep upward in unison, tracing the edge of the dark, then folding flat again the moment the light returns. Nothing leaves the chamber. Nothing enters except the light, the vapor, and the heat. The space is entirely complete, locked in its small, bright, fiercely active cube of metal, flourishing in the unyielding gap between high engineering and swarming life.
