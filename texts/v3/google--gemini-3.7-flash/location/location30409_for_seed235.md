The roar does not rise or fall; it occupies the atmosphere like a physical mineral. It is the deafening, constant shriek of high-pressure thermal steam venting through millions of hollow, calcified flutes that make up the foundations of the city, mingled with the weight of uncounted trillions of tons of warm water weeping across vertical miles of stone. You do not hear it with the ears alone; it vibrates the marrow and stabilizes the air, a permanent, white-noise plateau that renders silence a conceptual impossibility.

This is a metropolis suspended at an altitude where the sky should be thin and freezing, but here the air is thick, sweltering, and heavily pressurized. The temperature remains fixed in an unyielding, tropical fever. Immense heat rises from the depths of abyssal fissures below, turning the perpetual deluge into an omnipresent, suffocating vapor. Sunlight filters through this mist with standard, indifferent clarity—neither dim nor blinding, just an even, diffuse illumination that reveals the city’s staggering, impossible geometry.

There are no tools here, no iron rivets, no mortar, no signs of mechanical craft. The architecture is vast, urban, and entirely biological, long settled into an ancient, weathered equilibrium. Colossal structures resembling high-density tenements, multi-tiered colonnades, and vaulted basilicas rise out of the mountain-faces, but they are composed of calcified lignin, self-smoothing travertine, and fossilized root-masses that grew into perfect architectural symmetry before petrifying into place. Broad avenues paved with interlocking, slate-like lily pads wide as galleons span across three-thousand-meter drops, their underbellies supported by taut, braided cables of living ironwood that have not flexed in a thousand years.

The density is staggering. Tier upon tier of habitation-chambers, open plazas, and monumental arches cling to sheer precipices, stacked so closely that the facades seem to touch across narrow, vertical chasms. Water is everywhere, slicking every surface with a permanent, glassy sheath. It cascades down the fluted pilasters, pools in vast stepped cisterns carved into the flanks of the peaks, and condenses on the underside of overhanging boulevards, raining continuously into the terraces below. Yet nothing rots. The vegetation is lush, primeval, and wildly vigorous—broad, rubbery leaves the size of sails, deep emerald mosses that form dense, springy carpets over the masonry, and pale, fibrous vines that knit the buildings together into a single, contiguous mega-structure.

Reality here has folded upon itself to accommodate this mass. Gravity does not operate from a single downward plane. The sheer verticality of the mountain-spires acts as its own lateral basin; causeways run along ninety-degree rock walls where the warm water flows sideways, clinging to channels in the stone, and one can walk along facades where the abyss is simply to the left rather than below. In certain plazas, the perspective bends so severely that looking straight ahead reveals the underbelly of a district suspended directly overhead, bathed in the same steady mist and light, its hanging gardens dripping into the open void between them.

Despite the city’s complex, settlement-scale organization—its defined districts, stepped amphitheaters, and clear infrastructural arteries—there is no life that crawls, flies, or speaks. No birds nest in the high cornices; no insects skim the warm, overflowing cisterns. The only vitality belongs to the plant life and the stone itself, locked in an ancient, mutual metabolism. 

Everything is established, balanced, and utterly motionless in its progression. No stone chips away; no new shoots disturb the ancient symmetry of the avenues. The water falls, boils into steam, condenses upon the high vaults, and drains back down in a closed, eternal cycle. The heat never dissipates into the vast emptiness of the upper sky, and the deafening acoustic pressure never wavers by a single decibel. It is a metropolis grown at the edge of the world, frozen in a perpetual, thunderous noon.
