The heat gathers at the bottom of the basin like heavy oil. It does not lift or circulate; it pools over the wet silt and the broad, dark foliage, trapped beneath a canopy so dense and continuous that virtually no sky exists above it. In the darkness, the air is thick, warm, and smelling faintly of wet leaf mold, hot iron, and animal fur.

This is a lowland trough, wide enough to take days to cross, carved smooth long ago by slow-moving alluvial mud that has since settled into soft, rounded swells and shallow gullies. The ground is never fully flooded, but it never dries. Condensation beads on every downward-facing surface, running in silent, hair-thin trickles down the ribs of leaves and the undersides of exposed steel pipes.

Cables and unhoused industrial machinery cut through the wilderness in long, uninterrupted lines. There are no shelters here, no walls or platforms or abandoned quarters—only the raw, functional infrastructure of heavy industry laid directly onto the earth. Massive cast-iron conduits, four feet thick and joined by bolted flanges, stretch across the valley floor, carrying pressurized steam or cooling fluid from somewhere deep below to somewhere far beyond the basin. In places, autonomous pump stations—unpainted, massive, and humming with a dull, low-frequency vibration—sit half-buried in the soil, their pistons rising and falling with long, patient strokes. Lubricating grease has seeped into the surrounding mud, forming slick, tarry rings that the jungle plants root straight through without hesitation.

The vegetation is relentless, pressing tightly against every machine and boulder. Ficus-like roots with pale, fibrous skin wind around the hot iron casings, using the radiant mechanical warmth to fuel rapid, tangled growth. Broad-leafed aroids and thick-stemmed ferns shoulder against one another in total saturation; there is no open patch of dirt, no bare stone. Where one plant dies, three others are already rooted in its decaying crown, their leaves slick with ambient moisture.

Beneath and between these plants, the fauna exists in staggering, suffocating numbers.

In the dark, the landscape is almost entirely composed of living bodies. Thousands of small, blunt-headed lizards lie stacked three and four deep along the warm flanges of the steam pipes, their dry scales clicking softly whenever one shifts position. The mud between the root buttresses is churned by endless colonies of burrowing frogs, whose smooth, wet backs push against one another like cobblestones in a packed street. Above them, in the tangle of lower boughs and trailing electrical conduits, thick clusters of fruit bats and heavy, short-winged moths hang in continuous sheets, their wings twitching in the damp heat.

Despite this density of life, the valley is mostly quiet. The sound does not rise to a chorus; it is absorbed by the sheer bulk of living matter and soft foliage. What remains is a vast, muffled murmur: the continuous, soft rustle of millions of limbs dragging through wet mulch, the wet pop of amphibians repositioning in the mud, and the distant, rhythmic *clack-thump* of an unmonitored mechanical rocker arm moving through its stroke somewhere in the blackness.

The machinery provides a steady, artificial microclimate of localized heat that keeps the cold-blooded life active even in the perpetual absence of light. Snakes, thick-bodied and dull-patterned, drape themselves across insulated power cables, their weight sagging the heavy rubber sheathing toward the ground. Blind beetles, some as broad as a hand, crawl methodically over the brass pressure gauges, their legs tapping against the glass without registering the dials beneath.

Nothing happens quickly here. The water falls as condensation, collects on the skin of machines and leaves, drips into the mud, and evaporates back into the warm, closed air without ever forming a true stream. The iron rusts at a crawl, sealed beneath coats of grease and the anaerobic slime of algae that thrive in the heat. The animals feed, breed, and die in place, their bodies trampled immediately into the rich, warm loam by the sheer volume of their neighbors. It is a world fully occupied, operating in absolute dark, steady and self-contained, breathing slowly around the humming metal spine that runs through its center.
