The water here is not an ocean basin, but a cold, heavy sea suspended at an impossible altitude—a drowned high-altitude rift where the pressure is immense, the temperature rests just above freezing, and light never penetrates from above. 

Rising through miles of black, sunless water are colossal, intentional structures. They are not geological formations, nor are they wild reefs. Every surface, column, and spanning bridge has been grown and laid down through a deliberate, non-mechanical architecture: miles of petrified peat-masonry, interlocking iron-wood ribbing, and calcified kelp-struts woven into towering aqueduct-spires and open-vaulted cathedrals. There is no raw rock or exposed stone beneath it; the foundation of this world is entirely constructed of ancient, living botanical mass that has long since hardened into a dark, fibrous timber-stone.

Despite the scale of these constructions—which rise thousands of feet from unseen roots into the high water column—the landscape is defined by vast, empty distances. A single buttress of woven black reed-wood might stretch across a three-mile gulf of empty, ink-cold water before meeting another soaring colonnade. Between these colossal members lies only the open, silent abyss of the high sea, suspended in pitch darkness.

Sound, however, fills the void completely. The landscape is loud with a heavy, constant, hydraulic resonance. The oceanic current, moving steadily across the immense open spans, forces water through hollow flute-canals drilled into the living timber of the spires. The result is a perpetual, low-frequency organ drone—a booming, chest-vibrating chord that never modulates, never falters, and carries across vast leagues of water. Layered over this bass drone is the unrelenting acoustic chatter of animal life: the sharp, percussive clicks of millions of pale, blind crustaceans grazing on the fibrous walls, and the hydro-acoustic groans of long-bodied abyssal swimmers communicating across the emptiness.

The water itself is thick with fauna. In the wide black gaps between the timber towers, colossal, slow-moving filter-feeders drift like pale curtains, their skin ghost-white and translucent, feeding on the eternal snow of dead plant matter that drifts from the high canopies. Schools of ribbon-eels with vestigial eyes navigate the gaps in tight, synchronized ribbons, brushing against the smooth, worn-down flanks of the architecture.

Erosion here has finished its work. These structures have stood in the cold dark for so long that their edges are perfectly rounded, burnished by eons of laminar water flow into soft, aerodynamic curves. There are no jagged breaks or fresh fractures. The living substrate grows at the exact, microscopic pace at which the slow currents wear it away, locking the entire drowned territory in a state of absolute, time-invariant equilibrium. 

To drift through this space is to move between massive, silent black ribs rising out of nowhere and disappearing into nothing, surrounded by the deafening hum of water through ancient wood, in a cold and unchanging dark that has held its shape since the beginning.
