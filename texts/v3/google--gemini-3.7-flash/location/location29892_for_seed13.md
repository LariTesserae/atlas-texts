The elevation makes the air sharp and thin, holding a permanent, bracing chill that smells faintly of cracked slate and cold spring water. Here, thousands of meters above the lowlands, a massive high-altitude shelf spreads between sheer, unsoftened peaks. The geology is young: the mountains did not erode into gentle slopes, but thrust upward in clean, violent fractures—black basalt split along crisp geometric planes, pale quartz veins exposed like raw bone, and talus fields made of jagged, razor-edged slabs that have not yet had the centuries required to round their corners.

Spread across this high stone amphitheater is a city of immense proportions, though it breathes with extraordinary emptiness. 

The architecture is vast, monumental, and entirely manual. There are no iron cranes or complex pulleys here; the metropolis was built through sheer, staggering masses of stacked dry-stone, colossal lintels dragged across log rollers, and deeply terraced cyclopean causeways. Broad thoroughfares paved with carefully fitted fieldstones stretch hundreds of yards between low, flat-topped stone palaces and tiered megalithic courts. Despite the urban scale—the city covers miles of the mountain shelf, stepped deliberately up the rising contours of the rock—the buildings stand generously spaced. Immense plazas of bare, unpolished flagstone separate one district from the next, creating a rhythm of dense stone monoliths alternating with wide, open tracts of mountain sky.

Moisture clings to the place in a state of quiet suspension. It is not wet with rain, but perpetually damp with condensation. A persistent, cool dew beads upon the undersides of the great stone beams and glazes the sharp facets of the unweathered rock. In every fissure between the hand-hewn blocks, vegetation thrives with vibrant, cold-climate lushness: dense cushions of emerald star-moss, creeping alpine saxifrage with thick succulent leaves, and sprays of dark, stiff-fronded ferns that drink from the perpetual seam-water of the cliffs. The greenery does not rot, nor does it overgrow its boundaries; it sits in a dense, vivid fringe around the base of every monolith.

Light fills the valley with the stark, unblinking clarity of mid-morning. It is a normal, brilliant daylight, casting long, sharp-edged blue shadows across the pale masonry. 

Yet nothing moves across those stones. The dynamic of the valley is absolute zero—an unbroken, time-invariant equilibrium. 

The angle of the sun never tilts toward afternoon. The long shadow cast by the city’s highest watch-tower falls perpetually across the third step of the great southern terrace, never retreating and never advancing. The dewdrop hanging from the edge of a split-timber lintel remains bulbous and trembling, caught at the precise surface tension before the fall, reflecting the mountain peaks forever. The wind does not gust; it exists only as a faint, static pressure against the ear, carrying the same low, resonant hum through the gaps in the stone pillars without rising or falling by a single decibel.

Because of this stillness, the city is profoundly quiet, though it is far from dead. Fauna is abundant, woven into the architecture in a state of tranquil suspension. High on the dry-stone parapets, long-necked raptors with iridescent slate plumage sit perched in rows, their eyes bright and unblinking, watching over the vast plazas. Down in the wide thoroughfares, small herds of heavy-bodied, mountain-adapted caprids stand on the flags, their thick silver-grey wool beaded with frost-mist that never melts. Their breath forms tiny, white clouds in the cool air that remain suspended before their muzzles, stationary as carved glass. Small, smooth-scaled lizards rest on the sunlit faces of the basalt blocks, perpetually warm, neither fleeing nor foraging.

The tools of the builders still lie where the hand-labor was completed: heavy stone hammers with bound-sinew handles resting beside unplaced blocks, split-log sledges poised at the edge of the terraces, and hemp-twisted guide ropes taut beneath unmoving counterweights. There is no dust in the air, no scent of decay, and no sign of wear on the freshly chipped edges of the stone. 

The metropolis remains complete, perfectly cool, damp with the morning that never ends, balanced forever in the silent heights between raw rock and the pale, unshifting sky.
