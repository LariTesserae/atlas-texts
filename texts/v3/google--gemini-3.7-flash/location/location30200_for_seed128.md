The roar does not arrive through the ears; it hits the sternum first, an immense, mechanical pulse so profound and continuous that it ceases to be noise and becomes a physical state of matter. Miles above any true soil, locked inside the high structural ribs of a hyper-altitude conduit pylon, the acoustic pressure is absolute. It is the sound of super-pressurized fluids being sheared through narrow metamaterial channels, combined with the low, shuddering bass of field-stabilizers holding thousands of tons of aloft architecture against gravity. 

The space itself is narrow, almost secret—an interstitial vault no wider than a forgotten hallway, wedged between the radiant hulls of two major thermal exchange flues. There is no earth, no stone, no sky. Every surface is manufactured, though centuries of relentless moisture and thermal cycling have softened the edges into an organic, worn anatomy. The floor is a sequence of ribbed alloy plates, dished and hollowed where centuries of scalding run-off have etched smooth, glossy troughs into composite metals that were never supposed to corrode.

It is hot here, a thick, humid, greenhouse warmth generated by the endless bleed of nearby power-cores. The air is nearly saturated. Heavy vapor condenses continuously against the ceilings—lattices of carbon-fiber weave and dead optical cabling—gathering into fat, hot droplets that fall in a steady, unceasing drizzle. The walls weep. Clear, mineral-doped coolant and reclaimed humidity run down the smooth, black curvatures in braided curtains, pooling in narrow sumps where the vibration whips the water’s surface into a standing pattern of frantic, microscopic ripples.

Light is an afterthought. It survives only in the dull, ember-like glow of diagnostic diodes long decoupled from any receiver, and the faint, cold blue fluorescence of thin bio-synthetics: a stubborn, grease-like slime of engineered micro-algae that coats the undersides of cable trays. This slick film is the only plant life to speak of, a flat, non-flowering sludge that drinks the leaking stray electromagnetic fields and the rich chemical wash of the runoff.

Yet despite the isolation and the acoustic violence, the pocket is violently alive. 

The warmth and constant moisture have turned this mechanical crevice into an incubator. The fauna here have never known silence or light; they are creatures of tactile frequency and chemical gradients. Blind, segmented scavengers—the feral descendants of sub-dermal repair drones and opportunistic biological imports—bristle across the wet piping in astonishing numbers. Some are no larger than a fingernail, possessing shells of polished white composite that scuttle in tight, shivering herds over the slick surfaces, grazing on the algae and the shedding skin of dead conduits. 

Larger organisms, soft-bodied and fringed with feathery sensory antennae, anchor themselves directly over the vibrating seams where the heat leaks strongest. They spread translucent, webbed fans into the humid air currents to catch falling condensation and the dead micro-fauna knocked loose by the structure’s shuddering. Every few seconds, a predatory multi-legged thing—chitinous, matte-black, moving with terrifying, fluid speed—darts across a cable-bundle, snatching a grazing grazer before vanishing into the dark tangle of an open conduit port.

Everything is close, near-touching. To move here is to wedge one's body against warm, slick bulkheads that twitch with the passage of internal fluids, brushing aside thick curtains of dead sensor-strands that drip scalding condensation onto the shoulders. The air tastes faintly of ozone, copper, and warm oil. 

Cycles pass quickly here, governed not by suns, but by the operational load of the unseen machinery beyond the walls. Every few minutes, a deep valve shifts somewhere far down the spine: the deafening drone drops an octave, the temperature spikes sharply by a few degrees, and a sudden, violent gust of atomized vapor sweeps through the pocket, blinding what little visibility exists and setting every creature on the walls into a frantic, clinging dance of survival. Then the purge passes, the water drains back into its polished alloy ruts, and the wet, blind ecosystem resumes its churning, deafening life in the dark.
