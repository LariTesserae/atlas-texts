It sits tucked into an undershelf of the basement rock—a low, sweltering pocket sealed away beneath thousands of feet of dead basalt. The air here is not humid; it is desiccated beyond measure, stripped of every vaporous trace of moisture, heated from below by deep thermal beds until the ambient atmosphere sits at a continuous, scorching shimmer.

Despite the absolute lack of water, it is not barren. A dry, non-aqueous flora thrives here in aggressive abundance, anchoring itself into the mortar-lines and rooftops of an immense, abandoned settlement. 

The architecture is urban in scale yet primitive in execution. It is a city built entirely by hand, with simple wooden pivots, dry-stacked shale, sun-baked clay blocks, and rough-chiseled slate. There are narrow tenement rows, stepped plazas, and tall, square grain-towers, all constructed with the rudimentary geometry of early masonry. The structures have stood so long that their sharper cornices have been rounded by the dry scouring of convection winds, settling into an equilibrium where the stone seems almost grown rather than laid. Yet the city is mostly emptiness—the buildings are spaced across a yawning, hollow expanse, leaving wide, silent avenues of fine black sand between clusters of multi-story brickwork.

The vegetation that overwhelms this city is constructed of sulfur-chains, fibrous silica, and thin plates of mica. Long, blade-like fronds the color of burnt copper burst from the cracks in the flagstones, arching up toward the low ceiling of the vault. Thick, tangled carpets of dry, papery lichen cling to the walls, constantly peeling and shedding skin like scorched parchment. Tall, segmented reeds of brittle, tubular carbonate stand in the courtyards, growing rapidly in the baking draft, absorbing airborne mineral dust and rising several inches a day before snapping under their own weight to feed the carpet below.

There is no sunlight here. The light is a dim, dusky gloom, cast entirely by the dull infrared glow of the floor-fissures and the faint, cold luminescence of airborne pollen—tiny, dry sparks of static-charged mineral dust that drift through the alleys like sluggish embers. In this twilight, the city is a study in umber, rust, and velvet-black shadows.

Though completely devoid of animal life—no vermin in the ruins, no insects in the thickets, no birds beneath the rock ceiling—the pocket is shockingly loud. 

The sound is relentless, driven by the intense heat and the hyperactive metabolism of the waterless plants. The mineral fronds expand and contract with the pulsing thermal vents below, clattering against the stone walls with the dry, hollow roar of millions of wooden slats being shaken in a gale. Pods of baked silicates burst with sharp, rhythmic cracks like rifle fire, flinging hard seeds against the brick facades. Convection currents howl through the empty window apertures and down the flue-like chimneys of the houses, turning the entire urban layout into an enormous, discordant pipe organ. 

It is an ecosystem in violent, continuous motion without a drop of sap or blood: vines of woven carbon fiber creep visibly along the balconies, climbing toward the heat plumes, while beneath them, the floor of the square shivers as dry root-tendrils pry old stones apart to drink the rising heat. Everything is baking, roaring, and endlessly self-replacing in the dark.
