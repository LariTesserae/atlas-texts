At the absolute crown of the tension-spine—an elevation so high that the surrounding planetary atmosphere has thinned into a purple vacuum—there is an interior rupture. It is not an open summit, but a sealed void within the sheath: an unmapped pocket scarcely eighty meters across, hung directly over the primary load-bearing lattice. 

Down here in the pocket, the heat is constant, heavy, and motionless. It radiates outward from the bulkheads of dark, non-Euclidean alloys that form the floor, walls, and overhead ceiling. The air is pressurized, thick, and smells faintly of baked silicate and ozone, trapped inside this hollow like breath inside a kiln. 

Despite the cramped overall footprint of the space, its interior feels desolate and yawning. It is built like a tiered metropolitan ward, but scaled down to an eerie, breathless miniature—or perhaps built for intelligences that occupied volume differently than flesh does. Narrow, rectilinear towers no thicker than a human forearm rise twenty meters from the floor, clustered in tight, disciplined grids before breaking off abruptly into vast, dead gaps of empty floor. There are streets that are mere slits between monolithic slabs of dull, light-absorbing composite, laid out with the uncompromising precision of a municipal transit grid.

Yet almost nothing fills the space between these structures. The air is largely void, a hot, quiet gulf suspended between razor-sharp terraces.

The materials are young. There is no dust here, no rounded edges or slow crumbling. The architecture was born recently out of a high-energy shear stress: where the outer tension-spine shifted under stratospheric drag, this pocket tore open, and the automated fabricators beneath the floor instantly extruded an entire settlement-grid to stabilize the micro-torsion. The edges of the buildings are monomolecularly sharp; where a catwalk has been severed, the metal gleams like a freshly struck flint, clean and unoxidized, showing the raw, striated crystal boundaries of the alloy.

Moisture moves through the pocket in an erratic, mechanical cycle. Hidden somewhere within the lower sub-tiers, a cracked thermal-exchange conduit bleeds a steady trickle of recycled coolant—superheated water laden with mineral salts. The moisture rises as an invisible, scalding vapor, drifting upward into the stagnant dark of the ceiling. There, against the freezing thermal boundary of the outer hull, it condenses into fat, trembling beads. 

Every few minutes, a drop loses its grip. It falls in silence through thirty meters of dim air, perfectly spherical, catching the faint, rust-red status lights of the low towers before striking a hot horizontal plane below with a sharp, explosive *hiss*. The water immediately flashes to steam, leaves a temporary ring of white crystal salt, and joins the rising air once more. In the corners where the towers crowd together, this constant distillation leaves the vertical surfaces slick and dark, weeping hot grease-like moisture into shallow runnels that vanish into drains the size of keyholes.

The light is dying and low. There are no lamps, only the intrinsic phosphorescence of dormant systems. Thin, hair-like traces of pale amber run along the curbs of the empty streets, pulsing at long intervals—not like a heartbeat, but like a low-frequency data query waiting for an answer from a terminal that was never installed. In certain deep recesses between the blocks, the air has a faint, lavender luminescence where micro-scale fields warp the ambient heat, casting hard, elongated shadows that do not quite match the geometry of the towers throwing them.

It is entirely soundless for long stretches. The acoustic insulation of the hyper-dense alloys absorbs ambient noise almost instantly, preventing any echo. When a sound does occur—the sudden, dry *snap* of a structural strut adjusting to a fractional shift in external wind shear miles outside, or the heavy slap of a falling droplet—it is dry, isolated, and gone before the ear can locate it.

Nothing grows here. There are no spores, no moss, no scavenging micro-fauna in the gutters. The only dynamic presence is the sheer, ongoing computation of the structure itself. Occasionally, without warning, a six-foot section of roadway will silently decouple, lift three millimeters on a cushion of diamagnetic force, rotate precisely forty-five degrees to vent a pocket of thermal pressure, and lock back into the pavement with a faint, hydraulic sigh. The street resumes its absolute stillness, gleaming faintly under the hot, dark roof of the world, waiting for a population that was never factored into its blueprints.
