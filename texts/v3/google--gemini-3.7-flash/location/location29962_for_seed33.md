The altitude here has no clear bottom. Below the lower galleries, the air simply thins into an ivory-tinted drop that continues past the reach of sight, until perspective itself gives out. 

It is bitterly dry. Moisture does not exist as vapor, ice, or skin; any exposed organic matter sheds its water in seconds to the vacuum-cold atmosphere, stiffening into salt-crusted parchment. The temperature sits in a perpetual, hollow chill—not the fierce biting cold of a blizzard, but the dead, motionless cool of an open tomb suspended ten miles above a forgotten crust. The light is dim, filtered through a uniform, slate-colored sky that holds neither sun nor cloud, illuminating the expanse with a flat, shadowless gloom that makes distances impossible to calculate.

Across this vast, vacant volume of sky hangs a city.

It is not built upon a mountain; it has replaced the mountain, or perhaps the mountain was carved until nothing remained but streets, lintels, colonnades, and millions of tiered, doorless chambers. Yet there are no toolmarks. There is no mortar, no iron dowels, no timber, no conduit, no glass. The stone—a powdery, pale-grey shale interwoven with veins of talc and dry calcified marrow—appears to have crystallized directly into civil architecture. 

Avenues eighty yards wide stretch into the void for leagues, terminating abruptly in sheer drop-offs where the pavement simply ends above the gulf. Arcades of immense, slender pillars rise toward lintels that support nothing, stepping upward in dizzying, Non-Euclidean terraces. The urban density is staggering in design, yet utterly hollow in substance: billions of cubic yards of empty plazas, spiraling staircases that loop beneath arches to become their own ceilings, and endless residential honeycombs through which the cold, dead air drifts without obstruction.

The silence is near-total, measured only by the faint, dry hiss of stone-dust settling, or the occasional distant *tick* of a terrace shifting along a cleavage plane. 

Despite the silence and the absolute lack of machinery, the place is aggressively dynamic. The city is not static; it metabolizes space. In a slow, continuous choreography of spatial failure, whole districts quietly dislocate and drift. A neighborhood of narrow, five-story facades will shear cleanly away from its bedrock avenue, sliding across the thin air along invisible fault lines of gravity, rotating forty-five degrees as it migrates to dock silently with a higher terrace leagues away. 

Gravity here is not a downward pull toward a planetary core, but a local, fractured property of the architecture itself. A traveler walking along the underside of an inverted causeway experiences the dry limestone above them as a floor; step off the curb, however, and one does not fall down—one drifts sideways, caught in the lateral drift of the district’s migration, tumbling across miles of empty air toward some distant, perpendicular boulevard.

Vegetation has never existed here. In its place, the drier corners of the colonnades are crusted in a fine, powdery efflorescence—mineral blooms that sprout in brittle, branching rosettes from the seams of the shale. These are not plants, but self-organizing salt lattices that grow, fracture under their own weight, and collapse into fine grey talc over the course of hours, constantly replenishing the dust that coats every threshold.

Fauna is exceedingly sparse, adapted to the vacuum-dryness and the fractured physics of the heights. Occasionally, high in the vaults between two drifting aqueducts, something moves: the *strata-weavers*. They are biological, but only barely—creatures like kites of dried, translucent skin stretched over ribcages of flexible bone, possessing no internal fluids, their tissues sustained by the slow absorption of airborne mineral dust. They do not fly so much as fall deliberately between disparate gravitational fields, angling their flat, leathery bodies to catch the subtle updrafts of falling stone-grit. When they land on a vertical facade, they anchor themselves with needle-fine bone spurs, folding flat against the shale, indistinguishable from the masonry.

Further out, where the city opens into vast, multi-mile courtyards suspended over the void, the architecture begins to fray into pure abstraction. Columns split into fractals of smaller pillars; stairs narrow until the risers are microns thick, forming shimmering, razor-sharp ramps that lead nowhere. 

There are no signs of who or what was meant to populate these billions of rooms. The doorways are tall, narrow slits, perfectly uniform, opening into bare, cubic cells that contain only cold air and a thin layer of chalky dust. As the dim sky shifts slightly toward a deeper charcoal, the distant sound of an entire quarter realigning echoes across the expanse—a single, vast, grinding groan of dry stone on stone, lasting ten minutes, before resolving once more into absolute, freezing silence.
