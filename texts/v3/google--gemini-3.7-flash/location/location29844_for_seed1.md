The bedrock here was never stone; it is cast iron that has forgotten how to be smooth. It spreads out for hundreds of square miles across an ancient, drowned basin—a shelf of dark, pitted slag and fossilized gearing that sits barely two feet above a sea of freezing, mineral-rich slurry. 

Nothing was built here. There are no ruins, no foundations, no signs of hands or intention. The industrial order is primordial, an innate property of the geology. Millennia of cold, saline damp have rounded the edges of massive, naturally occurring crankshafts that turn sluggishly in the silt, driven by the subterranean expansion of freezing water. Miles of linked connecting rods lie half-submerged in the slush, reciprocating with the slow, terrifying patience of continental drift. The entire horizon is flat, worn down by eons of friction and frost-heave into a uniform, leaden terrace that stretches beneath a low, motionless ceiling of bruised purple fog.

The air is bitter, hovering just fractions of a degree above total freeze. A thick, wet vapor condenses instantly on any vertical surface, running down the flanks of cold-forged iron plates in greasy, black rivulets. It smells of tallow, kerosene, ozone, and ancient brine. Everywhere, water is weeping, pooling, and being crushed. Every downward stroke of a sea-level piston sends geysers of frigid, muddy foam twenty feet into the dim air, falling back as a stinging sleet that coats the iron in rime.

There are no plants. Not a blade of grass, not a speck of lichen or moss has ever found purchase on these ferrous flats. Where an organic world would have turf or peat, this basin has packed fields of cold, fibrous carbon shavings and wet iron filings, dense as wet sand, yielding with a dull, magnetic squelch under pressure. In the stagnant hollows between interlocking gear-clusters, thick colonies of pale, grease-eating slime molds occasionally form skin-like films over stagnant pools of hydraulic fluid, but they are blind, simple chemistries, barely alive.

Yet the basin is packed to suffocation with motion and presence. There is no empty space. Where the iron shelf dips into the freezing emulsion, herds of cold-blooded, machine-fleshed fauna crowd shoulder to shoulder. They are heavy, squat things the color of oxidized copper and wet zinc, lacking eyes or faces, their bodies structured like low-slung, multi-piston engines encased in thick, blubbery cowlings of vulcanized fat. They breathe with the rhythmic clatter of pneumatic valves, taking in the freezing mist and exhaling white plumes of superheated steam from dorsal exhaust ports. They move in vast, tightly jammed migrations across the flats, scraping their blunt, case-hardened lower jaws against the turning plates to strip away zinc crusts and magnetic scales. 

The sound is immense and continuous, an overlapping cacophony that sits deep in the chest: the heavy, syncopated *thump-hiss* of thousands of subterranean cylinders, the shriek of unlubricated bronze grinding against wet iron, the wet slosh of heavy bodies churning through grease-slush, and the deafening, regular *crack* of ice splitting inside cold water-jackets. 

Reality bends along the axes of this rotation. In places where the naturally occurring gear trains achieve certain ratios, the ambient gravity does not point straight down; it lists violently toward the turning hubs, causing the black water to pool on the vertical faces of enormous, turning sprockets without spilling. Light does not scatter correctly through the steam; it shears along straight, crystalline lines, casting dull, copper-colored glints off wet metal while leaving the spaces between the creatures in pitch-black shadow. Occasionally, the air pressure drops so suddenly in the wake of a massive compression stroke that the falling water flash-freezes in mid-air, drifting down as tiny, perfect cogs of ice that tinkle like shattered glass against the iron below.

Everything is wet. Everything is ancient, cold, and crowded. The horizon simply fades into the gray hum of steam and the endless, unceasing churn of a world that runs itself to death without ever having been started.
