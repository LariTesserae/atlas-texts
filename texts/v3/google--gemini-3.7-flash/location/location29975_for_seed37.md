It is no larger than the interior of a shepherd’s wagon, yet it contains the brilliance of an uncovered noon. 

Buried three hundred feet beneath the compacted silt of an unmapped valley, this hollow exists as an unsealed blister in the raw lithosphere. The stone here has not yet finished deciding to be stone; it is embryonic rock, pale and curd-soft, weeping a cold, clean moisture that never pools into streams but clings to surfaces in precise, geometric beads. The edges of the walls are not worn down by centuries of water or wind—they are freshly split, crisp as snapped marrow, crystalline planes still pushing their way out of the slurry of primordial clay.

Despite its subterranean burial, the chamber is flooded with a fierce, shadowless white light. It does not radiate from a single source, nor does it bounce logically between surfaces. Instead, the illumination seems to be a physical constituent of the air itself, hovering like a dense, luminous fog that leaves everything razor-sharp and over-exposed. Because of this glare, the air feels crisp and deeply chilled—a refrigerator cold that carries the smell of crushed flint, starch, and fresh well-water.

In this bright, tight stillness, reality is poorly stitched. Perspective does not taper with distance; the far corner of the pocket appears both yards away and pressing against the bridge of the nose. Droplets of condensation do not always obey the down-pull of the earth; some slide horizontally along invisible seams in the air, tracing the margins of geometries that have not yet been granted physical mass. Where the wall ought to be solid, it occasionally hiccups—revealing, for the duration of an exhale, a terrifyingly deep slice of empty, silver-blue sky, before snapping shut again into wet, white marl.

Yet the space is densely inhabited. A silent, frantic ecology thrives in the cold radiance. 

Small, paper-flat organisms—resembling translucent trilobites cut from cellophane—skitter across the emerging rock in vast, erratic migrations. They do not cross the stone by crawling over its contours; they seem to slip through its facets, vanishing into the milk-white crust and reappearing an inch to the left, chewing away at the unhardened mineral froth. Above them, hovering in the damp air like dust motes caught in a projector beam, are thousands of needle-thin entities made of jointed, iridescent filament. They beat their hair-like wings so quickly they produce no hum, only a faint, rhythmic shivering of the air, diving and tumbling in silent, complex choreographies as they hunt invisible spores.

Every few moments, a larger shape—something with the supple, many-jointed pale body of an unpigmented shrimp, but the size of a human hand—darts from a fissure. It snaps up a cluster of the hovering needles with a mouth that opens along five axes, its movement so instantaneous it looks less like swimming and more like a tear in the film. 

Everything here moves at the speed of a startled nerve, yet the total sound of the chamber amounts to almost nothing: the microscopic *snip-snip* of chitin shears, the soft, fat *tink* of an errant drop of water striking a quartz spine, and the vast, dead pressure of the earth sealing it from the world above.

Half-embedded in the developing rock floor lies a single artifact: three steps of finely smoothed, dark red slate, carefully mortared together. They lead nowhere, ascending two inches into the blank air before ending abruptly. The stone around them is slowly welling up like cooling wax, gently swallowing the lowest step. Near the top tread rests a tiny, carved notch—scarcely the size of a grain of wheat—resembling the keyhole of a chest that was never built. 

Around this tiny ruin, the world continues to assemble itself. Formations of selenite sprout from the ceiling like fast-growing fungal stalks, lengthening by fractions of a millimeter in real time, locking the blinding light into rigid, prismatic cages. The damp air turns over continuously, cold and vital, as the blind, thriving things scurry across the newly born stone, entirely at home in a space that has not yet learned how to hold together.
