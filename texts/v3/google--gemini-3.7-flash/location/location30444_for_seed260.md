The sky does not begin above the horizon; it begins beneath the feet. 

At this altitude—where the atmospheric mantle thins into a cold, dry vacuum of heavy neon and pulverized glass—the terrain has not yet learned how to weather. The land is freshly erupted from some geometric zero-point, jagged with razor-sharp planar facets that have never felt rain, river, or wind-blown silt. Immense hexagonal columns of basalt, pale orthoclase feldspar, and unoxidized iron lurch out of an abyssal floor thousands of leagues down, rising as sheer, vertical needles that pierce into the dim, violet-bruised twilight. There is no moisture here, not a single drop of aqueous humidity to soften the air; the cold is clean, thin, and brittle, hovering just above the freezing point of brine, though no liquid exists to freeze.

Yet, despite the bone-dry void, the towers are thickly overgrown. 

This is not botanical life as born of soil and rain, but a thriving, lush vegetation of dry silicates, sulfur-ribbon kelp, and sprawling carpets of electro-sensitive lichen that feed directly on the seismic static of the newborn rock. Thick velvet mats of cadmium-yellow and deep plum-colored moss coat the sheer vertical faces of the spires, dry as powdered ash to the touch, their fibrous, cellular stalks interlocking to form dense, spongy cushions. From the clefts of unweathered fractures sprout great weeping willows of spun glass and calcified pith—pale, translucent fronds that drape hundreds of feet down into the empty space between pillars. They do not blow in the wind, for there is no weather, but they shudder continuously, drinking the vibration of the world.

And the vibration is absolute. 

The sound in this place is deafening, a monolithic, continuous roar that presses against the skull like an iron weight. It is not the sound of storms, but the acoustic scream of the planet’s embryonic crystallization. Deep within the roots of the spires, tectonic plates that have never fully consolidated are grinding together in a slow, glacial lock, generating a low-frequency harmonic drone so loud it rattles the marrow of any bone brought near it. Over this bass hum shrieks a high, piercing resonance—the sound of the air itself shearing against the impossibility of the geometry, a constant, abrasive white noise that turns the silence of the void into an overwhelming physical assault.

Here, space does not strictly obey Euclidean rules. The sheer drop between the spires is immense, an open expanse of dim, yawning chasms, but gravity is inconsistent, fractured into local, embryonic planes. On certain towers, the lush, dry moss grows horizontally across empty space, rooted in invisible planes of tension. A stone dropped from an edge may fall downward for a thousand meters, then pause, drift sideways at a leisurely angle, and begin to "fall" toward the sky before settling into an invisible equilibrium where the upward pull of distant sister-spires balances the abyss below. The horizon curls slightly upward at the edges, like paper exposed to heat, revealing under-layers of raw, unformed geological crust that glow with faint, cold bioluminescence.

Through this deafening, vast loneliness, there are sparse, unmistakable signs of an ancient, crude habitation. 

Lashed across the crystalline gaps between close-standing pillars are primitive suspension bridges—made not of hemp or steel, but of braided silicate vines and dried, fibrous bark from the glass-willows. Upon the flat, sheer summits of certain spires sit small, circular redoubts of dry-stacked, unmortared slate. They are tiny, desperate perches built with simple lever-and-wedge mechanics: hand-shaped stone anchors pounded into embryonic rock fissures with crude diorite mallets, tethering fragile timber shelters made from petrified frond-wood. There are no signs of advanced metallurgy, no engines or wires; only the scattered detritus of a flint-and-bone culture that once learned to navigate the fractured gravity wells, setting out bone-tipped wind-chimes that shatter under the crushing acoustic pressure, leaving behind neat, ritual piles of chalky slivers.

Nothing moves quickly here. The cycles of this realm are patient, measured in millennia. The spires continue to extrude upward from the depths at a rate of a single millimeter every century, their raw, unrounded edges fresh as broken crystal. The dim, ember-like light from an occluded, sunless sky never quite sets and never brightens; it merely shifts between shades of bruised charcoal, plum, and cold zinc. 

There are no animals to hear the roar. No birds cross the vast, open gulfs between the spires; no insects crawl through the dense, dry velvet of the sulfur-moss. There is only the deafening, eternal chord of matter coming into being, the sprawling, waterless jungle of mineral-flesh, and the tiny, forgotten rope-bridges swinging out over a void that has no bottom.
