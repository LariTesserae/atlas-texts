It does not smell of rot, because rot requires moisture, and here there is none. The air tastes of scorched starch, warm terpene, dry silica, and the hot, floury dust of pulverized bark. 

Miles beneath an arid planetary shell, the rift opens into a vaulted chamber the breadth of a regional basin. It is not stone that defines this space, but an embryonic, hyperactive geology of living cellulose. Immense trunks—thicker than cathedral spires, grown not from seed but cast up like continuous volcanic extrusions of pith and structural resin—crisscross the chasm in tangled, violent tiers. These are not trees in any familiar sense; they are vast, unweathered conduits of pressurized, non-aqueous sap: dense, lipid-heavy oils and volatile aromatic waxes that circulate through ribbed, amber-walled arteries without a single drop of water. 

The light is pervasive and steady, radiating from the vascular walls of the flora itself. Great ribbed pods, translucent as oiled parchment, pulse with a slow, internal phosphorescence that illuminates the entire basin in a warm, tallow-colored noon. 

The sound is a continuous, deafening roar. It is an acoustic wall composed entirely of friction, hunger, and stress. The vegetation grows at such a violent, unmitigated rate that the cavern echoes with the explosive cracking of fresh timber snapping under its own torque. Fibrous boughs thrust outward in real-time, groaning like ship hulls in a gale. Beneath and around this botanical tectonic churn is the noise of the swarm: billions of dry, hard-shelled organisms moving across the wicker-work floors. There is the razor-rasp of trillions of serrated mandibles stripping raw pith, the heavy, drumming vibration of carapaces rattling against hollow trunk-chambers, and the high, shrill shriek of stridulating fauna whose friction-plates whine like circular saws against the dry bark.

Nothing is soft. The leaves are broad sheets of razor-edged mica-like silicate; the mosses are brittle carpets of needle-fine glass hairs that crunch into yellow powder under any weight. 

Fauna does not crawl through this world so much as saturate it like a fluid medium. Long-bodied, multi-jointed arthropodic creatures, plated in chalky white chitin, churn through the canopy in rolling, writhing knots, consuming the newly surged shoots almost as fast as they burst from the nodal joints. Broad, scale-winged flyers with wingspans like dried palm fronds launch themselves from high gallery-trunks, riding the intense thermal updrafts generated by the heat of metabolic friction. They do not drink; they feed on the volatile hydrocarbon dusts, the drifting starches, and the hyper-rich lipid condensates that bead like hot varnish upon the growing tips of the world-vines.

In this churning, dry furnace, there are signs of human or humanoid tenancy, though precarious and primitive.

Clinging to the flanks of the slower-moving megaliths of timber are scattered settlements—scaffolds lashed together with thick, sinewy bast-fibers and braced with the shed tusks of burrowing wood-grubs. These are not permanent villages, for nothing here remains still long enough to endure permanence. The inhabitants use flaked chert, volcanic glass, and the petrified thorns of ancient lower-strata roots to gouge out living quarters directly from the fibrous flesh of the leviathan plants. They must continually chop back the walls of their dwellings; left untended for three cycles, a carved room will be swallowed and crushed by the inward swell of new, weeping grain.

Crude rope-bridges of braided husk span the terrifying voids between surging branches, swaying violently as the timber below them expands and flexes. Small smoke plumes rise from primitive clay-lined kilns suspended in mid-air on stone-weighted baskets—smoke that is sweet, pungent, and heavy with vaporized oils, used to drive away the blind, burrowing mantids that chew through structural lashing.

Looking across the rift, the perspective does not settle. Whole tiers of the ecosystem are under active construction, throwing out fresh arches of raw, glistening lignum that bridge chasms three miles wide, while below, in the warmer depths of the basin, the older growth is systematically excavated and pulverized by armies of rust-colored borers into vast, billowing dunes of dry, shifting meal. It is an open, turbulent engine of pure biomass: dry, hot, deafeningly loud, and utterly relentless in its self-consumption and rebirth.
