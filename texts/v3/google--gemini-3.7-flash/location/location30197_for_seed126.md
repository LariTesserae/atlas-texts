The horizon does not bow to natural geography; it is a ruler-straight line drawn across an impossible distance. Under an incandescent, bleached sky that holds no clouds, the world presents itself as an infinite floor of worn, layered matter—a planetary terrace built of materials that have forgotten their original makers, smoothed by millions of years of dry thermal drift into an almost level expanse.

There is no soil here, and no native rock. The ground is a megastructural laminate, dozens of kilometers deep, composed of pressed silicates, sintered diamondoid tiles, dead optical buses, and pale, calcified resins. Over immense stretches of time, the uppermost skins have worn away, leaving a terrain of ultra-low relief: shallow, stepped basins that drop only a few centimeters over a mile; vast, polished flats where the grain of microscopic circuitry has been ground down to the texture of soapstone; and long, low berms formed where buried conduit-trunks have slightly buckled under eons of planetary settling. 

Everything is bathed in a dry, radiant heat. The air is entirely clear, thin, and devoid of moisture, hovering at a steady, basking warmth that makes the synthetic pavement hot to the touch. The light is fierce and omnidirectional—not merely falling from the brilliant white dome above, but bouncing continuously from the pale, chalky ground, so that shadows are reduced to paper-thin, razor-sharp outlines beneath the few vertical features.

Despite the near-total lack of water, the landscape is in constant, silent motion. 

The dynamics here are electrostatic and thermodynamic. Across the continental plains, tides of superfine, ground-glass dust migrate without wind, pulled along invisible electromagnetic lines that pulse through the substrate. These dust-fronts move in sweeping, silent sheets, glowing faintly with static discharge where they graze the raised rims of hexagonal plates. Subsurface heat-exchangers, operating on automated cycles that span weeks or months, slowly vent thermal blooms to the surface; when this happens, leagues of pale ceramic tiles shift in tone, warming from chalk-white to a dull, sunbaked amber, causing the air immediately above them to ripple with mirages.

The density of the built substrate is staggering. If one scrapes away the fine drift of powdered quartz, the ground reveals itself not as empty pavement, but as an endlessly compressed archive of mechanisms: nested capillaries, fiber bundles as thick as peat, and micro-machined strata overlapping without a single millimeter of wasted volume. It is a city crushed into a geological strata, then polished flat by the ages.

Vegetation exists only in rare, specialized anomalies. Along the seam-lines where two megaplates grind in microscopic friction, brittle "brambles" of spun silica and pale, piezoelectric glass sprout in tangled, knee-high thickets. They do not drink; they feed on the static gradient between the air and the deep ground. When a thermal wave passes, their translucent needles flex and shudder, catching the blinding glare like clusters of shattered mirrors. In shallow depressions where electrostatic dust settles deepest, dry, paper-like lichens formed of mutated organic polymers cling to the tile edges, inert and stiff as dried horn.

Fauna is rarer still. Occasionally, the flat expanse is crossed by a strider: a creature of jointed, ceramic-shelled limbs and blind sensory bulbs, no larger than a hound, moving with a fast, ticking gait. They graze upon the silica brambles or sweep the dust-tides for concentrated heavy elements, their carapaces bleached bone-white by the relentless sun. When two meet, they do not fight or mate; they merely pause, align their antenna arrays in a brief exchange of non-acoustic signals, and part ways across the blinding desert.

Above all, there is an overpowering, monumental silence. 

Sound dies quickly here. The dry air carries little weight, and the vast, porous ceramics drink vibration instantly. The only noises are microscopic: the faint, dry *snip* of a silica needle expanding in the heat; the whispering, paper-thin hiss of a static dust-tide sliding across a glass plain; and the deep, sub-audible hum that travels through the soles of one's feet—a vibration from hundreds of leagues below, where the core of the machine still turns over its final, endless calculations.
