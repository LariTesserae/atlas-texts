High along the knife-edge of a jagged, newly fractured massif lies a blind vertical cleft—no wider than a nave, sheer and deep, cleaved into cold slate and unweathered basalt. The rock here has not had time to soften. Its facets are fresh, flint-sharp, and unrounded by centuries of grit, slicing down in razor-straight shear planes that drop into an ink-black trench. 

At this elevation, the air is thin and biting, but inside the narrow gap, it churns with frantic, mechanical heat and violent turbulence. 

There is almost no light. The sky above is pinched off by overhanging rock lips and thick iron struts, leaving the depths in heavy, soot-tinged shadow, barely broken by the dull, cherry-red smolder of friction bearings and the brief, blinding sparks of metal grinding against fresh stone.

Despite the near-total darkness and the altitude, the cleft is choked with life. Dense, cold-hardy flora crowds every available ledger and joint in the rock: heavy curtains of black-green liverworts, tough-ribbed alpine ferns, and thick, wet cushions of peat moss that mat over the raw stone like sodden fleece. Because ambient water is scarce—the heights are generally dry and swept by freezing winds—the vegetation feeds entirely on the violent, rhythmic condensation thrown off by the machines wedged into the gap. Droplets mist continuously through the drafts, beading on waxy fronds and running down the stone in thin, intermittent rills.

The gorge is not inhabited, but it is occupied. Heavy, unpolished industrial machinery is anchored directly into the raw rock face with brutal economy—no masonry, no catwalks, no shelters. Massive cast-iron drive rods, six inches thick and slick with graphite grease, thrust and cycle in furious, reciprocating rhythms through the narrow space. High-pressure steam conduits, lagged in fraying canvas and iron wire, rattle against the basalt cliffs, trembling so hard they shake loose showers of stone dust into the ferns below. 

The sound within the chamber is deafening. The narrow rock walls act as an acoustic horn, amplifying the percussive thudding of mechanical pistons, the shriek of unlubricated bronze bushings, and the roaring gale of forced air being driven upward through the cleft. Air currents whip violently back and forth, tearing at the ferns, whipping heavy ropes of hanging moss into frantic spirals, and shearing plumes of cool, oily mist across the dark chasm. 

Everything is in immediate, kinetic collision. Fronds of broad-leafed mountain weeds are repeatedly slapped against vibrating steel housings, pulverized into a bitter, green-smelling paste that mixes with the smell of coal smoke, machine oil, and crushed quartz. Tendrils of pale, searching root systems crawl directly over the hot iron castings, seeking the moisture that sweats off the cooling jackets, only to be snapped by the sudden lurch of an automated linkage before regrowing into the next vibration cycle.

No birds nest here; no insects crawl through the sodden moss. The sheer volume of kinetic energy and noise renders the space uninhabitable for animal life. There is only the freshly cracked stone, the relentless, blind churning of iron and steam, and the dense, dark thicket of cold green growth clinging stubbornly to the machinery’s shaking ribs.
