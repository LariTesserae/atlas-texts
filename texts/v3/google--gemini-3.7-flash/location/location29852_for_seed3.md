At this altitude, the air is thin, chilled, and perpetually sodden with a fine, suspended mist that never quite resolves into rain. 

The geography here is unborn—or rather, it is in the violent, clean-edged middle of its first hour. The mountains do not roll or slope; they have not existed long enough for water to score them or wind to sand them smooth. Instead, colossal monoliths of slate, anthracite, and raw bismuth rise in unweathered, razor-sharp planes, their surfaces sheared flat as if sliced by a hot wire. These titanic facets jut miles into the dim, bruise-violet sky, untouched by the smoothing fatigue of deep time. 

Yet, clinging to these immaculate stone shears is a dense, soot-crusted industrial metropolis.

The city does not rest on the ground; there is no ground to speak of, only an abyss below where the rock faces plunge into absolute gloom. Immense iron frameworks, braced with millions of hand-driven rivets, bite directly into the newborn bedrock. Whole boroughs hang suspended from webbed steel gantries. Multi-story tenement blocks of dark clinker brick and corrugated zinc jut horizontally out from the cliffs, stacked in tiered shelves connected by shuddering hydraulic funiculars, iron catwalks, and counterweighted tram-cables that span mile-wide chasms of empty air.

Between these architectural clusters, the void is vast. The settlement is not a continuous carpet of stone, but an archipelago of hyper-dense iron districts strung across a gaping, open expanse.

Reality does not hold its shape cleanly around the edges of the rift. The laws governing matter are still settling into the fresh rock like cooling glass. In the broad gaps between cliff-faces, gravity bends along invisible seams; steam drifting from the boiler-houses of the lower districts does not rise toward the zenith, but slides horizontally, pooling against vertical rock walls like water on a tabletop. In certain sectors, a dropped iron wrench will fall three hundred feet, pause in mid-air with a low, metallic chime, and begin drifting upward into the cloud-deck at a leisurely, rhythmic crawl. The architecture compensates for this with dizzying pragmatism: staircases wrap around the outside of buildings on three distinct gravitational axes, and dockworkers tie themselves with tallow-greased tethers to structural girders that groan under contradictory pulls of mass.

Despite the cool chill, life is thick and relentless here. Condensation collects continuously on the cold ironwork, feeding a rampant, damp ecology that thrives in the gloom. Thick mats of broad-leafed liverworts, weeping ferns, and spongy, phosphorescent mosses choke the undersides of every gantry and wrap around the steam pipes, insulated by the leaking heat. 

The fauna is everywhere, loud and teeming within the industrial scaffolding. Swarms of blind, velvet-winged skimming-rays, each the size of a carriage door, drift between the suspended towers on thermal drafts, grazing on the airborne spores and coal-dust. Pale, many-jointed lizards with suctioned pads scuttle along the wet brickwork to raid the pantry boxes hung out from tenement windows. Deep in the dark voids between structural pylons, long-legged arboreal crabs weave nests out of discarded copper wire and shredded ferns, their mating calls ringing out like dull brass bells.

The soundscape is heavy and continuous. The steady, slow-breathing pulse of steam engines and coal-fired dynamos vibrates through the rock itself—a low, resonant *thrum-thrum-thrum* that sets the condensation dancing on every railing. Added to this is the periodic, deafening shriek of air released from pneumatic locks, the screech of steel wheels on suspended rails, and the wet, chattering cries of millions of roosting creatures echoing across the sheer stone faces. 

Nothing happens quickly. The machines are massive, built of cast iron and driven by immense, slowly turning flywheels that measure the hours in rhythmic, ponderous strokes. The tectonic plates of newly extruded stone settle with long, groaning sighs that last for days at a time, minutely re-angling the iron bridges without snapping them. 

In the dim light—a perpetual, twilight gloom cast by an obscured sun that never climbs high enough to illuminate the bottoms of the fissures—the city gleams with oil and moisture. Gas-lamps and open furnace doors cast long, smoldering amber reflections across wet black stone, while beneath the catwalks, the cold green light of climbing mosses and the slow drift of winged fauna illuminate the yawning drop into the unmade world below.
