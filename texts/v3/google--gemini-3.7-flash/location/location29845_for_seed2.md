The space is a drowned interior of continental proportion, a fresh and immaculate flooded megastructure where steel, water, and current form a closed, mechanized sea. 

There is no natural bedrock here. The floor, tens of thousands of feet below the overhead gantries, is an unbroken expanse of cross-ribbed structural plating, painted in heavy industrial zincs and naval grays that have not yet had time to peel or pit. Because the installation is young, the edges of every I-beam, flange, and gusset plate remain sheer and hard-angled. There is no thick silt, no deep layer of marine sediment; only a faint dusting of loose welding scale and the thinnest glaze of diatom film clinging to the upstream faces of the trusses.

The medium is clear, cold water—treated, oxygenated, and held at a constant, sharp chill. It extends for hundreds of miles in every direction, punctuated only at long intervals by vertical support pylons. These pylons are cylindrical towers of riveted iron and cast steel, wide as grain elevators, rising through the depths every five or ten leagues. Between them lies enormous, yawning emptiness: millions of cubic miles of pale, illuminated open water, utterly devoid of landscape until the distant gray grid of the next structural wall looms out of the visibility limit.

Light here is bright, artificial, and rigorously distributed. Suspended from high-tension catenary cables miles overhead, clusters of high-output sodium and mercury-halide flood arrays burn behind thick borosilicate domes. They cast long, clean shafts of white-green radiance down into the void, cutting through the water with almost no scatter. Where the light strikes the smooth expanses of metal, it creates razor-sharp caustics that dance across the submerged bulkheads, illuminating the sheer scale of the construction without softening it.

It is a loud world. Water conducts the violent work of the perimeter with unsparing clarity. The baseline sound is a deep, bone-rattling mechanical resonance—the continuous low-frequency drone of intake turbines the size of cathedral domes, drawing millions of gallons per minute through primary cyclonic filters at the distant edges of the basin. Layered over this hum is the sharp, percussive crack of pressurized pneumatic ballast valves cycling, followed by the rolling thunder of cavitation as high-velocity currents shear against static steel. Every metallic groan travels for miles through the dense, clean liquid, ringing off the trusses like struck bells.

These currents are the weather of the basin. Immense, invisible rivers—ten miles wide and moving at five knots—are generated by bank after bank of submerged bronze impellers. They circulate through the empty spans, creating deep thermal gradients, organized downwellings, and wide vortices that sweep through the structural lattice. 

Though plant life is sparse—restricted to pale ribbons of cultivated kelp clinging to the lower catwalks and fast-growing green slime along the warm casings of transformer housings—animal life is thriving and mobile. Enormous pelagic schools, millions strong, move along the artificial current lines. They are streamlined, silver-bodied fish introduced to crop the planktonic blooms that drift from the intake chambers. They move in vast, shimmering ribbons that weave between the pylons, turning in unison when a low-frequency pump pulse hums through the framing, flashing brilliant silver in the glare of the work lights. Solitary predators—heavy, cold-water sharks and sleek cephalopods—track the fringes of these shoals, using the sharp structural angles of the maintenance ladders and valve housings as hunting blinds.

Above, the ceiling is a continuous lattice of heavy steel decking, catwalks, and pressurized conduit runs, dripping with condensation where trapped air pockets form shallow, churning surfaces. Below, the structural floor falls away into pristine, unyielding geometric depths. Everything here has a purpose, a mechanical rating, and a blueprint; it is a brand-new ocean running under strict mechanical law, vast and hollow, working at the speed of an engine.
