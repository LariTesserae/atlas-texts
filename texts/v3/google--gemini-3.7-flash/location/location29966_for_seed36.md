It lies in a vast, collapsed depression hundreds of cubits below the rim of the world, yet it is flooded with a fierce, blinding brilliance. Above, the sky is not open air but a colossal ceiling of fused, milky quartz and crystalline selenite that captures the sun from above and refracts it downward without shadow. The light is total, white-gold and unwavering, filling the entire basin from floor to vault with a clean, dry radiance that makes the air itself seem like a hot, illuminated gel.

Despite the depth, the floor is not stone, nor is it earth. There is no soil here. The substrate of this regional basin is an unbroken, living mass—a continent-sized tapestry of colossal, succulent flora that grows so tightly interlocked that it forms hills, terraces, and canyons out of its own flesh. The plants do not rot, peel, or shed leaves. Because there is no wind to scour them and no rain to wear them down, their forms retain an embryonic, freshly minted sharpness. Stems thick as aqueducts rise with crisp, polygonal facets, unweathered and glass-smooth. Immense, paddle-like fronds the size of galleon sails overlap in dense, geometric scales, their edges as clean as cast wax. 

The heat is steady, radiant, and heavy, hovering in the upper registers like the air inside an unvented kiln. Moisture does not fall from above; rather, it exists as a permanent, motionless film. Droplets of clear, viscous condensation bead along the translucent ribs of the vegetation, clinging indefinitely without running. In the lower hollows, where the colossal root-trunks weave together to form deep gullies, narrow pools of warm, distilled water sit trapped in the cupped flesh of giant leaves—pockets of absolute stillness, clear to the bottom, reflecting the blinding white sky with mirror-like fidelity.

Scattered throughout this continuous green architecture are the remnants of some sparse, forgotten masonry. Low, dry-stone walls and solitary, square lintels of gray basalt sit cradled in the forks of massive branches or embedded directly into the side of living vegetal cliffs. Because erosion does not exist here, the tool-marks on these stones remain as sharp as the hour they were chipped, though the plants have gently engulfed them, pressing around the stone blocks like molten rubber cooling into form. There are no roads or stairs, only these isolated, silent structures, held in place by the living world, neither decaying nor being actively dismantled.

Life that moves is exceptionally rare. Occasionally, a pale, shell-backed creature the size of a thumb will creep across the face of a faceted stem, pausing for hours at a time, drinking from a single bead of condensation before vanishing into a fissure. There are no birds, no swarming insects, no rustling canopy. 

The silence is vast and pressurized. It is not an empty quiet, but the dense, heavy silence of an engine running at zero revolutions: the faint, sub-audible hum of millions of tons of cellular sap suspended under hydrostatic pressure, and the soft, infrequent *tock* of a single heavy drop finally slipping from a leaf into a pool below. The temperature never drops; the light never dims; the dew never dries. The entire sunken valley exists in an immaculate, permanent noon, trapped in a radiant equilibrium where nothing begins and nothing finishes, sustained forever by its own bright, suffocating moisture.
