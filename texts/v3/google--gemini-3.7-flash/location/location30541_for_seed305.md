The altitude is high enough that the atmosphere has lost its blue, thinning out into a hard, diamond-cut violet. The sunlight does not warm the air; it simply strikes surfaces with blinding, unfiltered intensity, reflecting off raw, un-weathered crystalline shale and polished iron sheeting. It is bitterly, steadily cold—a dry, sub-zero freeze punctuated only by the hot, oily breath of the biomechanical crust. 

There are no cities here, no roads, no foundations laid by architects. The industrialism of this plateau is entirely phylogenetic. Over an expanse the size of a subcontinent, lifted miles above sea level on newly fractured, razor-edged horsts and grabens, life has bypassed the soft cell in favor of metallurgy, combustion, and pneumatic leverage.

Every square foot of this vast tableland is occupied. The density is claustrophobic, an interlocking matrix where organism and apparatus are the same fact. The ground is not soil, but a thick, living felt of woven copper-filament moss and black, rubberized liverworts that secrete high-viscosity lubricating greases. Beneath this turf, deep basalt fissures are packed with interlocking, piston-driven siphonophorae—creatures whose muscular contractions drive natural hydraulic lines through miles of porous, un-eroded granite. 

The water here exists primarily as high-pressure steam and flash-condensing rime. When the deep mantle-chambers pump their boiling mineral fluids to the freezing surface, great geysers of vapor erupt into the sunlit glare, instantly crystallizing into micro-fine ice-dust that settles over the biological machinery like powdered glass. Pockets of dampness gather around the hot joints and valve-clusters of the larger fauna: warm, slick basins where colonies of acidophilic micro-crustaceans cycle through generations in the chemical runoff of heavy-metal metabolisms.

The fauna is everywhere, layered upon itself in staggering numbers. Colossal, six-legged locomotive ungulates—each the size of a railway tender, their carapaces forged of cold-rolled biogenic steel and sealed with black pitch—graze shoulder-to-shoulder on the razor-sharp scree. They do not chew; they crush the rock internally using quartz-lined gizzards that grind with a rhythmic, deafening clatter, extracting trace ores to replenish their plated hides. Their lungs are massive, externally mounted bellows of thick, leathery hide, expanding and contracting with an agonizingly slow, two-hour respiratory cycle, venting bursts of superheated exhaust that keep the frost from seizing their articulated knee-housings.

Between the flanks of these giants, smaller life fills every void. Swarms of brass-winged arthropods, their flight sustained by the continuous, high-pitched whining of internal flywheels, nest in the warm recesses of the larger animals' cooling ribs. They harvest the condensation from the exhaust vents, carrying droplets back to subterranean hive-furnaces that rumble beneath the permafrost.

The acoustic environment is relentless. It is not the noise of a wilderness, but the unified, deafening drone of an automated shipyard operating at an immense, patient remove. There is the persistent, low-frequency thrum of underground biological dynamos generating electromagnetic fields that make the needle-ice twitch on the rocks; the sharp, percussive *crack* of young stone splitting under frost-wedging and metal hooves; the dry, metallic hiss of a billion small appendages scuttling over raw slate; and the vast, slow exhalations of the bellows-beasts rolling across the valleys like distant artillery.

Nothing moves quickly. Because the cold is so absolute and the scale of the mechanical biology so heavy, the ecosystem operates on geological patience. A single step of an armored browser takes minutes of hydraulic pressure buildup; the copper moss creeps a millimeter a decade, slowly weaving its conductive mesh across fresh cliff faces left behind by recent fault slips. 

In the brilliant, shadowless glare of the high sky, the landscape extends without horizon-break: an unbroken, shimmering plateau of black grease, white frost, polished chrome-chitin, and blinding steam plumes, grinding quietly against its own impossible density.
