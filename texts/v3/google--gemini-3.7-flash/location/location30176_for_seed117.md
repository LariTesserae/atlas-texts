The chamber is not large—scarcely seventy paces from the weeping shale wall to the drowned bulkhead—but it is so tightly packed with weight and moisture that it feels vast from within. It sits in a low, cold sump beneath the foundations of an older, heavy world, where water collects after filtering through hundreds of feet of crushed slate and slag. 

The air is permanently chilled, smelling of wet iron, pulverized chalk, and the cold fat of ancient lubricants. Condensation here is not a film but a climate: droplets form on every overhanging edge, gather into silver beads, and let go with a rhythmic, multi-tonal cadence that makes the entire vault sound like a slow clockwork mechanism ticking underwater.

There is no leaf, stem, or seed here. In place of green life, the surfaces are smothered in dense, fibrous coats of grey-white mineral mold and thick, gelatinous mats of iron-oxidizing slime that feel like wet chamois to the touch. These mats drape over everything—over the natural ledges of water-polished stone, over the sunken cast-iron piping, and over the ribbed carcasses of half-submerged rotary engines. The machinery belongs to a forgotten heavy era: broad flywheels four inches thick, riveted plate-iron baffles, and massive copper manifolds that have long since ceased venting steam and now weep steady trickles of lime-heavy water from their joints. 

The erosion has had time to finish its work. The sharp corners of sheared bolts and fractured gear-teeth have been rounded down into soft, river-stone profiles by the constant passage of cold currents. Stalactites of black iron-rot and translucent gypsum hang in crowded curtains from the overhead girders, interlinking so densely that in places they form porous, hollow walls.

Despite the absence of sunlight and vegetation, the space is thick with living movement. In the shallow, ankle-deep basins that step down toward the central sump, thousands of pale, translucent creatures graze on the iron-silt. Some are shaped like segmented fingers, clad in flexible, horn-like sheaths, crawling by the hundreds over the submerged valve handles to scrape off the bacterial crust. Suspended in the deeper pools within the hollow turbine casings, blind, pale-bodied siphon worms pump continuously, their translucent lung-sacs swelling and contracting in steady, out-of-sync breaths that disturb the water into overlapping rings.

Light is sparse and sourceless, a faint, cold twilight carried in by the water itself, which carries a weak, milky phosphorescence from crushed calcite seams higher in the strata. It glints dimly off the wet curved surfaces of the cast iron and catches the oily, rainbow sheens that drift across the surface of the sumps. 

Movement is constant, but slow. Every few minutes, a pressure-differential in the buried drainage channels causes a heavy iron flap somewhere beneath the floor to lift with a dull *clonk*, releasing a surge of cool, clear brine that stirs the siphon-worms and sends the crawling carapaces tumbling briefly before they latch back onto the rusted iron grates. A low, subterranean vibration hums constantly in the teeth—not quite sound, but the resonance of deep earth and heavy metal resting in eternal, saturated balance.
