The air has the thin, iron bite of extreme altitude, cold enough to turn breath instantly into a coarse, drifting vapor that clings to the skin. Here, there is no stone, no soil, and no floor. The entire geology of this place is living wood, yet the world is not a crowded jungle; it is a hollow vastness—an immense, dark gulf thousands of cubits deep and wide, spanned only by colossal, arching ribs of botanical matter that bridge the freezing void like the skeletal frames of unfinished continents.

Because the growth is young and violently rapid, the bark has not yet weathered smooth. It is sharp, jagged, and ridged with glassy, jade-black plates that ring like flint underfoot. Immense spires—each the thickness of a mountain range’s spine—thrust upward out of the lower dark, tapering into needle-keen prows that pierce the cloud ceiling far above. Between these titanic struts lie miles of sheer empty air. To look over the edge of a trunk is to look into a black, howling drop where nothing catches the eye for leagues, save for the occasional, solitary spar of a cross-branch knifing through the gloom.

The darkness is nearly complete. Far above, beyond the upper latticework of limbs, a faint, cold twilight filters through thick mist, but it decays into soot-colored shadow before it reaches the mid-tiers. What little illumination exists comes in brief, cold flashes: static electricity bleeding off the needle-crests as the wind flexes the arches, or the dull, phosphorescent bruising around weeping resin-vents where the wood has split under its own tectonic torque.

It is relentlessly loud. The wind never ceases; it funnels through the negative space between the boles, sheared by their sharp edges into a multiphonic shriek that drops at times into a gut-shaking drone. Underneath the wind is the sound of the world’s internal engine: the wood itself groaning under immense tension, snapping lateral shoots out into the void with the percussion of artillery, and the deep, hollow resonance of moisture surging through capillary conduits the diameter of mining tunnels.

Moisture gathers wherever the wind is baffled. In the lee of the great ridges, dampness condenses against the bark, freezing into brittle rime or running down the troughs in rapid, frigid rivulets that tear away into spray when they hit an edge. Pockets of thick, cold slush collect in the saddles where branches bifurcate, sustaining micro-bogs of coarse, hair-like moss that insulate the underlying wood from the wind’s drying scour.

Life thrives in the vertical corridors. Furred, low-slung quadrupeds with multi-jointed claws grip the underside of the limbs, moving upside down with terrifying speed to avoid the surface gale. Pack-hunting gliders with wide, skin-membrane wings launch themselves from the high spars, riding the thermal drafts that rise from the warmer, fermenting depths below, their whistling calls piercing the ambient roar as they chase flocking, needle-beaked scavengers through the black gulfs.

Clinging to the seams and fissures of the bark are the marks of sparse human presence. These are not cities, but desperate, lashed encampments hunkered deep inside split wood for shelter. Dwellings are constructed from cured vine-leather, bone struts, and pinned splinters of dense heartwood, anchored to the substrate by thick hawser-ropes braided from canopy fiber. Hand-chipped flint and bone-adzes are used to wedge open sap-chutes, funneling dark, viscous fuel into crude stone-bowls where smokeless grease fires burn with a dim, yellow sputter.

To travel here is to follow narrow, notched tracks chopped directly into the knife-edge ridges of the limbs, perpetually tethered to guide-lines of knotted sinew. Every few leagues, where two colossal trunks have recently collided and fused in the wind, one finds the remnants of older crossings—shattered timber bridges crushed by the shifting architecture of the wood, their frayed ropes trailing out into the abyss, whipping uselessly in the cold, dark rush of the air.
