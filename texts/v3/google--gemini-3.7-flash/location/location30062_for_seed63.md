It is a mountain’s dry throat, elevated far above the lower world’s weather, yet enclosed within a vaulted, lightless blister of black basalt. The air here does not circulate with the outside; it is trapped, baked by the dormant belly of the peak below until it reaches the dry, breathless heat of a kiln that has finished firing but has not yet cooled. 

There is no moisture to soften the edges of the stone. Water does not condense here; any stray vapor that enters through the hairline vents in the ceiling is instantly starved and split by the baking mineral dust. The walls and floor are composed of a dark, iron-heavy stone that has been worn into undulating, greasy curves—not by water, but by the relentless, millenary friction of uncounted bodies pressing against it. 

The chamber is small, perhaps the size of a ruined parish church or a collapsed quarry pit, but it feels infinitely tighter because there is virtually no unoccupied space. The darkness is near-absolute. It is not an airy void, but an opaque, heavy shadow that feels textured, smelling of scorched keratin, dry chitin, and pulverized mica.

In this black, roasting basin, the floor and walls are entirely alive.

The fauna here does not rely on light, nor on green growth. It is a closed, swarming carpet of dry, segmented biology that has achieved total saturation. They are blind, flat-bodied things the size of a palm, clad in thick, heat-resistant carapaces that clack like dry shale when they shift. Beneath them are smaller tiers: needle-legged scavengers that sift the fine powder of shed skins, and pale, calcified grubs that bore into the basalt itself, metabolizing sulfur-rich veins in the rock. 

They are packed so densely that there is no bare stone left to see; to take a step would be to displace a dozen living, shifting plates of shell. They layer atop one another three or four deep in the corners, sliding over each other in an unending, dry convection. 

Yet for all this overwhelming mass of life, the space is almost noiseless. It does not hum or screech. The sound it produces is subtle, continuous, and vast—a low, powdery whisper, like millions of dry fingernails lightly brushing against coarse paper, or a mountain of dry lentils slowly settling into a bin. Occasionally, there is the faint *snap* of a cooling shell, or the soft, hollow crunch where an older specimen is crushed under the ceaseless, slow-motion crawl of the crowd.

Emerging from the churning carpet of carapaces are the only still things in the chamber: a few eroded, upright piers of stacked slate. They are not natural columns. Long ago, before the fissure sealed to this near-total dark, someone or something piled flat, dry stones into crude, dry-stacked pillars and low lintels. These structures have no mortar, but they have not collapsed; instead, they have been cemented by centuries of dry biological secretions and buffed to a mirror sheen by the millions of limbs that scale them continuously. The pillars serve as vertical highways, dense with ascending and descending swarms that cluster near the ceiling where the dry heat gathers in stagnant, shimmering pockets.

The ecosystem operates in a rapid, relentless churn despite the lack of typical resources. The cycle is purely thermal and lithic: the deep volcanic floor radiates intense infrared heat, which these creatures absorb through ribbed, metallic-hued dorsal plates. They graze upon the rock, upon the micro-crusts of chemical salts blooming in the heat, and upon one another. Reproduction is constant and silent; deaths are immediate and completely absorbed, the dead picked clean into dry dust within minutes, adding to the thin layer of mineral flour that coats everything.

There is a strange, breathless rhythm to the place. Every few minutes, a deep thermal pulse expands from the porous floor—a silent wave of dry, searing air that causes the millions of shells to flare their edges upward in unison to catch the heat. In that moment, the whisper rises to a dry hiss, the entire room shifts like a living dune, and then settles again into the hot, dense, enduring dark.
