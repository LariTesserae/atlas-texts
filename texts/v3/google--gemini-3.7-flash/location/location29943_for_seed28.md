The roar hits before the eye can adjust to the glare. It is not the sound of water or thunder, but the deafening, mechanical shriek of pressurized hot air forced through millions of hollow stems and carved stone vents. The pocket is an acoustic trap, an enclosed tear inside a sun-struck calcite shelf no wider than four or five city blocks, yet vaulted so high and hollow that the empty space within it feels cavernous and stark.

Blinding, white-gold light pours through a network of narrow skylight-slits in the ceiling, illuminating an astonishingly dense, miniature metropolis carved from bone-pale chalk and sun-hardened clay. Tier upon tier of hand-mortared tenements, stepped plazas, and cantilevered terraces climb the sheer vertical walls of the pocket. Timbered catwalks, lashed together with dried sinew and twisted grass rope, span the yawning emptiness between opposing cliff-faces. The architecture is ancient and distinctly urban—the layout of a thriving, highly organized city—yet it is stripped of all soft furnishings, open to the blast of the thermal drafts, and completely devoid of crowds. It stands as an immense, hollow honeycomb: fully constructed, meticulously layered, and vast in its inner clearance.

Nothing here is wet; not a drop of moisture clings to the stone, nor does the air carry the faintest scent of humidity. Yet the terraces and abandoned alleys are overwhelmed by a violent, arid lushness.

Spilling over every parapet and choking the lower plazas is a thick, impenetrable jungle of parchment-flora. These plants are entirely anhydrous, thriving on static electricity, baking heat, and airborne mineral flour. Towering reed-canes, hard as cured bamboo and yellow as old teeth, rattle furiously in the drafts. Beside them grow sprawling, fractal succulents with skin like dried snakeskin, their broad, stiff fronds vibrating with such speed in the wind that they blur into humming fans. Shimmering sheets of mica-weed hang like curtains of thin glass flakes from the eaves of the mudbrick dwellings, clacking together in deafening, chaotic polyrhythms.

The noise inside the pocket is immense, continuous, and physical. The entire canyon vibrates. The wind-catchers built into the roofs of the primitive houses act as giant organ pipes; combined with the screaming of the hollow reeds and the percussive clatter of the mineral ferns, the atmosphere produces a steady, chest-crushing roar that drowns out all thought. 

Movement within the pocket is driven entirely by thermal pressure and botanical kinetics. When the midday sun strikes the limestone roof directly, the temperature spikes, causing the dry-ferns to snap shut in instantaneous, whip-crack bursts, casting clouds of golden spore-dust into the updrafts. These dust-devils whirl across the vacant plazas, scouring the masonry and keeping the stone edges razor-sharp. 

Fauna is nearly absent, unable to endure the acoustic violence or find purchase in a system without liquid. Occasionally, wedged deep into the mortar-chinks of the lower terraces, one might spot a solitary, slate-backed tick-mite—heavy, flat as a flagstone, and weighted like lead to keep from being shaken loose by the resonant frequency of the rock.

The city appears settled forever in this dry, roaring equilibrium. Its primitive stairways lead to empty lookouts that gaze only across the blinding air-shaft at other identical, sun-scalded terraces. Over the centuries, the wind and the flinty vegetation have polished the hand-chiseled stones to a hard, waxy sheen, cementing the pocket as a self-contained engine of sound, sun, and dry, rattling growth.
