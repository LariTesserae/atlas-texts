The chamber occupies the spatial volume of perhaps six city blocks, though its interior geography refuses to settle into an ordinary grid. It is cold—a wet, cellar-floor chill—and immersed in a heavy, velvety darkness that swallows sight beyond a few dozen yards.

Here, the boundary between urban infrastructure and botanical tissue has ceased to be an analogy and become a single, raw substrate. The city is not overgrown; it is being *secreted*. Massive, square-hewn girders of lignified heartwood span the darkness, their surfaces bearing the clean, unmarred edges of freshly milled timber, yet they pulse with slow, pressurized xylem fluids. Multi-story tenement facades and brick-patterned bulkheads bud directly from thick, taproot pylons. Because the entire precinct is embryonic—barely days old in its current iteration—there is no decay, no patina, and no rubble. The corners are surgical and sharp; the sap that seeps from mortise-and-tenon joints has not yet had time to oxidize.

Despite the complexity of this architecture, the space feels overwhelmingly hollow. The built density is concentrated into gossamer-thin layers—suspended catwalks made of braided reed-iron, vertical avenues hanging like plumb lines in the void, and solitary, five-story clock towers anchored to nothing, swaying gently in the rising drafts. Between these structures lies immense, dark clearance: hundreds of thousands of cubic feet of cold, damp air where nothing exists except falling beads of condensation. 

Water gathers intermittently, not from rain, but as a fine, cold sweat upon the undersides of the wooden viaducts. Every few minutes, a heavy drop detaches, falling into the blackness below with a distant, hollow *tock* that echoes against unseen vaulting.

Industrial mechanisms grow here like organs. Heavy flywheels of fibrous, compressed bark turn on self-lubricating cambium axles, driven by the rhythmic hydraulic expansion of massive, tubular vine-pistons. Steam—cool and scentless, smelling only of crushed celery and river silt—vents from rows of fluted pith-chimneys. There are no fires. The motive force of this factory-city is an unmapped metabolism: roots drawing chemical torque from the dark air, driving reciprocating wooden crossheads and woven-fiber drive belts that stretch across the empty expanses between towers.

The silence is deep, but it is not absolute. It is a working silence, muted by the moisture and the fibrous walls: the wet *shlick* of valves opening, the faint, high-tension creak of structural timber taking on load, and the persistent, muffled hum of internal fluids cycling through thousands of miles of capillaries.

Life within this cold dark is frantic and abundant. Blind, white-shelled fauna—creatures shaped like long-legged calipers, soft-bodied beetles the size of flatirons, and pale, segmented mantids—scuttle along the wooden conduits. They do not merely inhabit the machinery; they are its maintenance and its current. Clusters of small, hairless mammals with cold-burning, phosphorescent dewlaps nest inside hollow gear-casings, their rapid breathing keeping the wood-iron joints from freezing. Flying organisms with broad, membranous wings made of dry parchment plane silently across the gulfs of empty air, trailing threads of sticky filament that connect turning spindles to distant, ratcheting valves.

Reality here operates under a distorted, municipal biology. A street corner will terminate in a sheer drop into black space, only for the cobblestones to curve smoothly up an invisible wall, carrying a row of unlit, wrought-fiber gaslamps into the ceiling. The gravity shifts in discrete, rectangular parcels: step across the threshold of a half-grown warehouse, and "down" shifts ninety degrees, aligning with a wall of tightly packed, ticking seed-pods that function as an automated ledger.

Things are constantly coming into being. In the quiet gloom, a fresh industrial gantry slowly telescopes outward into the void, cells dividing with a dry, rhythmic clicking like a dozen telegraphs operating at once. It reaches into the dark, wet emptiness, perfectly plumb, perfectly leveled, awaiting an industry that has no workers, only an ecosystem that builds factories instead of forests.
