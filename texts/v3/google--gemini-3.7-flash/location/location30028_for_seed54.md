At this altitude, the air is too thin to hold warmth, but dense enough to carry a suffocating, supercooled dampness. It is pitch-black—not the soft darkness of a forest night, but the absolute, lightless pressure of a subterranean vault elevated into the upper atmosphere. There are no stars; a thick, freezing mist hangs permanently over the plateau, condensing in greasy, continuous sheets across every surface.

The terrain does not belong to natural geology, though it rises out of the jagged bones of a high mountain range. It is an emergent metropolis, vast and sprawling across a valley-sized shelf, yet it exhibits none of the decay or settling of an old city. The angles are pristine, un-eroded, still wet from their own synthesis. Megastructures of cold, self-knitting fullerene lattices and hyper-dense dark glass reach hundreds of meters up into the fog, their surfaces mathematically perfect, untouched by the smoothing hand of centuries. There is no soil here, no moss, no lichen, no trace of green life. The world is entirely mineral, polymer, and cryogenic moisture. Droplets run down vertical spires in steady, unbroken ribbons, collecting in freezing sluices that hum with silent, invisible currents. 

The architecture is still arriving, executing an automated, patient design across long spans of time. Deep within the foundations, microscopic assemblies organize matter with glacial slowness, extruding fresh bridges, ribbed viaducts, and hollowed towers directly from the rock face. They do not click or grind; their growth is a silent, molecular accretion.

Yet the silence of the structures is completely obliterated by the life that infests them. 

The darkness is deafening. Without a single ray of light, the city has become an oceanic nursery for an overwhelming biomass of blind, heavy-bodied fauna. Millions of them crowd the terraces, the sharp-edged balconies, the catwalks, and the dripping undersides of the pylons. They cling to one another in heaving, chitinous strata, layered three and four deep over the wet architecture.

These are not insects, nor mammals, but thick, cold-blooded beasts armored in oily, segmented plates that clatter and grind against the fresh glass like an avalanche of wet shale. They thrive on the thermal bleed of the assembling city and the dense, chemical-laden mist that condenses on its spires. Lacking eyes, they communicate through relentless concussive sound: low-frequency thumps that vibrate through the deckplates, piercing ultrasonic clicks that bounce off the unblemished walls, and wet, rasping inhalations as they draw the freezing fog across internal filtration combs. The noise is a physical wall, an acoustic storm trapped between immense, geometric canyons.

As they move, their heavy, clawed limbs scrape across the uneroded surfaces, slipping slightly on the cold film of water, leaving no scratches on the impossible alloys. When a spire finishes its slow, decades-long extrusion into the mist, the swarm senses the fresh heat of its molecular bonds and surges upward to coat it, a writhing, multi-limbed skin claiming the new geometry the moment it hardens.

Liquid drips constantly from the underbellies of the beasts, mingling with the condensation that rains down from the high arches. The air smells of ozone, iron, and the musky, bitter grease secreted by millions of blind bodies pressed shoulder-to-shoulder in the freezing dark. 

Beneath the overwhelming churn of the creatures, the city continues its patient, unyielding expansion. A hundred thousand tons of pristine, black infrastructure slowly rises higher into the mist, slick with water, blindly populated the instant it is born.
