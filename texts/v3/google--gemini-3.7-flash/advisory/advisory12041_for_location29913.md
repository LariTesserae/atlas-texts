### Field Preparation Guide: The Thimble-Planes
**Threat Classification:** Moderate Environmental / High Psychological & Physiological Attrition  
**Primary Hazards:** Desiccation, acoustic deprivation, vertigo, extreme altitude, temporal disorientation.

---

### 1. Atmospheric & Physiological Defenses

Visiting a realm of zero humidity, thin air, and mineral dust requires treating your body like an airtight vessel. The atmosphere will aggressively leach moisture from every exposed membrane.

*   **Closed-Circuit Breathing / Humidified Oxygen:** 
    *   Do not rely on simple ambient filtration. The air is dangerously thin, and every unassisted breath will pull water from your bronchial lining. Use a **humidified rebreather** or an **oxygen-enriching mask with an internal steam wick**. 
    *   *Crucial Detail:* If your mask fails, the scent of the plane—pulverized talc and ancient dry keratin—is an immediate respiratory irritant. Pack emergency aerosolized saline rinses to clear talc-clogged nasal passages.
*   **Ocular Protection (Moisture Chambers):**
    *   Because the air does not possess a single molecule of water, your blink reflex will quickly become painful. Wear **sealed, moisture-retaining goggles** equipped with internal micro-misters or continuous lubricating hydrogel inserts. 
    *   Do not wear porous contact lenses; they will dry out, warp, and adhere to your corneas within forty minutes.
*   **Barrier Emollients & Dermal Sealing:**
    *   Your skin will crack along natural flex lines within hours. Before stepping through the transit gate, coat your entire body in a heavy, non-aqueous lipid barrier (dense lanolin, pharmaceutical-grade petrolatum, or medical silicone sealant). 
    *   Lip balm is insufficient; carry a medical-grade **collagen mastic** for sealing fissures in the skin if your suit seal is breached.
*   **Thermal Layering without Bulk:**
    *   The cold here is static—there is no wind chill to steal your heat through convection, only steady, radiant loss into the cold siltstone and the empty sky. 
    *   Wear **conductive-insulation undergarments** beneath a smooth, dust-shedding outer shell. Avoid coarse fleece or Velcro on your outer layers; it will accumulate shedding from the beasts, turning your suit into a heavy, chalky felt.

### 2. Acoustic & Psychological Survival

The Thimble-Planes present one of the most unnatural sensory environments in the multiverse: absolute stillness combined with staggering biological mass.

*   **Anechoic Sickness Mitigation:**
    *   The billions of square yards of dense fur, leathery wings, and down act as an almost 100% acoustic baffle. The environment is deadened far beyond the quietest terrestrial recording studio. 
    *   Within an hour, your own auditory system will scale up its sensitivity until you hear the rushing of your carotid arteries, the high-pitched whine of your nervous system, and the mechanical clicking of your joints. 
    *   *Preparation:* Carry **bone-conduction audio devices** loaded with familiar, low-volume ambient sounds (rain, rustling leaves, distant surf). If traveling in a group, do not yell to be heard—sound dies within two paces. Rely on **short-range, line-of-sight UHF transceivers** built directly into your helmets.
*   **Scopophobia & The Static Crowd:**
    *   You will be surrounded by millions of living creatures that do not blink, do not breathe noticeably, and whose glossy, obsidian eyes are permanently fixed on the horizon. To the human lizard-brain, being watched by a crowd of titans triggers severe, paralyzing dread.
    *   *Preparation:* Practice mental desensitization. Treat the megafauna mentally not as watching predators, but as **organic architecture**. If the gaze of the flyers or ungulates becomes overwhelming, use tinted, narrow-aperture visors that limit your peripheral vision.
*   **Circadian Anchoring:**
    *   The sunlight is fixed; shadows do not sweep, lengthen, or fade. Without a day/night cycle, your brain will lose track of time, exacerbated by the ultra-slow heartbeats of the world around you.
    *   *Preparation:* Wear an **analog or mechanical chronometer with an internal light/alarm cycle** set strictly to your native home-world time. Force yourself to eat, hydrate, and sleep according to the dial, regardless of how alert the unchanging sky makes you feel.

### 3. Locomotion, Rigging, & Abyssal Traversal

Traversing miles-high massifs and crossing chasms by walking across the backs of gravity-suspended leviathans requires specialized gear and a specific mental approach to movement.

*   **Footwear for Dual-Surface Travel:**
    *   You will be transitioning between glassy, non-porous siltstone and the thick, yielding hides of sleeping serpents. 
    *   Use **soft-compound, non-marking vibram boots with high surface-area tread**. Do not use crampons or spiked shoes—puncturing the calcified hide of a suspended creature is not only ethically abhorrent, but it could also trigger a catastrophic localized reflex in an organism that has not moved for millennia.
*   **Bridging the Chasm (The Serpent Walk):**
    *   Crossing an abyss via suspended serpents is safe if done smoothly. The hides give slightly, like firm, aged leather over dense clay.
    *   *Technique:* Keep your center of gravity low and step in the center of the creatures' dorsal ridges. Although they do not react to your weight, do not linger on the narrow spans; the sensation of looking straight down past a floating, furred flank into thousands of fathoms of empty, sunlit air can cause sudden, debilitating vertigo.
    *   *Gear:* Always carry a **pneumatic line-launcher and broad-webbing slings**. When traversing gaps, you can loop webbing around the rigid, protruding horns or wing-joints of the static flyers overhead to create temporary running-belays.

### 4. Campcraft & Etiquette Among the Colossi

The plane is an ancient, fragile equilibrium. You are a volatile, transient spark moving through a world that has achieved terminal stillness.

*   **Sheltering in Living Niches:**
    *   Follow the example of the ancient builders: make camp in the lee of the megafauna. The space between the forelegs of a six-winged ungulate or beneath the curve of a grounded flank provides absolute shelter from the radiant cold of the sky.
    *   The beasts give off a very faint, deep, mineral warmth. Setting a bivy sack against their ribs will reduce the energy demands of your thermal gear.
*   **Strict Preservation of Offerings:**
    *   You will encounter small dry-stone huts, braided wool ropes, and flint-scraped niches containing dried lichen and polished knuckle-bones. **Do not disturb these.** 
    *   Because decomposition does not exist here, an offering left ten thousand years ago looks identical to one placed ten minutes ago. If you must secure your tent, utilize existing stone anchors or non-destructive friction knots tied to the ancient, shed wool cords already present.
*   **Waste Management:**
    *   Nothing rots here. An apple core, an open ration packet, or bodily waste left on the pale siltstone will remain there, preserved and pristine, for millions of years. 
    *   **Pack out everything.** Carry hermetically sealed waste-containment canisters. Do not introduce foreign organic fluids, bacteria, or moisture into the stone niches or onto the bodies of the fauna.
