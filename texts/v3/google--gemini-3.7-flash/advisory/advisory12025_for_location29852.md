To visit this world and survive as an ordinary human, you must prepare for a rare convergence of hazards: **extreme verticality, uncalibrated physics, respiratory toxicity, razor-sharp geology, and profound vestibular disorientation.** 

This is not a place you simply walk through; it is a place you rig into, cling to, and negotiate one anchor-point at a time. Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Gravitational & Kinetic Adaptation (The "Broken Physics" Protocol)
The most fatal mistake a traveler can make here is trusting their inner ear. Gravity in this nascent rift is not a constant downward vector; it is a fractured field running along invisible seams.

*   **Vestibular Sedation and Training:** You will experience sudden, violent shifts in "down." Walking around a corner may instantly roll your perceived gravitational plane by 90 or 180 degrees. Take prescription-strength vestibular suppressants (such as high-dose meclizine, scopolamine patches, or multidimensional spatial-adaptation pharmaceuticals) three days before arrival. You must train yourself to ignore what your inner ear claims is level and rely entirely on external visual anchors.
*   **The "Tell-Tale" Plumb-Line:** Keep a 2-foot weighted bronze chain tied to your non-dominant wrist at all times, fitted with bright orange ribbons. Before stepping off an iron platform, onto a new staircase, or across an open chasm, flick the chain outward. If the ribbon falls left, floats upward, or hangs horizontally, *that is the local down*. Never step into a space without confirming its gravity axis first.
*   **Zero-Velocity Fall Dampers:** Standard parachutes are useless here (they will shred on structural iron or tangle in steam drafts). Instead, wear an inertia-dampening harness equipped with pneumatic catch-spikes and auto-camming fall arresters. 
*   **100% Tie-Off Discipline:** You must adopt industrial ironworker rigging habits. Use a heavy Y-lanyard with dual self-locking steel carabiners. One hook must *always* be locked around an iron girder, rivet flange, or braided safety cable before the other is unclipped. Never run; never leap.

### 2. Respiratory & Atmospheric Conditioning
The air is a punishing cocktail of high-altitude hypoxia, absolute humidity, coal smoke, volatilized machine tallow, and fungal spores.

*   **Altitude Acclimatization:** The atmospheric pressure resembles Earth at 12,000 to 14,000 feet. Spend two weeks sleeping in a hypobaric chamber beforehand, or carry a supply of Acetazolamide (Diamox) to accelerate acclimatization and prevent Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS).
*   **Dual-Stage Respirator (Heated & Filtered):** The air is thick with airborne coal-dust, abrasive bismuth micro-crystals, and liverwort spores. A standard cloth mask will turn into a sodden, suffocating rag within twenty minutes. You need a low-profile, half-face elastomeric respirator fitted with:
    *   **P100 particulate filters** (to catch soot and biological spores).
    *   **An organic vapor cartridge** (to neutralize sulfurous furnace gas and hot oil fumes).
    *   **A heated exhalation port or moisture trap** to keep the perpetual mist from condensing inside the filter elements and waterlogging your lungs.
*   **Supplemental Oxygen Bladder:** Keep a compact, high-pressure emergency oxygen cylinder tucked into your chest harness for transit through the lower districts, where heavy carbon monoxide pools against the sheer rock faces.

---

### 3. Thermal, Wetness, and Cut Protection
The monoliths are brand new. They are not worn or rounded; their edges are as sharp as fresh broken obsidian and sliced slate. Meanwhile, the constant mist and cold iron will suck heat out of your body rapidly.

*   **Aramid/Kevlar-Reinforced Outerwear:** Standard hiking gear, nylon shells, and gore-tex will be instantly sliced to ribbons if you brush against a raw bismuth outcrop or slate facet. Your outermost layer must be made of **heavy waxed canvas lined with cut-resistant aramid weave**. It should be slick enough to shed engine oil and tough enough to scrape against a fresh fracture-plane without tearing.
*   **Thermal Layering without Down:** Do *not* wear down insulation; the perpetual mist will penetrate it and destroy its loft. Use hydrophobic synthetic insulation (like heavy-duty PrimaLoft Gold) over merino wool base layers. 
*   **Gauntlets and Grip:** Wear heavy split-cowhide or goatskin work gloves reinforced with Kevlar palm-padding. You will be grabbing wet, ice-cold, rust-scaled iron and sharp stone coated in grease and slime. The gloves must be treated with pine tar or tacky wax to ensure grip on slick railings.
*   **Footwear:** Wear steel-toed, high-shank mountaineering boots with deeply lugged, oil-resistant Vibram soles. The soles must feature embedded tungsten-carbide micro-cleats to grip slippery liverwort mats, wet zinc roof-plates, and slime-covered catwalks.

### 4. Navigating the Ecology and the Built Environment
You are walking through a biome that treats industrial iron as its natural cliff-face ecosystem.

*   **Fauna Protocol:**
    *   *Blind Skimming-Rays:* These creatures ride the warm updrafts between districts. They are not predators, but their wingspans are massive and their skin is coarse like sandpaper. Do not wear loose, fluttering cloaks or ponchos; if caught in a ray’s wake, you can be pulled off a ledge by sheer aerodynamic displacement.
    *   *Copper-Nesting Crabs:* Arboreal crabs harvest discarded metal for their structural nests. **Tuck away all loose wiring, antennas, headphones, and exposed copper gear.** They have powerful shears designed to snip structural wiring and will cut through your electronics or pack straps if they spot shiny metal.
    *   *Suction-Lizards:* Carry all rations in **airtight, hard-sided composite or aluminum tins**. The local lizards can peel open soft dry-bags and will invade your pack while you rest on a funicular platform.
*   **Acoustic Protection:** The rhythmic, tectonic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of massive dynamos, paired with the shriek of pneumatic releases and metallic animal calls, will cause sensory fatigue, tinnitus, and low-frequency nausea within hours. Wear **dynamic acoustic earplugs**—the kind that filter out continuous low-frequency hums and loud industrial spikes while still allowing you to hear the click of a carabiner or the voice of someone speaking next to you.

### 5. Illumination and Field Tools
Natural light is a dim, bruise-colored twilight. The bottoms of the chasms are pure void.

*   **Amber-Spectrum Lighting:** White LEDs will backscatter off the dense, suspended mist, blinding you with a wall of white glare. Carry high-CRI, **amber or warm-yellow (2700K–3000K) flashlights and headlamps**. Amber light cuts through coal-smoke and fog without creating blinding halo effects.
*   **Chemical Light Sticks (Weighted):** Carry an assortment of heavy, weighted glow sticks. If you drop one down a gravity well, watching its trajectory will visually map the gravity gradient of the chasm beneath you.
*   **Tallow & Solvent Kit:** Carry a small tin of mutton tallow or paraffin wax (to lubricate your safety clips and keep them from seizing up in the wet, coal-gritty air) and a small vial of citrus solvent to clean axle grease off your hands and optical gear.

### Mental Preparation: The Weight of the Void
Finally, prepare yourself psychologically for the sheer scale of the abyss. There is no ground to fall back on; there is only the iron you are clamped to and the black mist miles below. 

The city groans constantly. The newborn earth beneath it settles with shuddering, seismic sighs that will vibrate straight through the soles of your boots and into your teeth. You must cultivate a state of **deliberate, slow, rhythmic calm**. Move at the speed of the city's great flywheels: ponderous, unhurried, checking every latch, testing every breath of gravity before you trust it with your life.
