The place is called the **White Throat**, though no one has spoken that name aloud here for generations. It is a cathedral-sized scoop hollowed from the eastern face of a mountain so raw and recently birthed by tectonic upheaval that the stone still holds the smell of shattered Earth's mantle. The elevation is extreme—lung-burning, consciousness-narrowing altitude where the atmosphere is attenuated to a glass-blue transparency. Yet the Throat itself is small: a single bounded chamber, roughly oval, perhaps fifty meters across at its widest and thirty deep, cupped within vertical walls of unweathered basalt that rise like the nave of an abandoned basilica.

The rock here is *young*. It has not yet learned the patience of erosion; the faces are knife-sharp, crystalline, with edges that will slice uncalloused skin. Fresh scree litters the floor in jagged palettes—gray, sulfurous yellow, black—untouched by the smoothing caress of time. The Throat opened only centuries ago, perhaps millennia, a geological infant shucked open by glacial retreat or a sudden calving of the mountain's flank.

Despite the altitude, the air is *damp*, though not wet in any flowing sense. There are no streams here, only a persistent atmospheric bleed. Moisture condenses on the cold stone with such regularity that the walls weep in slow, mineral-laden tears. The dampness gathers in hairline cracks and freezes, expands, melts again—a cycle of freeze-thaw so constant it constitutes the primary *dynamic* of the place. You can hear it if you hold your breath: the microscopic *tick* of ice crystals spawning, the *sigh* of water sliding a millimeter down a vertical face. The sound register is otherwise near-zero, a hollow silence that swallows footsteps. The air is too thin to carry the voice far; sounds drop dead after a few meters, falling into the emptiness.

For the **density** here is *empty*—a profound topological hollowness. Within the Throat's bounded volume, existence is scattered like sparse punctuation in a blank text. The center of the floor is naked stone, windswept bare, offering yards of exposure between any two points of interest. Objects—boulders, ice formations, the scattered residues of human presence—stand isolated in vast arenas of negative space.

Yet the place is **lush**, and here is the first strangeness. The moisture has allowed life to colonize every protected niche with *vegetation* of surprising generosity for the climate: emerald mosses fattened into velvet cushions, lichens in violent oranges and toxic yellows, and tiny, star-like alpine flowers that have no business blooming at this height. They cluster in the cracks where condensation pools, creating gardens no larger than a handspan but impossibly vivid against the mineral gray.

More abundance arrives with the **fauna**, which is not merely present but overwhelming—rating 2.4 on the scale of living presence—yet invisible at first. The population consists of millions upon millions of *ice springtails*—wingless, eyeless, translucent arthropods no larger than pinheads. They coat the damp walls in a living carpet several bodies thick, silver-white and iridescent. When the **bright** light strikes them—sunlight so intense and unfiltered by atmosphere that it borders on the violent—the springtails refract it into prismatic shivers. The walls appear to breathe, to shimmer with internal luminescence. This is the **weirdness** of the place: that the stone itself seems alive, pulsing with silent, microscopic industry. The springtails harvest the condensation, metabolize the algae, and die in layers that build a faint organic dust upon the scree.

They make no sound. They move in wave patterns, a slow migration following the sun-warmed dampness across the rock faces, creating ripples of biological motion that never rise above a whisper of texture.

Scattered through this hollow—accounting for the **built** parameter at 0.9—are the **primitive** traces of transient habitation. Three low walls of dry-stacked stone, waist-high, arranged as windbreaks in a rough triangle near the southern rim. The stones show **tech** of simple fabrication: percussion-flaked chert tools were used to shape them, though the edges remain crude. A cairn stands at the Throat's lip, crude and precarious. Faint red ochre handprints, faded to the color of dried blood, mark one overhang—the pigment blown or spat against the stone in some ritual of passage. These marks are occasional, lonely, suggesting visitors who came to harvest the springtails perhaps, or to shelter in the dead air of the hollow, then moved on.

The **temperature** remains locked near the threshold of pain—just below freezing during the day, plummeting viciously at night. Yet the **dynamic** energy of the place is high: geological youth asserting itself through constant micro-fractures, biological processes consuming and being consumed, condensation cycling through its phase changes, and the slow, tidal movement of the springtail colonies. It is a pocket of intense, quiet activity, a single lungful of mountain breathing in and out, bright as a blade, empty as a held breath, and teeming with invisible life.
