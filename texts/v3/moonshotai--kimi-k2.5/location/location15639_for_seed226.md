The **Singing Hollow** sits at the lip of the world—a single vault, cathedral-sized, suspended between cloud-level and the upper atmosphere where the air grows thin. It is not a valley but a room, a bubble of void punched into the mountain’s throat by a cooling god or a dying volcano, now mantled over with moss and silence. The scale is intimate, bounded; you could walk from one wall to the opposite in three hundred steps, yet within that compass, the world is being born.

The stone here is *emerging*—not eroded but incubated. The basalt walls are still warm to the touch in places, still weeping the heat of their creation centuries ago. They tick and groan with a constant, loud song (2.3): the sound of contraction, of crystal lattices finding their rest, of the mountain settling into its own skin. It is the noise of a house built yesterday still creaking at midnight, amplified by the perfect acoustics of the dome. Every droplet that strikes the floor pings like a bell.

For water (1.5) is everywhere, not as rivers but as potential. The cool temperature (0.7) grips the high altitude, drawing moisture from the air in a perpetual, invisible rain. Condensation beads on the black stone and runs in silver threads down the walls, collecting in pools that never warm. The rock weeps. In the center of the chamber, a shallow lake—no deeper than a kneeling person’s waist—reflects the sky-hole above, where normal light (2.3) pours down in a solid column, illuminating the dust motes and steam.

Vegetation (2.4) has claimed this wetness with voracious politeness. Ferns unroll their heads like bishops’ croziers from every fissure where heat meets moisture. Moss carpets the floor in yards of emerald velvet, thick enough to swallow footsteps. Hanging gardens drape from the ceiling’s edge, root systems naked and white, drinking the condensation directly. But the density is empty (0.2); despite the lushness, there are vast clearings of wet stone where nothing lives, halls of bare basalt between the groves. It is a garden designed for solitude.

And here, the **Urban** asserts itself—strange, because the technology is primitive (1.3), yet the *organization* is undeniable. The inhabitants—few, perhaps a score of beings—have built a city not of height but of concentric rings. Platforms of unworked stone, levered into place with wooden sledges and obsidian wedges, form terraces rising in steps from the central pool. Dwellings are carved into the warm walls where the rock is softest, circular rooms like cells in a hive, connected by covered walkways of woven reed and bone. It is settlement-scale, planned, zoned: a place for gathering, for drying moss, for storing the condensed water in ceramic vessels fired in the ge vents. But it is hollow. The great amphitheater at the north end could seat five hundred, yet sits empty, tended only by wind.

The fauna (1.0) is scarce as prayer. Perhaps a few blind crabs scuttle in the warm shallows, or moths with wings like parchment drink the stone-sweat. Mostly, the animals are the sounds themselves—the dripping, the ticking, the occasional *crack* of a new fissure opening in the floor, releasing a hiss of steam.

Weirdness (1.3) lives in the way the Hollow *breathes*. The central pool rises and falls by inches over the course of a day, not with tides but with the thermal expansion of the stone beneath it. The water flows upward through capillary channels in the walls, defying gravity in slow motion, then releases itself in weeping curtains. The inhabitants believe the stone is still growing, that the architecture of their city is being extruded by the mountain itself, new rooms pushing out like teeth, raw and jagged.

It is a place of slow dynamics (1.1). Change happens here, but geologically—over generations, the floor will lift, the pool will shift, the warm walls will cool and the ferns will retreat. The people maintain the city not by building new, but by following the stone’s lead, clearing the moss from emerging staircases, channeling the weeping water into new reservoirs as the old ones are pushed up and cracked by the rising floor.

To stand in the Singing Hollow is to stand inside a lung that has not yet decided to exhale. The air is cool and tastes of iron and wet stone. The light is clear. The sound is deafening in its patience—a constant, rhythmic thunder of creation settling into permanence. It is a town built in the womb of a mountain, waiting to be born.
