The place is called the **Oubliette of First Causes**, though it has no name in any tongue that can be spoken aloud. It exists in the hollow beneath a hollow, a bubble of pre-reality trapped between the earth’s crust and the molten certainty of its core. Here, erosion runs backward: the walls are not wearing away but *accumulating*, gathering mineral potential from the confusion of dimensions that seep through cracks in causality.

You enter through a throat of limestone that hasn't finished forming yet—you have to push through stone that is simultaneously solid and sediment, wet with condensation that beads upward against gravity’s tentative hold. The chamber itself is intimate, barely twenty paces across, yet the sparse distribution of its contents makes it feel cavernous. Emptiness occupies the center like a second guest.

The light is unbearable. It does not have a source because it precedes sources. It bleeds from the *emerging* geology itself—quartz veins that pulse with photons not yet assigned to particles, bioluminescent mineral accretions that bright as the chamber slowly crystallizes into existence. Shadows here are optional; some corners choose not to have them.

Water manifests as *potential* moisture. Droplets hang in the cool air like suspended pearls, drifting sideways until they encounter a surface that accepts them. The floor—if it can be called that—is damp with a film of liquid that might be water or might be the idea of water, cool to the touch (1.4), creating rivulets that etch not grooves but *raised scars* in the embryonic stone, building channels upward in defiance of topography.

Vegetation is sparse but tenacious: translucent fungal fans that grow against the bright light, feeding on photons rather than carbon. They cluster in the scattered architectural remnants—structures that were perhaps meant to be pillars or altars but stopped halfway through the thought of construction, existing as scaffolding for intentions that never solidified. These built-forms are made of the same accreting stone, grown rather than carved, single-standing and incomplete, like memories of buildings recalled by someone who has never seen architecture.

The fauna is abundant and impossible. The chamber swarms with *pre-insects*—chitinous things with too many wings that exist in multiple phases simultaneously, their life cycles overlapping in the same instant like stacked transparencies. They do not fly so much as *decide* their location, appearing in swarms near the condensation, drinking the suspended droplets. There are soft-bodied creatures too, jelly-pale and luminescent, that crawl along the reality-tears, feeding on the brightness itself. They are the only things that make sound: a subsonic hum, a vibration in the sternum rather than the ear, the quiet noise of matter organizing itself into biology (0.5).

The dynamic here is one of **becoming**. The chamber is in motion, though nothing appears to move. The floor rises by millimeters per hour as new stone precipitates from the damp air. The scattered structures subtly shift their angles, adjusting to geometries that haven't been invented yet. Predation is a process of *assimilation*—when one of the luminous grazers touches a fungal cluster, it doesn't consume it but rather *agrees* with it, and they exchange places in space-time, leaving behind a hybrid organism that is neither and both.

Reality breakdown manifests as *softness*. In the eastern corner, distance is negotiable—three steps might cross the room, or thirty, depending on how certain you are of your destination. Gravity occasionally forgets which way is down, pooling in the ceiling like spilled mercury before remembering itself. Time moves in spirals; you can see your own breath from five minutes ago drifting past, condensing on the future.

The ecology is the process of formation itself. Everything here is participating in the act of *deciding what it is*. The abundant fauna are not inhabitants but *symptoms*—living indices of the chamber's slow birth into actuality. They breed in the damp crevices of pre-formation, laying eggs that hatch before their mothers are born, swimming in the intermittent moisture that beads on surfaces still arguing about their chemical composition.

It is a quiet place (0.5), save for the hum of crystallization, the occasional *pop* of a water droplet achieving sufficient reality to fall, and the whisper of raw geology teaching itself to be stone. Cool, bright, damp, and scattered with the ruins of things that almost were, it waits—patient, embryonic, and entirely too full of life for its size.
