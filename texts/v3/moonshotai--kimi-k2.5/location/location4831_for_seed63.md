The **Cavity** exists at 4,100 meters, not open to sky but buried beneath a carapace of metamorphic stone—a bubble trapped during the mountain’s orogenic birth, sealed by a plug of fused quartz that cooled faster than the world around it. You do not enter; you breach. And once inside, the first sensation is not sight—there is none—but *pressure*. The air is anhydrous, a superheated soup of nitrogen and carbon dioxide heavy with sublimated metal salts. It carries no moisture (0.5), not even the memory of rain. To breathe here would be to scald the lungs with dry heat (2.6), a baking warmth that radiates from every surface, kept constant by the slow decay of thorium veins in the rock below.

It is small (1.4). Forty meters across at its widest, roughly ovoid, the dimensions of a cathedral nave compressed into a geode. The walls are not rough but *polished*, worn glassy by 400 million years of thermal breathing (erosion 1.6)—expansion at noon-warmth, contraction in the stone’s night, though “night” is a meaningless distinction here. The darkness is nearly absolute (light 0.3), a velvet null broken only by the occasional Cherenkov-blue spark of radioactive decay striking a fluorite inclusion, a flash so brief it leaves afterimages of the chamber’s true contents: the **Packed**.

The density (2.8) is suffocating. The space is not empty; it is *occupied*. Between floor and ceiling, from wall to curving wall, the air is a solid mass of **Thermophage Ribbons**. Each is two meters long, three millimeters thick, composed of layered silicon-carbide chitin that absorbs all light and sound. They are fauna (2.9) in the way that a glacier is fauna—slow, mineral, hungry—but they move. They must move (dynamic 2.2). The chamber is a thermal engine, and they are its pistons.

They float, suspended by hydrogen bladders electrolyzed from sulfide deposits, drifting in convection currents too subtle for human perception. They are packed so tightly that they form strata—layers of drifting, sliding bodies, sliding over and under one another with graphite-lubricated frictionlessness. There is no room between presences. A thousand per cubic meter. Ten thousand. They feed on the gradient: the floor is 82°C, the ceiling a “cool” 67°C, and they harvest this differential through thermoelectric metabolism, their bodies circuit boards converting heat into cellular order.

And they are silent (sound 0.9). The heavy gas dampens vibration; their ceramic bodies absorb the rest. The only sounds are geological: the *tick* of the ceiling contracting, the *crick* of barite crystals growing a nanometer further into the void. The Ribbons communicate through infrared pulses, through pressure waves conducted by the medium of their own bodies, through a language of heat and touch. When one dies—and they do, dissolving into silicate dust—it does not fall; it is caught, absorbed, crushed into the mass, recycled by the living carpet.

There is no vegetation (0.3), no fungal mat, no lichen. Only the Ribbons and the **Stone-Milk**, a precipitate of calcium and barium that oozes from the walls like slow sweat, forming the only “built” structures (1.0) in the chamber: accidental architecture. A rusted drill bit from a 1923 Soviet survey juts from the north wall, half-consumed by accretion, its steel now a mineralogical component. A melted polymer bottle from 1978, dropped down a vent, has become a glossy inclusion. These are the only marks of the outside world—scattered, faint, being digested by the geology.

The weirdness (1.8) lies in the *timing*. The Ribbons do not age linearly. Observe one: it bears a scar from a collision with the drill bit. Watch it drift backward in the current, and the scar heals, closes, vanishes. The chamber exists in a temporal stutter, a pocket where causality is thermodynamic rather than chronological. The Ribbons are not individual organisms but nodes of a single distributed event, a standing wave of biology that has achieved equilibrium. They are born, live, and un-live in the same motion.

The rules here are simple:
1. **Heat flows upward; life flows downward.** The Ribbons migrate from ceiling to floor to breed in the hottest zone, then rise to cool (relatively) and think (if thinking is what the infrared flickering is).
2. **No waste.** Matter cycles in a closed loop. The air is sterile of bacteria; decomposition is geological, not biological.
3. **Silence is structure.** Sound is energy lost. Here, energy is hoarded. The chamber is a bank vault of calories, and the Ribbons are the currency, packed tight, circulating endlessly in the dark.

It is a room-sized heaven for heat, a crowded void where nothing is wet, nothing is green, nothing is quiet in the sense of dead, but everything is hushed, packed shoulder-to-shoulder in the sweltering black, sliding past one another in a dance older than bones, older than wood, older than the sun that never reaches here.
