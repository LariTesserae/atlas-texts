The **Calcination Plateaus** rise seven kilometers above any theoretical sea level, yet the air here does not thin or cool. Instead, it presses heavy and shimmering, a superheated soup of nitrogen and metallic particulates that has never known moisture. This is a desert not of sand but of **processed salts**—vast hexagonal pans of lithium chloride and potassium-sulfate compounds that gleam like mirrors, reflecting a sky that seems, at wrong moments, to be reflecting them. The temperature hovers at the upper threshold of biological tolerance, but biology here has rewritten the terms of engagement.

There is no water. There has never been water. The 0.2 rating manifests as **ghost-moisture**—the *idea* of humidity that your skin expects but your eyes deny, a phantom sensation produced by the plateaus' particular thermodynamic insult. What little hydrogen exists is locked in ceramic sinews, in the chitinous shells of the **Orexorcs**, or vented instantly as superheated plasma from the seventy-meter dorsal stacks that punctuate the horizon.

These are the fauna. They overwhelm the landscape.

They are not animals in any Linnaean sense, nor machines in any fabricated one. They are **industrial organisms**, evolved or emerged (the distinction collapsed here eons ago) to perform the continuous refinery of reality itself. Each Orexorc stands the size of a cathedral, its body a slow-moving amalgam of blast-furnace stomachs, ball-mill joints, and cooling-tower spiracles. Their skin is barnacled with the waste-products of their metabolism: perfect cubic zirconium, flawless industrial sapphire, and strange, translucent metals that phase in and out of visible spectra. They have no eyes, no nervous systems recognizable to standard biology, yet they navigate with the precision of GPS-guided drills, following seams of ore that exist simultaneously in this dimension and three adjacent ones.

The **weirdness** is absolute, nearly at threshold. The Plateaus exist at a point where the fundamental forces negotiate badly. Gravity is locally variable—each Orexorc generates its own micro-gauss field, creating bowls of distortion in the salt pans where things roll uphill and shadows fall toward the sun. Time dilates around the larger specimens; their movements appear glacial (dynamic 0.9), yet if you watch for hours, you realize they are processing matter at industrial speeds, just *stretched* across temporal accordion-folds. You might see a boulder vanish into their mandibular mills, then watch the dust emerge eight minutes before the grinding begins.

And the **sound**. The sound is 2.2—loud, pervasive, geological in its constancy. It is the **Drone of Refinement**: the basso-profundo rumble of tungsten teeth against vanadium ore, the steam-whistle scream of pressure releases (though water is absent, they generate working fluids from compressed atmospheric nitrogen and distilled carbon extracted from the salt matrices), and the rhythmic *CLANG-CLANG-CLANG* of internal piston-organs that stand in for hearts. The sound doesn't travel through the air so much as conduct through the salt crust, making the ground itself a speaker diaphragm. It vibrates your marrow. It has been vibrating for three million years, a tempo so slow and inexorable that it has worn the plateau smooth—not by violence, but by sonic erosion, polishing the minerals into these vast, billiard-table flats that extend to the curved horizon.

**Scale** manifests as loneliness despite the overwhelming biomass. The Orexorcs maintain vast territories (density 0.4), each isolated in a bubble of its own acoustic shadow and gravity well. Between them stretches the **Null**, white and glaring, where the salt has been purified to absolute sterility. No vegetation—none—could breach these pans. The 0.3 rating is absolute; the only "growth" is the **Excreted Architecture**: spires of perfect crystalline lattice that the Orexorcs extrude not as waste but as *byproduct*, the way a pearl is a byproduct of irritation. These are not built (0.5); they are **grown**, yet they resemble cooling towers, scaffolding, and smokestacks so precisely that the uninformed eye searches for the factory complex that must surely own them. There is none. The factory *is* the organism. The industry *is* the ecology.

Here, **industrial** (2.4) means scaled conversion. The Orexorcs are performing the universe's slow alchemy, taking the chaotic sprawl of raw elements and enforcing order upon it through heat (2.7), pressure, and the breakdown of physical law. They consume the entropic future and excrete crystallized past. The **erosion** (2.4) is established, complete, settled—they have worn the mountains flat, digested the ore-bodies, and polished the continental plate to its current state of finished, gleaming, preposterous smoothness. This is a terminal landscape, not in the sense of death, but in the sense of a **completed process**, a refinery that has achieved optimum function and now simply *continues*, patient and eternal.

The light (2.1) behaves strangely here, normal in color but bent by the heat-shimmer and the gravity lenses. At noon, the Orexorcs appear to be burning, their dorsal vents releasing streams of photons that have been compressed into visible liquidity. At dusk, the shadows stretch in impossible directions, falling upward into the sky where they pool like ink in water, creating temporary night-voids that blot out stars.

Life here—if it can be called life—is **overwhelming** (2.6) in its refusal of the organic contract. There are millions of Orexorcs across the Regional Plateaus, billions if you count the **Microsmiths**, the fist-sized drone-organisms that swarm like flies around their parents, performing fine-detail work with plasma-torches grown from their own abdomens. They maintain the Excreted Architecture, polish the vent-stacks, repair the cooling fins. The air tastes of ozone and hot copper. The ground sings with infrasound.

It is a place where the distinction between the natural and the manufactured has been annihilated by heat and time. There are no ruins here because nothing has ever been built; there is only the endless, grinding, thermal **now**, the patient industry of organisms that turn geology into machinery, and machinery into geology, in a cycle so slow that the mountains have forgotten they were ever anything but salt, and the creatures have forgotten they were ever anything but the process of refining itself.
