There is no sky here, only ceiling—the upper boundary of an ocean that is not entirely water but something older, a primordial fluid that has never known air. The world is **submerged total**: every cubic meter is liquid, and every solid surface is artifact. There is no bedrock, no native geology, only the accumulated weight of hand-worked matter compressed by age into false geology. This is a planet-sized mausoleum of intention, where the distinction between construction and landscape dissolved millions of years ago.

**The Terrain**  
You drift through low elevations—trench-depths where the pressure would crush unprotected bone, though here the cold (barely above the freezing point of ancient seas) keeps the fluid viscous, slow-moving. The floor, if it can be called that, is a continuous carpet of eroded megastructure. Imagine ziggurats built by cyclopean hands using only stone tools, leveled by 2.8 erosion until they resemble wave-flattened mesas. The architecture is *dense*: walls within walls, chambers collapsed into chambers, creating a labyrinthine topography where verticality has been ground down into gentle, rolling elevations of worked granite and compressed bone-concrete. These are not ruins in the traditional sense; they are **settled forms**, having reached their final stable state. The erosion is complete—the sharp edges of primitive masonry have been worn to the curves of river stones, the gaps between blocks filled with sediment that is itself pulverized construction, layered so long that the layers have become strata.

**Materials**  
Everything is handmade, yet nothing is recent. The substrate consists of mortise-and-tenon joints the size of city blocks, fitted without metal (primitive tech: 0.8) using only leverage, wedges, and unknown organic cements that have fossilized into something harder than the stone itself. Between these immense fitted stones grows a lush vegetation (2.4) that requires no light—black kelp forests anchored to the masonry, enormous tube-worm analogs that are botanical rather than animal, fungal canopies that spread like coral across the horizontal planes. These plants are chemosynthetic or filter-feeding, drawing minerals from the slow dissolving of the architecture itself. They hang in **dense** curtains, multiple layers of growth occupying the same volume, crosshatched root systems gripping the carved stone until the boundary between built and grown is impossible to discern. The materials breathe: the stone is porous, saturated, slowly becoming plant matter; the plants are mineralizing, becoming stone.

**Atmosphere**  
The darkness is nearly total (0.2), a blackness relieved only by the occasional dim phosphorescence of bacterial colonies colonizing the mortar-lines. The cold is penetrating but stable, a thermal equilibrium maintained by the sheer inertia of the planet's water-mass. There is weather here, though it unfolds on timescales of centuries: slow currents that are to normal oceans what glaciers are to rivers, massive cells of brine shifting position over decades, creating gentle pressure-fronts that pass through the vegetation like silent storms. The density of the environment means that even when currents move, they move quietly (0.8), their energy absorbed by the thick fluid and the thicker growths.

**Sound**  
Mostly, there is the compression of silence. Sound does not travel far; it is swallowed by the dense plant matter and the ancient stone. Occasionally, a structural settling—some final adjustment as a million-ton block shifts by a millimeter after ten thousand years—emits a groan so low and slow that it registers as pressure rather than noise, felt in the sternum, a tectonic sigh from the architecture relaxing into deeper sleep.

**Ecology**  
There are no animals (0.4). The food web is strictly botanical and microbial. Gigantic, sluggish algae-forests filter the water, their fronds thick as tree trunks, covered in symbiotic moss-analogs that form spongy carpets. The only motion is vegetative: the slow opening of polyp-flowers, the creeping advance of rhizomes searching for new cracks in the masonry to exploit, the falling of particulate matter—organic snow from the upper zones—that accumulates in the depressions of carved glyphs, building up sediment at the rate of centimeters per century. Decomposition is glacial; matter is preserved, compressed, integrated into the growing substrate.

**Rules of the World**  
Here, time is the primary architect. The dynamic (0.6) is so patient that "change" is a geological verb. What was built high has eroded low; what was open has been filled by the slow avalanche of compression and growth. The world operates on principles of saturation—every space is filled, either by water, by the ghosts of primitive construction, or by the lush, cold dark that thrives in the absence of sun and fauna. 

Weirdness is minimal (0.2) because everything follows an internal logic of entropy and accretion. This is simply what happens when hands build with stone in a submerged dark, and then keep building, layer after layer, until the building becomes the world, and the world grows tired, and settles down to sleep under the weight of its own history, breathing once every millennium through the pores of its waterlogged stone.
