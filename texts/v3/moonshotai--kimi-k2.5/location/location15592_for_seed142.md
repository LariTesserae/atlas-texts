You stand on a tableland so elevated that the atmosphere has thinned to a ghost of itself, yet the cold of altitude never arrives. Instead, warmth—dry, metallic, persistent—rises through the soles of your feet from the bedrock below. The ground maintains a temperature of precisely forty-two degrees Celsius, heated by ancient geothermal reservoirs that have leaked upward for eons, their conductivity perfected by the very chemistry of the place. No wind stirs. The air is stratified, cool layers settling below warmer ones in a permanent inversion that has not broken in millions of years.

This is the **Carbide Plain**, a continental plateau eroded past the point of geography. Over 2.7 billion years, mountains here were worn to dust, valleys filled to grade, cliffs polished to mathematical smoothness. The erosion has reached its terminal state—*complete*—leaving a surface of silicon and diamond microparticles fused into a ceramic hardness, undulating gently with a radius of curvature that perfectly matches the planet. There are no rocks to stumble over, no cracks to fall into, only a vast, slightly bowl-shaped expanse extending to every horizon, finished as a machine part is finished.

And upon this eroded substrate rests the City, though "city" suggests bustle, and here there is only the **Static**.

Structures rise from the plain at intervals of several kilometers—sparse (density 1.2), scattered like seeds that never decided to grow closer together. They are industrial (tech 1.9), yet they resemble nothing built by human hands. Each is a tower of accreted machinery, seventy to eight hundred meters tall, formed from black metallic glass that seems to have precipitated from solution rather than been cast. These are the Final Factories, built (2.0) in layers that spiral downward into the bedrock and upward into the dark. They have no windows, no doors in the conventional sense, only openings where the internal geometry requires them, leading into labyrinthine interiors of polished vanadium and compressed carbon.

The darkness (light 0.1) is absolute and vast. No sun warms this world; it is either tidally locked to a distant star, or the light is occluded by cosmic dust, or perhaps the physics here simply absorb photons with greedy efficiency. Starlight, when it penetrates the high atmosphere, falls as faint as dust motes, insufficient to cast shadows. Yet the darkness is not empty—it has weight, a viscous quality, as if time itself has thickened here. You navigate by the warmth underfoot, by the subsonic thrum that vibrates in your sternum rather than your ears.

For sound (0.6) is mostly absent. The factories operate in eternal motion, yet they produce no noise because they have achieved perfect efficiency. There is no friction in their bearings—if they even have bearings—no turbulence in their cooling systems, no impact of piston or gear. Occasionally, a cosmic ray streaks through the thin air and strikes a specific catalytic panel, producing a brief, violet spark of Cherenkov radiation, and in its wake, a silence that feels *deeper* than before.

The dynamic of the place is zero (0.4). Nothing changes. The erosion stopped when there was nothing left to erode; the factories run but produce no product, convert no raw material, exhaust no waste except the ambient heat. They exist in thermodynamic equilibrium, motion without work, industry without output. A gear turning here has turned for a billion years at the same speed, neither slowing from resistance nor accelerating from momentum. If you were to mark a point on the ground with chalk, it would remain there, unaffected by weather, unblurred by time, until the heat death of the universe—or longer.

Water (0.2) is a memory bound in minerals. No rain has fallen. The atmosphere contains trace hydrogen that binds with carbon dust to form a smear of polymers and hydrocarbon mist, visible only as a faint haze when a rare light source flares, but it never condenses, never flows. The plain is sterile of vegetation (0.0) and nearly so of life, yet not entirely empty.

The fauna (0.8) is rare, strange, and slow. They are called **Residue Walkers**, though they are more mineral than animal. Towering, spindly frameworks of accumulated metallic salts—copper chloride, iron sulfate, vanadium oxide—migrate across the plain at the speed of growing crystals. They stand three to four meters tall, thin as reeds, and they move by accreting material at their base and dissolving it at their apex, "walking" toward cooler thermal signatures over centuries. They are the only things that change here, parasitic on the infinitesimal inefficiencies of the factories, and even they have grown so accustomed to stasis that when two meet, they may lean against one another for millennia before deciding to step around.

Between the scattered towers, the scale (2.4) opens into vistas that break the mind. You can see twenty, thirty kilometers in the dark, the black silhouettes of distant factories standing like gaps in reality. The plain is urban, yet emptier than any desert. There are roads, but they are dusted with the same polished ceramic as the wilderness, indistinguishable except for their geometric straightness. There are power lines, but they carry current that has flowed uninterrupted since before complex cells evolved in distant oceans, their insulators grown from diamond fiber, never degrading.

The rules of the world here are simple and absolute:

**Conservation of State.** A thing as it is, is. A door hanging open has hung open for epochs. A pool of mercury (should one exist) would hold its meniscus in perfect stillness, never oxidizing, never wetting the surface beneath it. 

**Perfect Entropy.** The warmth you feel is the only transaction permitted. Energy enters the system from below; it radiates upward into space at exactly the rate it arrives. No gradient persists long enough to drive change. 

**The Silence of Function.** The factories function, but their purpose—if they ever had one—has been sublimated into pure operation. They are monuments to the moment when the universe achieved a task and forgot to stop.

To walk here is to walk through the deep time of a world that has ceased turning in any metaphorical sense. Your footsteps leave no impression on the vitrified ground. Your breath, should you have any, would not fog the air. You are an anomaly of change in a place that has settled into its final form, warm in the dark, vast and sparse and endlessly, silently, industriously still.
