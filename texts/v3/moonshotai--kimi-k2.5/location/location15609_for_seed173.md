You descend through the thermocline into **The Cistern of Unending Pressure**, and the first thing that strikes you is the silence of the stone—if it can be called stone. 

Everything here is *made*, yet nothing is new. The chamber is small: barely sixty meters in length, a cathedral nave compressed and submerged. The walls, floor, and vaulted ceiling consist of a pale, synthetic material—perhaps a fossilized concrete or sintered ceramic—that has undergone **the Rounding**. Over millennia, the water has worked at the edges until all corners have disappeared. Every structural member flows into the next with the softness of worn river rock. Pipes that once jutted at angles now bend in gentle, elbow-like curves. Support columns have weathered into figures that might be mistaken for stalagmites if you forgot that no natural geology exists here; the substrate is entirely artifact, simply aged into resemblance of the organic.

The water is cool—maybe nine degrees Celsius—still and heavy, pressing against your suit with the relaxed grip of something that has all the time in the world. Light filters down from surface ruptures far above, or perhaps leaks from the chamber’s own failing infrastructure: dim amber panels set into the rounded ceiling, casting enough glow to silhouette the machinery but not to banish the shadows that pool in the high vaults.

And there is machinery. This is the heart of the space—**industrial**, yes, but industrial in the manner of a breathing lung or a beating heart scaled up to architecture. Three massive pistons dominate the chamber, each the width of a cottage, spaced far apart in the sparse densities of the room. They rise and fall with geological patience, *thud... thud... thud*, creating the **soundscape** that defines the place. Despite the water’s density, the noise is loud—low-frequency drones that resonate in your chest cavity, the grinding of sediment-polished gears, the hydraulic sigh of valves opening and closing in sequences older than any living memory. The water transmits these vibrations as a constant, shivering hum, a lullaby of maintenance without purpose.

Between the pistons, the water column is alive. **Fauna** is abundant here, though vegetation is nearly absent—only a few pale films of bacteria and the occasional ghost-white fern anchored to the polished junctions where pipes meet. But the animals have claimed the emptiness. Schools of eyeless silver fish move in synchronized orbits around the moving machinery, evolved to navigate the predictable paths of the pistons. Crustaceans have colonized the gear housings, building calcium cities in the slow-moving parts. translucent jellyfish pulse in the currents generated by the machinery’s action, drifting like living bubbles through the dim spaces between presences.

Because the space is **active**, not abandoned. The systems still cycle. Water is drawn in through grilled intakes low on the rounded walls, pressurized, heated minutely by the friction of ancient bearings, and expelled through ceiling vents in soft, rhythmic exhalations. These currents create microclimates—warmer eddies near the moving parts where the crustaceans cluster, cooler still zones in the corners where sediment swirls in lazy spirals, eternally stirred but never settled.

The **rules** of this world are hydrostatic and mechanical. There is no weather, only the pressure gradient between intake and outflow. There is no day or night, only the dim amber failing of the built lights and the absolute dark of the deeper recesses. Time is measured not in sun-cycles but in the wearing down of the synthetic stone—an erosion so complete that the chamber has achieved a kind of stability, an elderly equilibrium where the machinery’s motion and the water’s patience have agreed to a truce. The pistons will continue until their material submits to fatigue; the fish will continue until the lights finally die; the chamber will remain, small and complete, a sealed pocket of industrial purgatory that has forgotten its original function but continues faithfully performing it, round-edged and humming, at the bottom of a world that built it and left it to grow old underwater.
