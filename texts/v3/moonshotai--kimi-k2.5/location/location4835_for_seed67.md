The condensation never falls. It beads—on railings, on ductwork, on the carapaces of the things that crowd the walkways—fattening into silver hemispheres that tremble but never achieve the critical mass to drip. The humidity is absolute, a warm (2.4) wet (1.8) embrace that has saturated the ceramic air for as long as the oxidation counters have ticked, which is Forever, because here the Gradient is zero and the arrow of thermodynamics has been filed down to a nub.

You stand at the bottom of it all. Elevation 0.9—low, not in the sense of a valley, but of a floor. A single, continuous, continent-spanning floor laid down by something that finished its work yesterday, or perhaps is still finishing it now, though you cannot tell because the features are young (1.2) and sharp: tile edges that could slice paper, grout lines clean as laser-cuts, the outlines of serial numbers stamped into aluminum that have not yet begun to craze. No wind has scoured this place. No rain has fallen, only the eternal condensate, the breath of the machinery.

There is no ground. Only Building (3.0).

The scale (2.8) is such that weather forms under the ceiling—a vault of vapor-luminescent panels twenty kilometers overhead, glowing with normal light (2.5) that pools in the haze like milk. Thermal cells drift sideways, never rising, never storming, suspended in the dynamic stasis (0.2). The air smells of heated porcelain and ozone and the sweet, yeasty musk of the Fauna (2.6).

They are everywhere. Overwhelming does not describe the noise—they are mostly quiet (1.3), padded feet on wet ceramic, chitinous plates sliding over lubricant film—but the *presence*. The density (2.1) is architectural. Layers of them. Scavenger-crustaceans, each the size of a human torso, carpet the primary deck three bodies deep, their shells patterned in the safety-orange and caution-yellow of industrial signage, an evolution (or engineering) that camouflages them against the warning stripes painted on the floor. Above them, clinging to the undersides of pipes and conduits, hang basket-weaver arachnids the color of galvanized steel, spinning not silk but fine copper filaments to shunt static electricity. Higher still, in the thermal layers near the ceiling, drift gas-bladdered leviathans—translucent, brainless, pulsing gently as they filter the humid air for spores and metal oxides.

There are no plants (0.5). None. No lichen dares the tile. The only biology is animal, or the grey, slimy films of chemosynthetic carpets that grow in the drainage troughs, eating the condensate, being eaten, replaced, in a cycle that turns without changing. The fauna do not age. They do not breed, or if they do, the young emerge already-adult, already in their stations, clicking into the eternal choreography.

The tech (2.1) is industrial, brutally so, but silent. Magnetic bearings hum below the threshold of hearing. Pneumatic transport capsules glide through transparent tubes, carrying payloads of unidentifiable parts—gears, maybe, or femurs, or compressed data—in loops that have no beginning or end. Factories without workers, only the fauna tending them, wiping the condensation from sensor housings with their own abdominal hairs, lubricating joints with secretions that mimic 10W-40 motor oil.

You walk the aisles. The weirdness (1.6) is subtle, a wrongness in the synchronization. Every thirty meters, a scavenger-crab pauses to groom its antennae with its third right leg—a gesture that takes exactly 4.7 seconds. It has always taken 4.7 seconds. The copper-spinners above rotate clockwise, then counter-clockwise, then clockwise, a reversal that happens at noon, except there is no noon, only the unchanging glow, so they reverse because that is where they are in the Loop.

The place is a Maintenance Event stretched across a continent. It is the engine room of a world that has been paused mid-thought. The wetness is the sweat of its effort to remain still. The fauna are the antibodies of a planet-sized machine, keeping the tiles clean, the condensation beaded, the sharp edges sharp, ensuring that the Young construction never grows old, that the industrial heart beats without accelerating, without slowing, in a single, vast, humid, crowded, silent inhalation held forever.
