There is a breathing that does not exchange air, a warmth that does not flow. You stand—if standing is possible here—within the Continental Hollow, a void beneath the world’s skin vast enough to generate its own atmospheric tides, yet wrapped in darkness so complete it feels like a pressure against the eyeballs. The blackness is not empty; it is saturated. It has weight.

The terrain rises in blades. Not mountains—*spires*, *needles*, *lances* of a substance that is neither stone nor mineral but the precipitate of arrested time. They grow upward in fractal dendrites, sharp enough to slit a shadow, yet they are *young* formations, newborn in geological terms, their edges unrounded by any weathering because here, erosion has been voted out of existence. Dynamic stasis. The spires exist in a state of perpetual becoming—a crystal that is always growing and never gaining mass, a stalactite formed from vapor that sublimates and deposits in the same thermal breath. The result is a forest of glass-sharp obelisks packed so densely they nearly touch, creating labyrinthine corridors where the concept of "distance" feels negotiable. Space is compressed here, layered. You might reach for a wall and touch three walls simultaneously, your hand sensing pressure from angles that geometry cannot explain.

It is warm—uncomfortably, cloyingly warm. The heat has no source and no sink. It radiates from the crystals themselves, which trap thermal energy in a feedback loop of temporal recursion. Every moment of warmth that ever existed in this chamber still exists here, superimposed. The air is dry, desiccated to the point of dessication—there is no water, never was, the chemistry here runs on silicates and metallic vapors, on halides that dance as dry particulate clouds. The atmosphere is a saturated solution of mineral salts suspended in heavy, noble gases. When you move, you disturb strata of copper-smelling air that have rested undisturbed, theoretically, forever.

And in these strata: life.

It is abundant to the point of absurdity, a biomass that rivals rainforest canopies yet contains not a single leaf, not a tendril of green. The fauna—*Silicae*, though no taxonomist named them—are translucent, chitinous, multi-segmented things that filter-feed on the metallic vapor. They crowd every surface, clinging to the crystal spires in layers three, four, five creatures deep, their bodies glowing faintly with internal phosphors that do not illuminate so much as indicate: *I am here, I am here*. They have no eyes—the darkness made eyes obsolete eons ago. Instead, they navigate by sensing the thermal gradients of the warm void, or perhaps by reading the structural stress in the crystal lattice itself. 

They are silent. Almost. When one moves—sliding a crystalline leg from one hold to another—it produces a sound like a fingernail tapping wine glass, but this happens so rarely, and the density of bodies is so high that the cumulative effect is a sustained, subsonic resonance, a *hum* below hearing that makes your teeth ache. Mostly, they are still. The population is stable, unchanging. They do not breed here, nor do they die. They simply *are*, occupying their niches in eternal allotment, excreting fine mineral dust that becomes tomorrow’s crystal growth, which is also yesterday’s.

Scattered throughout this dense, warm, dark infinity are the signs of visitors who came with hands and intentions. A stone hammer, its haft fossilized by the temporal stasis, lies embedded in a half-struck crystal—frozen mid-swing. A circle of cairns, built from obsidian flakes, surrounds a depression where someone tried to dig for water that does not exist. Simple metal here—copper, perhaps, or soft bronze—oxidized to black velvet, shaped into hooks and awls left behind by beings who possessed early tool-using intelligence but not the metallurgy to survive long. Their shelters—hollowed geodes the size of coffins—pepper the crystal forest, empty or perhaps occupied by the Silicae who moved in when the builders stopped moving.

For here is the rule of this world, the physics that makes it *weird*: nothing leaves, and nothing changes, but everything is crowded. Time is a solid here, not a river. You are not experiencing a moment; you are inhabiting the whole duration of your presence simultaneously. The warmth is the friction of your own duration rubbing against itself. The density is temporal stacking—every creature that ever wandered in still wandering, every breath still being drawn, the scattered tools still falling through the air, perpetually landing.

It is a desert of moments, a subterranean expanse where biology learned to eat heat and geometry, where the young forever stay young, where the sound of a stone tool chipping crystal plays in infinite loop at the threshold of hearing, and where the darkness is not the absence of light but the presence of too much matter, too much life, too much time, pressed into one vast, warm, unchanging breath.
