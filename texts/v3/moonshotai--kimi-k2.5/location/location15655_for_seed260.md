The sound is not heard with the ears. It *is* the ears, the bones, the teeth—it is the compression of existence itself grinding against its own becoming. Here, at the uppermost rim of the Exsert Range, the world is still being extruded from the raw substrate of reality, and the noise of that birth throbs at a frequency that liquefies thought. Standing upon the Scar requires no earplugs; the body itself becomes a resonating chamber, every cell vibrating in sympathy with the tectonic screaming of matter learning how to exist.

This is a place of **emergent geology**. The mountains are not old. They have no memory of weather because weather has not yet learned to touch them. The stone is virginal, sharp-edged, and *moist* with a sheen that is not water—water cannot exist here, its molecular bonds refused entry by the physics of the Scar, which favors only dry mineralogic certainty and the lush, anomalous life that feeds on the scream. The terrain rises in jagged, unfinished planes, lifting toward a sky that hangs at an altitude of bruised indigo and static grey. The **elevation is extreme**, the air thin and **cool**—perhaps ten degrees above freezing—but the wind does not blow; rather, it *settles*, depositing itself in layers of heavy, oxygen-poor stillness.

**Lush vegetation** carpets the intervals between the raw, thrusting peaks, though no drop of rain has ever struck this stone. The plants are massive, fibrous, and obscene in their fecundity—turgid bulbs the size of carriages, accordion-pleated fronds that pulse with subsonic rhythm, forests of mucilaginous stalks that tower and weep a thick, amber sap in place of nectar. They do not photosynthesize; here, **light is dim**, a perpetual twilight filtered through auroral wounds in the sky where reality has not quite sealed. Instead, the flora **audiosynthesizes**. Their root-networks are tuned to the deafening drone, harvesting vibration as energy, storing the scream in cellular batteries that glow with a pale, bioluminescent blush—a scattered, sparse constellation of living lamps across the valley-wide **open scale** of the plateau.

There is **no fauna**. No insects stitch the air, no birds wheel against the broken sky. The only movement is the **slow**, imperceptible growth of the mountains themselves, rising millimeter by millimeter as the crust remembers how to be crust, and the patient, millennial unfurling of the sonic-gardens. The ecology is a closed loop of mineral and vegetable, locked in a mutualism of structure and signal. The plants break down the fresh, unweathered stone with acidic roots that excrete the sound-hum, softening the raw geology into nutrient substrate, while the stone’s continued extrusion provides new anchors for the next generation of scream-eaters.

Scattered across this **emerging** landscape are the **built** remnants—**primitive** cairns and hollow shelters of un-mortared slate, stacked by hands or hand-like appendages that belonged to no animal. Some are merely balanced stones, impossibly perched, held in place not by gravity—which here operates in *suggestions* rather than laws—but by the sonic pressure itself. They look like the first attempts of a consciousness learning to make shelter, or perhaps the involuntary twitches of the landscape trying to house itself. A few contain artifacts: flaked obsidian tools that cut not flesh but probability, bone needles meant to stitch shadows together, hollow gourds that when held to the ear do not echo the scream but instead offer a terrifying, distant silence.

The **weirdness** manifests in the way distance refuses to hold. A structure that appears as a distant dot may, after an hour’s walking, remain a distant dot, while a nearby boulder might suddenly occupy the space directly behind you. Shadows fall toward the light-source or pool upward into the dim sky like smoke. The horizon curves not downward but *upward*, suggesting the plateau exists on the interior of some cosmic sphere still under construction. Time here flows in **slow**, thick pulses; a blink might last a season, or a century might compress into the space between heartbeats (if hearts could beat here without shaking themselves apart).

It is a place of pre-creation, a nursery for a world that has not yet decided on its final form. The deafening roar is the sound of the possible crystallizing into the actual, a sustained note of cosmic parturition. And in the pauses—rare, infinitesimal gaps between the waves of emanation—there is a silence more profound than any void, a hush that suggests the universe holding its breath, waiting to see if the Scar will finish building itself, or if it will simply give up, unravel, and scream itself back into unbeing.
