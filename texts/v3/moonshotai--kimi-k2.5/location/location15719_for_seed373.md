Yet it is lush.

And in the troughs of these sonic storm-walls grows the garden.

Great spiraling ferns of glass and quartz rise from acoustic "soil"—nodes where standing waves have compressed the local physics into semi-solidity, creating raised beds of hardened resonance. Stability is death here—when a standing wave persists too long, the creatures starve.

You walk carefully. The ground beneath your feet is not stone but ossified vibration, layers of compressed acoustic weather packed down by the 1.8 erosion of eons. The scattered structures offer moments of silence—or rather, of structured harmony. When you stand within the field of the ancient tuning-pillars, the chaos outside dims to a manageable roar. Inside these zones, the air cools further, frost forming on the crystalline leaves in fractal patterns. The tech here—beyond modern, indifferent to contemporary physics—functions by refusing the turbulence. It creates pockets of denied-dynamic, spaces where the storm cannot enter, preserving seeds of quiet order against the entropy of the roar.

But you cannot stay in the shelters. The fauna knows these nodes are safe, and they crowd them—thousands of the glass-moths packed shoulder-to-shoulder, their wings chiming against each other, adding new layers to the din. The abundance is claustrophobic despite the vast emptiness beyond.

Here, the dynamic is not merely chaotic but creative—Solaris-like, birthing and destroying matter in time with the frequency shifts. And yet, it is weathered. It is settled. The garden has learned the patterns of the chaos. It knows which frequencies precede the calm, which harmonics bring the nourishing storms. There is a balance here, ancient as the pillars, arrived at through the long erosion of alternatives. The place is dry, cold, small, empty, and impossibly loud—but it is home to a lushness that has replaced aqueous chemistry with sonic metabolism, a verdant impossible reef thriving in the deep pressure of an endless, deafening shout.
