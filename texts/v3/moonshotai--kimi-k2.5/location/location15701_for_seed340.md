It hits you first in the teeth. Then the sternum. The sound is not merely loud—it is *occupational*, a physical matter that floods the ear canals and presses against the temples with the weight of wet felt. You have descended through a throat of obsidian teeth, down past the water table (extinct here, absent, a memory of chemistry), and into the **Resonance Pit**: a geode-cavern vast enough to swallow towns, lit not by sun but by the steady amber glow of cooling silicate veins in the ceiling five hundred meters above. The light is normal, even comfortable— ripe as late afternoon—but the air itself vibrates with a deafening roar.

This is the sound of emergence. The geology here is embryonic, raw, wet with mineral birth. The walls are not smooth from eons of water-caress but jagged as broken glass, spalling fresh basalt and quartz that has existed for mere centuries. It has not learned patience. And growing directly from this nascent stone, without soil, without rain, is a jungle so total it *is* the terrain.

**The Litho-Verdancy.** Without water (0.1/3) in the external environment, the vegetation has evolved into something else entirely: silicon-cellulose organisms, translucent and chiming, that metabolize heat and dissolved mineral vapor. They are pure plant-substrate, filling every cubic meter with layers of dense, near-touching life. Trunks of crystallized fiber— amber, rose-quartz pink, dirty opal—crowd upward in a four-tiered canopy. Their growth is the turbulence. They do not grow by millimeters per year but by centimeters per hour, a hyperactive storm-state of expansion. The friction of cellulose-strands rubbing against their neighbors produces the deafening drone—a subsonic bellow overlaid with the high, glassy shriek of new rings splitting. The air is warm (2.2/3), humid with the sweat of mineral precipitation, and laden with the scent of ozone and crushed nutmeg.

You move through a space that is architecturally unstable. A path that existed yesterday is now a strangled corridor, crushed between two leaning trunks whose collision sounds like a glacier calving. The floor is a treacherous mat of fallen crystal-needles, sharp enough to pierce boot-rubber, yet already half-buried by the upward rush of root-mats that pour like slow lava over the raw geology. The erosion here is pre-formation; the landscape is still deciding what it wants to be, and the plants are forcing the decision through sheer biomass.

**The Overwhelming.** The fauna (2.7/3) does not visit this place; it *infests* it. The density of animal life is suffocating. Your immediate field of vision contains thousands of presences. Thumb-sized beetles with wings of mica clatter in clouds so thick they darken the amber light, their mandibles clicking a secondary rhythm to the vegetative roar. Thermal-slugs—glistening orange tubes—trace the veins of warm stone, pursued by swarms of chitinous primates that look like bundles of wicker come alive, their shrieks barely audible over the ambient thunder. Everything is predator or pollinator or parasite. Crystal-ferns snap shut on insects with audible *thwacks*. Things that resemble tapeworms, but armored in keratin, drop from the mid-canopy to bore into the trunks, their drilling vibrations adding a dental whine to the cacophony. You are not walking through a forest; you are wading through a digestive system that has not yet learned to distinguish between mineral and meat.

Yet there are **scattered marks of intention** (built 1.0/3). High in the third canopy, lashed between a singing-palm and a geode-banyan, platforms sway. They are woven from cured fiber and bone, lashed with gut and stone-pegged. Primitive technology (0.7/3) only: hand-axes of sharp volcanic glass hanging in nets, containers made from the hollowed water-cores of fallen trees (the only accessible water in this desert, hoarded in biological canteens). The builders are not here now, or they hide. Perhaps they are the chittering things in the shadows, or perhaps they have evolved wax-plugged ears and sonar-screams to navigate this screaming world. The structures are temporary, always half-crushed by the encroaching growth, ephemeral as mayfly wings against the biological avalanche.

**The Weirdness** (2.5/3) is in the physics of sound. The chamber is an instrument. The ceaseless growth creates standing waves—nodes of pressure where the air is thick enough to lean on, antinodes where it thins to breathlessness. Sound becomes tactical. You can see it in the way dust motes arrange themselves into helical sculptures, spiraling in acoustic vortices. In the worst places, the resonance triggers micro-seisms; the raw stone shivers and sheds fresh flakes, adding to the storm of mineral noise. Time moves strangely here. A day on the surface might be an hour here, or a year; the hyperactivity of the garden makes clocks meaningless.

This is a place of **turbulent creation**, a desert that has replaced water with growth, a cave that roars like a waterfall made of splintering glass. To stand still is to risk being grown over, your boots mossed with crystal-lichen, your shoulders brushed by expanding fronds that ring like tuning forks. It is warm, deafening, and absolutely claustrophobic despite its scale—a living, screaming mass of pre-formation, where the only constant is the unbearable noise of things becoming.
