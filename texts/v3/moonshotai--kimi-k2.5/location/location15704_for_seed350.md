You descend through a layer of what appears to be cloud but is actually a froth—warm, protein-thick, and vibrating. The pressure clamps around you not like the ocean’s indifferent crush, but like a hand closing, intentional and fever-hot. You are entering the Sump; not a sea, though it is submerged, and not a factory, though the industrial scream never ceases.

The fluid here is the color of old bruises, translucent enough to see meters ahead but dense enough to slow your limbs, honey-thick and heated to blood-temperature. It is water in the way that molten glass is sand—transformed, laden with suspended particulates of rust, lignin, and something that glitters like mica but moves against the current. When you breathe here (if you breathe), the air tastes of copper and pollen and burning insulation.

Sound hits first, before sight. A deafening, bone-conducted roar that obliterates thought. It is the accumulated drone of ten thousand piston arrays, hammer-presses, and grinding mills, all operating underwater, their vibrations traveling through the fluid as solid waves. You feel the noise in your teeth, in the marrow of your femurs. It is the sound of industry without workers, of machinery that has forgotten its purpose but not its motion, chewing through raw material that arrives from nowhere, extruding products that dissolve back into the slurry before they can be cataloged.

Visibility is dim. Bloated, spectral shapes loom—industrial scaffolding overgrown with vegetation so lush it has become architectural. Great tube-worms of woven kelp, purple and phosphorescent, wrap around distillation towers that phase in and out of existence, their rivets popping into being with wet clicks, rusting in seconds, then smoothing back to new steel as reality stutters. This is young terrain; the erosion is minimal because the formations have not existed long enough to weather. Basaltic pillars jut from the sediment, sharp-edged and glassy, cooling from a molten state that occurred mere hours ago, already colonized by barnacle-like fungal mats that swell and burst in accelerated time-lapse cycles.

The ecology is exclusively vegetable, or vegetable-adjacent. There are no animals. No fish streak through the murk, no crustaceans pick at the debris. Instead, the vegetation has evolved to fill every niche that motion might occupy. Enormous lily pads the size of tennis courts drift on convection currents, their undersides hung with dripping, photosynthetic chains that function as predatory filters, trapping the organic flotsam of this drowned world. Bamboo-analogues creak as they grow, shooting upward toward the froth-layer at visible speeds, their hollow stems resonating with the industrial hum, turning the deafening noise into complex harmonics that make the water shiver. The density is suffocating—every cubic meter is crowded with biomass, machinery, and mineral precipitate, layered like a compressed sedimentary record of a civilization that cannot stop building.

The built environment is scattered but persistent. A single smokestack rises here, belching bubbles of inert gas that shimmer with oil-slick rainbows before collapsing. The turbulence is constant. The water—or whatever it is—does not flow in currents but in convulsions. Thermal vents open and seal like gasping mouths, spewing superheated plumes that send the lily pads spinning. The reality breakdown manifests as local storms of impossible causality: a section of forest might invert, roots drinking from the sky while leaves dig into silt; a grinding machine may briefly become a calcified fossil, then a sapling, then a machine again, stuttering through existential states while never ceasing its deafening grind. Gravity is a suggestion; debris drifts upward, sideways, or hangs suspended in spirals, obeying the whims of localized spatial distortions.

You move through a valley (elevation is low here, the whole place a depression in the world’s crust) where the industrial hum reaches a crescendo. A vast, half-completed engine the size of a cathedral shudders in the center of a clearing, its gears meshing with the fibrous trunks of trees that have grown through its mechanism, wood and iron fused into a composite material that weeps amber sap and machine oil. The engine is manufacturing terrain; fresh rock extrudes from its output chute, steaming and sharp, already being seeded by spores that drift like snow in the warm, thick dark.

There is no silence, only the roar. No stillness, only the churn of matter reorganizing itself. No predators, only the slow, relentless pressure of growth and fabrication. The Sump is a nursery and a graveyard, warm as a fever dream, drowning in its own profusion, endlessly birthing itself in the deafening dark.
