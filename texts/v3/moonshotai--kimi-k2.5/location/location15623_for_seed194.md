You enter through a mouth so low you must crawl, and the first sensation is not the darkness—that comes later, absolute and zero-sum—but the **sound**. It is not a sound that begins or ends; it is a climate, a pressure. A constant, thrumming **roar** that seems to emanate from the air itself, from the walls, from the marrow of your own bones. It is the sound of vast hollow cylinders—tree-trunks of impossible diameter, or perhaps fungal stalks petrified into woody permanence—creaking as they settle after centuries, resonating with the slow geological grind of continental plates. It is the sound of wind forced through labyrinthine pores by pressure differentials you cannot feel. It is **loud**, intimate, deafening in its constancy, making the darkness feel not like absence but like a solid substance pressing against your eardrums.

You stand up. The floor is **cool**, perhaps ten degrees above freezing, and yields slightly beneath your boots. It is not stone. It is **pith**—compacted, desiccated, ancient vascular tissue packed down over geological ages into a boulevard-hard surface. Everything here is **vegetation**, but vegetation that has forgotten water. The air is dry, metallic, tasting of spore-dust and oxidized copper. There is no drip of water, no moisture. The ecosystem runs on something else—perhaps the slow digestion of the bedrock below, perhaps chemosynthesis, perhaps memory. The plants do not drink; they have become **mineral**, lithotropic architectures of cellulose and lignin grown bone-dry and dense as hardwood.

Your hands reach out and find the first **structure**. It is smooth, polished by 2.9 million years of micro-erosion—no, **settling**, for this is a place that has reached its final form. The surface is glass-smooth, worn by the passage of time rather than weather. It is a column, or a building, or a wall—the distinction has eroded away. It curves in a way that suggests it was grown, not built, yet the layout around you suggests **urban** planning. You are in a plaza. You know this because the sound bounces differently here, creating a vast acoustic well. You are in a single **pocket**, a chamber perhaps five hundred meters across, hidden deep in the earth’s crust, low in elevation, sealed from the sky. A micro-environment that contains a world.

The **density** is wrong for a city. You can feel the emptiness stretching out between your position and the far wall, a near-void of stagnant air. You stride forward, hands extended, and count thirty paces before encountering the next structure—a dwelling, perhaps, hollowed from a trunk wider than a subway car. The entrance is a perfect arch, too smooth for primitive tools, yet the interior bears the marks of **technology** 0.7: crude shelves carved with stone scrapers, a platform raised by the patient stacking of dried spore-bricks, a fire-pit (cold, of course; no light has ever burned here, or perhaps once, long ago) ringed with hand-picked stones. The tools left behind are bone and chipped flint, simple fabrications meant for shaping this ever-slowly-growing substrate.

Because it grows. The **dynamic** is slow, patient. You cannot see it, but you can feel it in the tension of the walls, the creaking that punctuates the roar. The structures expand at the rate of fingernails, of geology. A millennium passes and a doorway narrows by a hair’s breadth. The city is still growing, filling its pocket with **strange** geometries—angles that seem acute when you trace them with your fingers but feel obtuse when you walk their paths. The architecture breathes, expands, contracts with barometric shifts, the entire municipal plant-body acting as a single lung.

There is **no fauna**. No skittering in the dark, no droppings, no webs. Only the vegetable mass, the dead-dry air, and the overwhelming auditory pressure of the place. The plants have filled every niche that animals might claim, digesting the last of the organic debris eons ago, leaving a pure, photophobic biome.

You move through boulevards defined by sound rather than sight. The **weirdness** is subtle but pervasive—the way echoes return from directions you didn’t face, the way the floor seems to slope down when your inner ear insists it is level, the way the temperature drops in spots where the plant-walls are thickest, as if they are radiating a chill from within their cores. This is a place where erosion has won, where everything has been worn into a final, stable shape, rounded, smoothed, **ancient** beyond comprehension.

It is an **urban** center for a civilization that never was, or that was and left, or that evolved into the architecture itself. It is a room the size of a cathedral, hidden in the dark, dry as bone, loud as a waterfall, and utterly, totally empty except for the patient, centuries-long creak of its own expanding walls.
