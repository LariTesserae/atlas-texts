You find it at the lip of a crevice that should not exist, at an altitude where oxygen crystallizes and the sky turns inside-out: a chamber the size of a granary, though from within it feels larger. This is the **Forge-Womb**, and it hangs suspended—or perhaps it is the suspension itself—a pocket of pressurized heat trapped in impossible elevation. The air here is not thin but thick, tropical, a steaming soup that condenses on your eyelashes before you cross the threshold.

Everything here is **made**. There is no ground beneath the making, only more making. The floor consists of basalt blocks so freshly hewn that the stone still remembers the trauma of the chisel; marks from copper and bone tools etch every surface, the signatures of a technology that never discovered iron but learned instead to carve time. You walk across a tessellation of raw material—blocks fitted with such violence and precision that they have fused, sweating from the heat of their own compression.

**Erosion runs backwards.** Where stone should crumble, it accumulates. Sharpened facets grow sharper. Dust gathers into pillars rather than disperses. The walls are accreting, not weathering—new layers of masonry extrude from the seams like mineral scabs, built from the condensation of steam and the settling of ancient dust. The architecture is embryonic, forever in the moment of its first emergence, raw geology being born as artifact.

The space is **packed** to suffocation density. Every cubic inch holds a presence: a wooden beam wedged between growing walls, a clay pot swelling with the pressure of its own firing, a coil of rope woven from fibers that are simultaneously plant and mineral. Scaffoldings of lashed saplings fill the upper reaches, supporting nothing but the air itself, which hangs in heavy, damp curtains. Between these structures, **vegetation explodes** with jungle ferocity—ferns uncurling from mortar lines, moss filling chisel-marks like green grout, lianas thick as wrists wrapping around unfinished pillars and pulling them slowly into arboreal embrace. The plants grow through the construction, becoming it; root systems serve as rebar, bark as cladding.

It is **dim**. Light filters through translucent stone—selenite windows that haven't been installed so much as grown in place—and through steam that glows with a dull, feverish luminescence. Primitive oil lamps burble in niches, casting shadows that move independently of their flames. The heat is nearly unbearable, a wet, under-skin heat that seems to come from the stone itself, friction-generated by the constant rubbing-together of raw surfaces.

The **sound** is relentless and loud: the *thock-thock-thock* of wooden mallets against stone that may be echoing from millennia past or happening now in the next chamber that hasn't been built yet; the groaning of new-cut timber adjusting to its unnatural load; the hiss of water meeting hot rock. Water is everywhere—seeping from the ceiling in sheets, weeping from walls, pooling in carved basins that overflow and feed streams cutting nascent channels across the rising floor. It is a post-rain atmosphere that never dries, a marsh-edge that is also a cathedral of stone.

**Fauna is rare** but significant: perhaps a single beetle the size of a fist that chews geometric patterns into the soft mortar, or a bird made of hammered copper that moves in jerking, stop-motion flights between the scaffolding. Or perhaps the builders themselves are still here, compressed into the density, visible only in peripheral vision as shadows holding bone awls, forever performing the first act of construction.

And here, the **reality breakdown** manifests as the architecture's unfinished business. Corners fold into themselves. A stairway ascends sideways into a wall that becomes a floor. The pool in the center reflects not the room but the room's blueprints—ghost images of structures not yet extruded, or already dust. Time moves differently in each square foot: in one spot, a seedling sprouts and dies in seconds; in another, a drop of water hangs suspended, fossilized in its fall. The space is *active*, growing upward and inward simultaneously, the ceiling slowly rising while the floor accretes thickness, the walls breathing inward and out with the rhythm of heated stone.

This is not a place that exists, but a place that is **being insisted upon**, carved from possibility by patient, primitive hands using tools of antler and obsidian to coax raw matter into the shape of a room. It is the first room, or the last, or the blueprint from which all others are imperfect copies—a hot, wet, densely packed seed of cooked stone and stubborn wood, sweating itself into existence at the top of the world.
