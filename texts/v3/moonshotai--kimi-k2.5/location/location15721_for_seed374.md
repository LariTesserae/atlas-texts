The roar comes first, before the heat, before the vision of it—a constant, bone-conducting thrum that obliterates the distinction between hearing and touch. It is the sound of water perpetually giving birth to stone. You feel it in your molars, in the hollow of your chest, a white-noise static that has not varied in pitch or volume for ten thousand years. Here, in this low-lying caldera, the world is trapped in the inhospitable, humid breath of its own morning.

You descend through a veil of steam. The light is dim, a perpetual pearl-grey twilight filtered through aerosolized mineral water. The sun—or whatever star burns above—is only a brighter smear in the mist, its photons scattered by the dense, warm atmosphere that presses against your skin like a damp cloth. The temperature hovers in the fever-range, not baking but stewing, a wet warmth that condenses on every surface and drips in a slow, geological rhythm.

There are no plants. No green. The color palette is mineral: bone-white, ochre, rust-red, the blue-grey of fresh silica. What passes for soil is a thin, crumbly skin of precipitated salts over sediment that is still, technically, liquid. The ground is *emerging*. It is a raw geology, not yet born enough to erode. Every step sinks slightly into travertine that is hardening even as you lift your foot, calcite crystals knitting together in real-time. Pools of supersaturated water weep from terraces, depositing microscopic layers that accumulate into architecture faster than any coral reef, yet slower than human speech.

And it is a city. A regional sprawl, valley-wide, extending beyond the steam-curtained horizon. The density is overwhelming. Structures press shoulder-to-shoulder, stacked in irregular, organic layers that recall termite mounds more than planned streets, yet there is an undeniable urban logic to the channels, the catchment basins, the archways that direct thermal updrafts. It is infrastructure made of sinter and tufa, a settlement-scale accretion of calcium carbonate and silica deposited by boiling springs that have never cooled and never will.

The builders are everywhere. The fauna. *Abundant* does not suffice—they are the medium through which the place exists. Calciphorids, you might call them: crab-sized, chitinous, pale as the stone they work. They move in tides, millions of legs clicking against the mineral crust, a sound that underlies the thunder of the vents like persistent rainfall. They possess technology, after a fashion—primitive, pre-metal, entirely manual. You see them wielding flakes of black obsidian, scrapers carved from the ribs of larger thermophilic grazers, bone levers. They tend the bacterial mats that precipitate the stone, herding them into geometric patterns, harvesting the soft, wet sediment before it sets, carving it with their simple tools into habitable cells, channels for hot water, storage cisterns that fill and overflow in endless, static cycles.

They do not sleep. The colony does not rest. It is a frozen equilibrium, a dynamic of zero change. The city is always half-built, always under construction, yet never progressing toward completion, never decaying. A terrace wall grows a millimeter a day; a channel silts with fresh deposit at the same rate it is dredged by workers. It is the morning of the world, held in amber. Yesterday’s archway is buried by today’s precipitation, and tomorrow’s workers will quarry it out again, recycling the same atoms in a closed loop of emergence. There is no erosion because time here is not a river but a held breath.

You walk through canyons of weeping rock, where water seeps from every pore, where the walls are damp and warm to the touch, sweating magnesium and sulfur. The air tastes of metal and electricity. The density of presence is claustrophobic despite the regional scale—every cubic meter holds a dozen calciphorids, a thousand smaller symbionts, the water itself thick with mineral plankton. It is an urban fabric, saturated, layered, every surface occupied, every niche exploited.

There is no sky here, only the lowering steam. No weather changes. No seasons. The springs bellow their constant note. The creatures scrape and build and harvest with stone tools in hands that have not evolved in eons, because here, perfection was reached in the moment of becoming, and to finish would be to end. So it remains: warm, wet, deafening, dim, and forever rising from the raw geology of itself, a city that is also a womb, never born, never dying, only making and unmaking in the mineral dark.
