The heat arrives first—not as a dry bake, but as a saturated, pressurized embrace, the air so laden with dissolved minerals that it feels like breathing through a warm, wet cloth. You have entered a space no larger than a confessional booth, yet the ceiling—if it can be called that—recedes into a violet haze several thousand meters above, a distance that contradicts the outer dimensions of the obsidian nodule that contains it. This is the contradiction that defines the place: a young volcanic glass bubble, freshly exhaled from a fissure not three days ago by the chronology of the outer world, sharp-edged and unweathered, yet within it, a verticality so extreme it seems to bore through the floor of reality itself.

The surfaces weep. Superheated water percolates through microscopic capillaries in the fresh obsidian, condensing against the slightly cooler upper strata and falling—or rather, collecting—in beads that defy gravity’s orientation. Here, gravity is a local negotiation. Walk three paces toward the eastern wall and your inner ear spins; the floor becomes a wall, the wall a floor, and the water droplets drift sideways, pooling in corners that exist in more than three dimensions. The erosion is nil, the features knife-fresh. Jagged shelves of volcanic glass protrude like half-formed thoughts, their edges still carrying the sting of their recent birth, unsoftened by time or wind.

There is no green here, no chlorophyll, no fibrous cellulose. The only biology is ancient and chemical: thin films of chemosynthetic extremophiles painting the lower strata in iridescent oil-slick sheens, feeding on sulfur compounds bleeding from the cracks. They are not plants; they are instructions written in protein, simpler than moss, older than leaves.

But the fauna—*the fauna*.

They are overwhelming. They fill the space shoulder-to-shoulder, or what passes for shoulders among creatures of silicon-carbide chitin and piezoelectric sinew. They are the size of thumbs, glass-crickets or crystal-rats or some taxonomy that has no Latin name, their bodies translucent enough that you can see the shadows of their neural packets flickering like Morse code through their abdomens. They blanket every surface in a living crust three layers deep, clinging to the vertical, the inverted, the impossible. They move constantly, a silent churn. Despite their numbers—thousands in this closet-sized infinity—their motion produces no chorus, no scrape of chitin, no click of mandible. The sound parameter here is nearly zero, a hush so profound it rings in the teeth. Perhaps the air is too thin, too mineral-charged to carry vibration; perhaps the reality-break that governs the space absorbs acoustic energy to feed its own impossible geometry. Whatever the reason, the ecosystem is a mime show, a silent film played at frantic speed.

They are active, these creatures, caught in eternal predator-prey cycles that last seconds. They hunt,they flee, they mate in brief spasms. And they use tools.

You will see them pause, select a flake of obsidian that has spalled from the chamber wall—a fresh, sharp piece no larger than a fingernail—and wield it. They use these shards to lever open the heat-sealed seams of mineral deposits, to duel over territory, to scrape the bacterial films from the glass. The tools are primitive: pre-metal, unworked, simply chosen for their edge and heft. But the creatures build nothing. No nests, no dams, no middens. They are nomads in a space too small for nomadism, carrying their weapons in their claws or mouths, leaving the architecture of the chamber utterly untouched, virgin, *built 0.0*. A creature will use a glass shard for a minute, discard it, and the shard will join the raining condensation in its slow, sideways drift toward the spatial fold that serves as the chamber’s floor-ceiling-wall.

The density is claustrophobic. The air itself seems crowded. You must turn sideways to navigate between the swarms, though navigation is complicated by the shifting gravity. The creatures do not acknowledge your presence; they are too busy existing in their overwhelming numbers, too packed-together to afford notice to the macroscopic anomaly stumbling through their folded world.

Light here is normal, diffused by the steam into a soft, sourceless glow that emanates from the obsidian walls themselves, as if the stone remembers the fire of its making and releases it slowly. Shadows fall at wrong angles, splitting into double images where the reality-break shears the photons.

And the dynamic processes—oh, they are active. The weeping water cycles every hour: condensing, beading, flowing upward through a loophole in physics, evaporating against a hot strata, raining down again as a fine, scalding mist. The obsidian cracks with the temperature differentials, *pings* of fresh fracture adding rare punctuation to the silence. The fauna swarms, molts, dies, is consumed by their own kind in a closed loop of mineral metabolism. Things happen here, violently, constantly, silently, in a space the size of a cabinet that contains the verticality of a mountain range, hot as a fever, wet as a lung, sharp as a new tooth, and absolutely, defiantly, naturally made—no hand has touched this, no mind designed it, yet tools flash in the half-light, primitive, ephemeral, immediate, as the inhabitants wage their quiet, endless war in the weeping dark.
