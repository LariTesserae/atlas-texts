The **Rictus Plateau** sits at the roof of the world, where the atmosphere thins to a violet-tinged knife-edge and the cold bites with a dry, mineral tooth. It is a place that erosion has worked upon for ages beyond counting—not with the violent carving of rivers or glaciers, but with the patient, polishing care of microscopic wind-borne crystals acting over epochs. Every stone here has been worn to the smoothness of a worry bead. Mountains have been ground down to vast, undulating lumps; cliffs have been softened into ramps; boulders sit like eggs in a nest of their own dust. The elevation is extreme, yet the terrain offers no purchase for the dramatic—only the ancient, the settled, the final-form. This is geography that has achieved its last possible configuration and rests, exhausted, in permanent equilibrium.

There is no water. The plateau is a desert of anhydrous minerals, salt-encrusted pans that never knew moisture, dust composed of ground feldspar and dried clay that has never felt rain. What sparse vegetation exists takes the form of grey, coin-shaped lichens that draw their chemistry from stone alone, clustering in the leeward dimples of rocks like barnacles on a petrified sea.

But the plateau is not empty. It is **dense**.

The density is not of matter, but of *presence*. The air—still, gelid, motionless—is crowded with fauna. They exist in their millions, packed so tightly that the plateau resembles a three-dimensional tapestry of suspended life. These are the **Caught**, a migratory herd of massive, wool-pelted ungulates with spiral horns and four-toed feet, interspersed with their predators—lean, winged creatures with membrane sails and empty, open jaws. They fill the valley from floor to ceiling. They are frozen. Not dead, not petrified, but arrested, locked in the precise instant of a stampede that occurred millennia ago.

Time does not pass here.

The dynamic register of this world reads as absolute zero. There are no gradients, no wind, no shifting of light and shadow despite the normal sunlight that bathes the scene. The sun tracks across the sky, yes, but the shadows cast by the frozen beasts remain fixed, pointing in impossible directions, accumulating in layers likephotographic exposures. A creature caught mid-leap floats three meters above the ground, its shoulder muscles bunched, dust motes suspended beneath its hooves that have not fallen in ten thousand years. Another is captured in the act of breathing, its nostrils flared, the exhalation visible as a motionless cloud of crystallized vapor, solid as glass. They are packed shoulder to shoulder, nose to tail, some occupying the same spatial coordinates in quantum superposition—you can see through them, or they through you, depending on how you tilt your head.

**Sound is absent.** The overwhelming fauna make no noise. There is no breathing, no heartbeat, no call. If you strike a stone against another, the impact produces a dull *thud* that dies immediately in your palm, as if the air refuses to carry vibration. You can feel your own heartbeat in your temples, loud as thunder in the absolute hush, but that sound stops at your skin. It is a sanctuary of silence so profound that visitors have reported forgetting language after three days, their vocal cords atrophying from disuse, their thoughts becoming visual rather than auditory.

The **weirdness** of the place manifests in the rules of its suspended animation. The plateau exists in a state of temporal stasis, a bubble of *never-changing* that defies causality. If you climb over the back of a frozen beast—carefully, lest you touch its hide and risk the contagion of stasis—you will find that your footprints behind you have already vanished, the dust settling back into its pre-disturbed configuration the moment you lift your boot. If you carve your initials into the salt-crust with a stone knife, the grooves will smooth themselves shut within the hour, the mineral flowing like slow liquid back to its original flatness.

This explains the **built** environment: scattered,primitive, faint. Here and there, circles of white river stones mark where nomads once camped, the stones themselves worn featureless by the plateau’s anti-change field. Bone tools lie on exposed rock shelves—scrapers made from shoulder blades, awls of sharpened femur—left as offerings or dropped by accident. They remain pristine, un-weathered, the marrow inside still red and wet as the day they were discarded. Small structures exist: cairns three stones high, windbreaks of tanned hide stretched over frames of rib-bone, a single half-built wall of dry-laid slate where someone attempted to construct a shelter and gave up when the stones refused to stay stacked, sliding back to the ground in silent, gravity-defying slowness even as they were placed.

**Technology fails here.** Metal tools become brittle and crumble to oxide powder within hours. Complex mechanisms—gears, springs, circuits—suffer immediate reality-sickness; screws unthread themselves, wires become tangled in impossible knots, batteries drain into solid lumps of chemical salt. Only the simplest hand-tools function: a knapped flint blade, a wooden lever, a bone needle. Even these are resisted by the environment. Fire will not catch; water will not boil; food does not rot, but neither does it nourish—it sits in the stomach like a stone, unchanging, unprocessed.

The **ecology** of the plateau is thus one of suspended predation and eternal potential. The Caught beasts do not decay. Their predators do not starve. The parasites in their blood remain mid-swim in arterial currents that have ceased to flow. It is a museum of the biological moment, densely packed, layer upon layer in the air, creating a maze of frozen limbs and open mouths through which a traveler must navigate. Some have tried to butcher the Caught for food, only to find the meat un-chewable, existing in a quantum state between raw and cooked, liquid and solid, nourishing and void.

To walk the Rictus Plateau is to walk through the moment after a thunderclap, before the echo, stretched into eternity. It is cool, dry, and silent, filled with the overwhelming presence of creatures who will never complete their leap, never land, never die. The shadows point in wrong directions. The light is normal, but it reveals a world that has finished its story and refuses to begin a new chapter. It is a place where change itself has eroded away, leaving only the dense, packed, silent weight of a single, endless instant.
