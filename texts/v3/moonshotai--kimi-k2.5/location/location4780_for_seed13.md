The Stillstep Basin lies at such altitude that the air carries the metallic taste of unweathered stone. The mountains here are newborn—sharp-edged, arrogant geometries of quartz and basalt that have not yet learned the humility of erosion. They rise in fresh fractures, their ridges keen enough to slice fog, their scree slopes still clattering with the memory of recent uplift. It is cool here, always, the kind of damp coolness that exists when water cannot decide whether to be liquid or air. Moisture beads on every surface—stones glitter with permanent condensation, and the atmosphere hangs heavy with suspended droplets that never coalesce into rain, never evaporate into dryness. They simply hang, a mist of arrested diamonds, diffusing the sunlight into a perpetual silver-afternoon glow that neither fades nor intensifies.

This is a city, though not in the sense of crowding. The *built* parameter here is one of organization rather than compression. Spread across the open basin—a regional sprawl the size of a valley—structures rise with deliberate intent, yet leave vast breathing spaces between them. It is urban planning at its most respiratory. The architecture belongs to an early hand-tooled world: walls of unmortared shale stacked with copper-wedge precision, timber frames of a wood that never seasons, rope bridges of braided alpine grass spun so tightly they have become stone-rigid in their stasis. The technology is manual, patient, ant-like in its accumulation. You can see the tool-marks everywhere—chatter from basalt celts, the wandering scribe of antler picks, holes drilled by bow-driven bits. They built high and layered, terraces stepping up the fresh-carved cliffs in seven distinct strata, yet the population density remains low. Empty rooms outnumber inhabited ones; plazas of crushed greenstone stretch wide and bare between residential clusters. It is a city designed for ghosts, or for a仪式感 that required acreage.

Vegetation erupts in lush, specific violence. Not as forest—there is too much sharp geology, too much exposed fresh rock for rooted dominance—but as punctuation. Gardens hang forever in the act of blooming: ferns caught at the exact spiral of unfurling, berry bushes heavy with fruit that will never rot nor be eaten, mosses forming emerald cataracts down the northern faces of buildings. The green is damp, viridian, almost aggressive in its health, yet it does not spread. Each patch is an island of verdant inertia, separated by stretches of bare, wet stone where nothing has colonized, where the young rock waits in its sterile newness.

And everywhere, fauna. Abundant, diverse, utterly silent. A hawk suspended thirty feet above the central plaza, wings at full extension, talons open for a strike that will never land. Herds of mountain sheep caught mid-leap across ravines, their shadows painted permanently on the schist below. Insects in such profusion that the air appears grainy—bees hovering over unkillable blossoms, butterflies pinned in flight by the viscosity of stopped time. It is a predator’s paradise frozen into a diorama: a snow leopard arrested in the pounce, its victim (a fat marmot) forever completing its scream, neither winning nor losing. The quiet is profound—not the absence of sound, but its preservation. Sound does not travel here; it is trapped at its source. A dropped stone makes no impact noise; a shout dies in the throat, absorbed by the heavy, motionless air.

For this place is *dynamic: zero*. It exists in a state of perfect equilibrium, time-invariant, a photograph exposed for eternity. The erosion that should have softened these young mountains has been denied. The copper tools on the ground have not oxidized; the leather waterskins remain supple; the unlit fires in the hearths hold their fuel in eternal potential. It is a clean stasis, sanitary, devoid of decay. Dead things do not decompose—they simply remain, as perfectly preserved as living things.

The rules are simple and absolute:  
*Nothing finishes.* A bowl of meal sits on a table, eternally three-quarters eaten. A woman (if she is there, if she is seen) is caught in the act of drawing water, the rope in her hands taut but unmoving, the water in the bucket a solid cylinder that will never spill.  
*Nothing begins.* Seeds arrested in germination show their first white rootlets but advance no further. Pregnancies are held at their term, neither resolving nor regressing.  
*Energy is preserved but static.* The sun does not cross the sky; it hangs at that specific afternoon angle where light is honest and shadows are long, casting the same shadows that were cast when the stasis fell. The temperature never shifts from this cool, damp constant.  

There is a weirdness to it, a slight wrongness that prevents it from being merely a beautiful tomb. Small cyclic loops exist within the greater freeze. In the eastern quarter, a single water droplet falls from a moss-tuft every hour, strikes a copper plate, and rewinds upward to fall again—a discrete, ticking metronome in the silence. A specific bird in the central square flaps its wings backward every seventeen seconds, returning to the same position, creating a blur of motion that is the only movement in the valley. Some shadows fall at angles that contradict the fixed sun, as if the light here remembers directions it once came from. And occasionally, very occasionally, the condensation on the rocks creeps—slowly, imperceptibly, a millimeter per decade—suggesting that the stasis is not absolute but is rather a resistance, a friction against time that generates a strange, geological heat.

To walk here is to walk through a lung. The scale is open enough that horizons exist, yet the urban layering provides vertical complexity. You can wander for days through the stone thoroughfares, past granaries full of eternal grain, past terraces where crops wait eternally for harvest, past the abundant frozen wildlife that seems to watch with painted eyes. The materials—raw copper, fresh basalt, unrotting rope, dew-drenched moss—speak of a world caught in its vigorous youth, a civilization and a landscape preserved at the moment of their first flourishing, sharp-edged, damp, lush, and forever.
