**Preparation Protocol for the Dry Meridian**

You are going to visit a place that should be dead but is instead *fervently alive*. You will walk through a garden that has never known dew, in a city built by hands that may not have had fingers, beneath a sky of stone that glows like a dying hearth. The Dry Meridian is not hostile—it is simply *absolute* in its nature. It does not want to kill you; it wants to make you dry. Your preparation must begin with the understanding that here, water is not scarce—it is *absent*, chemically and conceptually abolished. You are bringing a wet body into a universe that has resolved never to drink.

---

### **I. The Body: Drought-Proofing the Organism**

Begin hydration **seventy-two hours prior**. This is not standard desert protocol; this is submersion therapy. Drink until your cells distend, then drink more. You are filling a reservoir that will have no access to the outside world once you pass the fissure.

**Skin Sealing:** The atmosphere will leather you instantly. Apply medical-grade barrier creams—lanolin-based, occlusive, hydrophobic—to every centimeter of exposed skin. Consider full-body sous-vapeurs (sealed moisture suits) used by deep-crust miners. You are not trying to breathe through your skin here; you are trying to prevent the air from *mining* you. Nail beds, nostrils, ear canals—these are evacuation points for moisture. Seal them with petroleum wax or silicone plugs.

**Pulmonary Prep:** The air is thick with pressure (deep bedrock compression) and anhydrous mineral dust. Your lungs will try to humidify each breath and fail, causing micro-trauma. Train with SCUBA regulators set to dry-gas mixtures, or use industrial respirators with moisture-retention cartridges. Do not rely on simple facemasks; the dust here is crystalline, microscopic, and will glint in your alveoli like the thorns of the lithophytes themselves.

**Pressure Equalization:** The descent creates a barometric sinkhole. Learn the Frenzel maneuver or carry auto-inflating ear valves. The "thick" air presses against the eardrums with the dull insistence of depth; without equalization, you will experience vertigo in the amber gloom, and vertigo here is fatal.

---

### **II. Footwear and Thermal Protection**

The floor is a **2.8 intensity bake**—hot enough to raise blisters through standard boot soles in minutes. You are not walking on soil; you are walking on a heat-radiant substrate that may still echo with distant magma or radioactive decay.

*   **Boots:** Insulated logging boots with cork-nitrile soles at least 3cm thick, or better, aerogel-inserted extreme-environment boots. The goal is not just insulation but *decoupling*—you must break the thermal bridge between your body and the stone.
*   **Gait:** Walk lightly. The hexagonal flagstones in the urban sectors are thermally warped (crazed by centuries of expansion). They are tripping hazards made worse by your need to avoid sustained contact. Practice walking on your toes, rolling through the foot quickly. Do not sit on the ground. Ever.
*   **Cooling Systems:** Wear a phase-change cooling vest beneath your sealed suit. The ambient temperature varies (the crystals create "thermal shadows" to cool rivals), but the baseline is scorching. You cannot sweat effectively here—the air will desiccate the moisture before it cools you. You must bring your own cold.

---

### **III. Navigation in the Non-Euclidean**

The urban layout follows "no human logic"—alleys designed for serpentine or radial symmetries, turning at angles that accommodate different shoulder widths, different gaits, perhaps different numbers of limbs. Standard mapping will fail you.

*   **Breadcrumb Trails:** Do not use organic materials (bread, thread, chalk). The lithophytes are hungry mineral accretions; organic matter might trigger growth or chemical reaction. Use **metal wire**—copper or aluminum—unspooled and left in your wake. It will not rot, and it will ring softly against the crystal floors, creating a sonic trail.
*   **The Fissure Fix:** Before descending, set up a **laser line** or high-tensile guide rope anchored at the entrance. The ovoid is larger than it feels (density 0.4), and the periphery fades into darkness. It is terrifyingly easy to walk for minutes through bare rock and lose your orientation in the slow cyclone of rising heat.
*   **Reading the Thermal Shadows:** The crystals compete via thermal warfare, casting cooling shadows to stall rivals' metabolisms. Learn to read these microclimates: a sudden cool patch in an alley likely means a dense thicket of lithophytes overhead. Use this to navigate—the "cold" paths lead to crystal growth; the "hot" paths lead to the scorched periphery and potential dead ends.

---

### **IV. Sensory Management: The Loud and the Dim**

**Sound (1.5 Intensity):** The chamber denies silence. The perpetual mineral chorus—the thrumming *om* of expanding silicate, the glassy chimes of contracting mica—will vibrate in your sternum. It is not deafening, but it is *total*. 

*   **Ear Protection:** Bring variable-attenuation earplugs. You need to dampen the constant drone to prevent auditory fatigue, but you must remain able to hear the **gunshot cracks**—the sound of larger crystals fracturing under thermal stress. These cracks roll for twenty seconds off the curved walls; if you hear one close, freeze. A falling crystal spire is a spear of glass.
*   **Sonic Navigation:** The crystals ring when the convection currents move them. You can use this. The sound changes density near walls (dampening) versus open plazas (resonance). Learn the acoustic signature of the circular megalith plaza—it rings with a specific harmonic that can guide you back toward the urban center if lost.

**Light (0.6 Intensity):** The sullen amber-orange phosphorescence of the thermocrystals creates a perpetual sunset that never changes. 

*   **Neutral Illumination:** Bring a low-intensity, full-spectrum light (5500K) to check true colors—this will help you distinguish between different mineral types (the iron-rust reds vs. the amber growths) which may indicate path stability. However, use it sparingly; your eyes will adapt to the amber gloom, and switching between spectra causes disorientation in the non-Euclidean geometry.
*   **Shadow Literacy:** The light filters through translucent crystal fronds, casting long, dancing shadows of the slate walls. These shadows move constantly with the convection cyclone. Do not let them hypnotize you; the "motion" here is mineral, not animal, but it triggers deep predator-detection instincts.

---

### **V. Interaction Protocols: The Gardens of Stone**

You will want to touch the lithophytes. They look like ferns, like coral, like brain-masses—lush, inviting, abandoned in their growth. **Do not.**

Your body heat (~37°C) is a thermal shock to their system. Touching them creates a local contraction; you might cause a fracture, or worse, trigger a growth response. The crystals are "active and hungry," digesting stone. Your finger contains minerals; your sweat (if any escapes) contains salts. You are fertilizer, and the crystal's grip, while slow, is relentless.

*   **The Narrow Path:** When moving through dense thickets of mica-fine filigree, move with the convection current, not against it. The crystals are already flexing in that direction; moving against the grain increases the chance of snapping a spire and triggering a cascade collapse.
*   **Respect the Drainage:** You will encounter bone-dry drainage channels (absolute zero wetness). These are the most stable paths through the settlement—the builders anticipated fluid, or ritually banished it, and these channels cut through the maze with logic that predates the non-Euclidean weirdness. Follow them, but expect them to end abruptly at terraces or plaza edges.

### **VI. Psychological Containment**

The weirdness is subtle (0.6) but cumulative. You are in a sealed kiln that sings to itself, a model city built for a population that evaporated, filled with plants made of glass that almost spell words. The geometry is wrong. The heat emanates selectively—walking from one alley to the next, you will pass through invisible thermal boundaries that have no visible source. This violates thermodynamic intuition and induces creeping paranoia.

*   **The Sensation of Being Watched:** There are no fauna. No insects, no bats. But the ecology is predatory (thermal gradient warfare), and the crystals grow in patterns that "almost form topographic maps of places that don't exist." You will feel observed. This is a cognitive artifact of the non-human geometry and the constant sound. Do not trust it. Do not speak aloud to the "watchers"—sound carries strangely here, and hearing your own voice distorted by the mineral acoustics can trigger dissociation.
*   **Timekeeping:** There is no day or night, only the perpetual amber sunset. Bring a mechanical chronometer (quartz may react to the mineral field) and enforce strict rest cycles. Hypersomnia is common in the thick, hot air; hypothermia is impossible, but "thermal stupor"—a lethargy induced by the constant heat and drone—is a real threat.

---

### **VII. Emergency Protocols**

*   **If Lost:** Stop moving. The convection cyclone spirals upward; feel the air movement on your face. The center of the ovoid is the warmest; the periphery is cooler but leads to darkness. If you are cold, move inward toward the crystal thickets. If you are overheating, move outward, but lay metal wire as you go.
*   **If Desiccated:** You will not feel thirsty—your mucous membranes will dry too quickly to signal properly. If your vision tunnels or your joints ache (synovial fluid thickening), you have minutes. Inject subcutaneous saline if you carry it, or retreat immediately. The fissure is your only exit; there is no water table to tap, no condensation to collect.
*   **Crystal Collapse:** If caught in a falling spire or fracturing "brain-mass," do not struggle. The crystals are sharp but brittle. Wrap the wound immediately in sealed bandages—blood contains water and electrolytes; you cannot afford to lose it to the anhydrous air, and the scent (if any) might trigger unknown mineral behaviors.

---

### **VIII. The Leaving**

You will emerge through the fissure into a world that has water, and it will feel obscene. The air will taste wet, heavy with humidity you never noticed before. Your skin will crack as it rehydrates too quickly. Do not drink for the first hour; sip. Your body has adapted to absolute zero moisture; to flood it is to shock it.

Bring nothing out but recordings and dust. The lithophytes grow by accretion; they are the slowest fire in the universe. To take a shard is to steal a voice from the chorus, to silence one chime in the eternal drone. And the Meridian, for all its empty density, its hot silence, its lush desiccation, is still singing. Let it sing.

Go sealed, go insulated, go light-footed. Walk the dry channels. Listen to the stones breathe. Andremember: here, lushness is not life as you know it. It is intention without water, growth without wetness, a garden that burns instead of blooming. Prepare to be amazed. Prepare to be dry.
