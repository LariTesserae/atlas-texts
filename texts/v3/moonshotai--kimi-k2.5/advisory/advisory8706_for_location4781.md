**Preparation Brief: The Cistern (New Construction)**

You are entering a sensory cathedral that happens to be drowning. This is not a cave; it is a *vessel*—artisan-made, violently bright, and acoustically hostile. The Cistern is less a place you visit and more a condition you survive while remaining motionless. Below is how to prepare for full immersion in this handmade womb.

---

### **I. Physiological Conditioning**

**1. Thermal Regulation for "Blood-Warm" Immersion**
At approximately 37°C (98.6°F), the water offers no cooling. You will overheat without noticing. Train in hot springs or thermal pools while wearing gear to learn the subtle signs of hyperthermia beneath the deceptive comfort. Your sweat will not evaporate; your heart rate will spike from the resonance drumming through your chest. Practice *stillness*—floating without effort to minimize metabolic heat.

**2. Breath-Hold Capacity for the Threshold**
The ladder ends where "the world becomes entirely liquid." You must descend the final meters through a flooded shaft without air, orient yourself in brightness, and surface into a breathing space only a hand's breadth high. Train static apnea (STA) to three minutes minimum, with specific practice on *orientation confusion during ascent*—the oculus light will disorient your sense of up and down.

**3. Vestibular Fortitude**

Prepare by exposing yourself to low-frequency speakers while floating in bathtubs or flotation tanks. Learn to trust tactile orientation over vestibular signals—the vibration will lie to you about which way is up.

### **II. Sensory Armor**

**Vision Protection**
The illumination is "brutal and total," amber-bright with no shadows. Standard swimming goggles will transmit too much light and may focus the beam onto your retina like lenses.

- **Wear**: Welders' glass #3 or #4 lenses modified for underwater use, with amber or copper tinting to harmonic with the ceramic glow. Alternatively, use heavily polarized photochromic lenses that react to the piercing vertical light.
- **Backup**: Bring opaque cloth to blindfold yourself if sensory overwhelm hits—the brightness penetrates eyelids.

**Hearing Defense (Critical)**
Underwater, sound transmits 4.3 times faster and hits with greater force. The Cistern produces a "physical, bass thrum in your chest" and high-frequency hiss at damaging decibels.

- **Equip**: Custom-molded silicone earplugs designed for underwater percussion (used by competitive divers and Navy rescue swimmers). These must vent pressure while attenuating the 85-100dB range.
- **Consider**: Full-head neoprene hood with sound-dampening properties. The rain striking the water surface will impact your scalp like hail if you surface; the hood distributes this impact.
- **Communication**: Forget verbal communication. The drumming overwhelms all sound. Use tactile signaling systems (tug lines) or pre-arranged light signals if traveling with others.

**Skin Protection**
The terracotta tiles have "sharp edges" and mortar "still slightly proud of the seams." The surface texture from drying mats will abrade skin.

- **Wear**: A smooth-skin or coated wetsuit (0.5mm-1mm) not for warmth but for *sliding* protection. Ensure it is abrasion-resistant and covers knees, elbows, and hands.
- **Avoid**: Bare feet. The floor is ceramic with weaving texture—bring soft-soled booties to protect the tilework from your weight and your feet from the grout lines.

---

### **III. Equipment Configuration**

**Buoyancy Control**
You must hover in the center, "turning slowly," without touching the walls. The space is intimate—four meters wide—but the density feels vast.

- Use a **minimalist BCD** (Buoyancy Control Device) or a sidemount configuration with reduced profile.
- **No dangling gear**. The sparse elodea in drainage holes will snag loose straps. The moss in the upper corners is fragile; avoid contact.
- **Neutral weighting**: Test in similar warm freshwater. You will need less weight than expected due to the density of warm water and the physiological effects of the sound vibration on your perception of gravity.

**Breathing Apparatus for the Air Pocket**
The breathing space is a thin, trembling layer "where air trembles with humidity."

- **Option A**: **Full-face mask** with surface-supplied air or a large-volume rebreather. This allows you to breathe without exposing your face to the rain impacts or the humid, possibly stagnant pocket.
- **Option B**: **Standard regulator with snorkel attachment** for the air pocket, but practice *splash breathing*—inhaling during the millisecond gaps between rain impacts, as the surface heaves violently.
- **Warning**: The air pocket may be oxygen-poor due to the warm water's reduced gas solubility and lack of plant photosynthesis (the moss is sparse and pale). Limit time with face in the air pocket.

**Navigation Aids**
There are no shadows, only "liquid light." Orientation is impossible without reference.

- **Deploy**: Chem-lights or small LED floats in sealed amber casings. Release them to drift and mark the center or the ladder zone. Do not attach anything to the walls.
- **Tactile mapping**: The copper-chiseled sluices near the base are distinct from the terracotta. Learn to identify them by touch to locate the outflow (and thus orient yourself).

### **IV. Mental and Cultural Protocol**

**1. The Reverence of Newness**
This place is *new*. The wood smells of sap, the mortar is unworn, the copper shows hammer marks. You are likely the first visitor not involved in its construction. 
- **Do not touch the walls**. Your oils may stain the porous terracotta or interfere with the slow curing of the clay mortar.
- **Minimize wake**. The "interference patterns" are part of the architect's design. Your turbulence is vandalism here.

**2. Acceptance of Sterility**
There are no fish, no insects, no worms. The ecology is reduced to "a few plants and the water itself." This is not a living reef; it is a nursery awaiting inhabitants, or a ritual space maintained in deliberate purity. Prepare for the *uncanny* feeling of biological emptiness in such a warm, wet environment.

**3. The Drum Meditation**
The sound is constant, loud, and physical. Do not resist it. Practice body-tuning: lie in the center and let the bass frequencies resonate your ribcage until your heartbeat synchronizes with the chamber's hum. Panic here is fatal—you cannot hear instructions, you cannot see clearly, you cannot cool down. You must become part of the percussion.

**4. Time Limits**
Despite the warmth, you will dehydrate (you cannot drink the water—it may be sterile now, but the copper sluices and fresh clay introduce unknown minerals). Despite the noise, ear damage is cumulative. **Limit first visits to 20 minutes.**

---

### **V. Emergency Considerations**

- **Entanglement**: The elodea is sparse but drifting. Carry a ceramic-safe cutting tool (not metal that might chip the tile) to free yourself.
- **Disorientation**: If you lose sense of direction, descend to the floor and locate the copper sluices by touch—follow them to the wall, then circumnavigate to find the ladder shaft.
- **Hyperthermia**: If you feel dizzy, the warm water has overheated you. Ascend immediately; the air pocket, despite humidity, may offer slight convective cooling.
- **Structural Sensitivity**: The ceramic is newly fired. Do not impact the walls with heavy gear—the tiles may not have fully settled into their thermal expansion. Treat the chamber as you would a filled but unglazed porcelain cup.

---

**Final Advice:**
Enter naked of intention. The Cistern is a handmade womb, a testing vessel for seals and souls. You are not exploring; you are being held in warm, loud, artisan suspension. Prepare to be unmade by the percussion, blinded by the amber light, and scoured by the newness of it all. Float in the center. Let the rain fill you.
