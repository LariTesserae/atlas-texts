**Field Manual: Pre-Departure Protocols for the Rictus Plateau**

You are preparing to enter a geography that has achieved its final form. The Plateau does not welcome change; it tolerates your presence the way a stone tolerates a mayfly—as a brief, irritating vibration that must be smoothed away. You will be walking through the densest biological traffic jam in the multiverse, suspended in a moment that refuses to break. Prepare accordingly.

---

### I. Physiological Preparation: Becoming Temporary

**Altitude Acclimatization (Minimum 6 Weeks)**
The atmosphere at the roof of the world is a "violet-tinged knife-edge." You will be operating at extreme elevation with oxygen deprivation that mimics hypoxic brain death. Begin training in hypobaric chambers immediately. You must be able to function cognitively with blood-oxygen levels that would render an ordinary person unconscious. Practice complex decision-making while breathing through a restricted tube. If you cannot solve a puzzle while oxygen-starved, you will not survive the disorientation of the Plateau.

**Surgical Preparation (Critical)**
Remove all metal from your body. This includes dental fillings, surgical pins, pacemakers, joint replacements, and piercings. The Plateau’s reality-sickness affects metal at the molecular level. Fillings will crumble to oxide powder within hours, potentially poisoning you or leaving you with open cavities in an environment where infection does not progress (your immune response will freeze, but so will bacterial decay—resulting in a perpetual, painful stasis of illness). If you require corrective lenses, use carved crystal monocles with leather straps—glass and plastic are stable, but metal frames are not.

**Fasting & Gastrointestinal Voiding**
Eat your last substantial meal twelve hours before entry. The Plateau’s anti-change field means digestion ceases. Food will sit in your stomach "like a stone, unchanging, unprocessed." You will not defecate while inside; you will not urinate in any meaningful way (liquid will remain in your bladder as a static lump). Empty yourself completely before crossing the threshold. Bring no food supplies—they will neither nourish nor spoil, merely occupying weight and space. Hydrate excessively before entry, but accept that you will not feel thirst once inside, only the dry mineral tooth of the cold.

**Cold Conditioning**
The cold bites with a "dry, mineral tooth." It is not the wet cold of tundra but the absolute desiccation of anhydrous minerals. Train in cryogenic dry chambers. Harden your skin with mineral oil baths to prevent the cracking that will occur in the first hour. Your extremities will be at risk, but paradoxically, frostbite will not progress—it will simply *be*, frozen in its initial state, neither worsening nor healing.

### II. Psychological Conditioning: The Silence and the Density

**Auditory Deprivation Training (Minimum 30 Days)**
Sound does not travel. The air "refuses to carry vibration." You will hear your own heartbeat "loud as thunder in your temples," but that sound stops at your skin. Begin now: wear noise-canceling devices that allow only internal sounds. Learn to think without subvocalization. Practice communicating through gesture, facial expression, and touch. After three days in the Plateau, vocal cords atrophy and thought becomes visual. You must establish a visual language system before entry—agree upon specific hand signals for *danger*, *time to leave*, *do not touch*, and *I am losing coherence*.

**Claustrophobia & Spatial Disorientation**
You will navigate a three-dimensional maze of suspended megafauna. The Caught are packed "shoulder to shoulder, nose to tail," floating at various heights—some mid-leap three meters up, others nose-down in dust that has not fallen. Practice moving through dense obstacle courses while blindfolded, using only your hands to map space. Learn to climb over large, irregular surfaces (simulate with foam sculptures) without relying on visual cues, as the shadows "point in impossible directions" and "accumulate in layers like photographic exposures." Your visual cortex will attempt to process contradictory depth cues; meditation techniques for dissociation will help you navigate without vertigo.

**Temporal Disassociation**
Time does not pass. You must internalize a biological clock. Practice counting heartbeats to measure hours. Set a mental alarm for 48 hours (never stay the full three days). The Plateau will trick you into staying—without hunger, thirst, or fatigue, you may forget to leave.

### III. Equipment Manifest: The Pre-Industrial Kit

**Permitted Materials Only:**
- **Stone**: Knapped flint blades, obsidian cutting tools, river stones for weight or throwing.
- **Bone**: Needles, awls, toggles, fasteners. Ideally fashioned from the bones of the Caught themselves (harvested from the perimeter, never from the interior density).
- **Wood**: Hardwood poles (ash or oak) for vaulting over beasts, wooden levers, toggles.
- **Leather**: Untanned hide for grip, tanned hide for containers (though liquids will not pour properly).
- **Crystalline/Ceramic**: Glass lenses, ceramic blades (surprisingly effective).
- **Hair & Sinew**: For cordage. Rope must be braided from natural fibers; synthetic cord will tangle into "impossible knots" immediately.

**Prohibited (Will Fail Catastrophically):**
- Any metal (iron, steel, aluminum, copper).
- Electronics (batteries drain to solid salt, circuits suffer "reality-sickness").
- Fire-starting equipment (fire will not catch; matches are useless weight).
- Complex mechanisms (gears unthread, springs unwind).
- Chemical reactants (they solidify into inert lumps).

**Clothing:**
Wear layers of wool and felt. No zippers, no metal buttons, no eyelets. Fasten garments with bone toggles and leather thongs. Your boots should be soft-soled leather (moccasin-style) to allow sensitivity when climbing over the Caught, but with reinforced soles for the salt-encrusted pans. Bring extra layers—the cold is absolute, and you cannot warm yourself by fire.

**Navigation Tools:**
Since footprints vanish "the moment you lift your boot," you cannot mark trails with disturbed earth. Instead, bring a **bone-chip trail**: a pouch of small, distinctively shaped bone fragments. Drop one every hundred paces. They will not move (the Plateau respects the stasis of its own materials). Do not use string or cord to mark paths—it will tangle in the anti-change field.

**Timekeeping:**
Bring an hourglass filled with the dust of the Plateau itself (gathered from the perimeter). Standard sand may behave unpredictably. Alternatively, use a marked candle—but remember, the flame will not light. You are carrying the candle only to measure duration by its static length, or to check against your internal heartbeat count.

### IV. Navigation Protocols: Moving Through the Moment

**The Three-Dimensional Maze**
The Caught exist in "quantum superposition"—some occupy the same spatial coordinates. You can pass through certain beasts by tilting your head or shifting your center of gravity, but others are solid. Test surfaces with your bone pole before putting weight on them. The ungulates are massive, wool-pelted; their predators are lean with "membrane sails." Avoid the open jaws of the predators—they are frozen in the act of biting, and the teeth are razor-sharp edges in stasis.

**The Contagion of Stasis**
**Never touch the hide of the Caught with bare skin.** If you must climb over them (and you must), use your wooden poles to vault, or lay down leather hides as a barrier between you and the beast. Skin-to-skin contact risks "the contagion of stasis"—you will join the stampede, frozen mid-stride, your nostrils flared with a cloud of crystallized vapor that will never disperse, your shadow pointing in the wrong direction for ten thousand years.

**Shadow Navigation**
Do not trust shadows for direction. They point in "impossible directions" and accumulate in layers. Navigate by the position of the sun alone, but be aware that the light reveals a "world that has finished its story." If you become disoriented, look for the leeward dimples of rocks where the grey, coin-shaped lichens cluster—these indicate consistent wind patterns from before the stasis event, a fossil of meteorology.

### V. Sustenance & Biological Function

**Pre-Loading**
Consume 5,000 calories and 3 liters of water immediately before entry. You will not metabolize while inside, but your body will require the stored chemical energy to maintain heat and consciousness. Think of yourself as a battery with a fixed charge.

**Waste Management**
You will not produce waste. Your kidneys will cease filtering; your gut will cease peristalsis. This is not comfortable—it is a sensation of fullness and pressure that increases with time. Do not stay longer than 48 hours, or the backlog of metabolic byproducts in your blood may cause toxic shock upon exiting (when time resumes).

**Medical Considerations**
If you are injured, bleeding will not clot properly (the platelets will not activate), but neither will you bleed out (the blood will not flow). A wound will remain exactly as it was at the moment of cutting—a static slice. Bandage with leather strips. Do not attempt surgery; the tissue will not heal, and sutures will slide out as the flesh refuses to change.

---

### VI. Emergency Procedures

**If You Touch a Caught Beast:**
Do not panic. The stasis spreads slowly from the point of contact. If you brush a flank with your hand, immediately amputate the affected limb using your flint blade. The stump will not bleed, and you may survive to exit. If the contact is torso or head, accept your fate. Lie down comfortably; in ten thousand years, when the stasis breaks, you will complete your fall.

**If Lost:**
Stop moving. Sit on a boulder (the smooth, worry-bead stones). The Plateau has achieved "permanent equilibrium"; you are the only variable. Wait for your trail of bone chips to guide you back, or for your companion to find you. Do not wander—the density of the Caught increases toward the center, and you may become trapped in a cage of frozen limbs.

**If Technology Fails (Inevitable):**
Abandon it immediately. Do not attempt to repair your metal gear; the oxide powder will contaminate your supplies. Proceed with bone and wood only.

**Exit Protocol:**
You must leave before the 72-hour mark. At hour 60, begin moving toward the perimeter regardless of your objectives. Language loss is irreversible and begins as a subtle shift—words feel "too loud" in your mind, then meaningless. If you find yourself unable to remember the word for "sky" or "hunger," run. Do not stop until you cross the threshold where wind returns and your shadow aligns with the sun.

---

### VII. The Return

When you exit, time will crash into you like a wave. Your digestion will resume violently; you may vomit undigested food from three days prior. Your wounds, if any, will begin to bleed or hurt suddenly. Your urine will return in a rush. The silence will echo in your ears for weeks.

You will carry the Plateau with you: the habit of visual thought, the intolerance for noise, the memory of walking through a thunderclap that never faded. You have visited a place where geography has achieved its last possible configuration. Be grateful that you are still changing, still decaying, still alive enough to leave.

**Final Note:** Do not bring souvenirs. The bone tools you find, the lichens, the dust—if removed from the Plateau, they may resume their decay explosively or collapse into quantum foam. Travel light. Travel silent. Travel fast.
