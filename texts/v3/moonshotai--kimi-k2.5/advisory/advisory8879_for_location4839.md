**Preparing for the Plateau of Final Geometry**

You are going to a place that has stopped becoming. This is not a wilderness to conquer, nor a ruin to loot—this is a continent that has achieved its final draft, where entropy has lain down to sleep and time pools like old water. You cannot "survive" the Plateau through force of will; you must instead learn to be *temporary* within the eternal. Below is how to prepare for visiting a geography that has agreed upon its own perfection.

---

### **I. The Body: Preparing for Altitude and Aging**

**Respiratory Architecture**  
At 2.3 elevation, the air is an afterthought. Breathing feels like sipping oxygen through taut linen. Standard compressed air tanks are useless—metal seams will crystallize and fail under the accumulated weight of accelerated time. Instead, acclimatize for six months in hypobaric chambers until your hematocrit reaches levels normally seen in high-altitude camelids. Bring liquid oxygen stored in **glazed ceramic ampoules** (never glass; the silica resonance with local glass-ferns can cause sympathetic fracturing). Supplement with crates of **oxygen-generating crystals**—potassium permanganate derived from your home world, dispersed in chalk-lined wooden chests.

**Temporal Inoculation**  
Time does not pass on the Plateau; it accretes. You will age faster than your calendar suggests. Before departure, mark your body with **ochre and woad tattoos**—organic pigments that will fade visibly if time begins to pool heavily on your skin. Pack no medicine with active compounds that degrade (antibiotics become poison in days; insulin polymerizes into amber). Bring only **dried, crystallized biologicals**—lyophilized herbs, desiccated fungal poultices, and stone-ground mineral salves.

**Circadian Annihilation**  
The light is permanently fixed at late-afternoon gold. There is no night to reset your melatonin, only the endless bruised violet of an oxygen-starved sky. You must train your body to sleep in amber-lit perpetuity. For two months prior, live in light-sealed bunkers illuminated only by sodium-vapor lamps set to 2700K. Learn to trust your exhaustion rather than darkness.

### **II. Equipment: The Lithic Standard**

**The Prohibition of Metal**  
Do not bring iron. Do not bring copper. Do not bring aluminum, titanium, or alloys. On the Plateau, metallurgy is a temporary enthusiasm. Iron oxidizes to dust between dawn and noon; copper verdigris-eats itself to nothing within a week. Even gold behaves strangely—too honest for a place where physics lies.

Your technology must regress to **1.4**: the age of stone, bone, obsidian, and sinew.

*   **Cutting Tools:** Knapped obsidian blades set in mammoth-ivory hafts (harvested ethically from your home tundras, or synthetic ivory-ceramic composites). Obsidian is glass, and the Plateau respects glass—it is kin to the glass-ferns.
*   **Containers:** Gourds, turtle shells, or sealed terra-cotta. Water skins must be double-lined: exterior of oiled leather, interior of flexible birch-bark laminate.
*   **Fasteners:** No zippers, no buckles. Use **rawhide lacing**, antler toggles, or cords of braided slow-liverwort (a moss that resists the Plateau’s temporal pressure).
*   **Structural:** For shelter construction, bring **bone needles** and sinew thread to repair the ancient leather-stone domes you will occupy. Do not attempt to hammer stakes into the permafrost; the earth is finished being penetrated.

**Navigation Tools**  
Shadows fall at impossible angles. Compasses spin in gravity wells. You cannot navigate by sun, stars, or magnetism.

*   Bring **thousands of meters of silk cord**, dyed in distinct hues. The megaliths stand exactly 1,000 paces apart in a grid that arrogantly ignores topography. String your lines between them to create a tangible web of geometry. Touch-navigation is the only reliable method.
*   Carry a **plumb bob of solid sulfur** (resistant to aging, responds to the Plateau’s negotiable gravity by spinning lazily toward the "center" of the local distortion).
*   Mark your path with **cairns of selected stones**—never remove rocks from the Plateau, but stack those already present. Paint them with stripes of copper sulfate (the same verdant hue as the local lichens) to distinguish your marks from the natural growth.

### **III. Acoustic Discipline: The Silence Protocol**

The Plateau exists at **0.6 quiet**—not silent, but nearly. Sound behaves as a solid. When you shout, the noise leaves your throat as a milky sphere of compressed air that drifts until it collides with stone, only then releasing its sonic payload hours later as a whisper.

*   **No Verbal Communication.** Develop a system of **touch-sign**—pressure on the shoulder, tapping codes on bone, or the passage of written slate-tablets between party members.
*   **Whisper Protocol.** If you must speak, exhale into the earth itself. Press your lips to the weird-warm stones and murmur; the sound will conduct through the geology and emerge as a subsonic rumble your companions can feel through their boot soles.
*   **Noise Pollution Hazards.** Avoid sudden impacts. Dropping a stone tool creates a sphere of impact-sound that may roll downhill (or uphill, depending on local gravity) and strike a glass-fern, causing a sympathetic fracture cascade that could deafen you hours later when the acoustic sphere finally releases its stored energy.

### **IV. Sustenance: Eating a World Without Animals**

The fauna rating is **0.5**. There are no birds to eat, no insects to bite you, no mammals to hunt. The only protein comes from the slow-coral and the lichens—but beware.

*   **The Slow-Coral:** Those crimson meadows are technically edible—they are calcium phosphate neural networks—but they process thoughts at glacial speeds. Consuming them introduces **geological time into your digestion**. Eat a mouthful, and you may experience a week’s worth of contemplative stasis while your body processes it. Bring enzymes from your home world to break down the frozen filaments, or risk entering a meditative coma that lasts until your supplies rot.
*   **Glass-Ferns:** Their silica fronds will shred your mouth, but the antifreeze sap can be harvested. It is a glycol solution toxic to human kidneys, but if you bring **activated charcoal derived from oak** (not metal-filtered), you can neutralize it into a sweet, sustaining syrup. Collect it by drilling into the fern bases with obsidian augers—never cut; the fracturing sound is dangerous.
*   **Spore-Bread:** The crystalline spores that glitter like mica dust are everywhere. Inhalation leads to silicosis of the soul—a hardening of the empathic centers. Bring **oil-infused linen masks**. However, the spores can be harvested on waxed leather sheets, dampened with your own saliva, and fermented into a chewy, nutrient-dense flatbread that tastes of copper and winter.

**Water Acquisition**  
Water exists as *potential*. It never rains, only suspends.

*   Wrap your tents in **oil-wool felt**. The hoarfrost that grows by the century will accumulate on the fibers; each morning, wring out the cloth into your gourds. The water will be pre-oxygenated by the altitude, thin but pure.
*   **Do not drink from the stones directly.** The weird-warm rocks create thermal shock; water melted on them flash-freezes into new crystalline shells. If you must drink from a stone pool, use a **warming bag of heated stones** (carry them in your armpits first) to gradually thaw the liquid without triggering the flash-freeze reflex of the environment.

### **V. Shelter and Movement: Living Within Geometry**

*   **Sleep in the Alignments.** The primitive builders understood the Plateau’s negotiable gravity. Sleep *between* the megaliths, where water flows uphill, and you will wake with your blood pooled in your head, your dreams distorted by micro-gravity. Instead, camp *beside* the stones, using their thermal mass (they hold the weird-warmth) to create microclimates that repel the freezing mist.
*   **The Bone Domes.** Seek out the mammoth-bone shelters. The leather-stone that covers them has tanned for epochs and provides the only truly "dead" material on the Plateau—something that has already finished changing and will not digest you while you sleep. Check the walls for slow-coral incursion; if the crimson turf has reached knee-height, abandon the dome—it is being thought-processed into the neural network.
*   **Movement:** Walk slowly. The ground breathes with geological respiration. Sudden movements disturb the equilibrium. Place your feet as if you are arranging megaliths—deliberately, with finality.

---

### **VI. Psychological Armor: Resisting the Finished**

This is the most critical preparation. The Plateau is not hostile; it is *complete*. It induces a profound existential vertigo—a sense that your own unfinished nature is an obscenity against the perfected erosion.

*   **Bring Unfinished Things.** Pack a notebook with pencils (graphite and wood, never metal mechanical pencils). Deliberately leave sketches incomplete. Write stories without endings. Sculpt clay that you never fire. In a world where everything has achieved final geometry, your *potential* becomes your armor.
*   **The Mantra of Becoming.** When the silence presses in (and it will; the only sounds are the subsonic settling of the continent and the crack of expanding glass-ferns), recite: *"I am not yet what I will be."* The Plateau cannot digest purpose; it can only preserve completion.
*   **Limit Exposure.** Despite all precautions, do not stay longer than **twelve days**. After two weeks, the temporal accumulation will begin to crystallize in your joints; you will feel the weight of centuries in your knees. You will begin to agree with the stones that stillness is wisdom. Leave while you still want to leave.

---

### **VII. Emergency Protocols**

*   **If Lost:** Do not look for landmarks. The megaliths all look the same. Instead, stop moving. Lie flat on the slow-coral. It will begin to process you as a nutrient, but slowly—over decades. You have time. Wait for the spore-snow to coat you; when it forms a drift against your body, you will know which way the wind flows. Follow the wind uphill (it seeks the center). The center is the only place with enough gravity to hold you down long enough to think.
*   **If Trapped in a Sound Sphere:** If you shout in panic and watch your voice drift away as a milky orb, mark its trajectory with sulfur dust. Do not be present when it strikes a surface and releases its stored sonic energy—it will strike with the full force of your original panic, amplified by hours of suspension.
*   **If Aging Accelerates:** If your hair grays overnight, or your fingernails grow thick and yellow, you have camped in a "time pocket." Abandon all gear. Walk naked (clothing ages faster when worn in these zones, becoming stone-stiff) to the nearest megalith and press your spine against it. The stones are "finished" and will leach the accumulated years into their own ancientness. You will feel hollowed out, but young.

---

You are visiting a museum where the exhibits are continents, where the gift shop sells only silence, and where the exit door is hidden behind centuries of gentle erosion. Pack stone. Leave hope of change behind. And remember: the Plateau is not waiting for you. It is not waiting for anything. It is waiting *itself*—a verb that has become a monument.

Go gently. Go briefly. Do not finish anything while you are there.
