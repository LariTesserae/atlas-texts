**Preparation for the Sintercrown Plateaus**
*A Field Guide for the Human Multiverse Traveler*

---

### **I. Acclimation: The Body Against the Mineralogic**

**Altitude Deception**  
You will be tempted to prepare for hypoxia and cold. Resist this. The Plateaus sit seventeen kilometers above the basal plains in an atmosphere that is thick, warm, and wet—geothermically heated from below and pressurized by the sheer weight of the three-kilometer-deep city pressing down upon itself. Your lungs will work harder than expected, not from thinness, but from the density of moisture-laden air that feels like breathing through warm silk. Pack decongestants and bronchodilators; the saline condensation will irritate alveoli over time, leaving you with a chronic chemical cough.

**Hearing Protection (Non-Negotiable)**  
The ambient sound is not merely loud—it is structural. The drone of seventeen trillion maintenance arthropods meshing their brass gear-teeth, the basso profundo of geothermal vents, and the ultrasonic whine of charging capacitors will enter your body through your sternum before your ears. Standard earplugs are insufficient; you require active noise-canceling bone-conduction communication systems. Without these, you will be unable to coordinate with companions (conversation requires pressing cheek to cheek, shouting into the cavity of another's ear) and you risk "vibration sickness"—nausea caused by constant low-frequency resonance in the gut.

**The Shell Protocol**  
Everything here weeps. The condensate beads are not pure water but mineralized saline solution, heavy with calcite and metal salts in suspension. Within hours, unprotected skin will develop chemical burns; within days, clothing will stiffen into abrasive armor as the water evaporates and leaves its lace-vein deposits. You require a full-body sealed suit with hydrophobic ceramic nanopore fabric—preferably in non-reflective matte blacks or deep rust-reds. *Do not wear polished metals or oxidation-prone alloys.* The thumbnail-sized arthropods—filigreed brass bodies, mica wings—will swarm you if you present as something in need of cleaning. They do not bite, but they *excrete* a hard enamel polish while consuming oxidation. Travelers have been found starved to death, pinned beneath glittering carpets of these creatures, their bodies slowly laminated into the architecture.

### **II. Hydration Strategy: Living in the Currency**

**The Paradox of Thirst**  
Water is simultaneously everywhere and treacherous. The air is pregnant with it; you could squeeze the mist and drink. Yet the condensate carries dissolved minerals that will calcify your kidneys within weeks. You have three safe options:

1. **The Pulse-Harvesters:** These metallic barnacles cluster on every ledge, their diaphragms beating in slow, hypnotic rhythm—in*hhhhhh*-ale, ex*hhhhhh*-ale. Learn their individual tempos; each unit produces a droplet of distilled potable water at predictable intervals. Attach flexible silicone tubing to their outflow valves (expect resistance; the fauna may tighten their sphincters if they detect non-authorized extraction). Carry collapsible bladders rated for high-saline content for the days when you cannot reach a harvester.

2. **The Migratory Globules:** In thermal corridors, water crawls *upward* against gravity, forming slow-moving livestock-balls the size of heads. These are hunted by specialized siphon-creatures. You may intercept a globule, but you must drink it entirely before it reaches the siphon-predators' territory, or you risk being treated as competition. The water in these globules is pure, heated by geothermal vents—body temperature or higher.

3. **Calcite Lace Mining:** Collect the salt-calcite residue left in worn hollows of the thoroughfares. Dissolve carefully in collected condensation and filter through ceramic membranes. The mineral content is lower than direct drinking, but the taste is aggressively metallic.

**Corrosion Vigilance**  
Your equipment will rust. Your zippers will fuse. Your electronics will short. Pack everything in nitrogen-flushed hard cases with desiccant sacs. When you open them, do so under the cover of a maintenance swarm—their presence temporarily depletes the ambient moisture through their metabolic processes.

### **III. Navigation: The Horizonless Geometry**

**Shadowless Orientation**  
The silver diffusion of light through the perpetual mist creates a claustrophobe's nightmare: no shadows, no directional light source, no sunset or sunrise to mark time. The city exists in eternal featureless daylight. You must abandon visual reckoning. 

- **Inertial Navigation:** Gyroscopic compasses are essential. The magnetic fields here are unpredictable due to the massive oxidized copper infrastructure, but mechanical gyroscopes function reliably.
- **The Feel of Down:** Though water climbs in thermal corridors, gravity remains consistent. When disoriented in the maze of layered architecture, pour a drop of water. If it crawls upward, you are in a vent-zone; if it falls, you have your bearings.
- **The Worn Hollows:** Follow the ancient thoroughfares—channels carved not by glaciers but by the patient feet of centuries. These are safer than the narrow conduits where pipe-worms bore constantly, sealing micro-fractures with resin that can entomb the unwary.

**The Months-Long District**  
Understand scale. You may walk for three months and remain in the same borough. The conurbation is continental. Do not assume you can "cross the city" in a lifetime. Set modest goals: "I will reach the next thermal vent" or "I will follow this arterial bridge for six weeks."

### **IV. Fauna Interaction: Etiquette of the Machine-Ecology**

**The Carpet Rule**  
The maintenance arthropods are not hostile, but they are absolute. When the swarm flows across the avenue—a glittering river of brass and mica—*do not attempt to walk through them*. Stand still. Let them flow over your feet, your legs, your shoulders. They are searching for oxidation to consume. If you carry rust—blood, iron supplements, steel tools—they will concentrate on those areas. The sensation is of being tasted by sandpaper. Wait until the carpet passes. Attempting to brush them away triggers a defensive swarming instinct; they will coat you to "clean" the disturbance.

**Grazer-Unit Avoidance**  
The dog-sized cleaners perched on cornices appear motionless for days, processing mineral precipitates with their abrasive tongues. They are not statues. They move with *hydraulic suddenness*—all stillness, then a jerking blur of motion. Maintain a five-meter clearance. They do not hunt biologicals, but their grooming cycles involve shedding calcite dust and occasionally dislodging debris (or humans) from their perches.

**Constructor-Colony Respect**  
The buildings are alive. Constructor-colonies devour and regurgitate architecture over centuries. Do not seek shelter in alcoves that appear "half-formed" or unusually smooth; you may be sealed inside as the colony finishes its regurgitation cycle. If you must rest, choose surfaces that show the characteristic "weathered smooth" patina of complete equilibrium—evidence the local colony has finished with this section.

**Pipe-Worm Protocol**  
These pneumatic segmented creatures hiss through walls, sealing fractures. If you hear a wet, pressurized hiss behind a surface, *move*. They do not distinguish between structural cracks and human limbs, and their resin sets faster than concrete.

### **V. Temporal Adjustment: The Geological Mindset**

**Chronometer Discipline**  
Time moves slowly here. The fauna operate on patience measured in epochs. A grooming cycle takes a decade. You must bring a mechanical clock—wind-up, non-electric—and ritualize its consultation. Without this, you will fall prey to "Plateau Drift," a psychological condition where you begin to match your metabolism to the city. You will find yourself standing motionless for hours, days, convinced that waiting for a door to open (or a wall to digest) is a reasonable use of an afternoon. Set alarms. Force movement every four hours.

**The Long Now**  
Do not expect change. The architecture you photograph today will be identical in fifty years. Do not wait for the condensate to stop falling. It has wept for epochs. Accept that your journey here is a fleeting perturbation in the city's metabolism.

### **VI. Psychological Fortification: Against the Wrongness**

**Do Not Analyze the Coordination**  
You will notice it immediately: the arthropods anticipate each other. Swarms turn corners simultaneously; harvesters pulse in sympathetic rhythm across kilometers; grazer-units on opposite sides of a ravine move in mirrored motion despite no visible signal. Do not attempt to locate the central processor. Do not theorize about quantum entanglement or distributed hive-minds. The realization that the city is a single three-kilometer-deep breathing organism will press against your sanity. Accept the choreography without seeking the choreographer. To understand the coordination is to begin the process of absorption.

**The Breathing Walls**  
You are walking through the digestive tract of something ancient. The pipes are not pipes; they are esophagi. The condensate is not rain; it is perspiration. When you sleep, you are nestled in the pores of a mineral beast. Repeat this mantra: *I am a tourist in a body.* Do not become paranoid about being digested—unless you stop moving. The city notices stasis.

**Silver Light Psychosis**  
The featureless silver glow, shadowless and diffuse, destroys depth perception and circadian rhythm. After three weeks, travelers report hallucinations of "the Reverse"—the sensation that they are actually inside a solid object looking out, rather than moving through space. Carry a flashlight with a focused beam. Shine it on surfaces to create *artificial shadows*—proof of dimension, proof of exteriority.

### **VII. Emergency Protocols**

**The Upward Flood**  
If caught in a thermal corridor during a surge (usually signaled by a sudden increase in warmth and a reversal of condensate flow), do not swim against the current. The water moves slowly—perhaps two kilometers per hour—but inexorably. Allow yourself to be carried to the siphon-creature territories; they are usually equipped to filter and release, whereas fighting the flow leads to exhaustion and drowning in warm, mineral-thick soup.

**Resin Entombment**  
If sealed by a pipe-worm or constructor-colony, remain absolutely still. The resin cures through catalytic reaction to motion and carbon dioxide. Slow your breathing. Wait. The colony may decide you are a structural element and move on; rescue is more likely if you do not struggle and become fully embedded.

**Swarm Overload**  
If completely covered by maintenance arthropods (the "Brass Shroud"), curl into a ball, protect eyes and mouth, and emit a continuous low-frequency hum (achievable by vocalizing into your sternum). This frequency mimics the city's geothermal background and may confuse the swarm into categorizing you as infrastructure rather than cleanup-target.

### **VIII. Departure Considerations**

There are no ports. There are no elevators down the seventeen kilometers to the basal plains. The city grows upward like stalagmites; it does not plan for exit. You must find a geothermal vent strong enough to provide lift (packing a wingsuit with ceramic thermal tiles is recommended) or commit to the long walk through the compressed lower strata—three kilometers down through the fossilized layers of forgotten functions, where the air grows thick and the mechanical fauna grow larger, older, and less recognizable.

Alternatively, simply stay. Many do. The warmth is seductive, the water is endless, and the silver light erases the memory of shadows. You will find yourself standing motionless on a bridge, watching a bead of condensation crawl upward, waiting for nothing, feeling the slow click of gear-teeth in your own heartbeat, becoming another polished, breathing component of the Sintercrown.

*Pack carefully. The city is patient. It has been waiting for you for seventeen kilometers.*
