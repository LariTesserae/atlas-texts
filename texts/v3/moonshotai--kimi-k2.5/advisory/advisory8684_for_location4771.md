**Preparation Protocol for the Rinse**  
*Classification: Static Preservation Zone / High Temporal Viscosity / Attention-Sensitive Geology*

---

### I. Physical Conditioning (The Crawl & The Squeeze)
You will enter on your belly through peat that never thaws, then descend a slope of **slickstone**—a mineral worn glassy not by time, but by *attention*. This is your first warning: the geology here responds to observation.

- **Contortion Training**: Practice moving through spaces narrower than your shoulders without raising your heart rate. You must be able to exhale completely, compress your ribcage, and slide forward without panic. Claustrophobia is fatal here; the interstices between dwellings admit no thrashing.
- **Slickstone Technique**: The descent is polished by millennia of focused gaze. Wear soft-soled boots with micro-siping (vibram rubber compounds), but expect to slide. Learn the "controlled fall": keep your center of gravity low, arms tucked to prevent instinctive grabbing (which creates sound), and use your hip bones as runners. 
- **Core Stability**: The vertical accretions of the city require climbing through gaps no wider than a thickened wrist. Practice chimneying between close walls without shoving or pushing—any forceful contact creates vibration in a silence that is theological.

---

### II. Wardrobe: The Saturated Cold
The environment hovers just above freezing at **80% humidity**—the air is saturated to the point of viscosity. Your breath will not cloud; it will hang as static mist and bead on your clothing. This is wet-cold, the most dangerous kind.

- **Wool, Only Wool**: The text mentions vapor clinging to "hair and wool." Synthetics will trap condensation against your skin and conduct heat away. Wear dense, lanolin-rich wool (Icelandic lopi or similar) in loose weaves that can hold moisture without collapsing their insulative structure. 
- **Silence Weave**: Avoid Gore-Tex (crinkles), Velcro (tearing sound), zippers (metal click), or any hardware. Use bone or antler buttons, leather toggles, felted wool closures. Your clothing must be absolutely silent when saturated.
- **The Drip Management**: Everything will bead—your eyebrows, lashes, hair. Wear a wool cap that extends to a visor to prevent condensation from your forehead blinding you. Gloves must be thin enough to allow tactile navigation of the root-cord bridges, but wool-lined to prevent finger stiffness.
- **Footwear**: High-ankled, soft leather, treated with beeswax (not synthetic waterproofing, which cracks in cold). The floors are polished clay and ceramic-like moss plaster—slippery when beaded with dew.

---

### III. Sensory Calibration
**Light**: The ice dome traps and amplifies low-latitude sun into a perpetual milk-bright noon. There is no shadow, no dusk, no relief for your rods and cones.

- Bring **red-tinted glacier glasses** with side shields. Not to block brightness—the light is diffuse—but to create artificial contrast so your brain can perceive depth in the monochrome glow. Without them, you will suffer "white blindness" and misjudge the vertical gaps between bridge decks.

**Sound (0.0)**: The silence is absolute. You will hear your own heartbeat, the creak of your eyeballs, the rush of blood in your ears.

- **Desensitization**: Spend 48 hours in an anechoic chamber before arrival. Learn to tolerate the internal orchestra of your body without anxiety. 
- **Acoustic Padding**: Pad all gear with felt. Tape down every strap. If you must carry water, use soft flasks, not rigid bottles (sloshing is audible). 
- **No Voice Protocol**: Do not speak. The silence is not empty; it is *full* of held potential. Breaking it creates a wound in the fabric of the place that does not heal.

---

### IV. Temporal Anchors (Critical)
The primary danger is **synchronization**—your heartbeat slowing to match the stasis, becoming "established" like the ferns and condensation.

- **Mechanical Metronome**: Wear a wind-up mechanical watch (quartz may fail in the humidity; batteries die) with an audible tick. The sound must be loud enough to disrupt your entrainment with the environment's zero-dynamic state. Set it to 72 bpm or higher.
- **Pharmaceutical Accelerants**: Carry sublingual caffeine tablets or low-dose ephedrine. If you notice your pulse dropping below 50 bpm, or if time seems to "thicken," administer immediately. Do not wait for fatigue; by then you are already integrating.
- **The 90-Minute Rule**: Set a hard extraction deadline. The Rinse is the size of a banquet hall—there is no need to linger. Mark your entry time on your skin with waterproof ink (the humidity will destroy paper).

---

### V. Navigation & Attention Management
The city is packed (2.6) with no streets, only interstices. The bridges overhead create shifting geometric patterns of light.

- **The Unfocus Technique**: The slickstone was worn by attention. Do not stare at surfaces; use peripheral vision. If you focus too intensely on a wattle wall or a suspended moth, you risk "finishing" it—accelerating its erosion or, worse, drawing its stasis into yourself. Practice "soft eyes": look at the spaces between things.
- **Breadcrumb Protocol**: Do not use string (it becomes established, frozen in the humidity). Do not use chalk (dust floats, never settles, creating confusing constellations). Instead, carry a small vial of **liquid smoke** or condensed breath—release a puff at turns; the vapor will hang suspended as a marker for approximately 20 minutes before dispersing into the general saturation.
- **Verticality**: The dwellings stack four to five stories. If you climb, test each willow bridge with only a fraction of your weight first. They are lashed with sinew that has been paused in tension for centuries—strong, but brittle if disturbed.

---

### VI. Biological Safety
The fauna are abundant but static. The flora is paused in photosynthetic gasp.

- **Respiratory**: The air is thick with suspended spores from the bone-colored fungi and the static dust of ages. Wear a **silk or fine wool balaclava** over nose and mouth—not a respirator (too loud, too crinkly). The fabric will filter spores while allowing silent breathing.
- **Zero Contact**: Do not touch the moths, the salamanders, or the ferns. They exist in a state of perfect turgor. Your body heat and oils will disrupt their equilibrium, causing them to crumble into dust or, more dangerously, transferring their stasis to you via skin contact. Wear thin silk inner gloves under your wool mitts.
- **The Berry Rule**: You will see clay bowls containing berries in a permanent state of *being berry*. Do not eat them. Do not even smell them deeply. They are not food; they are geological specimens of a moment that refused to pass.

---

### VII. Psychological Defense Against the Established
The weirdness (1.3) manifests as absence-in-presence—the cloak with the shoulder impression, the fingerprints in floating dust.

- **Identity Mantras**: Before entering, memorize a short, rhythmic poem or sequence of numbers that requires active recitation. Repeat it silently every ten minutes. The silence and stasis will erode your sense of self-boundary; the mantra is a thread back to your dynamic nature.
- **Avoid the Stools**: You will see seating carved from whale vertebrae. Do not sit. The posture of rest invites synchronization. If you must pause, remain standing, weight on one leg, ready to move.
- **The Soot Ceiling**: Notice the black lace patterns of ancient fires fixed on the ceiling. Do not bring flame. Do not bring light sources brighter than the ice-dome's milk-light. Adding new soot patterns is impossible—the ceiling rejects change—but the attempt creates cognitive dissonance that induces nausea.

---

### VIII. Emergency Extraction
If you feel your heartbeat synchronizing with the silence, if the air begins to feel breathable rather than viscous, if you find yourself thinking *"I could simply wait here"*:

1. **Move Immediately**: Crawl toward the slickstone slope. Do not look back at the city. Looking back is what created the entrance—attention erodes the stone, and your gaze will lengthen the passage, making escape impossible.
2. **Physical Tether**: If traveling with a partner (recommended), maintain a line of braided sinew (not synthetic rope) between you. If one becomes established, the other must pull them out by main force. Do not wait for the established partner to "come around"—they won't.
3. **Thermal Shock**: If extraction is delayed, apply cold packs to your carotid arteries (carry chemical cold packs in an interior pocket). The shock can disrupt the metabolic slowing.

---

### IX. Post-Visit Quarantine
Upon exit, you will carry the humidity in your wool for days.

- **Decompression**: Spend 24 hours in a warm (not hot), dry environment. Do not rush the thawing—rapid temperature change can cause the suspended stasis in your lungs (if you inhaled deeply) to crystallize.
- **Medical Scan**: Check for "temporal lag"—a condition where your cellular metabolism continues to operate at Rinse-speed. Symptoms include: skin that does not bruise when pinched, tears that do not fall, and a heartbeat that remains stubbornly slow despite exertion. If detected, aggressive physiotherapy and thermal stimulation are required.

---

**Final Warning**: The Rinse is not hostile. It is a haven for things that could not bear to pass away. It will not attack you; it will simply make room for you. Pack light, move quietly, and remember: **entropy is waiting outside, and it is jealous.**
