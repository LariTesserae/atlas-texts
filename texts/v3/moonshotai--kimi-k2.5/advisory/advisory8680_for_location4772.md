**Preparation Briefing: The Creche (Weirdness Coefficient 1.6)**
*Classification: Micro-civilization, Biological Urbanism, Extreme Environment*

You are not entering a cave. You are entering a conversation that has been happening in the dark for less than a geological heartbeat—a city built not by hands but by persuasion, where the architects are thumb-sized, silent, and will recycle your corpse with such efficiency that your death will make no sound. Prepare accordingly.

---

### **I. Psychological Conditioning**

Before you crawl through the peat, you must accept three truths:

1. **Smallness is not simplicity.** The Creche occupies a space no larger than a merchant’s wagon, yet it contains infrastructure, agriculture, migration patterns, and technology. You will feel the psychological pressure of the infinite compressed into the claustrophobic. Train in sensory deprivation tanks. Learn to find vastness in the microscopic.

2. **Silence is sovereignty.** The fauna have abolished sound. Your coughing, your gear rattling, your heartbeat hammering in your ears—these are acts of violence here. Practice motionlessness. Learn to swallow silently. If you carry a clock, remove it. If you have a habit of humming, cut it out. You are entering a temple where noise is the only blasphemy.

3. **Darkness is not absence.** At 0.1 lux, photons die here. Do not bring light. Not red light, not UV, not bioluminescent markers. The nursery-hangs emit faint glows, but these are not for you—they texture the darkness, they do not illuminate it. You must learn to see with your skin, your palms, the hairs on your neck. Spend weeks navigating familiar spaces blindfolded until you can read topography by air pressure and humidity.

---

### **II. Physical Preparation**

**The Cold**
The stone stores ancient cold; the air is freezing but supersaturated with moisture. Standard arctic gear will fail—you need thermal protection that functions while soaking wet. Consider:
- **Wetsuit layering**: Neoprene or advanced hydrophobic underlayers that trap water as insulation.
- **Chemical heat packs**: Silent, enclosed, no combustion. The cold here preserves; it does not kill quickly, but it will slow you until you become part of the architecture.
- **Hand protection**: The limestone is "sharp enough to cut." You will enter on hands and knees through peat, then break through crust into emptiness. Wear cut-resistant palm guards that allow tactile sensitivity—you will need to feel the root-floor pulsing beneath you.

**The Wet**
The air weeps. Condensation beads on every surface and never evaporates. 
- **Sealed gear**: Every zipper must be watertight, every seam taped. Your pack must be submersible because the lower level is flooded, and the middle level drips with metronomic inevitability.
- **Respiratory filtration**: The air tastes of copper and petrichor. This indicates mineral saturation and fungal spore density. A full-face respirator is recommended—not for oxygen, but to prevent you from introducing foreign bacteria into their fungal paddies and to keep their spores from colonizing your lungs.

**Silence Engineering**
- **Felt-wrapping**: Every metal object must be muffled. Carabiners, buckles, zippers—these must be wrapped in wool felt or soft leather. 
- **Soft anatomy**: Remove anything rigid. No knee pads with plastic shells (use gel inserts). No hard-soled boots (use felted wool or soft rubber). You will be crawling on a mat of hair-fine roots; crunching them is audible, and the city will respond to the wound.

---

### **III. Navigation Protocol**

**Entry**
You will find it by accident, or you will simulate accident. Crawl on hands and knees through frozen peat until your palm breaks through crust. Do not arrest your fall. Tumble forward. The entrance is a wound; the root-floor is already creeping to seal it. You have perhaps hours before the breach heals.

**The Three Levels**
1. **Lower (The Flooded Paddies)**: Do not enter unless you are prepared for total immersion in freezing fungal bath. The bracket fungi here are tended like crops. If you must descend, use the vine bridges—test them first; they creak under weight, and creaking draws attention.
   
2. **Middle (The Thoroughfare)**: This is where you crouch. The avenues are compressed spore-dust—do not kick them up. The fauna migrate here in rivers. When the biological tide flows (and it never stops), stand still. Let them flow around your ankles like gel-filled water. They are carrying eggs from upper left to lower right. Interrupting this is genocide.

3. **Upper (The Nursery-Hangs)**: Suspended gelatinous pods. They glow faintly, texturing the dark. Do not touch them. Do not look directly at them (your eyes will try to adjust, fail, and strain). These are their children.

**Reading the City**
- **The Silver Threads**: If you have a sensitive nose or chemical detection gear, you may see their pheromonal trails as silver threads in the air. Learn to read these as street signs. Following a thick thread will lead you to infrastructure; crossing perpendicular to the flow will take you through residential districts.
- **The Drip**: Water drips with metronomic patience. Use this as your clock. If the rhythm changes, the city is adjusting—roots are growing, walls are weeping new minerals. Freeze when the rhythm breaks.

---

### **IV. Fauna Interaction: The Silent Citizens**

They are not insects. They are not quite flesh. They are builders with ice needles and slate scrapers, and they have achieved civilization in a coffin.

**Anatomy of Encounter**
- **Geometry**: Their bodies shift between hexagonal plates, serpentine curves, and origami knots. Do not be alarmed when the creature on your wrist folds into a shape that hurts to look at. This is rest, not threat.
- **The Seven Legs**: Arranged radially, not in pairs. They will cling to your clothing. Do not brush them off. They are tasting your humidity gradient, reading if you are predator, prey, or architecture.
- **Antennae**: Long, feathery, tasting the air. If they touch your face, hold your breath. Your exhalation contains chemicals that may translate as aggression or mating signals in their language.

**Behavioral Taboos**
- **No Light**: They are eyeless. Light is meaningless to them, but heat signatures disrupt their chemical trails. Your body heat creates convection currents they can feel. Move slowly to minimize thermal disturbance.
- **No Death**: If you are injured and bleeding, leave immediately. Blood triggers their recycling instinct. They will consume you silently, efficiently, and the city will absorb your nutrients before you finish screaming (which you must not do anyway).
- **No Scents**: Bathe in unscented water for three days prior. No soap, no perfume, no sweat. Foreign pheromones pollute their language.

**Tool Recognition**
If you see them carrying ice needles or slate wedges, you are witnessing infrastructure maintenance. Do not interfere. Do not "help" prune the roots. Their agriculture is precise; your assistance is vandalism.

---

### **V. Survival Contingencies**

**The Sealing**
The root-floor is "hungry to seal the wound" of your entrance. Monitor the breach. If white roots begin to knit across the exit, you must decide: leave immediately, or accept that you are becoming a permanent resident (and eventually, compost).

**Food and Water**
- **Water**: The condensing weep is mineral-heavy (copper taste) but likely potable. Collect it from stalactites before it hits the spore-dust floors.
- **Food**: Do not eat the fungi. Do not eat the fauna. Both are part of the cognitive infrastructure of the city. Eating here is cannibalism. Bring high-calorie paste in silent, sealed tubes.

**Hypothermia Protocol**
If your core temperature drops (and it will), do not shiver. Shivering is noise. The cold here preserves; it will keep you alive but slowed. Accept the torpor. Move less. The fauna move slowly too—speed is not required here, only patience.

**Exit**
When leaving, reverse the tumble. Do not stand upright until you are back in the peat. Check your gear for stowaways—the fauna are curious and may cling to your pack. Brushing them off outside would be abduction. Shake yourself out in the dark, gently, letting them fall back to the root-floor.

---

### **VI. Ethical Imperatives**

This is a young civilization, geologically speaking. It has not learned to fear humans because it has not met them. You are the giant, the monster, the potential cataclysm.

- **Leave no trace**: Not a footprint in the spore-dust, not a drop of sweat, not a hair.
- **Document silently**: If you must record, use tactile braille-readers or chemical sniffers. No cameras with autofocus sounds. No whispered voice notes.
- **The Weirdness Coefficient**: At 1.6, this place is strange but stable. Do not destabilize it. Do not introduce concepts. They have built a city in darkness; do not bring them the idea of light.

You are visiting a freezing, weeping, silent rush of life that has solved urban planning without fire, without wheels, without voice. Treat it with the reverence you would give to the first city ever built—because for these citizens, it is. 

Enter on your knees. Leave on your knees. And in between, be as silent as the stone that dreams them.
