**Preparation Brief for the Singing Vault**
*Classification: Bio-Architectural Habitat, Pre-Metallurgical Tech Level, Extreme Sensory Environment*

---

### **I. Psychological & Physiological Conditioning**

Before you descend, understand that you are entering a **lung**, not a room. The Vault is a twenty-meter-deep bubble of sustained metabolism—warm, wet, and violently alive. You will not find silence here, nor solitude, nor any surface that does not bear the fingerprint of ancient intention. 

**Acclimatization Protocol:**
- **Heat Training:** Spend three weeks in a sauna maintained at 32°C (89°F) with 95% humidity while performing manual tasks. The Vault’s "blood-warm" environment induces profuse sweating that cannot evaporate; you must learn to move efficiently without overheating.
- **Sensory Overload Inoculation:** Subject yourself to 72-hour periods in anechoic chambers filled with recorded bat clicks, beetle stridulation, and irregular dripping. The Vault’s sound is not merely *loud* (2.5)—it is a *pressure* that resonates in the sternum and dental fillings. You must train your nervous system not to panic when your own heartbeat is drowned out by the sizzle of ten thousand echolocation calls.
- **Claustrophobia Desensitization:** Practice navigating crawlspaces lined with dried organic material (corn husks work well) while blindfolded. The Vault’s honeycomb of terracotta cylinders reduces open space to narrow, wandering corridors where you cannot raise your arms. The walls *breathe*—literally, with the collective respiration of the Glass-Wing Bats. You must learn to accept this motion as background noise, not threat.

**Medical Prep:**
- **Histoplasmosis Prophylaxis:** The air is thick with spores, guano dust, and fungal matter. Begin a course of antifungals three days prior.
- **Broad-Spectrum Antiparasitics:** Pseudoscorpions, mites, and gnat larvae will find you. Accept this, but deny them purchase.
- **Inner Ear Stabilization:** The constant humidity and pressure changes can disrupt balance. Equilibrium exercises are mandatory.

### **II. Equipment Specifications**

**The Envelope (Your Second Skin):**
You need a suit that handles **condensation, not precipitation**. The water in the Vault doesn’t fall so much as *accumulate* on you.
- **Base Layer:** Merino wool or advanced hydrophobic polymer that wicks sweat away from skin despite ambient saturation. Cotton will kill you here—it stays wet and breeds bacteria.
- **Shell:** Tyvek or similar breathable-yet-impermeable coverall, hooded, with elasticized wrists and ankles. The scarabs are sparrow-sized and inquisitive; they will crawl into cuffs and collars.
- **Gloves:** Thin leather, tactile-sensitive. You will be climbing agave ropes and bone ladders; the ancient fibers are surprisingly strong but abrasive.
- **Footwear:** Soft-soled, high-ankle boots with excellent grip. The floor is stone paved with centuries of mineral accretion—treacherously slick—and you may need to grip cylindrical terracotta edges while climbing.

**Sensory Mitigation:**
- **Hearing:** Bring **variable-attenuation earplugs** (not noise-canceling headphones—you need to hear the structural groans that warn of shifts in the ancient masonry). The Vault’s song ranges from infrasonic rumbles to ultrasonic clicks; filter the pain, preserve the data.
- **Vision:** 
  - Primary: Headlamp with **red-spectrum LED** (minimum 300 lumens). White light disrupts the bats and draws the hunting blind snakes.
  - Secondary: Bioluminescent patches (no batteries, no heat) strapped to your shoulders and knees. The oil lamps in the Vault smoke heavily; you need backup that doesn’t asphyxiate you further.
  - Eye protection: Clear goggles sealed against the "snow" of spores and bat under-fur that drifts in the light-shafts.
- **Respiration:** A half-mask respirator with P100 filters. The scent profile—burning tallow, bat musk, composting guano—is overwhelming and psychologically disorienting.

**Navigation & Survival:**
- **3D Mapping Gear:** The Vault is a vertical maze. Standard compasses spin uselessly near the geothermal flues. Use inertial navigation or deploy breadcrumb beacons (sound-emitting, low-frequency, non-disruptive to bats).
- **Water Collection:** Paradoxically, you need this in a place dripping with water. The condensation on the petrified brick is mineral-heavy and biologically active. Bring a **solar still** or filtration straw for the collection bowls beneath the drips—their clay surfaces actually purify the water through slow percolation.
- **Quarantine Protocols:** Seal all gear in antimicrobial bags. The Vault’s ecosystem is closed and balanced; your skin flora could be biological warfare here. Wipe down with alcohol before entry.

### **III. Entry & Movement Protocol**

**The Descent:**
You will likely enter through one of the six light-shafts—primitive chimneys lined with glazed tile. These are **not staircases**. They are vertical shafts barely wider than your shoulders. You descend by chimneying (pressing back and feet against opposing walls) or by the ancient agave ropes, which creak but hold. 

*Check the ropes.* They are centuries old, maintained only by the constant humidity that prevents desiccation. Test each handhold with 50% weight before committing.

**The Three Laws of Locomotion:**
1. **Move Like a Ghost:** The bats react to sudden changes in air pressure and temperature. Move slowly enough that your body heat dissipates before it reaches the cylinders. Fast movement creates panic ripples—thousands of wings beating at once creates windstorms that can knock you from the ropes.
2. **Three Points of Contact:** The ceramic matrices are unstable. The terracotta cylinders are hand-thrown, irregular, and stacked in honeycombs. Never trust a single handhold. The "walls" are actually the outer layer of the nursery; you are climbing on their nursery walls.
3. **Respect the Floor:** The scarab beetles are engaged in sacred work, rolling dung to the composting pits. Do not step on their grooves—these are roads older than your civilization. Walk on the paving stones between the grooves, or risk enraging a sparrow-sized beetle with shovel-mandibles.

### **IV. Biological Hazards & Etiquette**

**The Inhabitants:**
- **Glass-Wing Bats:** They will not bite you unless you grasp them, but their urine is acidic and constant. Keep your hood up. If one lands on you, freeze. Let it investigate. They are checking you for parasites—let them.
- **Scarab Beetles:** Generally indifferent unless you obstruct their dung-rolling. If one raises its forelimbs, back away. They can stridulate loud enough to be painful at close range.
- **Pseudoscorpions & Mites:** They live in the brick interstices. They are hunting each other, not you, but they will explore any crevice in your clothing. Check seals hourly.
- **Blind Snakes (Occasional):** Descend through light-shafts to hunt. If you see one—pale, sinuous, moving with purpose—freeze and look up. They are deaf to the Vault’s song but sensitive to vibration. Let them pass.
- **Fungus Gnats:** They will form living clouds in your exhaled CO2. Do not wave at them; you might strike a bat.

**The Central Dais:**
There is a carved soapstone platform at the spiral’s center. The shallow basin there holds water that never overflows, vibrating with the chamber’s drone. 

**Do not touch the water.** The surface tension is maintained by infrasonic standing waves; introducing your finger might collapse a containment field older than iron. It may be a sensor, a battery, or a memorial. Treat it as holy.

---

### **V. Emergency Procedures**

**If You Fall into the Cylinders:**
You will plunge into a tube filled with bats. They will panic. You will be cut by claws and wings, deafened by their screams. 
- **Protect your face.** Curl into a ball.
- **Do not flail.** You will crush juveniles.
- **Wait.** The colony will resettle in 3-5 minutes. Then climb out slowly using the rope ladders.

**If You Become Disoriented (The Song Sickness):**
The constant noise can induce nausea and vertigo. 
- **Find a light-shaft.** Look up at the daylight. The beams are your only cardinal direction.
- **Hydrate** with your purified water.
- **Hum.** Match your vocal frequency to the falling drops (*tonk... tonk...*). It resets the inner ear.

**If the Temperature Drops:**
The geothermal system is ancient. If you feel the warmth fade, the flues may be blocked or shifting. **Leave immediately.** The bats will enter torpor, the humidity will condense into actual rain, and you will face hypothermia in 30°C air (wet cold is deadly).

---

### **VI. The Departure**

When you ascend, you will carry the Vault with you—the smell of tallow and guano in your hair, the ringing in your ears, the phantom sensation of breathing walls. 

Quarantine your gear for 72 hours. The mites, the spores, the microscopic life of this place has evolved in isolation for millennia. Do not introduce it to other worlds.

And when you sleep afterward, you will hear it still: the *sizzle-click* of the Glass-Wings, the heartbeat of the drips. This is normal. The Vault has added your breath to its song, briefly, and the echo takes time to fade.

**Remember:** You are the first biped to enter in centuries who did not build it. Walk softly. The bricks remember hands.
