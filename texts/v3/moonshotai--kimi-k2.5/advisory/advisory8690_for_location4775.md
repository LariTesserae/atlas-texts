**Preparation Protocol for the Resonance Plain**

You are not preparing for a journey. You are preparing to become a temporary, tolerated imperfection in a world that has achieved geological enlightenment. The Plain does not care if you survive, but it will notice if you violate its fundamental axiom: *stability is virtue*. Prepare to move as the water moves—lazily, without turbulence, across surfaces that have forgotten the meaning of "uphill."

---

### **I. The Body: Amphibious Adaptation**

**Footwear Abandonment**
Standard boots are an act of violence here. The gneiss weeps; the humidity presses up through any sole; your feet will never be dry. Bring **amphibious trekking sandals** with drain ports and soles rated for wet basalt-grade friction, or better yet, accept the ancient contract: bare feet calloused thick as leather. The stone is smooth as glass and warm—if you can tolerate the silver sheen of water that will permanently coat your skin. If you must wear shoes, choose those designed for canyoneering: quick-dry, grippy, and willing to hold water rather than futilely repel it.

**The Fungal War**
Every crevice of your body will be constantly moist. Pack **powdered antifungals** in industrial quantities—not for treatment, but for prophylaxis. Treat your feet, groin, and skin folds twice daily with clay-based barriers before the morning condensation begins. The lichens here grow in centuries; athlete's foot will colonize you in hours. Bring **silver-threaded socks** if you can find them; merino will rot.

**Skin as Membrane**
You will breathe through your skin here, whether you intend to or not. The humidity is total, a second atmosphere pressing against the first. Wear **loose, long, light-colored linens** that can wick without chafing, or garments made of unprocessed hemp that welcome the moisture rather than fight it. Tight synthetic fabrics will become bioreactors of bacteria within a day. Accept that you will be damp. Stop checking if you are "dry." Dryness is a memory from other worlds.

### **II. Sensory Hardening: The Drone and the Haze**

**Auditory Conditioning**
You cannot escape the chorus. The marsh frogs do not sleep; they maintain a bass note so constant it will seem to come from inside your skull, a tinnitus of the landscape. Before arrival, spend weeks sleeping with **brown noise generators** set to 80 decibels to acclimate your nervous system. Bring **bone-conduction earplugs**—not to silence (silence here is physically impossible), but to shift the frequency into something your inner ear can negotiate. Do not bring noise-canceling headphones; the dragonflies (sparrow-sized, metallic, territorial) sometimes investigate foreign objects, and you need your peripheral hearing to track their approach through the thick air.

**Visual Calibration**
The light is diffuse, filtered through the eternal haze of evaporation. The horizon is a straight line drawn with a ruler; there are no landmarks, no shadows with sharp edges, no perspective cues. This causes **horizontal vertigo**—the brain panics when it cannot judge distance. Train yourself beforehand by navigating whiteout conditions or featureless salt flats while blindfolded, using only a gyroscopic compass and the feel of air pressure changes. Bring **polarized lenses with yellow tinting** to cut through the vapor haze, but prepare for the psychological weight of seeing *nothing* but stone and sky for hours.

**The Fog Protocols**
At dusk, visibility stratifies. You will see your feet clearly, the middle distance as cotton, and the horizon will cease to exist. If you must move at night, use **ultrasonic proximity sensors** or a white cane—the condensation fogs all optics within minutes. Mark your camp with **chemical light sticks** hung at varying heights; sound carries strangely in the humidity, and you cannot trust auditory location when the bass drone vibrates through your chest cavity.

### **III. Navigation in the Finished World**

**The Tyranny of Flatness**
GPS may struggle with a surface that varies in elevation by millimeters across kilometers. The Plain offers no handholds for satellite triangulation. Bring an **inertial navigation unit** or learn **celstial navigation using circumpolar stars** (the haze often clears overhead even when it veils the horizon). More importantly: abandon the desire for progress. Distance here is measured not in kilometers but in patience. The water sheets migrate at the speed of continental drift; match their velocity.

**Water as Road**
The shallow sheets and millennia-old puddles are your only topography. Learn to read the **silver sheen migration**—the water retreats into microscopic fractures at night and bleeds upward by day. Walk where the stone is darkest; that is where the gneiss is currently weeping, and the footing, while wet, is less likely to harbor the slippery algal films that coat the dryer (relatively) high points.

### **IV. Temporal Adjustment: The Long Now**

**Psychological Entrainment**
Humans are built for pulses—storms, sunsets, seasons. The Plain operates on geological steady-state. A cloud takes three days to cross the sky. You must decelerate your metabolism of sensation or you will hallucinate from boredom and auditory sameness. Practice **Zazen meditation** for months prior, specifically the technique of *shikantaza*—"just sitting"—until you can experience three hours as a single breath. Bring a **mechanical pocket watch** with a visible escapement, not to tell time, but to remind yourself that mechanical rhythms still exist when the endless afternoon seems frozen.

**The Patience Diet**
Eat slowly. Digest slowly. Your body will want to match the tempo of the lungfish gulping air with prehistoric lassitude. Pack foods that require lengthy preparation—dried beans, whole grains that must be soaked (though not in the local water, see below). The act of cooking will anchor you to human temporality; if you eat only ration bars, you risk temporal dissociation, slipping into the Plain's four-billion-year perspective where your lifespan is a fleeting thermal fluctuation.

---

### **V. Biological Protocols**

**The Water Question**
The water sheets look pristine—ancient,.filtered through stone older than photosynthesis—but they are alive with **chytrid spores** and slow-moving protists that view your immune system as a novel substrate. Boil all water for **twenty minutes** (the altitude is effectively sea-level, but the pathogens here are patient). Better yet, bring a **solar still** and extract humidity from the air rather than drinking the stone's sweat; the atmospheric moisture is sterile, the puddle water is a biological archive.

**Predation (Optional)**
Predators exist but are "lazy," operating on the despair of abundance. Still, the dragonflies are large enough to puncture skin with ovipositors, and amphibian toxins here have had eons to diversify. Carry **broad-spectrum antivenom** for unknown alkaloids. Move slowly through the cycad groves; the tree frogs trill from specific perches that have been used for centuries, and disturbing their locations (marked by generations of guano chemistry) is considered "change," which is "suspect."

---

### **VI. The Ethics of Impermanence**

**The Satiated Landscape**
You will want to build a cairn, arrange stones, leave a mark. **Resist.** This place is *finished*. To add is redundant; to alter is an insult to four billion years of refinement. Camp on bare rock and leave no compression in the algal films. If you must shelter, use a **self-supporting hammock with rock-sling straps** that distribute weight without grinding the lichens—remember, their expansion is measured in centuries, and your footprint will outlast your civilization.

**The Song**
Do not shout. Do not sing loudly. The drone has achieved a steady-state; your voice will introduce dissonance. If you must communicate with companions, use **subvocal bone microphones** or gestures. Whispers carry far in the humidity. Better yet, learn to communicate through touch or in writing. Let your presence be a silent parenthesis in the endless amphibian chorus.

Walk slowly. Breathe through your skin. Listen to the bass note until it becomes your heartbeat. Do not try to leave a mark.

Leave only when the stone has forgotten you were there—which, if you have behaved with appropriate humility, will be approximately three seconds after you depart.
