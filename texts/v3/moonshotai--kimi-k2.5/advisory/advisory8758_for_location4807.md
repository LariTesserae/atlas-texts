**Preparation Guide for the Basalt Terrace of Unmoving Water**

**Risk Assessment:** Minor physical hazard (laceration), Major psychological hazard (temporal dissociation, sensory monotony), Extreme existential hazard (stasis assimilation).  
**Duration Recommendation:** No more than 72 hours objective time. Sunset to sunrise is not advised.  
**Companion Protocol:** Mandatory minimum of two travelers, bound by wrist-line (see Section VII).

---

### I. Silence as Equipment
You are not preparing for *quiet*. You are preparing for **0.3**—a sound rating lower than the vacuum of an anechoic chamber, a silence so architectural it will press against your eardrums. On the Terrace, you will hear the瓣膜 of your own heart snapping shut, the aqueous humor sloshing in your eyeballs when you blink, the creak of your cartilage settling.

**Training:** Spend seventy-two hours in a sensory deprivation tank *before* departure. This is not optional. You must learn to tolerate the orchestra of your interior without panic. Tinnitus—normally masked by ambient noise—will become your companion. Accept it, or it will become a scream.

**Gear Muffling:** Every item must be deadened. Pad all metal with felt soaked in beeswax. Replace nylon straps with oiled leather (synthetics crinkle). Apply silicone gel to every zipper, every buckle. Your water canteen must be wrapped in twelve layers of wool felt; even the gurgle of liquid moving inside a vessel is a violation here. Practice moving in complete silence. If you can hear your own footsteps in training, you will thunder like a god on the Terrace.

**The Vow:** Do not speak once you breach the cloud layer. Not whispers. Not breath. The air itself resents vibration. Communication must be through pre-arranged hand-signals or touch-presses. throat microphones that pick up subvocalization are permitted for emergencies, but transmitting any noise into that space feels, intuitively, like bleeding into clean water.

---

### II. Armor Against the Newborn Stone
The geology here is *young*—not gentle, not weathered. The basalt has not yet agreed to be soft. It retains the memory of the magma’s violence.

**The Cut Protocol:** Wear garments woven with basalt fiber or Kevlar-threaded canvas. The obsidian edges will slice through denim like through fog. Gloves must be chainmail-lined; you will need to steady yourself against walls, and every surface is a blade recently drawn from the forge. Wrap your palms in soft copper foil (carry spare rolls)—the inhabitants used copper tools for a reason: it dampens the ringing that stone-on-skin contact produces in this silence.

**Footwear:** Standard hiking boots are too loud and too thin. You need felt-soled tabi boots with puncture-resistant Kevlar midsoles. The porosity of the basalt creates irregular, glass-sharp cavities that will penetrate rubber. You must walk without footfall—the inhabitants move like mist settling—but you must also avoid laceration. Practice *komusō* walking: rolling from heel to toe with no impact, weight distributed through the hip.

**Ocular Protection:** The aqueous haze filters light into a perpetual underwater shimmer, but the humidity condenses on eyelashes. Bring vented goggles treated with hydrophobic nano-coating. Without them, you will be constantly blinking away beads of warm water, and the sound of your own blinking will echo in your skull.

### III. Chronological Anchors (The Stasis Defense)
This is the critical preparation. The Terrace exists in dynamic equilibrium—a photograph of a world holding its breath. Age does not touch stone or fern. If you remain too long, the equilibrium will claim you. Wounds may cease to heal (or cease to bleed). Hair may stop growing. You may become part of the architecture, standing silent between the stone shelters, waiting.

**Bring Entropy with You:**
- **Mechanical watches:** Wind them hourly. The ticking is your heartbeat’s proxy. If the watch stops, you have been there too long.
- **Ice clocks:** Carry insulated flasks of crushed ice. Observe the melting. In a place of stasis, phase change is rebellion. When the ice stops melting, leave immediately.
- **Rust tablets:** Iron filings pressed into salt tablets. Watch them oxidize. The red bloom is proof that time still passes for you.
- **Journal:** Mandatory entries every thirty minutes objective time. Use a graphite stylus on clay tablets (paper will saturate). Write what has changed about *you*: "My left calf itches." "I am hungry." If you cannot find a difference between entries, you are beginning to equilibrate.

**Buddy Binding:** Tie yourself to your companion with a 10-meter line of red silk. The color red fades quickly in the aqueous light; if your partner’s line begins to look gray to you, you are losing color perception—a first symptom of assimilation.

### IV. The Hydrological Paradox
Water is abundant and absent. It saturates the air at 2.4 (supersaturated—impossible, yet here), condenses on your skin warm as blood, yet leaves no wetness behind. It fills pools that never evaporate, drips from eaves that never erode.

**To Drink or Not to Drink:** The text suggests the inhabitants "take water that is replenished but never changed." This suggests the water is chemically perfect, perhaps too perfect. It may lack the chaotic impurities that drive biological processes, or it may preserve you in your current state—trapping injury, trapping intoxication, trapping youth.

**Filtration Strategy:** Bring a solar still. Collect the condensate and re-boil it, introducing impurities (a pinch of soil from your home dimension) to break the equilibrium. Do not drink directly from the pools. The water tastes of nothing, and nothing tastes like forever.

**Hydration Status:** You will not feel the water evaporating from your skin because it doesn't. You may overheat despite the mist. Bring cooling vests with phase-change materials (paraffin waxes), as your sweat will not cool you—it will simply bead and fall, soundlessly.

---

### V. Nutritional Void
There are no animals. The ferns and mosses photosynthesize in eternal stasis—they do not grow, fruit, or die. They are decorative, not edible. A salad of Terrace vegetation would pass through you unchanged, or worse, might bind you to the chemical stasis of the rock.

**Rations:** Dehydrated, high-calorie, noisy foods (crackers, nuts) must be repackaged into soft squeeze-tubes to avoid chewing sounds. The silence makes chewing audible to yourself, which can induce paranoia. Bring foods that *change* as you eat them: ripening cheeses, fruits that brown when exposed to air. Watch the oxidation with each bite. It keeps you real.

**Waste Management:** Defecation and urination are acts of change and chemistry. The equilibrium resists them. Bury waste deep in imported soil, or carry it out. Do not introduce your biology into the terraces; the stone might reject it, or worse, preserve it—an immortal insult to the silence.

---

### VI. Navigating the Vast Stillness
Sightlines run for miles, yet the mist softens edges. The town-sized expanse is sparse; buildings are separated by naked rock. You can see your destination, but the sameness of the terrain will disorient.

**Mapping:** Use ultraviolet markers. The aqueous haze filters normal light, but UV penetrates. Mark your path in fluorescent chalk that will fade slightly (a comfort) over hours. Do not rely on the stone itself; it is uniform black, and the pools reflect the sky in perfect mirrors, creating vertiginous false floors.

**The Hot Springs:** Avoid the steam columns. They rise *unmoving*—a physical impossibility that suggests localized time dilation. Passing through one might deposit you elsewhere on the Terrace hours or days later, or compress three steps into three years of objective time.

---

### VII. Emergency Protocols: The Slowing
If you or your companion begins to move too smoothly, too quietly—if their footsteps cease to make even the softest felt-muffled thump—if they stand too long staring at a fern that is neither growing nor withering:

**Immediate Extraction:**
1. Do not touch the stone. The obsidian transmits the stasis like a conduit.
2. Administer "chaos stimulus": Break a glass vial of strong scent (ammonia, peppermint). Smell is the last sense to equilibrate here.
3. Force movement: The companion must physically carry the affected traveler off the plateau. Do not wait for them to "agree." By the time they agree, they are architecture.
4. Descent: The climb down is more dangerous than the ascent. The young stone cuts ropes. Use chain ladders of soft copper links (malleable, silent) rather than aluminum carabiners.

---

### VIII. The Departure
When you leave, you will carry the silence in your ears for days. The sound of wind in trees will sound like screaming. Rain will feel violent.

Check your body carefully. Count your scars—ensure you have not lost any. Check your fingernails; they should have grown in the time since you entered. If they haven't, see a doctor who understands temporal physics.

Drink something bitter, something that burns, something that changes flavor as it sits in the glass. Remind yourself that you are not stone. You decay, you grow, you make noise. Celebrate it.

The Basalt Terrace of Unmoving Water is beautiful the way a held breath is beautiful—suspended, precious, and suffocating. Visit, admire the eternal moment, but remember: you are a creature of breath, of out and in, of noise and silence in alternation. Do not let the photograph develop you into its negative.
