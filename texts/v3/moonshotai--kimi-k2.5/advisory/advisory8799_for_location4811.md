**Preparation Protocol for the Green Manufactory**  
*A Comprehensive Field Guide for the Interdimensional Traveler*

You are preparing to enter a space where the distinction between architecture and organism has dissolved completely. The Green Manufactory does not house industry; it *metabolizes* it. You will not be walking through rooms so much as navigating the digestive system of a continent-sized tree that has politely agreed to tolerate human-scale commerce within its vasculature. Treat this not as tourism but as a temporary symbiosis. Prepare accordingly.

---

### **I. Physiological Conditioning (Begin 2-3 Weeks Prior)**

**The Humidity Protocol**  
The chamber maintains 98% humidity at 26–28°C. This is not "tropical weather." This is *respiratory*. You are entering a lung. Your sweat will not evaporate. Your skin will never dry. 

- **Acclimatization**: Spend time in botanical conservatories or sauna complexes, but crucially, remain clothed during the sessions. You must condition your dermis to the sensation of perpetual dampness without pruning or maceration. 
- **Antifungal Regimen**: Begin systemic antifungal medication three days before arrival. The air carries spores from centuries of lignin decomposition. Without pharmaceutical intervention, you will develop dermatophyte infections within hours and respiratory mycosis within days.
- **Hydration Strategy**: Paradoxically, you must drink more water than you lose. The saturated air confuses the body's thirst mechanisms. Pre-hydrate with electrolyte-dense solutions, but avoid diuretics. Your urine should remain pale despite the sweating.

**Temporal Recalibration**  
The Manufactory operates on dendrochronological time. Before arrival, practice *waiting* as an active discipline:
- Sit in silence for forty-five minutes without checking a timepiece.
- Learn to perceive the 45-second industrial exhalation cycle—the "breath" of the hydraulic accumulators—as your new heartbeat. Download a metronome app and listen to 0.75 BPM (one beat every forty-five seconds) until your nervous system synchronizes with this glacial pulse. If you cannot feel the groove of this rhythm, you will find the environment maddening.

### **II. Wardrobe & Personal Equipment**

**The Narrow Passage Doctrine**  
The density is absolute. You will turn sideways. You will duck under bulging pneumatic lines wrapped in bark. The defile entrance alone requires compressing your shoulders between fused buttress roots.

- **Silhouette**: Wear form-fitting, non-bulky clothing. No backpacks—use chest-mounted utility rigs or thigh holsters. The walkways are shelf fungi and aerial roots; a protruding carabiner or loose strap can snag, causing you to lose balance over the black water pools.
- **Fabric**: Moisture-wicking synthetic base layers covered by waxed canvas or treated leather. Cotton rots here; wool mildews instantly. Your outer layer must resist the constant mineral condensation dripping from above while allowing your body heat to escape.
- **Footwear**: Soft-soled, high-ankled boots with aggressive tread for the polished cork-like surfaces of the compressed root walkways. The shelf fungi become slippery with condensation. Gaiters are mandatory—the floor topology of root-knees and shallow reservoirs will attempt to pour black water into your boots with every step.
- **Headgear**: A close-fitting cap with a brim to shield your eyes from the "calculated irrigation"—fat drops of condensation that fall with surprising force after their journey through the canopy layers.

**Essential Gear**  
- **Silica Infrastructure**: Carry industrial quantities of desiccant. Store electronics (your multiverse transponder, lighting, documentation tools) in vacuum-sealed pouches with color-changing humidity indicators. Paper documents must be synthetic or sealed in waterproof laminates; standard cellulose turns to pulp within hours.
- **Lighting**: The diffuse overhead light (1.6/3 intensity) fails in the alcoves and beneath the heavy root masses. Bring bioluminescent markers or chemical light sticks rather than battery torches—the humidity destroys electrical contacts, and the heat from lamps attracts condensation that shorts circuits.
- **Respiration**: A half-mask respirator with organic vapor cartridges. While the air is rich in oxygen, the "sweet-rot smell of cambium under hydraulic pressure" can trigger nausea or hallucinations in the unacclimated. The mask also protects against spore inhalation during the periodic releases of steam from the enlarged lenticels.
- **Navigation Tools**: The address system is carved into living cambium. Bring soft graphite pencils (ink runs) and waterproof notebooks to transcribe the growth-ring notations that serve as street addresses. A compass is useless due to the iron content in the walls; instead, bring a declinometer to measure the angle of the root-knees, which grow consistently toward the chamber's moisture gradient.
- **Maintenance Blade**: A small, sharp pruning knife. Not for defense—the Manufactory is largely devoid of aggressive fauna—but for personal mobility. Occasionally, new growth encroaches on the walkways between trims. You may need to snip a vine or shave a tender shoot that has blocked your path during your stay. **Etiquette**: Only cut what impedes your immediate passage, and never during the active 45-second exhale cycle.

### **III. Psychological Preparation**

**Embracing Entrapment**  
Claustrophobia is the primary psychological hazard. The walls are growing. The service corridors are gaps between compression-growth rings. You will feel the pressure of the wood.

- Practice confined-space meditation. Visualize the wood not as imprisoning but as *embracing*. The Strangler Fig and Ironwood have agreed to this arrangement; they are not crushing you, they are holding the space open against their own nature.
- Accept that you cannot rush. The elevators ( platforms descending on woven stem-fiber ropes ) take an hour to descend fifteen meters, and this is considered *prompt*. If you demand speed, the environment will break you. Adopt the mindset of a gardener, not a tourist.

**Sensory Management**  
The soundscape is loud (1.8/3) but non-industrial in character. It is the sound of digestion, of hydraulic pressure, of water finding level.

- Bring earplugs filtered to allow low-frequency passage (the subsonic groan of the root torsion, the wet gurgle of the cellulose membranes). You must hear the 45-second exhale to time your movements—attempting to speak or navigate during the synchronized hiss of steam from the lenticel-vents is futile and potentially dangerous (the steam is hot and the vibration disorients).
- The constant percussive drip of calculated irrigation creates a psychological effect similar to water-torture for some. Exposure therapy with dripping taps is recommended.

### **IV. Safety & Biological Hazards**

**The Black Water**  
The reservoirs at the lowest points are "black as tea," reflecting the tangle above. 

- **Never touch the surface** with bare skin. The water is not merely rainwater; it is coolant mixed with pale green sap, containing biochemical extracts from the manufacturing process. It may be caustic, medicinal, or psychoactive depending on the Manufactory's current production cycle.
- **Pneumatophore awareness**: The snorkel-like roots piercing the surface indicate submerged root systems. Do not disturb them; they are breathing for the architecture. If you fall in, do not thrash. The water is often shallow (inches deep in many places), but the root-knees beneath can trap limbs. Move slowly, like syrup, toward the nearest fungal shelf.

**The Living Architecture**  
- **Do not lean against walls for extended periods**. The wood is warm and pulses with hydraulic flow. Prolonged contact can cause pressure sores exacerbated by the humidity, and in rare cases, the cambium may attempt to seal over your clothing if you remain motionless for hours.
- **Watch for growth**: Over the course of a day, you will not see the walls move, but over a week-long stay, you may notice doors (oval plugs of wood on bronze hinges) becoming tighter in their frames. If staying long-term, mark the wall with a scratch (acceptably rude in an emergency, but inform the local stewards) to gauge if the passage is narrowing.

**The Exhale Cycle**  
Every forty-five seconds, the chamber vents steam through bark lenticels. 
- **Warning**: The steam can reach 60°C and carries concentrated biochemicals. Stand clear of the vent-pores (visible as swollen knots in the bark).
- **Synchronization**: Use the exhale to mark time. The fifteen seconds of the cycle (groan to gurgle to hiss) is the only window during which certain heavy valves operate. If you need to open a sealed container or use precision tools, do so during the "inhale" phase between cycles.

### **V. Cultural Conduct & Etiquette**

**The Pace of Commerce**  
If you are here to trade or consult, abandon schedules. The workers (if present) move with dendritic slowness. A conversation may involve ten minutes of silence between sentences while the speaker listens to the water circulation.

- **Pruning Etiquette**: If you witness a worker trimming growth to maintain a walkway, observe the angle of the cut. It is always forty-five degrees, mimicking the chamber's cyclical breath. If you must cut during your visit, mimic this angle.
- **Addresses**: Locations are identified by growth-ring counts and cambium scarring (post codes carved into the living wood). When giving your location to others, describe the wood's texture and the sound of the nearest water-drop percussion ("I am at the third shelf-fungi past the copper-lined hollow where the drips fall heavy").
- **Offerings**: If you must bring gifts, bring desiccants (rare and valuable here) or sharp steel (the trees incorporate iron but cannot forge it). Never bring fire or open flame.

**Documentation**  
Photography is difficult due to humidity and diffuse light, but permitted. However, the flash may disturb the bioluminescent patches (if present) or confuse the local fauna—the pale crustaceans that filter the effluent are sensitive to sudden light. Use red-filtered illumination.

### **VI. Emergency Protocols**

**If Lost**: Stop moving. The chamber is only fifty meters at its longest span, but the density creates labyrinthine effects. Listen for the 45-second exhale; its intensity increases toward the hydraulic accumulators (the "heart"). Move toward or away from the sound depending on your destination. Look for the verdigris brass fittings on the pneumatic lines—they often mark main thoroughfares.

**If Trapped by Growth**: Do not panic. The wood grows slowly, but if you find a passage narrowing behind you, use your pruning knife to cut along the grain (never across) and call out in low tones. High frequencies are absorbed by the moss; low tones travel through the xylem.

**If overcome by humidity**: Symptoms include hallucination (the "breathing" of the walls becomes aggressive), heat exhaustion despite moderate temperature, or fungal vertigo. If dizzy, lie flat on a shelf-fungi walkway—their leathery surface provides stable purchase. Do not lie on the root-knee floor; the black water pools are deceptive, and drowning occurs in six inches of liquid if disoriented.

---

### **VII. Departure Preparation**

Before exiting through the defile (where the buttress roots form the keystone), check your gear for *stowaways*. Seeds, spores, and small cuttings adhere to fabric in this environment. The Manufactory's biota is invasive by nature—Strangler Fig does not recognize borders. Shake out cuffs and pockets. 

Remove your respirator only when you have cleared the lignin-arch threshold and feel dry air on your face. The return to normal atmospheric humidity will feel like desiccation. Drink water immediately; your body has been operating in a closed metabolic system and will need to rebalance.

You will carry the scent of the Manufactory with you for days: the sweet-rot of cambium, the mineral tang of condensing vapor, the green note of sap-coolant. This is not contamination. It is a souvenir from a city that has achieved the patience of geology. You have breathed with the wood. You have moved at the speed of cambium. You will find ordinary architecture dead and disappointing afterward.

Safe travels. Move slowly. Wait patiently. The trees are watching, but they are kindly.
