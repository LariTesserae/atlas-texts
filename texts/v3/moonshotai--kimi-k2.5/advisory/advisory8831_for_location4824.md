**Preparation Protocol for the Sump**

*For the traveler about to enter a closet that contains cathedrals, filled with water that has forgotten how to be empty.*

---

### **I. Physical Encasement: The Density Suit**
Do not bring standard diving equipment. The Sump is not moved through via buoyancy but by *parting*. You need a **pressure-differential suit**—a full-body second skin rated for at least 50 meters of hyperbaric equivalency, though the Sump is technically only "deep" in terms of saturation, not depth.

The water here (saturation index 2.9) has the consistency of liquid glass. It will not flow around you; it will press *into* you, seeking to fill every pocket, every pore, every moment of emptiness in your schedule. Your suit must be seamless, hydrophobic at the quantum level, and equipped with micro-heaters to combat the 8°C thermal lock. This is not "chilly"—this is a temperature designed to slow mitochondrial dance. Without thermal regulation, your metabolic rate will drop within minutes to match the geological patience of the chamber.

You will move not by swimming, but by **wrenching**. Install wrist-mounted hydraulic shears rated for dense polymer cutting—you will need them to part the basket-weave of pale vegetation. The stems (somewhere between fern and eelgrass) are tensile and alive, growing in directions that make "forward" negotiable. Bring a helmet with a faceplate that can withstand accidental impact with "young" erosion (1.4): the stone teeth are Calcium Carbonate daggers still being born, sharp enough to slice standard polycarbonate.

**Breathing apparatus:** Rebreather only. Compressed gas tanks will implode under the density parameter (2.6). The rebreather must recycle your own atmosphere slowly—your breath will hang in front of you like a jellyfish, reluctant to disperse.

---

### **II. The Temporal Anchor**
Time in the Sump is a sedative, not a sequence. A bubble takes days to rise. A sound takes hours to finish entering your ears. Your thoughts will begin to crystallize—literally accreting like the calcite formations that grow in real-time from the walls.

**Mandatory equipment:** A **chronometric beacon** worn on the exterior of your suit, broadcasting to your home dimension on an angular momentum you're not experiencing. Do not rely on internal clocks; your circadian rhythm will flatline into the 8°C slowness. Set the beacon to scream when you have been inside for more than **ninety minutes subjective**—which may only be three minutes by your metabolism, or three weeks by the outside world. When it screams, you must leave immediately, regardless of where you think you are in the "closet."

Bring a **kinetic watch**—mechanical, not digital. Watch the second hand. If it begins to drag, sticking at points on the dial, you have stayed too long. Your personal physics is beginning to match the Sump's.

---

### **III. Cognitive Shielding: The Anti-Accretion Protocol**
The Sump digests memory. It will make you part of its "scattered architecture"—a calcified thought, a human-shaped formation of regret and recollection growing from the wall.

Before entry, undergo **cognitive offloading**. Write down everything you love, everything you fear, your mother's face, the taste of coffee. Store these in a mnemonic vault outside your body—a crystal drive, a thought-crystal, a piece of paper sealed in a vacuum tube attached to your suit. Do not try to remember things while inside; retrieval will be sluggish, and the attempt to recall will cause the memory to plate onto your synapses like mineral deposit.

Maintain a **mantra loop** in your helmet speakers. Not music—too rhythmic, the Sump will sync with it and slow it down until it becomes a geological drone. Instead, use stochastic white noise, random number sequences spoken in your native tongue. This prevents your thought patterns from settling into the "patient structures" of the chamber. If you find yourself thinking in calcite—slow, layered, permanent—trigger the white noise immediately.

---

### **IV. Navigation in Non-Euclidean Compression**
The Sump's weirdness (2.6) means distance is a liar. Six feet may be six inches or six miles depending on the fold. 

**Do not use lights.** The light level is 0.2—near-total darkness—and introducing photons here causes stress fractures in reality. The bioluminescent flashes (Cherenkov-blue physics errors) are warnings, not illumination. If you see them, look away; they are places where matter is forgetting how to behave.

Instead, use **tactile cartography**. Spool out a line of carbon monofilm behind you—not to find your way back (the line will have grown into the wall by the time you turn), but to feel the *texture* of space. When the line goes slack, you are in a compressed fold. When it goes taut against nothing, you are approaching an expanded pocket.

Move by **consensus with the vegetation**. The translucent matrix is your only true map. If the stems part willingly, follow them. If they resist, you are trying to move through a "wall" of folded space. The vegetation grows into the water, not up through it; treat the pale weave as a three-dimensional lattice you must climb through, not around.

---

### **V. Sensory Modification**
Sound arrives as pressure (0.8), not vibration. Standard ears will misinterpret the geological sighs as silence, then as crushing depth. Install **bone-conduction dampeners** set to filter frequencies below 2 Hz. The sound of a root cracking stone happens over hours; you don't want to feel it happening inside your jaw.

Your suit should have **haptic feedback** on the exterior skin. You will not hear the water, but you must feel its density (2.6) as a constant, gentle crushing. If the pressure feedback fades, you have entered one of the "voids within the packed"—a pocket of impossible emptiness. Do not stay there. Emptiness here is unstable and hungry.

---

### **VI. The Fauna Protocol (Singular)**
There is one creature: the translucent flatworm, hand-sized, motionless since the chamber formed. It does not eat; it observes. 

**Do not observe it back.** Its primitive eyes have adapted to see the physics misfires—the blue Cherenkov flashes. If you make eye contact, you may begin to see what it sees: the breakdown of causality, the erosion of logic. This is how the Sump claims you. flatworm is a sensor, not a predator, but the data it collects is toxic to human sanity.

If you encounter it adhered to a calcite ledge, look at the ledge, the water, the weave of pale stems. Never the eyes.

---

### **VII. Entry and Emergency Extraction**
The Sump is accessed from above—the last dissolved limestone. You will lower yourself into the water, not jump. Jumping creates displacement; the Sump prefers stillness.

**Entry position:** Vertically, spine aligned with the pull of the non-existent current. Let the concentrated water accept you like a glove. Do not struggle. Struggling creates heat; heat attracts the calcite. The chamber is shrinking (accreting inward) even as you enter.

**Exit strategy:** You cannot swim up. You must climb the vegetation lattice, hand over hand, pulling yourself through the weave. The exit hole will appear smaller than you remember—this is correct, the chamber has grown inward. Your suit must have **refractive camo** to slip through the narrowing gap without snagging on new stone teeth.

If you feel your thoughts slowing, if you cannot remember why you came, if you see your own reflection in the calcite and it looks like architecture—**cut the line**. Sever any tether to the Sump immediately. The part of you that wants to stay is already accreting.

---

### **VIII. The Final Preparation**
Visit a slow place first. Sit in a sensory deprivation tank for eight hours. Stare at a single stone until you can see it growing. Listen to a recording of ice forming. 

The Sump is not hostile; it is simply full. It is a geological mistake that has found a way to be infinite. You are entering a space that is birthing and dying, where water is too concentrated to admit you, yet holds you completely.

Pack light. Bring no air. Bring time, sheathed in metal. Bring your memories, stored safely outside yourself. 

And when the chronometric beacon screams—*when it screams*—climb. Do not look back at the scattered architecture. Do not wonder if that new formation looks like your spine, mineralized and patient and perfectly still.

Just climb. The closet is small, but the way out is farther than you think.
