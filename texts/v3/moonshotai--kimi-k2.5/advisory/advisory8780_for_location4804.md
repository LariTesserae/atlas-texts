**Preparation Brief: The Factory Without Workers**  
*For the traveler who must clock in where the planet works the night shift.*

---

### I. Physiological Hardening: The Altitude is Not a Place, It’s a Condition

You are not visiting a mountain. You are visiting a **process** occurring at 6,000 meters, where the atmosphere has been stripped to a managerial minimum. Do not acclimatize as if for Everest. Here, the air is not merely thin—it is *industrial*, metallic with copper ions and ionized particulate from the vents. 

- **Pulmonary Reframing**: Train in hypobaric chambers set to 0.5 atm, but introduce sulfur dioxide and metallic aerosols to the mix. Your lungs must learn to extract oxygen from air that tastes like pennies and static. Bring a rebreather not for survival (you can stand, briefly) but for *cognitive function*—the hypoxia here induces a specific delirium where the geological processes appear sentient, which they are not, though the distinction blurs.
- **Hemoglobin Augmentation**: Pre-load with erythropoietin boosters, but accept that your blood will never be sufficient. The Factory operates in a near-vacuum; you are a soft machine intruding on a hard vacuum process. Move slowly. Think glacier, not goat.

### II. Thermal Wardrobe: The Cold of the Warehouse Floor

The cold here is not weather. It is **climate control**—the managed chill of a facility that must keep its product (molten rock) from cooling too fast, yet maintain ambient temperatures that prevent organic decay. It is absolute, specific, and weaponized.

- **Layer for the Threshold**: You need gear rated for -40°C ambient, but with **ventable panels** at the thighs and lower back. The geothermal seeps create microclimates of 80°C steam within arm’s reach of flash-freezing ice. You will walk through thermal gradients of 120°C across three meters. Your suit must be a mobile cleanroom, not an expedition parka.
- **Cut-Proof Everything**: The basalt terraces are *knife-sharp*—not metaphorically, but with edges that have not yet been softened by a single raindrop. Obsidian sheets lie like broken glass in a loading dock. Gaiters must be Kevlar-weave; gloves, chainmail-lined. A stumble here is a laceration to the bone.
- **Respiratory Heat Exchange**: The air is so dry and cold that exhalation creates ice crystal architectures in your bronchial tubes. Use a thermal exchange mask that warms incoming air with outgoing breath, or you will develop "factory lung"—a hemorrhagic cough that produces glittering sputum of frozen blood and sulfur particulate.

### III. Hydration Strategy: Drinking from the Assembly Line

Water at 1.7 is not scarce; it is **strategic**. It seeps from pneumatic fissures, flash-freezes into villi of ice, and sublimates before pooling. 

- **The Weeping Rock Protocol**: Do not bring water. Bring **extraction wands**—heated titanium probes that can tap the thin threads of geothermal seepage running down raw basalt faces. The water is mineral-heavy, laden with lithium and rare earths from the sorting process below. It tastes of electricity and stone. Filter it, but not completely; the minerals will sustain you better than distilled water.
- **Ice Villi Harvesting**: The rime ice that coats every surface is your primary source. Collect it with ceramic scrapers (metal will freeze-weld to the rock). Melt it against your body heat in flexible solar thermal pouches. Do not eat the ice directly; it is cold enough to freeze-burn your esophagus.

### IV. Sensory Apparatus: Tuning for the Mostly Quiet

Sound arrives in **bruised packets**, truncated by the thin air. The sub-bass thrum of the magma chamber (20km down) vibrates in your teeth, but a shout travels only meters before dying.

- **Hearing**: Bring bone-conduction headphones. Not for music, but to amplify the low-frequency industrial drone of the planet’s mantle. This is your early warning system. When the thrum shifts from a B-flat to a D-sharp, the hydrothermal vents are about to cycle—pressure is building. You have 90 seconds to move to high ground before the assembly line coughs steam.
- **Vision**: Light is 1.2—permanent twilight filtered through volcanic particulate. Standard headlamps will bleach out the subtle sulfur-yellow glow of the fumaroles, which are your only true navigation beacons. Use red-spectrum low-lumen lights, or better, learn to navigate by the **heat shimmer** and the bioluminescence of the ice worms threading through the glaciers (they glow faintly blue-white in the absolute dark).
- **Proprioception**: The emptiness (density 0.2) creates agoraphobia of the soul. Practice walking in whiteout conditions while staring at your feet. The scale is open—20 kilometers of cathedral space—but the horizon is close. You will feel simultaneously exposed and entombed.

### V. Fauna Interaction: Quality Control and Maintenance Crews

Life here is not wild; it is **operational**. The snow fleas, ice worms, bar-headed geese, and pikas are not an ecosystem but a maintenance staff.

- **The Black Snow**: Do not disturb the aggregations of *Hypogastrura* (snow fleas). They are processing windblown organic matter—pollen, spores—into bioavailable nitrogen for the cushion plants. They are the factory’s recyclers. Walk around them; their populations are dense but localized, like oil slicks on snow.
- **Ice Worms as Infrastructure**: The *Mesenchytraeus* are not parasites but **lubrication**. They melt micro-channels through the glacial ice, preventing pressure buildup that would crack the factory’s "cooling system." If you see a cluster of them, you are standing on a hydraulic pressure point. Move.
- **Pika Territories**: These are your fellow inspectors. They have mapped the thermal gradients. Watch where they run—they know the safe paths between the knife-rocks and the steam vents. Do not feed them; they are optimized for the sparse vegetation (1.4) and surplus calories will disrupt their foraging algorithms.

### VI. Navigation: Mapping a Living Blueprint

The landscape is **emerging**. A map drawn yesterday is archaeology today. The basalt terraces accrete visibly; obsidian sheets crack and shift hourly.

- **Temporal Waymarking**: Use biodegradable dye markers that sublimate in 48 hours. Permanent markers are lies; this place rewrites itself. Mark your route with **thermal tags**—small heat packs that melt distinct shapes into the rime ice. They will last until the next hydrothermal cycle.
- **The Caldera as Clock**: The 20-kilometer complex is navigable only by the rhythm of the vents. Learn their cycle: the eastern vents cough every 12 minutes, the western every 17. Navigate by the steam plumes, which function as chimneys in this roofless facility.
- **No Footprints**: Your passage leaves no trace because the land is too busy becoming. Do not rely on backtracking. The ice sublimates; the steam realigns. You must navigate forward only, as if on a conveyor belt.

### VII. Psychological Conditioning: The Industrial Sublime

This is not nature. It is **geology as heavy industry**, and you are the only conscious worker in a facility that does not require you.

- **The Night Shift Mentality**: You will be alone. The "mostly quiet" is not peaceful; it is the hush of a vacuum chamber. Meditate in anechoic chambers beforehand to prepare for the absence of auditory feedback. Your footsteps will sound like they belong to someone else.
- **Accepting Obsolescence**: The Factory produces mountains with mechanical precision. You are not a climber conquering a peak; you are a temporary contaminant on the cleanroom floor. Adopt the humility of a maintenance worker on a night shift—observe, document, do not interfere.
- **Time Dilation**: Watch the basalt cool and crack. Sit for six hours and see the terrace grow a centimeter. This is slow-motion violence. You must learn to perceive geological time as a rapid manufacturing process, not a static landscape. Bring time-lapse equipment; your biological memory cannot hold the rate of change.

### VIII. Emergency Protocols: When the Factory Notices You

If injured, if lost, if the hypoxia wins:

- **Shelter**: Do not seek caves. The fissures are pneumatic tubes feeding the surface. They may vent 200°C steam without warning. Instead, dig into the **permafrost lid**—the frozen ground is stable and insulates against both cold and heat. A snow cave here is a sarcophagus; an ice-trench is a foxhole.
- **Rescue Signals**: Sound does not carry. Use **sulfur fires**. The yellow blooms around vents are flammable. A magnesium flare burned in a sulfur deposit creates a column of white smoke visible across the caldera, riding the thermal updrafts.
- **Extraction**: If you must leave, do not run. The sharp rock, the thin air, and the flash-freezing moisture punish haste. Walk with the deliberation of a piston. The Factory has no emergency exits; you must walk out the way you came, if the way still exists.

### IX. The Essential Packing List

- **Oxygen**: Nasal cannula with demand valve, not a tank (too heavy for the sharp terrain).
- **Suit**: Vacuum-rated thermal layers with ceramic plating at shins and forearms.
- **Tools**: Geological hammer (for testing ice stability), titanium extraction wands, ceramic ice scrapers, sulfur-proof goggles.
- **Navigation**: Inertial compass (magnetic fields are chaotic here due to the magma chamber), thermal imaging monocular.
- **Sustenance**: Lipid-rich paste foods; digestion requires oxygen you don’t have. Avoid fiber.
- **Documentation**: High-speed camera for capturing the crystal growth (quartz accretes in cubic meters per day here), bone-conduction recorder for the sub-bass thrum.
