### **I. Pre-Arrival Conditioning: The Body as Permeable Membrane**

**Respiratory Calibration**
Begin training six weeks prior. The Garden’s atmosphere is not "thin" mountain air—it is *thick*, swimming-pool dense with moisture and processed oxygen. Swim laps while wearing a snorkel restricted to half-ventilation. Practice breathing against resistance; your diaphragm must learn to extract oxygen from air that behaves like warm soup. If you smoke, stop immediately—your alveoli need maximum surface area to process the ionized humidity.

**Temporal Vertigo Inoculation**
Spend time in natural limestone caves while wearing noise-canceling headphones playing infrasound (14-20 Hz) at low volume. The Garden subsonic hum—felt in the molars rather than heard—disorients the vestibular system. You must learn to distinguish between the vertigo of height (the catwalk entry) and the vertigo of *temporal wrongness* (standing on stone younger than your shoes).

**Tactile Sensitivity Training**
The ground is not ground. It is pre-stone: calcium carbonate and silica deposits with the consistency of warm wax, soft cheese, unset jelly. Practice walking on memory-foam mattresses soaked in warm water while wearing weighted boots. Learn to distribute your weight across the arch and ball, never the heel—a puncture here wounds geological infancy.

### **II. Equipment & Apparel: The Non-Reactive Shell**

**The Exosuit**
Pack a **full-body immersion suit** of unbleached hemp canvas or untreated cork-fabric. Synthetic waterproofing is prohibited—the chemical cocktails weeping from the ceramic pipes will polymerize with Gore-Tex or nylon, encrusting you in a rigid mineral shell within hours. The suit must be:
- **Loose-fitting**: Your body will swell slightly in the pressurized humidity
- **Hooded**: To catch the supersaturated droplets that bead on hair and eyelashes (left unchecked, they form concrete-like accretions overnight)
- **Porous at the joints**: To allow the Garden’s breath to reach your skin, preventing pressure differentials that cause bruising

**Footwear**
Soft-soled **tabi boots** with micro-suction cups. Standard hiking boots crack the embryonic stalagmites; bare feet dissolve in the weak carbonic acid pooling in the terraces. The suction cups prevent slipping on the bio-film of engineered mosses, which secrete glycoproteins like banana peels.

**Respiratory Apparatus**
A **brass-and-ceramic rebreather** (no plastics, no aluminum). The Garden’s air is actively desalinated and ozonated—you do not filter *out*, you must filter *in*. Bring spare lime canisters to scrub the excess CO2 your lungs will struggle to expel against the density.

**Headgear**
**Wide-brimmed waxed canvas hat** with a chin strap. Not for sun—the grow-lamps emit no ultraviolet—but to protect against the scheduled "rain events." The warm rainfall is supersaturated with silicates. It feels like being slapped with warm gelatin. Without a brim, it will fill your ears and nostrils with accretion nuclei.

### **III. Navigation & Movement: Three-Dimensional Wading**

**The Entry Transition**
The service catwalk bridges two peaks in standard gravity. The Garden portal is a *pocket of inverted gravity*—you will feel the moment of inversion as a sudden blood rush to the head, a falling-elevator sensation upward. Clip your harness to the catwalk anchor *before* stepping through. Many travelers panic and claw at the air, tearing the nascent root mats that stabilize the chamber.

**Movement Protocol**
- **Never rush**: The stone deforms under pressure. Standing still for ten minutes creates a footprint that becomes a permanent topographical feature.
- **Use the vascular scaffolding**: The brass ribs and ceramic valves are your handholds. The biological matter is fragile; the mechanical infrastructure is load-bearing.
- **Chest-level mist navigation**: Carry a **thermographic lens**—the mist layers at chest height are thermal boundaries. Crossing them without warning triggers the convection currents, scattering your scent/particles into the sensor network and potentially inducing an unscheduled micro-storm.
- **Root trapeze nets**: The suspended matts above are traversable (they are the "ceiling" from the inverted perspective), but test each frond with a **tuning fork** (440 Hz). If the moss vibrates with a clear tone, it is anchored; if it thuds, it has not yet bonded to the substrate beneath.

### **IV. Biological & Chemical Safety: The Sterile Garden**

**Contamination Prevention**
You are the dirty thing here. The Garden is a total construct with a biodiversity rating of 1.1. 
- **Sterilize internally**: Three days prior, take narrow-spectrum antibiotics to minimize your bacterial cloud. Introducing E. coli to this sterile system is like tossing a grenade into a nursery.
- **No exfoliants**: Do not use skin creams or perfumes. The genetically retro-engineered bryophytes respond to organic compounds by aggressive etching—their root acids will micro-channel your epidermis, mistaking you for fresh limestone.

**Chemical Exposure**
- **The pools**: Each terrace is a different saturation of calcium carbonate. Test with **litmus paper**—the pale blue pools are safe for wading; the milky white ones will petrify your pant hems in minutes.
- **Condensation management**: Carry a **squeegee scraper** (wooden handle, rubber blade). The ozonated condensation that beads on your eyelashes must be cleared every twenty minutes. If it evaporates naturally, it leaves sharp silica crystals embedded in your skin.

**Fauna Encounters**
If you see the **wingless paper moth** or the **blind nutrient shrimp**, freeze. Document them (they are malfunctions, not features), then retreat slowly. Their rarity indicates system stress; your presence may be causing a cascade failure.
