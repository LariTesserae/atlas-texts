**Field Preparation Briefing: The Octant (Sub-Datum Construct, Age: 93 Years)**
*For the Ordinary Human Multiverse Traverser*

You are not descending into an ocean. You are entering a wound that has not yet begun to heal—a cathedral of intent where geology should be. The Octant is not a habitat that grew; it was poured, grid-aligned, and left to age in fast-forward. Treat it like a factory floor submerged in liquid nitrogen and lit by distant sodium fires. Your body will lie to you about scale, pressure, and time. Do not listen.

---

### **I. Physiological Pre-Conditioning (4°C Thermal & Pressure Regimes)**

**Cold Adaptation:**
The water is locked at four degrees Celsius—dense, viscous, and indifferent to your mammalian comfort. Unlike natural abyssal zones, there is no geothermal respite, no hydrovents offering warmth. This is the temperature of water that has forgotten the sun.

- **Pre-acclimatization:** Spend three weeks in a walk-in cryochamber set to 2°C. Learn to distinguish between "uncomfortable" and "dangerous" when your fingers move like rusted machinery. Your dexterity will drop by 40%; test all equipment while wearing thermal restriction mittens.
- **Metabolic Loading:** Increase caloric intake by 60% for two weeks prior. The thermal conductivity of sterile, desalinated water (which this functionally is) strips heat faster than natural seawater. You will shiver in a way that feels like machinery grinding.

**Pressure Considerations:**
At four kilometers, natural physics would crush you at approximately 400 atmospheres. Here, pressure is *managed*—a dynamic maintained by reciprocating cathedral-pumps. The hydrostatic load may fluctuate marginally with the diurnal thermal breathing of the structure. However, assume standard abyssal protocols apply until verified.

- **Equalization Strategy:** Unlike natural trenches, the pressure here is *active*, not passive. You may experience sudden sinus compression during pump cycles (the 22-Hertz thrum). Practice the Frenzel maneuver until it is autonomous.
- **Gas Mix:** Do not use standard trimix. The atmosphere is artificially maintained; off-gassing polymer sealants from the Floor may interact with high-pressure oxygen. Opt for a closed-circuit rebreather with industrial-grade scrubbers capable of filtering volatile organic compounds (VOCs). The "faint tack" you feel on your gloves? Those are curing epoxies. Do not inhale them.

### **II. Equipment Specifications: Sharps, Slicks, and Signals**

**The Armor:**
The Floor is nine decades old and has not been weathered. It is *knife-sharp*.

- **Drysuit:** Not neoprene. You need a rigid-shell atmospheric diving suit (ADS) or a minimum of a Kevlar-ceramic weave undersuit. The expansion joints between ferrocrete slabs remain capable of slicing standard rubber. One wrong crawl across the substrate and you’ll vent trimix into the cathedral.
- **Gloves:** Titanium-mesh palm coating. You will want to touch the Floor—to feel the absolute newness, the violet anodization of stress-ribs. The ceramite tiling will shred bare skin or standard latex. Bring spare glove sets; the polymer tack will adhere to and compromise seals over time.

**Sensory Augmentation:**
- **Vision:** Sodium-vapor light (589 nm) renders reds as black and greens as hallucinatory silver. Fit your helmet with a notch filter tuned to 589 nm to reduce glare, or accept that you will navigate by monochrome contrast. Carry a personal flood lamp tuned to 450 nm (blue-white) to spot the rust-orange algal films on north-facing ridges—your only reliable biological landmarks.
- **Sound Management:** The 22-Hertz baseline (the "Octant Hum") resonates in the sternum and can induce panic, nausea, or religious awe. Install active noise-canceling ear inserts capable of phase-reversing infrasound. However, keep one channel open for the ultrasonic integrity sensors (the "pings")—these are your only warning of structural micro-fractures or approaching inspection submersibles.

**Navigation in Low-Density Continents:**
- **Beacons:** In a space the size of a small continent filled with nothing, "lost" is a default state, not an exception. Deploy micro-sonar buoys every 500 meters when traversing the atrium. GPS is useless; the artificial datum plane creates spacetime curvature anomalies. Use inertial dead-reckoning or simply follow the Cartesian grid of sodium arcs (spaced 800m apart—count them).

### **III. Temporal Orientation: The Artificial Tide**

Time here is mechanical, not tidal.

- **The Six-Hour Cycle:** Every six hours, the pumps reverse flow to prevent particulate settlement. This creates scheduled current reversals of surprising violence—up to 4 knots in the central atrium, designed to scour the Floor clean.
- **Synchronization:** Set your chronometer to Pump Standard Time (PST). The "spore release" of the engineered algae coincides with the slack water between reversals—this is when the silver clouds of filter-feeders and the predatory mackerel-analogues converge. It is beautiful and dangerous. Schedule rest stops during these windows, but remain alert; the predators are bio-engineered and may not follow natural timidities.

---

### **IV. Fauna Protocols: The Bio-Mechanical Interface**

The residents are young, engineered, and optimized for this specific artificiality.

- **Filter-Feeding Chordates:** Billions strong, translucent, ribbed like radiator fins. Harmless, but disorienting. They school in "weather systems"—vast, moving fronts that block visibility. If caught in a bloom, swim perpendicular to the flow; they will part like curtains, but the psychological pressure of being surrounded by living glass can trigger panic.
- **Armored Crustaceans:** They move in the expansion trenches, tapping the ceramite. The sound is not Morse; it is incidental, the clicking of mechanical scavengers. Do not disrupt them. They are the only biological agents keeping the Floor’s joints clear of algal buildup. If you hear typing, you are near a trench—useful orientation data.
- **Predatory Mackerel-Analogues:** Bio-engineered for speed in open, illuminated voids. They hunt by silhouette against the sodium glare. When crossing illuminated zones, keep your profile low relative to the ceiling lights. They avoid shadows but will test unlit objects with exploratory bites that can crush a standard suit hose.

### **V. Psychological Preparation: The Weight of Emptiness**

This is the critical section. You can survive the cold and pressure and fail here.

You will swim for hours and see nothing but the jade shafts of light and, perhaps, a faint haze that is the opposite wall of a structure too large for intuition to hold. The density of matter is vanishingly low. This is not like the open ocean, which teems with invisible life; this is a *factory* that happens to be underwater, and most of it is empty.

- **Agoraphobia Under Pressure:** The psychological dissonance of vast space combined with crushing depth (even if artificially managed) produces "The Cathedral Squeeze"—a dissociative panic where the traveler believes they are falling upward into the ceiling grid. Fix your eyes on the small: the barnacle-free ceramite, the twitching of a single jellyfish (translucent, drifting like litter), the violet gleam of a titanium rib.
- **Industrial Sublime:** The pumps do not stop. The hum is your heartbeat now. Accept that you are inside a machine that is still curing, still settling, still waiting for its purpose to arrive. The Octant is young—only ninety-three years. It is a newborn continent. You are swimming through its amniotic future.

**Mantras for the Long Swim:**
*"The Floor is new and sharp."*
*"The light is grid and geometry."*
*"The tide comes every six hours."*

### **VI. Emergency Protocols**

- **If Lost:** Ascend to the sodium-light grid. Follow the lights to the nearest corner where the cathedral-pumps vent. These are maintenance nodes with docking submersibles.
- **If Suit Breach:** The water is sterile—no bacterial infection risk, but immediate hypothermia. Emergency patches must bond to polymer sealant; carry solvent wipes to clean the Floor's surface before applying adhesive.
- **If Trapped in Reversal Current:** Dive for the Floor. The current is strongest in the mid-water column; the boundary layer at the ceramite surface (due to friction against the sharp, unweathered tiling) is significantly slower.

---

**Final Note:**
When you drag your hand across the Floor and feel that faint tack of sealant, resist the urge to wipe it away. That stickiness is the Octant's youth. You are touching a place that has not yet been softened by time. Treat it with the respect due to something sharper than you, colder than you, and vastly, echoingly empty.

Bring a beacon. Stay sharp. Count the lights.
