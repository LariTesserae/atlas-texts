**Preparation Protocol: The Gullet**

Visiting the Gullet requires surrendering your assumptions about "caves," "ecosystems," and the basic physics of how quickly water falls. This is not a place you explore; it is a process you temporarily join. An ordinary human must prepare for thermal saturation, biological permeability, temporal viscosity, and the psychological unraveling that occurs when the world stops making noise.

---

### **1. The Pre-Descent: Immunological and Temporal Calibration**

**Medical Prep:**
You will need a **chemosynthetic immunity suite**—not standard cave bacteria protection, but a month-long regimen of gut and lung flora replacement. The air here doesn't just contain pathogens; it *is* a soup of extremophile microbes that metabolize sulfur and methane. Without preparation, your first breath will seed your lungs with organisms that view your alveoli as fertile substrate. Pack **spore-lock inhalers**: the fungal density is such that a sneeze here creates ecosystems. You must also undergo **temporal adjustment therapy**. The Gullet operates on "syrup-time," and your nervous system will panic when it takes four seconds for a droplet to fall from ceiling to floor, or when a salamander moves with the fluid grace of a creature unbound by normal gravity. Spend two weeks in strobe-light sensory deprivation tanks to recalibrate your threat-response to slow-motion stimuli.

**Psychological Screening:**
Do not enter if you have unresolved claustrophobia or require auditory cues to locate yourself in space. The silence here is *structural*—the moss, the moth-down, the very humidity absorbs sound waves. You will lose proprioception in the density. Candidates must pass the "Fern Test": being sealed in a small, heated greenhouse for 72 hours with no light except phosphorescent fungus, touching nothing but damp vegetation, speaking to no one. If you hallucinate walls breathing, you are ready.

### **2. The Suit: A Second Skin for the Skin-Floor**

Do not wear hard-shell exosuits. The floor of the Gullet is not load-bearing stone; it is a ten-foot-deep lung of compressed organic matter that *breathes* and releases methane when compressed. You need a **distributed-pressure bio-suit**:

*   **Material:** Triple-layered silicone mesh with internal capillary cooling networks. The heat is "feverish," geothermal, pressing against you like a living body. The suit must wick heat while preventing the ambient humidity (which "beads on eyelashes" and turns surfaces slick with microbial film) from touching your skin.
*   **Respiration:** Closed-loop rebreather with methane scrubbers and copper-ion filtration. The air "tastes of copper and rotting orchids"—this indicates heavy metal particulates and complex organic breakdown. Standard filters will clog within minutes. Your exhalation must be captured and sterilized; you cannot introduce foreign CO2 into this closed chemosynthetic cycle.
*   **Tactile Shielding:** The "violent density" means you cannot extend your arms without touching life. Wear gauntlets coated with **non-stick biorepellent**—not to protect the ecosystem from you, but to prevent the "tapestry" moss and epiphytes from anchoring to your sleeves. Everything here grows on everything else, and given time, your suit would become substrate.
*   **Footing:** Snowshoe-like **humus distributors**—wide, flexible pads that spread your weight across the rotting mat to prevent punching through into methane pockets or disturbing the crabs and salamanders flowing beneath. The floor is "warm skin"—you must walk on it as if traversing a living membrane.

### **3. Descent: Entering the Throat**

**Rigging:**
The descent is through a sinkhole past fossilized coral and volcanic ash. Use **static kernmantle rope** treated with anti-bacterial coatings—standard rope will bloom with mold halfway down. Your harness must include a **torso stabilization system**; the shaft narrows to a "pale blue coin" and then nothing, meaning you will rappel through a transition zone where air pressure and temperature spike rapidly. The shift from surface to Gullet is abrupt—like entering a sauna through a mail slot.

**Lighting:**
Do not bring bright white lamps. The Gullet's illumination is **chartreuse bioluminescence** from bacterial colonies. Your lights mustmatch this spectrum—pale green, low lumen—otherwise you will bleach the phosphorescent bacteria and blind the local fauna, disrupting the three-dimensional food web. Bring **UV-filtered red lamps** for personal navigation that won't trigger photosensitive fungal spore release.

### **4. Ground Protocol: Moving Through the Lung**

**Spatial Awareness:**
The chamber is "not much larger than a village church" but packed with vertical life. You are entering a space where predators occupy the same cubic meter as prey, just at different heights. **Do not look up quickly.** The "hanging gardens of moss" create soft partitions—you may collide with curtains of ancient vegetation that have achieved "the density of tapestry."

**Movement Discipline:**
Move in **syrup-time**. Sudden movements create pressure waves in the humid air that disturb the silent moths (which absorb sound and "parachute down on threads of silk"). Walk with the deliberation of a creature underwater. When the floor "breathes"—expanding and contracting with thermal changes—pause and let it move beneath you. Fighting the rhythm will exhaust you; the heat is "immediate, pressing."

**The Silence Protocol:**
You will want to speak. Resist. Sound dies here, dampened by the "sickly" air and the "flesh" of the environment. Speaking creates condensation that falls as new droplets, altering the micro-climate. Use **tactile signaling** or pre-arranged light pulses. If you must make noise, understand that the *pop* of gas bubbles from the hot seeps below and the *thwick* of droplets are the only legitimate sounds here. Your voice is an intrusion.

### **5. Biological Interaction: The Conspiracy of Life**

** fauna encounters:**
You will encounter:
*   **Cucumber-sized translucent salamanders:** Do not touch. Their "wet gelatin" skin is likely a mucus barrier against chemosynthetic toxins. They are frictionless and may flow over your boots.
*   **Millipedes:** Ceiling-dwelling, finger-width. They eat the algae that grows in drip-tracks. If they fall on you, do not brush them off violently. They are part of the moisture-management system.
*   **Pale soft-shelled crabs:** Scuttling through the humus. They are deafened by the substrate; your vibrations are their warning. Step lightly.
*   **Moths:** Hanging like "inverted fruit." They are soundless, covered in down that absorbs vibration. If you disturb them, they release "puffs of fine spore-dust" that glitter. Do not inhale.

**The Chitin-Castles:**
These hollow calcified cocoons are structural but brittle. Do not climb them. They are "sedimentary records of biological ambition," home to new tenants. Treat them like coral reefs—alive with things you cannot see.

### **6. Emergency Extraction**

If you must leave quickly:
*   **Methane buildup:** If you smell sweetness overwhelming the copper-orchid scent, the floor is off-gassing aggressively. Lie flat to distribute weight and crawl to the rope. Do not run—the compression of your steps will trigger more release.
*   **Temporal disorientation:** If "syrup-time" causes panic (your heart beating faster than falling water), inject **temporal stabilizers** (chronamine blockers) and focus on the *thwick* of droplets—the only reliable timekeeping in the chamber.
*   **Biological adhesion:** If the "violent density" of vegetation traps you (ferns closing fronds, moss curtains entangling), do not struggle against the pressure. Cut the suit layer if necessary—sacrifice the gear, not your calm. Panic sweat here creates new microbial blooms on your skin.

---

### **7. The Return**

Emerging from the Gullet, you will carry the silence in your ears for days. The sun will seem vulgar, loud, and fast. You will be dehydrated despite the humidity—the chemosynthetic heat draws moisture from you constantly. You will need **decontamination showers** that cycle through copper-binding agents (to remove the taste of the air) and thermal cooling (to bring your body down from the "feverish embrace").

Most importantly, you must accept that while you were down there, breathing in the lung of the world, the Gullet continued its cycle: things dying and being born in syrup-time, indifferent to your passage, breathing in the dark. You were temporary. It is ancient.

Leave nothing. Take only the memory of how warm the darkness can be.
