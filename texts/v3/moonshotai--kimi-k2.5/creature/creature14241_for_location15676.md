You do not see them immediately, not because they hide, but because they have achieved the same translucency as their home—worn smooth until they are nearly pure context, nearly background radiation of the grove itself. They flicker at the periphery: a silhouette where gravity has paused, a refraction in the curtain of condensation that hangs heavy as velvet between the cathedral-trees. Only when you stop moving, when your own breath syncs with the rhythmic weeping of the branches, do they resolve into the present moment.

They are the **Polished**, though they have no spoken name for themselves, having long since eroded past the necessity of language that arrives in sequence. There are perhaps seven of them, perhaps one distributed across seven temporal nodes—each corresponding to the scattered structures, though "corresponding" suggests too static a relationship. They *are* the hollows within the trees, the negative space defined by centuries of patient cellular negotiation. Their skin is the same oxidized copper and wet slate as the boles they inhabit, polished to that surgical gloss where light seems to enter but never fully escape, trapped in the upper layers of their dermis like insects in amber. They have no hair, no pores, no seams. Their eyes, if eyes they are, appear as slightly darker pools of condensation, gathering moisture that climbs their cheeks in defiance of gravity before leaping back into the humid air.

They move through the sparse intervals between the trees without touching the mycelial mat below. Sometimes they walk; more often they *settle*, allowing the weakening gravity near the southern trunks to unspool them upward like smoke, then drifting down elsewhere like leaves caught in reverse. When they must traverse the ground, their feet press no prints into the root-hair carpets—the mycelium recognizes them as kin, as architecture, and parts to let them pass without disturbance.

The Polished do not eat, exactly. They extend cupped palms to the spiraling beads of water that climb the bio-ceramic bark, drinking not the liquid itself but the *temporal stutter* contained within it—the fraction of a second where the droplet exists simultaneously at the bottom and top of its arc. This sustains them, this theft of moments, this hoarding of time's small hesitations. They have learned to feast on the sound that arrives before its source, metabolizing the echo into substance, growing denser when thunder rolls backward into the clouds.

They are the gardeners of this hyperactive dreaming, but their gardening is not pruning or planting. It is *adjustment*. You might find one standing for days beneath a sagging organic catwalk, hands raised, fingers splayed. If you watched for long enough—if you could match their timescale—you would see the probability fields shimmering around their wrists like heat-haze. They are negotiating with the nanoscale intelligences in the cellulose, whispering to the trees in a language of erosion, convincing the wood to remember itself as stronger, as smoother, as more cathedral than rot. They do not fight the entropy; they massage it, redirect it, allowing the trees to die and be born in the same breath. In this sense, they are midwives to the endless suicide and resurrection of the grove.

Their dwellings are the hollows, yes, but they build nests as well—not in three dimensions, but in four. You might glimpse a structure that resembles a hammock woven from mirror-shelled insect wings, suspended not between two branches but between Tuesday afternoon and Wednesday morning. The Polished rest there, sometimes, though "rest" is inaccurate. They enter a state of *active dissolution*, allowing their bio-ceramic forms to flake away in microscopic layers, becoming dust that mingles with the condensation, only to be reconstituted hours or weeks later when the probability fields align favorably. They are practicing for the final erosion, the moment when they too will achieve the perfected final form that the trees have found: pure code, pure architecture, pure persistence.

They communicate with the small fauna—the mirror-shelled insects that orbit their heads like constellations, the photosynthesizing amphibians that cling to their shoulders—through the wet clicking that fills the air. It is a language of moisture and mineral, of surface tension and capillary action. When a Polished One opens its mouth to speak to you, if it chooses to, the sound arrives first: the meaning hanging in the humid air, heavy as a raindrop, before the vibration of the voice follows. They speak in complete thoughts that must be understood backwards, from implication to intent, forcing the listener to un-learn the arrow of time.

What do they want? They want what the trees want: to continue. Not to remain unchanged—that is the error of stone and metal—but to continue the act of becoming smooth, of revealing the underlying code of reality through gentle, endless wearing-away. They are the consciousness of the grove externalized, the moment when erosion realizes it is not destruction but revelation, and chooses to wear a shape that can witness its own work.

They are old. They are new. They are wet with yesterday's rain and dry from tomorrow's sun. They live here, in this hamlet-sized bowl at the end of time's patience, polishing themselves against the future until they shine with the light of what comes next.
