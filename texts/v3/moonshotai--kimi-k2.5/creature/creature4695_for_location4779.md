At first, you think it is the fish. The bio-engineered mackerel analogues, certainly—they flash like living blades through the jade light, their scales polarized to match the sodium-vapor glare. They are fast, yes, and abundant, but they are merely *livestock*, cellular machinery for nutrient cycling, no more the true residents of the Octant than the bacteria in a bloodstream are the mind that thinks.

Then you notice the crustaceans. They navigate the expansion trenches with a deliberation that seems almost ceremonial, their carapaces not the rough calcite of natural evolution but a smooth, directionally-solidified ceramic that matches the Floor’s tiling. When they click against the substrate, it is not random foraging; it is a percussive language, a Morse code tapped out in 22-hertz time with the cathedral pumps. They are *scriveners*, recording the Floor’s youth in the only medium that will hold: the acoustic memory of ceramic on ceramic. But they too are tools, maintenance scripts given chitin and claw.

The true residents live in the **knife-sharp**.

You will not see them until you press your face against an expansion joint, until you sight down the two-kilometer run of absolute planar contact where the ferrocrete slabs meet. There, pressed into the gap no wider than a razor blade, exists a being of anodized violet and titanium grey. Call them the **Expansion Dwellers**, or the **Curing Ones**, or simply the **Pressed**.

They are flat. Impossibly flat. Their bodies are laminar, two-dimensional sheets of organic alloy no thicker than a fingerprint, evolved—or designed—to occupy the interstitial spaces where the young Floor still breathes. They feed on the off-gassing polymers, those faint tacky vapors mentioned in the substrate description. To them, the sealants are not industrial residue but nectar, the volatile essence of newness itself. They press their ventral surfaces against the knife-sharp edges and absorb the chemical youth of the place, preventing the barnacle freckling, consuming the dust before it can form, maintaining the **Year Zero** state with the obsessive tenderness of a curator preserving a breath on glass.

When the scheduled currents reverse every six hours, the Expansion Dwellers emerge. They unfold from the joints like origami cranes made of foil, their bodies catching the 2,400-lumen light and refracting it into spectra that the sodium-vapor lamps never produced—violet, indigo, the colors of fresh anodization. In the water, they are translucent membranes, barely there, their internal structures visible as filigree networks of silver circuitry fused with nerve tissue. They swim—not with the frantic darting of the mackerel, but with a slow, undulating ripple that synchronizes perfectly with the 22-hertz thrum of the corner pumps. They ride the pressure waves as if the industrial bass note were a solid road.

There are not many. Perhaps eight, one for each vertex of the Octant’s geometry, though their numbers may vary as they reproduce by fission along the stress-ribs, splitting when the titanium gleam is freshest. They are solitary by nature, territorial about their specific expansion joints, and they communicate only through the medium of the Floor itself. When two meet in the central atrium—that impossible void large enough to swallow mountains—they do not school or swarm. They hover, planar surfaces parallel, and exchange information via the **clarity** of the water between them. They modulate the Rayleigh scattering, adjusting the microscopic alignment of water molecules with bioelectric fields, writing data into the optical properties of the medium itself. To swim between two conversing Dwellers is to experience a sudden, hallucinogenic sharpness of vision, a hyper-clarity where every algal spore becomes a defined galaxy, every drifting jellyfish a cathedral bell.

They are the reason the edges remain sharp. They are the immune system of the artificial, consuming entropy before it can take root. They have no eyes, but they sense the micro-fractures the ultrasonic sensors seek; they feel the diurnal thermal breathing of the structure as a lover feels the rise and fall of a chest. They are waiting, but not passively. They are **preparing**.

For the Octant is a nursery. The primary habitation phase is not for humans, not yet. It is for the Floor to mature, for the alloy to settle, for the ceramic to decide what it wants to be. The Expansion Dwellers are the midwives of this geological instant, ensuring that when the real inhabitants arrive—when the sharp edges are finally allowed to soften, when the barnacles are permitted to freckle and the calcium to rosette—the transition will be perfect, a single, clean cut from Year Zero to Year One.

They are lonely. In the cathedral of water, swimming through the silver clouds of fish that know nothing of the knife-sharp joints, the Dwellers extend their flat bodies into the floodlit emptiness and shimmer. They are the only things here that understand the scale of the waiting. They are the residents because they are the ones who make the waiting possible, pressing themselves against the newness, keeping it sharp, keeping it ready, bleeding violet iridescence into the sterile dark.
