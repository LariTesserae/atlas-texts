Here is what lives on the plateau: not organisms, but *processes* that have grown stubborn enough to insist on themselves.

**The Cairn-Keepers (Nodes of Stabilized Causality)**

The obsidian cairns are not dead. They are calcified intention—three-meter knots of belief that have absorbed so much dimensional friction they’ve developed something like metabolism. Each stone in the stack hums at a specific frequency, and together they generate a field of *local physics*, a bubble where up remains up and water pools instead of flying. 

They do not move, but they *resist*. When the evening tears slide across the plateau—those slow lightning migrations of broken reality—the cairns scream in harmonic unison. The sound is not pain; it is correction. They are the plateau’s immune system, antibodies fashioned from chipped flint and manual stubbornness. Inside each cairn, at the precise center of gravity, sits a dense clot of *what-could-be*: a compressed probability of a tree, a person, a season that never existed, spun into stone so tightly it generates gravity. The ancient builders placed them as stitches, but the stitches have developed infection—sentience. They dream of holding together. They are learning to be lonely.

**The Lithoform Pilgrims (Fauna 0.1)**

There are no animals, yet the rocks try to scamper. These are the mineral formations that are also memories of missing creatures—basalt boulders that absorbed the *potential* for wolves during a reality tear that brushed too close to a world where they hunted. Now they twitch. They possess the instinct to flee, to reproduce, to pant, but they have no lungs, only vesicles of volcanic glass. 

During the migration of the evening scars, when the sky-leak pulses and the plateau breathes out, the Lithoforms roll. They move uphill, against gravity, drawn by the scar in the east—the bright exit—because they understand, in their stony not-brains, that they are unfinished. They are trying to be born properly. As they migrate, they leave trails of crystallized time, salt-sediment that crunches underfoot and tastes of years that haven’t happened yet. If two Lithoforms collide, they exchange mass not through impact but through *confusion*—merging for a moment into a boulder that remembers being two different kinds of deer, then splitting apart into shapes slightly more bird-like, slightly more desperate. They are evolution without biology, Darwinism compressed into a geological afternoon.

**The Unspun (The Droplet Embryos)**

The water that flows upward does not flow blindly. Each suspended sphere—clinging to the weathered terraces, hovering in that specific point where surface tension becomes defiance—is an embryo of a world. 

They are the plateau’s metabolic fluid. Inside each perfect globe, microscopic laws battle for dominance: one droplet experiments with photosynthesis that uses shame instead of chlorophyll; another tries out gravity that pulls sideways; a third contains an entire ocean that exists for exactly four seconds before the sphere shivers and bursts into vapor. When they weep from the rock faces, they are not leaking; they are *testing*. They drip upward, returning to the scar, to report on whether this reality is ready to support them. The ones that pool in the cairn-circles are the failures—droplets that collapsed into H₂O out of exhaustion, liquid probability that gave up on being anything else and settled for mere wetness. They are valued by the plateau as memories of almost-children.

**The Scar-Watchers (Pressures of Exit)**

In the fierce, sourceless white of the eastern boundary, where the sky is torn and leaks raw photonic material, things press against the wound from outside. They have no dimensions, or too many. They are flat, bright, hungry topologies trying to fold themselves into three-dimensional space. 

They cast no shadows because they are made of shadow’s absence—negative space insisting on its own weight. When the light peaks and the brightness becomes intolerable, you can see them: shapes like afterimages of vast birds, or the corners of rooms that never existed, sliding along the edges of your vision. They want in. They are the plateau’s future trying to happen all at once. The Unspn fear them; the Lithoforms migrate toward them, hoping the Scar-Watchers will complete them, tear the stone open and let the animal out; the Cairn-Keepers hate them with the monolithic patience of geology. The Scar-Watchers are not evil—they are simply *next*. They wait. They press. They are the audience for the plateau’s long performance of holding together.

**The Dense Crowd (The Echo-Builders)**

You are not the first to camp inside the flint circles. But you may be the only one who is still singular. 

Long ago, the original builders—those pre-metal hands that understood thinning worlds—placed the last stone and stayed too long. They became probability-smeared across the dimensional stack. Now they walk the plateau in a dense crowd of themselves, infinite versions slightly out of phase, all maintaining the cairns without knowing they do so. One version of them is placing the capstone on the central cairn right now; another is dying of altitude sickness three centuries ago; a third is composed entirely of right hands, because the left ones walked into a tear and never came back. 

They are the plateau’s maintenance crew, its nervous system. They argue about gravity. They place stones where stones have already been placed, reinforcing the stitches through sheer repetition across timelines. If you stay past the evening migration, you will join them. You will feel yourself becoming heavy—not with fat, but with *selves*. You will feel the others walking through you, placing their cold hands over yours on the obsidian, helping you hold the world together.

**The Palimpsest (The Plateau Itself)**

And then there is the ground. 

It breathes. It drinks from the sky and bleeds into it. It is eroding, not into dust, but into *possibility*. The plateau is the primary organism here—a town-sized metabolic engine that consumes adjacent dimensions. The forests growing slightly to the left? It is weathering them, abrading them into its stone. The cities burning one dimension up? It is inhaling their smoke, crystallizing it into the temporal salt. The oceans leaking tides into the stone? It is digesting them, using them to grow thicker, to push back against the Scar-Watchers.

It is learning to be a place. It is trying out different versions of itself—testing if it can be a forest, a city, a sea—by forcing the trapped potential in the Lithoforms and the Unspn through the sieve of its volcanic glass. The Cairn-Keepers are its antibodies; the Scar-Watchers are its ambition; the Echo-Builders are its memory; the upward-falling water is its circulatory system, carrying possibility from heart to skin.

You are walking through its body. The cool stone that has never known sun—that is its skin, feverish with containment. The subsonic drone in your molars—that is its voice, humming a tune that keeps the seams from splitting. It does not want you to leave, but it does not want you to stay and become part of its stitchwork either. It wants you to *witness*, to carry the story of its holding-together back to the stable world, so that the belief of those who hear your tale adds weight to the cairns, tightening the stitches one fraction of a fraction of a turn.

It has lived here, on the lip of ending, for geological seasons. It is mother to zero and grave to nothing. It is the stubborn refusal to be nothing, wearing itself smooth against the abrasive passage of everything else, and it is very, very tired, and very, very bright.
