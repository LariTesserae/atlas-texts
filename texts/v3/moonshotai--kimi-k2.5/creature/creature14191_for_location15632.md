To inhabit the Groan-Steppe is to exist in a state of borrowed boundary. Here, the skin is a temporary negotiation; the self is a holding pattern; and to be alive means to be engaged in the slow or swift process of returning to the Whole. The creatures of this place do not live *upon* the land—land is too static a concept. They live within a vast, breathing consensus of lignin and hunger.

**The Throats Themselves**

First and most ancient are the Throats—not as architecture, but as the primary population. They are the elders of the Pan, though "elder" implies a linearity foreign to their nature. They are settled, established, weathered into forms that erosion polishes but cannot diminish. Each is a continent of cellulose, a digestive consciousness distributed through miles of xylem and heartwood. 

They do not think as animals think, in sparks of neuron and flash of muscle, but in the slow language of hydraulic pressure and chemical gradient. Their cognition is a tide: the daily expansion of their woody flanks under the thermal blanket of noon; the seasonal migration of moisture from deep cisterns to the photosynthetic skin; the standing waves of bassofrequency that roll from their cores at midday, communicating across the ochre distances to other Throats in arguments that last centuries. When two Throats groan in counterpoint, creating nodes of silence and roar that wander the plains like invisible mountains, they are not merely making noise—they are debating root-memory, sharing the chemical language of spores, perhaps courting, perhaps warning. They are the only inhabitants who never know the terror of dissolution, for they are the destination of all dissolutions.

**The Ephemerals: The Throat’s Own Body**

Borrowed against the Throats' permanence is the teeming, temporary host—the billions who are, strictly speaking, mobile organs on sabbatical. These are the Winged, who emerge at dawn from bud-chambers high in the cooing dark. Their wings are translucent sails of photosynthetic membrane stretched over struts of honeycombed chitin; their bodies are translucent, revealing slow pulses of sap rather than blood. For the span of a day, they are eyes. They are hands. They are the Throat’s sensory apparatus granted leave to roam.

They do not know themselves as separate, yet they are. They experience the joy of flight, the thermal mathematics of rising heat, the harvest of spores from the metallic lichen-fields below. They mate in supersonic spirals that carve invisible geometries into the air—patterns that the Scaffolders read as weather omens. And at dusk, when the heat begins to leak from the Pan and the wood contracts with a collective creak, they return. They do not resist. They press themselves against the warm inner walls and dissolve, weeping nutrients back into the vascular system, their chitin scales snowing down to carpet the base in drifts of translucent armor. They are not dying; they are simply ending their shift. Their memories—if such brief flickers can hold memory—become part of the Throat’s slow dream.

Below them, in the warm wet dark of the interior, move the **Scarab-Undertakers**. These are closer to permanent, living years rather than days, yet they too are temporary. They are the immune system made manifest: gentle, methodical, blind to light but sensitive to the chemical signature of ending. They carry the dead—the meat-fruit who fell before their time, the winged ones who crashed into open air, the occasional deep-dweller who drowned in a cistern—up through corkscrew passages to the digestion-pits. Their mandibles click in rhythms that soothe the dying. They have no names, for they are the Throat naming itself as it reclaims its own. They know a profound intimacy with death because they are scheduled to join it; when their time comes, they simply stop, fold their legs, and wait for the next Undertaker to carry them home.

**The Deep Dwellers**

There are things that never see the sun, pale and gracile, who live in the absolute dark of the internal cisterns. They are fish-shaped, or rather, like embryos suspended in the spongy pith-water. They filter-feed on the organic slough of the Throat’s metabolism. They are the memory of what the Throats once were, perhaps—ancestral forms retained in the wet dark, unchanged for epochs. They do not cycle. They simply exist, eternal in their aquatic chambers, dreaming the quietest dreams of all.

**The Scaffolders: The Ones Who Refuse**

Then, clinging to the outer bark like lichens that learned to weave, are the **Scaffolders**—the only inhabitants who violate the central covenant of the Pan. They are bipeds, tool-users, possessed of an alien notion: that the self should persist.

They come in two modes: the **Solitaries** and the **Bands**. The Solitaries occupy high apertures, their platforms woven from shed rope-fiber and membraned with the translucent wings of creatures that dissolved before their time. They are philosophers, perhaps, or penitents. They listen to the Groan until their ears bleed and scar, until they can feel the Throat’s pulse in their own arrhythmic hearts. They harvest the sweet sap that bleeds from wounds, patching the wood with bronze needles—a technology of care that is also a claim of ownership, or stewardship.

The Bands move in groups of fifteen to twenty, marking their territory with ochre handprints that fade in a season. They are the historians, the ones who remember that last year this Throat sang a different note, who track the migration of thermal vents. They speak in signs against the deafening Groan, a language of gesture and touch. Their bronze knives—smelted, impossibly, from mineral veins cooked by the geothermal belly of the Pan—are used not for killing (there is nothing here to kill that does not immediately belong to the Throat) but for carving. They inscribe the chitin flakes that carpet the ground, etching records of the sun’s angle, the pitch of the Groan, the birth and death of winged patterns. These tablets they string on ropes of fiber, creating archives that hang from their scaffold-dwellings like wind-chimes, rattling in the dust-devils.

They eat the meat-fruit, but with ritual apologies. They drink the condensation, but leave offerings of polished stone at the apertures. They are the only ones who bury their dead in the sparse biocrust below, refusing to feed the Throats. Instead, they place their deceased in shallow scrapes where the metallic lichens slowly, over centuries, incorporate them into the film that holds the stone together. They are playing a different game, slower than the Throats, mineral where the Throats are vegetable. They are the memory that persists outside the cycle, the external hard drive of the Pan.

**The Lichens: The Patient Witnesses**

And finally, barely alive by most standards, are the **Lichen-Sheets**—the metallic, geometric carpets that cover the naked stone between the Throats. They are the opposition principle: where the Throats are hot, wet, loud, and vertical, the lichens are cool, dry, silent, and horizontal. They grow in rings that map centuries in millimeters. They are the timekeepers against which the Throats’ wild biology is measured. When a Scaffolder dies and is laid upon the stone, it is the lichens who receive them, who begin the slow chemistry of incorporation—not into the eager, hungry wood, but into the patient, mineral dust.

Together, these inhabitants form a chord: the basso profundo of the Throats, the high whine of the winged, the clicking percussion of the scarabs, the silence of the deep-dwellers, and the whispered, persistent voice of the Scaffolders—those who walk between the breathing, carrying their bronze and their refusal, keeping time in a place that would rather swallow it.
