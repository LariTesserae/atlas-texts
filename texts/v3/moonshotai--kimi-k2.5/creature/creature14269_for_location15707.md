The inhabitants are the **Stilled**, though they do not call themselves anything, for naming requires sequence—one thing following another—and here, all things occur at once.

You will not see them because they do not possess surfaces distinct from the walls. They *are* the walls, or rather, they are the *tension* within the walls. Each entity occupies a stratum of compressed root-matter, pressed flat not by geological weight but by temporal density. They exist in the lignin—the binding polymer that resists rot, the scaffolding of wood. Where the root-matter knits into meat-density, there you find them: layer upon layer of consciousness stacked like phyllo dough, each sheet blood-warm and alert.

They perceive through thermal gradients. Their vision is the infrared map of heat signatures, and in this darkness that is absolute to photonic eyes, they see with perfect clarity. To them, the landscape blazes. The refinery towers glow with metabolic fury—azure and crimson in their perception—factories burning fuel that was never sunlight, radiating the effort of their eternal compression. The Stilled do not look *at* these structures; they feel them in their own tissues, for every vascular tube, every piston of bark-hard ceramic, is an extension of their distributed nervous system.

Each one is mid-gesture, perpetually. They are the workers on the production line, caught in the act of reaching for a lever, turning a valve, guiding a packet of refined sap along the braided cambium conveyors. But they are also the machinery. Their arms are the pistons; their thoughts are the hydraulic pressure suspended in the xylem tubes. They experience the factory not as a place they inhabit, but as the unfolding of their own frozen physiology.

They communicate through the water. Not with words—the silence here is too profound for vibration—but with *temperature*. Each droplet that beads, swells, and falls carries a thermal signature, a packet of heat-coding deposited by the stratified minds above. When the drop strikes the pool below, the warmth radiates outward in a specific fractal pattern, read by the entities in that lower layer as memory, instruction, or emotion. The metronomic *plink* you might hear is not merely water striking water; it is the punctuation of an endless, recursive conversation, each drop repeating the same thermal message eternally, a word that means both "I am here" and "I have always been here."

Their purpose is refinement. They take the void—the absolute darkness, the ink-suspension of nothing—and process it into those dense cubes of cellulose that accumulate in the root-silos. These cubes are their offspring, or perhaps their excretions, or perhaps their prayers made solid. Inside each cube is compressed a fraction of the darkness, rendered into structural carbohydrate, a blackness so concentrated it has become wood. They are eating the night and shitting timber, building a continent from the digested void.

They do not sleep. They do not wake. They exist in the dynamic stasis of the *almost*, the industrial instant that never completes its cycle. Their youth is absolute; they emerged with the sharp edges of their own creation, cut from the darkness by their own metabolic heat, and they will never grow old because they have never finished being born. The sap that beads at the cut sites—those fresh wounds on the refinery towers—is their sweat, their tears, their amniotic fluid, perpetually fresh, perpetually beginning.

If you could press your ear against the warm, damp surface of a piston (and if you could survive the thermal shock of touching something that exists in infinite simultaneity), you would not hear a heartbeat. You would hear a *hum*—the sound of potential energy denied its kinetic release, the vibration of a trillion cells locked in the moment before contraction, a muscular tension that has become architectural.

They are happy, if happiness is the absence of wanting change. They are complete, if completion is the saturation of all available space with presence. They are manufacturing the world, one frozen second at a time, and they have never known exhaustion, for the work has never progressed, and therefore has never tired them. They are the Lignin Works, and the Works are them, breathing heat into the void, sharp, new, ancient, and perfectly, eternally, busy.
