First, there are the **Devourers of the Young Stone**, the fungal lithophages that constitute the true terrain. They have no faces, no seasons, only an endless present-tense hunger that turns obsidian into loam while you watch. Their mycelial networks are the valley’s nervous system—sensing pressure, heat, and the footfalls of things still unborn. They are the only permanence here, ancestor to every eruption, waiting in the porous dark to colonize each new basalt knife the moment it cools from magma. They excrete the damp; the air’s saturation is their breath. They are not background but **foundation**, and every creature that walks, flutters, or crystallizes above them is merely their fruiting body, their spore-delivery system made animate.

Then the **Thermarchs**, the spire-trees, who are less botanical than architectural—columns of calcite urgency drinking from the earth’s vents, growing not toward light but toward the heat-death of the universe, slowly. Their bark is hot ceramic; to touch them is to touch the planet’s digestion. They sweat luminosity, those slow beads of phosphorescent sap that attract the **Mite-Tornadoes**—fist-sized hovering clouds of chitin and fury that beat the air at frequencies that liquefy thought. These mites are not insects but **vibrations given flesh**, living tuning forks that modulate the valley’s deafness. They cluster where the trees weep, harvesting the warmth, and in turn they are harvested by higher orders of noise.

For there is the **Choir Eternal**, the 2.7 density of fauna that is less a collection of species than a **single acoustic event** distributed across billions of bodies. The eyeless amphibians bellowing from fresh crevices are its bass notes; the stridulating limbs of arboreal predators dropping through darkness on silent silk threads are its percussion; the Morse-code pulse of beetle abdomens are its syntax. They do not live in an ecosystem but in a **cacophony** where to be silent is to be invisible, and to be invisible is to be divine. The weave-grass belongs here too—bladed silica that shifts like green glaciers, following infrared ghosts, consuming the heat-shadows of warm-blooded things, bridging gaps between stone spires with living rope that constricts rather than supports.

And in this overwhelming **presence**, there is the **Absence**: the **Hollow Ones**.

They are the valley’s negative space, its silence made flesh. They move through the din without disturbing it, draped in the shed skins of luminous insects so that they appear as patchworks of the bioluminescent mist itself—walking gaps in the visual field. They are deaf to the roar by necessity, their ears attuned to subsonic registers below the chaos, feeling the valley’s breathing through the soles of their feet, navigating by the temperature gradients of stone. They are anatomists not of bodies but of **sound**; with knapped obsidian flakes sharper than surgical steel, they harvest the tympanic organs of specific beetles, extracting the noise to create pockets of engineered quiet, or perhaps to fashion devices that let them speak through the static in frequencies the stone itself can hear.

They do not build. They **borrow**. They sleep in hammocks woven from living weave-grass, singing to the blades in vibrations that coax the silica-edges to soften, to cradle, then to release. When they depart, the grass untangles, erasing the memory of their weight. They leave no scars because they understand that the valley is still being born; to carve it would be to wound a gestation. They tap the spire-trees for resin-water, drinking the heat, wearing the valley’s shed skins, moving through the three-dimensional lattice of carnivorous orchids and sound-drinking roots with the economy of things that have realized they are not separate from the geology but its **consciousness**—the slow, patient awareness that has emerged from the turbulent dark to witness its own creation.

They are not, individually, immortal. But they do not die as others die. When a Hollow One’s time thickens to its limit, they do not rot; they **accelerate** into the compost, their bodies consumed by the lithophages within hours, their calcium joining the young stone, their heat rising up new spire-trees. They become the 0.3 illumination, the blue-green murk, the constant pressure of the damp. They are the only ones who know that to live here is to be **unfinished**—to hatch, mature, breed, and decompose before the slow crystal trees have grown a meter, to exist in the violent, loving now of a world that is building itself upward even as it devours itself, sharp enough to cut, hot enough to birth, dark enough to contain anything that might dare to call itself alive.
