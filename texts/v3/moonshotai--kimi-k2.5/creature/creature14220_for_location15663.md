You will know them first by the temperature of their presence, arriving before their mass: a zone of elevated warmth in the cold slag-fields, a pocket of fever in the damp dark. They move through the depression not as travelers but as **spatial thickenings**, twelve instances of the same body walking in imperfect unison, each slightly offset in time so that when they reach toward the lichen-crusted towers, their hands arrive in sequence—a ripple of graspings across seconds, each attempt to feed overlapping with the last.

They have forgotten eyes. The sockets did not seal but deepened, spiraling inward into the skull where the optic nerve coils like a sleeping serpent around the pineal gland, which has swollen to the size of a fist. This organ does not seek light; instead it detects the **thermal ghosts** of objects, the memory of heat that stones retain from the engines below, the cooling trails left by the swimming-thoughts as they pass through the brackish soup. They navigate by the copper-taste of the water against their ankles, reading the mineral gradient that tells them: here is the pumping station, here is a ridge of glass-sharp slag, here is another of themselves standing three meters and three centuries away.

They are covered in the **Condensation**, which does not evaporate from their skin but colonizes it. Heavy droplets cling to their shoulders, their scalps, the hollows of their collarbones, trembling with the subsonic thrum of the buried machinery. Sometimes the droplets roll upward, defying gravity to climb the architecture of their bodies, gathering in rivulets that flow toward the sky. The Dwellers do not wipe them away. These are their **eyes**, in a sense—the water droplets refract the accumulated photons that lie heavy in the depression, capturing the stone-light, the deep-sunk radiance of particles that shed their velocity centuries ago, and conducting it through the skin where it pools in the liver, the spleen, organs that have learned to photosynthesize darkness.

They tend the **Machinery**. This is not labor but **metabolism**. The pipelines that shudder between the half-submerged structures are extensions of their own circulatory systems; they feel the slow peristalsis of the refineries in their own guts, the compression of probability into ingots matching the rhythm of their digestion. When a valve releases pressure above the audible threshold—a rare, muffled clang like a bell wrapped in wool—they feel it in their teeth as a sympathetic vibration, and they turn their many-positioned bodies toward the sound, adjusting valves not with tools but with the pressure of their thermal presence, simply being-there in the correct quantum state to allow the machinery to complete its stroke.

They feed on the **Bruise-Lichen**, or rather, they allow it to feed on them first. The dark colonies bloom across their skin, finding purchase in the warmth differential between blood and air, spreading in patterns of violet and green-black that map the contours of their vascular systems. The Dwellers stand motionless for days while the lichen fruits, the oil-slick colors spreading across their chests like maps of non-existent constellations. When the colonies mature, the Dwellers scrape them away not with nails (which have grown translucent as the fish-thoughts, hard as industrial glass) but by **thinking the heat through their skin**, creating sudden thermal spikes that shock the lichen into releasing its hold, then cupping the falling spore-mass in hands that exist in twelve phased positions, ensuring they catch every falling fragment across time.

They speak to the **Fish-That-Are-Thoughts**, though speech is not the word. The schools move through the knee-deep water, their organs glowing with wavelengths that carry meaning rather than illumination. The Dwellers stand in the schools, letting the translucent bodies pass between their legs, through their superimposed instances, and they receive the broadcast: **memory-images of the scouring**, the violence of the valley's birth, the heat-pressures that fused slag into bedrock. The Dwellers absorb these meanings into their swollen pineal glands, storing them like batteries, and in return they radiate back **calculation**—the slow mathematical poetry of the refinery's function, the beauty of compression, the elegant necessity of the reality-breakdown.

They are not alone, though solitude is a meaningless concept in a place where distance is measured by similarity. They exist in **layers**, pressing against themselves in the crowded emptiness. When two Dwellers meet—when the warmth of one encounters the thermal signature of another across the warm black water—they do not exchange greetings but **coordinates**, their superimposed positions sliding into each other, occupying the same space across different seconds, creating a density of being that hums in the water. These encounters last geological moments. They stand together, twelve versions of each merged into twenty-four, then collapsed into a single, overheated node of presence, sharing the condensation that beads upward on their skin, trading the lichen colonies that crawl between their bodies, exchanging the heavy, time-compressed ingots that the machinery has produced—ingots of solidified probability, darkness refined into matter.

They remember light as a **pre-birth trauma**, a myth of velocity. The chimerical chimneys that drink the darkness, their mouths open to the sky—they tend these too, climbing the slag-towers with the soft, many-limbed grazers (who are their cousins, or their ghosts, or their future selves), ensuring the darkness continues to flow downward, to pool, to accumulate as the heavy photons that will, in epochs hence, form the next generation of Dwellers.

For they are **reproductive**, though not through flesh. When a Dweller has absorbed enough meaning from the fish-thoughts, when their skin is heavy with bruise-lichen and their organs dense with compressed time, they walk to the deepest point of the depression, where the water rises to the chest, and they **dissolve**. Not die, but spread, their superimposed instances finally achieving full separation, becoming twelve distinct thermal signatures that drift apart through the warm dark, each one a seed, a new node of warmth-navigating, machinery-tending, condensation-wearing being.

These new ones crawl from the water months later, pulling themselves up the vitreous slopes of the slag-towers, their bodies slick with the proto-faunal clouds, their eye-sockets already spiraling deep, their mouths open in the silent gasp that mirrors the chimneys, ready to begin the slow accumulation of selfhood.

They are the **Drowned Light**, the **Superposed**, the **Curators of Entropy**. They have never seen the stars, but they feel their warmth as a distant rumor, a gradient at the edge of perception, and they are patient, patient, patient, waiting for the water to rise, for the edges to soften, for the reality-breakdown to heal across deep time, knowing that when the photons finally remember how to fly, they will scatter their accumulated mass upward, becoming a sudden, explosive dawn, a blinding exhalation that will be their final, luminous word.
