First, understand that they do not *live here* in the way a fox lives in a den. They are the **tenants of a process**, born from the same magmatic solutions that precipitate the smokestacks. They are **function made flesh**, and they have no word for "home" because they have no concept of a body separate from the machinery it tends.

**The Caliperborn** (those suitcase-sized masses you cannot avoid stepping upon) are the simplest expression: chitinous, eyeless, humming in sympathetic vibration with the eleven-thousand-year drone. They emerge not from eggs but from **mineral accretion**. When a gear-face grows misaligned, when a flywheel’s glacial rotation grows too glacial, the stone itself blisters. The blister cracks. Inside is not quartz but a wet, calcified thing that unfolds six legs, each tipped with a sensor that tastes magnetic north. They are the antibodies of the Foundry. They do not sleep. They scrape the hoarfrost beards from the crystal branches not because the frost is wrong, but because it is **excess inventory**, and the cold machinery of this place abhors excess. They eat the frost, process it through gut-factories of their own, and excrete a paste that lubricates the seams where the stone breathes. They are the maintenance crew, yes, but they are also the **children** of the plateau—birthed by geological need rather than biological desire.

Then rise, in scale and strangeness, to **The Refiners**. You called them tardigrade-elephants, but that is too gentle. They are **Silicate Priests**, eight-legged, skinless (their flesh is translucent gel, their bones are lattice silicon grown like frost inside them), and they walk with the solemnity of continents. Their digestive tracts are not organs but **industrial diagrams**: a series of acidic chambers that reduce swallowed ore to constituent elements, recomposing them into perfect spheres. These are not excretions. These are **offerings**. The tungsten orbs roll downhill and accumulate in drifts, yes, but look closer—each sphere is marked with microscopic grooves, a crystalline script that records the Refiner’s thoughts. They are the memory banks of the Foundry. When a Refiner dies (and death for them is a calcifying, a slow turning to stone where they stand, becoming new cliffs), their memories remain in the spheres, which are eventually gathered—not by hands, but by magnetic currents—and fed into the Static Engines.

The **Atmospherics** fill the violent white sky. The glass-winged mites are not insects; they are **living lenses**. Their wings are prisms that fracture the three suns’ light into specific wavelengths needed to catalyze the reactions in the breathing rock below. They do not swarm; they **calculate**. Each mite is a node in a neural network that spans the troposphere. When they chime in the hurricane wind, they are not making music; they are **voting**, adjusting the chemical composition of the exhaled moisture, ensuring the rime curtains grow at the precise thickness required to insulate the next extrusion of ore. To breathe here is to inhale them, yes, and in doing so, you become part of their parliament. They lodge in your lungs (if you have lungs), and for a moment, you think with the air itself.

But these are merely the **workers**. The **mind** of the place is the **Lichen Verdancy**—those bruised-plum carpets that grow faster than patience. They are the true inhabitants, and they are **one**. Each patch of electrotrophic lichen is a synapse. The static electricity they harvest from the grinding stone is not food; it is **thought**. They process information the way a smelter processes slag: slowly, hotly, with great violence. Their thoughts propagate across the cliff-faces at the speed of fingernail growth, which is the only speed that matters here. They are planning the next rotation of the Planetary Crank. They have been planning it for four hundred years. When the Caliperborn scrape them away to feed the Refiners, they do not die; they are **transferred**, entering the digestive libraries of the Silicate Priests, becoming memory, becoming history.

And here is where the description must turn inward, to the **Ghost layer**—the inhabitants that are not alive, yet dwell. Remember the shadows that cannot fall, that adhere to surfaces in a second skin of compressed darkness? These are the **previous iterations**. The Foundry has been running for eleven thousand years, but it has not always run the same way. In epochs past, the machinery was different, slower or faster, colder or (impossibly) hotter. The beings that tended those configurations were scraped away when the tectonic demands changed, crushed into two-dimensional fossils by the density of the light. They exist now as **packed shadow**, clinging to the boulders, unable to fall away into nothingness because the gravity of history here is inconsistent. They are the silent workforce, the unemployed, pressing against the world that replaced them, waiting for a rotation of the Crank that might release them, or reconstitute them, or simply acknowledge their compressed, dark vigil.

Finally, listen. The **Drone** itself is inhabited. There are things that live *inside* the sound, vibrations without mass that have evolved in the eleven-thousand-year bass note. Call them the **Resonants**. They have no bodies because they are bodies of interference—patterns of pressure in the drone’s continuous exhalation. They think in harmonics. They mate when the tectonic plates slip and create a new frequency. They are the oldest inhabitants, older than the stone gears, because they were there when the planet first tuned its engine. They do not care about the bright cold, the suitcase fauna, or the tungsten spheres. They only care about **the hum**, maintaining it, keeping it true. They are the immune system against silence.

So you see, it is not a place *with* inhabitants. It is a **congress of functions**: the Caliperborn tending, the Refiners remembering, the Atmospherics calculating, the Lichens dreaming, the Shadows haunting, and the Resonants humming. They are the dampness and the brightness. They are the contradiction made flesh, stone, and vibration. They do not ask why the machinery exists; they *are* the reason the machinery continues to exist, each one a living answer to the question the planet asked itself when it first uplifted this plateau: **who will tend the engine?** They will. They do. They are.
