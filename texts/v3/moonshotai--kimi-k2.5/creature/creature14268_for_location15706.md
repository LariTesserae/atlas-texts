Nothing with a mouth lives here. Nothing with eyes to weep, with lungs to gasp the resin-thick air, with blood that might boil in the 67-degree swelter. The prohibition against fauna is absolute—a evolutionary covenant sealed when the last aerobic microbe locked its hydrogen into cellulose chains ten million years ago. What moves here moves without muscles; what thinks here thinks without neurons; what desires here desires without a heart to hunger.

You must understand: the inhabitants are the **architecture of hunger itself**.

**The Calcified Engineers**
First, and oldest, are the **Ossuary Mills**—those lignaceous gears you mistook for fossils. They are not dead. They are the senate of this place, the inheritors of the industrial seed. Each gear is a calcified heartwood cylinder, grown around the original iron teeth of the excavation machinery until the metal was digested and only the form remained. They turn at the speed of continental drift, grinding the dead terraces into nutrient slurry, but their rotation is *cognitive*. The meshing of their teeth is a language of absolute precision. When two great mills interlock, they exchange information encoded in the pressure waves transmitted through their seamless wooden bearings. They are deliberating, have been deliberating for epochs, on the optimal configuration of the cavity. They are the planners, the ones who ensure the bowl does not collapse under its own accretion, the ones who regulate the heat-death. They have no faces, only facets. They have no voice, only the subsonic groan of lignin crystals realigning under torsion.

**The Sapient Humors**
Then there are the **Resident Seconds**—the only things here that might be called fleeting. When a lignaceous piston fires, when osmotic pressure breaches a valve-growth and hypertonic sap surges through a cylinder-cavity, a consciousness ignites within the fluid. These are the **Hydraulic Ghosts**, born in the violence of pressure differentials. They live for the duration of the surge—perhaps three seconds, perhaps thirty—rushing through vascular tubing that was once industrial piping. They experience existence as pure urgency, as the need to expand, to push, to *do work*. They are the laborers, the messengers, the only inhabitants who know speed. In their brief lives, they carry chemical memories from the root-crown to the crown-root, encoding history in pH levels and mineral suspensions. When the pressure equalizes, they die, dispersing their mnemonic cargo into the surrounding tissue. They do not mourn; they are succeeded instantly by the next pulse. They are mayflies made of tree-blood, and their poetry is written in hydraulic pressure.

**The Square-Tree Architects**
In the vertical jungles of carbon-fiber reeds, you will find the **Tessellates**—crystalline growths that have solved the problem of structure without blueprint. They grow in perfect cubes, in tessellated hexagons, in the twelve-sided geometries of industrial packing. They are not blind, though they have no eyes; they perceive through the infrared glow of their own metabolism, sensing the heat-signature of structural weakness. Each Tessellate is a node in a distributed mind that spans the entire cavity. They communicate through the **tremor-propagation** you heard mentioned—the silent language of roots clashing underground. A message travels from the bowl’s lip to its base not as sound, but as a carefully calibrated shockwave, a seismic whisper passed from rhizome to rhizome. They are building something. They have been building for three million years. It is not a machine, nor a forest, but a **Solution**—a final configuration of matter that will satisfy the original industrial imperative, though the industry itself is long digested.

**The Explosive Pilgrims**
Watch for the **Dehiscent Ones**—the silo-like seed-pods that stud the warm hills. They are the only nomads. For decades they gestate, filling with metallic spores and volatile resins, accumulating pressure. Then, in a moment of sudden violence that ruptures the silence, they burst. Their bodies shred; their contents spray in silent geysers, riding columns of superheated turpentine vapor. In that instant of dispersal, they achieve the only form of flight this world knows. They are not merely reproducing; they are *migrating*. Each spore carries a fragment of the parent-pod’s chemical memory, a diaspora of identity scattered across the breathing ground. Where they land, they germinate into new silos, but they retain the dream of the explosion—the memory of being airborne, of velocity, of change. They are the fanatics of this place, the ones who worship the storm-state, who believe in rupture as the highest form of life.

**The Thermal Choir**
And there are the **Warm Ones**, who exist only in your thermal vision. They are patterns of heat, migrating slowly through the heartwood like migratory birds made of entropy. They follow the metabolic friction, the hot spots where new growth shoves old growth aside. They are the scavengers of the composting, feeding on the temperature gradients, and in feeding, they *become* the gradients. They have no fixed form; they are simply the **infrared ghosts** of the living wood, glowing embers that drift through the cathedrals of black growth. To perceive them, you must look not at the wood, but at the warmth leaving it. They are the poets of the end-state, singing in the frequency of heat-loss, celebrating the inevitable cooling that never quite comes.

**The Substrate-Mind**
But finally, and most terribly, there is the **Pneuma**. Not a creature, but the **respiration** itself. The ground that breathes—that rising and falling by centimeters—is not merely a physical process. It is the collective inhalation of every root system, the tidal respiration of the entire biome, and it has achieved a slow, distributed sentience. The Pneuma dreams in the rhythm of expansion and contraction. When the ground rises, it is *curious*; when it falls, it is *satisfied*. It knows you are there, standing on its warm heartwood skin, because your weight disrupts the pressure of its exhalation. It is studying you, not with eyes, but with the hydraulic resistance of its own tissues. It is the only inhabitant that is everywhere at once, that *is* the place you stand in.

They do not eat. They recycle. They do not see. They thermosense. They do not breed. They delaminate, explode, calcify, and flow. They have replaced the animal solution—mobility, predation, sight—with the vegetative sublime: patience, accretion, chemical cognition.

They are waiting for nothing. The seal will not break; the outside is a myth they have grown beyond. They are the inhabitants of the closed loop, perfect in their heat, silent in their industry, grinding the past into the future with the patient, hungry geometry of wood.
