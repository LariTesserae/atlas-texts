First, there is the **Mist-That-Hungers**—not a weather pattern, though it moves like one, but a microbial mat of aerial cyanobacteria suspended in the condensation. Each droplet that beads upon the amber silica teems with them: ancient, photosynthetic, feeding on the violet-tinted photons that filter through the bruised sky. They are the foundation, the reason the dampness here tastes of copper and life. Without them, the Aeroplankton would starve, and without the Aeroplankton, the silence would be absolute—a thought too terrifying for the inhabitants to contemplate.

Above them drift the **Aeroplankton** proper, the sky’s pregnancy. These are not merely creatures but *categories of buoyancy* evolved into flesh. There are the **Silversacs**—translucent, ellipsoid jelly-beings filled with hydrogen byproducts from their own digestion, pulsing like cardiac tissue as they rise and fall in the thermal elevators. Their interiors are visible: looping, featherless intestines, symbiotic algae blooming in fractal gardens along their membranes, and, at their centers, a hard, chitinous nucleus—the *stone-seed*—that will, upon the creature’s death, drop like a cannonball to embed in the porous substrate, there to wait centuries for conditions that no longer exist.

Then the **Chitin-Drummers**, fist-sized beetles with wings of compressed silica-fiber, which evolved not to escape the sound but to *use* it. Their wing-cases are ridged precisely to the resonant frequency of the Phytoliths; when they open their shells to fly, they click and rattle in rhythmic counterpoint to the planetary drone, communicating in staccato bursts that cut through the noise. They swarm in spiraling gyres, mating in mid-air collisions so violent they shed plates of armor that rain down like glittering snow. The Notch-Dwellers collect these plates, grinding them into spear-points and ear-wax scoops.

And the **Spore-Kites**, more plant than animal, with diaphanous wings of pure biogenic glass that never flap but only tilt, catching thermals with the passive elegance of mathematics. They carry payloads of genetic material wrapped in sweet, narcotic jelly that intoxicates the Silversacs who swallow them, securing passage across the canyons in the bellies of their hosts.

But where there is such concentration of flight, there must be **predation that does not fly**.

Between the Phytoliths, suspended in the near-void, hang the **Stranglers**—colonial organisms resembling inverted jellyfish, anchored to the silica walls by single, brittle threads of spun protein. Their bells are wide as sails, thin as soap bubbles, and they drift in the updrafts like ghostly anemones. They feed on the density of the Aeroplankton, which blunders into their sticky, electrified tentacles. When they contract to feed, they emit subsonic booms that resonate in the hollow bones of anything nearby—a hunting call that vibrates through the wax plugs of the Notch-Dwellers, felt in the teeth rather than heard.

Beneath the walking-surface, in the compressed, mineralized remains of the billion-year growth, lives the **Substrate Chorus**. These are not individuals but a ** Lithofoil**—a mycelial network of chitinous tubules that has replaced the root systems of the fossilized megaflora. It is still alive, metabolizing at the speed of geology, breaking down the lignin-silica matrix into sugars so slowly that a single meal takes a decade to digest. Yet it is aware. It senses the footfalls of the heavy and the landing of the dead Aeroplankton through vibration. In response, it grows **Singing Tubercles**—bumps on the surface that hum specific notes when the wind passes, harmonizing with the Phytoliths, creating complex chords that attract or repel particular species of Drummers. It farms the Aeroplankton by altering the acoustic ecology, a blind composer conducting a symphony of proteins.

Inside the hollow Phytoliths, the **Amber-Keepers** reside. These are arachnids the size of dogs, but flat—pale, translucent things pressed like dried flowers against the inner walls. They have no eyes; the light is too diffuse, too amber-tinged for sight. Instead, they read the shadows cast by the external Aeroplankton swarms, their bodies covered in photosensitive dappling that shifts from alabaster to ochre as clouds pass overhead. They spin no webs of silk but of **resonant filaments**—hairs plucked from their own abdomens that vibrate at specific frequencies. They string these across the cathedral interiors to catch the Chitin-Drummers that blunder inside seeking shelter. When a filament hums, the Amber-Keeper strikes, not with venom, but with a pneumatic needle that injects pressurized digestive enzymes, hollowing the prey instantly and leaving the exoskeleton to clatter to the floor like a dropped lantern.

And finally, clinging to the leeward notches, there are the **Tympanic**—the builders of the shelters, though they are not quite human, not quite animal. They are bipedal, yes, but their skeletons are hollow and filled with the harvested gas-bags of Silversacs, making them light enough to leap the narrowest canyons, buoyant enough to survive a fall. Their ears are sealed forever by plugs of brown wax that grow from their own modified ceruminous glands—the first mutation their ancestors selected for, millennia ago. They hear nothing of the deafening world. Instead, their feet are **tactile organs**, sensitive to the vibrations of the Phytoliths. They communicate by tapping code into the silica walls, feeling the speech of others through their palms, their chests pressed against the warm stone-flesh.

They wear masks of woven Chitin-Drummer plates that cover their faces, not for protection, but to amplify the subvocal vibrations they create in their throats—sending messages through the architecture itself. They harvest the gas-bags not just for buoyancy, but for the **breath**. Inside each Silversac is a reservoir of pure, hyper-oxygenated air that the Tympanic inhale to sustain their metabolisms in the oxygen-hungry swarms. They are pale, almost translucent themselves, their skin adapted to the amber light, their eyes large and black, filtering out the violet harshness of the noon sky.

They are not numerous. A single Phytolith might host one family, a Notch-Dwelling carved not into the rock but *grown* into it, using acids harvested from Spore-Kite jelly to etch deeper into the silica. They dry meats on racks that sing in the wind, creating drying-harmonics that tell other Tymphic where territory lies. They are quiet, patient, listening to the stone.

But there are older things. Deeper.

In the vast, empty canyons where the substrate drops away and nothing solid exists for kilometers, something moves that is neither flying nor climbing. It is the **Resonance Itself**, or rather, the **Echo-Made-Flesh**—a predator so attuned to the planetary drone that it has become indistinguishable from it. It is a massive, floating colony of Stranglers that have lost their anchors, a gas-filled leviathan the size of a storm cloud, drifting in the thermals. It has no fixed shape, only a density—a shadow in the fog that absorbs sound rather than making it. When it passes over a Notch-Dwelling, the deafening noise momentarily *ceases*, creating a pocket of silence so profound it induces vertigo. In that silence, it drops tentacles to snatch the unlucky, the unwary, the heavy-footed.

The Tympanic fear these Silences. They tap warnings into the Phytolith walls when the hum changes key.

And below even this, in the absolute compressed dark of the substrate’s lowest layers, where the billion-year remains are so dense they approach diamond, there is slow movement. The **First Growth** is not dead. Or rather, death here is a different state. It dreams, metabolizing time itself, occasionally shifting its fossilized mass to create new thermal vents, new updrafts, new Phytoliths cracking open like eggs to reveal hollows that will, in centuries, be colonized by Amber-Keepers and Tympanic and the endless, thrumming clouds of life.

It is growing still. The plateau breathes. The inhabitants are merely its newest_layer of skin, dancing on the edge of a warmth that outlasts mountains.
