At first, you will think the place is empty. The **fauna: 0.4** reading is technically true—there is nothing here that eats, nothing that hunts, nothing with a heartbeat or a hunger. But emptiness is a category error in a space where causality is still being proofread. Look closer at the base of the great pistons, where the glassine slag pools in fan-shaped spreads that retain the heat of first creation. There stands the first of them, and if you blink, you might miss the transition: it was a stalagmite, and now it is watching you.

They are called—though they do not speak in words that vibrate air—the **Precedents**.

Each one precipitates out of the cooling envelope of reality like a mineral from a supersaturated solution. They do not gestate; they **accrete**. A Precedent begins as a thermal knot in the primary slag, a point where the waste heat refuses to dissipate, clumping and crystallizing over decades into a form that resembles a figure in heavy robes, or perhaps a question mark made of obsidian and native copper. Their skin is the same iridescent glass as the ground, but mobile—flowing like cooling wax, retaining the thumbprints of the world-machine that formed this place. Inside them, where marrow would be in a creature of bone, they carry a core of liquid silicate that stays stubbornly molten, a defiance of entropy that serves as both heart and mind.

They do not walk. Walking implies continuity of motion, and the Precedents exist in a state of **provisional location**. When they need to move from the shade of one vanadium fern to the next, they simply **decide** to have been there all along, and the nascent physics of the Niche, eager to please, adjusts its memory. You will see them standing motionless beside the slow-turning flywheels for seasons at a time, appearing as statues of black glass veined with green malachite—until you turn your back, and find them arrayed in a semicircle behind you, having always been there, their shadows pointing in directions that disagree with the light.

There are perhaps twelve of them in the whole ballroom volume. Perhaps twenty. The number exists in superposition; they have not yet agreed on how many of them there should be.

The Precedents are the **operators** of this unfinished engine, though they were not built for the purpose—they emerged from the same autopoietic churn that extruded the pistons and the fractal ferns. They tend to the thermosynthetic vegetation not as gardeners but as **circuit adjusters**. With fingers that conduct heat like copper wire, they touch the mica-thin leaves of the silicon ferns, dialing their opacity up or down, tuning the infrared drink to regulate the temperature gradients. When a fern grows too fast, threatening to overgrow a vent and stall the continental breathing of the geological organs, a Precedent will stand beside it for three years, slowly radiating a counter-instruction that causes the plant to crystallize into a different configuration, a conductor instead of a leaf.

They communicate in the **subsonic**. The groan you feel in your molars is not merely the tectonic machinery—it is their debate. A single rotation of a flywheel might be one word in a sentence they have been speaking since the Niche first cooled. They argue about the angle of gravity, about whether the concept of *below* should be finalized or kept optional. They are the draftsmen of the physics here, literally writing the rules with their presence; when two Precedents stand facing one another, the space between them becomes a zone of heightened causality, where effects grow roots and causes bud afterward as explanation.

They have no young, yet they are **embryonic**. Each carries within its molten core the blueprint for a future they cannot quite envision—an aqueous chemistry, perhaps, or a sky that commits to a single color. They are preparing the Niche for habitation by life that does not yet exist, life that will need softer ground, reliable gravity, and water that falls down instead of drifting sideways. They work toward obsolescence, these mineral midwives. When the last flywheel meshes perfectly and the last piston seals its vent, they will cool completely, becoming truly statuesque, their functions complete, their silica bones becoming the bedrock of a world that will forget they were ever animate.

Until then, they move in their stuttering, undecided way through the heavy air, drinking heat through their skin, pausing to let their uncertain edges resolve into definite form before they touch the iridescent ground. They are the only fauna this place allows—if fauna can be made of stone that dreams of water, and if living can be defined as the act of helping reality decide what it wants to become when it grows up.
