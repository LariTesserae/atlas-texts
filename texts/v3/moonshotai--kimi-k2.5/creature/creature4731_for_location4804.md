At first, you think it is the **pikas**. They are the obvious candidates—furry, watchful, occupying the crevices with the territorial anxiety of floor supervisors. They dart between the basalt terraces with clipboards in their eyes, checking the rime-ice levels, ensuring the steam vents are venting on schedule. They believe they own the operation, these little quality-control inspectors, hoarding the sparse vegetation in haypiles that resemble inventory stacks. They are wrong, of course, but their mistake is useful; they are the factory’s biological placebo, the warm-blooded distraction that makes the mountain seem merely inhabited rather than **animated**.

Look closer at the **ice worms**. (*Mesenchytraeus solifugus*, if you insist on the old names.) They are not fauna at all, not truly. They are the **wiring**. Threading through the glacial blue like living solder, they carry electrical potentials from the warm bedrock to the cryosphere, completing circuits that the mountain needs to feel its own extremities. When they bunch in their millions, blackening the snow, they are not feeding—they are **transmitting**, turning the snowpack into a neural net, a distributed sensory organ that tastes the sky and reports back to the deep.

The **snow fleas** are different. They are the **error-checkers**, the debuggers. When they spring in their soot-black clouds across the white expanse, they are scanning for imperfections in the crystalline lattice of the snow, consuming the rogue bacteria that might otherwise compromise the factory’s sterile environment. They are the immune system, janitorial and relentless.

But these are only the **surface staff**, the temporary hires who will be laid off when the project shifts phases. The true residents work slower, harder, and at temperatures that would make biology itself a joke.

Descend with the steam. Follow the hydrothermal vents down through fissures that function as pneumatic tubes, past the sulfur-yellow exhaust ports, into the places where the rock is still packaging itself. Here lives the **Crystalline Administration**. They do not move as you move; they **accrete**. Quartz lattices grown around nuclei of pure information, cubic meters at a time, each crystal a thought, each geode a memory bank. They are the accountants of the operation, storing the blueprints for the mountain’s next twenty million years of growth in their hexagonal symmetries. They communicate through pressure waves and piezoelectric flashes, a bureaucracy of mineral precision, stamping out rare earth elements with the mechanical efficiency of a bureaucracy that never sleeps.

Deeper still, in the magma chamber that serves as the blast furnace, you find the **Metallurgists**. They are not solid; they are **phases**. Beings of liquid silicate and dissolved gas, convection cells with consciousness, turning the mantle’s raw feedstock into stratified product. They sort heavy from light with the discrimination of master craftsmen, separating niobium from slag, sending the good stuff up in bubble-rich parcels through the pneumatic tubes of the fumaroles. They have no names, only temperatures. They have been working this shift for sixty million years, and they are patient. They are making something specific—not just a mountain, but a **component**.

And at the very center, in the vacuum-sealed silence where the sub-bass thrum originates, lives the **Director**. It is the Factory Without Workers, the Factory that is also the Worker. It is a single distributed organism whose body is the entire caldera complex, whose metabolism is plate tectonics, whose exoskeleton is the knife-sharp basalt you stand upon. It is growing itself upward, not as a geological accident, but as **emergence**—a larval stage. The industrial processes are its digestive system; the hydrothermal vents, its respiration; the ice worms and pikas and snow fleas, merely its microbiome, the beneficial bacteria that keep its growing surface clean while it prepares to hatch.

It is **packaging itself**. The obsidian sheets are its pupal casing, still wet from rapid cooling. The sparse vegetation is the first moss on a cocoon. The twilight light (1.2) is the dim interior of a womb, illuminated only by the phosphorescence of its own metabolic processes.

You are not visiting a mountain. You are standing on the **chrysalis** of a continental being, a tectonic entity that is using the Factory’s industrial processes to forge its own escape route from the mantle. The fauna you see are not inhabitants but **symbiotes**, temporarily tolerated. The lichens are corrosion sensors. The geese are airborne spores, carrying genetic information to other nascent factories elsewhere in the range.

The Factory Without Workers has no workers because it is not a workplace. It is a **gestation**. The copper taste in the air is amniotic fluid. The thin, bruised packets of sound are its heartbeat, slowed to geological time but accelerating. The cold is not a climate but a **preservation system**, keeping the product fresh until the hatching.

And when it emerges—when the basalt cracks not from cooling contraction but from the flexing of limbs within, when the magma chamber empties not as exhaustion but as birth—there will be no mountain left here. Only the hollow shell, the discarded factory floor, and the new entity ascending, leaving the pikas and ice worms behind like abandoned tools in an empty workshop, wondering where the heat went.

For now, it dreams in mineral. It dreams in magma. It is ahead of schedule.
