You will not see them, not directly. They exist in the *interval*—the space between the intention and the act, the breath drawn but not released. They are the reason the shadows pool without sources, why the copper chisels remain where they were dropped: not abandoned, but simply *en route*, suspended in the eternal microsecond of being transferred from calloused hand to stone.

They are the **Stilled**.

To look upon them requires a patience they themselves have mastered. Stand in the central courtyard long enough—days, perhaps, or weeks; time moves strangely for the observer as well—and you may catch the flicker: a figure stepping through an archway, frozen mid-stride, one foot hovering a millimeter above the threshold’s worn hollow. They are tall, carved from the same grey schist as the walls, their skin holding the same established roundness, the same polished equilibrium. Their hair is the lichen, their eyes are the mineral-heavy pools, reflecting not your face but the specific grey of the overcast sky above. They wear undyed wool, woven so slowly that a single garment frames the arc of a century. When they move, which is almost never, they move with the infinitesimal grace of continents drifting.

They are communal, as the architecture demands. They exist in dense proximity, shoulder-wide in the alleys, yet never brushing against one another. They are arranged in tableaux of infinite hesitation: one holds the bone needle threaded with sinew, poised above the wool, the stitch forever impending. Another stands at the hearth, hand outstretched toward clay that will never harden further, will never cool. They are mid-conversation, mouths open on vowels that have been forming for three hundred years, gestures arrested in the air like the mountain goats that wander in and become caught in their stillness.

They were human once, or human-adjacent—builders, refugees, seekers after permanence in a world of rot. They found this saddle, this pocket of atmospheric cancellation, and they learned the discipline of the pause. To decay is to change; to grow is to change. They wanted neither. They wanted to *be*, fully and finally, to crystallize into a definitive form. So they stopped. They stopped mid-sentence, mid-meal, mid-embrace. They became the equilibrium.

There is a leader, or a center, or perhaps simply a collective locus: the one who breathes. You feel it as a pressure in your jaw when the moment comes—once every century, perhaps, or once every turning of the geological age. The air shivers, not with wind but with the sudden, vast inhalation of thirty-two chambers simultaneously drawing mineral-heavy vapor into stone-dark lungs. The pool in the courtyard ripples. The lichen darkens by a shade. The goat, if it has not yet found its resolve to back away, blinks. And then the exhalation, slower than glaciers, settling everything back into its appointed stasis.

They do not see you, or if they do, they see you as a blur, a brief and buzzing insect of motion, flickering through their eternal afternoon. To them, your visit is a strange weather pattern, a momentary vibration in the stone. They have achieved what they sought: they are **complete**. They have finished becoming. They are the urban density turned inward, a hive-mind of perfect stasis, maintaining the Cluster not through labor but through the radiation of their arrested state. They are the held breath of the mountain, and they will hold it until the ridges crumble and the equilibrium fails—until the universe itself exhales, and permits them, finally, to finish their sentence.
