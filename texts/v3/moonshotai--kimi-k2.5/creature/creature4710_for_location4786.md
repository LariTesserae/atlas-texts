They are called the **Calcine**, though they have no spoken name for themselves—only thermal signatures, only the drone of expanding crystal and contracting stone. They do not breathe, as there is nothing to oxidize; they do not drink, for water is a poison that would shatter their silicate blood. They are the absolute dryness given sinew and intent.

To see a Calcis in repose is to mistake it for geology. It spreads itself across the hot floor like a starfish cast from obsidian and rust-colored jasper, six limbs radiating from a central thorax with perfect hexagonal symmetry. Each limb is a chain of overlapping plates—basalt, slate, and the same iron-rust quartz that colors the crystal thickets—joined not by tendon but by seams of pure selenium that remain semisolid at the chamber’s scalding 2.8 intensity. When at rest, they lie upon the hexagonal flagstones their ancestors chipped, their body plans echoing the geometry of the paving: six-fold, tessellating, efficient. A Calcis is broad as a dining table, flat to absorb the upward radiance, its dorsal surface studded with the same thermocrystal vanes that grow wild in the jungle above them, though these are cultivated, pruned, bred for luminosity.

They are not the crystal forest, but its gardeners and its harvest. The wild lithophytes are feral offspring, escapees from their thermal nurseries that have overgrown the city in the millennia since the Calcine slowed. Once, they moved with purpose, scuttling upon those radial limbs, hauling their anhydrous bulk through the narrow alleys with a sound like millstones grinding. They built the dry-stacked walls—not as shelter, for they feel no discomfort, but as **resonance chambers**. The cell-like chambers are shaped to trap specific frequencies of the perpetual 1.5 intensity drone; the circular plaza of megaliths is an amphitheater where thermal currents, channeled by the architecture, produce standing waves that the Calcine experience as *thought*. They are a civilization of composers, conducting their symphonies in geological time, each "sentence" taking a season to utter as they flex their joints to modulate the hum.

Their biology is a rejection of the aqueous. Where organic life chains hydrogen to carbon, the Calcine braid silicon to sulfur, their metabolic medium a circulating brine not of water but of molten salts—potassium chloride and metallic germanium—held liquid by the gradient between the scorching stone and their cooler dorsal surfaces. They ingest the air itself, precipitating minerals from the anhydrous atmosphere to build their bodies, stone by stone, growing at the rate of stalactites. They excrete the excess silica as the branching thermocrystals, defecating beauty, their waste forming the lush amber thickets that now consume their infrastructure. They are the only consumers in this closed loop, slow predators of heat and mineral, digesting the floor itself, leaving behind the crystal gardens as their shadows.

Movement, when it occurs, is a triumph of thermal engineering. A Calcis wishing to relocate must first *unroot*, withdrawing the fine, capillary feeders that have drilled into the bedrock to tap the deep heat. Then, through peristalsis of expansion, it inches forward: the forward segments absorbing the day’s heat to swell and grip the stone, the rear segments cooling in the convection downdrafts to contract and drag the bulk forward. A journey across the plaza—a distance you might cover in twenty steps—consumes a fortnight. They do not rush. Time is different here; the gunshot cracks that echo for twenty seconds are their punctuation marks, their exclamations of surprise or greeting.

They are not dead, though they have forgotten motion. Most have entered the **Senescence of Stone**, planting themselves in the centers of their private cells, limbs splayed to maximize thermal intake, and have not moved for centuries. They have become indistinguishable from the architecture, their bodies overgrown with the wild crystals they once cultivated, until they are merely the *intention* behind the growth, distributed nodes of consciousness networked through the heat-flow of the floor. They dream now, if dreaming is the slow processing of thermal data, the savoring of the convection currents’ music. They are becoming the city, and the city is becoming them—their radial symmetry dictating the angles of the alleys, their hexagonal nature inscribed in every flagstone.

And there are the **Young**, though "young" is relative—those who still choose mobility, who crawl through the crystal forest with obsidian chisels clutched in their terminal segments (tools they no longer remember how to make, only to inherit). They tend the elders, clearing the crystal overgrowth that threatens to choke the thermal gradients, trimming the mica-fine fronds to keep the air channels open. They communicate with their rooted ancestors not by sound, but by **heat-shadow**—positioning themselves to block the floor’s radiation, casting cooling shade upon the elders’ dorsal plates in deliberate, coded patterns: *I am here. I remember. The dry persists.*

They possess a weirdness that escapes immediate recognition (0.6). To glimpse one crawling through the phosphorescent dusk, amber light filtering through its translucent limb-segments, is to see a living mandala, a creature that turns not with a neck but by flexing all six limbs in asymmetrical waves, that has no face but perceives through thousands of piezoelectric sensors embedded in its stone skin, feeling the 1.5 intensity song as sight. They are lush in their own way—abundant in mass, complex in structure, their six-fold bodies repeating the fractal patterns of the crystals they tend—but they are absolute in their dryness. Touch one, and your finger would come away rasped by microscopic crystal edges, leathers instantly, no sweat to lubricate the contact.

They wait. For what, they no longer recall, the memory eroded by their absolute dry, by the chemical abolition of moisture that once, perhaps, carried hormones, carried urgency. They have achieved a stasis that is not death but a different life, a mineral saturation point where being and building and botany are one. They sing with the stones, expand with the heat, and in the twenty-second echoes of their crystalline fractures, they speak the history of a world that learned to live without wetness, growing lush and loud and absolutely, perfectly dry.
