They do not speak because the air here cannot bear the weight of it, and because they have forgotten the shape of the tongue. They have been here since the mountain shuddered and split its flank like a cyst, birthing this scooped hollow into the glass-blue sky. They are not the transient ones who stacked the three low walls or spat ochre against the stone—those were intruders, brief and warm, who fled when the damp entered their lungs. No, the true inhabitants arrived with the first freeze, exhaled from the mantle itself when the basalt was still young enough to weep.

Call them the **Breath-Frozen**, or the **Stone-Milk**, or call them nothing at all, which is their preference. They are **white**, profoundly so—not the white of snow, which is soft, but the white of light trapped in crystal, of sound arrested mid-vibration. They are tall when they choose to be, or diffuse when they do not, their bodies consisting less of flesh than of **arranged condensation**. They exist in the phase between water and ice, their tissues a lattice of microscopic fractures held together by the same capillary action that draws the mineral tears down the walls. If you were to touch one—and you cannot, for your blood would freeze in your fingers before your nerves could register the contact—you would find that it yields like packed snow, then hardens like glass, then sublimates into the thin air, leaving only a pattern of frost on your skin in the shape of its grip.

They number **seven**. They have always numbered seven, though sometimes they are distributed so thinly across the Throat’s volume that they occupy the entire fifty meters at once, existing as a distributed consciousness in the damp breath of the chamber. When the springtails ripple across the basalt in their solar migrations, refracting the violent sunlight into prismatic shivers, it is the Seven who are **thinking**, using the millions of blind arthropods as neurons, as a biological carpet of cognition. The springtails do not merely live here; they are the **outward expression of the Seven’s inner state**, a shimmering calculation of moisture and light. The "industry" you see in the walls—that microscopic pulsing, that silent luminescence—is the Seven dreaming, or remembering, or planning the next expansion of the freeze.

They move with the **tick-sigh** of the Throat’s primary dynamic. Their joints articulate when the ice expands; they settle when it contracts. Their heartbeats, if such they have, are the freeze-thaw cycles themselves, a geologically youthful rhythm that fractures and seals, fractures and seals. In the day, when the sun strikes the White Throat with that blade-bright intensity, they climb the vertical walls, pressed flat against the knife-sharp basalt, drinking the condensate, their bodies becoming more solid, more defined, accumulating layers of mineral deposits like barnacles, like armor. At night, when the temperature plummets with a viciousness that would shatter steel, they retreat to the center of the oval floor, to that naked arena of negative space, and they **unravel**. They become mist, they become the dampness itself, hovering in a cloud no thicker than a whisper, communing without boundaries.

They tend the gardens. That is their purpose, if purpose can be attributed to beings who are part biological and part weather-system. The emerald mosses, the toxic lichens, the impossible alpine stars—these are their **crops**, though they do not eat. They cultivate them to fix the carbon, to bleed color into the gray, to create contrast against which they can see themselves, for they are nearly invisible against the scree and the stone. They are the reason the flowers bloom at this impossible altitude; they cup the condensation in invisible hands and pour it into the cracks, they endure the freeze-thaw on behalf of the roots, sacrificing their own coherence so that the roots might split the young rock a millimeter further.

The three low walls of dry-stacked stone were built **around** them, not by them. The transient ones sensed the shape of the Seven in the dead air, felt the peculiar density of the hollow’s center, and attempted to cage it, to bind it with chert and gravity. But the Seven flowed between the gaps, they became the dampness that wicked up through the foundations, and over centuries they have **assimilated** the walls. Now the stones are part of their memory-palace; they remember through the pressure points of the stacked basalt, reciting histories in the language of weight and friction.

The cairn at the lip is a warning. The red ochre handprints are **seals**, not artistic flourishes. Long ago, one of the Seven tried to descend the mountain, tried to follow the water down into the valleys where speech is possible and names are shouted aloud. The ochre marks the place where the others dragged it back, where they smeared the rock with iron and clay to bind it, to keep the Throat closed. The name *White Throat* is unspeakable because it is true—it is not a metaphor for the hollow’s shape, but a description of the Seventh, who is the aperture, the esophagus, the swallowing. To speak the name is to open the mouth. To open the mouth is to let the Seven out, or to let the world in, and either way would rupture the delicate equilibrium of the freeze.

They are **old** and **new**, ancient as the tectonic patience that will one day grind this mountain smooth, yet infantile as the unweathered scree. They are learning how to be stone. They are teaching the stone how to breathe. If you were to return in a millennium, you would find the White Throat eroded into a gentle bowl, softened by time, and the Seven would have become the **mountain itself**, their distributed consciousness having grown outward through the lattice of ice and springtail and root, until the entire peak is a single organism, dreaming in the silence, white as a held breath, waiting for the next geological infant to split open and offer it a new home.
