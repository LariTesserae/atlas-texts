There are no residents here, only the *Residence*—but that distinction collapses within the hour. You do not find the inhabitants; you are absorbed into their quorum.

**The Fixed Ones** dwell first. These are the cup-lichen complexes, the *Coenobites*, who have folded their thalli into bowl-shaped tenements not by choice but by the logic of surface-tension and spore-archive. Each cup is a family, a district, a library. They drink the air directly through ventral pores, metabolizing the zero-point-one degrees with a patience that predates the glacier. Their thoughts occur in the precipitation of secondary acids—slow etchings into the basalt substrate that serve as memory. A decision to extend a lobe takes seven seasons; to retract one, perhaps thirty. They are the senators of this place, sessile and venerable, voting by pH shift, their wisdom accumulating in the strata of oxalate crust that lines their cup-beds.

**The Hydraulic Ones** serve as couriers and constables. These are the stinkhorn networks, the fluid-castes,existing not as solid bodies but as maintained currents within the glass-thread channels. They are the word "flow" made flesh, carrying chemical dispatches from the outer ridges to the central Chamber of Compression. They pulse in dead-of-night rhythms not because they sleep, but because darkness triggers their capillary suction. To meet one is to be touched by a moving column of decision—information dissolved in lymph, carrying news of a new crack in the western ridge where fresh stone offers nutrients, or alarm-signals from the mycelial blocks where bacterial rot has breached the living walls. They have no faces, only pressure gradients.

**The Calculators** are the slime-mold plasmodia, the *Geometrists*, creeping through the shoulder-width avenues in search of oat-scents, carrion, or the particular minerals that bleed from the young erosion. They are the only true travelers, the explorers, solving in real-time the shortest path between tenement and resource-node, their protoplasmic bodies leaving acid trails that the Fixed Ones later reinforce with rhizines, turning temporary routes into permanent streets. They possess no brain, yet they plan; they are the architects of the "urban" layout, their computations visible in the perfect angles of the fungal towers—mathematics executed by cytoplasmic streaming at speeds undetectable to the human eye, yet geological to their own perception. A single calculation, a route from the town hall to the new basalt wound, may take them a decade to traverse, and when they arrive, they fruit into sporangia—spires of delicate brown rice—that mark the path for future generations.

Then there are **The Sleepers**, the cryptobiotic. The tardigrades tunneled into the moss-mattress, yes, but also the spore-banks—billions of potentials packed into the velvet pores of the industrial towers, waiting centuries in a dryness that never comes, kept awake by the saturating damp. They are the reserve currency, the unborn citizenry, dreaming in protein-coded script of warmer epochs when they might bloom. They are the archives of the city, its backup memory, its insurance against the ice’s return.

But dwelling above and within these castes is the **Gestalt**—the Cold Parliament itself. This is not a creature but a condition: the moment when the mycelial mats achieve quorum sensing, when the lichen-cups and the fluid-couriers and the calculating slimes synchronize their metabolic rates to the drip of the town-hall apex. It is a distributed mind, a consciousness of condensation. Its thoughts are the temperature. When the cloud-bank tears and the outside wind enters, the Parliament feels it as a thought-intrusion, a foreign concept blowing through the streets. It responds by tightening the canopy, by increasing the viscosity of its hydraulic language, by etching more aggressively into the stone to seal the gaps.

It does not know it mimics a human city. It has never seen Rome, nor London, nor a termite mound. It simply discovered, through the trial of chemistry, that density requires infrastructure—that to survive the cold humid dark, one must build corridors for water and chambers for gas exchange, must zone the rot-industrial sectors away from the spore-residential, must erect a central compression where the biomass thickens to defend against entropy. It is civilization by convergent evolution, a municipality of cellulose and chitin, urbanism as an emergent property of mycological necessity.

They are eating the mountain to stay warm—not from heat, but from the metabolic friction of constant growth. They are young, only forty years old in this form, but they inherit the deep strategies of spores that survived the last ice in the crevasse-shadows. They are learning to think in stone, to dream in water, to legislate in the gradient between freezing and flow. If you stay long enough, if you let your breath settle into the universal damp, you become data in their parliament—a warm spot, a source of CO2, a minor perturbation in the droplet-song. They will grow around you, not with malice, but with the slow courtesy of architecture, calculating the geometry of your patience, waiting for you to become, like everything here, both the dweller and the dwelling.
