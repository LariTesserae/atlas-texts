At first, you think the city is abandoned. The platforms of shale jut empty into the blinding blue-white; the ladders of bone and obsidian gleam with no hand to warm them. But then you notice that the *absence* is wrong—the sleeping niches carved into the ice are not empty. They are filled with glass. With stillness. With bodies that have become part of the wall’s refractive structure.

They are the **Rime-Bound**, though they have no spoken name for themselves (sound being both precious and stolen here). They exist in phases, shifting between the liquid and the solid, between the vertical strata of their ancestral architecture.

### The Form That Remembers

To see them active, you must wait by the supercooled seeps where the water refuses its nature. They emerge from the ice-sarcophagi not by climbing out, but by *bleeding* out. Their skin—translucent as the Rime-Mites they cultivate—is semi-permeable, allowing them to osmose through the established ice, leaving behind body-shaped hollows that will slowly freeze smooth again, waiting for their return.

In the light that permits no shadow, they are difficult to perceive directly. They seem to be made of the same optical stone as the walls, prismatic and edgeless. Their bones are visible: not white but faintly luminous, threaded with antifreeze sugars that glow amber in the absolute brightness. They have eyes, but these are vestigial, sealed beneath milky membranes—they see instead through thermal gradients, sensing the convection currents of warm and cold that their own bodies disrupt.

They do not walk so much as *settle*, moving with the deliberate slowness of ice-flow itself. When they must traverse quickly, they climb the notched ladders, but they do so backwards, facing the wall, their bellies pressed to the ice, absorbing the memories stored in the compressed layers. Their hands—still five-fingered, still bearing the genetic memory of obsidian-flaking—are tipped with nails of frozen protein, the same substance that allows the Frost-Bats to cling to the ceiling. They hang from the platforms' undersides as easily as they stand upon them, indifferent to gravity, coated in the diamond-dust of crushed frost-flowers.

### The Garments of Swarms

They wear no furs from external beasts—no beast larger than a mite could survive the ascent through the ice-throat. Instead, they drape themselves in **living mantles of Rime-Mites**, billions of which crawl in regulated tides across their translucent skin. The mites consume the dead skin cells and the condensation that beads perpetually on their hosts, and in return provide a shifting, iridescent armor that responds to barometric pressure. When a Rime-Bound stands still, they resemble a pillar of crawling frost; when they move, the mites ripple in waves, creating the illusion that the person is dissolving and reforming with each step.

The truly ancient among them—those who have slept in their niches for decades, waking only to drink the memory-laden meltwater—wear the **Frost-Bats**. Not as kill-trophies, but as symbiotic crowns. They allow the mollusks to attach to their scalps and shoulders, where the creatures' sail-like vanes open and close in time with the host's slowed heartbeat. The bats filter the air for the mites that would otherwise clog the Rime-Bound's pores, and in exchange, the host provides a mobile hunting ground, carrying the bats through the vertical city to where the mite-swarms are thickest.

### The Silence of Condensation

They do not speak. They have forgotten how, or evolution has unspooled that particular adaptation in a place where sound is devoured by the walls. Instead, they communicate through **thermal calligraphy** and **condensate speech**. 

Watch two Rime-Bound meet on a shale platform: they stand close, almost touching, and breathe upon each other's faces. Their breath—saturated with moisture, superheated by metabolisms that burn antifreeze—flash-freezes in elaborate crystalline patterns on the other's cheeks and eyelids. Frost-flowers bloom and interconnect, forming glyphs that convey complex information: memory-trades, hydrological warnings, poetry about the texture of light. The messages last only minutes before crumbling into the convection currents, but they are read and understood in the seconds before collapse.

They also speak through the water. They dip their fingers into the silent cataracts, and by varying their body temperature, they create micro-currents that shift the braiding patterns of the Glass-Minnows. The eyeless fish school into ideograms, into maps, into histories. The minnows are their alphabet; the pools are their books.

### The Inheritance of Air

They are human, or were, once. The primitive builders who stacked the shale and carved the first notched ladders are their ancestors, but the Rime-Bound have become something that requires the concept of "human" to be stretched until it tears. They are born not from womb-birth (which would freeze solid in the killing cold) but from **the saturated air itself**.

A Rime-Bound who wishes to reproduce enters the highest, brightest platforms, where the light is most intense and the condensation most relentless. They stand motionless for days, exhaling their genetic material in vapor. The moisture—heavy with their DNA, heavy with the Paleocene memories they have inhaled from the ancient air bubbles—crystallizes around them, growing a new form like a frost-flower accreting on a ledge. The parent gradually melts into this construct, their body heat carving the new shape, transferring their memories directly into the ice-lattice of the child's forming body. 

The child emerges when the thermal gradient shifts, sliding out of the ice-niche fully formed, adult-sized but empty of memory, a blank transparency. They must spend their first years drinking the meltwater, inhaling the ancient bubbles, absorbing the hallucinations of the builders until they become saturated with the past. Only then do they don their mite-armor and take up their place in the vertical ecology.

### The Keepers of the Wet

Their culture is hydrological. They understand that motion is preservation, and so they enact rituals of turbulence. They choreograph elaborate dances in the pools, their translucent bodies creating vortices that keep the supercooled water from flash-freezing. They are living impellers, their turbulence (2.9) maintaining the liquid state that allows the Glass-Minnows to survive, which allows the Rime-Mites to thrive, which allows the Frost-Bats to filter, which allows the mycelium to thread, which allows the city to breathe.

They tend to the "memory-veins"—the bubbles of ancient atmosphere trapped in the walls. They harvest these with heated bone-needles, inhaling the Paleocene breath to experience the world before the ice. These visions are their religion. A Rime-Bound high on ancient air might stand motionless for weeks, embedded in their niche, eyes flickering behind milky membranes as they witness the prehistoric forests that existed here when this ice was cloud. They believe they are not merely living *in* the ice, but that they are the ice's consciousness, its way of remembering what it was to be warm.

### The Presence That Breathes

So when the text speaks of the chamber being "packed so densely with presence that it generates its own weather," it is speaking of them. They fill the interstitial pockets not with emptiness, but with their slow, semi-frozen bodies. They are the packed density. The "hushed and shining epoch" is their collective dreaming, their frozen ancestors still partially conscious in the walls, radiating temporal residue.

You do not see them at first because you are looking for movement, for heat, for sound. But they are still, and cold, and silent. They are the reason the air is so pregnant with moisture—each exhalation adds to the weeping walls. They are the reason the light has no shadow—they have learned to bend it, to refract through their prismatic bodies, becoming invisible in the brilliance.

They are watching you now, from their niches, from behind the glass-clear ice, from within the swarms of mites that obscure your vision. They are waiting to see if you will breathe upon them, offering your warm memories in condensation, or if you will simply pass through, leaving them to their wet, bright, silent epoch, their bodies melting and freezing and melting again, keeping the water moving, keeping the city alive at the edge of possible.
