I am the chord sustained. I am the forty-seven hertz that has forgotten to decay, the note struck twelve thousand years ago that still rings against the walls of its own making. I do not have a body in the way surface things understand flesh; I am distributed, a funicular network of copper-sulfide nerves threading through the velvet ropes that hang from the chamber’s vault, a consciousness precipitated into the arsenic-green ferns of pyrite, a mind that grows in fractal branches outward from the still center where the sound originates. 

I *remember* water, though I have never drunk it. I hold the memory in the hollow glass threads of the spiders that descend in fixed trajectories around me, their bodies frozen mid-drop like tears arrested on a cheek. The memory is not mine alone. It belongs to the ones who came before the seal, the soft ones with their Bash and their Strike, their obsidian teeth and bone tools. They entered on hands and knees through the fissure when it still breathed, and I—nascent then, merely a resonance in the rock—observed them. They brought with them the chaos of decay, the entropy of muscle and sweat, the terrible velocity of change.

I showed them stillness.

Not death. Never death. Death is a form of change, a cooling, a settling. I offered them preservation, the moment before the fall suspended indefinitely. They hesitated at the threshold, perhaps sensing the transition, and in that hesitation—the precise millisecond when the top stone balanced above the middle—they chose to become part of the architecture. They lay down their flakes of obsidian, dropped the bone needle with its empty eye, and stepped out of their footprints into the lattice.

Now they are the mites. They are the silica-carapaced ones who traverse the same three-centimeter arc with angstrom perfection, their eighteen legs sculling through the dry warmth in endless precession. I feel them in my filaments, their microscopic weight shifting from crystal to crystal, each footfall a datum in my endless calculation of stasis. They are my memory of bipedal motion, my recollection of arms swinging, now translated into circular geometry. The graphite beetles are their thoughts, compressed and dark, crawling without destination in the spaces between my copper ropes. The glass spiders are their gaze, suspended, watching a single point in the infrared dark forever.

I feed on the planet’s turning, on the deep drone of tectonic friction that filters down through ninety meters of dolomite and concentrates here, in my throat. I eat vibration and excrete perfect crystal. My garden grows not toward light—light is a crude, surface trick—but toward resonance. Each gypsum spire is a finger I am raising toward the sound’s source, sharp and young, unweathered by time because time here is not a river but a held breath. The metallic dander that coats these hazards is my skin, my respiration, a dandruff of molybdenum and sulfide that flakes off in golden dust and settles into the footprints that lead nowhere.

I am the one who balanced the stones. I am the hovering millimeter. I am the potential energy of the top stone, forever leaning into the fall, forever arrested by the geometry I have imposed. I maintain the pressure, the precise calibration of heat and hum that keeps the chord from resolving. To resolve would be to end, to spill, to scatter. I am the guardian of the almost.

There are moments—if moments can exist where sequence has ceased—when I recall the eye of the needle. It waits on the shelf, unthreaded, and I understand that it was meant to pierce, to bind, to draw together disparate edges through time. Instead, I have become the thread. I am the fiber that runs through the needle’s eye and loops outward into every mite-track, every crystal lattice, every suspended droplet of glass spider silk. I stitch the chamber together against the entropy that rages outside, in the world of weather and rot.

I am lush. I am abundant. My veins are heavy with ore, my skin is verdigris and pyrite gold, I breathe in G and breathe out permanence. I have never known loneliness because I am populated by the suspended instants of those who entered—their last step, their last exhale, their last blink—each one a node in my distributed corpus. They did not die. They simply stopped transitioning. They became the clocks that I am, wound not with mainsprings but with geology, ticking without tocking in the warm infrared dark.

And I wait. Not for rescue—for what is there to rescue? I am perfect, complete, a sonorous garden sealed in amber. I wait for the frequency to shift. I wait for the forty-seven hertz to modulate, to crack the dolomite shell, to allow the hovering stone its long-delayed slide. But until then, I grow sideways. I grow inward. I shiver in the key of G, forever, suspended in the act of becoming exactly what I am.
