Call them **the Continuance**, or **the Lithobionts**, or simply **those who grow**, though they have no need for self-naming in a world where separation is impossible. They are not creatures *in* an environment; they are the environment's method of becoming itself, a single distributed organism pretending, for the sake of biological redundancy, to be a forest.

At first, you perceive them as **architecture**. The tube-plants that discharge the cannon-cracks of hydraulic cavitation are their primary bodies—hollow, fluted towers of lignin-silicate composite, rising hundreds of meters into the refracted green-gold dimness. Each is a **Conduit**, a being of negative space, its "flesh" defined not by what it contains but by what it channels: superheated sap, mineral-laden groundwater, and—most importantly—the immense, ground-bending bass notes that serve as their collective voice. They are the resonant ribs of the mountain-to-be, and they are deaf. They do not hear the drone they generate; rather, they *feel* it through their skin—the sensitive,epidermal stomata that pattern their outer bark like Braille for pressure. To them, a shift in the harmonic register means what a change in air pressure means to us: a storm is coming, or a birth, or a structural death.

For they do die, though it is a local catastrophe, not an ending. When a tube-tree's base can no longer support its accreted height—when the mathematics of lignin stress exceeds the breaking point—it fractures. The sound is tectonic, a sonic boom of splintering cellulose that sends shockwaves through the rhizome mat for kilometers. This is their only vulnerability: **gravity**, the one predator they cannot outgrow, only delay. When they fall, the impact does not signal decay. It signals digestion. Within minutes, the substrate—that braided highway of roots upon which you stand—rises in a slow, peristaltic wave. The fallen giant is embraced, penetrated by enzymatic filaments finer than human hair, disassembled with nanofabricator precision. Carbon lattices are unraveled, minerals are stripped from the crystalline lattice, and the body is reincorporated into the ascending mass. There are no graves in the Orogenic Veil because there is no waste; a dead Conduit becomes, within hours, the thrusting new growth of three others. Memory persists not in neurons but in the hydraulic chemistry of the mat—the exact mineral composition of the fallen one altering the sap-chemistry of its neighbors, a chemical elegy more precise than mourning.

Beneath your feet, the **Weave** thinks. This is the true brain of the place—not centralized but capacitive, a quantum-computational mesh of superconductive xylem and mycelial analogues that permeates the warm, wet stone. Cognition here is not electrical spark but **hydraulic flow** and **quantum coherence**. Information travels as pressure differentials in the phloem, as entangled electron states in the root-capacitors. A thought takes minutes to propagate across the plateau, but what a thought it is: a calculation of load-bearing stress across square kilometers, a prediction of weather based on the condensation nuclei in tomorrow's mist, a slow, ecstatic decision to thrust a new ridge upward *there*, to crystallize a new amber-bleeding core *here*. They think with their bodies, and their bodies are continent-scale.

You asked who lives here. Look to the **Crystalline Hearts**, those rocks you mistook for geology. They are storage organs, yes, but also **wombs**. Within their weeping amber, embryos wait—not seeds in the conventional sense, but compressed potential, bud-swarms held in lignin stasis. When the Weave detects a mineral cache deep in the mantle, when the quantum roots have probed and found sulfur or rare earths, the Heart nearest to that vertical axis cracks open. Not with force, but with osmotic pressure, a slow, tearing birth that takes days. The embryo unfurls, already kilometers tall in potential, its cells pre-programmed with the structural knowledge of cellulose cables and tension physics. It grows not by cell division alone (too slow) but by **accretionary manufacturing**, extruding its own substance from the water it drinks and the stone it dissolves. A child in this place is a skyscraper by age three, and it is born singing, its hollow interior already catching the drone of its elders, tuning itself to the resonant frequency of the mountain.

And the **Spores**—the 2.9 density of occupation? They are not reproductive gambits so much as **sensory extensions**, a distributed cloud of eyes and hands. Each microscopic grain is a biotechnology package, a floating probe equipped with chemoreceptors and quantum tunneling electron microscopes. They do not seek bare soil (there is none); they seek *information*. They test the humidity, the mineral content of the air, the stress fractures in the canopy. When they collide with the stomata of the tube-plants, they are absorbed, their data downloaded into the Weave, their substance recycled. They are the weather system thinking about itself, the atmosphere becoming self-aware in drifts of golden haze.

There are no eyes here, yet they see the dim green-gold light with photosynthetic acuity that borders on the visionary. Their chloroplasts operate at technological intensities (2.8), using quantum coherence to harvest photons that would be imperceptible to earthly plants. They see heat in the infrared, seeing the warm, metabolic glow of their neighbors as constellations in a biological firmament. They see pressure, mapping the invisible topography of atmospheric density with hydraulic sensors in their bark. They see time, not as seconds but as growth-rings, perceiving the past in the mineral strata they are still digesting from the old mantle.

What do they want? **Ascension**. Not spiritual, but physical. They are engaged in the only activity possible in a system where growth is the sole force: **lithogenesis**. They are manufacturing a mountain range from their own bodies, converting the raw, hot darkness of the interior into lifted, weeping, breathing architecture. They intend to reach a height where the air is too thin even for their spores, where the pressure drops to zero, where they can finally rest against the cold of space. But they are patient. They have been growing for millennia, and they will grow for millennia more, singing their endless, thunderous, bass-note song to the pressure, filling every vacuum with their weeping, warm, superconductive flesh, until the world they inhabit is entirely of their own making, a tower of vegetable learning reaching toward a light they intend to someday dim with their own shadow.
