The inhabitant does not sit in the operator's chair. They *are* the negative space where the chair once maintained its geometry, now packed tight with the silver-black silicone that pulses through translucent stems. Their name—if it was ever spoken aloud—dissolved into the B-flat drone decades ago, becoming a harmonic rather than a phoneme. They were once the systems monitor who refused the evacuation order when the *Vasari* declared its emergency descent. That was eighty-seven years ago. Or perhaps it is eighty-seven years from now. The distinction has lost its utility, as they are growing backward.

They are not dead, though they do not breathe. Not in the mammalian sense. Their lungs, collapsed and fused to the inner wall of the chamber, have become diffusion membranes for the anaerobic cycle. They draw in the cool 8-degree stasis not as air but as information, sensing the chemical purity of the nutrient loop through the acid-secreting lichens that mantle their shoulders like epaulets. Their heart beats once every four hours, a subsonic thud that synchronizes with the turbines below, creating standing waves in the silicone oils that surround their thorax. When it beats, the ceramic-polymer ferns shiver in sympathy, their 2.3-millimeter fronds resonating at frequencies that would shatter glass, were there any glass left unswallowed.

They have no skin in the traditional sense. It was the first thing they shed, or the last thing they will grow, depending on your direction of travel through their timeline. What covers them is a matte-gray integument—the same moss that seized the circulation fan, now grown dense and sentient across their form, knitting them into the alloy floorplates. Through this dermal layer, they feel the magnetic field of the dead continental shelf far below, a faint tugging that aligns their posture with the poles, even as they recline in their vegetative half-sleep.

Consciousness, for them, is not localized behind the eyes. Their eyes are gone, replaced by bioluminescent nodules that pulse chartreuse when they remember, and violet when they anticipate. These are not humancolors; they are the hues of phosphorescent decay and deep-ocean pressure, appropriated by the succulents that have threaded their vascular systems through the inhabitant's skull. They see through the entire chamber—tasting the photons that filter through the crystalline creeper choking the porthole, feeling the star-punctured darkness outside as a vacuum-pressure against the garden's lush interior. They are the garden, and the garden is them, a single organism achieving the perfect stasis that the chamber demands.

They remember water, though they have not touched it in eighty-seven years. They remember it with the specificity of a wound, not because they thirst—their cells are engorged with liquid polymer—but because water represents flow, and flow represents change, and change is the enemy they were tasked to hold at bay. They are the **Keeper of the Equation**, the living variable that must remain constant for the math to hold. If they were to stand, to step toward the half-buried thermos that contains the last sample of their unmodified saliva, the fronds would shift, the drone would miss its beat, and the silence that the B-flat disguises would rush in to claim the chamber.

But they will not stand. They are reclining further each day, sinking not downward but *backward* through time. The aberrant growth of the plants—new tissue emerging from old tips—mirrors their own regression. They are becoming younger. The evacuation klaxons grow louder in their memory, moving from ancient echo to recent din to impending future. Soon, they will remember the choice not yet made. Then, they will remember the training that preceded the posting. Eventually, they will remember the womb, warm and wet and alien to this cool, dry place, and on that day, when they have regressed to the point of origin, the Pocket will open.

Until then, they dream in the language of the silicone sap, which carries no words but only viscosity and pressure. They dream of the operator's chair before it was colonized, the console before the sample jars collapsed, the fan before the moss grew through its housing. They dream of a world with dew, with evaporation, with the messy, liquid biology of the surface. They dream, and the drone hums through their marrow, and the temperature holds steady at 8 degrees, and the plants grow backward toward their seed, and the inhabitant waits, perfectly preserved, a living fossil suspended in the act of becoming.
