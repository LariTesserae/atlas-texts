The **Manufactories** do not sleep. They are vertebrate in their posture, columnar and jointed, but their flesh has become architecture—trunks of lignin-hardened alumina, pith replaced by ceramic insulation, cambium layers that are conveyor belts of phloem running nitrogenous slurry up to the catalytic crown. They are individuals, yes, each claiming a territory the size of a small nation, but they are also *processes*—embodied algorithms of acquisition and refinement. You might mistake them for scenery, for the Titan-trees have achieved a stability that mimics geography, but they are awake, calculating, engaged in the slow, grinding calculus of chemical economics. 

Within each, a heart beats that is a **piston-cluster**, a muscular engine the size of a cathedral, thudding in a rhythm that has not ceased in three thousand years. The trees are ancient, and their age is measured not in rings but in the accretion of industrial byproducts—layers of heavy metals plating their inner cavities like lead-lined vaults. They solve problems. When a seam of unexploited phosphate is detected by exploratory roots, the cambium bulges overnight, extruding a new branch, a dedicated refinery-architecture grown like bone spurs, to process the find. They compete not with speed but with *efficiency*, waging wars of osmosis and ionic exchange across kilometers of contested clay, their roots releasing chelating agents to poison rival intake systems, or, in times of scarcity, trading ammonia for sulfur through mycelial cables that function as fiber-optic nerves, a dark web of carbohydrate and data pulsing beneath the dust.

Above the white clay, the **Lightningwalkers** make their migration. They are not birds—birds cannot ride this thin, hammered air—but rather *kites*, frameworks of conductive cartilage and desiccated tendon stretched over gas-blight hollows. They have no need of wings; they surf the static fields. With four or six trailing tendrils of nerve-fiber, they ground themselves to the Trees’ highest branches, drawing the differential between the ionized stormheads and the earth, skating along violet arcs with their mouths open, gulping ozone and the fried remnants of spore-clouds. They are abundant, breeding in the warm stacks of the Manufactories, their cries silent against the thunder, their life cycles tuned to the electrical frequency of the Vent Song. When two thermal columns collide and the sky bruises into storm, they mate in the flash, their bodies briefly luminous as filaments before they scatter, seeding the stratosphere with their eggs, which drift down like ash to root in the next warm exhaust.

At the roots, in the microclimates of industrial heat where the permafrost is being mined, live the **Unction-Beetles**. They are the size of fists, chitinous, their carapaces graphite-black to radiate the surplus heat. They are living lubricant, drawn to the grinding interfaces where tectonic plates of bark shift and the great pistons seize. They secrete a wax heavy with molybdenum sulfide, greasing the biological machinery, and in return they are permitted to drink the condensation that beads on the coolant veins, the only liquid water for miles. They are surgeons and mechanics, cleaning the valves of accumulated sulfur, their mandibles clicking in time with the thud of the pistons, a percussion section to the Trees’ orchestral grind.

Higher up, in the stacks and vent-shafts that riddle the upper trunks like hollow chimneys, dwell the **Resonance-Doves**. They are deaf, their cochlear bones fused by generations of exposure to the roar. They do not sing; they *vibrate*, pressing their breasts against the ceramic walls to feel the infra-sound of the manufacturing, communicating in tremors that travel through the lignin. They nest in the warm updrafts, feeding on the mineral-rich thermals and the insects that are drawn to the bioluminescent coolant—beetles and flies that have evolved to metabolize the phosphorescent algae that grows in the Trees’ vascular systems. Their droppings, rich in rare earths, are harvested by the Trees’ epidermal roots, a closed loop of sustenance.

Beneath them all, in the absolute dark of the sub-clay where the water is locked in ice and being actively sublimated, lies the **Mycelial Parliament**. This is not a creature but a *consensus*, a distributed mind of fungal filaments and taproot extensions, the combined unconscious of the Manufactories. Here, the chemical warfare and trade are reconciled, the stock exchange of ions moderated. It thinks slowly, its synapses firing over days, but it thinks deeply, optimizing the Steppe as a single engine. It remembers the mountains that were ground down to make this tableland; it remembers when the air was thicker and the Trees were smaller. It is the accountant of erosion, ensuring that the 2.2/3 rate of destruction remains sustainable, that the Trees do not consume the world faster than the world can be pressurized into new substrate.

And there are rumors, in the 1.3/3 weirdness, of **Something Being Made**. Not growth for growth’s sake, not merely the processing of sulfur and nitrogen, but a *product*. In the oldest Manufactories, the ones whose bark has become indistinguishable from weathered concrete, there are chambers that have grown sealed, secret, where the pistons pump not to circulate but to *compress*, creating pressures and temperatures that fuse hydrogen into helium in microscopic, biological reactors. The Trees are manufacturing something at the limits of chemistry, something that requires the Vent Song to sing at specific harmonics to catalyze reactions in the high atmosphere. The Lightningwalkers cluster there, thick as flies, drinking the strange radiance. It is not alive, what they are making. It is not dead. It is the reason the Steppe generates its own weather, its own pressure systems, its own defiance of entropy. It is the thing that lives in the place, and it is still being born.
