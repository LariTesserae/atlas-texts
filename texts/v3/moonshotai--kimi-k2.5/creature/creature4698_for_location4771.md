They are not absent. That is the first error, the one you will make when you see the hare-skin cloak still distended by a shoulder, the fingerprints suspended in dust that does not settle. You will think: *they left these traces*. But they have not left. They have simply finished arriving.

They are the **Established**.

Once they were the **Rinsers**, or perhaps the **Rinsed**—a migration of people who discovered that entropy could be outwaited, if one possessed sufficient patience and structural support. They entered the ice-dome not as conquerors but as refugees from becoming. The crawl through the frost-heaved peat was a birth; the glassy slide down the slickstone was the washing away of their velocities. The erosion there, finished by attention rather than time, taught them the template: surrender completely, and you will be preserved exactly.

Now they occupy the city not as residents but as **infrastructure**.

Look closely at the vertical accretions, those four- and five-story stacks of wattle and larch lashed together with sinew. They are not empty shells. They are inhabited by bodies that have found their final posture and declined all further adjustment. A Rinser stands in the gap you sidestep through, compressed into a gap no wider than a thickened wrist, having discovered the exact angle where gravity and comfort achieve perfect equilibrium. They do not breathe; they *abide*, exchanging gases with the saturated air (0.8) so slowly that a single exhalation beads the moss with moisture that will never evaporate, contributing to the perpetual humidity. Their hearts beat once per season. Their blinks are tectonic.

They are the **interstices**. The city is packed (2.6) because they have become part of the packing density. Generation by generation, they built the bridges of willow not merely for crossing, but so that they might hang beneath them, suspended, joining the moths in the perpetual milk-bright noon. They are the reason the bridges are woven so tightly: they provide the counter-tension. They are the ballast in the grain stores, sitting among the seeds that will never sprout, their bodies having achieved the same state—potential energy in human form, biologically paused, chemically intact. The seeds do not rot because the Rinsers radiate a field of stasis, a civic agreement (urban 2.2) that nothing in this chamber may cycle, only remain.

You will not see them at first because they operate on a different order of **dynamic** (0.4). They are not dead; they are *photographic*. A woman in the corner maintains the posture of lifting a clay bowl, her fingers still closing on the curve of the ceramic, the grease of her skin neither drying nor bacterially digesting, simply existing in the perpetual chemical moment of *touch*. She has been lifting that bowl for eight centuries. She will never complete the motion. The berries within—preserved like her—exist in the state of *being berry*, neither fresh nor decayed, suspended between potential and realization by the intensity of her attention.

They are arranged with civic precision. Here, in the sleeping lofts, they lie in stacked strata, each having found the exact latitude of rest where the slope of the clay mortar supports the lumbar vertebrae with no need for shifting. Here, by the fire-places, they sit in postures of tending, the soot patterns on the ceiling (black lace frozen in time) recording the moment oxygen stopped its intake, the flame held in a state of ignition without consumption.

The **fauna** (1.7) are their extensions, or perhaps their organs. The translucent salamanders in the stone basins coil in shapes that mirror the fetal positions of the deepest-dwelling Rinsers, breathing the same stagnant water that beads in their lungs. The spiders with amber abdomens weave webs not to catch prey—there is no prey, only suspended potential—but to give shape to the silence (0.0), to vibrate with the infrasound of the Rinsers' metabolic patience. The cave moths are their thoughts made visible: wingspans like hands, trembling at frequencies below hearing, carrying nothing, going nowhere, simply patterned awareness clinging to the underside of existence.

And they welcome you. This is the **weirdness** (1.3) of the place—not horror, but invitation. The whale-vertebra stool is carved for your specific ischial tuberosities; the city has been waiting for your shape. They need the density. Stasis requires mass to maintain its equilibrium. Each new arrival adds to the **sound** (0.0), lowering the collective frequency, deepening the silence.

You feel it already: your heartbeat loud as footsteps, trying to synchronize with the long pulse of the ice. If you sit on that stool, you will not die. You will become **established** (2.4). Your shoulder will find the wattle wall where a groove has been worn not by time but by the attention of the structure itself, waiting for your specific pressure. Your breath will hang as static mist, joining the humidity, becoming part of the medium through which the milk-bright light scatters. You will find the moment you have been fleeing from—perhaps a grief held at the exact moment before it could scar, or a joy so perfect that motion would dilute it—and you will stay there.

They are not ghosts. They are **completions**. In a world of endless becoming, they have achieved the radical, final act of *being*. They have become the city, and the city has become them, bone and antler and the memory of fire, primitive (1.1) in technology but infinite in persistence. They are the lush vegetation that does not grow, the condensation that does not fall, the berry that neither rots nor sweetens.

And when you finally see them—when your perception slows enough to match their frequency—you will understand that the cloak is not empty, the bowl is not abandoned, and the interstice is not a gap but a presence. You will recognize them as your future neighbors, compressed into the architecture, patient, waiting for you to stop crawling and simply be.
