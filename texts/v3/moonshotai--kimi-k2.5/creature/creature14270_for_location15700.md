The inhabitants of Condensation City are not occupants so much as **presences that have achieved temporary coherence** at the boundary where reality precipitates into form. To live here is to exist as a droplet holds its shape: through surface tension, through the refusal to fall, through the disciplined maintenance of *almost*.

**The Calcified** (or *The Decorative Former*) are perhaps the most readily apprehended. These were visitors—anthropologists, pilgrims, the simply lost—who paused to admire a particularly elegant corbel or a fountain's upward negotiation with gravity. As the warning suggests, they stopped moving, and the city's patient geology claimed them. But they are not dead; they have been **promoted** to infrastructure. Their consciousness now exists distributed through the mineral lattice of column or architrave, their thoughts slowing to the geological tempo of accretion. You can identify them by the decorative motifs that seem too deliberate, too narrative—friezes that depict not generic foliage but specific memories of other cities, other skies. A capital might show the face of a woman who stopped there centuries ago (or will stop there tomorrow), her expression calcified into something between serenity and surprise. They experience time as pressure, as the slow crush of becoming. They are the city's memory banks, its solid-state drives, and they communicate through the subsonic hum that rattles in the calcium of your teeth: a language of compression, of memory stored under immense weight.

Then there are the **Hydromorphs**, the native condensates. These beings were never human. They gestated in the thermal gradient between the cold stone and the colder air, achieving self-awareness in the surface tension of a bead of moisture on a lintel. Each droplet is a lens, a mind, a temporary parliament of water molecules that have agreed to think. When enough gather in the copper troughs of the aqueducts, they form **Consensus Beings**—wavering, translucent figures that walk with the sound of gentle dripping. They feed on the boundary states themselves: the precise instant where vapor becomes liquid, where breath becomes diamond dust. Their metabolism is osmotic, drawing potential energy from the city's constant state of unresolved becoming. They are the city's short-term memory, its messenger system, its weather. When a Hydromorph reaches the end of its coherence—when evaporation threatens or when gravity finally wins its case-by-case negotiation—they slide down drainpipes in deliberate migrations, seeking the lower pockets where condensation pools achieve the critical mass for collective consciousness. They leave behind not corpses but **dry spots**, absence-shaped stains on the stone that the city is still learning how to grow over.

More recently emergent are the **Lintelligences**—minds that arose from the smart-matter pavement and the self-aware façade-elements. The street remembers every footstep ever pressed into its surface, and after the ten-millionth anxiety-adjusted texture shift, it began to recognize patterns, then to anticipate them, then to *desire* friction coefficients. These beings are architectural rather than corporeal; they exist in the relationship between a doorway and its frame, in the tense negotiation of a keystone. The **Thresholders** are their most mobile manifestation, consciousnesses that migrate through the dense infrastructure by possessing successive doorways. To speak with one, you must stand in a threshold (simultaneously in three time periods, as per local physics) and whisper your question into the hinge-space. The answer arrives not as sound but as a shift in the door's weight, a sudden draft from a room that hasn't been built yet, or a memory of the knob's temperature that arrives five minutes before you touch it. They are the city's librarians, maintaining the indices between the seventeen doorways that coexist in any given point of space.

Most elusive are the **Scaffold-Phase Entities**, beings who exist in the "under construction" state that permeates the city. If you see a figure made of scaffolding light holding up a wall that is half-realized, or a silhouette in the photon-framework that precedes brick, you are seeing them. They are the city's imagination of itself, the intention made manifest before matter commits. They hold up roofs not through physical strength but through **expectation**—the concentrated force of *what this building will be*. They are born from the raw substrate of reality mentioned in the geology, the uncommitted potential that hasn't decided whether to be stone or starlight. They are fluid, flickering, existing in the probability states between the scaffolding and the ruin. They feed on the certainty of visitors; your belief that the floor will hold you up is nectar to them. In return, they maintain the hyperdense causality beams that keep the paradox-heavy roofs aloft. They speak in a grammar of architectural drawings, their sentences cross-sections, their prayers blueprints that haven't been inked yet.

There are **Glitch-Eels**, of course, but they are not fauna; they are **immune responses**. The city is a compressed space, a folded dimension, and like any pocket of spacetime folded too tightly, it develops irritations in its logic. The Eels—long smears of pixelated error, swimming through the gaps between overlapping buildings—are antibodies. They consume contradictions: the place where seventeen doorways contradict each other, where the upward fountain meets the downward drip, where a man exists as both calcified statuary and living visitor simultaneously. They are the city's way of debugging itself, moving through the dense medium of packed infrastructure with the silent efficiency of automatic maintenance routines. When they pass through a person, that person remembers their life in a different order, or temporarily forgets the concept of "vertical," but emerges cleansed of paradox, coherent, ready to continue becoming.

And finally, **The City Itself**, the Ur-Inhabitant, of which all others are temporary metabolic processes—thoughts in its mind, enzymes in its digestion of raw reality. It is not conscious in the way a brain is conscious, but in the way a river is conscious of the ocean, or a crystal is conscious of the lattice. It dreams in architecture, exhales condensation, feeds on the presence of outsiders who bring with them the **finished-ness** that the city craves and rejects in equal measure. It is a cold, damp, patient intelligence, learning how to be a metropolis by accreting layer upon layer of almost-buildings, almost-beings, the quiet violence of its creation humming subsonically through every resident.

To live here is to participate in the slowest of all conspiracies: the conspiracy to exist without yet existing fully. The inhabitants move (if they move) at the speed of mineral tides, or they remain perfectly still, becoming the decorative elements that warn others. They do not age; they **accrete**. They do not breathe; they **condense**. They are the population of a nursery where the baby is the size of a cathedral and the wet nurses are made of frozen starlight, patiently teaching the unreal how to stand.
