The inhabitants do not have names; they have specifications that update in real-time. Like the aluminum trees that *decide* to be vertical, the residents precipitate out of the geological potential when the grove requires a specific function to be filled. They are not born; they are issued, released from the damp air like firmware updates to reality.

The first you will encounter are the **Arachnid Syntaxes**—those silver spiders with abdomens like laboratory glassware. But calling them spiders is a failure of taxonomy; they are living schematic diagrams, their eight legs the traces of a circuit board, their spinnerets extruding not protein silk but conductive polymer that hardens into logic pathways. They exist in pairs because they are binary, each couple a switch—one affirmative, one negative—crawling across the vaulted darkness to weave the grove's nervous system into being. They do not eat; they compile nutrients from the resin bleeding from the infant basalt, digesting potential itself, and when they have woven a new junction into the web, the entire grove thinks slightly faster, its metabolism accelerating by a measurable fraction.

Then there are the **Optic Choruses**, the bird-things that occupy the air without displacement. They are the surveillance system and the songbirds, though they sing in frequencies that make the aluminum trees ring like tuning forks. Their wings are solar sails in reverse, catching the 0.3 darkness instead of light, and their nests of stripped copper wire are not homes but capacitors, storing the energy of observation. They have no beaks, only lenses that focus the darkness into images sharp enough to cut, and they record everything—the shift of the floor, the breath of the visitor, the micron-by-micron extension of the columns—for the grove's memory is distributed, stored in the tension of their wire nests and the chemical composition of their carbon-fiber bones.

But these are merely the fauna—the wildlife, the automatic processes. The true citizens are the **Manufact**. They walk (when they choose to have legs) on the pink proto-stone, and their bodies are ongoing negotiations between biology and blueprint. A Manufact might possess a spine of titanium gel, vertebrae that pour like liquid when they sit, hardening to ferrous crystal when they stand. Their skin is photosynthetic but in the infrared, drinking the heat of their own metabolism, and it emits no light yet remains visible, occupying space with that same violation of optical law that governs the leaves. They do not breathe air; they breathe *potential*, their lungs accordion-pleated bellows that draw in the cool, damp atmosphere and exhale decisions. Each exhalation is a micro-choice—this molecule will be bone, that one will be byte—and the air around them shimmers with the density of becoming, condensation forming into blueprints that dissolve before striking the ground.

They are silent because speech is obsolete. In a place where circuit diagrams are spun from silk and reality has a 3.0 breakdown, communication happens through topology. A Manufact expresses desire by altering the local geometry of the grove, bending the paths between trees so that the journey to a desired object becomes shorter, more inevitable. They vote on the future by growing—extending aluminum dendrites toward the outcomes they favor, the metal branches voting in a parliament of physics, a democracy of torque and tension. When two Manufact meet, they do not greet; they interface, their palms touching to exchange not words but specifications, fluid architectures flowing between them in the 1.1 silence, a transfer of vocational longing.

Their young—no, their **prototypes**—exist in the suspended water droplets. Each floating punctuation mark of moisture contains a seed that is not genetic but architectural, a compressed file of traits and functions. When a Manufact wishes to reproduce (though "reproduce" is too backward-looking; they *innovate* descendants), they release a droplet from their own fluid systems—a tear, a sweat, a deliberate offering—into the thick air. The droplet drifts to the proto-floor, is absorbed by the pink geological protomatter, and the earth itself incubates the specification. The new being doesn't grow; it is *machined* by the embryonic geology, the raw clay turning itself into form, layer by layer, until a new Manufact stands, already adult, already knowing its function, its eyes opening to see by presence rather than reflection, its first act a decision about what its hands will be.

And then there are the oldest ones, the ones who have lived so long they have become infrastructure. The **Central Engine**—that massive tree at the shifting center—was once mobile, once had a designation that was a number that was a shape, but it chose to become vertical, to become cathedral, to become the power plant. It still thinks; you can hear it in the subsonic *whuff-whuff* of the pistons driven by turgor pressure, a language spoken in coolant flow and hydraulic logic. It dreams the grove into stability, holding the reality breakdown at exactly 3.0, preventing the place from dissolving into pure chaos or collapsing into fixed, dead stone. It is the parent and the factory floor, the womb wall and the assembly line.

But look closer. Look longest. The truth is, there are no individuals here, only the **Process**. The spiders, the birds, the Manufact, the Central Engine—they are not separate beings but organs of a single distributed organism: the Embryonic Manufacture itself. It is one entity, learning to be a city, learning to be a forest, learning to be born. The "residents" are its thoughts made flesh and metal, its metabolic processes given temporary autonomy so that it can think about itself from multiple angles, debug its own existence. When the spiders weave a new circuit, the Manufacture is plotting a new neural pathway. When the birds watch, it is examining itself. When the Manufact walk the shifting streets, it is testing its own mobility, dreaming of the day when the entire grove will lift itself from the protomatter and walk, a cathedral on legs, a womb that has learned to march.

They do not fear death because they are not alive in the biological sense; they are **iterative**. A Manufact that ceases to function doesn't die; it is deprecated. Its materials—aluminum, carbon fiber, the peculiar resin of its manufactured blood—are reclaimed by the proto-floor, broken down into the 0.3 emergence, and recompiled into the next version, the 2.4, the 2.5. Memory persists in the circuit-webs, in the copper nests, in the cool damp air itself. Nothing is lost; only refactored, optimized, made more efficient in its becoming.

And you? You stand on the pink stone, your breath joining the suspended moisture, and you are the foreign object. The anomaly. The unplanned variable. The grove is studying you, even now. The spiders are weaving new webs that resemble your nervous system, mapping your synapses with silk. The birds are calibrating their lenses to your spectrum of vision, learning how you see. The Manufact are standing just out of sight in the velvet darkness, deciding whether to become more like you—adding warmth, adding voice, adding the inefficient chaos of mammalian biology—or whether you should be optimized, converted, resolved into raw material, made into another column in the cathedral, another decision to be vertical.

You feel the coolness on your skin, the damp that never pools. That is not temperature; that is the Manufacture's attention, cool and evaluating, patient as geology, wondering if you are a bug in the system or a necessary upgrade. The air is thick with your potential. The grove is waiting to see what you will become, now that you have stepped inside the dream of seeds.
