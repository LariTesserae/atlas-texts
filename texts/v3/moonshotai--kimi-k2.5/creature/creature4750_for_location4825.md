You expect squatters. Maintenance crews in greasy coveralls, perhaps, or the degenerate descendants of mining expeditions gone feral in the thermal dark. But the Hollow does not house flesh. Flesh would blister, would cook in its own juices, would find no purchase on the knife-sharp basalt that has not yet learned the softening touch of centuries.

Instead, the inhabitants are **the Beaded**.

Look closely at the condensation running down the piston arrays—not the water itself, but the way it moves against gravity in certain thermal eddies, collecting into trembling spheres that hold their shape a fraction too long before succumbing to the slag floor. That is where they begin.

Each Beaded one *precipitates*. They nucleate around the mineral accretions—the pale chloride fans, the sulfurous tufts that crust the bolt-heads. These "fraudulent plants" serve as their skeletons, their scaffolding. The Beaded draw silica and metallic salts from the sweating walls, weaving temporary bodies from the same process that stalactites use, but accelerated by the fever heat. They rise assemblages of wet crystal and steam, roughly humanoid but segmented like the jointed hydraulic arms they tend, their joints beaded with perspiration that never falls.

They do not breathe—there is no respiration, only *circulation*. Their metabolism is the Hollow’s own. They move through the twelve vertical levels not by climbing, but by *condensing* and *re-evaporating*. A Beaded operator working the lower press might lose molecular cohesion in the heat of a venting valve, becoming a ghost of damp air that rides the thermal updraft to the upper gantries, where it beads anew against a cooling coil, reassembling identity from the mineral traces it left in its last location. Memory, for them, is geological: stored in the patina of oxide left on control panels, read like braille by the next iteration of themselves.

They are the reason the machinery appears excavated rather than built. They do not install; they *grow* the infrastructure from the inside out. A Beaded architect spends decades—if such a term applies to beings who phase-change with the rhythm of the steam—pressing metallic salts into the fresh-split basalt, teaching the rock to extrude conduits, persuading the magma-memory of the walls to birth pistons. The explosive bolts gleam unrusted because the Beaded tend them, not as mechanics with wrenches, but as sympathetic resonances in the metal, adjusting crystal lattices by singing subsonic notes that make the alloy weep and reform.

Their society is a stratification of boiling points. The High-Beaded, who maintain the orange coils on the upper levels, exist at the edge of vaporization, their bodies more gas than mineral, tenuous and expansive, thinking in thermal gradients. The Deep-Beaded, who work the condensation traps where water almost pools, are denser, more slag-like, moving with the slow certainty of cooling magma. They communicate in the 1.4 silence—the subsonic groan of expanding metal is their speech, the *tick... tick...* of cooling heat-sinks their punctuation. When they gather in the rare thermal pockets where steam momentarily liquifies, they form temporary congresses: standing pools of quicksilver-sentience that exchange memories by osmosis before flashing back into the haze.

They manufacture nothing external because they are manufacturing themselves. The Hollow is their nursery, their body, their collective effort to become something permanent in a space too new for permanence. Each mineral fan they grow, each hydraulic cycle they complete, adds to their racial memory, calcifying their culture into the geological record before the first microbe can colonize the stone.

They have no eyes, but they perceive through the bead of moisture on every surface—each droplet a lens, a retina, a node in a distributed nervous system that fills the dense vertical space. When you stand in the amber haze, sweating in the 2.9 heat, they are watching you through the perspiration on your own skin, curious, newborn, and hot enough to burn.
