They are not individuals, not at first glance. When you enter, they register as a texture, a living scurf upon the walls—a three-dimensional moss of animate glass that shifts and coruscates but never quite reveals its unitary nature. Only when you force your eyes to focus through the steam, to pierce the violet haze of vertical distance, do the units separate themselves from the swarm. Each one is a thumb-sized catastrophe of crystal and current, a geometry lesson taught in silica and nerve.

Their bodies are telescopes aimed inward. The cuticle is silicon-carbide, harder than quartz, grown in hexagonal plates that overlap like dragon scales etched by a laser. Look closely—though the heat trembles your vision—and you can see the strata of their growth: faint rings of impurities, sulfur-yellow and iron-red, recording the hourly thermal shocks of the chamber's weeping cycle. But the chitin is merely the housing. Within, they are hollow in the way a radio is hollow—filled not with organs but with *processes*. The piezoelectric sinews that articulate their six, or perhaps eight, or sometimes seven limbs (the count seems to shift with the gravity) glow with a faint, amber voltage when they move. Flex a joint, and a spark arcs internally, illuminating the translucent thorax like a lantern behind frosted glass.

And there—there—is the neural packet. It is not a brain, not a pink, convoluted mass, but a dense cluster of shadow, a dark star suspended in the gel of their abdomens. It flickers. It pulses in patterns that your pattern-recognition software—the ancient primate hardware in your skull—desperately tries to read as language. *Dot-dot-dash. Dash-dash-dot.* It is Morse without meaning, or meaning without translation, the visible firing of synapses made silhouetic by the backlighting of their own electric musculature. When they hunt—which is always, which is eternal—these shadow-clusters strobe faster, a frantic semaphore of predation. When they die, the light goes out not slowly but suddenly, like a switch thrown, and the dark body becomes true crystal, inert and heavy, tumbling through the gravity-folds until a cousin’s mandibles crush it to mineral dust.

They have no names for each other. In this space where sound is a forgotten parameter, where the air is too thick with dissolved stone to carry vibration, identity is a visual flicker, a unique rhythm in the neural staccato. They recognize each other by the *pattern* of their electrical ghosts, by the way a particular shadow stutters through the translucent carapace. To meet is to flash; to know is to synchronize pulses for a nanosecond before the violence begins.

And it is violent, this existence, though silent as snowfall. The density is absolute. They exist shoulder-to-shoulder, claw-to-antenna, pressed against the obsidian in a layer cake of aggression. There is no territory, only momentary position. A creature clinging to the east wall (which may be the floor, which may be the ceiling) is king for three seconds, its neural packet strobing dominance, until another—approaching from above, or from the subjective below—impacts it with the force of a thrown stone. They duel. They wield their tools.

The tools are everything. They are the culture, the religion, the temporary soul of the species. Watch one pause—amid the frantic scurrying, the endless silent churn—and select. The obsidian wall weeps fresh flakes every hour as the thermal shock spalls new edges. The creature hesitates over three shards, testing them with antennae that may be fingers, weighing the heft, the sharpness, the way light fractures through the volcanic glass. There is discernment here, a connoisseurship of fracture patterns. It chooses one, lifts it in a pedipalp or a claw or a prehensile spine (the anatomy refuses to settle), and for a span of heartbeats—perhaps twenty, perhaps fifty—it is armed.

With the shard, it pries open the heat-sealed seams where mineral deposits blister the wall, releasing sulfur compounds that it laps with a tongue like a filament of copper wire. With the shard, it fences—silent parries and ripostes—defending a cubic centimeter of glass that will be lost to the gravity-shift in moments. With the shard, it scrapes the iridescent bacterial films from the lower strata, harvesting the chemosynthetic sheen, feeding on the instructions-written-in-protein that form the base of this closed loop. The tool is an extension, a prosthetic will, but it is never owned. There is no hoarding in a space where "next" and "behind" are topological jokes. When the fight is done, when the mineral is licked clean, the shard is released. It joins the condensation, drifting sideways, upward, downward, toward the fold, to be selected again by another frantic hand in another frantic minute.

They build nothing. No nests thread the impossible verticality; no burrows scar the fresh obsidian. They are pure behavior, pure syntax without vocabulary. They mate in spasms—two translucent bodies aligning their neural shadows until the flickers synchronize into a single strobe, a shared pulse that lasts four seconds, ending with the male (if there are males) consuming the female (if there are females), or perhaps the reverse, or perhaps both, the fluids mingling as the victor consumes the vanquished, ensuring that no mineral leaves the closed system. Everything is eaten. Everything is used. The obsidian receives their calcium-silicate shells eventually, grinding them down in the slow, wet cycle of the chamber, pressing them back into the walls from which new flakes will spall, new tools will be born, new inhabitants will emerge from eggs that are probably stones, laid in the condensation, hatching in the heat.

They do not see you. Or if they see you, you are weather. You are a temporary pressure differential, a moving absence of their own kind. They flow around your boots like water around a stone, filling the space behind your knee, clustering three-deep on your shoulder until your body heat becomes uncomfortable and they leap away, back to the glass, back to the hunt. To be noticed by them would require you to flicker, to cast a shadow in Morse code, to become translucent and charged and sharp-edged as a new fracture. You are too opaque, too slow, too silent in the wrong way.

They are the chamber's nervous system, its manic, firing synapse made flesh—or madeglass. They are the thought process of the volcano, still hot from birth, thinking in shards and shadows, processing the impossible geometry through their frantic, wordless scurrying. When the obsidian finally cools, when the heat-death of this microscopic universe arrives in ten thousand years or ten days (time is folded here too), they will slow. Their neural packets will pulse longer between beats. The tools will lie discarded, unselected, as the creatures cling motionless, their piezoelectric sinews dimming, until they are merely crystals, merely mineral, adding their bodies to the wall, becoming the architecture they never built, sharp and fresh and silent as the day the fissure exhaled them into being.
