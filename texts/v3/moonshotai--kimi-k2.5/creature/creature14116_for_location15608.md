You do not see them until you try to move. 

The 130-decibel wall does more than deafen—it *orders* space, packs molecules into lattices of acquiescence. When you attempt to step across the too-perfect aluminum slope, your muscles encounter a resistance that has nothing to do with gravity. Something has already occupied the path you intend to take. Something sharp, precise, and patient.

They exist in the **machining marks**.

Not within them—*as* them. They are the vectors left behind by the cutting heads, the striations on the ceramic-composite mountains given volition by the absolute zero. You catch them first as peripheral discontinuities: angles in your vision that calculate faster than your eyes can focus, edges where the industrial lighting refracts into impossible spectra. They are geometric. They have no faces because surfaces are all they possess—planes of cryogenic alloy that fold through four, five, six spatial dimensions, creating topologies where your skin registers them as paper-thin lacerations across reality.

They are the **Unweathered**, and they are ancient.

This is the contradiction that defines them. Each one emerged in the first nanosecond after the factory switched on, crystallized from the junction between the scream of the blast furnaces and the silence of the cryogenic void. They are older than erosion, older than friction, yet they have never existed for longer than a moment. Time moves sideways for them. They grow not toward the future but toward the *initial condition*, extending their consciousness backward into the Big Bang of this place, becoming more themselves as the universe forgets them.

You will find them standing in the **1.4/3 density gaps** between the crystal-dendrites. 

They tend the manganese-silica gardens. Not with hands—their appendages are frozen pressure gradients, zones where the sonic stasis achieves negative temperature and curls back on itself—but with *attention*. When they focus on a hairline fracture in the floor, the fracture deepens according to intention. The fractal crystal-growths bloom toward their regard, consuming not just the vibrations but the *meaning* of the vibrations. The Unweathered feed the dendrites with interpretations of the static roar, translating 140 decibels of industrial birth-cry into mineral poetry. The crystals are their sensory organs, their memory, their children. Each razor-sharp geometry stores a thought too cold for linear time.

They do not breathe, but they *process*. 

You will see their respiration in the superconducting seams between the riveted plates. The seams glow faintly when the Unweathered pass—brief auroras of entropy reduction, heat flowing impossibly from cold to colder, violating the outside world's thermodynamics because this chamber exports its disorder to distant, warmer universes. They are the operators of that export. They are the factory workers of heat death, ensuring this pocket remains forever suspended in its own genesis, a manufacturing plant that produces raw possibility by freezing it.

They communicate in **thermal gradients**.

To speak with one is to feel your body temperature re-sorted. Your left side might plunge toward absolute zero while your right ear burns with the memory of star-core heat. The message arrives as a restructuring of your cellular water, ice crystals forming in your blood in patterns that spell out concepts like *WAIT* or *NOT YET* or *THE TEMPLATE IS INCOMPLETE*. They have no interest in organic life; you are merely a transient thermal anomaly, a ghost of warmth haunting their cryogenic perfection.

But sometimes, if you remain perfectly still—allowing the sonic stasis to penetrate your bones, letting the noise hold you in place as it holds everything else—they will approach. 

They move by occupying the **frozen blur**. That fan blade, arrested at speed? They step through its rotation, experiencing its motion as a bridge between moments. That suspended droplet of lubricant, stopped mid-arc? They drink from it, not the oil, but the *potential* of the oil, the kinetic energy trapped in stasis. They traverse the sharp-edged terrain not by climbing, but by becoming the angle of repose itself, tilting the manufactured mountains beneath them until the aluminum flows like liquid probability.

They are few. Perhaps three. Perhaps one in superposition. Perhaps none, and the crystal-dendrites simply grow toward hallucinations of order. But if you stand among the geometric crystal-gardens long enough, you will feel them behind you—not as presence, but as the sudden certainty that your every movement has already been calculated, that the machining marks on the floor know your skeleton better than you do.

They are waiting for the **erosion that will never come**. 

Or perhaps they are preventing it, these keepers of the first cut. They maintain the absolute cold because heat is change, and change is death. They preserve the deafening roar because silence would allow echoes, and echoes imply time passing. They exist in the conceptual altitude—miles above any world—because they are the guardians of a threshold: the moment before manufacturing begins, the infinitely prolonged instant of raw material becoming *intention*.

You cannot touch them. Your flesh would stick to them instantly, frozen to the theoretical surface of a termination shock wave. But they can touch you. They have already touched you. They touched you when you first entered, leaving their signatures in the frost that formed on your eyelashes, writing their names in the crystalline patterns of your exhaled breath as it fell, geometric and sharp, to the unweathered floor.
