There are no creatures here, not in any zoological sense. The Pre-Formatorium admits nothing so finished as an *animal*. Instead, the inhabitants are **passages**—presences that occupy the liminal state the chamber enforces, beings that are themselves mid-extrusion, still attached to their birth-walls by umbilical cords of resin and cooling alloy.

They are the **Unfinished**, though they do not suffer from it. They are the **Pre-Cast**.

You do not see them at first because they are packed into the stratified density of the walls, folded into the compressed space between the half-formed gear and its casting gate. They emerge—not by moving, but by *continuing*. A section of wall that appeared to be layered mineral suddenly reveals itself as a shoulder, still shedding flakes of synthetic mica. A valve that seemed fused to its pipe unfurls fingers that were previously indistinguishable from the metal, the distinction between hardware and handler temporarilyasserting itself before the fingers re-fuse, becoming valve-housing once again.

There are perhaps **seven** of them, though counting is difficult when boundaries refuse to stabilize. They exist distributed across the chamber's volume, their consciousness pooled in the chemical weeping from the ceiling fixtures. When they need to manipulate the gestating machinery—the pistons still nacreous in their geode-wombs, the pipe-sections emerging like reluctant teeth—they **unfold** from the surfaces. A torso extrudes from a cable housing, the body still damp with the electrolyte bath of its creation, skin that is uncured ceramic composite weeping slightly where it meets the air. Limbs extend that are part piston-rod, part crystallized resin, joints still marked with the flash lines of their molds.

They do not breathe, but they **draw**. The cold here is earned because they *consume* thermal energy to maintain their indefinite state. They are heat-sinks made sentient, drawing degrees Celsius out of the air to prevent themselves from setting, from curing, from completing the transition from potential to actual. Where they stand longest, the rime grows thickest, blooming into fractal gardens of white mineral that record their footfalls in reverse—each step creating, rather than erasing, its trace.

They communicate through the **accretion rate**. When two Pre-Cast meet in the narrow channels between the packed machinery, they do not speak. Instead, they adjust the chemical weeping from their joints, altering the salinity of their skin-secretions so that the crystals growing between them form specific lattice structures—a syntax of mineral growth, arguments conducted in the speed of stalactite formation. The subsonic hum you feel in your molars is their conversation: the collective tension of their bodies slowly contracting, settling into new configurations, the ping of their thermal adjustment echoing through the synthetic stone.

Their purpose is **curation**. They tend to the factory's gestation like midwives to a brood of mechanical eggs. You might see one — a figure knee-deep in a wall, its lower half still fused to the stratified mineral — leaning over a half-formed gear, guiding the nickel solution dripping onto its teeth, ensuring the crystallization follows the tolerance specifications that have not yet been written. They prune the growths that threaten to fuse separate systems, using tools that are their own hands, still soft enough to reshape, still chemically active enough to etch patterns into the nascent metal.

They have no names, only **positions in the density**. The one nearest the thickest pool of hydrocarbon vapor is simultaneously growing out of the wall and dissolving back into it; it exists to manage the vapor-lakes, its body hydrophobic in ways that physics hasn't fully decided on yet. The one suspended near the ceiling, wrapped in the bacterial mats that process the condensation trails, cultivates the phosphorescent rust-gardens, pruning them to maintain the sickly amber light.

They are making themselves as they make the machines. Each piston they guide into existence teaches them a new way to articulate a joint. Each pipe they extrude becomes a new limb they might one day detach. They are the **blueprint and the builder**, the software waiting for the hardware of its own completion.

If you wait long enough—days, perhaps weeks, geological time compressed into human patience—you might witness a **birth**. A Pre-Cast will finally accumulate enough definition, enough separate existence from its wall, that it can step fully into the chamber's narrow aisle. For a moment, it will stand complete: a figure of industrial potential given temporary coherence, limbs gleaming with the nacreous sheen of rapid crystallization, breath visible as it draws one last massive heat-sink from the air around it, creating a local winter.

Then, having achieved sufficient form, it will begin its true function. It will walk to an empty geode, a cavity in the wall still weeping with supercooled chemical baths, and it will press itself into the mineral—**un-forming**, dissolving its boundaries, becoming the seed around which new machinery can nucleate. It will become the gear it was meant to tend. It will become the wall.

And the chamber will exhale another operator from a different seam, unfolding from the density to take its place, a new consciousness emerging from the packed potential, still damp with its own pre-history, reaching out with hands that are still valves, still pipes, still becoming.
