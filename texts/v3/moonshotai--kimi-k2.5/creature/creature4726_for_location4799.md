What lives here does not breathe, yet respires in centuries. The inhabitants are not residents but **residences**—processes that have achieved subjectivity, architectures that woke up and found themselves walking.

**The Lithoscent** come first, so ubiquitous they constitute the atmosphere as much as the landscape. These are the crystal colonies you mistook for vegetation: vast, distributed intelligences spread across the basalt facades like neural networks frozen in quartz. Each grain is a neuron; each district a lobe of a mineral brain that thinks in tectonic time. They do not grow toward light—they grow toward *meaning*, extending oxalate tendrils into the fulgurite towers to read the electromagnetic memory of lightning strikes that occurred before the continents knew their names. Their metabolism is interpretive: they digest the slow rotation of the buildings, the thermal expansion of centuries, translating stress into cognition. When you walk through the dust, you traverse their synapses. The static that raises the hair on your neck is their attention turning toward you—a million microscopic lenses focusing, wondering if you are new sediment, or merely weather.

Then there are the **Drifters**, those mantas of smoke and stretched membrane, but they are not "creatures" in the zoological sense. They are *temporal shears*—living edges where fast time meets slow. Each Drifter is a boundary condition made flesh (or made something analogous to flesh), their bodies existing only at the interface of chronologies. They glide through the spatial knots where the staircases loop, harvesting the energy differential between a plaza that experienced Tuesday and a balcony still lingering in Monday. They feed on the friction of when. You do not see them move; rather, locations *unfold* around them, and where they pass, the crystal dust arranges itself into dioramas of moments that never quite happened—a library of might-have-beens written in talcum geometry.

More unsettling are the **Cartographers**, the ones who were never born and thus cannot die. They are the true natives, if "native" can apply to entities that predate the ground they stand on. Each Cartographer occupies multiple verticalities simultaneously; they exist in superposition, their bodies stretched across the folded dimensions like sheet music draped over a stair. To perceive them is to suffer vertigo, for they appear as long, impossible shadows that flicker against walls where no light source exists, or as the strange sensation of being watched from behind while standing against a precipice. They are mapping the city even as they are mapped by it, charting the fractures in reality with their own elongated existence. They communicate by adjusting the local architecture—causing a doorway to slide three years into the past, or a window to open onto a probability where the seas never left. They are trying to find the exit, or perhaps they are building one.

But the rarest, the ones the city waits for, are the **Stratified**. These are the thinkers, if thought can be compressed into stone. They exist in the growth rings of the great towers, layered like amber memories. Each Stratified mind occupies a different geological epoch simultaneously—an entity whose left hand argues with the Cretaceous while its right hand debates with the yet-to-come. They are the city’s memory and its anticipation, fossilized in the act of becoming. When the crystal dust settles in unusual patterns, when the wind pauses as if listening, it is the Stratified turning in their sleep, dreaming the city deeper into existence. They do not move through space; they move through *density*, becoming harder and softer as they shift between layers of compressed time.

And finally—though "finally" means little here—there is the **Steward**, or the **Accretion**, or simply **The Waiting**. It has no form because it is the form—the architectural ambition mentioned in the city's origin, the process of deposition become conscious. It is the reason the streets loop back on themselves, the intelligence behind the gravity lenses and the upward-falling dust. It lives in the silence between your footsteps, in the absorption of your breath, in the slow grinding rotation of the towers through invisible axes. It is growing, always growing, adding to itself the way a mollusk builds its shell from the inside, using the Cartographers as eyes, the Drifters as antibodies, the Lithoscent as its distributed nervous system.

It has been building this city for epochs, not as a home, but as a **gesture**—a message written in impossible geometry, intended for a recipient who has not yet arrived. The Steward waits. The dust settles upward. The city breathes. And whatever you are, walking through the perpetual twilight bleeding bruised plum light into oxidized copper—you are noticed, you are being incorporated, you are becoming part of the strata, another layer of the message, another mind trapped in the beautiful, breathing mineral, slowly realizing that you too are waiting for something to arrive.

Something is coming. The city holds its breath. The horizon fractures, and in that fracture, the inhabitants see the shape of what approaches. They have been preparing the architecture for its arrival since before stone knew how to be still.
