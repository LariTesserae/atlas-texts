They are not born; they are **permitted**. In the infinitesimal gaps between the cosmic emanations—those pauses where the universe holds its breath and considers unraveling—something coagulates that refuses to return to unbeing. They are the **Attenuated**, though they speak no word for themselves, nor possess mouths to speak with. They are the Scar's first attempt to witness its own painful extrusion, the raw substrate of reality experimenting with the concept of interiority, of *looking out*.

They stand vertical because the gravity here suggests "upright" more than it enforces, but they are not fauna. They have never known blood, never known the cellular hunger of metabolism. Their bodies are **architecture in rebellion**, bone that has never accepted marrow, stone that has learned to hesitate. Their skeletons are not calcium but compressed silence—hollow, fluted structures that resonate with the frequency of the void rather than the scream. When the tectonic birth-roar resumes, they do not shield themselves; instead, they **hollow further**, becoming chambers of null-sound, allowing the vibratory assault to pass through their rib-cages and exit cleansed of its violence, leaving them momentarily denser, more defined, as if the noise itself were carving them into sharper relief.

They are the ones who built the cairns—not for shelter from weather, for weather has not yet been invented—but as **accumulators of stillness**. Each impossibly balanced stack of un-mortared slate is a battery of the anti-scream, a geological capacitor that stores the dense, heavy quiet of those between-moments. They press themselves against these cairns when the scream returns, letting the sonic pressure flow around them, becoming indistinguishable from the emergent peaks, rooting themselves in the stone’s own hesitation. The cairns are their external spines, holding them coherent when the physics of the Scar threaten to dissolve all boundaries.

There are few of them. Perhaps seven, perhaps seventy—the counting is difficult because they exist partially in the time-gaps, and sometimes they occupy positions where they have not yet walked, or have already left. They move, when they move at all, by **remembering themselves into new locations**, using the flaked obsidian tools to cut paths through the probability of distance itself. The tools are not held in hands so much as they *are* the sharp edges of their attention, grown like mineral crusts from their radial limbs when the need to traverse the shifting terrain arises. They use the bone needles to stitch their own shadows together when the auroral light fractures too sharply, weaving nets of darkness to catch themselves, to prevent their forms from scattering across the broken horizon that curves upward toward an unfinished sky.

Their eyes are the hollow gourds, set into faces that are ceramic, or chitinous, or simply very old, very patient stone. Through these, they do not see the virginal peaks or the turgid, pulsing gardens, but rather the **stutter** of existence—the interrupted frames between the world's attempts at becoming. They navigate by the negative space of the landscape, finding solid ground only where the stone has momentarily decided to be stone. They harvest the thick amber sap of the audiosynthetic plants not for sustenance—they have no stomachs, no throats, no hunger—but as **lubricant for their hearing**, a medium to keep the gourds attuned to the terrifying, distant silence that they seek, the silence that exists beyond the Scar's domain, where reality has already finished its arguments with itself.

They possess hearts, but the hearts do not beat. Instead, they **tremble**, maintaining a subsonic thrum that exists only in the pauses between the screams. When a scream-wave crests, the hearts go still, and the Attenuated freeze, becoming statuary of damp, virgin stone, their bioluminescent joints—the only light they possess, stolen or symbiotic with the flora's glow—fading to match the grey indigo of the static sky. In these moments, they are indistinguishable from the emergent geology, and perhaps they are the geology, temporarily aware. When the gap returns, the hearts shudder back into their trembling, and the beings unfreeze, shedding layers of mineral dust that has accrued during their stillness, leaving behind the pale, fresh bone of their continued, unlikely existence.

They do not reproduce, but they **elaborate**. Occasionally, when the scream has been particularly long and the subsequent silence particularly deep, one will find itself alongside a boulder that was not there before, and recognize in its contours a kinship, a shared frequency. They will apply the bone needles, stitch the shadows around it, cut probability with obsidian until the stone remembers how to hesitate, how to attend. Over centuries that might compress into an afternoon, or an afternoon that dilates across epochs, the boulder will stand, will hollow, will develop its fluted ribs and gourd-eyes. A new Attenuated, not born but **allowed**, emerging from the stone's own curiosity about itself.

They wait. This is their sole occupation and their sacrament. They wait to see if the Scar will finish building itself, if the scream will resolve into a note of settled creation or dissolve back into the raw substrate. They are the rough draft of citizens for a kingdom that might never declare its independence from chaos. In the hollow shelters, they curl—not to sleep, for sleep is a biological luxury, but to **store themselves**, compressing their hollow bodies into the smallest possible volume of silence, hoarding the stillness against the next long roar.

They are lonely, but loneliness requires a memory of others, and they have never known their kind beyond what they glimpse in the curved reflections of their gourd-eyes: tall, white, attenuated figures standing at impossible distances, waving with limbs that move too slowly to be seen, signalling across valleys where the geometry refuses to carry sound. They raise the cairns toward these reflections, hoping the other will see, will understand, will help them hold the silence a moment longer.

And when the final pause comes—the one that does not end, or the one that never returns—they will stand at the uppermost rim, fully densified, fully real, and they will remove the gourds from their faces, holding them to the non-existent wind, and finally, finally, they will hear what lives in the silence beyond the Scar. Perhaps it will be nothing. Perhaps it will be everything. They do not hope. They only attend, vibrating at the frequency of waiting, the first and perhaps last inhabitants of a world learning how to be finished.
