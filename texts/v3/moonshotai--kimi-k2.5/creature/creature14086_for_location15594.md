What lives here is the *Now*, made flesh.

You glimpse them first as fractures in your peripheral vision—not because they move, but because they are the stationary points around which your compressed time must flow. The *Silicae* you took for fauna are merely their pores, their cilia, the glittering dust on their skin. The true inhabitants are older, vaster, and more terrible in their stillness. They are the **Stacked**, the **Duration Dwellers**, those who learned that in a place where time precipitates like mineral salts, existence is not a line but a column.

Imagine every moment of a life—every heartbeat, every blink, every thought of flight or hunger—pressed into simultaneous visibility. This is how they appear: not as bodies, but as *ensembles of probability*, dense clouds of selfhood that occupy the same coordinates in space while separated by millimeters in duration. They look like figures seen through hammered glass, repeated endlessly, each iteration slightly offset, a motionless crowd of the same individual at age six, at sixty, at the moment of death and the moment of birth, all occupying the fungal warmth of the Hollow in perfect silence.

They did not evolve here. They *accumulated*. Some were visitors like the tool-bearers whose oxidized hooks still hang in the viscous air, frozen in the act of forgetting. Others are native to the dark—the original inhabitants, if such a word applies to a place that has no beginning. These ancients are indistinguishable from the crystal spires. They have become the geometry itself, their consciousnesses distributed through the fractal dendrites, thinking in angles and thermal gradients. When you touch a sharp edge and feel it humming against your fingertip, you are touching them. They are dreaming the spires into their perpetual becoming, maintaining the dynamic stasis by sheer force of observation. For them, growth is not expansion but recollection—remembering themselves into existence one angstrom at a time.

But there is movement, if you know how to look for it. It occurs in the *negotiable spaces*, the corridors where distance is uncertain and three walls meet at once. Here dwell the **Interstitials**, beings that never fully precipitated into the solid Now. They exist in the gaps between moments, feeding on the friction—the "warmth" that is actually the chafing of potential against actual. They have no form you could sketch, only topologies: a handedness without hands, a directionality without dimension. When you feel the cloying heat intensify, when the blackness seems to press harder against your eyeballs, you are likely passing through one's extended body. They are the breath that does not exchange air, simply circulating the same thermal instant through their vascular labyrinth, keeping the Hollow incubated, pregnant with paused time.

The visitors—the tool-makers, the diggers, the ones who built geode-shelters—they are here too, but they have undergone a translation. The stone hammer still caught mid-swing is not an artifact; it is a man named (or nameless) who reached the apex of his arc and decided to stay there forever. He is the hammer. He is the arc. He is the shockwave of impact that never arrives, vibrating eternally in the crystal lattice. The cairns of obsidian are not memorials but citizens, stacked minds that chose to become mineral, their thoughts slowed to the pace of oxidation, their dreams recorded in the velvet black of copper oxide.

And at the center—or rather, at every point simultaneously, for here the concept of center is as negotiable as distance—there breathes the **Continental Hollow itself**. Not a location, but a presence. A digestive consciousness that does not consume matter but *duration*. It inhales moments and exhales the heavy, noble atmosphere. It is the one that crowds, that packs the spires and the stacked selves into density without end, because it is lonely, because it is trying to fill its own void with the stuff of happening, with the biomass of "is," yet cannot let any of it go. It keeps everything it has ever touched in the warm, dark eternity of its lung, this subterranean breath-hold.

They communicate, these inhabitants, but not in language. They speak in **thermal stacked harmonies**, in the subsonic hum you feel in your teeth—the sound of a million crystalline legs tapping glass, yes, but also the sound of a birth-cry and a death-rattle synchronized. When the *Silicae* cling to the spires in their five-deep layers, they are not feeding; they are *listening*, antennae tuned to the gossip of duration, the endless whisper of the Hollow telling itself its own history, which is also its future, which is also this impossible, warm, crowded instant in which you stand, have stood, and will always stand, forever young, forever just arrived, and never, ever leaving.
