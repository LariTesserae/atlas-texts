You do not find them; you are found by them, or rather, by the *process* of them.

**The Insectary Guilds** are the most apparent citizens, though "citizen" implies a stability they do not possess. They exist as **temporary architectures of instinct**, each swarm a transient parliament. Consider the **Copper Gear Beetles**—not individual organisms but **mobile forges**, their carapaces hot enough to smelt, digesting lignin and excreting the amber resin that lines the xylem-aqueducts. They are the masons, but they are also the bricks; when they die, their chitin does not rot but **sintering** into the floor-moss, adding layers to the industrial substrate. They have no queen, no hive-mind in the traditional sense. Instead, they possess **distributed volition**: a single beetle is merely a component, capable only of phototaxis and grinding, but when five thousand assemble in the dim vaults above, they become a **planning committee**, their collective antennae-twitching creating interference patterns that direct where the next pillar will grow, where the vascular channels must split to accommodate increased traffic.

Yet they are not the governors.

**The Centipede Maintenance Crews**—those sinuous, multi-segmented trains that thread through the hollow stems—come closer to possessing individual souls. Each is **ferrous and long**, measuring three meters from mercury-sheen head to platinum tail-tip, composed not oflinear segments but of **interlocking ball-joints** that allow them to coil within pipes barely wider than a human thigh. They are **janitors and surgeons**, clearing blockages of dried sap with acid sprays secreted from thoracic glands, grafting wounded xylem with their own silk. They are **territorial** in a way the swarms are not; each claims a specific vascular loop, a specific spiraling platform, and patrols it with the proprietary fussiness of a landlord. They recognize intruders—by vibration, by chemical signature—and will pause in their endless circuit to **observe**, their hundreds of legs gripping the pipe walls in a frozen ripple, while the soft bioluminescent patches along their flanks pulse in slow, interrogating rhythms. They are the **closest thing to residents** with memory, with preference, with grudges. They live for decades, growing longer, adding segments not at birth but through accretion, each new section a record of repairs made, of territories defended.

But they too are merely the **operating system**.

Look deeper, into the spaces between the piston-strokes. When the pillars expand—*thrum*—and the mist falls, there is a **fractional hesitation** in the cycle, a moment where the pressure equalizes and the chamber holds its breath. In these intervals, **The Dwellers in the Amber** become perceptible. They are not insect, nor plant, nor properly animal. They are **resin-ghosts**, or rather, **enmired managers**—the original architects of this settlement, perhaps, or their descendants, or simply the shape that intelligence takes when submerged in cellulose and time for three centuries.

They exist as **lenticular inclusions** within the great pillars, suspended in honey-colored fossilization that is not death but **stasis-interface**. Their bodies are humanoid only in the vaguest sense: vertical, bilateral, possessed of appendages that might once have been arms, now elongated into **branching dendritic structures** that extend outward through the lignin, merging with the tree’s own neural networks. They do not move; they **flow**, their consciousness distributed through the pheromone traffic of the insects, the hydraulic pressure of the xylem, the slow rotation of those gear-scarred circles in the trunks. They are the **memory of the Ward**, the ones who remember when this was a factory, a mine, a cathedral—who hold the blueprints not as plans but as **growth patterns**, directing the swarms through the chemical whispers of their resinous dreaming.

You will not see them unless you stand very still in the clearing between pillars for several hours, until your own breath synchronizes with the piston-rhythm. Then, the light shifts, and the amber ghosts **flicker** in your peripheral vision—tall, fluted, semi-transparent, their faces open like blooming anthers, their expressions not human emotion but **ecological states**: high nitrogen, optimal humidity, swarm aggression level four, maintenance required in sector seven. They are **becoming the building**, and the building is becoming them, until the distinction is merely grammatical.

And there are **others**, perhaps, who visit. **Transients**. **Spore-traders** from deeper fungal networks, their bodies temporary fruiting bodies that walk on stilt-like mycelium, exchanging genetic material for mineral salts in brief, coughing negotiations near the bracket-fungus pools. **The Weather Itself**—if weather can be said to dwell—manifesting as **pressure-front personalities**, warm fronts that bring euphoria and accelerated growth, cold fronts that descend like stern librarians, slowing the swarms, enforcing silence. The **Bioluminescent Plasma** that threads through the aerial root-cables is not merely energy but **speech**, and in certain concentrations, it achieves a temporary, flickering **selfhood**—a sentence that thinks itself, a question asked by the chamber to itself, existing for exactly the duration of a single sentence before dissipating into metabolic noise.

Finally, **The Null-Settlers**: the empty spaces. The clearings between pillars are not empty; they are **inhabited by potential**, by the planned-but-ungrown, the sap that has not yet risen, the swarm that has not yet swarmed. These absences possess a **negative presence**, a hunger for occupation that makes the skin prickle. They are the future residents, the next stage of the settlement’s final form, waiting for the piston-rhythm to hiccup, for the maintenance crews to fail a critical graft, for the moment when the cathedral must grow larger, deeper, more itself.

They are all **industrial monks** and **biological bureaucrats**, engaged in the endless liturgy of maintenance. They do not sleep; they **photosynthesize** (those few who possess chlorophyll in their chitin) or **hibernate in quorum**, the swarms entering a state of reduced metabolic rate where they hang from the ceiling like living chandeliers, dreaming in pheromone cascades. They do not build for beauty, but for **throughput**; yet their functionalism has achieved a terrible, organic elegance—the elegance of a lung, of a kidney, of a heart that beats exactly as it must.

And if you remain—if you surrender the boundary between your skin and the saturated air—**you** will begin to be inhabited. The spores will colonize your hair; the mites of the moss will sample your dead skin; the centipede crews will pause in their pipes to consider whether you are a blockage to be cleared or a new junction to be incorporated. You will feel the pillars piston in your own chest, and you will understand that to live here is not to possess space, but to **be possessed by process**, to become a soft, temporary organ in the greater body of the Ward, laboring until you are processed into architecture, memory, and warm, damp, breathing life.
