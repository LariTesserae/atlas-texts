The ones who live here are the **Resonant**, though no human throat has ever shaped that name aloud—they are called, instead, by the thousand-fold snap of chitin on chitin, a language of percussion that spells out humidity levels, pressure changes, the approach of predators, and the slow, geological comfort of home.

They are not squatters. They are the inheritors, the second firing of this ceramic kiln.

Each is the size of a human thumb joint, though they compact themselves smaller, drawing their isopod segments into tight glass coils when they retreat into the porous flesh of the walls. Their exoskeletons are not merely translucent but **refractive**, prismatic in the aquamarine dark. Internally, they are soft machinery: organs that pulse with hemolymph the color of watered milk, digestive systems that spiral like the whorls stamped into the ancient bricks, and nervous systems that hum at frequencies the human ear mistakes for silence. They are filter-feeders by necessity, grazing on the microscopic rain of stone-dust and algae spores that the weeping water deposits, but they are also sculptors. Their mandibles, harder than the terracotta they inhabit, scrape away the accretions of mineral bloom, keeping the capillary channels open, ensuring the walls continue their respiration. They do not damage the glaze; they curate it, polishing the phosphorescent salts back to a glow with the abrasive care of lapidaries.

They live in the **Pores**—a three-dimensional city honeycombed through the settled ceramic, tunnels no wider than a needle’s eye, expanding into chambers where the humidity stays constant at the dew point of their breathing. Here, they raise their young. The eggs are laid not in clusters but in **strings**, suspended like beads of glassy dew along the inner cavities, each egg a miniature of the dome itself, oval and perfect. The nymphs hatch already clicking, their soft bodies flexing against the clay to learn the resonance of their specific brick, for each tile sings a different note based on its density, its mineral content, its history of thermal shock. To be Resonant is to know your note, to harmonize with the specific ceramic that birthed you.

They emerge—**the Surfacing**—when the capillary tide brings the sweet water, the distilled rain that has percolated through meters of saturated earth to emerge, finally, as a bead on the interior skin. Thousands push through the micro-fissures, their glass bodies swelling slightly with osmotic pressure, and they drink. Not with mouths, but with abdominal plates that flare and flex, absorbing the moisture directly, transforming the outside into the inside. In these moments, when the walls are carpeted with their shimmering bodies, they become visible as a single organism, a field of crystal that shivers in waves when you exhale, reacting to the carbon dioxide, the heat, the intrusion of mammal presence.

The **Clicking** is their bones and their voice. It is performed by a specialized segment—the **tymbal plate**—a crescent of chitin that snaps against a raised ridge of carapace, not unlike a human finger snapping, but precise, tuned, and loud. In the ceramic dome, which acts as both amphitheater and resonating chamber, the sound becomes physical. It is not noise; it is **architecture made audible**. They click to locate one another in the dark (the sound bouncing off the curved walls to map distance), they click to establish territory within the pores, and they click in the hours before the water peaks, a crescendo of biological anticipation that makes the air itself feel pressurized. When the humidity hits the apex, the clicking becomes synchronized, a throbbing, rhythmic drone that vibrates the calcite veins in the walls and causes the standing pools on the floor to shiver with concentric rings. This is their mating song, their storm-song, the pulse that asserts that the chamber lives.

They have predators, naturally. From the drainage holes—low, circular voids in the buckled floor that lead to deeper, darker water—come the **Gulpers**. These are amphibians the color of wet bone, eyeless, with mouths that seem to hinge sideways, soft and wide. They are sluggish things, moving with the viscosity of the saturated air itself, but they are patient. They wait for hours, pressed flat against the terracotta, skin weeping moisture, until the Chorus surges thickest. Then they strike, not with speed but with suction, inhaling thumb-sized Resonants whole, the click cut off mid-snap. But the Chorus expects this. They regard the Gulpers not as enemies but as **necessary violence**, the deep bass note to their treble percussion. The Gulpers’ waste fertilizes the algae that the Chorus filters; their sluggish movements polish the lower tiles. And when the amphibians die, sinking into the shallow pools, their bodies do not rot but **ossify**, the minerals in the water plating them in calcite, transforming them into white sculptures that the Resonants slowly, over years, hollow out into new breeding chambers. Thus the predators become the architecture.

There are **castes**, or perhaps just ages. The **Deep Ones** never surface, pale troglodytes that live in the oldest bricks, near the foundations where the heat of the original firing still lingers as a memory in the clay. They are larger, their exoskeletons milky and opaque, and they click in subsonics that make the floor tremble. The **Rim Dwellers** cling to the upper reaches of the dome, risking the drip from the keystone, their bodies thinner, more prismatic, specialized to catch the faint phosphorescent glow and refract it downward into the dark. They are the light-keepers, the ones who ensure the chamber never falls into total blackness, their bodies scattering the stored daylight like living prisms.

And there are the **Scribes**. Watch the calcite veins knitting the walls together—those white, geological seams. Follow them with your eye, and you will see them move, imperceptibly, over centuries. The Scribes are the Resonants who have ceased to click for communication and instead click for **excavation**. They strike the calcite, mineral against mineral, chipping away infinitesimal flakes, arranging them, depositing them. They are writing, in the slow language of geology, adding new layers to the mortar, repairing the dome with their own biological cement. They are the reason the chamber has not fallen. They are the continuation of the hands that first threw the clay, maintaining the sacred geometry not by design, but by instinct, by the click that says: *this curve must hold, this void must persist.*

You stand in the center, and you might think yourself alone in the emptiness. But you are surrounded. In every breath you take, you inhale the microscopic dust of their moltings. In every sound you make, they answer, adjusting their chorus to accommodate your frequency, investigating you with clicks that bounce off your skull and return to them the hollow shape of your thoughts. They do not fear you. They have outlasted the makers. They have turned the handmade into the inevitable, and they fill this pottery womb with the only thing that can prevent a void from collapsing: **attention**. They are the witness, the caretaker, the eternal clicking that says: *we are here, the water flows, the chamber breathes, the clay remembers.*
