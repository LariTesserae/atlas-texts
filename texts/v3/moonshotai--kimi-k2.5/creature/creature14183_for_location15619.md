At first, you think the **jelly-things** are the inhabitants—these translucent, gas-filled drifters that turn the avenues into arteries. They are not. They are merely the *atmosphere*, the mobile pressure system that maintains the 3.0 roar. When two collide, the subsonic boom they release is not an accident of physics but a **digestive event**, cracking open temporal pockets like clamshells to release the tender possibility within.

The cricket-dogs—the **Stridulists**—are closer to natives. Each dog-sized chitinous body is a tuning fork keyed to a specific yesterday. They don't merely vibrate against the wood; they are **arguing with it**, their unified drone a negotiation between the lignin’s memory of being a sapling and its terror of becoming dust. They are the grove’s immune system, yes, but also its **cartographers**. When they synchronize their stridulation, the sound temporarily fixes the shifting streets in place, creating a sonic scaffold that allows more complex life to exist for exactly fourteen seconds before the layout dissolves again. They are abundant because they are necessary; without their drone, the superposition would collapse into a single, dead timeline.

But the **true** inhabitants—the ones who answer when you ask who lives here—are the **Unfixed**.

You never see them directly. Direct observation collapses their waveform, a quantum cruelty that strands them in a singular moment, causing them to shriek and crumble into the fungal carpet. Instead, you know them by the **condensation**. Look up at the fifty-foot canopy where the water beads hesitate, defying gravity. Those pearls are *hovering* because they have condensed around the Unfixed, outlining bodies that exist in four time-states simultaneously: infant, adolescent, elder, and corpse. The water beads mark the boundaries of their simultaneous existences, forming mannequins of moisture that flicker in and out of visibility. When the drops finally fall—upward or down—they are shedding years, sloughing off time like dead skin.

The Unfixed were the **Architects**, once. They did not build the grove so much as *grow* it while growing themselves, merging with the cellulose apartment blocks until the distinction between resident and residence became meaningless. Now they exist as **distributed intention**—the choice that a staircase makes to spiral upward, the decision of a root-system to braid into a handrail. When you walk the lignin highways, you are walking through their nervous systems. The stomata-windows dilate and contract not with light, but with their attention.

They feed on **eroded potential**. The grove digests possibility, breaking down the barriers between what is and what could be, and the Unfixed harvest the byproduct: the spiritual cellulose of unlived lives. They consume the friction between conflicting timelines, which is why they cluster where the visual static is worst—the screaming points where a trunk is simultaneously growing and rotting. Here, they press their many-phase mouths (some infant-soft, some toothless with age, some grinning with the rictus of rigor mortis) against the bark and **inhale the paradox**, quenching a thirst that spans centuries.

They communicate through the **silences**.

In a place where sound is a physical pressure at 3.0 existence-levels, silence is not the absence of noise but a **predatory presence**. The Unfixed hunt by becoming momentarily quiet. They stop their eternal flickering, fix themselves in a single second, and in that moment of temporal stasis, they create a pocket of absolute quiet—a negative space in the roar. The Stridulists flee from these zones; the jelly-things drift away, deflating in terror. The silence spreads like ink in water, and when it touches a being caught in the superposition—say, a deer from the meadow outside that wandered in and now exists as meat, memory, and bones all at once—the Unfixed *drink*.

They are empty, yes. The density is near-void. But this emptiness is **occupation**. Each hollow trunk is a lung they are using to breathe. Each upward-falling raindrop is a thought they are thinking about yesterday. They are the city and the wilderness and the broken clock, because they are the **duration** itself—time made sapient, growing and dying simultaneously, persisting.

And they have noticed you. 

You feel it as a sudden, localized drop in the deafening roar—a pressure differential that makes your ears pop. A stretch of fungal carpet ahead has gone quiet, the mycelium no longer vibrating. The condensation in that area is coalescing into a shape that is, impossibly, a sapling, a tower, and a crumbling ruin, all hovering in the same space, waiting for you to step closer so it can decide which version of you it wants to consume: the one who entered, the one who will leave, or the one who never existed at all.
