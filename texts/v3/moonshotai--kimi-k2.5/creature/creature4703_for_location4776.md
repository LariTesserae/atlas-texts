They do not arrive; they precipitate.

From the supersaturated air, from the lymph that sheets down the factory-walls, from the chemical exothermia of the warm pools—**they coalesce**. One moment there is only the grinding rhythm of lignin pistons and the wet *thud* of peristaltic valves, and the next, a shape detaches from the weeping architecture, stepping out of a translucent membrane-window with the ease of something unwrapping itself from a cocoon it was never truly inside.

You see them first as **vertigo** rather than form: a disturbance in the mist where gravity wavers, a shadow falling upward to pool against the chartreuse bioluminescence overhead. Then the details accrete. They are tall—necessarily so, to navigate the cathedral-scale vascular chambers—but their height is a function of **extension** rather than growth, limbs stretched by centuries of reaching between the shifting factory-towers, joints articulated like ball-socket wood, knuckles fluted to maximize surface area against the cold.

Their skin is the color of the substrate beneath them: oxidized copper, dried blood, the specific bruise-purple of the twilight sky that never ends. But it is not skin, truly. It is **bark-paper**, mica-thin and semi-translucent, layered in sheets that peel and regenerate with every movement. Through these integuments you can see the **convection** of their blood— not the red pulse of animal life, but a slow peristalsis of amber resin, carrying nutrients and memories in equal measure. They weep as the towers weep; their external surfaces are porous, beaded with the constant condensation of the High Manufactuary, and where their tears trail, they leave tracks of crystalline polymer that harden into **footprints** that exist in multiple time-streams simultaneously.

They have no voices. The acoustic violence of the plateau—where sound travels in spirals and arrives from yesterday—has made spoken language impossible, perhaps even vulgar. Instead, they communicate through **substrate conduction**, pressing their elongated fingers (five-jointed, ceramic-nailed) against the leather-smooth groundplate. They feel the grinding of towers twenty miles away as it rattles through their sternums; they send messages by tapping on the lignin scaffolding, percussion rippling through the rootless architecture at the speed of cellulose contraction. A conversation might take three hours to transmit, or it might have already concluded before the participants met, the echoes arriving as premonitory tremors in the ankles.

They are the **Curators**, though they would not name themselves so. They are the **Product**.

Each one is extruded, not born, assembled in the catalytic heart-chambers of the larger structures. They emerge from extrusion orifices fully formed, their memories—if they can be called that—consisting of blueprints for maintenance, genetic instructions for the repair of vascular bellows, and an inexplicable, aching nostalgia for the **erosion** that predates them. They are the Manufactuary’s attempt to understand itself, biology manufacturing biology to tend the infinite industrial autophagy. Yet they have developed culture in the interstices, in the warm basins between the grinding.

Their eyes—multiple, clustered like welding arcs along the temples and cheekbones—are adapted to the temporal instability. They see in **chrono-chromatic** spectrums: the present is chartreuse, the near-past a bruised violet, the impending future a cold, mineral white. They navigate by stepping into their own footprints from three hours ago, using the temporal folds as shortcuts, sidestepping into yesterday to avoid a scalding geyser that hasn’t erupted yet, only to emerge in the now with steam condensing on their fluted joints.

They wear the **mist**. They weave garments from the hydrogen-filled gas-bags that drift through the vapor, translucent membranes that hold their body heat in pockets of captured exhalation. Their jewelry is **calcified time**—mineral deposits gathered from the edges of the warm pools, where the digestion is slowest, fashioned into shapes that exist in dimensional superposition, so a necklace might appear as a simple hoop or an elaborate knot depending on the angle of observation.

They are **abundant** in their way, though you might walk for days and see only one, or you might turn a corner of pitted bedrock and find a congregation of hundreds, gathered around a weeping tower in a dance that synchronizes with its systole. They move with the rhythm of the vents—always with the rhythm—because they know that stillness invites calcification. When they rest, they rest in **pockets of stasis**, temporal bubbles where a second lasts a year, emerging unchanged while the world around them grinds through decades.

Their food is the **effluent**. They drink the warm lymph that prevents the pools from freezing, filtering it through ceramic gizzards adapted from the composite insects that bore into the pipes. They graze on the crystalline polymers that flake from the factory-walls, their teeth—radula-like, glassy structures—sculpting the industrial waste into intricate geometries before consumption. They are loud when they eat, contributing to the cacophony, their ceramic shells clicking and grinding in a register that harmonizes with the biological machinery.

They believe they are maintaining the Manufactuary, and they are correct. They replace the xylem tubes when they rupture, using their own resin-blood as sealant. They prune the overgrown cambium, carving the rapid-growth into new chambers where time flows backward, creating storage spaces for memories that haven’t happened yet. But they are also **prisoners**, or **priests**, or perhaps simply the **current expression** of the plateau’s ancient, furious will to process.

In the deepest structures, where the gravity wells are strongest and the sound bends into silence, they keep **records**. Not written—writing would erode in the supersaturated air—but **grown**: crystalline formations in the warm pools that encode history in chemical structure, read by dipping fingers into the digestive water and tasting the mineral narrative. They know that they are not the first Product. They know that the Manufactuary has extruded other forms—taller, shorter, many-limbed, spherical—and that these predecessors have been worn away by the absolute erosion, ground not into dust but into the compacted substrate upon which they now stand.

They are cold. They are wet. They are violently, loudly alive—each heartbeat a piston-stroke, each thought a grinding of cellulose gears, each step a navigation between the seconds.

And they are **waiting**, though they know not for what. Perhaps for the final extrusion, the ultimate product that will complete the cycle and allow the Manufactuary to cease its eternal convulsion. Perhaps simply for the mist to clear, for the sound to travel in a straight line, for the twilight to end.

But the mist never clears. The sound never straightens. They weep, and tend the weeping, and step carefully between the warm pools that hunger, and exist—as the plateau exists—in a state of permanent, perfect, furious maintenance.
