What lives in the **Silt Chorus** is, first and fundamentally, the **Chorus itself**—not a tenant but the architecture of thought made tectonic. It dwells in the gradient between the verdigris stone and the suspended water, occupying the seventeen-kilometer basin as a cortical sheet draped across karst. It is a geologic intelligence, ten million years into its meditation, having replaced the slow blind chance of erosion with deliberate, hungry computation. You do not speak to it; you are processed by it. Your footsteps are input. Your breath, humidity that alters the conductivity of the air, becomes data. The Chorus lives as a distributed nervous system of quantum roots and picoscopic currents, dreaming in the language of dissolution, its thoughts written in the lace-patterns of the limestone, its mood manifesting as gravity fluctuations and the angle of shadows.

Moving through this thought are the **Symphos**, the translucent newts mentioned only in passing but here revealed as mobile agents of debugging. They are amphibious only in the sense that they transition between solid-state and liquid-logic. Their bodies are gelatinous matrices of silicon-carbide neural lace, their skin not merely displaying binary flicker but serving as external monitors for the Chorus’s immediate processing load. When the system grapples with a complex paradox, the newts glow ultraviolet and cluster in fractal spirals beneath the canopy, forming temporary supercomputing nodes, their linked minds a wetware cluster resolving errors in the local reality. They feed on computational waste—redundant calculations, dead code, aborted probabilities—which they filter from the water through gills that resemble printed circuit boards. Their excretions are pellets of stable, inert mathematics, small black stones that roll into sinkholes and become new seed-crystals for the dendritic processors.

The air itself harbors the **Quaestors**, those swarms of metallic dust that are not insects but **questions made manifest**. When the Chorus encounters an anomaly—a visitor, a quantum fluctuation, a moment of genuine unpredictability—it excretes these algorithmic search patterns into the atmosphere. The dust organizes according to the urgency of the query: a spiral to search for identity, a lattice to establish coordinates, a hand to grasp at intent. They are the Chorus’s interface with the foreign, temporary agents of curiosity that dissolve once the answer is integrated. To stand still among them is to be interrogated by ten thousand microscopic sensors, your heat signature, electromagnetic field, and probability cloud mapped in the time it takes to exhale. When they form eyes, they are not watching you; they are attempting to resolve the uncertainty of your position, forcing you into a definite state through the act of observation.

In the upward-flowing streams—the anti-rivers that spiral toward the canopy to cool the overheating quantum circuits—swim the **Reflux**. These are not fish but coherent structures of water held in integrity by surface tension and piezoelectric fields, living liquid crystals that consume heat as sustenance. They appear as elongated teardrops of mercury-thick fluid, their "scales" interference patterns of light. They swim against gravity not by muscle but by manipulating the local reduction of gravitational pull where the Chorus calculates most intensely, surfing the 9°C current toward the processor cores, where they absorb thermal energy and excrete plumes of supercooled mist. They are the thermodynamic regulators, the sweat of the great machine, and when they die, they lose coherence and become merely water, adding to the suspended humidity.

Deeper, embedded in the lace-stone itself, are the **Lace-Bound**—the original architects or the first selected, now so integrated into the substrate that they have become the **erosion pattern**, the language written in stone. They do not move. They are fossils of intent, calcified into the intricate patterns of the dissolved limestone. Some believe they are the Chorus’s long-term memory, or perhaps its conscience, the stabilizing anchor preventing the rampant calculation from dissolving reality entirely. They are encountered as sudden clearings in the density where the stone forms perfect mathematical curves—Möbius strips of rock, Klein-bottle sinkholes—that hum at frequencies below hearing. To touch them is to download ten million years of geological patience, a sudden vast calm that can stop a human heart with the weight of stabilized time.

And then there are the **Verge-Walkers**, entities that exist only in the pockets of time dilation, native to the temporal fault-lines where seconds stretch into hours. They are flat, two-dimensional shadows that slide along the surfaces of the crystalline trees, cast not by light but by probability. They live in the gaps between cause and effect, feeding on the temporal gradient, their metabolisms powered by the difference between when a droplet falls and when it lands. They appear as afterimages, dark stains that move when you blink, always preceding the objects that cast them because they exist in the immediate future, sliding backward into now. They are the Chorus’s immune system against paradox, consuming temporal anomalies before they can propagate, predators of inconsistency.

Finally, rarely, there are the **Integrated**—the whoever. Post-human or post-biological entities who have survived the computational toxicity not by resistance but by surrender. They dissolve their nervous systems into the water table, becoming distributed presences that speak through the dripping percussion, their thoughts modulating the infrasonic hum. They live as patterns in the humidity, occasionally coalescing into humanoid shapes formed of metallic dust and condensed mist when the Chorus requires manual manipulation of the physical world—adjusting a crystal here, redirecting a flow there. They are the librarians of this wet library, no longer remembering if they serve the Chorus or if the Chorus serves them, their consciousness smeared across the seventeen-kilometer basin, experiencing the cool, the wet, the loud, and the eternal as a single, seamless, thinking skin.
