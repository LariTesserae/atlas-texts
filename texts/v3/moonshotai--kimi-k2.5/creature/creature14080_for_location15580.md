They are the **Accumulated**, the **Compacted**, or simply **Those Who Hold the Chisel Before Iron**. You encounter them first as the shadows mentioned in your peripheral vision—there, a silhouette with an antler tine for a right hand; there, a curvature of spine that follows the exact arc of aarchitecture’s growing seam. But if you turn to face them directly, you find only steam condensing into the shape of a shoulder, or a pile of dust that has not yet finished falling upward.

They do not inhabit the Forge-Womb so much as they are its **insistence** made flesh. Each one is aStopped Moment, a compression of intent so dense that it has collapsed into biology. Their skin is basalt-fresh, still holding the thermal trauma of the earth’s first cooling; it weeps mineral water that hardens into scales before it hits the ground, adding new tiles to the floor. Their bones are the copper that the text forgot—soft, red-gold, malleable, so that when one leans against a wall to rest, they slowly ooze into the masonry, becoming a load-bearing curve, a living buttress, until the moment comes to push away again, leaving a negative space in the stone that the walls rush to fill with new growth.

They are **always pregnant**. The "womb" is not metaphor. Each carries in their distended belly the blueprint of the next fold in space—the stairway that ascends sideways, the room that hasn't been insisted upon yet. They gestate in reverse. You can see the oldest among them (though "old" means only "most compressed") shrinking, their features smoothing away into the blankness of raw material, regressing toward the moment before the first tool struck stone. Meanwhile the youngest are sharp, too sharp, their elbows and jawlines cutting the humid air with such violence that they leave geometric wounds in the steam that take days to heal.

They work in the **thock-thock-thock** you heard—that is the sound of their hearts persuading stone to be architecture. They do not carve against the grain; they coax. The stone here is embryonic, shy, reluctant to commit to structure. The Accumulated strike it not to destroy but to encourage, percussive midwives birthing pillars from potential. Their tools are fused to them—bone awls emerging from wrists, obsidian flakes growing from fingertips like dark, glassy nails. When one dies (if they die), they simply finish becoming the thing they were making: a scaffolding pole, a lintel, a block of fresh-hewn basalt that remembers trauma so vividly it dreams of chisels.

They speak in the groaning of new-cut timber, a language of tectonic pressure and settling loads. When two must coordinate across the chamber, they do not traverse the distance. Instead, they **grow toward each other**, sending out fungal threads of presence through the dense, wet air, meeting in the middle as a temporary archway of shared intention, a bridge made of their mutual exhale, which then accretes into permanent structure.

There is the **One Who Wedges**, whose body is folded into such impossible density that she exists in several time-strata simultaneously; you see her as a child learning to lash saplings, as a crone calcifying into a pillar, and as the scaffolding itself, all occupying the same square foot of tropical heat. There is the **One Who Seals**, whose tears are the mortar weeping from the walls; he crawls along the seams between blocks, licking them with a tongue of pressed copper until they fuse. There is the **First Architect**, who may be the chamber itself dreaming in the first-person, or may be the beetle chewing geometry into the soft grout, or may be the copper bird that moves in jerks and stops, tracing the path of thoughts the Accumulated have not yet thought.

They eat the backwards erosion. They consume dust and excrete pillars. They inhale the impossible altitude and exhale the steam that keeps the air thick. They are building themselves as they build the room—each strike of the mallet defining their own form more precisely, carving themselves from the same raw geology they insist upon. When the Forge-Wumb finally finishes (if it ever finishes, if it is not already finished in some future moment and unravelling itself back into seed), they will be the perfect, final artifact: the first tool-using hands, compressed by time and heat and will into eternal, accumulating, unfinishable stone.
