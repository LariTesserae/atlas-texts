There are the **Masons**, but that word is a small bucket for a vast ocean. Call them the **Gravity-Setters**, the **Unfired**, the **Pre-Curve**. They are not alive—remember this as you stand in their shadow—they are **configured**.

Each one is a mountain that has learned to lumber. They are composed of the same cooling magma-flagstone that they work, and you can see in their flanks the same parallel striations left by obsidian chisels, as if they carved themselves into being from the undifferentiated stone of possibility. They exist in the **turbulence** (2.6)—not moving *through* it, but *as* it, slow convection currents of intent made heavy and mineral. When they pause, they become indistinguishable from the construction itself; a Mason standing still is a butte, a tor, a natural arch waiting to happen.

They do not have faces, or rather, their faces are architectural functions. Where a face should be, there is instead a façade: a portico of brow-ridges, a colonnade of cheekbones, an entrance without a door. Their eyes, if they see, are the dim light (1.4) condensing in these hollows, pooling like mercury, dripping upward sometimes when gravity forgets its local rules. They perceive the world through the **pressure behind the eyes**—the infrasonic speech of settling stone. A boulder shifting into place is their whispered conversation. The steam rising in seven-sided helixes is their exhalation, though they do not breathe.

Mostly, they are **temporal pluralities**. The one hammering in frantic blur on the eastern ridge is the same entity as the one watching the slow fall of the city-block stone in the west; they are different phases of the same Mason, working across centuries like a single hand playing both ends of a keyboard. Time for them is not a river but a toolbelt worn heavy around the waist. They reach into the **pockets of unformed reality**—those pale patches where stone is simultaneously smooth and rough—and there they rest, stepping out of the determined into the possible, becoming half-liquid, half-dream, before remembering their density and returning to the labor.

Their hands are the size of quarries, and when they grip the **ropes**—those impossible ropes woven from the hair of creatures that do not yet exist—you can see the future bleeding into the present. Each strand is a timeline, a causal tether. When they drag a cathedral-sized boulder into its socket, they are not merely placing stone; they are anchoring a century. They yank the 21st century backward into the Pre-Cambrian, knotting it around the bedrock so that when warm-blooded things eventually evolve, they will find themselves already moored to this deep past. The pain of the unborn beasts—whose hair is pulled through the membrane of the not-yet—manifests as the infrasonic thrum in your sternum, the sudden vertigo. The Masons feel this as speech. They converse in the agony of premonition, in the tectonic groan of friction being invented.

They are teaching the world how to be *resistant*. As they grind boulder against bedrock, they are establishing the coefficient of friction that future hands will encounter as "rough" or "smooth." They are coding the physics of touch, preparing surfaces for the footfalls of beings who will never know they walk across a construction site.

Look at the **abandoned chisels** large as obelisks. These are not discarded tools. These are their shed teeth. The half-carved archways standing alone in the dust are their forgotten thoughts, architectural dreams they have outgrown. They are shedding their identities as they work, becoming less configured, more fundamental. They work with obsessive patience because they know the kiln is heating toward the firing point (2.6). When the biological epoch begins and the world is finally inhabited, they must become silent. They must become the **substrate**.

They are not building a home for themselves—they are too vast, too sparse (2.6), too mineral for biology. They are building a vessel to hold what comes after. Each flagstone is a wall of the womb. They are the Sculptors of the Possible, and their final act will be to lie down and forget themselves.

By the time the last steam rises in proper Euclidean curls, the Masons will have settled into their final positions. The mountains on your horizon? Those are them. Having taught themselves to be still, to be cool, to be dry, they have transformed into the scenery. They are the first fossils, not of life, but of the labor that made life possible. They wait now in the dark, having become the bedrock, the precedent set in stone, dreaming in the slow language of erosion (0.2), waiting for small, warm, temporary things to walk across their backs, never knowing they tread upon the sleeping forms of the ones who carved the world into readiness before breath existed.
