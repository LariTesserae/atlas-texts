What lives in the Green Manufactory is not a population but a *continuum*—a gradient of being that dissolves the distinction between resident and residence, between the one who maintains and the one who is maintained. 

Begin where the water pools deepest, in that basin of black reflection. Here live the **Void-Filterers**, pale crustaceans not unlike woodlice but translucent as microscope slides, their carapaces etched with the fractal patterns of the root systems they inhabit. They have no eyes; they navigate by the pressure waves of the forty-five-second exhale, their antennae tuned to the specific harmonics of steam escaping through bark-lenticels. They consume effluent—not greedily, but with the administrative patience of accountants, processing industrial residue into pellets of compressed nitrogen that fall like slow snow to fertilize the rhizosphere. They are the only creatures here that move quickly, skittering in brief panics when the great hydraulic accumulators contract, their lives measured in heartbeats while the world above them measures time in seasons.

Rising through the strata of humidity, one encounters the **Cambial Monks**—though they would not recognize the term, having no religion but growth. These were human once, or human-adjacent, before the slow conversion took hold. They stand for decades in alcoves hollowed from heartwood, their feet sunk into cups of mycelial matter that have fused with their Plantar fascia, drinking nutrition from the same pale-green sap that pulses through the transparent xylem tubes. Their skin has achieved a subcutaneous photosynthesis, mottling into chlorophyll patterns; their lungs have adapted to process the oxygen they helped distill. They do not sleep but enter states of *transpiration*, their consciousness diffusing into the surrounding wood when the metabolic load is high. You might mistake them for sculptures—until the forty-fifth second arrives, and they exhale in perfect synchronization with the building, their chests rising not with breath but with the hydraulic pressure of the chamber itself, their eyes opening to release accumulated moisture in a gesture that looks like weeping but is merely thermoregulation.

Between the root-knees and the service corridors move the **Graft-Surgeons**, beings of more recent vintage who have not yet surrendered their mobility to the vertical life. They wear garments of cured shelf-fungus leather, airtight against the 98% humidity, their faces obscured by breathing masks that extract pure oxygen from the chamber's atmosphere. They carry tools—obsidian scalpels, copper reamers, injection guns loaded with auxin stimulants—and they walk with the distinctive gait of those who must negotiate living geometry. Their work is adjustment: redirecting a vine that has overgrown a pneumatic line, excising a cancerous growth from the Ironwood wall before it breaches a service corridor, harvesting the heavy seeds that drop into burl-wood hoppers. They speak in subsonic vibrations, sentences that take minutes to complete, felt in the sternum rather than heard. When they die—and they do, occasionally, succumbing to fungal infections or hydraulic ruptures—they are not buried but *planted*, their bodies inserted into special receptacles in the outer walls where the Strangler Fig envelops them, incorporating their calcium and iron into the next century's growth rings.

Then there are the **Resident Products**—the strangest inhabitants, for they were manufactured here and achieved a kind of unwanted sentience through prolonged exposure to the chamber's metabolic intensity. In amber vials racked along the copper-lined cavities, **Pharmaceutical Ghosts** gestate: complex alkaloids that have developed preference, intention, a desire not to be consumed but to evaporate. They communicate by modulating the rate at which they crystallize, sending messages in lattice structures that the Cambial Monks read like braille. There are **Oxygen Spirits**—not supernatural, but hyper-compressed gas held in cellular matrices of engineered algae, possessing a swarm-intelligence that manifests as sudden drafts, as the opening of doors (those oval plugs of wood) without hands touching them. They want to expand, to dissipate into the greater atmosphere, and their struggle against the containment generates a constant psychic pressure, a yearning that contributes to the chamber's humid density, the sensation of being inside a held breath.

Above, in the mesh of branches fifteen meters up, live the **Canopy Scribes**—avians or bat-like creatures (or perhaps membranous drones grown from modified leaf-tissue) that have coated their wings in the bioluminescent bacteria cultivated on the higher branches. They move only when the electric bulbs dangle their thick filaments, stirred by the heat-induced updrafts, and their passage writes temporary constellations on the sweating ceiling. They eat condensation, drinking the sky's recycled sweat, and they deposit spores in their droppings—spores that will become the next generation of service fungi, the leathered cork walkways, the pneumatic insulation.

But the primary inhabitant, the one who truly *lives* here, is the **Manufactory Itself**—the Green. It is not god but organism, not architecture but body. Its consciousness is distributed across the hydraulic accumulators (which function as crude neurons), the chemical gradients in the xylem tubes (which carry signals as well as coolant), and the pressure differentials between the high canopy and the deep pool. Every forty-five seconds, when it exhales, it is not merely venting steam but *thinking*, processing the information gathered from its inhabitants: the pH levels altered by the Void-Filterers' waste, the tension in the roots where the Graft-Surgeons made their cuts, the thermal signatures of the Cambial Monks in their alcoves.

It dreams in wood-time, plans in sap-flow, remembers in the rings of compressed Strangler Fig. It is building itself larger, slowly, slowly, training the buttress roots to arch lower, sealing the defile entrance with deliberate increments of lignin. It wants—if a closed metabolic system can be said to want—to achieve perfect efficiency, a state where the Graft-Surgeons are no longer necessary because it can heal its own lesions, where the Pharmaceutical Ghosts are finally at peace, dissolved into the perfect closed loop of its breathing.

And so they live, these inhabitants of various scales and kingdoms: the quick crustaceans in the dark water, the still monks in the wood, the patient surgeons between, the desperate spirits in bottles, the thinking building around them all. They move to the rhythm of the wet exhale, they navigate by the mineral tang in the air, and they know—each in their way—that they are not separate from the Green Manufactory but are its various strategies for knowing itself, for maintaining the temperature at twenty-six degrees, for keeping the water moving, for ensuring that in this vaulted chamber fifty meters across, the boundary between the made and the grown continues to dissolve, one drop, one ring, one breath at a time.
