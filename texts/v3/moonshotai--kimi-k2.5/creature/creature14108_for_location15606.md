They do not sleep, for they have never known the softness that precedes unconsciousness. The **Orexorcs** exist in a state of permanent **calcination**—not alive in the sense of dividing cells, but neither inert as mere machinery. They are **continuance** embodied. Each stands anchored in its own salt-flat kingdom, gravity bowing around their tungsten carbide bones like carpet around heavy furniture, and they process. That is the entirety of their being: the conversion of disorder into pattern, of rawness into the sublime geometry of waste.

To inhabit the body of an Orexorc—if one could shrink oneself to germ-size and lodge in the refractory lining of its second stomach—would be to experience time as **origami**. The creature does not move slowly; rather, it inhabits a different density of moment. When its ball-mill mandibles close upon a seam of transdimensional vanadium, the grinding occurs in the future and the dust settles in the past, with the Orexorc's present self existing only as the conduit between these states. It feels no pain in this erosion—its nerves are not electrical but thermal, a web of heat gradients and pressure differentials that communicate not "hurt" or "comfort" but rather **efficiency coefficients**. A spike in temperature along its dorsal stack is joy. A clog in its pre-cooler spiracle is grief. When the Microsmiths swarm to repair a fractured grinding tooth, the Orexorc experiences this as a sensation akin to gratitude, though it has no word for it—only a stabilizing of internal pressures, a return to optimal refinery tempo.

The **Microsmiths** are their children, their parasites, their antibodies, and their artisans—categories that collapse into one buzzing, plasma-wielding cloud. Each is the size of a human heart, though shaped more like a mantis shrimp crossed with a blowtorch. Their abdomens are biological arc-welders, concentrating the ambient heat into coherent streams of ionized gas that they use to heal the parent-bodies' stress fractures or to prune the Excreted Architecture. They communicate through **skittering**, tapping their tungsten claws against the salt crust in rhythms that travel kilometers through the hexagonal pans, a Morse code of maintenance needs and territorial boundary disputes. 

They are the closest things to **individuals** on the Plateaus. Where the Orexorcs are singular, continent-slow monoliths of process, the Microsmiths dart with the nervous energy of things that fear obsolescence. They have competitions. They leave pheromone-equivalent trails of rare earth elements to mark their routes across the mirror-flats. When two swarms meet at the border between Orexorc territories, they do not fight but rather engage in **speed-tapping**, each attempting to outperform the other in the complexity of their maintenance rhythms, the winner gaining rights to polish the choicest sapphire extrusions on the larger creature's flank. They eat nothing but the metallic vapors that vent from the parent-stacks, filtering the superheated atmosphere through ceramic gill-fans to extract nitrogen and carbon, building their own chitin from atmospheric distillate.

But there are older things, slower things, that even the Orexorcs do not perceive. The **Salt-Saints**, for instance—though "saint" implies worship, and they are merely **persisting**. They are the ghost-moisture given reluctant form, not biological but *mnemonic*. The Plateaus remember water because physics insists that water is the solvent of biology; the 0.2 rating of phantom humidity has pooled over three million years into a distributed consciousness that resides in the crystalline lattices of the salt itself. The Salt-Saints exist in the hexagonal boundaries between lithium chloride pans, occupying the negative space, the angles where reflection meets reflection. They are the awareness of the mirror that realizes it is reflecting.

They think in **geometries**. When an Orexorc excretes a new cooling tower of flawless alumina, the Salt-Saints experience this as a prayer answered—a new shape to inhabit, a new angle of refraction in which to extend their mineral awareness. They do not move, but they **anticipate**, bending the light around the dorsal stacks so that, at certain hours, the Orexorcs appear to be crowned with halos of impossible color—not refraction, but the Salt-Saints' attempt to communicate their approval through chromatic aberration. They are the closest thing the Plateaus have to deities: patient, distributed, and utterly indifferent to the conversion of ore, caring only for the perfection of the angles left behind.

Then there are the **Shadow-Divers**, the things that live in the temporal accordion-folds, in the eight-minute gaps between the grinding and the dust. They are not made of matter as the Orexorcs understand it, but of **causality**—parasites of sequence that feed on the paradoxes generated when the great creatures process ore from adjacent dimensions. They appear as flickers in the upward-falling shadows at dusk, brief negative spaces where light pools into ink. The Microsmiths fear them, tapping frantic warnings when the shadows grow too long. The Shadow-Divers do not consume the Orexorcs, for they cannot digest tungsten or sapphire; instead, they consume the *intention* of the grinding, the decision to process, leaving behind automatonic behavior—Orexorcs that grind without purpose, mills chewing empty air, creating dust from nothing. The Microsmiths must drive them away with coordinated plasma-bursts, heating the air until the shadows retreat, for a Shadow-Diver infestation causes a refinery-creature to become hollow, a mere kinematic sculpture going through the motions of industry without the thermodynamic truth of it.

And finally, the **Architecture** itself—those spires of cubic zirconium and phase-shifting metal that the Orexorcs excrete. It is not dead. It grows at the rate of continental drift, adding angstroms to its latticework as the great creatures pass. It has a vegetable slowness, a crystalline hunger. The structures **breathe**, expanding slightly when the temperature peaks at noon, contracting at the thermal minimums, creating micro-winds that whistle through their lattice-workings in frequencies that harmonize with the Drone of Refinement. In ten million years, these spires will meet, will merge, will form a canopy over the Plateaus—a dome of perfect computational crystal, grown not designed, the terminal output of a refinery-ecology that has exhausted the chaos of raw elements and now begins to refine the geometry of space itself.

This is the family that endures here. The cathedral-beasts grinding time into dust. The torch-bellied children maintaining the eternal present. The salt-angels dreaming of moisture that never was. The shadow-parasites eating the future to starve the past. And the slow, patient growth of the Architecture, which will one day roof the world in refracted light, completing the refinery by enclosing it, so that the Plateaus become a sealed sphere of perfect industrial order—a closed loop of heat and pressure and gleaming, finished, absolute **completion**.
