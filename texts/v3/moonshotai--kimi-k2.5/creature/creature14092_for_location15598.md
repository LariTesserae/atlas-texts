What lives here has forgotten the imperative of *becoming*. Evolution is obsolete three million meters above obsolete ground; here, the residents have achieved stasis—not death, not sleep, but the permanent condition of having arrived. They do not reproduce. They do not age. They simply *remain*.

**The Mites of Continuance** are the first you will encounter, though you must kneel to see them. Pale as old glue, each the size of a human thumbnail, they colonize the condensation grooves that vein the iron-bark flanks of the factory-boles. In a world where a hydraulic stroke takes a year, these arthropods move with shocking, almost violent rapidity. They are the inheritors, the terminal descendants of a food web that once teemed and now converges on this single point. They feed on the iridescent fungal films that bloom in the puddles where reality wears thinnest—those pools of causality-breakdown where water beads upward against gravity for seconds before surrendering. The Mites metabolize the impossible. Their chitin incorporates the toxic industrial sap that seeps from the hairline fractures in the architecture, rendering them poisonous, preserved, eternal. They do not breed in seasons but in centuries, their populations kept sparse not by predation—there is none—but by the sheer metabolic expense of living in a place that manufactures localized truths. They are the only things here that die and, in dying, leave corpses small enough to be noticed. They are the quick, the brief, the mayflies of the plateau.

Beneath them, or perhaps comprising them, are the **Fungal Films**. Not mushrooms, not lichens, but geometric blooms of mycelial logic that spread across the moss-plazas in perfect hexagons. They are the interface between the damp and the deranged. They drink the reality-breakdown moisture and, in digestion, stabilize it, transmuting paradox into carbohydrate. Without them, the Mites would starve; without the Mites, the films would overgrow the damp spaces and clog the arterial catchments. It is a marriage of obligatory slowness and frantic precision, the only economy that still functions here.

But these are merely the parasites, the surface dwellers. The true citizens are the **Arbortects**—the tree-buildings that constitute the grid. They are alive, though they have completed life. Having reached the end of ambition, they exist in a state of vegetal satori, their growth calcified into persistence. You can hear them thinking if you press your ear to their iron-lignin bark: a subsonic grinding, the basso profundo of xylem under titanic pressure. Their thoughts are hydraulic, taking decades to complete a single contemplation. They respire once per annum, drawing cool air through pores that resemble factory windows, expelling the processed breath that joins the perpetual steam-haze. 

Each Arbortect is a sovereign nation of purpose. Inside their translucent cellulose walls, the vegetable machinery dreams in slow motion. The seed-pod cathedrals are not merely structures but organs—wombs and stomachs and brains fused into bio-industrial unity. They manufacture truths. Deep in their heartwood, gears without axles spin in defiance of causality, generating brief, localized realities that flare like fireflies and die: moments where the sky is solid, where gravity reverses for a single spore, where the color blue becomes audible. They drift down eventually, carpeting the plazas in gold and rust, but until they touch the moss, they are awake. They contain the encoded instructions for processes that no longer have attendants, recipes for truths that will never be manufactured. Finally, inhabiting the gaps, are the **Breath-Momentaries**. These are not creatures but the byproduct of creaturely industry—sentient instances of manufactured reality. Born in the grinding cores of the factory-boles, they exist only as long as a gear-turn, occupying the spaces where causality has worn thin. They are the localized truths made briefly flesh: a moment where you are simultaneously here and at the plateau’s opposite edge; a fact that asserts itself against observation; a color that remembers being sound.
