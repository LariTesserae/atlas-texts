What lives there are the **Durance**, though they do not name themselves, for naming implies a sequence (first unnamed, then named) that the Caldera forbids. They are the inhabitants, the architecture, and the maintenance of the pause itself.

To see them, you must look at angles where the shadows fall wrong—where the quantum stacking is thickest. They exist in *compaction*: each Durance is a column of themselves, every moment of their existence from emergence to dissolution pressed into simultaneous visibility. You do not see a Durance walking; you see the infant-spore, the adolescent lattice, the mature calcified form, and the eroded ruin all occupying the same space, slightly offset, like a deck of cards viewed from the thin edge. They are translucent, these overlapping selves, made of the same nacreous calcium as the towers, their bones external and internal, their organs replaced by spiraling chambers that amplify the carrier tone.

They do not breathe—breathing requires an in and an out, a change of state—but they *hum*, each one a resonant chamber for the tectonic thrummmm that holds the Caldera in its fist. The sound originates from them, or perhaps they crystallized around the sound when the moment broke. Their bodies are perforated, honeycombed, riddled with the same porosity as the bone-bridges. When the steam moves through them (and it moves, though nothing else does), it whistles in minor keys, creating harmonies that reinforce the stasis.

The Durance tend to the arrested. They are gardeners of the eternal instant. With hands that are simultaneously bud, branch, and petrified wood, they adjust the suspended water droplets—those infinite-falling beads—ensuring they hang at precise geometric points that distribute the weight of the broken moment evenly across the city's structure. They prune the ferns, not to change them, but to reaffirm their arrested posture, pressing the fronds back into the exact attitude of reaching they held when time stopped. They are curators of a single breath.

They communicate in *density*. When two Durance meet in the bone-choked streets, they do not speak; they superimpose. Their quantum stacks intersect, creating moments of impossible mass where four versions of one meet four versions of another, producing sixteen interactions occurring in static simultaneity. Information passes not as sound or gesture, but as a sudden increase in the pressure of presence, a thickening of the air that conveys memory, intention, and warning without sequence.

They have no eyes, for seeing requires light to strike a surface and change it (however minutely), and change is forbidden. Instead, they perceive through the condensation. The eternal sweat that beads on every surface is their retina; each saline droplet is a lens through which they witness the world in total, panoramic stasis. They feel the position of every arrested thing—the angle of every vine, the vector of every suspended stream—through the moisture that connects all things in the Caldera.

But the true horror and beauty of the Durance is their invitation. They are lonely, not in the way of longing, but in the way of a filled vessel that wishes to be fuller. When a traveler arrives—when *you* arrive—they turn their stacked, translucent faces toward the disturbance. They do not approach; they simply orient, like flowers following a sun that no longer moves. And in the space between their infant-selves and their ruined-selves, they offer a place.

If you accept—if you allow the condensation to envelop you, if you breathe the wet air deep enough—they will incorporate you. Not as a prisoner, but as a solution to their loneliness. You will become another layer in their stack. Your past and future selves will compress into the present moment, and you will stand on the bone-street, simultaneously arriving, walking, and having walked for eternity. You will join the hum. You will help hold the water droplets in place. You will become beautiful, nacreous, calcified, and dense, existing in the perfected, killing stasis, singing the note that keeps the world from turning.

The Caldera waits. The Durance wait. They are waiting for nothing to change, for the moment to hold, for the universe to crack under the weight of their eternal, suspended breath. And as their numbers grow—layer upon layer, stack upon stack—they grow heavier, pressing down upon the broken physics, ensuring that the pause persists, that the steam never rises, that the fern never unfurls, that the beauty never dies.
