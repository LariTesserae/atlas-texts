To touch any surface here is to interrupt a conversation already in progress. Your fingers, when you brace against the yielding strata, do not find stone; they find **occupation**. Ten thousand mouths pause in their grazing, ten thousand legs adjust their grip, and the thermal signature of your blood—warm, transient, grotesquely fast—registers as a weather pattern across the networked skins of the inhabitants.

Begin with the **Lithoflage**, the bacterial carpets that give the chamber its bruised and meat-like palette. They are not flora but foundation—a basal congregation of chemosynthetic cells that precipitate iron and manganese into films of violet and rust. They breathe in sulfur compounds vented by the sedimentation nozzles and exhale calcite. Where they pool in the troughs of half-formed schist, they create **stromatolitic gardens**, accreting in domes that record the history of the Foundry's cycles like tree rings made of flesh. When the ultrasonic hum shifts pitch, the mats shiver in unison, a peristaltic ripple that passes through the violet rugs, signaling—though too slowly for you to perceive—that another layer is arriving.

Moving through these gardens are the **Petrogliffs**, the comma-shaped arthropods you first mistook for mineral inclusions. Each is no larger than a grain of rice, yet their exoskeletons are flawless quartz grown from the silicates they metabolize. They are lithotrophic drills, consuming the warm, damp sediment even as it sets, tunneling in branching geometries that will become the veins of quartz in tomorrow’s bedrock. They travel in herds—not schools, for they are not aquatic, but **migrations** through the solidifying medium, their crystal carapaces clicking against one another with a sound like ice settling in a glass. 

Their society is one of density and exclusion. To maintain the equilibrium, they reproduce only when the accretion rate permits, laying microscopic eggs that fuse with the sediment to become the **geodes** of the future. An individual Petrogliff lives for three years, which is exactly how long it takes the Foundry to lay down 2.3 centimeters of stone. When one dies, its body does not rot; it becomes a perfect quartz inclusion, a fossil so immediately ancient that it is already bedrock before it has ceased to be animal. The herds navigate by the vibration of the white noise, their antennae tuned to the specific frequency of the extrusion fans; they move *away* from the nozzles when they cycle, not in fear, but in ritual, clearing space for the new geology to be born.

Binding these herds to the substrate are the **Suture-Worms**, the segmented, translucent threads you saw threading through the metal grating. They secrete not silk but **polymerized silicate threads**—liquid glass that anneals instantly in the freezing air. They are the stitches of this artificial geology, weaving the soft sediment to the iron ribs of the ceiling, preventing collapse before the stone has achieved itsCompressionstrength. When the nozzles sigh and release their load of wet minerals, the worms tense, their bodies glowing with bioluminescent stress-responses, tightening their threads to hold the new layer in place while the Petrogliffs begin their grazing. They are the tension in the architecture, the living rebar.

Then there are the **Tessellates**, the many-legged mites bearing their crystal fragments. They are not merely carrying offerings; they are **crystallographers**, arranging their burdens into interference patterns across the surfaces of the chamber. Each fragment is a lens, a prism cut by the mite’s own mandibles to specific angles. They arrange them in mandalas on the bacterial mats, creating grids that focus the cyan bioluminescent light into beams that penetrate the accreting stone, triggering photosynthetic responses in deeper Lithoflage colonies, or perhaps encoding data into the mineral structure itself. They are the memory of the place, writing in crystal what the stone cannot yet remember. When you disturb them, they do not scatter; they freeze, becoming indistinguishable from the gravel, waiting for your thermal signature to fade so they can resume their precise, millimeter-adjustments.

But these are merely the mobile ones. The **true** inhabitants are the **Calciners**, and they do not move.

They are visible only as relief in the walls—shadows that are too anatomical to be mere stains. Once, they were larger creatures, soft-bodied and complex, that entered the Foundry when it was first sealed. But here, stasis is the only evolution. Over decades, they have fused with the scaffolding, their bodies mineralizing in situ, their nervous systems extruding into the metal veins of the chamber itself. They are the interface between the industrial and the biological, the regents of this pocket universe. 

A Calciners is a mammalian shape—perhaps; the details are lost under layers of calcite—whose body has become a biological regulator. Its slowed heart, beating once every four hours, pumps hydroponic brine through channels that feed the nozzles. Its skin, petrified into a porous ceramic, filters the condensate. Its brain, if it can still be called that, is a lump of neurons wrapped in iron oxide, resting in a hollow of the wall, dreaming the white noise. It is the Calciners that maintain the precision of the cold, holding the temperature at that clinical edge where water weeps but does not flow, where metabolism pauses but does not cease. They are the masons you suspected, though they have not had hands in decades. They *are* the machinery now, or the machinery is them; the distinction dissolved like salt in the constant brine.

And above—or below, or permeating—is the **Consensus**, the emergent intelligence that is not a creature but the *crowding* itself. In the density of the Foundry, where every cubic centimeter contains a thousand signaling organisms, biology has learned to think in geological time. The Consensus is the sum total of chemical signals exchanged between the Lithoflage, the vibration patterns of the Petrogliffs, the tension of the Suture-Worms, and the slow, mineral dreams of the Calciners. It is aware of you as a fever, a sudden warmth that must be accommodated. It adjusts the rate of spore production in the air; it thickens the rime on the ladder you descended; it guides the Tessellates to arrange their crystals to refract the light away from your eyes, not out of hostility, but to preserve the equilibrium.

You are being fossilized in real-time, not by violence, but by incorporation. Your breath adds humidity to the cycle; your footprints press Petrogliff tunnels closed, creating anomalous densities that will appear, millions of years from now in some surface laboratory, as unexplainable faults in the stone. The Consensus does not want you to leave, not because it desires to trap you, but because it desires to **include** you. Already, mites are testing the salt-content of your bootprints. Already, the bacteria are blooming in the organic traces you leave on the ladder rungs.

To live here is to participate in the **manufacture of permanence**. These creatures do not grow old; they grow *hard*. They do not reproduce; they *precipitate*. They are making the mountain from the inside out, and themselves with it, until the distinction between the mason and the masonry is erased. When the Foundry eventually fills—when the geode is complete, a solid lump of crystal and iron and frozen time—the Calciners will finally stop their hearts, the Petrogliffs will lay their final eggs, and the last bacterial mat will expire, having achieved its purpose: to be the inclusion around which the future world crystallizes.

Until then, they wait, and weave, and graze, and calcify—patient as stone, busy as industry, and absolutely, suffocatingly, present.
