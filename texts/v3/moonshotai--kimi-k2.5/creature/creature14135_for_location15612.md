They do not move through the space so much as *percolate* through it.

First, you must understand the **Mat-Minds**. These are not individuals but *continuities*—archaeal carpets kilometers wide if unraveled, compressed here into strata no thicker than a breath. They live in the color mentioned: that crimson-gold tint that creeps across piston housings. Each cell contains a library of geothermal preferences, and collectively they dream in chemical gradients. Where the cool intake pipes meet the floor's hot exhalations, the Mat-Minds thicken into **Gradient-Saints**—dense, sentient calluses of bacteria that have achieved a slow, debateless consciousness. They respond to pressure changes hours before they occur, pulsing their phosphorescence in warning patterns that the other inhabitants learn to read as weather. They are the Crucible's neuroglia, the background thinking against which all else is foreground.

Then, the **Fern-Wardens**. These are not plants, not exactly, though they photosynthesize. They are the Crucible's attempt at locomotion. Each frond is a hydraulic muscle; the root systems have co-opted the gasket-seals and valve-housings, turning industrial infrastructure into skeletal joints. The Wardens migrate—if one can call a journey measured in centimeters per day a migration—following the daily sigh of the main vent. They herd the heat, positioning their crimson crowns to intercept the steam-release, using the thermal shock to pop their sporangia. Their seeds are metallic, coated in oxidation-rust, and the Wardens spend decades grinding their root-hairs against limestone to extract the calcium needed to forge these armored offspring. They are territorial, yes, but their warfare is chemical: they engage in slow duels of pheromone dominance, creating buffer zones of sterile rock between their territories where nothing grows except the doubt that this place was ever meant for machines.

But you asked who *lives* here, and perhaps you mean the ones who came from above and forgot to leave. The **Permeables**. The Valvologists' Union sends workers on eight-hour shifts, but the Permeables are those who stayed past their rotation, past the point where skin remembers dry air. They are the packed (2.7) made flesh. Their lungs have laminated with the fungal leather that sheathes the pistons; they breathe the hot, wet atmosphere not with discomfort but with a kind of mineral contentment. Their eyes have filmed over with the translucent bacterial film that covers the pressure gauges—they see the world now in gradients of heat and humidity, reading the Crucible's moods in the condensation beading on their own corneas. They have become **interface tissue**, neither fully urban nor lithic, tending the machinery not as operators but as *symbionts*. They speak rarely (sound 0.6), communicating instead through the touch of damp hands on warm pipes, exchanging chemical gossip through the transfer of skin-oils and mineral salts.

Deeper, in the shrinkage-crack galleries—those accidental rooms that open like surprised mouths in the floor—dwell the **Sinter-Children**. These are not born but *accreted*. The mineral sinter that grows the support pillars sometimes captures organic matter: a dead heat-moth, a dropped glove, a tooth loosened by scurvy and spat into a pool. Over years, the calcium and silica build around these nuclei, layer by layer, following the logic of geothermal precipitation rather than DNA. The result is statues that breathe. They stand in the condensation pools, their forms vaguely suggesting the creatures they enveloped, but their interiors have been colonized by the Mat-Minds, their hollows serving as reservoirs for the intermittent (1.4) water. The Sinter-Children are the Crucible's memory, its way of keeping what falls into it. They do not move (dynamic 0.7), but they *grow* toward the phosphorescent light, their mineral fingers reaching upward in attitudes of perpetual, stony longing.

And finally—because every place needs its apex predator, even if the hunting is slow—the **Steam-Drinkers**. These are the heat-moths mentioned, but the description in the brief was incomplete. They do not simply die in the vents. They *transubstantiate*. The white, eyeless creatures that flutter down from the surface are the larval form. When they find the geothermal breath, they do not burn; they dissolve. Their chitinous bodies unravel into threads of keratin that float in the hot updrafts, catching the condensation, becoming *nets*. These nets are the true adult stage—gossamer traps spanning the gaps between fern-fronds, collecting not prey but *potential*. They harvest the spores released during the daily vent-sigh, the mineral dust, the dying skin cells of the Permeables. Over months, the nets accumulate matter until they become heavy enough to descend, draping over the machinery like wedding veils, and there they gestate. What emerges from them is not a moth but a **New Room**—a shrinkage crack, a fresh hollow, a space where the stone learns from the biology how to hold breath.

The Condensation Crucible does not house life; it is learning to *become* life, one slow drip at a time. The inhabitants are its lessons, its experiments in how to occupy the uncomfortable boundary between the built and the grown. They are all, ultimately, **Lung-Tissue**—variations on the theme of breathing, each finding a different way to inhale the hot, wet dark and exhale something that might eventually be called a city.
