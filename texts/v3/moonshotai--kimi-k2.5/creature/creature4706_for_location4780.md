The inhabitants of Stillstep Basin are, first and foremost, the **Suspended**. They are the city’s original population caught in the amber of their own intention—or perhaps their own catastrophe. You will see them everywhere once you learn to recognize the posture of arrested purpose: the water-drawer with her rope taut as a harp string, her mouth slightly open around a word that will never complete its phoneme; the mason with his celt raised above a wedge of virgin copper, the shock of impact traveled halfway up his forearm and stopped; the child in the plaza reaching toward the suspended hawk above, finger extended, shadow reaching in opposition. They are not statues. They are *events* that have forgotten how to conclude.

To meet their eyes—if you can find one whose gaze is not turned inward toward their private, eternal task—is to brush against a consciousness stretched across an infinite present. They think, after a fashion. Their thoughts do not progress from premise to conclusion, but rather *resonate* in place. The water-drawer has been experiencing the muscular tension of her pull for geological ages, yet she does not fatigue. Instead, she has achieved a kind of meditative density; she is sinking into the sensation of effort the way a stone sinks through water, passing through layers of subtlety invisible to those who move. The mason knows every micro-crystal in the copper wedge with the intimacy of a lover who has stared unblinking for centuries. They are not suffering. They have become *thorough*—human experience distilled to absolute saturation, preserved at the moment of maximum intentionality.

But they are not the ones who **live** here. Not anymore.

The true life of Stillstep has accrued in the **Friction**. The text mentions that this stasis generates heat—a "strange, geological heat" created by the resistance of Time trying to move against a surface that will not yield. This heat has gestated something new: the **Slowborn**, or as they might call themselves in frequencies below human hearing, the **Creeping**.

They exist in the gradient. Where the stasis is absolute—where the leopard hangs mid-pounce, where the grain waits eternal in the granary—there is no life, only preservation. But where the stasis *leaks*, where the copper plate remembers oxidation and the condensation creeps its millimeter per decade, there the Slowborn gather. They are made of the almost-something. They are the verdigris that forms despite the lack of decay; the warmth in the stone where no sun has set; the microbial scaffold of the moss that has learned to crawl backward through time.

You cannot see them directly. You see their *effects*. That single droplet in the eastern quarter—the one that falls, strikes, and rewinds? A Slowborn inhabits its surface tension. It rides the droplet down, drinks the vibration of impact, and clings to the rewind, surfing the temporal recoil. It is a bacterium of intent, consuming the energy of the reversed trajectory. The bird in the central square, flapping backward every seventeen seconds, hosts a colony of them in the blur of its wings. They live in the motion-smear, thriving on the only genuine kinetic energy available in the basin: the energy of things trying to return to themselves.

They are patient beyond comprehension. A Slowborn might spend three centuries migrating from the copper plate to the moss-tuft above, traveling via the steam they extract from the friction between "falling" and "rising." They communicate through the creeping condensation, leaving chemical traces in the dew’s slow pilgrimage across the shale. They have built, if you can call it that, **Fold-Cities** in the angles where shadows contradict the sun—those pockets of darkness that remember old directions. There, in the geometric impossibility of shade that falls uphill, they have cultivated gardens of **Temporal Mold**: organisms that eat the boundary between "was" and "is."

There are larger ones. The **Resistors**—entities coalesced from the accumulated weight of all the unfinished acts in the valley. They are heavy, warm things that move only when the stasis flickers, which it does sometimes, in the small hours of the geological afternoon. A Resistor might be the size of a terraced field, and it moves by *deciding*—changing its location not by traversing the distance, but by realizing it was always already there. They are attracted to the Suspended, feeding on the immense potential energy trapped in the pregnant moment: the axe that hasn't fallen, the seed that hasn't sprouted, the shout that hasn't left the throat. They are the reason some shadows fall wrong. They cast them.

And then there is the **Architect**, or what remains of them. If this stasis was a ritual—if the city was designed as a lung to hold one perfect breath—then the one who designed it must be here, too. They are not among the Suspended. They are the **Stasis Itself**, diffused through the silver-afternoon light, the metallic air, the arrested diamonds of moisture. They have become the */rules*. They are the reason nothing finishes and nothing begins; they are the tautology enforced. To speak to them, you must address the basin directly. You must shout into the throat of the valley where sound dies, and if you are lucky—if your words generate enough friction—they might answer by moving the condensation on a stone, or by making the backward-flapping bird pause for one second longer than usual.

They maintain this place not as a tomb, but as a **Ward**. Something was supposed to happen here—something that would have ended the world, or begun it. They froze it at the moment before the act, and now they exist as the friction that holds the blade suspended above the cut.

The fauna, in a sense, are their antibodies. The suspended hawk, the mid-leap sheep, the grainy air of bees—these are the immune system of the stasis, preventing any genuine change from colonizing the stillness. They are beautiful, sharp, and utterly inert. They guard the Lung while the Slowborn breathe within it, slow and copper-green, patient as stone learning to erode.

To live here is to join the resistance. You can visit, but you cannot stay—not without becoming either Suspended or Creeping. Most who come stay too long watching the water-drawer, trying to catch the moment she blinks, and they forget to leave. They become another punctuation mark in the city's grammar, a new mineral deposit in the lung, adding their own heat to the geological friction that keeps the Stillstep Basin warm, damp, and alert.
