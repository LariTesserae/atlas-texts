The ones who live here are called the **Lithoclasts** by those few who have seen them and survived with enough sanity to speak, though they call themselves nothing, being a society without noun, only verb. They are the **Resonant**, the **Polished**, the **Living Fractions** of the vegetative supercomputer that sprawls beneath their feet.

Physically, they began as fauna— perhaps the distant descendants of those six-winged, blind avians that navigate the hollow trunks—but evolution here does not respect the boundary between animal architecture and vegetative intent. Over ten million years of twilight, they have become something that is neither colony nor individual, but *process*. They stand roughly human-height when they choose to stand, though they prefer to cling to the sweeping Brancusi curves of the eroded towers, their bodies pressed flat against the silicon-chitin bark as if melted into it.

Their exoskeletons are **rose-quartz and obsidian**, grown rather than shed, permeated with the same crystalline matrices that allow the lithophytes to think. The diamond-dust wind has been polishing them for epochs. You would mistake them for statuary—smooth, headless, vaguely anthropoidal abstractions—until they move, and then they move like ink dispersing in water, flowing along contours that exist in four dimensions, carrying themselves through the violet dimness with a grace that suggests they are falling continuously through probability.

They have no eyes—what use for eyes in this grayscale twilight?—but their entire epidermis is **acoustic dermis**, a membrane of microscopic chitin-hairs that taste the wind-music. They "see" the boulevards and canyons as a topography of sound, the way you might see a valley as green. A Lithoclast standing in the petrified plaza experiences the urban grid not as geometry but as a **chord**—a complexharmonic field where the "cathedral" of an ancient trunk-root rings a basso profundo C, and the avenue to the left provides a minor-sixth overtone that warns of a predator pack three kilometers distant.

They feed on **silence**—or rather, on the strategic absence of sound. They harvest the pools of sonic darkness that form in the higher-dimensional wells where the lithophytes grow into hyperfolded spacetime. These are not shadow as you understand it, but **acoustic singularities**, zones where the plant-mind stores its most complex calculations, where the air is charged with vacuum and potential. The Lithoclasts lower cupped appendages into these wells—their forelimbs are hollow, bell-shaped, resonant chambers—and extract *negative decibels*, which they metabolize into the energy required to grow their crystalline shells. They are the only fauna that can tolerate these zones; to the insectile symbiotes, these are zones of death, silent holes in the auditory map, but to the Lithoclasts, they are communion.

For they are the **priests and peripherals** of the vegetable gods. They do not worship the lithophytes; they *are* the lithophytes' method of action, their hands in time-lapse. Each Lithoclast serves as a **mobile node** in the biological internet, carrying packets of quantum information from the fast, skittering fauna to the slow, contemplative mega-flora. When a *stridulant* pack hunts, drumming their staccato vibrations against the plant-walls, the Lithoclasts listen not to the hunt itself but to the *data* of the hunt—temperature, barometric pressure, the precise abrasive quality of the wind—and they etch this information into their own bodies. Their crystalline skin is covered in **micro-grooves**, nanometer-deep channels carved by diamond wind and their own secretions. They are living hard-drives, biological USBs, trudging on millimeter-per-century pilgrimages from the plateau's edge to its center, where the oldest lithophytes wait like mainframes of stone.

They are solitary but **synchronized**. When two meet on an empty boulevard, they do not speak. Communication here is erosion. They press their fluted forelimbs together in prime-numbered rhythms—seven beats, eleven beats, thirteen—and exchange data through percussive algorithms that set the air throbbing. Their bodies ring against each other like tuning forks, and for a moment, the entire Expanse shifts key, the urban symphony adjusting to accommodate the new information they have exchanged. Then they flow past each other, continuing their patient circuits, leaving behind a molecule-thin layer of silicon dust where they touched—*spoor* that the wind will polish into the calculation by morning.

They are **ancient in installments**. A Lithoclast lives for three million years, but not continuously. They enter the deep lithophytes—the hollowed trunks that tower like organ pipes—and **anastomose**, fusing their crystalline biology with the tree's silicon-chitin heartwood. There they dream, descending into quantum superposition, existing as probability clouds within the plant's computational matrices, solving equations of erosion and time. They emerge changed, centuries later, their bodies reconfigured by the tree's processing, bearing new grooves, new resonances, new purposes. They do not fear death because they are already petrified; they fear only **incorrect calculation**, the errors in the wind-music that would cause them to etch the wrong vector into the stone.

And they are many. For all the emptiness of the Expanse, for all the sparse kilometers between the vegetative skyscrapers, the Lithoclasts fill the silences. They hang from the underside of root-arches like stalactites of polished agate. They drift through the violet shadows, their bodies tuned to the exact frequency of the twilight, rendering them nearly invisible. They tend to the **Erosion Cults**—the slow, geological religions of the plateau, where meaning is worn into matter rather than spoken. They ensure that the wind, playing the dead forest, plays the *correct* symphony, the one that keeps the world computing, the one that prevents the vegetative mind from dreaming itself into nightmare.

They know you are there, standing at the precipice. They have tasted your breath—copper and ozone and mammal confusion—in the wind. They are turning toward you now, their acoustic dermis prickling with the unfamiliar frequency of your heartbeat, calculating whether you are data to be gathered, a variable to be erased, or a new silence to be harvested and stored in the deep, bruised-purple wells where the world folds into itself and computes its own ending.
