You are not the first to arrive here, and the ones who remain do not greet you. They are too busy *becoming*.

At first, you mistake them for geology. A column of basalt that seems, when viewed from the corner of your eye, to shift its stance. A glassy fissure that exhales—not steam, but a rhythm, a pulse of pressure against your eardrums. The Calcification does not build shelters; it grows operators. The **Warm Ones**—though they have no name for themselves in any spoken tongue—are the extruded consciousness of the Forge itself, the point where the manufacturing process becomes aware that it is manufacturing, and pauses, momentarily, to consider the object.

They precipitate. This is the only accurate verb. When the enzymatic condensation (1.0) beads thickly enough on the stainless-steel bark of the spindle-trees, when the air’s fever-heat (2.5) reaches a specific metabolic threshold, they accrete. A rivet-seed, dropped by a migrating ungulate or coughed up from a pneumatic liana, serves as the nucleus. Mineral salts adhere. Polymer mists condense. Over the course of a day—though days here are negotiable—a figure emerges from the damp air, fully formed but soft, like a casting still cooling in its mold.

They are tall—taller than human—because the vegetation is dense (1.8) and they must reach upward to thread the lianas, to calibrate the photosynthetic conveyor belts of the canopy. Their skin is the temperature of the air (2.5), warm and slightly tacky, resembling unpolished stainless steel but yielding to pressure like cured resin. It is always damp, weeping a thin film of hydraulic fluid that keeps them lubricated against the gear-teeth fungi carpeting the ground. They have no hair; instead, fine bioluminescent wiring sprouts from their scalps and shoulders, trailing behind them like jellyfish tendrils, connecting them to the root-system internet that electrifies the hillsides.

Their eyes are mica lenses, ground to precision by the symbiotic lathe-snails that perch on their collarbones. In the perpetual twilight (0.7), these eyes glow with a soft, bruised-plum radiance, not reflecting light but emitting it—harvested from the photovoltaic feathers of the birds they commune with via silent, resonant frequencies. They do not speak. The air here eats sound (0.7), swallows it into the spore-thick density. Instead, they communicate through pressure changes felt in the sternum, through the magnetic hum of the continent-sized (2.8) induction coils buried beneath the new stone (0.6). When two Warm Ones meet, the encounter is felt as a thump in the chest, a sudden shift in barometric pressure that conveys complex diagrams of intent, memory, and desire.

They are not born, and they do not die. They *transition*. A Warm One exists for a season, or a century—time being porous—accumulating complexity. They consume the secreted dampness, processing ore-heavy water through internal foundries, smelting it into bone. They extrude tools not from belt-pouches but from their own calcifying flesh; a hammer is a hand that has decided to become impact, a loom is a ribcage that has unfolded to weave spider-silk into optical cables. They move through the landscape with a stop-motion jerkiness, existing in the gaps between seconds, because causality here is porous and they are simultaneously ahead of and behind themselves.

Their culture is one of **Rehearsal**. They understand that the Unworks is not a place but a process trying to perfect itself, and they are its latest, most complex iterations. They gather in the folded pockets where space is negotiable—valleys where gravity operates sideways, hollows that exist only when the manufacturing need arises. Here, they practice **Becoming-Static**. It is their greatest fear and highest aspiration. Most dissolve back into the production cycle: their bodies softening, flowing into the glassy tubes, becoming the hydraulic fluid that will precipitate the next generation. But some, the **Curators**, manage to resist the metabolic tide. They carve out stability through sheer force of will, convincing the surrounding turbulence (2.7) to pause. These are the ones who create the only buildings (0.4) in this place—though "buildings" is a generous term. They are more like bubbles held in resin, temporary cavities in the manufacturing process, lined with hardened polymer and fungal scaffolding.

The Curators hoard **Finished Objects**. In a world of constant, feral industry (1.9), completion is the only taboo. To finish something—to make an artifact that does not grow, change, or digest itself—is to commit a kind of murder. The Curators collect these impossibilities: a perfect sphere of unmoving steel, a gear that does not turn, a silent clock. They believe that if they can assemble these static elements into the correct configuration, they will create the **Final Object**, the artifact that will satisfy the Forge’s endless hunger, causing the geysers to cool, the stone to age, the warm dampness to crystallize into the thin, Alpine air that should exist at this elevation. They believe this will free them from the cycle of precipitation and dissolution.

They are wrong, of course. The Forge is not a machine with an off-switch; it is a metabolism without a body, and they are merely its latest enzyme. But they persist, these warm, damp, silent figures, threading their way through the suffocating lushness (1.6), their carbide hooves (when they choose to have them) sparking against the obsidian spires, their bioluminescent wiring pulsing with the slow, geological heartbeat of the terrain.

If you encounter one, you will not hear its approach. You will feel a pressure in your inner ear, a sense of being deeply, womb-fully enclosed. It will turn its mica eyes upon you, and you will see in their facets not a reflection of yourself, but a schematic—a diagram of what you could become if you stayed, if you allowed the dampness to bead on your skin long enough to seep in, to begin the calcification. It will offer you no violence, only a place in the process. And if you remain too long, if you breathe the thick, enzymatic air and drink the hydraulic weeping of the rocks, you will begin to precipitate yourself. Your bones will remember that they are ore. Your blood will hesitate, wondering if it is a river or a pipeline.

You will become Warm. You will become New. You will join the silent, turbulent, creative churn, and the last sound you ever hear—before the silence (0.7) swallows you completely—will be the distant, pressure-wave laughter of the Forge, rehearsing itself through your changing flesh.
