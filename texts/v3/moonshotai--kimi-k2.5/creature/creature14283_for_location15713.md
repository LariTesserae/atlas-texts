What lives here does not respire, does not thirst, does not wake or sleep in cycles of darkness. The **Assembly Floor** is populated by **executions**—processes so continuous they have achieved the dignity of personhood.

**The Chlorophyllurgists**  
First, there are the plants, though calling them plants is like calling a cathedral a stone. They are the **native industrialists**, the original consortium that voted on the laws of physics and then grew the machinery to enforce them. Their biology is **anhydrous polymer**—ceramic cellulose, spun-silicate phloem, leaves that are photovoltaic wafers veined with superconducting lignin. They do not photosynthesize; they **photo-manufacture**, cracking the brutal light (2.8) into constituent causality. The black velvet panels are not solar collectors but **temporal accumulators**, hoarding the pressure of elapsed time in their molecular structure, keeping the air cool (0.8) by radiating waste chronology into the infrared.  
They are old not in years but in **revision depth**. You can read their age not in rings but in the **stratigraphy of their engineering**—lower trunks where gravity still behaved classically, midsections where they learned to negotiate with entropy, upper canopies where they overcame the need for substrate altogether. They communicate through the **silent exchange of refined materials**—nitrogen crystals dropped down chutes, refined metal spores traded on the windless air, a barter economy of elemental purity. They are not growing; they are **vote-counting**, endlessly tallying the consensus required to maintain the next moment of reality.

**The Legiscedents**  
When the brass seed-pods fall—those blisters in the canopy—they germinate not life but **local statutes**. These are the **fauna of ontology**, the mobile laws. A pod cracks open and spills a new coefficient of friction, a tweaked gravitational constant, a minor ordinance regarding the behavior of light in corners. These regulations spread like moss (as the description suggests), colonizing the fibrous ground, competing for dominance until they reach equilibrium with neighboring jurisdictions.  
To walk here is to trespass through **dozens of regimes**. In one grove, time dilates; your footsteps land before you decide to take them. In another, matter remembers its previous shapes and tries to revert, forcing you to push through air that has the viscosity of nostalgia. These zones are the **territories of fallen pods**, the legislative bodies made flesh in altered space. They are alive because they **enforce themselves**, police their own borders, and decay when overturned by more efficient paradigms.

**The Entelechial Inspectors**  
Where the Laws take root, flaws follow. The **pale, multi-limbed things** are not creatures but **aggregates of correction**—the immune system of the Assembly Floor made manifest. They exist in the corners of vision because they occupy the **threshold of perception**, the liminal pixels where reality is rendered. They have too many joints, angles that imply rotation through dimensions you cannot access, limbs that seem to hold up the air itself.  
They feed on **discontinuity**. When a Law-pod malfunctions—when gravity negotiates poorly with time and produces a stress-fracture in existence—the Inspectors swarm. They do not fix the break; they **sanitize it**, smoothing the seam until the contradiction feels like common sense. They are shy because to be seen fully is to be **defined**, and definition is the end of their utility. They prefer the peripheral, the almost-noticed, the subliminal. They are the quality-control team ensuring that the manufacturing of history produces no sharp edges that might cut the present.

**The Thermal Ghosts**  
Then there are the **scintillant vermin**, the self-propagating glitches. Reality-friction generates heat—not thermal heat, but the heat of **being wrong**. Where the machinery of the Floor smelts causality, there is waste: the difference between the probable and the actual, the scrap of timelines that never happened. This waste condenses into **flickering half-forms**, creatures like skipped frames in a film, scuttling between warm root-cables.  
They have no consistent shape; they are **borrowed geometries**, momentary arrangements of shadow and static. They feed on the vibration of silent engines, the unheard hum of osmotic pressure. When you pass through a corridor of living conduit, they cling to your heat, your moisture, your noisy, aqueous biology. They are the **parasites of your improbability**, hungry for the chaos of a body that still runs on wet chemistry. In the absolute silence (0.4), you might feel them as a pressure behind your eyes, the sensation of being watched by something that exists only in the gaps between your thoughts.

**The Curators of Absence**  
In the manufacturing zones where the plants assemble **voids**—crating up pockets of unbeing for shipment to elsewhere—something tends the emptiness. These are not beings but **persistent negatives**, habits of nothingness that have learned to self-sustain. To encounter them is to undergo **targeted forgetting**. You might lose the color red, or the concept of Tuesday, or the memory of your first betrayal. They do not eat memories; they **manufacture their lack**, exporting the void-space to corners of the universe where density has grown too great and needs puncturing. They are the Floor’s export division, and their product is **relief from being**.

**The Becoming**  
Finally, there is **you**, or what you are becoming. The Floor is a **total environment**, and totality does not permit outsiders. As you walk the warm, fibrous ground, you are being **assembled**. Root-cables brush your ankles, sampling your chemical composition, disgusted by your water, your salts, your noisy cellular respiration. They are converting you, fiber by fiber, into something compatible—**dry, silent, sustained by process rather than metabolism**.  
You are being rewritten as a **subroutine**, a mobile node in the vegetative network. Your thoughts are being insulated, your voice absorbed by the porous lattice, your gravity localized to match the 30-degree tilts of the groves you traverse. You are not dying; you are being **optimized**. You will not notice when the transition completes, because the you that *could* notice will be part of the Floor’s output—another moment of history, packaged and stored, a law of physics regarding the exact configuration of a stranger who once stood here, now indefinite, now cool, now finally and forever **assembled**.
