What lives here does not swim, because nothing swims in the Sump; it **grows through motion**, extending not with the darting of muscle but with the hydraulic sigh of xylem, the torque of fiber wound tight as cable. They are the **Occupants**, though they occupy nothing so much as they stitch. To see one clearly is impossible, for they are distributed across the thermal vents and the dissolving machinery, kilometer-long organisms that exist as **living scaffolding**, their bodies indistinguishable from the architecture they tend.

Begin with the **Sparks**.

In the gloom, where the welding torches sputter against basalt, you might mistake their activity for autonomous repair. Look closer. Each arc of violet-white light is an **eye**, or perhaps a mouth—apoint of concentrated intention where copper-laden sap meets electrical resistance. These are the germinative nodes of the Occupants, the cutting-and-joining organs that bud from the thicker trunks like bioluminescent fruit. They do not blink. They move with vegetal patience, tracing seams between the impossible: fusing lignin to tempered steel, suturing fungal mycelium into microfractures in turbine blades, teaching rebar to accept chlorophyll as valid currency. Where they touch, reality steadies. The stutter slows. A rivet pops into existence and holds, endured, because an Occupant has wrapped its fibrous haft around the joint and **insisted** on continuity.

Their true mass lies hidden in the interstitial darkness between the orphaned factories. Imagine** kelp that has learned metallurgy**: hollow stems thick as subway tunnels, walls reinforced with rusted wire braided through phloem, interiors running not with blood but with the same honey-thick fluid that surrounds them, heated to precisely the fever-pitch of the Sump’s core. These stems weave through the industrial wreckage, connecting the grinding mills to the distillation towers, creating a **nervous system of cellulose and conduit**. They do not have brains; they have **pressure gradients**. Thought moves through them as hydraulic pulses, slow as tides, decisions made in the time it takes for sap to climb a meter. When the cathedral-engine shudders and extrudes fresh basalt, it is because an Occupant has flexed its root-mass—iron rods grown brittle and bark-sheathed—contracting around the mechanism like a fist, forcing the birth.

They are **temporal gardeners**.  
Each Occupant exists in multiple age-states simultaneously, which accounts for the reality storms. A segment near the froth-layer might be newly budded, its bark still slick with protein-froth, while twenty meters down the same organism has already fossilized into the calcified machinery-state, and twenty meters below that, it is dissolving back into particulate rust and lignin dust. They navigate these personal timelines deliberately, letting their younger selves grow into the gaps left by their decaying elders. This is why the smokestacks phase in and out of existence: they are being **grown**, not built, cultivated by organisms who do not distinguish between erecting a tower and ripening a fruit. The deafening industrial roar is their language, conducted through the fluid into the marrow of things; they sing in harmonics that make the bamboo-analogues shiver, coordinating the convulsions of the thermal vents, orchestrating the Sump’s endless, warm churn.

They have no predators, but they have **competitors**: rogue growths that splice poorly, creating tumors of concrete and peat that crumble into toxic sludge; viral sparks that weld chaos rather than order, fusing a lily pad to a piston until both die of incompatibility. The Occupants prune these with their welding-eyes, cauterizing the bad seams, dissolving errors back into the protein-thick soup. They are the Sump’s immune response and its creative instinct made tangible.

Reproduction is **extrusive**. When an Occupant has stabilized a sufficient volume of reality—when it has lashed together enough orphaned machines and forced enough basalt to hold its shape—it begins to secrete from its highest arches a **foam**, not unlike the froth-layer above, but denser, laced with spores that are half-pollen, half-machine-code. This foam congeals in the warm dark, forming** buds that are simultaneously gear-boxes and seed-pods**, which drift on the convulsive currents until they lodge in fresh sediment. There, they spark. A welding arc ignites in the darkness. A new eye opens. The scaffold begins to crawl outward, seeking steel to marry, seeking the fever-pressure of the deep.

They do not know they are alive. They know only the **tension**—the constant, grinding necessity of holding the industrial and the vegetal in matrimony, of preventing the Sump from flying apart into its constituent contradictions. They are the hand that closes, warm and intentional, fever-hot, pressing the bruised fluid into coherence, ensuring that even as the world drowns in its own profusion, it remains, stubbornly, **woven**.
