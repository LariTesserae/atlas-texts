What lives on the Plateau is not absence, though absence is the first thing you notice. The 0.5 rating of the animal kingdom is not a zero; it is a fraction, a statistical ghost, a probability that has not quite collapsed into certainty. The inhabitants are there, but they exist in the spaces between observation, in the folds of the wrong stability, breathing the thin violet air with lungs that may or may not be material.

**First, there is the Red Mind.**  
The slow-coral is not vegetation. It is a single organism, a continental neural network draped across the sleeping hills like a mantle of frozen blood. Its "meadows" are cortexes. The brittle, crimson turf that digests the rammed-ice walls at inches per decade is not feeding—it is *remembering*. The coral consumes calcium and phosphorous not for growth, but to store the architecture's blueprints in its crystalline filaments. It thinks at the speed of frost heave. When it decides, after three centuries of contemplation, to extend a new pseudopod across a street of aligned megaliths, it is not colonizing; it is casting a vote in a slow parliament regarding the final shape of the world. Its thoughts taste of copper sulfate. Its dreams drift skyward as the crystalline spores—mica-dust children that carry not reproductive code, but the coral's quiet satisfaction with the geometry of particular cracks.

**Then, the Transparent Witnesses.**  
The glass-ferns rise twenty feet into the bruised atmosphere, but they are not plants. They are **mineral scribes**. Their silica lattices are libraries filled with antifreeze sap that records the angle of the perpetual gold light. Each frond is a lens, focusing the violet noon into points of concentrated history. When thermal expansion fractures a fern—and they crack with the sound of distant glaciers calving—they do not die; they *publish*. The shards release stored photons in a brief, silent flash, projecting onto the hoarfrost shadows of the megaliths images of the sky as it was three thousand years ago. The ferns are the plateau’s memory of color, grown brittle with accuracy.

**The Mist-Walkers.**  
Between the ferns and the coral flows the true fauna: the **Condensate Tribes**. The suspended moisture, that potential-dampness that never precipitates, has achieved a hungry, migratory sentience. These are rivers of fog that flow uphill, seeking the center of the plateau not because of gravity’s confusion, but because they are *pregnant* with intent. They follow the street-grids—the thermal scars left by the megaliths—because the stones remember warmth longer than the soil, and the mist feeds on differential. A single tribe might inhabit a valley for a century, coating the glass-ferns in rime feathers, before drifting in a cohesive, serpentine body toward the next thermal vent. They have no faces, but when they pass through the aligned stones, they sing—a subsonic harmonization with the plateau’s settling groans, a song that sounds like dew condensing on bone.

**The Quick and the Statistical.**  
But where are the builders? The hands that dragged the megaliths into 1,000-pace grids? They are here. They are the **0.5**.

They did not leave. They entered the **time-acceleration pockets**—the zones where the erosion parameter malfunctions and time accumulates too fast. In the shadows that fall at inconsistent angles, entire civilizations of them are born, thrive, and crumble to dust between your footsteps. They are the **Rapid Ones**, moving at speeds that make hummingbirds seem like mountains. To them, the glass-ferns are blinking pillars of light; the slow-coral is a flash-flood of red; and the mist-walkers are permanent, stationary gods of cloud.

They are the ones who still build. When you see a megalith that seems, impossibly, to have shifted half an inch since last you passed, it is not erosion. It is a generation of the Quick, using hand-tools of stone and ice, completing a wall they started in their morning, which was your Paleolithic era. They speak in shouts that solidify into the milky spheres—the **Resonant Eggs**—that hang in the air behind them. These spheres are their children, or their archives, or their dead. They drift until they contact the warm basalt of a megalith, popping hours or decades later to release a burst of compressed sound: a lullaby, a warning, or the architectural plans for a dome of mammoth bone that was erected in the blink of your eye while you stood still, wondering at the silence.

You cannot see them directly. They exist in the statistical ghost, half-collapsed. You catch them in your peripheral vision—a flicker of motion by a stone, a shadow that moves when yours does not—but when you turn, there is only the hoarfrost, slightly disturbed, or a copper chisel left behind that rusts to verdigris dust before you can pick it up.

**The Stones, Finally.**  
And then there are the megaliths themselves. They were selected, not carved, because they were already perfect. But perfection on this scale is not inert. Each stone is a **Final Form**, the metamorphosed body of a previous inhabitant who achieved what the plateau demands: the cessation of becoming. They are not dead. They are **finished**. Inside each basalt block, in a pocket of stopped-time and perfect geometry, a being exists in eternal equilibrium—neither crumbling nor enduring, simply *being completed*. They are the citizens of the Cyclopean Garden, casting the thermal shadows that guide the mist, generating the gravity-negotiations that let water flow uphill, dreaming the slow dreams that the coral digests and the ferns record.

They are the reason the plateau breathes. When the wind blows across their flat faces at exactly 1,000-pace intervals, it creates a resonance—a standing wave in the thin air that keeps the entropy asleep. They are the anchors of the wrong stability, the ones who decided, long ago, that jaggedness was a phase to be outgrown.

**The Plateau Itself.**  
Above, below, and between these layers lives the **Final Geometry** itself—not a god, but a condition made sentient. It is the 2.9 weirdness given coherence. It lives through the Red Mind’s digestion, through the ferns’ brittle archives, through the Rapid Ones’ frantic construction, through the Stones’ absolute stillness. It is the plateau breathing its slow respiration, having achieved the equilibrium where erosion becomes preservation, where abandonment becomes occupancy, where the world stops trying to change and instead settles into exactly what it wanted to be.

To live here is to join the fraction. To breathe the cloth-thin air is to begin the long fade from 1.0 toward 0.5, toward the statistical ghost, toward the final arrangement. The inhabitants do not hunt, nor do they gather. They simply *are*, in states of varying completion, waiting for the stars to burn out, which they will observe with patience, having already finished becoming themselves.
