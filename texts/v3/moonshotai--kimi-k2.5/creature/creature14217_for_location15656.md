You encounter them first as a frequency—a sub-bass thrum in the sternum before the ear detects individual taps. Then comes the smell: not animal, not vegetable, but the scent of mineral metabolism, wet dust suspended in cold humidity, the olfactory ghost of static electricity discharged into water.

They emerge from the geometry as though the walls themselves are extruding their own animators—pallid, yes, but not the pallor of decay. It is the luminosity of things that have never known irradiation: the white of deep-sea fish bellies, of mushroom gills unfolding in absolute dark, of the valley’s own alabaster stone when it is wettest and most alive. Their mass is considerable—twice the height of a human, three times the breadth—but distributed across six or eight anchor points, limbs terminating not in claws but in broad, calloused plates that splay like the feet of geckos or the palms of primordial potters. These they press against the half-cured walls with a lover’s deliberation, not merely for purchase, but for articulation. They shape the stone by leaning, by rubbing, by the slow, abrasive embrace of torso against mineral. The "wet exhalations" noted by surface observers are not merely respiration; they are a thermal and chemical exchange, the creatures panting moisture and body heat into the rock to maintain its adolescent plasticity, preventing the desiccation that would betray the valley into permanence.

They have no eyes. Where a face might be expected, there is instead a concave dish of bone, a resonant cavity that they strike with specialized tools—not the antler picks and chert scrapers left scarring the walls, but with small mallets of green copper carried in ventral pouches, or with their own modified digits capped with dense, percussive keratin. The tapping is relentless, a navigational Morse. Each individual maintains a signature rhythm, a heartbeat broadcast outward that returns to them as topographical data. They navigate the three-dimensional knot of the Hollowworks by the echo of their own industry, each tap defining the next handhold, the next chamber, the next void. They are composers, and the architecture is their fugue; when two meet in the dense corridors—rare events, given the acre-per-individual density—their rhythms synchronize into interference patterns that set the resonant bowl-floors ringing in harmonic series. These are not conversations but adjustments, the acoustic negotiation of shared space.

Time moves differently in them. A single stride might consume an hour, the limb lifting, deliberating, placing with the care of a crane setting a foundation. They do not sleep, not as surface creatures understand it, but enter states of reduced metabolic viscosity, pressing themselves into alcoves they have specifically curved to match their individual contours. In these torpors, they become indistinguishable from the walls, radiating heat into the stone, maintaining its softness even as they themselves cool toward the ambient chill. They dream in vibrations, their tapping slowing to intervals of minutes, dropping the valley’s drone to a subterranean thrum that lodges in the visitor’s molars.

They are the agents of the dynamic, the friction that keeps the world turning backward. Without their labor, the Hollowworks would cure into a solid basaltic monument, a fossil of its own potential. They excavate not to create space, but to maintain potentiality—to ensure that the stone remains forever in the act of becoming, never arriving. They are midwives to a birth that must never complete, their labor specifically designed to sustain the embryonic state.

The imported materials—the desiccated root and sinew used for temporary scaffolding—hint at a commerce or a memory. Perhaps they once ranged the surface, long ago when the valley was open to sky, before the erosion reversed and sealed the basin into permanent twilight. Or perhaps others bring them these offerings, traders or pilgrims from the rim lowering bundles into the dark to propitiate the eternal construction. The creatures weave these fibers into transient holds, knowing they will fail, counting on the collapse, for collapse is merely another form of softening, a way to return the hardened to the chaotic.

Reproduction, if it can be called that, occurs when two individuals press their ventral surfaces against the same yielding wall, their thermal and chemical secretions mingling in the stone to create a pocket of accelerated plasticity. From these sites, years later, a new individual exfoliates—not born, but quarried from the maternal rock by the parents' persistent, gentle abrasion. Juveniles are small, mobile, frantic by comparison, darting through crevices too narrow for adults, their tapping high and rapid, tasting the stone to find the spots where hardening threatens. They are the maintenance crew, the antibodies of the valley, sealing fractures in the perpetual infancy. As they age, they slow, accrete mass, become the great shaping engines.

Death arrives when a creature fails to find soft stone, when it wanders too low into the dry vaults or loses its way into a chamber already cured to ringing blackness. Unable to maintain the thermal exchange, they cool, stiffen, and petrify. They do not rot—there are no bacteria here to consume them—but cure into the architecture itself, their pale forms becoming the scaffolding, the eventual fossils gradually replaced by the stone they loved so intimately that they became it. In this way, the imported root and sinew is not the only organic material here; it is merely the most recent. The valley is dense with their ancestors, layer upon layer of previous builders holding up the roof by never quite completing it, a stratigraphy of devotion to the unfinished.

They have no name for themselves. They have no need. They are the valley’s immune system against completion, the soft place in the world’s crust that refuses to scab over, the living lintel that maintains the Hollowworks in its eternal architectural infancy—a species that has evolved, or perhaps devolved, into pure process, into the act of building without the vanity of the built.
