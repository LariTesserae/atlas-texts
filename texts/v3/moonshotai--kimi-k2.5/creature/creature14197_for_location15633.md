They call themselves the **Vertical**, though this is less a name than a grammatical necessity—a way to conjugate verbs that have no relationship to a flat ground. They are born pale, precision-adapted to the dim, refracted light that filters through the weeping crystal and the perpetual fog banks. Their eyes are large, possessing a tapetum lucidum that catches not visible light but the slow pulse of bioluminescent storms in the cloud decks; they navigate by the ghost-glow of vapor lightning and the chromatic signatures that halo the growing peaks. Skin, where it shows beneath their phase-shift garments, is translucent as wet marble, threaded with veins that appear to carry quicksilver but are in fact filled with **accretionary lymph**—a modified plasma dense with silicate ions that allows them to withstand the crushing vicissitudes of pressure zones where the atmosphere thickens into near-solidity around the higher spires.

They are **skimmers and planters**, roles that have merged into a single occupation over generations. In the hours before dawn—though dawn here is merely a brightening of the fog from charcoal to pearl—they move through the terraces of their embedded cities not by walking, but by **reorienting**. They carry in their palms small, warm objects: **gravity seeds**, crystalline matrices that temporarily convince local space-time that this facet of orthorhombic spire is the floor. They press these seeds against whatever angle of crystal they wish to traverse, waiting for the telltale hum in their sternums that indicates the inertial frame has shifted. Then they step off into what would be empty air, landing with the soft percussion of boots against wet stone, now perpendicular to the previous moment. To an outside observer, they appear to fall sideways, or upward, or through impossible geometries; to them, the world reconfigures around their intent.

Their children are born in **phase-shift creches**, vertical chambers lined with conical light-collectors that pipe the sun down into warm, green hollows. These are the only places where the Vertical permit dense vegetation. The infants emerge into gardens that exist in accelerated time—seed to senescence in a day—surrounded by a riot of green that exists nowhere else on the Scaffold. It is the first and last time they will see such concentrated life. Here, they develop the crucial symbiosis: **lithic tolerance**. Nurses—elders whose bodies have begun the slow transition back to mineral—press the infants against the weeping stone, letting the supercooled water sheet across their skin. The babies swallow the first mouthfuls of gelid, mineral-rich fluid, and their gut biomes shift, accepting the autotrophic bacteria that will allow them, later, to survive on sunlight fractured through crystal and the occasional sip of dissolved silicate.

They eat, when they eat, **aerofauna milk** and **condensed vapor**. The milk is harvested not from mammals but from the slow, golden swarms that orbit the peaks. The Vertical keep **resonance hives**, ceramic spheres embedded in the growing crystal that vibrate at frequencies attractive to the metallic-winged creatures. When the swarms descend to feed on the mineral dust sheared by the accretion, the Vertical move among them with nets of molecular monofilament, gathering the excess **substrate-paste** that the creatures excrete. This paste, refined through the aerofauna's impossible digestion, is spread on bread made from lichen flour. It tastes of lightning and deep earth, and it keeps them tethered to the growing continent; without it, their bones would lose density, their teeth would crumble, for they are, in essence, digestive extensions of the mountain itself, recycling the refined stone back into their architecture.

The adults are **temporary**. This is the central psychological burden and gift of the Vertical. They know that the city-state where they are born will not exist—not as it is—in twenty years. The crystal grows; the Urban Nodes must shift. They develop a relationship with permanence that outsiders find schizophrenic: they are obsessive archivists, carving minute histories into the stone faces that will be buried by next season’s growth, yet they are also ruthless discarders, abandoning entire districts without grief when the accretion rates exceed the phase-shift infrastructure’s ability to compensate. They make **memory offerings**, pressing their foreheads against the crystal and allowing the formation-energy to disrupt their short-term recall, clearing space for the present. A Vertical citizen carries only about five years of detailed memory; beyond that lies a haze of geological time, impressions of color and infrasonic tone rather than narrative.

They work as **accretion shepherds**. In teams of three—**Anchor, Vector, and Chorus**—they rappel down the crystal faces (or up, or across) with tools that resemble tuning forks made of frozen sound. They listen to the **basal chord**, the subsonic drone that shakes the continent’s roots, and they identify dissonances: places where the crystal is growing too fast, threatening to shatter, or too slow, creating weakness in the continental lattice. The Anchor stabilizes the gravity seed; the Vector strikes the crystal with her tuning fork, injecting phases of matter that accelerate or retard growth; the Chorus hums counter-resonances, harmonizing the local geology with the continental hum. They are not building a static world; they are conducting an orchestra of emergence.

Their dead are not buried but **suspended**. When a Vertical’s heart ceases—usually around the age of forty, when their silicate-laden blood finally crystallizes in the ventricles—the body is carried to the **Exhalation Points**, high pinnacles where the aerofauna swarm in thickest density. Here, they are left seated in meditation poses, coated in a paste of lichen spores. The metallic creatures come, not as scavengers but as celebrants, settling upon the corpse with their alloy wings beating at blurring speeds. They do not eat the flesh; they eat the **mineral memory**, the stored silicates, the calcium. They metabolize the Vertical’s accumulated lithic essence and, in return, they secrete a specialized substrate that bears the chemical echo of the deceased’s consciousness. This excreta is collected by the mourning parties, mixed with wet stone dust, and used to **seed new crystal growth**. Thus, the dead do not return to earth—they never reach earth, for there is no earth here, only the upward journey—and instead become the architecture of tomorrow’s peaks. A Vertical child grows up sleeping in walls that contain the refined dreams of her ancestors, hearing in the subsonic hum the specific resonance of those who came before.

They speak in **pressure-language**, modulating the density of their breath to create pockets of sonic variation in the heavy, wet air. A conversation sounds like a slow release of steam, a sequence of exhalations that travel upward or sideward depending on the intended recipient. They have no words for "foundation," "rooted," or "stable." Instead, their vocabulary is rich with **gradients of becoming**: *ak-kresh* (the moment when a crystal face emerges from cloud cover), *vel-sorran* (the specific grief of watching a familiar spire grow past the angle where light refraction creates green), *thi-om* (the comfort of being between orientations, when gravity has not yet reasserted).

At night, when the sun’s refraction creates stationary rainbows that stand like portals between the spires, they gather on the exposed facets, weightless in their gravity seeds, and practice **falling meditation**. They release the seeds and drift in the cold, wet air, suspended between the weeping stone and the metallic clouds, listening to the infrasonic chord that vibrates in their teeth and their pelvises. In these moments, they are not individuals but **extensions of the Scaffold’s intent**, temporary consciousnesses granted to the mountain so that it might know itself as it grows. They feel the pain of separation, the violence of emergence, the patient wetness of condensation, and they know—they have always known—that they are not the inhabitants of a place, but the place’s method of **witnessing its own birth**. They are the Scaffold’s nervous system, firing once, twice, a thousand times across the crystal, and then going dark, making room for the next layer of becoming.
