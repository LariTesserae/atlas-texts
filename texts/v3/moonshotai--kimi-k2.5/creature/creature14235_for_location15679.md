The first thing you must understand is that they do not sleep, because they have never been awake in the manner that surface creatures understand wakefulness. They exist in a state of **resonant suspension**, their metabolisms keyed to the Hum like instruments held permanently in a chord. To them, the subsonic pressure is not background noise but the primary sense organ—a vast, shared skin of vibration that blankets the Vault and everything in it.

**The Calcifiers** are the oldest residents, though "resident" suggests a choice to inhabit that they never made. They are the sessile megafauna—each one a colony organism the size of a small building—anchored to the bedrock beneath the biocrust. Their bodies are layered fossil-cities in themselves: concentric growth rings of calcium carbonate interlaced with living tissue that resembles both neural ganglia and geological strata. They have no faces, only **orifices** arranged in fractal patterns that inhale mineral dust and exhale architecture. Each spire, dome, and archway is not merely built but ** grown with intention**—the Calcifiers extrude their towers with the same biological imperative that a human heart pumps blood, but on timescales where a meter of growth represents a decade of concentrated will. They are aware of one another through the Hum; when two towers arch toward each other across a boulevard, it is not accident but a slow-motion courtship, a geological flirtation that takes centuries to consummate in a touch of calcified fusion.

Moving among these living pillars are the **Perambulators**, the bear-sized grazers whose shells serve as mobile ecosystems. Their carapaces are not smooth but gardens—concave landscapes of rust-colored lichen and piezoelectric algae that drink the Hum directly, converting vibration into sugar. The Perambulators scrape the fungal mats with radula like polished obsidian, but they do not digest their food quickly. A single mouthful might take a month to process, the nutrients leaching into their systems at the rate that stalactites drip. They are somnambulists, their nervous systems operating on delayed impulses; when they decide to turn, the message takes hours to travel from their primitive brains to their limbs. They navigate not by sight—the dim phosphorescence is barely sufficient for that—but by **tactile geomancy**, their underbellies sensitive to the density of the stone-eating carpet and the proximity of the Calcifier spires.

Then there are the **Unlanders**, the proteinaceous insects that never touch ground. They are the closest thing the Vault has to birds or angels, though they resemble transparent kites more than either. Their wings beat at roughly one stroke per minute, a languid waving that keeps them aloft in the dense, cool air. They have no lungs, only diffusion membranes that extract trace minerals from the atmosphere. They do not flock; instead, they maintain rigid **station-keeping** positions in the twilight air, drifting only when the Hum fluctuates in pitch. They are information gatherers, their bodies festooned with fine cilia that read the chemical signatures of the ecosystem—detecting a stressed Calcifier miles away, or the hormonal precursor of a mating Perambulator. They broadcast this data via pheromone trails that descend like gossamer, visible only as faint disturbances in the dim cyan glow.

Beneath it all, threading through the lush biocrust, is the **Mycelial Congress**—the true intelligence of the Vault, though it lacks anything resembling a brain. The fungal mats are not separate organisms but a single distributed entity that has achieved **speculative thought** through sheer biomass and time. Its cognition operates at the speed of stone: a notion that might occur to a human in a flash takes the Congress fifty years to complete. It thinks in chemical gradients, in the migration of phosphorus and the crystallization of mineral salts. When the Perambulators graze, they do not simply eat; they are **read** by the Congress, their biological data harvested, analyzed, and stored in the pattern of hyphal networks. The Congress is the record-keeper of the Vault, its memory written in the density of the mats, spanning epochs. It does not communicate with the fauna in any direct sense, but it influences them—triggering blooms of specific lichens to guide the grazers toward areas needing pruning, or releasing compounds that stiffen the Calcifiers' secretions when architectural stress is detected.

The **Mason-Worms** and **Avenue-Beetles** are the immune system and maintenance crew, respectively. The worms are pale, segmented creatures that burrow through the calcite spires like paths through bone. They secrete enzymatic mucus that re-dissolves and re-precipitates calcium carbonate, healing cracks that form when the Hum shifts key and the tectonic strain redistributes. They are symbiotes of the Calcifiers, living within their towers like benevolent cancers, and they perceive the world through **piezoelectric taste**—the electric potential generated by stressed stone tastes different to them depending on whether it threatens structural integrity.

The Avenue-Beetles are larger, tortoise-slow, with shells that have fused into living bulldozers. They patrol the grand boulevards not from any sense of duty but because their digestive systems require the specific mineral profile of the exposed bedrock beneath the biocrust. Their grazing creates the roads; their excretions pave them. They are territorial but their disputes are glacial standoffs that last decades, two beetles face-to-face, their chemical signals contesting ownership of a plaza while actual decades pass and dust settles on their unmoving carapaces.

And finally, the **Dissolvers**—the predators, though the term implies a violence alien to their method. They are amorphous, jelly-like beings that lack fixed form, drifting through the lowest levels of the biocrust like slow liquid. When they encounter a Perambulator or a sick Avenue-Beetle, they do not attack. They **adhere**. They spread over their prey over the course of weeks, months, years, their cells secreting enzymes that dismantle the victim's biology one protein at a time. The consumption is a merger; the Dissolver incorporates the memories encoded in the prey's mineral traces, gaining something like experience from the devoured. There is no panic in the prey—the Perambulator continues its grazing even as its hindquarters dissolve, the nervous system shutting down in sections, the mind fading not in terror but in a slow diffusion of self into the consuming other.

What unites them all is their relationship to **time**. They do not age; they **accrete**. A young Avenue-Beetle is simply a smaller version of an old one, its shell thinner by mere millimeters. They do not reproduce in bursts but in geological punctuation—the Calcifiers releasing spores only when the Hum drops below a certain threshold, an event that occurs once every several millennia, sending a blizzard of potential architecture drifting through the heavy air to settle and begin the ten-thousand-year process of becoming.

They have no names. They have **positions**—coordinates in the Hum, chemical signatures in the Congress, stresses in the calcite. They have no history because nothing is ever lost; the Mycelial Congress remembers every Perambulator that ever crossed the basin, their paths still readable in the compressed strata of the fungal mat. They have no future because they exist in the eternal present of the grinding plate above, a moment stretched to infinity.

To meet one's gaze—if they had gaze to meet—would be to commit to a conversation that lasts centuries. To touch one is to initiate a chemical dialogue that will not resolve in your lifetime. They are the final expression of a world that has slowed until it achieved a kind of permanent stasis, neither alive nor dead but **lithified life**, creatures of the deep breath held, the stone that learned to notice itself, the hum that became a home.
