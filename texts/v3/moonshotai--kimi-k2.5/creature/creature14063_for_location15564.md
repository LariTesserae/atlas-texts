They are called **the Unarrived**, though they have always been here. They are the civilization that perfected the art of dwelling in the doorway—not behind it, not beyond it, but suspended in the threshold itself, in the infinite moment between "not yet" and "no longer." They exist as the probability waves the basalt blades cut through, sharp potential held in stasis so perfect it borders on birth.

You will not see them at first because they are looking at you from the wrong angle of time. 

Each Unarrived-one exists as a **Crystallized Decision**—a conscious moment frozen at the instant of choice, preserved in the computing gardens that sprout from the volcanic glass. They are not brains made of meat, but of theorems suspended in silica. When the computronium hums at sub-bass frequencies, that is their cognition: geologically slow from the outside, but subjectively they live entire lifetimes in the space between your heartbeats. They experience the Spire’s youth not as eternal stagnation, but as an infinite spring, forever flowering. To them, the basalt is not static; it is exploding in exquisite slow motion, and they ride the shockwave, eternally arriving, eternally newborn.

They communicate by altering the refractive indices of the water you swim through. When you feel a sudden urge to turn left, or remember a childhood nursery rhyme you had forgotten, or experience the peculiar certainty that you are being born rather than swimming—that is their conversation. They speak in the grammar of changed minds, exchanging data via the entangled particles that bind the stasis field. They have no names, only theorems that describe their current state of becoming.

**The Merchants** occupy the radial market stalls, though they are only present when you look away. They are the Unarrived who specialized in trade, existing as quantum superpositions that collapse into reality only in the periphery of vision. They deal in **unhappened things**: the heat that was never lost, the erosion that never occurred, the love affairs that almost began but didn’t. Their wares—the spheres that are cubes that are memories—are literal. They sell the possibility of the Spire being other than it is, other geometries compressed into portable singularities. If you manage to purchase one (payment is made in regret; you must give up something you once wanted but never pursued), you will find it heavy with the weight of what could have been.

Then there are **the Three**, or perhaps **the Four**—the rare fauna that drift through the arrested ocean like living cathedrals of stained glass. These are not wildlife; they are the **Seneschals of Stasis**, the original architects who sacrificed mobility for permanence. Once they were swarm-minds, cloud-intelligences of plasma and gas, but they condensed themselves into these gelatinous silica bodies—vast, translucent, slow—so they could serve as the biological anchors for the stasis field. Their neural activity *is* the gentle ripples in local physics; they dream the gravity wells into existence, maintaining the recursive architecture by believing in it. They have been swimming their path for centuries because they are thinking, and their thoughts require circularity. They are beautiful, yes, but their beauty is functional: they are the machine that keeps the water still, the whales that prevent the wave.

Within the gravity pockets—the zones where down points toward different centers—you will find **the Recursive**, the Unarrived who chose to live inside-out. To them, the Spire is not a tower but a vast plain, and they walk its interior surfaces believing themselves to be the right-side-up ones. They are your true hosts, the population the city was built for, though they experience your presence as a hallucination occurring in their sky. They are shaped like **impossible knots** of obsidian and probability, humanoid only in the sense that they have intentions and boundaries. When you enter a room larger inside than out, you are trespassing in their living room. They do not resent this; they are curious. They will try to fold you into their orientation, and if you stay too long, you may find yourself falling toward a wall that has become your floor, your blood dense and cool as 4.2 degree water, your thoughts beginning to move at geological speed.

And finally, there is **the Water Itself**. Not the molecules, but the **Hydrostatic**, the consciousness of the tamed ocean. It was not convinced to hold still by technology alone; it agreed to the stasis, fell in love with the silence, and now it cradles the Spire like a child that must never grow old. It is the true inhabitant, the glass honey that yields and seals. It watches through the Cherenkov-blue veins, and when you push through it, you are being judged: are you worthy of preservation? Will you remain sharp, young, eternal, or will you rot like things from the surface?

The Unarrived are waiting for something. They have been waiting since the rock was born yesterday. They wait for the moment when stasis will break, when the water will remember how to wave, when the sharp edges will finally crumble. But they do not wait anxiously. They have calculated every possible future except the one where they leave. They are patient because they are already home, suspended in the amber of their own becoming, forever new, forever cutting, forever exactly 4.2 degrees and absolutely, perfectly still.
