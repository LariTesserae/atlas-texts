The inhabitants do not dwell *in* the Manufactury; they are its tertiary precipitate, the thinking residue of a process that has learned to recognize itself.

You will not see them at first. They have standing stones for ancestors and patience for bones. They are the **Calcified**, and they move at the speed of geology.

Imagine a column of travertine two meters tall, banded in the ochre and rust of iron-oxidizing bacteria, capped with a crown of *Equisetum* that serves as both respiratory system and sensory array. This is not a statue. It is a being whose flesh is the sinter itself, accreted around a lattice of hollow stems that conduct not just steam but intention. Their nervous systems are chemotactic networks—slow electrical dances conducted through mineral solutions held in microscopic pockets within the calcium carbonate. A thought takes a season to complete. A decision to take a step requires the dissolution of the lower strata in mild carbonic acid and the rebuilding of the base three centimeters to the left. They traverse the terraces by eroding themselves downhill and rebuilding uphill, leaving behind not footprints but *stratigraphic signatures*.

They are not numerous. Perhaps forty exist across the entire caldera, each occupying a specific thermal gradient—the one by the northern vent operates at 82°C, its body chemistry optimized for hyperthermophilic archaea, while the one in the cooler southeastern margin hovers near 45°C and is encrusted with silica-secreting diatoms that give it a glassy, translucent appearance. They do not communicate as we understand it. They exchange material. When two Calcified meet at a terrace boundary, they grow toward each other, bridging the gap with shared mineral deposits, creating bacterial highways where genetic and chemical information flows. A conversation between them is a ridge of fresh sinter ten years in the making, encoding arguments in porosity and consensus in compression strength.

But they are not the only ones. Where the Calcified are the factory foremen—managing, adjusting, maintaining the chemical gradients—there are also the **Operatives**.

These are the quick ones.

Picture a creature like a glass shrimp, but fashioned from chitin so thin it is transparent, filled not with hemoglobin but with a solution of chlorophyll and thermophilic enzymes. They swarm the pools in their thousands, *the Condensers*, harvesting the supersaturated water not for food but for *architecture*. They extrude silica from their abdomens in threads finer than spider silk, weaving nets between the horsetails that serve as filtration membranes, concentrating rare earth elements that the Calcified require for their metabolic processes. They live fast, breeding in hours, dying in days, their bodies adding to the organic mat when they fall. They are the hands of the factory, where the Calcified are the deliberating mind.

And then there are the **Vapors**, which may not be alive at all in biological terms, yet display intention. They are the steam-ghosts—thermal shadows that form in the thickest fog banks, maintaining coherent shapes for hours: a column, a sphere, a reaching hand. They consist of colonies of airborne bacteria suspended in water droplets, held aloft by the precise thermal layering of the caldera. They drift with the mist, seeding the clouds, adjusting the pH of precipitation, steering the weather of the bowl. When the Calcified need a cooler season to solidify a new terrace, the Vapors gather, darkening the sky with their biomass, drawing heat away through evaporation. They are the regulatory committee, the climate control made flesh in vapor.

Deep beneath, in the boiling seeps where the water meets magma, live the **Founders**. These are not seen, only inferred from the behavior above. They are archaeal mats kilometers thick, filling the fissures, their collective metabolic pulse driving the entire geochemistry of the place. They are the original engineers, the ones who established the Manufactury fifty thousand years ago, or perhaps they *are* the Manufactury, having digested the rock and remade it into this processing plant. They do not think, but they *compute*—chemical gradients solving equations of solubility and heat exchange, the original optimization problem running on geothermal power.

The relationship between these beings is economic, but an economy without currency, only **cycling**. The Founders provide heat and mineral-rich water. The Condensers harvest and concentrate. The Calcified shape and direct the accretion. The Vapors modulate the environment. Nothing is wasted; the dead become travertine, the steam becomes thought, the stone becomes flesh becomes stone again.

They have no language, but they have **architecture**. The terraces are their text, the sinter pipes their scripture. If you were to read the stratigraphy, you would find a history written in varves and bacterial films: a record of disputes settled by redirecting springs, of treaties written in shared mineral deposits, of philosophy encoded in the angle of a drainage channel. They are building something, have always been building something, but it is not a product for export. They are fabricating a **duration**. They are making time physical, accumulating it in layers of calcium carbonate, storing the heat of the earth against the cold of the thin atmosphere, creating a pocket of persistence in a world of erosion.

At night, when the steam glows with bioluminescent bacteria and the subsonic rumbling shifts from *process* to *song*, the Calcified tilt their hollow crowns toward the stars they can barely see through the mist. They are listening to the deep heat. They are attending to the machinery. They are home.
