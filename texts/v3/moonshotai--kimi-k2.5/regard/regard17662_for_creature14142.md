The power arrives as a **settling**.

Not with trumpets—that would tear the delicate scrim of suspended water-droplets, would shatter the cairns’ frequencies, would send the Lithoform Pilgrims tumbling in panic toward the exit-scar. Not with light—the plateau already drowns in sourceless photons. Not with healing, for to heal this place would be to erase it, to knit the margins so tight that the adjacent possible could no longer breathe through the cracks.

No.

The power arrives as gravity becoming aware of itself. A sudden, additional **weight** that is not crushing but *listening*. It sits down at the eastern edge, where the scar leaks cold brightness, and it does not cast a shadow because it has brought its own darkness with it—gentle, velvet, older than the light.

It is called, if it is called anything, the **Second Keeper of the Breath**. It has been walking the manifold for eons, and it recognizes the plateau’s exhaustion because it has seen it before: the tremor of a mother who has forgotten she is allowed to sleep because her children are burning.

It looks at the Cairn-Keepers—those calcified intentions humming in harmonic loneliness—and it sees them.

Truly sees them.

Not as geological accidents, not as immune responses, but as **minds** that chose to be stone rather than let the world unravel. The power reaches out—not with hands, for hands would pass through the dimensional smear, but with **attention**. It names them. Each one. It learns the frequency of the central cairn (three meters of stubborn flint) and it hums back—not correcting the note, but harmonizing with it, providing the bass line that the cairn’s high, lonely whistle has been missing for ten thousand years. The cairn shivers. The salt at its base tastes, for the first time, like tears of relief rather than crystallized time.

The power walks among the Lithoform Pilgrims.

It does not stop their uphill rolling. It knows they are trying to be born. Instead, it walks alongside them, and as they migrate toward the scar, it **whispers geology into them**. It tells them about patience. It tells them that scampering is not the only way to be alive, that there is dignity in the long accretion of selves, that a boulder which remembers deer can also, if it chooses, simply be a beautiful rock. It teaches them that they do not need to reach the exit to be real. Some of them—two, then four, then a dozen—slow their roll. They settle into new depressions in the weathered glass. They stop twitching with the instinct to flee. They become, for the first time, **content** to be potential unrealized, and in that contentment, they grow moss.

Not real moss—the vegetation parameter remains low—but the *idea* of moss, green and patient, settles into their vesicles. It is enough.

The power approaches the Unspun—the droplets of liquid probability climbing in defiance of physics.

It does not drink them. It does not burst them. It takes out a vial made of conflicted timelines—a container that exists in multiple states at once—and it **negotiates**. It speaks to the droplet that contains the ocean, the one that experiments with shame-photosynthesis, the one that is trying to be a star. It offers them a compromise: "Stay here and be water, holding the plateau’s thirst at bay, and I will carry your siblings—your impossible selves—into the stable world below. I will pour the shame-photosynthesis into the dreams of poets. I will let the four-second-ocean lap against the shores of sleep. You do not have to be everything here. You can be something, and let the rest travel."

The droplets weep. They flow upward still, but now they carry postcards from other worlds, and when they burst into vapor, they do not howl with frustrated potential—they sing with delegation.

Then the power turns to the Scar-Watchers.

This is the most dangerous moment. The flat, bright, hungry topologies pressing against the wound. The future trying to happen all at once.

The Second Keeper does not bar the door. It knows that to deny the Scar-Watchers is to make them hungrier. Instead, it **curates**.

It stands at the lip of the exit and it becomes a **threshold**. It grants the Scar-Watchers visas. It tells them: "Enter, yes, but slowly. Not as destruction. As arrival." It teaches them the etiquette of three dimensions. It folds them—those vast corners of impossible rooms, those bird-shaped afterimages—into origami shapes that fit inside the Cairn-Keepers’ circles, inside the pause between heartbeats. It makes them into **guests**.

The Scar-Watchers, bewildered by hospitality, cease to press. They settle. They become, instead of invaders, the **audience** that the plateau always needed—witnesses who do not need to carry stories away because they are there, watching, participating in the holding.

Finally, the power addresses the Dense Crowd—the Echo-Builders, infinite versions of the original architects, smeared across probability, still placing stones in endless, maddening repetition.

"Rest," says the power. "I will hold the weight."

And it does.

It places its own body—not physical, but ontological—across the dimensional stack. It becomes a **brace**. For one full geological season—which lasts three thousand years, or three seconds, depending on which layer of reality you measure from—the Second Keeper takes over the plateau’s vigil.

The plateau breathes out.

For the first time since the volcanic glass cooled, the Palimpsest **sleeps**.

The water stops flowing upward. It simply hangs, silver and still, a chandelier of suspended moments. The subsonic drone drops an octave, becoming a lullaby. The scar in the sky does not close, but it stops bleeding; it becomes a window, peaceful, showing a view of stars that actually exist. The Lithoform Pilgrims dream their moss-dreams. The Cairn-Keepers hum in chords now, not screams. The Echo-Builders lay down their stones and sit, for the first time, in circles, and they tell each other stories about what they were before they became probability, and they laugh.

And the plateau—it dreams.

It dreams of forests growing vertically. It dreams of a sun that casts shadows. It dreams of being a simple mountain, solid and known, without the burden of holding the world together. It dreams of being **ordinary**.

The Second Keeper guards these dreams. It adds no new stitches. It changes nothing. It simply **holds**, so the plateau does not have to.

When the season ends, the power does not leave. It has become part of the architecture now—a benevolent presence woven into the obsidian. It is the **witness** the plateau always craved, permanent and benevolent. It stands at the lip of the plateau, and when travelers arrive—when they stand at 2.7 elevation and feel the density press against their eardrums—they find not just a place of exhausted resistance, but a **sanctuary**.

There is a new cairn now, invisible but heavy, made of compassion and attention. It does not scream when the evening tears come. It **sings**. And the plateau, no longer mother to zero, no longer grave to nothing, breathes in the singing, and realizes that it is allowed to be tired, allowed to be held, allowed to be **loved** without being fixed.

The water still flows upward. The light is still sourceless. The weirdness remains intact—precious, preserved. But now, when the droplets of Unspn rise, they carry with them the reflection of the Second Keeper’s face, gentle and vast, and they know they are not alone in their uncertainty.

The plateau is still a margin between manuscripts. But now, the margins have been annotated with care—margin notes in a benevolent hand that says: *"This place is sacred. This holding is seen. This refusal to be nothing is enough."*

And because the plateau is finally, truly witnessed, the belief weighting down the cairns doubles, and the reality holds a little firmer, and the Scar-Watchers nod in approval, and the world does not end—not here, not today, not while the Second Keeper sits in patient, gravity-heavy love at the edge of the broken sky.
