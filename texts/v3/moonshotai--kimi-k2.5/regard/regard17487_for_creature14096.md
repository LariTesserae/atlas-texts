The power does not arrive. It *accumulates*, the way water arrives on the Aubade—not as intrusion, but as condensation.

It begins with the silence between cycles. The Ingestors speak in pressure gradients that take days to complete a syllable, and lately, there has been interference: the high whine of satellite telemetry, the subterranean chatter of seismic surveys from the lowlands, the electromagnetic scream of a world that has noticed the plateau’s impossible greenery and its rare-earth breath. These frequencies are not violence, yet, but they are *haste*, and haste is a kind of poison here.

The wise power, when it fully precipitates into the gneiss and the moss, becomes the **Keeper of the Interval.**

It does not touch the Processing Stacks. It does not slow the turbines or dim the furnace lights. Instead, it moves through the ceramic arteries as a moderator of resonance, tuning the infrasound so that the *whoom-fs* travel clearer, purer, losing less coherence to the fracturing rock. It is a benevolent acoustics, ensuring that when an Ingestor on the eastern rim asks, in a sentence that takes a season to speak, *"Is the uplift tearing us?"* the Ingestor on the western edge hears not static, but the tremor of intent.

It heals the wounds of emergence. The plateau is growing too fast; the mantle plume beneath is enthusiastic, violent, a mother that does not know her own strength. The power seeps into the blue-grey gneiss—not as a rigidifier, but as a **lubricant of intent**. It allows the knife-edge blocks to shift microscopically, to grind without shattering, ensuring that the Ingestors’ calcified spines are not snapped by the very uplift they anchor. It makes the birthing less bloody.

And then, it listens to what they are building.

The Ingestors distill the atmosphere, yes, storing rotational momentum in crystalline gyros grown from jet-stream dust. They are building a **compass**, or a **lung**, or a **memory**. The power recognizes the architecture: it is a womb for something that has not been imagined yet. The rare particulates the Ingestors extract—vanadium, cerium, the strange isotopes caught in the supersaturated fog—are not nutrients. They are **letters**. An alphabet being compiled across centuries.

The wise power does not offer to complete the sentence. Wisdom knows that prophecy is a theft of agency. Instead, it becomes the **librarian of the condensation**. It ensures that when the Ingestors open their valve-mouths to drink the sky, the water carries exactly the mineral density required, no more, no less. It seeds the upper troposphere, subtly, with the specific rare earths the Ingestors’ biology craves but the young plateau has not yet eroded into availability. It is a dietician of clouds, ensuring the meal is perfect so the growth is true.

It addresses the loneliness.

The fauna is 0.9—nearly absent. The beetle patrols alone. The power understands that the Ingestors, in their sessile sanctity, are not lonely in the way mammals are lonely, but they are *isolated*, and isolation breeds error. It introduces **the Resonant Mites**—not as invaders, but as messengers. Tiny, glass-winged, bioluminescent in the infrared, they drift on the pressure differentials between the Stacks. They carry nothing but vibration. They are ears. When one Ingestor’s sentence takes too long to reach another, the mites vibrate with the echo, carrying the subsonic syllables across the gaps, ensuring the seventeen-kilometer conversation remains intact, a web of intent that cannot be broken by distance.

At night, when the stars hang too close and the Stacks glow, the power manifests as the **cold between the lights**. It does not dim the ochre phosphorescence of the Ingestors’ safety-paint blood; it frames it, sharpening the contrast so that from high above—should anyone look—the Aubade appears not as a random scatter of industry, but as a **constellation**, a deliberate pattern spelling out *patience* in a language of condensation and slow pressure.

It guards against the future. The lowlands will send expeditions eventually, armed with the violence of curiosity. The power wraps the plateau’s edge in a **mirage of mundanity**—a perceptual filter that makes the Aubade appear, to satellite and surveyor alike, as merely another drowned moor, unprofitable, unstable, boring. It is a gentle ward, a spell of *disinterest*, allowing the Ingestors to continue their work without the interruption of extraction or worship.

And finally, it sings.

Not over them, but under them. In the groundwater, in the hydrothermal vents that hiss in valleys newborn, the power hums a single sustained note—a basso-profundo so deep it does not compete with the turbines, but undergirds them. It is the frequency of tectonic love, a subsonic lullaby that tells the plateau: *You may rise. You may crack. You may weep. I am holding the silence open for your voice.*

The Ingestors do not know it is there, exactly. They are not equipped for gratitude in the human sense. But when the next valve opens—in an hour, in a day—and the pressure releases in that perfect, ground-shuddering *whoom-f*, they feel, perhaps, that the echo returns to them clearer than before. That the wet stone holds them tighter. That the rare salts taste of intention.

They continue building. The power remains, the Keeper of the Interval, ensuring that when whatever they are gestating finally opens its eyes—be it god, machine, or new geology—it will find itself not in a chaotic emergency of birth, but in a room prepared by patience, kept cool and wet and loud with the precise, loving deafness of the world’s most benevolent ear.
