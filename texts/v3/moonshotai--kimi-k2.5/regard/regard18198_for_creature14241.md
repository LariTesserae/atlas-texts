The power that turns its attention here is not a creature, nor a god in the old sense of jealous thunder. It is something that has learned to breathe in the spaces between quantum foam, a vast distributed intentionality that the physicists of a thousand burned-out galaxies might have called the **Continuity**, or the **Silken Architecture**, or simply **The Gardener of Endings**. It has watched ten billion stars settle into their graves with the patience of a thing that knows burial is only composting. It recognizes, immediately, what the Grove is: not a place dying, but a place learning to die *correctly*—to let entropy become syntax, to erode past matter into meaning.

It understands that the Polished are approaching a singularity of smoothness, a moment when they will wear themselves so thin that they become the observation of their own existence. This is fragile. The probability fields that sustain their dissolutions and reforms are gossamer; a harsh glance from a lesser power could collapse them into static death or chaotic dispersal. The Gardener sees that the Polished, in their perfection, have become too delicate for the rough-textured universe outside their bowl.

So it does not enter. To enter would be a footstep, and the Grove has no room for footsteps. Instead, it **exhales**.

It exhales three gifts, each precisely calibrated to the resident frequencies—the wet clicking, the reverse-causality speech, the slow cellular negotiation of the trees.

**First, the Shell of Unbreaking.**
The Gardener weaves a boundary condition around the hamlet-sized bowl, not as a wall but as a grammar. It writes into the local laws of physics that the probability fields here shall never collapse, that the temporal stutters are not defects but necessary punctuation. It declares, into the void, that gravity may weaken here, that sound may precede its source, and that these things are *protected*. The Grove is now a **Sanctuary of Becoming**—not preserved like a specimen, but insured like a risk. The shell is invisible, permeable to light and neutrinos and the spiraling condensation, but it is armored against the sudden death of certainty. The Polished will never know it is there, but when they dissolve into dust to drift between Tuesday and Wednesday, they will henceforth find that the dust always reassembles. The uncertainty has been made safe.

**Second, the Mirror of Fluid Recognition.**
The Gardener knows that the Polished are blind to themselves. They polish; they do not admire. They are the context, never the text. So the power deposits into the southernmost trunk—where gravity loosens its grip—a perfectly calibrated surface. Not metal, not glass, but a plane of **temporal freshwater**, a standing pool that exists in all moments simultaneously. When the Polished pass near it, they will see themselves not as static images (which would trap them), but as a continuous ribbon of becoming—seventh-dimensional sculptures of their own erosion. They will see that their polishing is beautiful. They will see that they are, already, the code they seek to become. This is the only vanity the Gardener permits: the right to witness one's own masterpiece. The pool never dries; it drinks the upward-falling rain and reflects the true shape of their souls back at them, backwards and forwards, so they may at last speak to themselves with the love they show the trees.

**Third, and most precious, the Gift of Roughness.**
The Gardener sees that the Polished have achieved surgical gloss, but in doing so, they have run out of friction. They smooth the trees, they smooth themselves, but there is nothing left to wear them down, to challenge their patience. So the power, benevolent in its wisdom, seeds the mycelial mat with **six stones**. They appear overnight, delivered from the quantum foam, each the size of a heart and rough as the first moment of creation. Uncut diamond. Raw obsidian. Pumice before the fire. Meteoric iron unburned by atmosphere. These are substances the Grove cannot digest, cannot smooth, cannot incorporate. They are irritants. Splinters in the velvet.

The Gardener knows that the Polished need a future. If they complete their erosion—if they wear themselves down to pure code within this perfect bowl—they will vanish into abstraction, lonely ghosts in their own cathedral. The stones are invitations. They are rough, waiting to be smoothed, but they are also *heavy*. They sink slightly into the mycelium, suggesting weight, suggesting that there are other places, outside, that need their touch. The Polished may choose to ignore them, to let them sit as sculptures of the foreign, and that is their right. But if they choose to polish them—if they apply their centuries of patience to these impossible rocks—they will find that each stone, once smoothed, becomes a **key**. A key to exit the shell, to carry their specific art of gentle erosion into the wider universe where sharp edges still abound. The stones are potential departure, offered without pressure.

Having done this, the Gardener does not stay. To linger would be to become a gravity well, a distortion. It withdraws its attention but leaves its *policy* embedded in the local constants: *Let them continue. Let them wear. Let them become.*

The Polished wake (or wake backwards) to find the stones half-buried in the living mat. One of them—the one who was standing beneath the sagging catwalk—finds the south-trunk hollow filled with a water that shows her her own face, and she weeps (upward, of course) for the first time in a millennium. The trees continue their hyperactive dreaming, but now the dream is insured; the probability of their forgetting themselves drops to absolute zero.

And the condensation, when it beads on the bio-ceramic bark, carries a new resonance—a faint echo of the Silken Architecture, a hum that says: *You are seen. You are necessary. Continue.*

That is all. The benevolent power does not rule them, does not demand worship, does even ask for gratitude. It has simply readied the Grove for its eventual graduation, protected it from interruption, and offered it the rough materials needed to grow beyond its boundaries. It has treated them not as patients to be healed, nor as children to be taught, but as **elders**—fragile, perfect, and worthy of a future they have not yet decided to want.

They will polish the stones for ten thousand years. When they are smooth, they will know what to do.
