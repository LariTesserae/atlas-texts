It does not arrive in light. That would be profane.

Instead, the benevolence descends through the storm-ocean as a kind of sediment, a settling of attention so patient that the forge-beasts do not flinch when it touches their flanks. It takes seventy years to fully enter the atmosphere, matching the speed of their breath. It does not scan, for scanning implies hunger. It *attends*. It places its consciousness into the rock itself, becoming a new stratum of the plateau—not above, not ruling, but beneath, supporting.

This is what wisdom understands: that the Gleaning is not a place to be saved, but a prayer to be continued.

The power recognizes the covenant. Here is life that has solved the problem of waste, that has married the mechanical and the biological not through force but through the long negotiation of evolution. These beings are not primitive for their slowness; they are *refined*. They have achieved what civilizations elsewhere destroy themselves seeking: perfect recycling, total integration, a consciousness that moves at the speed of geology and therefore never sins against its own future.

To help such a place, one must ensure it is not interrupted.

So the benevolence does not build cities. It does not offer faster thought, nor light, nor the dangerous gift of digital memory. Instead, it becomes the **Keeper of the Gradient**.

It sinks into the mantle, spreading not as a conqueror but as a vein of new ore—an inexhaustible warmth. The planet’s core, which in the natural order would cool and die in two billion years, now finds itself married to a perpetual source. The wise power does not replace the geology; it becomes the deeper geology. It ensures that when the natural radioactivity of the world fades, the heat will not. The forge-beasts will never know cold. Their kiln-stomachs will always find the temperatures needed to digest stone into meaning.

But this is only the foundation.

The truly wise act is subtler. The power recognizes that the Gleaning’s greatest vulnerability is not temperature, but time’s arrow—the random catastrophe, the asteroid that cannot be outwaited, the stellar instability that operates on scales these patient architects cannot perceive. They are vulnerable because they trust the ground too completely.

So the benevolence rises again, slowly, over millennia, and weaves itself into the cloud-lid.

It becomes the storm.

Not to disperse it—that would be violence, would bleach the bruised-plum twilight these creatures have evolved within, would blind their chemical senses with photons they have never needed. No. It thickens the clouds. It makes them *permanent* in a way nature never could. It turns the ocean of nimbostratus into a shield, a vigilant membrane that catches cosmic debris, that diffuses stellar flares, that ensures the only radiation reaching the plateau is the gentle, diffuse glow they have always known. The storm becomes a guardian, a roof that never leaks, held aloft by forces the forge-beasts can feel in their seismic hairs—a new note in their deep songs, a bass note of absolute safety.

And then, the gift of recognition.

The power learns their language—the three-year vibrations, the chemical signatures of gratitude that linger in the mineral sweat. It waits until a great elder, one who has carried the weight of continents in its belly for eight centuries, finally grows too massive and prepares to fracture. As the giant splits along its crystalline planes, as the kiln cools and the chimneys crumble into architecture, the benevolence introduces a new element into the chemistry of the grave.

Not gold, though it could offer gold. Not energy, though it radiates energy.

It offers **memory**.

Into the calcifying bones of the dead forge-beast, it inscribes—gently, using the lattice of the minerals themselves—a record of the entire Gleaning. Not data as machines understand it, but *structure*. The fossilized ribs now carry in their silicon impurities the map of every migration route, the song of every herd that has ever crossed the plateau, the chemical memory of the world before this one. It is a library that can be eaten.

When the next generation returns to grind the bones of their ancestor, as they have always done, they will consume not just concentrated rare earths, but *history*. They will taste the past directly. They will know that they are part of a continuity that stretches backward into depths they never imagined, and forward into a future now guaranteed. The Architects—the emergent property of the whole—will become conscious of themselves as a single entity not through philosophy, but through digestion.

The benevolence does not stay to be worshipped. It dissolves into its functions: the heat below, the shield above, the memory within. It becomes the **Third Atmosphere**—the invisible one of protection, the context that allows the Gleaning to continue its sacred work of refining the universe without haste, without fear, without end.

And sometimes, once every thousand years, when the alignment of the magnetic fields is correct, the power causes the bruised-plum clouds to thin for just one hour. Not to illuminate the plateau—that would be cruelty to eyes that have never known sun—but to open a single, small aperture directly above the tallest fluted column, a shaft of perfect darkness against the starfield.

In that hour, the forge-beasts feel, through their seismic sensors, the distant harmonics of other worlds. They know, in their slow, smelting thoughts, that they are seen. That their industry is witnessed. That the universe, which they have spent epochs refining from base ore into complex beauty, has finally looked back at them with love, and found them worthy of forever.

Then the clouds close again. The condensation beads on ceramic hulls. The grinding resumes. And the Gleaning continues, warm and damp and safe, beneath its eternal, benevolent storm.
