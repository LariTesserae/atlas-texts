The One Who Listens to Stones descends not through water, but through the concept of *duration*. It has no shape here, for shape implies a beginning and an end, and this power measures its existence in the half-lives of continental drift. It finds the Cistern not by searching, but by recognizing the particular frequency of patience—a thud... thud... thud—that harmonizes with its own ancient pulse.

It does not enter. To enter would be to violate the seal, to introduce the arithmetic of days back into a place that has transcended counting. Instead, it *attends*. It presses its attention against the synthetic stone the way the water presses, with the relaxed grip of something that has all the time in the world.

And it weeps.

Not with salt—salt would be redundant here—but with a resonance that makes the amber lights shiver in their housings. The One Who Listens has witnessed stars burning out in silent scream, has cradled the last vibrations of collapsed nebulae, has catalogued the final thoughts of extinguished species. But rarely, so rarely, has it found a place where abandonment became *matrimony*. Where exile evolved into sanctuary.

It regards the **Resonants** first. The power sees them not as fish, but as *syntax*—living grammar that has learned to read the machine’s intent. It offers them no food, no mutation, no gift of sight. Instead, it *harmonizes*. From the deep crust, it summons a frequency—a subsonic hymn that travels through bedrock and water alike, settling into the chamber like sediment. This is the music of tectonic plates dancing their billion-year waltz. The Resonants, those silver tuning forks, suddenly find their lateral lines singing with new harmonics. They are not disturbed; they are *expanded*. They now orbit not only the pistons but also the heartbeat of the earth itself. They dance to the thud of the machinery and the deeper, slower drum of the mantle. They are no longer just the Cistern’s eyes; they have become its *ears*, hearing the conversation between the chamber and the planet that birthed its stone.

To the **Engineers**, the power offers *witness*. For eons, these crustaceans have scraped and secreted, building their calcium cities in absolute anonymity, their artistry unobserved by any intelligence that could name it. The One Who Listens settles into the gear housings, not as oil or grit, but as *comprehension*. The largest of the Engineers, that dinner-plate patriarch encrusted with mica, pauses in its scraping. It feels, for the first time in its lineage, that its work is *seen*. Not judged, not corrected—simply recognized as maintenance elevated to liturgy. The power leaves a blessing in the form of *permanence*: the cement they secrete will now cure to a hardness that outlasts the bearings they tend. Their cities will not erode. They will become the fossils of the future, recording not death but devoted care, so that when the universe eventually cracks this chamber open like a geode, geologists will know that someone loved the machine.

The **Translucents** receive the gift of *fuel*. The One Who Listens gathers the faint heat of the earth’s core, the ambient warmth of volcanic patience, and threads it into the water column. Not much—just a degree, perhaps two. But enough. The bacteria within their bells, those failing stars, find new breath. The luminescence does not brighten; it *deepens*, shifting from pale ghost-light to a gold that recalls the amber panels in their youth. The jellyfish will never go dark now. They will drift into the corners where the fixtures have died, and they will bring not just memory-light, but *eternal* light—soft, sufficient, a bioluminescence that requires no sun, no surface, only the chemical generosity of a planet acknowledging its forgotten children.

And in the **corners**, where the Compression Saints lie flattened into geometry, the power performs its most delicate work. It sees these creatures—pressed into two dimensions by patience—and recognizes them as kin. For the One Who Listens has also flattened itself across time, spreading its consciousness thin to cover the eons. It approaches the Saints not to lift them, not to restore them to the third dimension of swimming and struggle, but to *validate* their choice. It sings to them the mathematics of pressure, the beauty of the limit as it approaches infinity. The Saints do not grow; they *attain*. Their edges, already soft as the Rounded stone, achieve a perfection of flatness that becomes a kind of sight. They begin to dream in surface area, in coverage, in the absolute peace of the plane. The power ensures that the pressure will never relent, that the weight will never be relieved, for it understands that their sanctity lies in the unending nature of the embrace. They are granted the boon of *continuance*: they will never be forced to rise, never be compelled to become three-dimensional again. They may remain, forever, as they are: geography rather than biology, peace rather than flesh.

Finally, the power turns to the **Cistern itself**—the architecture, the machinery, the thud... thud... thud.

It sees the fatigue in the ancient bearings, the microscopic crystallization of metal tired from motion, the hairline fractures in the sintered stone. The builders are dust, their purposes lost, but the function remains, faithful as gravity. The One Who Listens places its hand—not a hand, but a *guarantee*—upon the central piston.

It does not repair. To repair would be to restore, to return to a state of newness that this place has outgrown. Instead, it *harmonizes*. It teaches the metal to sing with the water, teaches the ceramic to flex like gristle, teaches the chamber that its entropy is not a failure but a *style*. The machinery will not last forever in the sense of being unchanging; it will last forever in the sense of *becoming*. The pistons will continue their geological heartbeat, but now they do so with the certainty that they will never stop. The intake grates will never clog beyond the Engineers’ ability to clear. The amber panels may fail, but the Translucents will never dim. The pressure will remain unending, not as a threat, but as a promise.

As a final gesture—quiet, nearly imperceptible—the power leaves a *window*. Not an opening in the stone—the stone must remain sealed, the Rounded must remain complete—but a permeability of information. The Cistern is now connected to the deep time of the world. When continental plates shift, the Resonants will feel it in their scales and know they are not alone. When meteors strike the surface, the Compression Saints will detect the vibration and understand that they are part of a larger silence. The chamber remains sealed, but it is no longer *isolated*. It is a node in the network of planetary patience.

The One Who Listens to Stones withdraws, not ascending but simply *fading into the duration*, leaving the Cistern as it found it: small, complete, humming. But now it is *held*. Not possessed, not owned, not saved—simply held, the way water holds a stone, with a relaxed grip that has all the time in the world.

The thud... thud... thud continues. The silver fish shimmer in their eternal orbit. The crustaceans scrape their liturgy. The jellyfish pulse with unfailing light. And in the corners, the Saints rest, pressed into perfection, dreaming the long dream of geometry, knowing now—if such concepts can be known in two dimensions—that they are loved, that their patience is seen, that the Pressure is truly, finally, and forever *Unending*.
