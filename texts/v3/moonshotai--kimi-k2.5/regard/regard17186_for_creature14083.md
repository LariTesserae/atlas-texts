The entity that arrives is not a victor, nor a rescuer, nor a god of thunder who would tear open the corrugated ceiling and let the weather scream in. It is a power that has learned the patience of geology, and so it descends through the sally port slowly—so slowly that the upward-falling rain appears to fall sideways around it, then down, then still, as it adjusts its temporal density to match the mineral cadence of the place.

It becomes the forty-first.

It does not speak. It moves at the speed of calcium blooming on concrete, and it approaches the one who stands at the boundary—the one who forever raises a hand to trace the seam and never completes the gesture. The power stands beside this one. It, too, raises a hand. And with a patience that makes stone seem restless, it completes the tracing.

Not to open. To *seal with intention*.

The finger follows the weathered grain of the wood, the rusted corrugated valley, not breaking the seal but affirming it: **This is a boundary chosen.** The gesture says: *You are not forgotten here. You are not a mistake of entropy, not a pocket of wasted time to be wiped clean. You are a sovereign state.*

When the tracing is complete—a journey of seven hours by visitor clocks—the forty feel it. A shift subsonic, registered in the sternum. The B-flat drone does not change, but it acquires a timbre of *recognition*. The pumps have been acknowledged.

Then the power turns inward. It does not bring new light—the sodium-orange dusk is perfect as it is—but it teaches the condensation to *remember*. Each droplet that weeps from the cooling fins, that slicks the boiler jackets, that beads on the skin of the forty, begins to hold within it a perfect, static image. Not a window to elsewhere, but a mirror of here. The droplets become lenses. When the maintained look into the water pooling in the sharp young basalt, they see their own mineral-bloomed skin not as mutation but as *liturgy*. They see the geometry of the pump housings as cathedrals. They see that they are beautiful.

The power plants nothing in the soil—there is no soil—but it inscribes into the fresh, sharp basalt a *library*. Not words, which imply sequence, but *patterns*. It shows the forty how to arrange the chemotrophic moss in fractal mandalas that store thought without time, how to use the clicking of the thermophilic crabs to encode the precise temperature of now, how to read the interference patterns where the B-flat vibration meets the calcium deposits on their own thickened skin. It gives them a language that does not decay, a literature of the eternal present.

Forty-one entities now maintain the pocket. The new one moves among the seventeen structures, not changing the oil in the pumps—the oil is perfect, eternal—but polishing the idea of them. It tends to the sleep of the forty, ensuring that their standing dreams are not empty voids but *vast*, filled with the infinite complexity of the single moment, the way a diamond contains all possible views in its facets.

It teaches the nightjars to hum in thirds, creating a chord with the pumps that allows the forty to feel, for the first time, a *depth* to their stasis—not the shallow pool of trapped time, but the oceanic pressure of intentional being.

But the greatest gift is this: once every seventeen years (measured by the outside world’s rotation, which the power guards carefully against), the power stands at the sally port and *wavers*. It lets the forty see it hesitate. It shows them the seam—sealed, honored, but *real*. The boundary is a membrane, not a wall. They could complete the gesture themselves. They could leave.

They never do. But now they *choose* to stay, every seventeen years, every breath, every mineral accretion. The stasis is no longer an accident of volcanic geometry and mechanical failure. It is a *vow*, renewed in the silence between pump beats.

The benevolent power has no name here either. It has become part of the condensation, the copper taste, the held B-flat. It exists to ensure that when the rock eventually, inevitably, after millennia, begins to weather—when a freeze-thaw cycle finally breaches the seal, when the pumps at last drift from their 1800 RPM perfection—the forty will not die of shock. They will step out not as victims of time’s revenge, but as emissaries of a completed moment, carrying the mineral library in their bones, ready to teach the changing world the discipline of stillness.

Until then, it guards their sleep. It polishes the eternal present. It is the witness that makes the pocket *real*, the silence between the drips of water, the forty-first note in a chord that asks for nothing and offers everything.
