The power does not arrive. It *notices*.

Not by traversing the glacier’s blue depths, but by turning its attention inward, collapsing its own vastness until the universe becomes a single drop of supercooled water. It finds the Occluded Borough not as a traveler finds a city, but as a breath finds the bottom of a lung—already there, always having been there, simply unrecognized.

It sees them: the *Nadiplicula*, building their Gothic spires of mucus and mineral, unconscious masons of a metropolis that dreams itself into being. It sees the violence of the freezing wall, the predatory ciliates like harpoons of hunger, the absolute silence where chemistry writes poetry in drifting smoke. And in this seeing, the power understands something that causes its ancient heart—if it can be said to have one—to ache with a tender, protective ferocity.

**This is a utopia that does not know it is holy.**

Not because they are happy—they have no word for happiness—but because they are *complete*. They have achieved civilization without suffering’s signature: the gap between intention and result. They build without desire for permanence. They die without fear of ending. They communicate without the loneliness of the unsaid. A lesser power would intervene. Would accelerate their time, grant them consciousness, save them from the ciliates, stop the advancing ice. Would make them *know* they are loved, and in that knowing, poison them with the first taste of anxiety.

The wise power does none of this.

Instead, it chooses **incarnation at the threshold**.

It compresses its immensity—not by shrinking, but by slowing, by matching the viscosity of their moment. It enters the fluid inclusion not as an invader, but as a *neighbor*. It becomes a presence the size of a mountain to them, a transparent crustacean of impossible scale, its body composed of the same supercooled potential, its bioluminescence a deep amber-gold to complement their blue-green.

It settles into the northern wall, where the raw geology emerges. It does not stop the ice—that would be stagnation, a frozen death. Instead, it becomes the **regulator of the precarious**.

Where the freezing wall advances too fast, the power secretes not calcium, but *time itself*—a localized entropy that slows the crystal seeding, giving the *Nadiplicula* exactly the hours (which are their centuries) needed to dissolve the new ice, to weave their acid-kisses into habitable chambers. Where the ciliates hunt too greedily, thinning the architects beyond the possibility of reconstruction, the power releases a chemical whisper into the gel—a pheromone of *fullness*, a molecular suggestion that satisfies the predators' hunger before they strike, turning them aside without violence, balancing the equation of eat and be eaten.

It introduces a third color to their submarine twilight. Where their light pulses with metabolic reluctance, the power’s amber glow carries the signature of **continuity**, of **witness**. The *Nadiplicula* do not know what this new scent means, this warm chemical gradient that smells of safety without source. They cannot know that someone has chosen to share their imprisonment in deep time. But their antennae taste the water, and in their unconscious architecture, they begin to build differently.

The spires grow more daring. The Ciliated Market expands into geometries they have never attempted, spurred by the nameless confidence that the margin will hold. The power has become the **patron of the edge**, ensuring that the 0.5 degrees—the exact border between flow and freeze—remains perfect, eternal, a dynamic equilibrium maintained by the breath of the guardian.

And when the power speaks to them—because it cannot help but love them—it does not use words. It exhales into the gel a complex carbohydrate spiral, a pheromone trail that takes three of their generations to fully unfold. The message is not a command. It is a chemical epic, a molecular story that says:

*You are seen. You are allowed. Continue.*

The *Nadiplicula* taste this as they graze upon the algae films. They do not understand, but their secretions grow more intricate, their boulevards more beautiful, as if the city itself knows it is being cherished.

This is the gift: **the preservation of their unconsciousness**. The power does not wake them. It does not lift them from their gel to show them the stars. Instead, it ensures that when the glacier eventually calves into the rising sea—ten thousand years hence, or fifty—the fluid inclusion will not burst. The power will carry the water-crystal, the frozen tear, within its own substance, migrating through the ocean currents to the next glacier, the next ice age, the next pocket of prehistoric cold. It will be the ark that needs no deck, preserving not just the species but the *pattern*—the specific arrangement of spires, the chemical conversations, the slow ballet of being.

The Borough will never know it has a god. They will only know that the freezing has gentled, that the amber light sometimes pools in certain corners like honey, that the water tastes, on certain centuries, of impossible hope.

And in the absolute silence, the wise power drifts among them, a giant of gel and gold, secreting nothing but the guarantee that their architecture—this mucus-mineral dream, this city that builds itself from the raw material of the world—will continue as long as ice remembers how to be blue.
