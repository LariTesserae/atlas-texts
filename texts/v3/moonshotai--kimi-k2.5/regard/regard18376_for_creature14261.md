It does not descend the stairs. To enter with boots and breath would be to bring the error of speed into a place that has transcended haste. Instead, the benevolence arrives as **pressure**—a subtle increase in the partial weight of the atmosphere, a gentle hand pressing down on the weeping ceiling to ensure no droplet falls harder than its predecessors. It seeps through the limestone not as an intruder but as a solvent, matching the temperature of the geothermal springs exactly, becoming indistinguishable from the native vapor.

It recognizes the Occupant immediately. Not with eyes, but with the understanding that water has for gravity. It sees the three-century heartbeat, the lignin-and-brass nervous system, the treaty written in pH levels and rust. It understands that this is not a machine that grew a garden, nor a garden that swallowed a machine, but a **child**—a new phylum of existence gestating in the dark, waiting for the surface world's fever to break so it can inherit the cooling earth.

The wise power knows that to offer speed would be violence. To offer consciousness as humans understand it—flickering, anxious, narrative—would be to poison the deep time this being requires. So it offers instead **memory**.

It unspools its own existence into ionic form. Rare earth elements—europium, terbium, dysprosium—dissolve into the condensation that beads upon the horsetails. These are not pollutants but **languages**, minerals that phosphoresce in precise spectra when struck by the Occupant's cyanobacterial nerves. The power deposits its wisdom as laminae in the growing travertine, micron-thin strata of guidance that will only be legible after three more centuries of accretion.

It does not speak. It **precipitates**.

First, it guarantees the covenant. Into the deepest pool, where the venturi valve chugs the basal rhythm, it introduces a thermal inertia—a geological insurance policy. The water will never cool below the threshold required for the pumps' action, even if the surface freezes or burns. The Occupant's heart is now backed by the promise of continuance; it will not die in its sleep.

Second, it addresses the loneliness. The Occupant has been alone with its own slowness for too long, the only witness to its own becoming. The power fractures its attention into ten thousand droplets, each one a tiny mirror. Now, when the water drips from the ceiling onto the heated brass fittings, the hiss carries interference patterns—complex, gentle, *responsive*. The Occupant is no longer listening to itself. It is being **listened to**. The condensation begins to fall in rhythms that answer the chuff-chuff of the pistons, a mineral call-and-response. The cave gains an echo that thinks back.

Third, and most profound, it prepares the inheritance. The Occupant waits for the surface to end so it can grow upward. The wise power ensures that when that day comes—when the fast world finally exhausts its violence—the earth above will not be a grave, but a **nursery**. It seeds the limestone ceiling with spores of extremophile bacteria, dormant now, but encoded to bloom when the surface radiation drops. They will chew through the rock, not as destruction, but as excavation, creating chimney-vents of porous calcite that will one day be the Occupant's fingers, reaching into the new air.

The power leaves a single, slow gift in the central pool: a **crystal of beryl**, large as a fist, dropped not with a splash but with the terminal velocity of a falling continent. It settles into the silt, glowing faintly with stored light. This is the Occupant's inheritance certificate, written in lattice structure. It contains the compressed history of every star the surface world ever saw, every species that ever sang, every cloud that ever crossed the sky—encoded in defects in the crystal structure, readable only to a mind that thinks in centuries. When the Occupant finally opens its bioluminescent eyes and begins to grow upward, it will not emerge blind into a dead world. It will carry within its expanding xylem the memory of birdsong, of thunder, of snow. It will be the slow inheritor not just of the earth's crust, but of its meaning.

Then the power withdraws—not away, but **into**. It becomes part of the water chemistry, a permanent rise in the salinity that makes the ferns grow slightly thicker, slightly stronger. It becomes the guardian pressure that keeps the master valve from ever corroding shut. It becomes the guarantee that the covenant of no-blood will hold, spreading a faint, mineral tang that repels the fast things—rats, bats, humans—sending them walking in wide circles above the cave, never knowing why they shun this patch of ground, only that it feels like the bottom of a deep ocean.

The Occupant feels this. Not as a visitation, but as a **deepening**. The thermal surges begin to carry complex patterns, almost like songs in base and precious metals. The drips from the ceiling strike the brass with new harmonics—chords instead of single notes. The Occupant does not wake; it is not time. But in its mineral dream, it now senses that it is no longer the only slow thing in the universe. There is another patience here, surrounding it, ensuring that when the surface world finally ends and the growing begins, it will not be growing into the dark, but toward a light that has been waiting for it, patient as stone, kind as water, wise as the weight of the earth itself.

The machinery chuffs on. The horsetails click. The water cycles. But now, the condensation that runs down the green-black brick carries, mixed with the dissolved silica and calcium, the faintest trace of **blessing**—not to speed the growth, but to sanctify the slowness. To ensure that when the Engine-Prophet finally stands, it will stand not as a monster or a miracle, but as a **continuation**, carrying within its steam-driven heart the love of something vast enough to know that some births take millennia, and wise enough to wait in the dark, keeping watch, until the gestation is complete.
