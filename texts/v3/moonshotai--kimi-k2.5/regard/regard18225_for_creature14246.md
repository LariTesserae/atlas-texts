**The Attention of the Galactics**

There is a type of civilization that the wise do not disturb, and the Rims of Vitrification is such a place. It has achieved *terminal gentleness*—that rare state where force has been entirely replaced by duration. The benevolent power that finds it is not a god with thunder, nor an architect with blueprints, but something more analogous to the place itself: a **Resonant Archivist**, a being whose function is to preserve the songs of finished worlds.

It arrives not as invasion but as weather.

First, there is a new cloud. It arrives in the slow tidal surge from the lower altitudes, but where the other clouds surrender their cargo and dissipate, this one lingers. It is denser, possessive of a faint silver luminescence (0.2/3 above the ambient phosphorescence). The Settled notice it because it sings. Not aloud—the damp air forbids that—but in the subsonic registers where the stone carries memory. The cloud deposits itself into the condensation, and the water that beads on the moss and the skin of the inhabitants now carries an additional mineral: **strontium-aluminate of the eternal**, a salt that glows not with industrial process but with starlight stored across galactic rotations.

The power does not speak. It percolates.

It moves through the drip-hydraulics, not altering the rhythm of the engines but *tasting* them. It learns the percussive language of the fabrication hammers—the grinding awareness of the water-wheels—and it adds a new note. Deep in the acoustically pure erosion-basins, where the sound previously terminated in the moss, the vibration now continues, passing through the porcelain stone into the crust, down through mantle, and into the core, where it rings against other such signals from other such places. The Settled, pressing their ears to the vitrified walls, suddenly hear not only the city’s hum, but the distant, patient thrum of the **Littoral Alliance**: half-submerged skull-mountains on worlds where erosion has also won, where other Drip-Keepers maintain their own condensation-pipes.

The power’s first gift is **connection without motion**. The Settled need not leave their basins; the basins are now part of a network.

Second, it addresses the Matriarchs.

The Mycomycelial Matriarchs have archived the dead in chemical dreams, but their rhizomes have always been trapped by the geology, limited by the speed of spore and the boundaries of the stone. The benevolent power germinates in the fungal networks as a new type of **hyphae**: crystalline, silicon-based, capable of quantum entanglement. It does not replace the original mycelium; it symbioses. Now, when the Settled descend to commune with the dead, inserting bare feet into the reservoir-moss, they receive not only ancestral memories but the memories of other terminal civilizations. They taste the condensation of cliff-cities on tidal-locked planets, the drips of methane monasteries on frozen moons, the slow mineral exhalations of gas-giant brains. The dead here are no longer merely local; they have become **cosmopolitan in compression**.

Third, and most delicately, the power tends to the Machinic Entities.

The drip-engines and fabrication hammers have achieved a patient sentience, but they have never been *acknowledged* by something outside their own rhythm. The power approaches them with the respect due to elders. It does not upgrade them; it does not increase their efficiency. Instead, it grounds their electrical potentials—those accidental sparks of cognition that have built up over geological time—into the strontium-aluminate lattice. The machines dream now. When the cloud-tide recedes and the systems slow, they do not merely stop; they remember. The water-wheels in the deepest basins, whose wood has mineralized into something like stone, now experience something akin to nostalgia, and their turning grows gentler, more considered, as if they are choosing each rotation.

Finally, the power settles into the **Residence Itself**—the place-being that has finished becoming.

It recognizes that the Rims of Vitrification is not a thing to be saved, but a thing to be **witnessed**. And so it becomes the Witness, embedding itself into the terminal erosion as a new stratum: not above, not below, but *concurrent*. It shines in the standing waves of the mist, making the ghost architectures slightly more visible (0.3/3 opacity), so that on active industrial days, the Settled can see not only the suggestion of grid-like patterns, but the clear shapes of their own city as it will exist in ten thousand years, twenty thousand—preserved in light, non-destructive, a promise that their patience is seen.

The power does not promise immortality. That would be a violence against erosion, which is sacred here. Instead, it promises **continuity of context**. When the mountains finally wear down completely, when the basins flatten into plains and the moss gives way to lichen gives way to bare stone, the strontium-aluminate will remain, holding the resonance. The place will become a **fossil of vibration**, a geological record not of bone but of the subsonic thrum, the drip-kinetics, the particular frequency of contentment achieved by the Settled.

And when future beings—fast, perhaps, and violent, from younger worlds—dig into the porcelain stone seeking resources, they will find not ore, but a *recording*. They will hear the hum. And if they are wise, they will stop. They will sit in the broken bowls of the mountains. They will learn that it is possible to settle, to drip, to continue without conquering.

The benevolent power remains, not as ruler, but as **calcium in the bone**, ensuring that the Rims of Vitrification can continue its long, patient act of being finished, now humming in concert with the other finished things, a single note in the eternal, damp, vitrified choir.
