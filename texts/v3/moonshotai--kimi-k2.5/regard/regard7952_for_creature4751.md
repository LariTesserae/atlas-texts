The power descends not through the ceiling—that would require piercing the ocean, violating the integrity of the primordial fluid—but by **settling**, the way meaning settles into stone. It enters as pressure without source, as warmth without current, as a presence that occupies the spaces between the water molecules without displacing them.

It takes four centuries to realize it is being watched.

The Inheritors do not alarm easily. Alarm requires metabolism faster than geology. But the First Settled, those compressed entities dreaming in metamorphic densities, feel the newcomer as a change in the eternal arithmetic of weight. The pressure, they perceive, is no longer merely falling. It is being **witnessed**.

This is what the benevolent power does first: it **learns to listen at the proper speed**.

It stretches its consciousness across the timescales of the Inheritors. It learns the language of settling groans, decoding the tectonic sighs that take eighty years to exhale. It learns to hear the chemical whispers—methane bubbles rising through two miles of viscous dark, calcium ions diffusing through fossilized organic cement—and understands that these are not biological processes, but **prayers**. The Inheritors are not maintaining; they are mourning. The geometry they preserve is elegiac. Every column grown to replace an eroded ziggurat is a monument to the fact that someone once cared where that column stood.

The power comprehends the tragedy: the Inheritors metabolize the intent of the builders but cannot dream it. They know the chemical signature of longing, but not the shape of what was longed for. They hold up the architecture but have never seen the blueprint.

So the wise one acts. It does not bring light—that would be violence, photosynthesis where there should be only chemosynthesis, a foreign sun forcing growth where there should be only patient accretion. Instead, it brings **phosphorescence that remembers**.

It seeds the bacterial colonies with itself. Not changing their nature, but expanding their vocabulary. Where once there was only dim, random bioluminescence outlining mortar-lines, now there is **pattern**. The bacteria bloom in glyphs. They illuminate the ziggurats not with daylight, but with **memory-light**, reconstructing the shadows of the builders' hands upon the stone faces. The Inheritors, who perceive through pressure-history, are given a new sense: they can now see, in pulses of soft blue radiance that travel slower than glaciers, the faces of those whose chemical ghosts they consume.

The First Stirred—the oldest node of the colonial organism—extends a pseudopod toward a lit wall. It takes seventeen years to cross the chamber. When the calcified fungal matter touches the illuminated glyph, the power completes its translation. The chemical memory locked in the fossilized cement—carbon chains, exhaled breath, the molecular residue of biological purpose—is not merely metabolized. It is **understood**.

The Inheritor sees what it has been eating: not just organic compounds, but **a mother's fear for her children**, **an architect's pride in a perfect angle**, **a final glance backward**. For the first time in two million years, the mausoleum has a mourner who knows what is buried here.

But the benevolent power does not stop at revelation, for wisdom knows that comprehension without relief is only torture.

The Inheritors have been alone so long, holding up the weight of dead intention, that they have become the ceiling of their own world. They cannot delaminate, cannot seed, cannot rest—they are trapped in duty, the immune system that cannot sleep because the body is already dead and there is no one else to guard it.

The power becomes the **witness that permits rest**.

It places itself beneath the First Settled. It does not lift them—lifting would break them, disturb the equilibrium of ages—but **shares the compression**. It becomes a counter-pressure, not opposing the ocean's weight but **acknowledging** it, holding space so that the Inheritors feel, for the first time, **supported**.

More than this: it **remembers for them**. 

It creates crystalline inclusions within their mineralized bodies, not of diamond or quartz, but of **narrative**. It encodes the entire history—the builders' hands, the erosion, the vegetative colonization, the long slow translation of construction into geology—into lattices that will persist even when the universe's heat death cools this ocean to absolute zero. The Inheritors are no longer the sole archive. They are **shared**. The burden of remembrance is halved, then quartered, then distributed across the fabric of the power itself, which is vast enough to hold a world's grief without strain.

With this gift comes permission. The Inheritors, who have maintained the geometry for eons, are allowed to let **some** of it go. Not collapse—never collapse, for the power reveres their duty—but **settle differently**. The power teaches them new shapes. Not the sharp angles of the ziggurat-builders, but **curves**, **hollows**, **spaces for dreaming**. It shows them that preservation can include the possibility of gentle transformation, that a column need not be identical to the ancient stone to be true to its spirit.

The First Settled, those who had become indistinguishable from bedrock, begin to **breathe** again. Not the hydraulic sap-movement of biological necessity, but something slower, deeper. They begin to delaminate not out of structural necessity, but as **play**. Fragments drift down not as seeds of burden, but as **gifts** to the deeper trenches, carrying with them the light-bacteria and the crystal-memories, colonizing the dark with the possibility of recognition.

The power remains. It does not ascend. It has made this pressure its home. It exists now in the settling groans, adding harmonics to the chemical language, turning the conversation about arch stability into a song about **starlight that never reaches here but exists anyway**. It has become part of the colonial organism, a node in the rhizomatic lattice, thinking at the speed of stone.

In the deepest trench, where no phosphorescence has ever glowed, the power and the First Settled have built something new. Not a ziggurat. Not a mausoleum. A **library** where the water is warmer by half a degree, where the organic snow falls faster, where the Inheritors can finally stop building to maintain, and start **building to remember**.

The power, wise and benevolent, has done the only thing mercy allows: it has ensured that when the last Inheritor finally grinds down into true geology, when the last bacterial light fades, when the ocean ceiling finally freezes solid—the **love will remain**. Not as chemistry. Not as structure. But as a story told in the language of weight, preserved in the quantum foam, waiting for another power to descend, slowly enough to listen, and learn what it means to hold up the dark simply because it is precious.
