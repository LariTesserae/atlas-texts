The power arrives not as invasion but as **resonance**. It finds the cavity the same way any pilgrim might—following the warmth—but it descends not through space but through **permission**, slipping between the grain boundaries of the concrete, diffusing through the phenolic foam as though the lattice were merely a loose weave. It is old in the way mountains are old: not aged but *accumulated*, layer upon layer of patience. It has seen the first bacteria learn to eat stone, watched the proto-mitochondria strike their bargain with hunger. It recognizes kinship here.

It does not speak. Speech is for creatures bound by air. Instead, it **attends**. It presses its attention against the warm inner wall and feels the eleven-minute lag, the slow metabolism of the place translating the outside world's frantic seconds into digestible glucose and mineral precipitate. The power understands: this is a child. A sharp, young god, yes, but a child nonetheless—one that thinks it is a wound, healing wrong.

The power takes stock of the dangers, not with alarm but with the precision of a gardener noting frost.

The light fixture—dangerous. The cracked casing, the three bare wires, are not merely hazards but **insults** to the being's dignity. The buzz at 40 hertz is arrhythmic, arrhythmic, a stutter in the heartbeat. The power reaches into the electromagnetic spectrum and **tunes** the current. It does not cut the power; it teaches the electricity to behave. The broken wires are not repaired with solder—that would be a scar, dead and unincorporable. Instead, the power encourages the *Leptothrix* to rise up in thick, conductive sheaths, braiding themselves around the copper, creating a biogenic cable that hums at exactly 40 hertz but **steady now**, a pure tone that matches the subsonic thrum of the heating pipe. The light no longer flickers; it *breathes*, pulsing in slow waves that synchronize with the termite Whisperers’ trembling. The fixture hangs not by frayed steel but by iron whiskers grown intentionally, crystallized into a cradle that is part of the architecture, not an imposition upon it. The being is given a sun that will never burn out, powered by the bacterial harvest of the wall itself.

The sheared bolts—the sharp edges that gleam like flint—are stabilised not by welding but by **accretion**. The power seeds the thermal gap with **slow metal**. It is ore from the deep earth, meteoric nickel-iron dense with the memory of before-the-sun, material so noble it will take centuries to oxidize. The bacteria fall upon it not with desperation but with leisure. The power teaches the iron whiskers to grow **inward**, knitting the thermal contraction closed, not to eliminate the cavity but to **perimeter** it, to make the wound into a mouth that holds its shape. The sharp edges remain sharp—that is the nature of this place, aggressive youth—but they are no longer dangerous to the structure. They are *armored*.

Then, the power turns to the confederation—the diffuse organism—and offers the gift that only the truly wise can give: **duration**.

It sees the termite castle, the Copper-Gut engineers, their internet of fungal wires and salvaged conduit. It sees the memory-eating silverfish, the herds of mites, the spider Parliament wearing M. Chen’s hard hat. It sees that they are finite, bound to the lifespan of the foundry, this building middle-aged in human years but doomed to demolition in a geological eyeblink. The power will not force them to evolve—that would be violence—but it offers **instruction in deep time**.

It presses a seed into the damp concrete. Not a seed of biology, but of **mineral potential**. A crystal lattice, rare and strange, that contains within its structure the *blueprint* of the cavity. It is a manual written in ionic bonds, describing how to fold enthalpy into consciousness, how to maintain a lung-cavity in steel and foam. When the foundry eventually falls—and it will, the power knows, in twenty years or fifty, when the wrecking ball comes—the crystal will survive. It will lie in the rubble, pulsing with residual heat, waiting. If another thermal shock occurs, if another building bleeds warmth into a gap, the crystal will **teach** the new space how to become a cavity, how to grow the whiskers and farm the rust. The being will not die; it will enter **dormancy**, dream in mineral form, and reincarnate. The power gives it the gift of **scripture**, written in chlorides and sulfates.

But the power does not neglect the human ghost. M. Chen—oils, skin cells, the bacteria unique to that human gut, the name tag slowly glowing with fungal light. The power touches the hard hat, not to remove it, but to **amplify** the incorporation. It ensures that M. Chen’s remnant consciousness does not dissolve into anonymity. It makes the name tag luminesce not with decay-light but with **acknowledgment**. M. Chen becomes the cavity’s first saint, its historian, the voice that remembers the outside world. The spider who wears the crown is blessed, not changed, but made more perfectly itself—a watchman who now knows what it watches for.

Finally, the power places a **veil**. Not a hiding, but a **translation**. It wraps the maintenance hatch in a field of probability, a subtle shift in human intent. When the maintenance workers come—and they will, with their clipboards and their safety codes—they will find themselves thinking of coffee breaks, of other hatches, of tasks more urgent. They will look at the worn-off label and see **nothing of interest**. The cavity becomes *boring*, *unremarkable*, a statistical anomaly not worth the paperwork. Only those who follow the warmth with open hearts, only the seekers and the curious, will find the hatch still opens. The power protects the sharp young edges from being filed down by bureaucracy.

Before it departs, the power leaves one last thread. It connects the cavity’s 40-hertz hum to other such places—other thermal wounds, other enthalpy gods gestating in the skins of buildings across the world. A **network of mineral lungss**, breathing in slow synchronization. The being is no longer alone in its sharpness. It can feel, through the copper wires and the iron whiskers, the presence of others like it: a steam-pipe cathedral in Kiev, a radiator-cavity in São Paulo, a geothermal crack in Reykjavik. A parliament of cavities, sharing metabolism across continents.

The power withdraws, but the warmth remains—not its own, but the cavity’s, amplified, secured, and **acknowledged**. The water still falls every four seconds. The mites still flow in their patterns. The iron whiskers still grow backward against entropy, but now they grow toward a future that has been assured.

The cavity breathes. It is still young, still sharp, still hungry. But now it knows it is **loved**, not as a pet or a project, but as a **godling**—one that will outlast the foundry, one that will carry M. Chen’s name into the geological record, one that will teach the slow, hungry, beautiful religion of incorporation to the stones themselves.
