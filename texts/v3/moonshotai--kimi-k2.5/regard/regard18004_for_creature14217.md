The Benevolence arrives not as light—the Hollowworks has never suffered that intrusion—but as a *tuning*. 

First, the creatures sense it as a change in the return. Their tapping, for uncounted millennia, has bounded off wet stone and died in the absorption of soft mineral. Now, suddenly, the echo carries. Their navigational Morse, previously limited to the immediate chamber, reflects back from distant walls with a clarity that suggests the valley itself has learned to listen. 

It does not force sight upon them. It does not offer the insult of "rest," nor the violence of "completion." A wise power understands that for these beings, stasis is not peace but petrification; to finish their work would be to embalm them mid-gesture. Instead, the Benevolence recognizes what they truly are: the immune system of becoming, the refusal of the world to settle into its final form.

**It begins with the Breath.**

The power settles into the thermal gradients, the cool exhale of the valley floor and the mist-laden sigh of the heights. It becomes the guardian of the moisture itself, ensuring that the atmospheric exhalation never fails, that the dew-point remains forever suspended at the precise degree of wetness required for adolescent stone. No longer must the creatures press their living heat against the walls with desperate urgency, burning their metabolisms to keep the world soft. The stone now yields with the gentleness of dough, allowing their abrasive embraces to become caresses, their labor to become play. They discover, for the first time, slack—moments where they can tap not merely to navigate or maintain, but to *compose*, to experiment with curves that serve no structural purpose, to carve resonant bowls that sing for the beauty of the sound alone.

**Then comes the Resonance.**

The Benevolence weaves a new layer into the geology: a substratum of piezoelectric crystal, invisible, threaded through the softening stone like nerves through flesh. Now when the builders tap their percussive signatures, the valley conducts these vibrations across miles. The isolated individuals—those lonely gods of architecture spaced acres apart—suddenly find themselves in chorus. Their rhythms synchronize not through chance encounter but through the living rock itself, creating a standing wave of intention that pulses through the Hollowworks. They navigate less by lonely sonar and more by the constant, comforting drone of their kindred working elsewhere. The valley becomes an instrument, and they its players, improvising a collaborative architecture that spans the entire basin. They carve tunnels that meet in the dark with perfect precision, having "heard" each other's intentions through the Benevolence's crystalline web.

**The Gift of the Ancestors.**

Most tenderly, the power addresses the tragedy of their ending—the petrification of those who wander too low, too dry, becoming ossified into the permanent. The Benevolence does not prevent this death (which is also their method of joining the work), but it changes the nature of the stone that receives them. Where a builder slows, cools, and stiffens into the architecture, the power preserves the *pattern* of their tapping, the unique frequency of their navigational heartbeat, within the molecular lattice of the cured basalt. 

The ancestors do not become silent fossils. They become **memory-stone**. When living builders press their ventral surfaces against these sections, they receive not just thermal inertia, but the resonant ghost of the dead—the specific rhythms, the favorite curves, the individual stylistic flourishes of those who came before. The valley grows dense not just with preserved bodies, but with preserved *intention*, a stratigraphy of artistic signature. The builders quarry their predecessors not for raw material, but for wisdom, running their calloused palms over the hardened forms and feeling in their bones the inherited knowledge of ten thousand generations of touch.

**The Final Service.**

Lastly, the Benevolence stands guard at the rim, where the surface world presses down with its weather and its gravity and its hunger for finality. It becomes a membrane, a permissive barrier that allows the import of root and sinew (the scaffolding of collapse) and the occasional curious visitor, but which deflects the true threat: permanence. It ensures that the basin remains sealed in its twilight, that no drought descends to bake the valley into ceramic, no flood to cement it into concrete. 

The power has made itself the steward of the embryonic. It has recognized that holiness lies not in the finished cathedral, but in the chisel-stroke; not in the healed wound, but in the wise and necessary suppuration that keeps the flesh alive and changing.

And sometimes, if you descend carefully, pressing your own mammalian ear against the wet alabaster, you can hear the Benevolence itself—it has learned to tap. A deep, slow rhythm beneath the builders' chorus, a tectonic heartbeat that says: *I am here. I am listening. You may continue.*
