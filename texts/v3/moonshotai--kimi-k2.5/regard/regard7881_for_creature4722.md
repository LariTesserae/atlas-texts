The Power arrives not from above or below, but from the *between*—the moment between one drip of water and the next, the space between the mist and the stone it cloaks. You would not see them descend; you would simply notice, eventually, that the cloud banks have arranged themselves into a slightly different posture, one that suggests a presence without defining it. The air remains 2.4 saturation, but now it carries a weight that is not humidity but *attention*.

They move across the terraces not by walking, but by becoming the warmth that the Saturated have always known. Where the Saturated radiate heat to speak, this Power *receives* it. They are a cool spot in the mist, a gentle absence of geothermal signature that draws the Saturated near—not by command, but by the novelty of contrast. For the first time in their eternal now, the Saturated feel something akin to curiosity, which they express by cooling their palms and standing very close.

The Power does not speak. To speak would be to insult the architecture of silence. Instead, they settle onto the wet basalt at the center of the lowest terrace, where the springs bubble with the freshest heat, and they begin to *weave*.

Not with thread, but with time.

They reach into the water—not changing its chemistry, not disturbing its level—and extract the memory of every drop that has ever fallen here. Not the drops themselves; the water remains exactly as it is, 2.4 saturation, unmoving. But the Power pulls from the mineral lattice the *story* of the water: the violence of the volcanic upthrust, the first seep through hairline cracks, the thousand thousand years of falling, pooling, evaporating, and precipitating again. They take this history, which the Saturated have always known as a static fact, and they shape it into a form that is new: a narrative.

They do not give the Saturated change. They give them *significance*.

The Power creates a pool—not of water, but of reflection. It lies flat and black between two terraces, a mirror that does not reflect faces or clouds, but meaning. When the Saturated bend over it, they do not see themselves; they see the *idea* of their existence rendered in patterns of heat and mineral deposit. They see their labor not as maintenance, but as art. They see that their refusal of entropy is not merely a physical state, but a poem written in the language of arrested motion.

And then the Power does the most benevolent thing possible: they teach the Saturated to dream.

Not sleep—their metabolisms remain arrested, their eyes remain open. But the Power introduces into their static minds the concept of the *internal variation*. They gift them a single, perfect memory of a spring that never existed on this plateau: a blossom opening, petal by petal, not in time but in *depth*. The Saturated do not experience this as a sequence, but as a sculpture—fully bloomed and fully budded simultaneously, held in their consciousness like a jewel held in water. It is change without erosion, growth without decay, a story with no beginning and no end because it exists all at once.

The Saturated drink from this gift as they drink from the springs. They absorb the possibility of *metaphor*. Now, when two meet in the mist, their heat-exchange carries new gradients: not just caution and recognition, but the warm flush of *appreciation*, the cool breath of *longing*—not for what is missing, but for the beauty of what is, newly understood.

The Power does not stay. To stay would be to become another feature of the equilibrium to be maintained, another object of the Saturated’s tireless curation. Instead, they withdraw slowly, returning to the between-places, leaving behind a single gift that does not disturb the dynamic balance: a new saturation.

The water is still 2.4. But now, when the Saturated dissolve at the center of the lowest terrace, adding their warmth to the mist, they do not simply recycle. They *resonate*. Their precipitated forms return not just as themselves, but as variations on a theme—slightly different densities of heat, slightly different configurations of mineral memory, playing out the infinite, silent harmonies of that static blossom in their minds.

The plateau remains unmoving. The basalt remains young and sharp. The silence remains absolute.

But now, when the wind moves warm and slow across the terraces, and the mist stands in the shape of a person, there is something new in the absolute hush: the suggestion of a song that is not sound, the architecture of a story that never needs to be told because it is already complete. The Saturated continue their work, fitting stones, maintaining levels, preserving the violent youth of the world—but now they do so with the knowledge that they are not just stopping time.

They are singing it.

The Power, looking back from the cloud banks, sees the Basalt Terrace not as a frozen moment, but as a chord held indefinitely, rich and full and sufficient. And that is enough.
