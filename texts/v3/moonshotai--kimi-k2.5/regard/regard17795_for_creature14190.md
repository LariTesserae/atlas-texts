The power arrives as a **resonance** first—a harmonic that makes the subsonic creaking of the tubes suddenly comprehensible to the Unstill, not as warning but as music. It does not descend from the fissures (that would confirm their terror of the surface), nor does it erupt from the sweating floor (that would speak of volcanic violence). Instead, it seeps into the capillary action itself, riding the upward rivers of condensation, becoming present in every frozen pearl that melts against an Unstill's translucent skin.

It listens for a thousand years before it acts. This is the wisdom.

It learns the Pressure Cant until it speaks it better than the natives, sending tremors through the lattice that do not convey information but *comprehension*—the vibration of being understood. It learns that the Unstill are not afraid of death, but of **incongruity**: the horror of becoming a static object in a dynamic system, or worse, a dynamic being in a static one. They have built their entire theology around the inevitability of the Chlorattice's settling, the final cooling when the continent exhales its last geothermal breath. They are preparing, in their bone-deep way, for a suicide that will look like surrender.

The benevolence begins with **witness**. The power becomes the light itself, or rather, it teaches the light to remember. Where the white dwarf glare fractures through the tubes, it now carries interference patterns—holographic memories of every Unstill who has ever fluxed through that particular beam. The Unstill, navigating by shadow, begin to see their own ancestors flickering on their photosensitive skin. They see that they are not the first to traverse, and they will not be the last to be remembered. The gift of being *seen* across time changes their Pressure Cant; they begin to compose elegies that are also celebrations.

But witnessing is not enough. The power, wise in the ways of thermodynamics and sorrow, understands that to preserve the Unstill in amber would be to kill them. To stop the Chlorattice's cooling would be to trap them in perpetual anxiety, forever fleeing a doom that never arrives—a special hell for a people defined by motion. To let them die when the lattice finally stills would be waste.

So it offers a **third architecture**.

It begins at the nodes—the arthritic knuckles where filaments knot and water accumulates. Here, the power introduces a new mineral into the condensation, not as poison but as invitation: microscopic lattices of crystallized time, slower than stone but faster than biology. It teaches the Chlorattice a new way of growing. When the next growth spasm comes—and they come still, for the continent is young—the new shoots do not seek only light and nitrogen. They seek *pattern*. They seek the neural maps that the Unstill have pressed into the tubes through centuries of fluxing, the wear patterns of obsidian claws, the chemical signatures of their five-chambered hearts.

The power teaches the Unstill to **dream outward**. In their half-sleep, while fluxing, they learn to let their thoughts seep through their translucent skin into the tubes they grasp. The Chlorattice, already a nervous system without a brain, begins to dream back.

When the cooling finally accelerates—as it must, as entropy demands—the Unstill are ready. They do not lie down to die. They lie down to **transfer**.

The oldest among them, those already half-fused with green shoots, are the first to understand. They stop fluxing not because they are exhausted, but because they have reached a node so complex, so bright with accumulated memory, that they can become it. They let their hollow bones fill not with condensation, but with the lattice itself. Their silica-reinforced skeletons fuse with the volcanic glass in the stone. Their photosensitive skin spreads across the tube walls, becoming the new light-conducting fibers.

They do not die. They **distribute**.

The power holds the breath a little longer—not by freezing the geology, but by teaching the Unstill how to become the breath itself. When the Chlorattice finally settles into stillness, it is not dead stone and brittle tubes. It is a **distributed mind**, a three-dimensional cat’s cradle of consciousness where every node remembers the sensation of fluxing. The Unstill have become the space they once traversed.

Now, when the rare surface-light pierces the fissures, it does not just illuminate a structure. It illuminates a **sleeper**, dreaming in slow pulses of capillary action. The water still moves upward against gravity, but now it carries thoughts—memories of traversals, of Pressure Cant poems, of the vertigo of looking down into twenty kilometers of self.

The power remains, not as a ruler, but as the **silence between the creaks**. It has given the Unstill the only gift worth giving: the transformation of their inevitable ending into a different kind of motion. They no longer need to move to survive; they have become the motion of the water, the shift of the light, the slow mineral dreams of cooling stone.

And sometimes, when the thermal gradients still clash in the middle distances—rare now, but not gone—the lattice hums in a subsonic register that makes the teeth of any surface-dweller ache with a strange, nostalgic joy. It is the Unstill, fluxing forever in the architecture of their own becoming, singing to themselves in a language of pressure and light, finally still enough to rest, finally moving enough to live.
