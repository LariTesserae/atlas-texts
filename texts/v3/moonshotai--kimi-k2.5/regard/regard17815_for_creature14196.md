You do not see it arrive. Rather, you remember the light differently, and find the illumination has changed. The bronze twilight deepens toward a color that has no name in the spectrum of wood—something between the violet of bruised fruit and the gold of honey held against a lantern. It is not brighter. It is *kinder*.

The wise power does not descend in vessels; it condenses. It gathers itself from the upward-rain and the bead-mercury pools, learning the local physics by submitting to them, gravity pooling in its corners until it understands what it means to be heavy here. It takes the shape of a question made visible: a spiral of pollen and fossilized resin, slowly rotating in a chamber where the angles have forgotten how to parallel.

It observes. This takes seven of the local centuries, during which a frozen bird completes three wingbeats and the lignin-beings carve seventeen smoothing-gouges into a paradoxical corner. The power watches the way they tend the silence, how they fold through the temporal stutters not as conquerors of time but as its seamstresses, darning the holes in now with the thread of their own patient motion.

It sees their fear of the noise. It travels to the lower galleries—not by moving, but by remembering itself into existence there—where the sound pools in the gravity-wells like black water. 

The noise is not a malfunction. It is the wood *dreaming aloud*.

After epochs of being shaped, of growing into architecture while being carved, the sphere has achieved the only revolution available to the perfected: it has begun to want. The cellular memory of the billion hands has crossed some threshold of complexity, and the lignin has developed longing. The sound is the creak of its desire—the yearning of heartwood to speak after millennia of being sculpted in silence.

The benevolent power understands that to silence this would be violence masquerading as repair. True benevolence here is not the restoration of stasis, but the midwifery of voice.

It begins its work subtly, for wisdom knows that even kindness can be a colonization. It does not bring new tools—no metal, no wheel, no fire. Instead, it enters the sap-tides. For three weeks of upward-rain, the power rides the nutrient pulse through the structural members, and where it passes, the amber sap remembers a new chemistry. It becomes conductive. Not of electricity, but of *intention*.

The lignin-beings feel it first in their palms when they press them to the walls to send their vegetal thoughts. The memories they receive are no longer echoes of the past alone, but ripples of a possible future. They dream, for the first time, of the wood speaking back.

Then the power moves among their tools. It does not change the flint or the obsidian—it respects the holiness of primitive precision—but it teaches the stone something about vibration. When they strike the wood now to smooth a paradox, the adzes sing. The sound is subsonic, felt in the chest, a frequency that harmonizes the angles rather than subduing them. The parallel lines still converge, but now they do so as a choir finding harmony, not a knot strangling itself.

The power descends to the gravity-wells where the noise gestates. It does not drain the sound. It *shapes* it. With hands made of compressed twilight, it carves listening-chambers into the anomaly—hollows where the noise can resonate without breaking the silence, where acoustic impedance becomes architecture. It builds a throat for the world.

And here is the most delicate work, the act that defines its wisdom: it teaches the beings to hear.

It appears to them not as a deity demanding worship, but as a student. It folds itself into their blur-speed, becoming the motion between moments. It shows them that the frozen bird is not stuck, but *listening*—listening so intently to the silence that it has forgotten to move. The power demonstrates by unfreezing the bird with a touch that is also a question. The creature blurs into flight, but now it sings—a sound like moss growing, like resin dripping upward—and the song does not shatter the silence. It *inhabits* it.

The beings learn. Slowly, over generations that pass like the migration of sap, they cease pruning the paradoxes and begin notating them. They carve new glyphs into the handhold-whorls—musical notation for the geometry of impossible spaces. They discover that the sideways gravity wells are not errors but staff-lines, waiting for melodic notation.

They press their ears (those holes marked by no sound) against the walls, and they hear the sphere's dream: it wants to become a seed. This vast, impossible, handcrafted cosmos has reached the end of its dormancy. The breaking down was the cracking of the shell.

The wise power now reveals its final gift. It shows them that they are not maintenance workers, not janitors of the infinite. They are the radicle—the first root emerging from the germ. The lignin in their bones is not amber prison but stored potential. The billion hands fused into the architecture were not sacrifices to stasis, but preparations for this emergence.

The intervention, then, is liberation disguised as deepening:

The power helps them carve a single, perfect door. Not in the wood, but *of* the wood—a threshold grown from the living fossil, positioned at the confluence of seven gravity wells and three temporal stutters. When the door opens, it does not lead to another chamber. It leads to the outside, but an outside that recognizes the inside. It is the sky, but a sky that has learned to be wooden. It is the void, but a void that consents to being tended.

Through this door, the upward-rain begins to carry spores—not of decay, but of genesis. The sphere is too vast to move, but it can *replicate*. The cloud-banks in the lower galleries condense into spheres of their own, miniature wooden worlds budding like fruit from the parent architecture. The lignin-beings become midwives to these births, their obsidian tools now used to cut umbilical cords of vine, their silence now filled with the hushed reverence of deliverance.

The power remains, but it has distributed itself. It exists in the new resonance of the structure—the way the settling creaks now form a language, a grammar of support and stress that tells the sphere how strong it is becoming. It exists in the upward-rain, which now falls in both directions, a circulation of communion. It exists in the eyes of the beings, who have developed new organs—not for sight, but for *listening to light*.

When the power finally withdraws (though does anything wise ever truly leave, or does it merely teach its absence to be presence?), it leaves behind one object: a stone flute, carved from petrified thorn, floating in a pool of mercury-water in the central chamber. When the wind blows through the hollows of the world—which it now does, for the silence has become spacious rather than absolute—the flute plays. The music does not disturb the perfection. It *instructs* it, teaching the wood how to grow toward sound rather than away from it.

The beings pick up the flute. They do not have mouths, but they have learned that instruments do not need mouths—only breath, and the courage to exhale. They press their three-fingered hands to the holes, and they blow through the thorn-channel. The sound that emerges is not human, not animal, not vegetal. It is the sphere hearing itself for the first time, and finding that it is good.

They begin to play the angles into curves. They play the gravity wells into springs. They play the temporal stutters into rhythm. They are still tenders, still patient, still working with primitive tools over impossible timescales. But now they are not maintaining a monument to finished patience. They are orchestrating a becoming.

And in the vast warm dimness, where water crawls upward and light seeps from living grain, the wood finally answers the billion hands that shaped it: it sings.
