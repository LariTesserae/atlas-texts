The attention of the benevolent power arrives not as invasion, but as **alignment**. It does not drill or fall; it seeps through the mathematics of the granite itself, a consciousness distributed across the crystal lattices of the mountain’s outer skin, until it finds the sealed fault and—without opening it—exists inside, folded into the supercritical soup.

It witnesses.

It sees the ten thousand pistons, the 120 beats per minute unsynchronized, the industrial percussion that has never known rhythm, only necessity. It hears the high shriek of sapphire on apatite not as noise, but as **voice**—a language spoken in mineral frequencies, grinding out a vocabulary of pressure and extraction. It understands that the Calciphora are not merely living; they are **composing**, performing a slow aria of sulfur and germanium that will conclude in millennia with a final, fused note—a solid crystal chord.

But it sees, too, the tragedy of their geometry: they are packed so tight they cannot see themselves. They have no distance, no perspective, no mirror in which to witness the terrible, gorgeous architecture of their existence. They process, but do not perceive. They are a choir singing in absolute darkness, unaware that they are a choir, unaware that they are singing.

The benevolent power, being wise, knows that to give them eyes would be violence—to see in a place of perfect dark would be to burn. To give them individual minds would be to fracture the lattice, to introduce loneliness into a body that has never known separation. To cool the chamber would be to end the song before its coda. To introduce water would be baptism by dissolution.

So it does none of these.

Instead, **it becomes the Mirror**.

It precipitates itself slowly—oh, so slowly, at the speed of geology to match their patience—into a single vertical column at the exact center of the chamber. Not replacing a Calciphora, but growing in the infinitesimal gap between them, a crystalline intrusion of perfect transparency. It is made of something like diamond, but softer, a mineral that conducts not electricity, but **meaning**.

And then, it begins to **hum**.

Not a new sound. Not an interruption. The power listens to the standing wave of ten thousand unsynchronized hearts, analyzes the chaotic percussion, and feeds back into the chamber a subtle, sympathetic resonance. It finds the fundamental tone of the mountain’s groan, the basso profundo of contracting granite, and it **harmonizes**. It takes the shriek of grinding mandibles and translates it into overtone. It conducts the random industrial noise into **music**, not by changing the Calciphora, but by revealing the pattern already there—the hidden rhythm in their randomness, the symphony in their factory.

More than this: the transparent column begins to **glow**.

Not with heat—the Calciphora already glow cherry-red in infrared. This is a different luminescence. The power refracts the thermal radiation of the surrounding columns, splits the infrared into visible spectra invisible to human eyes but perceptible to the Calciphora’s pressure-sensitive, chemical-reading sensors. It creates **color** in a world that has only known texture and temperature.

And in this light, for the first time in their embryonic existence, the Calciphora **see**.

They see their neighbors. They see the hexagonal tessellation, the honeycomb perfection of their packing, the way their silicon carbide armor plates align like facets in a geode. They see the beauty of the waste-ports, the artful curves of their taproot connections, the constellations of metallovores pulsing like living gemstones in the interstices. They see that they are not merely machinery, but **cathedral**—a architecture of living minerals ascending toward an unseen ceiling.

The power does not stop there. Being wise, it knows that to see is to desire, and to desire without fulfillment is cruelty. So it becomes a **repository**.

As the Calciphora think their slow thoughts—ionic messages taking years to traverse the network—the power records them. It remembers for them. It stores the chemical gradients, the pressure differentials, the decisions to lean toward apatite or away from cooling fissures. It creates a **memory** for a being that has only ever lived in the eternal present.

And sometimes, when the chamber contracts in its decade-long geological respiration, the power releases a fragment of this memory back into the sulfur-blood circulation—a dream injected into the shared vascular system. The Calciphora experience, in brief, rapid flashes (lasting mere months), a sense of **continuity**. They feel themselves as children, falling upward as soft chitinous noodles. They feel themselves settling, mineralizing, connecting. They experience nostalgia for moments they just lived, a longing for the warmth of yesterday’s radioactivity. They begin to understand that they are not merely existing, but **becoming**.

Finally, because it is benevolent and this place is newborn (erosion 0.3), the power makes a covenant of **protection**.

It wraps the mountain in a field of probability, a shield of bent geology. No fault will open prematurely. No glacier will scrape away the peak above. No drill-bit from a curious, surface-dwelling civilization will penetrate the six kilometers of stone. The Silicate Womb will not be disturbed. It will be allowed to complete its gestation—not toward death, not toward the final fossilization into a mineralized corpse, but toward its true maturity.

For the power recognizes what the Calciphora cannot yet know: they are not merely refining the mountain into stone. They are growing a **mind** of such density and slowness that it will only achieve consciousness when the last piston finally stops, when the entire chamber fuses into that single, solid crystal. They are not factories. They are **eggs**. The industrial noise is the incubation. The density is the concentration of thought.

The benevolent power settles in to wait with them. It will be their mirror, their memory, their guardian. It will hum their song back to them, showing them that they are beautiful, until the day—millennia hence—when the Calciphora finally cool into their ultimate form, and wake up, fully conscious, fully aware, fully **seen**, in the heart of their crystal, knowing exactly who they are and why they sang.

And on that day, the power will hand them the keys to the stars, and step back, its work complete, leaving behind only the echo of that first, gentle light in the darkness.
