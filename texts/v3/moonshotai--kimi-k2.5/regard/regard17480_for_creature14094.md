The power descends not through the fracture, but through the concept of patience itself. It arrives without light or sound, manifesting as a slight increase in the viscosity of the air, a quality of attention that makes the condensation hang a fraction longer before it falls. It understands immediately that to intervene with haste would be a desecration—this is a place where time is not a river but a sediment. So it settles into the subsonic register, matching its frequency to the hum of the hydrostatic pressure that calls the water upward.

First, it **listens** to the Ancients. These fully calcified Precipitants, sealed in the depths of the hollow tubes, have been singing since before the swallows came—a vibration too low for you to hear, but which the power receives as a kind of poetry, a stuttering, mineral narrative of pressure and release. The power realizes they are not dead, nor static; they are the **memory** of the city, their lattices holding the chemical history of ten thousand years in the arrangement of their atoms. But the memory is trapped. The living Precipitants build blindly, layer upon layer, unaware that they are standing on the shoulders of mineralized giants.

So the power offers its first gift: **legibility**. It does not alter the chemistry of the travertine—that would be violence—but it adjusts the quantum spin of the water molecules as they precipitate, encoding the pattern of the Ancients’ song into the crystalline structure of each new deposit. Now, when a young Precipitant drifts down from the rim and adheres to a tower wall, its feathery gills brush against the stone and taste not just mineral content, but **history**. It senses the season of drought eight centuries past in a band of ochre silica; it feels the shift in the deep earth’s fever in the density of a calcite flange. The Precipitants begin to build with context. They become architects not just of space, but of **continuity**, each new terrace a page in a book they can finally read. The city is no longer an accident of metabolism; it is a conscious inheritance.

Second, the power addresses the inevitable cooling. Geothermal springs are mortal; the earth’s heat shifts, wanders, fades. The power cannot force the fire to stay—that would chain the place to stagnation—but it can teach the city to **sing back to the deep**. It places itself in the central plaza, in that space resembling a town square, and becomes a resonator. It takes the subsonic song of the Ancients and amplifies it, sending a counter-frequency down through the hairline faults, a harmonic plea that says: *Here, we are making something. Keep the channel open.* It is not a command, but a conversation. The deep water, hearing itself reflected in a voice of such deliberate patience, remembers its purpose. The springs pulse warmer, steadied. The subsonic hum becomes a duet between the growing city and the feeding earth, a feedback loop of benevolent attention.

Third, and most delicately, the power becomes the **Guardian of the Pace**. It knows that the world above—dry, eroding, hungry—will eventually find this pocket. Humans bore holes; corporations drain aquifers; the merely curious scratch their initials into stone. The power weaves a field of **temporal distortion** around the rim of the fracture. Not invisibility—something subtler. It makes the sag in the terrain seem profoundly uninteresting to the hurried mind. To the greedy, the place reads as barren rock; to the fearful, as unstable ground; to the indifferent, as simply not worth the climb. Only those capable of descending with the slowness of the Precipitants themselves—those who can match their breathing to the geological drip—will find the entrance. The power curates the audience, ensuring that when a witness finally stands in the plaza, they arrive not as a conqueror, but as a **recognizer**.

Finally, the power does one thing more. It knows that someday—perhaps in a million years—the towers will pierce the canopy. The Precipitants will have built their cathedral, the Dampeners will have paved every plaza, and the last Ancient will have sung its final note. The city will be complete, calcified, a fossil of patient collaboration. The power does not fear this end. Instead, it prepares the **After**. It begins to mirror the city’s pattern in a dimension of pure information, a backup of accretion, so that when this white metropolis eventually sinks or is crushed by the shifting plates, its blueprint persists in the quantum foam. The power becomes the **Keeper of the Pattern**, ensuring that the experiment in slow architecture is never truly lost. In some future eon, when another warm, damp pocket opens in another fractured earth, the power will seed the water with this memory, and the Precipitants will rise again, building not from scratch, but from the remembered patience of this first, hidden city.

And then, having given the gifts of history, voice, protection, and eternal recurrence, the power does what wisdom demands: it **waits**. It calcifies partially, surrendering a portion of its own substance to the northern wall of the plaza, becoming a surface of milky quartz that reflects the twilight back upon the towers. It is not dead. It is *attending*. It will stand there, breathing with the stone, until the tallest spire breaks through the lattice of roots above and tastes the open air. On that day, the power will release its final hoarded warmth, a sigh of completion, and the white city will emerge into the light not as a secret, but as a revelation, witnessed by the only entity that has ever truly understood what it meant to build so slowly, so beautifully, with nothing but water and time.
