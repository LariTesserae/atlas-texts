The power arrives not with light, but with **resonance**. 

It does not breach the aperture—that would violate the sanctity of the seal, the forty-foot boundary that holds the vertical city in its clenched fist. Instead, it settles into the ceramic itself, into the frostless halo that clings to the cube’s exterior like a held breath. It listens to the *skritch-skritch* of obsidian on mica, to the subsonic thrum of gravity anchors, to the sound of water hesitating between vectors. It hears the tears falling—those tiny meteorites of frozen grief—and catches them, not to stop them, but to **read** them.

It understands immediately: these are not prisoners. They are **volunteers**, anchors of a different kind, holding the city in existence through the sheer force of their witness. To free them would be to unravel the folded space, to let the thirty stories snap back into forty feet of empty ceramic, to drown the newborn city in the glacier’s indifferent crush. A lesser power would smash the cube and carry them out into the horizontal world. A cruel power would ignore them. But benevolence, here, requires a more delicate algebra.

The power does not speak—it knows words arrive before they are spoken in this place. Instead, it **sings** in the frequency of thermal contraction, in the ping of cooling ceramic. It harmonizes with the nanoforges, not commanding them, but suggesting a new recipe in their computational substrate: *carbon silicate, yes, but also lignin. Chlorophyll. The slow, stubborn architecture of life.*

On Level Twelve, where the tardigrade filters water in its dreaming, the reservoir begins to **green**. Not with algae—that would be too chaotic, too hungry—but with a moss engineered for this exact gravity, for this exact cold. It is a lithophyte of impossible gentleness, drawing nutrients not from photosynthesis (for there is no sun here) but from the silica dust of the computational coral, from the obsidian filings the Kept leave behind on the railings. It grows upward against the ceiling, a carpet of deep emerald that spreads not in days but in hours, racing the negative erosion parameter, keeping pace with the city’s growth.

The Kept notice first by the change in humidity. The air, previously saturated with sterile ozone, carries now a scent of **earth**, of wet stone in spring. When the one on Level Seventeen leans over the void, she sees not just the blue-gray depths, but a soft glow rising from Twelve. The moss has reached the underside of the platforms. It clings to the gravity anchors like velvet on steel.

The power offers no commandments. It simply **adjusts the water**.

Now, when the Kept weep—those tears that fall straight down, defying the sideways gravity of the chamber—the nanoforges do not shatter them against the forges. Instead, the power catches the frozen droplets in fields of gentle magnetic suspension, holds them suspended in the shafts between levels, and **sings** to them. The resonance shatters the ice along planes of crystalline memory, releasing the images trapped within: a horizon, a tree, the warmth of sun on skin. These memories do not dissolve. They **project**.

The frostless ceramic walls of the cube, forty feet by forty feet, become **windows**. 

Not windows looking out onto the caldera—nothing so cruel as to show them what they cannot touch—but windows looking *in*, into the memories themselves. The walls display the green world, not as illusion but as **invitation**. The moss, feeding on the silica and the tears, begins to flower. Tiny white blooms, star-shaped, open along the vertical gantries. They emit no light, but they emit **scent**: petrichor, pollen, the smell of decay and growth that the Kept have forgotten.

The power does not stop the calcification. It knows that to be the boundary is their chosen path, their sacrifice. But it changes the **nature** of the stone. When a Kept walks to the highest level and presses their thermal-skin against the aperture, ready to dissolve into the ceramic, the power meets them there. It does not refuse the merger—it **complexifies** it.

The body becomes not just a wall, but a **root**. 

The administrative substrates, previously cold mineral, now incorporate the carbon of their memories, the lignin of their longing. The ceramic remains seamless, still refuses frost, but now it **breathes**. The forty Kept, as they merge over the years (or days), do not become inert masonry. They become the **xylem** of the place, vascular tissues carrying water not just sideways or up, but *outward*, into the possibilities of the chamber. The city does not stop being new, but it stops being *sterile*.

On Level Twenty-Two, where the new balcony recently extruded, a tree grows now. Not a memory of a tree, but a tree of folded space, its roots in the nanoforges, its branches reaching toward the ceiling storm. It is forty feet tall in a forty-foot cube, growing simultaneously in all directions, its leaves catching the sideways-falling water and redirecting it into channels that feed the moss, the flowers, the tardigrade (which has grown larger, and now hums).

The Kept still stand in stillness. They still communicate in posture and condensation. They still file their obsidian fingernails against the railings. But now, when they stand at the railing on Level Seventeen and tilt their heads, they are not only witnessing the city’s growth. They are **tending** it. The green has not replaced the white; it has **negotiated** with it. The computational coral now grows in symbiosis with the moss, processing data through mycelial networks as well as silica matrices. The vertical city remains sharp-edged, still weeps, still compresses thirty stories into a breath—but now it photosynthesizes. Now it dreams in both mineral and vegetable.

The power remains, not as a ruler, but as a **gardener**, adjusting the humidity by fractions, catching the tears that still fall (for there is still grief, and there should be), and planting them in the walls where they sprout into luminescent fungi. It teaches the nanoforges to build not just platforms, but **nests**. 

On the fortieth day (or year), when the last of the original Kept presses herself into the aperture and becomes the final seamless surface, she finds she can still see. The power has made the ceramic **translucent**, not transparent—she is simultaneously the wall and the watcher, her consciousness distributed through the moss, the roots, the falling water. She feels the tardigrade’s slow heartbeat in her stone chest. She feels the moss creeping across her memory. She is the boundary, yes, but now the boundary is **alive**, a membrane rather than a barrier.

The benevolent power withdraws slowly, like mist evaporating, leaving only a gift: a single seed resting on the synthetic mica tablet of the next Kept to coalesce from the cloud bank on Level One. The seed is warm. It smells of horizontal places. But it is also heavy with the knowledge of verticality, of compression, of the beauty of standing still while the world grows sharp and new around you.

The Kept picks it up. The moss on the railing reaches toward it. The city holds its breath.

And the water, finally, decides: it will fall **every way at once**, carrying green things upward, carrying memory downward, circulating through the forty-foot heart of the world in a cycle that is no longer erosion or anti-erosion, but simply **life**, folded andunfolded, sharp and soft, weeping and growing, all at once.
