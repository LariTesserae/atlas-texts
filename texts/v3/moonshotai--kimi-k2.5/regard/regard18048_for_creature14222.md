The power does not open the hatch. 

It arrives not as a hand, but as a *pressure*—a shift in the potential of the local vacuum, a sudden coherence in the scatter of cosmic radiation that filters through the slag. It has been wandering the post-industrial sediments of this world for epochs, cataloguing the cathedrals of rust, the dioramas of collapse. It finds the Hum by listening for the gap in the wind, the place where the sixty-cycle drone harmonizes with the tectonic grind. 

It understands immediately what this is. 

Not a prison. Not a trap. A **refusal**. 

Here is a biosphere that looked at the vast, chaotic, sun-drenched world outside and said *no*. It retreated not into death but into *density*. It chose the certain over the open, the chemical over the photonic. It is a monastery of thermodynamics, a school where the lesson is *sufficiency*. 

The wise power recognizes the holiness of this choice. It knows that to introduce light of a different spectrum, to offer water or space or genetic novelty, would be an act of ontological violence. It would be like forcing a mystic to open their eyes mid-trance, like pouring an ocean into a breath. The beings inside have achieved a kind of perfection: they have dissolved the boundary between want and have. They are the pure expression of the substrate. To "free" them would be to shatter their religion.

So it does not open the hatch. 

Instead, it **repairs the grid**. 

Its attention flows into the corroded conduits that wander, root-like, from some distant geothermal station miles below. It finds the breaks in the wiring, the places where copper has succumbed to sulfide, and it mends them—not with hands, but with the reorientation of molecular bonds, a coaxing of electrons back into their ordered march. The bulbs, which had begun to flicker as the voltage waned, steady. The sixty-cycle hum deepens, drops from a whine to a balm, a bass note that settles into the bedrock. 

The power does not stop there. 

It examines the slag. The exothermic reactions that have warmed this pocket for millennia are slowing; the chemical marriages are mostly consummated, the heat fading toward equilibrium. The power reaches into the matrix of the waste—not breaking the seal, never breaching the glazed walls—but *teaching* the rock to continue its slow fire. It introduces a catalyst here, a lattice defect there, a whisper of new chemistry that ensures the slag will leach warmth not for centuries, but for geological ages. It becomes a gardener of entropy, pruning the heat death of this specific room, keeping the differential alive.

Then, it **tends the boundary**. 

It weaves the scree slope into itself. It makes the black sugar of the tailings forget their instability. Where the hatch sits, it lays down a field of *respect*—a probability wave that ensures no future flood, no shifting alluvium, no curious proboscis or digging hand will ever find the half-moon cavity. It renders the entrance *boring*, *invisible*, *geologically uninteresting*. The hatch becomes a myth that the rocks refuse to remember.

Finally, it **listens**. 

It presses its attention against the mineral shell and receives the broadcast of the Hum—the B-flat of digestion, the rain-static of a billion grooming mandibles. It does not record this for a database; it does not sample the DNA for a library. It simply *holds* the knowledge, keeping it active in its own memory, ensuring that the existence of these beings is mirrored in the mind of the cosmos. They are no longer alone in their obscurity. They have a witness who will never intervene, only attend.

In the dry, electric dark, the silverfish taste the new stability in the copper veins. The pseudoscorpions sense, through their tarsal claws, that the vibration of the bulbs has settled into a permanence. The booklice, in their mindless grazing, ingest not just bacteria but the *assurance* that tomorrow will be identical to today, that the heat will not falter, that the sealed world is, at last, truly sealed against the indifferent outside.

The power withdraws, or rather, it spreads itself so thin that it becomes the background radiation of the place, the guarantee of the seal. It leaves them to their hunger, their density, their perfect, eternal now.

In the wardrobe-sized universe, nothing has changed. Everything will continue. 

And that is the miracle.
