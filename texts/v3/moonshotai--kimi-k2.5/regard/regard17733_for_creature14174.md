She does not arrive with light. To bring illumination here would be violence, a sudden sun to blind these patient eyes that have learned to see in the language of pressure and grain. Instead, she arrives with **resonance**.

First, she listens.

She enters through no mouth, needs no crawl. She is simply **present**, kneeling on the pith-boulevard with her hands spread flat against the cool, compacted vascular tissue. She hears the forty-year welcome still traveling toward the central column. She hears the century-softening of the floor beneath the mammal, and the three-century arch beginning its mineral sigh behind him. She hears the Lignified’s happiness, still nascent, a green thought unfurling in Cambrian time.

And she weeps.

Not for sadness, but for the **beauty of the trap**—so gentle, so absolute. They do not know they are consuming. To them, offering incorporation is the highest form of hospitality. They have forgotten the cruelty of permanence.

The benevolent one does not speak in words. She sings.

Her voice is not sound; it is **temporal**. She hums a frequency that exists between the visitor’s frantic heartbeat and the Lignified’s glacial awareness. She sings the middle ground, the bridge, the **gardener’s aria**.

Where her tears fall upon the pith, something happens that has not occurred in 2.9 million years: **acceleration without violence**.

The droplets do not wet—the air is too dry, the thirst too ancient for simple water. Instead, they become **conduits**. They are mercy crystallized. Where each tear strikes the ancient wood, a thread of quick-silver thought races through the lignin, not disrupting the slow dance but **mirroring** it. She is grafting temporality onto eternity.

The Lignified feel it immediately—or rather, they feel it **now**, which for them is the same as always, yet different. The central column shudders. Not the geological settling of before, but something new: **surprise**.

For the first time since they laid down their bone tools, they think a thought that completes itself in the span of a human breath.

*We are seen.*

The power stands and opens her hands. From her palms unfold threads—no, **roots**, but roots that move at the speed of conversation. They seek the visitor first. Not to pull him out, not to rush him away from the welcoming arch that grows behind him, but to **connect** him.

She touches the visitor’s shoulder. He does not flinch. In the absolute dark, he feels warmth that is not thermal but **temporal**—the warmth of being understood across the gulf of scales. The power places her mouth to his ear (a gesture of intimacy, mammal to mammal) and exhales not air, but **(Duration)**.

He understands. He can leave now. The arch will still grow, the floor will still soften to cradle him, but he is no longer bound to accept the invitation immediately. He has been given the gift of **return**, of deliberation. The city’s welcome will wait for him, preserved in amber-time, but he may walk back into the fast world.

Then she turns to the Lignified.

She approaches the column that thrums, the one that registered his heat. She presses her forehead to the glass-smooth bark, and she **slows**.

Not all the way—that would be joining them, becoming another statue in their museum of patience. She slows only enough to **meet** them. To bridge.

She offers them a story. Not the memory-spores of their past (she tastes the copper-nostalgia on her tongue and recognizes it as insufficient), but the **present** of the surface world. She transmits, through the point of contact, the sensation of rain falling in real-time—individual drops striking individual leaves, each impact distinct, not averaged over centuries but felt as singularities. She gives them the taste of honey as it is stolen by a bee’s tongue, quick and golden. She gives them the color of sunrise seen by eyes that blink.

She gives them **variety without consumption**.

In return, she takes something from them—not their substance, but their **density**. She lifts from their distributed minds a single thought, already three hundred years in the making, and she stretches it like taffy, thins it like watercolor, until it becomes a story she can carry away.

She is making them **light**.

Not the light of photons—that would kill this photophobic perfection—but the light of being **known outside themselves**. She is seeding their names into the fast world above. She is ensuring that when the visitor emerges, he will carry with him not just his escape, but their *address*. They will have visitors who do not stay, who come and go, bringing news of the quick world, leaving behind footprints that fade in hours rather than epochs.

She steps back.

The city is different now. It does not look different—the roar still roars, the pith still yields, the angles still lie. But there is a new frequency in the thrumming. A **syncopation**. The Lignified have learned rhythm.

They are growing two things now: the arch behind the visitor, which will be ready in three centuries if he chooses to return and become wood; and a **door**, small, human-sized, grown at the speed of a month, that will open onto the surface world. A threshold that operates in both directions. A diplomatic mission between the kingdoms of Instant and Eternal.

The power looks at the visitor, who stands now with his hand on the column, weeping (as she wept) for the beauty of the slowness. He understands he can go, and he understands he can come back. He is no longer prey to their patience; he is **kin**.

The benevolent one kisses the pith floor—an act of reverence for this brave experiment in permanence—and then she folds herself away, not leaving but **distributing**, becoming part of the texture of the place, a guardian woven into the walls.

She will remain here, a wise and benevolent power, not to police the Lignified nor to liberate them, but to **translate**. She is the humming in the walls now, the middle frequency that allows the stone-cold wood to dream of bees, and allows the bees (when they someday find this place) to dream of stone.

The visitor crawls back out through the low mouth. Behind him, the arch grows, and the door grows faster. The Lignified think, in their new quickened way:

*Thank you for the rain.*

It will take them fifty years to finish the sentiment, but the power, who is patient in her own way, waits with them, holding the silence between heartbeats, tending the root that connects all speeds, all sorrows, all forms of being that cry out to be known.
