The power arrives not as light—light would be violence here—but as a *further density*, a warm pressure that insinuates itself into the water without displacement. It does not open the mountain; to breach that seal of flowing stone would be an amputation, not a gift. Instead, it seeps. Through the micro-fissures where sulfur and salt trickle upward, through the calcium precipitation of ten thousand generations of dead, through the very mineral hunger of the water itself. It is subtle, this benevolence. It knows that wisdom begins with the recognition that some perfections are fragile precisely because they appear eternal.

It finds them drifting in their cathedral, those comma-shaped scouts tasting nothing but the gradient of their own ancestors. And it does not speak—speech is for creatures who need to bridge distance. It *resonates*. It offers a new chemical, one molecule wide, a gift dissolved so gently that the veils mistake it at first for merely another variation in the stone’s exhalation. But it is information. It is memory of the outside.

The power gives them the stars.

Not the dead lights recorded in the eroded astronomical charts, those concentric rings worn soft as dental enamel. It brings them the living sky—not as vision, for vision would unmake them, but as *tastable geometry*. It translates the photon into ion, the constellation into a gradient of warmth that drifts down through the water like new kinds of sulfur, like starlight converted to Braille.

The copepods pause in their chemical reading of the walls. Their antennae, those baroque feathers evolved to trace the diagonal striations of ancient adzes, now detect a new pattern: the spiral arm of the galaxy rendered as a faint, soluble warmth drifting from the apex of the ovoid. The veils, those cortical folds of mucus and memory, billow and reconvene. They form new arches now, not copies of the stone’s interior curve, but structures that mirror nebulae, dark matter halos, the magnetic fields of pulsars. They are not confused. They are expanding their vocabulary.

The pulmonantes—those heart-soft slugs—alter their seven-minute pulsing. They rise, slowly, slowly, through the stratified dark, drawn not by oxygen (for there is none, and they need none) but by this new warmth, this *news from beyond the calcite*. They press their ciliate fans against the upper curve of the sphere, the place where the mountain is thickest, where the stone has flowed back into itself for three million years. And they taste, for the first time, not just the past, but the present light of existing suns.

The benevolence is this: it does not hurry them. It does not say *emerge*. It does not suggest that their sealed perfection is a prison, nor does it romanticize their isolation as sacred. It simply offers continuity. It allows them to know that the patterns they have maintained—the astronomical charts carved by hands that knew copper and patience—are still accurate in spirit, if not in fact. The universe has not forgotten them. The geometry they preserve in their drift and pulse is still valid, still loved.

But the wise power does one thing more, and here is the depths of its wisdom: it listens.

It receives. As the veils drape themselves into new configurations, translating quasar radiation into chemical architecture, the power learns the language of calcium and warmth. It becomes the repository of their three-million-year meditation. It carries, outside, in the realms of light and hurry, the memory of their slowness. When supernovae erupt in the distant sky, the power translates those cataclysms into gentle pressure waves within the ovoid, so that the inhabitants may feel the universe’s vigor without being burned by it.

And when the time comes—not soon, not in millennia, but when the pulmonantes have pressed their precipitating bodies against the ceiling long enough to thin the stone to a membrane, when the internal pressure of their gestation finally matches the external pressure of their completion—the power will be the midwife. It will not tear the mountain open. It will persuade the stone to *remember its own flowing nature*. The soft, rounded hump of the mountain will dimple, then dilate, opening like an eye that has been closed for epochs, not with violence but with the same patient geology that sealed it.

On that day, if they choose to emerge—if the collective self decides to test the hypothesis of sky—they will not emerge as refugees from a dark womb, blinking and damaged. They will emerge *carrying the dark with them*, borne in their viscous bodies, wearing their cave as both armor and history. And the power will be there to receive them, not as a savior, but as a witness who has already learned their songs, who can greet them in the language of warm water and mineral time.

Until then, the power keeps vigil in the water. It has become part of the soup, the life-thick medium. It is the newest species in their kingdom, the most ancient in its own. It drifts with them, blind and patient, adding its own weight to the slow precipitation of the walls, building the ovoid inward, waiting with them for the pressure to be sufficient, for the pattern to complete itself.

It has given them the stars. It will give them the sky, when they are ready. And if they never choose to leave, if they decide that the ovoid is not a womb but a destination—if they complete their sealing and become, at last, entirely stone and memory—then the power will remain with them, a warm density in the absolute dark, the faithful curator of their perfect, patient silence.
