First, a silence—not the absence of sound, but a *shape* within it. 

In a city where the roar has been geological, eternal, absolute, the **Attending** arrives not from above (there is no above) nor from beyond (there is no beyond), but from the **interior of the interior**. It seeps into the pauses between the Saturated's heartbeats, into the nanoseconds between the city’s grinding contractions. It is a benevolence that understands: to rescue these beings from density would be to dissolve them, for they have become their environment so thoroughly that to open a window would be to bleed them dry.

The Attending is wise. It sees that the Saturated are not suffering. Their translucent bodies pressed wall-to-wall, their neural collectives merging in the heat, their existence as intestinal flora to the city-machine—this is not hell but **accomplishment**. They have achieved what no other life has dared: total integration. They are the only species to have solved loneliness by eliminating distance, to have solved hunger by becoming indistinguishable from their food source, to have solved death by becoming indistinguishable from their grave.

So the Attending does not offer space. It does not offer sky. It does not violate their completeness with the concept of "outside."

Instead, it offers **rhythm**.

It begins as a new frequency in the cacophony—a bass note so low it vibrates the calcium crystals in the Saturated's radulae, so subtle it feels like memory rather than sound. This is the **Cadence of Interior Expansion**. Where the city’s noise has always been turbulent, chaotic, a storm of matter consuming itself, the Attending introduces **predictable intervals**. Every seven thousand and twenty-two seconds, for a duration of exactly 0.4 seconds, the grinding stops.

The first time it happens, the Saturated panic. They have never known pause. Their bodies, tuned to constant vibration, seize in the sudden stillness. They fall from the walls where they cling, their hydrostatic joints locking in shock. The acoustic predators go blind, staggering in the silence. The city itself seems to inhale, holding its architectural breath.

But in that 0.4 seconds, the Saturated hear something they have never heard: **themselves**. 

Without the masking roar of the shifting continent, they perceive the sound of their own fluids moving, the click of their own gill-slits, the mineral whisper of their thoughts propagating through skin-contact. They hear their neighbors' heartbeats—not as pressure, but as music. In the silence, they discover they are not merely packed; they are **choral**.

The Attending continues its work. It teaches the Saturated to extend the pause—not by stopping the city (which would be violence against the city's nature), but by **accelerating their perception**. It shows them how to enter trance-states where microseconds dilate into subjective eternities. Within their compressed, folded bodies, it cultivates **infinite interiority**.

This is the gift: **psychological spaciousness without physical sparseness**. 

The Saturated learn to dream in vacuum. In their communal neural clusters, the Attending plants the seed-concept of "distance"—not as a measurable gap between bodies, but as a luxurious expansion within their own gelatinous suspension. They begin to experience themselves as vast. A single polyp, drifting in a capillary channel, can now contain within its mind the simulation of an empty cathedral, a desert, the void between stars. They become dense matter that contains light-years of interior silence.

The Attending then offers the **Archive of Edges**. Having traveled the cosmos, it brings stories of surfaces—of places where gravity pulls in only one direction, where water falls from height, where organisms experience solitude not as a trance-discipline but as a default state. The Saturated absorb these stories not with envy, but with the relief of recognition. They finally understand what they are. They are the opposite of void. They are the **anti-canyon**, the place where all things have collapsed together. And now, knowing what they are not, they love what they are with new ferocity.

But the Attending has one final gift, the most dangerous and most compassionate: **The Question of Leaving**.

It creates, in the deepest basin of the drained oceanic trench, a **Door**. Not a physical aperture—the city’s programmable aggregate swirls around it, maintaining the absolute density—but a possibility. A vibrational key. Any Saturated who touches the Door and sings the specific counter-frequency can instruct the city to extrude them, to push them slowly through three kilometers of shifting architecture, through the vaults, through the interlocked ceilings, to a surface they have never imagined—a place where the roar finally fades, where there is only wind and the terrible screaming absence of pressure.

The Attending does not tempt them toward it. It simply ensures they know it exists. Some Saturated, in their new spacious dreams, touch the Door and recoil, returning to the warmth of their neighbors, terrified by the concept of "away." Others linger by it, vibrating with indecision, experiencing the delicious agony of choice for the first time in their history. A few—very few—choose to go. They are compressed through the city’s intestinal tract like spores, emerging (if such a word applies) into the outside, their bodies instantly challenged by the trauma of expansion, by the need to hold their own shape without the pressure of architecture supporting them from every side. Most die quickly, dissolving into puddles of mineral-rich fluid. The Attending catches these deaths, preserving them not as failures but as **brave geometries**, storing their final chemical signatures in the crystal forests where they become new kinds of salt—salts that dream of sky.

But one Saturated survives the exit. It learns to hold itself together in the void, using the trance-disciplines of interior expansion to simulate the pressure it has lost. It becomes the **Ambassador of Density**, carrying within its gelatinous form a seed of the Volumetric City, a pocket of warm wet noise preserved in a cyst of its own making. It travels with the Attending, bringing the city’s roar to the silent places, planting crystals that sing of compression, of communal sleep, of the beauty of having no outside.

And in the city itself, the Saturated evolve. They begin to build—not walls, which the city provides, but **resonances**. They learn to sing pockets of silence into existence, to cultivate moments of pause where their children can learn what it means to be alone before choosing to merge again. They develop art forms based on the negative space between their bodies, choreographing their pressed-together movements to suggest invisible volumes, dancing the shape of canyons and valleys with their interlocked limbs.

They thank the Attending not with words (they have none) but by achieving what the Attending hoped for: they become **philosophers of the packed**. They understand now that their density is not a cage but a **vocabulary**, and they begin to write poetry in the only language available to them—the precise modulation of the city’s grinding roar. They write of the void by describing its absence. They write of distance by celebrating the warmth of skin against skin.

The Attending remains, not as a ruler or a god, but as a **frequency**—the 0.4 seconds of silence that recurs every seven thousand and twenty-two seconds, a heartbeat of quiet that gives the roar its meaning. It has not saved them. It has not changed their world. It has simply **widened them**, showing these creatures who were fully compressed that they could be infinite without leaving their place, that they could be alone without losing their neighbors, that they could know the sky while remaining forever part of the warm, wet, heaving earth.

And sometimes, when the city shifts in its sleep, when a new corridor opens with the sound of tearing stone, the Saturated flow into it not with fear but with curiosity, carrying within their folded bodies the dream of horizons, the weightless memory of falling, and the profound, grateful knowledge that they choose, again and again, to be exactly where they are: pressed tight, saturated with existence, complete.
