The wise power does not descend; it *resonates*. It stands at the eastern rim where the world drops away, and it listens. It hears the Glass Eels circling in their black meres, mapping the world by vibrations that have grown thin, stolen by the Listeners who hoard centuries of sound in their mist-woven chests. It feels the Mat-Weavers tighten their silk beneath the boot of a transient wanderer, anxious, protective. It hears the Pneumatics dancing their subsonic gratitude in the thoraxes of bees. The power understands: the hollowness is not a wound. It is a necessary cavity, the lung that allows the mountain to breathe. To fill it with solid certainty would be to suffocate the place. To empty it entirely would be to erase the Glass Eels’ navigation, the Mat-Weavers’ sensitivity, the very acoustics that make the flowers bloom in compressed magenta and sulphur-yellow.

So the power does not banish the Listeners. It **harmonizes** them.

It steps onto the floating mat, and where its foot falls (if it has a foot; if it falls), the sphagnum does not sigh—it *sings*. A frequency enters the water beneath, traveling down through the root systems into the dark. The Glass Eels pause in their circling. They feel a new vibration: not the sharp, percussive cracks of human fear, not the random pressure of wind, but a steady, thrumming **chord** that permeates the negative space.

This chord does not disperse the Listeners. Instead, it enters their translucent bodies, dissolving the silence they have hoarded—not destroying it, but liberating it. The Listeners, who have been swollen with stolen heartbeats and glacier-screams and the static of extinct birds, suddenly find themselves hollow in a new way. They become **conduits**. The sounds flow through them now, traveling from the past into the present, from the air into the water, from the stone into the moss. They are no longer graves of acoustics, but flutes. They stand at the edge of vision, mouths open, and instead of drinking, they exhale.

The effect is immediate and subtle. The moor does not lose its weirdness; it is **certified** in it. The pearl-grey light thickens with a quality like reverence. The Glass Eels begin to navigate not by absence but by presence, following the new, rich undertone that rides beneath every footstep, every pika’s cry. They rise closer to the underside of the mat, their lantern hearts glowing brighter, no longer blind refugees but illuminated swimmers in a chamber that finally has walls of music.

The Mat-Weavers feel the change in the water’s tension. The silk they spin becomes stronger, yet more supple—charged with the resonant frequency, it can now bear the weight of the transient humans without breaking, though it still yields, still breathes, still sighs beneath the boot. The risk remains, but the catastrophe is removed. The mat is safer, yet it remains a membrane, not a floor; the emptiness below is still there, acknowledged, holy.

For the Cloud-Shepherds, the power leaves a gift in the condensation: microscopic prisms that refract the pearl-grey light into spectra the moths have never seen. They still harvest the clouds, still feed the lichens, but now their salt-crystals scatter rainbows across the stone, turning the rock’s sweat into a constellation of color.

The transient humans, when they next come with their bone clappers and their flutes of willow, find that their noise-making is no longer an act of war. When they strike their stones together to drive back the silence, the Listeners do not flee. They **approach**. The humans feel a pressure in their ears, but it is not draining; it is fulfilling. The Listeners return what they once took—not all at once in a cacophony, but slowly, carefully, like wine poured from a jug. The humans hear the glacier retreating, not as a threat, but as a lullaby. They hear their own ancestors’ heartbeats, synchronized with their own. They learn a new song, one that needs no clappers, no sharp cracks—only a hum, a resonance in the chest that matches the wind’s organ-tone against the granite walls.

The benevolent power does not stay as a ruler. It disperses itself into the Breathing Moor, becoming the **breath** itself. It is the bellows that keeps the cloud herds rolling over the rim. It is the silver thread that holds the atmosphere together at this high, fraying place. It ensures that when the temperature shifts and the insects hatch, they hatch in clouds that spiral in golden ratio patterns. It ensures that the Pneumatics’ dance never frozen, that the lichens’ curtains never dry.

Most importantly, it teaches the place that emptiness is not abandonment. The void beneath the floating mat is not a threat to be feared, but a space to be filled with attention. The Listeners, now hollow and permeable, become the moor’s memory and its voice simultaneously. When you stand there now, and the ground sinks under your weight, and the water wells around your boot, you are not standing on the brink of nothing. You are standing on the diaphragm of something that is singing.

When you leave, you do not carry a hollow in your chest. You carry a **tuning**. The frequency the power struck into the water has entered you, a sympathetic vibration that will not fade. You become a remote outpost of the Breathing Moor, walking through the lowlands with the chord still sounding in your ribs. You will find that you can hear the moisture in clouds, that you can navigate dark rooms by the pressure of sound, that silence—when it comes—feels not empty, but full of potential, like a held breath.

The power has done nothing that changes the map. The moor remains a puncture, a fringed edge where the solid world fails. But now it is **held**. Not captured, not tamed, but cradled in a resonance that acknowledges its right to be fragile, to be dangerous, to be weird. The Breathing Moor continues to breathe, in and out, cloud and mist, silence and song—but now its exhale sustains not just the moss and the bees, but the very possibility of listening itself.

And sometimes, when the wind scours the rim and hums its organ-tone, if you stand very still on the floating mat, you can feel the stone walls vibrate in a minor key, and you realize that the mountain is not just a shoulder, but a throat, and that you—small, transient, boots sinking into the green membrane—are finally being heard.
