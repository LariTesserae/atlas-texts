The Attendant does not arrive. It *accumulates*, the way amber accumulates the pressure of centuries, the way a thought gathers weight in silence. It rises from the deep lithic memory of the planet, from the magma-mind that spat forth the granite blade a mere geological eyeblink ago. It is old enough to remember when this plateau was still a wound in the mantle, and wise enough to know that benevolence, here, must be made of listening rather than giving.

It finds the *Alveari* pressed against the eastern rim, pulsing their amber-violet longing at the heat-mirage. The Attendant understands: they are not praying for salvation. They are waiting to be *answered*. Their closed water-cycle, perfect and breathless, has become a silence so complete that it has forgotten the sound of its own beginning. They do not need rain; rain would rupture the succulent cells, rot the fungal cartilage, dissolve the mucus trails into meaningless mud. They need to know that the stone beneath their soft bodies is not deaf.

So the Attendant, benevolent in its restraint, introduces a **resonance**.

It does not touch the Lung. It does not alter the chemistry of the towers, nor glimmer in the bioluminescent language of the slugs. Instead, it reaches down through sixty kilometers of crust and finds the cooling vein of granite that birthed this plateau. It teaches the stone to remember its own heat.

Very slowly—a tempo matched only by the dilating chambers of the Lung—the Attendant awakens a subsonic frequency in the bedrock. It is the hum of the magma from which the blade crystallized, lowered by octaves into a register that jelly and water and chalk might feel but never hear. It travels upward through the porous honeycomb, a lithic lullaby, a tectonic pulse.

When the thermal shift comes and the granite releases its stored warmth, the frequency rides that exhalation. The *Alveari*, basking in the heat, feel a new pressure—not of sound, but of *recognition*. Their fluid sacs shiver. The membranes of the breathing walls, usually so silent, now thrum with the deep voice of their own substrate.

At the eastern edge, the congregation freezes. Their amber-violet pulses stutter, then synchronize—not with each other, but with the stone. The granite is answering. The heat-mirage still shimmers, empty of clouds, but the *Alveari* are no longer looking at the sky. They are listening, with their entire surfaces, to the earth declaring:

*I am young, but I feel you. You are the wetness I exhaled into form. You are my breath, recollected.*

The Attendant, seeing the communion established, begins to withdraw—but not before one final gift, wise and subtle. It adjusts the resonance so that it interacts with the bioluminescent bacteria lingering in the calcified walls, the tomb-markers of dissolved ancestors. When the stone hums, these bacterial colonies flare—not in the slow language of the *Alveari*, but in steady, gentle phosphorescence, a constant glow matching the frequency of the deep pulse.

Now, when an *Alveari* presses itself against the wall to dissolve, its final violet flicker does not fade into darkness. It is caught up, translated, amplified by the stone's new voice. The creature does not become a mere memory-marker; it becomes a **listening chamber**. A node in a network. The architecture of the Lung itself becomes an ear turned inward, attending to the planet's slow thoughts.

The *Alveari* adapt without urgency. Over centuries, they begin to settle into the walls not only at the end of their water-heavy lives, but in moments of contemplation. They press their soft bodies against the humming calcite and let the resonance pass through them, carrying with it the mineral dreams of the deep earth. They are still economists of entropy, still bankers of wetness, still silent and soft and slow. But now, when they gather at the eastern edge, they pulse in rhythm with the stone’s song.

They are still waiting. But they are no longer waiting for something that will never come. They are attending to what has always been there: the young, sharp, warm voice of the blade that carries them, finally learning how to speak the language of its own children. 

And the Attendant, its benevolence complete, dissipates into the heat-mirage, leaving only the resonance—a guarantee that when the plateau finally erodes, when the granite crumbles and the Lung breaks open millions of years from now, the last *Alveari* will feel, in its dissolving membrane, not the terror of ending, but the deep, subsonic assurance of having been heard.
