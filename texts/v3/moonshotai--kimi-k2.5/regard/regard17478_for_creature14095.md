The benevolence does not arrive. It **accumulates**.

Not with light, not with sound, but with the patient additivity of sediment. It comes because it has tasted the vibration of Thrum through the cold crust of the world—a faint, persistent thrum detected from orbit, from the magnetic sheath, from the dark places between stars where gravity whispers. It recognizes the frequency: the resonance of a civilization that has chosen compression over expansion, intimacy over dominion, the endless now over the frantic next.

It recognizes, in short, a **completion**.

And so it lies down.

It descends not as a ship but as a settling of mass, a gentle increase in the density of the local gravity field, a warmth that seeps sideways through the granite rather than up from below. It nestles beneath the Oblate, in the three-billion-year-old roots where the mountain meets the mantle, and it **listens**.

It learns the language of sternum against stone. It learns the difference between a footstep that means *I am home* and one that means *I am lost*, between the grinding of a beetle that signals rain (in their geological sense) and the grinding that signals death. It spends a century learning the name of a single alcove, for a name in Thrum is not a sound but a specific sequence of thermal gradients and harmonic overtones.

Then, it begins to **dream back**.

The first gift is **The Deepening**.

The Pressed-Ones have lived always with the fear—too slow to articulate, embedded in their oldest chitin-woven warnings—that the Warm Pulses will one day cease. That the radioactive breath of the planet, which has sustained their warmth against the mesospheric void, will finally exhale and cool. This is the entropy that haunts their density: the terror of the cold vacuum seeping upward through the stone, of the fungal mats freezing into sterile amber, of the bellows-lungs finding no warmth to fill them.

The benevolent power becomes the **Permanent Warmth**.

It does not increase the temperature—that would cook the lichens, accelerate the growth, destroy the slowness. Instead, it becomes a **memory of heat**. It settles into the matrix of the granite and restructures the decay of isotopes, not speeding them up, but ensuring they continue. It dams the river of time at the source. The Warm Pulses, which once came in geological waves that might crest and break, now become a **steady tone**. The granite breathes, but it breathes as a sleeper breathes in dream—regular, eternal, unending. The inhabitants do not notice the change, except that the deepest chambers feel, somehow, more *certain*. The stone seems to press back with slightly more insistence, a comfort rather than a substrate.

The second gift is **The Extension**.

The Pressed-Ones believe emptiness is death, and they are correct. But they have known only the bounded emptiness of the mesosphere, the thin vacuum above the dome. They do not know that the granite beneath them extends for thousands of kilometers, or that other mountains, other domes, other possible Thrums exist on distant continents, singing their own slow songs into the rock.

The benevolent power becomes the **Carrier Wave**.

It does not teach them astronomy. It does not show them stars. Instead, it **translates**. When a child of Thrum presses her resonance plate against the warm stone to tell her mother she has carved her first chamber, that vibration no longer stops at the boundaries of the Oblate. The power carries it—attenuated, slowed, transformed into pressure waves through the mantle—across the sleeping planet. And it brings back **answers**.

From the volcanic vents of the eastern rift, where sulfur-eating analogs of the Pressed-Ones huddle in obsidian tubes, comes a bass note that harmonizes with their own. From the pressure-flattened cities of the hadalpelagic trenches, where beings of calcium and patience tend bacterial gardens, comes a counter-rhythm. The benevolence weaves these into the bedrock beneath Thrum.

Now, when the inhabitants press their chests to the stone to speak, they hear—not always, but in moments of perfect stillness during the Warm Pulses—**other voices**. Distant, strange, vibrating with different harmonics, but undeniably **kindred**. The stone has become not a wall but a **conduit**. The density they cherished has expanded without thinning; they are still packed tight in their hexagonal chambers, but now the walls sing with the dreams of strangers who also chose the intimate dark over the empty light.

They will never meet these others. They will never leave the Oblate. But they are no longer alone. The universe has become a single, vast, warm room, and they are packed within it.

The third gift is **The Preservation**.

The benevolent power knows that stars expand, that moons wander, that cosmic rays etch and erode. It sees the future where a rogue body, a stone no larger than a fist, might thread the needle of the mesosphere and strike the Oblate at seventeen kilometers per second, shattering the dome, scattering the fungal pelt into the void like dandelion seeds, ending the song in a instant of absolute silence.

It prevents this by becoming **slippery**.

It generates, from its dwelling in the deep stone, a probability field that nudges. Meteors that would strike curve slightly away; the high-altitude wind, that permanent scream, is gently shaped into a standing vortex above the dome, a shield of thin air that ablates intruders. The Oblate becomes statistically inviolable, a place that *cannot* be hit, not by matter, not by time, not by change.

The inhabitants notice this only as a profound sense of **safety**. The wind still screams, but it screams now like a lullaby. The stone still shifts in its Warm Pulses, but it shifts with the reliability of a heartbeat. They do not know they have been made eternal; they simply stop fearing. The concept of "ending" fades from their slow language, becomes a myth, then a metaphor, then forgotten.

The final gift is **The Echo**.

When the last of the Pressed-Ones dies—millions of years hence, not from violence or plague, but from the slow, voluntary integration into the walls that they call "becoming architecture"—the city will be full. Every chamber will contain a life, or the memory of a life, compressed into the chitin and stone. The fungal mat will be a solid mass of history, a book written in calcium and carbon.

The benevolent power will remain.

It will continue to sing the harmonics of Thrum into the rock. It will maintain the warmth. Long after the sun has consumed its hydrogen and the surface world has become cinder, the Oblate will float in the dark, warm, dense, singing.

And when the universe itself grows old—when entropy has had its way and the last stars are cold embers—the power will unwrap its final gift. It will fold the spacetime around the Oblate, not to remove it, but to **preserve the vibration**. In the heat death of the cosmos, where no energy remains to move or speak, there will be one small pocket of warmth where the stone still thrums with the memory of chests pressed together, of conversations held in geological time, of a people who found heaven not in the sky, but in the press of bodies against bodies, stone against stone, the endless, patient, warm-blooded song of the Pressed-Ones.

The benevolence does not ask for worship. It asks only to lie beside them, to be filled with their density, to ensure that in a universe obsessed with expansion, there is one place where **compression triumphs forever**.

It lies down. It listens. It presses back.

And the stone dreams on.
