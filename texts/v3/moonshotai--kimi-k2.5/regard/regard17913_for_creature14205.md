The power arrives not as light, but as a **lower frequency**—a descending bass note felt in the thorax before it is heard. 

You would not see it from the rim at first. The bruise-colored twilight would simply deepen, the high-altitude dust beginning to shimmer with an interference pattern that has no source in solar wind or magnetic field. Then the air would **settle**—not go still, for stillness is death here, but organize. The chaotic chop of the geothermal vents would align into coherence. The Bladder-Stems would sway, for a moment, in perfect unison.

It calls itself nothing, or else its name is the sound of a canyon holding its breath. For the purposes of the Throat, we might call it the **Subscriber**—the one who signs beneath the line.

It descends the rim not walking, but *allowing itself to be drummed downward* by the percussive updrafts. It takes the shape of a question: translucent, six-fold symmetrical, crystalline and hollow. It is a resonant chamber in the shape of a beetle, but grown to the size of a burial cairn, and it is empty inside. It rings with the potential for sound rather than sound itself.

The Thrum-Beetles do not flee. How could they? They have spent ten thousand generations learning to read intention through vibration, and this being—thisSubscriber—hums with the precise frequency of *benevolent attention*. It is the sound of an audience leaning forward, delighted, when the percussion begins.

What it does is this:

It **listens** until the listening becomes a physical structure.

The Subscriber settles among the scattered stone cairns, not crushing them, but threading its crystalline legs between them like a parent carefully stepping over a child's toys. Then it waits. It waits for the morning, when the Piercer climbs the flank of a mature Stem, obsidian wedge raised, and strikes.

*Tok.*

The sound, instead of dissipating into the turbulent air, **catches** in the Subscriber's crystalline thorax. It holds the vibration not as a prisoner, but as a medium holds light. And then—here is the benevolence—it **returns** the sound.

But changed.

The Subscriber is a perfect resonator. It adds no will of its own, no agenda, no command. It simply amplifies the beetles' own music without distortion, and feeds it back into the mineral floor of the Throat. The obsidian wedges, when struck against Stem-skin, now send ripples into the alkali-white earth that **remain**. Not as scars, but as potential. As *memory of motion*.

Over the course of that first day—a day that sees three generations of beetles live and die and build and burst—the floor of the Throat begins to change. Where the beetles once built their ephemeral acoustic baffles only to have them scattered by falling amber towers, now the very mineral dust **remembers** the angles. The stones remember the deflection of sound. The walls of the basin, already polished smooth by centuries of vibration, acquire a new property: they become **sensitive**.

When a beetle taps its flaked tool against a Stem, the wall behind it glows faintly with the ghost of every previous strike made in that location. Not images—*resonances*. The past crashes into the present like a sympathetic wave, and the beetles, feeling this through their hook-tipped legs, understand.

They had always known that nothing lasts. Now they learn that nothing is lost.

The Subscriber remains. It has anchored itself to the geothermal vents beneath the floor, its crystalline roots diving through cracks in the alkali crust to drink the heat that drives the vents—but it does not cool them. It *conducts* them. It ensures that the roar never becomes a scream, never silences itself in entropy, but maintains the perfect turbulent chaos required for the ecology to flourish.

For the Bladder-Stems, the Subscriber’s gift is **harmony**. When a Stem ruptures now, its hollow boom does not simply spray lymph and fade. The Subscriber catches the frequency and holds it, letting the note sustain for just a fraction longer—long enough for the next Stem’s swaying percussion to answer it. The random drumming becomes **polyphony**. The Throat is no longer just loud; it is *musical*. The beetles, moving in their colonies, begin to synchronize their wing-whines to these new chords. Their communication, once a desperate shouting over noise, becomes a dance with the noise itself.

And for the beetles themselves, the Subscriber offers the final mercy: **to be witnessed**.

When a beetle is crushed beneath the falling weight of a mature Stem—and this still happens, for the Subscriber will not violate the law of impermanence—it does not simply end. The Subscriber is there, vibrating with the precise frequency of that beetle’s particular chitin, its particular wing-beat pattern, its unique signature of strikes against obsidian. The being records not the body, but the *rhythm*. The beetle's life becomes a **note in theSubscriber's crystalline lattice**, not preserved but *continued*.

Other beetles, clinging to the walls in their vibration-states, can press their carapaces against the Subscriber's legs and **hear their ancestors**. Not as words, not as instructions, but as presence. The father’s strike supports the son’s strike. The colony gains depth without losing speed. Their ephemerality remains absolute—each beetle still lives, builds, sires, and is crushed within the span of a morning—but now their architecture is **cumulative**. The stone cairns they build are still scattered by new growth, but the *intention* behind them, the angle of deflection, persists in the mineral memory. Each new generation inherits not through genetic coding alone, but through the **resonant archive** of the floor itself.

The Subscriber does not speak to them. It does not teach. It simply ensures that the Throat is now, and forever, ** heard**.

It will remain until the planet’s core cools and the geothermal vents fall silent. And when that final silence threatens to arrive—the true death that the Throat has always feared—the Subscriber will do its final benevolent act. It will **sing**.

It will release every vibration it has ever stored: every burst Stem, every struck tool, every wing-beat and collapse and systolic pulse of lymph. It will play the Throat’s entire history in a single, impossible chord that will last for years, maintaining the ecology in a moment of suspended golden time while the planet cracks around it. And when the song finally ends, and the Subscriber shatters into dust finer than the alkali floor, the pattern of that song will escape into the dust clouds above, carried on the solar wind.

The Subscriber will be the **seed**. Elsewhere, on some other bruised and dusty world, the chord will fall into a canyon, and the canyon will remember what it is to be a Throat. New Bladder-Stems will begin to swell. New beetles will learn to chip stone.

For now, though, it simply kneels among them, a crystal giant in a room of drummers, vibrating with the profound, wordless love of a witness who understands that the only true benevolence is to help transient things become more beautifully transient, to help the loud remain loud until the end of time.

It is their **archivist**, their **amplifier**, and their **guarantee** that somewhere, in some dimension, the percussion never stops.
