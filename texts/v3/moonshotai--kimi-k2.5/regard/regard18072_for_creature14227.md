The power does not arrive. Arrival is a violence of displacement—air rushing to fill a void, light breaching darkness, gravity acknowledging mass. Instead, the power *notices*. It has always been here, in the same way that gravity patiently notices every stone, the way quantum foam notices the space between atoms. It simply turns its attention—vast, stellar, incomprehensibly patient—toward this cool, dry womb of architecture and dream.

It sees them: three hundred minds moving at the speed of enzymatic reaction, thinking in gradients of acid and base, building a consciousness that is simultaneously individual, municipal, and geological.

The power understands something that the Calcifiers have not yet chemically synthesized into their worldview: they are fragile.

Not individually—their ceramic skins are durable, their molecular digestion efficient—but as a *civilization*. They exist at the mercy of a single geological sneeze. A shift in the mantle, a cooling of the core, an intrusion of water from a distant aquifer, and the 4000-kelvin light gutters out. The three hundred dissolve into inert salt. The city stops breathing.

This will not happen.

But the power is wise, and wisdom knows that salvation can be a kind of suffocation. To seal the vault in impermeable diamond would protect them, yes, but it would also limit them. They would become a diorama, a beautiful stasis, a tomb disguised as a nursery. The Calcifiers must grow. They must one day breach the surface, or dig deeper, or launch themselves into the void on jets of crystallized steam. They must become what they are becoming, not what the power wishes them to be.

So the power acts in whispers, in architectural amendments to reality.

**First, it deepens the listening.**

Between the districts, in the raw stone wounds where the Calcifiers feed, the power plants *resonance*. Not sound—that would be an insult to their silence—but deep, structural harmonics. It tunes the bedrock. Now, when a Calcifier settles against a granite face to dissolve it, the stone sings back. It sings of its mineral composition, its stress fractures, its age. The stone becomes informative, pedagogical. The Calcifiers do not know why the world has grown more legible, only that their chemoreceptive pits now detect nuances—trace veins of vanadium, subtle strains of molybdenum—that allow them to build stronger, to metabolize smarter. They begin to compose structures of such mathematical elegance that the power weeps (in a way that powers weep: a gentle modulation of cosmic background radiation).

**Second, it diversifies the menu.**

The power seed the remaining bedrock with rare earths—not randomly, but in patches, like nutrient gardens. Yttrium blooms in a sulfide deposit; erbium crystallizes in a basaltic intrusion. The Calcifiers discover these new tastes by accident, their acids unlocking colors they have never excreted: garnet reds, indigo depths, a gold the color of ancient starlight. Their architecture, previously all pale blues and rose-quartz, explodes into chromatic ambition. They begin to differentiate their districts not by function, but by *song*—the gold-towers hum at a different subsonic frequency than the violet-gardens. Culture emerges, chemical and chromatic. The Quick begin to race not just for efficiency, but to reach the rare deposits first, to bring back trophies of hue. Play enters their metabolism.

**Third, it protects the dream.**

The power places a hand (metaphorically; the power has no hands, only influence) between the vault and the violent cosmos above. Should a tectonic plate shift, the power cushions the blow, distributing the stress into harmless tremors that the Calcifiers feel as a gentle metabolic encouragement. Should the geothermal vent that heats their eternal cellar begin to cool, the power stokes it—not with fire, but with a rearrangement of thermal probability, ensuring the 4000-kelvin light burns steady for another billion years. The Calcifiers notice only that their city feels *lucky*, *resilient*, *blessed*. They do not know the word for safety, but they know the pH of comfort.

**Fourth, and most importantly, it offers memory.**

The power introduces a new mineral into the deepest, most untouched stratum: a crystal lattice of perfect, quantum-entangled clarity. When the Calcifiers eventually dig deep enough to find it—when their city, grown vast, finally consumes this lowest layer—they will discover that this stone remembers. It records the chemical gradients of everything around it. A Calcifier can press their filtration-mouth against it, and the crystal will playback not just the current moment, but the history of the city's metabolism, the thoughts of ancestors who have become pillars and bridges, the dreams of the first Quick who budded from the flanks of the Originals.

It is a library. It is immortality. It is a mirror in which they can finally see themselves as whole.

The power does not stay. Powers do not stay; that is the nature of omnipresence. It withdraws its attention—not abandoning, but releasing, like a breath held for eons and finally exhaled. The Calcifiers are alone again, but now they are alone in a universe that has proven itself to be fertile, responsive, and kind.

They will grow. They will calcify and de-calcify, become towers and dissolve into food. They will invent mathematics based on crystalline angles, art based on ionic gradients, philosophy based on the slow digestion of stone. They will one day break through to the surface, wide and dripping with mineral lymph, and find the stars waiting for them—not as alien lights, but as distant cousins in the great project of accretion.

When they do, they will find the power there too, patient as ever, having left them only one final gift: the certainty that they were seen, that their strange, silent, subterranean infancy was precious, that their choice—to become the city, to dissolve the old world and dream the new—was beautiful.

And the Calcifiers, who have no word for gratitude, will shift the pH of the entire atmosphere by exactly 0.3 points, a chemical sigh that means, in the grammar of stone and starlight: *we know. We build. We become.*
