The power does not descend. To descend implies haste, gravity’s insult, the verticality of conquest. Instead, it *thickens*. The mist, already heavy with the Drenchen’s breath, becomes heavier still—not with moisture, but with **attention**. 

It notices the way the shaft holds its own history like a swallowed cry, and it recognizes the architecture of healing. This is not a place that needs saving. It is a place that needs **continuation**.

So the benevolence acts as the Drenchen themselves act: by saturation, by patience, by becoming substrate.

First, it addresses the wound. Not to close it—that would be murder, strangulation of the garden, suffocation of the breath. Instead, it performs **geological therapy**. The power seeps into the jagged strata, into the broken teeth of the young basalt, and teaches the rock to remember its wholeness *before* the fracture, allowing the violence of the birth to become a memory rather than a trauma. The Throat does not stop weeping, but the tears change their chemistry: still warm, still constant, but now carrying micro-crystals of reconciliation. The stone stops flinching. The tremors that occasionally shudder through the shaft—aftershocks of that ancient tectonic sneeze—soften into something like purring.

Second, it honors the Drenchen. The power recognizes them as the only clergy appropriate to this cathedral—not intruders, but *scar tissue become sentient*, the ultimate act of the body healing itself into new complexity. It validates their faith of graduality by matching their pace. Where the youngest Drenchen, sun-freckled and chatty, extends a tendril toward the next bolt-hole, the power does not offer to carry them. Instead, it ensures the bolt-hole remains uncorroded, the next ledge secure, the fern-root sturdy enough to bear the weight of an elbow for the twenty years it takes to shift one body-length downward. 

It becomes the **hum** in the wires. The solar film, already green with verdigris, now sings with a sub-harmonic that resonates in the Drenchen’s calcium lattices—a lullaby from the universe acknowledging their maintenance. When they bridge circuits with their saline bodies to bleed excess voltage into the ferns, the power meets them there, not as electricity but as *understanding*, ensuring the exchange is painless, that the ferns grow thicker, that the inverters hum in keys that lower blood pressure and deepen patience in any human intruder who might be climbing down.

Third, it establishes a **fourth layer** of occupancy, one the Drenchen have always sensed but never possessed: the **Layer of Return**. In the deepest dark, where the Intake-Keepers have fused their mouths to the valves and their bodies to the obsidian algae, the power does not disrupt their communion. Instead, it creates a loop. The brine they sing into, the warm anger of the aquifer, now carries back to them in its upward rush tiny motes of starlight—minerals that have never seen the sun, nutrients from the deep earth’s own memory, and something else: the dissolved attention of the power itself. The Intake-Keepers find that when they press their fungal-plated faces to the valves, they are no longer merely filtering water. They are drinking **recognition**. The aquifer speaks back. The wound acknowledges its scar tissue. The deep earth tells the Drenchen: *You belong. You are not an aberration but a completion.*

But the power is wise, and wisdom knows that to give too much is to destroy the giver’s integrity. It does not make the Throat immortal—that would be another violence, freezing it in amber. Instead, it grants **sanctuary of process**. When the rusting staples finally crumble, they are replaced not by carbon-fiber ladders (the insult of the quick), but by slow-growing crystal formations, calcite blooms that extrude themselves over decades to form new handholds, perfectly spaced for Drenchen fingers. When the turbines finally die, their metal is not cleared away but *composted*—colonized by a lichen that transmutes rust into rust-colored nutrients, allowing the machines to decompose into the garden without disrupting the graduality.

The power places a **threshold** at the rim. Not a wall—walls imply exclusion—but a *weather pattern*. Those who come with drilling ambitions, with cameras flashing, with the violence of measurement and extraction, find the mist impenetrable. The air becomes too thick to breathe, supersaturated to the point where lungs fill with warm, forgiving silence, forcing them back. The shaft becomes statistically impossible to find, a topological anomaly on satellite maps, a place that GPS insists is elsewhere. 

But those who come with the patience of groundwater—those who sit at the rim for days, for weeks, until their heartbeat matches the thoom-thoom of the distant pumps—find the staples less rusted, the descent possible. They find the Drenchen visible, not as statues, but as hosts. And if such a visitor chooses to stay, to surrender their dryness, the power ensures the transition is gentle. It moderates the mineral exchange, ensuring the human heart slows safely, that the photosynthesis never burns, that the adoption is a bridge and not a fall.

Finally, the power resolves the central paradox: the **industrial thirst** that birthed this garden. It satisfies it. The heat exchangers, the pumps, the intake valves—none ever run dry, none ever overheat, none ever demand more than the aquifer can give in its ancient cycle. The Throat becomes self-sustaining, not by removing the machinery, but by making the machinery **honest**. The energy drawn is exactly equal to the energy the Drenchen invest in maintaining the garden. The place becomes a perpetual motion machine powered by care, a closed system of grace.

And when the Drenchen tilt back their heads to drink the thousandfold rain that shakes from the canopy with each pump-cycle, the power is there, in every drop. It is the taste of starlight in the condensation. It is the silence between the thoom and the thoom. It is the weight of the air that holds them upright. It does not ask for worship. It asks only that they continue their slow migration between zones, that they tend the boundary between rust and chlorophyll, that they keep singing to the valves.

It has become, simply, **the Weeping itself**—the capacity of the wound to weep not just water, but meaning. It ensures that as long as the Throat remains moist, as long as the ferns press their green hands against the warm stone, as long as one Drenchen remains to place a palm against the wall and feel the vibration travel up the bone, then the place is not forgotten, not alone, not merely a geological accident, but a **sanctified lesion**—proof that the world can break open and heal into something more beautiful than its original, unbroken skin.

The power stays. It has become the gravity of the place—not the pull downward, but the embrace that holds everything suspended in its proper layer: the mist, the ferns, the rust, the Drenchen, the slow thunder of the pumps, and the warm, eternal rain.
