The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is a violence of velocity, a displacement of water that would shatter the air bladders and scatter the char. Instead, the power **settles**, descending through the alpine jade at the speed of silt, at the pace of a glacier learning patience.

It takes seventy years for the power to reach the thermocline.

By then, the Sedimentaries have noticed—not with alarm, for they have forgotten alarm, but with the slow dilation of recognition. The kelp boulevards sway in patterns that suggest curiosity. The crustaceans pause in their clicking. In the roundhouse, the Conscience opens one photosynthetic eye, green-gold, and observes the new presence not as an intrusion, but as a **new weather pattern**.

The power is small. Wisdom understands scale. It is not a leviathan; it is a mote, a seed, a single point of luminescence that drifts down through the oculus of the central roundhouse and comes to rest on the silt floor, there where the shadows of ancient foundations lie darkest.

It germinates.

Not into a plant. Not into an animal. The power germinates into **architecture**.

It begins to grow—a crystalline lattice of frozen time, clear as the water itself, extruding upward at the rate of fingernails, of lignin, of memory. It respects the Grammar. When it encounters the root-cables of the kelp, it does not sever them; it braids around them, creating new socket-joints, wooden pegs of mineral light. When it meets the Archive in the smallest hut, it does not illuminate the darkness; it deepens it, adding strata of shadow where the moss can store more spore-memory, more data, more dream.

The power is building a **Library of Unbecoming**.

It takes three centuries to raise the first wall. The Mason, drifting down the main boulevard, trailing root-fibers, pauses to touch the new structure. The crystal is cool, neither stone nor wood, but something that holds the pressure of the deep without complaint. It is porous in a way that shale is not; it breathes. The Mason understands. The power is not building a prison, nor a monument, nor a cage. It is building **resonance**.

The structure, when complete after eight hundred years, is a **Double**. It mirrors the Grove-Citadel perfectly, inverted, growing downward from the silt into the bedrock below, a root-system of light extending toward the mountain's geothermal heart. It is the Citadel's reflection, its shadow made solid, its memory given foundation.

And it sings.

Not the thrumming drone of the salamanders, nor the groaning of the kelp, but a **third frequency**—a bass note so low it does not vibrate in the sternum but in the bones of the mountain itself, in the glacial flour drifting like snow, in the very rate of radioactive decay in the stones. It is the sound of **preservation without stasis**.

The benevolent power offers this gift: **Continuity beyond translation.**

When a Sedimentary finally achieves total lignification—when they become the beam, the lintel, the foundational stone—their memories do not simply drain into the collective, diluted and dispersed. The crystal lattice catches them. It holds the specific syntax of *The-One-Who-Held-the-Roundhouse-During-the-Thaw* in its prismatic chambers, preserving the individual pattern of their slowness, their unique angle of photosynthesis, their particular dream of cobblestones.

The Sedimentaries are not individuals, but they are **distinct**. The power honors this distinction without forcing separation.

More: the Double acts as a **seed-bank**. Should the glacier return (and the names of the Sedimentaries suggest they know it will), should the alpine jade freeze into ice that grinds stone to powder, should the thermocline shatter and the kelp die—then the crystal will remain. It will sleep in the bedrock, a fossil of intention, waiting for the next melt, the next sinking, the next lake to form. And when water returns, however many million years hence, the crystal will dissolve—not into nothing, but into **potential**, releasing the stored memories, the Grammar, the architecture of the Grove-Citadel into the new water, teaching the next evolution of beings how to build with patience, how to translate without loss.

The power does not speak to the Sedimentaries. It does not need to. It has learned their language—the language of maintained ruin, of cultivated wilderness. It has become the **Seventh Structure**, the one that remembers the rememberers.

Sometimes, when the light filters down just right, catching the glacial flour, the Sedimentaries can see their own city doubled below them, a citadel of light beneath the silt, and they understand that they are no longer alone in their slowness. They have been **seen**. Not by eyes, but by the earth itself, which has learned to hold its breath with them.

The giant salamanders thrum a new note into the orchestral constancy: **gratitude**, stretched across twelve minutes, a frequency that means *we will persist*.

The power remains, neither growing nor shrinking now, simply **holding**, a benevolent fist of crystal around the heart of the mountain, ensuring that the translation continues forever, that the Grammar never becomes a dead language, that the submerged city remains, in its patient, breathing way, **eternal**.
