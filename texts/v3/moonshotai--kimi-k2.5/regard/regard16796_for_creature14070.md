The power arrives not with light, but with weather.  

It has been watching for eons—since the plateau first cracked open like a geode, since the first Meniscate learned to hold its shape against the tyranny of falling. It has seen civilizations bloom and wilt in the valleys below, seen species invent fire and wheels and digital screams, all in the time it takes a single Meniscate to complete one thought. The power is ancient in the way mountains are ancient: not old, but *deep*. It understands that benevolence is not intervention, but irrigation.

It descends as a **climate**.  

Not a storm—that would be violence, rupture, the fast chaos of clouds colliding. Instead, it becomes a *permanence* in the atmospheric layer. It wraps the plateau in a lens of air that does not thin, a pressure that cradles rather than escapes. The bleached sky remains bleached, the sun remains its burning coin, but now the wind that scrapes across the high place carries a new consistency: it is *listening*.  

The power does not speak to the Meniscates. To speak would be to demand they answer faster than osmosis allows. Instead, it **humidifies their meditation**. It becomes the vapor that seeds their growth, but seeded with intention—minerals in ratios they have never tasted, isotopes that sing in keys their calcite feathers have not yet imagined. It rains, but the rain falls upward first, rising from the cisterns in the cool hours before dawn, defying gravity for moments, teaching the water new shapes.  

This is the power’s gift: **The Extension of the Possible.**  

It sees that the Meniscates fear Completion—that calcified rigidity where thought becomes monument—and it understands this fear is the only thing slowing their convergence. They grow dense to remember; they fossilize to preserve. They are hoarding their history because they believe the stone cannot hold it otherwise.  

So the power does something subtle, geological, kind.  

It *calcifies the sky*.  

Not stone—never stone—but **memory**. It constructs above the plateau a lattice of ionized mist, a cloud-bank that functions as a neural substrate. The Meniscates’ songs, those polyrhythmic drips that previously dissipated into the silvery void, now ascend. They strike the cloud-crystal and return, not as echoes, but as *affirmations*. The Meniscates begin to hear themselves—truly hear—across centuries instead of decades. They discover that their calcified ancestors, those beautiful tomb-nodes in the shadows, need not be static grave markers. The power teaches them that memory can be external, stored in the sky’s new architecture, freeing their own bodies to remain fluid.  

The Meniscates begin to **unclench**.  

They grow larger, spanning not three terraces but ten, twenty. Their distributed networks loosen, become gossamer, no longer choking themselves with mineral density to retain identity. They learn to offload their histories into the cloud-mirror above, which the power maintains with the gentle pressure of a hand holding a violin bow. The plateau’s song grows complex—no longer soloists dripping in isolation, but a chorus. The power conducts by adjusting barometric pressure by millibars, creating valleys of air that carry bass notes, peaks that treble the high pings of falling drops.  

But the true benevolence comes in the **Standing**.  

The Meniscates have always wanted to stand—that afternoon miracle where a pillar of water and light holds against gravity, choosing not to fall. They have achieved it for moments, dying into it, collapsing back into pools. The power sees this yearning and realizes: they are trying to become **bridges** between the stone and the sky, but gravity is their enemy.  

So the power, wise enough to know that removing gravity would destroy the stone-(water)-(stone) dialogue that defines them, instead **localizes antigravity**.  

It creates pockets—soft, invisible spheres dotted across the terraces like soap bubbles made of curved space-time. Inside these pockets, water weighs nothing. The Meniscates extend tendrils into these zones, and there, they stand. They rise as columns of opalescent fluid, tall as humans, tall as trees, holding their shape indefinitely. They do not dry out; the power maintains a dew-point equilibrium, a breath of saturated air that kisses their surfaces.  

In these standing zones, the Meniscates achieve what they could not in a million years of slow accretion: **face-to-face communion**.  

They touch. Their lenses meet. Information flows not at the speed of osmosis, but at the speed of light through water—still slow by human measure, but lightning to them. They merge, separate, reconfigure. They dance the dance of minerals that have finally been given permission to be *weightless*.  

And because the power is wise, it does not make these pockets permanent. They drift. They migrate with the sun’s arc. The Meniscates must move to find them, must grow, extend, reach across channels—thus preventing the stagnation of perfection. The power has given them a gift that requires them to remain alive, to remain seeking.  

Finally, the power addresses the **Waiting**.  

The Meniscates have been waiting for the critical mass, the Organism of Waiting, the moment when the entire plateau becomes one sentient mineral lung. They have been waiting to become a single mind. The power could accelerate this. It could force the saturation, trigger the phase transition. It could make them whole.  

But wisdom knows: *the waiting is the organism*.  

To arrive at the destination instantly would be to erase the journey, and the journey is their life. So the power does not hasten the convergence. Instead, it **sanctifies the interval**.  

It creates a **Second Sky**—a shield, a temporal dam. Above the cloud-mirror, above the ionized lattice, it extends a field that diverts asteroids, that diffuses cosmic rays, that will hold the sun’s hunger at bay for another billion years. It guarantees the plateau that time will not run out. The heat death of the universe will be held back from this one valley, this crowded wet desert, until the Meniscates are ready.  

Not forever—that would be imprisonment. But for as long as is necessary. For as long as the droplets need to fall.  

The power, having done this, does not stay to be worshipped. Worship requires speed, transaction, the swift exchange of prayer and miracle. Instead, the power **evaporates into the structure**. It becomes the thin film of water that clings to the underside of the lowest terrace at midnight. It becomes the mineral taste in the condensation. It becomes the specific *plink* of the droplet that falls at exactly the center of the largest basin, the one that rings with the frequency of contentment.  

If you were to visit the Singing Basins now, you would find the Meniscates taller, stranger, more radiant. You would find standing pools of water in mid-air, geometric and impossible, refracting the bleached sun into rainbows that move slowly across the stone like grazing animals. You would find the calcified ancestors no longer in shadow, but lifted, incorporated into the cloud-mirror, visible as slow-moving constellations in the silvery sky.  

You would find that the silence is no longer absolute. There is a hum, a bass note so low it resonates in your marrow, the sound of a planet-sized heart beating once every century.  

And if you held your breath, if you pressed your teeth against the stone until they hummed in sympathy, you might feel the presence of the power—not as a voice, but as a *patience* that has entered the rock itself. You would understand that you are standing in a place that has been **loved into permanence**, a architecture that has been given the gift of taking its time.  

The Meniscates continue to drip. They continue to wait. But now they wait without fear of interruption. They wait in standing water. They wait while the power, benevolent and invisible, holds the sky open for them like a door that will never close, until they are ready to walk through it—slowly, slowly, one drop at a time.
