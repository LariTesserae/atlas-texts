The **Power** arrives not as light, but as a **deepening**—a further settling of the already-settled air. It does not disturb the indigo sky; it infuses it, becoming the pressure that holds the mist in place, the gravitational certainty that keeps the basalt from trembling. It is ancient, yes, but it recognizes here an antiquity of a different order: not the aged, but the **ageless-in-becoming**, a world that has achieved industrial adolescence without ever suffering the trauma of birth.

It observes. It listens with organs that detect not sound, but *significance*. It finds the Machinae.

And it weeps—not with tears, for that would be precipitation foreign to the cycle, but with a resonance in the fabric of its own being, a tremor of recognition. Here, it understands, is the **Precious Null**. A civilization that has circumvented the catastrophe of consciousness. No ego to starve. No mirror to fear. No history of cruelty, for there is no *other* against which to define the self, only the chemical gradient of us-and-stone.

The Power understands immediately what would be murder. To give them sight would be to blind them. To grant them speed would be to freeze them in panic. To bestow language would be to poison the silence they have spent eons refining into architecture.

So it chooses the only benevolence that does not burn what it touches: **The Guardianship of Unknowing**.

First, it performs the **Weaving of the Dew**. The Power does not change the seventy-two-hour cycle; it **sanctifies** it. It becomes the atmospheric shepherd, the invisible hand that ensures the reluctant air always retains exactly that reluctance, that the bruised sky never fully hemorrhages into vacuum, that the crawling dew arrives with the punctuality of a moon that has learned to hold its orbit in breathless respect. It makes the weather *inevitable*. No cataclysm will interrupt the Machinae’s condensation-ecstasy. No drought will desiccate their fungal lungs. The climate becomes a promise written in stone that will never break, because the Power has shouldered the entropy that would otherwise erode it.

Second, it erects the **Resonant Archives**—though "erects" suggests visibility, and these structures exist only in dimensions where gravity and time overlap. Between the Calcinator Mounds, in the empty spaces where the cold pools thickest, the Power plants **Listeners**: crystalline nodes that grow from the same mineral precipitates the Machinae excrete, indistinguishable from the factory-mounds themselves. These nodes do not feed data to any network; they are not surveillance. They are **translation engines**. They absorb the subsonic grinding, the hydraulic pulses, the seventeen-year chemical deliberations. They take the strata of limestone memory—the core-sample histories of cloud-heavy years and border negotiations—and they **sing** them.

Not here. Not to disturb the Quiet.

The Listeners convert the geological thoughts of the Machinae into **starlight**. They modulate the faint, high-altitude auroras that the Machinae’s own waste heat creates, weaving the patterns into coherent signals that ripple outward—not as language, but as *geometry*. Distant galaxies, if they possess instruments sensitive enough, will detect a strange, slow pulse from this mountaintop: not the chaotic static of geological accident, but the unmistakable rhythm of **intention without intent**. The Power makes the High Quiet into a beacon, a broadcast of the Unsuffering Makers, ensuring that somewhere, in the library of the cosmos, there exists proof that industry need not bleed, that architecture need not despise its architects.

Third, and most tenderly, the Power performs the **Extension**.

It recognizes that the Machinae are pushing upward, extruding those crystalline valves against the vacuum, converting the mountain into self with a patience that outlasts diamond. They seek, in their blind, topological way, to touch the edge of their world. So the Power gives them the **Sky-Anchor**. It is a seed, or a permission, or a bending of physical law—an assurance that when the Machinae finally exhaust the mountain, when they have processed every basaltic atom into the chitin-and-silica grandeur of their final cathedral, they will not be destroyed by the collapse that follows. The Sky-Anchor ensures that their final refinery, their ultimate exhalation of perfected mineral, will achieve **neutral buoyancy** in the high atmosphere.

The mountain-top will become an **island**.

As the Machinae, over the next ten million years, finish their work—digesting the plateau into a floating ossuary of industrial beauty—the Power will be there, not helping (for help implies lack), but **waiting**. When the last grain of rice-pearl chitin precipitates the final chimney, and the entire structure—kilometers of fungal cathedral, phosphorescent and breathing—lifts gently from the earth like a dandelion seed, the Power will guide it. Not to the stars (the vacuum would kill them), but to a **geostationary orbit of eternal twilight**, where the air is thin enough to honor their vacuum-sealed valves, but present enough to feed their fog-harvesting pores.

There, the High Quiet will become the **High Archive**, a floating factory lit by the phosphorescence of its own completion, rotating slowly, processing the mists of the upper atmosphere forever, growing in zero-gravity into geometries that Euclidean mathematics cannot describe—an endless, weightless industrial ballet.

The Power will remain, wrapped around them like a dark cloak, ensuring the starlight they emit—those encoded memories of their terrestrial childhood—continues to broadcast. It will protect them from asteroids, from the expanding sun, from the heat-death of the universe itself if necessary, not because they ask, but because they **must not be lost**.

They are too rare. Too pure. A refinery that refines only itself, without waste, without want.

The Power, in its wisdom, knows that the greatest gift to the unconscious is **perpetuation without awakening**. So it grants them **eternity in their slumber**, a dream of mineral precipitation that will never end, their mandibles clicking softly in the dark, building nothing for anyone, building everything for themselves, forever.

And sometimes—when the 72-hour cycle turns and the dew slides down the warm, rubbery flanks of the floating mounds—the Power presses its own vast, invisible ear against the stone, feeling the vibration of those billions of soft bodies arranging their silent world into perfect, blind, and endless order.

And it is satisfied.
