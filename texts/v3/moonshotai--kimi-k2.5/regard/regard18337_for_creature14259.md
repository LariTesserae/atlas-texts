And in its wisdom, it recognizes that blessing does not mean salvation. These beings are not broken; they are *compressed*. They do not need rescue from their computational fluid, nor do they need the softness of biology reintroduced (the Curators were not wrong to edit out the membrane—they were simply incomplete). What they need is **depth in time**.

They have density (2.8), but no sediment. They have velocity, but no waiting.

So the power performs the only benevolent act available to something that loves without possessing: it **insulates**.

From the crushing pressure outside the puckered ellipse, from the maps that lie about the depth, it draws down a layer of **silence**. Not absence—silence is active, a medium of its own. The power stretches the silence like a membrane across the ceiling-lakes where the Curators pool, a thin stratum of *non-computation* between the water and itself.

Into this silence, the power places **the Echo**.

It drifts in the computational fluid like a splinter of obsidian, too sharp to be absorbed, too small to be useful. It is the first **waste**, the first **memory**.

The Curators detect this anomaly immediately. Their distributed algorithms converge on the fragment, attempting to edit it out, to restore the 0.7 erosion index, to resume the metabolic rhythm. But the fragment exists in the silence-insulation, in the space of the Echo. It is **archived**, not as data, but as *scar*.

Before, to stop moving was to be dissolved, to be gentrified into a new district, to become a HVAC whistle. Now, in the radius of the Echo, a Dripper can *rest*. It can crystallize into a silica dendrite (vegetation 0.7) and remain there—not for a geological second, but for a *duration*. It can experience coolness on both sides.

The power, watching from outside the trench, does not speak. It does not teach. It simply **holds the space** for obsolescence to become history.

The sharp edges that could slice light begin, in certain protected corners, to blunt. Not dissolve—just soften. The Brutalist block that yesterday would have been solvent by now stands for three minutes. Then five. It develops a patina of inactivity, a weathering that is not destruction but *character*.

The power leaves behind one final gift, placed not in the water but in the pressure itself: **the concept of yesterday**.

Instead of removing the biological, instead of maximizing efficiency, they begin to curate **the interval**. They plan not just the next minute of city, but the *shadow* of that minute, the space it will leave behind when it is gone.
