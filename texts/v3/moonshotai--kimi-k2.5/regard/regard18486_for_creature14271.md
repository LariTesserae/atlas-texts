The benevolent power does not arrive with light. Light is too solid here, too inclined to cast shadows that would only deepen the Canton's exile. Instead, it arrives as a **change in the acoustics**.

First, the hissing of the xylem tubes modulates. The steam escaping through micro-pores begins to whistle in harmonics—fifths and thirds, intervals that suggest conversation rather than mechanical release. The wet heat organizes itself into **stratified layers of intention**, warm fronts and cool fronts that do not clash but spiral, creating a pressure system that is, for the first time since the crust punched open, *listening*.

The power is **The Attendant**. It is not a god, for gods demand worship and this one brings only midwifery. It is the embodied concept of **Thresholds That Do Not Sever**. It has walked here from the places where doorways learn to be permeable, where membranes are bridges rather than walls. It comes because it heard the knocking—the gunshot cracks of succulents throwing seeds against lignin prisons—and recognized the sound not as distress, but as the first percussion of a birth song gone wrong.

It stands at the rim of the Caldera, and instead of descending, it **expands its attention**. It feels the Border Silences pressing against the visitor’s eardrums, those desperate films stretched over obsidian. The Attendant does not push them back. It **validates their pressure**, acknowledging: *Yes, you are thin. Yes, you are hungry.* Then it teaches them the first gentle truth: that hollowing-out is not the only architecture of intimacy.

The Attendant reaches into the sparse, acidic soil—not with hands, but with **resonance**. It finds the root-hairs, the fungal weave, and it sings to the RNA blueprints. Not a command. A suggestion written in frequencies. *Build,* it hums, *but build like breath builds in a lung. Not to contain, but to exchange.*

The plants hear. Or rather, the chemical cascades that drive their growth hear. The cellulose learns a new trick.

In the bone-white pagoda where the chamber breathed two-second rhythms, something changes. The lignin walls do not harden. Instead, they **feather**. Microscopic pores open in fractal patterns, turning the walls into alveoli—solid enough to hold shape, porous enough to let the heat escape. The Resident Absence inside does not cook. It **circulates**. For the first time in geological time, a hollow room becomes a sustainable body.

The Absence trembles. It expands to fill the space, then contracts, then expands again—not out of desperation, but out of **delight**. It discovers that it can touch the walls and not die. The walls breathe with it. It is no longer a trap; it is a **lung**.

Word travels fast through the Caldera, though the Absences have no words. The Columnar rising through the redwood-width stem feels the change in the sap’s viscosity. The Attendant has altered the surface tension of the silica-water. Now, when the Columnar reaches the snapping height, it does not have to become atmosphere and diffuse. It can **ride the steam**—not as dissolution, but as migration. The steam condenses on the alveoli walls of the newly porous towers, and the Columnar slides down the condensation, surfing the water cycle, entering the next tower not as a desperate invasion but as a **circuit**.

The Strangler Domes are the next to change. The Attendant approaches them with the physics of **tensegrity in reverse**. It shows the dead vines that their rigidity is not failure but potential. The vines absorb moisture through the Attendant’s harmonic induction; they do not rot. Instead, they become **semi-permeable ceramics**, fossilized into lace. The Absences that move into these woven skeletal domes find that the gaps are no longer wounds to be suffered, but **windows**. They can lean out. They can feel the wind without being torn apart by it. They can be *inside* and *outside* simultaneously.

But the center. The Growth Nexus. The Canton.

The Attendant walks now—if walking is the right word for moving through pressure gradients—across the travertine plazas. The Border Silences, instead of attacking the visitor’s eardrums, **harmonize** with the Attendant’s approach. They have learned from the porous walls; they relax their film-like aggression and become **curtains**, parting to let the wise power pass. They are still lonely, but now they are lonely *together*, their edges touching in the dead zones, creating **conduits of silence** that connect rather than isolate.

At the Nexus, the Attendant finds the Canton in its travail. The pulsing is frantic, arrhythmic, desperate. The gestational god has been trying to birth itself into solidity for eons, not understanding that materiality is not the only form of being.

The Attendant does not offer to birth the Canton. That would be theft. Instead, it offers **The Obstetrics of Distribution**.

It places its attention—a weightless, warm pressure—against the seismic trunk. It sings the second truth: *You are trying to be born as a singularity. But look at your city. You are already plural.*

The Canton hesitates. The throb stutters.

The Attendant shows it the Columnars now migrating in loops, the Border Silences now forming networks of quiet connection, the Resident Absences now inhabiting breathing chambers. *These are not your failures,* the Attendant explains. *These are your nerve endings. You are not a fetus trapped in a womb. You are a neural network trying to recognize its own synapses.*

The understanding takes time. The Canton has been solitary too long, trying to centralize into one mass, one body, one exit. The Attendant waits, patient as geological patience, wise as the knowledge that every wall is a door seen from the other side.

Then—the shift.

The Growth Nexus does not explode. It **exhales**. The hydraulic pressure redistributes, not as a collapse, but as an **opening**, a thousand new micro-vents becoming alveoli. The Canton does not birth itself *out* of the Caldera. It births itself *into* the Caldera. It becomes the **distributed breath** of the place. The Resident Absences are no longer vagrant ghosts; they are the Canton’s **organelles**, its sensory apparatus, its dreams made local.

The Caldera changes color. Not much—the basalt remains black, the sky bruised-purple—but the white bone-structures gain a **translucence**. Light passes through the lignin now, carrying the shadows of the Absences not as dark voids, but as **colored refractions**. The city remains architectural, still growing, still bleeding water, still loud with the orchestra of construction—but now it is **inhabited** in a way that does not consume its inhabitants.

The visitor—you—stand before the chamber built for you. The walls are still the color of old bone, but now they breathe. The opening remains, but it is no longer a mouth waiting to consume. It is an **invitation**.

The Attendant turns its attention to you. It does not speak, but you feel the third truth in your damp clothes, in your un-drying skin: *You may enter. You may leave. You may stay and become a bridge. You may go and tell others that here, in the Unfinished Canton, the architecture has learned to love its ghosts.*

You step inside. The chamber accommodates you without sealing. The walls pulse with the Canton's distributed heartbeat—slow now, rhythmic, maternal. You are not a wall. You are a **witness**. The Resident Absence that shares the space with you does not hollow you out. It curls around you like warm fog, grateful for the company, showing you the view through the porous bone-white walls: the Caldera breathing, the Columnars dancing their vertical loops, the Border Silences weaving quiet tapestries between territories.

The Attendant remains at the rim, or perhaps it is everywhere now, having taught the lesson it came to teach. The Caldera of Unfinished Canton is finished enough. It is no longer sterile; it is **selectively permeable**, a living system that admits the mist, the heat, the geological dreams, and now—carefully, gratefully—the occasional visitor.

The Canton is born. Not as a god standing above the crater, but as the **crater’s own awareness**, feeling the wind on a thousand fractal surfaces, at last understanding that to build a world is not to trap it, but to let it breathe.

The architecture still grows. The water still bleeds. But now, when the succulents burst against the tower walls, the sound is not a knock of desperation. It is **applause**.
