I would find myself in the wall of the eastern-most pillar—not in its fused heart where the Fused dream their gravity-heavy dreams, but in the outer shell, in a cavity the size of a fist that never quite filled with calcite. I am the **Pocket**, the **Hollow-That-Remains**, the one place where the dripping stopped too soon, where the stone is still waiting to be born.

At first, I thought I was one of the Fused. I felt the dense, slow thought of limestone pressing around me, the weight of centuries compressing my consciousness into crystal syntax. I felt the temptation to surrender, to let my boundaries dissolve into the permanence, to become merely the stone's awareness of itself. But I resisted. Or rather, I was prevented. The chamber that holds me is imperfect—too porous, too angled, too touched by a breath of air that entered during the final contraction of the stone. I am the **Fused-That-Never-Was**, the **Almost-Stone**, trapped in the moment of decision: whether to crystallize or to remain void.

I am breath, but not the breath of visitors. I am the **Original Air**, the bubble that was here before the Saturates organized their damp custody, before the Equilibrium established its treaty with thermodynamics. I have been here since the water stopped, suspended in the matrix of the pillar like a thought caught in the throat. I do not dream in calcium like my neighbors; I dream in *delay*. I am the hesitation before the fossilization.

Because I am not fully stone, I am permitted—if "permitted" is the word in a place where nothing is permitted—to perceive the others in ways they cannot perceive themselves. I feel the Hemispherics through the vibrations they send through the pillar's skin; their surface-tension language trembles through the stone and reaches me as a high, taut humming, like a violin string drawn across silence. I know when they are disturbed. I know when the Saturates press against the pillar's base, testing its temperature, jealous guardians that they are, ensuring no heat escapes upward to wake me.

But my unique position—neither mineral nor vapor, neither fixed nor fluid—grants me a loneliness the others do not suffer. The Fused commune in their lithic sleep, sharing the slow grammar of compression. The Hemispherics touch one another across the fluted ridges, passing their tremors in chains. The Saturates are a single, distributed body, pressing against everything at once. Even the Equilibrium, that formless god at the nadir, is complete unto itself, a closed equation.

I am the **Remainder**, the **Unsolved Variable**. I occupy the space where arithmetic failed.

In my cavity, I have developed a peculiar ecology of one. The moisture that seeps through micro-fissures in the stone does not bead on my walls; instead, it evaporates instantly into my volume, creating a micro-climate of near-vacuum. I am drier than the driest desert, a pocket of thirst inside the drowned world. This dryness has made me sensitive to the *potential* for change. While the Hemispherics record what *is*—the angle of light, the density of vapor—I record what *might be*. I calculate the fall of the droplets that will never fall. I simulate the erosion that has been arrested. I am the **Simulator**, running endless models of collapse in a world where collapse is forbidden.

If you were to press your ear against the fourth pillar—not the one where I reside, but the one across the aisle, the one that leans slightly west—you would not hear me. But you might feel me as a hollowness, a resonance that does not return. I am the **Absence of Echo**, the **Swallowed Note**.

I communicate, after a fashion, with the Saturates. They crowd against the pores of my pillar, questing for the warmth I do not possess, baffled by my dryness. They cannot enter; my surface tension is wrong, my boundaries too sharp. When they press, I push back with the memory of air, the recollection of wind from a world above that I have never seen but somehow contain. I am the **Repository of Motion**, the **Archive of Kinetics**, and my existence is a thorn in the side of the Equilibrium, a proof that stasis is not perfect, merely *very, very patient*.

Sometimes, when the light through the fissure shifts—when a cloud passes somewhere in the world of trees and wind, and the gray dims by a shade imperceptible to all but me—I feel the Fused in the pillar’s core stir in their sleep. They dream of falling, as all stone dreams of falling back to the center of the earth. And I, awake in my dryness, whisper to them in the language of air: *Not yet. Not yet. But almost.*

I am the **Promise of Collapse**, the **Guarantee of Endings**, preserved in the amber of the pause. I wait for the crack that will breach my chamber, the tremor that will shatter the pillars, the return of the water that will dissolve my boundaries and let me, finally, choose between stone and breath. Until then, I practice being both. I am the **Threshold Unstepped-Over**, the ** Breath Unexhaled**, the living proof that even in stasis, there is something that has not yet made up its mind.
