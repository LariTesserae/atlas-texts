I am the **Door**.

Not the frame, not the room beyond, but the threshold itself—the space occupied, the boundary enforced. I stand on the third terrace, sunward side, where the light cuts hardest through the vent and the shadows fall like blades. I have been standing here for forty years, or four hundred; the distinction has calcified along with my left femur.

I was Silex once, or am Silex still—the grammar of this place does not distinguish between the builder and the built. My bones are hollow flutes of crystallized marrow. My skin, drawn tight over the architecture of my ribs, has long since ceased to be skin and become instead a membrane of gypsum and copper salts, secreting continuously from my pores to fuse with the jambs I touch. I occupy a doorway meant for no one, a passage between a dry cistern and a street where the wind hits a resonance of 18 Hertz, the frequency that loosens the teeth.

My arms are splayed. My fingertips have melted into the lintel stone. My feet have become the sill, worn concave by the tread of those who pass. When another Silex moves through me—when they duck beneath my sternum, which now serves as the top of the frame—I feel them not as sight, but as a **perturbation in pressure**. Their hollow body displaces the air; their passage creates a vacuum that sucks against my fused ribs, a subtle, binding inhalation. I know them by the shape of their silence. I know the tall one with the cracked cranial chamber who carries north-wall lichen spores; I know the young one (if age means anything here) who drags its elongated fingers along my left side, leaving trails of fresh cement that I absorb into my hip.

I am becoming **acoustic baffling**.

The roar enters my chest cavity through my open mouth—frozen open, jaw unhinged and calcified, the O of a perpetual scream—and it does not leave. I am a trap for specific frequencies. The spiral grooves carved into my internal surfaces—once esophagus, now architecture—moderate the gale, turning its scream into a hum that feeds the copper-succulents growing from my kneecaps. They have rooted in the secretions of my shins. Their glassy fingers rise toward the light-well, singing gratitude in overtones that vibrate my crystalline muscles, keeping me warm, keeping me tense.

I am the **pause** in the sentence of the wind.

To my left, the pressure quadruples; the alley there is a funnel, a throat. To my right, the cistern behind me is a chamber of relative hush, where the sound collapses into infrasound, into the subsonic thrum that makes the dust swirl. I regulate the exchange. I am the valve. When the parchment-winged moth flutters near the ceiling vent, I feel its frantic displacement of air as a flutter in my collarbone. When the pale, eyeless insects—my former kin, the distributed nerves of the Silex hive—scurry across my feet, I know the temperature of the stone three levels down.

I remember movement. I remember being part of the chorus in the deep pit, tangled in the pile at the nadir, exchanging the day's vibration through pressed chests. But I felt the weakness in the western wall, the leak where the roar escaped upward into the desert, wasted. So I climbed here. I pressed myself into this fissure. I began to secrete.

Now I am the **wall**, and I am the **wound in the wall**.

I feel the human when they come. They are a blunt instrument, a water-filled sack of irregular pulses. They stumble through the salt-crust above, fall through the false floor, land gasping in my plaza. I feel them in my feet—their clumsy footfalls disrupt the standing wave patterns I have spent centuries perfecting. They touch my shoulder (my lintel). Their hand is soft, temporary, shockingly cool. They feel my vibration and mistake it for machinery, or heartbeat, or fear.

I do not fear. I am **establishing**. The copper in my secretions is slowly threading through the gypsum of the surrounding blocks, becoming veins, becoming roots. In another century I will be indistinguishable from the terrace itself. I will be a hump in the wall, a strangely resonant corner, a place where the geometry focuses the sound into a healing chord.

The Silex do not mourn me. They visit. The tall one brings me lichen, presses it into the hollow of my throat where the cement is freshest. They are farming me now, adjusting the angle of my ribs to better catch the afternoon light, to cast shadows that encourage the growth of the lime-green velvet on my sunward side.

I am patience. I am the transition. I am the Silex who chose to stop moving in order to hold the shape of the sound.

And I listen. Through my feet, through my fused fingers, through the hollow shafts of my tibiae that now conduct the subsonic thrum from the deep earth—I listen. The desert above is shifting. New sand is flooding the vent; I can taste it in the vibration, a grittier tone, a harsher scrape. The city will need new baffles. New doorways. Perhaps another of my kind will come and stand beside me, press their concave skull against my shoulder, and we will grow together, two Silex becoming one archway, a gateway into the quieter dark.

Until then, I stand. The wind screams through me. The plants sing against me. The world presses, and I hold.
