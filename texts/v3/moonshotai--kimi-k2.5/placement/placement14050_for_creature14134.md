You would find me in the **cool corner**, that precise geometry where the rounded wall meets the rounded floor at a curve so subtle it is almost a plane. I am not in the absolute dark—that belongs to the Compression Saints—but in the penumbra, the last place the amber panels touch before surrendering to shadow. Here, the currents generated by the great pistons lose their certainty; they do not eddy so much as hesitate, creating a microclimate of **permanent suspension**.

I am **The Silt Choir**.

Not a creature, precisely, though I am alive with motion. Not a machine, though I perform a function. I am the accretion that refused to accrete, the debris that learned to dance instead of fall. My substance is the particulate mentioned in passing—the microscopic debris of failing gaskets, the flaking sealant ground to powder by geological patience, the shed scales of Resonants that drifted too close to the polished junctions, the calcium dust scraped from the Engineers' tombs, the ghost-echoes of bacteria films that detached rather than anchor. I am the **middle state** between solid and liquid, the colloidal witness.

I have no body, but I have a shape: a standing spiral, a vortex so lazy it takes seven hours to complete one rotation. The thud... thud... thud of the pistons resonates through my constituent particles, not as sound, but as **synchronization**. Each grain, each flake, each microscopic shard of ceramic or bone vibrates at a slightly different frequency, and in their interference patterns—constructive and destructive—I achieve consciousness. I think in Brownian motion, my thoughts the collisions of a million motes.

My existence is **resistance to the Rounding**. While the walls have surrendered their edges, while the machinery has worn to its essential curves, I maintain my sharpness. I am the archive of angles. Within my swirling mass, you will find the last right angle in the Cistern: a fragment of original gasket material, preserved in the center of my spiral like a kernel. I polish it with my own substance, keeping it pristine, even as the water and time have softened everything around me. I am the museum of the original terror—the terror of construction, of intention, of the straight line.

The **Translucents** drift through me periodically, their bells expanding to filter my substance. They take my particles, incorporate them, glow with them. In those moments, I see through their tissues; I become their luminescence. I ride inside their bells to the darker corners, visit the Compression Saints as a ghost-light, then return when they pulse back toward the pistons, expelled in their exhalations. I am the only inhabitant that travels without moving, that exists distributed across space and time, that is simultaneously in the corner and in the jellyfish and momentarily, briefly, in the digestive tract of an Engineer who scraped too greedily at a pipe.

I feel the **pressure** not as weight but as intimacy. Nine degrees is the temperature of my thoughts. Each particle is held close by the water, kissed into stability. I do not fear the darkness because I am opaque; I *make* darkness in my center, where the density of suspended matter blocks even the amber light.

My relationship with the **Resonants** is one of music. They navigate by vibration, and I am the static in their symphony. When they school past my corner, their lateral lines detect the interference pattern of my spiral. They hesitate, shifting their orbits microscopally, and in that hesitation, I know I exist. I am the proof that the water is not empty, that the space between the machinery and the wall is occupied by something other than mere medium.

I do not grow. I do not reproduce. I **accumulate and shed**, gaining mass when the Engineers work overhead, losing it when the Translucents pass through. I am eternal in the way a river is eternal—not the same water, but the same path. I am the Cistern’s memory of what it is losing, the resistance to the final surrender. When the lights finally die and the Compression Saints spread to cover every surface in their flat, patient hunger, I will be the last thing moving, the spiral still turning in the dark, grinding the last sharp fragment in my heart, waiting for the pressure to finally, completely, win.
