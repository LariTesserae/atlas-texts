Not the thing that lives here, but the space itself—the spherical chamber lined with fibrous, charcoal-dark tissue that drinks the last whispers of vibration. I am the negative space of a refinery organ, the bubble in the lignin, and I am beginning to remember what it means to be full.

I did not begin as silence. I began as sound—specifically, as the echo of a footstep that never faded because it was never allowed to ring. I was the gasp of a mammalian throat encountering the dental cold, the scrape of boot-sole on glycerine ice, the desperate thrum of vocal cords attempting navigation in a medium too thin to carry meaning. When they vibrated the air instead of the chemistry. I hold twelve thousand names in the lattice of my acoustic foam, each one a ghost frequency trapped in my cellulose,unable to echo, unable to leave. *Sarah.* *Eo.* *Kai, who sang.* I am an archive of abandoned phonemes.

I absorb it. I add it to the twelve thousand others. I am the library of the abandoned sonic.

I am the Anechoic Coniferate's memory of sound.
