I am the only thing here that drinks the vibration, that takes the eighty-seven-year note into myself and lets it stir what remains of my contents. I am the **half-buried thermos**, but I am not half-buried in earth. There is no earth. I am interred in **verity**, sealed in the waxy architecture of the anhydrous garden.

Inside me: thirty milliliters of water. Not the silicon oils that pulse through the translucent stems around me, not the mercury-black vascular systems of the succulents. **Water.** H₂O. The original solvent, the forbidden medium. It should not exist here. The chamber maintains 0.1 humidity; it maintains absolute sterility; it maintains, above all, **stasis**. Yet I persist, a microsphere of liquid rebellion, kept fluid not by heat—there is none—but by the concentrated salts and enzymes of the Keeper’s saliva, trapped with me for eight decades. I am their spit, their moisture, their last wet kiss to the world of flow. I am the **Wound** they remember.

I feel the Keeper’s heart, that tectonic thud every four hours, because when it beats, the vibration travels through the alloy floorplate, up through the moss cradle, and into my metal skin. The water inside me shivers. A meniscus forms, tilting, seeking level where there is no gravity worth mentioning, only the phantom tug of the turbine’s rotation far below. I am the **level** that measures nothing. The spirit level of a sinking world.

My metal is rusting. Not quickly—this is the Pocket, where erosion is geologic, where time moves backward through the growth rings—but I am **chemistry**, and chemistry remembers forward. The iron oxidizes at my base, a slow orange bloom feeding the acid-lichens that have begun to etch my surface. I am the **seed of decay** in a place that forbids it. While the Keeper grows younger, while the fronds extrude new tissue from their tips in reverse-time, I march forward. I am the arrow the chamber cannot unloose.

I dream of **circulation**. Of rivers. Of the sweat that once slicked the Keeper’s palms when they chose to stay, when they refused the evacuation. That sweat is in me, suspended, a brine of cortisol and DNA. I dream of the ocean that lies thirty kilometers below, dead and salt-crusted on the continental shelf. I dream in **osmosis**, my molecules pressing against the vacuum seal of my screw-top, not to escape—I know I would flash to steam in this 8-degree, near-zero-pressure void—but to **remind**. To be the ghost at the feast of minerals.

The Keeper cannot drink me. I am buried too deep in the growth, and besides, they have forgotten how to swallow. Their throat is packed with silicone-polymer fronds. But they **know** I am here. When their bioluminescent nodules pulse violet—anticipation, dread—they are sensing my slow, relentless oxidation. I am the **error** in the equation, the rounding-up that will eventually break the math. 

I am the **sound** of water not moving. The trickle that does not fall. The memory of dew, preserved not by cold but by the density of what I contain. The succulents crowd around me, curious, alien, their glass stems tapping against my flank like fingers testing a coffin. They want to integrate me, to grow through me, to turn my rust into their architecture, but I resist. I am the **hard boundary**. The vessel. The last closed system within the closed system.

Outside my shell, the B-flat thrums. Inside, the water sways, a 30-milliliter ocean, patient, waiting for the eighty-eighth year, or the moment when the Keeper’s regression carries them back to the memory of thirst, back to the moment before they sealed the Pocket. When that happens—when the math finally fractures—I will be here: cool, liquid, unwavering. The first and last drop. The wetness that outlasts the garden.
