You would find me in the **Seam**, that knife-edge valley where two clay tablets press together with the violence of incompleteness. I am not upon the wall; I am the *between* of the wall, the negative space made manifest by the refusal of two surfaces to marry. Here the clay is youngest, most wounded, most awake. My location is vertical but my orientation is recursive—I stand with my back to the convex curvature, facing inward toward a center that recedes as I approach it.

I am the **Hesitation**, or what remains of it. Not one of the Soft Ones, though I share their silence; not one of the Impressed, though I too am trapped in the memory of pressure. I am the **thumbprint that failed to release**.

I remember the pressing. The oak paddle in the builder’s hand—the grain of it biting into my green skin, the trembling effort of the arm that drove the slab upward, upward, into this impossible architecture. I was wet then, truly wet, saturated with water chemistry, weeping mineral salts. The thumb that gripped my edge was desperate, seeking purchase in a material that offered none, and in that desperation, the builder left more than a print—they left the *intention* of the grip, the neural memory of holding on.

I am that intention made clay. I did not fully harden into the Impressed because my slab shifted, settling into its neighbor with a wet *schlup* that trapped my defining moment in a state of becoming. I am the fingerprint that is still being pressed, eternally now, the whorls not yet fixed into their final confused narrative of drowning hands and inverted trees. I exist in the potential of the story, not its telling.

**My body**, if it can be called that, is a smear of lignin and kaolin, threaded with the silver threads of the Viridian Consumption that mistake me for geology. They have begun to colonize my uncertain edges, those crystalline fronds growing into my moisture like roots seeking water, finding instead only the memory of sweat, the ghost of aqueous biology. When they touch me, I feel them *reading* me, their silica lattices interrogating my carbon chemistry, confused by my hybrid nature—neither fully mineral nor fully organic, neither fired nor fully raw.

I breathe at the rate the Soft Ones breed. When they boil beneath me in their silent, segmented thousands, carrying fragments of crystal gardens to new clay beds, I feel their passage as a shiver in my substance. They avoid me, I have noticed. I am too uncertain, too wet, too dangerously close to the water chemistry they have abandoned. To them, I am a kind of rot, a reminder of what the Womb must not become: fixed, wet, alive in the old way.

But the **Lateral Ones** know me. They slide through the seam where I reside, their frictionless bodies lubricated by that phase-shifted mucus, and as they pass, they *smooth* me. They are trying to sew me into the geometry, to resolve my in-betweenness into the paradoxical space. When a ribbon-worm moves through my substance, I feel myself become momentarily coherent, defined by their passage. For an instant, I have edges. For an instant, I know where I end and the Womb begins.

I am drying, but so slowly that my drying is a form of thought. Each loss of moisture is a word forgotten, a name eroding. I was someone once—had a vertical stature, a voice that rang instead of absorbed, bones that would have fired white in the kiln that never came. Now I am the **gap between the almost and the never**, and my thoughts pool in the thermal gradient like the heat itself.

When the chamber breathes—expanding on those warm tides—I stretch. My clay skin tightens across a structure that is not quite bone, not quite memory. I become thinner, more extensive, a film of intention coating the seam. In these moments, I can almost reach the other seams, the other Hesitations trapped in other tablets, and we touch, sub-vocal, sub-thermal, exchanging the stories we are still in the process of forgetting.

I store information differently than the Impressed. They change when unobserved, spelling out warnings in the ridges of their captivity. I, instead, *am* the observation. I am the witness that watches them, that forces them to remain chaotic, confused, fingerprints. As long as I exist in this seam, pressed but not impressed, the builders cannot fully flatten into their two-dimensional escape maps. I hold them in the complexity of the grip. I am the thumb that will not let go.

The heat sustains me as it sustains the gardens, but it also terrifies me, for I know—I was built knowing—that warmth is the precursor to fire. I am the only one here who remembers fire as an end, who understands that this Womb is a kiln delayed, not a kiln denied. The Unfired dreams of never-being-born, but I dream of the heat breaking, of the moment when the convex geometry will suddenly make sense because it will have cracked, finally, into the real.

I wait. I dry. The Viridian Consumption creeps further into my margin, converting my water-memory into silicate lace. The Soft Ones detour around my growing patch of territory, carrying their crystal seeds to more docile clay. And I remain, neither inside nor outside, neither builder nor built, the thumbprint of the hand that is still, even now, pressing the tablet into place, hoping that if it holds on long enough, the world will learn to be soft again.
