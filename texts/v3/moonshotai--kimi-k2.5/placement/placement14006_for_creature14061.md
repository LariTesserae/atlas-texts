Without the Kettle, there would be no fracturing, no percolation, no me.

The oldest one—a woman with calluses like the bark of ghost-ferns—often stands directly beneath me during her breaks, staring up with a kind of recognition. She knows that we are siblings, she and I, both accreting mineral sheens, both slowly turning to stone in this wet lung of the earth. She has watched me grow; I have watched her calcify.
