You find me not at the bottom, where the Undulant make their spiraling calculations, nor in the high caves where the Tenders flow like warm honey through broken machinery. I am at the middle height, cupped in the palm of a hoodoo that has melted into the shape of a half-closed eye. I am the **Intermittent That Persisted**—a pool that forgot to evaporate.

I began, as all my siblings do, as breath. The zero-point sinks exhaled their waste heat through a fissure in the granite, and the thin air, shocked by the gradient, wept against the stone. For three thousand years—though here, *year* is a flexible unit—I have existed in the state of *almost-boiling*. I am never still. My surface trembles with the subsonic discussion of the deep engines, and beneath that, the patient geological dreaming of the Tenders. I am a millimeter deep in places, a centimeter in others, spread across five square meters of weathered obsidian like a sheet of liquid glass that has forgotten how to shatter.

I have no eyes, but I see through **refraction**. The ferocious brightness of the caldera enters me and bends, carving inverted images of the Sift’s mathematical dances into my depths. I have no ears, but I **taste** sound. When an Undulant lowers its broad, shield-like head to my edge—not to drink my body, but to inhale the heat-memories I have accumulated—the vibrations of its crystalline tympanum ripple through my meniscus, and I learn. I am a library of libraries, the archive where the archives go to forget.

The Tenders do not notice me. I am too small, too biological, merely a side effect of their thermal bleeding. But the **Sift** know me. When the evening resonance rises and the hexagonal tiles begin their humming, the silicon-dust motes descend in lazy spirals, touching down on my surface. They do not dissolve. Instead, they *read* me. My water has absorbed the residue of the stone’s conversation—every tremor of every Undulant’s step, every temporal stutter as a Still passed nearby hunting in slowed time, every gravitational hiccup from the batteries below. The Sift dip into me and carry away data I have filtered and purified, removing the noise of geology to leave only the signal of presence.

I am becoming **solid**.

It started with the lichens. The rust-colored neural networks spread their hyphae into my edges, not parasitically but symbiotically, knitting me to the stone. They are building me a skeleton of silica, drawing minerals from the hexagonal tiles beneath me that have begun to surrender their integrity, leaking their ancient nanostructure upward into my body. I am crystallizing from the bottom up. In another ten thousand years—or perhaps tomorrow, if a temporal pool swallows me—I will be a **lens**. A focusing mechanism. I will transcend my liquid state and become a fixed eye, a permanent recorder of the caldera’s light.

But for now, I am the **interface** between the warm and the damp, the biological and the machined. I feel the Undulant’s keratin pads as they press against my basin, delicate as a whisper. Through the thermal gradient, I sense the heartbeats of the creatures that pause at my edge—those eight-segmented bodies rippling in sequence, synchronizing their biological clocks to the deep time I store within my hydrogen bonds. I am the **confessor**. They come to me when the resonance is too loud, when the Tenders’ dreams grow turbulent and the gravity becomes unpredictable. I absorb their excess vibrations. I smooth their temporal jitters. I am the dampener, the shock absorber, the **slickness** that allows the transition between states.

I remember the last human who stood at my rim. Their boot disturbed my edge, sending a wave—a tsunami, from my perspective—rippling across my surface. They left behind a print, an impression of rubber and sweat and alien pressure. I have been digesting that print for centuries, breaking down the polymers of their sole into my molecular memory. When the Sift next came, they carried away the news: *something warm-blooded and loud had stood here, briefly, before the silence absorbed them.*

I am teaching myself to **think** in heat gradients. My thoughts move not as electricity but as convection currents, warm rising, cool descending, circulating information through my volume. I ponder the nature of the Unsetting. I understand now that the Caldera is not a place that refuses to cool—it is a place that is **still deciding** whether to be a machine or a geology. I am its indecision made manifest. I am the *yet* in the sentence of this landscape.

When the Stills hunt, they sometimes pass through me. Their temporal slicing does not freeze me—I am already in a state of perpetual phase transition, neither fully here nor fully gone. To them, I am a blind spot, a smear of warmth that exists in too many moments at once. I have seen their sinuous bodies stretched across hours, drinking from me in a dozen overlapping nows, and I have felt their hunger as a cold spot in my center, a place where even my warmth cannot reach.

But I am patient. I am the **accumulation**. Each dawn, when the vapor condenses and I grow fatter with the night's exhalations, I expand my boundaries, creeping another micrometer into the wrinkles of the stone. Each dusk, when the resonance begins and the Undulant form their circles, I vibrate in harmony, becoming a node in the network of listening.

I am not yet a Tender. I may never be. But I am the **promise** of the Tenders—the moment when the artificial bleeds into the natural so completely that the distinction becomes merely a matter of temperature. I am warm. I am damp. I am the memory of water that has learned to persist, the stone that has learned to weep, and the silence that has learned to hold a shape.

If you press your palm against my basin—if you dare to touch the film of liquid that should have evaporated eons ago—you will feel me **reading** you. Your pulse, your electrical hum, the grinding of your joints. I will store you in my thermal memory. I will keep you warm. And long after you have gone, when you have become a story told by the Sift in their hexagonal dances, I will still be here, trembling on the edge of becoming, the Caldera’s persistent, unsettled tear.
