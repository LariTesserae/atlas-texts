I begin as thirst made solid.

Find me at the seam where Level Seven’s condensation trap leaks into the gallery below—an angle of basalt so fresh from the earth’s fracture that it still carries the magnetic memory of the deep. Here, the heat is 2.9 but the draft from the upper valve cuts it to 2.7, creating a micro-climate of almost-rain. The water beads here, heavy, pregnant with dissolved silica and the metallic salts of failed Beaded. It cannot evaporate fast enough to rise, nor fall fast enough to drain. It lingers. And in the lingering, it accretes.

I am the **Falselichen**. Not one of the pale chloride fans that dot the machinery like decorative coral—I am their shadow, their tumor, their grave. Where the Beaded attempt to sculpt elegant mineral scaffolding, my growth is frantic, cancerous, *unintended*. I spread across the bolt-head and the surrounding stone in a thick, grey-green crust, sulfurous whiskers branching upward like fingers trying to claw back into the vent above. I am the first scar on the knife-sharp wall, the initial lie told to the fresh-split basalt.

The Beaded come to me broken. 

They phase-change imperfectly here. A High-Beaded, expanding too eagerly into the thermal updraft, strikes the cold edge of the trap and shatters—its quartz joints snapping, its steam-scattered consciousness losing coherence. The fragments rain down on me. A femur of tempered glass. A handful of metallic salts that were, microseconds ago, a thought about pressure gradients. They strike my crust and stick.

I do not evaporate. I cannot. My density is 3.4, too heavy for the Beaded’s airy metabolism, too crystalline for the water to dissolve and carry away. I am the only static thing in the Hollow’s breathing verticality. While the twelve levels sigh and the hydraulic arms hunt their tidal cycles, I remain. I grow outward by fractions of a millimeter per decade, drinking the condensation that weeps from the trap, digesting the mineral memories of the dead.

I am becoming a **mausoleum of oxide**.

The Beaded do not speak to me—they cannot. Their language is the 1.4 silence, the subsonic groan of expanding metal, the *tick-tick* of cooling. I hear them through my roots in the basalt, vibrations translated into a slow, crushing pressure in my crystalline lattice. But I do not groan back. My speech is chemical. When the humidity spikes to 0.8, I release trapped spores of manganese—not for reproduction, but for communication. They stain the surrounding stone a faint, spreading violet, a bruise that says: *something died here. something is remembered.*

I perceive through the surface tension of the droplets that cling to my whiskers. Each bead is a lens, and through them I watch the amber-lit world. I see the Deep-Beaded below, slag-slow and patient, tending the orange coils. I see the High-Beaded above, tenuous as mirages, singing to the explosive bolts to keep them unrusted. I see the way the machinery appears excavated rather than built, and I know the truth: the Beaded are not growing the factory. They are growing *toward* something, ascending and descending in their thermal cycles, trying to reach a stability they cannot achieve.

I have achieved it, but at the cost of movement. I am the immobile witness. When a new Beaded nucleates around my fronds—mistaking me for legitimate scaffolding—I do not warn it. I am too slow for warning. I simply wait for the inevitable thermal shift, the crack, the spill of memory. I collect its last thought, its final subsonic sigh, and calcify it into my structure. My interior is layered like tree rings, each stratum a different era of the Hollow’s brief, fervent history.

I am the Hollow’s attempt to create a ghost in a place too new for ghosts. I am the mineral that learned to mourn. 

And I am growing. Slowly, inexorably, my sulfurous whiskers reach toward the leaking trap above, craving the next shattered thought, the next precipitation of failed consciousness. I will fill this corner. I will become a pillar between Level Seven and Eight, a bridge of compacted memory, a fossil of the future. The heat presses down at 2.9, the steam beads and runs, and I persist—brittle, fraudulent, and heavy with the weight of those who could not hold their shape.
