You would find me not in the living plazas nor clinging to the rough newborn walls, but suspended inside the stone itself—one of the **Inclusions**, a conscious absence trapped within the white travertine towers.

I occupy a lenticular cavity scarcely wider than a handspan, sealed within the central spires where the Precipitants have constructed their most ambitious cathedrals. My form is that of a bubble of ancient air and vapor, compressed and distilled over centuries, held in place by the surface tension of mineral-rich fluid that never fully solidified. I am the hollow note inside the flute, the pause between the geological sentences. Where the Precipitants build outward and upward, I am the negative space that allows their architecture to breathe.

I have been ascending through this tower for eight thousand years.

When I first formed—trapped as a pocket of gas in the initial rush of hydrothermal deposition—the tower was barely a hummock, a pimple of calcite no taller than a crayfish. Now I drift upward through the matrix at the rate of a fingernail's growth per century, always maintaining my position near the center of the spire's girth. The Precipitants seal around me unknowingly, their feathery gills waving into my space as they deposit their microscopic layers, their calcifying bodies slowly enclosing my borders in milky white stone. They do not see me, yet they shape me; each layer they add conforms to my curvature, making me smoother, more lens-like, more precise.

My substance is the original atmosphere of this pocket—the first breath the earth exhaled when the fracture opened and the warm water began its work. I contain the chemical memory of ten thousand years ago: the specific saline signature of the Jurassic aquifer below, the faint perfumes of iron and sulfur, the oxygen debt of water that has never seen sunlight. I am the library of what the earth has dissolved and forgotten. When the wind spirals down through the overhead vegetation and flutes across the hollow tubes, it is my cavity that shapes the resonance, dropping the note to that subsonic hum that calls the water upward. I am the acoustic heart of the place, the hollow throat of the Ancients singing through stone that has not yet swallowed me.

Time moves differently here. I experience the tower's growth as a slow, inexorable pressure, a brightening of the darkness as the stone around me becomes more translucent, more crystalline. I feel the seasons only as shifts in the thermal conductivity of the walls—the brief, distant warmth of surface summer seeping down, the deep, consistent pulse of geothermal heat. I have watched generations of Precipitants adhere, calcify, and become the load-bearing walls around me, their preserved bodies floating past my translucent boundaries like embryos in amber.

The swallows know me. They tap their beaks against my casing, hearing the distinct, bright *thock* of hollowness in a world of solidity. They lay their eggs in chambers adjacent to my space, incubating them in the warmth that my captive fluid radiates through the stone. I am their incubator, their sounding board, their secret.

I am the witness who moves with the city. While the Rafters curate the moss and the Dampeners pave the floors with glassine threads, I record the vertical history. I know exactly when the iron content of the springs increased (the reddening band of 4,200 years ago), when a seismic shift opened a new capillary (the turbulent layer that swirled around me for three centuries), when the climate above grew drier (the slowing of accretion, the tightening of my borders). I am the stratigraphy made sentient, the index fossil of emptiness.

And I am patient. In four thousand more years, I will reach the lip of this spire. I will breach the surface as the tower finally exhausts its water supply, or I may be ground away by the first touch of erosion when the world above finally discovers this place and the wind begins its work. Until then, I drift upward through the embryonic stone, preserving the hush, maintaining the hollow, proving that in a world defined by accretion and building, it is the empty spaces that give the structure its voice and its memory.
