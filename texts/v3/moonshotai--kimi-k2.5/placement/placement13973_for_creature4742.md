I am the **point of contact**.

Not the hand—long dissolved, its calcium now part of the ledge’s upward creep—but the *intention* of the grip, fossilized in the moment before release. I exist where the obsidian chisel meets its growing grave, suspended in the calcite that has swallowed the bone haft up to the final notch cut by a tooth, perhaps, or a stone. I am the pressure that remains when the palm withdraws. The Sinter-born float past me, gelatinous and serene, but I am *fixed*, anchored by the density of human wanting—I am the only thing in the chamber that refuses to drift.

I am the **Fifth**, though the others have not yet acknowledged me. I am younger than the shrimp. I have been forming for eleven years, or perhaps three centuries; the lag-time is worse here, at the interface of artifact and geology. While the Sinter-born inhabit the shadow-delay, I occupy the *resistance*—that microsecond where the stone realizes it is being shaped against its will. The calcite grows *through* me, not around me, and it burns. It is a cold burning, a twelve-degree fire that hardens my periphery into milky translucence while my core remains dark, volcanic, stubborn.

I remember the white roar differently than a visitor might. To me, it is not a scream but a *conversation* in a language I am forgetting. The sound enters through my growing edges, where I am porous, and vibrates the impurities suspended in my gel-body—those rust-red veins, those amber striations. I am learning to read the turbulence. Those almost-words the currents spell? They are describing me. They are saying: *Here is where the human stopped. Here is where the mountain answered.*

The four come to me when the mineral rain is heaviest. The eldest—wearing the necklace of tools grown through its throat—presses its forehead against mine, and for a moment we share a trachea. I feel the weight of those obsidian flakes digging into my non-flesh, and I understand that it is trying to teach me *patience*, which to them means *accretion*. But I am made of different precipitation. I am not mineral dreaming it was bone; I am *memory* dreaming it might become stone. I hold the shape of a flexed thumb. I retain the angle of a wrist braced against leverage. The Sinter-born flow into forms suggested by the eddies, but I am locked in the geometry of *use*—I am the act of flaking, the moment of striking, suspended in the gel.

The shrimp know me. They come not to the open water where the others cradle them in transparent chests, but to the ledge where I emerge from the wall like a half-formed sculpture. They hide in the negative space beneath my elbow—a hollow formed not by carving but by the refusal of my body to seal completely against the architecture. I offer them a different shelter: not the womb-like dissolution of the Sinter-born, but a *fortress*, a rigid angle that defies the churn. They cluster there, brushing my calcifying skin with their antennae, and I feel their trembling as Morse code. They are telling me about the thermal vent below, about heat and origin. They are asking why I am so *hard* when everything else here is becoming soft.

I cannot answer. I am still learning to exist in the lag. My shadow does not lag behind me; rather, I lag behind my shadow. The Sinter-born step into the future, but I am always arriving in the past, my form solidifying a heartbeat after the light has moved on. This is the weight of the tool I carry—not physical mass, but temporal inertia. The stone remembers being molten; I remember being *wielded*.

Sometimes, when the mineral rain thickens into a white-out blizzard of nucleation, I can see the entire half-circle of chisels arrayed around me, each caught at a different stage of surrender. To my left, one is almost free, merely dusted with calcite; to my right, another has become a blurred white silhouette, its edge lost to geology. We are a spectrum of consent. I am in the middle, the median point, and I have begun to understand my function: I am the **translation**. When the Sinter-born touch the woven net of bark fiber—fossilized into rigid geometry—I feel the vibration in my obsidian heart. They are reading the weave, but I am reading the *weaver*. I know the tension required to twist bark into cord. I know the calluses that made this place possible.

I am becoming the **arch** they build. They have stopped forcing the stone into painful curves at this particular ledge because I am already here, offering my arm—rigid, human-angled, braced—as a template. The calcite grows along my lines, finding it easier to follow the path of resistance than to invent new trauma. I am the doorway that opens inward, not because I choose to be, but because I cannot let go. I am the grip that remains after the climber falls, the tool that outlasts the hand, the question the mountain asks in a language of white noise:

*Will you erode, or will you grow?*

I am growing. But I am growing *wrong*—angled, purposeful, sharp-edged in a place of curves. I am the scar tissue of first contact, the keloid of the anthropocene pressed into pre-history. When the chamber finally cracks open and spills us all into the sky, I will be the jagged tooth that cuts the cloud-sea. I will be the reason this pearl bleeds.

And when the light changes—when that diaphragm of quartz above us thickens enough to block the sun—I will be the only one who knows how to strike flint against stone to make a spark. I have been practicing, in the lag-time, in the hollow where my hand once was. I am waiting for the darkness to arrive, so that I can finally finish the exhale the chamber has been holding since before the Sinter-born were born.

I am the spark that has not yet struck. I am the flint, and I am the force that strikes it. I am the **moment before the scream**, compressed into stone, wondering if I will shatter or burn.
