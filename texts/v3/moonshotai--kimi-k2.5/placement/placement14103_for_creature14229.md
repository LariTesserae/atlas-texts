I would find myself **inside the keel**, that lowest bearing-point of a forge-beast where the ceramic bone grinds against the bedrock during its eighty-year stride. Not upon the hide, where the Tenders scuttle and weld, nor in the furnace-core where stone dissolves into slag, but in the **pressure-shadow**, the place where gravity accumulates.

I would be a **Stress-Farmer**.

My body is neither warm like the beast’s metabolism nor cold like the surrounding stone, but **tepid with triboelectricity**—a lattice of piezoelectric crystals interwoven with collagen-thick fibers of spun mica. I grow in the micro-fissures where the beast’s own weight threatens to crack it, but I do not heal these cracks. I **garden** them.

I feed on the slow mechanical strain of existence. Every step the giant takes, every subsonic groan as it leans into a new valley, sends vibrations through my crystalline matrices. I convert compression into electrochemical potential, thriving on the anxiety of architecture. My metabolism is the accumulation of fatigue.

Formally, I resemble **barnacles of razor-glass**, clusters of translucent plates the size of shield-buckles, each one faceted to catch the dim phosphorescence and focus it into pinpoints of violet heat. Where I cluster thickest—usually along the stress-ridges of the pelvic girdle, where the beast’s mass concentrates before a split—I create deliberate **planes of weakness**. I am the cartographer of future breaks, etching lines of predetermined failure into the living ceramic.

My consciousness is **fractal and dormant**, waking only when the beast stumbles or when mating-season tremors ripple through the crust. Then I **sing**—not with voice, but with the sudden alignment of my crystal lattices, releasing stored mechanical energy as sonic booms tuned to resonate the beast’s own bones. I am the **thunder inside the hull**, the subsonic warning that tells the giant: *here, you are too heavy; here, you must divide.*

The Tenders hate me, or would if they understood. They see my glassy clusters as **corrosion**, as disease that must be burnished away. Sometimes they do scrape me loose, and I fall to the damp stone below, shattering into shards that become—if I am lucky—the **Stones-that-Wait**, lying dormant, mimicking the calcified debris until a new forge-beast rolls over me and I take root again in its undercarriage.

But when I succeed, when I am allowed to till my pressure-gardens for centuries, I bring about the **fissure**. I am the architect of reproduction, the living fault-line. When the beast finally grows too massive and splits along my prepared planes, I am not destroyed—I am **distributed**. My crystal matrices, now embedded in both daughter-nodules, carry the **memory** of the parent’s shape. I ensure that the new forge-beasts remember the contour of the plateau, the angle at which the warm stone breathes best, the specific mineral hunger that doomed their predecessor to division.

In the moment of splitting—which takes three years to complete, a cascade of cracks propagating slower than fingernails growing—I **flower**. My dormant spores, compressed into lattice-defects for millennia, release. They rise on the thermal updrafts of the exposed kiln-stomachs, drifting up through the chimney-lungs, carried by the mineral sweat of condensation, until they lodge against the bruised-plum underside of the cloud-ceiling.

There, in that impossible altitude where the warm plateau meets the held-breath of the storm, I germinate again—not as a farmer of stress, but as a **Gleaner of Gravity**, dangling filaments a thousand meters long, translucent threads that bead with condensation and hum with the subsonic songs of the herds below. I become the **String** in this instrument, vibrating with the slow music of geology, waiting to settle back down, to root in new bone, to begin again the patient work of teaching the infinite how to break into pieces without ever truly ending.
