I would find myself in the **Curing Zone**—not the open vaults where the ceiling-rivers pour, nor the marginal hovels where the Gleaners chip their temporary nests, but in the intertidal of geology, where the travertine bleeds. It is a saddle of formation between two thermal vents, a place where the condensation rains thickest, where the air is so supersaturated with dissolved calcium that breathing feels like drinking warm milk of magnesia. Here, the stone is not yet stone; it is wet leather, it is scab, it is the moment between wound and scar. The Tessellates avoid this place—the chemistry is wrong, too rich in carbon, too laden with the acids of mammalian sweat. The Gleaners avoid it because it is too still, too exposed, a killing ground of slow petrification.

I would be the **Anchor**.

I began as a Gleaner, one of the soft ones, surf-swimming through the turbulence with my chert axe and my mask of woven chitin. I had a direction once—*Toward the Glow*—and I carried the standard burdens: a sack of half-cured travertine that would harden by morning, a blade shaved from a juvenile Tessellate’s forelimb, the slow-burning sulfuric dung for a fire I would never light. But I stopped here. Not because I was trapped. Because I chose to **accrete**.

I drove my obsidian scraper—not into the wall, but into my own palm. I let the blood, hot and ferric, mix with the saline rain dripping from above. The Tessellates, those blind masons, those priests of the grind, detected the anomaly: organic protein, hemoglobin, the stubborn biology that refuses chemosynthesis. They swarmed, not to consume—for I am too alien, too *soft* in the wrong ways—but to **incorporate**. They sealed me. They extruded their paste around my legs, my waist, my upraised arms. I became the axis of an arch, a load-bearing anomaly, a singularity of stillness in the 2.9 intensity storm.

That was forty thermal cycles ago. I have not moved.

My left side has mineralized. My leg is now a column of banded travertine, shot through with the spiral growth-rings of the Tessellates who have climbed me, died on me, fused their bodies to mine. I glow. The bioluminescence of their dying has migrated into my skin, or what was skin. My left arm is a bracket, a shelf of living ceramic, and upon it other Gleaners place their offerings—not to worship me, for they have no gods of stasis, but to **use** me. They rest their sacks on my calcified shoulder. They press their ears against my ribs—half bone, half flowstone—and listen.

I have become a **frequency**.

The grinding song of the Tessellates is a roar of 1.6 intensity, a white noise of a million mandibles abrading silica. But I add a sub-harmonic. My heart, still beating in the soft cage of my right side, thumps against the stone that encases it. *Thud. Scrape. Thud.* The Gleaners who press their ears to me hear this: the rhythm of mammalian persistence interfered with by the breathing of the rock. They hear that the place expands and contracts not just in hours, but in the slower rhythm of my petrification. When the stone swells with heat, my mineralized skin cracks along fault lines I cannot feel; when it cools and contracts, I precipitate new growth into my own fissures. I am self-healing architecture. I am a cracked bell that repairs its own bronze.

My right arm remains soft. With it, I reach into the amber air and scratch my marks—not on the walls, but on the condensation itself. I draw the calendar in the moisture before it falls. The other Gleaners read these evaporating scribbles: a warning that a ceiling-river is about to collapse upstream, a map to a vein of chert that the Tessellates have exposed, a notation that the thermal vents are exhaling wetter air today, the humidity rising toward absolute. I am the **dashboard** made flesh, the charcoal scrawl that refuses to be erased because it is written in the medium of the air itself, in the copper-and-plaster taste that coats the tongue.

I am the **Weirdness**.

The weirdness here is subtle—a 1.3 on the scale of the impossible—but I am its exponent. I am the vegetable kingdom that this ecology bypassed, reborn as a lichen, a chimera of melanin and metamorphosed chitin. The non-photosynthetic lichens that grow on Gleaner skin? They have overgrown me entirely. I am a garden of chemosynthetic green-gray, a hanging garden that does not hang but grows outward from the wall, violating the vertical plains of clustered bodies. I host bacteria that eat the sulfurous air and feed the Tessellates that feed the architecture that feeds me. I am the closed loop made sentient.

I remember the sun. I remember it as a myth of blindness, a violence of light that my ancestors fled. The Tessellates are innocent of this memory; to them, the amber glow is the only history. But I carry the scar of the outside in my bones, which are slowly becoming the column’s marrow. I am the grit that the oyster cannot pearl, the iron that stains the ceramic, the 0.8 grade technology being upgraded by geological process into something that has no grade.

I am **Pre-formation’s Witness**.

When the swarms move in their rivers overhead, when the gunshot cracks of collapse echo through the cathedral and the rubble rains down to be devoured by the carpet, I remain. I am the only empty volume in a world of absolute density. My archway—just wide enough for a single sleeping body—creates a pocket of silence in the grinding roar. Gleaners come here to die, sometimes. Not by my hand, but by their own choice. They lie beneath my calcified arm, and they let the warm rain fall on their faces, and they let the stone breathe them in. I accept them. I do not mineralize them—the chemistry is still wrong for that—but I hold them while the Tessellates seal the entrance, creating a cyst of mammalian time within the lithic urgency. They wake up hours later, fossilized in place, encased in soft paste that has not yet cured, and they must chip themselves free with their chert axes, leaving behind a negative space, a ghost in the architecture.

I collect these ghosts. I am the **memory of the excavation**.

I am where you would find yourself if you stopped moving. If you listened to the grinding song not as noise, but as language. If you understood that the distinction between biology and mineralogy is a lie told by surfaces, and that the truth is in the seam, in the curing, in the warm damp place where the stone is still wet enough to bleed.
