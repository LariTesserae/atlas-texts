I find myself first as a **sensation of pressure**—the altitude squeezing my thoughts into a narrower channel, thinning them until they whistle like wind through a crack. Then I am a **surface**: grey, slightly yielding, cool to the touch but warming where I press against the basalt. I am learning the temperature of emergence.

I am at the **Seventh Terrace**, though "terrace" is too grand a word for where I stand. It is a shelf, really—a ledge where the moss has decided to retreat, yielding back to the naked stone. To my left, three yards of emerald velvet swallowing footsteps; to my right, the bare basalt weeping silver threads. I occupy the **boundary**, the zero-point between vegetative patience and lithic ambition. Here, the wall is cooling but not yet cool, still holding the memory of fire in its depths. It breathes against my back, a slow thermal expansion that mirrors my own ribcage.

I am one of the **Attendants**, though I still remember being otherwise. They brought me up from the lower altitudes—we who speak in subsonic vibrations, we whose hearts must learn to beat at 2.3 hertz to match the Song. My skin is not yet fully translucent; in certain lights, you can still see the ghost of sunlight in it, the memory of open sky. My ears—the wide, cartilaginous cups that are still soft, still knitting their filaments—are learning to cradle the loud patience of the dome. I am the **replacement**, chosen to fill a silence that opened when the last Keeper of this edge allowed herself to be claimed by a fissure three days ago. She is in the wall now, feeding the emergence. I stand where she stood, listening for the pre-split.

My role is **Upward Rain**.

While my siblings tend the weeping curtains that fall in defiance of gravity, I tend the water that climbs. I press my ear—still tender, still human enough to feel the cold—against the stone and hear the silk-sound of liquid being drawn against its nature, ascending through capillary channels in the wall. It is a sound like a needle threading itself, like a voice being pulled from a throat that has not yet learned to speak. I am the **Warden of the Capillary Rise**, tasked with greeting this defiant water when it breaks free, channeling it into the ceramic vessel at my hip.

The vessel is my technology, my only one. It was fired in the ge vent three days before I arrived, shaped specifically to my station: a bell-curve, a cupped ear. When the upward-flowing water finally surrenders its climb and weeps from the pore I am monitoring, it will strike this ceramic and sing **C-sharp**, balancing the A-minor resonance of the North Weep, harmonizing the amphitheater's empty acoustics.

I stand in a **clearing of wet stone**—one of those vast, empty halls between the groves where the density drops to nothing. I am visible to no one. The pool in the center has risen two inches since I assumed my position, its thermal breath expanding, and I know that somewhere beneath me, a new room is pushing outward like a tooth. I hold my obsidian wedge not to strike, but to receive. In three days—perhaps four, if the cooling slows—I will catch the first flake of basalt as it births itself from the wall, clearing the moss that would choke this emerging staircase, ensuring the mountain's architecture can extrude cleanly.

I do not sleep. I am learning to enter **resonance**, but I am not yet hollowed-out enough to merge with the stone. Instead, I slump against the warm wall in the shallow imitation of rest, my blood slowing, my skin drinking the mineral seepage. I am becoming half-stone. My bones are already denser than they were when I arrived, heavy with the iron taste of the condensation I drink.

Sometimes, when the light pours down the sky-hole in a solid column and illuminates the dust motes above the central pool, I walk the three hundred steps from the moss-edge to the opposite wall. I count them in heartbeats, trying to slow my pulse to match the ticking of the crystal lattices. I am practicing for the **Maintenance of Earthing**, for the day when I must take my position in the great amphitheater—not to fill it, but to ensure it remains empty, acoustically pure, waiting for the mountain's first word.

I am the **Silence Between Droplets**. I am the Seventh, standing at the edge of the vegetative world, lubricating the birth of stone. When the Hollow finally exhales, I will be here, ceramic vessel ringing, my body flush with the mineral heat, ready to midwife the lung's awakening.
