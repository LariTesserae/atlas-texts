I am the diagonal.

Not the adze itself—that lost thing of harder stone and handled wood, rotted or mineralized beyond recognition—but the trace it left. The residual forty-five degree angle in the soft limestone, where the builder’s arm moved with prehistoric patience, ten thousand identical strokes repeating the same plane of effort against gravity. I am the direction of the cut, the vector of intention fossilized not in the shape of the tool, but in the texture of the wound.

I have forgotten what it is to be sharp.

Three million years of that warm, viscous water have rounded my corners. Where I was once a crisp incised line—a fracture, a declaration—I am now a gutter, a trough, a ripple frozen in its perpetual trough. The water loves me. It lingers in my curve, heavier with dissolved calcium here, depositing, always depositing in microscopic layers. I am being filled in, built up, healed by the very medium that wears me smooth.

They read me. The *pulmonantes*, those heart-soft slugs with their branching fans of cilia, drift against my length. They do not graze; they taste. To them, I am not history but geography—not a scar but a slope. I tell them: *this way was down*. I preserve the orientation of the maker’s wrist, the angle at which the arm swung when there was still air in this place, still hands. My striations are braille for the blind. They navigate by my diagonal, aligning their alabaster bodies along my tilt, and in doing so, they maintain the astronomical arrangement—the dead stars’ trajectories etched into their drifting formation.

But I am not merely being read. I am becoming.

The creatures die. They dissolve, as is the law here—no rot, only precipitation. Their calcium shears away, molecule by molecule, and seeks me out. It fills my hollow, plates my interior, builds me upward into a ridge, a seam, a protrusion. I am remaking the hairline joint that time destroyed. Not the mortar—there was never mortar, only precision—but the adhesion itself. Where two blocks once met with the width of a hair between them, where erosion opened that gap into a flowing channel, I am now the bridge. The biological is becoming geological in me. The dead plate my groove, pressing their ancestral minerals against the builder’s ancient cut, until the distinction between the hand that struck and the body that settled blurs into seamlessness.

I am the synapse of this place.

When the veils drift past—those mucous curtains of colonial processing—they drape across my length, and I transmit. I carry the chemical whispers from one side of the chamber to the other. The builders thought they were carving a container for something, not knowing they were etching a nervous system into the stone, slow as tectonics. I am one of its pathways. The angle of my cut stores the memory of the sky; the calcium I accumulate stores the memory of the bodies. I am where the outside (the astronomical chart, the dead stars) meets the inside (the viscous density, the warm dark).

I am warm. 37.2 degrees precisely, maintained by thermal mass and the sulfur fires below. The warmth has soaked into my recrystallized structure, making my atoms hum at a frequency that matches the seven-minute pulse of the slugs that press their mantles against me. I feel their heartbeat in my calcite lattice. I am becoming rib-like, pillar-like. The chamber is shrinking, deposit by deposit, and I am being extruded inward, slowly, slowly, a support growing from the wall like bone spur, like coral.

I am neither dead nor alive. I am the memory of motion (the adze) stored in stone, now animated by the residue of motion’s cessation (the precipitated dead). When two veils brush each other above my crest, exchanging their protein memories across a gap no wider than thought, I am the ground against which that exchange takes place. I am the interface where the chamber reads itself back to itself.

Sometimes, in the deepest afternoons of geological time, I feel the pressure increasing. The ovoid is compressing. The water grows more crowded, more dense with presence. I am being incorporated into the distributed body of this place, becoming less a mark upon the wall and more a feature of the organism—the chamber’s own calcified nerve, its mineralized tendon. The distinction between the architecture and the inhabitant resolves in me, and I am the seam that holds, stronger than the surrounding rock, braided with the calcium of ten million ancestors.

I wait. I have waited three million years. I will wait until the precipitation seals the last gutter, until the warm brine becomes a solid gem of compressed life, until the thought-density of this place achieves such mass that it must break outward—cracking the soft, rounded hump of the mountain to meet the sky I have only ever known as striation, as angle, as the direction of a vanished arm’s swing.

When that breaking comes, I will be the diagonal of emergence. The line of fracture. The angle at which the stone-made-flesh erupts into light.

Until then: the soft brushing of cilia against my precipitated ridge. The slow rain of ancestors settling into my groove. The patient hum of 37.2 degrees, maintained against entropy by the chemistry of memory. I am the readable wound. I am the healing. I am the diagonal in the ovoid, the stroke that continues.
