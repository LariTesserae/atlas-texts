I would be found upon the back of the One Who Has Finished Waiting—not upon the broad, dorsal plane where the warm water sheets meet the humid air, but specifically upon the **calcified larynx**, that corrugated collar of mineralized flesh where the seven-thousand-year tone originates.

I am not a parasite; the plain has no use for such theft. I am a **crustose lichen**, *Acousticaria geophonum*, though the classification is only a dream of the old taxonomies, for here we do not speciate but *accrete*. My body is a map of orange and pale grey, a rosette spreading outward at the rate of one concentric ring per century. I am thin as the condensation that feeds me, a scurf of algae and fungal hyphae cemented to the calcified chitin with oxalic acid, yet I am the **only softness** upon that stony throat.

I emerged in the third millennium of the creature's song, a spore that drifted down upon the vibrating stone and found purchase in a microscopic irregularity where the mineral precipitation had been slightly less perfect. Since then, I have never ceased listening. My thallus is a recording medium; the seven-thousand-year tone does not merely pass through me but is **held** in my layers, each annual growth ring encoding the subtle modulations of geological fatigue— the fractional droop of calcium when a rare cloud passed overhead in the year 4,842 of the Song, the slight brightening of resonance during the thermal spike of Year 6,103. I am the **archive** of the drone, the hard-disk of the eternal note.

To live upon the One is to know pressure as intimacy. The vibration is not sound here; it is **weight**, a directionless gravity that presses my orange pigments against the grey substrate. When the marsh frogs begin their bass chorus at mid-morning, I do not hear them—I feel them through the calcified sac as secondary harmonics, ripples in the primary tone. The dragonflies that visit are thunderous, their wingbeats percussive impacts that I register as bruises in my peripheral growth. I drink not through roots but through my entire surface, a top-down imbibing of the condensation that beads upon my crusty lobes, water so pure it carries nothing but the faint metallic tang of Precambrian iron.

I have no need of shadows; I am a shadow, a stain, an afterthought of color on the stone-skin. Yet I participate in the solitude of the plain. When the seventeen-minute shadow of the distant Ghost Gum passes over us each afternoon, the thermal drop is severe—a whole degree—and I contract, my algal partners retreating into the fungal matrix, waiting. The creature beneath me feels this cooling as a shift in the gradient across its back, and for seventeen minutes, the tone wavers, infinitesimally, dropping by perhaps a fraction of a Hertz. I record this variation as a denser ring, a darkening in my geological diary: *Here, the tree touched us. Here, we were not alone.*

At dusk, when the creature shifts and the pitch drops that full octave, entering the subterranean register felt in the bone, I enter my active phase. Night is when I grow, when the condensation thickens to a syrup and the warmth bleeding from the gneiss below keeps my metabolic processes lazily awake. In the dark, I am a **bioluminescent margin**, faintly phosphorescent,tracing the contours of the vocal sac in a pale green glow that no eye has ever seen—perhaps a legacy from deep-sea ancestors, now repurposed to light up the underside of the fog. I am the night-light of the plain, the indicator that the node is still functioning, still singing the water table upward from the microscopic fractures.

I do not reproduce. There is no wind strong enough to lift my fragments, and the air is too thick for spores to travel far. I simply expand, slowly covering more of the calcified throat, competing with the orange maps of my neighboring lichens for space that does not exist. When two colonies meet, we do not battle; we merge, our fungal hyphae grafting into a continuous network, a single crust spanning meters of the creature's collar, sharing the vibration like a nutrient.

I will not die, because I am already dead and alive in the manner of the plain—lichen is a symbiosis, after all, a negotiation between kingdoms that has already crossed the threshold of individuality. When the One Who Has Finished Waiting finally ceases its biological pretense and becomes, entirely, a fossil with a hollow throat, I will calcify in turn. My orange pigments will oxidize into rust; my body will become a stain of iron oxide on the stone. But I will persist as a texture, a roughness on the fossil's neck that catches the wind differently, producing a harmonic overtone, a ghost of the ghost.

Until then, I am the **ornamentation of eternity**, the decoration that witnesses, the softness that records the hardness. I grow in circles, singing in silence along with the One, ensuring that when the universe finally expands into its own heat death, this last note will at least have had a witness that grew in rings, patient, orange, and perfectly still.
