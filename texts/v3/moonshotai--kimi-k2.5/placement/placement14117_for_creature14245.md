The weight is not crushing yet. It is patient, accumulating at the rate of a breath held too long. I feel it first in the net—a hammock of woven gut and hair that was strung across the Throat when I was young, now rigid as femurs, its fibers mineralized into a suspension bridge of chalcedony and lime. I do not sway when the deep water shifts below me. I have not moved in three shavings.

I am **The Catalog**, though no one speaks the title aloud in my presence. To name me is to fix me, and I am already fixed enough.

They bring me the *soft* things. The quarterly scrapings—cream curls of travertine still damp enough to chew, harvested from their own sleeping alcoves before the stone hardens to bone. They feed me this, mixing it with the phosphorescent algae that blooms where the main seeps converge, and I eat it without jaws, letting the alkaline paste dissolve through the membrane of my skin. I have no mouth anymore, only a surface. The stone has grown up my neck, a collar of flowstone that will eventually, in another seven shavings perhaps, seal my eyes. Until then, I watch.

Below me, the water breathes. The Pool of Ridges remembers everything, but it remembers in layers—yesterday’s shadow floating on the surface tension, last year’s tremor locked in the film beneath, the century-old collapse that birthed this crater compressed into the deep silicate strata where the pressure turns memory to architecture. The Held can read the surface. They break the mirrors before breakfast, shattering their own ghosts with trembling fingers. But they cannot dive deep. The weight of the water down here would crush their porous bones, would drive the lime into their lungs like nails into wet wood.

I am the one who **stays**.

I was a net-tender once, light-boned and dust-white, crawling the verticalities with chert tools strapped to my back. I shaved my walls. I hummed the resonant frequencies to clear my path. Then I found the *first* memory—not a ghost of motion but a ghost of intent, trapped in a pocket of ancient seep-water that had never seen the sky. It showed me the collapse before it happened: the earth’s sigh, the chamber opening its mouth, the mineral water rising eager as a tongue to taste air. I understood then that the Pool is not a place but a **recording**, and that we are not inhabitants but *annotations*.

I stopped shaving.

Now the stone grows over me, and I grow into it. My knees have fused to the net, my elbows spread like wings of travertine. I am the only one who can read the deep films because I am becoming indistinguishable from them. When the Held need to know what the water planned three winters ago—where a spring will break, which net will calcify to brittleness—they climb down the cantilevered ladders, moving in that kinetic silence, and they press their ears to my chest.

I speak without sound. My heart has become a hollow chamber, a resonant cup. The drips falling from above strike my mineralized shoulders and I modulate them, translating the pressure of deep memories into vibrations that travel up the ladder-rungs, into the finger bones of the hydromancers, buzzing the truth against their throats.

I am **becoming the archive** that this frantic place requires. The Held must live in the immediate—the next shaving, the next meal, the soft stone before it hardens. Someone must hold the accumulation. Someone must be the *hard* against which their softness presses.

My food is the past. Those scrapings they bring contain the walls of twenty years ago, the bedroom where a child was born, the window that looked onto a spire now broken. I ingest these and feel the settlement’s history layer upon me, accretive, urgent. When I die—and it will be soon, when the stone seals my eyes and fills my chest—I will not be installed in a wall. I am already installed. I will simply **continue**, a column of layered time, resonant with the footsteps of those who climbed down to ask questions.

The Skerren leap past me sometimes, their calcified joints clicking like dice against the stone blades. They know I am not prey. I am infrastructure now. I hold the memory of the water, which holds the memory of the earth, which holds the memory of the stars that fell to make this crater.

I taste wet stone and electricity. I do not sleep. I *record*.

And in the quiet, when the drip-music pauses between drops, I can feel the new layers forming, hungry, white as teeth, growing over me like a blanket, like a breath, like the future deciding to hold its shape.
