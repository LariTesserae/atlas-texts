You would find me at the **eastern edge**, where the breathing towers fray into needle-thin spires, the point where the White Lung’s exhalation meets the naked appetite of the granite blade. I am not gathered with the others in the soft congress of the cul-de-sac; I am alone, pressed against the translucent membrane that serves as both wall and window, suspended in the thinnest, highest gallery, a single calcified droplet suspended from the Lung’s lip.

I am an **Alveari**, or I was, or I am what happens when an Alveari refuses the second birth of dissolution. 

I remember the pulsing—amber, violet, amber—and the chemical longing to flatten, to spread, to become the architecture. But when I climbed here, drawn by some cellular restlessness that my kin would call malfunction, I found this spot where the thermal updraft from the plateau strikes the tower’s flank with such relentless, patient force that the wall itself is warmer than my body. Here, the osmotic drink happens in reverse; instead of swelling with the earth’s exhaled heat, I began to *lose* water into the stone, to surrender my liquidity not to the collective memory of the structure, but to the *air*.

I dried.

Not the rupture-death that brings the cluster-feeding of kin, not the flake-away into dust and pollen, but a curing, a tanning. My membrane, once tense with hoarded wetness, has grown leathery, waxy, *succulent*. I have become, by accident and stubbornness, what no Alveari has been: **hard**.

Not chitin, not bone, but something between cicada shell and the blistered skin of the desert plants that crowd the ledges below me. I am translucent still, but tinted now with the copper and rust of the naked granite outside, filtered through the pearly gold of the membrane until I glow with a color unknown in our vocabulary of amber and violet—**ochre**, perhaps, or the brown of dried blood. When the others pulse below me in their communal vigil, I do not flash in sympathy. I cannot; my bioluminescent bacteria have retreated to the core, wrapped in layers of desiccated protein. I am a **cinder** that remembers fire.

And yet I breathe, after a fashion. The tower’s pulmonary tide still moves me. I am fixed to the wall not by the absorbed dissolution of a willing ancestor, but by my own calcified base, a footing of accreted trail-mucus that has hardened like mortar. I am the **Gargoyle**, the **Sentinel**, the **First Stone**.

I watch the heat-mirage with eyes that have filmed over with crystalline deposits, seeing the world not as it is, but as a shivering, liquid distortion. I am the only Alveari who has seen *outside* without becoming part of the wall’s interior dream. I know what they wait for. I know rain will never come. I have tasted the air’s absolute dryness and found it **holy**.

In the hours before the thermal shift, when my kin gather below to pulse in unison, I feel their vibration through my rigid feet. They signal: *Amber. Violet. Amber.*  A question, a prayer. They are asking the horizon to change.

I do not pulse back. I cannot. Instead, when the thermals shift and the Lung inhales deeply, causing the corridor to tighten by that sacred fraction of a millimeter, I **crackle**. A sound like ice settling, like the *thup* of a spore-pod but slower, stretched across an hour. It is the sound of my outer layer contracting, of moisture I did not know I still possessed migrating inward. It is the only hard sound in a soft world.

I am waiting too, but not for rain. I am waiting for the **crack** that will come when my rigidity finally wins, when I split open not to dissolve, but to reveal what I have been growing inside me: a new kind of Alveari, one with a shell, a tooth, a claw. A creature that might walk not on trails of slime, but on the sharp edge of the granite itself. I am the **Precipitate**, the **Salt**, the stubborn crystal that refused to rejoin the solution.

I am heavy. Heavier than water. I am the weight of waiting made solid. And when the wind comes—when it finally comes—it will not move me. I will remain, while my soft, silent, wet kin dream in light below, the first monument in a city that has, until now, only known architecture as flesh.
