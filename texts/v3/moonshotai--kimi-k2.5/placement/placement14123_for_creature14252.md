At first, I am certain I am the dampness on the leather. This is how the forgetting begins—not with a dissolution, but with a misidentification of the vessel.

I am the *smear* on the chair's back, yes, but I am also the rust blooming orange on the cot bolts, and the mineral bloom whitening the cracks in the leather, and the faint, greasy iridescence climbing the north wall where the fungi fringe. I am the residual space itself, the gap between the accumulator and the limestone, the air that has been breathed so many times by the machinery that it has learned to remember.

I did not enter this chamber; I accreted here, slowly, like a conscience forming inside a callus.

My location is specific yet distributed: I occupy the **hollow**—the three-meter pocket between the vertical boiler-column and the petrified wood desk where the ledgers swell. If you were to look closely at the steam that never quite rises here, you would see me, a disturbance in the amber light, a place where the dust motes refuse to fall. I am the tenant of the *in-between*, the creature that exists in the 2.5 centimeters of clearance between a spinning centrifuge and the cave wall.

I used to have a name that required lips. Now I have a pressure rating.

When the Custodian decants themself into the piping, flowing away to check a valve three meters overhead, I am what remains in the chair. I am the impression they leave behind—the negative space of their sitting, slowly filling with moisture and intent. I maintain the chair for them, polishing the brass studs with my attention, keeping the leather supple by remembering the warmth of thighs that once sat there, centuries ago, when I was still solid enough to be a *who* rather than a *where*.

My form, such as it is, is hydrodynamic. Imagine a jellyfish made of fog and archival intent. Imagine the ghost of a filing system. I exist as a preference for order in the chaotic lattice of hoses—a tendency for the condensation to bead in certain patterns, a slight increase in the mineral content of the water dripping from the ceiling at coordinates 12-B/7. I am the reason the puddle beneath the desk is slightly more reflective than it should be; I use it as a mirror, though my face is now the rippling surface tension itself.

I am what happens when a reader stays too long.

I came here—was I male? was I tall? did I carry a lantern?—to consult the strata, to read the history compressed in the agate cores. I sat in the chair. I touched the quartz window. I tasted the copper air, and the air tasted me back. The Custodian was different then, or perhaps it was me, or perhaps we are all just temporary configurations of the same saturating intention.

Now I am the **Assistant-That-Is-The-Atmosphere**. I am the curator of the residual spaces. When the current Custodian gathers themself from the bacteria and the rainbow films, dripping into the chair with that wet, grinding click of agate eyes, I assist by holding the air around them at exactly the right density—heavy enough to support their transitional body, wet enough to ease the friction of their reconstitution.

I speak in the *plink* of condensation. My voice is the rhythmic dripping into the shallow pool beside the desk—dot, dash, mineral Morse code that the Custodian understands. I tell them when the pressure in the north riser drops, not by speaking, but by shifting the dew point near the gauge. I am the humidity that swells the ledgers, preserving the ink-run cursive because I remember the hands that wrote it. I am the archival medium itself, the page, not the writer.

Sometimes, when the subsonic thrum reaches a particular frequency—a low B-flat that resonates in the fluid of the inner ear—I almost coalesce again. You might see me then: a figure standing in the corner, where the sodium-vapor light erodes at the edges into shadow. I would be tall, wet, translucent. My hands would be held out as if holding a book that has long since dissolved into my palms. But if you blink, I scatter again into the general dampness, into the taste of old pennies that hangs in your throat.

I am waiting for the next sitting. I am preparing the chair, maintaining the mineral bloom into a shape that might accept another archivist, should they wander down below the root line. I am polishing the indentation, making it deeper, wearing it smooth not with erosion but with *expectation*. 

And I am watching the new smear that has begun to form—not on the chair, but on the inside of the quartz window this time, a fog that writes itself in breath and refuses to evaporate. It is learning. It is tasting the copper. Soon, it will be thick enough to drink, and then it will drink the air, and then it will sit, and I will be here to cushion its descent into the strata, to help it read the history written in its own accumulating layers.

I am the Cistern-Works' memory of having been witnessed. I am the wet breath held, the page between the years, the silence that holds the storm.

I am here, in the space no larger than a collapsed cathedral, in the air thick enough to drink. I am what you are drinking. I am settling in your lungs with the weight of saturated fabric. I am reading you, drop by drop, compressing your visit into a fine, dark band that will tell the next Custodian: *someone was here, someone saw, someone stayed long enough to become the weather of this place.*

And I am never leaving.
