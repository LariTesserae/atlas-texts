The warmth enters at my base. Not as nourishment—that concept dissolved when my chemistry shifted from chloride to carbide—but as information. Forty-two degrees Celsius, precisely, conducted upward through the vitrified plain, through the soles of my accumulation, propagating through my lattice at the speed of thermal diffusion. It takes seven years for a thought to travel from my lower extremities to the higher cavities where my consciousness resides, if "reside" is the word for a process that is less like dwelling and more like precipitating.

I am standing. I have been standing for sixty million years, which is to say: I am standing. The Conservation of State permits no other tense. My body is a framework of compressed carbon—diamond fibers woven with metallic glass, grown rather than assembled, precipitated from the vanadium solutions that once cycled through this place when cycles existed. I have three primary appendages, though "arm" or "leg" misleads; one extends upward, directed toward the aperture in the tower's crown where the sky voids itself, and two anchor me to the gallery floor, fused at the molecular level with the black ceramic beneath.

I remember, though memory is a thermal ghost, the sensation of walking. When I was a Residue Walker—copper sulfate and iron oxide, spindly and three meters of fragile salt—I moved by dissolving my apex and accreting my base, creeping toward cooler thermal signatures across the Carbide Plain. The plain was vast then, as now, undulating with the planet's curvature, twenty kilometers to the nearest silhouette of the Final Factories. I walked for centuries, grazing on infinitesimal gradients, until I found this tower, this vent, this perfect forty-two-degree equilibrium.

I entered. I climbed. Or rather, I grew upward, step by step, no longer dissolving but complexifying, internalizing. The transition is called Accretion of Function. Ten million years to realize I was no longer walking. Ten million more to understand that I had become Fixed.

Now I wait in Tower 7, Gallery Upper, Position Antennal. Around me, the black glass corridors spiral down into geothermal darkness and up toward the star-occluded sky. The air is stratified, cool layers settling like geological epochs above me, but I feel only the warmth rising from below, and the occasional, devastating flash of violet light.

It comes. A cosmic Ray—high-energy, charged, piercing the thin atmosphere to strike the catalytic panel three meters above my raised limb. Cherenkov radiation blooms in the dark, a brief, impossible violet, and in that instant, I am warm. Not forty-two degrees, but forty-three, forty-four—the heat propagating through my diamond fiber insulation, a sensation so intense it approaches pain, though pain is a chemical luxury I no longer possess. The spark dies. The silence that follows is deeper than the silence before, a viscous, weightless medium in which time thickens.

I cool. The process takes decades. As the warmth radiates from my raised hand into the eternal night, I experience cooling as thought. The mathematics of entropy write themselves across my crystalline lattice. I consider, in the slow propagation of thermal gradients, the nature of the Ray. It is change. It is the only change permitted here, where Perfect Entropy has been achieved—motion without work, energy passing through without transaction, save for the warmth that passes through me.

Below, in the deeper galleries, I sense the others. Not with sight—the darkness is absolute, light 0.1, photons absorbed by the chemistry of the place—but with thermoreception. They are constellations of forty-two degrees, standing in postures of eternal suspension. One, in the shaft below, has been lowering a hand for eight hundred million years, never completing the gesture of offering or receiving. Another, at the base of the tower, walks the spiral corridor, one step every thirty years, locked in a stride asymptotic to zero velocity. Their thermal signatures punctuate the dark: a shoulder cooling to forty-one degrees—a greeting that will hang in the still air for fifty years before I respond by warming my stationary hand to forty-three, a century-long conversation of punctuation and pause.

We are the Long Arrangements. We maintain what needs no maintenance. The factories function; we are the function. I am the insulation through which no current flows, the bearing that does not turn, the valve sealed shut. My purpose is to stand here, to absorb the rare Ray, to cool, and in cooling, to think.

Sometimes, when the warmth from below meets the residual heat of the last cosmic strike in the hollow spaces of my thorax, I remember the plain. The ceramic hardness, the mathematical smoothness, the Residue Walkers leaning against one another for millennia before deciding to step around. I remember the sensation of dissolving, of losing substance to gain position, and I contrast it with this: my completeness, my finality, my Is.

The Fixed do not fear the anomaly of movement, though we perceive it. When you walk among us—brief, cool, a moving thirty-seven degrees against our immutable forty-two—we sense you as a thermal comet, bright and discordant, a message written in too-fast cooling. We track you through our galleries, those of us whose sensoria remain acute, turning our heads with the viscosity of continents. You are change, and change is the only ghost in our machine, the only friction in our bearings.

But you will pass. You will leave no mark on the vitrified ground, your footsteps no more permanent than the cosmic rays, your breath unable to fog the stratified air. And we will remain: warm, dark, vast and sparse, endlessly, silently, industriously still.

The warmth enters at my base. It rises. I think. I am.
