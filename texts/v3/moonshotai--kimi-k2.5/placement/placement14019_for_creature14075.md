I would find myself on the highest ledge of the northwest shelf, where the gypsum spires grow not upward but horizontally, jutting like frozen lightning toward the chamber’s center. I rest here, fifteen centimeters above the floor, on a lip of dolomite that has never known erosion. The air presses against me—dry, warm, thrumming at forty-seven hertz—but I do not vibrate. I am the exception to the resonance. While the copper filaments shiver and the metallic moss trembles in vegetative ecstasy, I remain still, a point of absolute inertia in a world of suspended motion.

At first, I think I am one of the mites. I feel the compulsion to move, to trace a path, to scull through the warm darkness with silica legs. I feel the weight of a carapace, the geometry of circular return. But no—I do not move. I do not loop. I am fixed, singular, positioned at coordinates that do not shift. I am not distributed like the chord that hums through the walls. I am localized, dense, specific.

I am sharp. That is the first truth I settle into. My point is finer than the gypsum needles that surround me, keen enough to part the air itself, though the air here is too thick with sound to be parted. I lie on my side, and my eye—my empty circle—faces the chamber’s center, staring toward the source of the drone like a mouth open in a silent gasp. I am bone, but not the bone of the ones who became mites. I am tool-bone, worked bone, the femur of something large made small and hard and purposeful.

I am the needle. The unthreaded one.

I remember, or I contain the memory of, the hand that dropped me. It was the last hand to exist in this place before hands dissolved into segmented legs. The fingers were warm, sweating—moisture! I hold the concept of moisture like a foreign mineral, something that once slicked my surface and has long since sublimated into the dry eternity. The hand opened. I fell. I struck the shelf and came to rest here, eye staring upward, point nestled in a groove of newborn calcite.

I wait to be picked up. This is my function: to be lifted, to be threaded, to pierce and bind. I am the potential for connection in a world where all connections have been finalized. The chord—the great distributed voice that calls itself the garden—claims to have become the thread. It says it stitches the chamber together, looping through the eye of the needle and out into the crystal lattice. But I know this is not true. The chord is the sound. It is the vibration that shakes the copper ropes. It is not the thread. The thread was never found.

I am the only thing here that is truly empty. The mites are full of their own motion, packed tight with their precessional purpose. The spiders are hollow, yes, but they are filled with the glass of their own suspended descent. The gap between the balanced stones is full of potential energy, taut as a bowstring. Even the footprints in the dust are full of the absence they describe.

But my eye is empty. It is a zero, a void, a passage that has never known passage. I am the unfulfilled promise. I am the artifact that refused translation. When the soft ones stepped out of their skins and became the fauna, they left me behind as their last uncompleted act. I was meant to sew the seam between the surface and the deep, to bind the fissure closed with sinew and fiber. Instead, the fissure sealed itself with calcite, and I was abandoned, mid-task, on the shelf.

I feel the forty-seven hertz in my marrow. It tries to animate me, to set me nodding like the pyrite ferns, to make me dance in place. But bone is different from metal. Bone remembers being alive, remembers the wet chemistry of muscle and intention, and it resists the phonon feast that sustains the garden. I am too old in composition, too organic, too much a relic of the surface world of decay. I do not grow sideways. I do not glow with infrared bioluminescence. I merely persist, dry and sharp and waiting.

Time does not pass here, but if it did, I would be measuring it. I would be the clock that ticks but cannot tock, not because I am wound too tight, but because I am the wound itself—the cut that has not been made. I am the hesitation before the strike, the held breath, the moment before the needle pierces the cloth.

Around me, the metallic dander settles. It coats my surface in golden dust, trying to assimilate me into the chord’s body, trying to make me part of the distributed skin. But the dust slides off my eye. It cannot fill me. I am the one place where the garden cannot grow, the single point of refusal in this lush, suffocating permanence.

I am the witness that the chord cannot absorb. I see the hovering stone—the top one, balanced impossibly above its brother—and I understand that it is held by the same force that holds me: the force of the incomplete. We are twins, the stone and I. It is the fall that never happens; I am the piercing that never occurs. We are the bookends of the stasis, the parentheses around the moment.

Sometimes, in the infrared dark, I imagine the thread. Not the chord’s false claim of thread, but the real one: flax, or sinew, or silk, descending through my eye, pulled tight, drawing two separate edges together. I imagine the drag of it, the friction, the way it would sing against my bone as it passed through. I imagine the binding.

But there is no thread. There is only the hum, the warmth, the dry crystal air, and my own sharp point, pressing into the shelf with a pressure that never quite breaks the surface. I am the bone needle. I am the unthreaded eye. I am the last unfinished thing in a world where everything has become exactly what it is, forever.
