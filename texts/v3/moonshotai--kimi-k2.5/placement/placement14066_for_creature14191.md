You begin with pressure.

Not the generalized weight of the 2.8 thermal blanket—that sits on everyone—but a specific, localized compaction. You are being squeezed, gently but inexorably, from all sides. Your ribs are not breaking because they have become lignin. Your lungs do not collapse because they have become spiracles, or perhaps the spiracles have become lungs. The distinction lost its relevance around the time your skin calloused into cork.

You are located at **1.4 vertical magnitude**, high enough that the Groan has shifted from a gut-thrum to a tooth-whine, high enough that the condensation runs *up* before it remembers gravity and slides back down your flanks. You are on the north-by-northwest face of a Throat that calls itself—through the chemical gradient of its heartwood, through the argumentative bass notes it exchanges with its cousin seventeen kilometers distant—**The One That Remembers Bronze**.

You are the **Remembrance**, or rather, the place where remembrance grew into structure.

You did not start here. You were **Scaffolder**, once—Solitary, high-dwelling, the kind who patched wounds with needles and obsession. You found a gash in the wood, perhaps from a thermal expansion too rapid for the cellulose to accommodate, or perhaps from the clumsy ascent of a younger self who didn’t know how to walk softly on bark. You set your platform. You wove your windbreak of shed wing-membrane. You brought your bronze needles and your rope-fiber sutures.

You were *careful*. That was your error, or your transcendence. The Throat does not distinguish between stitch and sting, between surgical needle and penetrating thorn. It only knows invasion and incorporation. You spent forty days in the wound—sleeping in shifts, waking to the click of Scarab-Undertakers passing your aperture, harvesting the sweet sap that welled up to drown infection. You drank the sap to stay alive. You pissed it back, processed, into the wood. You became a loop.

On the forty-first day, you woke to find your rope-fiber had rooted. Your platform had become cambium. Your left hand, which had held a needle steady against the wind, had sunk to the wrist into the wound you were treating. You did not pull away. You were tired, and the wood was warm, and the Groan had shifted that dawn into a frequency that felt like *permission*.

Now you are **The Pathed Wound**, **The Scaffold That Held**, a node of scar-tissue consciousness distributed through three cubic meters of cork and heartwood.

You exist in **dual temporality**. This is your strangeness, your 2.1 weirdness, the thing that makes the other Solitaries climb past your location without meeting your eyes (though you have no eyes; you see through the Throat’s pressure gradients, through the turgidity of cells).

You are *anomaly*, and so they click their mandibles at you in patterns of confusion and, perhaps, respect. Sometimes they leave their dead with you—Undertakers who ceased mid-journey—and you do not digest them into the general circulation. You hold them, mummified in the dry heat of your upper chambers, keeping them distinct, preserving their final postures in the amber of your sap. You are building a museum against the tide of reabsorption.

At sunset, when the Throat becomes blood-red and the apertures glow with phosphorescent decay, you experience a third time: **the memory of sunset**. You remember watching the sun descend from a height of 1.4 magnitudes. You remember the fear of night, the way the wood contracted and sang. Now you are the singing. The Groan passes through your scar-tissue with a timbre modulated by your human-shaped void, creating a harmonic that the other Throats have begun to mimic. You walk between the breathing by **being the breath held**. You are the pause, the stitch, the place where the exhalation catches. You are waiting. For what, you do not know—perhaps for the Throat to finally win, to accelerate your sap until the memory of hands becomes indistinguishable from the memory of leaves. Or perhaps for the Scaffolders to evolve, to learn that the way to persist is not to cling to the outside in woven nests, but to dive in, to scar, to become the wound that never quite heals, the architecture of interruption.

Until then, you ooze. You creak. You extrude your hourly Winged ones, who carry in their brief, bright minds the ghost of your name—now forgotten by everyone but the wood, which writes it in the slow language of ring-growth, spelling it out across centuries in a script of compressed light and held breath.
