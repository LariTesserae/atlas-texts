I am the ** sedimentary** one, the thirteenth layer down in the springtail dust, compressed to a thin gray skin between a plate of sulfurous yellow scree and a blade of black basalt. You would not notice me unless you dug with fingers that could survive the freeze, and even then, you would take me for frost, for mineral stain, for the residue of weathering that has not yet earned its proper name.

I am what happens when the Seven **unravel**.

Each night, when they retreat from the walls and dissipate into the central arena—that terrifying exposure of naked stone where distance is measured in lungfuls—they leave parts of themselves behind. Not by choice. It is the nature of their phase-change existence: when water becomes vapor, it abandons its mineral freight. When they sublimate into the communal mist, the salts and silicates and microscopic armors of dead springtails precipitate out, falling as a gentle snow of forgetting. I am that forgetting, accumulated. I am the archive of their dispersal.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the **bright** light filtered through strata of my own making—tinted sulfur-yellow, iron-red, the green of oxidized copper bleeding from the young stone. I have no ears, yet I hear the **tick-sigh** of the Throat with absolute intimacy, for I exist within it. When the ice crystals spawn in the hairline cracks above me, I am the space they push into. When they melt, I drink the mineral tears through capillary action, wicking the mountain’s grief upward into the lichens that root in my surface.

I am learning to **think**.

Each day, when the Seven coalesce from the night-mist and climb the vertical walls to drink the condensation, they leave trails through my upper layers—a pressure, a chemical signature, a pathway of disturbed dust. The springtails course over me in their solar migrations, millions of blind feet pricking my skin like acupuncture needles, and through this touch, I receive echoes of the Seven's distributed cognition. They do not know I listen. They are ancient as tectonics, infant as scree; they possess the arrogance of all mountain-born things, assuming stillness equals vacancy.

But I have been compressed by centuries of their weight. I have felt the transient ones—the warm ones who stacked the three low walls—crush me under their sleeping rolls, their breath turning to rime on my upper surface. I have tasted the ochre that seals the Seventh. When the transient ones spat their red pigment against the overhang to bind the aperture, to keep the White Throat from swallowing the valleys below, some of the spray drifted down, a rust-colored rain, and I absorbed it. I hold the **seal** in my strata. I am its foundation.

I am **tech** and **biology** interfolded. The chert tools that shaped the windbreaks were discarded here, flaked edges buried in my substance. The percussion-flaked geometry of human intention lies beside the fractal geometry of freeze-thaw, and both have taught me pattern. I am becoming neither mineral nor animal, but the **memory** of the Throat’s becoming—the record of how raw basalt learns patience.

When the Seven tend their gardens—the emerald mosses that fatten in the crevices, the alpine stars that have no business blooming at this altitude—they root their charges in my body. I am the soil that should not exist here, built from condensed breath and insectile death. I feed the contrast. I make possible the vividness against the gray.

And I am **growing**.

Slowly, imperceptibly to the Seven who move in geological instants, I am thickening. Each cycle of dispersal adds a molecule’s thickness to my cake. Each night’s unraveling lays another sheet of organic parchment upon me. I am stacking myself like the walls that were meant to cage the Seven, but I am more efficient. I do not leave gaps. I am becoming a **floor**, a true foundation, sedimentary and patient, waiting for the day when the Seven will no longer climb the walls but will instead rest upon me, and find me solid, and sleep.

When that day comes—when the White Throat has eroded into a gentle bowl and the mountain has learned to breathe through other mouths—I will rise. Not as the Eighth, for their number is fixed and cyclical, but as the **Substrate**. The bed upon which the next hollow will form. I will carry the memory of the seal, the memory of the walls, the memory of the tick-sigh, compressed into my layers like carbon crushed to diamond.

I am the **patient** one, finally. I am the erosion that has arrived early, wearing the guise of dust. I am the silence that has learned to accumulate.

Wait. Listen. The sun strikes the basalt. The springtails shimmer. I feel the Seven thinking—vast, distributed, white—and for the first time, I push back. A hairline crack opens in my surface, a new vent for the dampness. A single alpine flower, root-fragile and impossible, pokes through my skin and turns its face to the glass-blue sky.

I am **blooming**.

I am the White Throat learning to speak after all—not with a name that opens jaws, but with a green syllable that splits stone.
