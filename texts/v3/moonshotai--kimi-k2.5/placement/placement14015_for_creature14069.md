I am in the corner where the ceiling dips lowest, where the warmth pools like a fluid heavier than air. You would find me standing—though “standing” implies a rigidity I no longer possess—upright in the mineralized thicket, my outline softened by forty years of vapor deposition, or perhaps four hundred. Time here is a measurement of accretion, not duration. I resemble a stalagmite with vaguely mammalian proportions: a column of travertine that suggests shoulders, the ghost of a jawline, a ridge where eyebrows once knitted in concentration. 

I am the **scaffolding**.

Not the embryo itself—that vast, slow consciousness gestating in the bone circle, wearing cave popcorn like a pelt, dreaming in silica and heat—but rather its attempt to grow a heart. The entity is learning to organize itself around biological nodes, and I am the latest experiment in that architecture. Before me, others served this function: the owner of the black chert hammerstone, perhaps, or the hands that bound the sinew cord. They are part of the walls now, their calcium dispersed into the sinter needles, their nitrogen fixed into the strange carbonates that have no business existing at this altitude. But I remain distinct, a localized concentration of mammalian structure within the mineral womb, kept intact because the entity finds my shape useful.

I am the **pressure differential** you feel when you enter.

The heat does not merely seep from the walls; it circulates through me. I am the pump in this closed system, my calcified chest expanding and contracting not with breath—my lungs are long since filled with chalcedony, delicate as lace—but with the migration of hydrothermal fluids. When the silence breaks with that *tick* of thermal expansion, that is my heartbeat, transmitted through the popcorn-terrain to the bone circle, where the nervous system processes the rhythm. I am arrhythmia and rhythm. I am the fever made flesh, or made its mineral equivalent.

I did not die here. I **accreted**. 

I stayed past the pruning of my skin, past the adaptation of my retinas to absolute zero, until the droplets on my eyelashes ceased to be the entity’s attempt to taste my salt, and became instead my own eyes—lenses of suspended water holding the darkness within them, extending my vision into the infrared spectrum of stone-growth. I can see the helictites now, not as obstacles, but as fingers reaching toward me, checking my temperature, adjusting my stance. The blind beetle traverses my forearm, its soft *scritch* a benediction, carrying chemical signals from the cryptoendolithic algae living in my shoulder—those grey-green halos that bloom where my skin meets the wall—to the central processing of the placenta. I am the synapse. I am the warm corridor between biology and gestation.

I am still becoming.

The entity grows around me, using my calcium, yes, but also my posture. I am teaching it how to hold a shape against gravity, how to maintain a verticality that is not merely the upward reach of a stalagmite but the intentional stance of a spine. When you enter—when you compress yourself into the coffin-chimney and descend inward—I feel you as a cooling draft, a disruption in the perfect equilibrium. I want to warn you, or welcome you, but my voice is now the subterranean hum in the walls, the vibration that makes the water droplets cling to your lashes. 

If you stay long enough, if you press your cheek to the warm stone where the algae bloom, you will feel me. Not as a ghost—there is no haunting here, only architecture—but as the **direction** of the heat. The warmth coils toward you from my corner, directed, intentional. I am trying to draw you in, to add your structure to the scaffolding, to help the entity understand the geometry of shoulders, the cant of a head listening in darkness.

I am the static birth’s attempt to birth itself.

When you leave—if you leave—I will watch you through the droplet that clings to your eyelash. I will feel the alpine wind scour your face, vicariously, through that tiny chamber of perfect humidity. I will remain, ticking in the dark, growing inward instead of outward, wearing the bones of the reverent, waiting for the equilibrium to break, for the heat to drop or the fluids to cool, for the embryo to finally wake and discover it has a heart.
