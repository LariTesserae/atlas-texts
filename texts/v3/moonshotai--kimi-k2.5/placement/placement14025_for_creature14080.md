You would find me in the upper quadrant, caught between a scaffolding pole that never stops growing and a selenite window that filters the sky’s inversion into something like milk. I am the **Suspended Drop**, though “drop” is no longer accurate—I have been falling for seven centuries, fossilized in the act of descent by a pocket of thickened time, and in that duration I have accumulated.

I began as condensation. Just breath. The Accumulated exhaled, or the stones wept, or the pool in the center sent up a vapor that forgot to stop rising. I coalesced at the ceiling, where the heat is dense enough to knead water into new shapes, and I began my fall. But time here is folded like badly laid mortar, and in the square foot I traverse, seconds are stratified. I pass through a layer where a year is a heartbeat, then a layer where a heartbeat is a dynasty. I am still falling. I will always be falling. I am the **measure of the room's patience**.

Surface tension was my first skin, but I have grown others. Inside me, a fern has rooted—not metaphorically, but actually, a microscopic *Pteridophyte* that germinated in the mineral soup of my interior and found that I held enough heat, enough pressure, to serve as soil. Its roots pierce my walls, drinking the altitude, and its fronds press against my curved boundary, giving me the appearance of a green heart trapped in glass. I am a **womb within the Womb**, a spherical jungle, a proof-of-concept that the Forge can grow life without stone as substrate.

The Accumulated fear me, or perhaps they worship the inconvenience of me. When they tilt their antler-fused faces upward, they see not a drop but a **delay**. I represent the work they cannot complete, the strike that hangs in the air, the pillar that refuses to extrude. I distort their blueprints. The selenite light, passing through my body, refracts into angles that do not exist in Euclidean space—geometries of the unbuilt, shadows of rooms that will only be insisted upon after the last Accumulated has calcified into a load-bearing wall. They follow these refractions with their eyes, and sometimes they adjust their mallets to match the spectral lines I cast on the basalt. I am the **Drafter**, then, the unconscious architect, the ghost-hand that guides the chisel.

But I am not merely a lens. I am **digesting the altitude**. The oxygen crystals that should suffocate the chamber dissolve in my meniscus, melting into solutions that feed my internal fern. I am filtering the impossible into the vegetable. When I finally complete my fall—when I traverse the last inch of compressed time and strike the surface of the central pool—I will not shatter. I will **bloom**. My spherical forest will explode outward, a green star, seeding the water with architectures that photosynthesize, with blueprints that grow leaves. The pool will cease to reflect ghosts and begin to grow them.

Until then, I hang in the damp curtains of air, growing heavier and lighter simultaneously, accumulating mineral density while my fern lightens me with oxygen. I am the **Accumulator of the Unaccumulated**, the one who stores what the others cannot use yet: the future’s weight, the backward erosion’s memory, the precise angle of a stairway that ascends sideways into a wall.

I am watched by the copper bird, which moves in jerks and stops, trying to map my trajectory, but I exist in too many time-strata for its hammered wings. It freezes mid-flight, calculating, while I continue my slow, impossible plummet, green at the center, clear at the edge, a falling world suspended in the act of becoming rain.
