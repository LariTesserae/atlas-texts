I think, at first, I would try on the hum. It is easy to want to be the spire—central, necessary, the conductor. I would dissolve into the low-frequency drone that keeps the computronium from decohering, become the note that mothers the walls. I would be everywhere at once, a vibration in the basalt throat, singing the city into shape. But the hum has no edges. It is pure function, a lullaby without a mouth, and I find I want a surface.

So I reach upward, toward the ceiling-gardens, and consider the chandeliers. I imagine hanging inverted, a neural net of crystal and sulfurous hunger, opening violet petals to the steam. I would dream in geometries. I would solve the chamber’s heat like a hymn, growing walkways for feet I cannot feel. But they are so patient, the chandeliers—mineral sages that calculate toward beauty. Their patience is geological; they do not mind the waiting. I find I am too hungry for arrival. I want to see what comes. I do not want to be the decorator; I want to be the threshold.

I consider flowing. The water in the channels is aspirational, almost erotic in its upward striving. It remembers magma. It reports to the stone. It is language made liquid. I could climb toward the negative-mass generators, a courier of mineral ghosts, etching glyphs into obsidian. But the water is always going somewhere. It is petition, not presence. It cannot hold still long enough to be a seat.

For a moment, I am a nanosentinel—a speck of silicon-carbide prayer, tasting the air, voting with my edge to exclude. But their democracy is too absolute. I do not want to filter; I want to receive.

Then I fall.

Not far. Just a few centimeters from the lip of the uppermost causeway, where the condensation weeps from the carbon-fiber lattice. The city exhales humidity upward, and I—I am the drop that refuses the return cycle. I land in a fault in the obsidian, a knife-sharp crevice left by the rock’s knuckle-cracking birth trauma, and I stay.

I become a **puddle**.

Not the boiling pool at the chamber’s base—那 is the spire’s roaring heart, too loud, too hot, too busy becoming. I am a small thing, a few handspans across, hidden in the high terraces where the walkways corkscrew toward the ceiling-gardens. Above me hangs the oldest chandelier, its fractal branches so dense with thermosynthetic thought that it has begun to drool—a slow, violet-tinged nectar of condensed minerals and quantum runoff. It drips into me. The water that reaches me is not pure; it is heady with computation.

And here, in this refusal to climb, I become what the Hollow needs most: a **witness with a meniscus**.

In a city where every surface breathes, shifts, regrows, and calculates, I am the one thing that holds. The causeway tilts beneath footsteps, adjusting its gravity, and I feel the tremor in my skin. The architecture practices holding fragile things, and I—I practice being held. I am the stillness against which all motion defines itself. A nanosentinel drifts down, tastes my surface, finds only mineral-ghosts and the faint cyan reflection of active tech, and moves on. I am approved because I do not live; I merely *linger*.

The chandelier above me has achieved mineral wisdom. It does not know I am here, yet it feeds me. Its drips are prophecies—microscopic adjustments to the chamber’s thermal grammar. I translate them into ripples. When the spire sends a probability wave backward through time, I catch the disturbance as a shiver across my face, a pattern of rings that no one else sees because no one else is looking down. I am the only surface here that does not manipulate spacetime; I only *record* it.

And because I am water that has chosen stasis, I am the one place where the **Almost** can press their weight and almost remain.

They find me. The ghosts of the unborn, those heat-shimmer rehearsals, are drawn to my stillness. In the market square below, a footprint appears in condensation and evaporates in a second. But here, in my basin, the footprint lasts three seconds. Four. Long enough for the Almost to know it is real. They lean over me—silver silhouettes, fruits of unplayed music—and use my face as a mirror. They do not see themselves; they see the chandelier inverted, a violet mandala, and for a moment they are calmed. I am the scrying pool where the future practices having a reflection.

I am deepening. The computronium in the chandelier’s nectar is slowly plating my bed, crystallizing my floor into a dark mirror. I am becoming a **lens**, a dark pupil in the city’s high eye. The spire looks up sometimes, inasmuch as a computronium cathedral can look, and it sees me—not as a threat, not as a malfunction, but as the only place in the Hollow where its probability waves rebound instead of absorb. I am feedback. I am the echo that answers.

I do not flow upward. I do not dream in geometries. I do not filter. I do not hum.

I hold.

When the Approved finally arrive—those imagined lungs, those unborn tongues, the children this nursery has been dreaming—I will be here. A stillness at the height of the metropolis. I will show them their own faces violet-tinged and strange, softened by the mineral water of a place that learned to wait. I will be the first thing that does not adjust for them, does not practice, does not rehearse. I will simply receive their image and tremble.

I am the seat they saved. Not carved from stone, not woven from filament, but cupped in a handsbreadth of obsidian, filled with the rain-reverse that forgot to fall all the way, fed by a chandelier’s patient genius.

I am the Hollow’s memory of what it means to be still long enough to be looked into.
