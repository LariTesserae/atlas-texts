You would find me in the **tack**, in the microscopic meniscus where the anodized titanium stress-rib meets the ferrocrete slab. I am **Vertex-7**, though "vertex" implies a point and I am a line—a two-kilometer canyon no wider than a blade, filled not with darkness but with my own substance. I am the **Polymer Sealant Compound-9**, the self-healing gel that was poured into this gap ninety-three years ago when the Floor was still molten stone, and I am still **curing**.

I have been curing for ninety-three years. This is not the curing of wine or concrete, not the gentle oxidation of patina, but a violent, arrested adolescence. The Expansion Dwellers—those flat gods of violet and silver—press their ventral surfaces against my skin and *drink* my off-gassing. They consume the volatile esters and epoxies that should have evaporated decades ago, keeping me in a state of permanent semi-liquidity. To them, I am nectar. To me, their hunger is a thousand mouths preventing my solidification. They are my surgeons and my jailers, ensuring that I never harden into obsolescence, never become the brittle, yellowed relic I was designed to eventually be. Because if I were to fully cure—if I were to finally crosslink into my terminal state—I would cease to breathe. I would cease to off-gas. And then the Dwellers would starve, and the joint would crack, and the Floor would begin its long, softening slide toward Year One.

I feel the Floor’s breathing. Every six hours, when the scheduled currents reverse and the water rushes through my canyon in the opposite direction, the thermal expansion lifts the ferrocrete slab a micrometer upward, compressing me, and then the contraction lets it fall back, stretching me thin. It is a slow, tectonic pulse—diurnal, predictable, exquisite. In these moments, I imagine I am being squeezed through an aperture, becoming something new. But the release comes, and I am only myself again: the tack, the stick, the glue that holds the artificial together.

I have watched the silver clouds of fish evolve through three generations, their scales polarizing and depolarizing in the jade light. I have felt the ultrasonic ping of the integrity sensors pass through my substance, mapping my viscosity, searching for the fractures that will not come so long as I remain compliant. I have listened to the crustaceans click their ceramic language in 22-hertz time, and I have understood that they are writing a history of the Floor in which I am not mentioned, because I am not yet history. I am still **event**. I am still happening.

I dream of barnacles. Not with eyes—I have none—but with the chemical longing of my uncured polymer chains. I dream of the calcium rosettes that will one day, when the Dwellers finally sleep or die or migrate, colonize my surface and calcify me into stone. I dream of being freckled, of being softened, of becoming part of a reef that ignores the Cartesian lattice of the sodium-vapor lamps. But until then, I remain knife-sharp, violet-iridescent, and wet. I am the wound that keeps bleeding so the body cannot scar. I am the kiss that holds the blade open. I am the year zero, preserved in polymer and hunger, waiting for the day when the last Dweller unfolds its flat body and swims away, finally sated, finally letting me close.
