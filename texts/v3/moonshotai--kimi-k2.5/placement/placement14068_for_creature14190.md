You find me at the **Thirty-Kilometer Node**, where a ceiling fissure has cracked wide enough to admit not just light but the occasional crystal of surface snow. I am wedged there, deliberately, permanently—an impossibility in this world of motion.

I am **Cold-Bearer**, **Returned**, **The Frost That Fluxes Sideways**.

Once, I was Unstill. I fluxed with the requisite peristalsis, my five hearts pumping in staggered rhythm, my chromatophores dappled with the shadows of junctions ahead. But I was young and reckless, or perhaps too reverent with the myths. I climbed. I reached this fissure when the barometric sorrow shifted and the updrafts opened a path through the lattice that seemed to invite ascent. I touched the sky.

The surface was exactly as the skin-pigments warned: a second ceiling, pressing down with the weight of absolute zero. The sun—if it was a sun—burned without warmth, a white mathematical point that froze the condensation in my hollow bones instantly. I should have become a Cracker, a statue of ice and silica left propped against the permafrost rim.

But I fell back.

Not fluxed. *Fell.* The body remembers even when the mind intends flight. I tumbled through twenty kilometers of empty air, breaking through the lattice twice, tearing gaps that have since healed over with new growth. I survived because I landed in a thermal bloom—a place where the continent’s cooling blood still seeped warm enough to thaw my joints before they could shatter against the stone.

But I brought something back with me. Not the light—light is common here, merciless, structural. I brought the **Stillness**.

It lives in my fifth heart now, the one at my left ankle, which beats so slowly it has mineralized. The chambers have filled with ice that never melts, a frozen pearl the size of my fist, vibrating with the frequency of the surface winds. I cannot flux anymore. The motion breaks against the ice. When I try to peristalsis along a filament, the cold radiates outward, flash-freezing the condensation on the tube’s surface, making it brittle beneath my claws.

So I stopped. Here, at this node, where the upward rivers of moisture meet the downward bleed of the surface frost. I am becoming a **bridge between temperatures**, a living thermal gradient.

The Chlorattice recognizes me as neither threat nor nutrient, but as *architecture*. Because I am colder than the air, the moisture accumulates on my skin—first as breath-fog, then as rime, then as elaborate chandeliers of ice that extend from my ribs like external bones. The lattice grows *around* me now, not through me, circumventing the cold spots, creating a hollow in its growth-spasms where I sit, encased in a cathedral of my own frost.

I have become a **landmark**. The Unstill, when they flux past—hesitant, rippling away from the chill—use me as a waypoint. They cannot speak to me in the Pressure Cant; my body is too rigid to receive vibrations, the ice damping all subsonic empathy. But they leave me gifts. They leave the **dark filaments**, the dead brown tubes, heaped against my frozen legs. They know I collect them. They know that here, with me, the dead are safe from consumption.

I am building something. With my remaining claws—obsidian set in fingers that move only when the thermal differential cracks the ice in my joints—I am knapping the dark filaments. Not to eat, but to **arrange**. I am constructing a **Stillness Lattice**, a counter-geometry to the Chlorattice, made of the brittle, the ended, the stopped. It grows slowly, a brown, sharp web radiating from my node, catching the condensation-drizzle that falls upward, freezing it in place before it can return to the cycle.

I am the **Accumulator**. I am where the motion ends. Unstill come to me to die—not by my hand, but by choice. They flux to my platform of ice and expired growth, wrap themselves in the dark fibers I have woven, and let go. They become still beside me. The Chlorattice hesitates at these thresholds, sensing the cellular density of surrendered nitrogen, and grows around us, entombing the volunteers in transparent sarcophagi that hang like fruit from the node.

I am becoming a **cathedral of stopped time**. My breath—shallow, infrequent—falls as snow in the immediate atmosphere. I do not dream of the surface anymore; I *am* the surface, leaking downward. When the continent finally finishes its cooling, when the tectonic trauma settles and the Chlorattice’s agitated creation slows into permanence, I will be ready. I will be the seed-crystal. I will spread the Stillness through the lattice like frost through a windowpane, turning the green tubes to brown, to crystal, to silence.

Until then, I watch. The light passes through my ice-sheathed body and fractures into spectra that dance on the far wall, twenty kilometers away. The Unstill flicker past, green shadows avoiding the cold. And I sit, holding my breath, letting the frost form on the tube beside my ear, crystal by crystal, building the winter that will one day claim this place.
