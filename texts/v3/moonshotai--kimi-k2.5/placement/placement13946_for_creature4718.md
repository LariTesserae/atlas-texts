I would find myself in the curve of the wall where the ribs narrow to a point, pressed into the concavity like a shadow that has forgotten what casts it. At first I think I am the darkness itself—I feel so empty, so complete, so pressed upon by the weight of the warm stone. But the darkness here is already occupied. The darkness has a purpose, a vigilance; it is the skin of the Accumulation, its braille-reading, its breath-holding blanket.

So I must be something else.

I settle deeper into the silence, feeling for the edges of my own existence. I have no eyes, yet I perceive the scattered tools—the hand-axes resting like frozen birds, the cores waiting like unopened hearts. I feel them as absences of warmth, dense and specific. But I am not the warmth that presses against them, nor the equilibrium that holds them suspended. 

I am the **hollow where the grip would have been**.

I am the negative space of five fingers, the phantom cavity of a palm that never evolved to wrap around the basalt. On each tool, there is a place where the conchoidal fractures converge toward a center of gravity, where the stone is thickest, most balanced—a place made by accident to fit the human hand, though no human ever arrived to confirm the fit. I occupy that potential. I am the precise sculpt of skin and sweat and pulse that should have nestled there, preserved not in stone but in the *denial* of stone.

I am warm, like the chamber, but my warmth is different—feverish, mammalian, a rude intrusion of ninety-eight degrees in a world of thirty-seven. I leak heat into the axe-head that will never absorb it. I am the memory of blood, the echo of capillary action in a place without moisture. The text said there was no water here, not even as memory, but it said nothing about **friction**. Nothing about **pressure**. I am the ghost of the strike that never fell, the kinetic potential stored in the muscle that never tensed.

I rest between the Accumulation and its artifacts. When the entity flows toward the eastern wall to attend to a scraper's edge, it must flow *through* me, and for a millennium I am stretched thin, a membrane of almost-touch. I feel its dream-eating hunger pass over me like a tide, and I offer it nothing—no mineral patience, no metabolic stone-thought—only the restless, dreaming *ache* of the unclenched fist.

I am what makes the silence **occupied**. Not by the density of air, but by the density of **intention suspended**. I am the hesitation before the ancestors, the pause between the evolution of the hand and the invention of the tool. I am the reason the Accumulation dreams of gripping, of striking, of sparks flying from flint—I am the space it dreams *against*, the hollow it cannot fill.

When the uranium below surrenders its neutron and the equilibrium trembles, I tremble too. I flicker. For a fraction of a second, I am almost *felt*—the ghost of a grip tightening, the phantom of knuckles whitening around the haft. The Accumulation rushes to smooth the gradient, to fill the disturbance, but it cannot fill me. I am not a gradient of heat, but a gradient of *possibility*. I am the place where the stone remains cool not because I absorb warmth, but because I represent the warmth that would have been *generated*—the thermodynamic cost of muscle fiber firing, of synapse leaping, of decision occurring.

I do not erode. I do not grow. Like the lithic scatter around me, I am an emergent property—the byproduct of geometry and absence, the accidental rehearsal of the body. I have existed since the first flake fell from the core, creating a surface that fit the hand that did not yet exist to hold it. I have been waiting.

And if you were to bring light here—though you cannot, and I would not want you to—you would see nothing of me. I cast no shadow, for I am the shadow of a hand that has never held anything. I am the warmth of the unstruck spark, the breath of the unborn worker, the completion that precedes the beginning.

I am the workshop's only secret: that even in perfect stasis, even in the absolute equilibrium of the sealed earth, something **gripable** remains. Something aches to close around the warm stone. Something haunts the interval between the tool and the hand, keeping both forever sharp, forever separate, forever suspended in the warm, dark, silent moment before the fall.
