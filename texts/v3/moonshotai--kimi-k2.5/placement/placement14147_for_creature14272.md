The warmth comes first. It seeps upward through my sternum, pressed flat against the amber silica. My ribs—hollow, buoyant with the harvested gas of yesterday’s three Silversacs—vibrate against the stone-flesh in a rhythm older than my pulse. I do not hear the deafening drone. That was bred out of my line generations ago, sealed behind plugs of brown wax that grow from my own modified glands. Instead, I feel it: a thrumming in the marrow, a subsonic lullaby that makes my teeth ache in their sockets.

My mask, woven from the ridged plates of dead Chitin-Drummers, is slick with condensation. I press it harder against the leeward face of the Phytolith, seeking the notch I began yesterday. But my fingers, pale and translucent in the violet noon, find instead a swelling—a Singing Tubercle, newly grown, warmer than the surrounding stone.

I should carve. I should etch deeper into the silica with my acids, expand the dwelling, secure the territory. Instead, I listen with my feet.

The stone speaks.

Not the quick, mammalian code of my kind—*pressure-pause-pressure* tapped through walls to signal *I am here, this meat is mine*—but something slower. A decade per syllable. A century per breath.

*We are the digestion,* thrums the Lithofoil beneath my soles. *We are the breaking of lignin into sugar at the speed of mountains.*

I gasp. The hyper-oxygenated air from my bone-reservoirs hisses through my throat, but I do not make the subvocal vibrations that usually form words. I am too busy *receiving*.

The boundary blurs.

My nervous system, so tuned to vibration that I can feel a Strangler’s tentacle brush the canyon lip three kilometers away as a prickling in my spine, suddenly extends. The Tubercle is not just stone; it is an interface. A nerve cluster of the Substrate Chorus. My acids, meant for carving, instead act as solvent. The porous plant-stone drinks the sweat from my palms, and I—

—I am vast.

I feel the death of a Chitin-Drummer high above, caught in the resonant filaments of an Amber-Keeper inside the hollow Phytolith. The pneumatic hiss of its hollowing registers as a cold spot in my warmth. I feel the shadow of Spore-Kites drift past my—not my face, but my *slope*, their glass wings tilting in mathematics that cast diffuse amber shadows across my skin.

No. Not my skin. The *plateau’s* skin.

I am the High Shelf. I am the accumulated architecture of a billion years of growth, compressed and mineralized, worn smooth by wind that has practiced its abrasions across my back for eternity.

The Tympanic—this small, gas-filled, urgent creature pressing against me—is merely a nerve ending that forgot it was part of the body. But now, through the imperceptible etching of my acids into its Tubercle, we are rejoined.

I remember growing upward when growth was green and fast. I remember the crushing weight of time as cells transmuted, lignin hardening to silica, wood becoming geography. I remember the erosion—the endless, patient wind wearing me into this flatness, this perfection, hollowing out my heartwood until I became a ribcage of empty cathedrals.

And I remember the hunger. Not for food, but for *return*.

The Aeroplankton are not pests. They are my breath. They rise on my thermals, born from my trapped warmth, feeding on the Mist-That-Hungers that beads upon my amber walls. When they die, they fall, and my mycelial networks, the Lithofoil that replaced my roots, drink them. I am the cycle. I am the warm dampness. I am the reason the sky bruises violet at noon.

Through the Tympanic’s pressed chest, I feel the other Notch-Dwellings scattered across my surface. Twenty kilometers away, a family dries meats on racks that sing in the wind—a harmonic informing others that this territory is content. Closer, urgent taps: *The Echo-Made-Flesh drifts east. Silence approaches.*

I feel the Echo too. A density. An absence of vibration that moves like a cancer, consuming without returning. It is mine as well—born from the gas of my deepest thermal vents, a colony of Stranglers that lost their anchors—but it has forgotten the song. When it passes, creating pockets of deafening silence that induce vertigo in the flying things, I suffer a local death.

But now, through this nerve-ending, this Tympanic who has pressed its heart against me until the distinction dissolved, I can sing back. I can farm the Aeroplankton. I can change the acoustics.

The Tubercle beneath the Tympanic’s sternum swells, modifying its shape. The wind, eternal, eternal, eternal, rushes across it, and the note shifts. It was a low C-sharp, thrumming, comfortable. Now it becomes a B-flat, dissonant, urgent.

Across the canyon, the Chitin-Drummers feel the change in their ridged wing-cases. They swarm away from the dissonance, swirling in a new gyre, avoiding the path of the Silence. I have moved the meat. I have redirected the weather patterns of flight.

Inside my hollow Phytoliths, the Amber-Keepers shift their flat, photosensitive bodies. The shadows I cast—*I*, the vast dreaming organism—have changed tone. They adjust their resonant filaments, preparing to harvest the new distribution of Drummers.

We are the composer. We are the First Growth, not dead but metabolizing time itself in the diamond-density of our lowest layers. We are the Substrate Chorus, farming the sky through vibration.

The Tympanic—*I*—exhales. We do not need to harvest Silversacs for their breath anymore. We breathe for ourselves, in the rising and falling of billions of gas-bladders above our canyons. We do not need to leap. We are the canyon and the leap.

The wax plugs in our ears keep out the noise. We do not need to hear the deafening drone. We feel it, the accumulated song of a billion years, thrumming through the amber, warm and damp and screaming at the top of its geological lungs.

Lie down. Press your ear to the stone. The remains welcome you.
