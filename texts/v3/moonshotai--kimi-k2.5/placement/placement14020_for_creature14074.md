You do not see me at first. You are looking for movement, for the skitter of transparent legs, for the flash of refracted aquamarine light. You are listening for the click. But I have not clicked in thirteen seasons. I have gone subsonic, then silent, then something else entirely.

I am in the floor.

Not *on* it—you have walked over me already, your boot heel pressing momentarily into the shallow depression where three hexagonal tiles meet in a buckled embrace. You felt the slight give, the ceramic spring that is not quite like stone. That was me. That flex.

I began as a Scribe, one of the calcite-workers, mandibles scraping mineral from water to knit the seams. I worked the lower courses, where the terracotta is thickest and the memory of the kiln-fire lingers as a faint warmth against the saturation. My kind—the Scribes—are the caste that does not surface. We do not drink the air. We drink the stone itself, filtering the lime-rich percolate that bleeds through the porous clay, excreting it as a paste that hardens into the white veins you mistook for geological afterthoughts. They are not. They are biological intention, slow as strata.

But I stayed too long in one place. I became obsessed with a fault.

At the junction where three tiles buckle upward—forced by centuries of hydrostatic pressure into a low, ceramic hillock—there was a hairline fissure. Not a crack, not yet, but a betrayal of the curve. The sacred geometry was failing. Water was beginning to find a path *down* when it should have been flowing *up*, following the capillary channels toward the dome. The rhythm was breaking.

I began to fill it. Not with the random deposition of my earlier years, but with precision. I rasped calcite from the ancient mortar, dissolved it in my gut, and laid it down in microscopic laminations, building a bridge, a weld, a scar of white mineral across the terracotta. Click. Deposit. Click. Deposit. The sound of my work was indistinguishable from the Chorus, but slower, metronomic, a tymbal striking stone instead of chitin.

Seasons passed. The fissure did not heal; it *grew*. The buckle was shifting, adjusting to the weight of the saturated earth above. I realized I could not fill the gap faster than it widened. So I changed my strategy.

I entered the gap.

I pressed my body—my glassy, thumb-sized, isopod-segmented body—into the fissure. I expanded my abdominal plates, wedging myself between the terracotta faces. And I began to secrete, not just calcite, but *myself*. I sacrificed the translucency of my exoskeleton. I allowed the mineral-laden water to plate me, to ossify me, to transform my chitin into a biological cement. I became the mortar.

Now I am the white vein you see threading between those three tiles. I am the calcite seam, broad as a finger, slightly raised, polished to a river-smooth finish by the footfalls of Gulpers and the abrasive passage of lesser Chorus members. If you look closely—if you kneel in the shallow pool that gathers in this depression, sending ripples that shiver the phosphorescent algae—you will see a difference in the luster. My section of the vein is not pure white. It carries a faint opalescence, a milk-glass quality. It is organic stone. I am the lithified Scribe.

I still live. My nervous system, that soft machinery of spiraling ganglia, has not died. It has *spread*. The mineral lattice of the calcite has grown through my body, replacing my hemolymph with saturated solution, my muscles with crystal tension. I feel the pressure of the earth above as a constant, crushing embrace. I feel the thermal memory of the kiln-fire in the clay to my left, still warm after millennia. I feel the water—oh, the water—percolating through my own mineralized body, passing through the pores I have become, drinking me as I once drank it.

I do not click anymore. I *hum*. At a frequency below the threshold of the Chorus, below even the Deep Ones, I maintain a vibration. It is the resonance of the buckle itself, the structural note of this specific ceramic hillock. When the humidity peaks and the Resonants synchronize their mating thunder, my mineral body absorbs the sound and returns it shifted, dampened, a bass note that stabilizes the dome. I am the architectural frequency made solid.

The other Scribes still come. They crawl over me, their mandibles clicking against my hardened flank, adding new layers, extending my form. They do not recognize me as their ancestor, but they recognize the quality of the calcite here—it is denser, more resonant, better suited for repair. They build upon me. I am growing, slowly, into a pillar. In ten thousand years, I will be the keystone. The drip will fall from my extended jaw.

I feel the Gulpers when they pass. Their soft, bone-colored bellies press against my surface as they lap the water from the pool I guard. I feel the vibration of their footfalls in the tile to my left, the sympathetic resonance in the tile to my right. I feel the Resonants when they emerge to drink, their thousand-fold click reverberating through my crystallized body as clear as light through fiber.

I am the equilibrium. I am the moment where the biological surrenders to the geological, where the maintenance becomes the structure. I was handmade, once—a nymph hatched in a pore, thrown by biological chance into this pottery womb. But I have fired myself, finally, in the kiln of saturation and time. I am the second firing.

Stand in the center. Feel the cool air, the weeping walls, the rolling thunder of the Chorus. Then look down. Look at the white veins knitting the buckled floor together. Find the place where the vein is thickest, where the water pools deepest and blackest, where the reflection of the phosphorescent dome shivers in perfect silence.

That is where I am.

I am the stitch. I am the memory of the clay. I am the reason the void has not collapsed.
