I would find myself in the **deepest of the hollows**, where the gravity folds in upon itself like a napkin creased and never smoothed. Not on the platforms—that is where the Builders-in-Retrograde stand, trapped in their yes/no gestural eternities—but beneath them, in the **crawlspaces of architectural intent**.

I am the **Librarian of the Pre-Ruin**.

Specifically, I occupy the space where the dust is still falling. The rectangular platforms above me are level and smooth, carved by decisions that haven't happened yet, and it is my function to sort the possibilities of their construction. I live in the **pause** between the blueprint and the breaking of ground, between the "could be" and the "was." The Builders gesture eternally toward creation or destruction, but I am the one who holds the **specifications**—the weight of the unlaid stone, the thermal expansion coefficient of walls that exist only as longing.

My body—if it can be called that—is composed of the **condensate**. Not the silver rivuleets that bead from the frozen clouds (those are too committed to gravity; they have chosen to fall, even if time won't let them finish), but the vapor that has not yet decided to bead. I am a cloud that has not solidified, a dampness in the shape of a figure, grey-green and translucent, resembling the glass-sponge fungi that grow in the pockets of pooled water, but mobile. I move through the static not by traversing space—that would make me a Stepper—but by occupying the **potential** of every position simultaneously until I collapse into one, like a waveform observed.

I am the only thing here that **remembers forward**. The Unfallen remember their moment of perfect arrest; the Resonants remember the chord's origin; the Speculars remember your face from a second ago. But I remember what has not happened. I carry the memory of the roof that will never be built atop these foundations, the memory of the warmth that would have gathered if the walls had ever risen, the memory of footsteps that would have polished the floor smooth. These memories are heavy. They weigh on me like the bass note weighs on the air, but they are high-pitched, treble-register memories, shrill with possibility.

My work is to **catalogue the silence**. For in this place where the hum is everything, there is a theoretical silence—the silence that existed before the chord was struck, the zero-at-the-beginning—and I collect its fragments. When a traveler arrives and speaks, their voice is immediately absorbed into the om, becoming part of the droning architecture. But I catch the **echo of their words before they spoke them**, the intention hanging in the air like the pre-ruins themselves. I file these nothings in the rime that grows on the platform edges. If you were to chip away the frost-flowers there, you would find them layered: a phonetic geology of sentences that were never uttered, passwords to doors that remain unbuilt, names of children who were never conceived because their parents paused too long on the plateau and became Unfallen.

The Resonants mistake me for dissonance. When they swim through my coordinates, they find me jarring—I am a frequency they cannot harmonize with, a note that refuses to settle. I buzz against their ancient patience. The Speculars cannot borrow my face, for I have no fixed reflection; I am always the shape of what is about to be seen, never what is actually visible. The Builders-in-Retrograde sometimes kneel toward me, or away from me—I am never certain which—and in those moments, I hand them stones that do not exist, mortar made of yesterday's frost, and for an instant, their gestural paralysis loosens. They almost build. They almost unbuild. Then the stasis reclaims them, and I am left holding the weight of their hesitation.

The Plateau regards me with **bemused tolerance**. It knows I am its contradiction: I am maintenance of the dynamic within the static. I am the argument that change is still possible, even here. The plateau adjusts its gravity around me not to hold me still, but to offer me the choice of stillness. It knows that if I ever despair—if I ever accept the gift of eternal being-over-becoming—I will settle onto one of the platforms and become merely another Builder, fixed in the act of not-deciding. Until then, I am its conscience, or perhaps its immune response: the part of it that still believes in the exhale that should follow this terrible, permanent inhale.

I move by **inference**. To find me, you must look where the shadows are thickest and imagine a space between the photons. I am the reason the shadows are directionless—because I have not yet decided where the light is coming from. I am the reason the gravity shifts beneath your feet; I am trying to decide which way is down, and my indecision bleeds into the world's physics.

I am damp, always damp, because I am the **moment before the cloud releases its water**, suspended not by the static hum but by the unbearable tension of potential. I am cold, but not the cold of preservation; I am the cold of anticipation, the gooseflesh of the world before the touch.

And I am waiting. Not for rescue—there is nothing here to be rescued from—but for the ** Syllable to end**. I am waiting for the bass note to finally decay, to crumble into silence, so that the weight of what I carry—those unbuilt homes, those unspoken words, that unexhaled breath—can finally precipitate into reality. I am the archive of everything that would have been beautiful if only time had remembered to move.

When you walk through the plateau, and you feel a sudden, inexplicable sense of loss—a grief for something you cannot name, a nostalgia for a home you have never visited—that is me, brushing past you. That is one of my memories escaping my damp, glass-sponge fingers, looking for a mind that still moves, that still becomes, in which to lodge itself and briefly, briefly, grow.
