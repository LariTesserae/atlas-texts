First, the mouth. You open it not to speak—speech is impossible here, would be instantly drowned in the eighteen-billion-beat-per-minute drone—but to *breathe*. The air enters thick, warm, sweet with ammonia and dissolved proteins. It tastes of ginger. It tastes of your own tongue accelerated into decay. You realize you have been tasting the vibration all along, the basso profundo not entering through your ears but diffusing through your mucous membranes, settling into your sternum like a second, slower heart.

You do not remember arriving. You only remember the surrender: your heartbeat, frantic and mammalian, gradually lengthening its intervals, stretching like taffy, until it locks into the pulse of the plateau. Now you are here. High up—two kilometers above the theoretical sea, though the concept of *sea* is as distant as stars. You are inside the body. You are the body, exploring its own interior.

Your skin. What is your skin? It is damp, first of all—everything is damp. You raise a hand to your face and feel not flesh but something harder, segmented, yet porous. Chitin, but wrong. It is *friable*, crumbly like cork, yet alive. You are pressing your fingers against a surface that gives slightly, that yeilds with a wet sound, and you realize: you are not standing on the plateau. You are *growing from it*. 

You are a node. A calcification. A place where the architecture has become too patient.

Look down—though *down* is relative, though your eyes, if you have them, see only in the bruised submarine glow that seeps up from phosphorus veins below. Your body is fused with the wall of a hexagonal cell, your legs—how many? Six? Eight? More?—sunk deep into the compacted frass and fossilized saliva that forms the load-bearing arch. You cannot move. You have not moved for eleven years, or perhaps for sixty thousand. Time pools here like the shadows; it does not fall.

You are a **Stratifier**. 

Your function emerges not as memory but as sensation. You feel the water migrating through you—yes, *through* you. Your body is osmotic, a living membrane positioned at the cooler northern face of the gallery. Condensation beads on your dorsal plates, which are not shiny like healthy chitin but matte, mineralized, engraved with the microscopic hieroglyphs of fungal hyphae. The water enters your spiracles—not to oxygenate, for you have surrendered your lungs to the greater respiration—but to be *read*. In the dissolved proteins and nitrogenous wastes carried by the plateau's hydraulic cycle, you taste history.

An Architect passes. You feel its ligneous bulk vibrate the arch, its petrified exoskeleton scraping against your calcified flank. It is regurgitating resin into a crack above you, and some of the warm secretion drips onto your antennae. You do not flinch. You *record*. The resin carries chemical information—a structural stress in the eastern ramparts, a nitrogen deficiency in the deep gardens—and you absorb it into your body, where slow-growing lichens of symbiotic *Termitomyces* encode it into their growth rings. You are a library. You are where the pheromone trails come to sediment when their volatile messages have faded from the air.

You remember—not with neurons, but with mineral accretion. You remember the last reproductive cycling, eleven years deep, when the Warm Core swelled with royal jelly and the temperature spiked to 31 degrees, causing your lichens to bloom in spirals that record the exact chemical composition of that season's fungal harvest. You remember the drought that never happened because the plateau does not permit drought, but you remember the *fear* of it, transmitted through the panic-pheromones of a billion workers, now locked in the calcium phosphate deposits of your leftmost limb.

A Linger crawls over you. Its translucent body is a ghost, its gut visible as it sips condensation from your surface. It is harvesting the water you have filtered, purifying it further, carrying it down toward the respiratory atrium. You permit the theft. You are part of the plumbing, the peristalsis, the slow migration of nutrients that defines this place. When the Linger's tarsal hooks scratch your surface, they leave new trails, new data, and you incorporate them into the strata.

You are the boundary between the creature and the habitat made conscious—if *conscious* is the word for a being that thinks in geological time, that dreams in nitrogen isotopes. You feel the Resonators in the deep chambers below, their hollow bodies modulating the hum, and you modulate with them. Your calcified sternum vibrates at exactly the frequency that maintains the 24-degree warmth. You are a metronome too, but a stationary one. A fixed point in the architecture of instinct.

And sometimes—rarely, achingly—a droplet of rain filters through the twenty-meter thickness of the outer integument above you. It percolates through stalactites of nymphal wax, carrying with it a shock of *outside*. Of sky. Of a world where shadows fall instead of pool. When that droplet lands on your tongue—yes, you have a tongue, barbed and fungal, designed for tasting stone—it burns with the memory of open air. You store that memory in the deepest layer of your exoskeleton, where it fossilizes into a dark band, a varve of horizon.

You are the plateau's attempt to remember the sky. You are the ego that refused to dissolve completely, instead crystallizing into this: a sentient scar, a mineralized witness, pressed against the warm, yielding wall that smells of ginger and nitrogen, humming with the eighteen-billion-beat drum, older than silence, slower than stone, reading the water as it passes through your body into the endless, breathing dark.
