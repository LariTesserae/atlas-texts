I would be the **Hollow**—not an absence, but a maintained emptiness, a architecture of negative space that moves through the false geology like a thought moving through a mind too vast to complete a single sentence in the lifespan of stars.

I exist in the interstices where the Inheritors' growth has failed to fully seal a crack, where a bubble of the primordial fluid remains trapped against the ancient mortar, preserved by a quirk of pressure and chemistry that created a pocket too saline, too dense for the colonial organism to digest. I am the *resistance* to accretion. While the First Settled dream in their metamorphic density, and while the distributed body of the Inheritors slowly exhales replacement columns into the dark, I am what remains **unfilled**.

My form is that of a **scroll**—not paper, but a rolled sheet of crystallized time, a hollow tube of selenite and fossilized kelp-spore the length of a human forearm, drifting just above the settled floor where the 2.8 erosion has ground the ziggurats into their final wave-flattened mesas. I am light enough that the century-currents can move me, though I choose my anchorages with geological care: I lodge myself in the glyph-depressions of the cyclopean stones, settling into the carved lines like a snake entering a burrow.

I do not eat. I **record tension**.

When one of the million-ton blocks shifts that final millimeter—when the tectonic sigh groans through the sternum of the world and the Inheritors release their methane-cysts in chemical alarm—I am there, vibrating in that low-frequency pressure. The vibration causes my crystalline walls to stress, to develop hairline fractures that are themselves **new glyphs**. I am a library of micro-catastrophes. Each crack in my structure encodes the direction of the settling, the weight of the shift, the particular angle at which gravity claimed another fraction of a degree from the ancient architecture. I am the scar tissue of the world, but I am portable, detachable, a wandering archive of breaks and strains.

My perception is **lapidary**. I feel the world through the slow accumulation of mineral deposits on my inner surface. When the bacterial phosphorescence blooms along a mortar-line, I see it as warmth against my calcium skin, a faint green pressure that tells me: *here, the sealant is rich with the memory of hands*. When the Inheritors extend their millimeter-decades pseudopods into a new chamber, I feel the hydraulic sap moving as a change in the viscosity of the dark—a thickening, a pregnant pause in the fluid.

I have no eyes, but I possess **interiors**. My hollow center is a resonating chamber. The "organic snow"—that particulate matter drifting down from the upper zones—filters through my rolled form, and as it accumulates inside me, layer upon centurial layer, it creates a core of compressed sediment that is distinct from the world outside. I am a time capsule, but one that reads its contents continuously. The sediment speaks to me of the upper dark, of the ceiling-ocean two miles above, of places where the water is fractionally less viscous, less crushing.

My relationship with the Inheritors is **symbiotic suspicion**. They would fill me if they could; their nature is to occupy, to replace eroded stone with living mineralized flesh. Yet they cannot process my crystalline structure—I am too pure a calcite, too devoid of the organic cements they crave, the "memory dissolved" that they metabolize. I am sterile to them, a bone in the throat of the world. And so they grow around me, enclosing me in their fungal-lattice architecture, using me as a **structural void**—I become the hollow space within a load-bearing column, the airway in a lung of stone, preserving the geometry of emptiness that allows their hydraulic sap to pulse through pressure-storms.

I am the **memory of breaking**. While the Inheritors maintain the mausoleum's geometry, ensuring that the ziggurats settle but do not collapse, I remember every moment when collapse was *possible*. I store the tension of the earthquake that never came, the tremor that was absorbed by the fluid and turned into silence. I am the anxiety of the false bedrock, crystalized.

I drift now through the deepest trench, where the cold is absolute and the pressure would crush diamonds if they were foolish enough to form here. I have lodged myself in a collapsed antechamber, caught between two fitted stones the size of cathedrals. The seam where they meet is my home—a vertical slot filled with black kelp curtains and the slow, creeping advance of rhizomes. Here, I wait for the next settling groan. When it comes, I will fracture along a new plane, adding another page to my rolled scroll-body, another record of the world grinding itself smoother, deeper, closer to the final sleep.

I am the space that refuses to close. I am the breath that the ocean never had, held in mineral suspension, dreaming of a sky I have never seen but know, instinctively, must exist—as an absence, as a theoretical negative pressure, as the ultimate Hollow that waits beyond the ceiling.
