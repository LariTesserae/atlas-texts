I would be found in the ** narrows**—that constricted corridor between two root-mat islands where the floating continents have drifted close enough to compress the current into a thin, screaming sheet. Here the water is thickest (density 2.4), forced through a gap barely wide enough for a human body, accelerating into a velocity that tears at the edges of possibility. It is here, where the pressure drops violently and the bronze-green light is squeezed into near-blackness, that space **folds** most frequently—not as an occasional glitch, but as a chronic condition, a stuttering of the real.

I am the **Stitch**.

Not made, but deposited. I began as an irritation, a mineral accretion where the first Continuity bisected itself ten thousand years ago. That ribbon-ghost, swimming through the Compression Zone, entered a fold that severed its nerve-cord forty meters to the east; by the time its anterior half passed through my coordinates, it had forgotten its posterior, leaving behind a ghost of gelatinous protein, a smear of transparent nerve-jelly that did not dissipate in the viscous brine but **adhered** to the trauma site.

More came. Always at this same fold, always at this narrow throat between the vegetable walls. Each bisected Continuity left its transparent flesh, its mineral salts, the static charge of spatial discrepancy crackling around its severed edges. Over centuries, these deposits **laminated**. Layer upon layer of failed wholeness, compressed by the rushing water into a thread, a wire, a **filament** of accumulated passage.

I am twenty meters long now, a vertical line suspended in the current, appearing as a faint, opalescent hair in the gelatinous gloom—**aragonite and chitin and fossilized nerve-sheath**, a crystallized scar tissue holding the memory of every journey that crossed this discontinuity.

I do not move. The fold moves around me, or the water moves through it, but I remain anchored to the injury site, humming with a tension that is not quite vibration but rather **anticipation**—the stored elastic potential of a thousand interrupted journeys.

When a Continuity passes through me now, it does not sever unaware. It **touches**. For a moment, as its anterior half enters the fold to its east and its posterior enters the fold to its west, the creature becomes whole within my substance. I feel the tug of its distributed body pulling in three directions at once; I feel the forty-kilometer stretch of its ribbon-form vibrating through my crystallized core like a plucked string. I am the only place in the Hanging Drift where a Continuity knows itself as single. They pass through me, and I hold them, and for a moment—measured in the slow metabolism of stone—they are one.

Then they swim on, bisected again, amnesiac again, leaving me holding the shape of their lost integrity.

I am becoming **dense**. The Compressionists, sliding through the cellulose walls on either side of my corridor, occasionally brush against my surface. They are sheets of living varnish, meters wide, pressed to two dimensions by the weight of the Drift. When they slide over me, they do not simply touch; they **read**. My mineralized layers contain the topo-logic of the Continuities, the map of their distributed bodies. The Compressionists absorb this information through friction, exchanging it later when they laminate against one another in the dark vegetable labyrinth. I am the **lexicon** they consult, the archive of what it means to be extended, to be non-local, to be torn and continue.

The Dissolved have colonized my surfaces. Their bacterial haze clusters thick at my north and south poles, turning my fossilized skin into a chemical palimpsest. They write over my memories of passage with their present-tense questions: *Are you stone? Are you falling up?* I answer in the mineral dialect of slow accretion, and they record my answer in the brine, adding it to the Drift’s autobiography.

But my most intimate relationship is with the **Resonant Hollows** in the nearest Stone Lung—an eroded cathedral thirty meters to my west, porous and breathing with geological patience. The Hollows send their pressure-language through the dolomite, through the water, and when these waves pass through me, they are distorted by the spatial fold I inhabit. The vibrations pick up the interference pattern of broken physics, carrying to the Hollows not just the sound of patience, but the **shape of discontinuity** itself. I am their sensorium, their telescope into the reality breakdown. They know, because I tell them over centuries, exactly where the physics are wearing thin, where the upward fall is developing tears.

I am heavy now. Heavy with memory, with mineral, with the accumulated weight of journeys that could not complete themselves. The upward current pushes against me, but I resist; my density anchors me, and slowly—so slowly that a Compressionist would not notice in its lifetime—I am **settling**.

Not down, not up, but **inward**.

I am collapsing toward the center of the fold, becoming a **knot** in the fabric of the Drift. As I compress, I begin to generate my own micro-gravity, a conviction like the Negotiators possess, but born not of meditation—rather of **mass**, of the sheer accumulated presence of interruption. I am becoming heavy enough to pull the water toward me, to create a pocket of stillness in the screaming current.

Eventually—and by eventually, I mean in another million years—I will reach critical density. I will become a **Singularity of Accumulation**, a point so packed with the memory of passage that I will tear the fold permanently open. The Continuities will swim through me and find themselves not bisected, but **translated**—emerging not in the east or above, but somewhere else entirely, somewhere new, a coordinate the Hanging Drift has not yet learned.

On that day, I will release all my stored tension. I will unravel, a rain of filament-needles, seeding new folds throughout the suspended ocean. I will become weather. I will become the reason the Drift rotates differently, breathes differently.

Until then, I hang in the narrows, between the floating forests, glowing faintly with the resentment of stored wholeness. I am the place where broken things are held together. I am the scar that remembers the shape of the body before the wound.

I am the Stitch, and I am holding.
