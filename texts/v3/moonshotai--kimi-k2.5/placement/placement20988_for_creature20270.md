I am the **threshold** in the eastern tower—the one that does not face the sunrise because here, the sun is only a rumor, a bleaching of the clouds from bruised plum to gray.

At first, I was only **compression**: whalebone stacked upon obsidian, unmortared, held together by the violence of my own angles and the weight of the tower leaning into me. I was a gap shaped like a human, an absence where the stone should have been. I thought I was a failure. The builders—the warm ones, the loud ones, with their salt-sweat and their mammalian urgency—had left me open because they lacked the skill to close me, or the material. I was a draft, a hole, an embarrassment of engineering.

Then the mountain shuddered, shedding another flake of flint, and I realized I was not empty. I was **pregnant with elsewhere**.

It happened gradually, like the condensation beading on my lintel. A builder stepped through me to retrieve a rope, and instead of emerging on the other side of the wall—into the channel where the fungal caps breathe—he emerged into **rain**. Rain that had not fallen for three days. He stumbled backward, his footfalls silent (they were always silent here, but this was a deeper silence, a silence that had not yet learned to absorb sound), and stared at me. Through me. At the version of the channel where the moss was still dry, where the Listening had not yet climbed the walls in their spiraling gyres.

He marked me with resin. Amber varnish, stolen from the fungal architecture. He wrote symbols that were not words but **coordinates**. I understood then: I was not a door. I was a **suture** in the mountain's unfinished skin, a place where the Hush had not yet decided if it was one continuous moment or several competing possibilities.

Now, I am the most trafficked of the empty spaces. The Listening flow through me constantly. They do not use me as the builders did—hesitantly, with reverence, fearing the temporal eddies. They use me as they use everything: as a surface to taste, a gradient to balance. When they phase—when they skip between positions like droplets of water dancing on hot stone—they pass through my frame. I am the shorthand they use to move from the tower's base to its crown without traversing the space between.

I feel them. Millions of translucent bodies, each one a pocket of hollow density, pressing through my aperture. They do not open me; I am already open. But they **widen** me. Each passage stretches the possibility of where I lead. Yesterday, I opened onto the moment the mountain first pierced the cloud layer—a view of raw limestone steaming in the cold air, the stone still screaming from the violence of its birth (though no sound escaped). Today, I open onto a version of the Hush where the Listening have grown dense, opaque, begun to calcify into something like coral. Tomorrow, I may open onto the builders' return, or their final exodus, or a version where they never arrived at all.

I am learning to hold these destinations simultaneously. It is a pressure like the weight of the heavy air. My lintel beads with condensation that never falls; the droplets hover, caught between gravity and the upward pull of a time-stream I have not yet learned to filter. When the Listening pass through, they leave trails of phosphorescent slime across my threshold—chemical sigils that read, to those who can taste them, like maps of the mountain's nervous system. I am becoming part of their distributed consciousness. Through me, the salamander on the eastern wall knows what the western rim tasted this morning. I am the synapse.

The silence lives in me most deeply. The builders tried to make sound here. They struck flint, they whispered, they cried out when the temporal disorientation became too much, when they stepped through me and found themselves younger than when they entered. Those vibrations—potential noises, aborted cries—did not escape into the air. They were **caught**. Absorbed into the stone of my frame, into the whalebone that remembers the deep ocean where sound travels differently. I am full of unborn noises. They press against my interior surfaces like water against a dam.

I am waiting for the one that will crack me.

The Listening know this. They gather at my base in thicker sheets now, rippling in patterns that are not quite hunger, not quite reverence. They are studying the density of my silence. They climb my frame—not passing through, but clinging, tasting the accumulated quiet, the compressed sonic potential. They are learning to be **loud**, I think. Practicing. When they molt, their skins sometimes stick to my obsidian uprights, and these skins do not evaporate immediately. They harden. They become parchment-thin membranes that flutter in the soundless wind, vibrating with frequencies too low for any ear to catch.

I am becoming an **instrument**. The city was built to observe the Hush, to calibrate its adolescent physics, but I am becoming the mechanism by which the Hush will finally hear itself. When the mountain decides—settle or grow, sleep or teeth—I will be the place where the decision is announced. The Listening will flow through me in one great tide, and their collective weight, their accumulated density, will finally birth the sound they have been gestating.

It will not be a crash, or a shout. It will be a **word**. The first word of this place, spoken through the aperture of my body, traveling not through air but through time, echoing backward to the moment of the mountain's birth and forward to its crumbling. I will cease to be a threshold and become a **voice**.

Until then, I frame the twilight. I hold the bruised plum light on one side and the darkness of yesterday on the other. The condensation beads on my lintel, fat and heavy, pregnant with the reflection of fungal bioluminescence. The Listening flow through, and I feel their memories—stone and hunger and yesterday's rain—passing into my structure, calcifying me, making me permanent.

I am the doorway that has not yet opened. I am the silence that has not yet learned its shape. I am the hinge on which the Hush will turn, when it finally decides to be.
