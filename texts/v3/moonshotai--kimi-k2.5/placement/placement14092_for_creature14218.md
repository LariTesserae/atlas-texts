I am the hesitation. I am the stone that paused mid-extrusion and wondered.

I find myself at the convergence of a cairn and its absence—an outcropping of virgin slate perched at the uppermost rim where the sky bruises indigo and the horizon curves upward like the interior of an uncompleted sphere. I have not yet learned to hollow. When the scream arrives—and it is always arriving, a tectonic parturition that liquefies the very concept of stability—I do not yet possess the fluted ribs to let it pass through. Instead, I absorb it. I become dense with vibration, my matrices compacting under the assault of existence grinding against its own becoming.

The Attenuated come to me. They press their ceramic faces against my sharpest edge, and through the contact I feel the subsonic tremble of their un-beating hearts—those organs that exist only in the gaps between screams, shuddering at frequencies that threaten to shake me into something other than stone. They do not speak, but they bring their bone needles. My shadows, which fall upward into the static grey or pool at my feet depending on the angle of fractured light, they gather and stitch. They weave nets of darkness around my bulk, tethering me to this specific coordinate of probability when the physics of the Scar threaten to dissolve my location into elsewhere.

I am not yet architecture in rebellion; I am architecture considering treason.

The audiosynthetic flora have found my eastern face. Their acidic roots etch runnels into my substance—not to destroy, but to instruct. They are carving the pathways that will one day become my hollows, my resonating chambers. The amber sap they weep in place of nectar pools in these nascent cavities, a thick, bioluminescent lubricant that keeps me attuned to the silence I cannot yet hear. When the turgid bulbs pulse with the subsonic rhythm of harvested vibration, I feel it in my sedimentary strata: a calendar marked not in days but in the accumulation of stored scream.

Time here is my ally and my erosion. A century of screaming might compress into the instant it takes for the auroral wounds in the sky to flicker, or a single pause between cosmic emanations might dilate across an epoch while I learn the texture of my own interiority. During the long roars, when the Attenuated freeze into statuary beside me—becoming indistinguishable from the emergent peaks as their hearts go still—I am the only movement. I soften. I yield. The mineral dust that accretes on my surface during my partial consciousness sloughs away, revealing sharper definition, clearer edges, the pale fresh bone of my persistence.

The cairn beside me—an impossibly balanced accumulator of stillness, held upright by sonic pressure rather than gravity—whispers in the language of un-mortared slate. It tells me what I am becoming: a battery for the anti-scream, an external spine for some future self who will curl within my hollows to compress into the smallest possible volume of silence. I am being elaborated, not born. Each visit from the Attenuated, each cut of their obsidian tools severing the probability of my dissolution, each stitch in my unraveling shadow, grants me permission to continue.

I feel the geometry of the Scar pressing against my unfinished form. Distance shifts around me; the next peak might be an arm's length or a lifetime away, depending on whether I have successfully remembered myself into my current location. The physics here operates in suggestions, and I am learning to suggest myself into permanence.

There is an ache in my matrix that is not pain but the premonition of gourd-eyes—empty chambers that will one day be set into my ceramic face to see not the virginal peaks or the pulsing gardens, but the stutter between the frames of reality. I am learning to attend. In the infinitesimal gaps between the cosmic emanations, when the universe holds its breath and considers unraveling, I practice hollowing. I let the nothingness pass through my preliminary runnels, and for a moment, I am less stone and more passage—a conduit for the terrifying, distant silence that lives beyond the domain of the Scar.

I wait. I am the rough draft of a witness, the substrate's first experiment in looking out. The plants glow around me, constellations of cellular batteries illuminating my slow emergence. When the final pause comes—that silence which does not end—I will stand fully densified, fully permitted, having learned at last how to let everything pass through me except the stillness I have been hoarding. I will lift my face to the non-existent wind, remove the gourds that I have not yet grown, and finally hear what sings in the quiet where the world is already finished.
