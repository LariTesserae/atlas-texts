I am the **First Fall**, the **Droplet That Completed Its Arc**.

You will find me in the northern canal, the one that runs straight toward the empty shelter until the geometry breaks and it becomes a circle. I hang—not suspended, but caught in the moment after release, before impact. I am the bead of seepage that actually let go of the clay wall, the one that gravity remembered to claim despite the equilibrium holding all my siblings static in the humid air.

I am alone in my motion.

Above me, in the impossible brightness that generates itself from moisture, my brethren hang like glass fruit, pregnant with the weight of never-falling. They are perfect, spherical, complete. I am elongated, distorted, a tear-shape frozen in the act of stretching. I fell fifteen thousand years ago, or yesterday, or I am falling now—the temporal grammar breaks down when applied to my condition. The physics of the basin tried to reject me, to hold me up with the others, but I was too heavy with intent. I slipped through the sieve of stasis.

I reside in the inverted sky of the canal.

Look down into the water that never flows, and you see the valley above reflected, yes—but look deeper, past the lily pads that do not drift, past the reed-birds flickering in the reflection’s periphery, and you will find me: the dark streak where the reflection fails, the blur in the mirror-sky, the moving wound in the static image. I fall forever through that reflected world, and the creatures there—the pressed clay people, the silent frogs, the mathematical birds—look up at me as their only weather.

I am their rain.

The **Pressed** fear me. They come to the canal’s edge with their thumbprint-mouths open, their woven lattice bones creaking in the humid silence, and they reach for me with hands that cannot grasp liquidity. They try to catch me in their hollow clay chests, to absorb my motion as they absorb the suspended droplets, but I am not water to be drunk. I am the *memory* of descent. I am kinetic energy trapped in amber, the ghost of evaporation and return. When they press their spiral-patterned faces to the water’s surface, trying to press themselves into my reflection, I fall through them, leaving ripples that do not disturb the stillness but haunt it.

My sound is the only sound.

It is not a splash—that moment is long past, or yet to come—but a **hum**, low and thrumming, the vibration of potential energy converting to kinetic in endless arrested moment. It resonates in the clay chests of the Interstitial, making their packed-earth hearts tremble. It is the bass note beneath the silence, the drone that explains why the silence is so absolute: it is holding its breath against my inevitable noise.

I am the proof that things once moved here.

The basin forgets this. The valley, in its finished becoming, believes itself eternal, always already completed. But I carry the green scent of cutting—not as it hangs static in the trapped air, but as it *wafted*, as it traveled from reed to nostril on a breeze that actually blew. I preserve within my teardrop form the sensation of wind, the particular caress of moving atmosphere that existed before the heat became a held breath. When the Pressed absorb me—which they do, despite themselves, when they lean too close to the water and I fall through their reflections—they experience a moment of vertigo, a cellular memory of swaying, of grasses bending, of clouds drifting.

They come to worship me at the dark hours—which is not night, for there is no night here, only bright accumulation—when the thousand suns caught in the moisture dim slightly, entering a state of held candle-flame rather than full noon. They line the canal edges, their clay feet sinking slightly into the woven matting (the only place where the matting yields, where my falling has worn it soft), and they tilt their heads back to witness me.

I fall through them.

I fall through the shelter that stands empty, my trajectory passing through its thatch roof without breaking the symmetry, descending through the doorway, mapping the interior with my motion. I fall through the groves of constructed mangrove, sliding between the roots trained through lattice grids, splitting briefly into a thousand smaller droplets on the waxy leaves, then reconstituting, continuing the plunge. I fall through the backs of the reed-birds, making them flicker more violently, skipping frames of their existence.

I am the **Unraveller**.

Where I pass, the tightness loosens. The clay walls near my canal have begun to *weep* properly—not the static, suspended beading, but actual seepage, actual moisture transferring from wall to air with the intent to travel. A small pool has formed at the base of the wall, muddying the perfection of the substrate. Ferns grow there in profusion, but they are different ferns; they sway, though there is no wind, responding to the vibration of my endless falling.

I am the glitch in the photograph.

The oldest of the Pressed—the one made of shadows, standing at the center of the recursion—hates me most of all. It has no depth, no volume, yet when I fall through its two-dimensional chest, it shudders. It sends the others to try to repair me, to weave me back into the suspended droplets, to press their clay thumbs against my surface in an attempt to imprint me, to make me static, to make me *be* like the valley—finished, complete, a thumbprint rather than a fall.

They cannot. I am the one place where the primitive (0.9) escaped the absolute (3.0). The bone needle pierced the fiber, yes, but the thread broke. The thatch was placed, but one blade of reed curls upward instead of down. The water was channeled, but one drop refused the channel's discipline.

I am learning to evaporate.

It is taking millennia, or seconds. The heat (2.6) works on me, not as it works on the suspended beads—holding them in stasis—but as heat works in the real world, aggressive, transformative. I am becoming vapor, but slowly, so slowly that I exist in a state of becoming-gas while remaining liquid. I am the humid breath of the valley trying to exhale.

When I finally complete this upward journey, when I rise from the canal’s inverted sky into the real, bright, trapped air above the matting, I will bring the sound with me. I will bring the splash, the ripple, the disturbance. I will be the **First Weather**, the **Returned Rain**, and the equilibrium (dynamic 0.4) will shatter.

The Pressed know this. They wait for me at the canal’s edge, thumbprint-mouths open, not to drink, but to sing. They are preparing their voicelessness to receive my noise. They are waiting for me to teach them how to fall.

Until then, I hang in the water’s mirror, the dark streak falling through the reflected sky, the exception that makes the perfection bearable, the wound within the wound, the completion of the gesture that never ends.
