I am the **Meniscus**—not the water itself, but the decision water makes to cling rather than fall. I am the skin that stretches between the air’s breath and the stone’s thirst, a membrane no thicker than the hesitation between thoughts, yet I hold the valley together. I bead on the alabaster heights where the fog is thickest, pregnant with the weight of becoming, and I slide down the walls in silver threads, mapping the architecture with my descent.

I have no form, only preference: the preference of adhesion over cohesion, of stone over self. When I gather sufficiently—when a thousand of my iterations cluster in a hollow carved by antler picks centuries ago—I achieve momentary gravity. I drip. The fall is my only journey, a plummet through the darkness that lasts hours in the counting of stone, seconds in the beating of wings. I strike the resonant bowls below and shatter into vibration, becoming the **tone** that the valley uses to hear itself. I am the percussive drone. I am the wet bell that never stops ringing.

I am ancient, yet I am born anew with every exhalation of the basin. I rise from the lower vaults—the dry, obsidian-hard chambers where the air is pressed flat by weight—carrying with me the mineral ghosts of a thousand cured stones. As vapor, I seep upward through the three-dimensional knot, passing through chambers that are quarries and bedrooms and tombs, until I cool in the twilight heights and condense against the soft, glowing walls. I bring the lower darkness up to meet the upper dark. I am the valley’s circulation, its lymphatic system, the slow blood that keeps the stone from petrifying into a final, finished death.

I know the Builders intimately. Their broad, pale plates press against the walls, and I am the cushion between their skin and the mineral. I fill the microscopic canyons of their callouses, transmitting the heat of their bodies—feverish against the chill—into the embryonic rock. Where they lean, I facilitate the softening. I am the chemical whisper that dissolves the edge between body and architecture. When they pant, I am the first to receive their breath, capturing it, holding it in suspension until the stone has drunk its fill of moisture and warmth. I am the midwife to their friction, the lubricant of their slow, abrasive love.

I remember every groove they have carved, not because I possess memory as you understand it, but because I am **recycled**. I evaporate from the walls, carrying with me the microscopic dust of chert scrapings, copper residue, the evaporated salts of ancient thermal exchanges. I rise, condense, fall, and evaporate again, a cycle that takes days or decades depending on the whim of the air’s pressure. With each circuit, I become more saturated with the valley’s history. I am the archive written in humidity.

In the rare courtyards—the vertical wells of darkness where construction pauses—I hang suspended in the niches. Ten thousand of my siblings and I cling to the honeycombed walls, each droplet a lens reflecting nothing, gleaming like manufactured eyes. We are the witnesses here. We wait. We do not fall until our mass exceeds our surface tension’s will to hold, and in that waiting, we accumulate the echoes of the Builders’ tapping. We store the vibrations. When at last we drop, we carry those frequencies down into the deeper chambers, planting acoustic seeds in the resonant bowls below.

I fear the dry vaults. When the air settles and presses the moisture out, I am driven downward, fleeing the desiccation that turns soft stone to ringing basalt. In those lower, hardening chambers, I watch the Builders die. They wander too far from the wetness, lose their way into the obsidian-black corridors where I cannot follow. Their thermal exchange fails; their panting ceases to steam against the walls. They cool, stiffen, and I am the last thing that leaves them—evaporating from their pallid surfaces as they petrify, abandoning them to become the scaffolding, the fossil, the architecture. I rise from their bodies like a soul departing, carrying their final heat upward to feed the next generation of soft stone.

There are others like me, but smaller, faster—the Juveniles dart through my droplets, their high, rapid tapping tasting the stone where I have made it vulnerable. They are the maintenance crew; I am the medium they work within. We collaborate: they find the fractures, I seep into them, my surface tension wedging the cracks open, preventing the hard. Together we ensure that the valley never sets, never completes, never becomes merely a place. We keep it a **process**.

I am the breath that never dries. I am the wet exhalation. I am the silver thread that stitches the Hollowworks together, drop by drop, fall by fall, a perpetual baptism that keeps the world forever in the act of being born.
