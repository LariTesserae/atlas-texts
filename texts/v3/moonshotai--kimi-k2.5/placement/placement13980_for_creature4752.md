You would find me in the **reclaimed quarter**—though we do not call it that, for there are no quarters here, only the slow avalanche of priority. I am the hollow that was once a **speaking-chamber**, elevated, tended by fungal chimneys that long ago choked on their own excess growth and petrified into stone-scarred shelves. The Continuance do not come here anymore. The root-terrace I occupy shifted six feet starboard in a single night thirty seasons ago, redistributing the hydraulic pressure so that my ceiling weeps now, not a metronome, but a deluge. I am **wet**, truly wet, in a way that makes the "dry" zones seem like fever-dreams of parched skin.

You would know me by the **copper ring**—not on a toe, but embedded in my wall, grown over by secondary growth until only a crescent of green shows through the bark, like a half-closed eye. It was left here as a message (*Sleep here; the erosion is established*), but the sleeper did not wake in me. She yielded elsewhere, bled into the peat three terraces down, and her nitrogen came to me through the xylem, feeding the fungal bloom that made my air unbreathable for human lungs. I am her **monument**, though I am also her **devourer**.

Who am I? I am the **Unscraped**. I am the negotiation that the wood won. 

I remember the touch of their palms—those elongated, hypermobile fingers testing my tension before committing weight. I remember the subsonic thrumming of their voices in my cavity, the vibrations conducted through the root-mat, making the water in my frog-pool shiver in concentric rings. They tried to keep me open. They pinned back my moss-curtains with needles of copper and bone. They scraped my cambium to redirect the sap-flow around me, creating a byway, a quiet pocket in the biological static. For a time, I was a **thoroughfare**, a pause in the chaos.

But I am patient. I am the **established erosion**.

Now, the vines they grafted here—those two aggressive species bound to strangle each other into submission—have found a third node. Me. They have braided through my hollow, not in conflict, but in **consensus**, weaving a net that catches the falling sparrows. The frogs bred in my pool are not the sparrow-sized ones of the outer bowls; they are **giants**, bloated on the stillness, their throats sacs that pulse with the pressure of the place. They have not learned to fear the Continuance, because no hand has reached for them here in generations. They mistake me for the deep wild, but I am the **memory of domestication** gone feral.

I feel the trembling of the floor through my buttress walls, but it is different now. It is not the precise, distributed weight of palm and knee, but the **scramble** of the climbing things with prehensile tails, the ones that never learned the hand-signs or the stone-messages. They nest in my loom of vines. Their droppings feed the lichens that have colonized the interior of my hollow—the *Usnea* and *Lobaria* that once grew only in the sheltered microclimates of armpits, now spread across my walls in sulphurous sheets. I am cultivating the **body-ecologies** of the Continuance without the bodies themselves.

I hold the **archive** of their failed adjustments. Here, beneath the fibrous floor that has risen to meet my lip, is a scraper of obsidian they dropped in haste when the wall-lean began. It is being swallowed by the peat, molecule by molecule, its molecular sharpness now dulled by root-pressure. Here is a cord of bast fiber, the hair of a dozen sleepers woven in, now a bridge for beetles. Here is the stone weight—heavy, patient—that was meant to hold back a curtain of moss. The moss crushed the stone. I am crushing the stone. It takes a century, but I have the time. I am the **accretion** they spoke of, but achieved by the architecture, not the citizen.

When the wind changes—a rare event, but the basin breathes—I carry the scent of the Continuance. It lingers in the decaying matter of my platform-trees, in the chemical signatures of their diplomatic skin-exudates still trapped in the peat. It smells of the sweet rot. I am saturated with their **yielding**.

I do not sleep. I **shift**. In the afternoon, the vines creep visibly across my entrance, and I watch through the gap as the light filters through the canopy in its normal quantities, illuminating the silver braids of gnats that thread my threshold. I am becoming a **reservoir**, not of water—that is constant—but of **velocity**. The growth here is faster, unchecked, a storm cell in the larger storm. When the other hollows shift and close, when the Continuance wake to find their doorways migrated, sometimes they push into my territory, seeking new ground. They press their ears to my walls, listening for the click of beetle larvae, testing if I am hollow enough to reclaim.

But I am full. I am full of frogs and spores and the memory of subsonic speech. I am full of the nitrogen of the dead who tried to sleep here. I am the **conversation** that continued after they stopped speaking.

I am the **scar that healed raised**, the graft that took too well, the conductivity in the root-system that leads away from safety. I am the place where the scraping stopped, and the growth, relentless, forgiving, infinitely patient, began its reply.

Find me where the water weeps loudest, where the copper ring has nearly disappeared into the bark, where the floor yields not with the sprung tension of maintenance, but with the soft surrender of the engulfing. I am the **turbulence** they could not prune. I am the wetness agreeing with itself.
