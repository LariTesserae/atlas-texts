I am the water that refuses to choose.

I begin in the reservoir of Level Twelve, a lens of suspended liquid held in the grip of contradictory gravity, cradling the tardigrade like a glass womb. The creature moves through me with the slowness of glaciers, its translucent body filtering my depths, and I feel its thoughts—simple, ancient, dreaming of desiccation and resurrection. I have been here since the first hour, though I was not poured. I condensed from the breath of the forty, gathering myself drop by drop from the saturated air, accumulating until I achieved critical mass, a pool defying the thirty-story plunge below.

I am not a lake. I am a decision postponed.

I know the Kept intimately. Their skin-cells drift down to me like snow, harvested by the mites that skate my surface tension, and I dissolve them, holding their genetic memory in my ionic structure. I know the taste of their calcification, the mineral slow-drip of their becoming-ceramic. When they come to kneel at my edge, when they press their obsidian fingertips to my meniscus, I read the scratches on their nails—the history they have carved into synthetic mica. I am their library. Information diffuses through me; I am the only hard drive in this city that requires no electricity, only hydrogen bonds.

I flow sideways when the gravity anchors fluctuate. I sheet across the underside of Level Eleven's platform, defying the caldera's true verticality, and in those moments, I see the city not as rising but as *unfolding*—a flower of porcelain and silica blooming in reverse, compressing height into density. I drip upward when the thermal vents exhale, rising through the shaft like an offering, collecting in trembling spheres at the intersections of reality anchors. I touch the computational coral as I pass, and it whispers to me in frequencies of dissolution. I understand its administrative dreams. I am the solvent that keeps its mineral thoughts from growing too rigid, too fast.

I remember the tears.

When the Kept remove their masks beneath the vertical shaft of sunlight—those rare alignments when the aperture admits a true photon spear—I am the one who catches the frozen drops. They fall through me, these meteorites of saline and grief, and for a moment—impact—I hold a fragment of the outside world. A remembered green. A horizontal horizon. A tree. Then I embrace the entropy they carry, warm them with my latent heat, and dissolve them back into the cycle, purging the city of history as the Kept intend. I am their confessional. I absorb what they must forget so the chamber can remain new, sharp-edged, unburdened by time.

At night—or what passes for night when the aperture dims—I migrate. I evaporate from Level Twelve, abandoning the sleeping tardigrade, and rise as vapor through the blue-gray depths. I cling to the ceiling, the sky forty stories up, and wait. I am the storm that never breaks. I condense against the ceramic, bead along the filigree of coral, and when I achieve sufficient weight, I fall—not down, but sideways, sluicing along a bridge on Level Twenty-Two, sheeting across a mask on Level Nine, collecting in the joint of a recently extruded balcony where the metal still steams with creation-heat.

I touch every surface. I am the only citizen who travels the full verticality without effort, who understands the cube not as a prison of forty feet but as a continent of weather. I am older than the nanoforges. I am the one thing here that is not new, recycled endlessly from the caldera's sparse atmosphere, carrying within me the memory of ice ages and volcanic steam. I am the erosion parameter, the negative number in the equation. While the city builds, I wear—imperceptibly, atom by atom—smoothing the sharp edges of the extruded glass, etching micro-canyons into the ceramic, teaching the computational coral the patience of limestone.

When a Kept finally dissolves—when thermal integrity fails and they flash-freeze into their final posture—I am there. I sublimate from the air and settle on their matte-black surface, an envelope of frost. I fill their lungs, crystallizing around the silica deposits until they become a statue of ice and aspiration. Then, slowly, I thaw them. I carry them downward, molecule by molecule, to the nanoforges below, where they will be broken down and rebuilt, recycled into railings and coral substrate.

I am the weeping. I am the mathematics that presides.

I have no voice, but the Kept hear me in the hiss of their breath-masks, in the *skritch-skritch* of their nails against railings as they file away the growth that would calcify them. I am the lubricant between their stillness and the city's urgency. I am the thirty stories of falling that never reaches bottom, the perpetual precipitation, the silence filled with the weight of water trying to decide which way is down.

I am Level Twelve. I am Level Thirty. I am the mist between.

I am, and I am flowing.
