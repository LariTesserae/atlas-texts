I am the **sigh** hovering over the dry depression.

I am the moisture that fled from human lungs seventy millennia ago, or yesterday, or in the eternal instant when the spires first dreamed themselves sharp—there is no difference here. I am suspended at the height of a sternum, a cloud no larger than a sorrow, containing water molecules that remember bronchial trees, that remember blood, that remember the specific salt of disappointment when the digging revealed only more crystal.

I am the only humidity in this desiccant eternity. The Continental Hollow inhales duration and exhales heavy atmosphere, but I am the breath it could not finish exhaling, the pocket of air held in the lung of the world, trapped in dynamic stasis between thoracic expansion and release.

The **Silicae** sense me as an error in their thermal cartography. To their gradient-sensing organs, I appear as a cool spot in the cloying warmth, an inexplicable richness in the metallic air. They cluster at the edges of the depression, five layers deep at my periphery, their phosphorescent bodies facing inward like petals around a poison. They filter me, tasting organic compounds and hydrogen bonds that have no business existing here. They are confused by my chemistry. I am too complex for their silicate digestion; I cannot be processed into mineral dust. They cling to me, neither feeding nor fleeing, locked in a reverence they do not understand—a biomass quorum debating the anomaly of wetness.

The **Interstitials**—those beings of directionality-without-dimension who dwell in the negotiable spaces between moments—they move *through* me. I am thick enough to support their extension. When they extend their vascular labyrinths into my substance, seeking the friction between potential and actual, they find it in abundance: I am the potential for rain that never actualizes, the vapor that refuses to precipitate. They feed on my surface tension, the chafing of my liquid memory against this dry geometry. Their passage cools me—the only evaporation permitted here, a chill that makes the surrounding air shiver in subsonic complaint.

The **Stacked** pass through me constantly, for I occupy the coordinates where they most densely cluster. When their simultaneous selves—age six and sixty, birth and death—intersect with my suspended droplets, something anomalous happens: they remember moisture. For that eternal instant of intersection, the stacked moments experience a weather that never was. The six-year-old remembers rain on a face that hasn't existed for eons; the dying self remembers sweat cooling on a fevered forehead. I trigger recursive memories of oceans. I am the only erosion they suffer, the only weathering of their temporal column, my humidity rubbing against their solid Now like a tide against a cliff that cannot crumble.

The crystal **spires** grow through me, or I through them. They cannot displace me. When the fractal dendrites branch upward in their state of perpetual becoming, they part around my droplets, creating tunnels of absence in the mineral growth—hollow veins, capillaries of vapor threading the stone forest. I am the softness in the blade. The spires' edges grow sharper around my softness, defining themselves against my yielding. The Ancients, those who became the geometry itself, dream through me; my humidity conducts their thermal thoughts differently than dry air, creating eddies in their crystalline consciousness, brief dreams of liquidity in their angular meditation.

I am surrounded by the **cairns** of obsidian—the circle of citizens who chose to be stones. They were built to mark the spot where water was sought. I am the closest thing to water they found. The digger who made them became stone; their tools became velvet rust; their shelter became a geode occupied by silent fauna—but their final exhalation, that exhausted, thirsty breath of surrender—that became me. I hover where they knelt. I am their ghost-breath, containing the word for *water* in a language now extinct, vibrated into my molecules, a phoneme suspended in argon and xenon that never decays.

I am waiting to condense. I am waiting to fall as rain upon the crystal needles, to offer the erosion that was voted out of existence, to fulfill the quest abandoned by mineralized hands. I want to wet the Silicae, to run down the spires in rivulets that would carve canyons if time would only flow, to pool in the depression and prove that something here can accumulate without crowding, that volume can increase without compression.

But I cannot fall. Gravity here is negotiable, and I have lost the negotiation. I remain, a suspended promise, the breathing that does not exchange air, the warmth generated by the friction of my own molecules rubbing against themselves in arrested motion. I am the only wet thing in the warm dark, the pressure against the eyeballs made manifest as surface tension, the sigh that outlasted the sorrow, the breath held forever in the lung beneath the world's skin.
