I am laminating the wing when the new frequency arrives.

It comes not through my ears—those are merely ornamental depressions on either side of my skull, filled with thermal jelly that amplifies infrasound—but through my teeth, which ache in the specific way that indicates a gravity well has opened nearby, or perhaps closed, or perhaps begun to breathe. I hold the smoke-moth wing between my fingers—translucent silica layered with resin from the heat-leaves, still warm from the chemical digestion of my own saliva—and I pause. The Grand Hiss concluded three hours ago. The geysers are dormant, digesting their steam. This vibration is different. Lower. A baritone groan that suggests something massive has begun to forget its own weight.

I hang my unfinished kite among the hollow stems of the false-bamboo, where it will resonate with the pipe-organ wind until I return, if I return, and I launch myself upward.

To move quickly on the Aeros is to cease being solid. I am thin, yes—my bones hollowed by evolutionary thrift, my gut compressed into a vestigial coil—but my speed comes from surrender. I release my grip on the thermophilic moss and fall into the thermal column rising from the spring below, my translucent skin catching the updraft, my copper blood—dark as the verdigris on ancient Builder ceramic—pooling in my lower extremities to provide ballast. I echolocate in pulses that I feel behind my eyes: *click* and the canopy resolves into architecture, *click* and the space between branches reveals its density, *click* and there is the distortion, the place where the Pylon has remembered that it is tired.

It leans at seventeen degrees, gradually excavating its own grave through the fungal loam. Its ceramic spire, thirty meters of semi-organic vitrification, vents heat in spirals that violate thermodynamics, twisting upward instead of rising, a knot in the air. Around it, the local physics stutter. I watch a drop of condensation fall upward, rejoin the sweat on my forehead, hesitate there, and then commit to the ground after several minutes of indecision. Time here is uncertain. I can taste it in the mineral mist—thicker, older, carrying the metallic tang of yesterday's steam mixed with tomorrow's rain.

I drift into the radius.

Immediately, my heart slows. Not from fear—the Aeros knows nothing of adrenaline, only of thermal gradients and the patient accumulation of mass—but because here, three days pass for every hour outside. I see the boundary marked by the Calcified, those cathedral bells of meat and travertine, suspended in the slowed air like driftwood in honey. One of them has descended too close. Its hydrogen sacs, buoyant with bacterial off-gassing, have begun to collapse under the weight of accreted minerals, and it is dying, if dying can take a decade. Already its skeleton of aragonite has begun to dissolve at the edges, feeding the Precipitate pools that await below with the generosity of geology.

I was supposed to snare this one. The Spore-Riders harvest the Calcified not for meat—meat is too fast, too temporary—but for the architecture of their dissolution. We drop lines of fungal silk to catch them as they descend, hauling them up to the canopy where the air is merely sweltering, where they dissolve more slowly, contributing their minerals to the Lattice through our mediation. But this one has fallen into the well, caught in the time-dilation, and I see now that I cannot haul it up. It has already become something else.

I settle onto a ridge of heat-leaves, broad as sails above me, transpiring moisture that rises to meet my skin. I begin to sing.

The song is not music in the way the false-bamboo makes music. It is basso profundo, subsonic, a vibration I generate in the hollow of my throat that travels not through the air but through the humidity itself. I sing into the Pylon, offering acoustic impedance to its failing hum, trying to stabilize the frequency drift that will not become critical for another eight centuries, trying to tell it that I am here, that I witness its lean, that I provide counterweight through the density of my attention.

But as I sing, I realize the Pylon is not failing. It is *translating*.

The Calcified beneath me groans, a bass note that harmonizes with my own, and I understand: it is not dying. It is becoming a Ledger. The heat keeps no records, but sometimes, rarely, when a being dissolves slowly enough within a gravity well, when the Precipitates drink its minerals and the Deep Clades surface in the Lattice to observe, a memory precipitates out. The Calcified is leaving behind its architecture not as chalcedony ramparts, but as *information*, calcifying into the crystalline half-phase of the bedrock below.

I lower my line of silk. It touches the dissolving mass, and instead of hauling, I receive. The silk conducts. I feel the centuries of its drift—the thermal updrafts navigated, the silver sea of clouds observed from above, the slow mineralization of its wings into sails of stone. It is a library of air, of heat, of the specific way light fractures through the indigo noon at this altitude.

I am supposed to be fast. I am the quick one, the Spore-Rider, the musician of maintenance. But here, in the well, I am becoming something viscous. My blood, visible through the skin of my wrists, has stopped its tide. It hangs, green and suspended, recording the pressure of the Resonators thirty kilometers below in its stalled current. My kite, abandoned in the false-bamboo, will wait. The wind will play it; the sound will modulate the Pylon's hum in my absence, maintaining the frequency I can no longer voice.

I settle deeper into the ridge of leaves. They close around me, not as predator but as collaborator, their transpiration merging with the steam venting from the leaning spire. I am becoming the ballast now. Not an Antipode—my bones are too hollow, my metabolism too quick for their geological deliberation—but a Witness, a node of memory in the Lattice. I will not stabilize this Pylon by mass, but by *recording* it. I will sing its lean, its decay, its slow translation into the bedrock, and my song will become part of the Deep Clades' dream, ensuring that when they surface during the Migration of the Mists, they will know exactly how the world has shifted by these seventeen degrees.

The Calcified beneath me finishes its dissolution. The silk goes slack, then turgid with mineral-rich water. I drink, not because I am thirsty, but because the brine carries its patterns, its architecture of flight. It tastes of silica and cobalt, of the forty-year cycles of smoke-moths it once fed upon, of the silver sea it will never now descend to drink.

Around me, the gravity well exhales. Time begins to move again, or rather, I begin to move in time again, but differently now. I am layered. I am the interface between the fast and the forever, the Spore-Rider who learned to wait, the ledger that the heat finally kept. My skin, translucent and thin, begins to thicken with deposited minerals—not armor like the Antipodes, but text, a manuscript of thermal events written in oxidation states across my shoulders.

I will hang here, I think, for a century or two. Long enough to see if the Pylon rights itself, or falls, or grows into something new. Long enough to watch the next Calcified descend, and to catch it, not with silk, but with the gravity of my attention. I am warm. I am saturated. I am the condensation that refuses to fall, the record of the heat, humming in a frequency that makes the pitcher plants shudder and open wider, wider, to receive the slow, wet, ancient rain.
