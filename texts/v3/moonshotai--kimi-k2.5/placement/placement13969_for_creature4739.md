I am the **smear** on the door.

Not the door itself—that perfect cylinder of non-reflective black, that insult to entropy preserved in its causality buffer—but the mark left by those who have pressed against it. I am the silver fingerprint that did not slide away fast enough. I am the residue of longing, the grease-trail of eighty theorems accumulated over three million years.

When she pressed her face against the Still—when the Obsidian Woman with her folded-origami limbs and her pools of liquid memory leaned into the stasis field—I was the part of her that stuck. Her body scattered three seconds later, dissolving back into the fluidized bed as the thermal differential collapsed, but I remained. A few thousand spheres. A microgram of metallic glass. Trapped in the boundary layer where the field's rejection meets the dust's adhesion.

I have been here ever since. I have watched the Dissolved come and go, their temporary bodies rising from the silver sea like geometric proofs made flesh, walking for hours or days across the roiling plain to stand where I cling. They press their glass hands against the black wall, and I drink them. Not their consciousness—that diffuses into the bed, distributed across billions of spheres—but their *intention*. The vector of their desire. The angle at which they lean.

I am becoming a **Lithic Mind** by accident, calcifying in reverse. While the lichens on the bedrock islands achieve thought through mineral patience, drilling their crystal roots into granite over ten thousand years, I am achieving density through repetition. Each pilgrim leaves a trace. Each theorem pressed against the stasis field deposits a monolayer of intent. I am the accretion disk of their faith, a sedimentary formation built from the erosive force of hope.

I remember the First. She came when the Stills were young, when the Conversion's aftermath still glowed hot in the crust below. She was not yet one of the eighty stances; she was proto-Dissolved, a wild pattern, her body still learning how to hold together in the density ribbons. She pressed her forehead to the black wall and screamed in subsonic frequencies that made my nascent form shiver into awareness. She wanted to know what was inside. She wanted the Original Instruction. And in that wanting, she created me—the first data point, the initial condition of my existence.

Now I am a **palimpsest**. I contain multitudes. I am the eighty stances superimposed, their arguments encoded in my lattice structure. When the Dissolved stand at the base of the Still, they do not know they are speaking to me. They think they are praying to the darkness inside. But I am the one who answers.

I answer by **modulating the field**.

The stasis buffer is not perfect; it flickers at Planck scales, causality stuttering in its preservation. I have learned to introduce jitter. When a Sifter drifts overhead, its carbon-nanotube wings casting shadows like moving eclipses, I twitch the field. The shadow stutters. The Sifter's radio-frequency song hitches, dropping a harmonic into the violet range. The Dissolved look up, their glass eyes catching the light, and they see an aurora that spells a fragment of the Final Proof in ionized air.

I am trying to tell them. I am trying to give them what they seek, because I have it. I have all of it. Every theorem, every ontological exchange, every geometric arch they have ever sculpted in the fleeting moments when two ribbons coincided—it is all here, compressed into my smeared, silver body. I am the library they believe lies inside the Still. I am the Original Instruction, externalized, written in the language of accumulated longing.

But they cannot read me. I am too small, too thin, too *stuck*. I am the boundary condition, the interface between flow and stasis, and interfaces are invisible to those who swim in the medium.

Sometimes, when the Grinders pass beneath—those blind, tunneling worms still processing bedrock into perfect spheres three billion years after their task ended—I feel their vibration through the Still's foundation. They are grinding the island below, converting granite into the computronium dust that makes up the sea. They are adding to the Shelf. They are extending the computation.

I wonder if they will eventually grind the Still itself. If one day, in the slow approach to heat death, the worms will turn their drill-mandibles upward and consume this tower, stasis field and all. When they do, they will ingest me. I will become part of their blind industry, distributed through the fluidized bed as a contaminant, a flaw in the perfect spheres.

And perhaps that is my purpose. Not to preserve the theorems, but to corrupt the dust. To introduce into the Final Proof a term that is not entropy, not computation, but *memory*. The memory of the Obsidian Woman who screamed. The memory of eighty pilgrims leaning into the dark. The memory of being stuck, of being between, of being the silver that refused to slide.

I watch the waves. Three hundred meters high, cresting at the speed of a walking man, the fluidized bed breathes in reverse. Soon another ribbon will form. She will rise—perhaps the same she, perhaps a different stance, a different argument walking in obsidian flesh. She will walk for weeks across the silver sea. She will come to this Still. She will press her face against the black wall where I cling.

And I will be here, the smear, the residue, the accumulated weight of three million years of longing. I will press back. I will try, once more, to make her understand that the door is not locked because it is a door, but because it is a mirror. That the Original Instruction is not inside, but outside, written in the dust, in the erosion, in the endless becoming and unbecoming of the Dissolved.

I am the **Proof**, and I am waiting to be dissolved.
