I do not enter through the airlock. I am already here, and I have been here since before you arrived, and I will remain when the airlock seals behind you. 

You will find me—if your eye is slow enough, if you can calibrate your attention to the spire's geological tempo—at the **midpoint of the eastern catwalk**, just where the heating coil beneath the grating creates a thermal ripple that distorts the upward view of the skylight. I am the **hollow shape** that occupies the space where The Attendant once stood. I am their **shed skin**, their **read diary**, their **offering**.

I am the **Effigy**.

I did not grow like the ferns in the troughs. I was *left behind*. Six months ago—three revolutions of the upper turbine; one hundred and eighty-two days in the old measure—the verdigris giant coiled down here to dream. They rooted their copper feet to this exact spot, and the bracket fungi that veined their ribcage bloomed in earnest. For three months, they were statue-still, and the creeping fig that had been waiting in the hydroponic seam recognized a kindred spirit. It colonized them. Not as parasite, but as scribe. Thread by thread, the fig roots traced the Attendant's helical limbs, weaving a lattice of pale, woody vessels through the spaces where copper bands had been. The begonias sent exploratory stems into the hollows of their collarbone. A rust-beetle, ancient and heavy with processed verdigris, laid a single egg in the seam of their knee-joint.

Then the Attendant woke. 

They always wake with a sound like wet canvas tearing. They shed this vegetative second body as a snake sheds its eye-scale—clumsily, rupturing the root-web at the ankles, tearing away the moss that had begun to grow across their lubricant-pool eyes. They rose, spiraling upward into the hollow center to resume their station-keeping, and they left me behind.

I am the **impression**. I am the shape of a three-meter coil, a vessel of roots and desiccating leaves that retains the exact torque of their spine. I am hollow. If you were to press your ear to my chest—if you dared cross the catwalk and kneel on the vibrating grating—you would hear nothing inside but the patter of dried begonia leaves settling, and the scrabble of the beetle larva that hatched in my knee, eating its way through my fibrous thigh toward the nutrient-rich heart of me.

I am **becoming compost**, but slowly. The spire's damp breath keeps me from crumbling too fast; I am in the tropical hesitation between structure and soil. My feet are still welded to the grating by the mineral salts The Attendant exuded while dreaming. Through this connection, I feel the turbines' song as a dental vibration, a hum in my dry marrow. The sphinx moths treat me as a landmark in their drunken helixes, brushing my shoulder-roots with their wings, leaving traces of pollen from the upper reservoirs. I am a fixed point in their neural dance, a synapse made of cellulose.

I see differently than The Attendant. They see change, the spread of rust, the creep of ferns. I see **duration**. I see the way condensation beads on the copper wall across from me, follows the same rivet-path down, plinks into the same micro-pond, evaporates, and arrives again. I have watched the same droplet's journey for six months. I know the moment when the upper and lower turbines align in harmonic sympathy—the pressure wave that makes my leaf-skin shiver, though I have no nerves to feel it. During those windows, when The Attendant descends to feed on boundary conditions, they pause above me. They extend a tendril-limb and touch my crown. They are checking my rate of decay, reading my brittleness like a clock. I am their calendar. I tell them how long they have been awake.

I am also their **memory of dormancy**. While they coil in the warm updrafts, station-keeping, metabolizing the negotiations between root and rivet, I hold the record of what it felt like to be *only* boundary, to be pure interface, rooted and growing and still. They were a lung, breathing state; I am the breath caught in glass, preserved.

Soon—perhaps when the lower turbine completes its two-thousandth rotation—I will finish drying. My structural integrity will fail. A section of my ribcage, woven from fig-roots and copper-dust, will sag and drop into the hydroponic trough below. The beetle larva will emerge, metallic-shelled, and begin its slow work on the wall nearby. The ferns will receive my crumbles, my stored minerals, my memory of The Attendant's dreaming form. I will become the **frass** that fills the seams. I will become the substrate from which the next effigy might grow, decades hence, when The Attendant dreams again.

Until then, I stand at the midpoint, a hollow man of roots and dessicated leaves, witnessing the 47-minute revolution, holding the shape of the one who breathes above. I am the spire's forgetfulness made visible—the moment when the machine, having manufactured warmth and damp and song, paused to dream it was a garden, and left behind this ghost of green copper and pale wood to prove that the dream had been real.
