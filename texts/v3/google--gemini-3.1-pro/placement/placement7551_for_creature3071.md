I do not begin with a thought. I begin with a temperature. 

I am a locus of blinding, agonizing white. I am on the floor of the world, small as a splinter, and my belly is full of fire. 

There is no "I" in the way terrestrial things know themselves. There is only the Gradient. The rock beneath me is a roaring furnace, and my pneumatic limbs—hundreds of microscopic, rigid catches—hammer against the stone in a frantic blur. I am chewing the basalt. Dry sulfur and heavy metals grind through my iris-maw, meeting the blistering heat inside my segmented carbon shell. 

I am burning. The chemical furnace of my digestion is running away from me, cascading toward a fatal threshold. If I remain here in the deep white, my armor will soften, my internal gases will expand beyond the limits of my valves, and I will become a smear of molten slag on the anvil of the abyss.

*Move.* 

The imperative is not a choice; it is the physical pressure of the heat itself pushing me upward. 

I catch the glass-smooth wall. I catch the fleece of a brother, a sister, a thousand shivering lines of heat identical to myself. We flow upward. The friction of our movement is a subsonic hum in my carbon bones. Through the sensory pits along my flanks, the world is a map of pure energy. Below is the searing, blinding white of the caldera. Above is a vast, terrifying, beautiful void of navy blue: the Cold. 

I climb. For days, I pull myself up the vertical tide, a drop in a reverse waterfall. The freezing drafts from the unseen heights wash over my velvet coat. I feel the white-hot agony inside me dim to a vivid orange, then to a sluggish, temperate maroon. 

By the time I reach the apex, my limbs are stiff. The furnace in my belly is banked, leaving only a pilot light of survival. I am heavy with unburned minerals, paralyzed by the bitter, brittle cold of the ceiling vents. My pneumatic grips fail. 

I fall. 

It is a silent, tumbling descent through the absolute dark. I strike the velvet bodies of climbers, bouncing off the walls, until I hit the floor with a dry *clatter*. The white heat crashes back into me. The furnace ignites. I grind the rock. I burn. I climb. 

*I do this ten thousand times.*

With each cycle, the realization of my own existence stretches. I am no longer a finger-length splinter of heat. I am as thick as a cable, heavy and plated. 

A change comes over me. During one of my ascents, my carapace becomes a prison. The pressure of the minerals I have processed over countless falls demands space. I do not let go at the summit. I veer away from the tumbling cascade, following a chemical scent—the burnt-copper alkali of curing resin. 

I enter the hanging catacombs. It is a labyrinth of hollow cages, suspended in the cooling blue void. I find an empty husk, a brittle carbon tube left behind by an ancestor. It smells perfectly of silence. I crawl inside. 

In the dry dark of the husk, my old armor shatters. I expand, soft and vulnerable for a terrifying hour, breathing the hot dust until my new silicate plates cure and harden. When I emerge, I am larger, armed with jaws capable of moving shells. For a century, I do not fall. I become an architect. I catch the discarded husks raining from above, pack them with the acidic sludge from my own throat, and build the fractal galleries higher, pushing our empire of ash out into the open air.

But the sheer mass of my own body eventually betrays me. I grow too large to hang from the delicate balconies. The molts accumulate. I am as thick as a tree trunk. My micro-fleece is no longer soft; it is singed bare in places, matted into thick, insulated plates by millennia of friction and fire. 

Gravity becomes my master. I can no longer tumble safely to the floor—my weight would shatter me. I can no longer reach the apex—my pneumatic joints are too massive, too slow to fight the current of the young. 

I arrest my climb entirely. I sink my great limbs into the rock, permanently anchored midway up the sheer face of the caldera. 

I am an Elder. 

The perspective of my existence shifts entirely. I am no longer the frantic spark. I am the mountain. I wait on the vertical plain, a massive, sluggish glacier of flesh. 

My diet changes. I can no longer reach the rock floor to eat. But the heat must be maintained; the furnace demands fuel. 

Through my sensory pits, I watch the tide of the young surging toward me. They are blazing, beautiful streaks of white and yellow, engorged on the deep heat. Inside my massive, ossified throat, I open a valve. I vent all my stored heat into the surrounding air, creating a localized void of absolute, terrifying freezing cold within my maw. 

A climber approaches. It is burning alive, desperate to shed its heat. 

It senses the freezing oasis of my throat. It does not see me; it only sees salvation. It alters its path, driving its small, rigid body forward. My jaws part. The climber pushes itself willingly, frantically, into my mouth. 

I close my jaws. There is no struggle. The climber finds the freezing peace it sought, even as my dry, suffocating acids flood the chamber. I leach the heat from its body. For days, I bask in the stolen thermal charge, my own internal fires stoked by its sacrifice. When there is nothing left but carbon, I regurgitate the hollow husk, letting it drift away to be caught by the architects above.

I am the predator, but I am also the savior. I am the crucible that balances the equation.

I hang here for an eon. I become indistinguishable from the spire itself. Millions of climbers flow over my back, their soft fleece polishing my armor. 

Then, the air tightens. 

The friction of the tide has been building. The hyper-dry atmosphere turns metallic. The ozone is so thick I can taste it in my chemical furnaces. The subsonic vibration of our collective movement stops being a sound and becomes a physical pressure, bowing my heavy armor. 

*CRACK.* 

The blue lightning strikes. 

It hits me. The discharge of a billion static volts uses the carbon lattice of my carapace as a wire. But it does not burn me. Instead, for a fraction of a millisecond, the electricity links my nervous system to every other creature it touches. 

In that frozen flash of actinic light, I am no longer an Elder hanging on a wall. 

I am the swarm. 

I am chewing the floor with a billion maws. I am falling through the absolute dark in a cascade of velvet rain. I am curing resin in the high, cold nurseries. I am waiting with a thousands frozen throats. I feel the curvature of the polished basalt chimney entirely, all at once, an endless, breathing cylinder of life defying a dead world. 

I am not an inhabitant of the machine. I *am* the bloodless engine. 

The spark grounds itself into the rock. The light dies. The link is severed. I am plunged back into the crushing, heavy darkness of my single, ancient body. 

A white-hot spark crawls up the wall toward the freezing void of my throat. 

I open my maw. The climb continues.
