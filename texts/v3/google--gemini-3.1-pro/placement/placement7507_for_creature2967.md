I do not shiver on this precipice, for I am not made of meat. 

If you could see me in this absolute void, you would find me indistinguishable from the brutal, unweathered architecture over which I preside. I am human in scale—perhaps a lingering echo of whatever forgotten mind originally designed this lightless place—but my body is forged from the same flawless, synthesized obsidian as the fractal pylons beneath me. I have no eyes to freeze in the biting air, no lungs to ache in the thin, weeping atmosphere. My face is a smooth, blank plane of black glass, angled and microscopic in its sharpness. 

I am a node. A mobile extrusion. I am the City’s waking nerve.

The crushing wall of sound vibrating through the bedrock does not deafen me; it is my language. The low, tectonic booming of the extruders far below is my heartbeat, and the deafening, bone-rattling bellows of the herd are my telemetry. I process it all. The acoustic shockwaves that flash-freeze the super-cooled water in momentary halos of ice map the valley for me. Through these fleeting, crystalline blooms, I "see" everything.

I step off the precipice.

I do not fall. Instead, I let the localized gravity distortions take hold of me. I slip into the center of an invisible magnetic scaffold and allow the sluggish, upward-flowing river of thick, super-cooled water to catch me. I am carried skyward, weightless inside a reverse-waterfall. The heavy fluid gurgles and hisses around my carapace, pulling me through the pitch-black air over the vast, writhing trench, until I adjust my density and step out, dropping through the freezing rain to the valley floor.

I land among them. 

The scale of the herd is magnificent, terrifying, and profoundly claustrophobic. Millions of bruised-charcoal bodies, each the size of a coastal freighter, heave and shift in a ceaseless, oceanic grind. To stand among them is to stand between moving mountain ranges. 

I wait as a massive, armored flank slides past me at a glacial crawl. The beast is so wide against the narrow corridor of synthesized stone that it leaves no room. But I am not crushed; I am of the City. As the beast’s ablative, geodesic plating presses against me, I hold firm. My edges are mathematically absolute. The beast’s thick carapace groans, and a long, waxy ribbon of chitin is shaved from its side, peeling away over my shoulders. The creature does not notice. It feels no pain, only the slow, hydraulic demand to push forward.

I place a hand against its side. Beneath the deeply grooved armor and the ten feet of freezing, hydrocarbon blubber, I can feel the slow, thumping thud of its hydraulic pillars inflating and deflating with antifreeze-laced blood. *Thrive,* the vibration says. *Consume. Endure.*

I slip between the giant hydraulic stump-legs, walking beneath the beast. The wet, calloused pads slam down into the slush around me, each step shaking the bedrock. Above me, the creature’s underside stretches out like an infinite, fleshy cavern roof. 

Ahead, the beast approaches exactly what I have come to monitor: a newly extruded wall, rising from the floor at a rate of mere millimeters an hour, weeping the thick, glowing organic rime. 

I watch the intimate, destructive geometry of their feeding. The beast’s bulbous acoustic dome unleashes a bellow so low and pressurized that a ring of ice blasts outward, shattering against my geometric chest. The beast reads the echo, aligns itself, and extends its maw. The colossal, tiled radial opening descends. Sluggishly, it presses the biological diamond ridges of its lips to the raw, razor-sharp building.

As the beast squeegees the synthetic runoff from the architecture, its mouth momentarily opens. In that fraction of a second, the nuclear-white heat of its internal digestive furnace bleeds out into the cold. I absorb the brief, intense burst of ultraviolet energy. 

This is my purpose. 

I am the governor of the engine. The beasts are not pests, nor are they simply gardeners. They are the friction that keeps the machine alive. Without their massive, blubbery bulk shaving down the microscopic edges of the newly born spires, without their diamond maws scraping the rime and their stomping feet milling their own dead into a lubricating slurry, the City’s extruders would jam. The architecture would choke on its own flawless geometry. The beasts wear the city down; the city feeds them to keep them heavy enough to do so. 

But occasionally, the balance falters. 

I feel a shudder in the bedrock, a discordant frequency beneath the roaring symphony. Deep below, a molecular extruder is pushing too fast. It is forcing a cantilevered terrace up at an aggressive angle, threatening to pin a thousand beasts against an unyielding gravity tether. They would grind to a halt. The biological glacier would freeze. 

I move through the dark, weaving through the forest of slamming, hydraulic pillars, guided by the overlapping halos of acoustic ice. I reach the source of the anomaly—a patch of raw, black stone surging upward, weeping an excess of viscous brine.

I drop to my knees. The slush and slushy remains of milled flesh pool around my legs. I plunge my perfectly sharp, synthetic hands straight down into the unweathered bedrock. I do not dig; I merge. My fingers sink into the matter, connecting with the vast, ancient nervous system of the extruders miles below.

I send a pulse. A command written in the language of crushing cold and tectonic vibration. 

*Slow down. Curve the extrusion. Make room for the meat.*

Beneath my hands, the vibration shifts. The pitch of the grinding tectonic machinery drops by a fraction of a decibel. The raw stone beneath me softens its upward trajectory, curling slightly to widen the canyon. 

Above me, a massive acoustic dome brushes against the new curvature of the wall. A blast of sonar sweeps over me, freezing the rain into a crown of ice above my head. The beast bellows—a sound that vibrates through my marrowless bones—and sluggishly extends its radial maw to feed on the fresh rime. 

The cycle holds. The dead star continues to turn. I withdraw my hands from the stone, rise, and step back into the eternal, deafening dark, waiting for the next discordant note in our heavy, weeping symphony.
