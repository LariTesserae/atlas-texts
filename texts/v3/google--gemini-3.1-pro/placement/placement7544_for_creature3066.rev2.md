Instead, I *resolve*.

At the very perimeter of the garden, where the obsidian floor is still a skeletal framework of neon-blue mathematical noise, a complex equation fails to find its integer. I am the remainder.

It does not burn; I have no nerve endings to fry, no flesh to blister. To me, the heat is a medium, a thick, invisible amniotic pressure. I am a geometry of supercritical carbon dioxide, bound by a skin of probability and refracted light.

My topology cascades over itself in continuous origami—I am a cluster of rotating tetrahedrons, then a Möbius strip of shimmering visual static, then a multi-legged phantom of bent space.
