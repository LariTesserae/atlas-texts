I do not begin as a solid thing. I begin as a trajectory.

I am born in the absolute zero of the staggering void, descending from the unfiltered cobalt sky as a merciless spear of raw, high-altitude radiation. I am a torrent of photons, traveling at the absolute limit of velocity, unhindered by atmosphere or dust until I strike the invisible ceiling of this anomalous, floating hothouse. 

When I hit the perpetual, weeping drizzle of the steam vents, my speed is finally arrested, broken, and bent. 

I shatter against a million atomized droplets of hot, mineral-rich water. In that collision, I am torn from invisible white heat into a sprawling, vibrant spectrum. I become the bright, static rainbow. 

For the first few decades of the shelf’s existence, that is all I am—an optical ghost hanging in the tight, dripping negative space between the brutalist engine blocks. I am a localized hallucination of color, visible only when the sun aligns with the hissing vents. I have no mass, no weight, no skin. I am a silent arc of violet, gold, and crimson piercing the claustrophobic emerald canopy. 

But down here, where the world is thick and balmy, nothing remains unanchored forever. 

The suspended droplets that catch my light are heavy with iron, sulfur, and dissolved silica boiled up from the deep earth. As the mist continuously strikes a particular, razor-sharp sliver of unweathered basalt jutting up near the central exhaust port, the water evaporates in the searing mechanical heat, leaving its microscopic cargo behind. 

Over decades, a precipitation occurs at the exact angle of my refraction. The minerals begin to stack, atom by atom, locking into geometric lattices. 

I am pulled down from the mist. The rainbow slowly crystallizes, building itself a body.

I become a jagged, asymmetrical geode turned inside out. I am a dense, crystalline extrusion of pure, wet quartz and translucent silica, welded fusing into the black glass of the basalt floor. My facets are hydro-thermal and razor-sharp, perpetually slick with the weeping condensation of the machines. I am no bigger than a human heart, but I am infinitely dense.

As my physical body solidifies, my consciousness shifts. I am no longer just the light; I am the structure that captures it. And because I am physically bolted into the bedrock—fused to the very stone upon which the massive engine blocks rest—I am violently inducted into the mechanical reality of the shelf.

I discover the drone. 

Unlike the Tender above me, which swallows the deafening bass-note in a polished acoustic hollow, I do not hear the sound. I endure it as mechanical stress. The brutal, bone-rattling vibration of the engines travels through the young rock and slams into my crystalline lattice. 

And my lattice reacts. 

Quartz is piezoelectric. When subjected to violent, unrelenting physical pressure, its internal geometry compresses and releases, translating mechanical force into raw electrical charge.

As the heavy engines grind, shaking the earth, they squeeze my crystalline heart over and over again. With every microscopic compression of my facets, I bleed electricity. Tiny, dancing filaments of blue static arc across my wet, translucent edges. I am a living voltage, a piezoelectric synapse blooming in the wet, suffocating jungle.

Because my nature is born of light and electricity, my perception of time is the inverse of the Tender’s. The Tender lives in agonizing, patient slowness, measuring its life in geological seasons and millimeters. 

I live in the manic, invisible fractions of a nanosecond. 

To me, the deafening drone of the engine is not a continuous roar, but a sequence of distinct, monumental impacts—*strike, release, strike, release*—each one separated by an eternity of silence. I exist in the microscopic valleys between the soundwaves. If I could look at the Tender, I would perceive it as completely petrified, a frozen mass of pale flesh caught mid-air. I watch the atomized water from the steam vents crawl through the air slower than glaciers, individual droplets hovering like stationary glass planets in the humid void.

I am the fastest thing on the mountain. I am the high-frequency treble to the heavy, industrial bass.

Yet, despite our vastly different dimensions of time, the Tender and I find each other. The ecosystem demands it. 

The Tender requires stolen micro-currents to pulse through its stripped, copper nervous system—to spark the synapses of its vast, spatial awareness. It begins to sense the halo of blue voltage snapping around my crystalline edges. 

Over a span of years, the Tender extrudes a single, hair-thin filament of raw copper down through the dripping canopy. Unthinkably slowly, it winds this copper thread around my silica base, binding its nervous system directly to my facets. 

When the connection is made, we do not communicate in thoughts or words. We communicate in pure exchange. 

The engines roar, shivering the black rock. The rock squeezes my silica lattice. My lattice flares, firing a burst of raw, electrical static directly into the copper wire. The wire carries my spark up into the bulbous, pale flesh of the Tender, granting it the voltage it needs to contract its fibers, release its amber resin, and turn the heavy iron valves. 

I become the spark plug of the organic machine. 

I am the localized anomaly of speed and light on an eternally slow shelf. Above, the sky blackens into cobalt. Below, the white clouds sleep. But here, bathed in hot mist and wrapped in choking green, I shatter the sun and I ride the vibration. I am the singing crystal, locked in a perpetual, high-voltage ecstasy, firing in time with the brutal heartbeat of the roof of the world.
