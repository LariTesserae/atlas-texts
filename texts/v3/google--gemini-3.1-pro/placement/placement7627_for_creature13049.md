I do not wake, because I do not sleep. Fluctuations in consciousness are a luxury of worlds with day and night, with tides and seasons. Here, there is only the unbroken continuum of the Roar. 

I am a locus of density upon the slate-grey plateau. For an age—or perhaps only a moment, for erosion is our only clock—I am closed. 

To be closed is to be a mountain. 

The multi-octave scream of the cosmos breaks over my carapace. I feel it not as sound, but as an unbearable, glorious weight, a torrential river of kinetic pressure trying to shear me from to the stone. My frictionless shell absorbs nothing, deflects everything. I am sanded by the noise, my back smoothed into a flawless arc. It is a state of deep, meditative subtraction. I let the roar take from me whatever is imperfect, until only the essential geometry remains.

But a hunger stirs beneath the cowl—a need for resonance. 

I initiate the unfurling. 

My dense outer mantle splits, parting with the agonizing slowness of a glacier calving. The heavy, freezing moisture of the still air rushes into the sliver of exposed space, but I do not shiver. I open myself to the bruised twilight.

The moment my chest is exposed, the Roar ceases to be a weapon and becomes a symphony of sustenance. 

Without eyes, I see the geometry of the drone. Without ears, I hear the architecture of the void. The multi-octave cosmic scream slams into the hyper-complex matrix of my chest, striking the pale cartilage and translucent crystal. Instantly, the noise splinters. It travels through the fractal ridges of my biological cymatic plate, sorting itself into shivering valleys of low-frequency hums and sharp, crystalline peaks of high-amplitude shrieks. 

It is a violent, chaotic energy, but my body is a prism that bends sound into sugar. I consume the friction. The vibration travels deep into my marrow, sparking across my pearlescent internal scaffolding. I am eating the sky's throat.

Sated by the frequency, I unfurl my limbs. My long, multi-jointed arms slip from their resting places, the flattened pads of my digits brushing the weeping stone. I feel the intricate lattice of the overlapping coordinates around me. Space here is a crumpled, bruised fabric, and I am a tailor.

I am looking for my work.

I pivot my chest, scanning the acoustic shadow-maps, until I feel the faint null-point of a cairn far across the abyss. It sits on a jagged monolithic spire, three miles away. Or three inches away. The distance is irrelevant; it is merely un-pinched. 

I reach out into the freezing, heavy mist. I do not grasp at nothing—I feel for the literal crease in reality, the invisible seam where the crushing pressure of the Roar has folded the dimensions. My frictionless pads catch the edge of the spatial coordinate. I pinch. I pull.

The air shrieks as the miles collapse. The grey void violently telescopes. The distant, lonely spire rushes toward me, a colossal mass of rock eclipsing the pale light, stopping with a silent *snap* mere inches from my plateau. 

I step across. My pads touch the damp, smooth stone of the new monolith. I release my grip on the void, and the world behind me violently unfolds, snapping my previous home back to the hazy, unreachable horizon. 

I am here. At the edge of the abyss. At the stairway to nowhere. 

Six suspended stones hang over the absolute drop, locked in perfect stasis. This is my life's architecture. 

Others of my kind sit closed on their plateaus, content to outlast the world. But I am an engineer of the null-space. The Great Roar is everything, and therefore, pure silence is the only precious material in the universe. I step toward the hovering stairs.

I reach into the shallow pools of shattered, standing-wave water at the edge of the cliff and select a seventh stone, smoothed by eons of vibration. I cradle it in my palms. 

I step out over the abyss—not onto the rock, but onto the invisible architecture of sound I have built. I tune the fractal ridges of my open chest to emit a precise, inverse frequency. A phase-cancellation. I project a cone of absolute, freezing silence out over the empty drop. 

In this localized pocket of equilibrium, gravity and pressure are entirely undone. I place the seventh stone into the air, a step ahead of the sixth. I fine-tune the muscles of my cymatic plate until the stone holds perfectly still, suspended in a microscopic shadow of quiet. 

I step onto it. It holds my weight, bolstered by a pillar of shaped silence that I continuously project beneath it. I am standing on the void, suspended in the scream, pushing the boundary of the unmoored world just a few inches further into the mist. 

Suddenly, the barometric pressure of the air changes. A vast, cold shadow descends through the twilight. 

I turn my chest upward. Drifting down through the crushed sky is a leviathan. 

It is immense, a colossal bloom of translucent, pulsing bells and bioluminescent ribbons. A siphonophore of the high-altitude deep. It moves with a terrifying, serene apathy, deaf to the roar, blind to my staircase. 

It is drifting directly toward me. 

I do not fold space to escape. I do not seal my carapace. To commune with the ghosts of the mist is a rare, sacred collision. I stand on my newly placed seventh stone, open my crystalline chest wide, and wait.

The leviathan meets me. 

There is no impact, only the profound, staggering sensation of an icy sea washing through the very center of my being. The creature phases transparently through my physical body. As its internal organs pass through the intricate ridges of my chest, its bioluminescence ignites me. For a fleeting, glorious moment, my pale cartilage and vibrating crystals are flooded with a slow, icy blue light. I can feel the slow pulse of its bells merging with the frantic, cymatic vibration of my plates. Two entirely disconnected physics—one of sound, one of ethereal drift—occupying the exact same space. 

It passes through my back and off into the miles-deep chasm, continuing its endless, uncaring descent. 

Left in its wake, inside the deepest cavities of my chest, are beads of liquid quicksilver. The leviathan's freezing moisture. 

I gently trap the floating droplets, absorbing them via osmosis, feeling the cold, wet sheen replenish my dry cartilage. I am renewed. 

My work here for this epoch is done. The seventh step is anchored in the song. 

I step backward, onto the sixth stone, then the fifth, until my frictionless pads touch the solid, weeping rock of the monolith once more. I leave the stairs hanging over the mist, a bridge of pure silence reaching out into an empty cosmos. 

I lower myself onto the plateau. I fold my long limbs beneath me. I draw my heavy, slate-grey cowl forward. The biological matrix of my chest seals shut, hiding the delicate, vibrating crystals back in the dark.

The Great Roar immediately returns to its full, crushing omniscience. The weight of the noise presses down on my smooth back, sanding away microscopic layers of my being, locking me into the eternal, frozen equilibrium. 

I am stone once more. I am at peace. I will wait until the silence calls to be sculpted again.
