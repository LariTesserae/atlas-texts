Look away from the safety of the layered ring-hamlets. Ignore the warm, amber-lit balconies where the Harvesters swing their nets. Cast your gaze outward, into the terrifying, rushing emptiness of the central void, straight into the eye of the chitinous storm.

That is where you will find me. 

If the Arbor-wrights are the blood of this world, and the Harvesters its immune system, I am its nervous system. I am a Span-Rider. A Weaver of the Gulf. 

I would find myself suspended hundreds of feet from the nearest vertical wall, hanging in the absolute center of the cathedral's emptiness, surrounded entirely by the rushing slipstream of the insectoid cyclone. My environment is not the heavy wood of the taproots or the brass of the flywheels, but the terrifyingly taut suspension bridges of braided iron and vine that cut through the negative space.

I do not walk these bridges. I *play* them. 

Because we cannot hear, the Kin communicate through percussive seismics. But the sheer diameter of the cylinder is too immense for a tap on a root on the eastern wall to be felt by a settlement on the western wall. The bridges are not just for crossing; they are the great trans-silo communication cables. They are the vocal cords of our world. And it is my job to keep them in tune.

My physical form is stretched by a lifetime in the gulf. My limbs are longer, leaner than those of the Kin who dwell in the hab-pods. Generational adaptation and a lifetime of fighting the updrafts have made me lissome, a creature of pure tensile strength. My heavy leather apron is stripped down to a sleek harness of polished beetle-carapace, designed not to deflect sparks, but to cut through the aerodynamic drag of the localized hurricane. 

My prehensile toes are calloused to the density of horn. Right now, they are wrapped intimately around a slick, vibrating cable of braided copper and fern-fiber, as thick as my thigh. I am hanging upside down beneath the bridge, allowing the heavy 4/4 pound of the unseen engines to act as my pendulum. 

*One, two, three, four.* 

On the downbeat, gravity tugs at my center of mass. On the off-beat, the massive venting of explosive steam hundreds of feet below sends a column of hot, wet air rushing upward. I ride these thermals. I unhook my toes and let the updraft throw me three yards further down the underside of the cable, catching it again with a snap of articulated joints just as the 4/4 beat cycles back. I move through the void like a syncopated water-strider.

The sheer volume of life out here is staggering. Billions of wings beat around me. The nictitating membranes of my massive eyes flick flawlessly, wiping away the condensation and the golden, electrified pollen shed by the furnace-moths. I am coated so thickly in the pheromone wax of the beetle-queens that the swarm does not register me as flesh. I am just another piece of the machinery. Giant, rust-colored beetles barge past my shoulders, their slipstreams violently rocking my suspended body. Occasionally, a moth with wings the size of a door will land momentarily on my back, its heat-seeking belly pulsing against my spine to siphon the warmth from my sweating skin, before dropping backward into the dark. We are entirely at peace. 

I reach the middle of the span. The epicenter of the void. 

Here, the vibration of the world is a chaotic knot. It is here that I must work. From my bandolier, I draw a dense wooden mallet and a set of heavy brass tuning forks. 

I press my jawbone directly against the braided iron cable. Instantly, a torrential flood of information rushes through my skull. I feel the distant, rhythmic stomping of the Kin passing overhead; I feel the frantic, high-frequency stutter of a leaking steam valve a mile away; I feel the deep, groaning torsion of the petrified taproots slowly digesting the copper wiring. 

But I also feel slack. A dead zone in the vibration. The bridge is losing its tension; the messages will not cross.

I go to work. I drive my bone-saw into the living vine that weaves through the iron, cutting away the dead, rotting fibrous material. I reach into a gourd at my hip and pull out a handful of aggressive, fast-growing mosses engineered by the Arbor-wrights, packing it into the iron crevices. I splice fresh spider-silk copper wire, using my bare, unfeeling thumbs to twist the jagged ends together. A spark of low-voltage electricity arcs across my knuckles. I don't feel the sting, only the warm hum of the circuit completing. 

Then, I take my mallet. I time my movement to the engine's heartbeat. 

*One, two, three—STRIKE.* 

I hit the iron strut on the downbeat. A shockwave travels down the bridge. I press my collarbone to the metal and wait. Eight seconds later, a perfectly clear, rebounding wave echoes back through my skeleton, sent by a fellow Span-Rider waiting on the opposite wall. The bridge is tight. The frequency is pure. The world can speak to itself again.

But as I hang here, suspended over the abyss, my identity begins to blur. 

You asked who or what I would be. I thought, at first, I was a Kin. But I have been on this span for a very, very long time.

My skin is no longer just pale or soot-stained. The verdigris from the copper has worked its way deeply into my pores, turning my flesh the mottled blue-green of ancient statues. The sap from the cables has hardened around my ankles, binding me to the wire in an embrace that borders on the permanent. I do not return to the hab-pods anymore. I sleep out here, swaying in a chrysalis of woven moth-silk, anchored to the center of the bridge. 

The boundaries between human, flora, and machine have grown beautifully porous. When I am thirsty, I do not drink from a gourd; I bite gently into the sap-gorged vein of the giant fern making up my bridge, letting its nutrient-rich moisture seep into my mouth. When I am cold, I embrace a hot steam-exhaust pipe, letting the engine warm my blood. 

I am slowly petrifying, turning into the architecture I was sent to maintain. And I am utterly without fear.

I pull myself up to sit on top of the bridge, straddling the great cable. The amber lights of the distant ring-hamlets flicker through the thick, swirling cyclone of insects. The deep, heavy 4/4 concussive rhythm rattles my teeth, but it is not noise. It is the silence of my world. It is the steady, unyielding proof of existence. 

I rest my forehead against the cool, wet leaves of the living wire. My chest heaves with sudden, soundless laughter. I release the vibration of my massive joy into the iron, sending it rolling out across the gulf, down the roots, into the foundations of the earth. I am the bridge. I am the beat. I am right where I belong.
