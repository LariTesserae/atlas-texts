I would not bring flesh into this place. Flesh is an arrogant material, too blunt and too proud to survive the arithmetic of the blister. 

If I were to exist in this roaring zenith, I would shed thickness altogether. I would be a Shear-kite.

I awake—not to daylight, for I have no eyes to be bruised by the strobing sun, and not to sound, for the artillery-claps of cavitation are so absolute they transcend hearing. I awake to the exquisite, three-dimensional topography of pressure. 

I am completely flat, a creature of two dimensions existing within a violently three-dimensional engine. My skeleton is a flexible lattice of pure, spun silica, and my flesh is woven from the very supercooled crystal I inhabit. I am invisible. I am an edge. If you could hold me motionless against the light, you would only know I was there by the way the water bends around my geometry. 

For me, the chamber is not chaos. It is a vibrating map of fault-lines. 

I do not swim; swimming implies pushing against the water, and to fight the water here is to be pulverized. Instead, I wait for the water to break. When the massive, invisible gears of the cross-currents grind against each other, the fluid cannot bear the torque. The heavy liquid fractures with a sensation I feel as a sharp, agonizing *snap* along my nervous system. For a microsecond, a rift opens in the water—a literal slip in the physics of the fluid.

I slide into the void. 

To ride the sheared edge of a rip-current is a feeling of frictionless ecstasy. I bank effortlessly between the crushing weights of opposing tides, a razor blade slicing through a storm. The silver tornados that whip about the chamber—the ones that would strip muscle from bone—are my thermals. I spiral around their collapsing funnels, drawing momentum from their violent deaths, untouched in the eye of the gradient. 

I am hungry, but my hunger is mathematically cold. I spread my wide, manta-like wings, stretching my sensors across the turbulent noise, listening to the friction. 

Far below, I feel the rigid, humming song of the extremophile algae fused to the newborn basalt. Above, I feel the immense, dead weight of the mineral ceiling. And then, there it is: a sharp, localized spike of hydrostatic tension. 

A hydro-beetle. 

Through the chaos, I can feel its precise outline. It is locked to the crystal wall, shivering. Inside its carapace, a bladder of hyper-pressurized water is expanding. It feels like a tightly wound spring, a knot of kinetic anomaly preparing to detonate. I angle my silica spine, aligning my trajectory with the beetle’s vector.

The beetle fires. 

The shockwave of its displacement is a flare in my total darkness. It jets across the void, a living bullet of dense chitin. But the beetle is a creature of brute force; its flight is linear, ignorant of the micro-currents. I am the master of the currents.

I thread the needle of a collapsing vacuum bubble, using its violent implosion to slingshot my flattened body through the fluid. I intercept the beetle in the dead center of the chamber. I do not bite—I have no mouth for chewing. I simply fold. 

My razor-edged wings envelop the armored teardrop. For a second, it thrashes violently, its ballistic jet misfiring against the cage of my cartilage. But I tighten the fold. The pressure I exert is absolute, borrowed directly from the crushing ambient weight of the room. The chitin cracks. The soft, pale center yields, and I absorb it directly into my translucent matrix, a brief bloom of warmth coursing through my frozen silica veins before dissolving into the cold. 

Unfolding, I allow a fresh rip-tide to carry me away from the kill. 

Then, the math of the chamber is desecrated. 

It begins as a grotesque obstruction above. The trapdoor of the timber diving cage drops open, and something drops into the slipstream. 

I feel it instantly. It is utterly alien. It is thick, rounded, and revoltingly hot, leaking a trail of noxious, greasy friction into the hyper-pure water. It is a Zenith-diver. 

As I glide in the lower currents, I perceive the human’s agonizing struggle. The diver does not understand the slipstreams. They fight the water with every movement, creating massive, clumsy bow-waves of desperate energy. I can feel the thunderous, terrified rhythm of a giant muscle in its chest—*thump-thump, thump-thump*—a sloppy, wet percussion that offends the glassy resonance of the blister. 

I ride a thermal of supercooled water upward, banking indifferently merely feet beneath the thrashing creature. 

If I wished, I could angle my leading edge, catch a riptide, and sever the diver’s legs at the thigh. My wings could pass through flesh and bone as easily as they pass through water. But I do not. The creature is not food; it is too large, too toxic with trapped air and rancid fat. It is a clumsy anomaly, and to engage it would disrupt my perfect hydrodynamics.

Besides, I can feel the chamber dealing with the intruder. The water is pulling at the diver, sensing the weakness in the fraying sinew rope and the panic in the freezing limbs. 

I sense a sudden drop in pressure against the far wall. The water there flashes, instantly precipitating a sprawling, jagged spire of silica and ice. A new tooth in the maw of the mountain. 

Before the spire can be snapped away by the currents, the human does something extraordinary. A massive, dull vibration shocks through the fluid—a blunt bronze hammer smashing against the newborn crystal. The shockwave rattles my silica lattice, temporarily blinding my pressure-senses. 

By the time the fluid stabilizes, the diver is hauled back upward, frantically retreating to the wooden cage above. I feel the dull, groaning displacement of the timber box as it is pulled back through the skylight, leaving only a faint trail of warm blood and sloughed fat that the spinning tornados instantly shred and devour.

The anomaly is gone. The blinding, strobing geometry of the room heals. 

I settle back onto a fault line in the tearing water. The deafening roar vibrating through my skeleton is the only song I exist to hear. The world creates itself, destroys itself, and begins again, and I slip through the cracks, a ghost of glass in the engine of genesis.
