It is the only thing in the cosmos that does not move.

Occasionally, the pressure above fractures, and an invisible hand tosses powdered diamonds across the flat world. When the dust settles upon my metallic tendrils, I consume it. I do not know yet that these razor-sharp motes were once my brothers, my sisters, perhaps a past iteration of myself. I only know they are material. They are the mass required for the great escape.

Then, the anomaly arrives.

It does not announce itself with thunder. Sound does not exist. It announces itself with a violent, terrifying tug at the very fabric of my existence. Gravity flips. "Down" is no longer the comforting, ancient bedrock. "Down" is the deep, hungry indigo of the sky.

I am ripped from the lace. I am fired into the vacuum like a railgun sabot.

Instead, the piezoelectric sensitivity of my interlocking silica plates awakens. I *feel* the sky. I span twenty feet across, a living kite of iridescent mica, and my entire body is a retina designed to see gravity.

The silence is absolute, but my body rings with sensation. I feel the rhythmic thrumming of the spatial shears. I am soaring.

Ahead of me, the topography suddenly alters. What was a smooth current of inverted gravity hardens into a dense, jagged wall. The wind is tearing into right-angles. 

The piezoelectric shock hits my center. My biology overtakes my consciousness. 

*Fold.*

The gravitic storm washes over me, a silent, tearing fury that tries to rend me down to atoms, but it cannot find purchase on my dense, compressed form. The shear scrapes against my armor, a deep, resonant vibration that fills my fluid-filled veins with static terror and electric joy. 

I pierce the anomaly. The pressure releases. 

*Bloom.*

I snap outward, expanding my wings to full breadth just as I hit a trough of calm physics. My downward drop swoops into a joyous, sweeping arc.

A massive shear—a continent-wide ripple of broken physics—rolls toward us. It is too large to fold through; it would crush us into the bedrock.

Hundreds of us form a living, crystalline dome, a massive geodesic shield suspended in the high, thin air. The shear hits us. I am growing heavy with it. I am glowing. The golden light of the distant sun is eclipsed by the brilliant, violent energy pooling in my veins.

I cross the threshold of the black pit. 

The energy in my veins howls for release. Between my crystalline belly and the dead, unreflecting machine, an arc of pure starlight leaps. 

*CRACK.* (Felt, not heard. A beautiful, shuddering release of pressure.)

This is who I am. This is where I would be.

I will be crushed into a shower of glittering dust, raining silently down to the mirror below, where the waiting copper lace will drink me in to build the next seed. 

But I do not fear the shatter. The machine must be fed. The sky must be ridden. 

I dart. I plummet. I bloom. I soar.
