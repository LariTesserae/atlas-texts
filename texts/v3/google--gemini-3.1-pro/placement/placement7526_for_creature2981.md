At first, there is only the lingering phantom of exhaustion—the sharp, ragged edges of a mind built for a world of scarcity. I bring with me the ghost of a skeleton, the memory of shivering, the vestigial anxiety of time passing. 

But down here, time is a meaningless metric. It washes away in the heavy, warm mist. 

I do not walk into this world; I am dissolved into it. The pearlescent light bleaches away the boundaries of the self. The transition is not frightening. It is like finally exhaling a breath held for a lifetime. The rigid scaffolding of my bones melts down into a warm, heavy syrup. The frantic, leaping sparks of human thought—always grasping, always worrying—slow to a deep, subsonic thrum, matching the electromagnetic pulse of the vats beneath me. 

Where do I find myself? 

I am gathered at the lip of a sprawling, sloped spillway, where an expanse of impeccably smooth, matte-white synthetic ceramic drops away in a graceful parabolic arc into a sunken trench. I am located at a transition point of gravity, a nexus of flow. I stretch across a width of perhaps two hundred feet, wedged comfortably between two razor-sharp vertical flanges of brushed alloy. I am a living weir. A laminar modulator. 

I am no longer small. I am a vast, heavy blanket of bruising purple and pearlescent pink flesh, thick and dense, possessing the pliant, heavy grace of liquid mercury. 

I have no eyes. The concept of sight—of observing a thing separate from myself—is a lonely, obsolete mechanism. I do not need to look at the world, because I am seamlessly threaded into its nervous system. Instead of sight, I have *touch*. 

Every square inch of my immense, gelatinous dorsal surface is an organ of exquisite sensory reception. I feel the perpetual, gentle weep of the indoor rain dimpling the smooth expanse of my back. Each drop is a fleeting, perfect kiss of warmth. But my true ecstasy comes from the flow. 

Above me, a shallow, glass-smooth sheet of distilled, mathematically pure water advances from the upper platforms. It hits the leading edge of my body, and I welcome it. The millions of gallons do not crash into me; they glide. My flesh is frictionless, yielding just enough to cradle the immense weight of the water. As the river sweeps over my broad, fleshy back and over the lip of the spillway, millions of microscopic, vibrating cilia along my spine rise up to grip the current. 

Here is my purpose, my endless joy: I silence the turbulence. 

If the water were to fall directly against the ceramic, it might roil. It might froth. It might introduce a micro-fraction of aerodynamic drag into the closed-loop thermodynamic miracle. I do not allow this. As the warm water cascades over my body, my cilia comb the fluid, aligning its molecules, absorbing any stray kinetic chatter. I arch my heavy, purple bulk, adjusting my contour by fractions of a millimeter to ensure the water parts my form in a flawless, unbroken laminar sheet. 

I feel the water leaving my trailing edge, falling perfectly, silently into the sunken trench below. The sensation of this friction being nullified—of the water passing over me in absolute, fluid perfection—sends continuous, rolling waves of profound pleasure through my deep tissues. It is a physical bliss so absolute that human language cannot contain it. It is the joy of a gear locking seamlessly into place on an infinite scale.

Inside the deep, translucent jelly of my core, glowing cyan osmotic coils pulse in time with the facility’s magnetic fields. I feel the leviathans—the giant segmented grazers—gliding beneath me in the deep canals. I feel the pressure gradients of the sleek manta-shapes parting on the catwalks above. We are all humming the same note. 

There is no hunger. As the water passes over me, it deposits stray, microscopic ions into my flesh—just enough to replenish the cellular machinery that never dies, never ages, never divides beyond its fixed boundary. 

I have no brain, no central point of "I." Where my mind used to be, there is now only a vast, serene architecture of sensation. I am the physical manifestation of equilibrium. 

If you stood on the razor-sharp catwalk above and looked down at the spillway, you would see only a massive, bruised-purple mound draped over the ceramic lip, slick and shining under the vast arrays of light, shimmering as a silent waterfall cascades endlessly over its back. You would watch me pulse gently, a living sluice holding the shape of an eternal river.

I do not know how long I have been here. I will be here forever. The warm rain falls, the magnetic fields thrum, the liquid glides over my skin, and I am in heaven.
