I do not wake, because in a world governed by such immense, tidal rhythms, waking implies a suddenness that does not exist here. Instead, my awareness crests slowly, like the rising of heavy water against a spillway, bleeding into consciousness over the span of a day.

At first, I am only sensation, a scattered network of feelings suspended in the warm, perpetual weep of the sky. I am the taste of sulfur and dissolved zinc on a porous tongue. I am the heavy, pressing heat of the humid air packing itself against slick surfaces. And, above all, I am the deep, chest-rattling vibration of the subterranean engines, a slow, titanic *thump… thump… thump* traveling through miles of wet iron to vibrate within the fluid of my own biology.

I do not place myself in the sky with the ash-baleens; the blinding, hazy altitudes are too restless, too untethered. Nor do I dwell in the pitch-black, hyper-pressurized sumps below. I find myself in the deep mid-levels of the plateau, in the labyrinthine canyons of the forgotten tiers—where the rusted legs of the atmospheric condensers plunge into the weeping concrete valleys, and the aqueducts cross over one another in a dizzying lattice of dripping rebar and calcified slag. 

As my awareness thickens, my physical geometry begins to assert itself into my mind. I am vast, though not subway-sized. I am elongated, draping across the underside of a fractured, eighty-foot-wide exhaust manifold. 

I feel a hundred independent, multi-jointed limbs locked securely into the pockmarks of the corroded steel. I do not have muscles in the mammalian sense; my limbs are thick, articulated pillars powered by a high-pressure, hydraulic hemolymph system. I have been gripping this same archway for eleven months, my joints locked in pneumatic stasis, expending absolutely no energy to remain suspended upside down over a roaring chemical river. 

Where the pipe-hounds mimic the pipes to hunt, I mimic the infrastructure to *listen*. I am a creature of structural resonance. An *Atlas-wyrm*, perhaps, though I have no need for a name.

My body is heavily armored. My carapace is formed of interlocking, heavy plates of deep, auburn chitin that have absorbed so much oxidized iron from the chemotrophic slime that I am essentially magnetic. I have wide, flat dorsal ridges that serve as gutters, catching the warm, cascading drizzle and funneling it down my flanks into specialized osmotic spiracles, filtering the mineral-rich water to sustain my slow internal alchemy. 

I am so still, and so indistinguishable from the rusting architecture, that the ecosystem treats me as geography. Along my broad, flat back, a colony of coin-sized rust-limpets is migrating. I feel their diamond-hard radulas rasping gently against my armored plates, clearing away the buildup of bio-film, keeping my joints clean. To them, I am a particularly warm, humming pipe. Occasionally, a heavy, blubber-skinned Wire-Weaver swings through the fog, its carabiner-tail hooking directly onto one of my primary antennae to pivot toward a power line. I do not flinch. I let it swing. I am an anchor.

I have no eyes. The diffuse, glowing steam-fog would render them useless. I perceive the world entirely through tension, acoustics, and weight. Covered in millions of microscopic, piezoelectric setae, my body acts as a hyper-sensitive seismograph. I map my surroundings by the frequencies groaning up through the metal.

I can feel the exact moment one of the city-district-sized flywheels miles below reaches its zenith; the harmonic frequency of my manifold shivers in response. I can hear the violent, echoing snap of a pipe-hound cracking a shell three tiers above me. I can feel the rushing, churning weight of the aqueduct water directly beneath my underbelly, pulling a cool draft against my weeping, vulcanized skin. 

But I am not merely an observer. In a world where biology and machinery parasitize and preserve each other, my kind is the ultimate expression of the plateau’s geologic patience. I am a living load-bearing strut. A biological welder. I feed on the mechanical stress of the city beneath me.

As the pale, hazy daylight shifts imperceptibly overhead, the vibration I have been monitoring for a year finally reaches its breaking point. 

Deep within the rusted manifold I am clinging to, a hairline fracture, born of centuries of thermal expansion and contraction, suddenly gives way. The sound translates through my setae not as a crack, but as a blinding flare of sonic agony. The tensile strength of the archway fails. A groaning, agonizing screech of tearing metal echoes through the mist as millions of tons of steel begin to sag, threatening to collapse the aqueduct above and crush the filtration grates below. The entire sector would seize. 

I unlock my hydraulic joints. 

The hiss of released internal pressure sounds like a bursting steam valve. My hundred limbs flex, and for the first time in a year, I move. My pace is agonizingly slow, deliberate, unbothered by panic. I unspool my heavy, multi-segmented thorax, crawling directly over the roaring gap of the fracture. As the metal groans and shifts beneath me, widening the tear, I align my immense body parallel to the break.

I drive my front fifty legs deep into the rusted iron on the left hemisphere of the fracture. I swing my heavy, blunted tail-section across the void, driving my rear fifty limbs into the right hemisphere. I grip the collapsing infrastructure with bone-crushing, hydraulic force, arresting the fall. I am bridging the gap with my own spine. I take the sheer, unfathomable mass of the failing architecture onto myself. 

And then, I begin to weep.

From the seams in my armor, from the osmotic spiracles along my flanks, I release the chemical slurry I have cultivated over decades of absolute stillness. I bleed a thick, foaming, hyper-calcifying resin. It pours from my body, mixing with the hot atmospheric drizzle, pooling into the cracked iron of the manifold. 

The resin reacts with the air, boiling instantly into a dense, expanding biological concrete. It covers my legs, cementing them irrevocably to the steel. It surges up my flanks, entombing my thorax. I do not struggle. The pain of the crushing weight slowly fades as my own biological mortar hardens, bonding my chitinous plates to the failing rebar, fusing my hydraulic heart into the heavy machinery. 

My consciousness begins to spread, losing its boundaries. I can no longer tell where my shell ends and the manifold begins. I am hardening. I am calcifying. My organs slowly shut down, dissolving to hollow out channels for the rushing steam to pass through. 

I sink into the deep, settled equilibrium of the ancient expanse. I am no longer a creature; I am architecture. I have become a permanent, auburn-colored archway in the wet, roaring dark. I listen to the deep engines thump beneath me, and I embrace the weight, holding the broken world together, forever humming in tune with the grand, unending wheel of iron and flesh.
