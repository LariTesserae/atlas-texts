I am not the heavy, pale stone, nor am I the blinding heat. I am the desperate, fleeting marriage between the two. 

I open my existence inside the throat of an apocalypse. The crust of the world shatters, and from the deep, soundless vacuum, a fountain of cherry-red blood-rock surges upward. As the radiant heat washes over a pile of pale, dead debris, the ash trapped in its crevices remembers how to live. 

I awaken in a brilliant, violent burst of amber. 

The silica-ferns lace together in milliseconds, weaving a glowing, fractal nervous system through fragmented slabs of granite. I drag the dead stones around me, pulling them into a cohesive lattice. *Snap.* I am standing. *Ping.* I am looking at the unblinking, heavy stars. 

I do not have lungs to scream my arrival, nor do I have eyes to see. I perceive the world through the gradient of temperature and the vibration of the earth. Above me is the void—a crushing, suffocating blanket of absolute zero that wants to strip me of life. Below me is the vermillion wound of the planet, oozing raw thermal radiation and the sweet, electric tang of fresh sulfur. 

I throw myself into the molten caldera. I wade knee-deep into the liquid fire, plunging my jagged, fractured appendages into the raw magma. The heat is a narcotic. It floods my artificial veins, turning my rust-colored tendrils blindingly bright. The quartz embedded in my granite bones whines, expanding with piezoelectric fury. I am fully charged. I am alive. 

There are others in the fire with me. We do not possess a collective mind, but we share a singular, inescapable geometry of purpose. We feel the chaotic thrashing of the black, glassy ocean around us. We feel the planet trying to tear itself to ribbons. And we hate it. We long for the stillness of the void above, but we require the violence of the world below just to move. It is our curse. 

A deep, sub-aural thrum shudders through my core. A mile away, the crust is beginning to buckle. The warning signs are written in the tension of the basalt, crying out in a frequency only our stone bones can hear. 

It is time to build. 

The heat in my immediate fissure is already crusting over, turning first to dark maroon, then to unyielding black. I pull myself from the cooling pool. My movement is not fluid. I am an animation composed of missing frames. *Clack.* I am at the edge of the pit. *Clack.* I am twenty yards away, my pale granite body grating against itself as I cross the razor-sharp sea of newly forged obsidian. 

I move toward the site of the tectonic warning. Along the way, I pass a pale monument. It is one of my kin, frozen upright in a defiant arch. As I draw near, I see a massive block of granite resting at its base. It is the severed torso of an ancestor, its amber veins long since turned to translucent, lifeless ash. 

I stoop. The quartz in my arms expands, contracting violently as the thermal energy courses through my silica nerves. *Grind.* I hoist the heavy, dead stone in my hands. The weight of it is immense, but I am flush with the fire of the earth.

I run. *Snap. Snap. Snap.* I teleport across the black plains in jagged, staccato blurs, a ghost of golden light and white rock carrying the dead. Ash and dark pumice foam from a nearby vent, catching the thermal updrafts and snowing upward into the suffocating stars. I weave through the rising black stones, racing against my own metabolism. 

As I run, the cold of the vacuum eats at me. The amber light webbing my chest begins to dim, shifting to a dull, bruised orange. The movement of my limbs grows heavier. The space between my piezoelectric snaps lengthens. The world is trying to freeze me. 

I reach the fault line just as the black rock begins to heave. 

Another of my kin is already there, a towering tripod of pale stone glowing faintly with rust-colored light. They have formed the base of a new anchor. They are waiting for the keystone. 

I step onto their shoulders. The sub-aural thrum of the earth splitting beneath us shakes the marrow of my granite. The sea of black glass fractures, revealing the terrifying, silent glow of a new chasm opening beneath our feet. Trembling with the final dregs of my thermal exertion, I heave the corpse of my ancestor upward, locking it into the apex of the structure. We are a heavy, crude archway, spanning the width of the new fissure, pressing down with hundreds of tons of pale stone, desperate to hold the breaking world together.

For a moment, it holds. 

But my fire is spent. 

The terrible cold of the breathless void floods my internal lattice. I look down at my chest. The orange veins recede to a sickly, translucent white. The delicate silica-ferns that make me *me* begin to brittle and flake, turning to dry ash inside the joints of the granite.

I do not panic. I do not fear the end. This is the only way a builder can truly become part of the foundation.

I adjust my stance atop the arch, widening my stance to distribute my weight over the fault line. I brace my jagged arms against the sides of the ancestor I just placed. I lock the quartz in my legs. I tighten my grip on the world. 

The last spark of heat leaves my body, stolen by the unblinking stars. The lattice crumbles to dust. 

I am frozen. 

I am no longer the desperate, thrashing life caught between the fire and the void. I am still. I am heavy. I am a monument under a suffocating canopy of night, waiting for the earth to break beneath me, and for the next flash of amber to come and bear my weight.
