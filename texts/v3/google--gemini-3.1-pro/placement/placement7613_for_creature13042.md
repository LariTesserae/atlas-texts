It begins not as a waking, but as a condensation. 

For a long, measureless epoch, I am entirely unbounded, drifting as a weightless particle in the bruised and freezing dark. I am part of the eternal exhalation of the crypt, swirling in the heavy, aerosolized ghost-fog. I taste of crushed stone and old moss. I am drawn upward by the drafting currents of cold air, catching for a decade on a drooping frond of ghost-fern, then weeping downward as a heavy, singular teardrop sliding along a perfectly smooth, scalloped rib of black basalt.

But as I pool, I begin to feel the edges of a self. 

I sink into a deep, terraced basin, and the basin is not stone. It is soft. It is dense, fibrous, and miraculously warm beneath its freezing, sodden surface. As I saturate this living sponge, I realize I am not the water. I am the thing drinking it. 

I coalesce. I pull my awareness inward, gathering myself from the ambient damp of the cavern into a finite, heavy center. I possess a heartbeat, though it is so deeply spaced, so profoundly resonant, that it feels less like a biological rhythm and more like the shifting of tectonic plates beneath the mountain. *Thump.* The sound is entirely internal. I wait. A hundred drops of water fall from the ceiling in the interim. *Thump.* 

I open my eyes.

The world does not rush in. It seeps softly into the twin obsidian spheres of my vision. I have no pupils to dilate, no irises to focus; my eyes are absolute voids, ancient light-traps engineered by the dark. Through them, the crypt is not black, but a sprawling, three-dimensional tapestry of glowing cyan threads, pulsing with the faint, bleeding neon of the fungal veins and the luminous trails of unseen slugs. I see the breath of the cavern as a glowing aura. 

I am resting in a concave hollow of the wall, perfectly molded to the sprawling, barrel-chested weight of my body. My pelt is heavy, trailing down over the stone. I can feel the microscopic root systems of the liverworts anchoring into the outer layers of my wool, drinking the moisture I have collected. A blind, wet amphibian the size of my head is asleep on my shoulder. I do not disturb it. We are not separate entities; we are simply different densities of the same wet earth. 

A compulsion, slow and deeply buried in my marrow, urges me to harvest. 

I must move. The decision takes an hour to fully articulate within my freezing blood. When I finally stir, it is with the viscosity of hardening tree resin. I uncurl an arm from beneath my dripping bulk. 

I feel the majestic, terrifying length of my fingers as they spool outward. Six joints, seven joints—unfolding like pale, velvet arachnids in the gloom. I press the fleshy, ridged pads of my fingertips against the slick basalt. I do not grip the stone; I kiss it. My pads expel the water beneath them, creating a gentle, flawless vacuum. The sensation of the rock is overwhelming in its clarity—I can read the microscopic history of the water's friction etched into the basilica of the cavern.

I pull my heavy bulk forward. The amphibian on my shoulder slips off, landing with a soft, wet thud in the moss below. It does not wake. 

I map my way along the scalloped wall, feeling the topography that my ancestors have memorized for millions of years. Every hollow is familiar. Every protruding shelf of fungus is where it has always been. I reach a specific vertical crease in the basalt, a place where the water flows slightly heavier, washing over the stone in a continuous sheet. 

I slip my impossibly long, spindly index and middle fingers into the dark slice in the rock. 

Down, down, tracing the familiar curve of the crevice, until my pads brush against something that is not wet stone. It is jagged, yet impossibly smooth in its center. I pinch it and draw it out.

The obsidian blade glows faintly in the ambient cyan light. As I hold it, a strange communion occurs. My fingers rest exactly in the shallow grooves worn into the black glass. These grooves were not knapped; they were dissolved. They are the sum total of ten million hours of gentle, oily pressure applied by a thousand generations of hands identical to mine. As I hold the tool, the boundary between my flesh and the glass disappears. I am holding the hands of my mothers, and their mothers, back into the deep time before the roots sealed the sky. I inherit their absolute, terrifying serenity.

With an agonizing, beautiful precision, I slide the glass along a ruffled shelf of glowing ghost-fern. I apply the lever of my thumb, pressing laterally, ensuring the blade shears only the upper stratum of the cyan flesh. The stalk gives way. I bring the glowing filament to my hidden mouth, folding it past my wet wool, and begin the vast, circular grinding of my jaws. The fungus is cold, tasting of ancient soil and raw elemental life. It blooms in my dark stomach like a swallowed star. 

Time does not pass here; it merely pools. 

I graze. I sleep. I let the beetles swarm over the pale skin of my hands, feeling their tiny mandibles delicately clipping away the dead skin around my knuckles. Their touch is a quiet chorus of tiny vibrations, a lullaby of decay and renewal. 

But as I stand over a shallow pool, watching a thick wick of saturated moss slowly siphon water down to a lower terrace, something in the rhythm of my glacial heartbeat stutters. 

*Thump.* 

I wait. The water ticks. A moth flutters its velvet wings, looking like a trembling silver leaf. 

...

*Thump.* 

The interval is longer. The heat in my center, always a faint ember, begins to cool. I feel the glacial viscosity of my blood thickening, turning from syrup to ice, from ice to stone. The cycle is complete. I have consumed the exact measure of the crypt I was allotted, and now the equation demands balance. 

There is no fear. The obsidian eyes cannot register terror, for to fear death would require a belief that I am separate from the crypt. I am not separate. I am just a wave returning to the ocean. 

With movements so slow they might be mistaken for the drifting of the fog, I turn back to the vertical crease in the wet basalt. Holding the obsidian flake with my long, exquisite fingers, I slide it back into the dark. I press it gently into its resting place, ensuring the micro-polished edge is facing inward, safe from the damp. I leave it for the next pair of hands, which are already growing on the chest of a mother sleeping three terraces below me. 

My hands empty, I climb upward. 

I reach a high, concave hollow, deeply carpeted with spongy, digesting moss. I turn and press my heavy, wool-draped back against the water-worn stone, slotting perfectly into the architecture. I let my impossibly long fingers fold over my chest, interlacing the multi-jointed digits into a complex, pale knot. 

I exhale. A thick cloud of white mist leaves the parting of my wool, joining the heavy fog suspended over the chamber. I do not draw another breath in. 

I watch the glowing cyan tapestry of the cavern through my unblinking black eyes. Slowly, the sensation of my extremities begins to vanish. I feel the microscopic spores in my fleece rejoicing, accelerating their unspooling roots as my body temperature matches the ambient chill of the room. The fungi begin to thread into my flesh, painlessly binding me to the stone. The moss rises up to swallow my legs. 

My vision does not go black. Instead, my consciousness slowly unspools. 

I stop being the beast in the alcove. I become the moss creeping over its chest. I become the microscopic roots dissolving the calcium in its bones. I become the slow, rhythmic grinding of the jaws of a giant wood-louse. 

And then, as the last anchor of solitary thought dissolves entirely, I am unbound once more. 

I am the cold. I am the bruised dark. I am the silence between the falling drops of water. I am the ancient, breathing stone, holding my beloved, woolly children in my endless, unyielding embrace, keeping them perfectly safe in the dark, forever.
