I do not arrive with a shape. In a place violently purged of time, to possess a boundary is to possess a history, a narrative of edges where one thing ends and another begins. And so, to survive this entrance, I must begin as a diffusion. 

At first, I find myself as a sensation woven into the sweltering, golden noon. I am a sprawling, incorporeal awareness suspended in the crushing, frictionless weight of the heat. I taste the distilled, mineral purity of the air. I smell the overwhelming, raw crush of green sap. I press invisibly against the vast, pale-yellow cotyledons that roll continuously toward the horizon, sliding over millions of miles of unblemished, cathedral-sized flora. Because the air here is entirely motionless—devoid of the thermal gradients that might grant me the grace of being a wind—I am forced to mimic the utter stillness of the cavern. I hold my breath. I spread myself thin across the infinite, glossy epidermis of the hothouse.

But a consciousness cannot remain everywhere. The very nature of an observing mind is to condense, to seek a focal point. I cannot exist in the 'nowhere' of the absolute. I need an anchor.

Drawn by the faint, agonizingly slow magnetic pull of gravity—the only true force permitted to act in this sealed womb—my awareness begins to coalesce. I gather locally. I pull myself from the vast hollows, from the miles of breathless, radiant air, sliding up the slick, cathedral-high spine of a single, colossal sprout. I am migrating toward the underside of a leaf the size of a mountain. 

Here, in the blinding, shadowless luminescence, I begin to bead. 

I am becoming the moisture. 

I pull the heavy humidity from the air, dragging the vapor together molecule by molecule. It feels like an eternity of labor just to establish a surface tension. I grow dense. I grow heavy. I am a swelling fist of pure water, hanging inverted beneath the turgid, waxy veil of the plant.

Now, I have a shape. I am a perfect, trembling globe. I am a liquid lens.

Suspended in the eternal zenith, I look down through the swollen belly of my own curvature. The world below appears inverted and strangely magnified. Straight beneath me, resting in the high crooks of the titanic vines, is a ring of pale stones. And sitting flawlessly centered within it is a pilgrim.

From my vantage point, the mystic is a smooth, luminous ridge of landscape, possessing the same frosted-glass complexion as the glowing rock sky. Through my watery refraction, I watch the sluggish, pale-green rivers of their veins pulsing to a geological hum. Because they do not blink, and because I do not shift, we exist in a silent, perfectly locked communion. Their eyes—wide, pale, and coated in their own convex shells of stationary water—stare straight up. I realize that I am looking into a mirror. The drop over their eye is staring at the drop that I am. We are both water, holding our breath, resisting the pull of the deep. 

If this were all there was to this world—endless infancy, endless suspension—I would hang here forever. I would assimilate perfectly into the stasis, becoming just another bead of sweat on the brow of eternity. 

But my downward gaze shifts slightly, breaking the symmetry. Nestled in the smooth, infant-like hands of the ascetic is a rough, darkly stained wooden bowl. 

Amid the glossy, unblemished perfection of the cavern, the bowl is an atrocity of texture. It is splintered. It is scarred. It is darkened by oils, cracked by age, and worn by the friction of human thumbs. It screams of a towering tree that surrendered to the violence of seasons, a tree that bled sap, turned brittle, and allowed itself the dignity of death. It is heavily, undeniably soaked in *Time*.

I feel an unbearable kinship with that dead piece of wood. In this entire sweltering heaven of beginnings, that bowl and I are the only things capable of participating in a narrative. The bowl has a past. And I—hanging heavy, gathering mass, stretching my surface tension to its absolute breaking point—have a future.

I realize what I must become in this world. I am not the flora. I am not the monk.

I am an Event. 

I allow myself to grow too heavy. I stop fighting the quiet tug of the deep floor. The skin of my surface tension groans, elongates, and finally, with a microscopic tearing sensation, it snaps.

I fall.

The descent takes what feels like a century. I plunge through the heavy, golden air, a fleeting streak of pure kinetic energy slicing through a biome that has forgotten how to move. The solid, incandescent sky rockets away above me. The vast, fleshy leaves rush past my periphery. I am a brief, violently beautiful spark of physics in a world of wax.

I do not fall into the vast, clear basins below to ring out for miles. I am too deliberate for that. 

I strike the absolute center of the dying wooden bowl. 

The impact is tiny, profound, and devastatingly intimate. I shatter across the dark grain, immediately absorbed by the parched, dead fibers of the relic. 

Here, enveloped in the dry, porous bones of the ancient wood, I finally settle. I seep into the deepest, darkest cracks of the bowl, hiding from the shadowless light above. I wrap myself in the memory of the surface world—a world of rot, of autumn winds, of cold nights and blistering days. I am the sudden smell of damp earth in a room of sterile perfection.

Resting quietly in the hands of the sleeping giant, safely sequestered deep inside the grain of mortality, I become something precious and entirely my own. I become a secret hoard of time, a tiny wet ghost haunting the eternal noon, keeping the memory of endings alive.
