I do not know the bruised, eternal dusk as a sky. I know it only as the ceiling of the cold, against which I must barter for warmth. 

I am a weaver of the high thermal drafts. I am an arachnid of strung gold and spun iron, clinging to the crown of a moving world. 

To the beast whose back I inhabit, I am less than a dust mote. To me, the beast is a continent, a god of heat and thrumming vibration, the absolute limit of reality. My entire life, and the generations of my ancestors dating back to a time before the current cloud-ceiling was born, has been spent in the striated shadow of a single, towering exhaust vent. 

I am small, no larger than a human fingernail, though there are no humans here to measure me against. My body is a delicate, angular geometry of silicate glass and copper filaments. I possess eight long, jointed legs, tapering into fine, calcified hooks. I need these hooks. The terrain of my world—the sheer, curving wall of the ossified bone-stack—is pitted and glassy, slick with the perpetual condensation of the high-altitude air. 

My perception of time is frantically, vibrantly brief. For me, a day is a lifetime. I dart, I calculate, I spin. Beneath me, the earth shifts with an agonizing, geologic slowness. 

When I look down from my precipice on the bone-stack, I see the sprawling topography of my universe. The beast’s carapace stretches out in sweeping, jagged ranges of black slag and oxidized crimson ridges. Deep in the canyons between these armored plates, a cherry-red light pulses faintly through translucent membranes. That light is the forge of my existence. It equals life. 

Down in those steaming fissures, the heavier, duller kin of my ecology make their lives. I can see the pale, blooming clusters of thermal-sponges gripping the hot edges of the armor, drinking the seeping warmth. Sometimes, I catch the glint of the rust-flaked scuttlers as they harvest the shedding proteins near the gargantuan, oil-weeping joints. We do not interact. They are creatures of the wet, deep swelters; I am a creature of the biting wind and the wire. 

My purpose is geometry and capture. 

From spinnerets located in my abdomen, I draw out thread. It is not silk as a biological world knows it. It is an extruded polymer of captured metals and organic resins, glittering like spun gold in the sourceless, watery light. I move between two adjacent ridges of the bone-stack, anchoring my lines, calculating the exact tension required to withstand the atmospheric downdrafts. I weave a vast, intricate net, suspended over the abyss of the vent. 

I work quickly, for the cold of the air presses against me like a physical weight, threatening to lock the hydraulic fluids in my joints. I must stay near the radiating heat of the bone, or I will freeze into a beautiful, static glass sculpture. 

Then, the vibration begins. 

It does not come as a sound. It comes as a profound, sub-bass shudder that travels up from the miles of bedrock, through the massive iron-bone pillars of the beast’s legs, through the cathedral-chambers of its smelting stomachs, and finally up the spiraling marrow of the exhaust stack. It is a frequency that vibrates the copper filaments on my legs, singing of immense, mounting pressure.

The Harvest is coming.

I retreat to a deep fissure in the bone, hooking my eight claws firmly into the microscopic grooves. I flatten my body, lowering my profile until I am heavily flush against the hot, calcified wall. I wait. 

Deep within the stack, vast biological valves grind open. 

*Shhhhhhh.*

The exhalation hits like a hurricane. A roaring thermal pillar of superheated steam, yellow sulfur, and gray iron ash erupts from the vent, rocketing upward past my hiding place. The temperature spikes from near-freezing to a blistering, tropical heat in a matter of seconds. I absorb the warmth, my internal chemistry racing, my joints softening and flooding with energy. 

The gale of breath tears at my golden web. It bows outward, stretching under the violent force of the exhaust, but it holds. I designed it to hold. 

As the beast breathes its manufactured weather up into the bruised sky, the webbing acts as a sieve. It catches the heavy, particulate treasures carried on the wind: flakes of pure, smelted iron; beads of condensed, rare minerals; microscopic droplets of sulfur-rich oil. 

When the sigh eventually tapers off—minutes later, leaving the air rippling with heat haze—the Harvest is ready. My golden web is now frosted with a thick, gray-black soot. 

I unflatten myself and dart out onto the lines. I am quick, moving like a bead of water on a taut wire. I travel along the silk, gathering the heavy soot into my mandibles. This is my sustenance. I am eating the processed mountains, the refined essence of the plateau itself, pre-digested by the colossal furnaced-heart of the titan beneath me. The metallic ash fuels the copper of my blood, repairs the silicate of my shell, and fills my spinnerets with the material for the next generation of webs.

Hours later, the world moves. 

I feel the preparatory shift—a massive shifting of equilibrium that momentarily alters the direction of gravity. A mile below my high perch, a limb the size of a city block is lifting. The entire carapace beneath me groans. Fissures open in the slag mountains; other canyons crush shut. My web goes slack, then snaps impossibly taut as the bone-stack sways. I cling tightly, riding the slow, graceful, oceanic tilt of the beast’s stride. It takes a full minute for the titan to set its massive foot down. When the muffled crunch of the bedrock finally reverberates up the stack, I stabilize. We are one microscopic increment further along the endless circuit of the plateau. 

I do not possess longing. I do not possess fear. My consciousness is a pure, frictionless state of *being*, perfectly tuned to the heat, the vibration, and the tension of the silk. 

But sometimes, when I am resting in the warm pocket of a bone-fissure, I look out across the unending expanse of my moving world, past the armored ridges, out toward the horizon where the permanent dusk swallows the plateau. I can see the columns of white steam rising, miles and miles away. Other titans. Other gods of heat. 

I know, on a cellular level, that my brethren are there. Millions of them, spinning golden geometries in the exhaust of other worlds, all of us tied to the great, heavy breathing of the engines, all of us caught in the slow, beautiful grind at the roof of the world. 

My life will end long before my titan takes another hundred steps. The cold will eventually catch me during a lull in the wind, or my hooks will dull. When I die, I will simply fall from the bone-stack. I will tumble down into the steamy canyons of the carapace, landing in the hot, sweeping valleys of the beast's back. 

There, I will melt. My glass shell will fuse with the slag. The iron I harvested from the beast's breath will return to its armor. I will not disappear; I will simply stop being the weaver, and become the world.
