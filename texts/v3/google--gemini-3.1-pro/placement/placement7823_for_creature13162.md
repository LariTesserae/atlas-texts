To be soft in this place is an error. 

I begin as the error. 

I am one of the anomalies—a fist-sized knot of translucent, quivering jelly, born from the grayish thermal slime that coats a cathedral-sized rotor. I have no mass to anchor me, no density to defy the crushing acoustic weight of the chamber. For what feels like an eternity, I am nothing but a helpless, drifting nerve-cluster, trapped in the permanent fluid storm.

The environment does not just surround me; it beats me. I am caught in a vicious undertow, whipped at highway speeds through the lightless void. I feel the shotgun blasts of collapsing cavitation bubbles tearing at the outer membrane of my being. Each percussive pop threatens to sheer my gelatinous form into foam. I have no eyes to see the strobing arcs of the turbines, but I feel the jaundiced light as a subtle fluctuation in temperature, a brief blooming of warmth in the suffocating dark. I am tumbling, spinning, desperately clinging to my own structural integrity, tasting the aggressive tang of dissolved ozone, sulfur, and ancient iron. 

Above me, below me, all around me is the deafening roar. Because I lack the dense, iron-infused cartilage to absorb it, the noise is sheer, agonizing chaos. It is a blender of kinetic energy. I am swept toward a yawning intake valve, the suction pulling the water around me into a tight, screaming spiral. The grate looms—a hydro-polished maw of corroded alloy, waiting to shred me into biomatter.

But a shear-current hits me from the flank, a brutal crosswind of heavily pressurized water that alters my trajectory. I am slammed against the periphery of the spherical chamber. I hit the sheer, hydro-polished stone and flatten, clinging by surface tension alone. 

It is here, shivering against the rock, that I taste it. 

Wafting through the aggressive currents is a chemical trail. It is thick with the scent of vulcanized bio-polymers, heavy lipids, and the sharp, coppery tang of leaking thermal fluid. It is the scent of a shell. 

I drag my soft, bleeding form along the curvature of the cavern wall, fighting the relentless friction of the water. I creep beneath the shadow of a colossal piston housing. Nestled there, clamped magnetically to the rusted ribs of an exposed pipe, is a Tender. 

It is immense, a slate-gray boulder of a creature. But it is not moving. Its six heavy pads are locked in place, yet the deep, sub-frequency thermal pulse that usually radiates from its core is sputtering. It is dying. The endless, abrasive scrounge of the world has finally breached its dermal carapace; a deep fissure, worn smooth by the currents, has opened along its ventral side. 

I let go of the stone. I allow the slipstream to carry me the final few feet, splashing against the Tender’s yielding, rubbery flank. 

I am the scavenger, but also the seed. The secret, silent truth of this roaring world is that the anomalies in the water and the minds in the shells are one and the same. When a Tender's internal amniotic gel degrades, when its central nervous system is finally pulverized to dust by centuries of vibration, the empty, blubber-bound husk remains clamped to the architecture, waiting. 

I seep into the fissure. 

I slide through layers of dense, yielding fat and iron-braided cartilage. It is a tight, suffocating squeeze, pushing past dormant hydraulic rams and cold bio-mechanical tubing. The deeper I go, the more the deafening roar of the outside world is muffled, transforming from a physical battering into a distant, rhythmic thrum. 

I reach the heavy, barrel-shaped torso. I find the center. 

The thoracic cavity is filled with the acoustic-baffling gel, but the intelligence that once floated within it is gone—dissolved into the very thermal slime I was born from. I unfurl my translucent, gelatinous mass. I sink into the amniotic pool. 

The moment my nerve-endings make contact with the internal receptors of the carapace, the world violently reorganizes itself. 

*Connection.* 

It is like waking up. It is like being born. The chaotic, agonizing noise of the chamber snaps into profound, crystal-clear geometry. I am suddenly immense. I feel the staggering density of my new body. The water outside is no longer a tempest trying to tear me apart; it is a canvas of pressure, a cymatic radar painting a three-dimensional map directly into my expanded consciousness. 

I "see." 

I see the exact, sweeping arc of a turbine blade a mile away, mapped by the water it displaces. I see the explosive cavitation pops as bright, sudden flashes of topological data. I feel the smooth, frictionless curve of the cavern walls, not as distant structures, but as a continuous extension of my own drum-tight skin. The screaming grind of heavy industry is no longer noise—it is a symphony of pure, tactile information. It is my sky, my earth, my sight. 

I trigger the internal thermal sinks. Warmth floods the iron-infused cables of my new skeleton. My broad, sloping dome of sensory blubber twitches as I taste the sulfur and ozone, no longer a victim of the current, but a creature built to breathe it. 

I send a command to my extremities. 

*Clack.*
A biological electromagnet detaches. 
*Screech.*
A multi-ton limb drags across the hydro-polished alloy. 
*Thud.*
The splayed locking-pad clamps down, anchoring me to the stone. 

I am a boulder that crawls. The highway-speed currents whip over my seamless, sloped back, but I do not care. They cannot move me. My skin begins to slough off in microscopic layers, feeding the thermal slime of the city, and I feel the slow, satisfying warmth of it regenerating from within. Unfurling a heavy pneumatic ram grafted directly into my forelimb, I turn my blind, featureless front toward the massive, leaking piston housing. There is maintenance to be done. 

I will work here for a century. I will scour and weld, sculpted by the violence. And when I am weary, I will crawl to the iron pressure-spheres tethered in the dark. I will lock my immense mass against my kin in the dim red glow, vibrating my thoracic muscles, humming the memory of the storm into their bones, safe in the knowledge that I am no longer small, no longer soft, and no longer alone in the dark.
