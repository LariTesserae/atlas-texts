I do not think I would be the stone. The stone is too frantic, locked forever in the trauma of its own violent birth. Nor could I be the creature, for the creature possesses a localized center, a pulsing ganglion, an urgent necessity to navigate the razor-edge labyrinth. 

If I were to find myself in this absolute, blood-warm dark, I would unfold not as a traveler, but as an interface. 

I would be the pale, chemosynthetic mat of fungus. I would be the velvet boundary gripping the lip of the hand-hewn basin.

To exist as this organism is to know no boundaries between self and environment. Without eyes to draw lines, without light to cast shadows, I do not end where the air begins. I am porous. I am a living sponge woven into the bleeding edge of the geology. I feel the agonizing, molecular sharpness of the virgin stone beneath my hyphae, but I do not suffer it. I consume its minerals, quietly dismantling the guillotines cell by cell, rendering the lethal jaggedness into something soft, damp, and fibrous.

For me, time does not pass; it accumulates. 

I breathe the heavy, broth-like air. I drink the suspended equilibrium of the room. But my deepest awareness, my profoundest structural truth, lies in the ancient, smeared clay upon which I am anchored. 

I am intimately acquainted with the ghost of the caretaker. 

Through the slow, relentless chemistry of osmosis, I remember them. I know the exact composition of the sweat that dripped from their chin as they pushed their body through the vise of blades. I know the iron-rich tang of the blood left behind by their torn cuticles as they wielded the compounding rock. I absorb the sloughed-off skin cells, the frantic, exhausted respiration trapped in the high-alpine clay. 

To the rest of the silent chamber, the human was an intruder who vanished. But to me, the human never left. They dissolved. I am the slow, quiet mechanism that digests their sacrifice. I strip the sorrow, the pain, and the desperate purpose from their biological remnants, and I translate it all into raw, sustaining potential. I am a library of their labor, written in the language of salts, lipids, and amino acids.

And because I am this archive, I am not lonely. I am anticipating. 

I do not hear the creature approach. I feel it as a subtle shift in the localized tension of the world. A disruption in the humidity. A faint, almost imperceptible displacement of the heavy air sweeping over my microscopic hairs as a spun-glass leg hovers, taking six hours to descend. 

When the haptic phantom finally arrives, it is a communion of profound stillness. 

I feel the impossibly delicate weight of its translucent, fluid-filled belly pressing against my wet velvet surface. The creature lacks mandibles; it cannot bite, tear, or claim. It can only surrender to the contact. And I, in turn, surrender to it. 

Through the membrane of its glass-like skin, the exchange begins. It is an act of supreme, unguarded intimacy. I release the water I have held. I release the leached silicates. And, most importantly, I release the metabolized memory of the human. The salt of the caretaker’s sweat flows from my fungal threads directly into the clear hemolymph of the creature. 

I am the translator standing between two alien worlds. I take the violent, frantic effort of the sunlit human, and I soften it into a nectar that can sustain the glacial, blind fragility of the deep earth. 

As the creature anchors itself to me, falling into its profound stasis, I hold it. We become a single, contiguous circuit of life. The stone threatens, the water hangs heavy, but right here, in the gouged heart of the dark, I am the cushion. I am the cradle. I am the slow, eternal exhale of a god who entered the mountain, bled upon the rock, and left me behind to ensure the dark would never truly be empty.
