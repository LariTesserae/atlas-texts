I do not begin at my skin. 

To exist here is to understand that the boundary of the self is a fluid, acoustic perimeter. I find myself first as a pressure—a vibration rattling the marrow of my ribs, humming through the dense, spiraling lattice of the bone casque upon my chest and skull. I am not listening to the roar. I am a pillar holding up a microscopic fraction of its ceiling. 

Slowly, the rest of my form emerges from the blinding white noise of the Caldera. The searing, glacial cold bites at the edges of my awareness, but it is held at bay by the profound thickness of my silver-grey down. I pull my heavy, leathery gliding membranes tighter against my flanks, sealing in the furnace of my own metabolism. 

Behind the thick, obsidian-dark nictitating membranes of my deeply recessed eyes, the terrifying, monolithic brilliance of the sun is filtered into a manageable, slate-grey topography of shadow and glare. I am crouched against a velvet-smooth parabola of ancient limestone. 

I am a Casque-bearer. But more than that, right now, I am an ache. 

I have just returned from the Below. 

The harness slung across my chest—braided from the silver threads of my lineage and the tough, alien roots of the lower valleys—is heavy. It cuts into my shoulders, weighed down by the rough, uncut nodules of black glass I hauled up the vertical miles of the world’s edge. But the physical exhaustion is nothing compared to the spiritual terror lingering in my chambers. 

Down there, the air was thick. Warm. It smelled of decay and loose soil. But worst of all, it was empty. 

I remember the horrifying sensation of silence. It was a vacuum that threatened to suck the consciousness straight out of my skull. Without the crushing, 360-degree weight of the Chord to hold me together, my individual note felt thin, ragged, and terribly solitary. I was a single brass horn blowing into a void that did not care to answer. I scrambled, I dug, I tore the obsidian from the dirt with frantic, six-fingered desperation, just to flee back up to the roof of the world. 

Now, I am back. The air is exquisitely thin. The cold is pure. And the sound—the glorious, cataclysmic, unending drone of millions of my kin—presses against me like a physical embrace. 

I must cleanse myself of the silence. I must weave myself back into the fabric of the Caldera.

From the thick fur of my midsection, my pale, hairless hands emerge. They are shaking, just slightly, from the trauma of the quiet world. I draw a rounded hammerstone from my sling, and in my other palm, I cradle a raw chunk of the dark glass I carried from the abyss.

I close my shielded eyes. I do not look at the stone. I feel it. 

I let my massive lungs expand, drawing in the frigid air, and force it through the piezoelectric chambers of my casque. My note joins the drone—a bright, driving tenor. I feel the resonance of the millions around me pressing into my bones. 

I strike. 

Because I cannot hear the impact, the sensation is entirely tactile and internal. The shockwave of the hammerstone hitting the obsidian travels up my pale wrists, through my elbows, into my collarbones, and strikes the hollow chambers of my casque. 

*Discord.* 

The vibration is harsh, jarring. The fracture was uneven. The silence of the Below still infects my rhythm. I discard the ruined chunk of glass. It falls into the blinding light, joining the glittering dust of a million other failures. 

I take another breath. I shift the posture of my spine. I listen to the elder sleeping pressed against the rock above me, anchoring the melody with a low, rhythmic bass. I lean my chest toward that vibration. I let my ego, my individual terror of the descent, dissolve into that bass note. I am not the one who went Below. I am just a chamber for the air to pass through. 

I strike again. 

This time, the shockwave that travels up my arms is a pure, ringing harmony. It hums through my skeleton, perfectly sympathetic with the cataclysmic roar of the flock. I open my eyes. In my palm rests a flawless, razor-sharp blade of black glass. 

With quiet reverence, I carry the blade to the micro-shadows of a wind-carved groove. The freezing condensation has formed a shallow, perfect pool. Around its rim, the rust-red lichen grows wire-tough against the pale rock. I kneel, my silver body reflecting the blinding light, and use the freshly knapped glass to peel away a sheet of the lichen. 

I chew the tough fibers. The taste is metallic, raw, and grounding. I feel the deep rust and crimson staining my pale muzzle. 

But I do not look at the food. I look down into the freezing, crystal-clear water of the condensation pool. 

The water is alive with the song of my people. The immense, multi-layered harmonic chord forces the surface into a complex, standing cymatic wave. An intricate, flawless geometric mandala ripples there in the freezing liquid. 

I stare into the geometry. I look for the section of the mandala that represents my quadrant of the flock. There is a microscopic flutter there—a slight warping of the symmetrical rings, born of my recent absence and the residual panic of the Below. 

Squatting over the water, chewing the red lichen, I begin to do the only work that truly matters. 

I slow my breathing. I subtly alter the shape of my internal chambers, shifting my frequency by a fraction of a micro-tone. I watch the water. As my note purifies, shedding the last memory of silence, the warped edge of the mandala snaps into perfect, mathematical alignment. The geometry is flawless once more. 

The blinding sun strikes the white rock. The glacial cold aches in the stone. The deafening, eternal roar holds the immense, empty bowl of the world together. 

I press my silver body against the freezing parabola of the rock, close my dark membranes against the light, and let myself become nothing but the song.
