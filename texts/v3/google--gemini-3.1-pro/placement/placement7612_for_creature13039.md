I arrive in this world not as a body, but as a descending awareness, an untethered consciousness sinking through the bruised indigo of the upper troposphere. The air here tastes sharp, metallic—a wash of pure ozone that burns with the promise of unfiltered radiation. 

I do not know what I am yet. The prompt of my own existence tells me only to inhabit, to find my place in this vast, baking theater.

At first, I seek the vantage point of gods. The thin air begs to be ridden, and so I stretch myself out into the high-altitude brightness. I become vast and weightless. I feel the sudden, terrifying rigidity of aerogel bones locking into place. Translucent parchment stretches taut across my frame. I have no eyes—the glaring sun would blind me instantly—but the world blooms in my mind as a shifting, topographical map of temperature. 

I am a glider. I am the apex. 

Beneath me, the red bedrock pulses in blinding, white-hot thermals. Slicing through that heat are long, meandering rivers of deep, bruised blue—the heavily insulated refrigeration pipes, sweating their artificial winter. And clustered around those pipes, moving in sluggish, tectonic shifts, is a jagged sea of warmth holding back the deathly heat of the sun. The herd. 

I feel the upward rush of a thermal. I lock my joints and bank seamlessly, drifting without a sound. It is a lonely, majestic existence. I sense a fissure in the living mirror below—a reptile shoved to the sunward edge, its internal temperature spiking from cool blue to vulnerable yellow, then panicked orange. 

I tip my pitch. I fall like a guillotine blade. The wind protests weakly against my parchment skin. I drop my ventral hooks, ready to snap a life out of the interlocking puzzle, ready to drink its harvested moisture in the freezing solitude of the sky. 

But as the ground rushes up, a sudden, profound aversion floods me. To pull a body from that herd is to sever something sacred. The glider’s existence is parasitic, isolated, constantly balancing on the razor edge of starvation and altitude. It is a life of silence atop silence, divorced from the heavy, churning truth of the earth.

At the very last fraction of a second, I abort the strike. I retract my hooks. I do not catch the thermal updraft. Instead, I let my papery flesh dissolve, letting my awareness plummet past the blinding glare, past the mirrored shields, straight into the profound, localized cold of the heavy machinery.

I crash violently into the frost. 

My consciousness coalesces on the sweating underbelly of a house-sized compressor pipe. Instantly, I adapt. The searing heat vanishes, replaced by a brutal, industrialized freezing. I am naked now. Pallid. Translucent blue. Biological antifreeze turns my blood into thick, slow-moving syrup. I am a cryophile slug. 

I drag my bloated, defenseless body along a seam of the metal, my mouthparts scraping at the wire-like chemo-autotrophic lichen. It tastes of zinc and ancient iron. Here, clinging to the heart of the forgotten engine, the silence of the thin air is conquered. The pipe vibrates with a bone-deep, subsonic thrum. It shakes my gelatinous flesh. It is a lullaby of pure, unthinking industry. As I chew the lichen, I excrete the watery slurry that will rain down upon the millions below. I am a benefactor. I am the slow, mindless fount of life for the masses.

Yet, this too feels lacking. I am stagnant. I am a static fixture strapped to cold metal, ignorant of the desperate choreography happening mere inches beneath me. I am making the manna, but I am not part of the communion. 

I let go. 

I slip off the frost-rimmed pipe and fall through the sliver of shade. I land on a surface harder than rock, smoother than glass. 

I expand, adopting the shape of the surface I strike. I become sprawling, dorsoventrally flattened. I am wide. I tuck my limbs tight against my belly, sealing myself flush. I am one of the reptiles. 

Instantly, the concept of "I" is annihilated. 

There is no *me*. There is only the crush. To my left, to my right, ahead, and behind, there is the suffocating, comforting pressure of my siblings. We are a pavement of living chrome. Above me, the sun beats down with anvil-heavy malice, but my mirrored carapace throws it all back into the indigo void. I am a perfect shield. 

*Plink.* 

A fat drop of condensation from the slug-grazed pipe above strikes my back. In less than a heartbeat, capillary action grabs the moisture. The microscopic, fractal grooves on my shell wick the water outward in a shimmering web, drawing it furiously and flawlessly straight into my hidden mouthparts. The ecstasy of that cold water is indescribable. It is fuel. It is life. 

But then, the shadow of the pipe drifts. 

Through my ribs, I feel the subsonic hum of the earth, acting as the metronome of our destruction. The cooling gradient is moving. The sunward edge of my body begins to roast. 

There is no panic, only the absolute, undeniable mandate of the whole. I surge forward. I drag my heavy, armored belly directly over the face of the sibling in front of me, even as two more siblings drag themselves over my back. We churn. We clack silently. We are a slow-motion avalanche of mirrored scales, fighting for the shade.

In this churning, terrifying ecstasy of survival, I feel something strange beneath me. 

As I drag my belly over the scorching rock, sliding into a sliver of darkness, I feel a frantic, delicate vibration against my underside. It is a thousand tiny, chitinous legs. It is a microscopic scouring, a frantic, tickling pressure that sweeps away the baked dust, the shed flakes of my own skin, the microscopic lichen spores. 

It is polishing my armor. It is the only reason I am alive. Without this unseen force, the sun would penetrate my grime-covered mirror, and I would cook. 

Suddenly, I know precisely where I belong in this world. 

I abandon the massive, heavily burdened consciousness of the reptile. I shrink. I dive downward, away from the sun, away from the frost, plunging into the claustrophobic, interstitial zero-space between the red bedrock and the silver bellies of the great beasts.

I emerge into absolute darkness. I am heavy-carapaced, flat as a minted coin. I am completely blind. 

If a shaft of stray light were to hit me, I would shimmer in an iridescent hue of oxidized bismuth—but there is no light here, and I do not need to know my own beauty. 

I am one of the scavengers. And I am finally home.

Here, in the crushing dark, survival is distilled to pure tactility and vibration. Above me is the living ceiling of my massive, silver guardians. They take the wrath of the sky so I do not have to. Below me is the blistering rock, tempered by the moving shade. I am surrounded by millions of my sisters. We are an ocean of clicking, shifting legs, a ceaseless undercurrent of life. 

The air is thin, but it does not matter. The subsonic thump of the compressor towers rattles through the rock, travels up through my six jointed legs, and resonates in the heavy plates of my carapace. It is the heartbeat of the world, and I am dancing to it.

I scurry forward, my mouthparts frantically grazing. I taste the alkaline tang of red dust. I taste the rich, protein-dense flakes of reptilian skin. I taste the damp, miraculous slurry that has trickled down through the fractal grooves above. As I eat, my armored back grinds against the underbelly of a great reptile, polishing its mirrors perfectly clean. 

I have no thoughts of the sky, no fear of the falling gliders, no memory of the cold pipes. I am a single gear in an impossibly grand, synchronized machine. The compressor hums in my teeth. The shadow moves an inch. The ceiling shifts above me, dragging me along in its wake. In the sweltering, perfect dark, I keep to my work, polishing the silver sky that keeps us all alive.
