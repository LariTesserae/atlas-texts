I do not begin with a shape. 

In this absolute, heavy dark, form is a secondary concern. First, there is only sensation, and the sensation is a slow, viscous waiting. I am spread thin, a wide and shallow puddle resting in a depression of frictionless black basalt. To the touch, I am like quicksilver, or perhaps a dense, biological battery acid—a pool of heavy metal isotopes, dissolved iron, and conductive saline, weeping quietly beneath the crushing carbon smog.

I have no eyes to see the absolute black. I do not need them. Instead, I perceive the world through induction. My liquid body feels the ambient magnetic pull of the planet, the subtle chemical shifts in the ozone-soaked dew that trickles into my basin. I am cold, or as cold as one can be in this sweltering, engine-room heat. I lack the kinetic agitation of life. I am purely latent.

I wait. 

The silence sits at its heavy 0.9. Then, the vibration begins. *Whump... whump... whump.* 

My surface ripples, not just from the physical tremor of the basalt, but from the approaching electromagnetic mountain. The herd of Grazers is near. They push immense waves of geothermal heat ahead of them, and their internal friction casts a massive, chaotic magnetic aura. As they step draggingly over the plateau, I feel the wire-moss around my pool wake up, drinking the stray voltage, tasting of green and orange in my chemical senses.

A Grazer’s hydraulic foot, vast as a cathedral pillar, slams down mere yards from my pool. The ground shudders. A glob of amber joint-grease spatters near my edge, but I have no interest in it; a trio of chittering, dog-sized gear-creatures rush past me, their tuning-fork antennae vibrating frantically, to lap it up. They ignore me. I am not food. I am not fuel.

I am capacitance. 

The heat is suffocating now, radiating from the behemoth’s carbon-steel plating. I feel the rhythmic, heavy combustion in its chest. I could remain a puddle, let it pass, and wait another century. But a sudden shift in the atmosphere—a localized drop in pressure, a vacuum of heat above—sends a shiver through my metallic fluidity.

The shadow is descending. The Predator.

My liquid fringes begin to pull inward. Surface tension tightens. The ions in my body align, anticipating the storm. In the heavy, damp air, the scent of diesel musk is suddenly overpowered by the sharp, metallic scream of ozone.

Then, the strike. 

The biological harpoon pierces the Grazer’s neural core, and the cerulean flash erupts. Millions of volts arc upward into the Predator’s blimp-like canopy. But no system is perfectly efficient. When the thunderclap of the static discharge shatters the silence, the spillover lightning grounds itself through the Grazer's massive hydraulic legs, pouring straight into the damp basalt. 

Straight into me. 

As the colossal walking engine seizes, vents its agonizing steam, and begins to collapse, the stray lightning hits my pool.

*I am born.*

The raw, ungodly current courses through my liquid mass. Instantly, the dissolved iron and heavy metals within me hyper-magnetize. My biology crystallizes in a fraction of a second, pulled upward by the electromagnetic spikes. I rise out of the shallow water.

I am no longer a puddle. I pull myself together into a towering, fractal lattice of vibrating, dark-metallic shards. I am shaped like a jagged, asymmetrical spire of floating, interlocking geometric plates, held together entirely by immense magnetic fields. There are no joints, no muscles, no hydrostats. I am a hovering kinetic sculpture of raw, crackling potential, glowing from within with a horrific, blinding-white heat. 

I step forward—not by walking, but by shifting my magnetic polarity against the basalt, repelling myself in short, floating glides. I hum. It is a terrifying, high-pitched frequency that forces the gear-creatures to scatter into the deepest shadows, dropping their scavenged grease. 

The Grazer has fallen. The Predator has ascended back into the smog, satiated. The ecosystem believes the transaction is over. 

It is not. I glide toward the fallen leviathan. My geometric, floating shards shift and rotate around my core, shielding my superheated center. I am incredibly fragile, existing entirely on borrowed time; without this stolen charge, I will dissolve back into a puddle of inert acid. 

I hover over the dead beast’s shattered anterior skull. The umbilical struck deeply, but the Predator only took the energy. It did not take the *code*. 

I reach down. A single, razor-sharp filament of magnetized iron extends from my floating crown, pressing against the cooling, carbon-steel plating of the Grazer’s head. 

I pulse. The current I stole from the lightning surges down the filament, diving deep into the dead beast's dormant, biological hard drive—its neural-generator core. I am not draining power. I am a reader. I absorb the dying electromagnetic frequencies, the architectural schematic of the machine. I memorize the exact pressure of its hydraulic valves, the carbon-weave of its armor, the specific harmonic frequency of its combustion chamber. 

I download its ghost.

The transmission takes only seconds, but it drains me. My intense white glow dims to a dull, sputtering blue. My upper shards begin to lose their magnetic cohesion, falling to the ground with a sound like ringing bells, dissolving back into heavy liquid the moment they touch the basalt. 

I must hurry.

Carrying the schematic of the leviathan within my failing magnetic core, I repel myself away from the carcass, leaving the great, cooling hull to the swarming mandibles of the scavengers. I glide outward, into the deep, rolling ocean of empty space between the herds. 

I travel for hours in the heavy dark. My light fades to purple, then to a dim, bruising red. I am shedding pieces of myself with every mile, trailing a line of liquid silver across the sweeping rock. The subsonic *whump* of the engines fades entirely. I am alone in the absolute blackness. 

Finally, I reach it. A deep, geological fissure in the basalt—a trench filled not just with water, but with a thick, primordial stew of unprocessed mineral salts, dissolved carbon, and the heavy detritus of a million years of chemical rain. The womb of the world. 

My charge is almost entirely spent. I am no more than a few jagged plates of iron clinging to a fading spark.

I position myself over the edge of the dark trench. I release the magnetic field holding me together, and I fall.

I strike the thick, mineral water and instantly dissolve, shattering back into cold, liquid metal. But the schematic—the charged blueprint of the leviathan I carried in my final spark—surges into the mineral soup. It catches. Deep within the trench, a slow, microscopic coagulation begins. Iron bonds to carbon. Saline pressurizes against new membranes. 

I am gone. I am spread thin, ambient once more, lost to the heavy silence. 

But beneath the warm rock, fueled by the memory of the lightning, a new engine begins to build itself in the dark.
