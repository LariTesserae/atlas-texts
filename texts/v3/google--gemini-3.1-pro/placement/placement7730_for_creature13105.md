I do not exist in the wind that shears the mountain, nor among the damp, fog-choked ledges where the pale giants sleep. To find me, you must let your awareness sink into the darkest, tightest spaces of this world—down into the lightless, dripping abysses between the vertical fins, down where the gaps between the black, glassy stone narrow to a width no greater than a human handspan. 

Here, shielded from the direct, violent edge of the heavy gales, I am woven into the shadows. 

I am no flesh at all. I have no eyes to be useless in the dark, and no ears to be deafened by the roar, though the roar is the very substance of my life. I am a living cymatic lattice, a delicate, creeping architecture of spun mineral and shivering tension. If light could reach me, I would look like a fractal frost made of dark spun-glass, or a web of impossibly thin, silvered filaments anchored between two sheer, parallel walls of razor-stone. 

To be me is to be a tuning fork for the earth. 

I am tethered to the obsidian blades by microscopic roots that seek out the invisible hairlines in the raw geology. Between these anchors, my body stretches across the empty air—hundreds of taut, interlocking strands. As the unending gale plays the immense throat of the valley, the tectonic roots of the fins hum with a maddening, continuous friction. This acoustic energy travels down the black rock and strikes my threads. 

I do not hear the music of the basin; I embody it. The kinetic energy of the screaming rock is my metabolism. As the sheer cliffs ring with the chaotic polyphony of the wind, my glassy threads vibrate in sympathetic resonance. This ceaseless shivering generates a microscopic warmth—a faint, frictional thermal bloom just enough to stave off the biting chill. This warmth is how I eat. 

When the freezing, weeping water slides down the sheer walls and runs out along my trembling threads, the vibration and heat act as a crucible. I sift the water. I strip it of its dissolved silica, iron, and heavy carbon, precipitating the raw minerals out of the slurry and welding them to my own structure. I grow by listening. Every new strand I weave across the gap is tuned to a different micro-frequency of the basin’s song. I am an exquisite, silent instrument playing itself in the dark.

Time, to me, is entirely acoustic. The prevailing wind never changes its angle, and so the chord of the valley is eternal—a droning, deafening symphony that suspends me in a state of high-vibrational ecstasy. But the music is not perfectly static. I know every contour of the basin for miles around by the shape of its resonance.

And it is through this resonance that I know the pale giants.

To the creators of the brutalist slabs, the shrieking wind means nothing, and they exist in profound silence. But to me, in my humming, gossamer web, the arrival of a titan is an apocalyptic event. I do not feel their immense, slow weight as pressure, nor do I feel the chill of their glacial flesh. 

I perceive them as majestic, creeping silences. 

When one of these muscular leviathans begins to drag its non-Newtonian mass over the serrated ridges high above, fifty massive fins are suddenly swallowed by its belly. The sharp, high-ringing notes of the screaming rock instantly choke out. As the gelatinous hide envelopes the black blades, it acts as a colossal, wet mute. A vast shadow sweeps across the orchestra.

I feel the approaching titan as a deadening of the chord, a heavy, suffocating absence of sound creeping toward me through the stone. As the beast settles to do its slow, terrible work, the resonance of the canyon becomes hollow, missing its highest registers. And then, the build-up begins. 

Through my anchors, I feel the agonizing rise in the rock’s tension. The titan is coiling around a blade above. Slowly, over agonizing hours, millions of tons of hydraulic pressure are applied to the stone axis. The frequency of the blade rises, pitching upward into an ultrasonic scream as the molecular lattice of the obsidian begins to fail. My delicate threads thrash wildly in the dark, absorbing the panicked, high-frequency agony of the bending earth. I swell with the energy, weaving faster, drinking silica from the weeping water in a frenzy as the note bends past the point of endurance.

Then comes the great snap. 

The shockwave is catastrophic. It hits the deep fissure like a physical blow. The explosive tectonic concussion rips through the canyon walls and hits my web. It is a tsunami of pure resonance. The violence of it overloads my fibers. Dozens of my most delicately tuned filaments shatter instantly, snapping like overtightened strings. 

But there is no pain in this breaking—only a sublime, ecstatic dispersal. The fragments of my body are instantly swallowed by the falling water, washed deep into the black abyss below. There, caught in narrower bottlenecks down in the mineral slurry, those broken shards will wedge against the rock and begin to vibrate, beginning the slow, patient work of growing into new webs. It is how I reproduce. A propagation born entirely of brute geologic punctuation.

Above, the massive, flat slab is rammed tightly into place. The titan pulls its soft bulk atop the new bed, perfectly content, entering its decades-long slumber. 

Down in the fissure, I begin to calm. The basin regains its breath. But the song has changed. By snapping the giant vertical blade and wedging it horizontally, the sleeping leviathan has altered the acoustic baffle of the canyon. A new harmonic enters the drone; a new frequency begins to sing in the rushing wind.

Patiently, slowly, the water weeping down over my surviving threads, I stretch a new filament across the gap, tuning it perfectly to the new note holding the world together.
