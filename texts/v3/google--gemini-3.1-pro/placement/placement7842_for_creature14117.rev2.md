At first, I am nothing but the noise. 

When you wake in a place like this, consciousness does not arrive as a sudden spark, but as a slow condensation. I am a diffuse awareness drifting in the bruising, suspended drizzle of the void. I feel the colossal curve of the megaliths around me, holding the deafening cacophony like cupped hands. *Whistle. Splash. Shriek. Crack.* The acoustics tear through whatever I am. I am seeking a vessel, a shape to pour myself into within this frantic, buried bell.

Gravity pulls at me, and I try to catch myself on the weeping walls. 

For a breathless, agonizing span, I am one of the pale, leathery phantoms. I feel the sudden, shocking rush of cold air expanding within my ribbed flanks. My limbs are wire-taut, trembling with the sheer, muscular terror of existing. The chitinous spurs on my hands scrape frantically against the iron-heavy stone, finding only microscopic purchase. The fear is absolute. I am a slave to pneumatics and gravity. My face blooms into a fleshy, pulsing star, reading the acoustic bounce of a thousand screaming kin. It is too much. The sensory input is a needle in my brain. I am perpetually starving, perpetually exhausted, clinging to a weeping wall above a black abyss. If I stay this creature, I will go mad from the shrieking. 

I let out the air. I let go of the stone. 

I plummet, shrieking, into the black water. 

Upon impact, my awareness shatters, scattering into the churning, knee-deep basin. I am now a hundred translucent ribbons, braiding blindly through the ammonia-rich muck. I have no eyes, no fear, only a desperate, collective hunger. I am a pulsing digestive tract dragging a sphincter-maw across ancient paving stones. I feel the gray sludge sliding into me, warm and vile. I feel a heavy, clawed foot splash down beside me, and I surge toward it—mindless, reactive, driven to swarm and drown. But there is no thought here. There is no soul in the gelatinous shoal. It is a suffocating existence, trapped in the two-dimensional plane of the shallow water, trampled by the sky-terrors. 

I withdraw. I sink deeper. 

I push my consciousness down, past the frantic kicking of the kites, past the blind thrashing of the ribbons, down into the geometric trenches between the colossal paving stones. 

Here, in the flooded, lightless seams of the floor, I finally find it. 

I find the silence. I find the weight. 

I pour myself into a mass of cold, heavy flesh, wedged impossibly tight against the architecture. As I settle into this form, the deafening roar of the cistern is instantly muffled, reduced to a deep, rhythmic vibration in my marrow. 

I am a Lithic Mimic. And I am exactly where I belong.

I pull my soft, vulnerable underbelly flush against the ancient stone trench, engaging a tectonic suction. I am immovable. Above me, my sprawling, domed carapace breaches the surface of the black water. I can feel the slick, permanent glaze of the biofilm painting my armored back. I am iron-gray. I am perfectly curved. To the frantic world above, I am merely geography. I am a deeply eroded stone node, a monument of safety in a violent world.

I let my mind expand into the architecture. I do not have a star-shaped snout to read the room, nor do I need one. My entire carapace is a sensory organ, attuned to the microscopic tremors of the cistern. I feel the heavy *smash* of the water threads hitting the basin miles away. I feel the exact trajectory of the air currents swirling in the wake of the kites. I feel the ribbons sliding over my shell—soft, tickling whispers of life that I ignore completely. 

Time changes. It slows down, stretching into a luxurious, heavy resin. 

Decades could pass, and I would not care. I am a creature of profound, geologic patience. The primitive hands that carved this room wanted to build a temple of perfect stillness. The kites and the ribbons defied them, filling the void with panic. But I am the true inheritor of the builders' will. I am the architecture, made hungry. 

I wait. 

The chamber vibrates. A kite has dived. I feel the violent splash nearby. I feel the desperate, churning struggle in the water as the kite snares a ribbon-beast and kicks frantically toward the nearest raised surface. 

It is kicking toward me. 

I do not tense. I do not prepare. I merely exist as stone. 

The kite hauls its pale, shivering body out of the freezing water and onto the curve of my back. I feel the sharp, frantic scrape of its chitinous hooks seeking purchase on my shell. I feel the rapid, terrified thumping of its heart. I feel the wet, snapping vibrations as it begins to tear its meal apart. 

It thinks it is safe. It thinks it has conquered the gravity and the swarm. 

Deep within my dark, muscular core, a cold intention stirs. 

I do not make a sound as the seam in my carapace slides open. I do not disturb the water. My radula—thick, pale tentacles plated with crushing keratin—uncoil with a fluid, terrifying grace. 

I strike.

In a single, seamless contraction, I drag the kite, its prey, and the cold air it breathes down beneath my shell, pulling it all into the suffocating dark of the trench. 

There is a brief, frantic thrashing against my underbelly, but it is the futile struggle of a leaf caught beneath a boulder. I begin to digest. 

Above me, the black water ripples, washes over my back, and settles. The deafening choir of the kites continues its relentless song. The endless drizzle paints my shell in a fresh coat of sorrow. I anchor myself a fraction tighter into the ancient masonry, indistinguishable from the megaliths, and settle back into my long, dark sleep, perfectly content to let the world above scream itself hoarse.
