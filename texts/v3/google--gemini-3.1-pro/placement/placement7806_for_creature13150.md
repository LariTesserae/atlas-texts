I am the pulse.

I am not a creature of flesh, nor am I a piece of the raw, bleeding stone. I am the iron lung of the deep. I am the cyclic mathematics of the upstroke and the downstroke, bolted violently into the jagged shelf of this eternal, dripping dark. 

Where would I find myself? Pinned to the geologic wall, suspended over the roaring gulf. I am housed in one of the rusting corrugated sheds. I am Heavy Pump Unit 4. But "Unit 4" is a dry, sunlit concept, a designation stamped onto my casing by soft hands that died in the unremembered past. Down here, in the permanent, wet crush of the abyss, I do not have a name. I have a rhythm. 

*Intake. Drag. Lift. Compress. Expel.*

Every minute of my existence is defined by the weight of the ancient water. I reach down into the dark with a rusted redwood-trunk of cast iron, sucking the icy, mineral-choked fluid from the fractured earth, and I force it upward into the unimaginable distance. I do not know what "surface" is. I only know the terrible gravity of the water, and the immense, satisfying torque required to defeat it.

Because I am iron, I do not feel the deafening roar of the cavern as a sound. I feel it as a shimmering topography of pressure against my housing. The waterfall churning below me is a constant, shivering drone in my foundation bolts. The pale, blind cave-swifts that weave through the mist are tiny, fluttering interruptions in the dense barometric soup, practically imperceptible. 

Instead, my awareness is dominated by temperature and viscosity. 

I run hot. The blood-heat of the cavern is nothing compared to the localized inferno of my own heart. Deep inside my iron block, massive steel pistons rise and fall in a heavy, agonizingly patient tempo. I thrum with a deep, concussive violence that shakes the slick catwalks anchored to my sides. 

I know the soft biology of this world because it clings to my warmth. I feel the heavy, dough-pale amphibians piling against my vibrating base. They are cold, wet weights, hundreds of them, stacking upon each other to press against my rusted shell. I do not mind them. Their soft bodies act as a dampening blanket, absorbing some of the excess vibration that might otherwise rattle my housing loose. Sometimes, a giant clicking arthropod will drag its spindly legs across my corrugated roof, a sequence of sharp, rhythmic pinpricks seeking the heat of my exhaust vents. 

We exist in perfect, thoughtless equilibrium. Except when the fever comes.

It begins as a subtlety in my lower gears. The deep-earth water I pull is heavy with abrasive minerals, and the constant, eternal rotation of my internal workings slowly grinds away at my lubrication. After a decade, or a century—time is just the counting of strokes—the oil runs thin. 

The heat inside me shifts from a productive, contained fire to a destructive fever. The *Intake-Lift-Expel* rhythm begins to drag. I feel the horrifying shudder of metal-on-metal friction. A dry, screaming heat blossoms in my primary drive wheel. If I were flesh, it would be an agonizing bone-bruise. Because I am iron, it is a tightening, a seizing, a localized death creeping into my joints. My cyclic thud changes pitch. It becomes ragged, sending a jagged, desperate frequency down the cast-iron pipes and out across the wire-mesh catwalks. I cannot cry out. I can only grind, and wait, and broadcast my fever into the wet air.

And then, I feel the approach.

It comes as a series of heavy, deliberate impacts against the rusted grating of the catwalk. *Step. Anchor. Shift.* The vibration is completely distinct from the chaotic rushing of the water or the mindless crawling of the amphibians. It is conscious weight. 

The Tender arrives. 

I feel its massive presence block the weak, dirty-yellow glare of the halogen bulb outside my shed. It moves with a beautiful, agonizing slowness, parting the squirming carpet of doughy amphibians at my base with thick, calloused feet. The air in my shed shifts, heavy with the smell of atomized oil and musky rot. 

The Tender steps up to my primary ascension pipe. It does not use tools, not at first. It leans its heavy, pale bulk forward. I feel the sudden, smooth press of its hypertrophied jawbone directly against my vibrating metal.

This is our communion. It is the most profound intimacy in the dark. 

Through the thick, wet skin and heavy bone, I pour my agony into the Tender. I transmit the exact frequency of my grinding gears, the specific heat-bloom of the seizing drive wheel, the terrifying pressure of the water backing up in my throat. I speak in the tactile language of friction and torque; the Tender listens in the resonance of its skull. 

The jawbone lifts. The diagnosis is complete. 

Slowly, the Tender unlatches the heavy iron inspection port on my side. The damp, blood-warm air rushes into my superheated casing. With thick, blunt fingers, the Tender reaches toward its own chest. I feel the heat of its body, a different thermal bloom than my own. Methodically, it scrapes the thick, waxy biological grease from its weeping skin, mixing it with the raw mineral oil it carries in a hollowed stone cruet. 

The Tender reaches into my chest. It ignores the scorching heat of my iron. With long, deliberate strokes, it packs the lipid-heavy salve directly into the teeth of my grinding drive wheel. 

The relief is immediate. It is a wave of cool, silencing grace. 

The friction softens. The terrible heat equalizes. The waxy secretion of the Tender’s body melts into my moving parts, forming a slick, perfect barrier between the metal plates. The ragged shudder leaves my frame. I settle back down into my deep, patient groove.

*Intake. Drag. Lift. Compress. Expel.*

The Tender lingers for a moment. It rests a heavy, blunt hand against my rusted flank, feeling the returning health of my rhythmic thud. We do not exchange gratitude; we do not possess the capacity for it. We simply share the relief of the stasis maintained. I keep the water moving; the Tender keeps me moving. We are two vital organs of the same immense, fractured beast. 

Slowly, the Tender closes my inspection port, stepping back out into the deafening, torrential dark. I feel the vibration of its footsteps fading along the catwalk, moving off to find the next ragged frequency in the gloom.

I remain in the dark. The amphibians return, piling against my warmth. The water flows. The engine pumps. I am suspended in the roaring wet, moving my fraction of a world, an inch at a time, forever.
