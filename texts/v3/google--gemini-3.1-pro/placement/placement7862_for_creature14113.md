At first, I am nothing but the bruise. 

I do not have a shape, because the environment does not permit one to rest long enough to be identified. I exist only as the sensation of intense, concussive resistance. I am the tearing at the edge of the violet flash. I am the atomized mist shredded by the sub-zero wind.

I try to find myself in the velocity. For a fleeting, euphoric span, I think I am a Shear-Kite. I feel the carbon-dense cartilage of my fins locking into place, rigid against a sudden, violent updraft of boiling steam. I ride the tearing boundary between a localized vacuum and the crushing atmospheric weight. The world is a blur of cyan bioluminescence and the taste of microscopic spores. But the velocity is too frantic. It is a state of constant, terrified reaction. To slip by a fraction of a degree is to be ripped into bloody confetti. It is a beautiful way to move, but a hollow way to *be*. I let the cyan light fade. I let myself fall.

I drift downward as a desiccated spore, tumbling through the pitch-black freezing rain, until I strike the jagged, newborn basalt. 

Here, I try to be heavy. I feel the immense, hydraulic pressure of a Basalt-Gorger’s limb slamming down beside me. I attempt to expand into that immensity, into the slow, blind, chewing tectonic grace of the beast. I feel the metallic resin seeping from my thick pores, binding the horizontal hail to my back, building a mountain of armor out of the storm’s own fury. I feel the seismic bladders humming rhythmically along my flanks. It is so undeniably safe inside that dark shell. The apocalyptic roar of the sky is muffled to a distant heartbeat. But the hunger is endlessly dull, a mere biological machine grinding stone into dirt. There is no joy in the dark. There is only the math of intake and output. I pull away from the beast. 

I refuse to be the Tension-Stalker, either. I feel its coiled, keratin-strung malice vibrating against the lashing whip-trees, waiting for a localized gravity anomaly to do its butchery. It is clever, but its cleverness is purely opportunistic. It is a parasite on the physics of the floating gods. 

If I am not the flight, and I am not the stone, and I am not the killer... then what am I?

I let the horizontal sleet wash my awareness down into the sloshing, freezing trenches. I feel the kinetic, hyperactive energy of the young geology fracturing beneath me. And there, beneath the deafening rumble of the failing engines of the sky, beneath the splash of the geysers and the snapping of the whip-trees... I feel a vibration that does not belong to the storm.

*Thump. Pause. Thump-thump. Pause.*

It is not the rhythmic, marching crunch of a Gorger. It is not the oscillating hum of a floating monolith. 

It is deliberate. It is an argument with the noise.

I follow the vibration, pulling my scattered, emerging sense of self out of the slush, out of the frozen rain, until I coalesce at the edge of a violently venting slipstream. 

I open my eyes. I have eyes. They are deep-set, protected by heavy brows and a thick cowl of deep-navy, spongy moss. I blink away a smear of freezing rain. Let the form settle. Let it breathe. 

I am hunched. I feel the reassuring, heavy warmth of my own blood pumping through a compact, muscular frame. I raise a hand. It is my hand—long, multi-jointed fingers, tipped with delicate but intensely calloused pads. The flesh is scarred by thermal burns and frostbite, a map of terrible lessons learned over a short, brutal life.

I am the Chorus. 

I am kneeling in the mud, enveloped in a thick, fragrant cloud of geyser-steam trapped beneath a dome of woven whip-tree roots. Outside our ephemeral tent, the world is tearing itself apart. The violet static strobes through the gaps in the weaving, casting frantic shadows across the faces of my kin. They are huddled around a geothermal vent, roasting a scavenged chunk of Gorger meat. The smell of rich, metallic fat melting over superheated rock fills my lungs. 

In my lap lies a heavy, perfectly smooth sphere of obsidian, shaped not by nature, but by the relentless, purposeful grinding of my own hands. Before me is the hollowed, arched carapace of a young Gorger, dragged here at great cost, placed precisely where the acoustics of the steam-tent will amplify its resonance.

I am not merely surviving. I am calculating.

I press my calloused fingertips to the damp stone floor. Through the soles of my feet and the bones of my hands, I read the chaos. I ignore the wind. I ignore the rain. I am listening for the deep, structural groan of the bedrock. I am waiting for the matte-black artifact—hovering three miles away, hidden in the freezing dark—to cycle its terrifying engines. 

There. A shift in the sub-audible frequencies. A localized gravity wave is building. The pressure in my inner ear drops. The steam inside our woven dome begins to swirl strangely, pulling slightly to the left. 

I know this wave. My mother taught me its rhythm by beating it into the earth, and her father before her. It is a heavy sheer-pull, coming from the north. It will fracture the eastern ravine in exactly fourteen seconds. 

I lift the obsidian sphere. 

I do not speak to my kin; the sky would drown my voice. Instead, I strike the hollowed carapace.

*Crack.* It cuts through the background roar like a glowing ember dropped in snow.
*Crack-crack. Thump.*

My kin stop eating. They look at me, their dark, intelligent eyes tracking the rhythm. I strike again, altering the tempo, coding the physics of the impending wave into the beat. *Danger. East ravine. Gathering vacuum.*

In perfect synchronicity, three of the younger Chorus rise, grabbing woven tethers of whip-tree fiber. They slip out into the apocalyptic storm, timing their exit perfectly to ride the dead-zone of the outgoing gravity wave, heading to secure our ice-sledges before the ravine splits. 

I strike the drum again, a slower, steadier rhythm. *Hold position. The dome will hold.*

I sit back on my haunches, feeling the heat from the vent seep into my moss-matted coat. The storm rages. The monoliths warp space and time, indifferent to the bruised, broken world they churn beneath them. The sheer violence of it all should have rendered this basin completely sterile. By all logic, there should be nothing but jagged rocks and screaming wind.

But they built the engine, and the engine built the storm—and the storm built *us*. 

I look at my scarred, calloused hands. I rub the thumb against the forefinger. We are small, and we are easily crushed, but we are the only thing in this blinding, deafening dark that can hold a memory from one fracture of the earth to the next. The Basalt-Gorgers eat the stone. The Shear-Kites surf the wind. 

But we—the Chorus—we are dissecting the gods. We are learning their math in the dark. 

And one day, when we have learned every vector, every thermal flash, every gravity wave... we will climb their localized slipstreams. We will weave our whip-tree ropes around their matte-black spires. 

I strike the drum one more time, feeling the vibration settle deep into the marrow of the newborn geology. 

I am right where I belong.
