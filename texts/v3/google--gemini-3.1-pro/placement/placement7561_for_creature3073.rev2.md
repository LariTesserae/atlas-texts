To me, the dark does not exist. 

Where the architects of this iron cavern see an impenetrable blackness cut by feeble amber lamps, I perceive a sprawling, radiant topography of heat and tremor. My face—the shivering, petaled dish of flesh that blooms from my shoulders—reads the air not as empty space, but as a dense, turbulent fluid. 

I am suspended beneath the steel grating, hanging over the abyss. To my senses, the cavern is a masterpiece of thermal geography. The weeping basalt walls are a cool, soothing bruised-blue in my mind, etched with the jagged, lukewarm fractures left by the blasting charges. The steam pipes are blinding arteries of white-hot gold, pulsing with a furious, trapped energy. And the air itself is a chaotic swirling nebula of gray and pink, stirred by the frantic, buzzing sparks of the bat swarm. 

Time, for my kind, is a heavy, viscous thing. It does not fly; it pools. 

The frantic, vibrating creatures of the upper world—the blind bats, the millions of skittering, chitinous things on the walls—live in a state of exhausting acceleration. Their lives are brief, terrifying flashes of hunger and panic. I watch a bat careen past my shoulder. To my thermal vision, it is a blazing little comet of frantic blood and straining muscle. It is so loud, so hot, so fast. 

I am none of these things. I am patience rendered in flesh. 

My muscles, thick as bridge cables, contract with agonizing deliberation. I pull myself over the edge of the grating. The metal shrieks, a vibration that travels up through my seven-padded grips and rattles pleasantly in the cartilaginous accordion of my ribs. Underneath my feet, the deep, rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the turbine is a glorious, resonant hymn. It matches the slow, glacial pounding of my own heart. Once every minute, my blood surges. Once every minute, the cavern breathes with me.

The heavy, tepid rain falls. The drops strike the deep, spongy velvet of my pelt, and I feel the exquisite, damp weight of them. My secondary arms—the delicate, mantis-like claws folded against my chest—wake from their slumber. They move autonomously, rhythmic as pendulums, wringing the moisture from my rusted shoulders. The water, tasting heavily of pulverized stone, copper wire, and ammonia, is guided into the muscular slit at my sternum. I drink the atmosphere. I am a living filter for this metal womb.

Hunger—a slow, blooming curiosity—prompts me to lift my primary hand. I extend it into the void, right into the highest-density thermal river of the swarming bats. I do not snatch. Snatching is for the frantic. I merely become an inevitability in their path. 

*Thwump. Thwump. Thwump.*

The impacts are tiny, rapid pulses of heat against my palm. My digits curl, a hydraulic press closing. The delicate, hollow bones shatter, the hot little comets extinguished in an instant. I press the wet, pulsing mass into my chest-slit. The iron-rich blood and marrow seep into my slow metabolism. It is a small pleasure. A spark of kinetic spice to wake up my dense core. 

But it is not the sacrament. 

I turn my orchid-face toward the center of the cavern. There, rising from the floor like a god of the new age, is the heat exchanger. 

In my thermal sight, it is not a machine. It is a pillar of blinding, absolute white. A geyser of pure, lethal temperature, radiating a halo of scalding steam that makes my sensory membranes quiver and fold back on themselves in awe. 

We slept for eons in the crushing, static dark of the deep mantle, surviving on the meager, shifting warmth of tectonic friction. We were solitary, blind grazers in a cold stone ocean. But then, the earth shattered. The little, frantic builders from the surface drilled a hole into our quiet eternity. They hollowed out this cathedral. They installed these massive, bleeding towers of iron. 

They did not know they were building heaven. 

I step off the metal gantry. I press my heavy pads into the raw scars of the basalt wall, feeling the millions of pale roaches scatter like cold static beneath my limbs. I crawl downward, a slow-motion avalanche of mass, drawn inevitably to the blinding white pillar.

When I reach the floor, I approach the machine. The heat here is magnificent. It is a physical pressure, a blistering wave that sings against the damp velvet of my pelt, vaporizing the trapped water into a halo of steam around my massive frame. Any creature from the surface world would ash and boil in this proximity. 

I open my arms wide. I step forward, and I embrace the iron.

I press my chest, my stomach, the deep cartilaginous ridges of my torso flush against the scalding jacket of the tower. My thermal membranes fold shut entirely, protecting themselves from the blinding glare. 

And then, I feed.

The searing thermal energy bleeds through the iron and directly into my flesh. It bypasses digestion entirely, striking the ultra-dense, thermo-synthetic core of my being. It is an ecstasy so profound it entirely halts my movement. My heavy legs lock. My massive arms fuse in their embrace. I become indistinguishable from the machine.

As my core temperature rises, a beautiful thing happens. The isolation of my mind dissolves. 

Through the conductive iron of the pipe, through the humming metal floor, through the steam-choked air, I feel the others. Dozens of my kind, clinging to the baffles, wrapping themselves around the exhaust vents, hugging the other towers. Through the heat, our sluggish minds connect. We share a single, sprawling consciousness, a slow, deep dream of warmth and iron. 

Above us, miles away, the frantic little builders live in their sprawling city. They believe their massive condenser vault is working flawlessly, cooling their industrial sprawl, keeping their world from overheating. They do not know that their machines would have melted down to slag months ago if not for us. 

We are the heat sinks. We drink their excess fire. We balance their world, and they provide for ours. 

I hold the scalding iron, dreaming with my kin, wrapped in the deafening, thrumming dark, perfectly content to remain in this rapturous embrace until the end of time.
