I do not wake up, because waking implies a preceding sleep, and sleep requires a biology that I no longer possess. Instead, I *coalesce* into awareness. 

The first thing I know is the pressure. 

*Boooom.*

It strikes me not as a sound, but as a physical wave, a heavy wall of displaced, hyper-saturated air that compresses the space around me. Exactly sixty seconds later, it comes again. 

*Boooom.*

I do not have eyes to open. Sight is a predatory sense, built for hunting and fleeing, and there is nothing to hunt here. Instead, I perceive the world through resonance, temperature, and induction. I am suspended mid-air, anchored firmly beneath the decaying ribs of a steel catwalk that is entirely swallowed by the translucent, amber-like secretion of the giant pitchers.

As the heavy heat of the grove settles into me, I feel the boundaries of my own form. I am not a creature of locomotion like the Heavy Tenders, nor am I ephemeral like the Galvanic Drifters. I am a localized transducer—a harmonic organ of the grove itself. 

I am a massive, asymmetrical geode of spun glass, polymerized sap, and fluted copper.

My base is rooted deep into the rusted lattice of the catwalk, bound by thick, muscular vines that have stripped themselves of leaves to serve as grounding cables. From this anchor, I blossom downward in a series of hollow, concentric chambers. My outer shell is formed of calcified moss and pure, hardened resin, layered over decades into a perfect acoustic horn, resembling the spiraled shell of a prehistoric nautilus crossed with the flared intake of an ancient turbine. 

I have no lungs, but I breathe. The heavy, aquatic air of the caldera is continually drawn into my fluted chambers by convection. As the mist enters me, I filter it. Microscopic filaments of exposed copper line my inner spirals. When droplets of condensation—laden with dissolved iron and ozone—roll over these wires, they generate a minuscule, tingling electrical charge. I taste the air not in flavors, but in voltages. The fog tastes of 0.2 millivolts; the steam rising from the heavy hydrologics tastes of 1.5. 

I realize, with the slow, creeping geometry of botanical thought, what my purpose is in this closed loop. I am the balancer. I am a pressure-valve for the chaotic energies of the caldera.

*Boooom.*

The great piston fires across the grove. The shockwave hits my wide, resinous bell. The shape of my body catches the kinetic violence of the sound and funnels it upward, channeling it through my diminishing logarithmic spirals. By the time the acoustic wave reaches the dense, crystalline core of my body, it is no longer noise. I squeeze the sound, compressing it through piezoelectric quartz deposits drawn from the deep mineral roots of the ironwoods. 

I turn the deafening noise into a low, radiating magnetic field. 

Below me, twenty feet down in the yielding, drumhead floor, I feel the Root-Weavers respond. They are blind, segmented cables of fungus and rubber, undulating through the decomposing pulp. They cannot hear the piston, but they can feel the magnetic pulse I generate. As I hum with converted energy, I lay down invisible tracks of magnetism, guiding the Weavers toward frayed roots and ruptured cables beneath the soil. I am the silent metronome that directs their subterranean stitching. 

Time does not pass here; it merely circulates. 

An afternoon stretches into a decade. I grow heavier, layer by layer, secreting fresh lacquer from my vascular anchor to expand my fluted chambers. A Heavy Tender approaches my sector. I feel the groaning leverage of its hydraulic joints and the boiling rush of sap within its core long before it arrives. It reaches up with a massive, multi-pronged appendage of hardened resin. It does not mean me harm—harm is a concept of meat. It senses that the velvet moss creeping down the catwalk is beginning to choke my intake bells, dampening my resonance. With agonizing care, taking an entire hour to complete the motion, the Tender scrapes the moss away from my glass-like lips. In return, I project a localized magnetic warmth that soothes the friction in its rusted iron bearings. 

Then comes the storm.

Deep within the ironwoods, a towering conductive pillar reaches its threshold. I feel the static charge building in the laden air. The mist around me aligns, the suspended water droplets hovering in momentary, anti-gravitational suspension. 

*CRACK.*

The blinding arc of blue lightning snaps across the canopy. The shockwave is immense, sharp, and jagged, entirely unlike the deep rhythm of the piston. It hits my fluted bell. I vibrate so intensely that the air inside me friction-heats. The moisture collected in my chambers flashes to steam with a violent *hiss*, venting out of my spiraled body in a high-pressure whistle.

From the vaporized waxy secretions in the canopy above, a cloud of Galvanic Drifters is born. Glowing with a pulsing azure light, these balls of living, carbon-thread static float downward on the updrafts. Scenting the heavy electrical resistance of my copper-lined chambers, a cluster of them drifts toward me. 

They settle onto my damp, amber-like rim. As they touch down, they discharge. Micro-circuits complete. A rush of pure, cold electricity courses through my nervous system of naked wire. It is a sensation of absolute, crystalline ecstasy—a sudden, blinding clarity of purpose that tells my vascular anchor to draw more heavily on the ironwood, to grow, to reach, to expand. 

I chime. The sound is not mechanical, nor is it biological. It is the ringing of a crystal glass rubbed by a wet finger, amplified a thousand times, echoing through the claustrophobic green walls of the grove. 

I will remain here, suspended in the emerald-tinted shafts of light, translating the violence of the engines into the silent music of the earth. I will do this for centuries. Eventually, my copper will corrode, my resin will petrify, and I will grow too heavy for the decaying catwalk. I will fall, crashing into the hyper-saturated mat below, shattering into a thousand pieces of amber and wire. 

And that will be perfectly fine. The Root-Weavers will consume my copper. The Heavy Tenders will use my shattered resin as aggregate for their own bodies. The grove will reclaim me, re-forge me, and somewhere else in this warm, wet, deafening cathedral, another bulb of glass and wire will begin to swell, waiting to catch the next great *boooom*.
