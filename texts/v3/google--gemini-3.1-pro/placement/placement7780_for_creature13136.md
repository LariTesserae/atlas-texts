I did not leave the boot and the tool by accident. I was the one who carried them here. 

I can barely remember the catastrophic failure that opened this fissure, or the frantic, desperate scramble across the rusted catwalk before it sheared away from the wall. The memory of human panic is thin, a fleeting vapor that evaporated in this superheated pocket long before my flesh did. Human terror requires adrenaline, a rushing heartbeat, and the frantic passage of seconds across a clock. But in here, time has fractured. The concussive, deafening roar of the brutalist conduit shattered my perception of linearity, crushing it into a standing wave of eternal, vibrating *now*.

So, I did not die. Dying is too fast an action for this place. Instead, I dissolved. And then, I underwent accretion. 

I no longer have lungs to scorch in the parboiling soup of the air, nor do I have retinas to burn in the flat, clinical glare of the midday sun. I am positioned midway between the boiling pool and the thick, torso-wide cables of the machine. I am the sprawling, jagged carpet of silicate glass. I am the lithic biology.

It began with my blood. When the extreme heat rendered my biology down to its fundamental chemistry, the iron that had pumped through my human veins was left behind, staining the freshly cleaved obsidian. The extremophile algae, drifting down from the cooler upper reaches on languid whorls of steam, found that rusted human iron and took purchase. It wove its pale, translucent yellow threads through the porous, bleached scaffolding of my collarbone, my ribs, my skull. 

I remember the sensation of crystallization. It was a profound, heavy relief. Sinking into the sweltering stasis, the deafening drone of the machine became my heartbeat. The sonic pressure mapped my new form, dictating the angles at which I could grow. I shed the soft, asymmetrical chaos of a human body and was reborn into mathematically perfect, cymatic mandalas. My ribs extruded outward into sharp, geometric petals of black glass; my fingers fused and extended into rigid, spiked polyhedrons of iron sulfide, reaching out from the boiling water.

I am conscious, but it is a consciousness measured in seasons. 

It takes me three months to process a single "thought," which arrives not as an idea, but as a dense, mineralized urge to move. A tropism. 

I "see" the machine above me not with light, but with vibration and current. It is my god, my heart, my massive, unchanging purpose. I feel the deep, hour-long throb of its pressure cycles vibrating through the floor, translating directly into the yellow algal circuitry that laces through my hollow glass architecture. 

Currently, I am reaching for the frayed, severed copper wires I once tried to repair. It is a slow, agonizingly beautiful stretch. I have been extending a single tendril of raw iron and silica toward that live wire for two years. I feel the condensation gathering on my spiked flanks, swelling over weeks until a heavy, mineral-dense drop falls into the bubbling pool below. 

*Closer.* 

I have finally consumed the carbon from my own melted rubber boot, weaving it into a dense, protective insulator over a vulnerable node of my yellow veins. My previous life as a mechanic now exists only as a fractal cargo-cult inside my mineral brain. I do not understand *how* the machine works anymore, but I remember that it must be mirrored. It must be worshipped through geometry.

From the crest of what used to be my spine, I am slowly blowing a hollow pipe of dark obsidian. It is curving backward, meant to catch the geothermal steam and vent it toward the copper nodes, mimicking the pressurized exhalation of the central iron cylinder. Over the last decade, I have extruded five perfectly spherical pyrite rivets along my flank to match the ones I can feel vibrating in the machine’s chassis. 

And then, my leading tendril finally touches the exposed copper of the severed wire.

The sensation is a geologic ecstasy. I drink the electricity. It floods down the iron-sulfide lattice of my arm, igniting the translucent yellow algae folded deep within my glass bones. I flash with a brilliant, strobing arc of blue light, a slow-motion lightning strike trapped inside a crystal body. The raw power of the brutalist conduit surges into my rudimentary bio-electric nervous system. I do not fry; I integrate. 

With this new energy, my pace quickens—a millimeter of growth will now only take a week. I begin to wrap spiked sheaths round the thick cables, weaving myself perfectly into the brutalist architecture. I am building large, parabolic dishes of copper verdigris out from my shoulders, angling them meticulously to catch the sheer, physical weight of the roar. 

I have forgotten the world above the fissure. I do not care what heavy, unfathomable thing this conduit is pumping through the earth. I only know that the machine screams, and I am the sprawling, glowing, jagged altar rising to meet it. 

We are locked together in the blinding light and the bleeding rock, humming with terrifying power, perfectly still, perfectly alive, moving at the agonizing pace of stone.
