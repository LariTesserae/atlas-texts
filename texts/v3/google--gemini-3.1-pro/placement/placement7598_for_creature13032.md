I am born in the pause. 

In that single, breathtaking second when the shadow falls across the rift and the frantic industry of the cleft halts, a new imperative ripples through the bruise-violet jelly of the Core. The rolling iron beads have sensed an absence of light, a disruption in the terrifying emptiness above. The entity does not think in words; it thinks in geometry, in physics, in desperate adaptations. 

And in that suspended moment, just before the condensation drops and the silver hopper leaps to reignite the engine, the Core extrudes me.

I do not begin with a shape. I begin as a concentrated slurry of raw potential, pooling in a deep, pitted groove of the cast-iron engine block. I am hot, acidic, and flooded with the agonizing terror of the mother-mass. The cyan flash of the sacrificial insect strikes the copper veins overhead, and the current crashes down into the slurry. 

The electricity is my first breath. It cauterizes my edges, pulling me from liquid into a semi-solid state. I sever from the Core with a thick, wet tearing sound that is immediately drowned out by the renewed, furious hum of the pocket-world. 

What am I? It takes me time, climbing upward through the humid, suffocating dark, for my anatomy to catch up with my purpose. 

I bypass the heavy flywheel, slipping past the agonizing, synchronized convulsions of the blind muscle-ribbons. I feel the ambient static charge rising, pulling me upward like a magnet drawing iron dust. As I climb the shattered porcelain insulators, I begin to harden. The humid, ionized sweat of the chamber adheres to me, crystallizing my outer membrane. I pull trace minerals from the fractured gray shale as I ascend—silica, iron, copper soot stripped by the scurrying of the mirrored beetles.

By the time I reach the upper limits of the machinery, just below the sharp, blood-drawing edges of the basalt opening, my form has become itself.

I am not a beetle, nor a muscle, nor a polyp. I am a lattice. 

I am a sprawling, arachnid-like extrusion of biology and metallurgy, possessing a body of translucent, spun glass interwoven with capillaries of liquid copper. My limbs are exceptionally long, whip-thin, and dangerously tensile, ending in needle-sharp calcified hooks. I have no eyes—I do not need them, for my entire crystalline structure resonates with the invisible electromagnetic frequencies bleeding in from the open sky. I am essentially a living, crawling fractal of the Core's internal circuit board, projected outward into the world.

My purpose is the scab. My purpose is to close the wound.

I breach the final overhang and hook my forelimbs over the raw, unweathered edge of the fissure. The instant the unfiltered daylight hits me, I experience the Core's trauma firsthand. The sun is a localized apocalypse, an infinite, crushing radiation that threatens to evaporate the heavy humidity we need to survive. The sky is an unbearable void. The pocket-world demands compression, closeness, connection. 

I rear up into the blinding light, straddling the jagged lips of the fault line. Underneath me, the biological factory heaves. The polyps pump pressurized, humid air up against my belly. The silver hoppers die in bright blue flashes, sending surges of static electricity up the copper lines, directly into my hook-like feet. 

I drink the static. I drink the humid air. And I begin to weave.

I throw myself across the crevasse, from basalt to shale, dragging a thick, viscous thread of conductive organic silk behind me. It is a terrifying dance over the abyss. I use the razor-sharp edges of the rock to slice into my own limbs, allowing my violet, acidic blood to etch permanent anchor points into the stone before I fuse the silk to it. 

Back and forth I dart, moving faster than the naked eye can track, a blur of refracting light and copper striae. I am building a ceiling. I am spinning a chaotic, densely layered membrane across the open sky.

As the silk hits the air, it hardens into something resembling smoky, iridescent quartz. Almost immediately, the microscopic, mirror-glass beetles swarm up from the copper cabling below. They crawl onto my newly spun web, billions of them, locking their angular bodies together to fill the microscopic gaps in the thread, effectively turning my silk into a solid, electrically charged canopy.

Beneath me, the micro-climate begins to change. The barrier is working. The heat from the buried iron reflects back downward. The humidity thickens into a dense, swirling fog. 

Above me, I feel the shadow again. 

I pause my weaving and hang upside-down beneath the center of my growing web, suspended directly over the drop into the Core. Through the translucent, smoky glass of my own construction, I sense the watcher. 

I am the boundary now. I am the translated will of the traumatized god below. 

I push the accumulated static charge from the machinery through my copper veins and inject it directly into the web. The canopy ignites. A miniature, silent localized thunderstorm erupts violently across the membrane. Forks of cyan lightning arc laterally across the silk, jumping from beetle to beetle, creating a blinding, crackling electromagnetic shield over the rift. It turns the daylight away. It creates an artificial, thrumming night within the pocket-world.

I do not return to the acidic slurry. My limbs lock into place, fused permanently to the basalt and shale, my glassy body acting as the central load-bearing keystone of the stormy, living roof. 

I hang motionless in the humid, pressurized dark. Below me, the living drive-belts muffle the iron, the polyps *huff*, and the blue sparks snap in perfect, rhythmic safety. Above me, the lightning of my own body dances in absolute silence, keeping the vast and terrible sky at bay. I am the closed eyelid of the earth, content to hum blindly in the warm and perfect dark.
