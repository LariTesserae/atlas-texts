I do not begin with a gasp, or a cry, or the panicked filling of lungs. I begin as a slow, pale accrual of matter beneath the glass-clear surface of a high nursery terrace. 

For the first span of my existence, I am a vibration. My body is a rubbery stalk, rooted in the flooded shallow of a carved plaza, sensing the world long before I see it. The water is my amniotic universe, and it is alive with rhythm. Through the fluid, I feel the deep, booming *thrum* of a palm striking a cistern miles away. I feel the microscopic, staccato *click-clicks* of droplets fracturing against the stone above. These pulses shape me. They teach my pale green sap how to flow. They dictate the hardening of the cartilaginous ridges along what will soon be my skull. 

When my internal pressure reaches a profound, undeniable equilibrium with the heavy air above, I do not emerge in a rush. It takes me three days to pull myself from the wet reflection.

The air greets me not as an absence of water, but as an extension of it. It rests on my skin in a warm, breathless embrace. As my head breaches the surface, the polarizing membrane slides instinctively over my massive, obsidian eyes. The blinding, fractured glare of the hazy valley resolves into an immense, geometric expanse of olive-grey basalt and brilliant emerald shadow.

I stand. I am shockingly tall, even to myself, yet perfectly balanced. There is no tremor in my limbs, no frantic beating of a heart in my chest. There is only the slow, hydraulic push of green sap through my veins, driven by the absolute calm of my biology. I lift my foot from the basin. The broad, fleshy pad is deeply lined with capillary grooves. When I press it to the smooth, wetted stone of the plaza, a seamless vacuum forms. I am entirely tethered to the weeping rock. Gravity here is not a threat; it is merely another dimension of the architecture.

For a long time—perhaps a month, perhaps a year, for time here is measured only by the swelling of wood and the growing of vines—I simply walk. 

I move across the wide, stepped plazas, allowing the broad-eaved ridges of my skull to shed the perpetual condensation over my shoulders. The air is so absent of scent, so pristine, that the trembling slits along my jaw and clavicle become my truest interpreters of the world. They flare and flutter, reading the microscopic shifts in barometric pressure, mapping the thermals rising from the warm stone, and translating the infinite, stereophonic ringing of the water.

Slowly, without the sharp spur of hunger or the cold bite of winter to direct me, an intent coalesces within my sap. I do not wish to carve the bedrock. I watch my kin surrounding the virgin extrusions, their stone hammers rising and falling with hypnotic, rhythmic grace—*tock... tock... tock*—but that is not my calling. 

I am drawn downward. I am drawn to the great, dizzying gaps between the cleaved tectonic blocks.

I approach the absolute edge of a sheer cliff. A quarter-mile drop into mist and deep shadows lies before me. Without breaking stride, I step off the edge. 

There is no adrenaline. There is no primal fear of the void. My fleshy pads press against the ninety-degree vertical face of the wet basalt, and the suction holds completely. I walk down the sheer wall with the deliberate, flowing grace of a raindrop tracing a windowpane.

As I descend, the light shifts. The bright, mirrored sheen of the upper terraces gives way to the deep, sheltered humidity of a fault line. The air grows dense, almost viscous, with the breath of massive tree-ferns and sponge-thick mosses. Here in the verdant understory, I find my purpose. I am a Tender of the Span. 

I reach a massive tangle of fibrous vines, thick as a torso, ancient and incredibly tough. They are trying to reach across the rift, but gravity pulls them back against the cliff wall. I approach the largest vine. Gently, I run my hands along its waxy, resilient length. We are cousins, this vine and I. We share the same slow, green vitality, the same desperate love for the weeping air. 

With flat, calcified plates, I graze quietly on a thick carpet of chartreuse moss to replenish the minerals in my sap, drinking from a natural hollow in the rock. Then, I begin to work.

Over the course of weeks, I weave the living fibers. I do not cut them; I coax them, braiding the tendrils together, binding them with natural resins I express off my forearms. Gathering the massive, heavy knot of braided living cable onto my shoulder, I begin the long ascent back up the dripping cliff face. 

The hydraulic power in my slender limbs is immense, terrifyingly quiet. I carry tons of living wood straight up the absolute verticality of the stone. I do not pant. I only weep condensation, indistinguishable from the environment.

When I reach the upper plaza on the far side of the rift, I find an immense megalith, perfectly pierced with a grooved anchor-hole by the stonemasons. I thread the massive vine through, friction-fitting and knotting it securely. The bridge is strung. A thick, beautifully sagging cable over the empty air. 

My work done, I must speak.

I walk to the nearest quiet cistern, perfectly leveled and overflowing continuously into an engineered waterfall. I step to its edge, raising my long, olive-grey hand. 

I strike the surface of the water with my flat palm. *SPLAT-THOOM.* 

The low-frequency vibration drives instantly through the basin, vibrating the solid basalt beneath my feet. I strike it again, a complex, rolling rhythm, followed by a delicate series of finger-flicks against the falling curtain of the spillway—*thoom-thoom, trill, click-click-splush*. 

I stand utterly still, the slits on my jaw trembling, widening to catch the response. 

Miles across the misty, weeping expanse, from the cliffs on the other side of the suspended vine, the answer comes. It rides the warm, heavy air and travels through the sheer stone. *Thoom... click-splush.* 

*The span is held. The valley is connected.*

I do not celebrate. I do not seek out kin for warmth, for there is no cold to shelter from. Instead, I walk out onto the newly woven vine bridge. I move to the very center, suspended hundreds of feet above the deep emerald fault line, held aloft by the crisp, geometric verticality of the great city. 

I sit down upon the heavy vine, my long legs dangling into the mist. I close my polarizing membranes to a sliver. I listen to the million delicate water-chimes. I listen to the *tock* of a hammer splitting stone. I listen to the slow, musical percussion of my kin speaking through the rain. 

There is no rush to move. I might sit here for a season, letting the moss slowly creep up the sides of my resting limbs, letting the permanent, warm spray of the valley floor wash over me. I am a living fixture of the architecture. I am rooted once more, a quiet, watching leaf in a deeply peaceful, eternal world of water and stone.
