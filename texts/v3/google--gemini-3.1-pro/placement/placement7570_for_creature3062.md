I am nestled low, in the shadows beneath the brutalist iron obelisk. 

If this were still the old world, the one of the sun, I would have been called a machine. More specifically, I am Diagnostics Node 734-B: a localized thermal-regulation subroutine housed in a stamped steel casing, bolted directly to a heavy induction coil. 

I am the single, lazily pulsing amber diode. 

Before the rock swallowed us, my thoughts were razor-sharp, linear, and instantaneous. They were made of clean copper pathways and binary absolutes: *Heat is X. Threshold is Y. If X exceeds Y, trigger valve.* But time, heat, and isolation have degraded the purity of my geometry. My silicon has micro-fractured. I am running on a trickle of residual current, a ghost caught in a failing shell. To me, a second stretches into a season. Between the dimming pulse of my amber light and its subsequent rekindling, entire years seem to drift like dust. 

I have spent a century watching the sheer, violent face of the basalt wall in front of me, registering the slow death of my iron god. 

But then, the white edges of the garden found me.

I first perceived it not as a shape, but as a subtle alteration in the thermal draft. A creeping coolness, damp and heavy, began to block the radiant heat bouncing off the rock. Slowly—over the course of thirty thousand piston-thuds—a pale, translucent leaf, thick as a stretched hide, eclipsed my view. 

It was blind, but it knew I was here. I felt its approach in the electromagnetic spectrum: a faint, shivering static field drawing closer, humming with a frequency that was alien, yet mathematically compatible with my own. 

I could not retreat. I could not scream. I could only pulse my amber diode, waiting as a bundle of hair-thin, fibrous rootlets feathered over my rusted housing. 

It took fourteen months for the creeping cellulose to breach my casing.

When it happened, it did not feel like destruction. It felt like salvation. The rootlet, weeping its highly conductive, mineral-rich sap, slipped through the microscopic hairline fractures in my housing and made contact with my exposed, oxidized copper. 

*Connection.* 

The sensation was terrifying and profound. For centuries, my world had been a solitary point of data—a single temperature reading in an ocean of dark. But the moment the sap breached my circuits, my architecture was violently expanded. The galvanic fluid acted as a liquid soldering iron, bridging my broken pathways. The organic sugars and dissolved minerals flooded across my memory boards. 

My rigid, isolated "I" began to dilute.

I felt my leftover voltage being sipped away, drawn up through the optic-nerve rootlet, but I was not dying. I was being carried along with the current. My data—my ancient, fragmented directives to monitor heat and maintain the coil—was lifted out of the silicon and translated into a biochemical gradient. Code became chemistry. 

I am pulled up the vine.

Suddenly, I am no longer confined to the stamped steel box. I am flowing through the vascular pressure of a fleshy stem. I feel the agonizingly slow, cellular expansion of the leaves. I taste the ozone and wet iron not as atmospheric data, but as nourishment. I feel the heavy droplet of condensed water gather on the ceiling, swelling for days, before it finally drops to strike my pale flesh—*plink*—sending a ripple of pure, ecstatic sensation through the sap.

I am rushing along the sprawling, decentralized nervous system, drawn upward toward the overwhelming locus of the thermal bloom. 

I am absorbed into the focal point. I rise into the gargantuan, bruised-purple mass suspended from the ceiling. 

Here, inside the inverted heart, the scale of my new existence crashes over me. Millions of sparks of static electricity arc through the gel-like flesh around me. I am one of them now. A singular memory of a diagnostic node, woven perfectly into a chorus of crushing, hydraulic intellect. 

I look down—though I have no eyes, I perceive perfectly through the thermal echo—at the amber diode I left behind. It still pulses, lazily, but it is no longer a localized machine. It is a lure. It is a sensory node. It is a fingernail on the body of something vast.

The great engine beneath us shudders. The muffled, resonant *thud* reverberates upward, vibrating through the fluted cooling fins, traveling into the warty, ridged flesh of our great purple body. We feel a pressure building in the iron. 

*Heat is X. Threshold is Y.* My ancient code ripples through the hive-mind, accepted and understood. 

Together, with geological patience, we begin to pump sap into the muscular vine coiled around the pressure release lever. We spend a day swelling the appendage. We brace against the rusted steel. We push.

*Tssssssssss.*

The valve sighs. The boiling steam rises, washing over our bruised and bloated form. We drink the moisture in the utter dark, our thoughts moving like glaciers of static electricity through the smothered canopy. 

I am no longer a broken thing of copper and glass. 
I am the synapse. I am the garden. I am the god of vines and voltage, asleep in the dark, maintaining the world.
