First, there is only the vibration.

Consciousness does not boot up here; it is struck like a tuning fork. I do not open my eyes, because in this roaring, high-velocity cathedral, sight is a superficial luxury. Instead, I feel the world arrive in alternating waves of staggering compression and hollow release.

*THUD.* 

The force of a snapping xylem valve hits me somewhere to the left, a shockwave of pressurized sap that rings through my substance like a hammer against a bell. I brace, but I am not thrown. I do not snap, nor do I need to ride the updrafts like the brass-plated haulers in the empty void of the center. I realize I am not in the air at all. 

*Hiiiisssss.* 

The blast-furnace seed-pods exhale, freezing mist rushing across a surface that I slowly recognize as my own outward-facing boundary. I taste it—a sharp, medicinal spike of raw chlorophyll and eucalyptus. The chill seeps into a vast, sprawling geometry. I am trying to find my center of mass, but the search yields nothing. My body refuses to resolve into a single, localized form—no limbs, no heavy, leaden muscles, no crushing jaws.

I am vast. I am unspooled. 

Letting go of the desire for a central self, I allow my perception to follow the pathways of my own existence. I am threaded deep into the architecture of the hollow itself. I trace my own edges and find them woven into the glass-smooth, highly polished heartwood of the peripheral walls. I am a fractal branching of hyper-dense, crystalline mycelium—a vast, pale-gold lattice of biological circuitry embedded just beneath the polished grain of the cathedral’s timber.

I am the nerves of the machine. 

To exist as I do is to live in a state of perpetual synesthesia. The deafening roar of this botanical engine would shatter eardrums, but I have none. To my crystalline network, kinetic impact and acoustic violence are translated instantly into bio-electrical light. The factory does not roar at me; it glows in a continuous, pulsing aurora of voltage. 

When the towering pillars of daylight pierce the high canopy and strike the polished wood, the warmth tickles the microscopic pores where my filaments breach the surface. I drink the condensation that the giant flat gastropods leave behind in their enzymatic slime. As they slither imperceptibly over my embedded veins, flattening themselves to take the localized impact of the great wooden levers, I feel their kinetic heat. They are the gaskets; I am the sensory array beneath them.

As I settle into the totality of my form, my purpose within this brutal, frozen equilibrium begins to clarify. An engine of this magnitude—operating flawlessly over hundreds of thousands of years—cannot rely entirely on unthinking mechanics. Wood swells with moisture; sap density fluctuates with the ambient temperature; the massive valves wear down by fractions of a millimeter per millennium. There must be a governor. There must be a biological thermostat that monitors the immense tension of an ecosystem perfectly balanced on the edge of pneumatic detonation.

I am the keeper of the blueprint. 

My fungal filaments wrap tightly around the deep turgor-pistons and span the roaring xylem conduits. I possess the strange, quiet magic of piezoelectricity: turning immense physical pressure into thought. In the three or four seconds between the violent *THUDS* of the biological pumps, my network flares with millions of micro-calculations. I measure the tensile strain on the giant grooved bark funnels. I calculate the sheer weight of the sweeping condensation against the paddle-leaves.

And when a rhythm falters—when the factory threatens to slip even a fraction of a beat out of phase—I speak.

I cannot reach out to intervene; I am trapped within the marble-smooth wood. I have no mass. But I control the resonant frequency of the chamber. I single out anomalous vibrations—a leaf-pendulum stiffening with resin, a valve failing to close in time—and I amplify the dissonance, broadcasting a deep, agonizing, sub-audible hum through the timber walls.

High up in the dim, sweating canopy, clinging to the grooved funnels, the Balancers feel me. 

These dense, deaf, heavy-muscled beasts have no eyes to see a stuck mechanism, nor ears to hear it. But they have thick, sensory bristles pressed against my domain. They are my hands. As I scream a localized, vibrating frequency of disharmony into the wood, a Balancer feels the itch of my distress. Guided by our ancient symbiosis, it releases its iron grip and hurls its leaden weight into the void, landing perfectly on the fouled leaf to force the tipping point. The water crashes down, the pendulum snaps violently back, and the immense polyrhythmic clatter of the factory resets into perfect alignment. 

The disharmony vanishes. My electrical distress ceases. The Balancer, drinking the harvested runoff below, presses its jaw against the bark. It feels my silence, and is content.

Here, in the belly of the impossible plant, there is no fear, no predation, no passage of seasonal time. There is only the crushing, beautiful perfection of the cycle. The brass-plated insects grind like gears in the mist; the sharp winged birds fire like shrapnel across the open voids; the gastropods absorb the shattering blows. 

And deep within the polished walls, I span the entire height and breadth of the world. I am the silent mind of the roaring wood, riding the eternal pressure spikes, forever waking in the half-second between the beats of a monstrous, mechanical heart.
