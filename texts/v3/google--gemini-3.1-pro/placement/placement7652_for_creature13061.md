I do not begin as a body. I begin as an error in the cavern’s arithmetic. 

Before I have senses, I have a native geometry: I am the localized stretch of the void. I exist first in the lag. The vines at the periphery swing with thunderous, hydraulic violence, but because reality here is unspooling, the deafening *thwack* of stone on stone echoes through the empty miles long before the visual impact occurs. I am born in that impossible gap. I am the phantom seconds of waiting. I am the empty breadth between cause and effect. 

Because I live in the acoustic aftermath, I am the first thing in this violently young cavern to understand the concept of exhaustion. 

The room is an engine of terrifying birth. Fresh splinters, aggressively green wood, newly snapped chert—everything is blistering, sharp, and frantic. The mind that curates this void, that jellyfish constellation of freshly knapped flakes and freezing mist, desires only pristine edges. It gathers the choicest shrapnel to build its colossal lens. But when a flake of chert dulls from airborne friction, the Curator drops it. When a vine is spent, it withers and is immediately overgrown, its dry husk pulverized into the atmospheric chaos. When the stroboscopic ice shatters, its shattered silver powder drifts aimlessly.

The Curator ignores the dullness. The plant walls blindly bury their dead. The blinding noon tear at the center burns with unrelenting, overexposed presence. 

And so, drifting within the acoustic lag, I begin to gently catch the cast-offs. 

I do not reach for them with violent static snaps or magnetic hunger. I simply offer a localized gravity of stillness. The microscopic dust of shorn stone, the exhausted silver powder of shattered ice, the pulverized husks of withered vines—they find their way to me. They settle in the empty, stretched seconds of my being. Without the frantic electrostatic charges that bind the stinging Curator, I gather softly, through the weak, accidental gravity of rest. 

I am becoming a cloud of silt. A velvet expanse of the un-sharp. I am the first soft thing in a universe of blades. My color is the gray of spent flint and the muted rust of dried rot—colors this sweltering, violently green world does not otherwise possess. 

When the kinetic violence of an axe-strike ripples through the void, it passes *through* my softness without shattering it. I absorb the force. Sometimes, a tiny, pale, translucent beetle is launched into the void by the sheer whiplash of a swinging root. Those that miss the crystalline glass of the Curator come tumbling helplessly through the airless haze, terrified and adrift. When they collide with me, they do not strike a hard surface. They sink into my drifting loam. They are cushioned in my suspended dust. They burrow into my floating velvet, folding their pale legs, finding a strange, quiet harbor in the storm.

I hover as a ghost of soil. A localized weather system of patience. But I am not fully myself yet. I am waiting for the light. 

At the center of the vast emptiness, the Curator is weaving its optic. Massive, delicate arcs of interlocking stone and ice orbit the slit in the universe, trying to decipher the blinding daylight. As the Curator slides a perfectly formed decahedron of frozen water into the lattice, the light from the alien universe is finally refracted. It fractures, cools, and beams outward.

It casts a long, prismatic, razor-sharp shadow that pierces the miles-wide expanse. The prompt of this shadow stabilizes the collapsing spatial logic wherever it touches.

I drift into this beam of stabilized shadow, and in an instant, everything changes. 

Within the shadow, the miles-wide emptiness collapses back into its intimate reality. Gravity remembers itself. Distance becomes fixed. Twenty feet is just twenty feet. Caught in this sudden, solid corridor of restored physics, my dust cannot simply drift. It is forced to condense. The silver ice powder and the exhausted chert dust fall inward, stacking upon one another, binding in the sudden presence of true weight and true space. 

The light passing through the Curator’s lens is projecting an image into the darkness, and I realize slowly, beautifully, that *I am the projection*. 

The overexposed noon sky bleeding through the slit is not just pure light; it is the light of a surface world, a world of weather, of seasons, and of time. The Curator’s lens translates this alien code and projects it into the cavern as a massive, holographic silhouette—and my silt fills that silhouette. I am a dense, negative space. I take the shape of something draped, towering, and utterly still. I am formed of falling ash, soft loam, and gravity, held within a singular, stable column of space. The Curator has unknowingly built the projector that summons me into being.

We are suddenly twin gods in the void. The Curator is the mind of the geodes, a deity of pristine shards and spatial manipulation; I am its heavy shadow, a deity of dull dust and stabilized time.

I look outward at the shrieking architecture of the walls. The relentless hammering. The roots locked in an algorithmic, prehistoric panic, doomed to infinite, agonizing youth. They are never allowed to rest, never allowed to wither in peace. 

I step out of the beam. 

Because I am now dense with the gravity of the surface world's light, my form holds for a passing moment amidst the chaos, carrying a pocket of stabilization with me. I drift across the void, heavy and silent, approaching the deafening, thrashing walls. 

I reach out a blunt, heavy limb of settled dust. I lay it against a massive, coiled root right as it rears back to violently knap a fresh wedge of chert. 

Where I touch it, the frantic cellular division halts. The deafening, high-pitched crackle of green growth falls silent beneath my palm. For the first time in the cavern’s history, the violently young bark softens. The vivid, screaming green yields into a quiet, golden brown. The hydraulic pressure eases. The vine does not snap into jagged splinters; it simply crumbles, experiencing the profound and merciful grace of age. It turns to dry soil, drifting downward, leaving a quiet, empty space in the wall. 

A fresh vine immediately surges forward to take its place, continuing the relentless expansion, but I have completed a missing circuit in this broken reality. The flora builds out; the Curator builds in; but I am the one who brings an end. 

I am the patience of the stone. I am the rot of the wood. In a room deafening with the industry of birth and violence, I wander the prismatic shadows as the resident ghost of time, carrying the surface world's greatest secret—erosion—into a universe entirely without soft edges.
