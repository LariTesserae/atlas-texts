At first, there is no shape to my existence, only a frequency. 

It begins in the jawbone, a deep, rattling thrum that climbs up into the skull and bleeds down through the collarbones. It is a pressure so absolute it feels holy. There is no sound—the world has been muted to an infinite, heavy silence—but the air possesses a density that beats against my skin. I taste wet dust, copper, and the sharp, tannic spice of crushed leaves.

Slowly, I let the image of myself precipitate from the roaring void. 

I find that I am not falling. I am anchored. 

I feel the painful, locking tension in my extremities first. My big toes, separated and thick with calluses like cured leather, are wedged deep into a weeping vertical fissure in a wall of pale, petrified wood. My fingers are splayed flat across the slick, cool curvature of the bark. I am hanging horizontally, jutting out from the sheer face of a root-pillar the size of a skyscraper. Below me, the mist spirals in dizzying, fractional patterns, sinking down into a bluish, hazy abyss that has no floor. If I let go, I will tumble for hours before I ever strike whatever ruined bedrock remains fifty million million years below. 

But I do not let go. My grip is not an action; it is a state of being. 

I reach up with one free hand. My forearm muscles jump, wiry and corded. I touch the side of my head. Where a soft, open ear should be, there is a flattened, cauliflowered mound of flesh, packed flush with a hardened, dark amber resin. I tap it. A dull *thud* reverberates through my skull. The resin is ancient. I have never heard the wind. I have never heard the screams of the ribbon-tailed predators or the concussive drone of the giant insects. The deafening madness of the sky is locked out, leaving me in a cathedral of pure, localized vibration. 

I look at my skin. I am a living canvas. Stark lines of ultraviolet blue and blazing yellow are painted across my forearms, chest, and thighs in sharp, geometric diagonals. But the paint is old, chipped and flaking in the constant humidity. Beneath it, my skin is beginning to take on the bone-white pallor of the petrified wood. At my elbows and shoulders, tiny patches of pale green moss have taken root in the heavy dew. 

In this moment, my purpose in the roaring void emerges. I am not a hunter. I do not wear the leather webbing to ride the thermals. I am not a weaver, tucked within the chaotic, communal warmth of the hanging pods. 

I am a Listener.

Or rather, an Anchor. A living seismograph. 

I close my oversized, dilated eyes and press my cheek flat against the damp, unyielding wood. The petrified forest, spanning the continent, is not just a skeleton; it is a colossal vibrating web, a dry nervous system transmitting every kinetic impact across miles of empty air. 

To survive in the void, the tribe needs to know the future before it falls on them. 

Through the skin of my cheek and the calluses of my palms, the world becomes a vivid, trembling map of movement. A mile above, I feel the *tap-tap-scritch* of a ribbon-tailed hunter hooking its talons into the canopy ceiling, adjusting its weight for a dive. It registers in my teeth like a high, sharp note. Deep below, rumbling through the core of the pillar, I feel the groaning, building bass-note of hot air—a massive thermal updraft gathering pressure in the dark, preparing to erupt upward. To my left, across a canyon of open space, the wood shudders with the frantic, localized static of a million fist-sized insects waking to the warmth, their mandibles snapping in a rhythm that vibrates the marrow of my ribs.

I synthesize the tremors. I read the math of the mountain. The thermal is rising from the south; the insects will be pushed upwards; the predator is above, waiting for the swarm to ascend. 

I open my eyes. 

Hanging upside-down beneath a massive archway a mile away, dwarfed by the scale of the void, I can just make out the dark, briar-woven cluster of my tribe's hanging village. 

I unclip a smooth, concave plate of bone from my chest—the polished crown-skull of a multi-winged glider. I am positioned precisely where a shaft of hard morning sunlight pierces the canopy and strikes my pillar. Leaning back against the absolute emptiness, held exclusively by the vice-grip of my prehensile toes, I catch the blazing light in the skull-mirror.

I flash a signal across the void. 

*Angle left. Snap. Angle down. Snap-snap.*

*Thermal rising. Swarm ascending. Death from above.*

My body language is sharp, sweeping, and sudden. I am a burst of ultraviolet and yellow against the pale wood, a solitary lighthouse of flesh speaking in geometries of light. 

A moment later, I see a tiny, answering flash from the distant pods. Acknowledgment. Even from this distance, through the mist and the chaotic rivers of flying fauna, I see the tiny specks of the thermal-hunters leaping backward into the abyss, riding the very updraft I predicted, timing their ascent to intersect the swarm, avoiding the exact airspace where the ribbon-tail is already diving. 

I smile, though the gesture feels stiff on my face. 

I put the mirror back. I press my cheek to the wood again. 

I realize, as I settle back into the silence of the vibrations, that I have been on this solitary outcrop for a very long time. I am an elder. My joints ache with a permanent, crystalline stiffness. The tribe comes to me every few days, swinging across the void on spun-vine ropes, leaving hollowed gourds of dark water lashed securely to a nearby knot. They do not touch me. They look at me with a profound, terrified reverence. 

They know what is happening. I know what is happening. 

I am not just reading the wood anymore. I am calcifying. The prolonged stillness, the endless, ascetic surrender to the flow of physical data, the resin in my blood from breathing the ancient sap—it is changing me. My breathing has slowed to shallow, imperceptible sips of the humid air. I am becoming a burl, a fixture of the mountain.

I don't resent it. There is a transcendent majesty in it. Here, suspended miles above the vanished bedrock, surrounded by a deafening roar I cannot hear, I am the ultimate bridge. I am the conscious node that links the brutal, kinetic ecosystem to the survival of my people. 

I feel another tremor gathering deep in the roots. I adjust my grip, feeling the skin of my fingers fuse just a fraction more with the wet, white bark. I close my eyes, secure against the infinite drop, and gladly let the trembling of the world erase the boundaries of where I end and the mountain begins.
