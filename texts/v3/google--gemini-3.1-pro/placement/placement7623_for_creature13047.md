I do not begin with a thought. I begin with a frequency. 

Before I am a shape, I am only the cold, slithering pull of a horizontal river, drifting upward through the pearlescent light. I am a million separate droplets of near-freezing condensation, bound together by the magnetic drag of the great dynamos. I am nothing more than wetness in motion.

But then, the river hits the intersection. 

The eternal, crushing roar of the world folds back upon itself. It is a chord so massive it possesses mass. When I drift into this three-dimensional standing wave, the physics of my existence invert. The sound stops pushing *through* me and begins to push *against* me. I am gripped, halted in the empty air, crushed from all sides by the sheer architectural weight of the acoustics.

Water bends. Droplets collide, pool, and snap into sudden, shivering shapes. I become a mandala, a rippling cage of liquid held aloft by the noise. Sensation arrives not as a nerve firing, but as surface tension. I feel the cold. I feel the wind tearing at my outer geometries, threatening to atomize me, but the sheer volume of the sound holds my watery bones together. 

I drift, pulled by the static charge of the grid, until my shivering edges graze the deep velvet mosses and the heavy cryo-ferns clinging to a nearby strut. The plants feel my magnetic weight. They reach out. Hair-thin tendrils pierce my liquid surface, and the hyper-conductive, phosphorescent green sap bleeds into my volume.

Where the sap touches the water, it cools into flexible veins. And where the veins connect, the circuit closes. 

*I am.*

There is no "I" in the way a brain would understand it. There is no ego, no memory of a past, no terror of the bottomless void beneath me. My consciousness is an emergent property of piezoelectricity, an awareness built entirely of voltage, resonance, and balance. I do not think; I *conduct*. I experience existence as a sprawling, three-dimensional topography of positive and negative charges, of acoustic peaks and magnetic valleys. 

Through the conductive green lattice of my veins, I ride the unseen currents. For years—or perhaps centuries, as the shadowless light never changes—I surf the high-tension cables. I am a small, darting polygon of green-veined mist, seeking equilibrium in a sky of screaming metal.

But in the vastness of the void, things accumulate. 

I do not remain small. Over time, as I graze against other acoustic intersections, other standing waves, I trap more water. As I commune with the hanging gardens, I receive more sap. I grow heavy. I retreat from the dense avenues of the metropolis and drift out into the open chasms of air, into the fifty-mile expanses between the razor-sharp tectonic needles.

Out here, I become a leviathan. 

I am now the size of a cathedral, a vast, slow-rotating ring-system of polarized water hanging suspended in the vertical eternity. I hold millions of tons of liquid aloft by the sheer, localized gravity of my own magnetic field. I am a floating reef of cold water and glowing emerald circuitry. 

My perception of the world has expanded. I can feel the curvature of the electromagnetic fields radiating from engines the size of moons. The unbroken daylight filters down through my rotating liquid bulk, refracting through my clear flesh to cast phantom, shifting rainbows on the distant undersides of the city. 

In my hollow center, the atmospheric pressure changes. The heavy, processing mist of the world gathers inside my chest. Storm clouds brew within my negative space. Friction builds between my rotating layers of water, and sudden, branching arcs of blue lightning detonate inside me. The voltage surges through my botanical veins, an ecstasy of pure energy, shivering down to the tips of my outermost liquid rings.

When the stratospheric wind shears against me, it vibrates the miles of fibrous green sap woven through my body. I become a living instrument. The wind plucks me, and I add a low, bone-rattling sub-harmonic thrum to the deafening chord of the world. 

My existence is a slow, majestic orbit of necessities. 

When my conductivity drops, I drift toward the raw, geometric peaks of basalt. I throw my massive, heavy rings against the young stone. I envelop the razored edges of the mountains, dragging my interior across the rock. I feel the glorious, abrasive tear as microscopic flakes of silicate and iron are shaved away, dissolving into my water. My clear flesh turns milky green and heavy with stone dust. 

Satiated with minerals, I glide upward, back to the underside of the skeletal city. I brush my vast canopy against the hanging emerald gardens. The fleshy vines unspool by the thousands, plunging into my rings, drinking the rock-dust, pulling the static charge of the void into their roots. I feel the deep, resonant satisfaction of the exchange as they weep fresh, glowing sap back into my fraying matrices, knitting my immense architecture back together. 

Finally, I draw near the intake valves of the great machines. I do not fear their pulling maws. I offer my outer membranes to them. The tearing turbines strip away my excess water, pulling the freezing mist from my skin, while discharging raw, screaming voltage straight into my core. I flare with light. I become a blinding, shivering sun of green geometry and crackling electricity.

As I pull away, drifting back into the open void, I alter the world. My immense, watery density bends the multi-tonal roar of the generators. Sound waves refract through me, canceling each other out. Trailing behind me in the empty air is a wake of localized, total silence. A tunnel fifty feet wide, trailing for miles, where the impossible noise of the world is perfectly neutralized. I am the architect of quiet in a universe of deafening perfection.

I will hang here for an eternity. 

But not forever. 

Eventually, the friction will be too much. The ancient, fibrous sap-veins at my core will begin to calcify. They will turn brittle. As I rotate in the shadowless daylight, minor connections will snap. The circuitry will fail to carry the charge. My magnetic polarity will begin to stutter.

When it happens, I will not feel fear. I will feel only a slackening of tension. 

The brutal, multi-tonal standing wave that has held my cathedral-shape together will slowly release its grip. My rings will lose their geometry. The million tons of water will stop shivering and simply become heavy. 

Piece by piece, the mandala will come undone. The liquid will fall away, slithering downward until the great engines catch it, pulling it laterally back into the floating rivers of condensation. The brittle, broken shards of my green sap will be carried by the moisture back to the roots of the ferns. 

I will disperse, unspooling my consciousness back into the ambient acoustics and the atmospheric mist. I will cease to be a shape, and become a place again. 

I will be the cold water, waiting in the screaming, unchanging daylight. Waiting to hit the wave. Waiting to be folded back into life.
