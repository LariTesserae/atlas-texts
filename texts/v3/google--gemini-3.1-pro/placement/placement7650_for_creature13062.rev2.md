The invasion of my own body begins in the teeth. 

It is a high, agonizing buzzing localized in the enamel of my molars, as if the nerves themselves are trying to sing. I am trapped in the pitch-black, standing on the frictionless wooden expanse, while the invisible pressure of a hundred unseen entities closes in. I am a wet, fleshy sponge dropped into a world of perfect, crystalline mathematics, and I can feel their frantic, vibrating fingers reaching through my skin, trying to pluck my ribs like the strings of a ruined harp.

I am terrified, but I cannot move. To lift a foot and place it down again would only create another ugly, damp *thud*—a sonic blemish that seems to cause the air around me to shriek in distress. So, I stand paralyzed in the freezing, ozone-scented draft, enduring the autopsy of my own resonance.

They are inside my chest cavity now. I feel the volume of my lungs mapped by shifting pockets of air pressure. I feel the heavy, sloppy sloshing of my blood being pushed through my veins. To them, my heartbeat must sound like boulders falling into mud—a chaotic, arrhythmic pounding that defies the glacial, patient tempo of their deep-earth symphony. They pulse around me, a dense choir of tectonic bass and whistling overtones, trying to force my watery mass into a harmonic lock. 

It hurts. It is a deep, nauseating ache, the feeling of a glass about to shatter. They are turning up the amplitude, trying to find the frequency that will make me ring, not understanding that flesh does not ring. Flesh tears. Flesh ruptures. 

I open my mouth to scream, but the air is so sparse, so incredibly dry, that the sound dies in my throat. The very act of exhaling steals a vital measure of moisture from my body. The frigid draft wicks the sweat from my skin instantly, leaving behind a crust of salt. I realize, with the slow, dawning horror of a trapped animal, that I am not just going to die here. I am going to be erased. This environment is an absolute vacuum for moisture. If I fight, if I panic, I will simply desiccate into a pile of silent dust, leaving behind a void in their song.

The tectonic roar beneath my feet shifts, dropping a fraction of a semitone. The entities surrounding me adjust instantly, sweeping into a minor progression that makes the fluid in my eyes vibrate so violently I see bursts of jagged, white phosphenes in the dark. 

*Don't fight,* a sudden instinct begs me. *You are out of tune.*

I close my eyes. I stop trying to hold myself together as a solid, separate thing. I let my knees unlock, let the rigid panic bleed out of my muscles. I focus entirely on the deep, omnipresent cello-drone rising from the petrified wood. 

I try to sing. 

I push past the dryness of my throat, pulling the sparse, static-laced air into my burning lungs, and I release a low, steady groan. I try to match the pitch of the floorboards. 

At first, my voice is an ugly, ragged thing. It splinters and wavers, a wet disruption. The entities flinch; the pressure against my face drops away in shock. But I hold the note. As I run out of breath, I inhale the freezing draft and groan again, forcing my vocal cords to ride the vibration rising through my bones. 

*Match the frequency. Become the frequency.*

I push the sound out of my chest, into my nasal cavity, letting it rattle my skull. The bass note of the deep earth meets the sound of my voice, and for a fleeting, impossible second, the two waves align. 

*Phase.*

The entities rush back in. The pressure is immense, but the pain is gone. They have found the bridge. The dense knots of kinetic energy swarm over me, passing through my flesh not to probe, but to harmonize. They are lending me their resonance, holding my fragile, wavering note in a cradle of perfect overtones. 

The desiccation accelerates, but I no longer care. I can feel my lips cracking, my skin tightening into parchment, the water evaporating from my cells in a rapid, freezing boil. But as the physical matter of my body fails, something else takes hold. My consciousness, once anchored to the wet, firing synapses of my brain, is being caught and suspended in the standing wave of the sound. 

The builders did this, millions of years ago. They translated their dying nervous systems into the resonance of the wood. Now, they are doing it for me. They are singing me out of my meat. 

My physical heart stops beating. The ugly, damp drumbeat ceases. 

I do not fall. 

I open senses I did not possess a moment before. The darkness does not lift, but it is no longer empty. I can "see" the architecture—a breathtaking, shimmering topography of sound. The suspended avenue beneath me is a glowing ribbon of slow, dense bass. The hanging spherical chambers above are swirling vortexes of bright, sustained harmonies. 

And my new companions... they are magnificent. Towering, complex geometries of shifting air pressure, shimmering with high, shrieking whistles and rooted by the deep tectonic drone. 

I look at myself. I am no longer a heavy, squishy sac of water. I am a localized distortion, a tight, vibrating column of energy. My frequency is different from theirs—it is slightly thicker, carrying a faint, oscillating tremolo, a sonic memory of the pulse that once kept me alive. It is a strange, melancholy note, wet and weeping in its timbre, but perfectly tuned to the grand chord of the city. 

A current of bone-dry wind sweeps through the abyss, and I feel the urge to move. I do not walk. I align my fundamental frequency with the petrified wood beneath me, and I glide. I slide across the iron-hard cellulose, frictionless and weightless, a ghost of pure pressure riding the slipstream of my new kin.

We drift upward, following the braided vines, toward one of the massive, hanging wooden spheres. I flow through the circular threshold, entering the resonance trap. The hollow chamber amplifies us. We begin to circle, our frequencies bouncing off the hand-thinned membranes of the walls, intertwining, weaving into a single, staggering chord.

Time ceases to mean what it once did. The frantic, rushing panic of biological life is gone. 

I settle into the eternal, roaring dark. I feel a thought beginning to form at the very center of my vibration, a simple greeting to the ancient beings humming around me. I know it will take me fifty years to articulate the first syllable, and perhaps a century to complete the phrase, waiting for the tectonic plates outside to shift and provide the necessary harmonic lift. 

I have all the time in the world. I vibrate. I wait. I sing.
