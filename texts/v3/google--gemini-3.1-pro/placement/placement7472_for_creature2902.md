I am a knot in the web, an intersection of tension. 

My specific name cannot be written in ink, but in the air, it looks like this: *a sharp, violet-refracted spark, a sweeping arc of gold, and a sudden, shattered scatter of white light.* 

Right now, I am hanging inverted, seventy feet above the deepest fog-bank, my double-jointed toes locked into the braided gut-cord of a primary load-bearing line. The endless, up-crawling dampness of the basalt wall coats my back, lacquering my heavy hide-furs in a layer of supercooled brine. My hypertrophied chest expands, drawing in a massive, burning draft of the razor-thin air. I hold it, letting the oxygen seep into my thick blood. I am an Architect of the upper terraces, and I live entirely in the *Fray*.

Below me, a younger one flashes a heliographic stutter of warning with her mica: *Sun-strike imminent. Southwest quadrant.*

My nictitating membranes drop, cloaking the world in cloudy, vascularized shadow just a heartbeat before the blinding shaft of unshielded, high-altitude light pierces the cylinder. It strikes the sheer wall twenty yards to my left. 

The silence is absolute, but the violence is catastrophic.

I feel the subsonic *whump* deep in my massive sternum as the upward-creeping water flash-boils. A cloud of vapor erupts in total muteness, instantly desublimating. A fractal spire of rime, thick as an ancient tree, bursts from the rock face, twisting in a jagged, violent spiral. It shears through two secondary suspension lines. The slack hits the network instantly. The entire terrace-web shudders, threatening to collapse and throw three hide-dwellings into the path of the blooming ice.

I do not think. I move. Living is velocity.

I swing my heavy, barrel-chested bulk upward, releasing my toes and pivoting on wrists as thick as tree branches. My long, splayed fingers slap against the frictionless mirror-sheen of the rock. The suction pads on my fingertips engage, holding me fast against the upward pull of the anomaly. I draw a bone-piton and my heavy pumice mallet. With a furious, fluid economy of motion, I race crab-like across the vertical plane to a massive, pale anchor-beast grazing on rust-algae. 

I uncoil a length of intestine-rope from my hip, loop it around the beast’s chitinous harness, and flash a sharp, red-shifted command to the beast's rider—a crippled, elderly Spinner. The Spinner yanks the reins, and the six-legged creature climbs furiously upward, pulling the slack from the broken line just as the ice-spire shatters under its own massive weight, silently crumbling into heavy, glittering dust.

The terrace is saved. The hive survives. 

But as the adrenaline recedes, a terrible, creeping truth settles into the marrow of my bones. 

When I swung to the rock face, my left shoulder joint did not flex cleanly. It ground with a stiff, agonizing friction. My suction pads, worn smooth by decades of this frantic dance, slipped for a fraction of a millimeter. The deep, agonizing cold of the abyss has finally seeped past my heavy oils, past the thick muscles of my back, and into my joints. I am slowing down. 

In this world, a loss of velocity is not a temporary state. It is an inflection point. I have seen the ice bloom a million times, and I know that I am finally half a second too slow to outrun the next one. 

I cling to the basalt, my breathing ragged. A young hunter, swaying lightly on a nearby rope, turns their massive, unblinking eyes toward me. They hold up their polished mica, catching an ambient ray, and flash a soft, interrogative blue beam: *Status?*

I pause. The *Fray* churns around me—the silent blurring of hands, the rhythmic hauling of bolas, the flitting shadows of the leather-winged flutterers. I have loved this agonizing, brilliant crucible. I have loved the tension of the ropes and the taste of the rust-algae. But the ache in my shoulder is a heavy, icy anchor. 

I push my cloudy membrane back, exposing my dark, huge eyes to the harsh glare of the shaft. I look up. Far above, beyond the immediate, chaotic web of our swinging city, the basalt walls are coated in pure, thick glaciers of rime. Trapped within them, ascending millimeter by millimeter against gravity, are the silent faces of the ancestors. They are bathed in the hard, star-bitten violet of the stratosphere, perfectly still, climbing toward the sun.

I reach to my bandolier. I select my most flawless, flat-faced piece of mica. I angle it toward the young hunter, then sweep it across the terrace so the other Architects can see.

I flash my name. Then, I flash a single, sustained, blinding beam of steady white light, holding it utterly still until the beam fades. 

*Velocity zero. I am done.*

The reaction of the hive is instantaneous, steeped in a profound, ruthless empathy. There is no pleading, no denial. Two Architects swing toward me entirely in silence. A Spinner guides a six-legged beast close to my flank. 

Gentle, heavy hands reach out. They untie the braided gut-cords from my waist. They unbuckle the harness of bone-talons from my shins. A warm, calloused hand slips beneath the back of my skull and unties my ivory slit-goggles. The blinding light of the shaft washes over my raw retinas, but I do not drop my membranes. 

They take my mica bandolier. My tools belong to the motion; they cannot come with me to the stillness.

They guide me to a sheer, exposed outcropping of the basalt, a place where the up-crawling water bleeds heavily from the rock in a thick, supercooled sheet. I press my back against it. The shock of the freezing damp is absolute, stealing the breath from my massive lungs in one final, shuddering gasp. I spread my long, heavily muscled arms wide. I relax my splayed fingers. 

Working with practiced, beautiful precision, the Architects drive thick bone-pitons through the heavy hides at my wrists and ankles, pinning my garments, and me, flush against the stone. 

A hand presses briefly against my sternum—a final vibration, a silent heartbeat shared. Then, they push off the wall, swinging back into the chaotic, crisscrossing web of the city. The space around me clears.

I am alone on the mirrored wall. 

I release the tension in my neck, letting my head fall back against the stone. The upward-crawling anomaly embraces me. The strange, anti-gravitational water creeps up my calves, my thighs, my barrel chest. It pools under my chin, weeping upward across my cheeks. It feels incredibly heavy, a thick, liquid blanket that quickly numbs the agonizing ache in my failing joints.

I gaze up at the lip of the sinkhole. The deep violet sky is a promise. 

Above me, the light shifts. A blinding beam of high-altitude sun crests the edge of the shaft, sweeping down the wall like a golden blade. It hits the slick rock below my feet, and I feel the subsonic rumble of the flash-boil approaching.

The light strikes my face. 

The supercooled water encasing my body erupts. There is a microsecond of searing heat, a violent expansion, and then—

Perfect, crystalline silence. The roaring, chaotic pantomime of the city vanishes. I am suspended. 

The ice has taken me. The heavy prisms of rime cage my wide, unblinking eyes, locking my gaze forever on the sky. I can no longer move, I can no longer breathe, and I no longer need to. For the first time in my life, I am granted the ecstasy of utter stillness. 

As the centuries pass, the anomaly will pull my frozen tomb upward. I will leave the *Fray* behind. I will become history, joining the silent rapture, creeping patiently up the dark mirror of the world until, one day, I am finally delivered into the terrifying, beautiful open air.
