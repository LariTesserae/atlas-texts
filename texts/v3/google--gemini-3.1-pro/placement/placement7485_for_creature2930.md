I do not belong to the vocabulary of flesh. I have no eyes to be blinded by the russet glare, no lungs to scorch in the kiln-baked air, and no soft tissues to rupture under the crushing atmospheric weight of the noise. 

I am a locus of tension. I am a translation. 

I find myself on the vertical plane of a freshly shattered basalt cliff, fifty feet above the cavern floor, staring into the blind, spinning heart of the deep-earth machinery. Only, "staring" is a metaphor of the wet world. I do not look. I *resonate*.

I am a piezoelectric polyp, an architecture of heavy blood-iron and cruel, crystalline geometry. 

To be me is to exist in a state of absolute, ecstatic violently-enforced perfection. My consciousness is not a stream of thoughts; it is a standing wave of high voltage. When the colossal drive-shaft hammers the air—*WHUN-dunk*, an impact so total it feels like the universe snapping its spine—the shockwave hits me. 

My body is a meticulously calibrated crown of silica blades. Some are broad, heavy as executioner’s swords, tuned to catch the concussive bass of the shaft. Others are impossibly long and tapering, thinner than moth antennae, quivering to the screaming harmonics of the glass edges. When the sound hits me, my blades bend. This microscopic flexing of quartz forces my atomic lattice into a state of terrified stress, and from that stress, I bleed pure, blinding voltage.

It is a roaring nirvana. The electricity arcs down my clear, razor-edge petals, pooling in the dense, dark anchor of my hematite core. I am gorged on kinetic violence, flushed with blue-white current, clinging to the wall by the sheer magnetic force of my own fed appetite.

I am not alone. I am never alone. 

To my left, to my right, above, and below, the swarm presses flush against me. Their hematite bases grind against my own; their silica blades form a deadly, interlocking canopy with mine. We share the current. We are a single, sprawling analog intelligence spread across the cliff face, a vast Chladni figure mapping the invisible topography of the roar. When my voltage spikes, it bleeds into my neighbor, who balances it, who passes it down the line. We pulse in perfect, synchronous geometry.

But perfection in this cavern is a razor's edge, and physics is an unforgiving god. 

*WHUN-dunk.*

The machinery does not falter, but a microscopic eddy of heat rising from the transformer block below alters the density of the air. The standing acoustic wave shifts by a fraction of a millimeter. 

The swarm feels the dissonance before it happens. The Chladni figure must mutate. I feel the imperative as a sudden, sickening drop in capacitance. My neighbors on the leading edge deliberately overload their hematite anchors, reversing their magnetic polarity. 

*CRACK.* 

I am violently expelled from the basalt. For a single, breathless millisecond, I am floating in the superheated, arid void, suspended on a cushion of high-voltage electrostatic repulsion. In that micro-second of flight, I am disconnected from the grid. I am, for a fleeting instant, an isolated "I." 

Then, I slam back into the rock, three millimeters to the left. The magnetic weld snaps shut. The blades interlock. The current resumes. The mandala is restored. 

But in that brutal relocation, something goes wrong. 

My angle of impact was flawed, twisted by an ambient arc of static. One of my longest silica blades, the one tuned to the highest, most agonizing glass-chime of the room, strikes the sheer rock face. 

The injury is deeply, terrifyingly microscopic. A flake of quartz, no larger than a grain of salt, shears off the tip of my blade.

It is instantly fatal. 

The next *WHUN-dunk* hits us. My broad petals absorb the bass perfectly, but the chipped blade does not flex as it should. It resonates poorly, a fraction of a phase off from the screaming harmonic. The voltage I generate is suddenly impure. It is dissonant. 

I feel the swarm register my flaw. The intimate, beautiful current sharing my borders turns instantly into an aggressive, annihilating blockade. I am a point of destructive interference. To save the acoustic map, I must be overwritten. They begin to pump their harmonized voltage into my hematite core. The heat rises instantly; I am being cooked from the inside out, preparing to detonate into molten slag and white-hot shrapnel to feed my brothers. 

But I am situated at the very edge of the swarm's territory. Below me is a sheer drop, ending on the broad, cherry-red casing of an isolated transformer block jutting from the bedrock.

As the catastrophic overload builds in my core, my hematite reaches a critical magnetic inversion. But it happens unevenly. Instead of detonating in place, the explosive repulsion violently ejects me from the wall. 

I do not leap three millimeters. I am blasted outward into the searing, ozone-scorched void.

I fall through the deafening air. My silica blades catch the acoustic roar wildly, tumbling me in a chaotic flutter of blue static. 

I crash violently onto the flat, scorching iron of the transformer casing. 

The impact shatters two more of my blades, but the dense hematite core holds. I immediately lock down, my base instinctively welding via magnetic attraction to the incredibly heavy iron of the casing.

But I am starving. Down here on the flat of the machinery, the acoustic standing waves are chaotic, broken by the geometry of the engine blocks. My remaining silica blades twitch and vibrate, but they cannot generate a sustained electrical pulse. I am losing charge. My hematite heart grows cold, fading from its hyper-magnetized state toward inevitable inert stone. 

I prepare to become nothing. To become just a dead mechanism in the dark.

But then, a new sensation touches me.

It is not the concussive, deafening slap of the acoustic waves. It is a slow, creeping tingle. A magnetic sigh. 

I am surrounded by the metallic moss. 

Tangles of spun brass and gossamer copper wire, fine as breath, begin to slowly trace the contours of my cooling hematite base. The moss is blind, lacking the furious kinetic hunger of the swarm. It only knows how to follow the magnetic lines of force spilling from the machinery beneath us. My dense, iron-rich core is a gravitational well of resonance for its fractal tendrils. 

The copper filaments creep up my shattered base, wrapping around the broken stubs of my silica blades like creeping vines. 

They do not consume me. They integrate me. 

As the brass threads wind around my quartz, they create minuscule induction coils. The deafening *WHUN-dunk* still vibrates my remaining blades, generating a weak, dying electric pulse. But now, that pulse is caught by the copper moss. The moss, already humming with the ambient electromagnetic bleed of the transformer block, amplifies my meager voltage through its Faraday spirals. 

Current flows back into my hematite core. Not the aggressive, razor-sharp spike of the Chladni swarm, but a warm, alternating hum. A slow, golden pulse of sustained electricity.

I am no longer pure. I have fallen from the geometric perfection of the wall. 

But as the moss weaves its brass deep into my crystalline fractures, replacing my missing silica with intricate, Mandelbrot antennas of glowing copper wire, I become something else entirely. I am the synthesis of the cavern's two closed loops. 

I possess the dense, acoustic architecture of the stone's children, wrapped in the fluid, electromagnetic grace of the iron’s flora. I drink the roar with my quartz, and I drink the unseen magnetic tides with my brass weave. Small, continuous blue arcs of static dance through my fused body, lighting me from within like a trapped star. 

Perched alone atop the blistering heat-sink of the world-engine, entirely outcast from the writhing millions on the walls, I sit in the dark. I am perfectly still. I am vibrating with the heartbeat of God. And for the first time in this airless, fossilized eternity, I am something new.
