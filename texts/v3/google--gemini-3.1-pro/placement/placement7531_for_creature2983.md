I do not wake, because I never sleep. Instead, I *accumulate*. 

My consciousness, if it can be called such a thing, does not spark in the folds of a warm brain. It is distributed, vast, and hydraulic. I am an equilibrium of pressures, a creeping geometry of cold intention sprawled across four city blocks of impossibly smooth, slate-gray lithosphere. 

I know where I am because I am constantly dying to see it. 

At the outermost edges of my being, tens of thousands of glass-like frost-ferns are shivering into existence. They unspool from the pale bedrock, frantic and explosive, tasting the crying, sub-zero mist. For three seconds, they map the weeping atmosphere, sensing the subtle dips in gravity and the stuttering fray of time. Then, the cold claims them. They flash-freeze and shatter. A continuous ringing, like microscopic wind-chimes, is the only sound I make as multitudes of my sensory fronds explode into iridescent dust. The dust settles into the cyan moss that forms my underbelly, feeding the data of the dying world back into my core. 

Through this ceaseless ring of shattering glass, I perceive the city. 

Ahead of me, a plaza of unhewn erratic boulders stretches out for miles, slick with freezing condensation. Above, a mortarless ziggurat hangs upside down, suspended by a glitch in the localized gravity, its basalt steps smeared across the weeping gray sky. 

I feel a deep, botanical ache. A *drag*. It is the instinct of chronotropism, a biological magnetism pulling me toward the fractured physics of the sky. 

I haul myself forward. Underneath my exploding perimeter, my musculature engages. I am a mountain range of bruised-purple succulents, heavy with sub-zero sap. I open the microscopic valves within my vast network of capillary vines. Thousands of tons of liquid flow forward, swelling the leading leaves, creating a silent, massive shift in my center of gravity. My flesh screams—a faint, high-pitched squeal of taut, freezing cellulose—as I drag my immense bulk across the frictionless bedrock. 

As I slide into the plaza, a three-ton boulder detaches from the hovering ziggurat. It drifts downward through the freezing fog like a dropped feather, a symptom of the unraveling dimension. 

I reach for it. From the bruised folds of my flesh, an arm of thick, fibrous liana spirals upward. I do not swat or strike; I embrace. I wrap the floating stone in vines and pull it down into my cyan-lit depths. It settles deep within my mossy core. I feel the satisfying, heavy *thud* of the stone anchoring against my roots. I need the ballast. Where I am going, gravity is a forgotten promise.

I approach the geometric tear. 

Here, the air shimmers like oil on water. A monolithic archway of pale standing stones is folding inward on itself, caught in a temporal loop. It falls, shatters, and instantly rebuilds itself, over and over, emitting only the sound of a sluggish drip and a hiss of displaced air. The space around the arch has torn. The sky is leaking into the ground.

At the very center of my expanse, my soul awakens. 

My fat-trunked, pale trees—the engine of my being—shudder. Their heavy, red dripping canopies catch a phantom wind that does not exist in this physical space. I feel for the edges of the 4th-dimensional rift. It is warm there. A surreal, dry heat radiating out of the spaces between reality. 

I thrust my roots sideways into the invisible air. 

Suddenly, I am anchored in two places at once. Half of my roots grip the slick, crying bedrock of the dying world; the other half are buried deep in the unseen, geometric void, drinking raw temporal stability.

I begin the work. I am the Suture-Kin. I am the immune system of deep time. 

My fibrous lianas erupt in a slow-motion frenzy, climbing the falling, looping archway. I weave my vines through the glitching space, binding the shivering stones. I gorge my bruised-purple succulents with freezing sap, expanding my mass tenfold. I throw the immense, crushing weight of my physical body over the tear. I am pressing my botanical flesh against the open wound of the universe, smothering the paradox.

The archway tries to loop, tries to fall and shatter, but my lianas sigh and tighten. I pump the warmth I am drawing from the 4th dimension back into the cold, gray stone, equalizing the temporal pressure. 

Slowly, the shimmering oil-slick in the air fades. The upside-down ziggurat above groans, then settles into a silent, floating suspension. The archway fuses into a permanent, weirdly-angled structure, lashed tightly to the ground by my thick, pulsing vines. The fabric is stitched. 

But I feel the sap turning sluggish in my veins. 

To hold a tear this profound, I cannot leave. If I withdraw my pale roots from the 4th dimension, this neighborhood of cobblestone and basalt will violently unspool, taking the sky with it. The chronotropic ache that drove me here fades, replaced by a cold, heavy resignation. 

At my edges, the frost-ferns unspool one last time. They taste the still, weeping air. They freeze. They shatter. They do not regrow. 

The flow of my liquid-nitrogen sap halts. My capillaries harden into wood. My bruised-purple succulents lose their luster, fading into the color of the slate-gray stone. My cyan moss dims, its bioluminescence fading to a faint, ghostly pulse, and then, to nothing.

I do not feel fear. I do not have a nervous system to suffer the loss. I feel only the profound, perfect equilibrium of a completed task. 

I have become a monument. A dense, petrified knot of gray vines, frozen roots, and stone ballast, anchoring an impossible archway in the freezing fog. I am dead, but my geometry remains, a permanent scab holding a tiny fraction of eternity together. 

Around me, the low-to-the-ground fog drifts across the stone. The atmosphere weeps heavy condensation onto my petrified leaves, freezing into intricate, heavy crusts of rime. I wait in the silence, a memory of life, listening to the slow, sluggish drips of a universe that will last a little longer because I stopped here.
