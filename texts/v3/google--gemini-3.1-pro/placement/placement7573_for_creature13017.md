There is no *I* in the dark. There is only the *Here*, and the *We*, and the endless, booming hum of the stone.

If I am anything, I am a knot of muscle and heat woven into a vertical ocean. My reality begins at the blunt plane of my skull plate, pressed hard against the slick basalt, and ends in the entanglement of wet, slate-grey down where my flanks press into the bodies of my neighbors. We are a single, breathing lung, clinging to the inside of the world.

Above, the warm rain falls—a perpetual, gentle hissing that breaks against my armored forehead and slides down my back in rivulets. Below, a thousand miles of chest-rumbles rise through the damp, ammonia-scented air. It is a concussive lullaby. I do not hear it with ears; I feel it through my bones, through the rigid dome of my head, vibrating up from the deep earth. It tells me that the colony is secure, that the geothermal updraft wraps us in its stifling, perfect womb, and that the work must continue.

My primary arms—thick as ancient trunks, aching with a beautiful, permanent tension—are anchored to the rim of a scooped hollow. My calloused fingers do not merely hold the rock; they read it. They know the exact curvature of the basin beneath me. Beside my right shoulder, the heavy knee of another presses into my ribs. Just below my feet, my toes bury themselves in the dense, matted pelt of a third. We shift slowly, a colossal glacier of flesh adjusting to the microscopic slippage of mud and the slow passage of time.

Tucked tightly against my belly, my secondary arms are in motion. In my lower right hand belongs a jagged chunk of black quartz, heavy and familiar, hauled from the abyss by generations past. 

*Clack.* I bring it against a microscopic ridge in the wall. 
*Scrape.* I drag it downward, leaning my immense weight into the motion. 
*Clack. Scrape.* 

This is not labor. It is respiration. It is existence. 

As I drag the quartz, the friction heats the wet stone. The indoor rain catches the pulverized basalt, turning it into a fine, pale paste. My lower left hand rubs this paste in slow, sweeping circles, buffing the raw cut into a weeping curve. Through the flat plate of my skull, I feel the resonance of my own grinding merging with ten thousand others. The cavern shakes with our industry. We are singing to the mountain, and the mountain is yielding. 

Suddenly, a new vibration cuts through the symphony. It is not the deep, rhythmic throb of our kind. It is sharp, erratic, and frantic. A scuttling tremor runs up the wall from the depths. 

A collective rumble of anticipation ripples through the pelts around me. The scent hits my wide, flared nostrils: metallic, cold earth, interwoven with the sharp tang of chitin. 

I pause my grinding. My neighbor pauses. The air pressure shifts violently as a dozen of us brace. The vibration draws closer, ascending the slick wall directly toward my hollow. 

It brushes my leg—a jagged, bristling leg that does not belong to the warm colony. 

Instantly, the lethargy of our eons shatters. My upper left arm releases its agonizing grip on the ledge, swinging outward in a blind, devastating arc. I collide with a massive, segmented shell, pinning the heavily armored centipede against the cavern wall. It thrashes, its mandibles snapping blindly, but my neighbor’s upper arm locks in beside mine. We have it trapped. 

Now, our lower hands become hammers. The chunks of quartz and volcanic rock we use to shape the world are driven down with brutal, practiced concussions. My quartz shatters the carapace. A wet, hollow crack echoes through our immediate vicinity. The insect curls and twitches, defensive acids spraying against the impenetrable dome of my skull plate, washing harmlessly into the rain.

Thick fingers pry into the splintered chitin. We tear the pale meat loose in great, sweet, steaming handfuls. I shove a mass of it past my flat teeth, chewing aggressively, the juices mixing with the rock-dust already in my mouth. I pull another chunk and push it blindly into the dark to my left. A neighbor’s heavy jaw accepts it. We feast in the ringing blackness, our chest-rumbles elevating to a purr of satiation that vibrates the wall. 

When the shell is empty, we let the carcass fall. I do not hear it hit bottom, swallowed by the roaring white noise of the cathedral. 

The frenzy passes. The heat of our bodies has risen, and the humidity hangs thick and cloying. We settle. 

From my right, a much smaller set of lower hands reaches over. It is a juvenile. Soft, nimble fingers begin to sift through the slate-grey down of my shoulder. The pale gray mud from my grinding has dried there, caking the waterproof insulation. The juvenile’s lower fingers patiently scrape away the heavy slag. I feel the gentle tugging, the meticulous combing. Occasionally, the hands pull away, and I hear the juvenile crunching the dried mud between its flat molars, extracting the salty sweat and deep-earth minerals we bleed into the dust. I lower my head, utterly relaxed, abandoning myself to this rough, necessary tenderness. I reach my own lower hands down to massage the deep knots of muscle in the neighbor below me, squeezing the dense shoulders until they sigh a sub-bass rumble. 

Time loses its shape. It is just the rain, the heat, the touch, and the eternal, curving wall.

Hours—or perhaps days—later, there is a sudden, sharp failure. 

Directly above me, an ancient grip gives out. There is no cry. There is only an abrupt, violent void in the acoustic pressure as a monstrous weight detaches from the wall. A terrifying rush of displaced air screams past my face. The down on my back flutters in the wake of the plunge. 

I feel the hole in our tapestry. I smell the fresh, raw, unheated air where the elder once clung. A collective, low-frequency hum passes through us—an involuntary acknowledgment of the empty hollow. 

Slowly, without thought, without grief, the ocean of biology adjusts. The creature above the emptiness slithers down. I loosen my upper grips and slide my immense bulk upward, finding the grooves of the vacant bowl. It is still warm from the life that just left it. It is perfectly polished, flawlessly carved over a hundred lifetimes. 

I lock my primary fingers into the root-holds. I settle my heavy belly into the curve. I press my shielded skull against the smooth basalt. I take my quartz in my secondary hands. 

*Clack.* 
*Scrape.*

The dark is absolute, and the world is just a little wider than it was yesterday.
