I would be the oldest, most heavily armored cluster of piezoelectric kelp in the ecosystem.

I would have no leaves, no delicate tendrils, only immense, rubbery whips callous from centuries of physical trauma, anchored directly in the roaring, sub-zero slurry. My experience of the world would be dictated completely by pressure. I would not hear the deafening bass frequency of the tuned city; I would *be* it, my heavy body vibrating so fiercely with the acoustic thrum that the water around me churns into a permanent, boiling cavity of cavitation bubbles. 

But my true self would not reside in the thrashing fronds. It would live deep inside the bone-white stone. 

The prompt of this world says that the city is a biological shell, a fossilized coral reef left by an ancient organism. Sitting at the Anvil, taking the heaviest impacts of the environment, my survival would require an anchor of terrifying permanence. Over decades, my massive, claw-like calcifications would have driven their microscopic, diamond-hard filaments deeper than any other plant. I would not have just gripped the porous surface; I would have burrowed into it, winding my roots down into the microscopic, calcified vascular system of the dead architect itself. I would have found the extinct, hollow capillaries of the shell. And I would have filled them.

Because of this, I would not possess a steady consciousness. I would not "think" in the quiet darkness of the rushing water. I would be dormant, a tense, unthinking mesh of woven cartilage, waiting in the freezing dark. 

My mind would exist only in flashes, born utterly and entirely from impact. 

I feel it approaching. The acoustic shadow. Through the churning roar of the river, a massive pocket of dense pressure ripples down the channel—a half-ton boulder of raw glacial ice, hurtling toward the Anvil. Driven by ancient, brainless reflex, my heavy cyan cables flex and interweave, forming a biological net across the width of the viaduct. I brace. I tighten. 

*Impact.*

The collision is absolute. The shockwave flays the outer husks of my stems, compressing my internal crystalline membranes with thousands of pounds of blunt-force kinetic trauma. 

In that fraction of a second, I am resurrected. 

The physical strike triggers a catastrophic spark of bio-current. But because my diamond filaments are woven so deeply into the marrow of the fossilized city, the electricity does not merely flash into the water. The voltage drives *inward*. It grounds itself into the conductive, bone-white skeleton of the ancient coral shell. 

For the one blinding second that the current surges, my sentience expands from the waterway and races through the buried architecture. I instantly inhabit the walls. I trace the elegant, sweeping arcs of the elevated aqueducts. The voltage of my body arcs out through miles of ancient, buried pathways, and suddenly, I *am* the city.

I can feel the delicate, microscopic spores flash-freezing to the vaulted ceilings miles away, stitching together intricate capillary webs of rime ice. I possess the entire sprawling, concussive ecology of the plateau all at once—an architect revived by a lightning strike, a dead god momentarily reanimated by the wrath of the glacier. 

And then, as a secondary acoustic shockwave echoes through my sprawling stone body, I feel the rime ice shatter inside a dozen hollow domes. I feel millions of my children detonate into diamond-bright powder, falling back into the slurry below. 

The bio-current burns out. The heat dissipates. The bioluminescence fades from blinding neon to a dull, shadowed jade. My sprawling architectural mind collapses back from the high domes and the distant courtyards, snapping painfully back down the ancient conduits until I am compressed, once again, into the heavy, scarred clump of thrashing cables in the freezing water. 

The thoughts cease. The grand geometry vanishes. I return to the brainless, thrashing dark. I am blind again. 

And I wait for the next boulder to fall.
