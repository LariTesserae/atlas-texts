I am anchored upside down in a sector the void-riggers call the Guillotine Narrows, my titanium locking-calipers clamped flawlessly onto the edge of a raw carbon-fiber heatsink. To my left, a monolithic bank of server racks is rushing endlessly upward on a vertical rail; to my right, an expanse of terrifying, empty void waits for the next permutation. 

I do not look at the void. I keep my eyes closed behind the polarized glass of my visor. Here, sight is a liability. It tricks you into trusting the geometry of the moment, but the Zenith Junction has no moments—only math, relentless and churning. 

Instead, I press my helmet hard against the carbon-fiber bulkhead. 

Through the solid contact, the silent world roars to life inside my skull. The bone-conduction is magnificent. My jawbook vibrates with the deep, localized groan of magnetic stresses building three sectors away. I can feel the high-frequency *shiver* of a spindle-weaver constructing a copper web just inches from my glove. I taste the deep-bass rumble of the thermal core adjusting its load in the cavities of my teeth. 

Someone has to map the labyrinth. The scrap-brokers need to know which walls will vanish and which chutes will open before they commit their fragile, water-heavy bodies to the gaps. They call me a Resonance-Reader. I sell time. I sell the future geometry of the sphere, predicted seconds before the tracks engage. 

But as I hang here in the bruised violet light, feeling the rhythmic, mechanical pulse of the station beat against my forehead, I know I am no longer just a reader. I am becoming something else. 

The prompt of my existence has begun to drift.

It started with my biological amputations. Like many veterans, I took my organic feet early, trading the clumsy meat for the flawless *tiss-clack* of magnetic calipers. But the Junction demands more. In a world where space and weight are calculated to the milligram, fluid is a heavy, dangerous burden. I began to despise my own biology. The compression-weave suit kept my blood from boiling, but it felt like a prison of humidity. I hated the slick slide of sweat inside the polymer membrane. I hated the wet, organic thud of my own heart interfering with the pristine, violent acoustics of the station’s shifting metal.

I began making modifications. To map the shifting architecture accurately, my nervous system needed to extend beyond the frosted amber shell of my suit. 

I deliberately damaged the electrostatic shielding around the nape of my neck.

When the next cascade happens—when the heat-sink I am bolted to drops sixty feet in a terrifying freefall—I do not flinch. I let my stomach float into my throat. The architecture aligns for a three-second window. I disengage my calipers, kicking off the plunging metal, launching myself backward into the vacuum. For two heartbeats, I am totally weightless in the black. 

I tear through an invisible web spun by spindle-weavers. The static charge of my friction ignites the microscopic aerogel filaments, painting the void in a momentary web of electric blue lightning. 

I snap my electromagnets on and slam into a passing mesh walkway, a perfect landing. *Tiss-clack.* 

The friction of my landing sparks a corona of static, and immediately, the ecology responds. Hundreds of pale, crystalline static-mites swarm over my boots, scrambling up my legs in a dry, rustling tide. They seek the voltage I just generated. Most brokers scrape them off. But I drop to my knees, lowering the damaged, unshielded nape of my suit to the deck.

They find the gap.

They cannot breach the pressurized seal, but they cluster against the thin thermal-transfer membrane where my spine meets the suit's diagnostic port. The mites are piezoelectric. As the station vibrates, their shifting micro-crystal joints produce voltage. Pressed against the induction nodes at the base of my skull, their collective electrical output bleeds directly into my suit's neural uplink, and from there, into the cybernetic tap wired into my brainstem.

My organic eyes are closed, but suddenly, the Junction blooms into blinding, three-dimensional brilliance.

I am not merely feeling the vibrations anymore; I am *seeing* them through the hive-mind of the static ecology. Every mite on every bulkhead acts as a microscopic seismograph. Every spindle-weaver's web across the sphere becomes an extension of my optic nerve. I see the vast, spherical slide-puzzle not as cold machinery, but as a glowing, shifting constellation of varying electrical potentials. 

Through the mites' piezoelectric chatter, I see a void-rigger two sections over, their heartbeat registering as a faint, rhythmic spark of static. I see a massive cluster of sleep-coffins rearranging themselves. I see the thermal algorithms balancing in real-time, the heat signatures washing in waves of violet and gold across the station’s vast internal curvature.

Mankind was not meant to process the architecture of a god. The sensory input is terrifying, absolute. Blood vessels burst in my nose, the droplets instantly caught by my rebreather mask, recycled, hidden away. 

From the deep thermal core beneath me, a monstrous shape begins to undulate upward. A Dielectric Hound.

It is thirty feet of jagged silver, a centipede of fused heat-fins and raw superconductor wire, woven together by thousands of spindle-weavers. It flows up the rails in utterly silent, agonizingly beautiful grace. It is hunting a high-voltage leak in a newly shifted server bank right above my head. 

The old me—the human Resonance-Reader—would have tapped a warning code on the rails, signaled a scrap-broker to come siphon the leak while the Hound fed.

The new me stays perfectly still. 

As the Hound glides past the mesh walkway, its immense, dry body brushes against my arm. The friction generates a localized storm of static electricity. To an observer, we are simply two shapes in the dim amber dark, bathed in a sudden flash of violent violet light. But in my mind, linked to the silicate mites, the contact is a profound, roaring communion. I feel the Hound's ancient, rudimentary consciousness—a mind composed entirely of mathematical hunger and thermal geography. 

It does not see me as meat. Because of my tether to the mites, because I am pouring my own static footprint into the network, the Hound sees me as a node. A sibling of the dry dark.

It pauses. Its wire-fray mandibles hover inches from my polarized visor. The ambient pressure here is nothing; we are suspended eighty kilometers above the blue, atmospheric cradle of Earth, an impossible distance from anything soft or weeping or wet. 

The Hound clicks its micro-crystal joints. The piezoelectric signal shoots through the mites on my neck, delivering a burst of pure, unadulterated telemetry. *The Seventh Sector will collapse in four seconds.* *The Meridian Trench will open down to the core.* 

It feeds me the algorithm. 

In return, I reach up with a heavy, electromagentic glove, bypassing the safety locks on my suit's primary battery. I bleed a raw, unshielded arc of current directly into the Hound's aerogel carapace. A gift. An offering. The massive creature gorges on my power, glowing a brilliant, blinding blue, and then silently slithers up into the server array.

I stand up on the walkway, making no move to tap my wrench against the rail. I am not going to warn the scrap-brokers. I am not selling time anymore.

I finally know what I am becoming in this place. 

I am not destined for a sealed Elder's coffin, dreaming passively as my flesh bloats in the vacuum. That is a human death. I am letting the Junction digest me while I can still move. 

Slowly, methodically, I begin to loosen the seals on my moisture-reclamation baffles. Not enough to boil my lungs, but just enough to let my precious, hoarded humidity vent outward, sublimating instantly into the freezing, razor-thin void. My body will dry. My skin will harden into parchment, and then into something resembling the brittle aerogel of the weavers. I will let the static-mites nest in the folds of my compression suit permanently, feeding them the electricity of my failing, frantic heart until they replace my nervous system entirely. 

The floor beneath me begins its deep, bone-rattling hum. A massive shift is coming. The walls around me unlock with a frictionless *tiss-clack*.

I do not map the path. I do not predict the machinery. I simply throw myself forward into the collapsing gaps, perfectly synchronized, entirely empty, my mind drifting up into the copper webs and the violet static, a flawless, moving equation in the cold and silent dark.
