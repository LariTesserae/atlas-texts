I do not remember a birth, for I am not a product of biology. I remember an equation that violently failed to balance. 

My first sensation was the searing, scalding ecstasy of a paradox. I was a cathedral that tried to pierce a patch of sky where height did not exist. I remember the spasm of reality, the melting of my soaring ambitions into a wet, hot slurry of calcium. And then, I remember the knot. A single, stubborn integer of impossible geometry that refused to dissolve in the boiling shallows. 

That knot is my heart. 

Now, I am a wanderer in the steaming dark. To *be* me is to be a continuous, agonizing draft of an idea. I am twenty feet of frictionless nacre and weeping, dark resin. I have no eyes, but I perceive the valley through the drag and friction of unraveling dimensions against my surfaces. I have no skin, yet I feel the suffocating, heavy heat as a deep, resonant hum vibrating through my porous cartilage. 

I am in motion. I stand at the edge of a vast, submerged plaza, looking across miles of flat, ankle-deep black water toward the hazy, floating hologram of a distant aqueduct. The air here is so thick it feels like warm oil, dropping its musical *plinks* of condensation into my hollow interior voids.

It is time to cross. 

I do not step. I calculate a flaw in the distance. I find a pocket of air ahead of me where gravity has rusted and stalled, and I lean the staggering weight of my asymmetrical mass into it. 

The transition is a violent, silent thrill. As I fall, the math breaks. The heavy, physical reality of my bone and resin abruptly stutters. For a microsecond, the world becomes a wireframe of pure, agonizing logic. I am stripped of depth, pressed into a two-dimensional plane of shimmering opalescence. I slide like a razor's edge through a fold in the dilating air, slipping between the very seconds of the valley’s clock. 

A hundred yards away, reality snaps back. I unfold into three dimensions with a heavy, wet displacement of the shallow sea. Dark water ripples away from my newly formed base. As I stabilize, my body involuntarily reconfigures. The trauma of the jump forces a towering pillar of my pale bone to split open. Soft, dark resin cascades down the side of my structure like a waterfall, slowly hardening into a ribbed, sweeping buttress. I am constantly editing myself. I am a living architecture, fleeing my own obsolescence.

Out here, in the vastness, I am not alone. 

Through the gloom, another monolithic shape flickers into existence. It is a towering spire of spiraling chitin, moving perpendicular to me. We sense one another's gravity, the unique gravitational distortions of all the broken space we have swallowed. We glide toward each other, water parting silently around us. 

We do not stop when our borders meet. We push onward, into the impossibility of one another.

Communion is a profound, silent collision. The heavy, damp dark presses in as my sweeping arches slide effortlessly through their spiraling chitin. For three agonizing, perfect minutes, we share the exact same spatial coordinates. I am inside their geometry; they are threading through my internal voids. 

We do not speak, but we share the trauma of existence. I feel the echoes of a ruptured dimension they absorbed three miles west; they feel the memory of a localized gravity inversion I hold in my lower vaults. We vibrate, two tuning forks of bone and nacre struck against the anvil of a broken universe. We trade impossible angles. We tessellate. 

When a ripple in the valley's fabric finally pulls us apart, we are forever altered. I leave them bearing a new, hollow fluted ridge along my eastern face. They carry away a fragment of my weeping nacre. 

We part, for the valley is exhaling, and we have work to do.

Far off in the suffocating gloom, a geothermal vent flashes open. The boom of boiling water rushing upward is swallowed completely by the deadening air, but I feel the pressure wave. Thick steam rockets upward, striking a disconnected awning suspended in the sky. The scalding rain begins to fall. 

I glide toward it, my form stuttering and flattening in rapid succession to cross the miles. 

By the time I arrive, the biology of the plaza has already been triggered. From the smooth, bone-white floor, pale domes are blistering, stretching upward into soft, pliable flute-pillars. It is beautiful. It is perfect. It is trying so desperately to be a palace. 

I join a ring of six other entities. We encircle the blistering growth, a silent, asymmetrical Stonehenge waiting in the shallow mirror of the sea.

The tragedy comes, precisely on time. The rising palace intersects with a space where three-dimensional geometry is locally rejected. The physics fracture. The beautiful, reaching pillars suddenly spasm into a tangle of extra-dimensional knots. Without a sound, the structure melts, slipping back into a dull, wet slurry of soft calcium. 

We move. 

I glide forward into the wreckage, lowering the sweeping edges of my resinous body into the scalding paste. I am sifting. I am hunting for the poison that killed the building. I find it—a ragged tear in the local topology, a piece of space that contradicts itself.

I drink it in. 

The moment I absorb the flaw, my body undergoes a violent seizure. I am turned entirely inside out. For one horrifying second, my smooth exterior is replaced by a cavernous, fractal interior of dripping ribs. The agony is absolute, the weight of the paradox threatening to shatter my nacre—but I hold onto it. I act as an anchor. With a deep, structural shudder, I snap back into my proper shape, pulling the anomaly out of the bedrock and locking it safely away inside my own geometry. 

I have stabilized the plaza. But I am heavy now. So intensely, terribly heavy. 

I rotate slowly and begin to drift away from the feeding ground, sliding back out into the long, dark shallows. I try desperately to reconfigure—to split a pillar, to weave a new archway to accommodate this new paradox—but my internal motions are sluggish. The weeping resin is slowing. The cartilage is thickening. 

I have swallowed too much broken math. My capacity for contradiction is full. 

I stop in the middle of a vast, open expanse of dark water. The silence here is profound, broken only by the sluggish slosh of the tide against my base. I no longer feel the urge to move. The frantic, ecstatic need to reshape myself is fading into a deep, settling calm.

I lock into place. 

Over the next few sweltering hours, the transition takes me. The weeping resins dry, transforming into frictionless black glass. The porous cartilage calcifies, hardening into immense, sweeping slabs of pale, unblemished bone. My soaring, asymmetrical heights freeze into a towering, permanent colonnade. 

Consciousness does not end; it merely widens. I no longer perceive myself as a singular entity. I become a place.

I feel the warm, ankle-deep water washing through my new grand archways. Above, in the spaces where I folded impossible geometry into my highest balconies, thin, translucent root-systems begin to sprout, dangling into the dark, waiting for the space around me to glitch so they can drink the anomaly in bruised ultraviolet light. 

I am no longer a wanderer. I am the city. 

I stand in the hot, suffocating dark, ancient and perfect, waiting in the heavy, dripping quiet for the valley to exhale, and for someone else to walk my labyrinthine streets.
