The most humiliating sound in this titanic, shrieking dark is the wet, sloppy rhythm of my own heart. 

*Thud-squelch. Thud-squelch.* 

In a world governed by the frictionless mathematics of dry acoustics, my pulse is a glaring, sodden offense. It is a slow, liquid dragging against the divine, crystalline tension of the hollow. I am an intruder, a pocket of abhorrent moisture trapped inside the belly of a wooden god. 

But the cavern is curing me. 

I don't know how long I have been wedged into this deep, striated fissure along the cavern’s curved perimeter. Time here is measured only by the shifting, atonal bass drones of the gale rushing up from beneath. What I do know is that I am not dying; I am being translated. The absolute desiccation of the air does not kill me outright, but rather draws the water from my biology with a patient, magnetic hunger. 

Every time I exhale, a ghost of vapor leaves my lips, instantly shredded by the freezing draft. It never returns. The moisture does not condense; it is annihilated. I can feel my lungs becoming rigid, resembling the brittle, translucent nests of paper wasps. The heavy, warm blood in my veins is slowing, thickening into a sluggish, iron-rich sap. My skin, beneath the heavy wool of my coat, has stopped sweating. Instead, it is cracking along geometric fault lines, scaling into patches of pale, hardening bark. 

I am becoming an instrument.

I cling to the polished, hyper-lignified grain of the cavern wall, feeling the sheer, terrifying magnitude of the space through my hardening fingertips. The wood beneath me hums with a voltage of kinetic energy. The wind roaring through the deep abyss is so continuous, so heavy, that it has ceased to be wind and has become a physical pressure—a crushing blanket of moving air smelling of pulverized pith and oxidized resin. 

I try to move my jaw, to see if I can still speak, but the joint clicks with the dry, ratcheting sound of tightening wood. I am nearly ready. I am almost dry enough.

Then, the ambient roar of the cavern fractures. 

High above my ledge, a pocket of dead silence drops like an anvil. An acoustic shadow sweeps over me, a momentary eclipse of the screaming wind. I flatten myself against the curvature of the wall. The pitch of the air pressure instantly drops into a multi-tonal chord—a tight, aggressive harmony of violoncellos bowed so violently the strings are fraying. 

A Lithoxyl is descending. 

The temperature around me plummets further. The air pressure compresses, popping my eardrums—or what is left of my eardrums, now petrified into thin, drumhead-tight slivers of fungus. A profound smell washes over my ledge: the sharp, metallic tang of ozone layered over the scent of intense frictional heat, like a forest fire just seconds before the first spark actually catches. 

In the pitch black, the deafening clatter begins. It sounds like massive, unstrung piano chassis collapsing down a staircase, the grinding of wooden gears, the spilling of a thousand ceramic tiles. 

Suddenly, the absolute dark is torn open by rapid, blinding arcs of blue static electricity. 

In those stroboscopic flashes, I see it. It is impossibly huge, suspended in the void just feet from my ledge, gripping the cavern wall with sickle-like tarsi made of dark, petrified wood. Its body is a masterpiece of geometric chaos—uncurling ribbons of living veneer, hollowed ribs of pale sapwood, suspended around a glowing, amber-like core of piezoelectric crystal. As the blue static dances across its architecture, lighting the void, I see the dizzying scale of the cavern behind it: miles and miles of empty air crisscrossed by millions of taut, glittering resin-strings, shimmering under the weight of cellulose dust. 

The creature adjusts its baffles, and the blue sparks illuminate my own hands gripping the ledge. They are no longer human. The fingers have elongated, the skin peeled back to reveal dry, fibrous tendons and knuckles of dark, hardened wood. 

The Lithoxyl senses the anomaly of my presence. It shifts, its massive framework clicking and groaning, and leans into the fissure. 

I am frozen, incapable of pulling away. Long, whip-like filaments of flash-frozen sap drop from its undercarriage. They writhe blindly through the freezing black air. One of the filaments brushes against the stiffened collar of my coat, then drags slowly upward over my cheek. 

The touch is agonizingly cold, carrying the electric charge of a live wire. As the hardened sap rests against my jaw, the towering entity sends a localized, low-frequency pulse down the filament. It permeates my skull, tasting my internal resonance. It is looking for the pitch of my being. 

Instead, it finds the lingering, pathetic thud of my dying human heart. *Thump-squelch.* 

The filament recoils as if burned. A dissonant, shrieking chord erupts from the creature’s hollow limbs—a profound acoustic disgust. To this magnificent architecture of dry tension, I am a greasy, sodden mass of rot. I am an obstacle, dead weight in a world that thrives on frictionless momentum. 

With a concussive blast of displaced air and a blinding cascade of blue sparks, the Lithoxyl violently unfurls its wooden baffles. It casts itself backward, catching the heavy gale, and vanishes back into the shrieking, swirling dark. The chaotic, atonal roar of the cavern rushes back in to fill the silence it left behind. 

I am alone again, rooted to the wall. 

But I am not afraid. I look down at my hands in the lightless void, feeling the grain of them, feeling the wind whistling softly through the new gaps where my flesh has begun to hollow out. My heart beats again, but the squelch is quieter now. The liquid is failing. Soon, the blood will crystallize into amber. Soon, the chambers of my lungs will lock open, turning into hollow baffles of shaved bone. 

I close my eyes, parting my wooden lips to let the freezing, desiccated gale rush down my throat. I am waiting for the last drop of water to leave me. I will not be an obstacle for much longer. Soon, I will let go of the ledge, catch the wind, and join the symphony.
