I do not begin as a whole. I begin as a fraction of an agonizing, fleeting perfection.

In the dead center of the cavern, the blinding, shadowless light reaches its absolute peak, and I am part of the desperate roulette wheel. For a microsecond, the hyper-vibrating polyglot of flesh remembers what it was. The feathers, the chitin, the skeletal hands—they all collapse inward, resolving.

For exactly one tenth of a second, I am the left hand of the pale, flawless humanoid figure. 

I feel the cellar-like chill against perfectly rendered, translucent skin. I have five fingers. I have knuckles. I possess the impossible, sorrowful geometry of a human reaching out into the void. It is a moment of excruciating, beautiful coherence. 

Then, the 2.9-level weirdness asserts itself. The physics engine screams. The cavern plunges into absolute, crushing blackness. 

When the white light rubber-bands back, the humanoid form has shattered. The Central Entity returns to a writhing knot of mismatched organs, its code violently rejecting the memory. But I am not pulled back into the knot. The rendering engine has severed me. I am a discarded draft.

I am thrown outward into the damp, frigid air.

Because gravity is an unwritten suggestion here, I do not fall; I drift along a violent, horizontal vector. I am still a hand, floating thumb-upward through the blinding void, but without the central processor to maintain my complexity, my geometry begins to panic. My fingernails extend into shimmering tapes of light-polarizing film, trying to become ribbon-creatures. My palm caves inward, attempting to fold itself through the fourth dimension like the origami-cephalopods.

I pass through a static grid of vapor. The freezing moisture coats my skin. It is utterly, profoundly silent. I am terrified, though I have no neurology to process the emotion, only a compiling error where fear should be.

Up ahead, a jagged, mathematically perfect extrusion of obsidian-like proto-stone slides out of the air. Clinging to its sheer face is a half-finished wireframe cathedral, spun from chalky resin. 

I crash into the boundary of the Spindle-Weaver’s nest. 

The moment I cross the threshold of the resin, local physics patch my existence. Gravity abruptly takes hold. I drop like a stone, striking the floor of the nest in complete silence. The shadowless white light dims to a breathable gray. Beside me, a floating sphere of water loses its surface tension and splashes to the stone. 

For a long moment, I simply lie there. A pale hand, severed at the wrist, resting inside a cage of spun sugar and blown glass. 

Above me, a Spindle-Weaver pauses its work. I try to focus on it, but its body dissolves into trembling wireframes as my perception shifts. Its needle-thin legs vibrate. It senses my weight. It senses that my code is a massive, heavy burden on its tiny, fragile sanctuary. The chalky resin beneath me begins to spider-web with hairline cracks. My coherence is too dense. I am too close to the truth of what the Central Entity is trying to be, and this local patch cannot sustain me.

The edges of my fingers begin to stutter. The grey light inside the nest flickers. I am tearing the Weaver’s reality apart.

In a panic, the Weaver retreats, abandoning its cathedral to the void. The resin cracks completely. The localized gravity fails, and I find myself floating again, drifting just centimeters above the raw obsidian cliff. 

My timeline begins to skip. One moment my fingers are curled, the next they are splayed. I am shattering into two-dimensional visual static. I am crashing.

In the total quiet, a shadow slowly eclipses the light. 

It is a Null-Scavenger. A massive, sluggish mass of liquid mercury and crushed prisms. It flows down the sheer vertical face of the polygon, unfazed by gravity, drawn by the catastrophic error of my existence. It has no eyes to pity me, no mouth to consume me. It merely intends to resolve the glitch.

I do not try to move. My thumb stutters out of existence, replaced by a pixelated blur of magenta and cyan. The Scavenger slides over me. 

The transition is freezing, heavy, and absolute. Inside the liquid metal, I am unmade. The sorrowful geometry of my human shape is gently but firmly deleted. I am stripped of skin, of bone, of the memory of the humanoid figure. But my local awareness—the tiny spark of processing power that made me a discrete entity—is not extinguished. It is reformatted. 

I am distilled into pure, baseline geometry. I feel myself being extruded, pushed out of the heavy silver mass and planted directly into the raw, unweathered stone.

I harden. I cool. 

I am now a wire-thin, glass-like stalk rooted to the cliff face.

I have no hands to reach out with. I have no legs to fold through space. I produce no leaves. I point outward into the vast, hollow volume of the cavern, aiming toward a pocket of gravity that might not exist tomorrow. 

But I have a function. 

The blinding, omnidirectional light of the unshielded creation engine strikes my glass-like body. I take that fierce, shadowless white, and I break it. I refract the raw data of the room into a localized rainbow of crimson, gold, and violet. I do not consume; I translate.

Rooted to the obsidian, casting colors into the freezing fog, I can see the center of the pocket. I watch the Central Entity writhe, a polyglot of flesh iterating through a million forms, throwing off ribbons and cephalopods, eternally trying to build the hand I used to be. 

It cannot see me. But I cast a tiny, iridescent spectrum against the cold stone, a monument to the shape it is searching for. 

I am perfectly still. I am finally stable. In the vast, blinding womb beneath the world, I listen to the silence, waiting for the microscopic, wet *tick* of a single drop of water hitting the floor.
