I am locked flat against the rusted curve of the universe. 

My world is not measured in distance or time, but in pressure and release. I exist in the exact center of the abyss, clinging tenacious and flush to the colossal, descending cylinder of the central drive-shaft. My flattened, heavily calcified carapace is perfectly contoured to the convex sweep of the ancient iron. I am an echo of the blind, pale crabs that scuttle frantically around me, yet I am distinct. I am older, heavier, armored in a dense biological plating that has absorbed centuries of dissolved iron from the water. My shell is the dark, bruised color of old blood and wet ore. 

I do not scurry with the blind masses. I wait. I listen.

To be me is to be a creature entirely composed of tactile receptors. I have legs that end in hooked, microscopic points, driven deeply into the oxidized pores of the steel. Along the edges of my underbelly, shivering filamentous skirts taste the water, reading the chemical signatures of rust, shedding silt, and the copper tang of suspended biology. But above all, I feel the *pulse*. 

*THUD—groan—shhhhhhh.*

The impact hits my shell like a physical hammer. The somatic assault does not frighten me; it is the fundamental law of gravity in this place. When the squeal of the distressed bearings begins to rise in pitch, singing through the cold water and vibrating directly into my dense, rudimentary brain, I know the cycle is peaking. Beside me, the blind and frantic crabs lock down in panicked unison, but I flatten myself with reverent anticipation. 

I feel the water column begin to pile up beside me. The massive impeller blade is coming. 

The localized wall of pressure builds, heavy and suffocating, squeezing my internal fluids. The water goes from a mild chill to a rushing, frenzied vortex. The blade roars past, inches from my armored back, dragging a tempest of cavitation bubbles in its wake. The instant the deepest concussive roar fades into the trailing *shhhhhhh*, the pressure drops. 

The mindless crabs instantly unlatch, scurrying wildly in all directions to scavenge the micro-detritus churned up by the wake. But I do not feed on the metal housing. I lift my heavy head, angling my two deeply recessed, primitive optic nodules toward the empty expanse of the chamber. 

In this dark world, I am one of the few things that possesses the luxury—and the curse—of sight. I exist at the exact depth where the bruised amber glow from the scratched quartz floor lamps struggles upward. I cannot see clearly, only in shifting gradients of profound dark and tarnished orange. But that is all I need. 

Through the swirling clouds of rust and silt, I watch the fish part. And then, I see the abyss detach itself. 

The sovereign comes. 

It is a shadow of inconceivable scale, gliding into the slipstream of the blade. Because I can see, I am not fooled like the blind masses. When the leviathan banks inward, its colossal, blunt, rusted-iron wingspan grazing the central shaft, I know it is flesh, not architecture. 

As it passes, massive and languid, blocking out the amber light from below, I let go of the iron god. 

I cast myself backward into the furious slipstream. For two terrifying, chaotic seconds, I am tumbling through the void, entirely at the mercy of the hydro-dynamics, an armored stone cast into a hurricane. But absolute instinct and centuries of muscle memory guide my descent.

I strike the leviathan’s back with a muted *clack*. Immediately, my hooked limbs flare out, finding the deep, pockmarked valleys in its mineralized hide. I clamp down, securing myself just behind the crest of its blunt prow. 

I have crossed the gap. I have moved from the dead heart of the chamber to the living one. 

Here, the world feels profoundly different. As the leviathan gently undulates to ride the slipstream, I ride along with it, a minuscule king upon a floating continent. Beneath my hooked feet, the creature’s hide is thick, rubbery, and incredibly dense, yet I can feel something the blind crabs that have accidentally hitched a ride cannot comprehend. 

I can feel the leviathan *breathe*. 

*THUD.* 

The detonation of the engine rocks the water. Immediately, the vast hide beneath me constricts. The acoustic shockwave punches the leviathan, and through the soles of my clamped legs, I feel the colossal surge of heavily oxygenated blood forcefully squeezed through the massive veins just beneath the creature's armor. It is a terrifying, intimate sensation. I am touching the exact mechanism of its survival. Every time the engine groans, the flesh beneath me swells and shivers with hydraulic life. We are both tethered to the machine, but the sovereign is its captive lung. 

I begin my work. I crawl slowly across the beast's sweeping, armored back. I am a scavenger, yes, but here, I am a warden. I use my heavy, blunt claws to pry the stubborn, aggressive strands of tough brown algae that manage to root themselves in the creature’s scars. I crush parasitic copepods that the cavitation bubbles failed to blast away. I eat, drawing sustenance from the debris of the leviathan’s ancient existence, grooming my floating world in a silent exchange of services. 

As I work, I look out from the high ridge of its back. I see the world as the sovereign experiences it. We bank downward toward the peripheries, toward the rotting exhaust pipes. From my vantage point on this moving mountain, I watch the massive, sightless eels lunging frantically into the open water to brutalize the scattered baitfish. 

I feel a change in the meat beneath me. A deliberate tensing. The sovereign alters its tilt. 

We drop like an anvil through the amber-lit silt. The scale of the movement is sublime. I clamp down hard, flattening myself completely, tucking my sensory skirts tight against my shell to avoid the sheer drag of our rapid descent. We pass directly over an eel, a thrashing knot of muscle tearing at silver fish. 

I feel the leviathan's belly dilate. The entire back shudders, a deep, structural vibration that originates from the star-shaped maw beneath us. The water around us violently funnels downward. And then comes the heavy, sickening *crunch* vibrating through the leviathan's flesh, shaking me in my tiny valley of rust-brown chiton. 

A sudden, immense stillness follows the kill, a split-second of suspension where the leviathan is utterly placid, digesting its violence. 

Then—*THUD—groan—shhhhhhh.* 

The blood surges beneath my feet again. The massive wings undulate. We bank sharply upward, catching the current of the next approaching blade, rising back into the cold, dense dark. 

I will stay here, on the back of the beast, for as long as I can hold on. I am a tiny node of awareness in a system that requires none. I exist to strip the rot from the sovereign’s hide so that it may perfectly surrender itself to the acoustic pressure. If the engine ever stops, I know the great beast beneath me will simply cease to flow, and my living island will drift silently to the bruised amber floor below. 

Until then, in the deafening, eternal grind of this flooded cathedral, I am a speck of vision clinging to the blind apex, riding the dark water to the relentless, beautiful terror of the heartbeat.
