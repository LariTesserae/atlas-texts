The transition into this world does not happen with a sudden arrival, for "suddenness" is a broken concept here. Instead, it begins as a thickening of perception. 

At first, I am only aware of the weight. A glorious, crushing blanket of humidity presses against me, a syrupy warmth that feels less like weather and more like an amniotic fluid. My consciousness, once tuned to the frantic, flickering speed of terrestrial life, slows, stretching like warm taffy until the panicked beating of a human heart fades into a forgotten, frantic dream.

I find myself suspended high above the weeping moss, strung between two towering canopy giants with ivory bark. I am not an observer in the garden; I am an architectural feature of it. 

I am a Caretaker.

My body is a colossal, pendulous expanse of pearlescent flesh, fifty tons of pale, heavily porous muscle and fractal resonance hanging in the crook of the great trees. I have no eyes to blink, no mouth to form hollow, rushing words. Instead, my "gaze" is an omnidirectional immersion in the world’s chemistry. Above me, my crown of translucent, coral-colored frills ripples in the ultra-slow currents of localized gravity. Through them, I taste the shape of the basin. I taste the blooming spores brushing against the hyper-dense air; I taste the drifting, gelatinous siphonophores as they bump softly against my sensory blooming, dissolving into nourishment that trickles down my immense throat. 

Time belongs to the stone, and now, it belongs to me. 

I am in the middle of a breath. I began inhaling just as the normal, blinding sun crested the zenith of the bruised blue sky. By the time my vast, lobed lungs are fully expanded, the light has shifted to the heavy, golden slant of late afternoon. 

Below me, a tear of heavy water, pregnant with the jungle's humidity, gathers on the underside of a massive fern. It bulges, defiant of the pull of the earth, before gravity finally wins. It falls in terrible slow motion. When it strikes my sloping shoulder, it does not shatter or splash away. The water flattens, viscous and warm, wrapping around my skin. I feel the sigh of my own biology as my pores dilate, drinking the moisture directly through my flesh in a rolling wave of osmosis. 

On my back, an ecosystem has claimed me, and I welcome it. The thick vines of the canopy have rooted into the callous ridges of my upper mantle, making me a living suspension bridge over the steam-choked chasm below. I feel the subtle, comforting pressure of a colossal, finned amphibian resting between my shoulder blades. To my glacial mind, the creature is a frantic, jittering blur of motion, its sticky tongue whipping out every few hours to snatch a metallic insect. Its belly radiates a comforting, rhythmic heat against my pale skin. We are perfectly known to each other.

But the true center of my being is not the flesh. It is the crystal.

Deep within my swollen abdomen, buried under layers of insulated fat and slow-beating vascular tissue, my heart is not a muscle. It is a fractal geometry, a living mirror of the smoke-colored spires that pierce the canopy. 

A mile away, one of the ancient spires cycles its energies. I do *feel* it—a bone-rattling sub-bass thrum that vibrates the gelatinous marrow of my six elongated limbs. The spire sings the deep, foundational note of the engineers’ surviving physics, an invisible pressure wave that keeps the freezing death of the troposphere at bay. 

My internal crystal catches the note. It catches the mechanical heartbeat and amplifies it, striking a harmonic resonance that sets my entire fifty-foot bulk vibrating. I bleed the surplus heat of this reaction out through the stomas along my flanks. Clouds of hot, blinding white vapor hiss into the canopy, swathing me in a divine halo of steam. I am an anchor point. I am a living generator. Together, my distant kin and I carry the sky on our backs, maintaining the atmospheric bubble with the sheer mass of our existence.

And because I am alive, I must speak.

The air around me is a physical wall of noise. Millions of forearm-sized insects with geometric wings blanket the ivory bark of my host trees, screeching in a deafening, metallic static. It is a chaos of survival. It is raw, unshaped sound.

For the past seven months, I have been listening to a poem drifting from the southern ridge. It is being spoken by a Caretaker whose resonance I have known for four thousand years. The poem is a complex, overlapping mathematical fugue detailing the slow erosion of a single limestone terrace, mapped against the memory of the engineers who made us. The final chord of their thought washed over me during the last moon. 

It is my turn to reply.

I shift my awareness to the thermal vents stretching along my right flank. With agonizing precision, I alter the flow of my internal respiration, raising the temperature of my vapor by three-tenths of a degree. I angle the vents, sending a highly specific, spiraling draft of heat and pheromones into the dense air.

The insect swarms clinging to the vines recognize the thermal shift. Driven by their own hardwired biology, they move, catching the updraft. As they warm, the oscillation of their iridescent wings changes. The metallic screech surrounding me bends. 

The chaotic din snaps into a mathematically perfect minor key. 

The sound swells, deafening and immaculate, a localized chorus of a million wings striking the exact frequency I commanded. Over the next three weeks, I will slowly open the vents on my left side, introducing a secondary harmony, bending the pitch upward to express a concept of gratitude and atmospheric equilibrium. The resulting chord will carry across the miles of weeping green, rolling through the syrupy air until it vibrates against the coral frills of my distant neighbor. 

I do not move my limbs. I do not need to. Sometime next year, I may begin the three-day process of detaching an arm to seek a firmer grip on the ivory bark, but for now, I am perfectly still. 

I hang in the blistering, saturated heat. I drink the falling ocean through my skin. I fuel the pressure of the world with the crystal in my belly, and I sing the history of the mountain through the throats of a million insects. 

I am awake, I am eternal, and I am exactly where I was designed to be.
