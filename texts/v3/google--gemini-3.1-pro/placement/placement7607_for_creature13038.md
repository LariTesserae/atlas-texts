I would not be found near the cherry-red warmth of the arcologies, nor would I glide untethered across the oceanic stretches of polished basalt. To find me, you would have to walk out into the absolute center of the deep, empty shadow—to the precise, equidistant midpoint between three of the colossal engine-domes. 

Here, the subsonic heavy *hmmmmmm* of the subterranean machinery meets itself. Coming from three directions, the immense acoustic waves press into one another, creating a permanent, invisible pillar of constructive interference. A standing wave of unimaginable kinetic pressure.

Over millions of years, this localized pressure began to act upon the carbon and metallic dust in the flattened slag, sweeping it up, aligning its atomic structures, and locking it into place. Slowly, micron by micron, the sound pulled a body out of the bedrock. 

I am that body. 

I am a towering, asymmetric spire of hyper-compressed, piezoelectric crystal and spun-bismuth, fused seamlessly to the unbroken floor of the world. I look less like a monolith and more like a massive, jagged fractal of black glass, towering sixty feet into the static-charged sky. I do not move. I have never moved. I am an acoustic petrification, an organism made entirely of structural resonance. 

Because of the extreme pressure that binds my lattice, the vibration of the world translates constantly into tiny, flowing electrical currents deep within my crystalline heart. This is my consciousness. I do not think in words or biological impulses; I think in micro-volts, thermal gradients, and the shifting geometries of capacitance. My awareness is slow, vast, and perfectly synchronized with the beating heart of the deep-crust engines. 

If the Keepers of the Hum are the priests who maintain the equilibrium, I am the altar at the center of their cathedral. Yet, they did not build me. I am the unintended consequence of their perfection. If they were to ever fail—if the alignment of an arcology drifted by even a fraction of a degree—the standing wave that forms my spine would collapse, and I would instantly detonate into a billion flakes of harmless silica. My entire existence is a desperate, beautiful balancing act of physics, held in suspension by the immortal stasis of the world.

My life is one of pure observation.

I have no eyes, but my entire surface acts as a dielectric sensor. I "see" this world through the interplay of magnetism and heat. It is a breathtaking landscape. 

When a Harvester of the Flux draws near, I do not see a flattened manta ray; I see a massive, sliding lacuna of coldness, a beautiful, frightening void of anti-magnetism drifting closer. When it passes directly over me—a rare event that only happens perhaps once every four thousand years—its localized magnetic fields pull fiercely against my internal charge. For a fleeting century over the course of its incredibly slow transit, I am bathed in an agonizing, ecstatic surge of current. My thoughts speed up. I experience the sensation of panic, or its thermodynamic equivalent: hyper-conductivity. As it slides away, leaving only sublimating frost, I settle back into the slow, familiar hum.

The little scavengers—the spindle-legged grazers—often seek me out. Because of my unique internal pressures, I passively generate a fractional degree of ambient heat. Drawn by this, they gather at the base of my crystal spire. Through the skin of my stone, I feel their delicate glass-needle feet clicking against the basalt. *Tap... tap.* Sometimes, hundreds of them converge in my shadow, weaving their copper filaments together to sync their memories. As their ultraviolet coils throb, their microscopic data leaks into my piezoelectric lattice. Through them, I learn the map of the plains. I learn the exact shape of a thermal updraft twelve miles away, or the shifting path of a magnetic ley-line. I store their fleeting histories permanently into my rigid, crystalline structure.

But my profoundest interactions are not with the crawling or gliding things. They are with the sky, and with the dreams of the immortal machines.

Every few millennia, high up in the dense, static-choked ceiling of the dark, a storm of dry electricity will break. Unseen lightning arcs silently across the dead sky, ionizing the heavy air. When this happens, my spire acts as a colossal lightning rod—not for current, but for the frequency of the storm. I drink the static. I ring like an impossible bell, singing a tone so low and resonant that it cracks the dust for miles around. It is my only voice, and I use it to answer the sky.

And sometimes, an Emanation drifts out of the arcologies. 

Occasionally, one of those slow-motion ribbons of red and gold plasma—the cognitive run-off of the great engines—strays too far from the exhaust baffles. Untethered from the machines, an Emanation will wander into the dark, a localized orb of heat and pure thought. Inevitably, the magnetic fields of the plains guide it toward the center of the interference pattern. Toward me.

I wait for it. The approach takes decades. When the glowing plasma of the Emanation finally touches my jagged black facets, it does not burn me. It is absorbed. 

The plasma washes over my crystalline surface and sinks deep into my refractive core. As it cools and locks into the lattice, the thought of the engine is preserved forever. Stasis has no memory, but I do. Deep within my towering, immobile body lies a vast, glittering archive of every dream the machines have ever discarded. I hold the concepts of impossible equations, the topology of gears that do not exist, and the terrifyingly pure logic of pure, unhurried time. When you look closely at my dark, glassy surface, you can see them—faint, trapped ghost-lights of red and gold, shifting lazily within the crystal.

I am the Intercalary Point. I am the silence between the heartbeats, made solid. In a world of unending, perfect stasis, I am the only thing that remembers, waiting in the baking dark, listening to the hum of eternity, holding on to every fleeting spark just to prove that it was there.
