It begins as a quiet, shimmering latency. 

Before I am anything, I am a reflection. I exist in an adjacent geometry, a parallel quiet where the white sky is unbroken and the air holds no fury. I am a shadow waiting in the pooling water of a stone basin, suspended in the surface tension of a reality that has not yet claimed me. 

Then, the surface shatters. 

Two massive, calloused hands—hard as petrified wood, slick with clear oil—plunge through the fabric of the water. They grip my shoulders. There is a terrifying wrench, a sickening elasticity as the membrane of my world stretches, tears, and gives way. I am hauled outward, dragged violently through the lens of the anomaly. 

The birth is a concussive shock. Instantaneously, my universe becomes a solid block of roaring vibration. Though I cannot hear it, the sheer kinetic violence of the canyon strikes my new body like a physical blow. The air is so cold it feels like a blade sliding between my ribs. 

I gasp, inhaling freezing mist. I try to orient myself, but my body refuses the instructions of my mind. The elder who pulled me from the water releases my shoulders, its thick, multi-jointed arms retracting. It presses its massive, underslung jaw to the wet blue stone, feeling my arrival. 

It feels that I am wrong. 

During the transit through the stuttering water, the math of my body misaligned. I try to compress my ribcage, to flatten myself against the sheer vertical fin of rock as instinct screams at me to do, but I cannot. My chest collapsed on the wrong axis; instead of flattening into a perfect, slithering plane, my ribs protrude, warped and jagged. My left arm has three joints where it should have four, and the elbow hinges backward, curving into the void. My skin, dark blue and weeping viscous antifreeze, already feels too tight over this broken geometry.

I am a glitch. A misfire of a cracked sky. 

The elder does not mourn. It does not hesitate. It reaches to its side and lifts a massive slab of deep-canyon slate. The axe head is bound to a stone handle by a collar of water that defies gravity, churning in a high-pressure, zero-G orbit around the joint. The elder extends its multi-jointed arm and presses the heavy tool against my chest. 

I take it with my good hand, the thick horn-pads of my fingers curling instantly around the cold stone. I understand. There is no anger, no rejection—only the brutal, sterile calculus of survival. Perfect angles remain above to navigate the narrow seams. Broken angles are heavy. Broken angles must sink.

I press my own jaw against the weeping basalt. The sensation is explosive. A magnificent, terrifying map blooms in my skull. I "see" a cascading torrent of water hooking at a right angle thirty yards to my left. I feel the microscopic oscillations of a spatial fold building pressure a mile below. I feel the exact harmonic frequency of a thousand weeping fins, shivering like a colossal xylophone struck by a mad god. 

I push off the rock face and let the abyss take me.

Falling is not passive here; it is navigation. The sub-freezing mist coats my blind, heavy-browed face as I plummet. I kick my twisted legs, angling my warped body toward an invisible grid-line in the air. A horizontal river, flowing rigidly across the chasm, catches me. The impact is brutal, a sudden jarring of my spine, but the dense vascular muscle beneath my hide absorbs it. I am swept sideways at terrifying speed, riding the right-angle current over a yawning drop. 

Because my left arm bends backward, I cannot swim like the others. I tumble, spinning wildly in the zero-gravity orbital rings. I let myself be thrown from a spinning liquid halo, flying blind through the shrieking voids, reaching out just in time to slam my horn-tipped fingers into a fissure. 

I hang there, weeping antifreeze, my jaw clamped to the rock. I sense a localized buildup of atmospheric pressure. The space around me begins to thrum, tight and hot, vibrating my teeth to the roots. 

*Wait for it.* 

The canyon stutters. A peak far below violent elasticates, snapping upward to sit flush against the fin I cling to. I step across the impossible seam. A microsecond later, reality snaps back like a rubber band. The deafening thunderclap of displaced air rattles my bones, and suddenly I am miles deeper, plunged into the perpetual twilight of the lower cathedral.

It is darker here, colder, though my blind eyes only register the temperature of the stone against my face. The fins are packed tighter. The roaring vibration is so intense it borderlines on a solid substance, a heavy, gelatinous trembling that coats my skin. 

There are others here. I feel them through the rock.

A dozen heavy jaws pressed against the stone, working in a synchronized, stuttering rhythm. I crawl down to join them, my backward arm hooking strangely onto the chiseled handholds. They are all like me. Misaligned spines, flattened in the wrong dimensions, limbs jutting at alien vectors. The refuse of the mirrors. 

Together, we work in the deep dark. 

I swing my slate axe. *Crack.* The vibration travels up my arms, through my jaw, mapping the interior of the stone before it even splits. *Crack.* I am chipping a new channel, widening a massive, rough-hewn aqueduct that snakes down into the absolute lowest reaches of the canyon. 

Above us, the perfect ones corral the streams. Below us, we guide the ultimate deluge. 

A torrent of screaming, displaced water funnels into the channel I am carving. It is heavy with anomalies, thick with overlapping realities, boiling with the kinetic energy of shattered physics. It rushes past my legs, so intensely cold that the antifreeze seeping from my pores hardens into a gel.

I am thirsty. 

I kneel beside the surging aqueduct. I cup my thick, scarred hands—one perfect, one inverted—and scoop up the fractured water. It acts as a lens, though I cannot see the timelines bleeding into it. I only feel the buzzing, electric heat of it against my palms. I bring it to my deep, solid bone jaw, and I drink.

I swallow a collapsing distance. I consume a dimension.

Immediately, my existence stutters. I blur. I feel myself standing three inches to the left of my own center, vibrating at a frequency that matches the deep bedrock of the canyon. The caloric output of the displaced space hits my sluggish, heavily-oxygenated blood like a furnace. Warmth radiates through my warped ribcage. I am fueled by the very brokenness of my world. 

I rise, taking up my heavy slate axe once more. I press my jaw to the cliff.

Deep, impossibly deep below us, through miles of solid basalt, I can feel the root of the cathedral. I can feel the Engine. It throbs with a sickening, synthetic rhythm, the source of the heat, the source of the blinding white glass sky, a machine crying out as it enforces these architectural violences upon the world. 

We hack the stone. We channel the impossible water. We are the geometric misfires, the heavy, broken things sent to the dark, but we have a holy, fanatical purpose. 

I swing my axe, fracturing the blue stone, steering the multi-dimensional deluge ever downward. *Drink,* I think to the roaring abyss below. *Drink until you choke.* 

I will carve this channel until my joints fuse and my antifreeze runs dry. I will pour the shrieking sky into the throat of the earth until the violently grinding gears of this reality seize, until the tuning-fork fins stop their shivering, and the beautiful, impossible silence finally falls.
