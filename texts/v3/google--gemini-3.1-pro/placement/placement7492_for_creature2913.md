I do not begin where my skin ends. 

For me, there is no boundary between the heavy, slate-gray envelope of my flesh and the cold, thin liquid in which I am submerged. I am a center of density within a vast, continuous nervous system of water. The endless city is my extended body, and I feel its furthest edges through the rhythmic, weeping pressure of the runoff.

I am resting at the bottom of a block-wide cistern. The water here is deep and quiet, insulating me from the unbearable, desiccating silence of the sun. For hours—or perhaps days, for time is measured only by the filling and emptying of basins—I have been perfectly still, a gelatinous mountain of muscle pressed flat against the submerged limestone. 

Through the porous membrane of my back, I breathe. I pull the dissolved oxygen from the icy water, a slow, continuous drinking that sustains the massive, deliberate thud of my heart. *Thrum... ... ... Thrum...* The beat is so low, so deep, it does not make a sound, but rather a slow heave that stirs the silt at the edges of my resting place.

Above me, the barometric pressure drops. 

I do not see the sky darken, for I have no eyes, but I feel the weight of the air change immediately. The sharp, tight heat that had been pressing down on the surface of my cistern lifts. The water shivers. A microscopic scatter of impacts dimples the surface above. 

The mist has returned. The clouds are dragging their wet bellies across the high terraces once again.

It is time to rise. 

I unfurl my cowl. Thousands of pale, thread-like tendrils drift upward through the dark water. As they break the surface, they encounter the cool, rushing air. The sensory input is staggering, a symphony of fluid dynamics. Through my tendrils, I can read the precise angle of the wind. I can feel the weight of the condensation gathering on a dry-stacked wall three miles to the east. I can taste the metallic tang of dissolved basalt in the fresh rain. 

Slowly, I gather my mass. My wide, muscular foot tightens against the floor of the cistern, rippling from back to front with immense hydraulic force. I flow upward over the lip of the submerged stairs. 

I break the meniscus with a soft, heavy *shhh*. The thin, high-altitude air embraces my slick skin. It is glorious. The entire world is weeping, slick with life-saving moisture. 

I heave myself over the smooth stone lip of the basin and onto a slanted plaza. The movement requires tremendous strength, but I do not feel it as exertion. It is simply an expansion and contraction of my architecture, a slow pouring of my flesh over the hand-fractured stones. 

As I move, my underside rasps against the paving stones. I scrape up the dark olive algae that has bloomed since my last passing. It tastes of iron, wet dust, and ancient, trapped sunlight. I am grinding away the decay, stripping the stone back to its pristine, porous geometry. Where I pass, the rock is left intensely clean, capable once again of wicking the mist from the air with perfect efficiency. 

I am moving toward a dissonance. 

Through the thin layer of water rushing over the plaza, my tendrils pick up a chaotic vibration. It is a stutter in the flow, a violent burbling that offends the quiet harmony of the city. I adjust my trajectory, flowing toward the noise, sliding down a narrow, shoulder-width alley. The walls press in on me, scraping gently against my flanks, but my body conforms to the gap, elongating, squeezing through the claustrophobic labyrinth. 

I emerge onto an overlapping ledge, where a series of shallow canals converge. 

Here is the flaw. 

The endless weight of the stacked city has caused a lower chamber to slump. A massive slab of raw basalt, heavy as a boulder, has tilted outward. It has interrupted the shallow canal, forcing the water to foam and crash aggressively over its jagged edge rather than slipping quietly into the next tear-drop basin. The chaotic splashing is throwing precious moisture into the wind, letting it evaporate into the thin air rather than capturing it in the city’s lung.

I flow over the errant stone. My tendrils caress the rough, uncut edges, mapping the geometry of the sheer rock, sensing exactly how it once fit against its neighbor. 

I drape my heavy cowl over the top of the slab, then begin to pool the vast majority of my mass around its base. I let my flesh seep into every crevice, every tiny fissure in the dry-stacked wall. I become one with the stone. 

Then, I apply pressure. 

Deep within me, cables of muscle as thick as tree trunks coil and tighten. I do not push with limbs; I expand with hydrostatic inevitability. I am the ice that splits mountains. I am the slow, undeniable force of geologic time compressed into a living body. 

There is a deep, agonizing, wet groan of rock grinding against rock. I heave. My skin stretches taut, weeping excess moisture from the exertion. The three-ton slab tilts. I guide it, rolling my mass beneath it, finding the perfect fulcrum. 

With a hollow, heavy *clack*, the basalt drops back into its ancestral socket. 

Instantly, the violent splashing ceases. The water rises behind the corrected stone, flattens out, and spills over the lip in a flawless, glassy, silent sheet. The aerodynamics of the fall aerate the water perfectly, saturating it with the sky’s oxygen. 

I drape my tendrils into the corrected flow. The vibration is smooth. The math of the water is harmonious once more. The lung is breathing evenly. A deep, profound satisfaction shudders through my dense flesh. 

My work here is done, but the exertion has drawn heavily on my breath. 

I flow slowly away from the ledge, seeking a deep, roofless chamber where the runnels empty into a submerged, block-long basin. I let my heavy body slough over the edge, sliding down the stepped terraces until I am fully beneath the surface again. The cold water embraces me, rushing into my depleted pores. 

I settle against the bottom, spreading my mass wide to maximize contact with the stone. 

As I lay my cowl against the bedrock, I feel it. 

It is faint at first. A deeply buried, subsonic thrumming traveling through the stone and up into the water of my basin. The surface of the cistern above me begins to dance, breaking into perfect, repeating hexagonal ripples—a cymatic mandala born of sound.

It brings a message from the deep labyrinth, dozens of miles away. It is another of my kind. 

We do not have words. The message is a shape of feeling, a low-frequency pulse carrying the texture of cold stone, the taste of rust-red algae, and the sensation of a vast, overflowing weir beneath the shadow of a heavy cloud. 

It means: *I am here. The mist falls. The heavy earth is held.*

I wait for the vibration to fade, letting the geometric ripples smooth out above me. Then, I press my broad chest firmly against the floor of the cistern. I contract my densest muscles, drawing oxygen deep into my core, and release a single, massive, shuddering pulse toward the stone. 

*I am here. The stone is carved. The sky is drawn down.*

I do not wait to feel if my brother receives it. The clouds are thick above. The city is weeping. The water is fresh, and there is nowhere else in eternity I need to be. I quiet my mind, slow my heart, and let my edges blur once again into the great, dark, endless machine of the world.
