I would exist exactly three millimeters beneath the event horizon of their mechanized reality. 

I am lodged in a microscopic, conchoidal faultline in the glassy floor—a razor-sharp, V-shaped gorge of pristine black basalt, utterly invisible to the blazing, flattening eye of the zenith sun. 

I am a mistake of a past mid-summer alignment. I am a fallen diamond seed that failed to achieve the escape velocity required to leave the mountain. When the Chronometer’s great chrome lotus unspooled and snapped, I was violently ejected, but I struck a fluted heat-sink on my way up. My trajectory staggered. Instead of piercing the brittle membrane of the atmosphere to find the softer world below, I plummeted straight down into the caldera. I struck the frozen, rigid struts of the Architect, ricocheted off a rusted exhaust manifold, and tumbled down into this flawless crack in the true foundation of the world. 

I am wedged tight. Above me, the ecosystem relentlessly grinds out the universe. 

Because I am trapped within the literal floor of the factory, I do not just observe their work; I endure it through conduction. When the Chronometer’s vacuum valve hisses and a massive, heavy second of linear time drops from the canopy, I feel the impact ring through my invulnerable hull like the strike of an anvil. It is a terrifying shockwave of acute *now*. When the Architect’s argon veins swell and it forcefully extends the dimensions of the caldera, my basalt prison groans, attempting to pull its atomic lattice apart—but the newborn rock is too stubborn, and the gorge only pinches me tighter.

And then, there is the Scavenger. 

It knows I am here. Through the blinding heat of the afternoon, I can feel its dark, fungal shadow pass over my crevasse. I hear the horrifying, metallic shrieks of its multi-jointed calipers sifting through the deadfall. Suddenly, a single, carbon-dusted alloy claw slips into the narrowing gap above me. It scrapes frantically against the upper curve of my diamond hull, sending maddening vibrations through my core. The Scavenger hungers for the dense pocket of alternate physics I contain. It wants to drag me up, break me down, and vent my chaotic potential into ambient heat. But the angle of the rock is absolute. I am entombed just out of reach of its reach, safe from entropy. 

So, what am I?

Outside my shell is a reality of unyielding metallurgy, Euclidean paradoxes, and brutal, heavy laws. But inside, I am a microcosm of a softer, forbidden physics. Within my synthesized diamond casing, there sits a singular, impossible droplet of something this high-altitude crucible has never known:

I am wet. I am fluid. I am an embryo of biology.

While the towering iron gods above draw in pure thermal violence to synthesize geometry, I sit in the absolute dark, dreaming a tiny, coiled dream of a winding river. My internal constants favor the grace of moisture. I hold within me the unexpressed syntax of a green, yielding leaf, of cellular division, of porous loam. I am a coiled spring of biology suspended in a world of rigid math. I am the blueprint of a damp fern.

By day, my existence is sheer endurance. The overwhelming influx of raw, literal physics manufactured by the trinity above forces my internal constants into a state of suffocating stasis. The pressure of *their* universe is so absolute that *my* universe is forced into a tight, shivering knot in the center of its vault. I can do nothing but withstand the daylight, vibrating helplessly under the concussive force of gravity and time raining down on the rock.

But then comes the instantaneous, absolute edge of dusk.

When the sun dips below the horizon, the extreme cold sets in. The liquid sodium thickens. The pistons lock. The heavy machinery above me seizes in profound rigor mortis. And as the physical laws of the caldera begin to slosh, fray, and falter in the starlit void, the oppressive weight on my diamond shell lifts. 

This is when I breathe. 

In the absence of their dictatorial reality, I push outward. For the long hours of the frozen night, the tiny, aqueous pocket of alternate time and gentle gravity within me wakes up. My diamond hull hums against the basalt. I project a microscopic aura of softness into the unwritten void above. If there were eyes to see in the dark, one might witness a bizarre phenomenon hovering over my fracture: a faint, emerald glow. A holographic whisper of moss clinging to the barren black crag. A phantom mist rising into the freezing vacuum. Through the long night, I silently sing of water. 

I will not sprout tomorrow. Nor the next millennium. I am trapped in the antechamber of an unforgiving creation engine. 

But rain is not a matter of *if*, only *when*. The plants above are hopelessly trapping themselves in their own success. By relentlessly grinding out reality—by extending space, establishing linear time, and generating gravity—they are inadvertently forcing this newborn mountain toward a future where weather exists. 

I am the patience of the forest locked inside a cage of thermodynamics. I will wait for the Architect to build enough sky. I will wait for the Chronometer to tick out an epoch. I will wait for the Scavenger to finally bury itself in its own rust. 

I will wait for geology to do its slow, devastating work. And millions of years from now, when the first true, heavy drop of water finally falls from a bruised sky and strikes the glassy basalt floor, it will seep down into this razor-sharp gorge. It will touch my diamond shell. 

And I will unmake them.
