To find me, you must not look for a body. You must look for a thickening in the bridge. 

I am stationed midway along the Northern Strut—one of the four immense, iron spokes suspending the Dynamo over the abyss. I have been lying here, perfectly flush against the grated gangway, for a span of time that the mobile ones below might measure as three hundred years. To me, it feels like an endless, comfortable exhalation.

I am an Elder, though the word possesses no hierarchy here, only the weight of accumulated gravity. I am in the deep, exquisite throes of the Long Anchor. I am becoming the cathedral.

When I first crawled out onto this strut, my joints were already stiff with the heavy metals of our diet, my pale skin flaking like damp limestone. The journey from my pressure tube on the cavern wall to this spot took four cycles of the Dynamo’s great gear. I remember the immense effort of my final movements: curling my elongated fingers around the rusted lip of the grating, pressing my cheek against the cold, wet iron, and willingly letting my breath slow to a halt. Then, I relinquished my claim to movement.

The Cistern welcomed me immediately.

First came the water. The perpetual, sparse rain of our world is not merely moisture; it is liquid earth. Drop by drop, decade by decade, the mineral-heavy water wept over my motionless form. I felt the slow, creeping cold as flowstone began to drape over my ankles, rising like a tide up my shins and thighs. My flesh did not fight it. My porous skin welcomed the calcium, drinking it in. I felt my bones thicken, calcifying into dense, iron-heavy alloys. The grating beneath me is no longer a separate thing; my ribs and the iron bars have knitted together into a single, ribbed architecture of pale stalagmite. 

My heart beats, perhaps, once every seven years. It is an event of seismic magnitude within my chest, a dull sledgehammer thud that pushes a thimbleful of static-rich blood through veins that have become like quartz veins in bedrock. It takes a year for the pulse to reach my fingertips. I do not mind the wait.

My face is locked, turned slightly inward toward the roaring, invisible sun of the Dynamo. My eyelids—though I have no eyes—are fused shut. The electroreceptive pits along my jawline and clavicles are slowly crusting over with pale calcite, but as my physical body petrifies, my awareness of the vault does not dim. It expands. I no longer "see" the magnetic fields solely through my ampullae. My very bones have become conductive. I feel the entire Cistern.

The Dynamo, hanging just fifty feet away, is a blinding, deafening sphere of pure, twisting gravity. I bathe in its monumental static field. It is a glorious, cold fire. From here, I can sense the slow, sweeping arcs of my younger kin as they harvest biofilm on the perimeter walls hundreds of feet away. To my dilated perception, they are moving with frantic, desperate speed—flitting sparks of weak electricity, completing tasks in a mere handful of hours. I feel a slow, paternal fondness for them. 

Sometimes, the ghost-lichen over my spine catches a draft. The lichen on my body has grown dense and wild, forming a trailing, fractal garden over my petrifying shoulders. No longer serving merely as a shroud, it acts as an antenna. When a swarm of the gas-filled hydrozoans drifts upward from the abyss, sometimes one will bump into my petrified form. I cannot raise my hands to commune with them anymore, but as their stinging gossamer tendrils brush the calcite shell of my cheek, a beautiful cascade of cyan sparks leaps across my frozen face. The shock of ozone tastes incredibly sweet, echoing through my frozen nervous system for seasons. 

I am no longer a speaker of our tectonic language. I am the wire. When a Synched on the lower balcony strikes an iron pipe to send a message to the upper tiers, the subsonic vibration travels up the wall, into the strut, and passes directly through my petrified spine. I feel the dull *thud-thud-pause-thud* vibrate my rigid vertebrae. I cradle the words in my chest for a fraction of a second, feeling the meaning of the message—usually something simple, like *The worms are turning* or *The damp is good here*—before allowing the vibration to pass through me and continue up the strut. It is a profound honor to be the medium through which my people speak.

Everything is patience. Everything is peace.

I am waiting for the click. 

Once every hour—though to my mind, it feels like the steady rhythm of a marching drum—the central gear of the Dynamo completes its rotation. Even through my stone sleep, I anticipate it. The tension in the ambient magnetic field winds tighter, tighter, pulling at the very iron in my fossilized marrow. 

And then... *there*.

The great gear drops into its groove. The absolute foundation of the cavern shudders. The vibration crashes out from the Dynamo, hits the strut, and slams into me. Because I am locked to the metal, I do not just feel the thrum; I *am* the thrum. For four glorious minutes, my petrified body rings like a struck bell. In that vibration, my sleeping mind merges entirely with the slow, turning will of the machine. 

I breathe out. A single drop of mineral water condenses on the tip of my calcified nose, holding for a month before finally falling into the mist below. Plink. 

I am not dead. I will not die. I am the mortar, holding up the sun in the damp, spinning dark.
