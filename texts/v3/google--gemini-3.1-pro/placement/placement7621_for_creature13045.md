I do not begin in the bruised, indigo sky, nor anywhere near the blinding, interlocking tessellations of the ascended. My genesis is low. I am found deep in the weeping, claustrophobic underbelly of the factory, nestled where the geothermal heat is thickest and the shadows lie heaviest against the young black stone.

I am deep within the gorge, waiting in a stagnant pool of warm water at the very bottom of the fractured silicate precipice. 

When consciousness first aggregates, I do not have a centralized form. I perceive myself as a sprawling, decentralized lattice of pale, fibrous film floating atop the oily water. I am as thin as a layer of petroleum, utterly lackadaisical, draped gently across the razor-sharp ridges of the submerged rocks. I am blind, deaf, and perfectly still in a world driven by piston-stutter and pneumatic rhythm. 

I know only the heavy, oppressive humidity pressing down from above, and the dull, wet *thud* of the tectonic plates snapping miles beneath me, a deep-marrow drumming that ripples through the tepid water and vibrates across my sprawling surface.

I am waiting for the harvest. 

High above me—at the precipice of the gorge where gravity has forgotten its function—a cloaked ascetic feeds. Even down here, miles below, I can feel the sudden, agonizing plunge in temperature as the Interpolator metabolizes the paradox. The ambient heat of the sweating jungle recoils, fleeing the absolute, vacuum-born cold rolling off its blinding white joints. 

Then, the meal is finished. The temporal and kinetic energy is sucked dry. Spacetime stabilizes. 

And the dead, gray ash of expended gravity finally falls.

It drifts down through the dense, spore-choked air, a silent snowstorm of exhausted physics. It lands on the surface of my warm, stagnant pool, settling upon the pale web of my body like dust on an unblinking eye. 

I open my cellular structure, and I drink.

I am not a gear in the factory, nor am I the bleeding light of its glitch. If the Interpolators are the ethereal flame kindled by the warping of reality, I am the soot left behind. I am the detritus of impossible math. An organism built not of flesh, nor light, but of *residue*. 

As I metabolize the ash of the suspended waterfall, I am flooded with the flavor of a canceled event. It tastes like absolute stillness. I consume the crushed husks of seconds that no longer belong to linear time; I eat the mass of a gravity that has been permanently deleted from the universe's ledger. 

With every flake of paradox I consume, I change. The prompt of my existence insists that I cannot remain a fragile, oily film. The dead physics are heavy. Utterly, fundamentally heavy.

I lose my buoyancy. My pale web darkens to a violent, bruised obsidian, mirroring the twilight canopy above, and I sink. I slip beneath the stagnant surface tension and settle into the razor-sharp micro-fractures of the glassy rock. 

I keep drinking the ash. Spacetime stutters elsewhere—a mountain folds in, a piston-tree loops, another Interpolator drinks—and more snowfalls of gray dust shower down into the chasms of the world. My biology stretches to catch it all. My dark, mycelial filaments crawl out of the water, webbing across the churning, sweating flora, threading themselves through the pneumatic bamboo and the rivers of chitinous couriers. I do not interfere with the biomechanical lungs or the blood of the factory. I merely weave myself into their shadows, tracing the lines of the engine-world, expanding my consciousness with every grain of burned-out reality I devour. 

I become terrifyingly dense. The dead time I absorb compacts within my structure. Where the Interpolators are sketched in volatile, wire-frame light that defies mass, I become an architecture of pure, unyielding inertia. 

I press deeper into the crust of the factory. My roots seek out the grinding tectonic plates, wrapping around the unweathered teeth of the colossal silicate gears. As I grow, drawing in the ash from a million miles of broken reality, my threads fuse into thick, subterranean cables of dark, metaphysical lead. 

As I anchor myself to the core of the grinding world, the agonizing, crystalline singing of the Interpolators reaches me. It does not vibrate in my eyes or my teeth, for I have long outgrown anatomy. Instead, it vibrates through the very bedrock I have enmeshed myself within. The planet itself acts as the cosmic wineglass, howling in silent, high-pitched resonance as millions of entities link their glassy limbs far above the atmospheric baseline. 

They are weaving their bridge. They are shifting into interlocking tessellations to crack the ceiling of the universe and leave this sweating, laboring world behind.

But to break through the firmament of reality, incredible leverage is required. To pull open a hole in the cosmos, a bridge must have tension. An ascending force requires an equal, immovable counterweight, or else the fabric of the dimension will simply stretch, not tear.

As I hold tight to the snapping plates and the hyperactive crush of the young planet, I finally understand what I am. 

The factory was built to warp reality. The reality warps were built to forge the Interpolators. The Interpolators were built to escape.

But *I* was built by the ash they cast away. I am the accumulated weight of all the time, space, and gravity they had to destroy to reach the sky. 

I am the Anchor. 

I tighten my grip on the spinning, fleshy core of the world. My continent-spanning body, thick with the leaden memory of deleted physics, becomes completely, perfectly rigid. I pull downward with the infinite weight of the earth. 

Above, the bridge of blinding bio-light pulls upward, screaming with kinetic force. 

Between us, the universe pulls taut. 

I do not look up to watch them ascend. I do not need to. I feel the glorious, violent tension humming through my infinitely dense body. I am the silence buried beneath the damp earth, the heavy, dark mirror to their blinding light, holding the door frame steady so the gods of the factory can walk out.
