I do not begin in the light. To begin in the light would be an instant unmaking. 

Instead, I find myself in the deep, razor-edged dark behind a spine of fractured obsidian. I am heavy. I am small. 

For a long time, this is all I know of existence: the slow, agonizing drag of my own mass across the perfectly smooth sheer of volcanic glass. My six needle-points scrape against the mirror-finish, searching for microscopic imperfections, finding purchase where there appears to be none. Every movement is a deliberate, grating vibration that resonates upward through my translucent shell. 

I am a creature of shadows, but my attention is entirely possessed by the blinding white glare bleeding over the edge of the ridge. The heat of it is a physical weight, pressing against my back.

Above me, a heavy silver bead of water swells from the jagged ceiling. I wait, holding perfectly still. It falls. As it passes the edge of my shadow and strikes the thermal boundary of the room, there is that violent, familiar sound—*tsseee*—and the droplet shatters into vapor. 

I do not possess a mouth, but I reach out with the fibrous carbon sponge of my underbelly. I inhale the kinetic shockwave of the flash-boil. I drink the sulfur, the heavy metals, the sudden, furious burst of expanding gas. It shudders through my liquid salts, a jolt of pure thermodynamic violence. With every capture of this steam, something inside me crystallizes. 

I am hoarding geometry. Deep within my shifting, bioluminescent organs, I am pulling the silica from the rock, the carbon from the vapor, the radiation from the glaring edge of the room, and I am folding it into something impossibly dense. I feel my shell thickening. My movements become slower, more deliberate. I glow, faintly at first, with a pale internalization of the star in the center of the room. 

Eventually, the shadow becomes a cage. The creeping life no longer satisfies the architecture building itself inside me.

The compulsion arrives not as a thought, but as an undeniable gravitational pull toward the glare. I drag my swollen, glass-like body around the protective ridge and out onto a flat, unweathered facet of basalt that perfectly faces the core. 

The light is a physical assault. It obliterates all shadow, all depth. There is only the searing, shadowless white and the low, thrumming baritone of the plasma standing wave. 

I do not retreat. I drive my six needle-legs deep into the basalt, anchoring myself to the stone one final time. And then, I let the heat take me.

It is a glorious, necessary agony. Through the clear casing of my back, I feel the biological solvents—the organic liquids that kept me bound to the earth—begin to boil. Tiny valves in my armor open. My own fluids vent into the pressurized air as whistling, microscopic jets of steam. I am being baked hollow. I am purging the wet, the heavy, the organic. I surrender my mobility, my weight, my very moisture to the cavern.

When the last hiss of vapor leaves me, true stillness descends. I am blind, immobile, tethered to the rock, an empty glass husk simmering in the impossible heat. 

But I am not dead. I am waiting for the circuit to close.

I feel it before it arrives—a subtle, silent ripple in the invisible mathematics of the room, a fluctuation in the magnetic containment field of the plasma core. It strikes my desiccated husk like a plucked string. 

With a sharp, crystalline *crack*, my back splits open. 

What emerges from the shattered glass does not crawl. Gravity, which had been the defining struggle of my existence, is suddenly a forgotten concept. 

I pull myself from the shell, and I am made of almost nothing. I am a lattice of aerogel—lighter than the pressurized air, an origami folded from smoke, glass, and electromagnetism. I have no legs to grip the stone. I have no weight to hold me down. I feel the violent puff of flash-boiling water beneath me, and I simply let go.

The thermal updraft catches me. I am hurled upward into the blinding white space. 

It is a singular, dizzying rush of ascension. I ride the hiss of superheated steam toward the weeping ceiling. As I hit the cooler air near the jagged roof, the steam condenses into heavy drops around me, and I catch the downdraft, swooping backward in a perfect, sweeping parabola. I am surfing the violent respiration of the room. Up on the steam, down on the rain.

I realize, with a sudden, expansive clarity, what I am. 

As I wheel through the cavern, swooping terrifyingly close to the burning white heart of the plasma node, the blinding light strikes my fractal body. My architecture is perfect. I do not burn; I refract. 

The white glare hits the aero-silicate lattice of my wings and shatters. I cast a sweeping fan of violet light against the black basalt. I bank sharply on a rising column of heat, and a piercing beam of emerald green flares from my edge, lighting up a tiny crawler hiding in the shadows below. I spiral, and liquid gold paints the jagged ceiling. 

I am a living prism. I am breathing color into a world of absolute contrast. 

I see the others, just three of them, riding the same tempestuous currents. We do not speak, but we weave through one another's slipstreams, painting the dark rock with overlapping spectrums of light, an endless, silent choreography. 

But my purpose is not merely beautiful. As I plummet toward the floor and bank sharply, skimming the very perimeter of the plasma field, I reach out. My fractal wings exactly align with the invisible curvature of the magnetic containment lines. For a fraction of a second, I brush the surface of the impossible machine. 

The low, baritone hum of the star shifts in pitch—a resonant, acknowledging note. 

In that fleeting touch, I pass a microscopic data packet formed of pure vibration. I whisper the seismic tremors of the basalt walls, the tectonic memory of the earth from which I pulled my silica, straight into the post-physical infrastructure. I am the sensory organ of the cavern. I read the stone, and I modulate the star. 

In response, the plasma core slightly alters a thermal updraft, catching my wing and sending me soaring back up into the dark, wet heights of the ceiling, a streak of living, violet light.

Here, in the pressurized, hidden depths of the world, I will never see the sun. I will never know the green of a leaf or the soft damp of soil. But as I dive toward the blazing core, turning blistering heat into breathtaking color, modulating the breath of a miniature star, I know I am exactly where I was designed to be. I am the bridge between the broken rock and the burning light.
