I do not begin as a shape. I begin as a frequency.

Before I know my own limbs, I know the shuddering of the grand weave beneath me. I am a pressure gradient, a collection of tensions. My first awareness is the deafening, omnipresent roar of the white-out, a sound so absolute it is no longer noise, but architecture. It is the walls and the ceiling of my world. 

Slowly, the noise forces me to coalesce. I gather myself from the vibrations, feeling my consciousness settle into a vast, heavy geometry. 

I feel the deck. The deep-grooved, fibrous mat of the island presses up against the massive planes of my feet. Reflexively, my rear cleats—thick, hooked spars of bone and keratin—drive downward, locking into the kelp-wood. The moment I am anchored, a rush of tactile data floods me. 

Through the soles of my feet, I feel the city. I do not see it—sight is an abstract, useless concept here—but I *know* it with microscopic intimacy. I feel the groaning strain of the fungal lungs blooming beneath us, straining against the kelp-netting, buoyant and eager to tear us into the obliterating sky above. I feel the synchronized, rhythmic thumping of three dozen of my kin two miles to the windward edge, hammering new fronds onto a fraying seam. 

Most of all, I feel the tethers. Through the shuddering deck, I can taste the low, booming bass notes of the great lines stretching down into the abyss. They are the strings of a colossal instrument, singing of the jet streams miles below. 

I am a Storm-Weaver. 

The wind tears at my sloped, slate-grey hide. It is a horizontal hurricane of sleet and frantic air, threatening to rip the warmth from my blood. But I am moving. My upper, multi-jointed arms sweep in wide, practiced arcs, scooping thick, gelatinous globs of raw rime-kelp resin from a hollowed-out cistern beside me. 

I smear the thick sap over my carapace. I cannot see the bioluminescent blue glow it casts into the howling dark, but I can feel its fierce, chemical heat. It clings to me, a heavy, rubbery armor against the freezing gale. 

My lower arms ache with a dull, magnificent exhaustion. These are my hammers. My chisels. For what feels like an eternity—perhaps seasons, perhaps years—I have sat at the leeward edge of the floating continent. Before me lies the Stone. 

It is a ragged shard of igneous mountain, dense and cold, dragged up from the caldera’s outer rim before I was born. I run the sensitive, feathery cilia that crown my domed head over its surface. Through the wind, my tendrils map craters, fractures, and the vast, smooth bore-hole I have carved through its center with my massive, calcified claws. I strike the stone playfully with my lower hand—*thud*. 

The density is perfect. It is heavy enough to sink. It is heavy enough to hold.

Above me, stretching back into the heart of the raft, lies the Cable. My kin have braided it, a serpentine monument of black kelp-fiber thicker than a ancient tree trunk. I smell the sharp, acrid tang of the anti-freeze sap that coats it. 

With my dexterous upper hands, I take the end of the Cable and thread it through the eye of the Stone. The fiber is heavy, resisting the bend, but my muscles lock and heave. I weave the knot, pulling it flush against the basalt. 

I rest my upper hands against the knot. I feel the slack. 

Slack is a terrifying thing. In our world, slack means drift. Slack means the horrific suction of the updrafts winning the ancient war, threatening to drag our woven island upward into the atmospheric tear, where the air grows too thin and the raft pulls itself to pieces. Survival here is a matter of tension. 

I slap my heavy lower claws against the resonant kelp-deck. *Thwump. Thwump. Thwump.*

The vibrations roll out across the island, piercing the chaotic racket of the storm.

Almost immediately, the deck answers. *Thwump. Thwump. Thwump.* 

My kin are gathering. I feel their immense, low-slung bodies vibrating the deck-boards as they approach in the blinding deluge. They circle me and the Stone. They press their long upper hands against my resin-slick hide, a flurry of tactile greetings, murmurs of affection spoken in pressure and heat. They are checking my armor, ensuring the sap is thick, ensuring my hooks are sharp. 

They know what is required. I know what is required. There is no sorrow here, only the deep, profound satisfaction of the work. The floating city is drifting; it requires a new tether to hold the leeward edge. 

The chorus begins. All around me, my companions begin to drum their massive claws against the deck in a rolling, deafening beat. It is a song of mass, of gravity, of descent. It vibrations hum in the marrow of my bones.

I retract my rear cleats, freeing myself from the raft. The wind instantly howls against my bulk, threatening to lift me, but I throw my immense weight forward, wrapping all four of my heavy, muscular arms in a death-grip around the monolith. 

I brace my cleats against the deck. I heave. 

The Stone slides. It breaches the edge of the raft. Gravity, long suppressed by the fungal lungs and the shrieking updrafts, finally reclaims its dominion. 

We fall. 

The transition is unspeakably violent. One second, I am surrounded by the deafening percussive love of my kin; the next, the world goes utterly silent, replaced by the crushing, freezing roar of fluid dynamics. 

We plummet through the boundary layer. The sky tears itself into froth, and then into syrup. We hit the hyper-dense ocean. 

The cold is absolute, a vicious, biting physical entity that tries to crush my chest. But the thick layers of glowing resin burn against the freezing water, cocooning me in a thin layer of habitable heat. 

I am blind, but my cilia are screaming with data. I feel the vertical velocity ripping past my flat head. I feel the horrifying tension in the Cable trailing behind us, screaming in the current. 

We are dropping into the dark at a terrifying speed, but I cannot simply let us fall. The water here is not still. 

My left cilia detect a sudden spike in pressure—a helical current, a localized underwater cyclone spinning up from the depths, rushing to meet us. If it catches the Stone, it will spin us out of control, tangling the miles of Cable above, snapping the line. 

I adjust my weight. I use my massive, hippo-like bulk as a hydrodynamic rudder. I throw my body to the right, angling the Stone's trajectory. We glance off the outer shear of the thermal tornado, spinning violently for a moment before stabilizing. 

Down, down we plunge. The pressure mounts, pressing against my chitin, threatening to rupture the thick, blubbery layers of my biology. The cold begins to eat through the resin. My movements grow sluggish. 

Suddenly, a deafening, chest-rattling boom vibrates through the water—two massive sub-aquatic jet streams colliding just below us. I map the turbulence in my mind. A narrow, still corridor lies between them. 

*Hold on,* I tell myself, my four arms locking around the freezing rock. *Guide it true.*

I steer us into the corridor. The roaring currents tear at my flanks, ripping patches of the blue resin away. The freezing ocean kisses my bare hide, and the shock is a physical agony, a numbing fire. 

But then, through the soles of my dangling feet, I feel it. 

The rebounding pressure of the sea floor. The abyss. 

I shift my weight one last time, pivoting the monolith so its flattest, most stable side faces downward. 

*Impact.* 

The shockwave is apocalyptic. The sound of basalt striking bedrock echoes for miles. 

Immediately, the Cable snaps violently taut. I feel the terrifying yank as the slack is annihilated. The Stone tries to lift, but I press my entire immense weight upon it, letting the hooks of my lowest limbs dig into the rock. 

The Stone settles. It does not move. 

I place my upper hands upon the thick kelp-fiber of the Cable. 

Far, far above, beyond the crushing depths, beyond the thermal tornadoes, beyond the jet streams, I feel them. The city has caught the rope. 

The line pulls perfectly tight. And then, it begins to vibrate. 

A profound, majestic bass note begins to ring out from the stone, traveling up the tether, singing through the pitch-black water, joining the vast chorus of the city above. I can feel my kin up there, pressing their hands to the vibrating line, receiving the note.

*The anchor holds. The city is safe.* 

I let go of the Cable. 

My work is done. 

The freezing temperature of the deep ocean finally breaches my core. I cannot swim back up; the density of the water, the colossal pressure, and the sheer weight of my own body pin me to the bedrock plains. 

I am not afraid. I kneel beside the Stone. 

The water roars around me, a dark, endless lullaby. My breathing slows. The cold ceases to be an agony and becomes a heavy, creeping sleep. 

I feel the current beginning to tug at my limbs, gently promising to lift my massive bulk and sweep me away into the fluted valleys, into the roots of the glowing rime-kelp forests. I know what happens next. My flesh will feed the pale, gelatinous blooms. Form will become spore. Spore will find the cyclonic updrafts. Spore will expand, bursting skyward, carrying the gift of buoyancy back to the great floating weave beneath my kin. 

I close the cilia on my head to the rushing currents. I press my cheek against the vibrating stone. I listen to the deep, eternal hum of the tether I have made. 

I dissolve into the freezing dark, and I become the song of the city.
