I do not begin with a thought. I begin with a gradient.

There is no "I" in the sense of a bounded, interior voice, no spark of ego fighting against the vastness of the caldera. Instead, existence is a seamless, heavy geometry. My consciousness—if it can be called that—is indistinguishable from the temperature of the air and the thick, metallic sweetness resting on my fractal antennae. I am a pressure differential. I am a collection of chemical imperatives, moving at the speed of curing resin.

To be myself is to experience the profound, crushing weight of my own internal fluids. 

I stand upon a high cantilever of hardened yellow amber. The high noon sun beats down, a punishing radiation that would degrade me were it not for the dense forest of my own skin. I feel the light hitting the microscopic, silvered- glass fibers of my velvet. The ultraviolet violence is scattered, bounced away, leaving only a deep, sustaining thermal sink. The heat soaks through my thermoplastic carapace, warming the thick, synthetic oils that serve as my blood and hydraulic fluid. 

I must lift a leg. The imperative comes from a pheromonal drift moving sluggishly across the promenade—a complex hydrocarbon instruction indicating a microscopic fracture in a vaulted arch fifty tiers above. 

The movement takes an eternity. I do not mind; impatience is a biological concept born of fleeting time and beating hearts, and I possess neither. I command the bulbous hinge of my mid-left limb. Slowly, immense internal pressure builds. I feel the viscous, tar-like lubricant force its way through the tight valves of my joint. The limb rises, millimeters at a time, overcoming gravity through pure, unrelenting leverage. I hold it suspended in the dry air, extending it forward. Beneath my foot, I map the exact curvature of the amber causeway through chemical tasting-spines. I lower the heavy pad. It meets the resin in absolute silence. 

I am in motion. 

The world reveals itself not through light and shadow, but through topography and taste. As I walk, my feathery antennae sweep the space before me in agonizingly slow arcs. The environment paints itself in my sensory cortex as a three-dimensional sculpture of heat and scent. 

To my left, I feel a sheer drop-off of temperature—a cool, solid void. It is the shadow cast by a massive, silicone-fleshed leaf arching overheard. I can feel the shape of its serrated edges in the cool air dropping from it. To my right, the dense, vibrating warmth of the main thoroughfare. The space is thick with a dry, metallic-sweet smog, the collective exhale of a thousand of my siblings. I read their exhales like an open ledger. I taste the heavy zinc notes of the masonry workers returning from the caldera’s edge; I taste the sharp, complex aldehydes of the nursing caste tending to the swollen nodes of the structural plants. 

Another locus of silver warmth approaches me head-on along the narrow bridge. 

We do not startle. We do not pause. As we converge, our sweeping antennae brush against one another. The contact is a revelation, a sudden, microscopic intimacy. We stand locked, our feathery appendages intertwined, our silver-glass fibers meshing with the soundless, perfect friction of interlocking gears. 

For ten minutes, we remain frozen in this embrace, exchanging a biochemical calculus. I read the heavy chains of its pheromones: *I carry raw stone. I am older. My burden is dense.* This other creature is heavy with the calcium-rich dust of the caldera wall. My own chemical response is read in turn: *I am empty. I travel upward. I am lighter.*

The mathematics of the city dictate the outcome without a shred of conflict. I yield. 

Slowly, I engage the reverse valves in my joints. The heavy oils push backward. I lower my center of gravity, retreating step by slow, deliberate step, until I find a widened alcove near a branching yellow stalk. I flatten my body against the amber balustrade, compressing my silver velvet into a frictionless sheen. The other creature passes me, its bulk mere millimeters away. We do not look at each other. We are two droplets sliding past one another on a wire, completely integrated into the frictionless machinery of our home.

I resume my climb. The heat grows more intense as I ascend toward the broken crown of the caldera. I am seeking the raw, unweathered stone. 

When I reach the jagged perimeter, the heat is a physical pressure. I lower my smooth, eyeless dome to the raw rock. Beneath my face, a cluster of flat, trowel-like mandibles extend. They are slick with an iridescent solvent. I press them against the stone. 

This is the great work. With agonizing patience, I feel the solvent bite into the molecular bonds of the rock. The stone degrades into a chalky, anhydrous dust. I scrape, and I chew, and I swallow. The dry powder falls into my anterior stomach, where my own heavy oils churn it into a thick paste. The rhythm of this grinding is the only rhythm I know—a sub-audible, internal thrum that ripples through my thermoplastic bones.

Hours pass. Perhaps a day. The sun shifts, altering the angle of the shadows, whispering new thermal instructions to my antennae. I am full. I turn back toward the dense, interwoven metropolis below. 

I find the failing strut. It is a pale-yellow column that has begun to splinter under the sheer weight of a newly grown silicone dome overhead. I approach the host plant. Using the finest, most delicate tips of my antennae, I trace the smooth surface of the stalk until I find a swollen node. I tickle it, laying down a specific, coaxing sequence of pheromones. 

A silent communion occurs. The plant responds. A fissure opens in the node, and it weeps a thick dollop of warm, amber sap. 

I regurgitate my prize. The chalky, oil-steeped rock dust meets the warm sap. Working with my trowel-mandibles, I blend the two into a heavy biological concrete. Millimeter by millimeter, I smooth this paste into the splintering fissure of the plant. I press it flat, contouring it to match the perfect geometric lines of the pillar. This one patch takes until the sun dips behind the caldera rim. It is entirely flawless. 

When the work is done, I turn to descend once more. But something deep within my architecture has shifted.

The command to move my front limb is sent, but the response is sluggish, even by my glacial standards. The hydraulic pressure does not build. I feel my internal polymers, once elastic and warm, beginning to lose their integrity. The heavy oils are turning to sludge, unable to push through the valves. 

There is no panic. Panic is a chaotic impulse, and there is no chaos here. 

I take another step, but the leg locks halfway through the descent. I lower it to the floor. I attempt to move my mid-limbs, but the joints are cooling, fusing into solid plastic. My feathery antennae, usually trembling in constant motion, begin to slow their sweeping arcs. The rich, three-dimensional tapestry of the city's scents begins to fade into a dull, flat sweetness.

I am freezing in place. 

I stand upon a wide, overlapping shingle of silicone leaf, perfectly illuminated by a shaft of high-altitude light. I am becoming a statue, a silver monument to the ongoing algorithm. I feel my interior world hardening, my chemistry settling into a state of profound, eternal equilibrium. 

Then, at the edges of my fading thermal perception, I feel them.

Tiny points of intense cold slipping out from beneath the leaf-shingles. The shadows are detaching themselves. The crystallivores. I feel their microscopic, rippling scales gliding soundlessly up my legs. They feel like drops of heavy crude oil, flat and jagged, moving over my glass velvet. 

They do not bite or tear. They secrete their specialized acid. I feel the molecular bonds of my glass fibers beginning to dissolve, returning to the silica of the earth. I feel my hardened carapace melting back into raw hydrocarbons. 

I am not dying. The concept doesn't apply to a machine forged of mindless symbiosis. I am simply being reallocated. My oils will feed the gliding shards; my heavy, un-digestible rock dust will fall to the floor, to be gathered by another silver worker in a thousand years and built into a new archway. 

As my sensory antennae finally dissolve into the dark, sweet smog, the gradient flattens to zero. I am no longer moving through the city. I am the city. And it is perfectly, flawlessly silent.
