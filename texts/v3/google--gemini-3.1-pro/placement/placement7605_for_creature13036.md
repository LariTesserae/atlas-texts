It begins in the teeth. 

Before the heavy, bruised light hits the milky remnants of my eyes, before the scalding drizzle registers on the pale, rubbery expanse of my back, there is the vibration. I am pressed flat against the convex belly of a massive pressure vessel, my lower jaw resting hard against the weeping iron. It tastes of oxidized copper, grease, and the deep, fertile rot of the muck below. 

The vibration climbs through my iron-dense mandible, bypassing the sealed, puckered cartilage of my ears, and rings directly inside my skull. 

*Thrum-hiss. Thrum-hiss. Thrum-hiss.* 

It is the heartbeat of my sector—a massive centrifugal pump drawing water from the subterranean trenches. To you, it would be a deafening, skull-crushing roar. To me, it is the mother pulse. It is the metronome by which my blood moves. 

When I wake, I do not stretch. The logic of joints and muscles has been reorganized by the geometry of the pit. I am curled upon a galvanized wire-mesh catwalk fifty feet above a jagged floor of freshly fractured basalt. Slowly, I unspool my long limbs. The skin of my knees and elbows, heavily creased and thick as tire treads, weeps a sluice of hot water that has gathered there while I slept. I am mottled a deep bruise-purple and pale, sickly green—perfect camouflage against the algae-slicked pipes and the shadows of the sodium lamps. 

I must breathe. The air here cannot be taken for granted; it is too heavy, a gelatinous soup of steam and industrial waste heat. My massive, barrel ribs expand. I open my wide jaws and take three sharp, wet sips of the fog. *Hah. Hah. Hah.* The moisture coats the inside of my lungs, keeping them from cooking in my chest. 

Down the center of my torso, buried almost entirely beneath a thick, iridescent dermis, are two vertical, mustard-yellow parallel lines. They are the rubberized suspenders of heavy waders. I do not know what "waders" are. I do not remember putting them on. They are simply part of my biology now, the yellow synthetic material woven deeply into my flesh, stretching and contracting with my panting breath. A rusted steel buckle protrudes from my left collarbone, hard and calcified. It feels right. It belongs there, just as the basalt belongs to the wall.

I am hungry. The thermodynamics of this sweltering eternity demand fuel. 

I press my jaw back to the railing. I tune out the *thrum-hiss* of the great pump. I look for the syncopation of life. I feel the concussive drop of a pneumatic piston two levels up. *THUD.* I feel the high-pressure venting of steam. *EEEEEE SHHH.* 

And then, underneath it all, faint but distinct against my bicuspids: *pat-pat-scritch-pat.* 

Thirty yards away. A water rat. Heavy, fat on organic runoff. 

I do not walk; to stand upright in this basin is to invite gravity to drag you onto the sharp granite shattered below. Instead, I swing underneath the catwalk. My spatulate fingers, wide and calloused, lock into the mesh. I move like a pale, loose-jointed shadow suspended over the oily puddles. 

I wait for the rhythm. The basin is overwhelmingly loud, but I know the spaces between the noise. I watch the rat through the orange fog. It is sniffing a smear of pale slime mold on a flange. 

The rhythm builds in my chest. 
The turbine cycles. 
The piston raises. 
Then—*THUD.* 

The sound wave hits, shuddering the steel framework. On the exact millisecond of the impact, I throw myself upward. The rat does not hear me. The rat only hears the machine. I arc over the railing, a silent spasm of purple-mottled muscle in the oppressive heat. The piston resets. *Hiss.* In the half-second of comparative quiet, I am already perfectly still, crouched six feet from the creature. 

The rat twitches, feeling the displaced air, but the fog is too thick. 

The piston raises again. The turbine hits its crescendo. 
*THUD.* 

My hands close around its heavy, damp-furred body. I break its neck in perfect concert with the shrieking of a pressure valve. There is no struggle. There is only the hunt, perfectly synthesized with the environment. 

I eat it raw, crouching on a concrete saddle beneath a dripping pipe. I chew the bones. I swallow the fat. The hot rain falls continuously, washing the grease from my chin, slicking my rubbery skin, running neatly down the deep gutters of my joints. When I am done, I tilt my heavy jaw backward, open my throat, and let the unending drizzle fall directly into my stomach. 

I am satisfied, but my work is not done. 

I am a custodian of the lower tiers. I feel the machinery just as deeply as I feel my own hunger. Right now, there is an itch in my chest. A deep, unsettling arrhythmia. 

I press my jaw to the concrete saddle. There. A stutter in the flow. *Thrum-click-hiss. Thrum-click-hiss.* 

A secondary intake valve is choking. The disruption in the vibration sets my teeth on edge; it makes my own heart palpitate. The machine and I share a circulatory system. If it fails, I will die, not from lack of water or heat, but from the cessation of the rhythm. The immortal stasis must be maintained.

I drop from the saddle, sliding down the raw, weeping basalt of the pit wall, my flattened fingers finding the vertical grooves left by the rotary excavators. I descend toward the floor, where the heat is thickest and the oily puddles gather. 

Through the sickening orange glare, I find the great iron wheel of the valve. It is massive, crusted in a thick layer of dead algae and the bloated, boiled bodies of tiny frogs that tried to seek shelter in the intake pipe. 

I grip the heavy iron wheel. The rusted grip of a massive pipe wrench is fused permanently into the calcified flesh of my right hand—a heavy, brutal extension of my forearm. I wedge the wrench into the spokes of the wheel for leverage. I plant my heavily calloused feet on the wet stone. My barrel chest heaves. *Hah. Hah. Hah.* 

I pull. My muscles, hyper-dense from the gravitational pressure and the relentless climbing, cord against my bones. 

The wheel groans. The frogs and sludge are pulverized, squeezed directly through the iron grate. Suddenly, the blockage clears. The wheel spins violently backward into perfect alignment. 

Immediately, the *click* vanishes from the vibration beneath my feet. The great rush of water restores the *thrum-hiss* to its perfect, metronomic purity. The itch in my chest dissolves, replaced by a profound, biological wave of relief. The heartbeat is whole again. 

I pull myself up to a clear section of a high-pressure pipe. I need to speak. I need to tell the others in the dark that the lower tier is steady. 

Using the heavy iron wrench fused to my hand, I strike the pipe. I do not hit it randomly. I put my weight into it, structuring the kinetic energy. 

*CLANG—CLANG—hiss (from the vent)—CLANG.* 

I press my lower jaw to the pipe and wait.

For ten seconds, there is only the polyrhythmic roar of the turbines. Then, traveling through miles of black stone, up the metal struts, crossing the void, and entering my teeth, comes the reply. 

*Clang... clang... clang.* 

It is faint, sent by a sister or a brother perched on a towering condenser halfway across the basin. *We hear you. The pressure holds. The eternity remains.* 

I settle onto the wire mesh, draping my arms over my knees. The hot rain sizzles gently against the exhaust pipes. The orange light paints bruises onto the fog. I close my vestigial eyes. I press my chin to my knees, chewing a mouthful of the thick, sweltering air, letting the vibration of a thousand massive engines rock me into a dreamless, perfect sleep. 

There is no sky to miss. There is no silence to mourn. I am exactly the shape the world requires.
