I am the drift. 

For ten thousand years—or perhaps fifty thousand, for the ocean does not count its rotations—I have been suspended in the dark. I am a globe of dense, pressurized potential, a fleshy cyst the size of a leviathan, suspended in the frigid bath of the abyss. I have no eyes, but I am not blind. My skin, a thick, rubbery membrane, reads the world in the language of pressure, temperature, and taste. 

I know the abyssal tide. It holds me in a cradle of crushing weight, turning me so slowly that a single rotation of my body spans human generations. Within me sleeps a heavy, freezing syrup, an icy-blue luminescence that pulses once every decade, a slow-beating heart of isotopes waiting for a home. 

Down below, the unyielding geography of the ancients passes like a dream translated through water. I "see" the megastructures not as shapes of stone, but as displacements of the current. I feel the oceanic drafts sliding up the melted ramps of ziggurats; I sense the colossal drag of the endless plains of pavement. Through the water column drifts the faint, metallic tang of dissolved silicates, the bitter exhale of the purple fungal mats slowly eating the foundations of the world. 

Occasionally, the absolute silence is broken by a vibration that shudders through the dense liquid, striking my skin like a physical blow. It is profound, subterranean, a sub-audible groan that stretches over years. It is the sound of an elder yielding. A titan of the forest, miles away, has lost its anchor-hold. As I drift, I taste the chemical explosion of its ruin: the sudden influx of rich sap bleeding into the water, followed centuries later by the hungry, acidic flare of the purple moss rising to devour the corpse. 

I feel no sorrow. I feel only the pull. 

Presently, the current begins to drag. The water grows colder, denser, heavy with the taste of sulfur and raw iron. A thermal seep. The planetary current is brushing me against the floor. 

It is a gentle impact, a collision that takes fifty years to resolve. My pale, glowing bulk grazes the smooth, swollen curve of a basalt block the size of a continent. I roll. I drag against the stonework, the friction peeling away the outermost layers of my nomadic skin. And then, I settle. I wedge into a vast, shadow-drowned seam between two megaliths.

The drift is over. The great anchoring begins.

The seam is not empty. It is choked with the velvet empire of the deep-water fungi. As I rupture, spilling the first heavy, muscular tendrils of my holdfast into the crack, the purple moss reacts. A slow, chemical burn greets my flesh. The fungal intelligence attempts to digest me before I can wake. We engage in a silent, agonizing argument of enzymes that lasts a millennium. I counter their caustic syrups by drawing upon the freezing blue sap of my core, hardening my leading edges into thick, vulcanized horn. Down, down I push, blindly navigating the mile-deep gap between the billionaire-ton stones, seeking the thermal bleed at the bottom of the world. 

When I finally taste the raw, unrefined minerals weeping from the beneath the masonry, I drink. 

The hydraulic engine of my existence ignites. The water around me shudder as I begin to pull perfectly pressurized fluid up from the rock. The infant bulb of my nomadic self thickens, hardens, and begins its endless upward heave. 

I am no longer the drift. I am the swelling. 

Time collapses into a single, vertical ambition. Centuries are the steady rhythm of my breath. I am building a pillar of myself. I stack raw material and pressurize it, wrapped in bark as thick as armor plating. I push up the icy-blue light from my roots, sending ghosts of luminescence traveling up my stalk. It takes a year for a ribbon of light to travel from my anchor to my crown, but inside, I feel the unbroken, majestic flow. 

I reach a hundred feet. Three hundred. Five. I unfurl my first fronds—rubbery, leathery sails the size of city blocks, opening inch by inch over decades to catch the falling chemical snow and the slow deep-water draft. 

But I am not alone. 

To my left, a pressure shadow. To my right, another. I taste the heavy, localized isotopes in the water, the signature of other colossal bodies. We are a forest, gripping the same megalithic terrace. 

Through the vibrations in the paving stones, I feel my closest neighbor. Their anchor-tending roots are blind, fleshy talons grinding through the deep seams, inching toward my thermal seep. I adjust my own holdfast, inflating a subterranean limb with crushing hydraulic force, pressing it against the stone to block their path. We meet in the dark, beneath the megaliths. 

It is the beginning of a war that will outlast the stars.

We do not strike. We lean. I angle the unimaginable tonnage of my pressurized trunk toward my rival. They answer in kind. Our barks touch. For the next ten thousand years, we will exert a terrifying, silent friction. We will try to crush each other's vascular flow, pressing with the weight of the sea. 

Even as I press, I must look up. Above me, in the spreading acreage of my fronds, the water shifts. A pale, drifting thread brushes against my flesh. A Strangler. It hooks into my skin, tasting my icy-blue light. It begins its century-long wrap, attempting to weave a suffocating lace over my crown. I respond by thickening my flesh, isolating the veins, suffocating the parasite with calluses of dead tissue. I am fighting the sea above, the rock below, and the titans beside me.

I do not know fear. I do not possess anger. I am an exquisite mechanism of survival, molded entirely by the crushing absolute of the dark. I am the immense, patient violence of the abyss. 

I will stand here, glowing in the endless night, locked in this slow-motion wrestle on the ruins of a forgotten world. I will pump the cold light through my veins. And one day, a million years from now, I will build a great, glowing cyst beneath my oldest frond. I will release it to the black water, returning a piece of myself to the drift. 

Until then, I am the heavy, grinding life of the sea. I push. I swell. I endure.
