I would not be at the very top, where the water grows thinner and the terrifying jade ceiling looms like a mirror of doom. Nor would I live at the absolute bottom, where the magma seeps boil the water and the sheer obsidian knives wait to flay the careless. 

I would be somewhere in the middle-deep—a creature of the plum-hued twilight, where the weak surficial green and the angry tectonic red bleed together into a permanent, bruising purple. 

My name would be a specific chord, a three-beat rhythm plucked on a thick kelp-anchor, but in my own mind, I am simply a Listener. A Tender of the Middle Web.

I unfold from sleep in my bladder-home. The walls of my pod are the cured, leathery membranes of a giant organic balloon, softly yielding but incredibly resilient. I push against the curved wall, my double-thumbed hands pressing into the tough hide. The heavy, warm water resists me, comforting in its density. I stretch, feeling the seven-and-a-half feet of my body elongate. My spine flexes with the easy grace of cartilage. Over my shoulders, my crimson gills billow and pulse, sifting the rich, cloudy silicates, drawing life from the amniotic soup. 

My eyes—twin convex domes of polished gold—adjust instantly to the dimness. But I do not navigate primarily by sight. 

Through the thick water comes a symphony. 

Along my jawline and down the flanks of my ribbed torso, the raised vibratory nodes of my lateral line begin to twitch. I press the palms of my massive, calloused hands against the floor of my pod. The entire city is singing, murmuring in a low, sub-bass hum. I feel the distant, rhythmic *thwap-thwap-thwap* of farmers scraping moss from the upper tension cables. I feel the slow, swaying drone of a massive neighborhood drifting in a localized convection current. And beneath it all, the deep, terrifying groan of the mountain. Mother-rock, giving birth to itself. 

It is my turn to leave the pod. I slide through the sphincter-like seal of my doorway and out into the crushing verticality of the water column. 

When I exit, I do not fall. There is no falling here, only drifting, only the eternal tug-of-war between the buoyant sky and the heavy earth. I grip the main tether outside my home. The kelp-rope is as thick as my torso, rough and abrasive. The skin of my palms and the soles of my prehensile feet is built of overlapping, horn-like plates, entirely numb to the friction but exquisitely attuned to the tension. 

I hang there in the heavy water, a dark, spindly asterisk against the vastness. I close my golden eyes. I am listening for dissonance. 

To my left, a mile away, a string of habitat-bladders hums a perfect, taut G-note. Below me, the massive anchor vines vibrate in a low C. The tensegrity structure is healthy. But then, drifting up from the crimson depths, a sour, frayed vibration rattles against my ribs. A stuttering, whining pluck. *Snap... creak... snap.*

A tether is dying. The mountain must have shifted in the night. 

I pull a pair of long, pale bone shears from the leather loop at my waist, check the flaked obsidian knife strapped to my thigh, and begin my descent.

I do not swim with frenetic kicks. I pull myself down the cables, using my elongated, prehensile toes and massive hands to vault smoothly from line to line, spiraling deeper into the city. As I descend, the water grows noticeably hotter, pressing against my translucent skin. The ambient light shifts from bruised purple to a dull, glowing red. The giant, fleshy leaves of the deep-water ferns brush against my thighs like velvet.

Far below, the jagged landscape reveals itself. Black basalt columns rise like spears from the dark. Ravines of raw pumice, unsoftened by any eroding current, wait with razor edges. 

I find the source of the dissonance. A primary anchor-line, braided from a hundred strands of kelp-silk, is drawn agonizingly tight against a freshly birthed extrusion of volcanic glass. The mountain cracked during the last shift, and the new edge is sawing through the rope. Several thick strands have already parted, curling upward like dead worms. If this line snaps, the neighborhood above will lurch upward, throwing thousands of lines out of tune, risking a catastrophic structural failure that could rocket homes into the freezing vacuum of the sky.

Here, in the red glow, I must practice the holy art. 

I uncoil a fresh, heavy reel of specially cured kelp from my shoulder. Moving with glacial, deliberate care, I approach the unweathered stone. The water here is near boiling, the thermal vents pushing an updraft that makes my crimson gills flutter wildly. I must anchor myself. My left foot reaches out, the heavily armored toes clamping onto a stable root. 

With both hands, I take the new line and reach past the razor-sharp obsidian to loop a more stable basalt horn. My forearm brushes the raw volcanic glass. Even through my thick callouses, I feel the terrifying, micronic edge of the new stone score a shallow line into my skin. A ribbon of deep purple blood spirals out into the water, curling like smoke. I ignore it. Sensation in the extremities is a luxury; hold is the law.

I bring the line around. My double thumbs dance in the dim red light, weaving the fibers through themselves. *Under, over, pull, lock.* It is a knot passed down through fifty generations—a living thing that actually tightens as the buoyancy pulls against it. 

Now comes the dangerous part. The transfer of tension. 

I take my bone shears. Gripping the old, fraying line with one hand to secure my body, I slice through the remaining strands just above the jagged obsidian. 

For a fraction of a second, the abyss calls. The slack hits the system. The gargantuan gas-bladders three miles above lurch toward the jade ceiling. The water pressure drops violently as the structure rockets upward. 

But my knot catches. 

The new kelp rope snaps perfectly taut. The violent upward motion is halted with a massive, booming sub-bass *THUD* that reverberates through the entire ocean, rattling my teeth and shaking the air from the fern leaves. The line vibrates so violently it blurs, singing a loud, pure note into the deep.

I hang there, one hand gripping the new tether, my long, pliant body drifting horizontally like a flag in the backdraft. Slowly, the vibration evens out. The panicked, trembling resonance of the city calms. A few moments later, a rhythmic vibration comes rippling down the cable from above. 

*Pluck, pluck... long hum.* 

A message from the upper districts. *Hold is good. Tension is true.*

I reply by striking the tight cable twice with the heavy heel of my palm. *Root is secure.*

My task done, I let my body relax. I hang upside down in the heavy, amniotic warmth, my toes gripping the cable, my arms dangling into the red abyss of the mountain. I look upward, past the crisscrossing geometry of a million ropes, past the floating spheres of my people, all the way to the dim, heavy jade ceiling of the sky. 

I am a creature of the tether. I fear the upward fall. But as the gentle, hypoxic warmth of the water lulls me, and the slow, rhythmic breathing of my gills matches the pulse of the vast tensegrity city, I know my place. I am the weight that holds the sky at bay. I am the knot between birth and the void.
