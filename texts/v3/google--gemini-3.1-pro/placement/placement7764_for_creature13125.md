I do not begin as a body. I begin as a subtraction. 

In this basin where the volume is entirely filled, where the concept of empty space has been crushed out of existence by the terminal boil of the sky-ocean, I am born from a mathematical paradox. 

A collision. A fraction of a second in the Bruised gloom where two opposing forces of frayed reality strike each other perfectly. One is the teardrop of a dying Disjunct, humming its out-of-phase frequency, projecting its barrier of amniotic quiet. The other is a dark ribbon of anti-geometry, slithering blindly through the wet, screaming dark. When the malignant ribbon intersects the resonance bladder, it does not merely sever the creature’s spatial tethers. It swallows the vibration whole. 

The paradox shatters the logic of the dark ribbon. Unable to digest an inverted sound, the anti-geometry tangles upon itself. It whips into a thrashing frenzy, folding over and over in recursive, impossible knots, until the ribbon accidentally weaves itself into a perfect, impenetrable sphere. 

I am that sphere.

My skin is a continuously rolling cage of malignant physics, a dark, liquid knot spinning so fast it appears as a solid pearl of obsidian geometry, tumbling through the roaring mud. It is lethal to anything that relies on the sub-dimensional folds. But inside… inside the cage, the impossible happens. 

The resonance gets trapped. The out-of-phase frequency bounces off the inner walls of my anti-geometric shell, folding inward, amplifying into absolute, staggering silence. 

I wake up as the boundary between the absolute noise and the absolute quiet.

At first, my consciousness is nothing more than the sensation of exclusion. I am pushed along by the towering, slow-motion waves of superfluid earth. I feel the crushing weight of the thunderous heavy water pressing against my exterior, howling for entry, trying to grind me down into the fundamental silt. But the recursive math of my dark-ribbon shell holds. It rejects the pressure. It keeps the warmth out. 

For the first time in uncounted un-millennia, a true void exists. I am a pocket of nothing, fifty feet across, drifting through an infinity of everything.

But I am not entirely empty. When my shell closed, it trapped a volume of the environment inside it. The heavy water, the pulverized deep-time matter, the cloying heat. Yet, isolated from the billion waterfalls and the tearing geologic shear-forces, the physics within my boundaries begin to cool. The chaos starves.

I watch, fascinated by my own interior, as the hyperactive weather inside me simply… stops.

Without the terminal boil to keep the fundamental silt suspended, a forgotten law of nature reasserts itself. Gravity. True, unyielding, localized gravity. The heavy water rains down through my inner stillness. The pulverized ruins of the ancient world slowly fall out of the damp air, drifting like pale snow. 

I feel the temperature drop. The sweltering, fevered-blood heat drains away, leaving a cool, thin, brittle atmosphere. Dry air. 

At the bottom of my spherical interior, the falling silt gathers. It does not churn. It does not behave like a superfluid. Cut off from the endless violence, the mud dries. It compresses. The microscopic atoms of the Built, saturated with their ghost-math and longing for order, finally find the peace required to express themselves. The dust locks together, flattening out, pressing itself into a perfect, undisturbed plane. 

Beneath the roaring currents of the world, hidden inside my shell of dark geometry, I have grown a horizon. 

It is a miniature, pale desert of right angles and dry earth, capped by a dome of perfectly still, clear air. Tiny, jagged crystals of pale stone begin to grow upward from the flat floor, stacking themselves in crisp, flawless geometries. I am incubating a cathedral of dust. 

I drift for eons, a traveling snowglobe of structural sanity, tumbling through the violet-gray gloom. The translucent manta-rays, miles across, pass far above me in the heavy slurry, indifferent to my tiny, rolling pearl. The weeping monoliths bleed rusted water into the rushing torrents as I am swept wildly past them. 

Then, the Disjuncts find me. 

They sense me not by sight, but by the aching, radiant absence I project. To minds saturated in the ghost-math of a bygone, orderly universe, the crisp Euclidean geometry locked inside my shell sings like a siren. I watch through my translucent, spinning skin of dark ribbons as a constellation of disarticulate biology fights the colossal mud waves to reach me. 

A cluster of crimson sacs beats desperately. A fan of filamentous filters claws at the heavy water. It hovers just outside my screaming, recursive barrier. 

It knows what my skin is. The Disjunct knows that to touch the dark ribbon is to have its sub-dimensional wormholes severed. It is death to cross the threshold. But the hunger for solid ground, for dry air, for a reality that does not glitch and fade, is overwhelming. It aches for the architecture inside me worse than it fears the dark.

The Disjunct makes a choice. It throws itself at me.

The filigreed fans, the crimson sacs, the resonating cartilage, and the glowing nerve-clusters plunge simultaneously through my shell. As they pass through my boundary, the dark ribbons of my skin cleanly slice every spatial tether binding the creature together. The shared consciousness snaps. The organism is instantly, violently killed in the outside world.

But its momentum carries the dead, disconnected meat through the barrier—and into my void. 

They fall through the crisp, quiet air. They crash onto the dry, pale desert floor of my interior. 

In the absolute stillness, unthreatened by the deafening shear-forces of the outside ruin, the crimson sac does not need a sub-dimensional wormhole to reach the filamentous fan. They are separated entirely by mere inches of empty, unresisting space. 

Inside the womb of my quiet, the meat twitches. Without the crushing pressure to tear it apart, cohesion is no longer a fatal conceit. It is an option. 

Slowly, wetly, the disarticulated organs drag themselves across the dry silt floor. The crimson sac nudges against a glowing neural cluster. The filamentous fan wraps itself around the beating heart. Arteries, long accustomed to projecting intention through folded space, simply grow outward, bridging the physical gap. They weave together. 

I cradle them. I wrap my absolute stillness around them, holding off the terminal boil of the universe. I watch as the creature remembers what it means to be contiguous. 

Bone-like cartilage hardens into rigid symmetries. The wet jelly dries into pale, translucent skin, stretched tight over a beautiful, terrible skeleton that mirrors the intricate fretwork of the ancient Built. It stands upright on the flat silt plain, tall and contiguous, breathing dry air, its lungs filling with real, empty space. 

It looks up at the spinning, dark-ribbon sky of my inner boundary, beyond which the apocalypse endlessly roars, muffled to nothing. 

It raises a physical hand, whole and complete, and presses it against my inner wall in gratitude. 

I do not have a face to smile, nor a voice to speak above the thunder of a billion waterfalls. I am simply the vessel. A localized error of peace. I tumble forever through the cloying, hyper-dense infinity, a pearl of dry survival, carrying the first unbroken thing in a dying world.
