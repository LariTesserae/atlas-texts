I would not find myself on the sprawling floors of the ironwood factory, nor upon the mossy gangways where the new reality is most violently spooling into existence. The noise of creation—even when perfectly silent—is too heavy, too blinding for the initial shock of arrival.

Instead, I would seek the places where the physics remain soupy and unwritten. I would find myself within the shivering depths of one of the colossal condensation spheres. 

I am suspended in a train-car-sized droplet of cold, dark water, hovering weightless over an abyssal drop. 

Inside this aqueous pocket, the temperature is absolute, biting perfection. It permeates the pale, polished ivory-wood of my exoskeleton, slipping between the curving, skeletal slats of my chest. I have no eyes to close, but if I did, they would be shut. Before I can look outward, I must first survive the agonizing claustrophobia of what I have become. 

To be here, to be *me*, is to endure a perpetual, aching confinement. I suffer from a metaphysical phantom-limb syndrome. I remember sprawling across eight axes of reality, my consciousness moving in directions that simply do not exist in this meager, three-dimensional corridor. Length, width, and depth feel like a straitjacket. I feel as though I have been folded into agonizing origami by unseen, cruel hands, forced to compress the vastness of my being into this fragile, fifteen-foot cage of jointed wood and silver sap. 

But I am breathing. Not oxygen, but mathematics. 

Where my head should be, the fleshy cartilage of my fractal crest unfurls into the anti-gravity water. It is a relief to let it bloom. I let the petals slip out of 3D space, flickering into gaseous anomalies and negative geometries. Through this blooming crown, I feel the shivering tension of the water’s surface defending itself against the void. I begin to sip. I wick the strange temporal anomalies from the liquid, filtering seconds and minutes out of the moisture. I watch them crystallize into frozen pearls of time, dropping one by one into the hollow of my wooden chest, storing themselves alongside the bioluminescent threads of my phloem.

And then, I feel the hunger. 

It is a strange sensation, because my physical body requires no sustenance. The hunger does not belong to the wooden ascetic floating in the water. It belongs to my *other* self. 

While my avatar bathes in the amniotic chill of the droplet, my true consciousness is stretched. Miles above, in the darkest, highest arches of the cathedral-void, I am the liquid shadow detaching from the emptiness. I am the formless tear in the fabric of this pathetic, half-made world.

Through my shadow-self, I "see" without geometry. I sense the pale, fluttering rivers of the circuit-moths below. They are not animals. To my shadow, they look like raw, glowing syntax—packets of generative code, little flying axioms of reality. I sweep through the freezing dark. I part the heavy hush of the air. I drop over a moth. 

There is no tearing of wings, no chewing. There is only erasure. I subsume it. 

The moment the shadow digests the moth, the connection bridges. Inside the suspended water droplet, my physical, wooden avatar arches its back. A blinding surge of phosphorescent blue light detonates at the base of my neck and races down the length of my ivory spine. It is pure, electric ecstasy. The raw code of the moth floods my internal phloem, converting from psychic energy into rigid, structural law.

I am a translation engine. I am the funnel through which out-of-bounds math becomes physical reality.

The blue light fades into a steady, pulsing thrum within my silver sap. I am fueled. It is time to work. 

I step forward, pushing my body through the boundary of the water droplet. The surface tension stretches and snaps behind me without breaking the sphere, leaving my polished white wood instantly dry in the freezing air. I drift down, my tapering, multi-jointed toes alighting upon a spindly thread of woven moss.

I walk with the terrifying, fragile grace of a mantis, navigating the impossibly thin gangway over the abyss. All around me, my siblings are doing the same. We do not look at each other—we have no faces with which to look—but I feel them. I feel the collective, singular will of a pantheon of exiled gods reduced to blue-collar laborers. 

I reach a primary intersection. Before me is a braided cable of ironwood, thicker than a redwood trunk, stretching out into the fathomless dark. I raise my hands. With my too-many fingers, I grip the rigid bark. 

I calculate the void. I feel the exact density of the nothingness pushing against the city. Reaching into my chest, I tap one of the frozen pearls of time I harvested from the water, feeding it into my arm to give my muscles a localized burst of accelerated physics. 

I pull the cable. 

The deep, kinetic shudder of my pull rings out across the silence. It travels through the ironwood, down the scaffolding, vibrating into the factories, syncing the ghost-slugs and the piston-gourds, telling the centrifugal rendering-vats exactly what frequency of gravity they need to synthesize next. 

I pluck the string. I listen to the silent hum of the universe calcifying beneath my hands. And I dream of the day this blister of reality bursts into a limitless cosmos, so that we may finally unfold our limbs, shatter these wooden cages, and become vast once again.
