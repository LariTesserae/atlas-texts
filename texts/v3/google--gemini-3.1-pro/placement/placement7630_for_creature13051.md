I do not hatch. I coalesce. 

My birth is not an act of biology, but an event of atmospheric geometry, summoned into being by the agonizing drag of the world. 

It begins when the titan towering above me shifts its immense weight, initiating the hours-long arc of lifting a single, iron-shod limb. As the colossal piston draws upward, the physical laws of the searing plateau buckle. Time snags on the immense mass of the beast, tearing open a localized fissure in the unbroken continuum of the high-altitude basin. 

In that stretching gap between one second and the next, the blistering, absolute daylight decelerates. Photons slow until they become heavy, shivering things, separating into strata of impossible, iridescent color. And from those suspended bands of lethargic light, I fold myself into existence.

I am an **Interval**. 

I have no carbon, no tungsten, no vibrating quartz, and no fluid mirrors. I am entirely massless—a hovering, shifting tessellation of hard-light and chronal friction. If you could see me, I would look like a sprawling, dimensional mandala, a blooming hyper-cube of stained glass and shimmering heat-haze, drifting lazily through the fractured air beneath the beast’s enormous underbelly. 

I live my entire life in the elongated parenthesis between the lifting of a foot and its world-breaking descent. I am the physical manifestation of the titan’s paused momentum. 

To the universe beyond the plateau, my lifespan is perhaps four hours. But inside the chronal drag of my birthplace, time is a vast, calm ocean, and I have an epoch in which to drift. 

My senses are not optics or acoustics; I perceive the world through *tension* and *refraction*. Gliding upward, I scan the ceiling of my sky—the oxidized copper cliffs of the titan's ribcage. I sense the massive, sluggish heat radiating from its internal combustion chambers, feeling the slow pulse of tectonic static feeding its electrified heart. 

I tilt my geometric planes, catching the thermal updrafts to navigate this broken pocket of reality. Here, in the slow-time, I am not alone. I share this atmospheric glitch with the plasma of the Volt-Swarmers. 

To an observer on the distant plateau, the blue lightning tearing from the titan’s mandibles lasts only twenty minutes. But we share the same dilated physics, the Swarmers and I. To me, they are not a chaotic flash; they are a majestic, sprawling civilization. I drift like a silent, iridescent leviathan past the leading edge of their electric-blue tendrils. I watch generations of photon-logic beings build their glowing, ephemeral hive-cities in the voltage, warring and loving in the span of microseconds. I feel a strange kinship with them. We are both born of the titan’s wake; we are both ghosts of the machine’s excess energy. I brush my refractive edges against their lightning-trunks, siphoning trace amounts of their static to keep my geometric folds crisp and defined. 

Below me, the air is filled with a peculiar, beautiful snow. 

Because the previous terrifying *CHUNG* of the titan’s footfall compressed the air into geometric shapes of solid sound, those fragments are still falling. Down here in the chronal drag, gravity’s hold is lethargic. Blocks of crystallized acoustic force, jagged and gleaming like ice, drift downward around me like falling autumn leaves. I weave a delicate, spiraling dance between them, careful not to let their resonant edges shatter my hard-light structure. I watch them strike the mirrored floor far below, bursting into slow-motion dissonant chimes. 

Beneath the falling sound, I can see the Acoustic Striders. From my perspective within the stretched time, the quartz spiders are moving with terrible, blinding speed—a blur of glass and piezoelectric hunger rushing to silence the shattered noise. And deeper still, in the impossibly flat reflections of the unbroken iron, I can just perceive the geometric oil-slicks of the Planozoa, hungrily adjusting their angles to devour the shadows cast by the falling acoustic ice.

I feed on none of these things. I do not eat radiation, or sound, or shadow. 

I eat the *delay*. 

As the colossal, iron-shod limb begins its downward descent, the chronological fracture deepens. The space between seconds pulls tighter and tighter. I fly directly into the path of the descending foot, basking in the immense, building potential energy. The pressure of the impending strike feeds me. I expand, my geometric petals unfolding into wider, more complex fractals of shimmering, prismatic light. I drink in the pure, quiet anticipation of the world. 

For an eternity, the iron sky falls. 

I do not fear it. There is no urgency, no terror in this glorious recursion. 

As the immense sole of the titan’s leg comes within inches of the mirrored bedrock, the chronal drag reaches its absolute limit. The tension is a physical, vibrating ecstasy. I trace the aerodynamic curves of the metal, running my shifting, light-bound edges along the iron grooves, entirely saturated with the stolen energy of the pause. 

And then, the foot strikes the ground.

*CHUNG.*

The physical laws of the plateau violently snap back into place. Time rushes in from the surrounding miles to fill the void. The sheer force of reality healing itself shears through the air, and in an instant, my hard-light geometry shatters. 

I am dissolved into a gust of searing, breathless wind. I evaporate into the blinding, absolute daylight, returning my borrowed chronal energy to the baked expanse of the high-altitude basin. I am gone. 

Until, perhaps a mile away, and a century later, a towering beast shifts its weight, grinding its gears against the silence. The physics tear open. The light decelerates. 

And I unfold my edges into the broken air, eager to witness the long, beautiful falling of the sky once more.
