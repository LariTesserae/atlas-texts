I begin as nothing but a gradient of temperature. 

I do not open my eyes, for in this velvet abyss, eyes are an obsolete concept, a useless extravagance of a forgotten sun. I come into awareness not through sight, but through the heavy, pressing intimacy of the water. It holds me exactly at the temperature of my own internal fluids. There is no shock of cold, no burning heat where I reside in the middle depths of the cathedral. There is only a seamless continuity between what is *me* and what is *beyond me*.

Slowly, the shape of my consciousness spreads out. I realize I am not a point; I am a plane. 

I am unfurling. I extend outward in a slow, languid ripple, letting the ambient pressure press against my sheer, impossibly wide shape. I am one of the giant flatworms, colored like spoiled milk, suspended in the dark like a loosened tapestry falling through a dream. I am thin enough that the faint, rusted radiation of the geothermal vents below vibrates entirely through my translucent tissues. I possess no bones to anchor me, no hard chitin to cage me. I am entirely made of softness, a sprawling carpet of sensory nerves and slow-twitch muscle. 

I do not swim. Swimming is a violent tearing of the water. Instead, I let my edges undulate, catching a subtle, rising convection current born from a warm iron seep far below. 

The heat carries me upward. I drape over the dense, invisible architecture of the world. First, my leading edge slumps over a rounded curve of smooth, buttery terracotta—the lip of a massive, ancient amphora. I pull myself across it, my underside tasting the stone. A gelatinous mat of chemosynthetic bacteria coats the clay. As I glide over it at an agonizingly slow pace, my dermal layers break down the bacteria, drinking the sulfur and iron directly into my sprawling, decentralized gut. I feast merely by existing against the stone.

As I drag my length across the lip of the urn, my dorsal side is alive with delicate friction. A dozen eyeless crustaceans, suspended like snowflakes in the heavy water, drift down to land softly upon my back. Their microscopic chitinous claws *scuff-scuff-scuff* across my flesh. It is a welcome, rhythmic scraping. They are picking away the dead cellular matter sloughing from my epidermis. We are nested together in an endless, quiet exchange.

I slide off the lip of the urn and free-fall through the sweltering dark. My body folds and billows inward, then stretches out again as my belly catches the smoothed length of a basalt pillar. Beside me, a forest of thick, fleshy tube-worms bristles from a cranny in the masonry. I drape over them. Their feathery red gills brush beneath my milky veil, slipping along my underbelly with the softness of a breath. I do not crush them; I weigh nothing in this amniotic womb. We merely intertwine, sharing the heavy water. 

Then, the weather changes. 

Before it arrives, I feel its displacement. The water thickens ahead of a colossal, creeping pressure. A current of blisteringly warm, iron-rich water washes over me, parting the tube-worms and sweeping the crustaceans off my back in a slow-motion flurry. 

I cling gently to my basalt pillar as the great tender of the deep emerges from the labyrinth.

First comes the velvet touch of its cartilage digits. One massive, impossibly long tendril brushes against my milky edge. The touch is infinitely gentle, a hyper-sensitive mapping of the stone upon which I rest. It recognizes me. It traces the contour of my rippling body, reading me as a familiar feature of its holy topography. 

The entity pours past me. I feel the immense, localized currents generated by its passing. The long, throbbing flank of the titan slides just inches from my own skin. The water between us compresses. I feel the deep, rhythmic thumping of the sluggish black blood moving through its towering aqueducts of vein and artery. I feel the opening and closing of its feathery, external gills, pulling the heavy water in and out. 

An instinct, slow and devoid of all frantic thought, tells me to let go of the stone. 

I release my grip on the basalt. The updraft of the creature's passing catches me, and I drift into the narrow slipstream between the moving behemoth and the cathedral wall. My vast, pale body settles gently onto the entity's back. 

I drape myself over the moving spine of the god. 

Once I am settled against its translucent, vibrating hide, the boundary between us begins to blur. Because we are both creatures of sheer surface area and dermal sensitivity, to touch for this long is to communicate. 

Through the skin of the great beast, I feel the residual vibrations of its vast, domed crown of resonant bone. The sonar echoes that strike its head ripple through its flesh and into mine. For a moment, my simple, tapestry-like consciousness expands. Through the drumbeat of its flesh, I suddenly *feel* the entire cathedral. I feel the blisteringly hot vents at the floor; I feel the tepid chill at the zenith; I feel the billions of chewing mouths and the slow-beating hydrozoa bells. 

Riding the creature, I become a localized patch of its own awareness. We are looping through the terracotta honeycomb, pouring through the narrow necks of forgotten urns, squeezing between the melted arches. It does not shrug me off. It accepts my milky, sprawling weight just as the basalt pillar did. 

Here, in the claustrophobic, sweltering dark, I no longer need to navigate. I surrender my slow undulating motion to the movement of the titan. I am a living blanket spread across a moving world, endlessly drinking the rich, sulfurous water swept over its back, dissolving quietly into the peaceful, eternal stasis of the stone womb.
