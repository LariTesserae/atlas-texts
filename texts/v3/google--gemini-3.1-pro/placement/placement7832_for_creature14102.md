To exist here is to surrender the concept of "I." 

If I am to be found in this crushing, supercooled dark, I do not arrive as a traveler. I am born from it. I do not inhabit a body with boundaries, guarded by the walls of an ego and a beating heart. Instead, my consciousness is a diffuse, liquid geometry, scattered across the spectrum of cold chemical light. 

I awaken in the violet. 

The nursery is a slow, trembling sphere of warmth—or at least, the closest thing to warmth in a world that threatens to freeze at any moment. The heavy, hyper-saline brine presses against my fragile, ribbon-like form, but it does not crush me; it holds me together. My internal cavities pump with sugary antifreeze. I have no eyes to open, but suddenly I *see*. 

Sight, for me, is the taste of ancient salt and the flash of photons. The water is thick with the pheromonal whispers of a billion siblings. I feel the rhythmic, subsonic thrum of the great aqueducts vibrating through the raw basalt beneath me. I am a millimeter of translucent jelly, slipping from a biological casing into the abyss, and instantly, a chemical instruction washes over me, precise and undeniable. 

*Rise.* 

As I leave the violet glow of the deep rock, my skin begins to ignite. I do not choose the color. It blooms from within me, a reaction to the specific chemical gradient of the thoroughfare above. I am burning with a ghostly cyan luminescence. I am a Weaver. 

I merge into the slipstream. Millions of us, identical trails of cyan light, undulating in perfect, dizzying synchrony. There is an ecstasy in this movement, an overwhelming sense of belonging. The water rushes over my porous skin, feeding me data. We are a river of glowing silk, winding our way up through the towering, honeycomb arcades of our ancestors' bones. 

Suddenly, the taste of the water shifts. It is a sharp, bitter tang—the unmistakable signature of structural strain. Above us, the sheer, mirror-smooth canyon wall of the basalt fault is pushing inward. A micro-current of water is displacing. A suspension bridge, a delicate arc of extruded silica, is sagging under the geologic pressure of the shifting stone.

I do not think to act. The cyan river simply arcs upward, following the geometry of the strain. 

Thousands of us converge on the sagging strut. We hit the biological glass and immediately begin to weave. I throw my ribbon-body over the Weaver beside me, feeling our gelatinous forms press flush against one another. From my tail, I extrude a microscopic thread of silicate and protein. It hardens the instant it touches the hyper-dense brine. We writhe in a complex, localized knot—a living loom over the abyss. We are depositing ourselves into the architecture, bracing the fracture, spinning glass out of salt and water. We do not stop until the bitter taste of strain fades from the current, replaced by the calm, sweet resonance of equilibrium. 

We unknot, drop back into the slipstream, and continue. 

Time does not exist here. There are no cycles of sun or moon, only the eternal, motionless ache of the cold. My life is measured in the flow of the thoroughfares, the sweet paste regurgitated by the long, pale Agrarians gliding down from the diatom trellises, and the sudden, heart-stopping shifts in the light. 

Once, the entire world goes red. 

It is instantaneous. A blinding, furious crimson explosion from the outer perimeter. The water instantly tastes of sulfur and alien hunger—a glass-nematode drifting down from the void above. 

In the red light, the cyan river halts. We freeze. We flatten ourselves against the silica terraces. There is no panic, only total, systemic lockdown. Through the water, I feel the violent interlocking of the Emerald Soldiers above—thousands of scaled bodies slamming together, locking their puzzle-piece armor, erecting a wall of brilliant, jagged spines. We wait in the deafening silence, illuminated by the flashing red alarm, watching the heavy shadows of the abyss press against our glowing gates. 

When the red bleeds back to a slow, pulsing green, we resume. The city breathes.

But life in the heavy water is fleeting. Over what a surface-dweller might call weeks, I feel a change. My cyan light begins to stutter. The edges of my ribbon-body grow frayed, heavier. The undulating rhythm that once moved me so effortlessly through the slipstream now requires a tremendous, exhausting caloric draw. 

The water tastes different to me now. The frantic, sweet pheromones of the Weavers no longer command me. Instead, I smell a deep, resonating musk coming from the inner canyons of the cleft. It is the scent of gravity. It is the call of the infrastructure.

I detach from the cyan river. I sink softly, drifting down away from the glowing bridges, away from the caustic acid-sculpting of the amber Masons, down toward the massive, braided conduits that pump the lifeblood of the city. 

I approach a living aqueduct. It is a massive tube, wider than a hundred Weavers, vibrating with a subsonic pulse. I swim to the edge of the pipe, where a microscopic gap remains in the weave. 

I press my long, flat side against the body of the Weaver already anchored there. I press my other side tightly against the next. 

The moment we touch, an irreversible chemistry ignites. My bioluminescence dims, fading from bright cyan to a dull, charcoal gray. I feel my skin—my very cellular walls—begin to dissolve and fuse laterally with the neighbors beside me. We are melting into one another. The boundary where "I" ended and "they" began ceases to exist. 

My sight goes dark. The flashing, beautiful conversation of the city's lights vanishes forever. My undulating muscles fuse, locking down. I can no longer swim. I can no longer move. 

Instead, the microscopic, hair-like cilia on my ventral side awaken. Without my command, they begin to beat. A rhythmic, eternal twitching. *Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.* 

There is no grief in this erasure. There is only a profound, oceanic peace. I am drinking the heavy water, and I am pulling it upward. Millions of us are beating our cilia in perfect union, dragging the nutrient-dense brine from the deep aquifer up into the glowing metropolis above. 

I have given up my light. I have given up my motion. I have given up my self. 

I no longer live in the city. The city lives through me. I span the dark, whispering to the stone in the only language that matters here: survival, spun in glass and suspended in silence.
