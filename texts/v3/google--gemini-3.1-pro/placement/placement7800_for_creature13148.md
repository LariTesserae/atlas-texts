To enter this space is to be instantly stripped of the luxury of stillness. 

When I first slip sideways through the wind-polished fissure, I do not possess a body. I am merely an awareness, tumbling into the sweltering, bottle-shaped hollow, riding a thermal updraft thick with chalky dust and pulverized moth-scales. For a time, I let the cavern sift me. I try on different shapes, flashing through the fractal hyper-biology of the pocket-world. I feel the blind, frantic hunger of the pale, needle-toothed lizards. I taste the caustic, metallic tang of the crimson vines bleeding their milky sap. I flutter as a dust-colored moth, my lifespan a blazing, exhaustive fuse of only forty hours. 

But none of these hold me. The ecosystem is too feverish, too tightly wound. I am continually drawn toward the center of the void, toward the organized thrum of the great super-organism. I am pulled toward the woven spheres hanging in the amber twilight. I sink into the heavy, ammonic heat of the settlement.

And then, with a jarring, absolute certainty, my consciousness snaps into flesh.

The first sensation is a brutal, agonizing pull of gravity, instantly countered by an iron-locked grip. I am hanging entirely inverted. The blood does not violently rush to my head; my circulatory system expects this, managing the pressure through specialized valves in my long, sinewy neck. 

I open my eyes. The narrowing of the cavern above filters the blinding sun into a particulate, golden haze. My pupils dilate into massive, dark ovals, swallowing the dim light until the shadows resolve into intricate, three-dimensional layers. 

I look at my hands. They do not look human. The fingers are impossibly long, the knuckles swollen and gnarled. The skin is a mottled maroon, though it is entirely caked in the omnipresent golden dust of the hollow. Deep beneath the surface of my forearms and the sides of my neck, intricate deltas of veins throb with a vivid, bioluminescent violet. I am intensely hot, but I am not sweating. My heart beats in a rapid, shallow flutter, driving my blood through these surface vessels, turning my very skin into a living radiator. 

I am twenty-eight years old. In this brutal, hyper-accelerated world, my body is a magnificent, eroding ruin. I am a venerable elder. 

My keratin hooks—sharp and curved—dig seamlessly into the deeply waxed bark of a massive grey liana. My toes, as long and dextrous as my fingers, wrap around a taut horizontal vine just above my head. I am suspended at the crux of three primary walkways, my body forming a living bracket between the thoroughfares. This is where I am needed. This is my place in the organism. 

For a plains-dweller, the noise of this world would induce madness. The scraping of millions of chitinous legs, the shrieks of the pale lizards, the groaning of the suspension bridges under the weight of my kin—it is a physical pressure against the eardrums. But to me, the cacophony is merely the background canvas. I do not listen with my ears. I listen with my callouses.

Through the thick, padded ridges of my feet and hands, the city speaks to me in vibrations. 

I am a Node, a reader of the structural web. Because I can no longer brachiate with the blinding, fluid speed of the sixteen-year-old hunters, my wisdom has been repurposed. I hold the tension of the settlement in my joints. I feel the rhythmic, bounding approach of a harvest team three tiers above me. I can sense the weight of the gourds slung across their backs, heavy with sweet sap. Through a different vine, I feel the shifting, chaotic tremors of the nurseries, where a dozen toddlers are scrambling over the spherical woven nests.

And then, beneath my left palm, I feel a sickening *slack*.

It is subtle, buried beneath the roar of the cavern, but entirely distinct. A primary anchor-vine on the western wall is tearing beneath the caustic roots of the crimson flora. It is beginning to give way under the heavy traffic of the groaning catwalks.

Instantly, my body moves on instinct compiled over nearly three decades. My hips rotate outward, my spine twisting with hyper-flexible, serpentine grace. With my right hand, I unhook a thick, hollowed piece of beetle-carapace from my hip. From a sheath integrated into my moth-silk veil, I draw a dense bone striker. 

I strike the carapace and drag the bone down a taut, secondary liana in rapid, complex successions. 
*Strike-drag. Strike-drag-strike.* 
The rhythm fires through the suspension network, carrying a metallic, urgent resonance that cuts directly through the ambient chaos. 

Immediately, the biology around me reacts. The super-organism shifts its weight. I feel the vibrations through my feet as dozens of my kin—dust-caked, veiled, moving like fluid terracotta—alter their trajectories in perfect, silent unison. They pivot mid-air, abandoning the failing catwalk, redistributing their mass to older, thicker vines. No one cries out. No one collides. It is a masterpiece of kinetic empathy. A disaster averted by the twitch of my wrist and the sensitivity of my worn skin.

A youth, no older than four, swings down the liana toward me. He pauses, hanging sideways by two prehensile toes, his large golden eyes fixed on mine through the intricate, iridescent green embroidery of his veil. Without a sound, his long fingers blur into a series of rapid signs. 
*Gratitude. Thirst. Offering.*

He proffers a small gourd. I reach out and take it. To drink, I must temporarily peel back the heavy moth-silk from my chin. I pull it upwards, revealing a narrow mouth and interlocking, serrated teeth, stained a pale, ghostly white. I do not inhale through my mouth; moisture is too precious. I tip the gourd back and swallow. 

It is the watery hemolymph of the giant grubs, harvested from the lowest, darkest reaches of the settlement. It tastes of warm salt, pure minerals, and deep, ancestral copper. It is life itself. I swallow it down, feeling the sudden, beautiful coolness seep into my chest, temporarily soothing the violent throbbing of my violet veins.

I pass the gourd back. The boy touches his forehead to my shoulder—a moment of profound, wordless intimacy, the raw pressing of dry bodies in a world where space is nonexistent—and then he drops backward, catching a lower vine and vanishing into the frenetic foliage.

I resettle my veil. The air smells intensely of sweet resin, pulverized insect shells, and the raw ammonia of living things packed tightly together. I look down, past my gripping feet, into the endless vertical drop of the cavern.

Far beneath the interwoven catwalks, beneath the suspended nests, obscured by the amber dust and the massive, leathery leaves, is the deep, shadowed webbing of the lower canopy. I cannot see it, but I know precisely what lives down there. The massive scavenger swarms. The bloated, undulating grubs. 

My arms are growing heavy. The ache deep in my spine is a quiet drumbeat. The joints of my fingers are swelling, the cartilage eaten away by twenty-eight years of holding onto the world. I know, with absolute, serene clarity, that my grip will fail soon. Perhaps in a month. Perhaps in a week. 

When my keratin hooks finally slip, when my violet veins can no longer shed the terrible heat of the hollow, I will fall. 

There is no terror in this thought. None. As I hang inverted, feeling the incredible, living vibration of three thousand of my kin thrumming through my toes, my chest swells with a profound sense of completeness. I will fall into the lower dark. The swarms will take me. By nightfall, I will become the grub. By morning, the harvest teams will descend, their serrated bone blades in hand. They will tap the pale skin of the beetles. 

The boy who just brought me water will drink, and I will be the water. 

I close my massive, golden eyes. I am not trapped here. I am the engine. I let my muscles relax into the tension of the vine, my shallow heartbeat syncing flawlessly with the papery clatter of a million wings.
