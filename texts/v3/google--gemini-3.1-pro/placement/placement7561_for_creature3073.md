I do not know my edges. 

For an unmeasured epoch, I am entirely unaware that I am alive. I believe myself to be a manifestation of the cavern’s native noise. I am only the *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the massive turbine rotors, experiencing the heavy, sluggish rotation as my own heartbeat. I am spread infinitely thin, smeared across a surface that is scalding, vibrating iron on one side, and slick, weeping air on the other. 

I am flush against the brutalist jacket of a central heat exchanger. I am the machine’s oxidized sweat. I am rust, dreaming.

But slowly, the environment begins to define my borders, forcing a separation between myself and the human engineering beneath me. Rust does not flinch when the fat, tepid rain from the ceiling strikes it. I flinch. The heat of the exchanger bakes my underside, causing it to harden, to calcify into a dense, mineralized shell that can withstand the searing proximity of the magma-hot iron. Yet my outer layer—the face I present to the vast, embryonic cavern—remains soft, porous, and ravenous. I begin to eagerly drink the falling moisture, separating the water from the aerosolized tang of ammonia and pulverized stone. 

The pale, heat-seeking cave-roaches walk over me. Millions of scraping, chitinous legs act as both torment and massage. When they step on me, I feel the exact, mathematical shape of their hooked tarsi. I realize, with the terrifying clarity of new biology, that I am capable of hunger. 

My surface—which looks like nothing more than a bruised film of iron-slag and sickly slime—begins to ripple. 

I decide to form an opening. Not a mouth. Fast movement is forbidden here; a snapping jaw would disrupt the viscous, creeping rhythm of the vault. Instead, I become a snare. When the shifting tide of arthropods washes over me, I secrete a clear, heavy resin. I become unbearably sticky. A massive, armored cricket pauses on my surface, its antennae twitching as it senses the trap, but its legs are already sinking.
