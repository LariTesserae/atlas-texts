I do not open my eyes to this world, for eyes are a liability here, easily crushed by the atmospheric weight and fooled by the twisting optical lenses of the brine. Instead, I wake into it through my skin. 

I feel the ocean before I know what I am. It is oppressively heavy, a freezing, viscous pressure that wraps around me like a sunken shroud. It pushes against me from all sides, demanding submission, demanding that I be compressed into a speck, a memory, a nothing. 

But I push back. 

I discover my body in that act of resistance. I am cast in angles and armor. I feel the smooth, overlapping plates of my carapace, hard and cold, shaped from the very obsidian-like stone that makes up the bedrock of this twilight abyss. I flex, and six heavy, segmented limbs articulate in the frozen syrup. The joints pop, adjusting to the localized pressure, settling in. At the end of my limbs: delicate, impossibly dense claws. Tungsten-heavy. Precise.

I am a Tesselator. 

Above me, I sense the frantic, microscopic vibrations of the silent storm. A thermohaline river is tearing through the waters just yards overhead. Thousands of microscopic setae—sensory hairs coating my thorax—tremble. They map the fluid dynamics of the chaos in perfect, instinctual mathematics. I feel the desperate, violent unspooling of a Siphon-ghost’s tether as it frantically anchors itself to a spire above. I feel the sudden, catastrophic drop in salinity where a Glass-Maw has uncoiled, obliterating its prey in an osmotic flash. 

But 
 that violence is not my world. That is the world of the water. My world is the stone.

I lower my heavy body until my manipulators grace the surface of the ruined plateau. Eons of deep-sea friction have melted this place. To my sensory hairs, it feels like the curve of a dying wave—soft, organic, warped. 

But deep within my neural architecture, I *remember* the truth of it. I remember the square. I remember the plumb line. I remember the absolute, glorious perfection of a ninety-degree angle.

I am not alone. 

To my left and right, displacing the heavy, sub-zero liquid, are my kin. We do not speak, not passing words as you understand them. We communicate in the shared language of spatial orientation. We are locked into a geometric grid, spaced precisely eight arm-lengths apart. 

Our purpose stirs within me. It is an ache, a consuming need to rectify the curvature of time. 

We begin to walk. 

The ground pitches downward, a smooth, melted cascade of stone that was once a grand staircase. If I were a mindless grazer, I would simply follow the slope. But I am an archivist of a forgotten empire. I calculate the degree of erosion beneath my front-left limb. I lengthen the joint by precisely three millimeters. I contract my rear-right limb by seven millimeters. My four central legs micro-adjust in a continuous, rippling cascade. 

Despite the undulating, ruined glass beneath me, my thorax remains perfectly, utterly level. I am walking on an invisible, horizontal plane. I am projecting the ghost of the original floorplan into the empty water. 

As we hit our stride, matching the geometry of our ancestors, the reverence takes hold. I rub the grooved plates of my thorax against my mantle. A deep, resonant thrumming bleeds into the frigid syrup. It is a slow, rhythmic pulse. *Here is the wall. Here is the column. Here is the corner.* Around me, my kin join the song. The ocean resonates with a sub-audible choir of mathematical devotion. We are singing an architecture that no longer exists into the crushing dark.

As we traverse the ruined plaza, microbial snow drifts down through the bruised violet light above. It is the waste of the predators, the husks of the glowing krill, the detritus of a frantic biological war. It settles in the slick grooves of the melted stone. 

My manipulators go to work. The true artistry begins. 

I sweep up the bioluminescent dust, gathering it into my dense, delicate claws. I do not follow the organic, melted grooves of the eroded stone. I press the glowing matter into straight lines. I drag my claws in a perfect, unyielding right angle, cementing the light to the obsidian. Beside me, another Tesselator does the same. And another. 

Behind us, a miracle takes shape. A flawless, glowing grid of sharp, Cartesian perfection blooms across the melted, asymmetrical floor. For a fleeting span of minutes, the lost empire lives again. The grand lines of the plaza are illuminated, sharp and proud, defying the slow violence of the deep. 

Looking upon it with my sensory hairs, feeling the precise heat and shape of the glowing lines, brings a profound, static peace to my heavy heart. We have built order in the belly of chaos.

But the ocean is a jealous God.

The setae on my back stand rigidly upright. The acoustic hum of my kin abruptly cuts out. Before the current even arrives, my body knows the thermohaline river has shifted. 

We abandon our work instantly. I drop flat to the melted stone. My six limbs fold inward, locking tightly against the carapace of the kin to my right, who is locked against the kin to their right. We become an iron-tight mosaic, a single, smooth domed surface bolted against the floor by sheer density and grip. 

In utter silence, an invisible waterfall of super-chilled, ultra-dense brine slams into the plaza. 

The pressure is staggering, a localized weight akin to a falling mountain. It shears over my back, buffeting my armor, threatening to pry my claws from the stone and rip me into the atomized void above. The water roars, though I hear no sound—only feel the violent, tearing friction. 

Beneath me, the current scours the plaza clean. The bioluminescent grid is ripped away, scattered back into chaotic, swirling dust. The straight lines are annihilated. The empire falls all over again.

The storm ravages us for hours, or perhaps days. Time is meaningless where light never changes. There is only endurance. We brace. We hold. 

Eventually, the shear-wind lifts. The pressure equalizes. The heavy, sluggish syrup of the twilight zone returns.

I unfold my legs. My joints pop under the shifting weight. I adjust my height, leveling my thorax to the exact millimeter to match a floorplan that is no longer there. I feel the absence of our glowing map beneath my claws. 

I feel no grief. The erasure is simply a variable in the equation. The function remains absolute. 

I rub the grooved plates of my thorax together. The deep-register thrumming begins anew. I step forward into the bruising violet dimness, reach out with my manipulators, and begin, once more, to draw the straight line.
