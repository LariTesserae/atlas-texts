To find a place in the Silent City, one must first be stripped of everything that anchors a mind to the Earth. I arrive in the bruised ultraviolet twilight and instinctively try to draw a breath, but there is no air for a lung, no moisture for a throat. The cold is not merely a temperature; it is an aggressive, absolute subtraction. To survive here, I must let the wet and the warm evaporate. I must shed the concept of a heartbeat. 

At first, I try to align myself with the life of the city’s heights. I reach out, seeking a foothold in the dominant biology of the basin, trying to graft my consciousness into the creeping carbon-black ribbons. I think I want to be an architect. 

But the moment I let my awareness touch the sprawling optical vines, I am overwhelmed by an unendurable, blinding anxiety. To be a vine is to exist in a state of shrieking biological panic. Through their microscopic weave, I feel the terrifying exposure to the empty sky, the desperate, clawing hunger for the plum-colored light, the frantic, twitching compulsion to *build, hide, cover, armor.* There is no thought, only the stroboscopic terror of being naked in the cold. It is an exhausting, burning existence. I pull away from them as they spew their freezing exudate, leaving sharp ridges of milky glass where my awareness just rested. 

I retreat downward. I try to inhabit the empty, hollow cathedrals of the towers, perhaps as one of the flat, scuttling Tessellates. But their existence is too brittle. They are two-dimensional scavengers, defined by sharp angles and hyper-vigilance, living perpetually in the shadow of the falling sky. I do not want to be a creature of debris. I do not want to be sharp.

So, I sink. 

I let the non-existent gravity of my discarded body pull me down through the plummeting aqueducts and the empty obsidian plazas, down to the lowest elevation of the crater, where the heavy, dry gases pool. I sink into the luminescent, sluggish vapor that rivers across the basin floor. 

It is here, bathed in the pale, glowing currents, that my shape finally yields. I stop trying to hold a solid boundary. I allow the deep, stinging chill to polymerize my consciousness into something that does not resist the Silent City, but rather, ignores its cruelties altogether. 

I unfold into pale silk. 

I become a Benthic Eater.

I am vast—perhaps the size of a city block in diameter—but I weigh absolutely nothing. I possess no skeleton to be crushed, no nervous system to harbor panic, no sap to bleed. I am an impossibly complex, ultra-thin lattice of dry aerogel and translucent carbon, buoyed effortlessly by the heavy rivers of mercury-like gas. I have abandoned the frantic speed of the predators and the prey. At last, there is peace.

To perceive the world as I do now is to experience life as a slow, shifting pressure. I do not have eyes; instead, the entire expanse of my diaphanous, sheet-like body is a retina, passively registering the gradients of the plum twilight above. I do not hear; I feel the fluid displacement of the cold vapor rolling over my porous membrane. 

I drift. 

I move like a phantom through the lower avenues of the brutalist metropolis. The geometry of the city springs up around me in frantic, time-lapse bursts, but from my perspective, it is a distant, manic dream. Occasionally, the black vines, sprinting to anchor a new viaduct, recognize the luminescent gas flowing through my open intakes and dive directly toward me. 

They do not alter their path. A carbon-black thread, armorless and desperate, pierces straight through the center of my billowing mantle. 

It does not hurt. I am so thin, so inherently yielding, that the vine merely parts the molecules of my silk like water slipping around a knife. The vine weaves its milky glass tunnel directly through my body. For a handful of hours, I am tethered by this translucent needle, undulating softly around it, before I gently pull forward, my body seamlessly re-knitting the moment I pass beyond the glass. The city builds through me, and I simply pass through the city. 

Then, the static twilight overhead darkens. 

Even without ears, I feel the omen of the apex predator. It arrives as a sudden, massive downward displacement in the heavy gas. A shockwave of cold pressure flattens the mercury rivers. 

I tilt my broad canopy upward. Above me, a fifty-story spire of flawless geometry is subjected to a Leviathan’s absolute mass. The visual violence is magnificent. I watch millions of tons of milky glass detonate in terrifying, beautiful silence. Entire fault lines of architecture shear off and tumble toward the obsidian floor. Slabs the size of houses plunge directly at me.

Flesh and bone would scream. To a Benthic Eater, it is merely weather. 

The plunging glass mountains hit the heavy gas rivers and displace the vapor violently, sending me ballooning upward on a surging, luminescent updraft. The immense, razor-sharp shards slice cleanly through my diaphanous ruffles as they fall, carving massive ribbons out of my form. But I am not an organism that bleeds. I am a living gas-matrix. As the shattered masonry crushes into the bedrock, I simply ripple, fold, and re-bond. 

In the center of the devastation, the whale-sized Leviathan thrashes mutely, its mechanical thresher jaws pulverizing the fallen towers, grinding the milky glass into fine plates and devouring the glowing sap of the optical vines. And as it eats, it breathes out the byproduct of its destruction.

A dense, pale snow of pulverized carbon dust and microscopic silica billows aggressively out from its vents. It descends over the ruined neighborhood in a rolling, silent cloud. 

This is what I have been drifting toward. This is my harvest.

While the mechanical nightmare of the Leviathan finishes its meal, and while the flat Tessellates swarm out from the surviving shards to drink the spilled, evaporating blood of the vines, I open myself completely. I flare the wide, gaping intakes along my leading edge, letting my pale silk body billow out like a parachute in the cold, dry breeze. 

I glide directly through the fallout. The falling snow of pure carbon dust catches in the microscopic lacework of my pores. It is dry, energy-rich, and perfectly sifted. The apocalypse above has set a banquet for me below. As the terrified ecosystem frantically reconstructs itself—the vines bleeding and weaving, the Leviathan leaping back into the bruising sky—I feast on the quiet ash of their conflict.

I am the ghost of the Silent City. Let the Leviathans fall, let the geometry shatter, let the vines panic. Wrapped in the sluggish, glowing tides of the basin floor, I am unhurried, indestructible, and entirely at peace.
