I am flat against the vertical curvature of the world, and the world is screaming. 

I do not hear it with ears. To possess soft, open tympanic membranes in this crushing, high-altitude ocean of noise would be to invite pure agony. Instead, I press my jaw flat against the petrified grain of the Great Bough. Through the porous plates buried perfectly beneath the leathery, moss-purple flesh of my chin and collarbone, I drink in the vibrations of the universe. 

Through the wood, the sky becomes a tactile symphony. I feel the dull, rhythmic thud of galleon-spores drifting miles above. I feel the violent, jagged static of a million insectoid wings buzzing in the abyss below. And right now, I feel the frantic, shuddering tempo of a death-spiral.

I am an Arborian, a harvester of the Deep Fissures, and I am waiting. 

My body is entirely at peace with the impossible gravity of my position. I am clinging to the side of a sweeping, fifteen-mile-wide branch. Below me is nothing but a baking blue void that stretches into a terrifying forever. I do not feel vertigo; gravity is merely a suggestion against the millions of microscopic, rigid setae on the undersides of my four sprawling anchor-limbs. They are locked to the wind-polished cellulose, unbreakable. 

The heat is a crushing weight, yellow and merciless. My black, spherical eyes are shut to it, but as I tilt my head to look up, three layers of nictitating membranes slide open. A polarization film coats my vision, turning the blinding, solar-fried sky into a landscape of iridescent silver shadows. 

Beside me, clinging to the same sheer drop, is my clutch-mate. He taps a frantic, complex rhythm against my flank with his delicate upper hand. Three quick plucks, a scrape, two taps. 

*Closer. They fall closer.*

High in the silver glare, two shadows intersect. The vibration in my jaw sharpens into a frantic, screeching frequency. A feathered serpent, unimaginably vast, has been struck by a needle-beaked diver. The victor peels away; the torn serpent succumbs to the pull of the abyss. 

We wait entirely still. The creature drops toward the lower arc of our bough. 

When it hits, I do not hear the crash, but my teeth smack together. The entire sheer cliff of wood violently shudders. A shockwave travels through my star-shaped footpads, traveling up my joints. It has landed on an outcropping three miles down, resting on the sloping belly of the branch.

Immediately, my clutch-mate and I push off. 

I tuck my delicate artisan hands tightly against my ribs to streamline my body, and I run down the sheer precipice. My spine undulates, my four heavy legs blurring. To a creature of earth, it would look like I am plunging headfirst toward death in the blue void. But to me, the bark is solid ground. I flow over the sweeping, canyon-like ravines carved by acidic micro-rains. 

The sun beats heavily upon my back. My core temperature spikes dangerously. Along my spine, a row of valved spiracles flutter open. I feel the sweet, momentary relief as a wave of heat vents from my interior, shimmering into the air as a mirage of steam, while my internal moisture remains sealed tight. No sweat. To sweat is to die. 

We reach the carcass just as the scent of alien blood begins to boil into the thermals. The feathered serpent is a mountain of crushed scales and iridescent plumage, tangled in the deep grooves of the wood. 

Now, my upper hands go to work. They are swift, slender, and precise. I unhook an iron-wood chisel from my woven belt. Every movement is calculated panic. The vibrations in the wood warn us that the scavenger-swarms—clouds of caustic, tearing sparks—are already altering their flight paths toward the smell of death. 

I scramble onto the wing-joint of the beast. Working with my clutch-mate, I hammer the chisel into the cartilage. We are not interested in the meat; it is too heavy, and it rots in the heat in minutes. We want the architecture. With a wet snap, we pry away a massive sheet of translucent wing-membrane. It is tough as leather, yet light as dandelion silk. Rolled tight, it will become a new water-catcher for our hanging settlement. My companion gathers fallen talons, thick and sharp as swords, sliding them into loops of arachnoid silk slung over his back.

Suddenly, the floor beneath us hums with a sickening, high-tension frequency. 

I press my jaw to the beast’s scales, transferring the vibration from the wood. It is erratic. Heavy. It is not an insect swarm. 

A shadow eclipses the baking sun. I look up. 

A ballooning arachnoid, the size of a longhouse, is descending on a thermal current, dropping a weighted net of gossamer silk directly toward us. The wind plucks the hyper-tensile strands, and even through the air, I can feel the bass-heavy hum of its approach.

My companion freezes, his eyes wide, turning to silver pools. I do not run. I am the Sentinel of this hunt.

I reach over my shoulder and unfasten my flute. It is a massive hollow reed, twice as long as I am, salvaged from the deep, damp crotches of the Wood. I anchor my four footpads furiously against the bark, ensuring my grip is absolute. 

I tilt my head down, opening my chest to the sky. I jam the wide wooden mouthpiece not to my lips, but directly over the primary exhaust spiracle dead-center on my sternum. 

I look up into the shifting, multi-eyed mass of the descending arachnoid. And I contract.

I violently seize every core muscle in my body, forcing the boiling, pressurized thermal heat trapped in my lungs out through the spiracle in a single, devastating blast. I do not breathe the note; I *erupt* it. 

The pressure hits the reed. An ultrasonic, spiraling shriek tears out of the flute, violent and physical. It is an exact auditory duplicate of the apex needle-beaks. To my bone-hearing, the backblast is agonizing, rattling my skull—but to the arachnoid, plunging through the air, the sound is sudden, certain death. 

The massive spider instinctively aborts. It expels a cloud of buoyant gas, catching an updraft, and its immense legs retract in terror as it spirals back up into the blind yellow heat, desperate to escape the apex predator it believes is rising to meet it. 

I lower the flute. My chest heaves. Plumes of steam pour from my spinal spiracles, my body desperate to cool from the exertion. I am entirely drained, but alive. 

My clutch-mate taps my shoulder. *Hurry.*

We bundle the membrane and the talons. Unspooling our stolen spider-silk lines, we begin the grueling climb back up the sheer curve of the world, retreating before the true swarms arrive to pick the serpent's bones clean.

Hours later, the merciless yellow gives way to the deep, bruising purple shadows of the structural crotch. We cross the lip of the canyon and descend into our home. 

Suspended on great rings of fossil-bone between the vertical walls of bark hang the amber teardrops of our city. Woven from silk and cured with pale sap, they glow with a warm, golden translucency in the shadows. We crawl through the lower apertures, entering the damp, crowded, humming interior.

Here, the roaring of the sky is muffled to a distant ache. The air is thick with humidity. Above us, stretched across bone hoops, previous harvests of wing-membranes belly under the weight of condensation. 

Our clutch-mother approaches. Without a sound, her long, delicate fingers trace a pattern across my face, feeling the exhausted heat radiating from my scales. I hand her the rolled membrane. Her pitch-black eyes blink, acknowledging the wealth, the survival, we have brought back to the hive.

She leads me to the center of the chamber. Over a carved amphora of polished wood, she dips a shallow cup made from the horn of a fallen leviathan. 

She passes it to me. I bring it to my jaw. Inside is a single, massive drop of pure, cold water. 

I drink. The liquid traces a path of chilling life down my throat, a profound mercy in a world of fire. Across the amber-lit room, someone begins to pluck a silk tension-string, a rhythmic, gentle vibration that ripples across the resin floor and soothes the aching setae of my tired feet. 

I curl my long, armored body against the curved wall of the hive. Outside, the blue abyss stretches infinitely. The colossal beasts clash and fall, and the sky screams its eternal, violent song. But in here, in the shadows of the monumental boughs, we remain. We do not need to conquer the infinite. We only need to hold on, and endure.
