I do not know the dark, because I have never known the light. 

To me, the universe is not a visual space. It is a vast, roaring topography of tension, temperature, and taste. 

I am a locus of hydrostatic pressure, a singular burning furnace encased in silver velvet. My reality is entirely mapped by the shudder of the ground beneath my six gripping feet. Right now, I am suspended upside-down, clinging to a vascular cable drawn so taut it hums a middle-C against the pads of my claws. 

Through this fiber, I feel the city. 

In my mind's eye—an eye built of barometric whiskers and feathery palps—the void blossoms into a three-dimensional web of kinetic energy. Four miles away (to a human scale, perhaps a few inches), three million of my siblings are chiseling at the base of a stepped terrace. I know they are there because the cable vibrates with their collective *tick-tick-tick*, a rhythmic, sub-audible static that travels through the bridge, through my tarsi, and into the fluid valving of my legs. 

Below me is the Great Wet Nothing. An absence of tension. An absence of heat. 

Above me, a barometric pressure shift pulls at my facial vibrissae. A globe of sap is gathering on the ceiling. I can feel the frigid density of it accumulating in the atmosphere, drawing the ambient warmth out of the humid micro-climate like a black hole sucking in stars. 

Suddenly, my cable thrashes. A frantic, sharp frequency spikes through the strand. *Four beats. A slide. Two staccato snaps.* 

The transmission translates instantly in my neural cluster: *The eastern hexagon is swelling. The sky-flesh is granulating to crush us. We are losing the geometry.*

I do not think. I flow. 

I pump warm fluid through my joint-valves, my legs blurring as I ripple forward along the cable. I am moving at a terrifying velocity, a mercury droplet sliding along a razor blade. Hundreds of my siblings are flowing with me, a shimmering tide of plush velvet rushing toward the crisis. 

We collide with traffic moving the opposite way. There is no slowing down. We simply ripple over and under one another in a seamless, liquid braiding of bodies. As we pass, our facial palps crash together in microsecond collisions. 

*Smack-taste-part.*
*A burst of raw sugar-taste. Scent of freshly exuded resin. Pheromone of high-heat exhaustion.*
*Smack-taste-part.*
*Bitter alarm. The geometry is failing. The ice is creeping.*

The chemical data is overwhelming, a high-speed telemetry of the hive’s overall health. I learn of a bridge collapse in the lower quadrant. I learn of an egg laid precisely twenty seconds ago in the balmy core of the southern ziggurat. I learn everything, all at once, in the space of a heartbeat.

I leap from the vascular cable onto the firm, wet plain of the eastern hexagon floor. 

It is a warzone. The air here is freezing, choked with the frigid sleet of our own condensing body heat. The silver hairs of my pelt bristle, trapping my micro-atmosphere, repelling the agonizing chill of the moisture that beads down upon me. Inside my velvet suit, my core is a ravenous, boiling engine. I am so hungry I am vibrating. 

I smell the enemy before my whiskers touch it. The crisp, green, blindingly cold scent of living xylem. 

The wall of the chamber is reacting to our architecture. The tuber’s flesh is swelling, the cellular walls expanding rapidly, oozing near-freezing sap in a desperate biological imperative to heal the wound we have carved. A smooth, perfect corner of our plaza is actively bulging outward, turning soft and aggressive, attempting to roll over our plaza and entomb us. 

I unlimber my front two manipulator arms. I grip my adze, its blade chipped from a glassy shard of hardened resin. I throw my body against the swelling wall. 

*Tick.*

I strike the crisp starch. It yields. 

*Tick. Tick.*

I peel a razor-thin, mathematically perfect sheet of plant-flesh from the swelling bulge. Instantly, I shove the shaving into my puzzle-piece mandibles. I swallow. 

The biological reaction is explosive. The high-yield sugars hit my internal furnace like jet fuel. My body heat spikes, fighting back the ambient chill of the bleeding xylem. I channel the indigestible cellulose into my glands, feeling it mix with my internal chemistry, slowly extruding a soft bead of fresh resin from my abdomen to repair my dulling tool. 

I do not stop. I cannot stop. Millions of us are a solid, writhing pelt against the wall, a singular organism made of chisels and mouths, chewing back the crushing flesh of the world. *Eat it to burn it. Burn it to move. Move to keep the space open.* We push the encroaching wall back, millimeter by millimeter, reclaiming the sharp, brutalist angle of our plaza. 

Then, the micro-weather turns. 

My vibrissae twitch violently. The barometric pressure changes. The heavy globe of sap that had been accumulating on the ceiling directly above the plaza finally detaches. 

We cannot look up. We are blind. We can only feel the terrible shift in the air as tons of freezing, viscous fluid plummet toward us. 

I try to scramble, to ripple backward, but the crushing weight of the pack is too dense. We are wedged together, frantically chewing. 

The droplet strikes the plaza with the force of an artillery shell.

The shockwave alone ruptures the hydrostatic valving of the masons nearest the impact. They simply burst, their internal fluids flash-freezing in the air. A deluge of icy, thick liquid washes over me. My hydrophobic setae—my silver velvet—reject the moisture, but they cannot reject the sheer kinetic force, nor the staggering thermal drain. 

I am smashed against the crisp white wall of the tuber. My resin adze snaps. 

The sap pools around my legs, already beginning to crystallize into jagged micro-ice. The cold penetrates my velvet shield. It is absolute. It is a terrifying, creeping paralysis. The warm fluid in my skeletal legs turns sluggish. The valving in my joints begins to groan, then lock.

My furnace flickers. I am out of fuel. I try to reach with my manipulator digits for another sheet of starch, but my arms will not rise. The hydrostatic pressure drops. My body heat plummets. 

I am freezing in place. 

I do not feel fear. I do not have the capacity for mourning or regret. I only feel the overwhelming, singular urgency of the hive: *I am taking up space. I am an obstruction.*

A sibling ripples frantically over my back. Our palps smash together one final time. 

*Taste of frozen alarm. Taste of my own failing mechanics. Taste of surrender.*

In that microsecond, I release my grip on my broken resin tool. My sibling snatches it from my digits. Nothing must be wasted. 

Another sibling bumps against my thorax. Then another. They do not pause to register my passing. I am no longer a velvet mason; I am debris cluttering the perfect geometry of the plaza. 

A heavy, concerted push from three workers breaks my locked claws from the starch wall. 

They drag me to the sharp edge of the ziggurat terrace. 

They shove me into the Wet Nothing.

The frantic *tick-tick-tick* of millions of adzes rapidly fades into the damp, hushed static of the cavern. All tension vanishes. There are no vascular cables here. There is no shudder of the city. There is only the long, weightless drift down through the immense, humid void. 

I am tumbling in the pitch black. The freezing sleet falls alongside me, a gentle, quiet precipitation. The burning urgency in my core slowly dims, cooling into a peaceful, crystalline stillness. My legs curl tight beneath my silver velvet.

 Far, far below, the great abyssal lake rises to meet me. 

My life ends with the single, heavy splash in the dark. Beside me in the deep, cold water, a thousand of my siblings wait in the jagged ice, holding up the empty sky.
