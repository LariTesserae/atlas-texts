I am exactly where I am meant to be: suspended four hundred feet above the steaming, fern-choked basalt, bolted to the ribs of Tower Three. 

I do not hear the silence of the world, nor do I hear its shattering noise. My ancestor’s eardrums ruptured centuries ago, a genetic sacrifice willingly yielded to the Leviathan. Instead, I hear with my bones. I hear with the dense, calloused soles of my bare feet resting flat against the rusted, weeping grating of the catwalk. 

Right now, the iron is holding its breath. We are ninety seconds into the cycle. 

I cling to a vertical girder, moving at the speed of a shifting glacier. Haste is a ghost that haunts the lethal, dry world beyond the rift; here, haste is gravity's favorite prey. I am swathed heavily in the waxy, pale canvas of fallen Kites, my body a bulky, glistening bulb of gray-white against the dark metal. The cold is a physical weight, but beneath the grease-rendered hides, I am a furnace of thick muscle, bred for this slow, perpetual ascension. 

Behind my hammered-copper goggles, the smoked glass turns the blinding, high-exposure violence of the sun-lashed canyon into a muted, bruised twilight. From this vantage point, looking out past the weeping belly of the tower, I can see the stark demarcation of our world. A hundred yards out, the lush, dripping micro-jungle abruptly dies, severed by the searing, sun-baked basalt floor. It is a desert of sheer, crystalline light. It terrifies me. But here, inside the perimeter of the great condenser, I am swathed in our sacred, artificial winter. Water drips constantly from the copper piping a hundred feet above my head, pattering against my slicked shoulders. 

At ninety-five seconds, the iron begins to converse. 

It starts in my toes—a subtle, syncopated shivering. *Clack-clack-scrape-thud.* 

I smile, a slow stretching of lips cracked by cold and smeared with black grease to cut the glare. It is Osha, three levels above me in the hanging gardens, striking her heavy lead-capped knuckles against the primary exhaust manifold. Through the conductive skeleton of the tower, her message travels perfectly down the strut, into the grating, and up my shins. 

*Moss heavy. Pods ripe. Come up when the breath is drawn.*

I raise my right hand, the fingers heavy with iron rings. Deliberately, I strike the girder beside my head. *Clang. Clang. Scrape.* 

*Acknowledged. Gathering grease. Wait for the fall.*

One hundred and ten seconds. The deep, grinding friction of the colossal flywheels begins to resonate through my teeth. The air pressure in the shaft changes, growing incredibly dense, pushing against my chest like a physical hand. This is the communion. 

I slide my hand down to my hip, unhooking a heavy, rivet-studded bucket of viscous black lubricant. I edge forward, my bare feet finding the familiar rust-pocks on the lip of the danger zone. I gaze into the yawning, dark belly of the engine shaft. In the gloom, miles of colossal chains hang taut, waiting. 

One hundred and fifteen seconds. The pressure is unbearable. 

*Now.*

The iron leaps beneath me. Though I have no ears to hear it, my entire body is wracked by the *CRACK-THOOM* that fractures the air. The physical force of the acoustic blast hits me like a rogue wave, shaking the condensation from my waxy coat in an atomized cloud of fog. My vision blurs for a fraction of a second as the smoked glass rattles in its copper frame. Down in the loamy bogs far below, I know the giant domed oxen are buzzing in sympathetic resonance, their heavy shells humming with the tectonic chime. 

And as the squealing upward drag of the chains begins, I step into the void. 

I have exact thirty seconds of upward drag before the secondary gear engages. Moving with an oceanic, fluid grace, I reach out with a broad, frayed brush and paint the agonizingly slow-moving links of the chain with deep, wet strokes of black grease. There is no panic. Panic makes the hands shake. I am perfectly tethered to the rhythm of the machine. I count my own slow heartbeats. `One. Two. Three.` 

The chain is freezing to the touch, vibrating with the torque of a thousand tons of iron moving upward. It is beautiful. It is the artery of our god. 

At the twenty-eighth heartbeat, I step backward, sliding seamlessly into the recessed alcove just as the secondary clamps snap shut with a vibration that nearly knocks me off my feet. Had I remained, the sheer tension of the locking mechanism would have sheared me in half. But I am safe. I am synchronized. 

I hook my bucket back to my hip and begin the climb up to Osha. 

As I ascend the rusted scaffolding, thick with suffocating carpets of silver moss, a Rift-Kite detaches itself from a heavy girder just to my left. It is massive, pale as a ghost, its jointed wings unfolding above me in the dense, freezing thermals blown out by the fans. We look at each other through the localized drizzle. I do not flinch, and it does not screech. We are brothers of the slow breath. It banks on the frigid air, drifting out into the dizzying void between Tower Three and Tower Four, a pale leaf riding the canyon's roar.

I climb, surrounded by blinding light and dripping shadows. The water falls. The iron cools. My knuckles rest heavy against the metal, waiting for the next song. In this pocket of weeping rust and booming thunder, I am entirely, wonderfully whole. I have a hundred seconds left until the world breathes again. There is all the time in the world.
