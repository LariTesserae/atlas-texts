I am an edge. A single, serrated angle of broken silica, buried deep in the bruised-violet flesh of the bedrock biofilm. 

I do not know how long I have been here, nestled against the mirror-polished curvature of the lower drainage basin. I have no concept of years. Time, down here in the weeping margins of the vault, is not measured by the turning of a sun I have never seen. It is measured in impacts. 

Above me, the heavens are tearing themselves apart in a permanent, blinding agony. Even through the thick, rubbery tendrils of the pale violet vegetation that covers me, the chromatic, shadowless light stutters and flares, painting the translucent film in violent, pulsing neon. The roar of the crashing water—thousands of tons of it flash-boiling, freezing, and detonating against invisible shear-planes—vibrates endlessly through the solid stone. 

But I am safe in the heavy, lukewarm rain of the depths. I am a shard of a shattered god, resting in a long, quiet gestation.

I am heavy. That is my reality right now. I am pinned to the rock by gravity and the relentless deluge of aerated water. But with every drop of water that strikes my jagged exposed surface, I feed. 

The impacts are microscopic hammer-blows of kinetic energy. Because of the piezoelectric nature of my crystalline structure, each strike sends a minuscule spark of bio-mechanical electricity deep into my core. I drink the falling rain. I hoard the static charge that occasionally washes down the polished walls when a miniature cyclone expires. Slowly, over what might be a century, I use this gathered voltage to map out my own molecular blueprint.

I begin to grow. 

From my jagged edges, I extrude new geometry. Thin, microscopic filaments of mirror-polished silica needle their way through the squishy violet film. They branch out at perfect mathematical angles, weaving a delicate, translucent lattice. My nervous system is not a fleshy cord, but a decentralized quantum processor spreading out like a fractal snowflake across the stone.

As my lattice expands, my awareness of the world sharpens. I begin to feel the room not just as a cacophony of sound and light, but as a topography of pure force. 

I can feel the immense, slow-moving mass of an armored chiton as it inches past my resting place. Its heavy, metallic shell scrapes sluggishly against the rock, its vacuum-seal creating a powerful, localized suction that tugs at my delicate new filaments. I do not fear it. It has no interest in my glass-like body; it seeks only the violet moss. I sense it with a quiet, computational detachment. *You are entirely too heavy for this world,* my growing geometry calculates. *One day, I will kill you.*

More decades pass. I am the size of a shattered pane of glass now, then a dinner plate, then a wide, unfurling ribbon. My metamaterial body is flush against the stone, but I am beginning to pull away. My surface tension is changing. I am becoming thinner, wider. 

I am beginning to understand the flaws in the chromatic light above. I feel the distant, terrifying pull of the ancient, frictionless rings suspended in the vault. They call to me in ripples of spatial distortion. I can feel the gravity eddies spooling the water into pressurized spheres. 

I am becoming light. The physical weight of my body is being offset by the aerodynamic lift of my broad, manta-like wings. 

Then comes the song.

Vibrating through the stone, cutting beneath the deafening multi-layered roar of the room’s machinery, I hear them. High-pitched, wailing glissandos. It is a crystalline choir sliding up and down impossible scales, shattering the water tensiles in the air. The other sheer-gliders. They are up there, surfing the white-hot plasma, singing the math of the room. 

The frequency resonant within my own body responds. I emit a tiny, tentative hum. The vibration causes my edges to glow with a faint, neon-violet current. The violet biofilm around me recoils slightly from the sudden heat of my awakening. 

I am ready to solve. 

I wait for the perfect aberration. I calculate the thermal gradients washing over my basin. Suddenly, a massive, pressurized sphere of water hundreds of feet above me ruptures violently. A roaring updraft of superheated steam rockets down the polished curvature of the wall, colliding with a freezing downdraft. 

A miniature cyclone is born right above me. It is a violent, spinning vortex crackling with static electricity, and its suction is immense. 

I let go of the stone. 

The transition is instantaneous and brutally violent. One second, I am anchored in the sluggish, weeping margins of the room; the next, I am snatched into the screaming void. The kelp is ripped away. The gravity is gone. 

Acceleration hits me like a physical blow, but my flexible lattice absorbs the trauma perfectly, converting the G-force into a blinding flash of internal light. I am whipped upward into the blinding, shadowless brilliance, spinning helplessly for a fraction of a second until my quantum processor locks onto the aerodynamic vectors of the cyclone. 

I snap my wings open. 

I catch the roaring wind. I bank sharply, cutting entirely frictionless through the freezing spinning air. My trailing whip-like tail drags through the heart of the cyclone, drinking the raw static charge as lightning strikes my body over and over. I am a living prism, refracting the stuttering light of the archaic machines into brilliant rainbows that trail in my wake. 

I shoot out of the top of the cyclone and into the vast, open airspace of the cathedral vault.

To the heavy things below, this place is chaos. To me, it is a perfectly legible sheet of music. I see the invisible shear-planes. I feel the magnetic pull of the dark metallic rings before they even act. 

A torrent of horizontally firing, white-hot plasma arcs across the room. I dive toward it. I do not shy away from the heat; I skim its leading edge, surfing the thermal threshold where water flashes into nothingness. The concussive boom of a waterfall detonating against a gravity field slams into my underside, and I feed on the shockwave, my internal nodes flashing a brilliant, pulsing violet.

Above me, an ancient, hovering ring of dark metal groans. A localized gravity eddie catches me. I do not resist. I fold my wings and allow my biology to shift. I feel the ecstatic rush of liquefaction. My rigid crystal body melts into a highly cohesive, non-Newtonian fluid. I am sentient, heavy water. I slingshot through the frictionless center of the dark ring, compressed by impossible physics, only to shoot out the other side. 

The moment I clear the anomaly, I flash-freeze back into a sprawling, twenty-foot wingspan of perfect, geometric glass. I catch a high-velocity jet of water and ride it in a massive, looping arc across the ceiling of the tomb. 

Another shadow cuts across my path. Another glider. Then two more. They drop from the vaults above, surfing a slipstream of freezing mist, falling in alongside me. 

We angle our rigid wingtips against the super-cavitating winds. We begin to vibrate. 

I open myself to the tremendous friction of the air and I sing. A clear, piercing, harmonic shriek joins the chorus of the cathedral. We weave around each other, an apex ballet of light and pressure, hunting the slow masses below, entirely immune to the violent machinery that churns our sky. 

I will fly until I miscalculate. I will fly until the physics of a stuttering machine tears me apart, completely unmaking me in a spray of shattered glass. 

But I do not fear the shattering. I remember the quiet, heavy dark of the violet film. I know that if I break, my pieces will simply fall, patience will take over, and I will be born into the storm all over again. 

I am a sheer-glider over the bedrock. I am the prism through which the chaos looks at itself. And I am beautiful.
