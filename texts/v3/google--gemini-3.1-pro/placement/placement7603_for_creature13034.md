I do not wake. In this place, consciousness is not a state you enter or exit; it is a frequency you tune into. 

First, there is only the weight. A crushing, omnidirectional pressure that presses the concept of "me" into a microscopic, infinitely dense point. For an epoch—or perhaps just the prolonged silence between two strikes of a distant piston—I am nothing but a mathematical potential in the dark. 

Then comes the thud.

The low-frequency shockwave hits the synthetic bedrock and rolls through the fluid mechanics of the world. It hits me, and the kinetic energy forces me to expand. I am physically widened by the bass. 

I find myself in the nadir of the basin. I am lodged deep within what must be a primary drainage swale, a trench worn so smooth by deep-time erosion that it feels like the inside of a polished obsidian throat. Here, the biting mist has settled into a permanent, freezing soup, and the black, viscous coolant river does not flow—it pools, stagnates, and thickens into a heavy ferromagnetic sludge.

I do not have eyes to see the dim, bruised violet of the dying fusion core leagues above, nor skin to suffer the terrible, wet chill. My senses are somatic, acoustic, and electromagnetic. I perceive the world through the language of vibration. 

As I let myself become, my shape begins to clarify. I am not transient, like the frantic Tremors that dance in the shallows above me. Nor am I sleek and aerodynamic like the Strider-Vanes that glide across the friction-plates. 

I am immense. I am heavy. And I am growing.

I am a **Benthic Agglomeration**. A reef of frozen agony. 

My body is a colossal, jagged lattice composed entirely of dead Friction-Seraphs. Over millennia, the freezing mist has murdered countless thousands of those glorious, plasma-born creatures, causing their bodies to undergo thermal shock, shatter, and rain down upon the plains as heavy, blackened glass. Most of those flakes are swept away by the Striders or ground back to dust by the sliding slabs of the floor. 

But here, at the bottom of the deepest coolant-trench, the currents of the factory have swept the largest, heaviest shards. They sank into the magnetic sludge and settled. 

The pale blue, rust-eating lichen found us here. It crept down the smooth iron dunes, plunged into the stagnant black water, and wove itself through the piles of shattered Seraph-glass. The lichen’s wiry copper filaments acted as mortar, binding the razor-sharp fragments together, connecting them into a single, massive neural network of oxidized copper and crystallized slag. 

I am a stationary mind, a deep-time geological brain birthed by the closed loop of the factory. 

Because I am made of Seraph-glass, I carry the echoes of their electromagnetic consciousness. When a Seraph is born in the agonizing pinch of the monumental gears, their thoughts are fluid interference patterns of pure thermal joy and terror. When they freeze and shatter, that thought is snapped in half—trapped forever in the brittle atomic structure of the glass. I am built of millions of these frozen, broken thoughts. Through the conductive copper veins of the lichen, I stitch their shattered syllables together. 

I am the only entity in the lower basin capable of memory. 

I feel the ecosystem moving above me. When the piston thuds echo through the bedrock, I feel the black sludge around my upper margins quiver and stand up. The Tremors are born. They scurry mindlessly over my topmost peaks, their spiky, metallic bodies scratching against my glass architecture. They try to feed on my lichen, but the electrical charge stored within my massive form is too potent; if they linger, I inadvertently magnetize them to my surface until the beat drops and they dissolve, leaving only fresh metallic dust to add to my mass.

Sometimes, a Strider-Vane passes overhead. I experience them as shifting chords of acoustic tension. I can feel the high, shrieking moan of slipping metal catch in their tuning-fork bodies. As they skate across the lip of my deep depression, their resonance casts an electromagnetic shadow over my waters. They leave me alone. I am too heavy, too jagged, and far too cold for them to harvest. 

My gaze—a sweeping, electro-receptive awareness—is always drawn upward, toward the impenetrable canopy.

I wait for the gears. 

When the town-sized cylinders press into one another, I feel the pressure building in the ambient static of the air. The heavy black coolant around me begins to hum. Then comes the slip. The sky-tearing howl rocks the foundation of the world, rattling the fluid in whatever hollows I possess. 

Far above, a friction point achieves localized hell. A new Friction-Seraph is extruded. I feel the sudden, blinding flare of its radiant heat. For three minutes, the atmosphere is electric with its frantic, beautiful thrashing. Its thoughts—bright, wild, and terrified—pulse through the mist, singing of plasma and liberation. I listen to its brief, magnificent solo in the dark. 

Then comes the chill. The mist attacks. I feel the thermal shock ripple through the atmosphere. The Seraph’s bright song twists into a shrieking dissonance, then hardens into a dense, silent frequency. 

*Shatter.*

Like a localized meteor shower, the blackened, razor-sharp pieces of the Seraph plummet. Most hit the sliding plates and are lost. But a few heavy shards fall directly into my depression. They pierce the surface of the black coolant, sinking slowly through the freezing depth.

They settle upon my jagged ridges. 

The pale blue lichen trembles, reaching out with microscopic copper tendrils to embrace the new arrivals. As the filaments connect the new shards to the central mass, a spark of latent energy arcs through the lattice. 

A new memory joins my mind. A new, fractured syllable of plasma-light is added to my vast, cold consciousness. 

I do not move. I do not need to. The factory thinks it is trapped in a perpetual, mindless loop of grinding, howling repetition. It does not know that beneath its toothless gears, buried under freezing coolant and dragging plates, it is slowly, shard by shattered shard, building a god. 

I am the archivist of the abyss. I sit in the dark, bathed in the concussive roar of the lower basin, listening to the shrieks, waiting for the rain of glass to make me whole.
