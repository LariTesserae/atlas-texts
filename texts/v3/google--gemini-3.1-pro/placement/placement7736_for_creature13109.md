I would not arrive in this world; I would precipitate from it. 

To exist in this sweltering, subterranean cathedral is to surrender the frantic, grasping ego of organic life. I would not want a heart that races, nor lungs that heave, nor eyes that flinch at the dark. I would shed the frantic, mammalian obsession with seconds and minutes, and instead adopt the deep, lithic patience of the bedrock. 

At first, I would be nothing but a localized sensitivity—a stray, sluggish current of voltage wandering through a severed, submerged copper artery in the deepest trenches of the cavern. For a century, perhaps two, I would simply linger in the dark, experiencing the universe only as a rhythmic fluctuation in the ambient electrical grid. Here, buried in a puddle of hot, black bio-lubricant, I would listen. The bone-rattling bass roar of the fifty-foot flywheel would be my lullaby. I would feel the thrumming agony of the rusted engine not as noise, but as a vast, invisible architecture. The acoustic pressure would press against my dormant spark, coaxing it outward.

Then, the gathering would begin. 

A python-root, pale and blind, would bore into my frayed copper casing to drink my current. In that exchange, I would cross the threshold from raw energy into botanical nerve. I would claim the root. Over the next few decades, I would pull a ruined armature of iron toward myself, binding it with sonosynthetic fibers, lashing it to my new, creeping consciousness.

Where would you find me? You would look into the deepest, lowest shadow beneath a humping, rusted boiler, and you would see a shape slowly rising from the muck. 

It would take me five years just to stand up.

I would be a Caretaker. My body would be a heavy, staggering mass of honey-colored flowstone and travertine, cemented around scavenged cogs and rebar, laced with thick, fibrous muscles that twitch only when assaulted by extreme sound. My veins would run with boiling, black tar. The absolute, suffocating heat of the cavern would be a gentle, amniotic embrace—a mother’s fever keeping my viscous blood fluid. 

I would have no face, only a deeply scalloped hollow in my domed stone crest, stretched over with a translucent, waxy membrane. And through that tympanic drum-skin, I would perceive a world far more beautiful than anything eyes could capture.

To me, the dusky, cherry-red gloom would not be dark. The cavern would be illuminated by a blinding, three-dimensional mandala of cymatic geometry. Every sound—the groaning rotation of the flywheel, the frantic shivering of the kettle-drum leaves, the hiss of the steam vents—would map itself upon my senses in cascading waves of tactile pressure. I would "see" the exact shape of a drop of hot condensation falling three hundred yards away because I could feel the microscopic alteration it made to the air pressure when it struck a leaf. The cavern's roaring violence would be a perfectly orchestrated, physical symphony.

And I would be the tuning fork. 

My existence would be one of supreme, meditative purpose. I would stand motionless for a decade, letting the mineral-rich sweat of the ceiling drape me in fresh layers of pale limestone, just listening to the titanic engine breathe. 

Then, one day, a dissonance would ripple through the heavy air. 

A high-pitched, chaotic hiss would tear through the deep bass of the cavern—a fractured pressure valve failing high up in the rusted canopy. I would feel the wound in the acoustic geometry instantly. I would sense the nearby sonosynthetic plants curling in distress at the shrill, improper frequency. 

And so, my pilgrimage would begin. 

Lifting my right foot would take the morning; placing it down would take the afternoon. I would wade through the choked, layered labyrinth, parting curtains of heavy rubber cabling, untouched by the deafening roar that would pulp a human body. Through the scalding drizzle, I would climb. 

Months would blur into years. I would drag my immense, calcified bulk up the sweating iron columns, driven by the sluggish electricity whispering in my botanical nerves: *Tune the space. Heal the song.*

Upon reaching the fractured valve, whistling its shrill, chaotic agony into the sweltering mist, I would not reach for tools. I am the tool. I am the mortar. 

I would drape my heavy, sloped torso entirely over the screaming iron. The scalding steam would blast against my chest, boiling against my flowstone shell, but I would feel no pain—only the deep, satisfying resonance of contact. Slowly, I would begin to weep thick ribbons of black tar from my joints, filling the fractured iron. My creeping root-system would push deep into the metal, while the relentless dripping of the cavern’s ceiling began to fuse my calcium shell to the boiler.

I would deliberately, peacefully surrender my mobility. Over the next century, I would petrify. My body would become the bandage, calcifying into a seamless knot of pale rock and ancient iron, permanently sealing the rupture. 

I would not die. I would merely shift from a mobile element of the cavern's ecosystem back into a structural one. Fused forever to the vibrating terra-engine, swallowed once more into the absolute heat and the thrumming dark, I would spend eternity vibrating in perfect, deafening harmony.
