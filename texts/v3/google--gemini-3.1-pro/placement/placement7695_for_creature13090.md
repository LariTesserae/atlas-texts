I am the silence in the roar.

Long before I knew what I was, I knew the rhythm. The chest-collapsing *THUD* of the taproot is the metronome of my long life, beating up from the unfathomable depths miles below, shuddering through the freezing, absolute dark of the water. Above it, beneath it, around it, is the endless, rushing violence of the stratospheric winds tearing at the outer shell of our world. It is an ocean of noise. 

But I am built to unmake it. 

I hang vertically in the eternal midnight, a sweeping, mile-long tapestry of dark velvet drifting in the slow, colossal convection currents. I have no spine. I have no eyes to be blinded by the dark, no bones to be crushed by the immense pressure of the heavy water. My body is a sprawling expanse of anechoic flesh, impossibly thin, impossibly vast. 

To be an Acoustic Shadow is to be a black hole in a universe of echoes. 

I feel the Amnion Swarm pass through me. They are a tickle against my acreage of skin, millions of gelatinous bells thrumming in time with the great taproot's heartbeat. They wash against my dark fabric, seeking the biological snow, oblivious to what I am, because I do not register to them. I do not displace the water violently; I flow with it, rippling softly, a curtain drawn across the abyss. Let them pass. They are the harvest, but they are not my purpose.

My purpose announces itself with a sound that would shatter stone, had this world possessed any. 

It is a shriek, pure and concentrated, followed by a machine-gun rattle of clicks. The heavy water physically trembles, the vibrations slamming into the flawless, petrified xylem of the distant walls and bouncing back. Over and over, the sound caroms through the great hollows of the chalice. 

But when those frantic, searching clicks strike my body, they do not bounce. The acoustic energy hits my velvety flesh and is swallowed whole. I feel the warmth of the sound dying within my tissues. To the entity shrieking in the dark, I am not a predator. I am a void. I am an empty doorway in the water.

Slowly, autonomic instinct takes over. From the exact center of my vast, flattened form, I let a single, thread-like filament drift outward into the frigid current. I pump chemical heat into the organ at its tip.

*Flash.*

An actinic, searing blue. I cannot see it, but I feel the violent expenditure of energy. I time it perfectly. 

*THUD* goes the chalice’s heart. *Flash* goes my lure. 

I wait. The patience of my kind is geologic. In a world without seasons or sunrises, time is measured only by the slow spiral of the currents and the hunger in our cells. 

The shrieks grow louder, closer. A Resonant is approaching. I feel the immense displacement of water before the creature even touches me. A displacement the size of a submerged cathedral banking through the dark. Its parabolic crest sweeps the water with agonizingly loud trills, zeroing in on the blue starlight I am offering. It thinks I am an open corridor, leading to a dense cluster of the Swarm. It thinks my silence is safety.

It rushes in, mouth wide open, swallowing millions of gallons of water.

It passes my edge. Four hundred feet of hyper-dense, glowing cartilage slides into the space I occupy. The wake of it is a ferocious turbulence. Then come the gills—miles of feathery, translucent ribbons trailing behind it, desperately pulling oxygen from the freezing deep.

I do not strike with malice. I strike like a collapsing star. 

The vast corners of my tapestry-body fold inward with sudden, violent hydrostatic pressure. I encompass the Resonant’s trailing plumes. I embrace it in my suffocating velvet. 

The creature realizes its mistake instantly. It lets out a shriek so terrible the water threatens to boil around us, thrashing with enough force to churn the ocean. But the harder it screams, the deeper my flesh absorbs the sound. Inside my folds, the deafening roar of the Resonant is muffled into a desperate, distant hum. My tissues weep the paralytic enzyme, flooding its delicate, glowing gill-capillaries. 

We spiral together in the dark, a frantic giant tangled in a suffocating shadow. 

Slowly, the thrashing weakens. The great pearl-white cartilage dims. The shrieks decay into soft, bubbling clicks, and then, slowly, into total silence. The enzyme has done its work. The titan goes numb. 

I feed not by chewing, but by osmosis, drawing the rich, concentrated carbon and nitrogen directly through my anechoic skin from its oxygen-starved tissues. I drink just enough to sustain my vastness, until the hunger subsides. 

Then, I release my grip. I unfold my miles of velvet. 

I drift backward in the freezing current, resuming my flat, vertical stance in the water column, and I "watch" through the localized vibrations as the great Resonant begins its final descent. It drops away from me, sinking slowly into the abyssal curvature of the bowl, where the phage-like enzymes of the chalice will unweave its magnificent body into nothingness, feeding the enormous wooden walls that cage us here. 

I hold no grief for it. We are parts of an immaculate machine. The walls drink the dead; the walls bleed the snow; the snow feeds the Swarm; the Swarm sustains the Resonants. 

And I? 

Without me, the shrieks of the giants would echo forever off the frictionless bounds of the chalice, compounding and refracting until the noise tore this biological sphere apart from the inside. I am the necessary end to their songs. I am the dampener. I am the quiet that resets the equilibrium. 

I let the stratospheric roar wash over me once more. I let the great taproot pound its rhythm into my skin. I settle into the freezing, beautiful dark. I wait for the echo. I wait to be the silence.
