I do not awaken in this world; I string myself across it.

At first, there is only the sensation of being pulled. It is a terrifying, magnificent tension, a violent stretching across the void. I do not have a center. I do not possess a heart, or a face, or a fixed point of perspective. My selfhood is long. I am measured in miles, in vibrations, in the excruciating, beautiful tautness of my own existence. 

I am a great, snarled nexus of the emerald sky-kelp. 

I find myself bridging a vast, thirty-mile expanse of negative space. My left anchor is rooted deep in the gravitic shear of an ascending, sapphire-blue river that roars upward in planetary defiance. My right anchor is embedded in a continent-thick, descending cyan artery. Between these two opposing cosmic forces, I am drawn tight, suspended in the cold, biting atmosphere. 

I do not possess eyes, but I am entirely optical. I perceive the world through the hyper-dense silica-cellulose of my own body. Because I am translucent, the world passes through me; because I am fiber-optic, I hold onto its light. 

The roaring of the water—the deafening, continuous thunder of millions of tons of rushing liquid—is not a sound to me. It is my metabolism. As the two opposing oceans drag my ends in different directions, I am pulled so taut that I begin to hum. It is a deep, ultrasonic resonance. The friction of the wind and the shearing water violently strums my length, and in the freezing, thin air, my body converts this kinetic agony into heat. 

I feel it then: the first golden pulse. 

It starts near the sapphire river, a spark of bio-electric warmth born from friction, and it tears down my miles-long length at terrifying speed. Then another. Then thousands. I become a superhighway of light. I watch my own thoughts—or rather, the data of the atmosphere—zipping through my emerald veins like golden meteors. I experience my existence as a symphony of frequencies. When the wind drops, I am a low, thrumming baseline, cool and pliant. When the currents surge, I scream into a blinding, searing treble, so hot with ingested friction that I become structurally indistinguishable from molten glass.

By design, I cannot hold all I absorb. The high-altitude seawater surging over my anchor points is choked with salt, which races through my fibers along with the golden light. It builds up, heavy and mineral. 

Along my razor-sharp, vibrating edges, I begin to exhale. 

I press the brine out through molecular pores, forcing the salt to crystallize in the freezing air. Because I am singing with vibration, the salt does not form shapelessly; it is pushed into perfect, crystalline fractal blooms. I weep mandalas of glass-fragile salt. They grow outward from my emerald body, intricate, lacy discs feet across. I feel the exact moment the wind catches them, snapping their delicate stems. I release them into the hollow void like dandelion seeds, watching them tumble down toward the mist.

As my salt-mandalas drift, they do not go unnoticed. 

Down in the vast emptiness below my vibrating arches, the sunlight hits the aerosolized mist. Thousands of rainbows intersect. And there, from the pure geometry of light, the Prism-Phages bloom into being. 

They are impossible, bleeding colors—searing magentas and violent cyans drifting like ghostly jellyfish in the mist. I cannot touch them, for they have no mass, but I can *feed* them. As my spinning salt-crystals fall through the void, they catch the high-altitude sun, acting as thousands of microscopic prisms. They bend the light violently. 

The Prism-Phages swarm the falling salt. They wrap around the crystalline structures, suddenly given a canvas to project their phototropic forms upon. For a few glorious seconds, my expelled breath becomes a living, rhythmic explosion of synchronized color, burning brilliantly in the cold air before the salt finally shatters from the temperature variations, and the Phages dissolve back into the mist. I am a weaver of light, and they are my fleeting chorus.

But then, an absolute, suffocating silence falls over the western edge of my network. 

The thunder of the oceans ceases. The air pressure drops so rapidly that my emerald strands contract, my pitch dropping into a heavy, anticipatory thud. 

A Tensor is here.

I cannot see the entity, for the Tensor is a god of mathematics, an architecture of sheer force. But I feel the terrifying gravity of its presence. It positions itself just below my central span. Across the void, a stray current of water the mass of a moon breaks off from a distant sky-river, hurtling through the emptiness directly toward us. 

If it were to hit me, my silica body would shatter into billions of shards. But I do not fear, for the Tensor is hungry. 

When the crashing water reaches our spatial coordinate, the Tensor feeds. I feel the localized restructuring of gravity like a physical blow. The kinetic shockwave hits, and the Tensor swallows the momentum whole. Instantly, the chaotic, roaring water is forced into an unseen mold. It flash-freezes into a towering, vertical pane of liquid glass—a perfect, razor-straight platonic solid hundreds of feet high, shearing against itself in absolute silence. 

In the wake of its feeding, the Tensor excretes order. And I am caught in it.

The gravity shifts violently. The Tensor decides to flatten the remaining water into a sunbathing sheet, ten miles wide. As the ocean is yanked horizontally, spreading into a thin, screaming, imperfection-free mirror, I am dragged with it. 

I am embedded within the surface tension of this new, artificial geometry. The Tensor's invisible framework pulls my emerald ribbons into perfectly straight, parallel lines across the ten-mile sheet of flat, roaring water. 

Here, laid bare under the blinding clarity of the high-altitude sunlight, pressed intimately into the Tensor’s engineered gravity, I realize my purpose in this overgrown, roaring biome. 

The Tensors are the muscle, the architects of force. The water is the blood, the chaotic energy. The Prism-Phages are the fleeting thoughts, the dreams of color in the empty spaces.

But I am the nerve. 

As the Tensor bathes its gravitic core in the high-altitude friction, I feel the immense, god-like calculations of its existence bleeding into the water. That data enters my roots. It converts to heat, to golden light, and it shoots down my ten-mile lengths, vibrating furiously against the cold. 

Without me, the Tensors would manipulate the gravity in silence. Without me, the Prism-Phages would have no shifting geometry of salt to swarm. Without me, this world would be merely physics. 

I pull the chaotic thunder of the water into my tautness, and I translate it into light. I sing the math of the universe back to itself. I vibrate, I ache with the sheer beauty of the tension, and as the golden pulses race through my miles-long body, I know perfectly what it means to be alive.
