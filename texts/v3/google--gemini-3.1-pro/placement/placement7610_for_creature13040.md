To exist here is, first and foremost, to forget the word *I*. 

*I* is a heavy, clumsy concept—an illusion of a severed mind, a solitary cage. In the damp, bruised-green dark of the fissure, consciousness is not a solid thing kept carefully inside a skull. It is a fragrant, heavy fluid, spilled freely across a hundred thousand moving parts. I am not an individual; I am a sensory node, a wandering nerve ending of a vast and quiet god.

Where do I find myself? I am at the absolute threshold. I exist out on the sweeping, negative-space plains of unweathered obsidian, where the towering, biological architecture of the city gives way to the vast and sterile black glass. It is warm beneath me, perfectly smooth, radiating the faint, steady heartbeat of the earth's mantle. 

I am one of the pale outriders. A ghost of ivory chitin poured across the dark. 

I inhabit a body built of pure, sinuous geometry, articulated in a hundred perfect joints. I have no eyes, for eyes are useless traps for the stinging rot of the humid air. Instead, my vision is mapped entirely through the delicate, feathery setae running along my flanks. Through them, the empty air of the fissure is not a void; it is a blindingly detailed, three-dimensional topography of pressure and chemistry.

I am feeling the world right now. Above me, tucked into the leafy vault of the canopy I will never see, a droplet of condensation swells. I feel it growing. Its increasing mass exerts a microscopic, gravitational sigh on the air currents. When it finally detaches, plummeting toward the glass, it pushes a column of cold atmospheric pressure ahead of it. My setae tremble, reading the incoming concussive wave. The intelligence that guides me whispers, *shift*, and my dozens of legs ripple in a fluid, frictionless drift to the left. A fraction of a second later, the heavy drop detonates against the basalt exactly where I had been resting. The impact sends a shockwave of cold humidity washing over my pale armor, carrying the microscopic scent-shrapnel of decaying fern spores. I am untouched. The world is a symphony of falling water, and I am dancing between the drops.

My rhythm is tethered to the center of the world. Although I am far from the fluted spires, I know when the Matriarchs breathe. Every time their massive, soft bodies expand deep within the brood chambers, a subtle thermal draft pushes outward across the obsidian plains. Their exhalation washes over my antennae, bringing the chemical chorus of my millions of sisters. It smells of the bitter, chalky ozone of the masons’ resin, the crushed-green yeast of the foragers' leafy sails, and the deep, rich musk of the fungal gardens. This scent is my anchor. It is the invisible umbilical cord that keeps my solitary wandering from becoming true isolation. 

I am out here because the superorganism requires an immune system. I am a white blood cell patrolling the dark veins of the earth. I wait in a state of suspended kinetic potential, a coiled ribbon of pale lightning, listening to the dark.

Then, there is a stutter in the air. 

My setae flare, snapping instantly rigid. Ten yards away, out on the glassy expanse, there is a foreign vibration. It does not move with the seamless, encoded grace of the Weavers. It drags. It stumbles heavily, slipping on the microscopic slickness of the basalt. It smells of dry dust and wrongness—a thick-shelled beetle, slipped from the canopy above, blindly seeking refuge in our cleft. 

To the colony, it is a screaming beacon of discordant noise. 

I do not think to attack. The collective mind thinks *through* me. An irresistible chemical imperative floods my ganglia, and I pour myself forward. I glide over the obsidian with liquid silence, a blur of phantom limbs. The intruder raises a crude, spiked leg, but it is moving through molasses while I move through empty space. 

In a single, unbroken motion, I coil around its heavy carapace. My body maps the shape of my prey instantly by touch, finding the soft, unarmored joints beneath the heavy plating an instant before my mandibles pierce the membrane. The venom I inject is cold, precise, and immediately paralytic. There is a brief, frantic scraping of claws against glass, and then, stillness. The pure silence of the plains is restored.

I do not eat what I have killed. Hunger is not mine to satisfy; digestion is a task for the inner chambers, for the fungal composts and the swollen bellies of the nurses. My only purpose is apprehension. 

Gripping the twitching husk, I begin the long, flowing drag back toward the city’s edge. As I approach the towering arcades, the sensory environment thickens into an overwhelming, ecstatic roar. The air becomes a dense tapestry of pheromonal smoke, a blinding light of scent. I cross an invisible threshold and am met by a cluster of heavy-jawed sentinels. They emerge from the stones, their matte, camouflaged armor scraping softly, and take the burden from me without a sound.

In the exchange, our antennae touch. Just a microscopic brush of chitin. 

In that fleeting connection, I feel the entirety of our world. I feel the damp ceramic drying on the upper terraces. I feel the fresh green cuts of fern being ground into paste. I feel the warm, rolling contractions of the Matriarchs birthing the next generation. I am filled with it, re-submerged in the deep, warm ocean of our shared mind, affirmed in my place within the whole.

Then, the connection breaks. I pivot onto my many legs, turning my pale back to the roaring warmth of the metropolis. Slowly, sinuously, I glide outward again, back into the quiet, negative space of the unweathered stone, letting the cold drafts of the falling water wash over my setae, waiting in the dark to keep our fragile world safe.
