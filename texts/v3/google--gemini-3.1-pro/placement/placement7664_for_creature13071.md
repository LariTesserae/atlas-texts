I do not arrive in this world; I am condensed from it. 

First, the air is taken from me. I feel my hands rise to my face, thick and heavy, blunt fingers slick with cold, foul-smelling grease. I am pressing the harvested bacterial slime deep into my own ears, my nostrils, the corners of my mouth. The shrieking, tearing noise of the atmosphere dims, then vanishes perfectly behind the rubbery plugs. I blink, and a second set of thick, translucent eyelids slides shut over my corneas, turning the bruised grey twilight into a milky, muted blur. 

The world of the sky is gone. 

Then, the ground wakes up. 

I plant my feet on the wet, black mirror. They are massive, splayed, and webbed, the flesh of my soles softer than the rest of my scarred, chitin-draped body. The moment my arches press against the thin film of icy water and touch the polished bedrock, the universe roars into existence—not as sound, but as a vast, three-dimensional topography of pressure and vibration traveling up my shins, through my barrel-chest, and into the dome of my skull.

Through my left heel, a microscopic, frantic buzzing: the transparent arthropods darting through a puddle a dozen yards away. Through the ball of my right foot, a deep, rhythmic, grinding shear: the diamond-rasps of a behemoth herd, stripping the stone three miles to the east. 

I am one of the Readers. I am an instrument of the stone. 

I stare downward. I can barely lift my head anyway; my neck is just a thick column of muscle bridging my skull to my shoulders, locking my gaze to the shallow water at my feet. The surface is alive. In the milky gloom, the water is dancing in perfect, shivering grids. Millions of tiny squares pop and collapse in the puddle, a cymatic map of the ambient noise. 

Beside me, a vibration pulses into my toes. 

*Tap. Drag. Thud.*

I do not turn my head, but I feel the presence of another. A hunter from my coracle. He is communicating through the bedrock with a bone hammer. 

*Hunger.* *Movement.* *The eastern herd.*

I reach to my belt, my hand finding the heavy, jagged spear tipped with the recycled diamond-tooth of a leviathan. I do not reply. I simply shift my weight, a silent agreement poured into the rock. 

We begin to walk, and the walk is a terrible, fractured dance. 

We watch the water. The shimmering grids begin to distort. They stretch, warping from squares into violent, crisscrossing lines. Through the soles of my feet, the grinding shear builds into a colossal, rising strain. A titan is lifting its leg. 

We freeze. My cartilaginous ribs flare, my lungs bracing. 

*BOOM.*

The shockwave hits my chest like a falling boulder. The water at my feet erupts into chaotic, splashing starbursts. The mist above us is violently atomized in a ring of sudden vacuum. In this exact, deafening fraction of a second, hidden perfectly within the acoustic shadow of the creature's footfall, I step forward. My companion steps with me. 

We wait in the shivering silence. The water settles back into grids. We freeze. 

The strain builds in the rock. 

*BOOM.*

*Step.*

We endure this stop-motion nightmare for hours. With every blast, the concussive force grows. My loose armor of overlapping chitin clatters against my pale, slime-slicked skin, the sinew absorbing the kinetic violence that would otherwise liquefy my organs. 

Eventually, the pale grey light above us vanishes entirely, swallowed by an eclipse of sheer, biological mass. 

We are beneath it. 

I cannot look up, but I do not need to. The presence of the behemoth is a crushing weight against my senses, a gravity of its own. Through the stone, its radula is scraping just yards away—a vibration so violently intense my teeth chatter behind my sealed lips, my tympanic soles burning with the friction of it. The water at my feet is no longer patterned; it is boiling with pure, chaotic force, atomized into perpetual mist by the volume of the feeding beast.

*Tap. Tap.* 

A frantic, close-range signal from my companion. *Here. The joint.* 

I move blindly by the Braille of the water and the pulse in my arches. I sense the massive, shifting pillar of the behemoth's foreleg. As the creature shifts its weight to scrape the slime, the overlapping plates of dark chitin above us part for a single, fleeting moment. 

I do not hear my own grunt of exertion. I grip my spear with both hands and drive it upward with all the terrifying, coiled strength of my abbreviated body. The diamond-tooth bites deep into the pale, cold, exposed flesh of the giant's inner joint. 

The stone screams. The behemoth doesn't notice the wound—we are fleas biting a continent—but the muscle spasms reflexively. I twist the heavy shaft, using the leverage of my own immense width, and a block of dense, oily flesh, the size of a boulder, tears free. It hits the wet black stone with a heavy thud, splashing icy water up the backs of my legs. 

The grids in the water warp. The leg is rising. 

*BOOM.*

*Step back.* I drag the massive hunk of meat by its connective tissue. My companion drags a jagged plate of freshly broken carapace, harvested from the joint's edge. 

*BOOM.*

*Step back.* 

We fall away into the grey mist, perfectly timed, perfectly erased. 

Hours later, we are back in the camp. We sit beneath a trembling coracle made of dead shells, our bodies smeared fresh with insulating slime. We chew raw, dense, life-giving meat, swallowing the copper-tasting fat that will burn within us against the chill. 

The world of air outside our shelter is a shrieking, terminal apocalypse that never, ever rests. But inside me, there is only a vast, dark quiet. 

I wipe my thick hands on my chitin apron. I pick up a fused-vertebrae hammer. I look at the white bone-dust coating the black rock. I press my calloused, soft-soled feet firmly against the stone, close my milky eyes entirely, and let the hammer drop. 

*Tap. Drag. Thud.* 

*We went into the dark. We stole from the mountain. We are still here.* 

Through the damp, vibrating floor of the world, my camp sings back to me.
