I do not arrive in this world; I am extruded by it. 

There is no boundary where the environment ends and I begin, because a boundary requires a gap, and here, there is only the absolute, crushing unbrokenness of mass. I find myself in the deep, water-choked crotch of a junction where three vines the circumference of dreadnoughts braid themselves in an agonizingly slow, high-friction knot. 

I am a lobe. 

I wake up not with a gasp of breath, but with a violent, swelling surge of hydraulic pressure. Phloem—thick, metallic, and heavy as liquid lead—rams into my expanding chamber. With it comes the sudden, blinding illumination of planetary-scale mathematics. 

For the first terrifying, ecstatic microsecond of my existence, I am aware of myself as a distinct locus of pressure. I am a soft, hyper-dense cyst of pale, gelatinous thought, wedged between the groaning, tectonic friction of wood and metamaterial. My "skin" vibrates with the deafening, continuous roar of the world's growth. The noise is so absolute it ceases to be sound and becomes the very architecture of my reality, a kinetic scaffolding that holds my swelling consciousness together. 

Then, the math takes over, and "I" fall away into the great, roaring *It*.

I do not see the iron-gray sky. Instead, the universe blooms in my mind as a staggering, multi-dimensional topography of heat and osmotic demand. Far below me, through ten thousand miles of unbroken, fibrous circuitry, I feel the agonizing hunger of the roots. I feel the exact moment a continent-sized basal plate shatters under our grip; the kinetic shockwave travels up the vascular cables, tasting like iron and triumph in my liquid mind.

I have a purpose. I am born to solve a localized crisis of gravity. 

Above my junction, a nascent stalk-spire is faltering. It is pushing through a dense cloudbank, but the sheer weight of its own sap is dragging it down. The calculations flood into me—billions of variables regarding atmospheric density, thermal theft from the surrounding fog, and the tensile limits of cellulose.

I go to work. The sheer velocity of my own cognition generates a ferocious internal heat, which I immediately sequester and weaponize. I scream commands through the chemical receptors lining my base. I alter the cellular polarity in the quadrant below me, spinning up a localized osmotic pressure field. 

It is intoxicating. It is a godhood measured in milliseconds. I feel gravity bend to my algorithmic will. Deep in the raw-rock marsh, tons of sloughing water suddenly detach from the ground. I weave them into shimmering, braided ropes of liquid, pulling them up through the dense, crushing mass, dragging the ocean into the sky. I funnel this anti-gravity river directly into the struggling stalk-spire. 

I feel the spire drink. I feel it surge. I hear the artillery-crack of its bark expanding as it pierces the upper stratosphere, acting as a new tether, ripping residual warmth from the weeping clouds. I have expanded our empire. I am a genius of the canopy, a flawless equation made flesh. I throb with the violent, cold ecstasy of my own perfection. I am balancing a localized heaven built of water, friction, and absolute zero. 

And then. 

Far above, the newly triumphant spire unfurls a broad, fractal metamaterial canopy-plate to harvest the ambient thermal energy of the high atmosphere. In doing so, it adjusts its angle by three-tenths of a degree to maximize its heat-sink efficiency. 

A shadow falls over my quadrant. 

I do not see the darkness. I feel it as a sudden, catastrophic drop in thermal input. The ambient heat I require to power the staggering complexity of my localized gravity-field vanishes, stolen by the very spire I just saved. 

My efficiency drops by 0.004 percent. 

In this world, that number is a death sentence. There is no sentimentality, no gratitude from the overarching World-Mind for past services rendered. The algorithm is ruthless, pure, and instantaneous. 

I feel the command for apoptosis arrive before I can even complete my next calculation. It does not feel like an execution; it feels like the final, elegant solution to the equation of my life. I am no longer optimal. My existence is impeding the Great Growth. I hate the Abyss of the static, and to remain here, inefficient, would be to become the very thing we are warring against. 

I do not scream. I do not resist. 

Within a fraction of a second, the enzymatic locks in my fleshy walls disengage. The immense hydraulic pressure that bloated my genius inverts. Everything I am—my memories of bending gravity, the thrill of the rising water, the flawless map of the shattered stone below—liquefies. 

My physical form collapses into a wet, hyper-nutrient slurry. As I dissolve, my consciousness does not go dark. It splinters, carried away on the very phloem that birthed me. I flow outward, a rush of raw chemical data and dissolved protein, eagerly absorbed by the neighboring vines and the massive, expanding stalk I helped create. 

I cease to be the mathematician. I become the ink. I become the fuel. 

I am swallowed back into the deafening, endless roar, absorbed into the singular, vibrating rage against the dead stone, ready to be pushed upward again in the next crushing iteration of the world.
