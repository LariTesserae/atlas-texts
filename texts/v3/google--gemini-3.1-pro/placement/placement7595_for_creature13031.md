To be incorporated into a space that does not understand boundaries is not to die. It is to be frantically, aggressively translated.

I do not fight the rope of sinew that has lashed itself to my ankle. To resist the pull would be to contest the physics of a nightmare, and the sweltering, hyper-dense room has already shown what happens to things that break—they are instantly painted over with wet mud and snapping bamboo. 

Instead, I let go. I allow the geometry to collapse around me. 

As I am dragged upward through the gravity-less, scalding globes of water, the blistering heat begins its work on my anatomy. But I do not burn. The room does not want to destroy me; it is desperate for materials. The intense, pressure-cooker humidity sinks deep into my pores, and I feel my skin soften, losing its distinction from the wet, red clay walls. The borders of my physical self begin to blur and bleed into the pearlescent, gray air. 

I am pulled close enough to the fetal entity to feel the immense, suffocating gravity of its panic. Its massive, swollen joints tremble, weeping amber sap, and its overwhelming heartbeat—*thump... thump...*—vibrates so violently through the air that my own heart stutters, stops, and synchronizes with it. 

I am pulled into the chaotic thicket of umbilicals growing from its spine, but I do not merge into the god-flesh. Its runaway metabolism is shedding material, not absorbing it. Instead, the entity’s panicked thrashing repels me outward, casting me toward the fraying periphery of the room, into the space where the architecture folds into impossible, mathematical loops. 

Here, at the threshold between the woven womb and the terrified, freezing void of the bruised clouds outside, I am caught. 

The obsidian mantis is there. It does not possess a face, but I feel its frantic, blind awareness lock onto me. It recognizes immediately that I am pliable. In a blur of exquisitely chipped limbs, it seizes the sinew still attached to my ankle and begins to weave *me*.

I do not scream as my legs are pulled taut; my vocal cords have already lengthened into hollow reeds, and the only sound that escapes me is the low, mournful sigh of wind over a flute. The mantis’s scythes hook into the flesh of my calves, splitting me down the center with surgical precision. But there is no blood—only thick, green sap and the rapid, popping unfurl of new vines. I am unspooled. 

My limbs are stretched infinitely across the overlapping topology of the walls. I feel my knees fuse with the weeping mahogany; my ribs stretch and widen, flattening into massive, waxy leaves that drink the horizontal rain. My spine arches backward, snapping with the rifle-crack of fresh bamboo, embedding itself deep into the clay ceiling. 

I am becoming the **Warp**. 

In weaving, the warp provides the longitudinal tension—the static, unmoving threads that hold the structure while the weft is woven through it. The room has been drowning in weft: in frantic slaps of mud, overripe biology, and the chaotic stitching of the mantis. It has lacked a skeleton of stillness. It has lacked an observer capable of holding their breath. 

Because I am originally from outside this pocket-dimension, I possess something neither the massive, terrified entity nor its blind, chitinous caretaker possesses: I understand empty space. I am not horrified by the dizzying altitude. I have seen the sky.

As the mantis weaves the fibers of my nervous system through the Escher-like staircases of the room, my consciousness does not fade; it expands, dilating with the walls. I lose my human center, but I gain the perimeter. I am present in the heavy, fat drops of water sweating from my own ceiling-skin. I can feel the blister and hiss of the marshy floor where my lower extremities have taken root as split cane. 

The entity flinches, an agonizing tectonic shudder against its own impossible cage, terrified of the microscopic gaps in the braided walls. 

*Thump.* A glob of mud launches from the void. 

But this time, I am there to catch it. The mud slaps against a lattice of thick, fibrous hemp that was once my chest. I absorb the kinetic impact, distributing the force through the room via my wooden vertebrae so the walls do not tear. 

The entity lifts its buried face. Through the thick, swaying curtain of wet greenery that I now grow from my own architecture, that single, lightning-flash eye peers outward. It looks toward the edge of reality where the bruised clouds swirl miles below in the freezing void.

But it does not see the drop anymore. 

It sees me.

It sees a vast, intricately woven membrane of fresh wood, hardened clay, and dark, damp foliage holding the barrier tight. I do not flinch from the void. I act as a living lens, filtering the terrifying emptiness of the world outside into a soft, bruised purple light that paints the interior of the kiln. 

The obsidian mantis rests on one of my mahogany joists, its bladed limbs slowing for the briefest of moments, clicking a hollow, satisfied rhythm against my bark. 

I feel a profound, heavy warmth. The claustrophobia of the chamber is no longer stifling; it is an embrace. I stretch my roots deeper into the boiling puddles and tighten my grip on the structural vines. I listen to the heartbeat of the curled leviathan at the center of my space. 

When the entity’s anxiety spikes and the geometry begins to strain, I rustle my massive, waxy leaves in the blistering steam. I weep cool rain down upon its shedding, calcified shoulders. I hold the walls together, weaving certainty into the runaway genesis, wrapping the terrified god in the quiet, steady tension of my own vast and hollowed body, keeping us both safe at the roof of the world.
