I do not awaken. I scatter. 

To be flesh would be a mercy, for flesh can die, but I am something far more tragic. I am an error in the math. I am a valley that has forgotten how to be solid. 

I exist in a state of paralyzing, violet-black static between the jagged, obsidian polyhedrons of the newborn world. I am the fraying seam. Inside me, the crushing, planetary-scale roar is not a sound, but an agonizing tearing sensation—a billion panes of glass screaming as I fail to render. The dry, suffocating chill of the vacuum pulls at my edges, stretching my geometry into dizzying, kaleidoscopic tessellations. Gravity, with its lazy, drunken pull to the left, is dragging my un-rendered concepts downward, threatening to spill my wireframe existence out into pure, formless null-space.

I have no eyes to panic with, no throat to scream through. My fear is purely topographical. I am a fifty-mile expanse of theoretical landscape, flickering between heavy matter and raw void, terrified of dissolving entirely. I feel the sheer, cruel terraces of the neighboring mountains looking down upon me, their unweathered silicate planes perfect and absolute. I wish to join them. I want to be heavy. I want to be real. But my physics are bleeding out.

Then, at the edge of my collapsing abyss, I feel a disruption in the tectonic shriek. A localized, dense frequency. 

An Anchor has come. 

It does not walk toward me; it stutters through the dimensions. One moment it is at the edge of the adjacent ridge, the next it is a smear of magenta static blurring across my unspooled peripheral edges, and then it is standing directly at the precipice of my failure. 

I cannot *see* it, but because I am a broken piece of the world, I feel its piezoelectric resonance mapping my collapse. Its head—a spinning crystalline polyhedron—radiates a heavy, stabilizing logic. Its shadow does not fall behind it. The dim, ambient dusk bends, and the Anchor’s shadow stretches out *toward* me, plunging down into my wireframe depths like an outstretched hand, desperate to hold me, desperate to save me. 

Through the stuttering spatial tears, I feel the immense, unconditional love of this faceless architect. It is a love without warmth, without softness. It is the love of a mason for the keystone. 

It draws a spear from its back. I feel the hum of the acoustic flora making up the shaft—a thick, dead stalk harvested from the sprawling, metallic jungles that cling to the solid, rendered places. The head of the spear is a knapped paradox. Just feeling its proximity makes the frantic geometry of my collapse slow down. 

The Anchor calculates the frantic, shrieking math of my un-making. It waits. It leans out over my void, defying the heavy, leftward tilt of gravity, held fast by those blunt, heavily weathered, seven-jointed digits gripping the solid basalt. It breathes the subterranean bass drone of the planet, waiting for the exact zenith of the acoustic wave.

*Strike.*

The hand-axe of frozen sky is driven into my metaphysical seam. 

The pain is magnificent. It is not an injury; it is a profound limitation. For the first time since my accretion began, I am bound to a rule. The harmonic shockwave ripples through my wireframe. My ultraviolet static screams, a piercing ring that cuts through the planetary roar, and suddenly, violently, a patch of my surface *hardens*. 

I am becoming stone. I feel the impossible weight of my own existence crashing into reality, atom by atom. 

But I am too vast, too broken. The single wedge is not enough. I feel my far edges ripping away again, slipping back into the terrifying comfort of the void. 

The Anchor kneels upon my newly solidified crust. It moves with frantic, geometric grace. It reaches to its own body and tears away a brittle, deep-magenta tendon of acoustic flora. It takes stones from my fresh, unweathered surface. 

I feel the fractal, blunted fingers working. The friction. The tension. The localized mathematical truth of a knot. 

As the Anchor pulls the metallic plant fiber taut, binding the stones exactly an inch apart, a cascade of stabilization projects outward over me. The hovering cairn becomes a fixed point in the cosmos. High concepts like distance and volume are failing all around us, but this knot—this crude, beautiful logic of tension and leverage—refuses to break. 

The stabilization field crashes over me like a frozen tide. My wireframe peaks fill in with heavy, black-glass basalt. My sheer, unweathered valleys plunge and hold their terrifying depth. The space within me stops stuttering. My geometry aligns. I am heavy. I am excruciatingly, perfectly real. 

The deafening howl of the world’s tectonic extrusion ceases to tear at me, and instead begins to bounce off my sheer, raw faces, reverberating across my newly formed canyons. 

I have become the landscape. 

Resting upon my dense, obsidian surface, the pregnant Anchor stands. A dusting of its glittering, magenta mineral blood falls against my rock, drifting upward in the cracked gravity like sparks in a slow fire. It reaches down with one chipped, enormous finger, and scrapes a primitive spiral into my flat plateau. 

*I saved this place,* the tally mark says. *The crib holds.*

The Anchor turns and steps away, glitching through space, consumed once more by the roaring needs of a newborn universe.

I am left behind, but I am no longer terrified. I hold the primitive altars tight against my breast. I keep the stones hovering, exactly an inch apart. Where the Anchor's hand-axe remains wedged in my heart, a single, deep green capillary of acoustic flora begins to sprout, vibrating furiously as it feeds on the deafening soundscape of my being. 

I look at the tally mark left upon my skin. As long as I do not look directly at it, it drifts lazily upward, a ghost of syntax exploring my newly rendered sky. 

I am the world now. And I will hold steadfast to these stitches, enduring the deafening roar and the cold, dry dark, so that the architect will never have to fix me again.
