The awakening is not a gentle surfacing, but a violent shatter. 

For twenty-one hours, my heartbeat has been a slow, agonizing drum—once, twice, perhaps three times an hour—echoing against the walls of my own massive ribcage. The antifreeze in my veins feels like sludge, my spine a column of fused glass. 

Then, the violet sky bleeds to white. The sun crests the jagged lip of the world. 

It hits the quartz, and the frost binding my knees to my chest vaporizes with a sharp *hiss*. The heat is sudden, brutal, and demands life. My lungs flare, expanding with a desperate, painful heave, sucking at the brittle air through my flared nasal slits. My black blood thins, racing. My limbs, folded like the impossible geometry of an origami insect, snap open with a series of sharp, joint-popping cracks.

But as I unfurl from my fissure, pulling myself out onto the sheer vertical face, I am slower than the others. 

A dozen white blurs streak past me, plunging downward into the blinding void to catch the updrafts, their silken ropes trailing behind them. I do not follow. 

I look at my hands. The heavy, dark keratin nodules at the ends of my spider-like fingers—the anchors that have held my life to this razor-sharp world for countless cycles—are chipped. Blunted. Worn smooth by thousands of impacts with the unforgiving stone. I know, with cold biological certainty, that if I dive into the central hollow today, my fingers will no longer bite deep enough into the micro-crevasses to arrest my fall. 

I am no longer an apex predator of the sheer drop. 

So, I do not dive. I let the frantic rhythm of the young hunters carry them away. I climb upward, moving with a careful, deliberate grace that belongs more to the stone than the flesh, finding my way to a protruding eastern ledge. It is a good place. It is deeply scarred by the elements, but the sunlight hits it early and leaves it late. 

I reach into the satchel made of pale bird-stomach strapped to my waist and pull out loose, unmortared shards of rock. I begin to drag them into a tight semi-circle. I am building a windbreak. 

As the flash-climate blooms around me—the walls sweating, the heavy obsidian moss swelling, the glassy krill-moths erupting into rainbow clouds—I settle into my shallow depression. I hammer three bone pitons into the quartz above me, my ruined keratin knuckles serving as the mallet. I tie a tattered canopy of dried grass to the bone. It casts a razor-thin sliver of bruised shadow over my head, just enough to keep the unmitigated radiation of the sun from baking the moisture out of my dark skin. 

Having secured my ledge, I do what I must do in this new phase of my existence. I lean forward, tilting my head, and press my heavy, elongated jawbone flat against the searing white cliff. 

Soundlessness vanishes. The mountain screams into my skull.

Through lithic conduction, I expand outward, my consciousness riding the crystalline veins of quartz. I stop being a single, decaying body on a ledge; I become the amphitheater. 

Through my teeth, I feel a rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* sweeping through the stone, forty spans below. It is a signal. A hunter letting another know the angle of their swing. 

I feel the faint, microscopic shiver of the heavy black flowers dragging their fleshy petals against the rock as they track the sun. 

Then, the great vibration. A sudden, massive *thud-crack* reverberating up from the depths, vibrating my molars. A hunter has dropped from an overhang, colliding with a four-winged glider in mid-air. Two heartbeats later, a violent, metallic *TANG* shoots through the quartz—a vine rope snapping taut against a bone piton, arresting their fall. Through the stone, I can taste the trajectory. I track the hunt not with my jet-black eyes, which are hidden behind the needle-slits of my bone mask, but by the pressure waves dancing through my jaw. 

I am the listener. The archivist of the three-hour hour frenzy. 

In this silent world, we possess no history, no spoken lore, no songs. But the stone remembers. As I press my cheek to the rock, I realize it is not just echoing today’s hunt. Beneath the sharp, frantic vibrations of the present, there is a deep, eternal hum. The accumulated friction of ten thousand cycles. The resonance of long-dead kin hammering deep pitons that still hold fast. The mountain is a library of impacts. 

I spend my three hours of daylight weaving a new vine rope. My blunted fingers are no longer suited for the violence of the hunt, but they are still strong enough to knot the tough alpine fibers together, snapping them tight with my teeth. The young will need these ropes tomorrow. 

And then, I feel the temperature begin to turn. 

I look up through the slit of my mask. The sun has hit the far rim. The shadow of the peaks is a dark wave rushing up the walls, erasing the blinding glare into deep, freezing purple. 

The symphony in the stone changes. The sharp thuds of the hunt become rapid, desperate scrambling. *Tap-tap-slide.* The hunters are retreating, dragging their bloodied kills into the deepest, narrowest squeezes, desperately trying to fold themselves away before the atmosphere snaps. 

I should retreat to a deep fissure, too. But the ledge I have built is shallow. 

I watch the shadow race toward me. I look at the rough vine rope I have just finished, a coiled offering for whoever climbs up to this ledge tomorrow. I take my crescent blade—knapped from milky quartz, its hilt wrapped in cured bird hide—and lay it gently on the frozen stone inside my little windbreak. I will not need it to sever spines anymore.

The shadow hits me. 

The heat vanishes instantly, replaced by an absolute, terrifying vacuum of cold. The sweat on the walls turns to glittering frost before my eyes. 

I draw my long legs up against my chest. I bow my head, tucking my flat face beneath my knees. I wrap my thick, white down-feathered hide tightly around my shoulders, drawing the edges inward to form a perfect, sealed sphere against the loose rocks of my windbreak. 

My violent, roaring heartbeat stammers. It slows. 

The blood in my veins begins to thicken, the black antifreeze taking hold, but the ambient cold outside my shallow shelter is too immense. The cold penetrates the down. It creeps into the marrow of my elongated bones. It bites at my lungs.

I do not panic. I press the side of my jaw firmly against the floor of the ledge one last time. 

My heartbeat drops. Once a minute. 
Once every two minutes. 
Once. 

As the great, deafening silence of the thin air reasserts itself, and the universe freezes solid around me, I do not slip into torpor. I slip into the stone. I become the resonance. I become the permanent, unmoving architecture of the cathedral, waiting to feel the tremor of a heavy knuckle against quartz, leaving my chipped blade behind for the next set of hands.
