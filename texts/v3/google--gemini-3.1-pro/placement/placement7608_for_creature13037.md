At first, the transition is merely an unspooling. I wait for my eyes to adjust, searching for the dimmest gray of a horizon, but there is nothing to adjust to. The blackness is not an absence of light; it is a physical substance, heavy and plush. After an eternity of straining, I finally let the habit of seeing go. I let the visual cortex quiet down, and as it powers off, the rest of the world rushes in with terrifying volume.

I try to find my footing, to feel the worn, frictionless stone beneath me, but there is no ground. I try to spread wings to catch the thick, humid updrafts, but I have no wings. I reach for the meaty, hydraulic vines, but I have no hands. 

My geometry is wrong. Human edges—legs, arms, spine—have dissolved. I am no longer rigid. I am yielding, permeable, and adrift in a space that defies the pull of the deep earth.

I wait, letting my new form coalesce in the roaring dark. 

I feel the temperature first. I am warm—blood-warm—but it is a heat that hums. It radiates from somewhere just below me, an invisible pillar of exotic energy that thrums with a slow, geologic patience. Above me, the air is cooler, dense with the scent of crushed ferns and wet loam. 

Then comes the sound. It is a physical pummeling. The echolocating trills and clicks of the hyper-kinetic swarmers are not just noises; they are sudden, sharp constellations of static electricity that prick against my outer edges. When they swoop past, their leathery wings cast wakes of displaced air that ripple across my body. I feel their frantic, fleeting lifespans like a nervous tremor shaking the atmosphere. And then, from the periphery, the amphibian bellows. It is a tectonic shift, a concussive shockwave that hits me, compressing me, pushing me inward before the pressure equalizes and I expand again. 

I am expanding. I am contracting. 

I realize, with a slow, blooming awareness, what I am contained within. 

I am suspended in mid-air. I am bound by the surface tension of a hovering, zero-gravity sphere of water, pooled in the invisible cradle of an ancient machine’s warped physics. As heavy drops plummet from the vaulted ceiling, they do not crash past me; they enter the gravity anomaly, slowing into lazy, curving spirals until they kiss the outer membrane of my spherical world, merging into it. 

And within this floating globe of trapped water, I am taking shape.

I am not the water itself, I discover, but something born of it. I have no skeleton. I am a loose, floating neural net, a constellation of translucent filaments, cilia, and gossamer membranes. If there were light to see me by, I might look like a microscopic jellyfish or a fragile, drifting lace of nervous tissue. But here, I am only defined by how I feel the water moving around me. 

I live in the threshold between the agonizingly slow and the violently fast. To the churning, massive world outside my sphere, I am nothing. But inside my hovering droplet, I am the center.

I feed on the cavern’s slow rain. As the fleshy vines on the distant walls exhale their biological snow, the heavy pollen drifts into the gravity well. It breaches the meniscus of my world, dissolving into the warm water. I feel the microscopic grains brushing against my extending filaments. I wrap soft, invisible tendrils around them, absorbing the manic energy of the jungle, letting the rich loam-taste of it sink into my center. 

For a long time—though whether it is hours or weeks, I cannot tell—my existence is a peaceful, floating meditation. I ride the slow, churning currents within the sphere, heated from below by the humming obelisk, fed from above by the weeping rock. I listen to the slow, metallic scraping of a Lithic-Grazier somewhere far down in the dark, methodically chewing the vines. Its chewing acts as a metronome for my heartbeat. 

But my water-world is not entirely safe. It is an oasis in the void, and the void is thirsty.

The warning comes as a sudden shift in the air pressure at the edge of my sphere. The chaotic, scattering clicks of a swarmer suddenly focus, tightening into a sharp, sonar beam locked directly onto my hovering home. The creature is diving.

I instinctively contract, pulling my delicate filaments into a tight, dense knot at the very core of the water sphere. 

The swarmer strikes the surface. The impact is a violent, echoing splash that sends shockwaves through my gelatinous body. I feel the frantic, vibrating heat of its fleshy radar-dish head, the frantic beating of its heart, just inches from me. It drops its jaw into the water, drinking greedily, taking great gulps of my universe. The boundaries of my sphere shrink rapidly. The water level is dropping.

But before the swarmer can finish, the air beneath us vanishes. 

The barometric plunge is nauseating. The giant amphibian hidden in the vines below has opened its cavernous mouth and expanded its massive lungs.

A terrifying vacuum engages. The gravity anomaly tries to hold onto the water, but the biological sheer force is too much. The bottom half of my floating world is ripped away. The water is sucked down in a violent torrent, carrying the screeching, thrashing swarmer with it into the abyss of the predator's gullet. 

The suction pulls at me, tearing at my outer filaments. I am stretched into a long, agonizing thread of tissue, caught between the amphibian’s vacuum and the machine's gravity. I cling to the upper hemisphere of the remaining water, anchoring myself in the center of the anomaly’s pull. 

A moment later, the concussive, booming belch of the amphibian rattles the stone, vibrating so hard my water-world froths with bubbles. 

I survive. I loosen my knot, slowly unfurling my surviving filaments into the remaining water. I am smaller now, missing pieces of myself, but I am not broken.

Above me, the cavern ceiling weeps. 

*Drip. Drip.*

Heavy droplets fall from the infinite dark, hitting the gravity well, spiraling inward, and bleeding gently into my damaged sphere. With every drop, my world expands again. The water brings new pollen, new minerals worn from the primordial rock. I extend my cilia, tasting the fresh rain, allowing the steady rhythm of the cavern to rebuild my home. 

In this deafening, sweltering void, underneath the frozen summit of a mountain at the roof of the world, I realize I am exactly where I need to be. I am not the ancient engineers, nor the apex predators, nor the frantic prey. I am the silent witness suspended in the middle of the miracle. I am a drifting thought in the lungs of the sleeping god, riding the warm currents in the dark, waiting for the next drop to fall.
