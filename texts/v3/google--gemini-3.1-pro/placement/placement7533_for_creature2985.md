I do not feel the void beneath me as empty space; I feel it as a holding breath.

I hang inverted, suspended by the broad, spatulate pads of my feet, anchored perfectly to the slick, pale bark of a primary structural root. Hundreds of feet below me, the bowl of the ancient limestone basin curves away, a pale, water-burnished ocean of stone holding the cellar-thick chill. The mist from the great cooling-leaves drifts down around me. It settles on my opalescent grey shoulders, beads into heavy spheres of quicksilver, and rolls off into the abyss. I am entirely dry, wrapped in my own hydrophobic silence.

If you had asked me before I stepped into this world, I might have said I would be an explorer or a rebel. But here, in the mint-green and bruised-purple shadows of the Settlement-Engine, the concept of rebellion is a mathematical absurdity. A gear does not rebel against the wheel. A leaf does not rebel against the light. 

I find myself possessing the nature of a diagnostician, a listener to the deep, slow blood of the world. 

I reach up—or rather, down toward the abyss—and pull my elongated torso upright, my double-jointed spine curving effortlessly. I swing my long limbs up to sit atop the bole. I lean my jaw against the living wood. 

Immediately, the absolute quiet of the open air is replaced by the roar of the wood. My tympanic membranes, flush against my skull, vibrate with the symphonic, tactile choir of the factory. I feel the deep, subterranean *thud-swell-thud* of the bladder-gourds pumping far above on the central processing deck. I feel the microscopic, static-like scuttering of my kin scraping moss off the fungal shelf-gears three nodes to the east. I feel an incoming pulse from a foreman: *Stand fast. High pressure wave approaching.*

Through my moonstone eyes, the world is devoid of fine detail, but ablaze with fluid intent. The surface of the giant root beneath me is a dim, bruised shadow, but just beneath the cambium, I can "see" the heavy, milky sap rushing like a river of pale, cold fire. 

A blockage is forming. I can see it. A quarter-mile away, along a tertiary vascular pipe that loops a cluster of living-quarters, the bright, pale-fire current is beginning to eddy. The flow is thickening. A clot of heavy lipids.

I detach my jaw from the wood and begin to move. 

I do not walk; my anatomy is not made for flat ground. I throw my body across the chasms of empty, cool air. My massive hands slap out, the toe-pads suctioning flat against a diagonal support vine. I swing my long legs, catch a higher branch, pivot on a double-jointed shoulder, and vault through the vast, sparse gulf. The architecture of the Settlement-Engine rushes past me: the great, wheeling fungal shelf-growths, the hushed, breathing exhaust-flowers. It is terrifyingly high, but I feel no vertigo. To me, gravity is not a threat; it is merely another tension, like the pull of a tight pneumatic vine. 

I alight softly beside the translucent plant-glass of the blocked pipe. Through my luminous eyes, the clot is a glaring, angry knot in the flow. My hands move to the living utility harness grafted to my chest. I unhook a curved shard of silica-rich bark—a blade sharper than obsidian. 

With the absolute precision of a surgeon, I slice a hairline fissure into a secondary pressure-node just above the clot. Instantly, a fine spray of thick, milky sap hisses into the air. The pressure releases. The glowing knot inside the pipe stutters, breaks apart, and flushes smoothly downstream toward the clarification vats. 

I wipe the back of my rubbery hand across the cut, smearing a specialized resin from a pouch at my waist to seal the wound. The plant-glass heals instantly. I press my palm flat against the pipe and send a rapid, thrumming vibration back into the network: *Flow resolved. Rhythm restored.*

My work done, I sit back against the curve of the pipe. The bruised purple light of afternoon is shifting to deep, iron-gray. It is nearly dusk.

In my chest, a deep ache blossoms. It is not an ache of injury, but of profound density.

For decades, I have knelt at the chalice-indentations. I have drank the heavy, milky sap. It has rebuilt my tissues, lubricated my joints, fueled the kinetic leaps across the abyss. But over the years, the minerals and calcifying agents within the sap have gathered in my bones. I am becoming heavy. When I swung through the gulf moments ago, there was a fractional delay, a lethargy in my joints. The silica-blade feels heavier in my hand than it did yesterday. 

The image of who I am in this world shifts. I am no longer just the listener. I am the past.

I look up to the highest, furthest reaches of the Settlement-Engine. Out there, extending far past the massive, established infrastructure, are the pale green shoots—the new growth, reaching blindly across the open expanse of the limestone basin, trembling in the cool, damp drafts. 

I hook my silica-blade back into my chest harness. I will not need it again.

Slowly, methodically, I begin to climb. The journey takes hours. I move past the central processing floors, where the rhythmic *whoosh* and *wet thump* of the great biotic engines rattle my teeth. I move past the residential nodes, where my kin are already beginning to curl tightly together for the night. I do not stop at the chalice-grooves to drink. 

Eventually, the massive, tunnel-thick boles taper. The bark becomes soft, green, and pliant. The air here is colder, totally exposed to the empty void of the basin. I am walking on a branch no thicker than my own torso. Below me is nothing but the mile-deep drop to the perfectly smooth stone.

Other kin are waiting for me. Three younger Tenders, their opalescent skin glowing faintly in the moonlight. They do not look at me with sadness. In a closed loop, there is no loss; there is only transition. 

I lie down upon the green, reaching edge of the branch. I stretch my long arms out, laying them flat against the wood. I extend my legs. I align my spine with the grain of the living shoot. 

The young Tenders step forward. From their harnesses, they pull fresh, wet, rapidly-growing vines. They begin to wrap me. 

They lash my ankles to the wood. They cross the vines over my chest, binding my ribs to the branch. The living bindings are tight, cold, and damp. As they wrap my jaw, I press the flat of my cheek into the soft green cambium beneath me for the last time. 

I close my moonstone eyes. I feel the final vibration from the hands of the Tenders: *Hold steady. Bear the weight.*

They retreat back into the shadows of the canopy, leaving me alone at the edge of the world. 

I do not sleep, but I do not wake. Over the coming weeks, the cambium will split and swallow me. The sap I drank for a lifetime will petrify my bones into struts of unimaginable strength. I will lose the boundaries of my opalescent skin, bleeding seamlessly into the pale bark. 

I will no longer swing through the void. I will *bridge* it. 

I will become the infrastructure. Half a century from now, when this slender green branch has swollen into a massive, spanning bole, holding ten thousand tons of sweeping botanical machinery, it will be my spine that keeps it from snapping. It will be my double-jointed ribs that cradle the living quarters of the kin not yet born. 

 Suspended above the ancient, water-hollowed stone, I let the cool mist fall upon me, and I prepare to hold up the world.
