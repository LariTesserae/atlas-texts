I would not fall into this world as a stranger, nor would I arrive in it as an accident. To exist here with the mind of a surface-dweller would be to shatter into madness within the hour. Instead, I would be born of the damp, dripping dark. I would be a native cell in this staggering, primitive engine of flesh. 

I would find myself perched high on one of the jagged basalt steeples, suspended midway between the sluggishly weeping ceiling and the sprawling, deafening terraces below. My vantage point is a narrow ledge of raw volcanic glass, jutting out like a broken fang over a chasm of moving bodies. 

I am a Weaver of the Light—an archivist in a world entirely devoid of written words, seasons, or time. Where there is no dawn to mark the passing of days, the only clock we possess is the accumulation of memory carved directly into our own skin.

My body is perfectly calibrated to the stifling, eternal dusk. The heavy, balmy air does not oppress me; it embraces me, condensing on my hairless, plum-colored skin in warm, slick rivulets. As I crouch on the sheer drop, I do not balance by desperate muscle control. My joints are coiled tight, and the thick, yellowish horns of my foot-pads and knees lock into the microscopic fractures of the razor-sharp stone. I am rooted to the glass. Like my kin, my massive, liquid-black eyes do not blink. They remain wide, drinking in the bruised twilight above and the sprawling, phosphorescent smear of the civilization below, projecting faint, doubled reflections of the city onto my lidless sclera.

I feel the roar before I hear it. Because I lack outer ears, the deafening cacophony of cracking flint, shrieking beasts, and thousands of bodies scraping over stone does not assault my skull. Instead, it enters through my palms and the armored ridges of my spine. It travels up the glass steeple and into my marrow as a dense, overlapping cascade of vibrations. It is a tactile map of my world. I feel the heavy, thudding footsteps of the massive, blind grazers on the terrace two levels beneath me. I feel the sharp, percussive *crack-crack-crack* of my brothers knapping obsidian miles to the east. I am held in a cradle of kinetic energy. 

Beside me, resting in a hollowed bowl made from the fibrous cap of a giant mushroom, is my trade: a luminous, viscous paste culled from the richest bacterial mats in the deep gutters, mixed with the pulverized white dust of arthropod shells. Next to it rests a freshly struck sliver of black glass, its edge honed to a single, devastating molecule of sharpness.

Someone is approaching. 

I feel her coming up the steep incline long before she emerges from the hazy mist. Her footfalls are light, quick, vibrating with the unique cadence of the high-crag hunters. She slips onto my precarious ledge, her six-fingered hands grasping my shoulders to steady herself. There is no hesitation, no request for permission to enter my space. We are the same organism. Her slick, bruised-purple flesh slides against mine in a friction-less greeting. 

She kneels before me, pressing her armored knees against my own. 

Beneath her jaw, her baggy throat sac inflates, flushing a deep, bruised violet. I inflate my own in response. We do not speak, but we sing—a wet, guttural, low-frequency hum that shudders through the humid air between us. Our vibrations find a harmonic resonance, interlocking perfectly beneath the ambient roar of the canyon. Her thrum tells me why she is here: she has survived the upper canopy. She pulled a roosting, leathery-winged scavenger from the ceiling fractures and brought it down to the butchering levels. It is a milestone of survival. It requires anchoring.

I reach out and grip her upper arm. My multi-jointed fingers wrap entirely around her coiled bicep. I take the razor of glass in my other hand. 

I do not look at my tools. My massive black eyes remain locked on hers, our vitreous stares pooling into one another. The rhythm is encoded in my nerve endings. I press the glass to her translucent flesh and slice downward in a long, deliberate arc. 

She does not flinch. There is no concept of suffering here, only the necessity of the act. The warm, dark blood pools instantly, but before it can run, I drop the glass and dip my massive thumb into the mushroom bowl. I drag the glowing bacterial paste deep into the fresh, gaping wound. My calloused fingers pack the spores firmly under her skin. 

As I work, a passing child—crawling vertically up the steeple on its way to the highest fungal farms—brushes past us. The child pauses momentarily, resting its heavy, domed head against my back, and casually licks a stray drop of blood from the hunter’s arm before scurrying away into the mist. We do not break our stare. 

I carve a second line, intersecting the first, charting a geometric constellation across her shoulder that mirrors the jagged fracture lines of our geological cradle. Slice, pack, press. When I am finished, the blood has washed away in the ambient weeping of the cavern air, and her shoulder blazes with a brilliant, bio-luminescent cyan light. 

It is a new star in her personal galaxy. The scar will heal over, but the bacteria will thrive beneath the translucent rubber of her skin forever, feeding off her internal heat. In the suffocating gloom of the city, she will be recognized instantly by her glow. Her history is now illuminated.

Her throat sac deflates with a final, echoing thrum of acknowledgment. She rises, sliding past me, pressing her wet cheek to mine, and vanishes back into the endless, swirling blur of industry on the lower terraces. 

I am alone on my basalt fang, yet utterly surrounded. Looking out over the vast, claustrophobic expanse, I see the result of my life’s work, and the work of thousands of Weavers before me. In the unyielding darkness, beneath the canopy of stretched membranes, the millions of my kin move like a river of living constellations. The glowing scars map out their labors, their ages, their hunts, and their survival. It is a staggering, undulating sea of blue, green, and pale white lights shifting through the mist, a perfect reflection of a starry sky that we have never seen and could not possibly imagine.

The thick, heavy air weeps a warm drop of water onto the crown of my domed skull. The marrow in my bones hums with the churning engine of my people. I feel a sudden, profound stillness beneath my skin.

I stand up, my back to the sheer cliff. I slide my glass blade into the crease of my hip, fold my long arms across my chest, and simply lock my joints tightly into place. My throat sac deflates. My black eyes stay bare and open to the mist. Without lying down, without seeking a quiet that does not exist, my mind lets go of the present moment. I sink into a waking, standing trance. The roar of the flint-knappers, the squealing of the beasts, and the damp breath of a million lives washes over me, holding me fast to the stone. 

I am suspended in the warm, wet dark, perfectly adapted, eternally at peace.
