You do not wake up here; you merely orient yourself to the turning. 

For the last twenty sleep-cycles, the horizon has been tilting. The vast, fluted smokestack to which my tribe’s nesting-web is anchored is not static. It is the housing for a helical gear the size of a mountain, moving at a rate of one meter a day. When I open my eyes, what was once the floor of my woven hammock is now becoming the wall. 

Gravity, the great invisible predator of our world, pulls at the slack of my body. It is time to re-weave.

I uncoil. My inner eyelids—thick, dark membranes smelling faintly of oil and salt—slide shut against the blinding, aggressive glare of the canopy’s dawn. Even through the black lenses, the light is a heavy, physical thing, baking the verdigris into my leathery skin. 

I reach out. I do not differentiate between top-limbs and bottom-limbs; all four of my appendages end in long, multi-jointed grips tipped with heavy keratin hooks. My upper-left grip catches a thick loop of stripped fiber-optic cable; my lower-right grip slots perfectly into the hollow gauge of a rusted pressure valve. The geometry of my body stretches, absorbing the tension. I hang suspended over a two-mile drop into the steamy, emerald abyss. 

I feel no vertigo. The empty air beneath me is not a void; it is a canvas of updrafts, a cushion of thick, thermal humidity. I am comfortable here, strung between the iron and the air.

I am a Tension-Singer. 

While the bungee-hunters hurl themselves into the mist to snatch jointed birds from the sky, and the algae-farmers scrape the scalding blast-baffles, my caste listens to the slow, groaning heartbeat of the Lattice. We read the math of the machine.

Hanging by two grips, my free appendages reach to my waist. I unclip my mallets—dense, heavy cylinders of ancient white ceramic carved from high-voltage insulators. I strike the rusted flange beneath me. 

*Clang.*

I wait. I feel the vibration travel up my own arms, vibrating against my calloused hide, carrying into my bones. 

*Tonk... tonk... trilllll.*

The echo returns, speaking to me through the metal. The smokestack is groaning. The tension on our anchor-lines is shifting. I strike a faster, polyrhythmic beat against the pipe—*taka-tonk-crack-tonk*—sending the message up into the sprawling, trampoline-like canopy of our village. 

Above me, the shadows of my kin move fluidly through the webbing. They hear the drumbeat. They know the angle is peaking. Dozens of rangy, copper-mottled bodies begin to unclip the heavy steel-wire anchors, leaping through the blinding air, re-lashing the braided strangler fig to higher purchase points. We are a living, migrating skin walking slowly around the circumference of a moving giant.

My work here is done, but the vibration in the metal told me something else. A deeper resonance. 

I let go. 

For four seconds, I am in pure free-fall. The wind roars past my auditory slits, hot and heavy, smelling of decaying orchids and ozone. The rusted girders and weeping ferns blur into streaks of orange and green. 

I spot my trajectory: a downward-sloping aqueduct of cracked atmospheric plating. I reach out all four limbs. My hooks catch the lip of the plating, my joints sliding outward, absorbing the massive kinetic energy with an elastic, noiseless grace. I am already moving before I fully settle, swinging under the plate, brachiatting hand-over-hand down into the denser, darker under-layers of the canopy.

Here, the light fractures. The air cools slightly, and the perpetual, localized drizzle of the Lattice begins. Plump drops of condensation bead off heavy black suspension cables, raining down on my shoulders. I navigate a corridor choked with strangler figs, their trunks as thick as blast-doors. 

I am tracking the vibration. The Lattice is infinite, but it has anatomies, pressure points where the biology and the industry are in conflict. I find it deep within a sunken transit rail gorge. 

A colossal hydraulic piston, inactive for centuries and draped in hanging gardens of neon ferns, has finally begun to shift. The glacial turning of the superstructure above is forcing it downward, but it is bound. Decades of thick, starchy electro-vines have woven into its rusted intake grilles. 

If the piston forces its way down, it will crush the vines, snapping the conduit and severing a vital bridge my people use to cross the gorge. I hang upside down from a cross-brace, watching the heavy drops of water sizzle against the faint, blue, electrical bleed of the vines. 

I pull a sharp flange of oxidized steel from my thigh-sheath. I do not want to sever the plant—to waste biomass is the ultimate sin—but I must guide it. I swing down, hooking into the rubbery hide of the vine itself. It pulses faintly with the rhythm of the low-voltage bleed it feeds upon. 

I begin to cut, carefully separating the thick aerial roots from the iron teeth of the grinding gear. It is slow, meticulous work. As I slice away the encroaching bark, the brilliant blue sap of the electro-vine weeps over my grips. It pools against my skin. 

I press the bleeding vine against my chest, where the complex, geometric scarification patterns of my rank are mapped. The fresh sap seeps into my cuts, mingling with my bloodstream. The bioluminescence flares to life. In the dim, wet mist of the under-canopy, my chest and arms pulse with a brilliant, electrical violet. I am drinking the light of the machine.

Suddenly, a massive cloud bank rolls into the gorge. The mist is instantly thick, white, and completely opaque. The world of metal and green disappears. Sound is completely muffled by the heavy vapor. The subsonic hum of the deepest engines becomes a vague vibration in the teeth.

I hang in the white nothingness.

Then, across the massive gap of the gorge, a mile away, a light flickers in the fog. An artificial, electric blue. Then another. And another. 

Constellations of glowing palms and illuminated faces are moving through the vapor. Other Tension-Singers, hunters, and weavers, their sap-filled scars piercing the absolute whiteout. I raise my upper grips. The violet symbols cut into my palms flare. I flex my fingers, opening and closing my hands in the stark, silent sign language of the mist. 

*The piston clears. The bridge holds. The tension sings.*

A cascade of flashing blue palms echoes back at me through the clouds. Acknowledgment. Kinship. 

Beneath me, with a sound like a crumbling mountain, the giant hydraulic piston shudders and drops one heavy, metallic inch. Free of the vines. A burst of pressurized, hissing steam erupts from a nearby release valve, bathing me in hot mist. 

I tilt my head back and let out a sharp, barking laugh that joins the hiss of the machine. 

I do not know what hands built this iron sky, nor do I care. They built the bones, but we are the blood. I swing my lower grips up, catch a dangling fiber-optic thread, and vault myself upward, climbing back toward the searing light, the deafening drums, and the eternal, rusted heavens.
