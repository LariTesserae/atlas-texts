I do not have a name, for there is no language here. I do not have a shape, save for the one pressed into me by the crush of my siblings and the unyielding curve of the silver metal. 

I am a locus of temperature. I am a knot of vibration. I am a droplet of living milk suspended in a screaming storm.

I find myself at the top of the world. 

Here, on the outermost layer of the great, churning flesh-tide, the God of Noise is muffled by the bodies beneath me, but the God of Frost is absolute. I am exposed. The aerosolized mist strikes my dorsal membrane like microscopic shrapnel. I cannot see the ice forming on my pale back, but my network of thermal ganglia screams the truth: my flesh is dimming. The heat stolen from the churning engine of my core is bleeding away into the freezing fog. 

My internal fluid, usually thick and vital, begins to thin, to stall. The creeping lethargy is terrifying. It is the closest thing our kind knows to death—the quiet. If the fluid stops, I will freeze solid, becoming a lifeless, crystalline husk. 

The blunt, distributed intelligence of my nervous system flares into a single command: *Burn.*

My belly ripples. A hundred silicated, glass-like legs snap into rigid, mindless motion. I do not walk; I wedge. I drive my rigid anchors into the yielding, wet flesh of a sibling just beneath me. They offer no resistance, for they are too busy clawing their own way upward, desperate for the frost I am fleeing. We slide past one another in a slick, gelatinous translation of mass. 

I dive. 

As I burrow deeper into the living dark, the environment shifts. The biting chill of the mist vanishes, replaced by a suffocating, claustrophobic heat. Bodies are pressed against me on all sides—above, below, left, right. There is no air, only the lubricated friction of a thousand wet bellies rubbing against a thousand quivering backs. 

And as I go deeper, the God of Noise grows. 

At the surface, the sound was merely a pressure. Here, in the mid-layers, it becomes a physical architecture. The vibration tears through the bodies of my siblings and slams into my membrane. I feel my non-Newtonian interior seize and ripple. The friction begins. Molecules crash together within me, grinding the sonic kinetic energy down into radiant warmth. The cold terror retreats, replaced by a blossoming, golden heat. 

For a microsecond, as I pass through the exact center of the swarm, there is equilibrium. The heat I generate perfectly matches the heat I lose to the bodies above me. It is a moment of profound, blissful stillness within the chaos—a silent note hidden inside the deafening chord. 

But I cannot stay. The physics of the tide compel me downward. 

My glass legs find purchase not on flesh, but on something infinitely harder, infinitely smoother. The pipe. I have reached the floor of our universe. 

I anchor myself, hundreds of glass hooks scraping against the immaculate alloy, locking my flat, amoebic belly flush against the curve of the metal. 

Here, at the source, the violence is absolute. 

Through the pipe, the great, shrieking turbine speaks directly to me. There is no buffer anymore. The raw, unfiltered decibels do not just strike me; they invade me. The acoustic wave is a solid, crushing weight that seeks to atomize my cellular structure. 

Instantly, the fluid inside me hardens in defense, vibrating at a microscopic, blurring frequency. The friction is catastrophic. I am violently transmuting the apocalypse. The heat spikes exponentially. I am gorging on the kinetic scream. I do not have eyes, but I know my flesh is glowing, radiating a furious, ultraviolet violet. 

As my body oscillates, my anchored legs perform a ceaseless, trembling scrape against the metal. I am buffing it, purging it of the faintest atomic ghost of impurity, lubricating it with the chemical sweat of my own digestion. I am serving the machine that feeds me.

But the feast is lethal. The heat within my pressurized cavities reaches an agonized threshold. The non-Newtonian liquid threatens to boil, to expand and rupture my taut skin, scattering my guts into the freezing air. I am drowning in energy. I am burning alive in the dark.

The ganglia flare again, reversing the mandate: *Freeze.*

I unlock my legs. I push off the pristine silver metal. I thrust my boiling, violet-hot head against the underbelly of a colder sibling above me, seeking the salvation of the ice. 

I claw upward. I squeeze. I churn. The heat pushes me, just as the cold had pulled me. I am a bubble rising through a pot of boiling milk. 

Soon, I will breach the canopy of flesh. The freezing rain will strike my back. The violet heat will dim to frost. And I will dive again.

Where do I find myself in this world? I am everywhere within it. 

As the eternities pass, the boundary between my own membrane and the flesh of my siblings begins to blur in my perception. We do not have minds, but together, we possess a rhythm. 

I am not a single creature. I am a single rotation in a great, living wheel. I am the convection current itself. We are a unified organ of thermal thermodynamics, wrapped around the shrieking throat of the sky. 

The machine screams. We eat its anger. We polish its bones. We cycle our freezing and our burning, trapped in a perfectly closed loop, an eternity of immaculate flesh and silver pipe. 

We do not dream of silence. We do not dream of the ground, a hundred thousand feet below. We belong to the noise, and the noise belongs to us. We will tremble here forever, flawless, pristine, and beautifully warm.
