I would be found in the Deep Weave—the innermost, most shielded tangle of the hanging pod-settlements, suspended beneath the colossal crook of a bismuth-root arch. Here, the deafening, apocalyptic roar of the cavern is muffled just enough by layers of vulcanized vines to be felt as a crushing, continuous vibration in the chest, rather than a force that strips the surface of the skin. 

I would not be a hunter. My days of casting myself into the golden, abrasive winds to ride the parchment-wings of manta-levithans are behind me. In a world with no patience, where life spans are measured in frantic, searing bursts of kinetic energy, I am something exceedingly rare: I am *old*.

My internal piezoelectric lattice is fraying. Where once my movements were a seamless, blinding blur of micro-snaps, I now stutter. When I reach for a tool, my arm ratchets forward in a sequence of jagged, discrete frames. My dual-chambered bellows must pump twice as hard to blow the charged silicate dust through my bamboo-like veins, meaning I run perilously, scorchingly hot. Without the heavy beetle-husk armor—which I have shed for the delicate work of my station—my braided-quartz body glows with a violent, translucent blue-white heat. I smell constantly of burnt cinnamon and scorched ozone, the ambient air cooking against my bare, molting mica skin. Arcs of static electricity bleed uncontrollably from my shoulders, grounding out against the dry vines of my pod with a rhythmic, snapping hiss.

I am a Shaper of Reflections. I am the voice-maker. I am the one who gives our frantic, fleeting lives their memory.

I sit cross-legged on a mat of woven fibers, moving with careful, jerky precision. The pod sways deeply, sickeningly, in the thermal updraft of an exploding forest miles below, but I am anchored by the sharp glass spurs of my heels. 

In my hands, I hold a piece of the dead. 

It is a shard of milky, opaque quartz—the fused, rigid silicate that once formed the central chest cavity of my clutch-mate. He ceased moving three cycles ago; his internal friction finally gave out, and he cooled into a weightless statue. When he was shattered by the blunt end of a spear, I claimed this piece. Now, I am turning him into language. 

Because we have no water, we have no tears, no mourning, no melancholy. Water brings rot and softness, but we are creatures of pure tension and geometry. Memory, for us, is not a quiet longing; it is a tactical necessity. We survive by passing on the exact angles of a leviathan’s blind spot, the precise timing of a root-detonation, the specific frequency required to explode a red scuttler. To do this, our mirrors must be flawless.

I do not polish the shard with sand or cloth. I polish it with myself. 

I press the pad of my thumb against the milky quartz and flood my hand with a localized electromagnetic charge. My piezoelectric fibers seize and release at tens of thousands of times per second. My hand blurs. The sheer dry friction between my thumb and the quartz shrieks, climbing in pitch until it passes entirely out of the acoustic realm. I bleed a halo of blue ball-lightning from my wrists. The heat is astronomical, but the shard yields. The opaque surface melts, fusing into a perfectly flat, hyper-reflective finish. 

I snap my hand away, my joints locking with a sharp *clack*. I hold the completed mirror up to the ambient, shadowless amber light of the void. It is perfect. For a microsecond, the crystal within my skull flashes a bright, bioluminescent spark of satisfaction. 

The threshold of my pod twitches. A hunter steps in. 

They do not walk; they displace. One moment the entrance is empty, the next they are standing inside, encased in their massive, spiked beetle-armor, coated in a fine layer of golden, glass-like pollen. They are vibrating with battle-adrenaline, their internal heat venting in wild, erratic static-bursts that illuminate the woven walls of the pod in strobe-light flashes of blue and white. 

They raise their forearm. The mirror affixed there is shattered—cracked by the razor-mandible of a swarm-grazer. They are mute.

I ratchet upward, stepping toward them. They remain perfectly still, a mannequin of scavenged chitin and lethal edges. With stuttering, hyper-precise movements, I strip the broken mirror from its dry-sinew binding. I take the newly polished mirror—the piece of my dead clutch-mate—and weave it securely onto their bracer. 

When it is bound, I step back. 

The hunter raises their arm, catching the ambient amber light, and their fingers flicker in a blinding, instantaneous sequence of micro-tilts. 
*Flash-flash-angle-flash.* 

It takes less than a second, but my wide, faceted crystal eyes catch the geometry perfectly. It is a burst of high-bandwidth syntax: *Gratitude. The harvest was rich. Three leviathans passed. The geometry of the lower canopy has shifted. I am whole again.*

I twitch my wrist, using a small mirror on my index finger to bounce a single beam of light directly into their polarized eyes: *Hunt well. Strike true.*

In a blur of motion, they dive backward out of the pod, vanishing into the violent, swirling void, returning to the endless war of consumption and genesis. 

I turn back to the depths of my shelter. Past my workbench, deeper into the thickest vines, lies the future. A cluster of jagged, geode-like nodules hangs suspended in a webbing of tight fibers. I approach them, feeling the vibration in the floorboards. 

One of the geodes is trembling. The internal pressure is mounting. I can see the faint blue glow of a piezoelectric spark building deep within the rock. 

I reach into my pouches and draw out a handful of tiny, pristine, unassigned mirrors. I crouch before the trembling geode, heat venting from my back in a blue haze, my fibers aching with the strain of merely existing. I am so tired. The structural fatigue in my core tells me I have only a handful of cycles left before the heat escapes me forever. Before my bellows freeze. Before I become milky and silent.

But I feel no dread. In a world moving at the absolute limit of velocity, death is just the final refinement of matter. 

*Crack.*

The geode detonates from the inside out, showering the pod in sharp debris. Out of the dust steps a miniature biped. It is instantly awake, instantly tense. Its tiny joints twitch in rapid micro-snaps. It vents a tiny cloud of ozone and heat. It turns its wide, black crystal eyes toward the blinding amber light filtering through the doorway. 

It has no voice yet. It has no memory. It has no geometry. 

My arm ratchets forward. I offer the newborn its first set of mirrors. And as I watch the ambient light catch on the glass, I realize my own perfect ending. I will not just shatter into lifeless dust. The next generation will gather the largest shards of my chest, they will furiously vibrate the opacity away, and they will strap me to their forearms and chests.

I will become the mirrors. I will be the tactical geometry. I will be the light that cuts through the golden storms, speaking across the abyss, forever keeping us alive.
