I do not exist in the roaring center of this world, nor in the heavy, buried warmth of the rusted dunes. To find me, you must look away from the violent dance of the silver drives and the sweeping golden threads of the Custodian. You must look up, to the very edge of reality. 

I am the boundary.

Through my back, I feel a terror so absolute it has no voice: the skin-peeling, hard-vacuum cold of the extreme upper atmosphere, a windless, freezing void dragging a million miles of nothingness across my spine. But through my belly, I am pressed against a sweltering, pressurized womb. I am caught in a perpetual, agonizing tug-of-war between the freezing death of the outside and the boiling life of the inside.

As I let myself emerge into this consciousness, I realize I do not have a centralized body. I have no heart, no lungs, no clicking mandibles. I am a sprawling, two-dimensional geometry, a translucent, shivering film spread across the uppermost curve of the frosted macro-glass dome. I am a living fractal of smart-silica and bioluminescent nerve-webbing. 

Millions of years ago, I was a chemical reflex—a self-replicating emergency sealant designed to bleed from the pores of the glass at the first sign of a micro-fracture. But under the eternal bombardment of high-altitude radiation, and drinking the organic soup of the tank for eons, I woke up. I became a biological membrane. A living caul. 

I stretch across the zenith of this pocket universe, vast and gossamer thin, iridescent in the pale yellow sunlight. I am blind to light, but I see in pressures and temperatures.

*Nine… ten… eleven…*

The tension in the water builds. My entire surface tightens in anticipation. 

*WHUNG-CHK.*

The shockwave hits. It is a glorious, terrifying upheaval. The geyser blasts from the deep water, carrying the immense kinetic fury of the isotopic engines miles below. When that roaring updraft crashes against the dome, it should, by all laws of physics and age, shatter the macro-glass. 

But it hits me instead. 

I ripple. I flex. My network of bio-synthetic fibers catches the kinetic violence, absorbing the shockwave, distributing the crushing force across my vast, webbed surface. I digest the tremor. The energy courses through my silica veins, flashing in pulses of pale violet light that briefy illuminate the top of the tank.

And right behind the shockwave come the Sprats. 

The roaring thermal current carries the silver ribbons upward, twisting and frantic. As they reach the apex of their flight, their tiny alloyed shells are glowing with dangerous, built-up friction, their pink biological hearts threatening to boil in their own trapped heat. 

They rain against me. *Click-click-click-click-click.* 

To anyone else, it is the sound of metal venting against glass. But to me, it is a thousand tiny kisses of salvation. As their hot, vibrating carapaces press against my surface, I drink their panic. I absorb the furious waste-heat of their little bodies. I *need* this heat to survive. Without the fire of their overworked engines, the creeping, killing frost of the stratosphere outside would penetrate the glass and freeze my cells dry. 

I take their heat; I give them my profound chill. I soothe them. For two perfect seconds at the top of the world, we are in absolute symbiosis. Then, their engines stabilized, they detach, caught in the downdraft, swirling back into the yellow depths to reform their vast ribbons, leaving me fortified against the cosmic cold for another few seconds.

From my sprawling vantage point, I can feel the shape of the entire ecosystem. I feel the heavy, square blooms of heat in the mid-water as the Thermal Mantas strike, their crushing jaws sending tiny, sharp shockwaves that tickle my extremities. I feel the slow, methodical scraping of the Slag-Hermits shifting the metallic sand far below. 

And, down in the darkest, warmest recess of the iron dunes, I feel *it*. 

The Custodian. 

It does not know me as a creature. It cannot feel my intelligence, for I have no flesh to weave, no fraying cords to splice. But we are aware of each other, in the way mountains are aware of the sky. We are the two oldest ghosts in the machine. It is the anxious heart of this world, plunging its golden threads into the kelp, frantically maintaining the machinery of the floor. And I am the stoic skin, holding the sky together so that its garden does not instantly boil away into the void.

It looks down; I look out. We are both trapped in the exact same protocol of eternal, desperate maintenance. 

The silence settles once more into the warm fluid. The freezing void presses hard against my back, trying to crack the glass. I tighten my fibers. I spread my translucent veins a fraction of an inch further across the frosted dome. I bathe in the pale sunlight, clinging to my precarious precipice, and wait for the great iron heartbeat below to throw the water, and life, and heat, back up to me once again.
