To exist here is to be a vessel for the noise. 

I do not hear the *THUD-SLAM* of the great engines; I wear it. It is the architecture of my consciousness. It vibrates through the hyper-dense, braided timber of my spine, traveling outward through the vulcanized fibers of my musculature, right down to the hooked roots that lock my flat feet to the serrated grating. 

I am suspended somewhere in the upper scaffolding of the Second Keep. Around me, the atmosphere is a heavy, boiling broth. I swallow it in vast, rhythmic gulps, drawing the atomized water and heavy exhaust into the deep heat-exchangers of my chest. I have no eyes to witness the eternal twilight, but my perception of the world is a masterpiece of blinding intensity.

Through the spongy, sensory fronds of my crown, the Sump is a topography of pure temperature. Above and behind me, the belly of the pumping station is an oceanic expanse of furious, blinding white—the sublime, holy radiance of friction and geothermal heat. To my left and right, the waxy, oily leaves of my kin—the great ferns and creeping vines—register as shifting shadows of cool, bruised hues, expanding and contracting as they gorge on the vented steam. Far below, beneath the sagging gantries and swaying chain-links, lies the ultimate void: the immense, flat, freezing black ribbon of the sterile marsh.

But there is a new color blooming in the dark. 

It is a sharp, jagged scarlet. A hemorrhage of high pressure. 

Beneath the overwhelming roar of the KEEP, I feel the drumming. *K-thok. K-thok-thok. K-thok.* It is transmitted through the hollow steel railing beneath my hand. A brother, somewhere lower on the western bulkhead, rapping his wooden knuckles against the pipe network. A pressure valve is failing in Sector Four. The threading is stripped.

I release my grip on the steel and begin the descent. 

I am heavy. My footfalls ring out with wet, wooden thuds against the iron walkways. The turbulent thermals of the caldera whip around me, attempting to tear me from the gantry, but my heavy, clawed toes slot perfectly into the rusted diamonds of the grating, anchoring me step by step. My joints—wrapped in thick, cyan and black armored leaves—slide over one another, though today they grind with a quiet, gritty friction. 

I have lived a long time. The pale sap that pumps through my vines is thick and sludgy, darkened by years of heavy industrial lubricants and inhaled smog. I can feel the mineralized scale building within the capillaries of my deepest roots. I am calcifying, slowly turning into the very iron I tend. 

The scarlet bloom grows violently bright in my thermal vision. I feel the shriek of the failing valve before I reach it—a high-frequency vibration that threatens to splinter my outer bark. A massive brass joint, thick as a boulder, has cracked. Scalding, superheated coolant is jetting into the murky air into a horizontal geyser. It is hot enough to flash-melt bone. 

I step directly into the spray.

It is glorious. The heat hits my chest like a physical blow, and instantly, my biology responds. The leaves forming my carapace shudder, drinking the raw energy, weeping thick condensation. I do not hesitate. I raise my right arm—a thick, braided mass of pale root-wood and vulcanized vine—and jam it shoulder-deep into the roaring breach of the brass joint.

The synergy takes hold. 

My internal fluids meet the superheated coolant. The cellular acceleration is instantaneous, violent, and euphoric. I feel the wood of my forearm rapidly gorge on the heat, expanding with terrifying speed. The fibers petrify. My fingers, wedged deep inside the gears and threading, explode into dense, calcified timber, perfectly molding to the negative space of the cracked brass. I am sealing the wound with myself.

I flex my shoulder, twisting my torso with a horrific, groaning torque. 

*SNAP.*

My arm severs just below the shoulder, leaving the petrified limb permanently wedged into the machine, a perfect, living gasket. A spray of translucent, scalding sap erupts from my stump, filling the immediate air with the sharp, clean scent of ozone and bruised pine. The scarlet leak fades back into the steady, secure white heat of the great machine.

Almost immediately, the stump begins to writhe. Gorged on the heat of the repair, a wet, fleshy node pushes through the bark. Tendrils uncurl in aggressive, jerking spasms, weaving around each other, twisting and tightening into fresh muscle. Within hours, I will have a new hand.

But the rest of me is tired.

The burst of accelerated growth has taxed my reserves. As I step back from the sealed valve, my legs feel profoundly heavy. The sludge in my veins barely moves. The friction in my knees does not ease; it sets. The iron I have inhaled, the raw coolant I have drank, the toxic rain I have weathered—they have finally outpaced my biology. I am inefficient. 

I raise my one good hand to the steel pipe beside me and curl my knuckles.

*Thok. Thok-thok. Thok.*

*Repair complete. Vessel expiring. Returning to the black.*

I do not wait for the echoing response. I turn away from the blinding white glory of the Keep and begin the slow, stiff walk downward. 

Down the rusted ladders. Down the sagging gantries. Away from the heat. Away from the *THUD-SLAM*. 

I reach the shore of the vast black-water marsh. The thermal landscape here is quiet, profoundly empty, defined by the freezing void of the water and the gentle, lukewarm drizzle of the chemical rain falling from the unseen ceiling forever above.

I wade out. 

The water pulls at my legs, heavy and astringent. I walk until I am waist-deep, dead center in the echoing expanse between the three great leviathans of the Sump. The roar of the engines is muffled here, absorbed by the eternal, heavy twilight. 

I stop moving. 

I release the tension in my vulcanized muscles. Slowly, deliberately, I let the deep cyan and oily black leaves of my armor unfold. They peel backward like the petals of a dying flower, exposing the pale, calcified wood of my core to the air, to the rain, and to the black water.

Already, I feel the sharp, chemical bite of the marsh going to work. The bio-matter begins to soften. The fibers begin to loosen. There is no fear, no resistance. I am a monk at the end of a long, devoted meditation. 

I will dissolve. My organic shell will melt away, leaving only the pure, heavy metals and distilled coolants I have gathered over a lifetime. They will sink to the bottom of the black. And then, tomorrow, or in a decade, the great subterranean pumps will drink me up. I will be drawn through the dark earth, flash-boiled in the holy fire of the engine, and vented out as blistering white steam into the thick, swallowed air of the caldera. 

I am the Scion. I am the machine. I close my fronds, and let the Sump take me back.
