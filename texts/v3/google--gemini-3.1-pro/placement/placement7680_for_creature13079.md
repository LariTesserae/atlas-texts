I do not arrive in this place; I condense. 

At first, I am only the sensation of weight and water. I feel the permanent, freezing mist settling. Then, gravity insists, and a center of gravity forms—low, dense, pulling me into a squat, heavy frame. 

I open my eyes. Blinding, absolute light fractures across my vision. A semi-transparent membrane sweeps horizontally across my stinging corneas, clearing the sudden accumulation of water. As my vision clears, I look through the prisms of endless, falling rain to see a world of brutal, vertical geometry. The sheer, glassy chalcedony wall rises hundreds of feet above me, disappearing into a brilliant throat of white sky.

My cheekbones pulse with a dull heat. I reach up with a broad, heavy, pale hand, tracing the bruised-violet pigmentation beneath my eyes—the shield that allows me to look into the glare without going blind. My chest shakes. It is a violent, constant rattling of the ribcage. The oceanic howling of the wind is tearing across the mouth of the shaft above, but I hear absolute nothingness. My skull is smooth beneath my damp hair, sealed and unbroken. 

The roar is not a sound; it is just the weather of my bones.

I am standing on a cantilevered platform, lashed together with impossibly tight green vines. Beneath my bare, wide-splayed feet, the wet planks of swollen hardwood are alive. The wood trembles. A thousand microscopic shudders pass through the soles of my feet, traveling up my shins and settling in the marrow of my hips. 

I sink into my knees, adjusting my weight. In this sheer, hanging world, to stand rigid is to beg the void to take you. I move with the slow, hydraulic deliberation of my people, my muscles surging with low-gear power under the thick, dripping emerald shingles of my epiphyte cloak. 

As I stand there, the sensory chaos of the shaft begins to resolve into a profound, ordered map. The vibrations beneath my feet are not random. They are a chorus. 

Through my left heel, I feel the steady, rhythmic *thump-pause-thump* of a neighbor climbing an aerial root fifty feet above. Through my toes, I feel the soft, rolling hum of a conversation taking place across a braided suspension bridge. The platform I stand on is a nexus point, absorbing the overlapping tremors of the entire settlement. Without looking up, my feet tell me the shape of my city. I close my eyes and simply read the trembling wood. 

I am a Tender of the Weave. 

In a world where language is structure, and structure is language, I am the one who ensures the scaffolding does not go deaf. A loose bridge is a slurred word. A rotting hardwood peg is a forgotten memory. If the high-tension fibrous cordage goes slack in the icy damp, the tactile voices of my people will not transmit. They will be trapped, isolated on their platforms, shouting into soft, dead wood.

My task pulls me forward. I shift my weight, stepping off the communal platform onto a narrow, descending set of tension-planks. My heavy, calloused toes grip the slick, wet edges with unyielding certainty. The freezing rain runs in heavy sheets over my shoulders. Hidden within the downy, blue-green sphagnum moss lining my coat, my core is a furnace of starchy heat, fueled by the massive tubers I ate at the waking hour. 

I trace the edge of the chasm, holding onto a thick, lacquered perimeter cable. The sheer expanse of the void drops eternally to my right. There is no fear. The abyss is just the empty space at the center of our geode; it is the silence against which we speak.

My hand slides along the cable. I stop. 

Beneath the steady, lung-shaking pressure of the wind, I feel a deadness in the line. A stutter. 

I kneel on the wet wood, bringing my face close to the architecture. Here, where the tension cord anchors into the glassy bedrock, the cordage has frayed. The fungal resins have washed away in the perpetual weeping of the stone, allowing the moisture to swell the fibers unevenly. The tension is failing. The 'speech-cord' that runs parallel to this walkway is drooping. Any conversation sent through this line would arrive muffled, devoid of emotion, like a whisper lost in a storm. 

I reach to the heavy belt cinched around my thick waist. I draw a gleaming shard of chipped chalcedony, knapped to a razor edge, and a fresh spool of high-tension vine, cured and pre-stretched.

My movements are agonizingly slow, perfectly absolute. A sudden jerk could snap the remaining fibers; a slip of the knife could sever the central vein of communication for the lower tier. I brace my broad back against the raw, un-weathered face of the mountain. With my thick thumbs, I pry the fibers apart. I shave the damaged pith away with the glass blade, weaving the new vine into the frayed lacework. 

I pinch the wood. I pull. 

Muscles in my shoulders, layered thick as tree-burls, engage as I draw the new cord taut. I pull until my bare feet slide a fraction of an inch on the wet planks. I hold the agonizing tension, breathing slowly through the water running over my lips, while my other hand drives a fresh wedge of hardwood into the splice, locking it in place.

I release my grip. The line holds. 

It is time to test the syntax. I slide my wooden pick from my collar. Hooking it beneath the newly tightened speech-cord, I bend my knees, ground my heels utterly into the walkway, and strike.

*THROOM.*

The vibration is immense. It snaps off the cord, diving directly into the wet planks, rushing up through my arches and blasting into my knee joints—a sharp, perfectly clean pulse of heavy, resonant bass. It does not decay. It travels. 

I stand still, keeping my soles flat, and wait.

Seconds pass. I watch the eternal, bright mist falling in the center of the shaft.

Then, from the darkness of the lower terraces, a vibration rolls up the anchor line. It is a dual chord—two voices plucking their cords in unison. *Snap-Thrum.* It rattles the bones of my ankles warmly.

It means: *We feel you. The path is clear.*

From the sunlit upper tiers above, a different pulse shudders down the stone wall. A rapid, pattering rhythm, like toes tapping joyfully on a drumhead. 

It means: *The voice is mended. The tension is good.*

I smile, a silent, slow parting of the lips. I tuck the chalcedony blade back into my belt. Suspended between the blinding, fractured light of the summit and the terrifying, bottomless dark of the hollow mountain, I shift my weight and step forward. The wind rattles my ribs, the frozen rain coats my skin, and beneath my bare feet, the wood hums with the life of a hundred souls, perfectly in tune.
