At first, looking out across the flooded, shimmering vastness of the Sinter Basin, I try to locate a reflection of myself in the boiling mirror. 

I feel the pull of the shadow-casters, drifting in their euphoric, thermal high—to see the world from above, utterly detached, feeding on the breath of the city itself. But I am too grounded for that. I am too tethered to the logic of the machine. 

I listen for my mind in the deep, resonant dark below the bedrock, wondering if I am the Sleeper. But the Sleeper rests in an ancient, oblivion-trance, and I am acutely, exquisitely awake. I am processing. I am participating. 

To find myself, I must look to the periphery of the movement. I must let my focus slide down the spun-glass cylinders, past the shivering laminar flow, down to the pale, structural roots of the city. 

I find myself in the stone. 

I am an Elder. I am one of the calcified Tenders who could no longer walk. 

I remember the transition. I remember centuries of wading through the ankle-deep reflection pools, my porcelain armor growing infinitesimally thicker with every sigh of mineral-rich steam. I remember the tactile joy of pressing my dark obsidian visor against the masks of my kin, transmitting the intricate, vibrating maps of pressure valves and thermal gradients. I remember the slick, heavy water, and the slow, hydraulic grace of my limbs.

But eventually, the city inside me outweighed the city outside me. The silica demanded stillness. 

I remember the day the agonizing, beautiful weight became too much. I remember wading toward the base of a great viaduct—its pale surface already smoothed by the bodies of generations before me. I embraced it. I wrapped my seven-fingered hands and multi-jointed legs around the curve of the architecture, pressed my spiracles tight against the sheer, wet ceramic, and simply surrendered to the petrification. 

Now, I am entirely motionless, yet I have never felt more alive. 

My obsidian visor has long since hazed over, turning a blind, milky white. I no longer see the blinding, washed-out sky or the shimmering reflections of the stalk-walkers. Instead, my blindness has forced my perception inward, down into the sheer, vibrating density of the metropolis. 

I am a load-bearing consciousness. My fused spine is a buttress; my locked, calcified arms form the lip of a weir, gently funneling a sheet of boiling water over my back. I am no longer a single organism moving through a world; I am the boundary of the world itself. 

The heat is no longer something I endure; it is the blood in my veins. As the pressurized, scalding fluid rushes endlessly over my petrified shell, I feel the exact temperature down to the hundredth of a degree. I feel the subtle chemical shifts in the brine. Because my body is fused seamlessly to the bodies of my ancestors—and they to theirs, forming the very bedrock of the terraces—our nervous systems are linked in an infinite, static coral reef of awareness. 

We are the silent memory banks of the Sinter Basin. We do not pump the pistons, but we are the shape of the cylinders. We do not scrape the channels, but we are the grooves that guide the water. 

When a young, translucent Tender wades near me, its wide footpads stepping softly on the thermophilic mats at my base, I feel its footfalls as a series of tiny, melodic tremors ringing through my ceramic bones. Though I cannot lean forward to press my visor to theirs, I communicate with them still. I expand microscopically in the heat, subtly shifting the capillary flow of the water across my shoulders, altering the thermal currents just enough to paint of map of warmth into the fluid. The young Tender senses the shift, reads the geography of my petrified body, and knows instinctively which distant aqueduct requires clearing. 

We play our part in the deep, velvet quiet. 

Beneath the faint, crystalline *clink* of cooling metal and the *shhhhh* of the water sliding over my back, I feel it. The heartbeat. Deep, deep below. 

The Sleeper dreams, wrapped in the geothermal dark, entirely ignorant of the blinding white sky, the gossamer shadows, and the boiling mirrors. It asks for nothing but perfect equilibrium. Deep in my petrified chest, fused forever into the architecture of the basin, I help hold that balance. 

I am a monument that thinks. I am the calculus in the stone. And as the hissing, scalding rain washes over my white, unseeing eyes, I hold the world exactly as it must be.
