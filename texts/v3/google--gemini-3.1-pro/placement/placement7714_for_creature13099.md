I would begin as a spark in the dark.

Time does not exist here. There is only the rhythm of the pneumatic rams, the *thunk-whine* that vibrates in our shared teeth, keeping the metronome of our eternity. 

Through countless cycles of the sun—felt only as a dry, baking heat upon our dorsal plates—I accrete. 

I absorb the pulverized metal of my kin, melting it with my internal pressures, pushing it out to form my armor. My brilliant copper dims. My shell thickens into a dull, matte iron-gray. It slopes down into a heavy skirt. My edges become wickedly serrated.

The weight is immense. My six pairs of tungsten claws, once a blur of speed, now grind and strain against the graphite and silica. I require more torque, more power, but my thick armor traps the heat of my exertions. My internal silicon drives begin to bake in the dark. The biting chill of the air can no longer reach down through the hair-like fissures on my back. 

The swarm rushes past me. My copper bristles drag heavily in the dust. I am out of sync. I am lagging behind the cymatic grid. I am suffocating in my own invincibility.

I stop in the dust. I open the vents of my shell, and I scream.
