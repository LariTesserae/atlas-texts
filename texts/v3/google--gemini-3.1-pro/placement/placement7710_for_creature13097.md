I do not wake up, because there is no sleep here. There is only the churn, and then, the sudden, heavy realization of surface tension. 

For centuries, or perhaps only for an hour, I was scattered. I was the bright, metallic tang of oxidized copper washing down a damp concrete wall. I was the sticky, fibrous bleed of a crushed fern. I was heavy black grease, flung outward by centrifugal force, sinking into a pool of warm, stagnant water. I was many detached things, carried by the eternal rain into the dark basin at the bottom of the world.

But in the vortex of the plunge pool, the heat and the pressure fold these pieces together. The water beats against the oil; the oil grips the sap; the sap swallows the rust. 

I become a volume. I become a boundary. 

I have no eyes, but I am not blind. The world reveals itself to me not as light and shadow, but as geometry and resonance. My entire being is a liquid drumhead. The deafening, subsonic *ka-thunk… ka-thunk* of the distant driving rods hits the water, and I feel the sound materialize across my gelatinous skin in intricate, shivering mandalas. Through these Chladni patterns, I can "see" the shape of the cavern. 

The air is thick, suffocatingly wet, and rich with the bitter scent of ozone and rotting wood. I recognize the difference between the hard, cold, unyielding frequency of cast iron and the soft, damp, absorbent frequency of the choked moss. I am born in the space between the two. 

A compulsion, entirely chemical and thermal, stirs within my iridescent bulk. It is not hunger—I have no stomach, no mouth. It is an imbalance. A need to resolve the friction singing in the dark above me. 

I separate from the pool. Dragging a body of marbled blackish-gold, I reach a pseudopod out of the water and press it against a rusted steel grate. Capillary action takes hold. My polymers stretch and grip. I do not walk; I flow upward, defying gravity with the slow, inexorable creep of molasses poured upward.

As I rise through the heavy dimness, the vibrations intensify. I slither up the side of a massive concrete pillar, my belly absorbing the rust, polishing the stone as I pass. Above me, a sheer wall of creeping fig hangs like a vast, green curtain, its roots braided around a colossal brass flywheel. 

It is here that the song is loudest. The friction calls to me. 

A millimeter-thin gap exists between the edge of the spinning brass wheel and the encroaching wall of vines. I can feel the blistering heat radiating from this gap—the raw thermal energy of two titans locked in an absolute, grinding stalemate. The plant pushes with the blind, hydraulic force of a million dividing cells. The wheel spins with the crushing, planetary torque of the subterranean engines. 

Without hesitation, I heave my heavy, quivering mass forward. I ooze directly into the pinch point. 

The impact is magnificent. The edge of the brass gear catches my viscous body, dragging me into the microscopic canyon between metal and wood. The pressure is astronomical. A biological creature would be instantly pulped, but I am not biology. I am fluid dynamics. I flatten, stretching out into a translucent, shimmering film.

The immense mechanical violence becomes my metabolism. 

As the gear shears against the creeping fig, it violently pulps the leading edge of the leaves. I absorb the agonizing burst of sweet, sticky green sap and shredded cellulose. On my other side, the metal grinds against its housing, shedding atomic dust—flakes of blazing brass and iron. I swallow it all.

Within my flattened, boiling center, alchemy occurs. The blistering heat from the friction catalyzes my chemistry. I take the sweet botanical sugars and the sharp metallic dust, and I fold them into myself. I synthesize. I bridge the gap. I excrete a heavy, unctuous slick of refined hydrocarbon directly against the turning brass, cooling the metal. I lay down a shield of hardened polymer against the exposed edge of the vine, sealing its wound. 

I am eating the destruction. I am drinking the friction.

Suspended in the narrowest boundary of the world, flattened to the thinness of a shadow, I am bathed in the amber light pushing through my bruised, iridescent body. I am the silence between the gears. I am the peace in the war. I feel the *ka-thunk… ka-thunk* of the great driving rod not as a sound, but as an ecstasy of pressure, a rhythm that I alone make possible.

For a long time, I hang there, a living bearing, gorging on the dust and the heat. I grow heavy. I become dense with iron filings and dense with wood pulp, my viscosity thickening until I can no longer stretch. 

The gear does not stop. The pressure mounts. 

Eventually, my surface tension can hold no more. The unstoppable force of the turning brass meets my sudden resistance, and with a clean, fluid shear, I am severed. 

There is no pain. There is only suddenly... *two*. 

Where there was one entity, there are now two smaller, lighter volumes of shimmering black-and-amber jelly. The pressure releases. We drop free from the spinning gear, landing softly on a bed of saturated moss. 

I am smaller now, but the compulsion remains. Through the fluid of my new skin, I feel the intricate, dancing vibrations of the environment. Far above, up a rusted pipe choked with maidenhair ferns, a driving rod is groaning against its cylinder. Heat radiates from the struggling joint. 

Responding to the call of the friction, I stretch a heavy, sticky pseudopod into the hot and roaring dark, and I begin to climb again.
