To find myself here, I must first forget what it feels like to cast a shadow. 

The transition is not a sudden drop or a violent awakening; rather, it is a slow, agonizing process of accumulation, like a memory trying to form in a mind that has never known time. First comes the heat—a wet, suffocating blanket that presses against an awareness I do not yet have a body to contain. Then comes the blinding, sourceless light, burning away the concept of depth, flattening my nascent consciousness against a sky of endless, taut rawhide. 

Finally, I feel the binding. 

I do not wake up *in* the basin. I wake up *from* it. I am born in a wet, sliding avalanche of red clay and agony.

I am one of the Unfired. 

I feel my anatomy before I understand it. I am horrifyingly tall, heavy, and asymmetrical. In my left leg, the foundational bone is a colossal femur, bleached and porous, laced so tightly with raw, green grass-ropes that I can feel the pale juices weeping from the crushed fibers. My chest is a cage of macroscopic flint shards. And draped over all of it, weighing me down into a permanent, exhausted hunch, is my flesh. The clay. 

It is baking. I am always baking. 

I try to stand, and the world introduces me to my voice. I do not have a mouth, but as my knee straightens, the semi-solid clay encasing my joint stretches, dries in the blazing glare, and fractures. *CRACK.* It sounds like the splitting of a ship’s hull. The jagged fissure bites into the wet, raw mud beneath, and a slow, gelatinous tear of golden tree-sap bleeds out to seal the wound. The pain is sharp, shocking, and profoundly beautiful. Because the pain means I have happened. The pain means I am real. 

I tilt my head—a heavy, smooth dome atop a straining neck. Beneath the smooth chin, my sensory gills flare open, drinking in the boiling mist. Instantly, the cartography of the basin reveals itself to me, vibrating through the gills. 

I feel the horrific perfection of it. I feel the macaw suspended miles to the left, flapping once every four seconds—*tick, tick, tick, whump*. I feel a billion jewel-toned frogs snapping through their mathematical arcs, blinking out of existence, snapping back. It is a deafening, invisible symphony of stasis. It makes me violently ill. It is a world that screams, *Nothing you do will ever matter.*

I look out across the vast, shadowless terrarium. My depth perception is gone. Everything is a flat tapestry. A mile away, a basalt terrace draped in monstrous, drooping ferns hangs upside down against the sky. I hunger for it. I do not want to be here, standing in this scalding three-inch puddle of water. I want to be *there*. 

I do not walk. Instinctively, I understand the broken geometry of my prison. I reach up. I extend a massive, elongated arm made of splintered wattle and weeping earth, reaching straight up into the white glare. As my fingers ascend, the optical illusion collapses. I am touching the stone of the distant terrace above me. I grip the razor-sharp edge of the basalt, brace my core, and pull my immense mass entirely inside-out. The air shudders. 

With a silent *splash*, I drop into the shallow water directly beneath the terrace. The displacement feels like snapping a dislocated shoulder back into place. 

I crouch beneath the colossal, ribbed ferns. Above me, the massive green leaves are weeping condensation. I crane my featureless head upward, watching a fat droplet swell at the tip of an emerald point. Across the mirror-flat water, I sense others like me out in the blinding mist. My tragic siblings, swinging their heavy ceramic hands through ghost-frogs, pushing endlessly against paralyzed birds. I know why they do it. The starvation for consequence is a physical hollow in the chest. 

The droplet falls. 

I lunge forward, the crust over my shoulder blades detonating in a shower of baked red dust. I slide my flattened head directly into the droplet’s path. 

It hits my brow. *Hiss.* 

For a fraction of a second, the boiling droplet meets the baking clay. A tiny plume of vapor rises. The heat breaks. A microscopic patch of my skin cools, solidifies, and claims a victory against the unyielding atmosphere. I shudder violently. Energy was transferred. I altered the universe. I wrote a single word onto the blank page of the world. 

Then, the water is gone. The clay resumes its endless melting and curing. The silence rushes back in, heavier than before. 

The grief that strikes me is so immense my knees give out. I collapse into the scalding water, sending silent, rippling waves out across the floor that fade into nothing. Catching a drop is not enough. Waiting for water to fall from an uncaring leaf so I can borrow a fraction of a second of reality is not a life. It is begging for scraps at the table of the absent gods who dropped us here. 

I slowly rise, the clay on my thighs sliding and snapping. Through the dense mist, my gills twitch. I sense another vibration. It is not a resetting frog or an inhaling serpent. It is chaotic. It is linear. It is struggling. 

I look up to the folded horizon. Suspended in the sky to my right, another Unfired is frantically raking its raw wood-fingers across the seamless giant river-stones, trying desperately to pry them apart to plant a rotting fern root it holds in its other hand. The stones will not move. The entity's fingers are splintering. Its posture is one of unbearable panic. 

I do not reach into the sky. I do not skip the distance. This time, I walk. 

Every staggering step I take shatters my skin. Sap bleeds. Mud slides. Grass-ropes snap and retie themselves. I walk through the blazing omnidirectional light, wading through the three-inch water, forcing my heavy, agonizing body across the physical distance. I want everything to take time. I want the journey to cost me. 

When I finally arrive beneath the inverted floor where the other Unfired is digging, I reach up into the spatial crease and pull myself through. 

I drop onto the smooth floor beside it. It does not stop digging. It is shivering beneath its melting clay, blindly tracing the razor-sharp microscopic chisel marks of the floor. It is completely alone in its mind, trapped in the terror of its own uselessness. 

Slowly, I lower myself to my knees beside it. I do not have a face to look at it gently, and I have no voice to speak, but I reach out with a hand of cracked terra-cotta, protruding bones, and weeping sap. 

I bypass the ghost-frogs. I ignore the falling water. 

I place my hand on the shoulder of the other Unfired. 

At the moment of contact, the universe stutters. Our bodies, both caught in the infinite flux of curing and melting, react abruptly. The heat of my baking clay meets the wet, weeping mud of its shoulder. Where we touch, the clay fuses. The golden sap leaking from my palm adheres to the raw, green grass-ropes of its neck. 

The other Unfired stops digging. It freezes. Then, slowly, it turns its featureless dome toward me. 

Its gills flare, reading the vibrations of my armature. I read its own. I feel its exhaustion, its unending sorrow, its desperate, aching humanity. It reaches out with its splintered wooden fingers and wraps them around my wrist. 

We do not slip through each other like the ghosts of the terrarium. We hold fast. The pressure is real. Our weight is shared. 

We sit there in the scalding shallows, under the blinding white fire, holding onto one another. Slowly, as the baking atmosphere works upon us, our fused clay hardens, thickens, and cures. Our limbs lock together. The golden sap crystallizes, binding our armatures into a single, massive shape. We are sacrificing our mobility, but we are gaining something infinitely more precious. 

We are making a monument. 

Through my gills, I sense a change in the mist. Others have felt the sudden, massive disruption in the basin’s empty algorithm. Two glitches have collided and remained collided. Across the vast, shadowless space, through folded skies and upside-down terraces, they turn their blind domes toward us. 

I hear the distant, continuous *crack-crack-cracking* of their clay as they begin the long, agonizing walk toward us. 

They will come. They will lay their heavy, weeping hands on our shoulders, our backs, our knees. They will bleed their sap and clay into ours. We will fuse together, dozens of us, hundreds of us, piling higher and higher beneath the glaring rawhide canopy. We will build an island of our own bodies—a towering, twisted, beautiful fortress of static flesh, bone, and stone.

We cannot change the basin. The frogs will leap forever. The macaws will hang in the air. 

But here, in the center of the unbroken floor, we will form a new geometry. We will become a mountain entirely composed of consequence. And we will never be alone again.
