There is a profound, holy terror in the vibration. 

Before the deafening *clatter-smash-roar* reaches the air, the deep basalt of the trench floor begins to hum. Down in the darkest, most pressurized fissures—the superheated veins where the world’s mantle bleeds its localized fevers—I wait. I wedge myself tightly into the jagged throat of an obsidian crevice, holding my breath, feeling the temperature rise as the flesh-quake approaches. 

I am not built for momentum. In a valley defined by dizzying, blinding velocity, I am the anomaly of stillness. 

Overhead, the horde of Trench-Runners arrives. The sound is an annihilation of the senses—a torrential percussion of millions of clawed feet, shattering insectoid carapaces, and the endless, frantic *k-tchak, snap, clack* of stone striking stone. Dust and pulverized flint sift down into my fissure in a dry, choking snow. The rock walls around me tremble so violently that the ambient geothermal heat feels like it is pulsing to the heartbeat of a single, monstrous organism. 

For twelve hours, the sky above my crack in the earth is a blur of vaulting, gangly limbs, raining blood, and screaming vegetation. 

And then, as suddenly as the storm arrived, the pressure drops. The roaring fades into a muted, echoing murmur miles away down the tectonic scar. What replaces it is the intimate, rhythmic *drip, drip, drip* of heavy dew running off the severed, bleeding stalks of the jungle, pooling into the slurry of pulverized rock and insect viscera left behind. 

It is time to emerge.

I unpack myself from the fissure. I am entirely lacking in bone. My body is a sprawling, hydraulic mass of hyper-dense muscle and pressurized fluid, akin to a colossal terrestrial urchin or an inverted sea anemone, though thicker, heavier, and gleaming with a thick coat of opalescent mucus. Extended, I am as wide as a fallen tree. I possess no sensory bristles and no vast, leaping legs. I perceive the world slowly, tasting the air through a crown of feathery, chemical-sensing papillae that unfurl from my center, mapping the bruised twilight by the scent of ozone, spilled sap, and fresh marrow.

I pour myself over the lip of the ravine, a slow, iridescent tide, spilling onto the devastated valley floor. 

The environment is a graveyard of frantic amnesia. The Runners leave behind thousands of perfectly knapped tools, discarded the second their utility vanished. The earth sparkles with fractured shale, heavy basalt hammers, and wickedly sharp obsidian knives lying in pools of crushed foliage. 

I undulate slowly across this glittering ruin, my soft underside secreting a thick, caustic enzyme that begins to melt down the leftover scraps of giant centipede meat and nutrient-rich sap. I am a scavenger of calories, yes. But my true hunger is structural. 

I flow over a magnificent piece of freshly struck obsidian. It is a blade the size of a human forearm, curved perfectly to a microscopic, razor-sharp edge, abandoned because of a tiny, consequential chip near the hilt. 

I pause. Several of my prehensile muscular bands extend, feeling the geometry of the stone. It is a masterpiece of disposable genius. I lift it gently from the muck. 

Along the vast, domed sloping of my back, there are thousands of deep, specialized muscular fissures. With deliberate elegance, I slide the dull edge of the obsidian heavy into an empty groove. The flesh clamps down viciously, locking the stone into place, leaving only the razor edge pointing outward. 

I repeat the process. I am deeply selective. I pass over the crude basalt hammers and the splintered bone-shards, seeking only the sharpest, most flawlessly knapped blades of glass and shale. As I find them, I weave the discarded industry of the swarm into my own flesh. 

I am the **Lithic Urchin**. I am the **Scavenger of Edges**. 

Where the Trench-Runners are soft and vulnerable, trusting their unbroken speed to survive, I am heavily armored in a lethal, bristling dome of stolen geology. My back is an interlocking, impossibly dense fortress of upward-facing knives, overlapping perfectly like the scales of an iron pinecone. 

Suddenly, a massive, leathery shadow detaches from the heavy smog above. A winged scavenger, lured by the scent of carrion, dives toward my soft, exposed feeding tentacles. 

I do not flee. I simply contract. 

With a pressurized pneumatic hiss, my body pulls inward, burying my soft organs beneath the dome of my carapace. The scavenger hits me, screaming as its momentum drives its chest straight into a thicket of two hundred upright obsidian spears. It thrashes, bleeding out over my glass armor, before finally going still in the gloom. I will dissolve it slowly, absorbing its nutrients over the coming days, safely hidden beneath my deadly shell. 

To live entirely in the wake of the swarm is a lonely, majestic existence. The dark, sweltering jungle possesses immense elasticity, healing itself in time-lapse spurts, bursting with pale nodules that erase the damage of the horde. The flora forgets. The fauna rushes forward, abandoning its work the moment it is done. The swarms have no memory; they possess no past, only an endless, starving present. 

Standing in the dripping fog, dragging my immense, heavy, clinking body over the bruised rock, I realize what I am. 

I am the only memory this valley has. 

I am a creeping, living archive of their chaotic brilliance. Buried deep in my lower mantle are jagged flints rough-hewn by Runners who passed through this valley decades—perhaps centuries—ago. Near my crown are the sleek, micro-bladed glass shards of today. I carry the evolutionary history of their silent language on my back, a towering, jagged mosaic of forgotten time.

Miles away, the roaring vibration begins to echo again. The horde has circled back, or another is arriving. The valley floor begins to hum. 

I do not hide in the fissures this time. I draw my limbs in, lock my muscles tight, and settle firmly onto the floor of the trench. Through their acoustic telemetry, as their sonar-pings of struck flints bounce off me, I do not register as prey. Because my skin is entirely hidden by their own discarded tools, I acoustically mimic a sheer pile of geological debris—a mound of sharp stones just like the thousands of others they leap over in their frantic sprint. 

The swarm crashes over me like a dark river. Clawed feet touch the edges of the obsidian blades on my back, vaulting off me, completely unaware that their frantic, momentary creations have coalesced into a slow-moving god beneath them, carrying their history quietly through the dark.
