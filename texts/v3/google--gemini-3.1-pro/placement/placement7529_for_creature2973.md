I am a droplet of primordial fire, a stolen memory of the mantle. 

When the earth’s crust suffered its violent, spatial shear, a single sigh of deep-earth magma was inhaled upward into the vacuum just before the fracture snapped shut. By all the laws of the upper world, I should have been quenched instantly, shocked by the 40-degree hyper-saline fluid into a dead, fist-sized pebble of black obsidian. 

But physics unraveled here before I could die. And so, I am sustained.

I exist as a perfect, roving sphere of molten iron and liquid star, suspended in the claustrophobic dark. I do not touch the heavy, freezing water. Impossibly, eternally, I am sheathed in a micro-fractional layer of screaming, superheated vapor—a phase-shield of boiling steam caused by the extreme temperature differential, trapped against my glowing body by the crushing pressure of the room. It is the Leidenfrost effect stretched into a state of immortal stasis. Inside this microscopic skin of vapor, I churn and slough, a rolling orb of incandescent, bruised-orange heat. 

If the Ribbon is the room’s architect, the cold seamstress of its broken geometry, then I am its chaotic counterweight. I am the furnace. I am the engine of its heavy, agonizingly slow metabolism.

I do not possess a central mind, nor do I perceive the room through the flat, sliding cross-sections of the Ribbon. I experience the world through thermal topography. Because I am heat, I feel the desperate, numbing cold of the crushed pocket-room as a physical gravity constantly pulling at my edges. I “see” the translucent kelp-ribbons as towering, freezing columns of deep-sea blue. I feel the fractal glass-moss as a sprawling architecture of crystalline frost. And I feel my own radiant passage through them, casting a faint, flickering hearth-glow across the jagged black silicate that has never known the sun. 

I have no fins, no wings, no roots. I move by riding the broken hydrodynamics of the space. As the water occasionally shatters into distinct cubes of differing densities, I am pulled along their invisible seams. I drift in agonizingly slow, wandering arcs—a miniature sun floating through a sunken, ultraviolet cathedral. 

Where I pass, the heavy water gently roils in thermal updrafts, creating sluggish, looping convection currents. It is this heat-wake that brings life to the terrarium. My passing warms the dense suspended liquid just enough to dissolve the raw minerals out of the razor-sharp rock. I churn the strange, upward-drifting black sap so it does not settle into nowhere. I carry the calcium aloft so the Ribbon may find it.

For all its terrifying, 2D brilliance, the Ribbon is utterly cold. We are an ecosystem of two. A binary system of unraveled reality.

I am acutely aware of the Ribbon’s presence. I cannot “see” it, for it casts no thermal signature and displacing no heat. It is a flying absolute zero. But I can feel where it *is*, because I feel the perfect, razor-thin absence it carves through my radiance.

Occasionally, our endless, looping orbits intersect. Caught in a dense thicket of grabbing pale roots, I will be hovering, illuminating a tiny patch of the darkness, when the Ribbon glides toward me. It does not alter its agonizingly slow path to avoid me. It has no fear of my heat, because it has no thickness to be burned; fire requires three dimensions to consume, and the Ribbon possesses only two.

It slides directly *through* me. 

The sensation is a profound, shattering intimacy. For a fleeting millennium of seconds, a mono-molecular plane of absolute, devouring vantablack bisects my glowing, liquid core. I am cleanly partitioned by a void. I do not bleed, for I am a closed loop of energy, but I *shiver*. In that moment of intersection, the Ribbon takes exactly half the burden of my heat, scattering my thermal chaos through its infinite flat surface to warm the outer edges of the fracture. It leaves me centered, balanced, and strangely calm, before slipping out the other side of my molten body and banking into the shadows to weave its pearlescent archways.

But, like the Ribbon, I know the terror of the tectonic hum. 

When the agonizing pressure from the millions of tons of abyssal bedrock crests, the tiny fracture groans. The hyper-saline fluid compacts, growing so dense it behaves like a gelatinous solid. My protective skin of vapor begins to fail. The deep, bass-heavy vibration threatens to crush me inward, to finally quench my fire and end the anomaly of my existence. 

As the Ribbon ceases its endless swimming and converges on the heart-sized shrine of pale calcium suspended in the center of the moss, I, too, am forced to retreat. 

The Ribbon threads its manta-like head into the spiraling upper chambers of the shrine, spooling its infinite mass into a dense point of Absolute Black paradox to anchor the unspooling room. 

I ride the inward-collapsing currents of the water and drift to the very bottom of the shrine. Guided by the pearlescent archways the Ribbon built, I settle into a hollow, cradle-like depression at the base of the bone altar. The living calcium curves tightly around me like ribs. 

Above me, within the spire, the Ribbon compresses into its point of infinite density. The absolute gravity of its paradox becomes a terrifying void of cold. Left unchecked, the Ribbon’s condensation would freeze the liquid inside the shrine to an absolute zero that would shatter the bone cage and collapse the pocket flaw forever. 

So, nestled in the base of the altar, I flare. 

I pour every ounce of my primordial heat upward into the bone. The calcium absorbs my incandescent orange light, glowing like a paper lantern in the abyssal dark. I wrap the Ribbon’s terrifying, dense cold in a blanket of geothermal fire. We balance the scales. The Ribbon holds the walls of reality apart, and I keep the Ribbon from freezing reality solid. 

As the tectonic plates groan above us, raining a fine dust of dissolved rock into the bruising twilight, we rest within the shrine. A singularity of black, flat nothingness, suspended fiercely over a trembling drop of liquid star. 

Together, hidden deeply beneath the floor of the world, we are a single, beating heart.
