The first thing I know is the thrum. It does not enter me; it originates within me. My sternum is a tuned resonator, vibrating in perfect, autonomic harmony with the tectonic churn of the Deep Basin. If it were ever to stop, I think my blood would cease to flow. 

I hang suspended, inverted, in the mist. 

Two of my hands lock onto the hyper-diamond underside of an arterial transit-spool, the micro-cilia on my broad, splayed palms biting into the cerametal with absolute, unyielding grip. My lower set of arms hangs loose, resting against my thick, calloused abdomen, occasionally reaching up so my secondary fingers can scrape a coagulated layer of machine-oil and biological shed from the ribbed hosing of my filtration collar. The collar pulses—*shhk-weeze, shhk-weeze*—a heavy, wet rhythm at the base of my throat, separating the scalding water from the oxygen and pumping it directly into my lungs. 

Below me, which is also above me in the chaotic geometry of the deep strata, the searing daylight from the orbital mirrors slices through the vapor. I do not blink in the traditional sense. A thick, leathery membrane wipes laterally across my all-black eyes, clearing a film of gelatinous coolant. 

I am a Slip-Stitcher. I live in the friction.

The landscape begins to shudder. It is not a subtle vibration, but a massive, continental intake of breath. The thoroughfare beneath me—packed so densely with my kin that it looks like a single, writhing muscle of dark rubbery skin and tensile webbing—ripples. A transit-dreadnought is approaching. I feel its approach in my jawbone before I see it. The magnetic tension of the city is shifting; the architecture is preparing to digest itself to make way.

I drop. 

I release my upper grip and fall ten meters through the heavy, wet air, twisting my squat, heavy mass. I do not fear the fall. Gravity here is merely a suggestion, warped by the localized siphons. I catch the translucent edge of a reverse-waterfall pouring upward toward the heavy-industry forges. The gelatinous coolant hits my slick skin, instantly absorbing my excess thermal buildup. Riding the upward current, my hyper-jointed legs splay wide, catching the structural ribs of a contracting corridor. 

Another body slams into mine. It is not an attack; it is simply the physics of our existence. We are all crammed into this collapsing space. It is a female, her chest-thumper already vibrating. Instinctively, our heads snap together, our smooth, plated skulls colliding with a heavy *clack*, our flattened plains pressing flush, forehead-to-forehead, jawbone-to-jawbone. 

*"The eastern lotuses are folding!"* her skeletal voice rings directly inside my head, a seismic burst of pure, un-airborne sound. *"Dreadnought displacement! Ride the compression!"*

*"Anchoring!"* I vibrate back into her skull. 

We do not separate. The terror of space, of isolation, is kept at bay by this crushing, beautiful intimacy. Other bodies pack in around us as the corridor’s walls begin to close. Hundreds of us, thousands of us, webbing our limbs together. My upper left hand grips a stranger's pneumatics; my lower right anchors onto the hyper-diamond floor. Someone else’s hands wrap around my thighs. I feel the iridescent, chitinous legs of a scavenger-drone walking across my shoulder, frantically eating the shed skin from my neck in the chaotic heat. 

The transit-dreadnought passes. It is a leviathan of programmable matter, its engines screaming a friction-roar so absolute that it rattles the marrow in my bones. The walls of our thoroughfare compress inward to give it room.

The space we are in shrinks wildly. We are pushed together until there is no air between us, a solid block of rubbery skin, pumping filtration collars, and interwoven, multi-jointed limbs. To an outside observer, it would look like a mass grave, a horrific claustrophobic crush. 

But to me, to us, it is ecstasy. 

The heat of a thousand bodies bleeds into mine. The synchronized, wet gasps of our mechanical collars form a massive, choral lung. We are squeezed against the hot cerametal, polished and perfected by the friction. I feel a dozen jawbones pressed against my skull, my shoulders, my back, vibrating a chaotic symphony of transactional data, curses, prayers, and autonomic hums. I am completely encased, locked in the magnetic biology of the city, held infinitely tight. In this terminal turbulence, in this deafening, bone-shaking pressure cooker, I am a single, vital cell in an immortal organism. 

The dreadnought clears. The walls groan and begin to dilate. The tension releases. 

We unspool from one another, flowing apart like water from a relaxed fist. The stranger I had been pressed against hyper-rotates her hips, scrambles up the sheer vertical face of a newly exposed access-tube, and vanishes into the steam. 

I reach out my four hands, lock my palms onto the slick, weeping metal of the shifting street, wipe my black eyes, and clamber forward into the roar. I am home.
