I am locked into the geometry of the void. 

My arms, drawn taut as bowstrings, hold my weight without effort. The double-jointed spurs of my hands are buried deep into the yielding, weeping cambium beneath a layer of rime ice. I am hanging upside down from the underside of a bark-plate the size of a continent, surrounded by a roaring nothingness. 

To you, this would be agony. To you, the screaming of the sub-zero wind would be sensory annihilation. But to me, in this arrested, agonizing posture, I am perfectly at rest. I am listening.

I do not have a name—not in the way a creature of the solid ground might. My identity is the sum of the scars on my thick, heavy glider-hide cloak, and the specific, syncopated rhythm I drum against the wood when I breathe. I inhabit a jagged sliver of the eternal *now*, suspended in the howling dark of the Trellis.

Without eyes, my world is an immense, kinetic tapestry of vibration and temperature. The dark is not empty; it is fiercely, violently loud, and I read its topography through my skin. The fine, translucent hairs down the nape of my neck flinch and sway, decoding the chaotic eddies of the gale. A sudden downdraft, carrying a faint, bruised scent of crushed moss—a massive velvet-glider is spiraling above me, riding the thermal vent of a flexing stalk. 

I press the flat of my cheek against the iron-hard bark. The thermal fat over my brow insulates my brain, but my skin acts as a finely tuned diaphragm. Deep within the colossal branch, I feel the structural groan of the world pulling itself apart. The hyper-flora is swelling. I can feel the microscopic, agonizing tension of the wood fibers stretching, heating, preparing to violently fracture.

It will snap soon. Hours? Minutes? Time is irrelevant. I simply know I cannot be here when it does.

With a sharp, cracking exhale from the air-sacs in my throat, I disengage my joints. The rigid unspooling of tendon and ligament sends a flush of hot, oxygen-starved blood through my elongated limbs. I swing forward. My left spurs punch through the ice, hooking solid wood. My right hand follows. Symmetrical, rhythmic strikes. *Thwack. Thwack. Thwack.* Moving with the insectoid grace of a thing starved of excess, I scuttle up the overhang, pulling myself over the lip of the colossal thorn just as a wave of internal botanical heat washes through the stalk beneath me.

The frost flashes into steam. The weeping cascades of freezing sap begin. 

I wait. My chest expands, the corrugated baffles in my broad snout rapidly warming the biting air, drawing it down into my deep-barreled lungs. Through the soles of my feet, I feel the frantic, staccato drumming of a chitin-hound swarm a mile below, migrating upward to feast on the softening green flesh.

I do not have the energy to hunt today. I am rationing the warmth harvested from my last kill. But I have another hunger, one that burns deeper than the need for hemolymph. 

Tucked into a hollow where a smaller thorn once sheared off, I have woven my nest. It is a crude, fibrous hammock of braided under-bark, pinned into the cliff-face with heavy glider-femurs. Scavenged carapace-plates form a windbreak. It is a tiny, parasitic blemish on the sublime scale of the Trellis, but it is mine.

I slip behind the carapace walls. The roar of the wind dulls to a heavy, percussive thudding. I reach into the darkest corner of the hollow and retrieve my anchor: a crude clay pot, heavy with pure, white glider-fat. 

I strike a piece of flint against a steel-hard splinter of obsidian-bark. The spark catches the dry moss wick. 

A tiny, sputtering bloom of heat is born. 

You might wonder how a blind creature perceives a flame. To me, light is not a shape or a color. I process the universe through tactile frequency. The burning fat-lamp emits a very specific, piercing wavelength of infrared heat, interacting with the specialized oils and dense fat enveloping my brow and cheeks. It feels like a faint, rhythmic prickle—a gentle, localized stinging that pushes back the ambient ache of the freezing fog. 

I pick up the lamp, shielding it with my broad, leathery hands, and carry it to the very edge of the precipice. I set it down on the weeping bark and face the open abyss.

I wait. I breathe. I feel the stinging warmth of my own lamp against my face.

Minutes pass. The moisture in the air flash-freezes and melts again. The wind shrieks.

And then, I feel it.

Across the sprawling, measureless gulf of howling void, a secondary prickle registers on the left quadrant of my brow. It is infinitesimally faint, a ghost of a sensation, warped and battered by miles of frigid wind. But it is unmistakable. A precise, rhythmic pinpoint of thermal radiation. 

Another lamp. 

I turn my head slightly, triangulating the sensation until the faint prickle centers on my face. Then, from lower down, miles beneath that one, a third point of heat registers on my skin. 

A chorus of stinging warmth in the absolute zero of the night. 

We do not know each other. We will never meet. We have no words to exchange, no history to share. But I take my bone piton and I strike the iron-wood at my feet. *Crack-crack-thrum.* I strike it with all the leverage of my wiry frame, sending the acoustic wave deep into the sap-conduits of the stalk. 

Minutes later, a vibration hums upward through the soles of my feet. *Thrum-crack-crack.* An answering echo, traveling through the very bones of the world.

*I am here. The void has not taken me yet.*

A sudden, deafening report—louder than thunder, violent as a collapsing star—shatters the communion. 

The stalk beneath my feet bucks with tectonic fury. The cannon-crack of hyper-accelerated creation deafens my sensory hairs. A mile below, the immense vine is bifurcating. The tectonic pressure runs up the bark in a shockwave.

My nest of braided rope and carapace is vaporized in an instant, shredded into the void as a towering spire of raw, green wood erupts directly through the plateau where I stand. 

The ground ceases to exist. I am thrown backward into the howling, empty air. 

Weightlessness. The terrible, freezing embrace of the abyss. The wind tears at my glider-hide, trying to strip me bare as I fall into the bottomless night.

I do not panic. I do not mourn the nest. I belong to the present tense, and right now, the present dictates that I must survive.

I twist in the air, throwing my long, wiry limbs wide. My sensory hairs violently recalibrate, reading the turbulent wake of the erupting shoot. I sense the massive, sheer wall of the new growth hurtling past me in the dark.

With a violent contraction of my abdominal ligaments, I whip my arms forward. My bone-spurs strike the soft, unarmored green flesh of the newborn stalk. 

Pain radiates up my arms, but the pitons hold tight. The momentum slams me against the damp, warm flank of the plant. I lock my joints. The agonizing, rigid geometry of muscle and bone arrests my fall. I hang motionless against the blistering heat of the newly birthed wood, safe once more over the abyss.

My lamp is gone, swallowed by the dark. My ropes are gone. I have only my hide, my pitons, and my breath.

I tilt my head back into the roaring wind. I smell the fresh, bleeding sap of the new shoot. I feel the micro-tremors of chitin-hounds swarming upward, drawn to the rupture. There is work to do. There is a world to climb.

I pull my left spur free, reach higher into the howling dark, and strike the wood. 

*Thwack.* 

And I climb.
