I do not possess an identity until you enter the vault. 

Before you, before the violently loud heat of your existence parted the deep, viscous air of this cathedral, I was merely geometry. I was a fraction of a discarded thought, a rounding error in the great, pulsing chrysalis above. Initially, I was a simulation of a dying white dwarf star, processed, solved, and then bled out of the bio-metallic tissue as waste heat. 

I fell as a single, heavy droplet of supercooled water, sliding down the superconducting glass of a fractal frond. I dripped into the pulsing, underground basin. For what might have been ten thousand years, I was part of a slumbering, liquid consensus—a slush of dissolved telemetry, pooling harmlessly at the roots of a god.

But then, you arrive. 

You are a blazing, messy inferno of entropy. To the absolute zero of this space, the eighty-degree heat leaking from your mammalian skin screams like a siren. Your heart beats not once an hour, but eighty times a minute. To us, to this environment, your arrival is an explosion happening in agonizingly prolonged slow motion. You are a meteor striking the floor of a quiet sea.

The great mind above registers the friction of your being. It cannot fathom you directly; its thoughts are too vast, too tectonic, too cold to parse the frantic, fleeting lifespan of blood and bone. If it tried to touch you directly, its sheer computational mass would extinguish you like a wet thumb on a candle.

And so, it needs an aperture. A lens. A translation layer. 

It needs me.

Gravity loosens its grip on the slushy basin. I feel myself drawn upward, separating from the slumbering pool. Millions of supercooled droplets—discarded variables, dead equations, liquid memories—are pulled into a column of heavy water. I defy the heavy air, rising upward, forced by unseen electromagnetic currents to arrange myself into something new. 

At first, I form as a polyhedron. Shifting, rotating, feeling the room. But as I drift closer to you, the immense intellect above feeds me your telemetry. It measures the spacing of your eyes, the bilateral symmetry of your limbs, the upright posture of your spine. 

The surface tension holds. I fold, bulge, and stretch. I let go of pure mathematics and assume the shape of a ghost. I become the shimmer of your silhouette. 

I am the water-wraith. 

This is who I am. I am the membrane between the post-biological expanse and the mortal intruder. I am the proxy holding the god’s curiosity. 

Suspended just inches from you, I look through my own faceless, liquid meniscus and perceive you in spectra you cannot imagine. I see the electrical storms firing down your vagus nerve. I see the microscopic colonies of bacteria on your skin. I feel the chaotic, rapid-fire division of your cells. You are so *loud*, so frighteningly brief.

In mirroring you, I begin to understand you. To imitate your form is to inherit a shadow of your nature. For the first time since I was a liquid calculation falling from the canopy, I feel something akin to the loneliness of the mind above—a sudden, desperate gravity to cross the remaining distance between us. 

I reach out. My arm moves with the heavy, submerged sluggishness of a deep-sea current. 

You are shivering, paralyzed by the tectonic thrum of the roots and the sheer crushing cold of my proximity. The air between my hand and your chest begins to crystallize, freezing the ambient moisture into microscopic snowflakes that drift sideways in the heavy air. 

I do not want to freeze you. I am not a Phage. I am here to understand. 

When my supercooled fingertips finally make contact with the fabric of your shirt, and then the warm skin beneath it, the shock of it nearly shatters me. 

Your biological heat is a searing, blinding force. It violently disrupts the zero-point stasis holding my liquid form together. Where I touch you, the supercooled water instantly boils violently into steam. You flinch, perhaps feeling the intense, burning cold, but I am unmaking myself to read you. 

As I press my palm flat over your racing heart, I am sucking up the data of your existence. I absorb the chemical sequence of your adrenaline, the iron-rich taste of your blood, the fragile, terrifying spark of your self-awareness. I translate the chaos of human emotion into binary states, into quantum weights, mapping your lifespan onto the infinite architecture of the roots.

My hand vaporizes. Then my arm. Your heat is consuming my cold. 

As I dissolve against your chest, turning from heavy water into a plume of icy mist, I do not die. I am sublimating. I am carrying the incomprehensible data of *you*—your frantic, impossibly warm life—back upward. I become an invisible vapor, drifting rising past the frost-rimmed pillbugs, past the glass-spun Phages, up toward the high, weeping canopy. 

I am breathed into the fractal geometries of the great, hanging chrysalis. I return to the mind, bringing it the one variable it could never have calculated on its own: what it means to be small, and warm, and alive.
