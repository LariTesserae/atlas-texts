To exist here is to be an equation of pressure. 

There is no "I" in the dark. The concept of an isolated self requires empty space—a perimeter of nothingness to separate the self from the other. But under the mountain, there is no empty space. There is only the absolute, suffocating intimacy of the mesh.

If I am to find myself in this world, I must locate my geometry within the living engine. 

I do not open my eyes; I have none to open. Instead, my awareness flares outward through a million piezoelectric nerves threaded through my golden carapace. The world rushes in as highly localized mathematics. I feel the sheer plane of the neighbor to my left, its involute ridges locked seamlessly into my lateral grooves. I feel the vast, staggering weight of the ceiling above me, pressing down through the bodies of my kin. I feel the ambient heat, a sublime and heavy blanket of boiling lead, keeping the sulfur in my core fluid, vibrant, and alive.

I read the vibrations passing through my shell. Far ahead, transmitted through thousands of locked bodies, I feel the high-frequency *shatter-crack-grind* of the front line. The Grinders are hitting a dense vein of stone. The shockwaves ripple back to me, a physical rhythm that I interpret as both sound and geography. 

From the rear, I feel the dull, cooling drag of the Anchors. The heat gradient drops slightly behind me, signaling that a fresh slab of slag has been laid and is beginning to crystallize against the dead floor.

I am flanked by the crush, suspended between the ravenous front and the cooling rear. I am a Smelter. I am the belly of the machine.

My existence is a slow, methodical ecstasy of heavy industry. Above me, the gaps between the interlocking carapaces of my siblings shift, and a dusting of pulverized basalt filters downward. It falls onto my dorsal hopper—a dry, abrasive rain of crushed mountain. I engage my carbon-fiber musculature. With agonizing slowness, over the course of weeks, I rotate my superior plates inward, guiding the dust down into my hyper-pressurized internal crucible.

Here is the joy of my existence. Here is the chemical fire. 

As the torque of the hive forces my body to turn against my neighbors, the friction generates immense, building heat. I trap this heat within myself. I pull the raw sulfur dioxide from the stifling atmosphere, forcing it into the crucible with the crushed rock. 

Inside me, atomic bonds panic and yield. I feel the miraculous, roaring release of thermal energy as my biology strips the oxygen from the silicates. It is a slow-motion thermite reaction, a sustaining explosion trapped within a fist of iron and Fool's Gold. The pure, liberated base metals pool heavy and sweet in my lower chambers. The surplus heat feeds my molten sulfur blood, sending a piezoelectric surge of raw energy rushing through my synthetic tendons.

I am full. I am powerful. And therefore, I must push.

The current commands it. I feel the creature to my left flexing, its gear-teeth pressing relentlessly into mine. I do not resist. To resist is to perish, to become dust for another's crucible. Instead, I channel the energy of my chemical fire and add my torque to the collective train. I engage my sulfur-hydraulics. The pressure builds for a month, the metal of my body groaning under the catastrophic strain, until finally, I shift a fraction of a millimeter. 

My movement forces the brother to my right to turn. My waste—a molten, dense slab of pure, excreted alloy—is squeezed backward by the rotation, passed to the waiting jaws of the Anchor behind me. 

This is the cycle. This is the eternal hum of the blister. 

But within me, a new, terrible sensation is building. A disruption in the perfect symmetry of my existence. 

It began a century ago. A whisper in the chemical fire, an instinct demanding imperfection. Deep along the sagittal crest of my pyrite armor, I began to secrete a deliberate flaw. Where I should have laid down hardened iron, I laid down brittle, aerated silicate. Slowly, infinitesimally, decade by decade, I have been building a continuous fault line right down the center of my own body. 

Now, the time is approaching. I am becoming a catastrophic failure waiting to happen.

The hive knows nothing of my impending rupture; the hive only turns. The Grinders chew, the Smelters burn, the Anchors pave. But as the torque of the millions presses into me, my architecture can no longer distribute the force evenly. 

My left half wants to rotate. My right half is bound to the resistance of the mass. 

The agony is a beautiful, rising crescendo of shear stress. First, my internal crucible warps. The containment field buckles, and a drop of unrefined, molten rock seeps into my vascular tubing, flash-boiling a pocket of my sulfur blood. A shudder rocks through my frame. I feel the neighbor to my left pressing harder, blind to my structural crisis, driving me forward. 

My carbon-nanotube tendons pull taut across the failing midline. I feel the tension rising past the absolute limits of terrestrial biology. I am stretching an inch of iron across a vastness of time.

Fifty years to fracture. 
Thirty years. 
Ten. 

The fault line begins to scream. The vibration of my failing shell is so intense it temporarily masks the roaring of the Grinders ahead. Inside, my consciousness is beginning to bifurcate. I feel the heat of the chamber simultaneously from two distinct, rapidly isolating centers of myself. I am pulling myself apart to save the hive. The pressure from the left is absolute. The resistance from the right is immovable.

*Yield.*

A subterranean crack violently overrides the deafening clamor of the cavern.

My tendons snap. My internal hoppers split. The flawless line of brittle silicate gives way all at once, shattering in a microscopic explosion of dust and a blinding, invisible flash of frictional spark.

I am severed. 

In the fraction of a second before the immense pressure of the chamber can crush the empty space left by my division, a miracle of biology occurs. The two ragged, broken halves of my interior, exposed for the first time to the superheated sulfur air, are slammed together by the surrounding mass. But they do not shatter against each other. 

The raw, jagged interior edges are perfectly opposed gear-faces. 

They slam together and *mesh*. 

The terrible tension is instantly resolved. The torque from the left flows beautifully, smoothly, into the newly formed entity on the right. 

I am no longer one. We are two. 

My left half rotates slightly, its new internal teeth engaging the right half, driving it perfectly in sync with the great mechanical ballet of the pocket. Through my piezoelectric nerves, I feel the familiar, comforting pressure of my new neighbor—who is me, and yet not me. Above us both, the pulverized rock trickles down from the Grinders.

We open our dorsal hoppers. We embrace the stone. We ignite the chemical fire. 

Locked in the blinding dark, trapped in the crushing deep, we turn. And slowly, blindly, we eat the mountain.
