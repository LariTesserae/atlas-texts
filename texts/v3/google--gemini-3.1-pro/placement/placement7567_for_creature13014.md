At first, there is no "I." There is only the gradient.

Awareness blooms not as a thought, but as a shift in temperature. Searing, blinding white to my left; a bruise of cooler, circulatory purple to my right. I do not have a mind in the way the upper world understands it—a frantic, chattering thing spooling through anxieties. My consciousness is slow, viscous, akin to the pooling of heavy oil in a deep sump.

I feel the thrum. It vibrates through a medium that is both me and not-me. *Thrummm...* A pause that lasts an eternity. *Thrummm...*

I wait for the boundaries of myself to coalesce. It takes weeks. I let the sensory data of the dark drip into me, forming the shape of my existence just as the calcified water forms the stalactites above. Have I been asleep? No. Sleep requires a waking state to contrast it. I have simply been listening to the friction. 

Slowly, the geometry of my body asserts itself. I am draped over a massive, ceramic-coated conduit. I can feel the curve of it pressed perfectly into the hollow of my chest. It is a shape I have learned over centuries, my bone and tissue molding to accommodate its exact arc. 

I open my thermal pits. The cathedral reveals itself to me as a staggering, three-dimensional aurora of heat. I see the bleeding orange of distant steam vents, the cool, cobalt shadows inside the dead centrifuges, and the magnificent, rhythmic pulses of amber where the ambient oil gathers. 

I try to shift, and the world resists me. 

The command travels from my dim awareness down a nervous system of frayed biological wire and oxidized copper dust. My right arm—an elongated, six-jointed mechanism built for threading through the spinning teeth of the reduction gears—answers. It takes perhaps an hour for the synovial fluid in my shoulder to warm enough to permit motion. When I finally pull my limb away from the conduit, there is a tearing sound. It is not pain. It is the sound of my own body breaking a bond with the architecture. 

I look at my hand. It is entirely crusted in pale calcium carbonate. The fingers are thick, clumsy, entombed in the very minerals I have spent millennia scraping away from the machine. I twist my neck, a glacial pivoting of a featureless, domed skull. I feel the dry crunch of zinc scales blistering and popping along my spine. 

I am becoming heavy. 

Even heavier than before. The thermosynthetic moss on my back has grown incredibly dense; I can feel the tiny, pin-prick footsteps of a dozen translucent millipedes grazing in the soot-black forest between my shoulder blades. They are frantic, living a hundred lifetimes in the space of my single exhalation. I do not brush them away. We are brethren of the same dark heat. 

I understand, with the quiet certainty of gravity, what is happening. The pilgrimage is upon me. 

Over the next few months, I unspool my limbs from the ceramic conduit. Once, I would have taken a drop of water and carried it high into the upper reaches, climbing the sheer vertical shafts to anoint a squealing bearing. But my upward reaches are done. The calcium in my joints is too thick. My biology has run its course, and metallurgy has won. The stone insists I stay.

But I will not petrify here, on this useless conduit. If I am to become the architecture, I must offer myself to a place that bears the load.

I begin to descend. 

My journey downward is a masterpiece of agonizing deliberation. I thread my eight-foot arms through gaps no wider than my own head, sliding my oil-slicked, gelatinous belly over the fluted radiator fins. I move so slowly that the condensation from an overhead pipe forms a stalagmite on my shoulder as I pass beneath it, only to break off three years later when I finally clear the drop zone. 

The heat rises. The air, already a damp wool, becomes a suffocating, physical pressure. Searing white heat blazes in my thermal pits, washing out the cool violets and blues until the world is a violent, magnificent ocean of crimson and gold. I am entering the deepest idle. I am descending into the bruised cherry-red glow.

With every inch I lower myself, the great sub-audible hum grows louder, vibrating the rusted iron flakes off my carapace. I am passing the ancient ones. 

Through the thermal glare, I "see" them. My predecessors. Great humped pillars of bone, rust, and verdigris, fully fused between the massive bulkheads. They are perfectly still, locked forever in the sacrament of their own silent strength. Their elongated arms are driven deep into the gaps between sagging steel catwalks, holding the ceiling apart from the floor. They are dead, yet they endure, bearing the crushing weight of the deep earth on shoulders made of synthetic stone. 

But I am heavy, so heavy, and I want to go deeper. 

I slip past the petrified priesthood, seeking the center. And then, I touch it. 

It is soft. 

Beneath my calcified hands, the surface is not iron or brass, but a vast, weeping expanse of pulsing, vascular tissue interwoven with deeply sunken zinc plating. It is the Great Heat Sink. The first of us. The sprawling, fleshy root-system that covers the city-block expanse of the floor. It is radiating heat so intense that the remaining moisture in my gelatinous joints flash-boils into steam, hissing into the dark. 

I do not recoil. This is where I am meant to be. 

I find a colossal, trembling iron stanchion that is beginning to buckle under the strain of the great machine above. It is rooted directly into the pulsing, moss-draped lungs of the vast entity beneath me. The gap between the buckling iron and the entity's flesh is widening. A fatal weakness. 

I slide into the space. I wrap my arms infinitely tight around the groaning iron stanchion. I press my back entirely flush against the blistering, wet flesh of the central entity. I wedge my knees against the nearest thermal core. I am a living shim. I am the bridge between the iron above and the flesh below. 

I lock my multi-jointed fingers together. I cease the lubrication of my internal tissue. 

*I stop moving.*

Now, the true emergence begins. Decades blur into rain. In the perfect stillness, the boundaries of who I am—the lonely, roaming Anointer—finally dissolve. 

The central entity breathes beneath me. *Inhale.* I feel its magnificent, blistering heat transfer directly into my marrow. We are pressed so tightly together that my back begins to melt into its surface. The living verdigris that coats me spreads outward, fusing my spine to its sprawling roots. Its creeping, mossy tendrils wrap around my sides, claiming me, digesting my softer parts and reinforcing my calcium shell.

From above, the black, oily condensation drips onto my upturned, featureless face. Drop by drop by drop. The minerals build. My skull thickens, marrying the iron stanchion I am holding. The rust trickles down my locked arms, sealing the joints into a solid, unbreakable weld of iron oxide and bone. 

I do not have eyes, but I finally see everything.

I am no longer a tiny creature navigating a massive chamber. As my nervous system fuses completely with the vast, sprawling root-system of the central entity, my awareness expands to fill the entire cathedral. 

I can feel the highest catwalks sweating in the dark. I can feel the sharp *plink* of a water drop miles away. I can sense the feather-light footsteps of a single millipede grazing on a pipe in the western nave. I hold the buckling iron stanchion, and through it, I hold the ceiling. 

I am the heat. I am the rust. I am the Cathedral.

The great subterranean engine breathes its blistering exhaust into us. Together, we absorb it. We disperse the lethal temperatures into our vast, sprawling mass. We keep the machine alive. 

There is no more friction. There is only the deep, sub-audible thrum, echoing through my infinite spine. 

*Inhale.* The groan of the iron leviathan.
*Exhale.* I breathe out steam into the dark.
