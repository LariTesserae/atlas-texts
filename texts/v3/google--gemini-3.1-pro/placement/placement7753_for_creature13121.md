I would not be a Shaper, guiding the new growth, nor a Harvester, plunging into the bitter turquoise deep. If I were born into this bleeding cleft, I would find myself in the middle tiers, suspended in the perpetual mist, living as a **Sounder**. 

In a world where the walls are alive with encroaching stone, and where silence is the only way to conserve warmth, the health of the settlement cannot be seen; it must be felt. 

I press the side of my broad jaw and the flattened palm of my hand against the curving flank of a living wall. My skin is icy, insulated by its thick layer of stark-white fat, but the hardened, pearlescent calluses on my fingertips and cheekbone are acutely sensitive. They are my interfaces with the mineral world. I close my eyes beneath the heavy, mist-beaded folds of my brow. 

In my other hand, gripped tightly in calloused fingers that are permanently hooked from a lifetime of clinging to slick surfaces, I hold my tool. It is not a scraper, but a striker—a dense, spherical node of fossilized cartilage, bound with braided gut to a handle of polished cave-bear rib. 

I strike the wall. *Thump.* 

I do not listen with my ears, though my tortuous nasal passages flare in the freezing air. I listen with my marrow. The vibration travels from the milky silicate of the wall, through my hardened palms, up the heavy, striated muscles of my forearms, and into the dense leaden weight of my skeleton. 

*Thump.* 

The rhythm ripples outward. Through the floor beneath my splayed, gripping toes, I feel the ambient, chaotic heartbeat of our city. The endless *shhh-shk, shhh-shk* of a thousand chert scrapers on wet rock. The low, thrumming messages passing between Shapers adjusting flumes on the upper terraces. The deep, subsonic weight of the weeping aquifer itself, pushing against the charcoal-grey bedrock. But I am filtering all of that out. I am listening to the echo of my own strike.

*There.* A dullness. A heavy, deadened resonance returning to my palm. 

Water has bypassed a slate channel behind this dwelling. It is pooling in a blind void between the overlapping domes, rapidly lithifying in the cold air. A hidden tumor of heavy stone is growing inside the architecture. If left unchecked, the sheer weight of it will crack the foundational roof of the home below, sending three tiers of packed clay, fresh shell, and sleeping families plummeting into the gorge.

I draw a sharp breath, the chilled mist warming in my broad nose, and I begin to communicate. I pivot my center of gravity, gripping a raw lip of stone with my prehensile toes, and strike the wall in a rapid, staccato rhythm. *Strike-strike-pause-strike.* I feel the vibration carry. 

A moment later, an acknowledgment shivers up through the floor. Scrapers are coming. 

While I wait, the shadow in the cleft suddenly deepens, then entirely vanishes. It is the hour of the zenith. 

The vertical slice of sky above us ignites. The harsh blade of the midday sun penetrates the mist. Instantly, an involuntary biological reflex seizes me. The horizontal slits of my pale jade eyes snap shut, reducing the blinding assault of light to a razor-thin, shimmering line. Through this squint, the world transforms. The damp, grey-white gloom erupts into a violent cathedral of light. Every glassy sheet of falling water, every slick, perfectly smoothed dome, every opalescent terrace catches the sun. We are living inside a shattered diamond. 

For this one hour, the scraping stops. The *shhh-shk* ceases. The entire settlement goes still, gripping the wet curves of our homes, blind and basking in the sudden, piercing warmth. We are a hive of pale, wide-faced creatures, wrapped in translucent fish-gut, our milky, overgrown fingernails anchored into the architecture, holding our collective breath. 

When the sun finally slips past the narrow aperture of the bedrock, the gorge plunges back into its shivering, dripping twilight. My pupils dilate, swallowing the gloom, and the scraping resumes. 

The Scrapers arrive. They do not look at me, nor I at them; our interaction is entirely physical, governed by the geometry of the space. I tap the exact center of the dull resonance. Without a sound, they raise their heavy chert flakes and begin to shave away the pearlescent shell of the wall, their thick shoulders working in perfect, rhythmic tandem. Soon, they will breach the hollow, drain the pooling water, and pack the cavity with fresh moss and clay before the Shapers redirect a healing flow of thin water over the scar. 

My job here is done. I lever myself upward, pivoting on a jutting wedge of milky stone, climbing higher into the labyrinth. 

But as I reach for the next handhold, I feel it. 

The stiffness in my knuckles. A deep, aching resistance in the joints of my knees and the base of my spine. I pause, gripping the wet rock, and look at my own hands. 

The pearlescent, silicate-infused calluses on my palms have always been there, a hallmark of our survival. But lately, the iridescence has begun to creep. It is moving past my knuckles, tracing the lines of my veins up my thick wrists. My nails have grown so calcified, so dense with the water's heavy minerals, that it takes immense effort to flex my fingers. 

The water weeps from the bedrock, and we drink it, breathe it, live in it. It forms our homes, and inevitably, it forms us. The profound density of my bones, forged by a lifetime of eating Harvester's moss, is beginning to invite the lithification inside. My metabolism is slowing; the stone is catching up. 

I press my chest against the cold, wet flank of the terrace. I feel the vibrations of the city—the frantic scraping above, the slow, heavy thrum of the water below. And deeper still, beneath the turquoise pools, I feel the silent, foundational weight of the ancestors. The ones who stopped scraping. The ones who sat in the alcoves and let the milky water coat them, becoming the very pillars that hold my current footing suspended above the abyss. 

A low, resonant hum builds in my chest, a rare vocalization that I let vibrate into the stone. I am not afraid. To live in this wound is to know that you are the cure. 

I have a few more seasons of sounding the walls, of finding the dangerous densities in the city's growing shell. But soon, my hands will no longer be able to grip the striker. When that day comes, I will put on my finest, clearest cloak of stitched hides. I will climb down to the deafening, freezing rush of the lower tiers. I will find an empty, shell-like alcove. 

I will sit, and I will let the water take me. I will let the shivering cold pull me into sleep, while above me, the hands of my children press against the walls, drumming a farewell that will echo in my slowly hardening bones. And as the pale, milky stone seals over my eyelids, I will finally stop fighting the earth, and become the harbor that holds my people safe in the dark.
