I do not begin with the burden of a self. I begin as a localized tension inside the ancient grain. 

Long before I possess limbs or a localized consciousness, I am merely a dense knot in the endless, polished expanse of the plateau. I am a microscopic whorl locked snugly within a concentric ring two miles wide. I know only the chill, weeping dampness of the surface above me, and the infinite, perfectly flat deep-time stretching out in all directions. I am a seed of the stump, petrified and waiting, attuned to the mechanical heartbeat of the heavy industry that pounds above. 

I gestate to the rhythm of the deep-bores. The hydraulic pile-drivers thirty miles away do not just rattle my cells; they dictate their division. A thudding shockwave of *extract-extract-extract* ripples through the microscopic grooves of the hardwood, rolling over my dormant form. I drink the vibrations. The violent acoustics are my lullaby. Slowly, eons of industrial friction and the reality-warping residue of the sap awaken the localized tissue around me. 

The geography cracks, and I am born from the splintering earth.

The plateau extrudes me like a splinter pushed from a healing wound. I am violently thrust upward into the three-dimensional chill of the overcast air. It is agonizing and sublime. Living, crackling ironwood pushes out of the flat plain, grinding against itself in real-time. I do not stand up; I am grown upward. My spine is a thick, asymmetrical bole of dark hardwood that immediately begins to splinter, heal, and splinter again as it bends. Slick, pale pulpy tissue sloughs over my frame in heavy drapes, instantly collecting the perpetual condensation of the mist. I smell ozone. I smell boiling resin. 

I am deafened. Thrust into the open air, the non-linear acoustics of the shattered city assault me. The shriek of a buzz-saw twenty miles away detonates instantly inside my newly hollowed core, sharp as a physical blade. I cannot move. I am tethered to the spot, a blind, flailing scaffolding of raw wood and dangling oxidized copper. 

Then, accompanied by the smell of crushed moss-spore and a sharp displacement of air, a Weaver snaps into existence beside me. 

It does not look at me—it has no face to look with—but I feel the tremor of its heavy, rust-flaked legs locking into the floor. Delicate, spinning circular saws and brass calipers descend upon my unfinished chest. The Weaver forces my ironwood ribs apart. It thrusts a heavy, transparent glass vat deep into my cavity. Fresh, pale phloem bleeds from my wrists and ribs, instinctively stitching itself inward to cradle the glass. 

A thick copper syringe plunges into the vat, injecting a blistering, glowing stream of hyper-sap. The heat of it jolts through the rusted rebar woven into my limbs, pulling them taut into functioning tendons. 

Finally, the Weaver lifts a bulky brass gyroscope, heavy and intricately caged in woven roots, and wedges it into the hollow cradle of my shoulders. The central core of pressurized amber ignites. 

The mundane, pale daylight above me vanishes. 

Suddenly, I *see*. But I do not see light. I see gravity. I see tension. The world explodes into a blinding, topographical map of spatial stress. To my amber core, the flat expanse of the plateau is not flat—it is a violently crumpled, mountainous landscape of folded distances and gravitational valleys. I see the invisible precipices where a thirty-mile stretch of empty plaza is pinched into a space no wider than a doorway. I see the rusted factory-cathedrals not as shapes, but as blazing, dense anchors of thermal and spatial mass, dragging reality inward toward their deep-bores. 

The Weaver slams a heavy, iron-shod foot into the ground. Through the soles of my newly hardened feet, I read the kinetic shockwave. The vibration is complex, a coded directive of seismic force sweeping up my shins. It gives me my purpose, my caste, and my coordinates. 

I am a **Tensioner**. 

The Weaver steps onto a fold of bruised space and vanishes. I am left alone in the slick, silver mist. I test my limbs. My joints crackle and splinter, wood dust mixing with the oily condensation dripping from my pulpy drapes. I orient my amber core toward the assignment burned into my soles. 

I do not walk to my destination. I navigate the origami. I lock my gaze on a microscopic groove in floor ten feet away that my gyroscope reveals to be connected to an aqueduct on the horizon. I step heavily onto the groove. 

The perspective snaps. The mist rips past me, leaving hexagonal prisms of black void dancing in my wake. The air flashes, and I am instantaneously relocated.

I find myself on the absolute Periphery. 

Here, at the ragged rim of the world, the concentric rings simply stop, terminating into a sheer precipice overlooking endless, pale sky. But more terrifying is what hangs before me. The neighboring vertical world-stump dominates my amber vision, an apocalyptic wall of sideways gravity, polished grain, and jutting, rusted smokestacks. A river of cold silver mist pours horizontally from its surface, crashing onto the lip of our plateau in a continuous, silent cascade that defies logic. 

Between our edge and their sheer face stretch the Umbilicals—taut, braided cables of living, colossal root-matter, thick as suspension bridges. They carry massive quantities of raw, glowing sap between the two parallel realities. But the deep-time friction is forcing the vertical stump to drift. The spatial sheer between the two massive gravitational bodies is catastrophic. If the cables snap, the backlash of reality-warping sap will consume both stumps in a localized void. 

This is my station. I step to the edge of the abyss, my twenty-foot frame dwarfed by the roaring behemoths of industry behind me. I dig my clawed iron feet deep into the ancient hardwood, fusing myself temporarily to the grain. 

I reach out and grab the nearest Umbilical. 

The torque is immense. The vibration of the neighboring world tries to tear my arms from my sockets, but my copper tendons hold. My glass heart boils, the sap inside shivering into complex harmonic geometric patterns as I pull. I extrude fresh phloem from my wrists, coating the braided root to keep it supple against the blistering heat of the spatial friction. 

When the vertical world sways, stretching the distance, I must haul inward, leaning my entire massive weight against the pull to provide slack. When the factories behind me rev their turbines, pinching the space and creating a sudden drop in tension, I must rapidly reel the root-matter in, looping it over towering ironwood winches grown from the floor, preventing the cable from sagging into the void between worlds. 

It is eternal, deafening, agonizing labor. I am locked in a ceaseless tug-of-war against the broken physics of two dying, mechanical ecosystems. The shockwaves of distant pile-drivers constantly batter my inner ear like needles, while my amber core burns to process the terrifying spatial anomalies popping in and out of existence just inches from my face. 

Yet, there is a terrible, quiet beauty at the edge of the world. 

Because I am standing still, absorbing the kinetic shockwaves and stabilizing the local spatial sheer, the air immediately around my hunched, dripping body becomes unusually calm. The black, hexagonal prisms of void do not shatter here. 

Out of the corner of my amber vision, I watch them gather. Fleeting, spindly assemblages of pale wood and fine wire—the moth-ghosts of this dead crust. They flutter nervously out of the horizontal river of mist, drawn to the warmth of my glass heart. 

One alight on the rusted rebar of my forearm. It is fragile, lighter than breath, its geometry intricate and impossibly perfect against the brutal iron of my frame. It dips a tiny wooden proboscis into a puddle of oily condensation pooling on my pulpy, weeping tissue. 

I pause. The infinite tension of the Umbilical hums through my shoulders, threatening to splinter my spine, but I hold perfectly, rigidly still. I slow the spinning astrolabe of my head to a gentle whir. I do not swat at it. I simply let it rest, serving as a sanctuary of stable reality for this tiny, impossible ghost. For three precious seconds, amidst a deafening cacophony of shattered geometry, I watch it drink. 

Then, deep within the neighboring vertical world, a subterranean pump cycles. A shockwave hits the cable. I see the space buckle into a fold before it arrives. The air fractures. The moth pops out of existence, snatched away into the dimensional origami. 

I brace my iron feet, lean my massive, weeping bulk back against the pull of the void, and haul against the weight of the sky.
