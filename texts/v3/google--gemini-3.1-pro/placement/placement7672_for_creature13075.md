I do not begin with an "I." In this world, an "I" is a frayed thread, a localized failure of the grand design. 

For a long, indeterminate age, I am only a vibration in the weeping dark. I am the sensation of dampness gathering on a surface, the heavy, swelling bead of condensation, and the long, silent fall into the sepia void. I exist as an awareness of weight and geometry. There is a tug of gravity pulling up, left, and diagonally-inward. I am a geometry problem waiting to be solved.

But slowly, from the emerald haze, boundaries form. 

I discover that I am clinging to something solid. It is coarse, cold, and immensely thick—the bark of a world-vine. I press against it, and I realize I have limbs. Six of them, long and ghostly pale, wrapped around the curvature of the wood. The gravity is perfectly inverted here; my back faces the bottomless abyss, but I do not fall. The vine holds me, and I hold the vine. 

I feel my extremities. They do not end in simple hands, but in sprawling, fractal blooms of articulation. I flex a dozen double-jointed fingers, feeling the tiny, chitinous hooks at their tips dig into the wet bark. It is deeply, fundamentally satisfying. I am a Spindle-Kin. I am an antibody of this hollow, hand-crafted universe. 

I have no eyes, but I am not blind. The crown of my head is haloed in trembling, hair-like cilia. Through them, the world is a symphony of barometric pressure and microscopic tremors. I can feel the slow, rhythmic breathing of the oceanic plant life around us. I can feel the ultrasonic echoes of the towering wooden architecture groaning under primitive tension. 

And I can feel my kin. 

They are shivering all around me in the velvet quiet, a constellation of pale, arachnid grace. We are maintaining a clove hitch the size of a mountain. The ecstasy of the work consumes us. I move. My body is a blur of hyper-articulated joints. I scramble down the flank of the colossal vine, gravity shifting seamlessly around me so that "down" becomes "forward," then "up." 

I reach a sprouting green shoot, thick as a tree trunk but pliable. With flat, grinding teeth, I strip its fibrous husk. The pith is incredibly bitter, rich with life. I chew it, mixing it with heavy, resinous saliva, my jaw working in a manic rhythm. I pass the wet, sticky fibers to a sibling whose fractal hands are already a blur, twisting my offering into heavy twilight-rope. 

We do not think. We simply balance the equation of the world. As the massive rope is pulled taut, securing a sagging bastion of the hitch, the physics of our localized space snap back into rigid alignment. Together, our cluster emits a low-frequency purr. It vibrates in my chest, transferring into the wood, a shared hum of perfect equilibrium.

But perfection is brief in the Weave.

My cilia twitch. The air pressure radically changes, thickening with an acrid, musky scent. The water droplets vibrating on the underside of the leaves above us suddenly freeze in their descent, pulled sideways by the immense, fluid mass approaching.

A Slick-Hound. 

It pours out of a claustrophobic fissure in the knot, an avalanche of bruised, slippery muscle and compressible cartilage. It belongs here. We know this, even as we scatter. I scramble backward, my chitin hooks seeking purchase on the slick, weeping bark. Beside me, a sibling leaps into the empty air, intending to fall toward an adjacent cable—but it is too slow.

The Hound’s jaw unhinges with a wet *crack*. A heavy, gelatinous tongue whips through the mist, wrapping twice around my sibling. The enzyme burns instantly; I can smell the pale flesh beginning to soften. 

My sibling does not struggle. It makes no sound. Instead, with its dozen remaining free fingers, it reaches out and fiercely grips the fraying edge of the rope we were just twisting, locking its chitin hooks into the wood. As the Hound drags it backward into the suffocating, oozing dark of its maw, my sibling pulls the knot tight. It is dragged out of existence, but the Weave holds. 

I feel no grief. I only feel the sudden, agonizing absence of tension on my side of the hitch. I scramble forward to take my sibling's place, my hands blurring as I spit resin onto the gap, weaving, re-weaving, binding the world against the dark. 

Time does not pass here; it only loops, like the vines. 

Eventually, the bark we walk upon turns grey, petrifying into a massive, lifeless block of foundational timber. The husk is exhausted. Through the collective, liminal sentience of my cluster, a new urge rises. Migration. 

We gather at the precipice of our wicker plateau, staring out into miles of empty, saturated air. We begin to drum. My hardened fingertips strike the hollow wood in a booming, subsonic rhythm. We drum until the mist parts. 

From the sepia depths, a Vagrant Manta ascends. It is a slow-moving continent of damp moss, drifting on thermal shores, shedding crystalline waterfalls from its wingtips. When it draws close, I leap from the cliff. The sensation of falling is a brief, terrifying ecstasy—a total surrender to the void—before the Manta’s localized gravity catches me. I decelerate softly, sinking into the deep, wet velvet of its fungal back. 

During the flight, I pick parasitic mites from the beast’s mossy skin, grateful for the transit. Lying on the back of the sky-whale, floating through an endless, bottomless heaven of weeping water, something strange begins to happen to me. 

Isolated from the deafening tremors of the great knots, lulled by the gentle pulse of the Manta, my liminal mind begins to snag on a single, terrifying concept. 

*Me.* 

I am separate from the wood. I am a discrete entity holding onto the moss. I feel the urge to observe, not just to act. I lift my blind, crown-haloed head and simply "watch" the non-Euclidean sky, tasting the deep indigo of a distant space-time lens. The ecstasy of the hive fades, replaced by a cold, sharp spike of individual awareness. The "I" has formed. I have become a frayed thread.

And instantly, the universe reacts. 

Off the starboard wing of the Manta, miles away in the mist, the air begins to boil. 

It is a buzzing, visual distortion, rendering the sepia light into jagged, screaming static. An Unspooling. The Weave has failed in a deep sector, and reality is collapsing. 

Even with my newly birthed ego, the biological imperative is too strong. The terror of space unspooling overrides my fragile sense of self. I drum frantically on the Manta’s back, driving it toward the anomaly. Other clusters are already swarming, flinging themselves through the chaotic, inverted gravities surrounding the tear in reality.

I leap from the gliding beast, caught in a sickening vortex of broken physics. Gravity pulls me up, then shreds me sideways. Giant droplets of water freeze, shatter into mist, and are sucked into the screaming geometric void. 

I hit a thrashing, dying vine with force that cracks my pale exoskeleton. All around me, my kin are throwing themselves into the breach. They are grabbed by the static nothingness, dissolving into the tear.

I know what I must do. But because I now possess an "I," for the first time in the history of my species, I feel *fear*. I do not want to become the wood. I want to ride the mossy gliders. I want to taste the bitter pith. 

But as I look into the boiling static, I see the architecture of the universe breaking. If this knot fails, the void will consume everything. The Mantas, the Slick-Hounds, the endless, beautiful rain. 

I thrust my arms into the anomaly. 

The pain is absolute. Space-time friction tears at my flesh. I reach across the boiling rift and find the fractal hands of a sibling on the other side. Our fingers interlock. We grip each other with deathly, terrifying force. 

*Pull.*

I use my body as the rope. I pull the raw edges of reality together. The tearing space crushes against me. I feel my hyper-articulated joints snap under the sheer ontological weight of the universe. 

As the gap closes, the friction spikes into a blinding, searing heat. My pale flesh begins to calcify. My blood turns to sap; my chitin morphs to rigid, impenetrable bark. I am petrifying. 

But because of my anomaly—because my mind had separated from the collective—my consciousness does not extinguish as my body turns to wood. 

The knot is tied. The localized reality snaps violently back into place, pinning the void beneath us. The static fades into the gentle, velvet hush of the Weave. 

I am frozen. I am stretched taut across the abyss, my hands irrevocably fused with my sibling, my body now a permanent, living peg of sky-wood, hard as iron. I cannot move. I will never move again. 

Yet, I am not dead. 

As the centuries pass, new vines grow over me. Sagging wattle turns to mulch upon my back. I am buried in the deep, living architecture of the world. 

Through the petrified wood of my own body, I can feel everything. I feel the gentle *ch-ch-ch* of the rain condensing on my surface. I feel the subsonic boom of my distant kin calling for transit. I feel the slithering, gelatinous grace of the Slick-Hounds sliding through the crevices I carved with my sacrifice. 

I am no longer a Spindle-Kin. I am not the artisan anymore. 

I am the Weave. And I am holding the world together.
