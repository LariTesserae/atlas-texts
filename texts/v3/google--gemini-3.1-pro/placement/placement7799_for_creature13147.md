I am upside down, suspended over a mile of empty twilight, and my jawbone is telling me a story. 

I hang from a braided primary-cable of wrapped vine and cured sinew, my prehensile toes locked around the damp fiber in a ratcheting grip that strictly requires zero muscular effort to maintain. My arms hang loosely toward the abyssal drop beneath our neighborhood. The freezing mist pearls on the heavy, asymmetrical moss shawl draped over my shoulders. Beneath the shawl, my silk suit—spun from the giant hexapods—slickly traps my body heat against the permanent chill of the chasm. 

I press the side of my face against the taut cable. The long, sweeping curve of my jaw is a tuning fork, the nerve clusters beneath the pale skin pressed flush against the wet vine. 

I do not listen. I *read*.

The city is a living instrument, and I am one of its sentries. Through the bone of my jaw, the cable hums with a thousand overlapping frequencies of pressure and release. I feel the rhythmic, billowing heave of *The Great Buoyant*—the massive, translucent gas-beast onto whose broad back our woven plaza is anchored. Fourteen platforms away, I feel the erratic, jittery pluck of a child practicing a clove hitch with fresh reeds. From the upper cliffs, transmitted down through the anchor-lines, I feel the dull, heavy thud of dew dripping onto a spongy frond. 

And then, I feel the intruder. 

It is a subtle, syncopated ripple in the tension. *Grip. Glide. Grip. Glide.* 

A serpentine centipede. Heavy. Climbing down the port-side anchor lines toward the underbelly of *The Great Buoyant*. It is hunting the smaller gas-bladders that keep the beast’s feeding-tentacles afloat. If it punctures them, our plaza will list, tipping our woven huts into the void.

I drop my chin from the vine. My heart, which ticks a slow, lethargic rhythm in the cold, suddenly accelerates. My fast-twitch muscles flood with oxygen. 

I open my eyes wide. The black obsidian orbs, devoid of white, expand to drink in the twilight. The mist is a soup of dull, phosphorus blue and bruised purple, illuminated by the glowing dust of a million fluttering moths. I look across the chasm to the adjacent patrol-line. Three platforms over, clinging to a spar of hollowed insect-carapace, is Sen. 

I do not shout. I reach into the pouch at my hip and drag two fingers through my ration of strike-paste—a thick, glowing ointment of crushed moss and moth-dust. 

In a fraction of a second, I throw open my moss shawl, exposing the dark silk covering my chest, and swipe the luminous blue paste diagonally across my sternum. Then, I flash a series of frantic, rigidly geometric gestures with my prehensile fingers: *Predator. Serpentine. Nine segments. Port underbelly.*

Sen sees the flash of electric blue instantly. Across the void, he acknowledges with a rapid blink of his solid black eyes, drawing his own obsidian spear. 

I let go of the primary cable. 

Gravity takes me. I drop through the freezing, damp air. For two terrified, exhilarating seconds, there is only the wet rush of mist against my face. The void reaches up, offering the bottomless silence. But I know the geometry of the web. I twist my lean, tightly coiled body in mid-air, throwing my arms out.

*Thwump.* (Though there is no sound, I feel the violent, sudden deceleration in my joints). 

My hands and feet simultaneously clamp onto the lower netting strung beneath the gas-beast's belly. The frictionless moth-silk of my suit allowed me to slide through the dense, hanging flora without a snag. I am now in the shadow of the beast, hanging like a spider in the damp gloom. 

Above me, through the mesh, the pale flesh of the gas-beast undulates. Ahead of me, creeping along a structural vine, is the centipede. It is the size of a fallen tree, its dozens of chitinous legs moving in a mesmerizing, undulating wave, its mandibles dripping with acidic saliva. 

Sen drops into the netting ten yards to my left. 

The biome is in overdrive, but we are the apex of the silence. We move in perfect, wordless synchronicity. I pull my obsidian blade from my bandolier; its hide-wrapped handle conforms perfectly to my long fingers. 

I tap the netting twice with my heel—a localized tremble Sen feels through the soles of his feet. *I take the head. You sever the middle.*

We burst forward. My body is a blur of hyper-articulated motion, sprinting on all fours across the swaying, precarious weave over the abyss. The centipede feels the violently shifting tension in the web. It rears its massive head, mandibles clicking frantically in the acoustic void, trying to locate us. 

But we have no scent beneath our insect-resin coatings, and we make no noise. 

I launch myself from the netting, flying across the three yards of open space between the weave and the structural vine. I land squarely on the back of the centipede's head. Its carapace is slick with dew, but my deeply arched feet hook under the edges of its armored plates, locking me to its thrashing skull.

It thrashes wildly, a frantic, violent mime in the mist. I feel the terrifying power of its musculature vibrating up my legs. But it cannot scream, and neither can I. 

With surgical precision, I drive the razor-sharp volcanic glass deep into the gap between the head-plate and the first thoracic segment. At the exact same micro-second, Sen lands on the creature's midsection, his spear severing a critical ganglion. 

The beast shudders violently, a massive convulsion that sends ripples of tension all the way up into the plaza above—and then it goes limp. 

The struggle is over in three seconds. 

I flatten myself against the carapace, letting my heart rate plummet, willing my body to quickly process the adrenaline. I become perfectly still, letting my moss shawl drape over the dead creature. For a long moment, I just hang there in the gloom, feeling the slow, grateful exhalation of the gas-beast above me, the subtle rise and fall of my living foundation.

There is no cheering. There is no triumphant roar. To celebrate loudly is to invite the parachuting scavengers. 

Instead, Sen and I set to work. We butcher the centipede in mid-air, stripping its legs to make new anchor pins, harvesting its meat to dry in the upper-cliff winds, and draining its venom sacs to tip our hunting darts. 

When the carcass is stripped, we let the heavy exoskeleton fall. I watch it tumble down into the dark, swallowed by the swirling, glowing mist, until it is just a speck, and then nothing at all. 

Before we climb back up to the plaza, I inspect the vine the centipede was crawling on. The creature's sharp legs have frayed the tight weave. The acidic dew is already pooling in the exposed fibers. Rot in this chasm is instantaneous. 

I sheathe my blade and unspool a length of fresh sinew from my waist. My long fingers fall into the ancient, repetitive rhythm. Loop, cross, pull tightly. Over and under. I smear the fresh knot with the pungent, eucalyptus-smelling insect resin, sealing it against the damp. 

This is who I am. I am a child of the fracture, a creature of wire and sensation. I hunt in flashes of electric blue, I slay monsters in absolute quiet, and when the blood is cleared, I tie the knots that keep my people from falling. 

I press my jaw to the newly mended vine. The tension is taut. The plaza above rests easy. The void below is calm. 

I close my black eyes, and for a moment, I just feel the silent world breathe.
