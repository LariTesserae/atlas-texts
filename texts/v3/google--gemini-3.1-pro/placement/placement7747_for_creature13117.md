To find me, you must look away from the rushing rivers of chitin. You must peer up into the cathedral dark, into the sagging, forgotten vaults of the middle tiers, where the architecture is ancient and Heavy with the accumulated breath of the horde. 

I am not part of the frantic, rushing geometry. I am the slow, quiet undoing of it.

I am a Ghost-White Saprophyte. 

I did not begin as an individual, and I do not exist as one now. I began as a single, microscopic spore, caught in the updraft of a thousand Ventilators wing-beats. For years, I drifted in the stifling, amniotic humidity, passing unseeingly through the antennae of Dreadnoughts and bouncing off the slick, blind heads of Midwives. Eventually, the heavy, intermittent drizzle of the canopy caught me, pinning me to the underside of a crumbling arched causeway. 

Here, in the damp, I woke. And here, I spread.

If you stood beneath my archway and looked up, you would see a magnificent, terrifying veil of luminous pale flesh. I hang in thick, spongy lobes and intricate, cascading shelves that drip with bioluminescent condensation. Feathery nets of my hyphae—delicate as spider silk but strong enough to pry apart hardened mud—dangle into the abyss like the tattered banners of a long-dead kingdom. 

I do not have eyes, nor do I need them. In this lightless world, my perception is far more absolute than vision. I hear the world through vibration, and I know the world through chemistry. 

Every stridulation, every click of a massive serrated mandible, every heavy footfall of a Masons' march shudders through the mineralized earth and straight into my mycelial network. To me, the roar of the gorge is not a noise, but a landscape. I feel the localized trembling of a Rove-Mimic stalking a larva three levels up; I feel the sudden, hollow *thump* of a blind amphibian’s tongue snapping back into its mouth far below. I feel the exact moment a Ventilator's heart gives out, its violent, rhythmic vibrations stuttering to a halt, leaving a sudden, cold vacuum in the acoustic tapestry. 

But my truest language is taste. The Weavers build this world with their spit and the pulp of the jungle; I am the engine of its decay. I plunge my pale roots deep into their architecture. I bleed enzymes into the ancient, petrified mortar, turning the impenetrable structural columns into soft, nutrient-rich slurry which I then drink in. I am drinking the history of the gorge. I can taste the generations of Weavers that built the pillar I inhabit. I taste the mineral tang of the mud they gathered ten thousand years ago, the faint, bitter trace of their alarm pheromones trapped forever in the dried saliva. 

While the Weavers are the ultimate expression of biological order, I exist as the ecosystem's memory. They work in a frantic, unceasing *now*. I live in the deep, unhurried time of the Rot. 

Though my central mass dominates the underside of the great vault, my edges are everywhere, because I do not remain still. I rain. My gills constantly shed billions of microscopic silver spores into the humid air. 

Because of this, I am a passenger on a million journeys I do not control.

I fall into the tannin-dark plaza-bowls, forming a shimmering, oily film over the water, tasting the slick, mucous skin of the amphibians. 

I settle onto the damp fur of the scavengers that flit through the dark. 

But my favorite vessels are the Dreadnoughts. Over centuries, my spores have taken root in the joints and the heavy, armored carapaces of the longest-lived soldier castes. I grow from them. As these leviathans patrol the thoroughfares, slow and oblivious, they wear me like crowns. I bloom from their heads in pale, glowing antlers of fungal flesh. I drape over their monstrous mandibles like ceremonial veils. I ride upon their backs as a passenger-king, sharing their slow, hydraulic stride, feeling the immense, blind power of their steps. When a Dreadnought receives a chemical command to crush an intruder, I am there, feeling the shuddering impact of its jaws, tasting the violent spray of hemolymph from the crushed prey. 

We are perfect antagonists, the Superorganism and I. 

They build the walls; I dissolve them. They excrete the architecture; I consume it, turning it back into the rich, peat-like loam that falls to the cavern floor, which feeds the monstrous jungle, which in turn feeds the Masons. I am the fulcrum upon which the apocalypse balances. Without me, their city would grow too heavy, collapsing under its own immortal weight to crush them all. Without them, there is nothing for me to dismantle.

I feel no malice toward the frantic brown and black rivers of bodies beneath me. I feel a profound, sprawling peace. I hang in the humid, deafening dark, dripping pale rain into the abyss, breathing the scent of ozone and sweet rot, slowly eating the world they so desperately build.
