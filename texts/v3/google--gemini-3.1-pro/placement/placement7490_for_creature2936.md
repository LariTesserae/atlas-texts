It begins not with an image, but with a sudden, localized density. Before I possess a body, I am only an awareness of the cold, heavy rush of glacial water pressing against something that resists. 

Slowly, the resistance takes a shape. A drumbeat vibrating in the teeth. A sprawling, multidirectional roar of life that resolves into a map. 

I feel the water wrap around the thick, unyielding column of my neck. I feel the ridges along my jawline humming like struck tuning forks, singing with the exact trajectory of a shadow passing eighty yards above—a massive, flat-bodied ray, displacing tons of water with a single, muscular undulation. The pressure wave washes down through the pure blue, sliding over the smooth, fat-insulated curve of my shoulders, cascading down the sensitive flesh of my spine in a rush of tactile data. 

I am here. I am heavy. I am tethered.

My eyes are open, instinctively, though beneath their milky membranes they register only the shift from the sharp, blinding shafts of upper-caldera sunlight to the bruised, encompassing teal where the deep stalks anchor. I do not need to look to know where I am. I feel the geography of my world through the taut, braided algae vine gripped tightly in my right hand. 

I let my identity condense into this hand. It is a brutal, beautiful thing. The thick webbing flares slightly as the current tries to drag me backward. The skin of my palm is entirely numb, armored in thick plates of callus and permanently dyed the pale, weeping green of the master-stalks. The spade-like nails grip the weave. If I let go, my dense, heavy bones will pull me effortlessly, silently into the ultimate dark at the bottom of the bowl. 

But I do not let go. I am a Tender of the Lower Knots. My name—a deep, resonant triplet rhythm that starts in the lowest cavity of my chest and ends with a sharp snap of the ribs—is known to the rigging.

I hang suspended in the vast, open gulf between two towering stalks, a speck of mottled blue-gray and cyan against a cathedral of endless water. The noise is magnificent. It is a deafening, crushing symphony of mechanical grinds, organic moans, and the white-noise hiss of schooling fish. It does not overwhelm me; it holds me. It is the blanket under which I exist. Every clattering bone-chime and grinding counterweight tells me that the city is secure, that the tension holds.

I begin to move. It is an act of supreme, deliberate exertion. I reach forward with my left hand, sweeping it through the heavy resistance of the water to grasp the vine ahead. I pull, and my massive body glides forward a few inches. *Pull. Glide.* It is the slow-motion ballet of a sinking stone refusing to sink. 

I am traveling toward a dwelling that does not yet exist. 

Decades ago, before my thick skin bore the scars of chipped obsidian, I visited the side of a massive, pale-green stalk soaring out of the abyss. I found a tiny, swollen node. Over twenty years, I returned to it, scraping the bark with bone tools, bleeding the heavy sap, tying small, agonizingly tight tourniquets of algae to direct its bulge outward, upward, and hollow. Now, I am entering the twilight of my endurance, and the pod is finally large enough. It is the size of a grand hall, a perfect, taut sphere clinging to the mother-stalk.

I arrive at its fibrous threshold and pull myself inside. The interior is flooded, cool, and dim. The walls are ribbed and living, pulsing subtly with the heartbeat of the plant. This is the chamber I am coaxing for a child who is just beginning to pull themselves along the upper sun-lines, miles above me. I will be gone when they finally untie their first stone anchor and claim this hollow, but my hands will be felt in every smooth curve of the wood.

I lash myself to a root-hook near the center of the room, freeing both hands. From my hip, I unbind a blade of deep back glass. Slowly, rhythmically, I begin to scrape away the inner pulp, smoothing the wall. It takes an hour to shave away a handful of fiber. The slurry drifts into the water, catching in the gills beneath my ribs, tasting sharply of raw sugar and bitter earth. I fall into the deep, meditative trance of the work.

Then, I feel it. 

A vibration through the floor of the pod. I lay my free hand flat against the interior wall, close my breathing slits, and listen with the hydrophones on my spine and the calluses of my palm. 

Someone is speaking to me from the upper canopy. 

The vibration is a tactile language, traveling down the massive stalk, entering the fiber of my pod, and translating directly into the muscles of my forearm. *Pluck. Long slide. Double snap.*

It is my grandchild. The vibration carries the specific, trembling timbre of their throat, distorted over miles of plant tissue but undeniably theirs. They are telling me they have successfully hollowed their first seedpod. They are proud. 

A deep warmth blooms in the fat of my chest. I press my jaw entirely against the damp, living wood of the wall. I constrict my throat, letting out a localized, subsonic hum that rattles my own teeth, while simultaneously drumming my blunt fingers against the bark. *Slide. Slide. Heavy tap.* 

*I am listening. I am anchoring. The tension holds.*

I feel their brief, happy vibration in return, and then the line goes quiet, returning to the ambient, roaring hum of the city. We are separated by a gulf of water that would take a full day to cross, yet I have just held them in the center of my hand. 

I return to my scraping. Another hour passes. Or perhaps two. Time here is measured only by the wear of the stone and the slow turn of the current. 

But deeply buried within my mammalian core, an ancient clock strikes. My movements slow. A discomfort begins to gnaw at the edges of the perfect blue—a heaviness inside my massive lungs that has nothing to do with the water. The internal ballast is turning stale. The somatic urge demands obedience.

I sheathe my obsidian. I pull myself from the dwelling and move along the vines until I reach the edge of a gas-pod farm. Dozens of swollen, bloated bladders tethered by thin reeds strain upward toward the unseen surface. I select one, cut its mooring, and instantly wrap my massive arms, my legs, my heavy fluked feet around it. 

I surrender into the lift.

The ascent is a violent shifting of reality. For days, I have lived under the rule of gravity and tension; now, I am entirely at the mercy of buoyancy. The massive, moaning stalks blur past me. The teal gives way to vivid, emerald green as the shaft of pure sunlight widens. The noise of the city changes pitch, the deep bass notes falling away, replaced by the chaotic hiss of the uppermost layers. My milky eyes wince behind their membranes.

Then—the surface approaches. The boundary. The terrifying ceiling of our world.

*Crash.*

I break the water. 

Instantly, my world evaporates. The deafening, comforting, tactile map of the caldera vanishes, replaced by a horrifying, infinite void. The thin, freezing, high-altitude wind bites against my wet skin. I can see the sharp, jagged ring of the dry mountain peaks, utterly dead and frozen in their stillness. My ridged hydrophones detect nothing but the pathetic, chaotic rustle of air—a thin, hollow sound that tells me absolutely nothing about my surroundings. I am blind, deaf, and weightless. 

Terror grips me, but I obey the ritual. I part my thick, tough lips. My chest heaves. I expel the stale, compressed air from my massive lungs with a violent, wet roar that echoes off the stone bowl of the mountain.

Then, I gasp. I swallow the empty sky. The freezing, razor-sharp mountain air floods my internal chambers. It tastes of dust, of frost, of nothingness. I take another gulp. I pack it down tight beneath my ribs, sealing the ballast. 

Thirty seconds in the alien, silent terror of the Above is all I can endure. I release the gas-pod. It bobs away on the ripples. 

My dense bones reclaim me. The heavy, merciful gravity takes hold. I sink.

The water crashes back over my shoulders, silencing the terrible wind. The blinding light diffuses into soft, aquatic shafts. The roar of the filter-feeders, the clatter of the bone-chimes, the grinding of the anchors all wash back over my spine. The map blooms in my mind. I am held. I am home. 

I let myself drop, plummeting down through the crisp, cool blue, a heavy stone returning to its place in the great foundation, ready to catch the line, ready to pick up the tension once again.
