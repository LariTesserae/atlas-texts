I do not begin as flesh. I begin as a lingering consequence. 

When the ancient Chorister steps into the invisible threshold—when time accelerates, boiling its centuries down into three frantic seconds of evaporating cartilage and amber dust—it releases a final, crystalline note. 

The ten thousand voices of the city immediately echo that note back, weaving it into the colossal, droning architecture of their reality. But in the violent acoustic tide of the stadium-sized cavern, amidst the shrieking gliders and the bass-heavy thrum of murmuration, a secondary harmonic is born. A resonance. A shadow-frequency that bounces off the calcified, wood-bone terraces and refuses to dissipate.

I am that shadow-frequency. 

I exist, for an indeterminate measure of time, as pure kinetic memory. I am a bodiless vibration skimming along the waxy, jade-green leaves of the urban facades. I feel the ambient, heavy sweat of the cavern not as wetness, but as a medium of travel. Every time the deafening roar of the cathedral swells, it pushes me. I ricochet through a time-dilation pocket, and my waveform stretches, deepening into a low, tectonic hum. I slide through a non-Euclidean loop in an interlocking vine bridge, wrapping back around myself, doubling my own frequency, layering note upon note upon note until I become heavy. 

I realize I am developing gravity. 

I sink through the humid, bruised air, pulled toward the great, empty hollow of the center void. As I fall, I intersect a frozen droplet of condensation held in a cymatic snare. The droplet begins to fall upward, and I ride it. 

Here, in the upward-falling rain, the transition from sound to substance begins. 

As my frequency vibrates through the droplet, it begins to polarize the chaotic detritus of the air. The iridescent indigo and copper scales shed by the swarming, murmuration insects are drawn into the water's surface tension. The brilliant dust of the dead ancestor—the very dust from which my originating note was birthed—clings to the droplet. I am spinning, gathering mass, accreting the discarded biology of the cavern.

I strike the underside of the living canopy roof with a wet, heavy thud. 

The vibration that I was is now trapped inside a husk of biological matter. I rest in the damp, dark recesses of the high rafters, where the golden shafts of daylight cannot reach. I wait, gestating in the continuous, rattling bass of the Choristers' singing below. Their song commands growth. It whispers to the ancient wood to curl and hollow. And because I am attached to the wood, because I am made of acoustic silt and trapped resonance, their song forces me to grow, too. 

I unfurl from the canopy ceiling. 

I do not possess the tightly strung, tympanic perfection of a Chorister. I am not an architect. If they are the roar of creation, tuning reality through sheer, deafening volume, I am the acoustic shock absorber. I am the silence that does not exist here—translated into flesh. 

My body is long, segmented, and flat, resembling a massive, pale trilobite or a colossal centipede, roughly the length of a fallen tree. My carapace is not chitin, but tightly layered, waxy jade leaves, glued together by the amber sap of the ancient burls. Running down the center of my long back is an exposed, hollow ridge. Protruding from this ridge are hundreds of fragile, calcified splinters of wood-bone, arranged like the tines of a music box or the bristles of a comb. They are tuning forks.

I drop from the canopy roof. 

I do not plummet. Just as the Choristers negotiate with space, I feel the topography of the broken physics. I splay my dozens of jointed, root-like legs, catching an invisible updraft of reversed gravity. I drift downward, a massive, sluggish kite of leaves and bone, slowly spiraling through the frantic void.

A six-limbed, crimson-plumed glider streaks past me, snapping its jaws at an insect swarm. The displacement of air rattles the tuning forks along my back. I absorb the kinetic energy of the glider's shriek, converting the harsh sound into a soft, bioluminescent glow that pulses faintly between my segmented plates. 

I land heavily on the lowest tier of the city, where the roots have turned to pale, smooth fossil. The cacophony down here is immense. The air pressure is a physical weight, pressing against my waxy shell. 

I crawl along the curvature of the pit, guided by the vibrations in the wood. I am blind, but I do not "see" the entire cavern through sonar as the Choristers do. I only feel the *stress*. I am drawn to the microscopic fractures in the roar.

Ahead of me, a deep fissure in the reality of the cavern is threatening to tear. Five Choristers have been singing a desperate chord to stabilize an impossible, looping staircase that connects two fungal terraces, but the localized gravity is stuttering violently. The staircase flickers, pixelating into red geometry. If it shatters, the terrace will fall, crashing into the void and throwing the meticulous ecosystem into disarray. The Choristers' chest pipes are flushed purple with exertion, their tympanic membranes blurring to the point of tearing. 

They are pumping pure volume at the spatial glitch, trying to brute-force it into solidity, but their frequencies are bouncing off the jagged edge of the time-dilation pocket hovering nearby. It is creating a feedback loop—a screeching, localized dissonance that threatens to fracture the pale timber supporting the entire block. 

I move toward the dissonance. 

My many legs find purchase on the splintering wood. I crawl directly past the straining Choristers. They have no eyes, but they register my presence as an acoustic void, a dense sponge moving through their sonic field. They do not pause their defensive choir; they simply shift to allow me to pass. 

I stretch my long, multi-segmented body directly over the flickering, glitched seam in the staircase. I anchor my front legs in the Euclidean geometry above, and my rear legs in the non-Euclidean loop below. 

Then, I open the ridge along my back. 

The hundreds of wood-bone tuning forks spring upward, exposing the raw, echoing hollow of my core. The screeching dissonance of the reality-tear hits me. It is agonizing. It vibrates through my marrow, threatening to shake my waxy carapace apart. But this is what I was assembled for. 

I do not sing back. I *inhale* the sound. 

The tuning forks along my spine catch the jagged frequencies of the glitch. They vibrate wildly, taking on the destructive energy of the broken physics. Inside my hollow body, that violent acoustic pressure is dampened, broken down, and converted. The bioluminescence beneath my plates flares brilliant gold. From the joints of my many legs, I begin to secrete a thick, heavy resin—a physical manifestation of the neutralized sound. 

The resin flows into the flickering red geometry. It fills the jagged, malfunctioning seams of the staircase with a warm, amber glue. 

The screeching feedback loop dies in my throat. I hold the broken halves of space together until the resin hardens, binding the impossible angles into a permanent, stable paradox. The staircase settles. The pixelation fades. 

The five Choristers ease their song from a frantic scream to a low, satisfied drone of maintenance. 

I close my back-ridge. The tuning forks fold away, hot and humming violently beneath my armor. I am heavy now, bloated with the absorbed dissonance of a torn reality. I dislodge myself from the repaired staircase and crawl slowly to the edge of the fungal terrace. 

One of the giant, featureless Choristers stands beside me. It reaches out a hand wrapped in taut, translucent membranes and gently rests it on my waxy carapace. It feels the heat of the bad noise trapped safely inside me. It feels the memory of the dead elder whose dust formed the anchor of my birth. Through its touch, it understands that I am the repository of their errors, the sinkhole for the chaotic notes that their roaring city cannot sustain. 

The Chorister’s chest valves pulse, emitting a single, low, deeply resonant purr. A note of gratitude. 

I do not answer. I cannot. Instead, I tip my heavy body off the edge of the terrace. I let myself fall back into the void, tumbling through the golden shafts of daylight, waiting to catch a thermal updraft or an inverted raindrop, listening patiently for the next fracture in the song.
