To understand what moves through this world, you must first unlearn the habit of looking at the architecture. The clay is merely a symptom. The continent of terracotta, the sprawling aqueducts, the impossible ziggurats—they are not the entities, nor are they the homes of the entities. They are the cast-off husks, the desperate, instantaneous scabs formed by a universe trying to heal itself. 

To see what lives here, you must stare into the sweltering, wet shadows, into the dense fog, and watch not for motion, but for *absence*. 

At first, you might mistake them for tricks of the heavy mist. A sudden, sharp parting in the vapor, a place where the dripping condensation abruptly ceases to fall, sheared off in mid-air by an invisible boundary. If you hold your breath and strip away the ambient roar of hissing steam and squelching mud, you will feel a sudden, violent drop in air pressure. The hot velvet atmosphere pulls away from you, sucked toward a localized vacuum. 

There is something wandering the plains. It is a creature made entirely of empty space.

It has no flesh, no cells, no biology to speak of. It is a severe, highly complex hole in reality, a wandering pocket of hungry, geometric void. As it drags itself across the limitless brick foundation, it displaces the moisture and the heat, creating a perfect, razor-sharp silhouette of nothingness. 

And reality panics. Nature abhors a vacuum, but the physics of this place absolutely loathe it. The moment this creeping emptiness moves, the ground violently reacts to fill the void. Wet slip extrudes itself upward with explosive force, desperately packing itself against the boundaries of the absent creature. 

This is what you are watching when a spiraling column of mud suddenly rises from the flat ground. You are watching the raw clay throwing itself against a colossal, invisible anatomy, desperately trying to plug a moving wound in space. 

As the entity continues to move, it leaves the clay behind. The sheer, radiating heat of the entity's friction bakes the mud into rigid terracotta in seconds. The creature slips free, moving ever onward into the dimensions of the fog, and leaves behind an empty, sun-baked shell—an archway, a viaduct, a megalith. The chaotic storm of masonry is merely the trail of its passing.

But as you stand close to a newly formed dolmen, squinting through the steam, the shape of the creature begins to make a terrible kind of sense. It becomes *more itself* the longer you look at the indentations left behind on the bricks.

The edges of this wandering absence are not smooth; they are textured with the excruciatingly precise contours of hominid hands. Thousands upon thousands of them. The creature’s shape is a fractal bloom of invisible thumbs, sweeping palms, flexing knuckles, and pinching fingertips, all scaled to massive, varying degrees. It does not look like a biological entity; it looks like the pure, unadulterated *instinct of craft*, amputated from a conscious mind and let loose to roam. 

It is an organism woven out of the kinetic memory of a potter’s hands. 

You trace the invisible anatomy as it lumbers past. It has no singular torso, no legs, no face. It moves by gripping the air, folding higher dimensions into tight pleats, and pulling itself forward along invisible seams in the atmosphere. The "hands" at the front of the void-mass reach, flatten, and press into the earth. The clay immediately surges up to meet them, parting into millions of individual bricks beneath the localized pressure of the phantom thumbs, mapping the infinite creases of the creature's grip. 

When it moves calmly, the architecture it spawns is orderly. It traces long, elegant aqueducts through the mist—the smooth, sweeping trail of a master builder dragging a continuous, sweeping trowel of nothingness through the space. 

But these creatures are rarely calm. They live in a state of deeply confused agony. 

They are ideas of creation that possess no vision. They have the supreme, god-like urge to mold, to press, to lift—but they have no eyes to see what they are building, and no solid bodies with which to stay and inhabit their work. They are trapped in a frantic cycle of touching a world they cannot hold. Because the moment they clasp the clay, the clay hardens to blindingly hot ceramic, forcing them to let go. They are perpetually burning their phantom fingers on the reality they just conceived. 

Their distress is what gives the landscape its violent, chaotic topology. When a creature panics, the spatial void spasms. The invisible hands writhe, clawing furiously at the air, sinking into dimensions where gravity holds no jurisdiction. The mud rushes up to pack against this flailing geometry, spawning staircases that corkscrew into nothing, arches that fold out of arches like a blooming flower, and mile-high ziggurats—the calcified timestamp of an entity thrashing against the sky. 

And then there is the hydrology. Over time, you notice a deeply symbiotic relationship between these creatures of absence and the boiling, gravity-defying waterways. 

The entities run hot. The friction of their hyper-dimensional movement creates the sweltering ambient heat of the universe. To soothe the searing burn of their own endless work, they drink. 

They trail long, invisible appendages down to the bedrock, tracing shallow canals with phantom fingertips. When they find the raw, mineral-rich aquifers buried deep beneath the conceptual plane, they pull the water upward. The boiling water defies physics because it is being swallowed. It flows uphill, racing through the twisting, woven aqueducts, siphoned directly into the negative-space veins of the creatures. For a few brief, silent moments, the water fills the void, giving the entity the closest thing to a physical body it will ever know—a sprawling, floating anatomy made entirely of boiling, bubbling glass-clear water, suspended in the heavy mist. 

But it cannot last. The sheer thermal intensity of the entity flashes the water into hissing steam almost instantly. The creature exhales, releasing those long, weeping exhalations of fog, resetting the thick, dripping ceiling of the world. 

There is a tragic, breathtaking lifecycle playing out across these infinite troweled plains. 

Far out on the horizon, you begin to recognize their birth and their death. To the east, the colossal step-pyramid building itself upside-down—hanging from a point of nothingness in the sky—is a young entity. It is an infant void, falling out of whatever higher plane birthed it, grasping desperately at the fog to slow its descent, pulling the earth violently upward to catch it. 

To the west, a thousand miles of primitive stone wall coiling tightly into a knot is an elder creature. It has wandered this sweltering darkness for millennia, leaving millions of miles of empty ruins in its wake. Utterly exhausted by the endless, mindless labor of its own existence, it decides to succumb. It stops moving. It wraps its countless phantom arms tightly around itself in an intricate, fetal knot. 

The clay rushes in, packing the curled void one last time. But because the entity ceases to struggle, the friction dies. The heat fades. For a prolonged, deafeningly quiet minute, the creature achieves stillness. The walls of reality fold entirely around it, crushing its empty space into a pinpoint, until the entity finally phases downward, vanishing completely from this plane of existence. The geometry loses its underlying architecture, melting instantly. What is left behind is merely a slick puddle of steaming, workable mortar.

This is the ecology of the clay universe. A solitary, weeping world without life, inhabited solely by the wandering ghosts of an absent creator's hands, doomed to forever build an empty metropolis to hide from a reality that cannot stop trying to touch them.
