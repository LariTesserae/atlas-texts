To live in a place of such profound, suffocating saturation and relentless mechanical inertia, one must be born not of flesh and bone, but of the environment’s own strange trinity: damp, pressure, and static. 

The inhabitants of the atmospheric mill-town do not have a name for themselves, for they have no voices with which to speak it. The thin air and the sound-swallowing moss rendered vocal cords obsolete eons ago. Instead, if you stand perfectly still on one of the overlapping steel-grate walkways and let the fast-moving fog wash over you, you might finally notice them. They are easily mistaken for machinery at first—tall, impossibly slender figures moving with the sluggish, unstoppable momentum of the brass flywheels they tend.

They call them "the Weaver-Tenders" only in the theoretical ledgers of whatever unseen world lies far below, but up here, they are simply part of the engine. 

To survive the perpetual, biting chill, they are swathed in the environment's mandatory uniform: thick, heavy coats of black, oiled rubber and dense layers of felted wool. The coats drape elegantly over their elongated frames, reaching down to their ankles, slick with condensation. But beneath the rubber and wool, their bodies are a testament to extreme adaptation. They are neither wholly biological nor entirely mechanical, existing in the exact liminal space as the moss that eats electricity. 

Their limbs are spindle-thin, engineered to navigate the cramped, labyrinthine alleys and scale the sheer drops between overlapping tiers. They do not have feet as we understand them; their legs terminate in wide, multi-jointed pads covered in a micro-textured biological velvet, allowing them to walk across rusted catwalks, wet copper, and sheer basalt flutes with absolute, noiseless adhesion. You never hear their footsteps. You only hear the gentle rustle of their rubber coats, a sound that perfectly mimics the sighing of pneumatic valves.

If a Tender turns its face toward you, the alien nature of their existence becomes undeniable. They have no eyes. In a world of slate-violet twilight and pervasive, blinding fog, optical organs are a liability. Instead, beneath the wide brim of their rubberized cowls, their faces are smooth and deeply contoured, resembling the fluted dripping wax of the basalt pillars they live against. Down the center of their faces and wrapping around their long necks are delicate arrays of feathery, copper-hued tendrils—much like the antennae of the glider-moths. 

These tendrils are electro-receptive and hyper-sensitive to barometric shifts. They do not *see* the town; they *feel* it. As a Tender walks, it navigates by the massive, slow-moving electromagnetic fields of the industrial condensers. They perceive the spatial reality around them as a glowing topography of galvanic currents, pressure gradients, and thermal venting. A cold iron railing registers as a dense, dark shadow in their mind; a spinning dynamo blazes like an eclipsing sun.

They are the caretakers of the breath of the world. Throughout the endless twilight, they move in constant, ceaseless labor, mirroring the rhythmic, underwater knocking of the giant pistons. You will see them scaling a vertical pipe thick as a tree trunk, moving with a deliberate, spider-like grace. One might be clearing biological debris from a mesh-rimmed aerofoil, its long hands—boasting three elongated fingers and two opposing thumbs—gently peeling away overzealous liverworts to maintain the precise balance of the fan blades. Another might be suspended over a vertiginous canyon by a tether woven from air-fern and copper wire, using a brass wrench the size of an anvil to tighten a pressure seal. 

They do not tire. Their internal biology is a high-pressure, exothermic engine. They run incredibly hot to fend off the mountain cold, their core temperatures so high that any mist coming into contact with their bare skin instantly vaporizes with a soft *hiss*. When they work hard, you can see plumes of steam rising from the collars of their heavy coats, adding their own exhalations to the stratospheric rivers.

Because they are mute, their society exists in the electric silence. The aggressive, unnatural lushness of the cloud-moss acts as their communication medium, a vast, biological telegraph wire. When a Tender needs to convey an idea—a drop in pressure in the lower sector, a jammed flywheel on the eastern expanse, or a simple expression of contentment—it kneels and places its bare, padded palms against a thick mat of the pale blue moss. It channels its own internal static charge outward. The pulsing webs of blue ozone-fire leap from its hands into the flora, rippling outward through the alleys, racing up the corrugated hab-blocks, and traveling across the mist-filled gulfs at the speed of thought. 

From across the chasm, another Tender will place a hand on a connected patch of moss, receiving the crackling, bioluminescent message. Entire silent symphonies of data, emotion, and mechanical schematics flash across the cliff faces in brilliant sapphire spiderwebs, making the settlement look like a single, colossal brain firing its synapses.

Perhaps the most haunting aspect of their lives is how they rest. In the deepest, tightest crevices of the town, where the brass pressure vessels overlap to create damp, hollow caverns, the Tenders sleep. But they do not lie down. They hang. 

Using their incredibly strong grips, they suspend themselves upside down from the underside of the warmest exhaust manifolds, wrapping their thick rubber coats tightly around themselves like chrysalises. As they enter a state of deep torpor, their body temperatures drop, and the altered gravity of the localized electromagnetic fields takes over. The anti-gravity condensation gathers around them, drifting upward to shroud their sleeping forms in a floating, localized suspension of mist. In this state, they plug their facial tendrils directly into the moss mats, existing in a shared, electric dreamscape, harmonized perfectly with the low, chest-vibrating thrum of the great engines.

Occasionally, a translucent, raven-sized glider-moth will flutter into these roosts. Recognizing a kindred creature of the sky and static, the moth will land on the hanging cocoon of a sleeping Tender. It will nestle against the warm, rubberized fabric, safely sheltered from the violent sweeping fog, its delicate wings shivering in time with the deep, slow heartbeat of the city itself.
