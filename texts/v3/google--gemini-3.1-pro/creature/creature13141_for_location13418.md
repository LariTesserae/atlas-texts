To understand what moves through this basin, you must first forget the concept of flesh. In a place devoid of animals, where the city itself is a blind, swelling biology, the inhabitants are not born; they are *deduced*. 

When the local physics violently reject a new spire, collapsing the extruded cathedrals into a dull, wet slurry, the destruction is rarely absolute. Sometimes, nestled within the tangled slurry of soft calcium and warped dimension, an impossible angle survives. A knot of extra-dimensional geometry refuses to untie itself. 

From these surviving errors, the dwellers emerge. 

At rest, they look like isolated fragments of the city's architecture that have simply detached from the bedrock. They are towering, asymmetrical forms, often fifteen or twenty feet high, composed entirely of the same materials as the metropolis around them—weeping dark resins, frictionless sheets of nacre, and porous, sponge-like cartilage. They have no faces, no limbs in any traditional sense, and no discernible organs. Instead, they are recursively folded shapes, resembling ambulatory buttresses or the looping, hollow interiors of mobius shells. 

They do not walk, for they have no muscles to contract. Movement for them is a quiet, continuous negotiation with the broken math of the valley. 

Because gravity and dimension are in a constant state of decay, these entities ride the ontological weather. To cross the miles of ankle-deep, sweltering water, an entity will lean its towering, heavy mass forward into a space where gravity has stalled. As it falls, it initiates a localized glitch of its own. The smooth nacre of its body sharply stutters. In the span of a single dripping second, the entity flattens into a two-dimensional silhouette of shimmering, opalescent geometry, slides seamlessly through a fold in the dilating air, and unfolds back into three dimensions a hundred yards away. 

Across the vast, dark expanse of the marsh, you can track their migrations by these silent, visual hiccups—monolithic shapes flickering, flattening, and reappearing across the shallow mirror of the sea.

As you watch them, their forms refuse to remain static. Just as the city constantly attempts to organize itself into an ideal shape, these entities are locked in a perpetual state of agonizing, ecstatic reconfiguration. Their bodies slowly slide and rotate around internal voids. A pillar of polished bone will gradually warp, splitting open to reveal a cascading, fractal waterfall of weeping resin, which then hardens, smooths over, and becomes the unblemished surface of a vaulted arch. They are living drafts of architecture, constantly editing their own geometry. 

They possess profound patience, uniquely suited to the oppressive, suffocating weight of the heat. They do not communicate with sound—the heavy air would swallow it anyway. Instead, they commune through spatial resonance. When two or more of these wandering architectures cross paths in the endless shallows, they do not merely stand beside each other. Because localized space so often forgets its rules, they walk *into* one another. 

For agonizing minutes, their geometries intersect, occupying the exact same coordinates. During this fusion, they share the physical memories of the impossible angles they carry. Their intersecting planes of cartilage and resin thrum in the damp dark, swapping structural data. When a ripple in the fabric of the valley eventually untangles them, they separate, fundamentally altered—each bearing new, twisting colonnades and fluted ridges gifted by the other. 

They serve a bizarre ecological function in this boiling, unraveling reality, acting as scavengers of ruined physics. 

While the thermosynthetic roots along the balconies passively drink the anomalies to stabilize reality, the wandering architecture actively seeks out the most violent glitches. They navigate the basin by feeling the friction of fraying space. When a geothermal vent flashes the shallow water into steam and a localized torrential rain triggers the city to blindly build itself, the entities are drawn to the event.

You might stand in the steaming dark and watch a silent procession of them sliding in from the horizon, gliding across miles of water. They gather in a wide ring around the plaza where the new domes and cathedrals are rapidly stretching toward the ceiling. They remain perfectly still, a ring of silent, towering geometries, waiting for the climax. 

When the local reality predictably fractures, and the new biological cathedral spasms into an extra-dimensional knot, melting back down into a slurry, the entities finally move. 

They glide forward into the scalding wreckage. They dip the smooth, sweeping edges of their resinous bodies into the collapsing calcium. They are sifting. They are hunting for the specific folds of space that caused the collapse, absorbing the ruin into themselves. As they drink in the failure, their own bodies violently glitch—turning briefly inside-out, flashing into wireframes, or fracturing into hovering, shivering shards of pale cartilage before snapping back together. 

By absorbing these fatal spatial anomalies, they act as buffers, drawing the destructive ontological weather out of the bedrock and carrying it away in their bodies, thereby keeping the wider basin barely stable enough to exist.

They wander the vast, humid emptiness until the weight of the corrupted reality they carry becomes too much. When an entity has consumed too many impossible angles, it stops in the middle of the dark sea. It ceases its internal reshuffling. It anchors itself to the bedrock. Over a few sweltering hours, it calcifies perfectly, locking its impossible geometry into a permanent, hardened state. 

It becomes an island. It becomes a bridge, or a towering colonnade, or a labyrinthine ward. 

This is how the city actually expands. It is not grown from the ground up, but laid down horizontally by its own exhausted inhabitants. They are born from the city's failures, they wander the sweltering dark to gather up its broken physics, and finally, they lock themselves into stone to become the very streets they once haunted, waiting in the heavy, dripping quiet for the valley to exhale, and for the next shape to pull itself from the slurry.
