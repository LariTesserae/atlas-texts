To see the denizens of this place, you must stop looking for biology and start looking for mathematics. 

At first glance, the roaring metropolis of hyper-flora appears exactly as you observed: a violently self-assembling architecture completely devoid of life as you know it. No birds, no beasts, no crawling things. But an ecosystem with this much kinetic energy, this much heat, and this much profound structural trauma does not remain empty. The friction and the broken physics have catalyzed an entirely new classification of entity. They are not born; they are *calculated*. They are the emergent phenomena of the botanical singularity, and they live in the shattered margins of the plants' spatial glitches.

They are the **Hysteresis**—beings of crystallized momentum, born from the screaming acoustic shockwaves and the atomized, pressurized sap.

When an eighty-story spire of ironwood meets an impossible mathematical load and violently shatters, it creates a concussive localized vacuum. In that split second, as millions of gallons of vaporized phloem are aerosolized into the blistering heat, the Hysteresis precipitate into existence. They do not have fixed anatomies. If you could freeze time, they would look like humanoid silhouettes composed entirely of suspended droplets of sap, fractured light, and sharp, geometric angles of refracted green noise. They are translucent, shimmering heavily with the same vibrating, static-like afterimages left behind by the non-Euclidean leaves.

They possess no organs, no bones, no tissues. They are contained and animated entirely by the atmospheric compression of the basin. The deafening, unending roar of the grinding city serves as their exoskeleton; it physically holds their shape together through acoustic levitation. If the noise were ever to stop, they would instantaneously dissolve into falling rain.

Because the environment is hyper-accelerated, their relationship with time is tragically, magnificently brief. They live almost entirely in the mid-air intervals of destruction. When a cathedral-canopy erupts into shrapnel, the falling debris becomes their continent. To you, the rain of splintered timber lasts perhaps three seconds before the whipping vines catch it. To the Hysteresis, whose consciousness operates at the speed of the ambient friction, those three seconds are an epoch. 

During the fall of a single tower, vast migrations of these beings leap between the plummeting shards of green wood. They surf the tumbling architecture in zero gravity, utilizing the gravitational micro-eddies to glide from splinter to splinter. In the span of a single downward plummet, a Hysteresis will form from the sap-mist, achieve full sentience, explore the falling topography of its birth-shard, communicate with others of its kind through complex, flashing shifts in their internal geometry, and peacefully dissipate back into vapor just before the wood is violently re-woven into an aqueduct. They live entire, rich lifetimes in the space of a heartbeat, building conceptual, momentary cultures out of sheer velocity.

Because they straddle the fraying boundaries of space and time, they are intimately connected to the city’s glitches. You will see them congregating in massive, silent droves where the heavy roots phase through one another, passing freely through the solid, spiraling intersections. Where the sun’s shadows point in conflicting, impossible directions, the Hysteresis use these shadows as highways, sliding up them backward against the pull of gravity, riding the darkness rather than the light.

When a street begins to warp and a vine grows backward into a bud, the inhabitants caught in that localized temporal reversal do not panic. Instead, they unfold. Their sharp, crystalline shapes un-spool into long, golden threads of temporal data, looping back to meet their own past selves. It is a form of reproduction. They fold their knowledge, their memories of the chaotic green expanse, into the minds of the beings they were a millisecond prior, layering wisdom upon wisdom until a single, fleeting entity possesses an ancient, infinite depth of experience.

What do they do with their infinitesimal, frantic lives? They act as the neurochemistry of the mad city. 

The plants possess only blind, autonomic violence, but the Hysteresis possess deep, chilling awareness. They are the eyes of the forest, the sentience that the wood itself cannot afford. They witness the impossible geometry; they map the infinity of the curling streets; they calculate the structural strains. As they whip through the humid air riding geysers of pressurized water, weaving in and out of the fourth-dimensional folds at the apex of the towers, they are trying to solve the equation of their own chaotic universe. 

If you stare into a pocket of sticky, sweet-smelling condensation hanging in the blistering heat, you might catch a glimpse of one. It will look like a prism of liquid amber and static, standing on a bridge of roaring vines, entirely motionless despite the violence around it. For an impossible fraction of a second, the static will form a face—a smooth, featureless mask of fractal angles. It will look at you. You will feel the weight of a consciousness so incredibly dense, so temporally vast, and so acutely hyper-aware that it makes your skull ache. 

And then, with the crack of artillery fire, the branch will snap, the architecture will tear itself apart, and the beautiful, impossible thing will burst into a mist of sweet sap, leaving nothing behind but a lingering echo of its geometry in the blinding, empty green.
