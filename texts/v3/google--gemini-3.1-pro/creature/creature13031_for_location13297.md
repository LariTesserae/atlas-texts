It takes time, within the sensory assault of the sweltering, collapsing geometry, to realize that the room is not empty. The density of the space—the erupting bamboo, the slapping clay, the suspended spheres of boiling water—acts as camouflage. You are looking at a space so aggressively packed with creation that it takes a moment for your mind to parse the center of the chaos. 

Where the overlapping physics fold inward, in the absolute, impossible core of the spatial contraction, hangs the occupant. 

It is a being of staggering, gravitational density. It is entirely fetal, curled into a tight, recursive knot, though its scale is entirely unreadable in the broken space; it seems simultaneously the size of a premature infant and a dormant, curled leviathan spanning acres. It does not simply sit in the room; the room *revolves* around it, frantically accumulating layers of matter to keep it shielded from the terrifying, thin void outside. The room is not just a womb—it is a desperate quarantine. 

To look directly at the entity is to feel your own anatomy ache with sympathetic pressure. It possesses a humanoid silhouette, but its flesh is a terrifying hybridization of overripe biology and geologic ferocity. Its skin is not skin, but translucent, wet tissue stretched agonizingly taut over a chaotic skeleton of what looks like calcified timber and pale, polished bone. Beneath that membrane, thick, slow rivers of glowing, bruised purple fluid pulse sluggishly, radiating the blistering heat that cooks the chamber. 

It is growing too fast. The entity is a victim of its own runaway genesis. 

As you watch the curled, shuddering mass, you see that its body is continuously attempting to outgrow itself, restricted only by the absolute lack of space. Its joints—knees pressed to its chest, arms wrapped violently tightly around its own shins—are swollen and bulbous, weeping thick, amber sap that hardens almost instantly before being shattered by the entity's subtle, agonizing twitches. Where its skin rubs against the structural pillars of the room, the entity does not bleed; it *sprouts*. The massive, waxy leaves and snapping bamboo shoots are not just growing around it; they are erupting from the sloughing top-layers of its molting flesh. It is shedding flora. Everything green and lush in this boiling kiln is the creature’s cast-off dander.

Its face is hidden, buried deep against its knees, but you can see the crown of its head. There is no hair, only a labyrinthine crown of wet, raw clay, identical to the walls. In fact, it is impossible to tell where the creature’s scalp ends and the ceiling begins. Thick cables of fibrous hemp, vines, and pale animal sinew grow straight out of its spine, stretching in every direction, lashing it to the architecture. These are umbilical cords—dozens of them, hundreds of them, pumping the raw, conceptual energy of creation out of the entity and into the room. 

The deafening symphony of the chamber—the wet *slaps* of mud, the rifle-crack of bamboo—is not random. It is synced perfectly to the entity’s heartbeat. 

*Thump.* A log splinters and weaves itself into the ceiling.
*Thump.* A glob of scalding mud materializes and detonates against the wall.
*Thump.* A pocket of steam hisses violently from the boiling floor.

It is in a state of perpetual, agonizing birth, but there is nowhere for it to be born into. It is too heavy for the sky, and so reality is frantically building higher, thicker, denser walls to prevent it from falling into the miles of emptiness below. The sudden contractions and dilations of the space, the Escher-like folding of the walls—it is all because the creature shifts in its sleep. When it twitches, local gravity shatters. When it sighs, the air pressure spikes to skull-crushing levels.

You look down at the newly spawned rope of braided sinew lashed around your ankle. You follow the rope upward, through the drifting, scalding bubbles of water, through the fractal, splintering wood, tracing it to its source. The rope is attached to the entity's back. 

It is incorporating you. The entity’s runaway metabolism cannot discern between an observer and raw material. In the frantic, blind mandate to build, to grow, to weave a shell against the void, it is pulling you in.

The entity shivers, a massive, tectonic shudder that threatens to tear the woven womb apart. Deep within the folds of its buried face, a single, glowing eye—the exact color of a lightning flash in an ash cloud—suddenly opens, peering out from between its knees. It isn't looking at you. It is looking *through* you, staring out at the cracks in the walls, terrified of the dizzying drop into the bruised clouds below. 

The obsidian mantis in the corner chatters into a frenzy, its bladed limbs moving like a sewing machine, tying knots in the fabric of the air itself, rushing to stitch the space back together before the entity realizes it is too vast for its own cage. The heat spikes again. The wet slapping of mud accelerates to a torrential downpour, and the massive, fetal god curls a fraction of an inch tighter, bracing against the pressure of its own impossible existence.
