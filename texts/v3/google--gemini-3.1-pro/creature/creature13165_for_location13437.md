Because there are no shadows in this basin to define edge, depth, or contour, you do not see them at first. The omnidirectional glare flattens everything into a blinding, two-dimensional tapestry. To distinguish the inhabitants from the glaring void requires a softening of your own stare, an untethering of the rigid way your mind expects shapes to behave. 

Only then do the aberrations in the steam reveal themselves. 

They are the Unfired. They are not the colossal architects who built this terrarium and stepped away, but rather the accidental, living residue of the construction process itself. When the gigantic hands packed the clay and snapped the peeling timbers, fragments were left behind—dollops of weeping, golden sap mixed with raw clay, splinters of bleached bone, and severed braids of green grass. In the stifling, scalding steam of the inescapable basin, these leftover materials did not rot. They festered into sentience. 

An Unfired being resembles a towering, emaciated mechanism of agonizingly raw anatomy. They stand anywhere from ten to fifteen feet tall, though their posture is perpetually hunched under the weight of the sweltering air. Their endoskeletons are pieced together from the razor-sharp macroscopic chips of the river-stones and slivers of mega-fauna bone, bound tightly by writhing, muscular coils of the green grass-ropes. 

Covering this haphazard armature is their "flesh"—a thick, viscous coating of the fired red clay, suspended eternally in a semi-solid state. Because the environment is a baking kiln, their outer layer is always on the verge of hardening into restrictive ceramic. But because the atmosphere is also suffocatingly wet with heavy steam, the clay is constantly weeping, melting, and sliding off their frames. Thus, they exist in a permanent, agonizing state of *curing*. To move at all, they must constantly break the newly baked crust of their own skin, radiating a sound like the snapping of dry subterranean roots and the wet, sticky parting of mud.

They have no faces. In an environment devoid of shadow and bathed in sourceless, blinding white fire, eyes would be a redundant cruelty. Instead, their heads are smooth, tapering domes of featureless clay that end in wide, flat sensory arrays resembling the gills of a mushroom. Through these gills, they breathe the heavy, boiling mist, filtering the microscopic kinetic vibrations of the algorithmic loops that govern the terrain. 

Through these vibrations, they perfectly understand the broken physics of their world. And unlike the looping serpents, the static macaw, and the instantly resetting tree frogs, the Unfired are not part of the algorithm. They are the glitches. They possess a horrifying, linear consciousness in a world without consequence or time. 

Watching an Unfired move is a dizzying, nauseating experience, for they exploit the non-Euclidean collapse of the basin's geography. Because deep space curves overhead, an Unfired standing a mile away does not walk the distance to reach you. It simply reaches up into the inverted canopy of the tiled floor above its head, grabs the lip of a basalt terrace hanging upside down in the distant sky, and pulls itself *through* the optical illusion. One moment it is a speck on the horizon; the next, with a terrifying, stuttering displacement of mass, it drops directly beside you, its massive, stilt-like legs splashing silently into the three-inch depth of scalding water. 

Yet, for all their towering horror, their existence is defined by a gentle, heartbreaking futility. 

The Unfired are starved for narrative. They are trapped in a zero-sum terrarium where nothing ever actually happens, and so they spend their infinite, static eternity trying to force consequence into the world. 

You watch one as it wades slowly toward a hanging wattle terrace overflowing with hyper-lush vegetation. It extends a massive, asymmetrical arm—fingers elongated into delicate spindles of raw, weeping timber. It approaches a colossal fern where a jewel-toned tree frog is trapped in its eternal, repeating leap. The frog vaults into the air, vanishes, and reappears on the clay pot. Over and over. 

The Unfired being positions its massive, ceramic hands precisely in the arc of the frog’s trajectory, hoping to catch it. Hoping to hold the creature, to bear its weight, to allow a single action in this world to land and resolve. 

The frog leaps. It passes cleanly, ghost-like, through the solid clay fingers of the Unfired entity, vanishes, and reappears on the clay pot. 

The Unfired does not roar; it has no mouth. But its broad shoulders slump. The freshly baked crust of clay around its collarbone fractures with a sharp, ceramic *crack*. Golden sap bleeds slowly from the fissure. It is a posture of profound, exhausted sorrow. 

Across the mirror-flat expanse, hundreds of them are engaged in similar, futile labors. Far out in the blinding steam, one is standing on its tiptoes, repeatedly trying to push the frozen macaw just an inch forward in the dense air, only for the bird’s wings to *whump* and reset the avian coordinates. Another is frantically attempting to dig into the continuous floor of interlocking river-stones, scraping its bare bone-fingers against the seamless joints, desperately trying to find soil for the unruly, overflowing epiphyte roots that have nowhere to grow. 

Their only physical solace comes from the water droplets. Because the condensation falling from the gigantic fleshy leaves is the only thing in the basin that mimics progression—building up, dropping down, and resetting—the Unfired revere it. When a fat, quivering teardrop of water finally falls from a leaf, an Unfired will scramble with breathtaking speed, dislocating through the broken spatial geometry, to slide its flat, featureless head beneath the drop. 

When the droplet strikes the Unfired’s skin instead of ghosting into the shallow water below, it does not reset. It hisses against the entity's baking, clay-covered form. For a fraction of a second, the boiling water cools the curing mud. 

In that fleeting, microscopic moment of evaporation, the Unfired entity shudders with relief. It has disrupted the algorithm. It has participated in cause and effect. Energy was transferred. 

Then the moment passes. The stifling hush returns. The macaw beats its wings in place. The serpents inhale in collective, agonizing unison. The blinding, shadowless sky burns without fuel. And the Unfired drag their heavy, weeping bodies through the scalding shallows, waiting for the next drop to fall.
