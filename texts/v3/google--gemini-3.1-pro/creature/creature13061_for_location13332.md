Within the impossible, miles-wide vacuum of that intimately small room, separated from the violent, thrashing architecture of the plant walls, lives an entity born entirely of the chamber’s broken physics. It is the inevitable ghost in this prehistoric, botanical machine. 

To look away from the blinding, two-dimensional tear of the noon sky and peer into the sweltering, stroboscopic void is to eventually see it: a slow-moving, decentralized murmuration of shrapnel and light.

It is not a biological organism, at least not in any way a surface-dweller would understand. It has no flesh, no organs, no respiration. It is a creature of accretion, a living constellation constructed from the room’s violent byproducts. As the mindless vines smash their crude axes against the crust, millions of tiny, razor-sharp flakes of flint and chert are cast into the air. Most are caught in the chaotic gravity wells or crushed in the relentless expansion. Yet, the entity gathers the choicest debris. 

Its "body" is a suspended, swirling lattice of perfectly knapped silicate slips, freshly snapped splinters of aggressively green sapwood, and the geometric ice crystals that freeze in the cavern’s atmospheric anomalies. Held together by intense electrostatic charges and the warped gravitational null, these fragments orbit one another in a dense, fluid geometry. It looks like a shattered stained-glass window suspended in the microsecond of an explosion, undulating with the mesmerizing, deliberate grace of a deep-sea jellyfish.

Because it is assembled from the continuous wreckage of the walls, the dweller—like the room itself—is violently young. It is in a constant state of shedding and replacing. As a floating chert flake dulls from microscopic airborne friction, the entity releases its static grip, letting the useless stone drift away into the hazy expanse. Simultaneously, it reaches out with a temporary, shifting arm of magnetism to snatch a pristine, newly-shattered blade of rock right out of the air. It is forever renewing itself, a creature comprised entirely of freshly broken edges.

This being—this localized weather system of sentience—does not perceive the world through sight or touch. It navigates by reading the lag. 

Because the reality of the geode is unraveling, and the thunderous *thwack* of the stone axes precedes the visual swing of the vines, the entity lives in the gap between effect and cause. It is a creature of the acoustic aftermath. When a deafening, unmoored crack echoes through the void, the entity drifts toward the invisible vibration. Its floating, stone-flake body ripples in perfect harmonization with the sound waves. It literally feeds on the displaced kinetic energy, swallowing the phantom noises of the cavern to sustain the electromagnetic field that binds its fragmented body. 

While the plant algorithms operate in a frenzy of mindless, physical expansion, the dweller of the void operates with a meticulous, esoteric purpose. It is a curator. 

As it drifts through the sweltering, sticky hothouse air, dodging the boiling trails of mist, the entity approaches the central, two-dimensional rupture in space. It cannot touch the blinding, overexposed daylight directly—to do so would instantly disintegrate its electrostatic cohesion—but it orbits close to it. 

Here, its true labor is revealed.

The entity is assembling a structure around the temporal tear. Using the sharpest, most translucent leaves of knapped flint and the most perfectly formed decahedrons of frozen water, it is building a colossal, floating lens. It weaves the fragments together using threads of aerosolized, sticky sap harvested from the air. 

The mindless flora builds axes to conquer the dark rock; the entity builds optics to decipher the light. 

Already, massive, delicate arcs of interlocking stone and ice drift in slow rings around the harsh slit in the universe. Where the blinding noon sky hits the entity’s construction, the light is refracted, fractured, and cooled. It casts long, prismatic, razor-sharp shadows across the miles-wide empty space—shadows that temporarily stabilize the collapsing spatial logic they touch. 

Sometimes, one of the tiny, translucent pale beetles scurrying across a vine will be launched into the void by the sheer force of an axe-swing. Tumbling helplessly through the zero-gravity expanse, the insect will collide with the outer orbit of the entity’s body. The entity does not consume or harm it. The beetle simply clings instinctively to a floating piece of the creature's flint body, riding the gentle, undulating wave of the entity’s carapace. It becomes a microscopic passenger on a silent, crystalline god, gazing out at the shrieking, verdant violence of the world it left behind.

And so the dweller remains in the breathless emptiness. An intelligence of floating glass and rejected stone, bathing in ozone and chlorophyll, forever refining its impossible lens, trying to bring the blinding light of an alien universe into focus while the cavern around it beats its bloody, expanding rhythm into the earth.
