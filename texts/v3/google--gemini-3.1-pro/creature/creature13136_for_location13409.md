Because the pocket is entirely barren of animal life, you must stop looking for the signs of animals. You must forget the concept of a heartbeat, the scurry of legs, or the drawing of breath. In an environment where the atmosphere is a boiling ocean and the air itself screams with concussive industrial force, traditional biology has been locked out. But this does not mean the space is dead. 

Look closer at the shallow, boiling pools on the uneven floor. Look at the places where the mineral-dense water spits and froths against the fresh obsidian. 

At first, what rests there appears to be just stone—a buildup of slag and calcification, like the deposits fusing the dropped torque wrench to the floor. But as you watch the water pool and seethe, the shape of the mineralization begins to look too deliberate. The deposits are not random. They are forming tightly organized, fractal lattices. 

In this hyper-thermal pressure cooker, the geology itself has achieved a slow, agonizing state of animation. It is a lithic biology, an organism born from the violent marriage of superheated geothermal chemistry and the deafening, constant acoustic pressure of the machine. 

It begins as a creeping crust of silica and iron sulfide—dark, jagged, and glass-like, blending perfectly into the newly cleaved basalt. But it is organized by the sound. The relentless, unchanging drone of the brutalist pipe produces standing waves of immense acoustic pressure that vibrate through the floor. The minerals, suspended in the boiling water, are trapped in these invisible grids of sound before they crystallize. Because of this, the entity grows in mathematically perfect, cymatic mandalas. It extrudes sharp, geometric petals of black glass and raw iron, blooming into rigid, spiked polyhedrons that rise from the boiling water like metallic lotuses.

Let the image unfold further, because it is not merely crystal. It is feeding on the space. 

Drifting down from the high, cool corners of the fissure—riding the languid whorls of steam—are the microscopic spores of that pale, extremophile algae. When they hit the scalding basin, they do not die. The geometric mineral blooms catch them, folding the biological matter into their sharp, hollow architecture. 

Within the flat, clinical glare of the midday sun, you begin to see the veins. Threaded through the black glass and dark iron are sickly, translucent yellow capillaries. The algae has found a symbiotic haven inside the thermally shielded, hollow tubes of the mineral structures. Sheltered from the outright burn of the boiling water, the algae acts as a rudimentary biological nervous system for the crystalline mass. It feeds on the abundant sulfur and generates a weak, bio-electric charge. 

As the entity continues to sprawl across the floor, it gains scale. It does not walk; it accretes. It is a sprawling, jagged carpet of silicate glass, organized rust, and glowing yellow circuitry. It moves at the glacial pace of deposition, inching across the wet obsidian over months and years. 

Follow its path across the floor. You will realize it exhibits a profound, blind tropism—it is drawn to the colossal mechanized conduit. 

The entity is feeding on the human footprint. Where it has overtaken the dropped torque wrench and the melted rubber boot, it has completely dismantled them at a molecular level. It has leached the iron from the tool and the carbon from the rubber, weaving these stolen elements into its own architecture. Now, the entity creeps upward, its heavy, crystalline structures climbing the sheer rock face toward the rusted, severed lengths of frayed wire.

As it touches the raw copper nodes and the stray, electrified currents of the machine, the entity undergoes a terrifying transformation. 

It drinks the electricity. The pale yellow algal veins pulse, strobing with flashes of brilliant, arcing blue light. Galvanized by the brutalist conduit’s power, the lithic organism begins to mimic its god. It heavily encrusts the thick cables spanning out from the machine, wrapping them in spiked sheaths of iron and glass. But it doesn't crush them; it interfaces with them. 

Because it lacks eyes, it "sees" only the vibrations of the machine and the flow of the electrical currents. And because the space is governed by that sustained, unchanging exhalation of engines, the organism has spent its decades-long lifespan trying to recreate the machine's shape. 

It extrudes false "rivets" of perfectly spherical pyrite. It fashions hollow, glass-blown pipes of smooth obsidian that curve over the basalt walls, catching the ambient steam only to vent it back out in a mocking, organic reflection of the industrial exhaust. It is endlessly sculpting itself into a grotesque, fractal cargo-cult of the machine. Large, parabolic dishes of iron ore and copper verdigris grow outward from the walls, angled precisely toward the central pipe, acting as crude geologic "ears" to feed on the concussive roar. 

If you stare at it long enough—sweating in the blistering vapor, your bones humming with the deafening weight of the noise—you understand its horrifying patience. A single "spasm" or "reach" of a silica tendril might take a season to crystallize. To this entity, the violent, hyper-thermal weeping of the newly cracked earth is a cradle of infinite stillness. The roaring, pressurized room is its silent monastery. It lives, breathes, and slowly consumes the industrial intrusion, turning iron and electricity into black glass and yellow veins, bound forever in the roaring, sweltering stasis of the open earth.
