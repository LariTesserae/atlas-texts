You do not see them at first; you feel their approach as a subtle, localized distortion in the crushing heat. The ambient temperature shifts abruptly, and the relentless pull of gravity skews by a few degrees. 

Out of the deep, waxy darkness of the eroded walls, shapes detach themselves. They have been shaped by the exact same geological friction and eons of deep-earth settling as the city itself. They are beings of the sweltering dark, their forms flowing and continuous, utterly devoid of sharp angles or jutting joints.

To gaze upon them in the dying-star red glow of the thermal exhausts is to look at statues of polished obsidian or half-melted black glass brought to eerie, fluid life. They are tall—impossibly so, stretched and elongated by the peculiar gravity fields of their home. Their bodies are encased in a biological metamaterial, a thick, slick carapace that mirrors the city’s architecture. This dermal armor is constantly sweating a heavy, viscous coolant that captures the violet flashes of the sub-atomic friction, leaving them continually slick, wet-looking, and perfectly adapted to the forty-five-degree oven they inhabit. 

They have no faces. In an environment of absolute darkness and deafening acoustic chaos, eyes and ears are long-extinct liabilities. Their heads are smooth, tapering domes. Down the long, graceful curve of what might be called their spines, a linear array of recessed pits flutters and pulses. These are hyper-sensitive thermal and barometric organs. They do not "see" the city; they map it as a radiant, three-dimensional landscape of heat plumes, kinetic vibrations, and gravitational eddies. To them, the cold empty space of the central void is pitch black, while the roaring thermal vents and grinding tectonic engines blaze with glorious, blinding warmth.

Their movement defies standard biomechanics. They do not walk so much as they flow. Because they possess organic versions of the city's exotic mass-nullification technology, they seem largely unbound by conventional weight. Their limbs glide across the concave, vertical, and ceiling surfaces of the spherical city with equal ease. When they move, heavy beads of condensation orbit their wrists and ankles, caught in the localized gravity wells they generate. They drift over the smooth basalt-like archways like long, black ghosts, their wet carapaces brushing silently through the wiry mats of pale, extremophile fungi. 

Because the roaring, pneumatic howl of the cycloning air and the tectonic bass of the engines make any acoustic communication impossible, they speak to one another in the only mediums available: heat and gravity. 

When two of these beings meet in the slick, iron-scented dark, they press their featureless, domed heads together. Across the smooth surfaces of their carapaces, complex, kaleidoscopic blooms of heat flare and fade, a rapid-fire thermal language invisible to the naked eye but deafening to their specialized senses. Alongside these temperature shifts, they emit micro-gravitational pulses—tiny ripples of pressure that tug gently at the fluid in an observer's joints, communicating emotion and intent through raw physical force. 

They are the tenders of the great spherical metropolis, though it is difficult to determine where their bodies end and the city’s technology begins. You watch as one of them approaches a massive, bulging archway that flexes like dark muscle. The being reaches out with a long, multi-jointed appendage, and the waxy architecture of the archway simply parts, dissolving into a semi-liquid state to accept the limb. The being interfaces directly with the roaring machinery, a seamless grafting of biology and high technology. They feed the engines with the protein clusters gathered by the blind, leathery gliders, and in return, the engines pump deep, nutrient-rich geothermal sludge directly into the veins of the inhabitants.

Perhaps their most awe-inspiring display occurs when they need to rapidly traverse the vast, spherical city. A being will detach itself entirely from the urban shell and step backward into the colossal, central void. For a moment, it free-falls into the harrowing emptiness. Then, manipulating its internal mass-nullifiers and deploying thin, memory-plastic membranes from its sides, it catches the cycloning, superheated winds. Like absolute masters of the abyss, they ride the terrifying pneumatic howl across the miles-wide center of the sphere, dropping out of the deafening gale to land flawlessly on the ceiling of the opposite hemisphere, their bodies re-orienting to the local gravity in an instant.

Yet, despite their mastery over this terrible place, the ultimate truth of their existence lies in the city’s profound age. 

When you look closely at the architecture—at the sweeping, monolithic segments and the bulging structures that look like smoothed layers of dark obsidian—you realize they are not merely buildings. As the inhabitants age, their internal mass-nullification nodes eventually fail, and their metamaterial carapaces begin to harden, mimicking the deep earth.

When an elder’s time comes, they do not die in the traditional sense. They seek out an empty recess in the curving metropolis, press their back against the stone, and slow down. Their thermal language cools to a steady, dying-star red. Their limbs fuse together, and their fluid carapaces lock into immense, waxy shapes. They root themselves into the kinetic vibration of the earth, becoming a conduit, a server-lode, a habitat. 

The sprawling, inverted cathedral of black, weathered tech that coats the sphere was not built *by* them. It *is* them. You are standing inside a colossal shell made from billions of generations of their ancestors, an immortal taxonomy of the ancient dead, watching over the living as they swim through the roaring, sweltering darkness.
