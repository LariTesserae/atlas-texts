To step into the blinding chasm is to initially mistake its inhabitants for part of the flora. At rest on the curving bone balconies, they look like colossal, spherical growths of white moss. It is only when one of these mounds unfurls, lifting a heavy, limb-like appendage to the light, that the illusion breaks.

They are a stout, hyper-adapted people, shaped over millennia by a singular imperative: hoard heat, and process light. 

Out in the vertical avenues, they are totally encased in their spherical coats. These garments are masterworks of ancestral tailoring, sewn from the hide and thick white wool of the six-legged sloths. The coats are so immensely padded that they erase the silhouette of the wearer, turning them into dense, solitary planets of fur that glide along the bone scaffolds. There is no stride to their walk; their lower limbs, wrapped in dense layers of felted plush and strapped into wide, gripping pedal-pads, move in a specialized, rolling shuffle. This keeps their center of gravity painfully low and perfectly stable on the ice-slickled, smooth-worn rungs.

Within the cavernous hoods of these coats, their faces are nestled in deep, insulating shadow. To survive the tyrannical glare of the parabolic cenote, their biology has heavily recessed their features. Their brows are pronounced, thick ledges of bone and fat that shadow the orbital cavities. Their eyes are sunken, narrow slits, but they do not merely squint. Over centuries of blinding exposure, they have developed a thick, secondary nictitating membrane—a heavily pigmented, iridescent black film that is permanently drawn across the eye beneath the eyelid. This biological shutter acts as a living polarized lens. Through it, they view their world not as a blinding white hell, but as a sharply defined, high-contrast landscape of silver geometry and deep cyan shadows. 

Beneath the eyes, their faces are dominated by broad, deeply ribbed noses. These are biological heat-exchangers, packed with winding, complex sinus labyrinths that aggressively warm the bitter air before it reaches their massive lungs. When they exhale, it is a long, slow, controlled drag of breath. Thick plumes of vapor drift from their hoods, instantly crystallizing into the suspended diamond dust that chokes the chasm. 

Because they exist in an acoustic void, their ears are entirely vestigial—tiny, flat ridges of cartilage buried under thick hair. But their hands are marvels of over-specialized evolution. 

While climbing, their hands are encased in heavy, suction-cupped leather mittens. But when they "speak," a slit in the mitten opens, and their fingers emerge. Their hands are shockingly long, delicate, and multi-jointed, completely at odds with their bulky bodies. The skin of their fingers is a deep, hyper-melanated charcoal color, protecting the vulnerable extremities from snow-blindness burns. It is with these long, spider-like fingers that they manipulate their mirrors. 

A mirror is bound to every inhabitant's palm with woven freeze-vines. The dexterity with which they maneuver these discs of polished abalone and mica is terrifying. Because their language is purely optic, their minds operate at a staggering framerate. Their visual cortex is vastly hypertrophied; they can parse thousands of individual flashes of light per second. A casual conversation involves an explosive, twitching blur of the fingers, angling the mirror at microscopic variables to catch the ambient glare and bounce it to a neighbor. A short, sharp flash of diamond-white might denote an immediate need; a rolling, shimmering refraction of cyan light might communicate a complex emotional state. The angle, the speed, the duration, and the color of the bounced light form a grammar so dense and mathematically perfect that they can recite epic poetry in a matter of seconds. To look down upon the city is to see a storm of glittering, silent static—a people thinking aloud in lasers.

But underneath the coats, and behind the glare, there is a softer, secret life.

When they retreat into the spherical, pelt-walled pods, they leave the blinding stasis behind. The hides pulled over the bone frames diffuse the furious sunlight into a thick, hazy amber glow. Here, in the agonizingly tight, humid quarters, they shed their massive coats. 

Without their armor, they are surprisingly small, squat, barrel-chested creatures. Their skin is thick and smooth, pale as milk, insulated by a dense layer of subcutaneous fat. Fine, velvety down covers their shoulders and backs, holding close what little warmth they generate. But the most striking feature of their bare bodies is a vivid, purplish network of surface veins that map their chests, inner arms, and abdomens. 

In the crushing cold of the outside world, these blood vessels constrict entirely. But inside the humid amber pods, the veins flush and swell, turning their pale torsos a bruised, mottled plum color. This is a mechanism for shared survival. In a world devoid of sound, where a voice cannot carry comfort, their society is intensely, necessarily tactile. Love, grief, exhaustion, and joy are communicated through the pressing of flesh. 

Inside a family pod, the damp air smells of salt and old moss. The floor is lined with foot-thick slabs of frost-plush. Here, the inhabitants pile atop one another. They form dense, tangled knots of bare limbs and flushed, radiating chests, locking together like puzzle pieces to minimize exposed surface area. They sleep, eat, and rest in these physical heaps. While the city outside is a chaotic crossfire of silent light, the interior of the pods is a realm of profound, physical weight. They communicate in the dark through rhythmic squeezing, the tracing of fingers along spines, and deep, resonant vibrations. Over-sized vocal cords, useless for speaking into the acoustic void outside, have been repurposed for this intimacy. Buried in a pile of their kin, they emit low, seismic purrs—frequencies too deep to be heard, but powerful enough to rattle the sternum of whoever they are holding. It is a shared, continuous heartbeat.

They are a people divided between two extremes. Out on the suspended avenues, they are isolated, silent astronauts swimming in a frozen sea of intolerable brilliance, their minds moving at the speed of light. Inside their tiny, dripping spheres, they are blind, soft creatures of the dark, fused together by warmth and heavy, wordless vibration. 

They do not fear the deep time that presses down on their cenote. They do not dream of the horizon or the winds beyond the ice walls. They know only the perfect equilibrium of the chasm: the endless polishing of the bone, the careful pruning of the ice flora, the flash of the mirror, and the warm, heavy chest of their neighbor in the dark.
