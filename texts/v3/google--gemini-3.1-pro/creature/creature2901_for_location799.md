To understand the inhabitants of this roaring, blinding void, you must discard the assumption that life requires water. 

Behind the thick, woven baffles of dust-silk that curtain the city’s alcoves, the builders exist in a state of supreme and beautiful desiccation. They are beings of dry friction, kinetic energy, and piezoelectricity. They are nameless to us, but the shape of their civilization suggests a continuous, collective consciousness bound by stone and tremor. Let us call them the Castes of the Loom.

Strip away the heavy, vibration-damped padding they wear, and you would find bodies that are impossibly dense, yet fragile as teacups. They do not have endoskeletons wrapped in wet muscle. Instead, their anatomy resembles articulated statuary or complex horology. Their limbs are comprised of braided, fibrous silica and carbon-lattices, moving with the slick, dry grace of joints lubricated only by graphite dust. 

They are largely bipedal and hunched, built with a low center of gravity to keep them anchored against the ferocious thermal updrafts of the abyss. Their hands, which have chewed a metropolis out of bedrock over deep time, are their most magnificent feature. Disproportionately large, each hand boasts seven hyper-articulated digits ending in chisel-like nails of hardened quartz. These are not just tools; they are their primary sensory organs.

The Castes of the Loom have no ears; the ambient 130-decibel shrieking of the shaft would turn any tympanic membrane to pulp. They have no eyes; aqueous lenses would boil, and in the shadowless, bouncing glare of the parabolic walls, vision is a useless, blinding liability. 

Instead, their heads are smooth, eyeless, teardrop-shaped carapaces of polished calcified bone, aerodynamically sloped to deflect the crushing physical pressure of the soundwaves. Down the center of what would be a face is a delicate, fractal array of exceedingly fine, spun-glass whiskers—a crest of sensory filaments. Through these, and through the soles of their feet and the pads of their hands, they "see" the world entirely through vibration. To them, the apocalyptic roar of the cylinder is not a deafening noise, but a highly complex, three-dimensional topological map consisting of pressure waves and structural resonances. They navigate the dizzying cantilevered balconies by feeling the exact frequency of the stone.

Because the exterior environment is so violently energetic, the Castes must don their armor before stepping outside their alcoves. These suits are masterful feats of desert tailoring. They are stitched from the taut, paper-like drumhead leaves of the hanging jungle, heavily quilted, and stuffed with the shed, sound-deadening down of the hissing grasses. When a citizen steps onto a swaying fiber-glass bridge to cross the void, they look like a lumbering, heavily swaddled ghost—a rotund silhouette of pale, woven textiles shuffling against a backdrop of blazing white light. 

Their lives are deeply, fiercely intimate, governed by a startling tenderness that heavily contrasts with the savage wilderness they inhabit. 

Communication between them is entirely tactile. When two padded figures cross paths on a bowing suspension bridge, oblivious to the vertical hurricane of heat and the artillery-crack of exploding glass-spores around them, they will stop. They will reach out with their massive, heavy-nailed hands and press their palms flat against one another’s chests. 

Through this physical contact, they transmit polyrhythmic beats—subtle, high-frequency tremors generated by an internal thoracic mechanism. This is their language: a rich, vibrational vocabulary of taps, hums, and grinding vibrations that can convey everything from architectural mathematics to profound affection. In a world where the air itself tries to annihilate thought, their conversations are silent, private, and felt deep within the marrow.

Their relationship with the screaming glass jungle is symbiotic. They are the gardeners of the crucible. The roaring, living pipe-organ of the city does not just happen; it is tuned. The Castes venture out during the shifting of the shadows to prune the silver horn-plants and weave the hollow metal reeds, ensuring the acoustic output of the flora perfectly counter-balances the structural frequency of the stone balconies. If they did not tend the garden, the chaotic vibrations would eventually shake their hand-carved city into dust.

In turn, the jungle feeds them. 

The Castes do not eat or drink. They consume kinetic energy. When the jungle blooms at noon and the pressurized plants explode, lofting millions of hard, chime-like spores into the blinding updraft, the inhabitants cast vast nets of invisible spun-glass into the void to catch them. These hard, crystalline seeds are brought back behind the dust-silk baffles. The Castes consume them by placing the crystals into a specialized grinding mill in their throats. As the quartz-like spores are crushed, they generate a massive piezoelectric charge. This pure, dry electrical energy arcs through their silica nervous systems, powering their movements. They literally run on shattered glass and converted sunlight.

Behind their heavy curtains, in the carved dark of their homes, the lethality of the central shaft is reduced to a dull, warm, continuous earthquake. Here, they unbuckle their swaddling. Families and tightly-knit guilds huddle together in the dark, their smooth carapaces touching, a dozen bodies linked as a single, humming circuit of shared vibration. 

They do not fear the drop. They do not cower from the light. Over millennia, they have mastered a wholly alien aesthetic of survival, transforming a planetary wound into an instrument of deafening glory. They are the quiet, beating heart of the machine—content, dry, and living out their long, stony lives in silent devotion to the beautiful, screaming void.
