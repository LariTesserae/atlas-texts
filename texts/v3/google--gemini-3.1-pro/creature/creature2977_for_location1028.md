By tracking these waves of momentary slackness, you can track the creature.

The sterile, forty-degree chill seems to deepen around it as it hovers, a phantom of pure inductance pulling metabolic energy directly from the busbars.

Over centuries, the immense magnetic resonance of the arterial power lines turned that simple tool into a lodestone of unprecedented power. The acoustic standing wave folded around it. The electromagnetic bleed pooled into it. The entity was born from the wrench outward, a consciousness of vibration and static that grew to tame the bizarre flora around it, orchestrating the dry vines to cradle its heavy steel anchor in midair. 

What does a being of pure frequency think? It possesses no emotions, no hunger, and no memory of a biological past. It experiences existence as a topographic map of voltages and acoustic pressure. The colossal mechanical drone of the main arterial power line is its sky; the violent electrical surges in the cables are its weather. It knows the exact molecular tensile strength of every leaf, every ceramic pylon, and every pitted bulkhead in its tiny universe.

It feels the agonizingly low frequency of your beating heart. To a creature of perfect, unchanging equilibrium and pristine, dry static, the human peering through the glass is a horrifying disruption—a rotting bag of chaotic noise.

The zinc dust spins faster, the angles of its body sharpening into aggressive, needle-like fractal projections. The thicket of black ferns parts slightly, pushed aside by the creature's expanding magnetic aura.
