To look at the titanic, mountain-sized world-engines is to look at walking continents, but to look closely—to dial your focus past the overwhelming scale of their colossal, grinding footfalls—is to realize that they are not mere inhabitants of this ecology. They *are* the ecology. The iron plateau is but the barren dish; the true biomes are the titans themselves, and the deeply fractured physics they leave in their wake. 

Just as a whale drags a menagerie of barnacles and remoras through the ocean, the world-engines support a parasitic and symbiotic kingdom of impossible, mechanized life.

Clinging to the sheer, mountainous cliffs of the titans’ oxidized copper carapaces are the **Slag-Gorgers**. To human eyes, they would appear as heavily armored crustaceans the size of naval submarines, but there is no flesh within them. They are animated masses of tensile carbon and spun tungsten, possessing heavily hooked, articulating limbs that bite deep into the titan’s calcified iron hide. They migrate slowly across the continent of their host’s back in sprawling herds, seeking out the towering, dorsal keratin-vents. When a titan exhales its slow-moving plumes of shattered atomic waste, the Slag-Gorgers swarm the rims of the vents. Their mandibles—fractal arrangements of hardened carbide—frantically scrape up the crusts of highly radioactive soot and cooled isotopic slag that build up around the exhaust ports. Through their immense heat-exchangers, they metabolize this waste to maintain their own localized magnetic fields. When one dies, its magnetic grip fails, and its massive tungsten shell plummets silently through the dragged time of the titan’s wake, eventually striking the mirroring floor below to remain entirely perfectly preserved for eons.

Beneath the armor plating, deep within the cage of the titans' exposed ribs, the ecosystem becomes fluid and terrifying. The weeping heavy lubricants—the boiling mercury and liquid gallium—do not merely bleed out into the air. They are farmed. Wading through the blinding, furious heat of the combustion chambers are the **Gallium-Leeches**. These entities are entirely fluid, held together only by focused electromagnetic tension. They look like undulating ropes of liquid mirror, fifty feet long, weaving through the titan’s internal gears. They have no eyes and no mouths; they feed via osmosis, pressing their liquid-metal bodies against the weeping glands of the titan, siphoning off the impurities in the boiling lubricants, filtering out the tectonic friction-dust the titan has eaten, and ensuring the great beast’s joints do not seize. They are the white blood cells of a biome built on heavy industry.

But the biology of the physical is only half the picture. Because the world-engines warp time and fracture reality, entire species have evolved to occupy the glitches in physics.

Consider the arcing tendrils of electric blue static that discharge from the titans' mandibles—lightning bolts that crawl through the chronal drag like growing winter trees, hanging in the sky for twenty minutes at a time. To an observer standing on the distant plateau, it is a slow-motion flash. But within the localized time-dilation of that crawling electricity, entire lifespans are playing out. 

These are the **Volt-Swarmers**. They are comprised of ionized plasma and pure photon logic. To you, their entire civilization lasts exactly twenty minutes; to them, it is a grand, generational epoch. As the slow-motion lightning branches, the Swarmers ride the leading edge of the current. They build ephemeral, impossibly complex, glowing hive-geometries out of the raw voltage, breed their sparks of young, war with rival colonies for the densest clusters of amperage, and die off as the base of the lightning-trunk finally reconnects with reality and fades. They are Mayflies of the electrical firmament, living a thousand lifetimes in the span of a single, agonizingly slow thunderclap.

Down on the ground, the mirrored, grooved expanse of the basalt and iron plateau is not as sterile as it appears. It is the territory of the **Acoustic Striders**. 

When the deafening *CHUNG* of the titan's footfall crystallizes into massive, geometric shapes of solid sound, these physical shapes flake off and drift to the floor, shattering into lingering, dissonant chimes. Life waits for these shards. The Striders are spindly, fragile things, standing perhaps ten feet tall, resembling daddy-long-legs woven entirely from vibrating, piezoelectric quartz and spun glass. They do not walk so much as they hum beneath the surface of the frictionless plateau, skating along the perfect grooves left by the titans. 

When a geometric block of solid sound shatters on the floor, the Striders converge with predatory speed. They possess no mouths. Instead, their forelimbs are tuned perfectly to the exact resonant frequencies of shattering noise. They wrap their glass-limbs around the writhing, chime-like fragments of solid acoustics, dampening the vibration. Through their quartz structure, they convert the raw sonic energy of the titan’s footfall into electrical sustenance, silencing the jagged shards of sound until they evaporate back into mere atmospheric pressure. 

And finally, there are the things that live in two dimensions. 

Because the shadows of the world-engines act as physical, interlocking gears upon the floor, a completely planar ecosystem has adapted to feed on the kinetic force they trap. Sliding across the frictionless iron surface are the **Planozoa**—organisms possessing length and width, but absolutely zero depth. They look like creeping oil slicks or living, shifting silhouettes. You cannot see them if you look at them on edge. They glide natively through the two-dimensional plane, waiting at the lips of the aerodynamic canyons. 

When the titans' impossibly complex shadow-mechanisms detach and slide away from the beasts, heavily laden with stolen kinetic energy, the squirming Planozoa swarm over them. They behave like predatory geometry, sliding their flat bodies over the spinning teeth of the shadow-gears, locking their own angles into the shadow’s cogs. Silently, flatly, they brake the shadows, absorbing the stored momentum directly into their flat mass, using it to push themselves further along the endless high-altitude basin.

In this breathless, searing absolute, there is no survival of the fittest in any traditional sense. There is no predator and no prey involving meat or water. There is only an unending, glorious, mechanical recursion. The titans eat the friction of the world. The parasites eat the runoff of the titans. The lightning eats the time. The glass spiders eat the sound. The flat beasts eat the shadows. 

It is entirely unnatural. It is perfectly alive.
