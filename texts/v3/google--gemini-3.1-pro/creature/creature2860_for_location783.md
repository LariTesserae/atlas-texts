The beautifully shaped obsidian hand-axe does not tumble forever. 

Caught in an aggressive upwelling of hypersaline brine, it spins head-over-end through the dark blizzard. Then, silently, its momentum is arrested. It does not strike the miles-deep ooze. Instead, it sinks into something softly unyielding, something that has been drifting just inches above the seafloor, perfectly camouflaged within the chaotic kinetics of the abyss.

What lives here is not shaped by predation, nor by sunlight, nor by the need to swim. It is shaped entirely by the merciless laws of fluid dynamics and crushing pressure. 

At first, it appears merely as a localized thickening of the marine snow—a sluggish, coalescing shadow within the endless hurricane. But as the obsidian axe is swallowed into it, the form begins to define itself against the roaring currents. It is vast—perhaps sixty feet across—but astonishingly flat, no thicker than a pane of glass at its outermost edges, swelling to a modest mound at its center. It is a creature of hyper-pressurized, translucent tissue, lacking a skeleton, lacking lungs, lacking eyes. To call it an animal feels inadequate; it is a benthic super-organism, a wandering apex of the abyssal plain. 

Its flesh is a marvel of deep-sea engineering, composed of tightly woven, poly-collagenous fibers that are entirely saturated with the pulverized silica of the dark blizzard. The water here is undeniably heavy, but the creature is heavier. It survives the tearing density shears and the freezing brinicles by offering no resistance. When the underwater waterfalls crash down from above, the entity simply flattens, its edges adhering to the soft, undulating dunes of grey-white mud like a colossal suction cup, letting the chaotic hyperactive turnover scream furiously over its perfectly smooth, hydrodynamic back.

But to hold its ground in a world of slow-motion avalanches and silt-laden rip-currents, flesh alone is not enough. The creature requires weight. It requires ballast.

Within the transparent canopy of its sprawling body, hundreds of dark, solid shapes are suspended. As the creature moves, contracting its edges in slow, rippling waves to inch across the ooze, these inner shapes shift and grind. 

They are the prehistoric artifacts. 

Over untold centuries, this entity—and others of its sparse, nomadic kind—has skimmed the seafloor, acting as a living sieve. It has no brain to comprehend the smooth curvature of a knapped flint blade, nor the primitive ingenuity of a basalt net-weight. To this creature, these objects are simply dense, intractable mass. It absorbs them through its ventral surface, drawing them up into internal ballast-chambers that line the perimeter of its massive, flattened bell. 

A heavy, crude harpoon carved from mammal bone is lodged securely in a muscular pocket near its leading edge, acting as an anchor-weight against the scouring currents. A dozen grooved throwing stones are perfectly spaced along its left flank, correcting a buoyancy issue caused by a recent meal of rotting marine snow. The newly acquired obsidian hand-axe is slowly pushed through a labyrinth of translucent internal canals by peristaltic waves, finally coming to rest against a fist-sized chunk of knapped chert.

Because the creature operates in an acoustic void—deaf to the roar of the deep, sensing the world only through the tactile rhythm of the low-frequency vibrations—it uses the artifacts to "see." 

As it ripples over the terrain, climbing the sheer face of an unstable mud-dune, the sheer physical exertion causes the ancient stone tools within its body to grate against one another. Flint scratches basalt. Obsidian clinks sharply against petrified bone. These tiny, high-frequency kinetic strikes reverberate entirely within the fluid-filled chambers of the creature's gelatinous mantle. It echoes through its primitive nervous system, a constant, internal clicking rhythm that informs the organism of its own orientation, speed, and spatial dimensions in the pitch black.

And when the turbulence of the environment reaches a fever pitch—when the abyssal tornadoes rip across the seafloor and the creature is forced to anchor itself with all its might to avoid being torn apart—the violent grinding of the stones within its flesh produces something miraculous. 

Piezoelectricity. 

The intense, localized friction of quartz and flint and basalt rubbing together beneath tons of atmospheric pressure generates brief, internal sparks of voltage. This raw kinetic energy triggers a chemical reaction in the creature’s dormant chromatophores. A bruised, phantom indigo light blooms violently to life within the animal's glassy flesh. It pulses through the dark blizzard like a dying star: *flash, flash, flash*, perfectly illuminating the silhouettes of paleolithic hand-axes and bone hooks suspended in the jelly. It is a spectacular, ghostly exhibition, unseen by any eye, firing into the freezing void until the underwater hurricane passes and the stones settle once more.

When it needs to feed, the creature alters its density. It relaxes the muscles gripping its collection of ancient stones, allowing them to hang low in heavy pouches, pulling the creature's outer rims down to scrape an inch-deep trench in the mud. As it plows forward, an intake valve opens at its center. It vacuum-sucks the ultra-fine ooze, filtering out the microscopic, decomposed remnants of surface life that fell millions of years ago, as well as the occasional pale, blind armored scavenger foolish enough to hunker down in the entity's path. Once stripped of nutrients, the barren silt is exhausted through dorsal vents, trailing behind the creature like smoke.

There is no malice in this entity. There is no intent. It is just remarkably, perfectly adapted to the nightmare of its home. 

In a lightless engine of cold water that erases wrecks and destroys continents, this translucent, sprawling leviathan survives by doing what the ocean itself does: it swallows the past. It wanders the endless, utterly barren plains of the abyss, wrapped in the silent hiss of microscopic friction, wearing a heavy internal armor forged by human hands thousands of years ago, a living, pulsing museum of the paleolithic, dragging humanity's earliest conquests through the dark and the mud forever.
