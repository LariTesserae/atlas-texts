To understand the inhabitants of this embryonic, howling world, you must first discard the concept of biology. Flesh is a liability of softer dimensions; fluids would instantly boil away in the vacuum-thin chill or be vibrated into atomized mist by the planetary roar. Life here is not gestated. It occurs as an act of geologic accretion. 

They are the **Lithic Anchors**. They are the immune system of a newborn, unstable reality.

If you were to stare across the terraced, obsidian expanses, you would not see them at first. When they are still, they are perfectly camouflaged against the cruel math of the mountains. They are humanoid only in the most abstract, topological sense: they possess a central mass to govern balance, limbs to traverse the sheer steps, and hands to manipulate the world. But they are comprised entirely of the landscape itself.

Their bodies are jagged matrices of unpolished silicate, dense basalt, and raw, dark-matter geometry. They have no faces—where a head should be, there is instead a rotating, crystalline polyhedron that shifts its facets in impossible ways, occasionally glitching into the same glowing wireframe that outlines the distant peaks. They do not possess eyes. In a world where light is dim and shadows fall backward, vision is useless. Instead, they are entirely piezoelectric. The deafening, glass-shattering shriek of the world and the subterranean bass drone are not just noise to them; this acoustic violence is their breath, their nervous system, and their sight. It vibrates through the crystalline lattices of their bodies, allowing them to map the fraying seams of reality in perfect, three-dimensional topography.

To watch an Anchor move is to witness physics struggling to render a sentient object. Because space stutters here, they do not walk in one continuous motion. They step forward, the dry chill crackling around their joints, and then—for a fraction of a second—they smear into a kaleidoscope of violet static. When the visual plane catches up, they are twenty feet further along the ridge. They navigate the lazy, listing gravity with haunting grace, locking their sharp-edged limbs into sheer vertical cliff faces, leaning outward over the void at angles that should instantly pull them down into the crushing dark. 

Their bodies are marred by deep, glowing scars of raw, un-rendered light—injuries sustained not from predators, but from the violent spasms of the world’s creation. To heal, they plunge their limbs into the ferociously dense acoustic jungles. You can see the remnants of this flora woven into them. Brittle, metallic fibers in deep, impossible magenta twist like tendons around their obsidian joints. The glass-like capillaries of the plants are threaded through their torsos, capturing the kinetic fury of the atmosphere and translating it into the energy they need to move. When one of them is injured, it does not bleed; it sheds a glittering, dry cascade of magenta mineral dust that drifts upward against the broken gravity.

But it is their hands that are the most tragic and beautiful part of their architecture. 

They are disproportionately large, featuring seven multi-jointed digits that fractal outward, allowing them to grip not just physical stone, but the edges of metaphysical concepts. These hands are heavily weathered, chipped, and blunted—the only things in this pristine, untouched landscape that show the wear of age. 

You find one at the edge of a vast, open fault line, where the bruised sky is bleeding down into the bedrock, threatening to dissolve a mile of mountain into pure, formless null-space. 

Observe the Anchor at work. It feels the rip in reality. The rotating, wireframe polyhedron of its "head" locks onto the anomaly. Its shadow does not fall behind it; the shadow stretches out *toward* the anomaly, desperate to reach it. 

The entity reaches to its back and draws a spear. The shaft is singing—a thick, dead stalk of acoustic flora still vibrating with ambient tectonic power. The head is that hand-axe of "frozen sky," knapped from a localized paradox. The Anchor moves to the edge of the glitching abyss. It does not think as we do. It has no culture of art, no songs, no hierarchy. Its mind is an agonizingly pure locus of geometry, tension, and immense, unconditional love for a world that is trying to tear itself apart. It is a midwife to creation, and its only imperative is to hold the crib together.

It swings the hand-axe, driving the conceptual wedge into the seam of broken space. It doesn't use muscles; it waits for the subterranean bass drone to reach its absolute zenith, and it strikes in perfect harmonic resonance with the vibration, using the acoustic roar to multiply its force. The impact sends a shockwave through the heavy, dry air. The wireframe of the fractured dimension shrieks—a sound distinct from the background noise—and begins to solidify into stone.

But the wedge is not enough. The Anchor kneels, gravity listing so hard to the left that the creature is almost entirely horizontal, suspended over the void. It gathers stones from the sheer terrace. With its blunted, fractal fingers, it pulls strands of metallic plant fiber from its own body. 

It begins to tie a knot. 

This is the miracle of their primitive survival. In a landscape where high concepts like gravity, relativity, and distance are actively failing, the mechanics of the Stone Age remain absolute. Tension. Friction. Leverage. A knot is a localized mathematical truth that reality cannot ignore. 

The Anchor calculates the intersecting frequencies of the collapsing space and binds the stones together, spacing them precisely an inch apart. As the knot is pulled taut, the woven acoustic fibers vibrate, projecting a localized field of stabilization. The stones hover in place. They become a cairn, not of warning, but of architectural mass. A stitch in the fabric of the cosmos. 

The sky stops bleeding. The mountain stops glitching into wireframe. The sheer, cruel terrace becomes solid to the touch. 

The Anchor stands, shedding a fine mist of sparking mineral dust from its joints. It reaches out with a worn, chipped digit and scrapes a tally mark into the unweathered basalt. A record of one more seam fused, one more disaster averted. If you look at the marking, it is perfectly still. But as the Anchor moves away and your gaze follows the creature, out of the corner of your eye, you can see the etching detach from the rock and drift slowly upward, a floating ghost of syntax looking for a permanent home.

The creature does not rest. It cannot. The planetary-scale roar continues, vibrating in the conceptual architecture of its mind, telling it that a hundred miles away, another valley is failing to render. Another mountain is slipping into the void. 

It turns into the dry chill, steps out across the impossible expanse, and glitches forward through space. A solitary, faceless architect made of sharp edges and sound, walking into eternity with a pocket full of stones and string, painstakingly building the universe by hand.
