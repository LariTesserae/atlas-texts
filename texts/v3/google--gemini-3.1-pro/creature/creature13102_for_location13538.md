To find what dwells here, you must stop looking for independent movement and instead watch the water for a shape that resists the current. 

In the deepest of the scalloped, waist-high basins, submerged entirely beneath the blood-warm mineral water, lies a form that at first appears to be merely another fold of sunken travertine. It is curled in a loose, suspended fetal posture, perfectly buoyant. The dense, silica-heavy water holds it in a permanent embrace, and because the temperature of the entity’s blood is identical to the temperature of the pool, there is no thermal shock, no shivering, no need to generate heat. The creature and the environment are a single, continuous system of warmth.

It is humanoid, though deeply, profoundly altered by the pooling of epochs. Its skin is the color of bleached almonds and soaked parchment, saturated to its absolute limit. You might expect the extreme waterlogging to leave it severely wrinkled, but centuries of this existence have smoothed it out. The skin has swelled and stretched into a taut, pearlescent membrane, slick and frictionless, reflecting the dim amber twilight exactly as the wet stones do. It bears no hair whatsoever; every pore has long since sealed over or relaxed into nothingness, leaving a biological veneer almost indistinguishable from polished flowstone.

The entity breathes, but not as surface creatures do. Lungs have become largely redundant in this suffocating humidity. Instead, respiration is a matter of profound, agonizingly slow diffusion. The entity draws the thick, heavy air into its mouth once every quarter-hour, holding it, allowing the capillary-rich lining of its throat and its highly permeable skin to draw oxygen directly from the swallowed vapor. Its heart beats only thrice a minute—a slow, muffled thump that reverberates through its chest cavity, perfectly in rhythm with the heaviest water droplets striking the cavern floor. 

When it moves, the transition from stillness to motion is so gradual it defies the eye. The pale limbs uncoil, extending into the water. Its anatomy lacks any sharp angles. Knees, elbows, and shoulder blades have been softened by generations of selective adaptation and a total lack of concussive impact. The joints are padded, the bones flexible, allowing it to contort and slide through the cramped, three-dimensional labyrinth of bulbous stalagmites and thick pillars with a liquid grace. Its fingers and toes are elongated, webbed with thick webs of connective tissue, the nails completely devolved into flat, smoothed-over pads designed to suction gently against slick rock without leaving scratches.

It rises, breaking the surface of the pool. The water sliding off its back makes no sound, absorbed entirely by the ambient white noise of the hissing steam. 

The face that lifts into the milk-thin twilight is an artifact of sensory subtraction. The nose is reduced to two flush slits beneath a sloping brow. The ears are sealed beneath smooth flaps of cartilage to keep the deep water out. Most striking are the eyes—vestigial, fused shut under translucent hoods of flesh. It has no need for vision in the shadowless amber glow. Instead, it "sees" entirely through touch and thermal reception. Its wet skin can register the microscopic shifts in air currents caused by a single falling droplet, and it navigates the crowded thicket of columns by feeling the subtle gradations of heat radiating off the various pools. 

Slowly, it pulls itself over the lip of the rimstone dam. It places its foot perfectly into one of the shallow, softened steps carved into the incline of a flowstone mound. The groove cradles its foot with absolute precision, and for good reason: it was not the chert chisels of ancient, surface-dwelling ancestors that wore these steps down strictly into this shape. It was the endless, unvarying repetition of this specific creature, climbing this specific mound, day after day, for a thousand years. The human artifacts left here—the steps, the water channel, the clay pitcher—were not abandoned. They are simply the childhood toys of an entity that outgrew them, choosing to remain behind as the rest of its kind departed into the sunlit world or faded into dust.

The entity’s sustenance is as minimal as its metabolism. It shifts its weight toward the warm weeping wall where the water trickles steadily. Leaning close, it unhinges a jaw that lacks teeth. Only a pale, raspy tongue emerges, broad and flat. Slowly, rhythmically, it licks the wet stone, skimming the mustard-yellow and rust-red bacterial glaze from the travertine. It feeds on the thermophiles, filtering the rich mineral and microbial paste against the roof of its mouth. Occasionally, its slow-sweeping hand brushes against one of the translucent, eyeless cave-shrimp. With a motion just a fraction faster than its usual lethargy, it plucks the tiny ghost from the water and swallows it whole, adding a sliver of calcium and protein to its geologic diet. 

Because the entity exists in this saturated enclave forever, the grotto attempts to claim it just as it claimed the clay pitcher and the copper ring. As the creature sleeps in the basins—sometimes for months at a time, suspended in a torpor nearly indistinguishable from death—the weeping water deposits fine layers of dissolved calcite and silica upon its back. Slowly, a pale, chalky crust begins to form over its skin, threatening to entomb it, to fuse it to the bowl of the pool as a permanent nodule. 

When the shell becomes too tight, restricting its slow heartbeat, the entity wakes. In an agonizingly slow ritual, it drags itself onto the damp floor and rubs its body against the bulbous stalagmites. The mineral crust cracks and flakes away like an eggshell, falling to the ground to dissolve back into the wet floor, revealing the raw, smooth, infantile flesh beneath. This shedding is the only clock the creature knows.

Having fed, and having shed its calcified shell, it moves back toward the deepest pool. It slides over the terraced edges, a pale ribbon of flesh descending back into the blood-warm water. It curls its limbs inward, sinking until only the smooth, unblemished crown of its head breaks the surface, glistening red-gold in the pearlescent light.

There are no thoughts in the mind of the dweller, no longing for a sky it has forgotten, no concept of past or future. It is not a prisoner. It is the living heart of the grotto. Wrapped in the amniotic steam, deafened by the eternal hiss and patter, it closes its sensory slits, slows its weeping pulse, and joins the stone in its endless, fossilized present.
