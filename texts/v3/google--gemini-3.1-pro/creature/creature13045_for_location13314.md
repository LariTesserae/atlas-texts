To find the true inhabitants of this place, you must stop looking at the gears of the factory and start looking at the friction. 

Ignore the rivers of chitinous couriers; they are merely the blood. Look past the pumping, fleshy flora; they are only the lungs. The apex residents of this world do not participate in the heavy, sweating physical labor of the biomechanical jungle. They are not born from the pressurized geothermal vents, nor do they feed on the nutrient-rich biological smog. 

They are born in the errors. They live in the frayed seams where reality stutters. 

When the mountain peak fifty miles away violently folds inward to hover inches from your face, you can see one entirely by accident. As the peak snaps back, leaving its silent, ripping vacuum, a figure is left behind, marooned in the space between distances. 

At first, it does not look like biology at all. It looks like an architectural hypothesis drawn in blinding, wire-frame bio-energy—a sudden, intricate tangle of neon-white geometry that continuously redraws itself to compensate for the shifting framerate of local spacetime. It has no continuous shape, shifting from a serrated, multi-limbed abstraction to a sleek, monolithic spire of light, driven by a logic completely divorced from evolution. 

But this raw, energetic form is too volatile for the humid, hyper-dense atmosphere of the factory. To remain in this dimension without dissolving back into the static of the cosmos, the entity must anchor itself. 

You watch as it drifts down toward the churning canopy, moving without disturbing the heavy air. It reaches into the chaotic biological overgrowth and begins to dress itself in the world's heavy, sweating matter. It reaches into a weeping fracture of black silicate and draws out threads of molten, glassy rock, weaving them through its wire-frame core to create a skeletal structure. It tears broad, fleshy, bellows-leaves from the pumping flora and drapes them over its burning joints to weigh itself down. It coats its raw-light extremities in the oily condensation that slicks the ridges.

Once dressed, the entity resembles a towering, cloaked ascetic, a moving extrusion of the factory itself—a body of fractured glass, bleeding light, and heavy, rubbery flesh, weeping warm water and trailing vines like the robes of a rusted king. 

They are the **Interpolators**. The glitches of the factory are not chaotic accidents; they are the intentional, designed habitats created *for* these beings. 

Observe the deep gorge where the waterfall of gray organic slag hangs suspended in gravity-less mid-air. One of these beings is there. It steps effortlessly onto the floating droplets of liquid slag, its footfalls producing tiny outward ripples of spatial distortion. It is grazing. Where gravity has forgotten its function, an immense amount of potential energy is trapped in paradox. The entity kneels on the floating waterfall, presses a hand of coiled, shimmering bio-light into the suspended droplets, and drinks the trapped kinetic force. As it feeds, the light of its wire-frame core flares from a cool white to a ferocious, blinding blue, and the slag droplets beneath it turn to dead, gray ash and finally fall. 

They feed on impossible physics. 

Look toward the jagged horizon, where the massive, piston-driven tree violently collapses and shatters in a localized, three-second time loop. A cluster of Interpolators has gathered there. They sit in a perfect, geometric circle around the anomaly. They do not move, but you can feel the sudden, agonizing drop in temperature around them. In a world defined by feverish, damp heat, these beings radiate absolute, vacuum-born cold. They are absorbing the friction of the stuttering time loop, metabolizing the shattered seconds to sustain their physical mass. 

Because they exist outside the physical rules of the factory, they also bypass its acoustic dampening. Thus, they are the only things in this sprawling, million-square-mile engine-world that truly make a sound. It is not a roar or a cry. It is a terrifying, crystalline singing. It is the sound of a wet finger being run along the rim of a cosmic wineglass, extremely high-pitched and vibrating not in your ears, but in the enamel of your teeth and the fluids of your eyes. 

As you watch them, the horrifying teleology of this world clicks into place. 

The factory did not build itself to produce the chitinous insects, nor the massive manta-gliders, nor the breathing forests. All of that staggering, sky-choking biological industry—the violent uplifting of tectonic plates, the massive venting of thermal energy, the claustrophobic density of life—exists solely to generate enough sheer kinetic and biological mass to bend spacetime until it breaks. 

The reality warps are not byproducts of the factory. The reality warps are the *furnaces*. 

And the Interpolators are the product. 

Now that you know how to see them, you realize the sky is full of them. Above the fleshy smokestacks, hidden within the bruised indigo smog, millions of wire-frame entities are drifting, anchoring themselves with heavy condensation and volcanic ash. The billions of tiny, bioluminescent pollinators flashing in synchronized waves across the valleys? They are not just insects. They are eyes. They are tracking the movements of these ascended beings, the blinking diodes of the living supercomputer monitoring the progress of its children. 

The entities are gathering high above the atmospheric baseline, linking their glassy, wire-frame limbs together. They are expanding, shifting their geometry into vast, interlocking tessellations, feeding off the temporal and spatial errors welling up from the sweating jungle below. 

They are weaving a new, sentient architecture. Piece by piece, stutter by stutter, they are cannibalizing the broken laws of reality to build a bridge out of this universe, leaving behind a world that pumps, and sweats, and diligently breaks itself to fuel their ascension.
