To survive in an environment defined by relentless friction and crushing acoustic weight, flesh must either be sheared to nothing, or it must learn to mimic the stone. 

The inhabitants of this spherical pocket have chosen the latter, though it is no longer clear if they engineered themselves for this existence or if the churning void simply eroded them into their current form over epochs. They are a species of ablative survivors, beings of immense density and unyielding mass. To watch one emerge from the heavy, reinforced pressure-spheres is to watch a boulder detach itself from the architecture and begin to crawl.

In silhouette, they resemble a fusion of deep-sea diving bells and bloated, deep-ocean isopods. They possess no sharp edges, no protruding features, no dangling limbs or external sensors. Like the brutalist city they inhabit, every contour of their bodies has been hydro-polished smooth. Beneath the swirling highway-speed currents, their dark, slate-gray exteriors glimmer dully—not armor, but an incredibly thick, yielding dermal carapace composed of vulcanized bio-polymers and heavy lipid layers. 

They are entirely devoid of gas-filled cavities. A lung, a sinus, or a middle ear would instantly implode beneath the percussive shotgun blasts of the steam vents and the deafening sub-frequency thrum of the turbines. Instead, their interiors are entirely fluid-filled, highly pressurized, and impossibly dense. Their skeletons are not made of rigid, easily splintered calcium, but of thick, braided cables of iron-infused cartilage. They do not fight the vibrations of the environment; they allow the acoustic kinetic energy to pass harmlessly through their gelatinous mass. 

Because the water is a screaming, chaotic tempest, their outer layer of skin is in a permanent state of sloughing off, microscopic layers of bio-polymer slowly melted away by the cavitating micro-bubbles and swirling silt, only to be rapidly regenerated from within. The grayish thermal slime that coats the walls of the settlement is not algae—for nothing grows here. It is them. It is the shed, dissolved residue of their own bodies, coating the iron and rock, acting as a frictional lubricant and atmospheric sealant across the urban sprawl. 

They have no eyes; eyes would be blinded by the jaundiced, strobing arcs of the lamps and scoured raw by the silt. What was once a face is now a broad, sloping dome of thick sensory blubber. They possess heavily baffled chemosensory pits arrayed like gills along their ventral sides, allowing them to "taste" the dissolved sulfur, iron, and ozone in the water, tracking chemical trails to find a leaking piston housing or a bleeding intake valve miles across the settlement. 

Most profoundly, they perceive the world entirely through tactile sonography. To them, the deafening roar of the chamber is not noise, but profound, intricate geometry. The explosive popping of thousands of collapsing cavitation bubbles acts as a continuous, omnidirectional radar sweep. The deep, bedrock-shaking thrum of the cathedral-sized rotors shapes the water around them. They read the changes in pressure along their highly sensitive, drum-tight outer skins, “seeing” the exact dimensions of a spinning turbine blade or a sheared blast door through cymatic resonance. The tempest paints a crystal-clear, three-dimensional tactile map directly into their nervous systems. 

To protect against the sheer kinetic shock of the environment, their brains are not housed in their heads. Their central nervous craniums are buried deep within their massive, barrel-shaped torsos, suspended in a thick, amniotic sac of acoustic-baffling gel—a biological substance nearly identical to the rare, blind, gelatinous scavengers occasionally swept into the intake grates. 

Locomotion in the hyperactive shear-currents is an act of violent, deliberate anchorage. They possess six short, immensely powerful limbs, arrayed radially around their lower mass. They do not have hands or feet; they have heavy, splayed locking-pads. These extremities are biological electromagnets, interwoven with ferromagnetic alloys and hydraulic micro-cilia. When an inhabitant moves, it does not walk—it locks, detaches, slides, and clamps again. You can hear their movement beneath the roar: the massive, metallic *clack-screech-thud* of a creature magnetically grafting itself to the hydro-polished stone, pulling its multi-ton body forward by an inch, completely oblivious to the vicious undertows ripping at its smooth, sloped back. 

They are the tenders of the void. You will find them clinging to the periphery of yawning intake valves or clamped to the rib-like exposed pipes, moving with agonizing slowness. Their tools are indistinguishable from their bodies—heavy pneumatic rams and sputtering arc-welders that are grafted directly into their forelimbs, powered by the same internal thermal sinks that keep their dense blood flowing in the darkness. They live only to pull maintenance on the gargantuan machinery, preserving an eroded equilibrium for reasons lost to deep time.

When they require rest, they crawl back to the isolated pressure-spheres tethered to the periphery. Inside these thick iron domes, the airlocks cycle, the water slows, and the acoustic fury is muffled to a distant heartbeat. 

Yet, they do not find comfort in the quiet. Having lived their entire lives navigating by the three-dimensional map of deafening noise, silence blinds them. The stillness of the resting spheres feels like sensory deprivation, an empty void far more terrifying than the fluid storm outside. To cope, they huddle together in the dim red glow of the thermal exhausts, locking their heavy, magnetic pads against one another’s carapaces. Vibrating their internal thoracic muscles, they emit deep, rhythmic, sub-frequency pulses directly into one another's bones—sharing heat, sharing the vibrations of the day's labor, and gently humming the tempest to one another until it is time to return to the roar.
