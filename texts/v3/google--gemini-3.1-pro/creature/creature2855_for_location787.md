To understand the beings that walk these vast, unweathered terraces, you must first discard the concept of a life lived chronologically. They are not born into a beginning, nor do they pull themselves toward an end. They do not possess a past to feel nostalgia for, nor a future to anticipate with anxiety or hope. 

Because their world does not know the passage of time, their consciousness is not constructed as a narrative. It is entirely spatial. It is a continuous, unbroken geometry of being. 

From a distance across the immense, pale plazas, they appear as solitary pillars of heavy, undyed wool. They are incredibly tall, standing perhaps eight or nine feet high, and painfully slender. The thick garments they wear drape like heavy fluting on a marble column. This wool is rough and raw, carrying the faint, sharp scent of ozone, crushed ferns, and cold stone. It belongs to the multi-legged, white-furred grazers that feed eternally along the misty shadow-borders. 

The relationship between the caretakers and these beasts is one of perfect, static reciprocity. Periodically, a caretaker will sit motionless beside a grazer in the freezing, dripping mist. With long, multi-jointed fingers, they will gently comb the thick white pelt. The shedding fur tangles directly into their hands, exactly matching the animal’s rate of follicle renewal. There is no shearing, no harvest season—only a perpetual, gentle extraction that leaves the beast entirely undisturbed. 

Beneath these heavy woolen mantles, the caretakers’ bodies are a reflection of the valley’s tectonic youth. Their skin is smooth, dense, and cool to the touch, resembling the pale, friction-fitted granite of their own architecture. Veins of subdued, pearlescent fluid map their slender limbs, pulsing not with the rapid, panicked rhythm of a heartbeat, but with a slow, hydraulic inevitability, like the deep-ground pressure that births mountains. 

When they step from the blinding, eternal noon into the freezing sanctuaries of permanent shadow, you can see their skin subtly shift in hue—from a translucent, milky ivory in the light to a deep, bruising violet in the cold dark. Their physiology is perfectly adapted to survive both extremes without shock, functioning as living thermal batteries that store the celadon sky’s radiation to carry with them into the pitch-black depths of the cisterns.

Their faces are serene plateaus of bone, dominated by eyes that defy the concept of a gaze. Set deeply into narrow orbital arches, their eyes are solid, reflective convexities of obsidian black, exactly mimicking the still, glassy water of the deep-shade pools. Because the sun never alters its sharp morning angle, they have no need for irises that constrict or dilate. Instead, their eyes capture the entirety of their surroundings at once in a state of permanent exposure. They do not look *at* things; rather, the world simply exists within them. 

You can watch them work for hours—though hours are entirely meaningless here—and begin to realize the profound nature of their silence. They do not speak, because spoken language relies on sequence: a beginning of a sentence, a middle, and a concluding thought. Spoken words signify change, an exchange of new information. Here, there is nothing new to say. Everything that is, already is. 

Instead, they hold communion through placement and proximity. A caretaker sweeping dust at the far northern edge of a miles-wide courtyard is intimately aware of another caretaker standing silently by a jade pagoda to the south. The angle between them, the distance, the relative position to the axis of shadow—this is their language. It is an intricate, silent choreography of spatial awareness. If one steps two paces to the left to align precisely with the geometry of a carved aqueduct, it sends a ripple of meaning through the entire collective consciousness of the valley. It is an endless, silent symphony of perfect positioning.

The tools they carry—the bound-grass brooms and primitive bronze adzes—serve a purpose far beyond mere maintenance. The sweeping of the sweeping of the plazas is a spiritual necessity. Without wind to blow debris across the rock, the dust they sweep is entirely their own creation. It is a closed, perfect loop: they strike the brutal, young rock with bronze, shearing off microscopic layers of granite dust. They sweep this dust across the mathematically leveled stone until it reaches the misty borders of the shadow-lines. There, the dust serves to remineralize the dripping emerald velvet moss, which in turn feeds the thick-furred grazers, who provide the bone for the tools and the wool for the mantles, allowing the caretakers to return to the sunlit rock and strike it again.

It is an ecology functioning exactly like a clock, but a clock whose hands never advance. The energy simply orbits in an infinitesimal, perfect circle. 

When a caretaker strikes the stone, the pure chime rings across the emptiness. That sound is their highest art. They carve the terraces and build the impossible suspension bridges not to conquer the landscape or house a growing population—their numbers are as fixed as the mountains themselves. They build to shape the acoustics of the valley. Every isolated pavilion, every deep courtyard, and every runnel of water has been positioned to create a specific resonance. They have turned the entire caldera into an incomprehensibly vast instrument. A bronze chisel strikes stone, and the resulting sound wave travels flawlessly across miles of empty air, passing through specific architectural apertures, bouncing off friction-fitted walls, and reaching a distant listener completely undistorted. 

And then there is the deepest anomaly they represent within this frozen world. Within a landscape where everything is locked into an absolute gradient—where shadows are as permanent as bedrock and the sun never arcs—the caretakers are the universe’s only variable. 

When one of these tall, wool-draped figures slowly walks across a massive, sun-drenched plateau, trailing their broom across the pale stone, they create the only moving shadow in existence. 

For a few fleeting moments, their slender silhouette falls across the sun-baked granite. It cools the rock. Moisture from the thin air hesitates, threatening to condense in this sudden, brief darkness. Then, the caretaker steps forward, and the stone is immediately reclaimed by the eternal, unwavering sunlight. They are the only creatures in the valley capable of carrying darkness into the light, and warmth into the absolute dark. 

Above them, the enormous pale-winged gliders sleep on the fixed thermal columns, suspended permanently in the icy celadon sky. Beside them, ferns sit eternally in peak bloom, perpetually weeping tears of condensation into cold-copper basins. And in the center of it all, the silent caretakers weave their path. They pause at the edge of an emerald-choked ravine. They raise a bronze polishing stone. They kneel with slow, fluid grace that requires neither breath nor effort. And they begin to rub the rock, endlessly smoothing a surface that has already been smooth forever, existing perfectly, completely, and permanently in the exact center of now.
