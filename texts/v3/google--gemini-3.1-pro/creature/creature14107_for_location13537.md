If you trace the groaning, braided ironwood cables inward, deep into the densest knots of the emerging urban lattice, you will find where the true architects of this cosmic sump reside. They do not live *on* the city; they are the terminal blossoms of its vast, industrial root system. 

At the hollowed-out centers of the most colossal intersections, suspended above yawning gulfs of pure emptiness, hang rows of translucent, teardrop-shaped husks. They are woven from the same silken steel-roots extruded by the centipede-swarms, but here the threads are spun so fine they resemble spun glass. Inside these cold, amniotic sacks, bathed in the pale blue glow of captured circuit-moths, the denizens of the un-space are distilling themselves into being. 

When one is ready to emerge, there is no violent tearing, no wet birth. The silk simply dissolves, metabolizing into the dark air, allowing the inhabitant to unspool its limbs and step lightly onto the woven moss of a spindly gangway.

To look upon one of these beings is to witness a profound, agonizing compression. They are the flotsam of the higher dimensions—entities accustomed to moving through seven or eight axes of reality, now forcing themselves to calcify into the restrictive, primitive geometry of purely three-dimensional space. 

Physically, they are towering, delicate ascetics, standing twelve to fifteen feet tall. Their bodies mimic the biomechanical flora they have willed into existence. They possess an exoskeletal framework of polished, ivory-white ironwood, jointed like bamboo but curved with the grace of human ribs. Beneath this rigid cage, there are no organs of meat or blood. Instead, their internal cavities are filled with churning, bundled threads of luminescent phloem and cold, silver sap that pulses in time with the giant lung-gourds of the surrounding factories.

They move with the terrifying elegance of a deep-sea predator and the frail precision of a clockwork automaton. Gravity has only partial hold on them. As they navigate the single-file bridges over the bottomless dark, their long, multi-jointed digits barely graze the moss. 

They have no faces. Where a head should sit atop their long, stalk-like necks, their bodies bloom outward into a shifting, complex crest of fleshy petals and pale cartilage. These crests do not rest in a stable shape; they are constantly unfolding and folding back in on themselves in impossible, esoteric angles, flickering between solid and gaseous states. It is a sensory organ designed to read reality breakdown. Through these blooming, fractal crowns, they "see" the raw mathematics of space, sensing the density of the void, calculating the exact metaphysical tension required to stretch the city further into the dark.

They are laborers of an impossible scale. You watch unnumbered hundreds of them scattered across the distant, suspended scaffolding of the abyss. Spanning the vast silences, they act as living tuning forks for the newly synthesized physical laws. 

A single entity will approach one of the immense, taut cables of ironwood that form the city’s grid. With hands possessing too many pale, tapering fingers, it will grip the cable and pull, testing the tension of the local gravity and time. Though the atmosphere swallows all sound, the action sends deep, shivering, kinetic ripples through the entire cellular structure of the city. They communicate entirely through these tactile, structural vibrations—a symphony of silent tremors passing through the roots, coordinating the synchronized growth of the urban lattice over miles of empty void.

When they are not tuning the cables, they tend to the suspended lakes. A tall, pale entity will step off a ledge, drifting weightlessly toward a train-car-sized droplet of water shivering in the abyss. Penetrating the surface tension of the sphere without breaking it, the being suspends itself inside the cold water. There, bathed in the aqueous dark, its unearthly crest unfurls completely, wicking the water’s strange, anti-gravity properties, separating the chemical components of the liquid into perfectly formed, localized pockets of time—storing seconds and minutes like frozen pearls within their fibrous chests to be used later when the localized reality begins to fray.

But the most haunting realization of what these beings are comes when you observe the broader ecosystem. 

You watch an entity standing at the edge of a mossy precipice, perfectly still, its pale flower-face pointed out toward the deep, empty gulfs of the cathedral-void. And out there, in the hollow spans of nothing, an unformed tear in reality—a liquid shadow—detaches itself from the dark. 

The shadow sweeps quietly through the low-lit air. It cleanly envelopes a hound-sized, phosphorescent circuit-moth mid-flight, erasing it into absence. 

As the moth is consumed, the tall, pale entity standing on the ledge shivers. A surge of dim, blue, circuit-board light races down the length of its ivory-wood spine. 

These beings are not falling prey to the shadows. They *are* the shadows.

The tall, beautiful, biomechanical bodies walking the spindly gangways are merely the three-dimensional "fingers" of much larger, unfathomable things. Unable to fully fit their vast, multi-dimensional consciousnesses into this tiny pocket of un-space, they exist in two parts: the physical, plant-like bodies that meticulously build the city frame by frame, and the liquid, predatory shadows that prowl the void. The shadows exist to feed on the raw, reality-generating code of the circuit-moths, digesting it and funneling that psychic energy down into their physical avatars.

They are building this city to serve as a reality-anchor. Working in absolute silence in the near-freezing dark, these exiled, compressed gods weave xylem, phloem, and physical law together. They dream of expanding their gothic blister of un-space until it bursts its cosmic seams, engineering a brand new universe from the runoff of the old, one tightly braided root at a time.
