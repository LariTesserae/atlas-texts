To know the inhabitants of this submerged cathedral, you must first look at a single, amphibious hand gripping a braided vine. 

It is a hand built for immense, sustained tension. The fingers are thick, almost brutally blunt, ending in spade-like nails of dark keratin. The palms are crosshatched with profound calluses, scarred by the edges of chipped obsidian and stained a permanent, pale sap-green from the fibrous bleeding of the giant stalks. Between the fingers stretch thick webs of tough, leathery skin, capable of flaring wide to catch the heavy water or folding tight to allow the hand to pull, hand-over-hand, along the taut riggings of the city. 

The creatures to whom these hands belong call themselves something that cannot be spoken in air—a name that is, instead, a specific, deep-vibrating thrum in the center of the chest. To a human eye, they appear as magnificent, slow-moving giants, a seamless merging of mammalian bulk and benthic adaptation. They stand nearly eight feet tall from the tips of their powerful, rudder-like fluked feet to the smooth, sloping crowns of their heads. Their skin is smooth, hairless, and heavily insulated by a deep layer of localized fat to repel the numbing chill of the glacial meltwater. In color, they mirror the caldera: the mottled blue-gray of river stones bleeding into a soft, pale cyan along their bellies.

They do not swim with the frantic darting of the multi-faceted fish. Because they are negatively buoyant due to dense, heavy bones, traversing the open gulfs requires them to either walk heavily along the thickest horizontal stalks, or more commonly, pull themselves along the network of tension-lines. Watching one cross the open void is like watching an astronaut maneuver in zero-gravity, if the vacuum of space were replaced by a medium of heavy, rushing resistance. Their movements are painfully deliberate, governed by a muscle density that prioritizes endless, slow endurance over sudden speed.

Because the caldera is overwhelmingly, violently loud, their biology has discarded the need for external ears. Their heads are sleek and highly streamlined. In place of recognizable ears, thick bands of highly sensitive, ridged flesh run along their jawlines, down their thick necks, and channel straight down their spines. These ridges are organic hydrophones. To these beings, the deafening roar of the grinding stone anchors, the moaning stalks, and the snapping bivalves is not chaos—it is a high-definition, tactile map of their world. 

They "see" the acoustics. They can feel the exact size, speed, and trajectory of a massive, armored ray gliding three hundred yards away just by the specific pressure wave it casts against their spinal ridges. When the bone wind-chimes clatter, the inhabitants do not hear a tinkle; they feel a sharp, rhythmic percussion in their teeth, warning them of a shifting downdraft of cold water.

Their eyes, though large and luminous like pale moons, are mostly vestigial, covered by a milky, semi-translucent nictitating membrane. The light shafts from the high sun are blindingly bright in the pure water. They use this muted vision merely to distinguish the vibrant greens of the living architecture from the deep blue of the void, relying entirely on touch and vibration for everything else.

Their faces are solemn, dominated by broad, flat noses that can seal entirely shut, and wide mouths framed by thick, tough lips. They possess a set of internal gills, hidden behind heavy, muscular slits on their ribs, extracting oxygen directly from the frigid, rushing currents. Yet, they are fundamentally amphibious. They maintain a pair of massive, slow-beating lungs. Every few days, driven by an ancient, somatic clock, one will untether a bloated, gas-filled seedpod, clutch it tightly to their chest, and allow it to carry them upward. Breaking the surface into the thin, freezing mountain air is a profound, almost religious ritual. Up there, in the dry wind, they exhale deeply, take in the raw, un-watered sky, gulp a fresh expansion of air to maintain their internal ballast, and dive back down to the comforting, heavy noise. The absolute silence of the surface terrifies them.

Communication is an act of physical connection. Because speech in the open water would be swallowed by the roar of the fauna, they speak through the architecture. To converse, two individuals will place a hand on the same taut vine. One will constrict the muscles in their throat, producing a low, localized drone, while simultaneously plucking and sliding their callused fingers along the thick fibers. The resulting vibrations travel instantly along the rigging into the hand of the listener. It is a language of tension—a tactile morse code of tight plucks, long sliding groans, and sharp snaps. A master weaver can lay their back against the inside wall of a hollow, botanical dwelling and "listen" to the entire settlement through the wood, feeling the distant vibrations of children playing on the lift-lines or hunters butchering a snap-shell clam.

Their psychology is defined by the generational slowness required by their tools and their homes. They do not build; they convince. A young inhabitant, barely out of infancy, might be tasked with lashing a stone weight to a minor shoot on a redwood-thick stalk. That inhabitant will visit the shoot every day, scraping the bark with obsidian, bleeding the sap, applying tension, and guiding its growth into a perfect, looping hemisphere. It will take forty years for the pod to balloon to the size of a room. Only when the inhabitant is an elder passing into twilight will the dwelling be fully hollowed out and inhabited. To live here is to inhabit the handiwork of your grandparents, while painstakingly shaping the walls that will shelter the unborn. 

They live in a state of profound, isolated unison. Though separated by vast gulfs of crystal-blue water, and spending hours painstakingly grinding bone against stone in total solitary focus, they are never truly alone. Every movement tugs at the web. Every scrape reverberates. 

They are the tenders of the submerged grove, moving with the heavy grace of sinking stones, their chests thrumming in perfect, rhythmic harmony with the deafening symphony of their endless aquatic sky.
