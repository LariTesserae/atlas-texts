To understand the denizens of this shattered, infinite stump, you must first discard the concept of a populace that *inhabits* a place. The beings here do not live *on* the plateau; they are extruded by it. They are the immune system, the architects, and the captive laborers of the world-stump, born from the violent marriage of petrified biology and deep-time mechanics. 

They are the **Cambium**. 

If you stand perfectly still in the mist, suppressing the urge to cover your ears against the teleporting shrieks of the buzz-saws, you will eventually see them. They do not approach linearly. They exploit the same spatial origami that wrecks your depth perception. Out on the horizon, against the pale, mundane sky, you might spot a silhouette—a lanky, asymmetrical spire of dark matter. Then, practically instantaneously, accompanied by a sharp displacement of air and the smell of ozone, it snaps into existence towering just ten feet away from you. 

The Cambium are massive, stretching fifteen to twenty feet tall, though their posture is perpetually hunched, burdened by their own extreme density. At a glance, they resemble monstrous, bipedal insects made of industrial salvage, but closer inspection reveals a horrifyingly beautiful, bespoke biology. 

Their skeletons are formed of the same ironwood as the factory-cathedrals: dark, polished, and infinitely hard. These wooden bones do not articulate via conventional ball-and-socket joints. Instead, the joints are gnarls of rapidly shifting, living wood that crackle and splinter with every movement, continuously grinding and regenerating in real-time. Woven through this wooden framework are heavy veins of oxidized copper and rusted rebar, serving as rigid tendons. 

They possess no skin, only weeping layers of the pale, pulpy plant tissue you see grafted to the factories. This tissue hangs from them in slick, heavy drapes, collecting the perpetual condensation of the mist. They drip constantly, leaving trails of oily water and translucent moss-spores on the polished floor. 

Where a human has a heart and lungs, the Cambium have heavy, transparent glass vats embedded within their ribcages of fused wood and steel. Inside these bell-jars boils the glowing, reality-warping sap of the world-tree. This is both their blood and their engine. As the hydraulic pile-drivers in the distance pound the earth, causing the puddles to shiver, the sap inside the Cambium shimmers in synchronized harmonic resonance. They are physically tethered to the rhythm of the city. 

They do not have faces. Where a head should be, there is a bulky, sensory apparatus that heavily resembles a brass gyroscope or an intricate astrolabe, spinning silently within a cage of woven roots. At the center of this mechanism is a single, faceted core of pressurized amber. They do not see via light. The mundane daylight above is useless to them. Instead, the amber core allows them to perceive the stresses, folds, and tears in the local gravity and dimension. To a Cambium, the terrifying leaps in distance are visible topographies. They navigate the broken space like spiders walking a web, knowing exactly which microscopic groove in the floor to step on to bypass thirty miles.

Because the acoustics of the plateau are violently non-linear, the Cambium have no use for ears or vocal cords. The deafening, overlapping roar of the industrial environment would instantly destroy delicate biological hearing. Instead, they communicate through kinetic shockwaves. When one needs to "speak" to another, it raises a heavy, piledriver-like limb and slams it into the polished hardwood floor. Through the soles of their feet, they read the vibrations—complex, coded dialects of seismic force. A heavy thud might convey an entire schematic of a pipeline; a scraping grind might signal a localized spatial collapse. 

When you observe them working, their purpose becomes clear: they are fighting an eternal, losing war against the very machinery they maintain. 

The extraction of the sap warps reality, yet the factories need the sap to function. The Cambium act as the mitigators of this paradox. You will see them gathered along the arcing, Moebius-strip aqueducts—sometimes clustered upside down relative to your gravity, their iron feet locked into the grain of the wood. Their elongated forelimbs terminate in complex, multi-tool appendages: heavy calipers, spinning circular saws made of hardened resin, and thick, brass syringes. 

When a dimensional tear occurs—when the shriek of a distant turbine fractures the air into those black, hexagonal prisms of void—a specialized caste of Cambium arrives. These **Weavers** do not just repair pipework; they stitch space-time back together using biology. They extrude fresh, pale phloem from their own wrists, threading it through the geometric cracks in the void. They bleed their own glowing sap onto the tears, soldering the edges of reality back to the flat, polished floor. The hiss of boiling resin meeting raw vacuum sends localized tremors through the mist. 

In the shadows of the colossal cooling towers, you might find the heavier castes—the **Dredgers**. These entities are wider, built explicitly for the immense gravity and pressure of the deep-bore wells. They are fused permanently to their stations, their lower halves grown seamlessly into the wooden floor. They lack the mobility of the Weavers. Instead, they blindly rhythmically plunge massive iron pistons into the wood, their amber cores glowing blindingly bright as they haul the volatile hyper-sap to the surface. They are solitary, mindless, and eternal, existing only to pump and breathe out thick clouds of woodgas.

They generally ignore you. To the Cambium, a flesh-and-blood observer is an anomaly too soft to register on their seismic language, and too dim to show up in their amber gyroscopes. You are a ghost to them, slipping between the folds of their shattered space. 

Yet, there is a distinct melancholic grace to their existence. During rare lulls in the tectonic pressure, when the pile-drivers briefly alter their tempo, you might witness a Cambium pause. The spinning astrolabe of its head slows. It raises a heavy, rust-flaked hand, and gently swats at one of the spindly, moth-like assemblages of wood and wire that flit through the steam. The motion is not aggressive. It is careful. Delicate. For a fleeting second, against a backdrop of reality-tearing industrial cathedral-factories and vertical, neighboring worlds, a towering monstrosity of iron and weeping wood engages in play.

Then, an invisible spatial fold snaps against its shoulder. The air flashes with black prisms. The roar of a piston twenty miles away detonates instantly inside your ear, and the Cambium is gone, instantly relocated to a shattered aqueduct on the horizon, locked back into the eternal labor of keeping the flattened world from tearing itself apart.
