To live in this place is not to endure the cold, but to feast upon the violence. 

Because there are no animals here—no soft, hesitating minds to process fear or seek shelter—the biological imperative of the plateau has discarded caution entirely. What thrives in the roaring, slush-choked arteries of the bone-city is a sprawling, unified ecology of concussive force. 

The jade and cyan flora that choke the waterways are not plants as one might understand them in gentler, sun-dappled ecosystems. They are heavily armored, biomechanical marvels, woven entirely from hyper-pliant cartilaginous cellulose and flooded with a toxic, gelatinous antifreeze. Because there is no soil, they anchor themselves directly to the polished, bone-white beds of the viaducts. Their holdfasts are massive, claw-like calcifications that drive microscopic, diamond-hard filaments into the porous surface of the ancient shell, locking themselves against the drag of the glacier melt with a grip of terrifying permanence. 

They do not merely survive the endless barrage of jagged ice boulders; they require it. The furious, whipping motion of their heavy rubbery fronds isn't just the passive effect of the raging current. It is predatory. They are piezoelectric lithophytes. They lack mouths or leaves, but their dense cellular structures are packed with crystalline membranes that generate metabolic electricity when physically struck. 

When a half-ton chunk of glacial ice hurtles down the aqueduct and smashes directly into a cluster of thick cyan stalks, the plants do not snap. Instead, they absorb the kinetic shock, their heavy bodies snapping backward like high-tension cables. The blunt-force impact triggers a rapid, cascading spark of bio-current down their stems. In the blinding alpine sun, surrounded by the deafening thunder of the collision, the dark jade fronds instantly flash into blazing, incandescent neon cyan as they convert the physical strike into pure caloric heat. Up and down the viaducts, as thousands of ice blocks smash through the channels, the vegetation pulses and strobes with furious bioluminescence, a rhythmic, blinding lightshow synced perfectly to the heavy drumbeat of the destruction.

This continuous barrage of impacts shapes a bizarre, brainless consciousness. There is no central nervous system, no quiet reflection, only an immediate, collective sentience dictated by pressure and vibration. Through the deafening bass frequency roaring through the rushing water, the kelp can "feel" the shape of the river. A particularly massive ice boulder tumbling from the glacier above casts a deep acoustic shadow, a ripple of dense pressure that precedes it down the channel. In response, the cyan mats flex and coil their muscular bodies, weaving themselves into a tight biological mesh to catch the blow and drink the kinetic energy, releasing clouds of nutrient-rich glacial flour that they trap in thousands of tiny, rasping pores along their lengths.

Yet, this furious cycle in the water is only half of the ecosystem. The roaring machine of the hollow city requires vast emptiness to function, and the life here has evolved to utilize that profound void without filling it. 

As the rushing slush flails against the kelp, grinding and splashing, it kicks up a heavy, perpetual mist. Caught within that moisture are the aerosolized spores of the cyan flora—microscopic, glassy diatoms floating free from the violent waterways. Driftwinds carry these droplets up and out over the immense, desolate plazas and into the hollowed, eighty-foot domes.

In a normal environment, the spores would settle to the floor. But inside these cavernous husks, the air itself is fiercely alive, vibrating with the chest-rattling thrum of the city’s acoustic amplifier. The intense resonance prevents the spores from falling. Instead, the microscopic seeds are locked in suspension, caught in invisible, standing sound waves. They dance in the vast empty spaces, forming gigantic, swirling cymatic mandalas out of the mist—perfect, rotating geometric patterns of floating moisture organizing themselves to the heavy bass notes of the environment. 

Eventually, the mist drifts against the inner curvature of the organic shell. The freezing temperatures flash-freeze the moisture, pasting the spores to the bone-white walls in thin layers of rime ice. Here, in the closest thing the city has to stillness, the flora experiences its brief, silent phase of gestation. The frozen spores drink the residual moisture, erupting into incredibly delicate, fractal networks of glowing cyan frost that spread in intricate, capillary webs across the vaulted ceilings. 

For a few hours, a dome may glow from within as the rime-ice lichen spreads, haunting the empty chamber without disturbing its vastness. But the peace never lasts. The ice river above provides no respite. Soon, a massive collision of slurry in the aqueducts produces a sudden, concussive spike in the bass frequency. The acoustic shockwave blasts through the hollow biology of the dome, and the sheer vibration violently shatters the rime ice. 

The frost bursts from the walls in a glittering explosion of diamond-bright snow. Countless millions of fully gestated, microscopic seeds cascade down through the vibrating air, falling back into the icy slurry below to aggressively anchor themselves and begin the violent, thrashing cycle anew. 

Thus, the profound emptiness of the city's architecture is meticulously maintained. It remains hollow, massive, and waiting, acting simultaneously as a colossal acoustic engine and an empty, resonating womb. There is no decay here to accumulate on the floors, no detritus to sweep away, no minds to mourn or adapt. There is only the immortal circuitry of the fossil-city, the blinding alpine light, and a singular, wrathful organism that knows existence only as a deafening, radiant collision.
