To prepare an ordinary human for a journey to the Endless Catchment—as this vast, quiet world of wet stone might be called—requires a shift in mindset. You are not preparing for a wilderness hike; you are preparing for a high-altitude, deeply flooded architectural labyrinth where the terrain itself is technically a building, yet entirely devoid of the comforts of civilization.

Here is the comprehensive preparation guide for surviving and experiencing this haunting, planetary-scale monument.

---

### 1. Physical Conditioning & Acclimatization
**The Challenge:** The air is thin, and the terrain is entirely three-dimensional. There are no flat roads, only stairways, ledges, and uneven terraces. 
*   **Altitude Prep:** You must train for high-altitude exertion. Before the jump, spend time at elevations exceeding 10,000 feet, or use hypoxia-training masks. Without acclimatization, the brisk air here will leave you gasping, inducing headaches, nausea, and severe fatigue within hours.
*   **The Stair Master:** Your lower body will take brutal punishment. Every step you take will be on hard, unforgiving rock, requiring you to step up, down, or across wide gaps. Train by climbing stairs with a heavy pack. Focus intensely on knee and ankle stabilizers; the risk of a sprained ankle on uncarved, jagged scree is incredibly high.

### 2. Apparel & Footwear (The War Against the Wet)
**The Challenge:** It is perpetually damp, brisk, and abrasive. The environment is actively trying to siphon your body heat through wet clothing and cold stone.
*   **Footwear is Life:** Do not bring standard hiking boots. You need *canyoneering* or *approach* shoes. They must feature sticky, soft-rubber soles designed to grip wet, algae-slick stone. High ankle support is mandatory due to the rutted, uneven dry-stack nature of the ground. 
*   **Base Layers:** 100% heavy-weight Merino wool. Cotton will kill you here. Merino retains insulating properties even when soaking wet and will not hold odors.
*   **Outer Shell:** A top-tier, highly breathable Gore-Tex (or multiverse equivalent) hard shell (jacket and pants). You will constantly brush against wet, weeping walls. 
*   **Gloves:** Neoprene or waterproof leather climbing gloves. You will frequently need to use your hands to scramble over slumped walls and shallow canals. Uncut, raw basalt and slate will shred bare hands in a day. 

### 3. Shelter & Sleep Systems
**The Challenge:** There is no soil, no wood, and nowhere dry. The ground is a heat-sink.
*   **Freestanding Tents Only:** You cannot drive a tent peg into dry-stacked stone. Your shelter must be entirely freestanding (a dome or geodesic design). Bring heavy, durable fabric to resist abrasions from the untreated rock beneath it.
*   **Elevated Sleep:** A standard sleeping pad is risky; if your tent floor floods from a sudden shift in runoff, you will wake up in freezing water. Bring an ultra-light, collapsible camping cot to elevate you above the stone, paired with a closed-cell foam pad to block the cold air beneath the cot.
*   **No Fire:** There is zero combustible material in this world. You cannot build a fire. All heat must be generated by your body and trapped by an extreme-weather synthetic sleeping bag (down feathers will lose all loft if they get damp).

### 4. Hydration & Rations
**The Challenge:** Water is everywhere, but it sits in open-air cisterns full of unknown algae and amphibians. There is absolutely nothing to eat.
*   **Filtration:** Do not trust the pristine look of the weeping rock. Multiverse biology is unpredictable. Sucking a parasitic organism from an alien amphibian could be fatal. Bring a mechanical pump filter (sub-micron) layered with UV-light sterilization or iodine tablets. 
*   **Food:** You must carry 100% of your caloric needs. Because the air is cold and the physical demand is sheer, plan for 4,000 to 5,000 calories a day. Since you cannot forage for fuel, pack an isobutane camp stove for boiling water. Most of your food should be freeze-dried or ready-to-eat (MREs, energy bars, pemmican). 

### 5. Navigation & Movement
**The Challenge:** The labyrinth is claustrophobic, three-dimensional, repeating, and lacks standard planetary markers. 
*   **Compasses and GPS:** Do not assume this world has a magnetic north. Furthermore, high stone walls will block line-of-sight and any theoretical satellite signals. 
*   **Ariadne’s Thread:** Treat this environment like a cave system. Establish a base camp and use physical wayfinding to explore. Bring heavy-duty mountaineering chalk to mark arrows on dry patches of stone under overhangs. Better yet, use brightly colored, biodegradable surveyor’s tape tied around loose rocks to mark your path.
*   **Lighting:** The narrow alleys will fall into deep shadow even during the day, and nights will be pitch black due to heavy cloud cover. Headlamps with massive battery reserves are essential.

### 6. Medical & Hygiene Preparation
**The Challenge:** Constant moisture leads to specific bodily breakdowns.
*   **Foot Care:** Trench foot is your primary enemy. You must bring drying powder (talcum/antifungal) and change into dry socks every night. Never sleep in the socks you walked in. Put waterproof bandages or moleskin on "hot spots" before they become blisters.
*   **Abrasions:** The raw, non-chiseled nature of the stone means minor cuts and scrapes are inevitable. Bring a robust first-aid kit with liquid bandage, waterproof surgical tape, and heavy antibiotics. 

### 7. Psychological Preparation
**The Challenge:** The sensory deprivation, acoustic deadness, and scale of the place can induce a unique form of agoraphobia combined with claustrophobia.
*   **The Silence:** The acoustic-dampening qualities of the porous stone mean you cannot rely on sound to find a lost partner. If you travel with someone, stay within visual range. A shout will barely carry down an alleyway. Consider bringing tether-radios with throat mics.
*   **The Scale:** The sheer, endless monotony of stacked stone—billions upon billions of rocks moved by muscle alone—can provoke profound existential dread. Visitors often experience "Scale Shock," an overwhelming sense of insignificance upon realizing a civilization built a world-spanning machine and then vanished.
*   **Grounding:** Bring small, brightly colored items from home (a photograph, a book, a piece of colorful cloth, an electronic device with music). The visual palette of this world is endlessly grey, olive, rust, and pale water. Having a tether to human vibrancy will keep away the melancholic trance that the rhythmic dripping and whispering water frequently induces.

### Upon Arrival
When you step out of your portal onto the fitted stones, stop. Do not move immediately. 

Take a deep breath of the thin, cold air. Let your eyes adjust to the ambient grey light. Listen to the soft, glassy *clinking* and the planetary hiss of sliding water. Secure your footing on the wet slate. Understand that the landscape is not dead; it is functioning perfectly, just as it was designed to do eons ago. You are moving through the veins of a vast, sleeping engine. Step carefully, leave no trace, and do not lose your way.
