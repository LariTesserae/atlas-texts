To exist in this place is to be subjected to a continuous, concussive trauma. 

First and foremost is the noise. There is no air to cushion it; the environment is entirely submerged in a deep, heavy, amniotic ocean, and water transmits sound with agonizing efficiency. The roar is absolute, multidirectional, and physical. It is the shrieking grind of colossal, unlubricated gears, the low-frequency thrum of hyper-cavitation, and the staccato, percussive booming of things simply ceasing to exist. The sound does not enter through the ears—it vibrates directly through the skeleton, turning the marrow into liquid tremor.

The water itself is thick, tepid, and suffocatingly dark. It hovers at a temperature just shy of feverish, warming the skin but offering no comfort. In this regional, deep-basin expanse, the seabed is a geography of sudden, violent trauma. There are no smooth stones or rolling dunes here. The terrain is composed of freshly sheared basalt, extruded tectonic glass, and jagged upthrusts of newborn bedrock. The geography is infant, cracked and splintered into impossibly sharp angles, as if the crust is being spat out from the mantle and flash-cooled by the sea in real time. 

Scattered across this low, jagged expanse are colossal silhouettes of industry. They are immense, solitary structures—miles apart, looking like the drowned engines of a planetary-scale factory. Titanic turbines, stripped of whatever housing they once wore, churn violently in the dark. Vast, mechanized pistons the size of city blocks pump rhythmically against the crushing pressure. Miles of thick, rubberized cables and exposed conductive coils lie draped over the sharp geological upthrusts like the entrails of mechanical gods. The machinery is fully electrified, bleeding raw, arcing voltage into the saltwater. Deep sub-aquatic transformers hum with catastrophic power, creating localized magnetic fields that rip the water into spinning, super-heated vortices. 

And clinging to every jagged rock, every spinning turbine blade, and every crackling high-voltage line is an explosion of violently lush vegetation. Because there is not a single animal in this ocean—no fish, no mollusks, no drifting microscopic zooplankton—the flora has inherited the entire water column. It is a choking, hyper-dense jungle of deep-sea gigantism. Great, fleshy leaves the size of sails whip helplessly in the mechanical currents. Ropes of black and bruised-purple kelp, fibrous as steel cable, coil around the industrial pistons. The plants do not photosynthesize; they feed on the immense ambient heat, the raw electrical bleed, and the kinetic friction of the water.

They grow at a terrifying, visible speed. Vines snake upward toward the dark, only to be sucked into the roaring intake valves of the terraforming engines. The turbines instantly shred thousands of tons of this lush vegetation into a fine, bright-green mist, which is forcefully ejected from the exhaust vents. This mechanized slaughter acts as a grotesque substitute for pollination; the green mist is hyper-saturated with spores that instantly latch onto the jagged rocks to begin the frenzied growth cycle anew. Life here is an unending, hyperactive meat-grinder of botanical creation and industrial destruction.

But the sheer kinetic violence of the water and the machines is only a symptom of a deeper sickness. Reality itself is buckling under the strain. 

The turbulence of this ocean is not merely physical; it is ontological. As you watch the strobe-light flashes of blue electrical arcs illuminate the dim, green-choked water, the physics of the space begin to glitch. Pockets of the warm, heavy current suddenly lose their liquid properties. A cubic acre of ocean will instantaneously transform into a vacuum grid of screaming, neon-rimmed geometry, entirely devoid of water, only to collapse back in on itself a microsecond later with a thunderous *crack* of implosion. 

The water forgets which way gravity pulls. Great rivers of fluid shear off and flow upward, carrying tons of screaming, fast-growing vines with them, before hitting invisible spatial walls and shattering like solid glass. The massive, scattered engines seem to be both causing and fighting this breakdown—their titanic gears desperately trying to anchor the physical laws of the space, even as the fabric of the dimension tears like wet paper around them.

The darkness is absolute, broken only by the violent, stuttering flashes of failing physics and electrical overload. In those brief bursts of light, the density of the place becomes overwhelming. Every inch of volume is filled with churning water, shredded plant matter, grinding iron, and the jagged, neon scars of reality ripping at the seams. It is a deafening, boiling, chaotic storm-state—an industrial womb locked in a perpetual cycle of aborting and regenerating its own existence, where the machines scream, the ocean shatters, and the eyeless weeds endlessly consume the wreckage.
