You stand at an immense altitude, breathing air so thin and dry it tastes faintly of ozone. The sky above is a deep, saturated indigo, fading to a blinding white around a sun that hangs like a brilliant, unwavering spotlight in the zenith. 

Below your boots, the ground is an expanse of raw, embryonic geology. The landscape has the sharp, terrifying perfection of a world just bursting into existence. Nothing here has been smoothed by time, wind, or water. There is no sand, no soil, no dust, no rivers. Instead, the crust is a jagged, freshly extruded mantle of obsidian, peridotite, and raw metallic ores. The rock fractures along perfect, razor-sharp cleavage planes, creating a terrain of geometric thrust-faults, deep untouched canyons, and vast hexagonal plateaus. The topography is a fractal hazard of sharp edges and mirror-smooth facets, untouched by the slow wearing of erosion, pristine and unweathered.

Despite the harsh, high-altitude clarity and the absolute lack of water, the landscape is not dead. Instead, it plays host to an impossible, sprawling biology. 

Rising from the stark, jagged bedrock are towering, organic mega-structures that look like the fever dream of a mechanized civilization—vast towers, sprawling arrays, and colossal cooling stacks. Yet nothing here was built. There are no foundations, no rivets, no walkways, no signs of intelligent intent or habitation. This is a wild, naturally evolved forest of pure industry. 

The vegetation is rooted directly into the raw silicon and iron of the planet's crust. Trunks as thick as skyscrapers are woven from braided carbon nanotubes and deep-earth superconductors, sprawling upward into lush, incredibly dense canopies. But rather than leaves of chlorophyll, these trees branch into millions of rigid, epitaxial fins and iridescent photovoltaic wafers. The foliage is lush and overgrown, an abundant, verdant tangle of thin-film arrays that shimmer with metallic greens, golds, and iridescent blues. Thick, weeping vines of coiled induction-wire drape from massive load-bearing boughs, while the undergrowth is a dense, tangled mat of crystalline circuitry and parabolic blooms turned to face the sky. 

You can see for miles across a vast, empty basin. The scale of the place is staggering. But for all the lush complexity of the forests, they are sparse in their distribution. They grow in massive, localized groves that span hundreds of acres, but between each grove lie miles and miles of empty, unbroken geometry—vast voids of pure, sharp bedrock where nothing exists at all.

As you step closer to one of the great groves, the temperature rises. The air is locked in a comfortable, dry warmth that permeates everything, radiating outward from the massive plants like the heat from an oven door left perfectly ajar. The flora act as giant thermal sinks, pulling telluric warmth from the deep crust and bleeding it slowly through their fractal arrays of cooling fins, creating a bubble of ambient, steady heat in the high-altitude chill.

But the most striking thing about the landscape is the silence. 

For all the visual implication of heavy industry—for all the massive, levitating rotors suspended within the transparent boles of the great trunks, spinning perpetually on frictionless magnetic bearings—there is no roar of engines. There is no hiss of exhaust, no clatter of assembly. Because there is no water, no weather, and no animal life to disturb the air, the silence is profound. The only sound is the faintest, sub-audible hum of immensely powerful electromagnetic fields, a silent pressure you feel vibrating gently against your teeth and collarbones. 

Everything before you exists in a state of absolute, unchanging equilibrium. The sun remains blazing and steady. The frictionless rotors spin continuously without ever accelerating or decelerating. The enormous solar leaves do not sway, for there is no wind to move them. There is no growth, no decay, no seasons. The sheer, sharp rocks will never weather, and the industrial flora exists in a perfect, time-invariant loop, silently refining the raw electrical and geological potential of the world in an eternal, unwavering stillness.
