You are suspended in a high-altitude ocean, a vast, frigid body of water held miles above the planetary crust. The light here is hard, bright, and crystalline, striking down through the unbroken surface from a thin, alpine stratosphere. Because there is no silt, no sand, and no soil anywhere in this world, the water is exceptionally clear. You can see for miles in every direction, an endless expanse of cold, pale blue.

There is no rock to anchor this sea. The ocean floor, stretching outward on a continental scale, is composed entirely of living tissue—a vast, pale, woven substrate of interlocking roots and fibrous plates. It is immensely old, smoothed into a polished, seamless basin by eons of water friction. This plant-mass is the architecture of the world, serving simultaneously as bedrock, continent, and machine.

Despite the fact that the entire environment is made of vegetation, the space feels cavernous and sparse. The ocean floor is a vast, empty plain, punctuated only at intervals of several miles by colossal, solitary structures. These are the Stalks. 

Rising from the fibrous basin up to the sunlit surface, the Stalks are hundreds of meters thick. From a distance, they look like the trunks of giant kelp or drowned redwoods, but as you draw closer over the immense, empty stretches of water, their true nature becomes apparent. This ecology has evolved to bypass the slow crawl of passive biology; it has achieved industrial mechanization through flesh and cellulose.

The noise hits you long before you reach them. It is profoundly, oppressively loud—an unrelenting, percussive symphony of heavy machinery churning underwater. 

The Stalks are enormous biologic engines. Their outer layers are armored in an iron-rich, metallic bark that has been worn completely smooth, gleaming with a dull, gunmetal sheen in the bright light. They do not sway in the currents; they create them. Deep within their hollow interiors, massive, heart-like hydraulic chambers expand and contract. You can hear the deafening, rhythmic *THOOM... THOOM... THOOM* vibrating through the cold water, hitting your chest like tactical pressure waves. 

Along the sides of these great pillars, vast biologicalvalves—thick as blast doors and fringed with hard, smooth cartilage—snap open and shut with the metallic *clack* of a factory press. As they open, they suck in millions of gallons of frigid water; as they violently contract, they jet the water out through high-pressure spiracles, driving the great, planet-spanning currents that circulate this sky-ocean.

Woven around the valves are immense, rigid leaves shaped like turbine blades. Coated in biologically deposited silica, they look and act like heavy glass impellers. The water rushing past them forces them to spin around the Stalk’s periphery, their roots grinding against internal fibrous gears. The grinding shrieks through the water—a high, metallic squeal that harmonizes perfectly with the deep bass of the hydraulic pumps. 

This mechanical rotation generates immense electrical differentials. Ribbons of bright blue bio-electricity, thick as tree trunks, arc crackling between the spinning impellers and the iron-barked hull of the Stalk. The sharp, sizzling *snap* of this raw power flash-boils small pockets of water, sending rhythmic trains of tiny, perfectly spaced bubbles rattling toward the surface.

There is abundant life here, but it does not behave like the wild, chaotic fauna of a coral reef. The animals are cogs, bound to the supreme, mechanized rhythm of the environment. Shoals of silver, armor-plated fish, numbering in the hundreds of thousands, move with terrifying, unvarying precision. They swim in rigid, geometric formations between the Stalks, timed perfectly to the acoustic beats. They approach the spinning impellers, darting through the crackling electrical arcs in the millisecond intervals between discharges, to scrape away accumulation and algae with razor-sharp teeth. They act as automated maintenance drones, keeping the biological factory perfectly clean, perfectly efficient. 

Thick, eel-like organisms, heavily insulated in rubbery blubber, plug themselves into the Stalks' secondary nodes. They absorb the excess electrical currents, glowing fiercely with internal wattage, before undulating away across the empty miles to deliver their charged energy to the ocean floor, powering the massive, unseen root network below.

Everything is in utterly perfect, terrifying equilibrium. At this elevation, with these mechanics, there are no seasons. The water temperature sits at an unchanging, frigid four degrees Celsius. There are no storms, no varying tides. No Stalk grows larger; no Stalk dies. The erosion is absolute and stabilized—every gear, every valve, every spinning blade has been worn into its absolute mathematically ideal shape millions of years ago, and is maintained exactly in that state. 

It is a world of deafening, roaring engines and crackling high-voltage arcs, vast empty spaces of bright blue water, and cold, relentless perfection. The great biological machines of the high sea pump, spin, and spark—never accelerating, never slowing down, frozen forever in the eternal, deafening rhythm of their own making.
