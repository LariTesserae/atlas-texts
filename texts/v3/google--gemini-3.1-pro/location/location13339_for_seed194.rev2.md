There is no light here, nor has there ever been. The landscape cannot be seen; it must be suffered, felt, and heard. 

Before you understand where you are, you are struck by the noise. It is a deafening, omnipresent roar, a deep acoustic resonance that vibrates inside the marrow of your bones. It sounds like a million wooden cellos bowing a single, endless, agonizing bass note, layered with a high, shrieking whistle that tears through the cool air. The volume is oppressive, making the chest ache and the teeth rattle, but it is not chaotic. It is steady, patient, and impossibly slow, shifting its pitch by only a fraction of a tone every few months as the tectonic pressures of the deep earth breathe and settle.

You are standing in the dark, enveloped in a chilling, bone-dry draft. Reaching out, your hands meet the landscape. It is not stone. It is not earth. It is smooth, cool, friction-less wood. 

This place is a hidden pocket, a subterranean cyst buried deep within the crust of an ancient, arid world. It is an enclosed, spherical micro-environment, perhaps no larger than a few miles across, but within this hollow cavity lies an entire botanical metropolis. The entirety of the space is dominated by a single, hyper-massive root structure that long ago colonized the void. Over millions of years, without a drop of water, drawing only on dry mineral exhalations and acoustic energy, this flora wove a world. 

And then, it was shaped. 

Running your hands along the surfaces in the pitch black, the sheer scale of the built environment reveals itself beneath your fingertips. You are standing on a suspended avenue of braided cellulose, iron-hard and perfectly level. The edge of the platform drops off into a terrifying, empty abyss. The air in this pocket is incredibly sparse; ninety-nine percent of the volume here is a yawning, hollow void, a massive expanse of nothingness cross-hatched by a fragile-feeling skeletal grid. 

Following the platform, you find the structures. Ladders entirely composed of knotted, petrified vines stretch upward and downward into the roaring dark. Hand-carved staircases spiral around colossal vertical pillars of wood. There are thousands of hanging spherical chambers, arrayed in dense, layered tiers like terraced housing on a cliff face. You can feel the primitive, manual craft in the architecture: joints held together by tension and ancient braided bark, pegs of hardened thorn driven into structural beams, hand-smoothed balustrades worn down by generations of unseen inhabitants. 

Whoever, or whatever, built this settlement possessed only the simplest of tools. There is no metal here, no mortar, no glass. Every plaza, every suspension bridge, every hollowed-out dome was created through weaving, tying, and slowly coaxing the living wood with stone blades. Yet the complexity is staggering. It is a true city, sprawling and dense with infrastructure, suspended like a delicate wooden chandelier inside a hollow sphere of earth. 

But it is entirely empty. There is no fauna, no vermin, not even the scuttle of a blind insect. The hands that braided these vines are gone, swallowed by deep time. 

The environment has settled into its final, primordial state. It is so ancient that the concept of decay no longer applies. Because the air is stripped entirely of moisture—a desiccated, non-aqueous atmosphere that smells faintly of static and cold dust—the wood cannot rot. Instead, it has fossilized into a state of supreme, eternal hardness. Eons of constant, roaring winds blowing through the hollow chamber have acted as a slow sandblaster, wearing away every sharp edge, every splinter, every tool-mark. The entire city feels as smooth as polished glass beneath your hands. The knots of the bridges have fused into seamless lumps. The thresholds of the empty wooden houses have been worn into deep, sweeping dips by the passage of millions of footsteps that ceased millennia ago. 

The weirdness of the place settles over you as the roaring drone continues to vibrate through the soles of your feet. This city is a massive, dry instrument. The architecture was not just built for shelter; it was tuned. The hollow spheres, the tense suspension bridges, the sweeping avenues of hollow wood—they were engineered to catch the deep-earth vibrations, amplifying them, riding them. The builders lived in a perpetual, deafening symphony of planetary stress, navigating the pitch-black void of their city not by sight, but by the echolocation of the roaring wind and the hum of the vibrating wood. 

Time moves at a glacial pace here. Nothing falls. Nothing grows. The temperature remains eternally cool, a crisp, chilling draft that circulates through the vast emptiness. When a subtle shift in the crust occurs outside this sealed pocket, the tension in the colossal root system changes, and over the course of a hundred years, the deafening chord of the city slowly bends to a new harmony. It is a dead, hidden masterpiece in the dark—a vast, empty, shrieking wooden grid, patiently waiting out the end of the world.
