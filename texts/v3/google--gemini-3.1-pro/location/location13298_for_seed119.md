This landscape is entirely contained within the weeping bounds of a colossal woven chamber. A hollow, bell-shaped environment of cathedral proportions, it is a staggering work of primitive, manual labor. The walls curve upward into a dizzying dome, constructed from interlocking timbers the thickness of ancient oaks, bent by forgotten hands into a relentless over-and-under pattern. Sealed millennia ago with thick slathers of bog-pitch and river clay, the structure has slowly petrified over centuries. It now rests in a state of resilient, weathered equilibrium; the woven basketry has mineralized into a stone-like shell, fused by time and pressure. Within these walls lies a staggering density of nothing. There are no supporting columns, no platforms, no internal structures to break the volume—just a vast, uninterrupted sphere of open, hollow air.

Sunk into a surrounding bog, the chamber’s low elevation ensures it is profoundly wet. Outer hydrostatic pressure causes the petrified pitch to bleed. Water weeps incessantly down the curving walls, pooling along the intricate seams and forming long, stalactite-like crusts of pale calcium that dangle from the black timber weave. The air is cellar-cool, heavy in the lungs, and permanently choked with intense, condensing mist. High above, at the very apex of the dome, the ancient weave has finally begun to unspool, leaving a jagged oculus open to the sky. Through this singular aperture, a slate-gray twilight filters down, illuminating the high fog but doing little to pierce the dimness of the lower bowl. A few sparse, ghost-white roots from the marsh above dangle through the oculus into the void, hanging perfectly still in the windless air, reaching blindly for moisture but terminating hundreds of feet above the ground.

The floor is a vast, man-made concavity of fitted wooden planks, deeply saturated, stained black, and seamlessly joined to the walls. It rests submerged beneath an ankle-deep lens of frigid runoff. Here along the bottom, the sheer emptiness of the air contrasts with a frenetic, bizarre ecology. The shallow water teems with millions of translucent, spring-loaded arthropods. Constantly moving among them are the room's heavyweights: sluggish, flat-bodied amphibians entirely lacking eyes or shells. Pale as grubs and roughly the size of millstones, they possess a heavy, calcified keel-bone protruding from their bellies. Driven by an ingrained, predatory rhythm as they pace the flooded basin, these creatures rear up on their stubby legs and violently slam their heavy keels down against the submerged timber floor.

The impact produces an earth-shattering *BOOM*. Because the wooden floor spans the entire width of the room like a taut membrane, and because the cathedral-sized empty volume acts as a flawless acoustic mirror, the bass frequency reverberates with terrifying power. The shockwave physically ripples the chilled water and vibrates deep in the chest, kicking off a violent biological chain reaction. Startled by the concussive force, millions of the microscopic arthropods simultaneously snap their bodies. They launch into the heavy air in vertical geysers, creating swirling clouds of silver, jumping static that hover briefly in the mist. 

That boom is the dinner bell.
