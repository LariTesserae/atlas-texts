The first thing you must understand about this place is the sound. It is not a noise, but a geography all its own—a physical, crushing architecture of sound that vibrates right through the marrow. It is the unbroken, multi-tonal roar of continent-sized dynamos and the howl of an endless stratospheric wind, layered into a chord of absolute, deafening perfection. Because the volume never fluctuates, it eventually becomes a kind of heavy, pressurized silence. 

You are standing in the high wireframe of a world. Below you is nothing but a vertical eternity of empty air and mist. Above you is a flat, shadowless canopy of pearlescent sky, radiating a steady, cool, and unchanging daylight. 

Thrusting up into this high-altitude void are vast pillars of rock—tectonic needles born from some sudden planetary violence so recent that the stone has not yet learned to weather. The basalt and silicate peaks are razor-sharp, fractured in perfect geometric planes, their edges gleaming and raw. Spaced fifty or a hundred miles apart, these young pinnacles serve as the anchor points for an impossible grid. 

Suspended between the jagged peaks is a skeletal, three-dimensional metropolis. It is urban in its staggering, grid-locked complexity: avenues of high-tension cables thick as rivers, tiered districts of towering generator housings, and layered transit strata of humming, unspooling conduits. Everything is heavily mechanized, driven by engines the size of moons. Yet, it is deeply, terrifyingly empty. There are no solid floors, no plazas, no enclosed volumes. It is an open lattice stretched across the sky of a giant world. A falling object here would plummet for days before hitting a surface. 

The air is brutally cold—hovering exactly at the razor's edge of freezing—and saturated with water. Up here, in the thin, high atmosphere, a perpetual, heavy condensation takes place. The steel struts and freshly sheared stone faces are constantly weeping. The industrial structures are designed to process this moisture. Powerful electromagnetic fields, generated by the hollow city's spinning turbines, pull the atmospheric wetness out of the air. This is the strangeness of the high grid: the water does not fall. Influenced by the sheer magnetic force of the factories, it forms into hovering, shivering horizontal rivers. Great slithering arteries of near-freezing water course through the empty gaps between the metal struts, flowing upward and laterally into the sprawling intake valves of the machines.

Despite the bitter chill and the bare, mechanized emptiness of the scaffolding, the grid is exuberantly lush. Clinging to the dark, raw rock and wrapping aggressively around the wet hyper-steel are dense, heavy mats of engineered vegetation. They are massive, plush things—emerald-green cryo-ferns, deep velvet mosses, and thick, fleshy vines that thrive on the constant wash of freezing condensation and static electricity. The leaves are broad and heavy, constantly shivering under the percussion of the ambient roar, beading with pearls of cold water. These thick botanical blankets soften the visual harshness of the metal, creating sprawling hanging gardens suspended in the void. They drink from the floating, magnetic streams of water, dipping root-tendrils into the airborne rivers as they pass.

But there is no one here to tend this garden, and no creature to inhabit it. The heavy, dripping fronds harbor no insects. The hollow, humming avenues of the suspended city see no birds. The ecosystem is completely devoid of neurology; it is a binary relationship between the living, electricity-drinking flora and the cold, wet, roaring machines. 

And it never changes. There is no day or night, only the steady, flat, silver light holding the fog in a state of glowing diffusion. The temperature never drops enough to freeze the water solid, nor does it rise enough to melt the frost that tips the emerald leaves. The rock does not erode. The machines never run out of momentum, screaming their eternal, unvarying industrial pitch into the void. The wind shears against the fresh edges of the stone, the water drifts through the air in perfect, suspended streams, and the lush vegetation stays locked in perpetual, dripping bloom. It is a loud, cold, vast equilibrium—a mechanized sky-city processing the mist forever, hanging empty in the heights of the world.
