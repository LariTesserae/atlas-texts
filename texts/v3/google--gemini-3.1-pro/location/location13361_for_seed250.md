You are suspended in a cool, biting atmosphere, bathed in the blinding clarity of high-altitude sunlight. There is no ground beneath you, and there is no ceiling above. Instead, the sky is dominated by an ocean that has been untethered from a planetary crust, drawn upward, and woven into a colossal, three-dimensional lattice. 

It is a roaring, hydro-dynamic architecture of water. Arteries of deep, oscillating blue seawater—some only a few hundred feet wide, others continents thick—arc through the stratosphere. Despite the astronomical volume of liquid, the overwhelming sensation of the environment is one of vast, echoing emptiness. The hollow voids between these suspended sky-rivers stretch for dozens of miles. You can gaze through the negative space, past curtains of drifting mist, to see distant ribbons of ocean looping toward the upper atmosphere, curving beautifully against a pale sky. 

The sound is absolute. It is a deafening, continuous thunder, the roar of millions of tons of water rushing at terrifying velocities. The environment is in a state of hyperactive turbulence. Where these massive oceanic veins intersect, they do not merely splash; they collide with the force of planetary tectonic plates. 

Because here, the physics of fluid dynamics have been commanded by forces far beyond modern understanding. There are no machines in sight, no metal struts or built structures, but the invisible framework holding the world together is undeniably engineered. It is a localized restructuring of gravity and surface tension. When two torrential sky-currents crash together, the water does not shatter into random spray. Rather, forced into unseen gravitic molds, the liquid flash-freezes into violently sharp, pristine geometric shapes. Immense, towering plains of vertical water shear against each other, creating hard, razor-straight edges and perfect platonic angles. It is a nascent, raw geology made entirely of pressurized liquid and friction—pre-formation, unweathered, emerging and dissolving from one second to the next.

The water itself acts as a massive prism. The normal, bright daylight strikes the sheer, glassy walls of the rivers and the aerosolized mist swirling in the vast open pockets, refracting into thousands of blinding, intersecting rainbows. 

Life here is profoundly sparse, and utterly devoid of fauna. There are no birds navigating the empty channels, no fish swimming in the roaring veins. But strung through the outer, transparent layers of the water-currents are solitary, miles-long ribbons of translucent green. It is a specialized, high-tension sky-kelp. It does not root in soil, but rather anchors itself directly into the localized gravity-shears, trailing along the chaotic currents. When a river accelerates, these sparse emerald streaks are pulled taut, vibrating like guitar strings in the heavy, chilly air. 

Suddenly, a shift occurs in the invisible grid. The turbulence escalates. A mass of water the size of a mountain range surges upward, breaking away from an adjacent artery. It defies all natural vectoring, climbing vertically into the cold air before the engineered gravity violently yanks it sideways. The water hits a tension-barrier and flattens instantly, spreading into a thin, screaming sheet of roaring ocean ten miles wide and perfectly flat, devoid of a single ripple or imperfection. It is an environment perpetually under construction, a colossal, chaotic engine in the sky, loudly hammering liquid into shape across the void.
