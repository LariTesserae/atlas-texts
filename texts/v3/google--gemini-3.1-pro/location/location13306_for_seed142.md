The air up here does not breathe; it bakes. It is an utterly desiccated atmosphere, entirely stripped of moisture, tasting faintly of ionized dust and old, warm iron. 

You are standing on a monumental expanse of high altitude, though you would never know it from the topography. Eons ago, this might have been a jagged alpine range, but time and an eternity of dry, abrasive friction have worn the mountains down to gentle, swelling plains of polished basalt and fused slag. The ground is terrifyingly smooth. It stretches out for miles in every direction, dipping and rising in slow, majestic arcs like a sea of black glass frozen in the moment of a heavy swell.

It is wrapped in near-total darkness. There are no stars, no moon, no sun. A perpetual ceiling of heavy, static-charged darkness presses down on the high plateau. 

Yet, the world is remarkably warm. The heat does not fall from the sky; it radiates upward from the stone and outward from the sparse, titanic architecture that dots the horizon. 

Spread miles apart across the worn plains are the installations. They are vast, settlement-sized industrial arcologies, but they do not look built so much as geological. Their once-sharp angles have been ground away by the same deep time that leveled the mountains. Now, they resemble colossal, slumped domes and sprawling, aerodynamic ridges of matte metal and obsidian. 

These are machines of unfathomable scale. Engines as wide as cities, humming with a slow, planetary voltage. They operate in a state of flawless, perpetual equilibrium. There are no smokestacks belching ash, no chaotic sparks, no bustling shifts of workers. The production cycles stabilized millennia ago. Whatever they are processing—perhaps the heavy tectonic stresses of the crust below, or the ambient electromagnetic fields of the dead sky above—they do so without variation. The gauges, wherever they are hidden, have not ticked a fraction of a degree in a million years. 

Because of this profound stability, the landscape is almost entirely silent. The immense kinetic forces at work within the domes are muffled by thousands of feet of armored casing and the frictionless glide of magnetic levitation. The only sound is a deeply resonant, subsonic thrum—a heavy *hmmmmmm* that vibrates through the soles of your feet and warms the marrow of your bones. It is the steady heartbeat of an industrial organism that has achieved immortality through stagnation.

The only light comes from the undersides and exhaust vents of these sprawling machines. It is a sullen, cherry-red glow, the color of dying embers, bleeding out from beneath immense metallic baffles. These thin ruddy lines cut through the pitch blackness, casting long, warped reflections across the perfectly smooth, dark ground. The red light barely illuminates the space between the structures, leaving vast oceans of warm, empty shadow where one installation ends and the next, five miles away, begins.

There is absolute sterility here. Not a microscopic spore, not a dusting of lichen, not a single ragged weed could endure the total zero of the humidity. The concept of a plant is alien to this high, smoothed-out world. 

Yet, it is not entirely void of life. 

Drawn to the ambient, oven-like warmth of the great engine-domes, solitary things navigate the smooth plains. They are rare, perhaps one encountered every few miles. Spindle-legged and entirely inorganic, these scavengers are woven from spun silicon and filaments of conductive copper. They are no larger than hounds, blind, and beautifully fragile. They do not eat or hunt; they simply drift through the dark, their glass-needle feet clicking faintly—*tap... tap*—against the polished basalt. They are thermovores, slowly sipping the radiated heat and stray magnetic flux of the giant machines. When they accumulate enough energy, their internal coils glow a faint, dim violet for a moment, before fading back into the dark.

Out here on the flattened roof of the world, there are no seasons, no weather, no dawns, and no decay. There is only the vast distance, the comforting, oppressive heat, the deep vibration of immortal industry, and the dark stretching on forever.
