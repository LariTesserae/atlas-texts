You do not see this landscape. You can open your eyes, strain your pupils until they ache, but it will not matter. There is an absolute, profound absence of light here, a darkness so structurally complete that to navigate this world is to surrender entirely to touch, temperature, and a deafening symphony of sound.

You are high up. The barometric pressure betrays the altitude—the air is thin in its oxygen, yet paradoxically heavy, crushed under a perpetual, condensing burden of moisture. It is warm, an uncomfortable, cloying warmth like the inside of a living mouth. Torrents of warm rain fall unseen from an endless night sky, and the air itself feels like it is sweating.

Underneath your hands—because you must crawl, or at least feel your way forward—the terrain is ancient. This is a mountainous plateau that has been aggressively, patiently smoothed over eons. There are no jagged edges left. The stone is slick, deeply pocketed karst, worn into colossal, weeping domes and slick obsidian-like chutes by millennia of warm, acidic downpours. The rock trembles constantly. 

The vibration is the first clue to the massive, scattered architecture of this world. There are no cities here, no sprawling grids or paved roads. Instead, spaced perhaps hundreds of miles apart across the vast, continental dark, sit titanic industrial engines. They are so large they generate their own localized gravitational pulls and weather systems. You cannot see them, but you can feel their colossal output. They are the source of the deep, subterranean *thrum* that rattles your teeth. They are ancient, planetary-scale terraformers or exhaust vents, humming with a staggering, electrified power. Unseen rivers of superheated coolant rage through artificial canyons, mixing with the unending rains to create the suffocating, humid warmth of the heights. Occasionally, you cross a remnant of their infrastructure—a perfectly cylindrical pipe the size of a redwood buried in the mud, or a vast grid of iron grating, rusted and velvety to the touch, spanning a bottomless, roaring gorge. 

Because of the unending dark, you might expect a barren wasteland. Instead, the landscape is choked with ravenous, lush life. 

Without photosynthesis, the flora has turned to the heat and the iron. The vegetation is dense, layered, and impossibly thick. Fleshy, rubbery stalks push against your legs; massive, unseen canopies interlock overhead, dripping thick condensation. Vines as thick as industrial cables wrap tightly around the smoothed stone and the scattered metal ruins, pulsing with thermo-synthetic sap. The plants are hot to the touch, feeding directly on the geothermal and mechanical heat radiating from the great engines and the warm rains. Pushing through the underbrush is like wading through a tangle of warm, wet limbs. Thornless but abrasive, the foliage grows in frantic, high-saturation densities, occupying every cubic inch of viable air.

And it is loud. The sheer volume of the landscape is a physical pressure against the eardrums. There is the endless, rushing roar of thousands of unseen waterfalls spilling over the eroded cliffs. There is the deep, rhythmic, mechanical boom of the distant engines—*whump... whump... whump*—shaking the atmosphere. 

Woven through this mechanical roar is the frantic, overlapping cacophony of an hyper-active ecosystem. The darkness teems with life. Fauna is wildly abundant, moving at a relentless, hungry pace. Nothing here has eyes. The creatures navigate through highly evolved echolocation and heat-sensing pits. The air cracks with high-frequency clicks, booming subsonic groans, and the frantic clicking of mandibles. Winged things, fleshy and vast, displace heavy curtains of air as they sweep just inches above the canopy. Swarms of multi-legged scavengers scuttle over your boots, vibrating like tuning forks. 

The strangeness of the world lies in how life has integrated with the mechanized violence of its environment. Predators here have learned to mask their hunting calls by mimicking the rhythmic grinding of the industrial engines. You hear what sounds like a turbine spinning up, whining with metallic friction, only to feel the sudden, wet displacement of air as something massive and biological lunges into a flock of chittering prey immediately to your left. 

Blood falls with the rain. The smell of ozone, rust, and damp earth is overpowering. The ecosystem is in constant, violent motion—creatures breeding in the warm coolant floods, thermal-vines cracking open ancient steel bulkheads to root in the electrified guts of the machines, predators hunting enormous, blind herbivores by the heat signatures of their digestion. 

This world is vast enough to have its own obscure physics. The electrical fields generated by the scattered, roaring mega-engines are so intense that occasionally, the static charge in the torrential rain causes your hair to stand on end, and the deep, invisible vibrations of the machinery cause the very raindrops to hover and dance in mid-air before splashing against the warm, fleshy leaves. It is a high-altitude ocean of hyper-dense, sightless life, surviving on the roaring, mechanical heartbeat of a drowned and darkened continent.
