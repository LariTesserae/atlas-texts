The air here is a lungful of hot, wet velvet. 

It is a world of dim, sweltering shadows, buried under an infinite ceiling of heavy mist. The ambient temperature hovers just below an intolerable fever-pitch, turning the moisture in the air into a constant, weeping fog. Everywhere, condensation pearls and streaks.

But there is no earth beneath this fog. There is no stone, no soil, no bedrock. The ground is a limitless, sweeping expanse of crude, hand-pressed terracotta bricks, stretching out flat and low to a horizon swallowed by the steam. 

This is an architecture born without architects. It is a nascent, embryonic world of primitive masonry, spanning the scale of a continent, completely devoid of life. No algae slicks the wet bricks; no insects traverse the sprawling mortar; no footprints disturb the surface. Yet the landscape is anything but still. 

It is fiercely, violently dynamic, though it shifts with the eerie, muffled quiet of a submerged dream. 

Miles away across the sparse, empty terracotta plains, a dim silhouette begins to writhe. Out of the flat ground, wet clay extrudes itself into being, rising in massive, spiraling columns. As it pushes upward, the mud instantly parses itself into millions of individual, sun-baked bricks. You can see the indentations of invisible thumbs pressed into their faces—the memory of a tool-using hand, manifested by a deeply confused reality. 

These columns aggressively self-assemble into staggering, mile-high ziggurats, megalithic dolmens, and sprawling, labyrinthine aqueducts. The construction happens at a furious, turbulent pace, a chaotic storm of masonry. Arches fold out of other arches like blooming flowers. Staircases build themselves into the mist, corkscrewing through physical space, bending at angles that make the eye ache, sliding through higher dimensions where gravity holds no jurisdiction.

Despite the monumental speed and scale of this creation, the world remains overwhelmingly quiet. There is no roar of collapsing stone, no grinding of tectonic architecture. Because here, nothing erodes; nothing decays. This is a realm of pure, raw emergence. When a mile-long viaduct of crude stone reshapes itself, the materials do not shatter—they phase through one another in a seamless, frictionless blur, shifting from a bridge, into a floating ring, and then flattening back into a paved courtyard. The only sounds are the soft, rhythmic squelch of wet slip, the long exhalations of steam, and the gentle, endless patter of heavy condensation dripping from lintel to floor.

The hydrology of the place is bound to its endless, alien labor. Shallow canals carve themselves spontaneously through the pavement, filling instantly with boiling, mineral-rich water that bubbles up from nowhere. The hot water flows *uphill*, defying local physics, carried by towering mud-brick aqueducts that twist like woven reeds into the heavy skies. The sheer heat radiating from the structures bakes the wet clay into rigid ceramic in seconds, locking it into place, only for a new wave of boiling water to wash over it, reducing it to workable slip again, resetting the canvas for the next silent explosion of geometry.

Because of the vastness of the geography, these hyperactive monuments are spaced far apart. Sitting at the center of the troweled plain, you are surrounded by miles of empty, damp, perfectly smoothed foundation. But on the distant edges of vision, the silhouettes of impossible industry play out in the gloom. 

To the east, a colossal step-pyramid builds itself upside-down, hanging from a point of nothingness in the sky, rapidly descending to meet the ground. To the west, a thousand miles of primitive stone wall coils into a tight knot, folding inward until it vanishes entirely from this plane of existence, leaving behind a slick puddle of steaming mortar. 

It is a world trapped in the frantic, mindless, deafeningly quiet act of its own invention, building an ancient, empty metropolis out of hot mud and dripping fog, forever and ever.
