Before the shape of the world can be seen, it must be heard. 

It is a roar. Not the hollow rushing of wind or the chaotic crash of a waterfall, but a dense, living wall of sound. It is a relentless, overlapping mechanical thrum: the grinding of millions of mandibles, the dry, electric rasp of chitin heavily stridulating, the collective drumming of billions of legs, and the low, vibrating hum of ventilation wings beating in unified, resonant waves. The noise is absolute. It presses against the eardrums, filling every frequency, making the heavy air physically vibrate. To stand here is to be immersed in a biological engine of staggering, deafening scale.

There is no light, save for weak, bruised slivers of twilight that filter down through ventilation shafts hundreds of feet above. As the eyes adjust to the profound gloom, the scale of the environment begins to reveal itself. This is a high mountain gorge—or at least, it once was. The bedrock, the original stone of the valley, is buried hundreds of feet below. Everything visible, every surface, every towering spire and sweeping archway, has been entirely constructed by biological imperative. 

It is a megalopolis of chewed wood pulp, mineralized earth, hardened saliva, and stacked guano. It stretches for miles, an enclosed, roofed-over valley of layered, labyrinthine architecture. Massive, corrugated pillars of dried mud rise into the darkness, merging into vaulted ceilings that support successive tiers of galleries, nurseries, and thoroughfares. 

Despite being entirely constructed, the environment is ancient and deeply weathered. It exists in a climax state of slow decay and steady renewal. The lower levels, compressed by the immense weight of the superstructure, have fossilized into a rich, peat-like loam. Older arches sag under the burden of centuries, their surfaces crumbling and dissolving, while fresh layers of wet, dark mastic are continually accreted by unseen legions above. There are no sudden changes here, no rapid seasons—only the patient, gradual cycling of biomass over millennia. The architecture is in a perpetual, slow-motion state of collapsing into compost and being rebuilt into architecture.

Though situated at a high, thin-aired elevation, the interior of the megastructure maintains its own aggressive microclimate. The collective metabolism of an overwhelming, uncountable biomass generates a thick, stifling heat. This warm air rises through the dark causeways, carrying respiratory moisture until it meets the cold exterior chill pressing down on the canopy vault. There, the moisture condenses in thick beads along the ceiling. It falls back down in an intermittent, tepid drizzle, pattering softly against the organic masonry. Shallow pools of tannin-dark, nutrient-rich water collect in sunken plaza-bowls, breeding grounds for massive, unseen things that ripple the surface.

Rooted directly into the fibrous, nutrient-dense architecture is an overwhelmingly lush, shade-adapted jungle. With no mineral soil to speak of, the vegetation parasitizes the built environment. Monstrous vines with leaves the size of dining tables crawl up the corrugated pillars, their deep, almost-black foliage desperate to catch the scattered pinpricks of light. Heavy mats of weeping fern and thick, spongy mosses drape over collapsed causeways. Ghost-white saprophytes and colossal bracket fungi bloom from the walls, feeding directly on the decaying structure. Aerial roots hang down in the damp, cavernous spaces like thousands of thick ropes, seeking out the condensation pools and driving wedges into the mud masonry, simultaneously binding the world together and tearing it apart.

Life here is absolute and inescapable. The density of fauna is suffocating. Every surface crawls. The builders themselves—blind, armored, moving in massive, specialized castes—pour over the pathways in rivers of rustling brown and black. But they are merely the foundation of a towering food web. Swarms of immense, flightless crickets and heavy-bodied cockroaches scavenge the organic debris. Giant centipedes, legs rippling in hypnotic, terrifying waves, actively hunt along the vertical mossy walls. In the damp depressions, pale, blind amphibians the size of small dogs sit motionless, croaking in deep, guttural belches that add to the overwhelming sonic wash. Scavenger birds and specialized, echolocating bats flit through the immense open spaces between the pillars, their wings brushing the damp air. 

To move through this landscape is to intrude upon an environment completely saturated with life. If you place a hand upon a wall to steady yourself, the wall squirms. If you step upon the spongy, yielding floor, the ground scatters. 

Yet, there is a profound stillness in the behavior of the place. It is not a frenzy. The deafening noise, the endless swarms, the dripping condensation—it is all steady, rhythmic, and perfectly balanced. It is a slow, churning permanence. A completely engineered biological world, built in the dark high altitudes, steadily breathing, slowly rotating its own matter through thousands of years of deafening, humid darkness.
