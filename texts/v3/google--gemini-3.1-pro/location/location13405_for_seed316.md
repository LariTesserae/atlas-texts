The boundary between earth and sky was erased eons ago. Here, at the bottom of the world—in a vast, low-lying continental basin stretching endlessly without horizon—there is only a dense, roaring continuum. The volume of this space is entirely filled; there is no void to be found anywhere. The atmosphere is a saturated, hyper-dense colloid of heavy water, warm mist, and pulverized, ancient matter. It is as thick as a deep sea and turbulent as a dying star. The sound of this place is a physical, crushing weight—a deafening, overlapping thunder of a billion waterfalls howling alongside endless, chaotic pressure waves. The noise is so total it vibrates the marrow in your bones, leaving you completely submerged in an eternal, terminal boil.

Everything here is profoundly old. The concept of erosion has been ground down to its absolute mathematical limit. There are no jagged edges left, no mountains, no hills, no stones. Whatever topography once existed has been digested over uncounted millennia into a frictionless, perfectly smooth slurry of fundamental silt. Mired in a cloying, sweltering warmth—the temperature of fresh, fevered blood—this mud behaves like a superfluid. Whipped by hyperactive geologic weather, the terrain does not stay on the ground. It surges upward in towering, slow-motion waves hundreds of feet high, mixing indistinguishably with the wet, heavy sky. You do not simply breathe in this environment; you filter it, drinking the tepid moisture that relentlessly condenses and cascades across your skin. 

But the chaos is not merely weather—it is a breakdown of physics itself. Because the matter here has been processed and worn down for so long, the fabric of reality has frayed almost to nothing. The turbulence extends beyond wind and water into the laws of nature. A colossal wave of mud arcing overhead might abruptly lose its subjection to gravity, flattening out into a ceiling of churning earth before shattering upward into rain that falls away toward the stars. Ribbons of dark liquid weave through the dense air, flowing in impossible, recursive knots. Spatial anomalies bloom and collapse in seconds; you might push a hand through the thick, wet resistance of the air, only to watch your arm stretch across a sudden fold in geometry, your fingers reappearing yards away. 

The ambient light is a murky, bruised violet-gray, a sickly twilight that seems to emit directly from the saturated water, endlessly scattered by the absolute density of the air. In this warm, sweltering gloom, shadowy shapes occasionally reveal the scattered remains of the Built. They have almost entirely surrendered to the eternal erosion—smooth, pale monoliths protruding from the roaring ground like river-washed humps of bone. They are the dense, stubborn anchor-points of a forgotten epoch. They do not decay anymore; they just sit as weeping rocks, bleeding heavy, rusted water endlessly into the maelstrom. Yet, when the reality-storms strike them hardest, these featureless stumps glitch. For a fractured second, they temporally project their original forms—soaring, impossible architectures of glass and intricate fretwork—before the turbulence screams through and shreds the ghostly memory back to blunt, wet stone.

Flora is almost totally absent, save for the immediate, violent slipstreams of these ancient artifacts, where life stubbornly persists. Pale, gelatinous string-weeds anchor themselves to the weeping stones with microscopic, unbreakable hooks. Lacking leaves or stiff structures, they are little more than raw, translucent nerves. They whip frantically in the deafening currents, photosynthesizing nothing, surviving purely by absorbing ambient warmth and the sheer kinetic friction of the screaming air.

Fauna is rarer still. Yet, due to the planetary scale of the chaotic continent, immense things occasionally drift through the murky upper layers of the storm. A sprawling, ghostly shape, miles across, might part the bruised clouds, gliding through the heavy atmospheric slurry like a translucent manta ray in an abyssal trench. Filter-feeding the chaotic, watery air, it possesses no eyes and no discernible organs—just biological machinery stripped to the barest minimum required to survive the reality-breakdown. It drifts in silence relative to the deafening roar, appearing for one terrifying, immense moment before folding itself through a tear in space and vanishing.

There are no days or nights here, no seasons to temper the madness. There is only the hyperactive, anthill-energy of a world that has reached the end of its life, yet refuses the stillness of death. It is a claustrophobic infinity, where the temperature is forever cloying, the density holds you in a crushing, wet embrace, and entire massive weather systems of screaming liquid earth collide into perfect, suspended mandalas of broken physics. It is the end of all things, endlessly roaring its chaotic destruction in the wet and the dark.
