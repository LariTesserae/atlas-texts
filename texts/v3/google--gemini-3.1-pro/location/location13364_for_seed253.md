You are adrift in a stratospheric ocean, drowning in the dark, miles above the earth.

This is the interior of a colossal, sky-bound chalice—an engineered biological sphere the size of a mountain valley, suspended in the freezing upper limits of the atmosphere by a taproot too vast to comprehend. There is no sunlight here. There is no stone, no soil, no glass, no metal. Every boundary of this world, every arching kilometer of its ceiling and sweeping descent of its floor, is made of ancient, hyper-dense cellulose.

It was constructed eons ago, grown through sheer biological architecture. Though there are no machines or tools in sight, the space is undeniably *built*. Nothing natural weaves itself into such perfect, calculating geometry. Immense, ribbed buttresses of iron-hard plant matter bow inward, holding back the unimaginable pressure of the water trapped within. Vaulted ceilings, miles across, interlock in flawless hex-weaves of pale, petrified xylem. The shaping intelligence is long gone, leaving behind a pure-plant ecosystem that has settled into an absolute, unchanging equilibrium. 

The environment has worn itself into a final, eternal state. Over millions of years, the slow, massive convection currents of the cold water within have eroded the engineered walls to a frictionless, glassy smoothness. It is all flowing curves and polished, sweeping concavities of dark wood, worn entirely free of splinters, leaves, or loose fibers.

The water itself is intensely cold, heavy, and absolute. Because the volume of this high-altitude membrane is so immense, the space feels overwhelmingly open and sparse. The structural ribs are leagues apart. Between them lies an expanse of frigid, pure, near-pitch-black liquid. 

It is incredibly loud. Water is a perfect conductor of sound, and this aquatic vault is a resonating chamber for the violence of the world outside and the life within. There is a continuous, deafening baseline roar—the sound of stratospheric jet-stream winds tearing across the colossal exterior of the pod. Beneath that roar is a rhythmic, chest-collapsing boom: the arterial thrum of the immense plant, pumping microscopic trace nutrients up from the earth miles below. 

Against this booming, omnipresent canvas of noise, the animals sing. Life here is incredibly abundant, but the sheer, cavernous scale of the enclosure means the density remains remarkably low. You can drift in the cold void for what feels like hours before encountering them, but when you do, they are legion. 

Massive, eyeless beings built of translucent cartilage and trailing, feathery gills glide through the gloom, their forms spanning hundreds of feet. They navigate by sound, emitting shrieks and clicking trills that ricochet off the polished wooden walls with deafening clarity. Beneath them swim immense schools of pale, soft-bodied organisms—millions of them moving as a single, shifting cloud, harvesting the biological snow that drifts down from the canopy’s vascular vents. In the open darkness, they look like nothing more than sparse dust caught in the occasional, incredibly dim flash of a predator’s biolhythmic lure.

There are no seasons here. No weather, no light cycles, no change. The interior temperature remains permanently, deeply cool. The water never drains. The ancient, built walls of polished plant-bone never rot or shift. The fauna lives, filter-feeds, dies, and dissolves back into the water to feed the great walls in a perfect, closed systemic loop. It is a loud, dark, suspended world, locked forever in heavily populated, echoing emptiness.
