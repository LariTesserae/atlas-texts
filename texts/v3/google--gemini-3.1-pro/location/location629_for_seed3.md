At this extreme altitude, the rock has no memory. It has not had time to weather. It extrudes continuously from the mountain’s core in pristine, shivering geometries—bismuth-like staircases of basalt, jutting out at violent, fractured angles into a sky that never brightens. The light holds at a permanent, bruised twilight, smothered by a heavy atmosphere that should not logically exist this high above the world. 

It is bitterly cool, but the air is drenched. A creeping, persistent dampness is the only weather. Condensation breathes itself onto every available surface, beading on the razor-sharp edges of the newborn stone and the riveted iron of the structures. Sometimes the droplets run downward; sometimes, confused by the failing physics of the peaks, they trickle slowly upward to pool in mid-air, forming trembling spheres of suspended water that drift through the gloom like jellyfish.

The soundscape of this place is deafening, yet profoundly patient. There is no frantic noise, only a vast, oceanic baseline: a continuous, grinding groan of tectonic birth and industrial exertion drawn out across hours. 

Clinging to these isolated spires of raw geology is an archipelago of colossal machines. It is a metropolis pulled apart. Dense, multi-layered towers of brutalist infrastructure—smoke-belching processing plants, churning extraction towers, and layered hab-blocks—rise like iron barnacles on the sharp stone. Yet, because the landscape is so vast, there is immense negative space. Each factory-citadel sits alone on its respective crag, separated from its neighbors by miles of empty, cold abyss. They are tethered to one another by swaying suspension cables thick as redwoods, heavy pipelines, and lonely tramways that vanish into the dark fog. 

The industry operates on a titanic, sluggish scale. Rotors the size of city blocks turn with agonizing majesty, pushing the heavy mist. The clank of a gargantuan piston dropping hits like a thunderclap, echoing across the voids, taking whole minutes to complete just one stroke. 

And yet, drawn to the radiant heat of the smelters and the weeping moisture, life explodes in localized jungles. Where the density of the built world is sparse, the vegetation is violently lush. Brilliant, unearthly greens and cyan foliage burst from rusting pipe-joints and the sheer black rock. Cascading ferns with fronds wide as sails spill over the steel catwalks. Velvet mosses, inches thick and slick with dew, swallow entire cooling towers in a matter of weeks, creating vertical swamps suspended in the twilight. These pockets of intense, humid vitality stand out like glowing emerald wounds against the slate-grey cold.

Despite the vastness of the empty air, fauna is abundant. Swarms of moth-like creatures the size of hounds, pulsing with a pale luminescence, gather around the dim sodium bulbs of the maintenance walkways, moving in heavy, fluid murmuration. Out in the deep gulfs between the factories, massive, slow-drifting biological dreadnoughts swim through the damp air. They are the size of submarines, with ribbed, moss-flecked hides and wide filtering maws, grazing on suspended water pockets and drifting spores. Occasionally, one gently collides with a miles-long suspension cable, sending a deep, resonant hum vibrating down the line, singing over the valley.

But if you watch the great, sparse engine of this place long enough, you realize the edges of it refuse to hold together. The extreme elevation has pierced the skin of reality, and the rules of the world are breaking down. The decay is not frantic, but casual. 

A pipeline stretching across a chasm doesn't merely vanish into the fog; it clips out of space entirely, its geometry severed, only to instantaneously re-emerge a hundred feet to the left. A towering industrial crane slowly swivels, but its massive jib passes directly through the unyielding basalt of a mountain peak without resistance, turning translucent for a few seconds before fully materializing on the other side. The slowness of the world is partly a distortion of time itself: the deafening crash of a steam hammer arrives minutes before the hammer actually strikes the anvil.

As the titanic turbines spin in the damp dark, they occasionally pull flakes off the world—brief, terrifying patches of static where the fog, the ferns, and the iron simply become unrendered voids. These grey boxes of nothingness hang in the air like dead pixels, before the mist rushes back in to fill the gap.

The city, it seems, is an attempt to stitch the sky shut. The mechanized foundries are not smelting iron, or if they are, it is merely a byproduct. They are processing the raw, wild geography into localized stability. They churn out heavy, riveted anchors of logic in a space that is actively dissolving. And every time a new slab of impossible, mathematical stone extrudes from the mountainside, shaking the twilight, a new armada of worker-drones is winched across the void, ready to bolt another layer of heavy, rusting, stubbornly physical metal onto it—pinning reality down before it can float away.
