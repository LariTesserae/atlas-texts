You are suspended in an ocean that has forgotten the earth. 

This is the Zenith Deep, a vast, free-floating hydrosphere that spans the entire planet, anchored miles above the dead crust in what should be the stratosphere. You exist within a warm, omnidirectional body of water, bathed in an amniotic heat that quickly dissolves the perceived boundary between the fluid and your own skin. It is perpetually dusk here. The light filtering from above is a dim, smeared bruise of plum and deep violet, unable to pierce the full thickness of the floating sea. 

Despite the lack of light, the water is overwhelmingly dense, choked with a suspended, three-dimensional jungle. Rootless, fractal forests drift in the currents. Colossal vines twist into tight, tightly-layered knots, their pale, semi-translucent fronds—some the size of cathedrals—pulsing as they filter the thick, gelatinous broth of the sea. The water is highly saturated with microscopic life, clouded with drifting, greenish-gold spores that provide a weak, localized bioluminescence. Everywhere you turn your gaze, your field of vision is crowded by organic geometry, a high-saturation tangle of lush, alien biology woven deeply into itself.

Caught within these tangled webs of vegetation are the Heavies. These are the remnants of the world below, torn loose when the atmosphere inverted a billion years ago. Once, they were towering mountain peaks. Now, eons of fluid friction and aeons of deep time have eroded these city-sized masses of black rock into perfectly polished, oblate teardrops and massive, smooth spheres. The concept of a sharp edge no longer exists here. If you brush your hand along their glassy equators, you might feel the faint, desperate ghost-marks of architecture: the shadow of an arched window smoothed over entirely, or a cyclopean stairway rubbed down into a gentle, sloping undulation. These pure, eroded megaliths drift pointlessly, lonely markers of a primordial past.

But despite its profound, deeply settled age, the Zenith Deep is fiercely, violently active. 

Immense, high-velocity jet streams of chilled water roar through the quiet, warm zones. These currents move with such concentrated force that they possess refractive boundaries, appearing as solid, sliding glass tunnels cutting through the ocean. Within them, the ecosystem moves at a terrifying pace—gelatinous blooms latch onto the floating stones, flower into sprawling lace-like colonies, and rot away into fine silt in a matter of minutes, feeding the endless cycle of the sea.

And it is in this motion that you see the fabric of reality actively rotting away.

Without gravity to anchor it, the physics of this ocean are unraveling. Space does not merely bend; it stutters, delaminates, and folds. You watch a tumbling monolith drift into a chaotic eddy, but as it spins, the stone physically elongates, smearing across three dimensions before folding into a spatial plane the water shouldn't possess. Directions are fluid. You swim toward a knot of bioluminescent ferns, only to realize the distance isn't closing—it is dilating outward, unraveling like a ball of yarn. A patch of water loses its depth entirely, rendering the massive stones passing through it as perfectly flat, two-dimensional voids. Currents braid themselves into Möbius strips, carrying trailing vines that loop endlessly through localized pockets of dead time, decaying and reforming in a permanent, stuttering loop. 

When the dense foliage parts, you can sometimes look "down" through the convex meniscus holding the bottom of this ocean together, gazing across miles of empty sky to the dry, dead earth below, realizing how impossibly high up you are.

The entire environment operates in a heavy, muffled silence. The roar of the internal jet streams registers only as a sub-bass vibration in your chest. The loudest sound is the occasional, immense, soft *thud*—felt rather than heard—when two mountain-sized stones gently collide in the twilight murk. In this decaying physics, the collision does not produce a soundwave; instead, it generates a slow-moving ripple that pushes gravity sideways, making the nearby sea-forests tilt in impossible directions.

Fauna is exceedingly rare, existing only as anomalies adapted to the breakdown. As you float through a pocket of folded space, a shadow briefly eclipses the bruised sunlight above. It is a silhouette of incomprehensible scale—a vast, silent bulk of rippling, translucent fins branching outward. It does not swim through the water. Instead, it glides through the frayed seams of reality itself, slipping behind the corrupted geometry of the sea, vanishing into a dimension just adjacent to your own, leaving neither wake nor sound in its passing.
