The light here is absolute, a high-altitude glare that strikes the world without the softening intervention of a thick atmosphere. There are no trees to cast shadows, no dust to scatter the beams. It is a blinding, ultraviolet-heavy radiance that turns the surface of the landscape into a shimmering, reflective expanse. 

You are standing on a rolling plateau shoved miles into the sky. The geology is violent and impossibly young—a raw, embryonic uplift of jagged basalt and sharply fractured slate that has not yet learned to wear down. But you can barely see the stone. Over the crumpled, new-born topography, a continuous, living skin has taken hold. 

The ground is entirely made of vegetation. It is a vast, interconnected mat of dense, alpine cushion-plants, thick sphagnum-like mosses, and hardy, fibrous succulents. They grow in enormous green, russet, and bruised-purple lobes, piled upon each other in mounds the size of houses, filling the valleys and smoothing out the sharp topography. There is no soil. The plants grow on the decaying, compacted layers of their ancestors. 

And the entire living mass is weeping.

The air is cool, hovering just above freezing, but the vegetation acts as a continent-sized sponge, trapping the relentless moisture of high-altitude condensation and passing weather fronts. Icy water pools in every depression of the broad, waxy leaves. It oozes from the seams between the giant moss-mounds. When you step, your boot doesn't hit dirt; it sinks into a dense, fibrous weave, squeezing out a rush of clear, freezing water that immediately floods your footprints. The landscape is a suspended ocean, held in place by billions of gripping roots and interlocking stems. 

But the overriding force in this place is not the light, nor the cold, nor the endless wet green. It is the noise.

It is a deafening, chest-crushing mechanical roar, so loud it swallows the wind. It vibrates up through the water-logged plant mass, turning the icy puddles into zones of frantic, shivering ripples. The sound comes from the scattered industrial titans that interrupt the vast, sparse plateau. 

Miles apart from one another, lonely in the open expanse, sit colossal extraction stations. They are brutalist cathedrals of iron, steel, and weeping rust, engineered for scaled production. Massive, electrified rotary turbines spin within their housings, drawing millions of gallons of water out from beneath the vegetative mantle. Thick, riveted pipelines, wider than tunnels, plunge directly into the living mat of the plateau, sucking the aquifer dry to feed some distant, unseen civilization far below the cloud line. 

These machines are not delicate. Exhaust vents blast pressurized steam into the cold air, where it instantly turns into searing white vapor that drifts low over the moss. Heavy diesel backup engines chug with a rhythmic, percussive thud that rattles the teeth in your skull. The roar of the water being forced through the great iron arteries is constant, a violent rushing sound magnified by the empty, open scale of the valley. There are no birds here to be frightened away, no grazing animals—the sheer volume of the machinery and the harshness of the altitude have rendered the space devoid of fauna. There is only the plant, the water, and the engine.

Despite the deafening violence of the sound, the actual movement of the landscape is agonizingly slow. This is a place of long, patient durations. The plants grow millimeters a year, slowly creeping up the sides of the rusting steel pipes, attempting to swallow the hot iron in cool, wet green. The draining of the plateau is equally gradual; the sponge is so vast that the furious, screaming pumps can only alter the water table by inches a decade. The industrial artifacts operate autonomously, their massive gears turning in a hypnotic, endless rhythm under the blinding sun. 

It is a world held in a state of suspended, roaring tension. The sheer volume of the sound demands immediate action, yet nothing happens quickly. The jagged, infant rocks slowly push upward from the earth's mantle; the unending mat of vegetation slowly drinks the sky; and the scattered, screaming engines relentlessly drink from the vegetation. The chilling water flows, the blinding light beats down, and the roaring vibration echoes across the empty roof of the world, on and on without end.
