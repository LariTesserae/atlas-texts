The first thing to claim you is the noise. It is absolute. A concussive, unyielding roar of hydrostatic violence that obliterates hearing and vibrates directly through the marrow. It is the sound of an ocean being pulverized inside a blender, a deafening, continuous thunder of cavitation—billions of vacuum bubbles snapping shut as opposing currents slam into one another.

You are entirely submerged, suspended in a localized, cathedral-sized sphere of violently churning water. 

This liquid enclave does not belong here. Looking through the dim, turbulent boundary of the anomaly, you can see the cold, jagged peaks of a young mountain range far below, shrouded in high-altitude twilight clouds. The water is suspended in the stratosphere, a floating pocket of ruptured physics held together by a repulsive, invisible membrane. Outside, the air is thin and freezing; inside, the water is unnervingly warm, matching the heat of mammalian blood. 

The gravity inside the sphere is shattered. You do not fall; you are swept up in a hyperactive, storm-state circulatory system. Warm localized gyres spin off into chaotic shears. Rivers of accelerated water snake through the slower, pressurized bulk of the sphere, folding in on themselves like kneading dough. Every few moments, physics stutters. The water briefly shatters into a rushing flock of dry, geometric glass shapes, tumbling over one another before melting back into liquid in a fractured heartbeat. Pockets of breathable air are spontaneously drawn from the outside, violently whipped into silver threads, and then crushed back into solution with explosive sonic cracks. 

Because the anomaly is young, the matter caught inside it has had no time to wear down. Suspended sparsely within the roaring darkness are shards of newly born terrain. Enormous splinters of black basalt and razor-sharp obsidian hang in the vast watery spaces, bleeding thin trails of mineral dust into the current. There are no smooth river stones here. Every edge is a weapon, a fresh fracture from wherever this earth was violently annexed. 

Among these scattered, tumbling rocks are the faint, undeniable marks of hands. Crude, monolithic stone blocks—perhaps once an altar, a lookout, or a primitive high-altitude sanctuary—have been sucked into the sphere. They fly through the swirling dark in slow motion. You can see a perfectly carved set of four dark stone stairs suspended in a momentary patch of calm water, entirely unattached to anything, before a vicious downdraft snatches them away, sending them spiraling into the gloom. 

Light is a scarce, mutilated thing here. The sun—or whatever star sits in the sky beyond—only penetrates the boundary membrane as a dim, bruised violet glow. However, the sheer kinetic fury of the water sometimes produces flashes of sonoluminescence: abrupt, ghostly strobes of deep blue light born purely from the friction and pressure of the collapsing waves. 

In these split-second flashes of blue, the utter sparseness of the sphere is revealed. Despite the deafening chaos, it is mostly empty space. A few lone splinters of rock. The distant, churning boundary wall. The swirling rubble of the scattered sanctuary. 

Life barely clings to this maelstrom. Scattered along the leeward faces of the largest, sharpest rocks are thin, pallid strands of a fibrous, ghost-white algae, whipping frantically in the currents. They do not photosynthesize; they seem to feed directly off the kinetic frenzy of the water. And occasionally, if you watch the spaces between the chaotic upwellings, you might glimpse a single, translucent shape riding the shears. It is a massive, eyeless thing, all cartilage and undulating fins, completely transparent save for a faint, bioluminescent nervous system. It does not fight the storm. It allows the turbulent reality breakdowns to sweep it from one end of the cathedral-sphere to the other, gracefully dodging the freshly-broken masonry and sharp obsidian shards that orbit this roaring, impossible womb. 

There is no rest to be found here. The environment is in a state of terminal agitation, a continuous cycle of breaking laws and brutal fluid mechanics. It is loud. It is warm. It is profoundly, terrifyingly awake.
