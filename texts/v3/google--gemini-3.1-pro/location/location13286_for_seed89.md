At altitudes where the external atmosphere turns brittle and exposed rock sheds its scattered snow into a breathless, freezing void, a narrow blowhole opens in the crown of a colossal peak. To pass through this fissure is to drop out of the harsh alpine extreme and into a wholly isolated, enclosed reality. Down the throat of the mountain lies a single, bell-shaped subterranean cathedral. There is no wide-ranging cavern network here, only this solitary, soaring vertical chamber, which traps a microclimate of surprising, humid warmth fueled by a deep-earth geothermal updraft. 

Inside the chamber, the darkness is nearly absolute. Only the uppermost ledges ever catch the faint, indigo sliver of daylight from the narrow summit crack; the rest of the plunge is swallowed in heavy pitch. Long before the eyes can adapt, the space announces itself through an immense, concussive pressure of sound. It is a deafening, booming amphitheater. The thick acoustic air shakes with an overlapping roar: deep, resonant chest-rumbles, the wet, slapping friction of heavy flesh, and an endless, rhythmic percussion. A bright, shattering *clack... scrape... clack... scrape* constantly echoes and reverberates against the curved walls.

If a flare were struck and dropped into the abyss, the sudden light would reveal a staggering biological density. Finding a bare foot of stone is impossible. The cavern is overwhelmingly alive; the walls themselves appear to breathe. The chimney is carpeted in a thick, undulating mass of fauna. The denizens of this cathedral are heavy, eyeless creatures, roughly the mass of large apes but lacking wings entirely. Possessing smooth, pale, plated skulls, thick pelts of slate-grey down, and four heavily muscled arms ending in calloused grips, they cling to the sheer faces—and to one another. They overlap in a staggered, vertical ocean of biology, roosting in a tightly packed, sweltering press of bodies that covers the vast expanse from bottom to top. 

The ecology of the cathedral operates on strict, gradual cycles. As the trapped geothermal heat rises through the dense architecture of breathing bodies, it collides with the freezing collar of stone near the mountain's summit. This dramatic temperature shift forces the humidity—drawn from deep-earth steam and the exhalations of a million lungs—to rapidly condense. It falls back down the chimney as a perpetual, warm drizzle. This intermittent indoor rain keeps the cavern slick and damp. Moisture runs down the stone, creating a muggy, stifling atmosphere, while sparse, stringy curtains of pale, extremophile lichen anchor weakly to the highest, coldest edges, feeding delicately on the rising draft of ammonia. 

The source of the deafening percussion is a strange, primitive industry that has shaped the entirety of the landscape. These blind creatures are slow, meticulous tool-users. Grasped tightly in their secondary, lower arms are coarse, unworked chunks of heavy volcanic rock and quartz, scavenged from deep fissures at the chamber's floor. For hours at a time, their heavy limbs drag these raw stones against the basalt walls. They do not build structures. They assemble no artifacts. Their tool-use is entirely subtractive. 

Over spans of time too vast to easily measure, this patient, collective grinding has completely terraformed the interior of the peak. They have eroded the mountain from the inside out. The walls are nowhere jagged or rugged; the erosion is a deeply established, settled form of biological weathering. The interior has been buffed down into fluid, sweeping curves to create tens of thousands of scooped, bowl-like hollows. Under the slick of the warm indoor rain and a coating of fine mineral-dust mud, the stone feels like dark, melting wax shaped by eons of friction. 

Life here operates on a scale of profound patience. The creatures inch over one another in a slow, churning migration along the vertical faces. They crack open heavily armored, deep-earth centipedes on their stone anvils, groom wet rock-dust from each other's pelts, and quietly return to their grinding. There is no rapid flux here, no fast predator-prey dynamic—only the steady, damp warmth, the relentless press of bodies, and a single, isolated sanctuary slowly carving itself wider in the ringing dark.
