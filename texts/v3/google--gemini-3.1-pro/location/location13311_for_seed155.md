The ground beneath your feet is an infinitely dense, perfectly smooth expanse of polished hardwood. It stretches in all directions toward a high, flat horizon, marked only by the sweeping, arcing lines of gigantically concentric tree rings, some of them yards across, others spanning miles. You are standing on the cross-section of a world-scale arbor, a primordial stump hovering at high elevation. Settled into its final, permanent state, eons of deep-time erosion and atmospheric sheer have worn this colossal biological plateau down to a sheer, glass-like flatness. There is no stone. There is no soil. The planet's crust is pure, petrified plant matter. 

The air is cool and thin, saturated with a perpetual, chill dampness. Condensation gathers on every surface, beading on the polished floor and gathering in the microscopic grooves of the ancient grain. Banks of silver mist roll across the flat plain, leaving a slick, weeping sheen behind them. Above, the sky shines with a stark, normal daylight—a pale, overcast wash that casts ordinary shadows and behaves with an unsettling mundanity against the landscape below. 

This plateau is not empty. It is a sprawling metropolis of heavy industry, engineered to a settlement-scale grid, yet mathematically sparse. Immense, towering factory-cathedrals are scattered across the vastness of the plateau, sitting isolated in the middle of empty wooden plazas that run for dozens of miles. These structures are monstrous hybrids of living plant architecture and mechanized iron. Colossal cooling towers are grown, rather than built, extruded from the floor itself into hollow boles of ironwood. Rusted steel scaffolding and thick copper pipes are bolted directly into weeping, pale tissue.

Though the factories are scattered miles apart, you are standing in a dense urban environment—if you understand how space here has cracked. 

Due to the deep-time friction of the industrial machinery tapping into the reality-warping sap of the world-stump, the geometry of the city has catastrophically broken down under the normal sky. The "space" between the solitary factories is a localized illusion. A street here is an invisible tunnel of warped distance. You might step toward a mechanized refinery that appears to be thirty miles away on the horizon, only to have the perspective violently snap on your third step: the factory is suddenly towering a thousand feet above you, its iron flanks radiating heat, the smell of ozone and boiling resin choking your lungs. Look backward, and the spot you just departed is now an infinite, receding line of flat wood grain vanishing into the mist. 

Elevated pipelines and taut cables of braided, living root-matter connect the widely spaced installations. But gaze down the length of an arcing aqueduct, and you will see it twist like a Moebius strip. Sometimes, the straight pipelines simply terminate in mid-air, bypassing the intervening miles through folds in dimension, seamlessly delivering glowing sap to glass vats suspended in a completely different sector of the plateau. Away in the distance, you can see another world-stump, another flattened plateau, but it is hanging completely vertical in the sky, a wall of polished wood and sideways-jutting smokestacks, with a river of cold mist pouring horizontally from its surface onto your own without acknowledging gravity.

The environment is overwhelmingly, violently loud, yet the acoustics obey the same shattered logic as the geography. The ceaseless operations of the city are a physical force. Massive, hydraulic pile-drivers rhythmically punch into the wooden surface, extracting pressurized, volatile amber. Spinning iron turbines grind against meshing gears made of rapidly regenerating wood. The roar of steam vents and the bass-heavy thudding of subterranean pumps are deafening. But because sound does not decay through the folded space, the shriek of a buzz-saw twenty miles away might happen instantaneously inside your inner ear, sharp as a needle. The grinding of pistons produces shockwaves you can see—ripples of force that tear across the damp floor and hit spatial anomalies, temporarily shattering the air into hexagonal prisms of black void before reality violently heals itself back to normal daylight. 

Despite the devastation of logic, the ecosystem is highly dynamic. The city is a living, breathing parasite in symbiosis with its substrate. Veins of fresh, green phloem pulse visibly along the ground at the edges of the tree rings, crawling toward the rusted foundries to repair structural damage. The factories inhale the mist and expire thick, fragrant clouds of compressed moss-spore and woodgas. 

Life here is almost entirely botanical and mechanical. Fauna is exceptionally rare, reduced to fleeting ghosts. Occasionally, out of the corner of your eye, you might spot spindly, moth-like assemblages of pale wood and wire shivering near the steam vents, drinking the condensation playfully before snagging on a spatial tear and popping out of existence entirely. 

You stand in the cold mist under an ordinary white sky, dwarfed by solitary, roaring behemoths of wood and iron. Around you, the floor vibrates with the thumping heartbeat of the industrial sap-pumps, the puddles of condensation shivering into complex geometric patterns as the space-time of the city stretches, folds, and screams.
