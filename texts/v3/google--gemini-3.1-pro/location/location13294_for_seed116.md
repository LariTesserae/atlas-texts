You are suspended inside a droplet of tropical ocean, held captive in the stratosphere.

This place is a single, spherical containment tank—perhaps fifty feet across—capstoning the summit of a colossal, sky-piercing mast. Outside the glass, there is only the terrifying, freezing expanse of the extreme upper atmosphere, a sheer drop of unimaginable miles down to a blanket of white clouds. But inside, the environment is entirely submerged, pressurized, and sweltering. The water is a heavy, bath-warm amniotic fluid, continuously heated by the slow, eternal decay of the isotopic engines buried somewhere in the mast below.

It is a pocket universe of pure, ancient industry, yet there is not a single sharp angle left in the entire room. Time and water have enacted a relentless, smoothing violence. The inner architecture—once a complex lattice of brass turbines, steel intake valves, and titanium struts—has been eroded into a landscape of soft, metallic mounds. The floor is not rock, but a solid reef of oxidized engine-slag and compressed iron flakes, fused into smooth, rolling dunes. The giant blades of the central impeller look like heavy, dark petals of melted wax, polished to a dull sheen by millions of years of liquid friction. Everything here feels fundamentally exhausted of its original purpose, having settled long ago into a final, highly stable, deeply aged state.

Despite this erosion, the machinery still breathes, and the acoustics within the liquid are deafening. Every twelve seconds, an archaic hydraulic pump hidden beneath the floor engages. The sound is a colossal, resonant *WHUNG-CHK*, a percussive shockwave you feel in your floating bones as much as you hear. This is immediately followed by a roaring surge, an immense exhalation of warm water blasting up through the central grate, creating powerful, swirling convection currents that crash against the thick, scratched glass of the ceiling before cascading back down the curved walls. 

Because of these violent currents, life here has adopted a strict, sparse geography. The vast majority of the tank’s liquid volume is completely empty—a wide, clear, open expanse of warm water bathed in perfectly normal, pale-yellow sunlight filtering in through the frosted macro-glass dome. 

But clinging to the grooved lee side of the giant impeller mounds, and rooted deeply into the rusted intake vents, the vegetation is staggeringly lush. It grows in dense, localized islands of green and rusted crimson. Thick sleeves of bio-synthetic wire-kelp undulate in the current. Fleshy, translucent hydro-ferns uncork from the seams of old access hatches, their roots feeding off the mineral-rich metallic decay. These botanical islands are incredibly dense but hyper-contained, leaving vast, empty plains of clear water between them. 

Within these open spaces, the fauna thrives in a state of high-velocity motion. Swarms of silver, thumb-sized creatures dart through the empty water. They blur the line between biology and machinery—segmented, multi-finned things that maneuver like fish but possess the interlocking carapaces of ancient capillary-cleaning drones. They move in vast, synchronized ribbons, thousands of them riding the roaring thermal updrafts of the central pump to feed on the microscopic algae sloughing off the glass. 

The ecosystem is frantically active. As the silver swarms ride the geyser to the top of the dome, larger, solitary predators detach themselves from the lush kelp beds below. These are broad, flat, ray-like entities that resemble drifting heat-sinks, their undersides pulsing with pale luminescence. They glide into the open water, intercepting the silver ribbons in sudden, chaotic bursts of speed. When a predator breaches a swarm, the silver things scatter, pinging against the thick glass dome with thousands of tiny, audible *clicks* that echo wildly through the chamber, only to reform their ribbons near the downward suction grates. 

It is a loud, warm, intensely dynamic little room. A terrarium of forgotten machinery that gave birth to its own biology, completely detached from the dead earth below. The sun shines steadily through the scratched, milky viewport, illuminating the smooth brass hills, the islands of lush wire-fronds, and the flashing silver dances of the swarms, all of them waiting for the next great, booming heartbeat of the deep-water pump to throw their world into glorious, raging motion once again.
