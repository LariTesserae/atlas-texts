You inhabit a sliver of dormancy in a hollow infinitely larger than you can see. 

Beneath you is a hexagonal beam of pale, translucent timber, eighty feet wide, slick with cold condensation. There is no stone in this world. There is no soil. Deep inside the earth's crust, an unfathomable root system has drawn apart, creating a subterranean void the size of a continent. You are suspended in its center, standing on the architecture of the plant itself.

It is bitterly cold. A fine, frigid mist hangs in the empty gulfs. The fog lingers constantly, leaving outcroppings slick with dew and gathering in small, perfectly still pools at the deep, V-shaped crotches where giant branches intersect. Breath plumes in the damp chill, but you must be careful not to speak too loudly. 

Not that your voice would matter. This space drinks sound. The porous, micro-fractal geometry of the pale cellulose absorbs reverberation entirely. Echoes are a physics this world does not permit. You could scream into the abyss, and the sound would simply flatten and die inches from your mouth. The prevailing atmosphere is a dead, heavy quiet, which makes the sheer scale of the void feel agonizingly intimate. 

Near the edge of the beam sits a single, precarious structure. It is a hanging pod, woven frantically from stripped pith and fibrous inner-bark, lashed together in rough, knotty bundles. It is dwarfed by the sheer drop beneath it. Tucked inside this temporary, primitive refuge is a small cache of tools: a hand-axe that looks like shattered bismuth but is made of razor-sharp, knapped wood; a scraper carved from bleached bone; a coiled length of damp fiber. Whoever crafted this surviving mechanism did so by hand, leaving only this faint, scratch-like mark on an otherwise indifferent universe. 

Then, two miles above you, the ceiling cracks.

You only hear a dry, muted *pop*. But suddenly, the dark void explodes into blinding, magnesium-white brilliance.

You have to squeeze your eyes shut. The light is aggressive, searingly bright, casting hard, rapidly swinging shadows through the mist. Tectonic pressure from the miles of earth above has crushed a vein in the mega-root ceiling, and it is bleeding deeply into the sub-zero cavern. 

The fluid that pours out is a highly pressurized, chemiluminescent sap. The moment this liquid hits the frigid, damp air of the void, it violently polymerizes. It does not grow like a tree; it flashes into existence. Spires of sharp, translucent wood self-assemble at miles per hour, thrusting across the massive empty airspace in jagged, geometric vectors. The molecular bonding sheds massive amounts of photons, creating a cold, blinding flare that illuminates the entire continental cavity. 

Now, in the floodlight of the reaction, you can see the scale of the empty world. Hundreds of miles of hollow airspace, crisscrossed by immense, dizzying bridges of glassy, alien timber. 

The cavern is in a state of hyperactive, turbulent chaos. The newly polymerized spears of wood shoot outward, colliding with other soaring beams in mid-air. Because the material expands too fast and forms purely hard angles, it cannot sustain its own weight. Colossal lattices, millions of tons of fresh, sharp-featured wood, shear off clean in the center.

They fall. Huge, crystalline stalactites the size of skyscrapers plummet toward the deep mist. But in the anechoic quiet, this apocalypse is a silent pantomime. A massive branch snaps mid-flight with a flat, dry *crack* that reaches you seconds later, and then the shards cascade silently into the foggy abyss below, looking like shattered glass sinking into milk. 

This violent, chaotic creation and destruction happens constantly. When one blinding flare begins to cool to a dull, chemical glow, another pressure fault cracks somewhere on the far horizon, sending new rays of blinding light and a new storm of rapidly thrusting, fracturing geometry across the gulfs. The space is a frantic bazaar of structural collapse, fresh edges, and shifting beams of light. Because the plant matter forms in straight, shard-like splinters, nothing here ever has time to weather. Every edge is perfectly young, infinitely sharp, and instantly discarded.

The massive displacement of air from the flash-growing timber drives slow, swirling wind fronts through the void, pushing the icy fog into vast weather systems. 

As the light from the recent burst cools from an eye-watering white to a pale, steady green-blue, a shadow slips through the glare. 

It is a massive, manta-like entity, its wingspan easily forty feet across. It has no eyes, navigating entirely blindly upon the cold thermal drafts pushed by the expanding wood. Its body is covered in a dense, mossy velvet that blends into the damp frost of the dormant zones. Soundless as a phantom, the creature coasts past the tip of a newly hardening, glowing splinter of wood. For a brief second, it brushes the wet, unpolymerized sap with a trailing membrane, drinking the raw geometry before it freezes, and then drifts away, sinking back into the freezing mist, searching for the next silent star to burst in the dark.
