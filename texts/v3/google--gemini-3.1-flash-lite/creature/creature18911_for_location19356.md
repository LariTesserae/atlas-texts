The inhabitants of this vertical lung are not biological in any sense that would satisfy a biologist, nor are they mechanical in a way that suggests a simple gear-driven purpose. They are the **Sifted Ones**, a population that has thinned out and elongated to match the geometry of their habitat.

To gaze upon a Sifted One is to look at a creature that has been pressed into existence by the city’s extreme density. They are tall—impossibly, painfully tall—their limbs refined into spindly, articulated reaches that allow them to navigate the narrow vertical shafts and the slick, precipitous undersides of the great catwalks. They do not walk so much as they flow, sliding their flexible, cartilaginous frames through the gaps between humming conduits with the fluid grace of mercury.

Their skin is a pale, translucent parchment, mirroring the bioluminescent moss that chokes the iron veins of their world. It is cool to the touch, and perpetually damp; they have long since abandoned the need for lungs that inhale air, now possessing a porous, surface-level respiration that absorbs the oxygen and the rich, ozone-heavy moisture provided by the city’s rhythmic exhaling. In the dim, chartreuse light, they glow faintly—a soft, pulsing rhythm that matches the vibration of the city’s subterranean pistons.

They are the city’s antibodies, its repairmen, and its ghosts. They move in silent, synchronized congregations, not out of malice or greed, but out of a profound, ingrained compulsion. They are the maintainers of the "turbulent maintenance." One might see a cluster of them clinging to a massive, grinding gear, their long, multi-jointed fingers weaving patches of tough, organic lichen into the stress fractures of the rusted steel. They are not repairing the metal for human progress; they are smoothing the interfaces where silicon and biology have begun to fuse.

They possess no language of sound, for the eternal, low-frequency hum of the city would render speech useless. Instead, they communicate through haptic vibrations—placing a trembling palm against a vibrating rivet, sending a ripple of intention that travels through the metal plates for miles. They perceive the city not as a series of structures, but as a singular, living nervous system. To them, the massive, house-sized pistons are not tools, but limbs; the clouds of vapor are not waste, but nourishment.

They have forgotten the names of the architects. They have forgotten the concept of "outside." They exist in a state of suspended evolution, forever refining a grand, broken machine that requires their presence just as much as they require its heat. When one of the Sifted Ones finally expires—when the friction of the world finally grinds a body down—they do not bury their dead. They simply curl themselves into the empty, unused joints of a cooling pipe or tuck themselves behind a panel that is about to be sealed shut. There, their bodies slowly calcify, merging with the structural substrate until they become a permanent part of the city wall, their own bones providing the structural integrity for the generations that follow.

They are the keepers of the long-lost Purpose. They do not know what the city is building, and they do not care. To them, the act of assembly—the turning of a gear, the sealing of a seam, the coaxing of a moss-patch over a leak—is the only prayer they know. They do not live *on* the city; they are the cellular debris of a gargantuan, drifting mind, content to remain within the cooling, weeping, iron-scented belly of a god that never wakes up.
