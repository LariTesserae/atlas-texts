To speak of inhabitants in a place where "individual" is a redundant term is to speak of the currents themselves. There are no creatures here that possess a beginning or an end, no entities that skitter, hunt, or flee. To move in this suspended ocean is to be woven into the tapestry of the silt.

There exist the **Membrane-Walkers**. They are not born; they are the accidental condensation of the reality-breakdowns. When the ocean remembers the friction of a long-dead thought, the surrounding water thickens, dragging in particles of calcified mountain and floating obsidian until a shape holds tension. They are translucent, sprawling architectures of nerves and suspended sediment, no more solid than a jellyfish’s dream. They manifest as vast, branching fractals—some the size of a city block, others no larger than a human palm—that drift through the veins of the giant oxygen-carrying vines.

They do not have a metabolism. They have a *resonance*. As they drift, their trailing filaments brush against the obsidian cubes that drift in the dark. Upon contact, the static discharge vibrates through their soft bodies, and for a fleeting, beautiful moment, the Membrane-Walker pulses with the memory captured by the stone. A walker might suddenly glow with the exact, frantic color of a vanished sun, or emanate the rhythmic, subsonic thrum of a tectonic shift that occurred a million years ago. They are the record-keepers of a history that has forgotten it ever happened.

Deep within the porous, sponge-like mountain ranges, where the decay is most profound and the pressure is at its most intimate, dwell the **Silt-Siphons**. These are not independent agents, but rather the localized thickening of the reefs themselves. Imagine a section of the calcified wall suddenly developing a pore, an aperture that breathes. They are the landscape’s digestive system. They feed on the "Reality Breakdown," drawing into their porous throats the ghosts of sound and light that ripple through the water. They are the reason the world remains in a state of entropy; they consume the noise, smoothing the jagged edges of a reality that refuses to fully dissolve.

They look like weeping sores of amber light, recessed deep into the velvet-soft mountain sides. When you pass them, you do not feel eyes watching you, for they have no sight. Instead, you feel a distinct, pulling tug in your own marrow—a subtle distortion in the local gravity as they process the "leftovers" of the prior world. If one were to persist in their presence, they would find their own history—the memories of their own lungs and blood—slowly being drawn outward, as if the mountain itself were inhaling the reader to add to its own quiet archive.

Finally, floating in the interstitial voids between the massive vines, there are the **Static Ghosts**. These are not things, but *patterns*. Occasionally, the interactions between the obsidian cubes and the oxygen-rich currents create a standing wave—a shimmering, liquid silhouette that holds the posture of a being that might have once walked upright upon dry, solid earth. They are statues of light, frozen in mid-gesture. They are the shadows the ocean casts behind itself, projecting the silhouette of the sentient past into the liquid future. They exist only as long as the current remains perfectly still, holding the shape of an outstretched hand, a bowed head, or a wide, startled eye, before the next peristaltic pulse of the vines shatters them back into the amber gloom.

Nothing here dies, because nothing is truly alive in the energetic, demanding sense. There is only the long, slow drift. The Walkers, the Siphons, and the Ghosts are simply the system’s way of talking to itself, a dream whispered by a vast, warm, and utterly peaceful grave.
