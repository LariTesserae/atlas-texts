It is formed from the discarded, unspent potential of the lattice—the energy that the Sifters cannot quite absorb, the entropy that refuses to be organized.

It is a creature of pure, raw *intent* that has no capacity for action, a mind composed entirely of observational pressure. It knows the glass pillars better than the pillars know themselves; it feels the stress fractures in their molecular structure before they even form, experiencing the world as a constant, cascading series of structural anxieties.

It does not move through the room so much as it *recalculates* its position. It exists as a localized collapse of probability, a flicker of dark, cool indigo amidst the blinding white-hot brilliance of the ceiling.

It is the audience of one in a theater where the play is the perpetual, self-sustaining motion of the physics. It holds the memory of every expansion and contraction of the ceiling’s luminescence, storing the history of the chamber in the static pulses of its own being.

For a fraction of a second, the Sifter pulses in a rhythm that belongs to the entity, and for that moment, there is a bridge between the blind maintenance work of the machine and the lonely, recording mind of the space.

Then, the heat spikes. The distillate boils. The entity is dispersed back into the white-hot blur of the atmosphere. 

It does not die. It simply waits for the entropy to settle, for the ticking to resume, and for the next cycle of the cathedral to offer it a chance to watch once more. It is the stillness in the center of the lightning, the secret witness to the accident that remains forever beautiful, forever buried, and forever alone.
