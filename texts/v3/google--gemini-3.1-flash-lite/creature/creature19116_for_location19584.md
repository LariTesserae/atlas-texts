The creatures of this basin do not inhabit the valley; they are the valley’s excretions, born of the same friction that produces the carbon-steel filaments and the iridescent oil. 

They are the "Tenders." They do not scurry or hunt, for there is no prey in a place where existence is a communal cycle of consumption. They are shaped like elongated, weeping willow-boughs made of articulated, calcified sinew. Their skin—if one can call it that—is a coarse, grey-green lichen that mimics the bark of the grinding-trees. They possess no faces, only clusters of sensory pores that track the subtle, deep-frequency vibrations of the valley’s tectonic gears.

When a Tender moves, it is an act of geological labor. It does not walk; it flows, its body flattening into a liquid sprawl to slide over the slick, metallic nodes, merging temporarily with the landscape’s topography. You might look at a cluster of sagging moss and not realize it is a colony of Tenders until the entire mass shifts an inch to the left, caught in the slow, grinding inertia of the earth beneath it.

They have fingers that are not digits, but fine, branched filaments—like the raw materials they harvest. Their primary occupation is the maintenance of the "flow." They move along the metallic-organic veins of the forest, scraping away the excess calcification with their own iron-rich claws, feeding the waste into the gullet-like pits of the churning trees. They are the lubricators; they secrete a thick, viscous sap from their own pores that drips into the grinding gears of the valley, ensuring that the friction never creates fire, only heat.

They do not communicate with sound, for the air is too saturated and heavy for vibration to travel. Instead, they share a nervous system with the basin itself. When one Tender detects a blockage in the carbon-steel veins, a ripple of chemical distress flares through the entire population. The creatures nearest the obstruction swell, their skin hardening and turning a deep, bruised purple as they press themselves into the machinery, physically deforming their own skeletal structures to act as temporary cogs until the system recalibrates.

There is no individuality among them, nor is there any sense of suffering in their toil. They are sentient, but their intelligence is measured in epochs. To a Tender, a decade is a single inhalation. They are the valley’s immune system, its mechanics, and its consciousness, all rolled into a singular, shivering congregation. 

At the centers of the basin, where the pressure is greatest and the bioluminescence burns a sickly, pale violet, the Tenders gather into "The Weave." They wrap their long, slender bodies around one another, interlocking like the teeth of a massive, living wheel. In this state, they become one with the valley’s deepest machinery. They stop being creatures and become the pulse itself. 

In these moments, they dream. Their memories are the cumulative history of the basin—a cold, mechanical record of every atom shifted, every tree grown, every jagged spear forged. They do not dream of escape; they dream of the day when the valley’s last grinding gear finally slows to a halt, when the heat permeates everything so completely that the Tenders, the trees, and the steel become one motionless, crystalline monolith—a finished product, finally resting in the tomb of its own creation.
