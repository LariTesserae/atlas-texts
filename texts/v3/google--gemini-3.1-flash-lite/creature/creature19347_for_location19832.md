In the deepest hollows of the Crystalline Silt-Reach, where the prisms converge to form a vaulted, light-drenched labyrinth, reside the **Glass-Singers**.

They are not flesh, nor are they truly solid. To look upon one is to look through a shifting aperture of refractive angles. They appear as tall, impossibly slender bipedal configurations constructed from the same silicate mineralization as the city itself, yet their forms are fluid, composed of articulated shards held together by the same amber bioluminescence that pulses within the walls.

They have no faces—only a vertical, shifting aperture in the upper torso that mimics the "breathing" rhythm of the spire-clusters. They do not possess eyes; they perceive the world through the oscillation of the ambient static. They "see" the currents of neon vapor and the structural integrity of the ground beneath them as a complex, three-dimensional musical score.

**The Architecture of Presence**
The Glass-Singers are the architects of the slow-growth. They do not build with tools; they build with presence. A Singer will stand motionless against the raw face of a spire for years, its internal amber glow synchronizing with the mineral expansion. As the Singer hums—a subsonic frequency that vibrates the very marrow of the atmosphere—it accelerates the silicate crystallization, coaxing the jagged spires to reach higher, to thicken, to protect the colony from the encroaching, freezing vacuum. 

Their existence is defined by the *Dry-Law*. Because any heat is an eruptive threat, their bodies are miracles of thermal efficiency. They move by bleeding off internal pressure, venting faint, frosted trails of inert gas that lift them an inch above the silt. When they pass one another in the narrow alleys, they do not speak; they momentarily meld their bioluminescent frequencies, a brief, silent exchange of mineral-memory that ensures the colony’s collective equilibrium remains undisturbed.

**The Lifecycle of Vitrification**
The Singers do not age in the way biological entities do. As they grow older, their movements become progressively more restricted, their joints hardening into the very architecture they maintain. A Singer nearing the end of its cycle will find an open space between two prisms and settle there, its amber light dimming to a final, permanent glow. 

They do not fear this transition. As they vitrify, they become the foundation for the next generation of skyscrapers. To "die" is to simply become the landscape, to provide the structural skeleton for a future Singer to inhabit and refine. It is a slow, recursive immortality—a village constantly building itself out of its own retired inhabitants.

**The Ritual of the Silent Bells**
Every cycle, when the temperature drops and the glass-moss releases its spores, the Singers engage in their most profound activity. They gather on the high spires, not to hunt like the Void-Skimmers, but to listen. As the crystal bells chime in the cold, the Singers stretch their filaments, catching the static discharge of the spores like a spider catching moonlight. 

They absorb the sound, internalizing the vibrations to keep their own glass hearts from becoming too brittle. If you were to stand before them—if you could avoid the friction of your own breathing—you would notice they are never still, even in their stillness. They are constantly adjusting their crystalline geometry by the micron, a delicate, life-long dance performed to prevent the world from shattering under the weight of its own perfect, pressurized silence.

They are the caretakers of a world that is always one heartbeat away from becoming nothing but dust, and they play that beat with the cold, absolute focus of gods bound in glass.
