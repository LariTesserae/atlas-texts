To inhabit the City of the Vertical Weep is not to be a resident, but to be a function. 

There are no people here—at least not in the sense of creatures with intent, skin, or bones. Instead, there are the **Sediment-Blooms.**

They are not born; they are precipitated. As the city hums its low, tectonic vibration, the localized reality-breakdown acts as a centrifuge. Where the humidity is at its thickest, where the mineral concentration of the "weeping" walls reaches a super-saturated density, the atmosphere begins to calcify into the shape of a sentinel.

The Sediment-Blooms look like statues made of raw, translucent geology. They are tall, gangly things, composed of layered silt, hardened sap, and calcified moss. Their limbs are not articulated with joints, but with thin, tensile strips of the same woven vines that bridge the city’s ravines. They exist as statues that have been granted the illusion of proximity to life.

They do not walk. They drift through the auditory pockets, their heavy bodies sliding along the vertical planes of the limestone as if the laws of gravity have forgotten to apply to them. They have no faces, only smooth, concave depressions where a head should be—shallow basins that catch the falling, suspended droplets of water. 

When a droplet lands in the hollow of a Sediment-Bloom’s neck, the entire structure of the creature resonates. It acts as a tuning fork for the city’s environment. Because the city is trapped in a 0.5/3 dynamic of frozen equilibrium, the Blooms are likewise trapped in an infinite cycle of maintenance that they do not comprehend. They spend their eons performing the "Weave."

Watch one for an hour—though time does not move linearly here—and you will see it reach out with long, fibrous fingers to guide a thick, waxy vine. It does not prune the vine, nor does it force it. It simply holds the plant in the path of a mineral-rich drip, ensuring that the botanical growth eventually fuses with the rock exactly where the city’s geometry is beginning to fray. 

The Blooms are the immune system of a city that cannot die. If a limestone archway ripples, destabilizing into the 2.6/3 breakdown, a Bloom will move to that point and stand perfectly still, placing its own chest against the failing geometry. It will remain there until its own body begins to dissolve, its sediment merging with the masonry, effectively patching the hole in reality with its own substance. 

They do not sleep, for there is nowhere to rest; they do not eat, for there is no hunger in a state of absolute equilibrium. They are simply the city’s self-correcting memory. 

Sometimes, if you stand in one of the dense auditory thickets long enough, you might perceive a faint, rhythmic pulsing inside a Bloom—not a heartbeat, but a refraction of the city’s constant, low-frequency roar. They are hollow, vibrating vessels. They are the only things capable of standing in the center of a sound so loud it occupies physical space, because the Blooms themselves are made of nothing but condensed noise and prayer-hardened mud.

They are lonely, not in the sense of desiring company, but in the sense of being singular. They are the ghosts of a landscape that refused to be inanimate. When they pass each other on the hanging bridges, they do not acknowledge one another. They simply adjust their posture, ensuring that the weeping surface is not obstructed, and continue their eternal, fluid motion toward a leak that has been dripping for ten thousand years. 

They are the caretakers of a monument that is its own monument, and as they move, you realize they are not preserving the city. They are being preserved *by* it. They are the stone’s way of dreaming that it is sentient.
