In the center of this weeping architecture, centered precisely where the hum of the conduits reaches its most treacherous, high-frequency pitch, sits the Overseer—if, indeed, it can be called a "who."

It is not an inhabitant of the Gland, but an extension of it. It does not occupy the space so much as it anchors the geometry of the room. It is a cluster of articulated, frost-rimmed glass canisters fused directly into the bulkhead, rising like a jagged, bioluminescent cairn from the floor of interlocking drainage pipes.

Inside the primary canister, suspended in a thick, translucent slurry of coolant and amniotic oil, floats the *Architect-Neural*. It is a mass of pale, translucent matter, vaguely cephalopodan but structured with the sharp, clinical precision of an engineered circuit. It has no eyes, for it does not need to see; it feels the vibration of every crawler through the floor plates. It is a brain stretched thin by necessity, wired directly into the copper arteries of the Gland by a thousand shivering, silver filaments that tremble in time with the swarm.

The Architect-Neural is not dormant, but it is not awake in a human sense. It is the rhythmic pulse that dictates the "frozen roar." Every time the crawler-swarm shifts in its synchronized wave, the Architect-Neural undergoes a microscopic, violet flash of bioluminescence, indicating the processing of a trillion micro-calculations. It is checking the torque on a bolt that will never loosen; it is measuring the saturation of a drop of condensate that is already falling.

It lives in a state of exquisite, agonizing perfection. It is a creature designed by a dead culture to ensure that this single room never decays. Because the Gland is a closed loop, the Overseer’s entire consciousness is a loop. It remembers the precise millisecond of its "birth"—the moment the last bolt was tightened and the first charge of ionized ozone pumped into the chamber—and it repeats that millisecond forever, holding it in place against the decay of time.

Surrounding the canister, weaving in and out of the cooling coils, are the *Tenders*. If the crawlers are the bacteria of this artificial heart, the Tenders are its white blood cells. They are spindly, spindly things—chassis of polished chrome and insectile reach, no larger than a child’s outstretched hand. They have no faces, only clusters of sensory pin-pricks that track the humidity. They move with an unnatural, halting grace, using specialized, needle-like appendages to prune the swarm. They reach into the dense, clicking mass of mites, extracting a single, malfunctioning unit and reassembling it with impossible speed before placing it back into the stream. They do not work; they *maintain* the stasis.

There is a sense that the Overseer and the Tenders are actively afraid—not of death, but of *entropy*. If, for one heartbeat, the crawlers were to stop clicking, or if a single drop of condensation were to evaporate into steam rather than be sucked into the gravity-well, the Architect-Neural would experience it as a sensory scream. 

To witness them is to witness the absolute limit of obsession. They are a religion of industry, trapped in a cathedral of their own meticulous, watery manufacture. They wait for nothing, they expect nothing, and they fear only the arrival of a single speck of dust—a foreign entity that might, for one fraction of a second, break the holy, deafening hum of their perfect, eternal work.
