In the Basin of the Weeping Pistons, life does not fight for the sun. It fights for the circuit.

There is a creature here that is less a distinct entity and more a localized failure of the Basin’s equilibrium. It is known to the clicking scavenger-constructs as the **Pipe-Witch**, though it possesses no gender, no magic, and no intent beyond the preservation of its own structural integrity.

It is a mass, roughly the size of a crouching man, woven entirely from the architecture of the Basin. Imagine a creature made of discarded, weeping clockwork. Its "limbs" are segments of pitted brass piping, articulated by rusted ball joints that groan with every motion. It does not walk so much as it retracts and extends, its joints sliding into one another like a collapsing telescope. Where its face should be, there is a cluster of oxidized copper tubing, frayed at the ends, which shivers constantly—a sensory array that tastes the air for the metallic tang of leaking current.

It clings to the underside of the leaning basalt columns, its belly pressed flush against the stone. It lives in the gaps where the weeping moisture is heaviest, absorbing the mineral-rich runoff until its brass exterior has crusted over with a thick, pulsating coat of the gelatinous polyps. It is a symbiotic nightmare—the Pipe-Witch provides the polyps with a mobile framework, and in return, the polyps act as a sensory skin, swelling and contracting to alert the creature to changes in the vibrations of the floor.

When it moves, it makes the sound of a heavy, water-logged door dragging over gravel. It is a scavenger of a higher order than the obsidian-shard constructs. It does not merely harvest minerals; it cannibalizes. It seeks out the "dead" machinery—the cold, seized gears that have stopped singing—and presses its frayed copper sensors against them. It enters a state of profound, static-filled meditation, vibrating at a frequency that matches the pipes. Bit by bit, it leaches the lingering, dying warmth from the copper, drawing the static into its own joints, turning itself into a living battery.

The Pipe-Witch is a hoarder of memories. Embedded within its twisted chassis are shards of etched glass and rusted dial-plates salvaged from the era of industry. It cannot read them, but their geometry pleases it. It rearranges these scraps with agonizing slowness, tucking them into the crevices of its brass frame as if trying to build a skeleton that might eventually remember how to move with purpose.

It is never truly alone, though it never gathers. It shares the Basin with others of its kind, drifting shadows of clicking brass that cross paths in the gloom. They do not communicate, but they recognize. When two Pipe-Witches meet, they do not fight. They pause, their copper sensors twitching in a rhythmic, discordant Morse code, exchanging electrical pulses like a brief, silent handshake. 

They are the janitors of a forgotten machine, the wet ghosts of an iron age. They are the only things in the Basin that seem to mourn the fact that the humming has ceased. They move through the dark, weeping condensation, waiting for the pipes to shudder back to life—not because they understand what the machinery once was, but because they are made of its debris, and they are terrified of the day the rust finally becomes total.
