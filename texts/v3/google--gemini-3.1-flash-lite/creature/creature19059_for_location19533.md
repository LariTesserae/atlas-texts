The inhabitants of the Rust-Sieve do not possess names, for names imply a distinction between the entity and the geography. They are the *Resonators*, though you would be just as accurate to call them the *Refractions*. 

They emerge from the shale not through birth, but by peeling away from the landscape itself. When a concentration of iron-dust reaches a specific density within the magnetic shadow of a Thrum-Pillar, the dust begins to vibrate in sympathy. Slowly, the grains coalesce, magnetized into delicate, shivering lattices. They form limbs that are less like bone and more like the needle-points of a compass, spindly and endlessly twitching. 

They are translucent, shifting constantly between the appearance of solid obsidian and a blurred cloud of iron filings. When they move, they do not walk; they calculate. They traverse the landscape by sliding along the invisible magnetic ley lines that tether the Pillars to the core. To watch them pass is to watch a glitch in a static photograph—a flickering sequence of movements that seem, for a heartbeat, to contradict the rigid geometry of the dunes.

A Resonator carries no internal organs. It has no stomach to fill, no lungs to burn, no heart to break. It is a hollow vessel for the hum. Its head—if the term can be applied to the uppermost aggregation of its shifting form—is a cluster of quartz-like sensors that spiral with the same rhythmic *thrum-click-shriek* of the Pillars. If you were close enough to one, you would not find eyes, but a singular, oscillating aperture that mirrors the moisture-patterns on the ground. 

They are the janitors of the eternal. 

Observe one as it pauses before a hexagon of lukewarm moisture. It does not drink from it; it *calibrates* it. The Resonator extends a limb—a wire-thin filament of conductive matter—and touches the surface of the condensation. The puddle ripples in perfect, mathematical concentricity. The Resonator is reading the electromagnetic feedback of the basin, sensing the minute discrepancies in the geological engine. If it detects a grain of grit an atom out of place, it taps the surface, and the entire patch of liquid shifts, the sediment realigning itself to maintain the basin’s terrifyingly perfect state.

They possess no concept of "self" because they are essentially extensions of the Thrum-Pillars, operating on a tether of pure physics. Sometimes, when the tectonic pressure deep beneath the crust spikes, a Resonator will freeze in place for a century, its silver-slick body catching the pale, neutral light, becoming a statue of pure function. They are not waiting for anything; they are simply fulfilling a directive dictated by the friction of the planet’s grinding heart.

There is no aggression in them. If you were to stand before one, you would be treated with the same indifference as a loose pebble or a patch of oxidation. To a Resonator, you are merely a chaotic variable, an object that lacks the decency to resonate in harmony with the engine. They might flicker around you, their limbs humming with an ozone-scorched heat, searching for the frequency at which your own body might be integrated—or, failing that, simply ignored—as they continue their endless, microscopic labor of keeping this graveyard polished, stagnant, and functioning until the end of time.
