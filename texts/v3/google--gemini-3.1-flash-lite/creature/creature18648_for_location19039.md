To call the occupants "inhabitants" feels an imposition of terminology they would not recognize, for they are less dwellers and more the byproduct of the world’s own metabolism. They are the **Silt-Striders of the Seams**, though they do not stride so much as they drift upon the edge of perception.

They are translucent, spindle-limbed entities, woven from strands of toughened silica and mineral residue. They possess no faces, only a complex arrangement of refractive nodes—spheres of polished obsidian that capture and distort the shifting, meridian light of the atmosphere. They look less like creatures of bone and blood and more like the living debris of the kiln, as if the floor itself, in its tectonic sighing, had accidentally pinched off a fragment of its own structural intent and granted it the capacity to move.

They live in the transition. They are perfectly attuned to the *thrum*. When the substrate begins its periodic, mile-wide shudders, the Striders flatten themselves against the ivory floor, their limbs interlocking with the geometric expansion joints. In these moments, they become indistinguishable from the architecture. They are the lubricators of the world. With long, needle-like appendages, they reach into the vents of the seams, clearing the microscopic blockages, harvesting the saline condensation, and delicately pruning the translucent, fungal stalks. 

They do not communicate, for there is no language in a world that is yet to be occupied. When they encounter one another in the vast, shimmering haze, they do not pause. Instead, they calibrate. They vibrate at a frequency that matches the cooling drizzle, a near-silent hum that serves to ensure their internal structures do not shatter under the thermal pressure of the floor. They pass through one another like ghosts of glass, overlapping their ethereal forms before drifting away toward the next set of monolithic pillars.

There is a sense of desperate, mechanical devotion in their movements. They are keepers of a museum that has no visitors. If a Striders’ limb becomes brittle and snaps under the intensity of the heat, it does not die; it simply falls into the grooves of the floor, where it is slowly absorbed back into the substrate, reclaimed by the vents to be turned into more floor, more growth, or perhaps—eventually—another Strider.

They are waiting for a pressure that never arrives, performing a custodial labor for a master who has long since abandoned the construction site. They are the echoes of a blueprint, living in the quiet, stifling intervals between the world’s tectonic breaths. They are the only things in this vast expanse that possess a pulse, yet their pulse is merely a rhythmic synchronization with the heartbeat of the kiln itself. They are not *in* the world; they are the world’s way of ensuring its own temperature remains constant, a collective, silent effort to keep the ceramic floor from cracking under the weight of an eternal, empty promise.
