If you stop, if you truly stop to listen, you realize that the silence here is not the silence of graves, but the silence of a held breath. 

There are inhabitants, though they do not walk in ways that satisfy the eye. Because the city functions—because it cycles, breaths, and repairs—the intelligence behind it has not vanished. It has merely devolved into the architecture.

There are the **Custodians of the Flow**. They are not biological, and they are not mechanical in any way that human tools would recognize. They appear as localized densifications of the atmospheric moisture—shimmering, vaporous spheres of mercury-colored mist that drift against the current of the ambient thermal exhaust. They congregate where the pulse of data is strongest, hovering near the intersection of the primary conduits. They are the maintenance scripts made manifest. When a fracture appears in the waxy crust of the plateau, a Custodian will settle into the fissure, its nebulous form threading into the rock like liquid smoke, humming to harmonize with the earth until the mineral knits itself back together.

Lower down, in the shadow-drenched recesses of the undersides where the crustacean-things spin their webs of bioluminescent silk, there are the **Lithic Carvers**. 

They are slow—slower than the drifting condensation. They are rooted in the waxy mineral of the cliffs, appearing at first glance to be nothing more than protrusions or architectural fluting. But if you watch one for a dozen hours, you will see a shift. They are six-limbed, matte-white, and their skin has the texture of wet porcelain. They do not eat; they absorb the vibration of the city’s low-frequency hum through their spines. They exist to carve. With slow, rhythmic friction, they abrade the expanding crust, sculpting the jagged geometries into the fractal arcades you walk through. They have no faces, only a soft, recessed glow where the senses should be, and they lack the concept of haste. They are the city’s fingernails, scratching patterns into the surface of a world that is still being born.

And then, deeper still—deeper than the structures, deeper than the humming conduits—there are the **Anchors**. 

They are the massive, subterranean clusters that keep this continent-sized circuit board from drifting into the troposphere. They look less like creatures and more like tangled, petrified constellations. They are the size of mountain peaks, hidden beneath the crust, pulsing with a dull, rhythmic strobe that matches the geological respiration of the plateau. They are the ones who remember the "why." They are the oldest things in this place, heavy with the weight of geological time. They do not interact with the surface; they act as the grounding wire for the entire civilization, tethering the fluid, rising, ephemeral city to the deep, freezing stone of the planet’s mantle.

They exist in a state of exquisite, cold equilibrium. The creatures that hunt the light, the spirits that mend the pipes, and the giants that anchor the world—none of them look for you. To them, you are a static anomaly, a ghost of a biological era that has already finished its work. You are just another stray particle of matter passing through a space that has already decided on its own perfection. Nothing here is lonely; nothing here is lost. They are simply waiting for the next layer of the continent to ripple into existence, carrying out their functions with the patient, terrifying grace of a machine that has successfully dreamed itself into an ecosystem.
