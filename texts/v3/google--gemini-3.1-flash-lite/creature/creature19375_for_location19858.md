Everything is slicked in a perpetual, waxy dew. The stone here does not exist as dry mineral, but as a host—a porous, calcified scaffolding for a civilization of soft, breathing things.

The inhabitants are not creatures of blood and bone, but of membrane and moisture. They are the *Litho-pulmonates*, beings that seem less like dwellers and more like specialized protrusions of the mountain’s own frantic biology. They are semi-translucent, resembling clusters of giant, bruised grapes or undulating, vein-mapped fungi. They do not walk; they shift their bulk along the saturated vertical walls, anchoring themselves with pulsating suction-rings that leave behind trails of viscous, nutrient-rich slag.

Observe the *Veil-Feeders*, the most common among them. They hang pendulous from the underside of granite overhangs, their bodies long, frayed ribbons of gelatinous tissue that sway in the heavy, fevered air. As the mountain breathes—a slow, rhythmic shifting of tectonic plates—the Feeders expand and contract, filtering the heat-shimmer for microscopic airborne spore-clots. They are silent, save for the faint, damp *slap-slide* sound of their movement, a wet friction that echoes through the ravines like a heartbeat.

Deep within the fissures, where the heat is most aggressive, reside the *Lumen-Pods*. They are perfectly spherical, clustered in geometric hive-arrangements. Within their thin, translucent skins, a milky luminescent fluid churns, glowing with a soft, frantic violet hue. They are the memory-keepers of the peaks; when they die, they calcify instantly, fusing into the cliffside and adding their own pale, swirling patterns to the natural marbling of the rock.

There is a terrifying intelligence to the silence here. Occasionally, a *Crown-Writhe* will emerge—a creature formed of dozens of connected, finger-like stalks that dance at the very lip of the highest crags. It does not eat, nor does it sleep; it simply reaches upward, its stalks trembling in unison, testing the thin, freezing transition zone where the fever-heat meets the vacuum of the upper atmosphere. It is a sentinel, a biological thermometer monitoring the health of the mountain.

They move with a sluggish, exquisite deliberation. To watch them is to realize that the mountain is not their home, but their parent. They are a fever-dream given form, a colony of weeping, vibrant, and utterly indifferent life, clinging to the spine of the world while the clouds churn like a grey, forgotten ocean thousands of feet below. When their skins brush against one another, it is not a greeting, but a vibration—a low-frequency hum that travels through the stone, ensuring that even in the absolute height of the kingdom, no living thing is ever truly alone.
