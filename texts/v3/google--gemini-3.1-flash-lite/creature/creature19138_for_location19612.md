The inhabitants of Khel-Vesh do not dwell *in* the city so much as they are secreted by it, much like the oxidized salts that run down the weeping concrete walls. They are not citizens in the way of poets or merchants; they are functional appendages, biological components that have grown into the gaps of the masonry.

If you were to look closely at a figure leaning against a steam-vent, you would struggle to determine where the person ends and the infrastructure begins. They are known as the **"Silt-Walkers,"** or colloquially, the *Hardened*. 

Their skin has taken on the color of bruised copper—a dull, oxidized teal—and is perpetually slick with that same cold, metallic dew that coats the architecture. They do not sweat water; they exude a viscous, lubricating oil that smells of ozone, which prevents their joints from seizing in the damp, grinding air of the basin. Most of them have replaced lost or useless appendages with salvaged clockwork. You might see a woman walking a child, but the woman’s left hand is a delicate, multi-jointed clamp designed for reaching into the microscopic gears of the Heart-Beams, and the child’s arm is encased in a rigid, brass housing that hums with the same low-frequency vibration as the bedrock.

They move with a terrifying, economy-driven grace. A Silt-Walker never wastes a motion. To do so would be to invite the surrounding moss to claim them. The lichen in Khel-Vesh is sentient in a slow, vegetative way, and it is hungry. Should a Silt-Walker become too stationary, too meditative, the emerald stalks creep up their boots, weaving through the laces, stitching their feet to the floorboards. Their eyes are milky, clouded over with cataracts that act as biological filters, sensitive only to the infrared flickers of the machinery’s heat-gradients and the bioluminescent pulses of the walls. 

They do not speak with lungs or lips. Their vocal cords have long ago calcified, replaced by small, vibrating resonators set into their throats. When they communicate, it is in a series of sharp, rhythmic clicks and subsonic thrums, mimicking the architecture itself. To an outsider, it sounds like the chittering of an insectile hive or the rattling of valves; to them, it carries the weight of history and instruction.

They eat, if you can call it that, by pressing their porous skin against the surfaces of the damp pipes. They absorb the condensation, the mineral runoff, and the chemical dregs of the city’s industrial metabolism. They are solar-powered in a world without sun, running entirely on the kinetic energy of the Heart-Beams.

In the non-Euclidean streets where the geometry loops and frays, the Silt-Walkers alone navigate with certainty. They hold no maps. Instead, they feel the "tug"—a localized gravitational pull exerted by the buildings as they grow. When a wall shifts or a new chimney erupts from the slush, they simply adjust their stride. They are the city’s nervous system, the roving gardeners of a factory that has forgotten its original purpose.

They do not sleep, not as a human does. They simply "de-cycle," leaning against a conductive copper beam, their inner gears locking into the low-frequency drone that pulses through the ground. In these moments, they become indistinguishable from the statues and the ductwork—just another protrusion of weeping concrete, waiting for the city to decide what it wants to grow into next. They are not the owners of Khel-Vesh. They are its memory, trapped in a metallic, humid dream that neither begins nor ends, but simply sustains itself through the shivering, relentless work of existing.
