In this pressurized node, the distinction between inhabitant and environment has long since dissolved. To look upon them is to look upon the city itself, manifested in a mobile, autonomous form.

They are the **Resonants**. 

They do not possess skeletons in the terrestrial sense, nor do they hold a stagnant shape. They are composed of a hyper-dense, semi-translucent cartilage that mimics the bone-white arches of their home, though it is far more supple, threaded with faint ribbons of bioluminescent nervous tissue that mirror the "streetlights" draped above. They are spindly, elegant things, composed of long, multi-jointed limbs that taper into fan-like cilia—fine as spun glass—which they use both to navigate the dense, heavy water and to caress the urban surfaces they nurture.

Their bodies are perpetually in a state of flux. To sit within a chamber is to witness a quiet, structural merging: a Resonant will press its dorsal surface against a budding polyp, skin porous and receptive, and as they shiver in the biting cold, they bleed calcium and mineral-rich secretions into the architecture. They are not merely living *in* the city; they are the city’s slow-motion circulatory system. 

They have no faces as surface-dwellers would define them. Instead, a cluster of sensory nodules—soft, iridescent spheres the size of pearls—sits where a head might be. These nodes do not see light; they sense the *thrum*. They feel the microscopic vibrations of the city’s rhythmic respiration. When two Resonants meet in the narrow arches, they do not speak. They entwine their cilia, vibrating at precise, cold frequencies that communicate the density of the water, the health of the calcified walls, and the slow, geological progress of the city’s growth.

Their movement is a deliberate, haunting choreography. They rarely swim; they drift and anchor. Seeing one move is like watching a star map being rearranged in real-time. They leave behind trails of sticky, translucent proteins that eventually harden into the fine filigree that decorates the chambers. 

Survival for the Resonants is a process of total absorption. Because the environment is so dense, they breathe by filtering the heavy, mineral-laden water through specialized, feather-like membranes located along their flanks. If they stop moving—if they yield too long to the biting chill—their own skin begins to harden, a permanent "becoming" that pins them to the walls. Many of the smooth, arched thresholds of this city were once individuals who simply grew tired, or whose cycle of respiration finally synced perfectly with the architecture, leaving them to stiffen into the very bone they had spent a lifetime secreting. 

They are delicate, shivering architects of a living tomb. They hold no concept of "outside," for to them, the world is a closed, pulsing loop of calcification. They are the gardeners of a garden that consumes the gardener, maintaining the walls with a quiet, clockwork devotion, forever weaving their own physical existence into the cold, glowing lattice of their birth. When you watch them, you do not see creatures living in a house; you see the house dreaming of itself, one Resonant movement at a time.
