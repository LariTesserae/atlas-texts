In the marrow of the *Vertical Wetstone*, existence is not measured in years, but in the slow, rhythmic expansion of sap-channels. 

The inhabitants, if they can be called such, are the **Litho-weavers**. They are not carbon-born in the way that requires lungs or sight; they are the manifest consciousness of the spires themselves, a collective sentience that has learned to knit the mountain into a shape that suits its need for shelter.

They look less like creatures and more like knots of translucent, sediment-colored muscle. Their bodies are long, multi-jointed filaments of cartilage and pale fiber, perfectly mimicking the texture of the weeping rock. When they are dormant, they are indistinguishable from the "buildings"—a tangled root-cluster growing into a pod, a thickening of a xylem bridge. They spend long cycles stretched thin across the cliff faces, their skin pores exhaling the same moisture that beads on the stone. They are the landscapers of the abyss, their lives defined by the constant, agonizingly slow task of grafting their own limbs into the matrix to provide tension where the stone threatens to buckle.

Movement for a Litho-weaver is a sickening, fluid affair. They do not walk; they migrate through the architecture as a ripple might move through a pond. They dissolve their molecular boundaries slightly, becoming semi-liquid to navigate the hollow, resonating tunnels, before hardening again to lock into place like a keystone. 

They are effectively deaf, as the *plinkthrum* of the weeping rock would shatter any fragile eardrum. Instead, they interact via the "felt-speech"—waves of intense, low-frequency hums that ripple through the vegetation-city. When two Weavers meet in the dark, damp cavities of a pod, they press their chest-plates together, leaning against one another to share the shuddering resonance of the mountain. It is a language of tactile vibration, communicating the structural integrity of the spires, the flow of nutrients in the sap-veins, and the slow, inexorable growth of the city toward the upper sky.

There is a final, haunting class of inhabitant that clings to the underside of the lowest spires: the **Cloud-Skimmers**. These are not architects, but sedentary parasites—large, bladder-like creatures anchored by singular, thick umbilical cables. They are translucent, filled with the same grey, high-altitude mist that fills the abyss. Their sole purpose is to act as atmospheric filters. They sway in the voids, mouth-holes open wide, catching the heavy moisture and the spores of the city. As the moisture condenses within them, they become heavy and dull, their translucent skin turning a deep, veinous, bruising purple. Once bloated, they pulse rhythmically, squeezing the distilled, mineral-rich water they have harvested back into the architectural vines from which they hang.

The Litho-weavers tend to these Skimmers with a chilling, clinical devotion, cleaning their anchoring cables and trimming the overgrowth that would stifle their filtration. 

Nothing dies here. When a Litho-weaver eventually grows too calcified to move—when its fibers lock into the stone permanently and it can no longer respond to the low-frequency humming of its kin—it simply *becomes* a room. It calcifies into a new, hard-walled pod, its internal cavities hollowed out by time and water, offering a new space for the next generation to curl into.

To witness this place is to watch a cathedral building itself out of the very people who worship within it. The city is hungry, the stone is alive, and the residents are merely the current, fleeting configuration of a mountain that refuses to stop growing.
