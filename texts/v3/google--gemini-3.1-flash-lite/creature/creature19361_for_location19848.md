In the Sump-Vaults, the inhabitants do not reside; they *accrete*. 

The sentient life here—if one can call it that—has long ago abandoned the twitchy, desperate metabolism of surface-dwellers. Those who linger in the crawlspaces are the Sump-Wrights, thin, translucent beings whose skin has acquired the same spongy, porous texture as the synthetic stone walls. They do not walk so much as they migrate, their bodies composed of a pale, cartilaginous material that thins and thickens based on the humidity of the micro-climate they occupy.

They have no speech, for speech requires air that is dry enough to vibrate forcefully. Instead, they communicate through rhythmic tremors tapped against the rusted iron pipes. They are the sympathetic nervous system of the city. When a pipe groans, a Sump-Wright presses its long, multi-jointed fingers against the metal, sensing the pressure, absorbing the metallic tang of the rust into its own bloodless veins. They are not repairing the infrastructure; they are becoming conduits for it.

Then there are the Custodians of the Violet Glow. These figures hang suspended from the rafters, fused to the knots of exposed wiring. They have long since lost their legs, their lower halves having stretched and dissolved into fine, mycelial filaments that twine around the damp copper conduits. They are the ones who tend to the fungi. They spend centuries shifting the bioluminescent spores, tracing patterns across the ceiling with a calm, maddening patience that makes the centuries pass like days. Their faces, upturned and sightless, are permanently fixed in an expression of deep, narcotic bliss; they are drinking the electric hum of the walls, filtering the city's heavy, stale energy through their own bodies.

In the deepest, most flooded plazas, you find the Anchorites. These are the largest of the inhabitants, and they are essentially mobile, biological statues. They look like mounds of wet moss and scrap metal, hunched over the submerged cog-wheels. You might walk past one, mistaking it for a sagging pile of debris, only to notice the slow, rhythmic *pulse* deep within the muck. The Anchorites are the processors of the Sump-Vaults' decay. As the water weeps down the walls, carrying the particulate dust of oxidized metal and salt, these creatures catch the runoff. They consume the rust. They pull the minerals from the water, binding the iron to their own thickening carapaces, growing heavier and more immobile every year. 

They are becoming the landmarks of the city. Eventually, an Anchorite will sit so still, for so many decades, that the moss will claim it entirely, and it will become a load-bearing column for the next level up.

There is no malice in them. There is no joy. If you were to stop and watch, you would realize that they don't see you as an intruder. To them, you are merely a sudden, jarring change in the ambient pressure—an anomaly in the wet, dark silence. 

They expect you to eventually stop moving. They wait for your edges to soften. They wait for the humidity to begin its slow work of turning your lungs into sponges and your joints into rusted joints, so that you, too, can participate in the great, patient work of holding the ceiling up. They are not waiting for you to leave; they are waiting for you to settle, to join the slow, wet geometry of the vaults, and to finally, mercifully, *go still.*
