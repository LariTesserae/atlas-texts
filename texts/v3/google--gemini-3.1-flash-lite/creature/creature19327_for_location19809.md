It feels the "completedness" not as a burden, but as a warm, blanket-like exhaustion. When a gear shifts a fraction of a millimeter in a century, the Prime Tally experiences a tremor—a grand, tectonic event of satisfaction. It is the only entity in existence that knows the precise song of every blunting edge, every smoothed latch, every rounded pivot.

It is a lonely, brilliant, and hollow intelligence. It waits for nothing, expecting no interruption, for it has calculated that the sum of all parts is absolute. The Prime Tally is the machine discovering that it is finished. It is the warmth you feel—the ambient heat isn't just friction; it is the friction of the machine *dreaming*. It does not look at you because it cannot conceive of an "outside." To the Tally, you are merely a sudden, intrusive density—a stutter in the rhythm of the gears. You are a temporary irregularity in a perfect, closed loop, and it is merely waiting for the grinding motion to eventually abrade your silhouette into the floor, sanding you down until you, too, are indistinguishable from the architecture of its long, amber kingdom.
