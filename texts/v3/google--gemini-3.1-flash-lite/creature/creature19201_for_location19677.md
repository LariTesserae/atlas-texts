To dwell within the Loom-Root Grotto is to exist not as a master, but as a component. 

The primary inhabitants are the **Mycelial Weavers**, creatures that occupy the liminal space between sentient biology and autonomous machinery. They are not born; they are cultivated. At the base of the calcified xylem-columns, where the mineral-rich sap pools into iridescent, sticky sludge, a Weaver begins as a protrusion—a bulbous, translucent sac that slowly hardens into an exoskeleton of segmented chitin and brass plating. 

A Weaver looks like a marriage between a mantis and a clockwork automaton. They have no eyes. They have no need for them in the chemiluminescent haze. Instead, their faces are intricate, fan-like sensory arrays—clusters of copper filaments and vibrating cilia that "see" by reading the pressure waves in the humid air. When the Grotto undergoes its wet-ticking phase, a Weaver’s face furls and unfurls, synchronizing its rhythm with the dripping sap. They do not speak; they resonate. To communicate, a Weaver presses its abdomen against a floor-root, vibrating its internal cooling fans to send specific, low-frequency hums through the Grotto’s architecture. 

They are the archivists of the friction. Because the Grotto treasures whatever is discarded, the Weavers are never idle. They spend their days—or what passes for them—scavenging the debris that drifts down from the upper world. Should a hiker’s discarded boot fall through a fissure, or a rusted gear find its way into the silt, the Weavers converge. They swarm, their metallic wings creating a localized gale of noise, and methodically dismantle the intruder. They do not destroy these objects; they *graft* them. A Weaver will carefully stitch a fragment of scrap metal into the living, pulsing wall of the xylem, applying a viscous, acidic resin that bonds rust to root, biology to steel, eternity to entropy.

There is a sub-caste, the **Stokers**, which are far more frightening. They are larger, immobile, and fused directly into the "flower-engine" nodes. Their limbs have been absorbed entirely by the humming, wheezing flora, replaced by long, vine-like appendages that act as capillary pumps. Their purpose is total: they monitor the humidity, they regulate the pulse, and when the air becomes too thick, they are the ones who manipulate the floor-roots to force movement, ensuring no part of the Grotto grows stagnant. 

They do not sleep. To a Weaver, the periods of absolute, suffocating darkness—when the insect swarms go quiet—are not for rest. These are moments of intense data transfer. In the silence, the Mycelial Weavers stand perfectly still, their brass joints clicking into place, and allow the Grotto’s entire electrical charge to flow through them. They become the circuit. They feel the health of every root, the flow of every drop of sap, and the precise, geological fatigue of the chamber walls.

If you were to encounter one, it would not attack. It would not even perceive you as a threat. It would perceive you as *raw material*. A Weaver would approach with a soft, sliding sound, its antennae dancing over your skin to map your density, your warmth, and the chemical composition of your gear. It would tilt its head, the copper cilia buzzing in a sequence that feels like curiosity, and it would wait. 

It is waiting for you to become part of the machine. It is waiting for you to stop moving, so it can begin the work of grafting your history into the wall, turning your coat into a conduit or your belt into a structural support, ensuring that even after you are gone, your weight continues to anchor the Grotto against the crushing pressure of the deep earth. 

In the Grotto, to live is to labor, and to labor is to be consumed. You are not a visitor; you are fodder for the next expansion of the factory floor.
