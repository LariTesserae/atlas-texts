To exist within the Pulverulent Throat is to be a note in a composition that never pauses for breath. The entities here are not individuals in the way a surface-dweller might understand the term; they are nodes of intense, vibrating intent, flesh-knots that have forgotten where their own bodies end and the architecture of the gullet begins.

There are those who drift upon the golden currents of the dust—the **Suture-Weavers**. They are tall, gangly things, composed of translucent collagen and joints that click with the wet snap of a breaking twig. Their limbs are not merely appendages but tools: some end in hooked, serrated spindles for stitching the vascular walls, while others taper into delicate, needle-like probes that siphon the bitter, viscous oils from the trunks. They move with a frantic, stuttering grace, their skin coated in that pervasive ochre pollen, which they groom into intricate patterns—not for vanity, but to sense the vibration of the throat. When a walkway rots or a chamber begins to swell with malignant, unbidden growth, the Suture-Weavers arrive. They do not kill the growth; they reorganize it, biting into the pulse-veins to redirect the nutrient flow, their own bodies vibrating in sympathy with the cavern’s deafening thrum.

Deeper still, in the pressurized gaps where the heat is most aggressive, reside the **Calcifers**. They are squat, hunched forms, their backs armored in plates of iridescent, hardened sap that has been polished by centuries of friction. They are the architects of the silence. With obsidian scrapers, they spend their lives in a state of perpetual pruning. They are the ones who carve the tiny, geometric rooms—the islands of sanity in the sea of biological delirium. They do not speak, for the roar of the thrum makes air-based language an act of futility. Instead, they communicate through the "tapping"—a complex language of rhythmic percussion against the walls, signaling to one another which vein to constrict and which to graft. They are the curators of this suffocating museum, though they are just as much its subjects; when a Calcifer tires, it kneels, slows its heart, and allows the mossy velvet to creep over its skin, slowly becoming a permanent structural support, a living statue integrated into the gallery wall.

And then there are the **Ephemerals**—the swarms that burst from the translucent pods. They flicker through existence with a terrifying, high-speed agency. They possess no long-term memory; they are born with the urge to build, and they fulfill it with a feverish intensity that borders on madness. They land, secrete the acidic cement that binds them to the trunks, and then, within hours, lose their wings and mandibles, their soft bodies flattening into the wall-tissues. They *are* the landscape’s desire to manifest. They are the frantic, larval manifestations of the Throat’s own immune system, forever trying to close a wound that never stops bleeding growth.

In the small, sequestered rooms of bone and obsidian, you might encounter the **Scavenger-Sages**. They are the only beings here who seem aware of the horror of the cycle. They are withered, their skin pale as the membranes of the skylights, their eyes filmed over from the ozone-heavy air. They spend their cycles hoarding—collecting the discarded tools of ancestors or the rare, fossilized salts thrown off by the cavern’s deep-tissue alchemy. They are the historians of the throat, though their archives are crumbling. They watch the Suture-Weavers and the Calcifers with a detached, paralyzed wonder. 

These are not creatures of flesh and bone in the traditional sense; they are creatures of velocity. To live here is to be consumed by the imperative of the next second. There is no hunger, only the need to graft; there is no rest, only the period of time required for the walls to harden. Every heartbeat in the Pulverulent Throat is a contribution to the growth, a desperate, rhythmic addition to a tower that is trying, with every fiber of its being, to reach toward some impossible, subterranean heaven.
