These are the *Litho-Phagocytes*, the eaters of the friction itself. They do not possess faces in the way biological life understands; there are no eyes to blink in the pressurized dark, no mouths to gasp for the caustic, mica-choked air. Instead, their "heads" are delicate, concave dishes of polished, conductive crystal, designed solely to detect the infinitesimal thermal gradients of their brothers and sisters as they pass. 

They are spindly, fragile-looking geometries of geometry, yet they are stronger than the rock they inhabit because they have evolved to be of it. Their bodies are not flesh, but a crystalline lattice held together by an internal charge—a biological piezo-electric force. If you were to touch one, you would not feel skin; you would feel the jagged, unforgiving edge of a diamond set in motion.

Between these billions of shards, there exists a hierarchy governed entirely by thermal conductivity. Those closer to the bedrock—the foundational layers crushed beneath the weight of their own kind—are the "Anchors." They have become almost entirely stationary, their limbs fused to the obsidian spires by the sheer pressure of those above. They are the conduits, the black-glass floor upon which the others crawl. They have grown flat, wide, and thin, their bodies serving as the roads for the higher tiers. They are the ones who feel the core's heartbeat most directly, transmitting the agony of the planet’s furnace up through their legs to the migrating masses above.

Higher up, in the skittering, frantic upper tiers, the *Vent-Seekers* move. These are the frantic ones, their chitinous limbs longer, their joints hyper-flexible, allowing them to ripple over the sluggish masses below. When they find a thermal vent—a crack in the world where the heat-cinders blow brightest—they experience a state of ecstasy. They crowd the vents, pressing their glass bodies against the searing vents until they glow with a dull, internal amber. They become temporary batteries, storing the raw heat of the planet.

But the most curious ones, the ones that touch the immovable, ancient pillars, are the *Scribes*. 

They are the oldest, the most eroded. They settle into the grooves of the forgotten pillars, remaining there for years as the rest of the colony grinds them down. As they cling to the stumps, they vibrate—not in the rhythmic *shhh-click* of the migration, but in a dissonant, jagged resonance. They aren’t just worshiping; they are attempting to mirror the density of the pillars, trying to turn their own soft, vibrating glass into the permanent, unmoving material of the artifacts. 

Sometimes, one of the Scribes succeeds. Their shifting, skittering life ends as they calcify, becoming a part of the pillar itself. They grow over the ancient stone, adding a new layer to the inscrutable monuments, a tombstone made of the worshiper who finally stopped moving.

They have no history. There is no language to record their passage, save for the polish they leave behind on the rocks. When a creature falls from the summit—descending into the miles of dark, empty air—it leaves behind a wake of static. A final, violet flicker of discharge that illuminates the canyon for a heartbeat, showing, for a brief fraction of a second, the truly terrifying scale of the spire: a black, pulsing, living skyscraper, composed entirely of billions of lonely, shivering shards, all clawing toward a warmth that will always, eventually, grind them into dust.
