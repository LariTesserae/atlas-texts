The inhabitants of the basin are not creatures of biology so much as they are creatures of maintenance. They are the **Suture-Kin**, and they look like the forgotten sketches of an architect who lacked skin.

They are spindly, translucent things—their forms suggest the spindly geometry of daddy-long-legs stretched into a humanoid silhouette. Their limbs are composed of a rigid, milky cartilage that catches the harsh, clinical glare of the ceiling fissures, turning them into prismatic flickers that vanish as quickly as they appear. They have no faces, only a smooth, pearlescent basin of bone where a brow might be, tipped with a collection of vibrating, thread-like sensory filaments that twitch in rhythm with the subterranean tectonic shifts.

There is a tragedy in their composition. They are semi-liquid, their internal organs visible as slow-moving, opalescent clusters of fluid that shift and reorganize depending on the labor they perform. When a Suture-Kin drags a slab of glass, its core glows a frantic, bruised violet; when it presses its body against the honeycomb to seal a crack, the core dulls to a flat, exhausted grey. 

They do not sleep, nor do they feed in the way an animal feeds. Instead, they graze upon the condensation. When the freezing dew descends from the high fissures, a Suture-Kin will press its entire length against the cold, wet surface of the city’s ribs, absorbing the oily moisture through their porous flanks. They seem to extract a kind of mineral sustenance from the friction—the heat generated by their constant *scrape-slide-thud* against the earth is what sustains their flickering, low-wattage life.

They do not recognize each other. They collide with the grace of passing shadows, their limbs interlocking momentarily in a tangle of glass and bone, only to pull apart and resume their frantic, individual tasks. There is no hierarchy, though there are 'Anchorers'—older, larger individuals whose limbs have become fused to the structures they maintain. These elders are nearly immobile, their bodies stretching across the gaps in the honeycomb like living mortar, their filaments constantly tapping against the stone to monitor the structural resonance of the city, reporting vibrations to a collective that no longer knows how to understand the data.

They are obsessed with the 'Correctness' of the architecture, yet the definition of correct changes with every pulse of the mineral light above. What one Suture-Kin builds in the bright phase, another will dismantle the moment the light shifts. It is an instinctual compulsion, a compulsive housekeeping for a home that was never meant for living beings.

If you were to stare long enough into the tangle of their long, spindly limbs, you would notice the absences. Here and there, a Suture-Kin will simply cease, its cartilage turning brittle and opaque. It does not fall; it is immediately set upon by three or four others. They do not eat their own. Instead, they carefully dismantle the fallen, breaking it down into raw, structural components—a rib becomes a support, a femur becomes a resin-scraper—and folding the remains back into the honeycomb of the city. 

They are the city, and the city is them. Nothing enters this place, and nothing leaves, because the Suture-Kin have forgotten that anything exists beyond the ribs. To them, the world is not a basin; it is a draft. They are the ink and the eraser, perpetually redrawing the floor plan of a vacuum, forever rushing to finish a monument to a god who left the room millions of years ago.
