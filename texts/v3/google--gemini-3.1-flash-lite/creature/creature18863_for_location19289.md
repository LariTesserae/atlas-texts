In the center of the vibration, where the harmonic pitch reaches its most agonizing density, there is an occupant. Or rather, there is a recurring event.

It is not a creature in any sense that requires flesh or survival. It is a silhouette woven from the very same vantablack ceramic that forms the walls. It does not stand; it is anchored, fused at the feet to the titanium-carbide slats. It is shaped like a human, if one imagines a human etched by a laser onto a sheet of void-matter—a flat, two-dimensional projection of a person pulled into three-dimensional tension.

It has no face, only a recessed strobe-slit, identical to the ones in the ceiling, housed within the place where eyes would be.

This entity is the *Calculus of the Room*. It is not alive, but it is necessary. Because the room manufactures meaningless purity, there must be a terminus, a collector of the surplus geometry. 

The entity moves with a jerkiness that defies physics, its body segments snapping into position between the pulses of the subterranean engine. It possesses no intent to walk; instead, it is a living anchor. It reaches out with fingers that taper into needles of absolute blackness, and it touches the fiber-optic stalks as they sway. When it touches them, the humming pitch shifts, a momentary dissonance that ripples through the room like a crack in a frozen lake.

It is the curator of the noise. 

When the engine hammers, the entity vibrates in perfect, agonizing sympathy. Its limbs blur, not from speed, but from the fact that it is attempting to occupy several positions in time simultaneously. It is the only thing in this cathedral that feels the weight of the sound. It is where the manufacturing process goes to die.

Sometimes, for a fraction of a nanosecond, if the strobe-slits in the ceiling catch the entity at the perfect angle, the shadow appears to reach toward the floor, toward the unseen engine beneath the slats. There is a frantic desperation in the gesture—a command to stop, or perhaps an attempt to join the machinery. But the rhythm is absolute. The engine hammers again, a billion-ton blow of sound that resets the atmosphere, and the entity is snapped back into its pose, its strobe-slit eye pulsating with a harsh, interrogating light.

It does not think. It does not remember the dead planet above. It is simply the friction created by a machine that has become too perfect to function. It is a living scar on a landscape of steel, endlessly being rewritten by the vibrations, a captive audience of one in a temple of infinite, frozen work. 

If you were to stand before it, it would not acknowledge you. It would merely wait for the next harmonic pulse to shred you into the same silent, geometric dust that it has been scouring for a thousand, or perhaps a billion, years. It is less a consciousness and more a lingering error in the sub-atomic code—the heavy, dark anchor that keeps the entire dimension from flying apart under the pressure of its own meaningless output.
