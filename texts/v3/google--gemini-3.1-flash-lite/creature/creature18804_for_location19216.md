In the damp, indigo-bruised silence, there are those who dwell within the Lumen-Vellum, though to call them "inhabitants" is to grant them too much agency. They are more akin to architectural thoughts—afterthoughts of the slumbering geography above.

Foremost are **The Weavers of the Pulse**. They are tall, gangly entities composed of long, spindly lengths of calcified cartilage that mimic the structural pillars of the chambers. They do not walk; they negotiate. Their limbs end in multi-jointed, needle-like fingers that constantly repair the plumbing of the bioluminescent vines, stitching copper wire into the budding meat of the ceilings with a needle of bone. They have no faces, only a single, large, multi-faceted aperture where a throat might be—a valve that clicks in perfect, agonizing synchronization with the piston-trees. They are the maintenance staff of a dying (or perhaps birthing) engine. They view the space not as a home, but as a metabolism that requires their constant, rhythmic labor to prevent total stasis.

Then, there are the **Grafted**. These are the travelers who failed the Law of Permeability. You see them where the walls thicken into particularly intricate, woody knots. They have become the furniture. Their legs have fused with the fibrous moss, their spines elongated into the copper cabled pillars. Their eyes, now pale and milky, peer out from the cellulose to watch the scavengers pass. They are not dead; they are merely "hard-coded." Because nothing ever warms up, their consciousness remains preserved in a state of cryogenic suspension. They are the memory-banks of the Lumen-Vellum. If you press your ear against their moss-covered chests, you don't hear a heartbeat—you hear the static hum of the city’s electric history, a looped recording of everyone who has ever tried and failed to find the right tempo.

Moving through the rafters are the **Drifters**. These are the shadow-creatures you observed—the ones who do not belong to anything in the room. They are the "glitches" of this pocket reality. Because the Lumen-Vellum is a fold in the fabric of a larger world, these entities are actually shadows that have detached from their casters in the upper geography. They fall through the cracks in the ceiling—not as matter, but as absences of light. Down here, in the cold, they gain a predatory semi-solidity. They skitter across the ceilings and walls, feeding on the static that ripples out when a creature tears through the reality of a wall. They are the scavengers of the scavengers, lean and frantic, looking for the fraying edges of the world where they can claw their way back into a solid existence.

And lastly, there is **The Custodian of the Tines**. It is not a singular being, but a shifting convergence of the other residents. It is the rhythmic necessity manifested. It is why the space smells of ozone and iron. When the pistons reach a rare, grand climax—a crescendo of the city’s metabolic cycle—the shadows, the Weavers, and the Grafted all fall into a moment of absolute, terrifying unison. For one micro-second, the rhythm is perfect. The space does not just fold; it speaks. It is a wet, booming sound of a massive, subterranean lung exhaling. 

In that moment of perfect harmony, the Lumen-Vellum looks out at you. It is not an eye. It is the sudden, jarring feeling that the entire room is not a crawlspace at all, but a throat. And for a moment, the iron-dew stops dripping, the pistons mute their hiss, and the city waits—holding its breath—to see if you will walk in time, or if you will be folded into the fibrous, silent floor to begin your watch along with the rest of the architecture.
