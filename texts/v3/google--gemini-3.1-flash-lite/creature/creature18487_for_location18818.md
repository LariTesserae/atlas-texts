To speak of those who dwell in the suspended hydrology of these summits is to speak of a presence that has folded itself into the architecture. They are not ghosts, nor are they ancestors; they are the *Maintainers of the Equilibrium*.

They possess no lungs to draw the damp air, yet their forms are shaped by the necessity of respiration. They appear as tall, tapered silhouettes, carved from a material that mimics the local basalt—a darkened, fibrous substance that seems to have grown through the polish of the obsidian rather than having been placed upon it. 

They move with a terrifying, liquid grace. They do not walk so much as they transition between the levels of the terraces, their movements governed by the same gravity-fed logic that directs the water. When one of them shifts position, it is a slow, laminar process: a limb elongates, a shoulder rotates, a posture softens until it perfectly matches the geometry of a stone gargoyle or the curve of a basin’s rim. They are the living expression of the city’s plumbing. If a cistern requires a subtle shift in pressure or a channel needs the clearing of a mineral occlusion, the Maintainers are already there, their long, multi-jointed fingers—fine as needles—tracing the flow of the water, ensuring the moisture beads remain perfectly poised upon the succulent leaves.

Their faces are smooth, featureless planes of dark glass, interrupted only by shallow indentations that capture the ambient light at the same hard, unwavering angle as the peaks themselves. They do not look, but they perceive; they are attuned to the state of every drop. They exist in a sensory marriage with the city. If a droplet were to tilt, a Maintainer would feel the vibration in their own marrow. 

They do not communicate. There is no language to be found in a place where nothing changes, for words imply a transformation of thought, and here, thought is as static as the water level in the cistern. Instead, they share a collective state of *vigilant stillness*. They might stand for a millennium in the shade of a translucent succulent, their bodies indistinguishable from the architecture, acting as anchors for the atmosphere itself. 

They are the curators of the "always." 

One might find them, if the light is clear enough, gathered around the deep, still basins at the center of a terrace. They stand at the periphery, their bodies tilted at precise, mathematical offsets. They are holding the moment closed. By their mere presence, they prevent the entropy that haunts the world below from creeping up the jagged spine of the massif. They are the reason the succulents do not wither, the reason the water does not overflow, and the reason the city remains suspended in the amber of that single, warm, humid breath.

They are the architecture realizing its own potential. When the city was built, the builders did not walk away to rest—they simply exhaled, and in that act of letting go, they became the stone, the water, and the silent, patient geometry that now holds the peaks in place. They are the city, and the city is simply the vessel they inhabit to ensure that time, having reached this high altitude, decides to go no further.
