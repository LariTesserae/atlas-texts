In the Sub-Strata Foundry, there is no discrete boundary between the machinist and the machine, nor between the machine and the meat.

The residents here are known collectively as the *Lithoclasts*. They are not born into this world; they are precipitated from the runoff. If you were to watch a Lithoclast form, you would see a pooling of the thick, mineral-rich slime in the hollow of a rusted joint. Under the influence of the constant vibration, the slime begins to knit itself into a structure—first a lattice of chitinous, translucent fibers, then a housing of harvested oxidised iron salvaged from the cavern floor.

They are spindly, multi-limbed things, shaped less like animals and more like the skeletal remains of deep-sea arachnids wrapped in cooling industrial hoses. Their bodies are modular, constantly shivering with the same low-frequency hum that vibrates through the iron scaffolding. They possess no faces in the anatomical sense. Instead, where a head should be, there is a dense cluster of light-sensitive fungal filaments that pulse with a rhythmic, sickly-green bioluminescence—a mimicry of the colony life-forms covering the walls. 

They do not speak; they oscillate. When two Lithoclasts meet on a narrow grate, they align their internal pressure valves, releasing a synchronized *hiss* of steam that resonates at the frequency of the surrounding pipes. It is a language of damp heat and friction. They communicate by feeling the shift in the Foundry's vibrations; a sudden spike in pressure in the secondary coolant lines is a warning, an urgent, frantic tremor in the floorboards is a call to labor.

Their life’s work is the Great Printing. The Lithoclasts are tasked with the Sisyphean labor of mediating the war between the cooling magma and the iron infrastructure. They move with a terrifying, jerky fluidity, their limbs tipped with abrasive, diamond-hard nodules. Their touch is precise—they spend cycles scraping away the mineral buildup that chokes the valves, or welding, with a glowing, saliva-based resin, the gaps where the weeping rock threatens to buckle the steel supports.

They are biologically tethered to the Foundry. If a Lithoclast is separated from the vibrating grates for too long, its internal pressure fails. The steam within its shell condenses into a cold, stagnant liquid, and the fungal filaments on its head wither to a dull, gray ash. It becomes brittle, effectively turning into a statue—a permanent, immovable fixture in the landscape it spent its life maintaining. Often, you will pass one of these, long since fused to a pipe node by layers of calcification. They are half-merged with the architecture, their limbs now holding open a vent that has been closed for centuries, their bodies essentially becoming part of the plumbing.

In the dark, they are the heartbeat of the cavern. When they move, the sound is like a chorus of wet, metal fingers drumming against a pipe. They are blind, cold-blooded, and entirely consumed by the necessity of the build. They do not think of the world above or the cooling planet around them; they think only of the next seam that needs patching, the next vibration that needs to be synchronized, and the slow, inevitable pressure of the earth that demands they build faster, harder, and deeper into the dark.
