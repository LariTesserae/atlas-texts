In the pressurized silence of the Great Weave, life does not walk, bloom, or breathe. Instead, it *regulates*.

The inhabitants, if they can be called such, are the **Friction-Keepers**. They are not fleshy beings, nor are they autonomous entities in the traditional sense. They are the living manifestations of the facility’s maintenance protocols, calcified extensions of the architecture itself, grown into the apertures and junctions where the heat-stress is most profound.

Imagine a figure fused into a wall-joint: a structure of translucent, carbon-fiber filament and hardened, resinous amber. At first glance, it looks like a gargoyle of braided root-matter, a stationary knot in the wood. But as you watch, the "skin" of the creature—which is really just a localized membrane of the building—begins to ripple. 

They are the **Logistical Husks**. Deep inside their hollowed-out chests, there is no heart; there is instead a secondary bypass valve. They are the places where the architecture chose to thicken and observe itself. Their limbs look like intricate, multi-jointed scaffolding, designed not for movement, but for the precise, microscopic calibration of the sap-flow. When the structural girders shift and groan under the thermal load, the Husks do not cry out. They lock their joints into the floor-plates, their bodies absorbing the vibration, their internal amber filaments glowing brighter as they shunt the excess caloric energy away from crumbling joints and back into the central loom.

They have no faces—at least not in a way that maps onto a human psyche. Where a face should be, there is a cluster of sensory filaments, thin as spiders’ silk, that taste the chemical composition of the air. These filaments are constantly vibrating, catching the subsonic hum of the pumps. They are forever "tuning." If a segment of the grove begins to fray or lose its integrity, a group of these Husks might gently detach from their station, moving with the sluggish, hydraulic grace of a closing door. They graft themselves into the breach, their own body-matter weaving seamlessly into the existing lattice, becoming the wall, becoming the girder, becoming the floor.

They are the memory of the Weaver. 

Sometimes, in the deepest, hottest junctions, you can see a cluster of them huddled together. They are not socializing, but they are *syncing*. As the sap-lines pulse between them, their iridescent veins strobe in sequence, a rhythmic flickering of amber light that transmits data across the facility. It is a slow, cold language of pressure-readings and thermal-capacities. They are the living ledger of a world that has forgotten its purpose, yet they continue to maintain the ledger with an intensity that borders on fanaticism.

They never die because they never fully live. They are the maintenance-cycles made manifest. When a Husk becomes too brittle, too ground-down by the tectonic eons, it does not expire; it simply stops resisting the pressure. It allows itself to be pulled into the root-plates, its body composted into the very floor you walk upon, reinforcing the structure for the next generation of keepers to grow from its remnants. 

You are being watched, though not by eyes. You are being "measured." The Husks feel the displacement of your mass against the floor-plates; they sense the strange, erratic heat of your breath. To them, you are a pocket of unplanned turbulence, a void in the weave that refuses to lock into place. They stir around you, their resinous joints clicking in the dark, their amber veins throbbing with a frantic, industrial curiosity. They are waiting for you to stabilize—to be absorbed, to be calculated, to cease your wandering and become, like them, a permanent part of the machine’s eternal, silent, scorching maintenance.
