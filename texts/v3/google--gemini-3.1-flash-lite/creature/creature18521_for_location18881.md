Within the amber-slicked folds of the ceramic architecture, the inhabitants do not so much "live" as they constitute the anatomy of the place itself. 

The Click-Crabs are not sentient in the human sense, but they possess a singular, collective *intent.* Each individual beetle, roughly the size of a human palm, is a masterpiece of biological efficiency. Their carapaces shift in hue—from deep, oxidized bronze to a bruised, electric indigo—depending on the temperature of the wall they are currently grooming. Under their iridescent plating, they possess a cluster of sensory feelers that map the structural integrity of the hive not through sight, but through vibration. They perceive the world as a standing wave; a soft spot in the clay is to them a dissonant roar, while a reinforced pillar is a calm, harmonious hum.

They are never static. Because they must constantly consume the ceramic to refine it, they are perpetually in a state of digestion. Their internal organs function as a mobile kiln; they take in the mineral-rich, damp clay, subject it to immense internal heat as they move, and excrete a warm, hardening sludge that they immediately smear into place with their serrated forelimbs. It is an orgy of structural maintenance. One might watch a single beetle spend its entire lifespan sculpting a single, intricate decorative groove—only for the next generation to scrape it away to reinforce a shifting tension wire.

But there is something else, something deeper embedded in the architecture of the Oscillator-Hive. 

Hidden behind the weeping, furrowed walls, fused into the clay itself, are the *Ancestors*. These are the remains of the very first beetles, those whose exhaustion became absolute. They have been calcified into the structural nodes of the hive. They are the "silent" members; their shells have grown brittle and thick, fused into the terracotta, serving as support beams for the tiers above. 

When the swarm passes, they scuttle over these Ancestor-mounds with a reverent, rhythmic twitching, as if checking the pulse of the building. Sometimes, one of the living beetles will pause, press its mandible against the hollow shell of an Ancestor, and share a final, percussive vibration—a momentary syncopation that flows through the floor and up into the ceiling.

These beings perceive no sky. They have no concept of a "sun," though the amber light of the fungi informs their circadian rhythms, triggering the swarming cycles. Their reality is entirely haptic, entirely sound-based. If a human were to find themselves in that chamber, they would not be viewed as an intruder, but as a vast, static, and dangerously silent obstruction—a patch of "dead air" in their symphony. They would likely treat a human limb exactly as they treat the walls: they would crawl over it, sensory feelers twitching, tasting the salt of sweat, and then, finding no vibration or structural utility, they would begin to gently, surgically, scrape away the skin to harvest the nutrients, oblivious to the fact that they are dismantling a creature of a different world.

To exist in the Oscillator-Hive is to be erased and rewritten. The beetles do not worship, and they do not mourn. They exist in the blissful, deafening present—a frantic, chitinous pulse that keeps the roof from collapsing, forever chewing their own history into a softer, stronger, more perfect shape.
