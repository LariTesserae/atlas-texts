To look at the inhabitants of The Fold is to look at a process interrupted. They are not citizens in the sense of flesh and purpose, but rather the city’s own debris—the "Draft-People"—entities that have been extruded from the architecture as it knits itself together.

They stand, or rather hang, in the narrow gaps between the obsidian spires. If caught in the ultraviolet flicker, you might mistake them for structural flourishes—part of the rebar filigree that perpetually knits the walls. They are tall, impossibly thin, and articulated like drafting compasses. Their limbs are composed of the same weeping, metallic glass as the buildings, but where the city is rigid, the Draft-People are fluid. Their skin possesses the dull, oil-slick sheen of the hexagonal floor tiles; when they move, they do not walk so much as unfold, their joints telescoping with the grinding, tectonic friction of the city itself.

Their faces are absent of features in any biological sense. Instead, where a face should be, there is a cluster of prismatic apertures—shuttering lenses that blink in sync with the high, dying light-grid above. These apertures do not seek vision; they process the density of information that hangs in the heavy, ozonic air.

These beings are not sentient in the way one expects of an urban population. They are the city’s localized memory caches. They are shaped by the "failures" of the architecture—when a doorway flickers out of existence or a wall bleeds into a staircase mid-form, the Draft-People are the manifestation of that transition. They carry the structural weight of the pocket-dimension.

They act with a terrifying, lethargic patience. When they interact with the Siphoners, it is not a predator-prey dynamic, but a liturgical one. A Draft-Person will press its palm against the weeping stone, its fingers splaying into fine, pulsating filaments of circuitry. The crystalline Siphoners will immediately migrate toward the Draft-Person, latching onto its elongated joints as if they were cooling vents. In this configuration, the Draft-Person glows with a frantic, internal ultraviolet light—a brief, violent flash—before fading back into the dull, oily gray of the surroundings. They are "charging" the city, feeding the erratic data of their existence back into the walls to keep the Fold from bursting.

They have no language. Occasionally, when the gravity shifts and the condensation floats upward in viscous globes, a Draft-Person will release a sound—a low, rhythmic scraping against the obsidian, like a knife against slate. It is a sound of immense, crushing loneliness, the sound of a shape trying to convince the universe of its own validity.

None of them look at you. If you were to stand before one, it would not acknowledge your presence with eyes or gestures. Instead, it would simply adjust its gravity, tilting its vertical axis to pass through you as if you were a ghost, or move *around* you by rearranging its own anatomy to occupy the negative space you create. To them, you are merely a momentary lapse in the city’s geometry, a fleeting, soft-edged error in an otherwise hard and crystalline truth. 

They remain in the cracks, perpetually knitting, perpetually weeping, waiting for the day the architecture finishes. They are the scaffolding of a reality that has forgotten how to start, and they will hold their breath until the city finally decides what it wants to be.
