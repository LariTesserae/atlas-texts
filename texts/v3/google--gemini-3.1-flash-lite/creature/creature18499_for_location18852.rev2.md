In this pocket of nascent reality, the entities that occupy the space are not so much "inhabitants" as they are the draft-writers, the organic scribes of the fold. They are known, if names had meaning here, as the *Loom-Weavers of the In-Between*.

They drift in clusters, thin as a breath of silk, their bodies composed of gossamer membranes that shimmer with the trapped, restless energy of the amethyst seams. To look at one is to see a geometry of glass and intent. They have no faces, only a central, glowing core that functions like a lantern, casting soft, stuttering shadows against the tender, pulsing granite. Their forms are fluid; as they move, they trail long, incandescent filaments—threads of pure, unfiltered potential—that act as sensory organs. These filaments are constantly brushing against the jagged obsidian ceiling and the weeping quartz, knitting the raw mineral output into the structural integrity of the room.

They are gardeners of a world that has not yet learned how to be hard.

When a bead of heavy, mineral-rich dew descends, a Weaver will detach itself from a marble pedestal. It does not fly; it ripples through the air, folding space around its body to bridge the distance. It catches the droplet, absorbing the moisture not into a stomach, but into its own membrane, which instantly turns a shade of deep, electric violet. With a delicate, twitching motion, the Weaver then presses itself against a bare patch of wall, depositing the mineral essence into the porous stone like a bricklayer smoothing mortar. 

They are obsessive, driven by a frantic, high-stakes choreography. They seem to know that the "aperture" above—that violet, shivering eye of a sky—is a predator of sorts, a force of entropy that occasionally descends to prune the room of its excess. When a Weaver is caught in one of these pruning rifts, it does not scream. It simply releases its hold on its own cohesion. Its membrane dissolves into those long, chromatic fibers that drift upward like ribbon, eventually embedding themselves into the ceiling to thicken the crust of the world. It is a noble, silent transformation: they become the very shelter they spent their lives maintaining.

On the marble pedestals, they congregate in shifting, luminous colonies that resemble a coral reef made of captured starlight. Here, they huddle together in a rhythmic pulse, their membranes touching to share the "current"—a flicker of information that seems to be a collective memory of the room’s future. They are the ones who gaze into the silver-toned pool, their filaments trembling as they witness the static-drowned machinery of the millennium to come. 

They do not fear the decay they see in the pool. To them, the ruin is simply another state of construction, a final, heavy layer to be added when the world is done with its youth. They are the architects of the prologue, forever busy, forever glowing, working with an unwavering, frantic grace to keep the seams of reality from pulling apart before the story is ready to be told.
