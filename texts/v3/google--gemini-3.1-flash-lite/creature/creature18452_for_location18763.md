To walk across the High Plateau of the Calcified Sigh is to walk upon the skin of an animal that has decided, collectively, to stop being alive. 

The inhabitants are not merely creatures; they are the gears of a machine that has rusted into a diamond-hard permanence. They are the **Litho-Mimics**, though "mimic" implies they are pretending to be stone, when in truth, the stone is merely the softest part of their anatomy.

They are shaped like geometry that has been forgotten. Many are segmented, resembling the husks of trilobites, but forged from translucent agate and rusted iron-ore. They cling to the floor in vast, interlocking colonies that form a living pavement. If you were to press your ear to the ground, you would not hear a heartbeat; you would hear the groan of geological plates grinding against one another, magnified by the chorus of a million silicate lives maintaining that exact, agonizing posture.

There is a specific caste among them, the *Anchorites*. These are the ones who dwell inside the dry-stone huts. They are larger, draped in heavy, layered plates that look like the scales of a pangolin, but heavier, each one a different shade of bruised purple or ochre. They do not live *in* the huts so much as they *are* the struts of the huts. A single Anchorite might form the entire lintel of a doorway, its claw-tipped limbs braced against two separate stones, holding the structure in a state of suspended collapse. They have been there so long that their shells have fused with the stones. They are the architects, but they are also the bricks.

Between these larger beings, in the gaps left by the movement of eons, drift the *Sliver-Spines*. These are creatures the size of a needle, translucent as spun glass, their bodies comprised of nothing but tension and a singular, sharp intent. They are the most terrifying of all, for they are the ones caught in the middle of being. You see them suspended at awkward, impossible angles: one mid-leap, reaching for a patch of the crystalline, wire-like lichen; another mid-shudder, its delicate, filigreed limbs vibrating—if, indeed, they are vibrating at all, or if that is just a trick of the exhausted eye.

They do not dream. To dream would require the flow of neurotransmitters, and there is no flow here. Instead, they *possess* the space around them. Because they have surrendered their three-dimensional depth, they have begun to leak into the shadows. This is why the shadows do not align with the light; the creatures have projected their own internal stillness onto the geography. They are printing their silhouettes onto the very physics of the plateau.

Do not mistake their stillness for peace. It is a hunger that has lost its target. They are caught in the final, desperate lunge of a hunting strike that was interrupted before it could connect. They are the realization of a thought that was never finished. 

If you look closely—though you fear what you might find—you will see faces on some of the larger, flatter specimens. Not eyes, but *indentations* that suggest the shape of a scream. They are the records of the exact moment the plateau clicked into its lock. They are the echoes of a sudden, universal paralysis. They have become the history of the world, rendered in immobile dust, holding onto the air with a grip that no force in the current universe could break. They are waiting for a movement that will never come, holding their breath until the plateau itself eventually turns into the fine, ionizing dust that currently fills their lungs.
