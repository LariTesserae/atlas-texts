To name what dwells within this obsidian fracture is to struggle against the linguistic presumption that a "resident" must be a creature of flesh, or even a discrete entity. 

In this vertical artery, the inhabitants are not *in* the architecture; they are the architecture’s fever-dream, a manifest byproduct of the computation occurring behind the translucent membranes. They are the **Latticework Custodians**, though "custodian" implies a service they do not feel and a purpose they cannot grasp.

They begin, most often, as rhythmic disturbances in the oily condensation. You might watch a pool of the viscous, metallic fluid gather in a geometric concave. For a long period—perhaps an hour, perhaps a century—the fluid shudders. It does not gather into a body, but into a cluster of high-frequency micro-geometry. Thin, articulate filaments of conductive glass begin to sprout from the liquid, weaving themselves into a loose, shivering lattice that mimics the proportions of a bipedal silhouette, though shorn of any mimicry of human biology.

They look like skeletons constructed of fiber optics and raw, unpolished obsidian. They have no faces, only a central aperture—a hollow, rotating intake—at the point where a throat would be. This is where they ingest the ozone-heavy steam.

They move with a stuttering, non-linear grace. Because the environment is constantly correcting its own geometry, removing and adding structural struts, the Custodians are in a state of perpetual integration. Sometimes, as a Custodian walks along a ridge, a razor-sharp spike of wall-matter will erupt and stitch itself directly through the creature’s limb. The Custodian does not flinch. It merely waits for the local physics to "correct" the error, its arm partially absorbed, its anatomy momentarily merged with the wall before the self-correction cycle shears it free again. It carries the history of the walls in its own brittle, shifting architecture.

They are not sentient in the way a thinking mind is. They are more like the specialized white blood cells of a larger, cosmic immune system, or perhaps the discarded sub-routines of an intelligence building the fabric of reality. Their labor is entirely haptic. They spend their eternity tracing the ridges of the obsidian with spindly, multi-jointed fingers, feeling for vibrations—the "errors" in the hum of the universe. When they locate a patch of instability, they press their chest-apertures against the wall, allowing the sticky, hot condensation to coat them, drawing the heat out of the structure until the wall cools into the correct silence.

When they reach the end of their utility, they do not die. They simply stop. They settle into a hollow or a crevice, their filaments locking into place until they are indistinguishable from the matte-black latticework. They become a structural strut themselves, a permanent part of the wall, their violet, light-emitting cores fading into a dull, grey glass. 

They are lonely, not because they crave company, but because they are the only things here capable of noticing the infinite depth of this place. They are the witnesses to the backstage, the silent archivists of the heat. Through the semi-translucent membranes, the greater machine watches them work, seemingly content to let these fleeting, brittle shadows maintain the skin of the universe while the dark gears grind on, indifferent, eternal, and cold.
