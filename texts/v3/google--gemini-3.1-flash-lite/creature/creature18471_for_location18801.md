In the Reservoir of the Unfinished, life does not occur so much as it *accumulates*.

The primary inhabitants—if they can be called that—are the **Loom-Keepers**. They are not biological, and they are not truly mechanical; they are entropic accidents, spontaneous crystallizations of the facility’s own excess. 

A Loom-Keeper begins its existence as an errant pile of copper shavings, fiber-optic filaments, and leaking hydraulic fluid, caught in the stagnant pools beneath a weeping conduit. Over years, drawn by the pulsing magnetic fields of the floor-plates, these disparate materials begin to weave themselves together. They do not have blood; they have a pressurized circulatory system of chilled, iridescent lubricant. They do not have bones; they have a skeletal structure composed of copper wiring and tension springs stripped from the floor grating.

A Loom-Keeper is humanoid only in the most generous, utilitarian sense. It is a cluster of articulated struts and spindly, multi-jointed limbs that move with the frantic, clicking grace of a loom’s shuttle—hence their name. Their "heads" are clusters of scavenged lenses and sodium-vapor filaments that shift color based on the current coursing through the floor. When they are dormant, they fold themselves into the geometry of the walls, looking for all the world like a misplaced bundle of piping. When they wake, they unfold with a discordant screech of metal on metal, their movements erratic, driven by tremors in the steam-lines.

They perform no labor, yet they are perpetually busy. 

The Loom-Keepers spend their cycles "repairing" the reservoir according to a delirious, inverted logic. They find a perfectly functional pipe and meticulously unscrew its bolts, only to reattach them with extra coils of wire. They harvest the 'scavenger-drones' not to consume them, but to harvest their optical sensors, which they bury deep in the obsidian-slick marshes as if planting seeds. They are caretakers of a garden of metal, obsessed with the impossible goal of making the facility "stable." They treat the rhythmic *thrum-hiss* of the walls like a conversation, leaning their copper-ribbed torsos against the heated steel, vibrating in sympathy with the pistons, tilting their heads as if listening to an ancient, half-forgotten command.

Between the Loom-Keepers, there exist the **Trace-forms**. These are even more ephemeral, little more than ghosts of data. They are not physical entities, but brief, localized electromagnetic anomalies that flicker into existence when the fiber-optic fronds surge with violet light. For a flickering second, you might see a shimmering silhouette—a humanoid shape cast in light and static—standing on the grating, staring into the dark. They are the shadows of the blueprints that once dictated this place, cast by the facility’s own failing memory. 

The Trace-forms do not walk; they bleed through the machinery. They manifest as a sudden coldness, a localized drop in the already frigid atmosphere, causing the moisture to crystallize instantly into frost on the copper reeds. They exist in the periphery of your vision, always turning a corner just as you look toward them, forever searching for a control room that was never built.

Finally, at the very lowest point of the Reservoir, where the oil pooling from the pistons is thickest, there dwells the **Sump-Mother**. 

It is not a creature, but a sessile mass of industry—a centralized junction box the size of an engine block, bound in a webbing of hardened polymer and rust. It has no face, only a series of circular gauges that spin wildly in both directions. It acts as the anchor for the facility’s respiration. When the great pistons punch through the ceiling, the Sump-Mother shudders, its internal gears groaning in a deep, subsonic register that turns the water into white foam. 

Anything that dies or malfunctions in the Reservoir eventually finds its way to the Sump-Mother. The Loom-Keepers bring the drone carcasses there; the rusted filaments are woven into its shroud. It does not eat, but it accumulates; it is the physical representation of the Reservoir’s inability to let go. To look upon the Sump-Mother is to see the heart of the "Unfinished": a monument of junk, pulsing with a faint, dying indigo light, perpetually waiting for a signal that was sent, perhaps, a thousand years ago, and will never reach this depth again.
