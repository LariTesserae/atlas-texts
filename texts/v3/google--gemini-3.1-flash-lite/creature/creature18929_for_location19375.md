To look closer is to see that the *weavers* are not merely workers; they are the substance of the city itself. They have no center, no core, and no individual identity. They are a distributed intelligence comprised of millions of silver filaments, each thinner than a human hair, functioning less like a society and more like an immune system or a localized weather pattern.

When they move, they operate in waves of prismatic motion. A single weaver is a flicker of light, a jagged spark in the gloom; a million weavers together moving through the basaltic canyons look like a slow-burning wildfire made of mercury. They do not possess joints or articulated limbs; their movement is magnetism, a cold dance of attraction and repulsion that pulls them across the vertical faces of the crystal spires.

They are the architects of the *weeping*, though they are not aware of the beauty they produce. As they work, they consume the very minerals of the geode wall, excreting a refined, hyper-pure silicate that they mold into new architecture. They are constantly eating their own past to feed their future.

There is a strange, haunting hierarchy within their swarm, though it is not based on authority, but on *frequency*. 

The elder filaments—those forged in the city’s deep-time past—have become encrusted with a heavy, diamond-dust patina. They move slowly, with a ponderous gravitas, humming at a low, sub-audible thrum that resonates through the geode’s basalt shell. When they drift, the younger, faster filaments—bright as needles and twitching with frantic, high-pitched energy—part ways, bowing in a geometric sense, allowing the elders to pass through the spire-gates. The elders are tasked with the structural integrity of the foundations, the deep, dark roots where the pressure of the geode is greatest. They are the ones who bear the weight of the mountain above.

The younger weavers, by contrast, inhabit the 'skin' of the city. They are the ones who react to the *tock*—the periodic, rhythmic drip of condensation. They are kinetic, frantic creatures, vibrating to an almost musical intensity. They perceive the falling droplet not as water, but as a crisis of topography. When the liquid hits the central plaza, the young weavers swarm the droplet before it can scatter, knitting a capillary web around it. They shepherd the water, guiding it into the micro-channels they have carved, turning the droplet into a flowing artery that feeds the city’s cooling system. 

They do not dream, but they do have a memory. It is a spatial memory, encoded into the very geometry of the glass. If a spire is toppled by a pebble’s shift or a mountain’s tremor, the weavers do not grieve; they scan the ruins, detect the lingering resonance of the old structure, and replicate it with agonizing, pedantic precision. 

They are the city’s thoughts manifested as physical form. Because they are constantly rearranging, the city is a fluid map of their collective, mindless logic. If one were to watch long enough—speeding up the centuries to a manageable tempo—the city would appear to breathe, to pulse, to open and close like the gills of a fish. 

At the very bottom of the geode, near the dark, volcanic roots of the stone, there are pockets where the weavers have gone silent. These are the "dead-zones," where the accumulation of mineral waste has become so thick that the filaments have become fused, locked into permanent, unmoving sculptures of tangled silver. These are the city’s history keepers. They stand as jagged, statuesque reminders of cycles completed. They are no longer part of the swarm, but they remain, etched into the foundation, providing the geometry upon which the living weavers build the next, and the next, and the next iteration of the glass horizon.

They serve a mission without an objective. They are a masterpiece of engineering that has forgotten what it was designed to achieve, serving only the internal, biting rhythm of the cold, the stone, and the drip.
