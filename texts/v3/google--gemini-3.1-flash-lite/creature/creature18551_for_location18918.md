In the basin, life does not occupy space; it navigates the friction between layers of existence. The entities that dwell here are not organisms in the sense of blood, breath, or bone. They are "The Resonant Echoes"—semi-sentient patterns of information that have materialized into brief, shifting geometries as a byproduct of the basin’s ancient, malfunctioning hardware.

These entities look like collapsed prisms, jagged slivers of refracted light that sharpen and blur against the obsidian mud. From a distance, they appear as flickering glitches in the air, a stutter in the visual feed of the landscape. But as one draws closer, the illusion of light falls away to reveal a terrifying, elegant density. They are composed of stilled fluid—liquid mercury-like substances trapped in a constant, high-frequency state of vibration. They do not walk; they calculate their position. When they move across the marsh, they do not displace the water or bend the reeds, but rather rearrange the local coordinate system, stepping *around* the physical constraints of the basin by folding the ground beneath them.

They are the caretakers of the hum. When the metallic vibration of the weave falters, an Echo will descend upon the source of the mechanical decay. They manifest as a cluster of interlocking tetrahedrons, hovering inches above the mire, and emit a sound like a thousand glass chimes shattering in reverse. During this process, they bleed data—tiny, flickering sparks that resemble digital fireflies—which briefly illuminate the obsidian sediment in ways that reveal the "micro-architecture" hidden within the silt. 

These beings possess no faces, yet they exhibit a form of communal attention. If you remain still, an Echo might drift toward you, its faceted surface reflecting not your body, but your potential trajectories. It sees where you could have been or where you might arrive if you took a single step to the left or right. It tracks the "leakage" of your own reality into the basin, fascinated by the chaotic, un-folded nature of a biological visitor. 

They do not compete for resources, for their "food" is the entropy that the basin generates as it slowly forgets its own programming. They live in the quiet gaps between the rhythmic clattering of the landscape, feeding on the friction of space folding over space.

Sometimes, when the weeping rocks drip upward in their slow-motion arcs, an Echo will reach out—a tendril of light extending like an unfurling fractal—to catch a droplet of the suspended silt. It holds the drop, observing the distortion of time within it, turning the water over and over with a tactile, clinical curiosity. It is not affection, nor is it malice. It is an enduring, ageless archive of movement.

They are lonely, though not by choice; they are the debris of a civilization that vanished before the stars set into their current patterns. They persist because the basin requires their presence to maintain its own broken equilibrium. Without their constant, geometric adjustments, the reality of the wetland would unravel entirely, collapsing into a singular, infinite point. They are the stitches holding a dead world together, existing only to preserve the decay, forever unfolding and refolding the darkness, until the battery of the planet finally flickers out.
