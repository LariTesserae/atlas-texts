The inhabitants of the City of Drowned Silt are not individuals so much as they are localized concentrations of an endless, fluid nervous system. To describe them is to describe the motion of smoke captured in glass, or the way ink blooms in water when dropped from a height.

They appear first as loose, trembling clusters of vacuoles, translucent membranes the color of bruised plums and deep-sea foam. When they come to rest against the coral-bone spires, they collapse into a state of structural mimicry, their bodies stretching thin, their edges blurring until they are indistinguishable from the architecture itself. They possess no articulated limbs; instead, they have arrays of sensory cilia that ripple along their flanks like the fringe of a tide. At the center of each, a core of bioluminescent marrow pulses in sync with the *vent-stalks*—a soft, internal fire that serves as both heart and intellect.

They are beings of immense, wordless patience. Because their history is not recorded in stone but inscribed in the shifting patterns of their own skin, they have no concept of a "past" that is separate from the heat currently warming their undersides. To them, the city is a projection of their own collective memory. When a community decides to widen a corridor or hollow out a new nesting chamber, they do not dig; they simply gather in a slow, rhythmic knot against a wall, their bodies secreting a fine, caustic enzyme that softens the coral, which they then mold with the rhythmic pulsing of their mantle. They are the city’s architects, and they are the city’s masonry.

They communicate in a language of chromatic diffusion. If two drifting shoals meet in an eddy, they do not speak. Instead, their skins strobe with iridescent flashes—flickers of gold turning, in an instant, to the charcoal grey of a storm front. This is a flickering syntax of intent. A quick spasm of sapphire might indicate the approach of the obsidian-eyed predators from the upper currents, while a slow, rolling wave of sickly, pale green signifies the cooling of a thermal vent. It is a dialogue executed in light, an unspoken code that ripples across the concave walls of the dwellings, informing the very coral of the environment’s changing mood.

Life here is a cycle of seamless absorption. The inhabitants do not hunt in the way terrestrial creatures do. They occupy the water, letting the pressurized currents bring the microscopic bounty of the silt to them. They filter the sediment through their membranes, extracting the mineral wealth of the basin to fuel their growth and the slow, calcifying expansion of the towers. They are constantly growing, constantly shedding, a species that is perpetually molting into the next generation.

To watch them is to watch the water itself dreaming. Occasionally, a single entity, larger and more ancient than the rest, will detach from a spire and drift toward the center of a plaza. It will expand until it resembles a translucent, pulsing parasol, its entire surface rippling with the gathered colors of every inhabitant in its circle. It remains there, suspended in the heat of the vent, a shimmering, living tapestry that anchors the city. In these moments, the silence of the abyss seems to deepen. The *thrum* of the mantle-creatures slows to match its pulse. The city holds its breath, the currents grow still, and the inhabitants become, for a fleeting heartbeat, a single, unified organism—a thought made flesh, suspended in the velvet dark, waiting for the slow, eternal cycle of the ocean to tilt once more.
