To exist within the High Drainage is to be an occupant of a clockwork lung, a witness to a respiration that never pauses.

If you look closely at the rusted gargantuan funnels, where the copper pipes meet the weeping granite, you will see the **Litho-Siphoners**.

They are not flesh, though they possess a slick, organic sheen that mimics the succulents clinging to the cliffside. They are modular, segmented entities—roughly the size of a man’s torso—composed of hardened, calcified cartilage and translucent, water-filled membranes. They are the caretakers of the machinery, though they do not understand the concept of maintenance. They understand only *saturation*.

A Litho-Siphoner moves by extruding a series of fine, needle-like cilia from its underside, anchoring itself into the hyper-saturated fiber of the moss-mats. They are slow, drifting across the metal girders with the deliberation of a tectonic plate. When they reach a junction where a pipe groans, they do not repair it; they merge with it. They press their permeable, gelatinous bellies against the hairline fractures in the iron, absorbing the leaking spray into their own bodies, filtering the minerals, and then sweating the purified moisture back into the intake valves. 

They are, in effect, the living gaskets of the High Drainage. 

Between the machines, in the deep, spongy hollows where the moss-mats are thickest, reside the **Mist-Weavers**. If the Siphoners are the maintenance crew, the Weavers are the architects of the condensation. They are spindly, multi-limbed creatures, pale as the bleached granite and nearly invisible against the refracted light. They possess long, gossamer filaments that trail behind them like frozen lightning. 

The Weavers do not walk; they suspend themselves between the peaks of the rocks and the rims of the copper funnels. They cast their filaments into the air, creating a vast, invisible web of static charge. This web acts as a magnet for the suspended water droplets in the mist, drawing the moisture toward the Condensers, forcing the clouds to weep where the machines can catch them. They are the reason the Drainage does not simply wash away into the abyss below. 

You will not hear them, for their movements are tuned to the exact frequency of the machines. But if you stand in the blinding white glare, you might notice the light warping around them—a slight refraction, a ripple in the ozone. 

And then, there are the **Sediment-Eaters**, the only beings here that seem to age. They are heavy, lumbering, stone-shelled creatures that dwell at the very bottom of the tiered slopes, where the runoff finally pools. They spend their entire lives consuming the crushed stone and oxidized iron dust that spills from the rusted pipes. They are the recycling system of this place, slowly grinding the raw, embryonic landscape into a fine, grey grit, which they deposit back into the roots of the moss. 

They are the most ancient-looking things in the Drainage, their shells encrusted with centuries of slow-growing lichen, their eyes milky and white from the constant, intense glare. They are the only ones who seem to acknowledge the passage of time. They don't look up at the clouds or the grinding machines; they simply eat the decay of the industrial labor, passing the waste back into the earth to ensure that the moss has something to cling to when the rock eventually gives way.

Nothing here speaks. There is no call of a bird or the skitter of a predator. There is only the low-frequency drone, the constant drip-hiss of the moisture, and the sight of a creature—part-geology, part-biology—slowly, painfully, keeping the world from dissolving into the sky.
