Those who inhabit Ophiuchus-Prime do not dwell within the city so much as they circulate through its nervous system. They are the *Resonants*—beings who have long since traded the crude biology of flesh and bone for a state of semi-liquid integration.

To look at a Resonant is to look at a silhouette that cannot decide on its own edges. They are tall, elongated figures, their forms draped in garments of iridescent mesh that look less like fabric and more like a captured oil slick. Because the atmosphere of the basin is so heavily saturated with mineral dew, the Resonants have skin that possesses a refractive, semi-translucent quality, like polished opal or the inner lining of a deep-sea shell. 

They do not walk; they undulate. Their centers of gravity are fluid, shifting in harmony with the nanolattice floors beneath them. When a Resonant stands still, their body blurs into the architecture, their limbs extending slightly to merge with the crystalline surfaces, tethering them to the city’s power grid. They are, in a sense, organic nodes—part of the infrastructure they inhabit.

They do not speak, for the air in the basin is too thick with mist and static for spoken language to carry without distortion. Instead, they communicate through a process of bio-harmonic projection. When two Resonants meet, they do not face one another in the traditional sense; they align their rhythmic pulses. You would see their chests brighten, a sequence of soft, violet pulses—the same frequency as the starlight harvest—flaring behind their translucent skin. They exchange data packets, memory-states, and sensory impressions in sharp, rapid-fire bursts of bioluminescence. 

Their internal lives are as horizontal as the time of the basin. They do not remember their history as a sequence of events but as a vast, interconnected map of patterns. They are the stewards of the migration; they watch the "tectonic herds" of buildings as a shepherd might watch a flock, guiding the nanolattice extrusions toward areas of higher light density, ever-adjusting the city’s shape to catch the dim violet rays filtering through the haze.

They eat the humidity. They have no digestive tracts in the terrestrial sense; instead, the porous, fractal-like structures of their throats filter the hyper-saturated mineral dew from the air, extracting the metallic salts and synthetic nutrients left behind by the city's self-replicating processes. They are, effectively, living filters.

At night—or what passes for night in a place where the sun is merely a shift in the intensity of the gray—they do not sleep. They "sync." They gather in the open, webbed plazas of the spires, their bodies interlocking to form geometric clusters, resembling nothing so much as a nervous system temporarily removed from a host body. In this state, they dream the city into existence, their collective sub-consciousness feeding the logic systems that dictate how the nanolattices will reorganize by morning.

There is a profound, aching loneliness to them. They have been shaped by the silence of the basin, and they have become as indifferent as the arthropods that scuttle below. They have no names, only identification frequencies; they have no homes, only temporary anchor points in a shifting lattice. 

They are the architects of a stuttering evolution, waiting—always waiting—for the moment when the ground finally stops cooling, when the city finishes its metamorphosis, and they can finally detach from the wires, stepping off the glass ridges to see what they have become once the circuit is finally closed. Until then, they are merely ghosts of a future that has forgotten how to be born.
