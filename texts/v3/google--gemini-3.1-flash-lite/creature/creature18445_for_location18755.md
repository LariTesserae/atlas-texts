Within the ribs of the Great Lung, the things that dwell here are not so much inhabitants as they are symptoms of the architecture. They are the artifacts of a slow, biological alchemy—the patient, sweating result of iron breathing in the sky for a millennium.

There is, first, the **Clockwork Apiary**. It does not house honeybees, but instead a swarm of *Aether-Borne Welders*. These are creatures the size of thrushes, their bodies crafted from scavenged brass filings and hardened resin, held together by the magnetic hum of the conduits they traverse. They have no wings in the traditional sense; instead, they flutter on rigid, vibrating slats of tempered copper that slice the humid air. They spend their short, brutal lives repairing the microscopic fractures in the brass, sealing leaks with a saliva that dries into a substance stronger than steel. They do not eat; they drink the oil-flecked condensation that pools in the iron grooves. They are the maintenance staff of a deity that stopped giving orders centuries ago.

Deeper in the verticality, tucked behind the massive, shivering pistons, live the **Verdigris Watchers**. They are not quite amphibians, nor are they fully animal. They are pale, elongated, and possess six limbs that terminate in hooked, obsidian claws. Their skin is so thoroughly colonized by the pervasive moss and oxidation of the Spire that they are functionally invisible against the walls. They possess no eyes, for in the absolute darkness of the machinery's core, light is a foreign concept. Instead, they navigate by "tuning" their bodies to the vibration of the Great Lung; they feel the tectonic shift of the gears in their bones. If a gear shudders, they move. If a piston sighs, they freeze. They are the pulses of the machine made flesh, waiting for the one day in a thousand years when a gear might finally complete its rotation. 

Then there are the **Root-Seekers**, the true ghosts of the Spire. These are not flesh, but a colonial intelligence formed by the conductive, copper-veined ivy. Over decades, the vines have grown into the gaps of the rusted iron, weaving themselves into the nerve centers of the machinery. They have begun to mimic the circuitry. In the deepest, wettest corners, you might find a cluster of leaves that hold a static charge, flickering rhythmically in sequence with the heartbeat of the Spire. These clusters act as a collective brain for the greenery. They are the ones who "decide" where the orchids bloom and which iron plates should be allowed to rust into dust. They are the green consciousness of the greenhouse, slowly turning the metal factory into a living, thinking forest that dreams in terms of hydraulic pressure and slow-motion photosynthesis. 

And finally, if you ascend to the highest, most shivering scaffolding, you might encounter the **Hollow Echoes**. They are the only beings that could be said to be "hollow"—translucent, jellyfish-like entities that drift in the updrafts of the steam vents. They are formed from the persistent condensation and the ambient hum of the chamber, a sort of atmospheric condensation that has achieved a flickering, semi-sentient state. They are essentially sentient clouds of mist and grease, catching the faint bioluminescence of the moss as they float between the grinding gears. They have no weight; they have no will. They simply exist to carry, and then release, the sound—trapping the lowest frequencies of the Spire’s roar in their watery bodies, only to let them out in long, mournful whistles as they are crushed against the tectonic plates of the walls.

These dwellers do not know the world outside the Spire. They do not dream of the ground. They are the gears of a living watch, ticking patiently while their world grows green and wet, waiting for the day the Great Lung completes its cycle and finally falls silent.
