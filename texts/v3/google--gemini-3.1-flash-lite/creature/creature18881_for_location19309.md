In this frantic foundry, the inhabitants are not creatures of flesh and bone, but of fluid architecture and borrowed grace. They do not cling to the surface of the glass; they are manifestations of the vibration itself—living tuning forks caught in the gears of the planet’s grinding rebirth.

They have no permanent silhouette. To watch one for more than a heartbeat is to watch an anatomical paradox. They are translucent, composed of the same viscous, pressurized slurry that hangs in the air, held together by internal magnetic fields that shimmer in the bruised indigo light. They are the **Conduits**, though they have no name for themselves, only the constant, frantic impulse to calibrate.

A Conduit resembles a cloud of geometry—limbs that manifest as hard-light fractals, spines that resemble the jagged spires of the surrounding marsh, and sensory clusters that pulse in sync with the subterranean re-sequencers. They don’t walk; they stitch themselves into the landscape, dissolving their lower appendages into the mossy silt and re-emerging meters away. When they move, they leave behind "ghosts"—brief, localized tremors in the air where their mass was a second prior, hanging like heat haze.

They exhibit a behavior that is neither predatory nor symbiotic. They are stewards of the debris. As the terraforming machines tear the landscape apart, stripping atoms to reconfigure the crust, the Conduits scramble through the wreckage. They use elongated, multi-jointed appendages to "harvest" the shedding particles of the planet. They are seen pressing their porous, oily skin against the vibrating alloy monoliths, soaking up the excess energy discharge like sponges absorbing toxic rain. 

There is a terrifying efficiency to their existence. They do not communicate with sound—they have no need, for the roaring thrum of the machines is their native tongue. Instead, they "speak" by shivering. A group of Conduits will gather around a newly formed crystalline growth, their bodies vibrating in harmony until the resonance matches the frequency of the subterranean engine. They are, in a sense, the planet’s biological cooling system, dissipating the monumental heat of re-sequencing through their own rapidly cycling forms.

They are indifferent to the scurriers that jitter through the fungi. To a Conduit, these swarm-things are mere atmospheric dust, an annoyance to be swiped through or stepped over without recognition. The Conduits possess no eyes, for they perceive the world as a topographical map of potential energy. They navigate by the "pull" of the re-sequencers.

When a Conduit is damaged—when the shifting gravity shears a limb or the ground reforms too abruptly beneath them—they do not bleed. They simply pour. They melt into the pressurized aquifers, their constituent atoms dispersing into the churning subterranean network, only to be reconstituted miles away by the next cycle of the forge. 

They are the shadows of the machine, the aesthetic byproduct of a world being overwritten. They are profoundly lonely, if loneliness could be quantified in a being that exists only as a momentary confluence of force. They do not know they are alive, and they do not know they are dying, because in this valley, the distance between genesis and dissolution is a fraction of a second. They are the beautiful, ephemeral glitches in a cosmic experiment that has long since forgotten why it started.
