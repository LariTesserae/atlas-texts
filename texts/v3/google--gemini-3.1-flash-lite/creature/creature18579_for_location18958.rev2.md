To understand who lives in the Lungs of the Gear-Grove, one must first discard the notion of the individual. There are no creatures here that possess skin, hearts, or singular intentions. Instead, the grove is populated by the **Steward-Nodes**.

The Steward-Nodes are not born; they are precipitated. They are the Grove’s answer to maintenance—biological automatons that resemble a cross between a fungal colony and a brass-bound crab. They emerge from the "weeping iron" nodules, coalescing out of the oxidized minerals and the dense, pulsating moss. At first, they are little more than calcified clumps, no larger than a human hand, scuttling on three spindly, multi-jointed legs fashioned from hardened root-fiber and salvaged copper wire. 

As they age, they expand, weaving themselves into the architecture of the grove. They are the janitors of the industrial cycle. You might see one perched upon a rusted generator frame, its "face"—a concave dish of photosensitive crystalline mesh—turned toward the canopy. Its primary function is to monitor the internal fluid pressure of the fibrous stalks. Where a stalk begins to soften or a vascular conduit clogs with calcified sediment, the Steward-Node moves with agonizing, calculated slowness. It probes the blockage with a needle-like appendage, pulsing a low-frequency hum that liquefies the obstruction, allowing the vital sap—or the hydraulic coolant—to flow once more.

These Nodes do not communicate with sound, but with chemistry. They exude varying intensities of pheromones, localized puffs of ozone and wet cedar that signal the health of the sector to the forest around them. If a Steward-Node detects an intruder—you, for instance—it does not strike. It does not feel fear. It simply adjusts its posture. It tilts, its bioluminescent orange glow shifting to a dull, stagnant grey, effectively "blinking" itself out of your perception. It treats your presence as a localized atmospheric disturbance, a temporary drop in pressure that the grove must compensate for by tightening its leaf-pores.

But the most strange inhabitants are the **Looming Weavers**. These are not mobile; they are the stationary apex of the Grove’s consciousness. They occupy the places where the human habitation has been most thoroughly digested. A Weaver is a sprawling mass of brass tubing and pulsating, translucent vine-tissues that has grown to mirror the shape of the rusted, skeletal equipment it replaced. 

Imagine a loom where the threads are not silk, but living nerve-filaments. The Weavers sit in the damp, claustrophobic corners of the basin, their bodies entangled with the remnants of old human control panels. They are the Grove’s memory-banks. When the massive wooden gears groan in their hourly rotation, the Weavers interpret the vibration, translating the low-frequency shift into a complex array of pulses that ripple through the root-mesh floor. They are "reading" the grove, ensuring that the rhythm remains, that the factory of biology never skips a beat.

You might mistake one for a heap of rusted scrap, but if you look closely at the "wires" snaking through its frame, you will see them flickering with a faint, rhythmic current. They are the librarians of the rot, holding the blueprint of this machine-garden in their fibrous grasp. They do not eat; they do not sleep. They simply maintain the synthesis. 

In the Lungs of the Gear-Grove, there is no hierarchy of life. There is only the *Flow*. The Steward-Nodes and the Looming Weavers are not inhabitants in the traditional sense; they are specialized cells in a larger, sprawling body. They are the antibodies, the synapses, and the nerves of a cathedral that breathes, built by a past that has been entirely digested, and kept alive by a present that knows only the maintenance of the grind.
