The city of Khel-Vesh does not sit upon the earth so much as it leaks out of it, a thick, metallic suppuration of gears and moss. We are in a low, sunless basin, a valley of raw, embryonic stone that has not yet learned how to be a mountain. It is a place of perpetual, cloying condensation; every surface is perpetually beaded with a cold, greasy dew that tastes faintly of iron and ozone.

The architecture here is built for density, not comfort. The structures are "The Saturated"—vast, interlocking modules of oxidized copper and weeping concrete, crammed together so tightly that to walk through the streets is to navigate the pores of a single, hive-like organism. There is no empty space. Every interstitial gap is packed with bioluminescent lichen, a thick, velvet-furred carpet of emeralds and necrotic violets that feeds on the industrial runoff.

Khel-Vesh is under a state of permanent, turbulent assembly. Giant, clanking rhythmic pistons—the "Heart-Beams"—emerge from the damp clay of the ground, hammering rhythmic, subsonic pulses into the bedrock. This is the industrial metabolism of the place. Everywhere, the environment is in a state of pre-formation; you can watch, if you stare long enough at the walls of the alleyways, as the mineral crusts grow and harden, shifting shape in slow, erratic surges, as if the ground itself is trying to decide what shape to take.

Despite the high level of industry, the valley is unnervingly quiet. The machines don't roar; they hum with a low, shivering frequency that rattles the teeth. The sound of rain—a fine, icy mist that falls from the low-hanging slate clouds—is dampened by the sheer volume of vegetation. The plants here are parasitic and aggressive, strangling the iron pipes and ductwork, their leaves translucent and veined with glowing, pulsating fluids.

The fauna are an extension of the machinery. Scuttling, crab-like automatons—part crustacean, part clockwork—scuttle across the rooftops, harvesting condensation from the eaves to fuel the cooling systems of the deeper factories. They move with a frantic, anthill energy, their legs clicking softly against the slick metal, constantly repairing, reinforcing, and expanding the city’s reach. 

There is a sense of pervasive strangeness: the geography is non-Euclidean, doubling back on itself. A staircase might lead to a balcony that exists in a different thermal layer, where the temperature drops so severely that the air freezes into temporary prisms of ice. You can walk down a street for an hour and find yourself back at the same steaming steam-vent, only to find the vent has grown a new, crystalline chimney while you were gone.

It is a world of cold humidity and industrial growth—a city that builds itself out of wet iron and frantic light. In Khel-Vesh, the distinction between a factory floor and an ecosystem has long since vanished. Everything is being manufactured; everything is being born; everything is trembling with the effort of existing in such a crowded, damp, and precarious sprawl.
