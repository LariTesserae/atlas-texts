The city of Orem-Vahl clings to the jagged, basaltic spine of a mountain peak that has only just finished cooling. Because the landscape is frozen in a state of eternal equilibrium, the stone remains sharp, unweathered, and agonizingly brittle, its edges as precise as a freshly fractured diamond. There is no erosion here; the rain that falls—a soft, persistent mist clinging to the high-altitude air—does not wear away the mountain, but merely beads upon it, perpetually wetting the moss that occupies every crack.

The architecture is a study in vertical ambition. Built by a people who favor manual masonry, the structures are composed of interlocking obsidian slabs and heavy, pale timber harvested from the dense, waxy-leafed forests that grow in the basins below. The city is a sprawling, sparse network of towers and suspended walkways that span the chasms between the younger, sharper peaks. It is an urban maze that feels less like a city and more like a permanent trellis draped over a skeleton of rock.

The light is constant—a steady, diffuse white glow that seems to emanate from the very atmosphere, casting no distinct shadows. Because time does not move, the mist is frozen in its descent, hanging in the air like a veil of translucent beads, allowing the eye to see for miles across the valley floor. 

Despite the cold, the ecology is thriving and bizarre. Great, flightless avian creatures with plumage the color of rusted iron patrol the stone walkways, their movements rhythmic and precise, never accelerating. They forage for the lichen that grows in the damp seams of the masonry. There is no urgency in their feeding; they occupy the same space and the same moment forever. 

The soundscape is defined by a profound, heavy silence, broken only by the occasional "drip-hush" of condensation falling from a high gable onto a stone floor—a sound that occurs at perfectly regular, unchanging intervals. There is no wind, for wind implies a gradient, and here, the air density is uniform, locked into a static pressure.

To walk through Orem-Vahl is to move through a display case. The buildings are adorned with simple, primitive carvings—geometric patterns etched into the stone with hand-tools that were clearly abandoned halfway through the work, left resting in a groove for an eternity. The inhabitants are few and scattered, mostly found sitting on the edges of the high terraces, looking out over the verdant, unchanging valleys. They are artisans of a finished world, their tools laid flat on tables, their meals set out in clay bowls that never empty and never cool. 

In this place, the climb to the summit does not tire the limbs, and the moisture on the skin never evaporates. It is a high-altitude sanctuary where the sharp, jagged beauty of creation has been caught in a glass bell, held in a breathless suspension, where the urban craft of the people has become an organic, permanent growth upon the mountain’s peak. Nothing will fall, nothing will grow further, and nothing will fade. The city simply *is*.
