The world is a bruised, warm fluid. There is no horizon, only an endless, churning gradient of bioluminescent heavy-water, thick as oil and stained with the iron-rust of a billion grinding gears. The pressure is constant, a physical weight pushing against the ribs, yet there is nowhere to sink—the gravity here is a loose, tangled suggestion, allowing the landscape to fold in on itself in impossible topologies.

Everything is submerged, yet there is no drowning. The medium is a liquid-atmosphere, a dense, oxygenated soup that carries the deafening screech of the world’s labor.

This is a factory without a master. The terrain is an ongoing, violent birth—massive, serrated basalt-analogues erupt from the silt, only to be sheared away by the erratic, high-velocity currents. From these raw, jagged outcroppings grow the "vegetation": immense, fronded structures of copper-wire and translucent, pulsing membranes. They do not photosynthesize; they draw kinetic power from the turbulent currents, their metallic stalks vibrating with a hum that borders on a scream. They are dense, a snarling thicket of industrial biology that reaches out like fingers, interlocking in a desperate, static dance.

The "built" reality is a tragedy of half-formed intention. Scattered throughout the tangled groves are rusted, colossal skeletons of impossible machines—a cooling tower that has fused with a coral-like cluster of glass pipes, an electrified bulkhead that has grown roots of silicon, a churning turbine that spins in the void, fed by no fuel other than the sheer intensity of the storm. These artifacts are not ruins; they are early-formation, twitching with residual, flickering power, glowing with a dim, sickly violet light that fails to pierce the murky density of the fluid.

Silence is impossible. The world sounds like a collapsing shipyard. Huge, liquid-pressurized pistons fire at random, deep-throated *thrums* that rattle the very marrow, followed by the high-pitched singing of strained metal friction. There are no animals here; the heat of the liquid, a steady, blood-warm 98 degrees, is too chaotic, too saturated with stray electricity, for cellular life to hold a steady form. 

The reality breakdown is the final, presiding rule. At the periphery of one’s vision, the liquid curdles into geometry. A wall of rain might suddenly solidify into a stack of bronze clockwork, only to melt back into a torrent of bubbles a second later. Objects exist in states of superposition—the rusted hatch is both closed and non-existent; the turbine is both rusting away and brand new. 

It is a place of hyperactive construction where nothing is ever finished. The turbulence is a creative engine, tossing up sudden, fleeting crystalline spires that shatter under their own weight, their debris falling into the abyss to be recycled into new, frantic shapes. It is a bazaar of pure potential, deafening and drowning, where the only inhabitant is the process of Creation itself, churning in the warm, lightless dark, creating beauty so raw and sharp that it defies the very concept of being seen.
