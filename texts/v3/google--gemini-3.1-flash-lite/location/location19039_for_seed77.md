The world is a vast, horizontal kiln. It is a continent-spanning slab of engineered ceramic and glass, a floor that stretches toward the horizon in a shimmering, hazy blur. There is no natural soil here; beneath your feet, the ground is a seamless, warm substrate of ivory-toned composite, scored with geometric expansion joints that weep a faint, saline condensation whenever the internal pressure of the world shifts.

The heat is the most persistent presence. It is a dry, pulsing warmth that rises from the substrate, constant and oppressive, carrying the copper scent of baked minerals. Because there is no topography—no mountains to catch the clouds or valleys to trap the air—the weather acts on a planetary scale. Every few hours, the atmosphere turns heavy and viscous. A localized, low-hanging mist coalesces in the air, a "sweat" produced by the cooling of the artificial ceiling far above. It falls as a fine, tepid drizzle for perhaps ten minutes, slicking the expansive plains, only to be reclaimed by the heat seconds after it lands. The water doesn't pool or erode; it vanishes into microscopic vents built into the seams of the floor, cycling back into the structural dark below.

Vegetation is a localized accident. It does not grow from dirt, but from the waste-ports of the construction. Spindly, translucent stalks—more fungal than leafy—sprout sporadically from these clogged seams. They are brittle structures, pale and veinous, pulsing with the same rhythm as the world’s internal cooling systems. They do not compete for light; they seem to exist solely to facilitate the exchange of gases between the subterranean architecture and the stifling, open expanse above. 

There is a profound, aching silence. Because there is no animal life and no wind-resistant terrain to create a whistle or a howl, the only sounds are the rhythmic, tectonic adjustments of the substrate. Every hour or so, a deep, vibration-heavy *thrum* rolls across the continent, a mechanical sigh as the world shifts a few millimeters to accommodate the heat-induced expansion of its own tiling. It is a sound felt in the marrow rather than heard by the ear.

The landscape is "under construction" in a way that feels permanent. Massive, pre-finished monolithic pillars—some miles wide, others thin as needles—rise out of the flat expanse without warning, their bases still raw and unfinished. They possess the texture of unpolished slate, cool to the touch even when the air is scorching, as if they are sucking the heat from the surrounding environment. They are not buildings in the human sense; they are structural buttresses for a sky that has no stars, only a shifting, glowing diffusion of light that mimics the meridian of a sun.

You could walk for months in any direction and find nothing but the recurrence of these same elements: the ivory floor, the sudden cooling drizzle, the lonely, translucent stalks of vegetation, and the immense, silent pillars. It is a world of total geometry, kept in a state of suspended animation, waiting for a function that has not yet been assigned to it. It is an enormous cradle of glass and stone, meticulously crafted, utterly empty, and perpetually warm.
