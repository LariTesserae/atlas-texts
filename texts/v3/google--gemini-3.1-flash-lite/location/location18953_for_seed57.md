The environment is contained within the hollowed, calcified ribcage of a leviathan that died long before the sea rose to claim the basin. It is a pocket-world no larger than a cathedral, submerged under miles of heavy, pressurized black brine. 

You exist in a state of suspended animation—a cold, static equilibrium. Because the water is so dense and pressurized, it does not flow so much as it *presses*. The space is saturated; there is no air, only the liquid medium, which tastes of ozone and ancient, crushed minerals.

**The Flora and the Decay**
The rib-vaults are draped in "weeping velvet"—a species of translucent fern that does not grow toward light, but toward the faint heat emanating from the marrow-less bone of the enclosure. These plants are the primary architecture. They are a deep, bruised indigo, their fronds overlapping with such density that the individual stalks are indistinguishable. You cannot swim here; you simply exist between the layers of vegetation, which are packed so tightly that you must push against the resilience of the stems to move an inch.

**Reality Breakdown**
Here, the laws of physics are soft, like wet clay. Because the light is absent—save for the bioluminescent pulse of a single, slow-drifting spore that cycles every few hours—gravity behaves inconsistently. You might find yourself falling upward toward the arch of a rib bone, only to have your orientation inverted by a localized fold in space. Sometimes, the water through which you move seems to harden into a solid, clear jelly for a fraction of a second, trapping you in a sudden, crystalline paralysis, before liquefying again into the heavy, cold dark.

**The Scattered Remains**
Among the packed stems, you find the "scatters": small, unidentifiable objects made of a material that feels like cold porcelain but is warm to the touch. They are finger-sized, perforated, and arranged in perfect, tessellated geometric patterns, as if left by a creature that organized the world by frequency rather than by hand. They do not appear to be built but rather grown, and they are slowly being subsumed by the ferns. There is no scrap of metal, no sign of a tool, no residue of a fire or a thought. They are ancient, abandoned seeds of an intelligence that never occupied this place.

**Senses**
The silence is absolute, yet heavy. The only sound is a rhythmic, low-frequency hum that resonates through the ribcage bones—a tectonic thrum that suggests the entire submerged basin is resting on something colossal and dreaming. When you move, the water does not bubble or swirl; it clings to you, viscous and resistant.

There is no fauna. Nothing swims here. If anything ever lived within this ribcage, it has long since dissolved into the nutrient-rich water. You are the only presence, a brief, dense interruption in a landscape that measures its own life by the imperceptible creep of the ferns and the cooling of the leviathan’s bones. 

To remain here is to lose the concept of "out." There is no surface to reach; there is only the thickening dark, the indigo pressure of the velvet flora, and the slow, breaking geometry of a reality that has forgotten how to be solid.
