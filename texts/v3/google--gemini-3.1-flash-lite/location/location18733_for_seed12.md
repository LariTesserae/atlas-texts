The world exists inside a gargantuan, pressurized bubble of suspended fluid—not a traditional ocean, but a miles-high column of bioluminescent, nutrient-rich jelly that clings to the jagged, spiring peaks of a mountain range that has never known the bite of wind. 

This is the Spire-Basin. Because the landscape is entirely submerged in this viscous, warm medium, "erosion" is a foreign concept. The geology here is raw and embryonic, consisting of obsidian-like rock that pushes upward like calcified bone, unweathered and sharp. There is no grit, no sediment, no rounding of edges. The land is under constant, slow construction by the hydrostatic pressure of the fluid itself, which precipitates new mineral layers onto the stone surfaces, causing the peaks to thicken and branch like growing coral.

The "urban" sprawl of the inhabitants—a soft-bodied, pale-limbed species—clings to these high-altitude needles. They build using a primitive form of lattice-weaving, utilizing the tough, fibrous stalks of the high-growth *Amnion-kelp* that stretches between the peaks. These dwellings are dense, layered clusters of woven pods, stacked one atop the other, threaded with bioluminescent conduits that pulse with a faint, amber light. Because light from above is strangled by the sheer depth of the fluid, the city relies entirely on this internal, rhythmic glow.

It is a quiet, heavy place. The density of the medium muffles movement; there is no hurried locomotion here. A traveler pushes through the water-thick air with the grace of a slow-motion dreamer. Sound travels far but loses its edges, turning into a low-frequency hum that vibrates in the marrow. You hear the creak of the kelp structures and the rhythmic dilation of the city’s light-nodes, but the fauna is rare and skittish—translucent, multi-tentacled drifters that avoid the light of the woven villages.

The ecology is a strange, vertical jungle. Giant, balloon-like flora tether themselves to the rock, their fronds drifting upward, catching the microscopic protein snow that drifts down from the upper limits of the basin. The architecture mimics this; the buildings grow as much as they are built, with the inhabitants grafting living moss onto their structures to reinforce the weave. 

Time here is measured by the color-shift of the atmospheric fluid. Every few cycles, the density of the jelly changes, turning from a hazy periwinkle to a deep, bruised violet, signaling a shift in the currents that bring nutrient clouds from the lower depths. When this happens, the entire city retracts, pulling its light inwards, and the inhabitants cling to their lattices, waiting for the surge to pass. 

There is a profound sense of "under-construction" here. You are walking upon a world that feels as though it is waiting for a final form it may never reach. The foundations are young, the structures are tentative, and if you listen closely, you can hear the faint, crystalline *snap* of mineral growth as the mountains themselves slowly, painfully, continue to stake their claim against the oppressive, life-giving fluid.
