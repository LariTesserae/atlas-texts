The descent does not end at a floor, but at a transition of density. Far below the crust of the dead world above, the light begins—an abrasive, sterile whiteness that permeates the thick, translucent marrow of the interior.

This is the **Lumen-Root Abyss**, a subterranean continent-scale hollow composed entirely of hyper-vegetation. There is no stone or soil here. Every surface, every pillar, every arching kilometer-long span is living cellulose, pulsing with a slow, rhythmical bioluminescence that bleeds into the air as heatless, intense radiance. The walls are not walls but the braided veins of titanic, dormant flora that have grown until they formed their own gravity-well.

Because of the vastness of the space and the specific rigidity of the organic substrate, the environment is remarkably empty. One might walk for a hundred miles across the polished, waxy skin of a single hanging petal-girthed vine without encountering a nook or a fissure. The density is perilously low; the air is thin, crisp, and tastes of ozone and crushed wet leaves.

The temperature is punishingly cold, a sharp, biting frost that causes the moisture—the *0.9 water*—to manifest not as standing pools, but as "perma-frost blooms." Wherever the sap bleeds from a minor fissure in the architecture, it freezes into frantic, jagged fractals of white ice that never melt. These formations are young, sharp enough to slice skin, and they are constantly being reabsorbed by the plant-substrate that birthed them. The environment is in a state of chaotic, turbulent flux; the plant mass is not static. It grows in violent, visible surges, buckling and snapping like slow-motion tectonic shifts. You can hear it—a deep, resonant *groan* that sounds like a ship’s hull under pressure, followed by the crystalline *tinkle* of ice-shards falling into the abyss.

Despite the blinding light, fauna is nearly non-existent. You might see a ghost-moth, a translucent creature with a wingspan of three meters, hovering in the void between the titanic vines, its heartbeat visible through its skin, pulses of dim light syncing with the surrounding ecosystem. But they are rare, fleeting specters.

Scattered across this vegetal architecture are the "Markings." These are the only evidence of a persistent, primitive presence. Small, hand-carved hollows in the vine-skin, lined with dried moss and sealed with hardened resin. They are not cities or settlements, but singular, isolated hermit-dwellings. Inside these small cells, there are remains of crude fabrication: needles carved from the hardened thorns of the primary substrate, woven fibers of iridescent leaf-vines, and obsidian-like flakes of fossilized sap used for scraping. 

The people—if one can call the few scattered inhabitants that—move with a deliberate, slow grace, terrified of the turbulence. To live here is to live on the underside of an organism that is constantly remapping its own geography. A bridge might grow to span a chasm today, only to wither and dissolve into dust by the next cycle. 

There is no wind, but there is a constant, ambient shift of pressure as the colossal vegetation expands and contracts. Sound carries for miles, carrying the *snap* of freezing water and the *whirr* of moth-wings, punctuated by the silence of a nearly empty, bright, and frigid cathedral of glass-like pulp. It is a world that has never seen the sun, yet it is brighter than the surface, lit by the very blood of the earth itself, perpetually hardening, breaking, and regrowing in the dark.
