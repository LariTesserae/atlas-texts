This is the **Lumen-Cavern of the Vernal Meridian**, a space that exists not as a cave carved by water, but as a vast, pressurized bladder of life-mass held beneath the crushing weight of the crust. 

Everything here is biology. There is no stone, no soil, no sediment. The floor, walls, and vaulted ceilings are composed of a hyper-dense, calcified root-cortex—a fibrous, ivory-colored scaffolding that feels like warm, cured leather. Because there has never been a rock-fall or a river to scour the surfaces, the geology is embryonic; the shapes are bulbous, rounded, and budding, as if the world were still holding its breath during its own creation.

The heat is absolute and constant, hovering at a fever-pitch that smells of sterilized ozone and sweet, fermented nectar. It is a dry, baking heat, though condensation clings to every surface in translucent, viscous beads—the only water present. These droplets act as magnifying lenses, catching the light that permeates the root-canopy above. The illumination is relentless, a bright, diffuse white-gold that seems to emanate from the very cells of the plants themselves, bathing the "town-sized" expanse in a sterile, shadowless clarity.

Movement here is restricted by the density of the growth. Every cubic meter is occupied by moss-like architectures, bulb-structures, and spiraling, vein-like filaments that pulse at a frequency so slow it registers as motionless. It is a place of static equilibrium. Nothing decays, nothing withers, and nothing grows quickly enough to be perceived; it is a frozen snapshot of a biological machine that has achieved perfect, unchanging efficiency.

Scattered amidst this are the "faint marks"—the built remnants of a forgotten epoch. You might find a singular, hollowed-out sphere of semi-transparent plant matter, suspended by thick, rhythmic tendrils. It is not a house, but a nodule of memory where the architecture has grown around a void. These artifacts are silent, unmoving, and entirely biological. There are no tools here, no metal, no stone, no sharp edges of artifice.

The ecology is terrifyingly sparse. You will hear nothing but the faint, rhythmic *hiss* of condensation vaporizing against the hot walls, and occasionally, the low, subsonic thrum of sap moving through a trunk wider than a cathedral. You might go days without seeing a single living creature. fauna is so rare that their existence seems vestigial—perhaps a single, pale, multi-jointed arthropod that spends its entire life cycle circling a single nutrient-rich node, never migrating, never hunting, simply existing in the humid, glowing heat.

To walk here is to tread upon the internal organs of a planet. There is no sense of time; the sun-drenched brightness, the pervasive heat, and the absence of wind create a feeling of total, suffocating certainty. It is a place that does not want to be explored, but merely to be, locked in the recursive loop of its own verdant, sweltering perfection.
