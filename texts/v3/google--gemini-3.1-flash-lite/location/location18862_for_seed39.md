The air here is not merely cold; it is thick, heavy with a pressurized, metallic humidity that clings to the lungs. You are standing on the spine of a jagged, sky-piercing ridge, a geological infant that hasn’t yet learned the grace of rounding edges. The stone is violent—raw, slab-sided protrusions of basalt and obsidian that feel as though they were shoved upward by a gargantuan fist only moments ago. There is no erosion to speak of; the rocks are sharp enough to flay skin, devoid of the softening touch of time.

Water is the primary actor in this dim amphitheater. It does not flow in rivers; it seeps. It drips from the underside of overhangs in rhythmic, glassy beads, and it pools in the hairline fractures of the rock. The atmosphere is a perpetual, low-hanging condensation—a grey, shivering curtain that hides the valley floor miles below. Every surface is slick, covered in a thin, cold film of moisture that vibrates with the low-frequency humming of the world beneath your feet.

You are not alone, though the expanse feels terrifyingly empty. The sound at first is deceptive—a heavy, muffled silence—but as you wait, the landscape reveals itself as a frantic machine. 

Buried deep within the raw, uncarved heart of the mountain, there is an industrial pulse that defies the wilderness. It is an subterranean engine, a sprawling, mechanical foundation that predates the very mountains sitting atop it. You can hear the rhythmic *thrum-hiss* of hydraulic pistons deep within the bedrock, the screech of iron on iron vibrating through the soles of your boots. It is an industrial heartbeat, a constant, flickering cycle of energy that warms the water and pushes the temperature up just enough to keep the moisture from turning into solid ice.

Despite the sparse vegetation—only the occasional, translucent, hair-like lichen clinging to the lee side of the boulders—the fauna is staggering in its abundance. Creatures here are not biological in the traditional sense; they are hardy, multi-legged arachnoid things, translucent and milky-white, scuttling across the wet rock faces with calibrated precision. Thousands of them move in rhythmic waves, trailing glowing, bioluminescent filaments from their mandibles. They are the scavengers of this industrial mountain, harvesting the mineral-rich condensation and the stray electrical discharges that bleed from the deep machinery into the stone.

They move with the mechanical twitchiness of a clockwork toy, predator and prey engaged in a silent, high-speed chase across the precipices. They do not seem to inhabit the landscape so much as they maintain it, cleaning the stone, feeding the hidden internal pipes, and cycling the chemical runoff back into the deep.

In this place, the rules of biology and industry have merged. There is no sunlight; there is only the dim, sickly-violet glow of the creatures and the occasional flare of sparks from the deep-earth fissures. There are no maps—there is only the climb, the constant vibration of the machine-under-the-world, and the wet, slick touch of a mountain that is still, in every sense of the word, being forged. You are standing on a planet that is literally under construction, a cold, weeping factory rising into the void, indifferent to your presence, echoing with the soft, metallic song of a world coming to life.
