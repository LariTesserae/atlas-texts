The world is a suspended, vertical ocean—a verticality of absolute saturation. Gravity here behaves not as a downward pull, but as a refractive tension, holding a continental volume of warm, thick, mineral-rich water in a state of suspended, semi-liquid suspension. It is a vast, floating monolith of primordial silt and suspended life, spanning tens of thousands of miles in every direction.

There is no horizon, only the diffuse glow of bioluminescent currents moving through the deep. Light travels through the water like liquid smoke, amber and bruised violet, filtered through layers of ancient, calcified detritus. Because the erosion is absolute, the landscape has long since ceased to be made of "rock" and has instead settled into a permanent, spongy state of organic architecture. Great, soft-sided mountain ranges—the remnants of a planet’s crust—have dissolved into porous, massive reefs of calcified root-systems and petrified sediment. They are so ancient that they have become soft to the touch, crumbling like damp cake when brushed against.

The vegetation is a riot of non-photosynthetic, siphon-feeding growth. Transparent, vein-like vines of immense girth stretch across the void, binding the floating mountains together in a vast, webbed continent of life. These vines pulse with a slow, peristaltic rhythm, circulating oxygenated water through the ecosystem. They are dense, layered, and crowded; to push through them is to be enveloped by a sticky, nutrient-heavy warmth that feels less like a forest and more like the inside of a living gut.

Fauna is nearly non-existent, for this world operates on a scale of physics where individuals are redundant. Instead, the "dynamic" of the world is purely process-driven. Great, shifting tides—rivers of denser, saltier water—cut through the ambient fluid, carrying with them the memories of the world’s final decay. 

The silence is absolute, fractured only by the "Reality Breakdown"—the weirdness that defines the world’s structure. At intervals, the water remembers the form of light or sound from a world that existed before the flood. A ripple in the current will suddenly manifest as the visual ghost of a lightning strike, or the sonic impression of a heartbeat that hits a thousand times a minute, only to vanish into the silt. Bits of "built" reality—small, obsidian-like artifacts that feel like polished thoughts rather than tools—float in the currents, untethered and purposeless. They are smooth, featureless cubes that hum with the vibration of static electricity when they touch the vines.

The atmosphere is heavy, held in place by the pressure of the surrounding liquid. There is no surface to break for air; one simply breathes the oxygenated liquid through the skin, or through the vents that occur naturally in the folds of the deep-reef mountains. 

It is a world of permanent, gentle degradation. Every process is a slow-motion collapse into stillness. The warmth is the heat of a system that has finally, after eons of struggle, finished its work. It is a warm, dark, liquid cradle, massive beyond comprehension, holding the scattered echoes of a civilization that never had steel, but instead played with the very physics of the world until the ocean rose up and claimed the heights to soften everything into the final, eternal rest.
