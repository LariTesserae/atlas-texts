The horizon does not exist here. Instead, there is only the *Looming Lattice*—a continental expanse where the boundary between organic biology and heavy industry has been filed away by eons of growth. 

It is a world of total fabrication. There is no soil, no rock, no water. You stand upon a floor of compressed, chitinous cellulose, hardened into a surface that feels like synthetic mahogany but pulses with the rhythmic, slow-motion thrum of a planetary power grid. Every inch of this world is constructed; it is a bio-industrial architecture where the walls are woven from vine-steel and the ceilings are vaulted cathedrals of bioluminescent, translucent leaf-scales.

The temperature is a crisp, steady chill, a perpetual refrigeration necessary to keep the immense, overheated vascular systems of the floor's infrastructure from melting into slag. Because there is no water, the chemistry of the world runs on cooling gels and viscous, neon-blue sap that pulses through transparent conduits embedded in the floor-boards.

The scale is sickeningly vast. You can stand on a structural node and look up for miles, seeing layers upon layers of gargantuan, photosynthetic factory-stalks that spiral toward a sky that is bright, harsh, and utterly artificial. The light does not come from a star; it leaks from the pores of the canopy, a relentless, white-gold radiance that penetrates every crevice, bleached and clinical. 

It is dense. To move is to negotiate a labyrinth of interlocking growth-pipes and tiered, modular bio-halls. Everything is near-touching. You might brush against a wall and feel the slow shudder of a piston-heart or the rasp of a fiber-optic vine seeking a new anchor point. There is almost no fauna; the few entities that exist are not creatures of flesh and blood, but fragmented, gear-driven spores that drift through the stagnant, ionized air like scraps of discarded blueprints. They do not hunt; they merely repair, stitching together the frayed edges of the environment as it continues to grow over itself.

The sound is a profound, pressurized quiet, punctuated only by the *click-flicker* of reality-thinning. Every few minutes, the physics of the site stutter. A section of the wall might briefly turn into a liquid mirror, or a sound from ten miles away might manifest directly behind your ear, muffled as if through a thick sheet of glass. It is a place that has been settled for so long that the "natural" and the "manufactured" have reached a state of perfect, weathered equilibrium. 

There is no rust here because there is no oxygen to initiate decay. There is only the constant, industrial maintenance of a biological machine that has outgrown its own purpose. Processes occur in cycles: the great sap-pumps heave in the basement levels, forcing nutrients into the high-altitude condensers, which then exhale a fine, shimmering dusting of pollen-gears that settle back onto the floors. 

You are standing in the middle of a self-repairing, continental organism. It does not want you here, but it does not know you are here. You are a loose thread in a garment that has been knitted and unknitted for a billion years, a guest in a cathedral of vegetable-steel that is currently dreaming of a desert it has never seen, while the sky brightens, and the floor hums, and the architecture grows another inch.
