Deep beneath the crust, in a hollow carved by tectonic spasms that have only just ceased to tremble, lies the Vented Sump. It is a chamber of cathedral scale but cathedral silence, a place where the atmosphere is thick, humid, and oppressive, hanging at a constant, feverish temperature that tastes of ozone and hot copper.

There is no natural light here. Instead, visibility is granted by the occasional, rhythmic pulse of low-frequency bioluminescence—not from living creatures, but from chemical leaks in the aging, industrial veins that run through the stone walls.

The terrain is a jagged, nascent geography of exposed bedrock and sharp, unweathered shale. It looks as if the earth was torn open only days ago. In this darkness, thick, oily mats of moss have begun to claim the dampest corners, thriving in the heat and the constant condensation that beads on every surface. The vegetation here is not green; it is a bruised, translucent violet, slick with a perpetual moisture that drips from the cavern ceiling with agonizing, rhythmic slowness. *Plink. Plink.* These drips are the only clock this place knows.

Scattered throughout the sump are the architectural skeletons of a forgotten production. There are no people here, and there is no trace of animal life—not even the smallest insect. The "industries" are comprised of solitary, rusted structures: a singular, motionless turbine half-submerged in a shallow, viscous pool; a series of articulated steel pipes that weep a clear, warm fluid; and a half-finished walkway that leads from a ledge into a wall of solid quartz. The technology is heavy, bolted into the geology itself as if the builders were trying to fuse biology with machinery. 

The air is sparse. One can walk for hundreds of paces across the fractured stone floor without encountering anything but a lone, scorched control panel or a patch of vibrating, humid moss. The space between the artifacts is pressurized by a heavy, invisible weight—a profound loneliness that seems to emanate from the silence itself.

Because the erosion is so young, the walls of the sump are terrifyingly sharp. One must pick their way carefully, for the rocks have not yet been softened by time or traffic. Everything is in a state of suspended, slow-motion decay. The machines are not breaking down; they are simply waiting, their internal gears fused by the heat and the damp, holding a charge that never dissipates and never triggers a cycle.

It is a place of static flux, a room-sized ecosystem that is perpetually on the cusp of an operation that will never begin. You are alone in the heat, watching the violet moss pulse in time with the leaking conduits, listening to the drip of water that never accumulates and the hum of electricity that has nowhere to go. It is a factory for nothing, buried in a basement of the world that has not yet learned how to age.
