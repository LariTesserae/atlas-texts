You find yourself not in a cavern, but within a "fold"—a pocket of reality tucked behind the skin of the world. It is a shallow, subterranean cavity, yet it glows with a fierce, unwavering luminescence, as if the very stone walls were made of trapped morning sunlight.

The air is cool, biting at the skin with a crisp, sterile edge. It is not dry, yet it does not rain; instead, the ceiling—a raw, unformed ceiling of jagged obsidian and translucent quartz—weeps. Beads of heavy, mineral-rich dew form in slow-motion rhythm, detaching to hang suspended in the air for minutes before plummeting toward the floor. When they strike the floor, they do not splash; they shatter into a fine, iridescent mist that coats the few scattered rocks in a layer of weeping frost.

This place is "under construction." The geology feels embryonic, as if the world forgot to finish layering this space. You can see the bones of the architecture: raw, bleeding seams of amethyst pulsing against grey granite, areas where the physics of "solid" simply haven't set yet. If you press your hand against the wall, the stone feels tender, almost warm, like waking muscle.

Life here is tiny, frantic, and densely packed into the silence. There is no wind, yet the space is loud with the movement of translucent, silken creatures. They are not insects nor mammals, but floating clusters of light and membrane, rhythmically expanding and contracting as they harvest the moisture from the air. Because the "built" environment is limited to a few scattered, circular pedestals of smooth, white marble—clearly not made by any human hand—these creatures treat the stone structures as high-ground, huddling upon them in shifting, bioluminescent colonies.

The weirdness is absolute. Gravity behaves like a suggestion rather than a law here; sometimes a loose pebble will drift upward to join a droplet of water in the middle of the room, spinning in a slow, hypnotic orbit. You might catch a glimpse of the sky through a fracture in the roof, but it is not the sky you know—it is a swirling, violet aperture that seems to be looking back at you, shivering in the cool draft.

There is no decay here, only a perpetual, pristine cycle of assembly. A creature dies, and its body does not rot; it unravels into fibers of pure color that drift into the cracks of the wall, becoming part of the geology. Everything is in motion, a quiet, high-stakes choreography of gathering dew and avoiding the slow, drifting rifts of light that occasionally prune the population.

It is a sanctuary of the unfinished. In the center of the pocket, a small, silver-toned pool sits in a hollow of raw, unweathered basalt. It is perfectly still. If you look into it, you don't see your own reflection; you see a version of this room from a thousand years in the future, filled with heavy, overgrown machinery that has long since ceased to function, drowned in a forest of static. 

The silence is thick, broken only by the *tink, tink* of the ceiling weeping, and the soft, organic hum of the living lights as they pulse in synch with the floor's heartbeat. It is a place that feels intimately small, a secret held in a cupped hand, protected from the coarse pressures of the world above. You are a guest in a room that is slowly writing itself into existence.
