This place exists within the thickness of a single, pressurized glass pane—an architectural aberration embedded deep into the wall of a subterranean engine room. It is a pocket-dimension no larger than a human palm, yet inside, the heat is a physical weight, thick as molten gold.

There is no water here. The "soil" is a dense, gray particulate of pulverized silicate and magnetized ash, shifting with the agonizing slowness of tectonic plates. Because the erosion is nearly non-existent, the landscape is sharp, obsidian-edged, and strangely naive; rocks do not look rounded by time but rather extruded, as if the reality of this space is still being printed by an unseen, frantic machine.

Projecting from this raw, mineral dust are the forests. They are not biological, but a strange, emerald-hued industrial synthesis. They look like veins of copper wire grown into the shape of ferns, their fronds tipped with crystalline buds that hum with a faint, electrical current. These "plants" provide the only lushness in the void, their matte-metal surfaces absorbing the ambient warmth of the engine room and converting it into a soft, glowing violet radiation. They do not drop leaves; they vibrate. When they touch, the sound is a melodic, high-pitched *thrum*, like thousands of tuning forks struck at once.

The atmosphere is a dry, static-filled mix of ionized gas and metallic dust. There are no animals; the organic would scorch or shatter under the high-frequency hum that permeates the sub-layer of the environment. 

The most disturbing aspect of this place is the evidence of its "production." From the flat, barren horizons of the silicate plain, colossal, pillar-like spindles rise—structures that seem to be under perpetual construction. They are made of polished brass and fiber-optic filaments, growing upward through a logic that ignores the constraints of gravity. Occasionally, the reality of the pocket-space breaks down; a section of the horizon will stutter, repeating a single loop of space-time where a copper fern unfurls and collapses for several minutes, or a patch of ground will suddenly become transparent, revealing a frantic, flickering mirror-image of the engine room outside, seen from a thousand different angles simultaneously.

It is a lonely, industrial terrarium. It feels like the motherboard of a god, abandoned while the soldering was still warm. The heat is constant, a 2.7/3 swelter that makes the air ripple, blurring the distinction between the static trees and the raw, unformed dust. There is no weather here, only the slow, patient rotation of the spindles and the occasional electrical pop of a "plant" reaching maturity, only to shatter into a cloud of iridescent dust that settles back into the gray, waiting earth. Silence dominates, broken only by the rhythmic, industrial heartbeat of the machinery that cages this pocket-realm, a reminder that something massive is churning just beyond the glass.
