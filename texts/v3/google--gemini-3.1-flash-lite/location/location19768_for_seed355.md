The air here does not merely hold humidity; it is a pressurized, aqueous slurry, a thick soup of lukewarm condensation that clings to the lungs. There is no sun, nor moon, nor stars. The sky is a heavy, bruised-purple ceiling of low-hanging silt-clouds that leak endlessly, not in drops, but in a continuous, slick sheen that turns every horizontal surface into a mirror for the pervasive, absolute darkness.

This is a city of weeping stone, a sprawling, low-profile necropolis built of porous, fossilized coral and river-smoothed basalt. It sits in a depression of sunken, silty plains, where the water table is so high that the ground sloshes beneath the weight of the architecture. There is no metal, no glass, no fire. The structures—labyrinthine, honeycombed blocks of calcified bone-white rock—were grown, not built. They branch like jagged, calcified lungs, hollowed out by the slow, eternal dissolution of the moisture.

It is a place of profound erosion. The buildings are rounded by eons of exposure to this caustic, mineral-heavy rain, smoothed into organic, slumped shapes that resemble melting wax. They do not look like houses; they look like the slow calcification of a geological process that has finally perfected the art of stillness.

Because the light is essentially nonexistent, save for the faint, bioluminescent weeping of the minerals themselves—a dull, sickly ochre glow that bleeds from cracks in the masonry—the residents, if one could call them that, do not perceive the world through sight. They are biological constructs of fluid and membrane, shifting masses of translucent jelly that pulse rhythmically, moving through the narrow, water-choked alleyways. They are not animals; they are local expressions of the climate, birthed from the damp, slowly digesting the stone walls to fuel their own liquid forms. 

They are silent. The soundscape is dominated by the *drip-hiss* of fluid moving through porous stone, the soft, wet slap of shifting slime against slick rock, and the terrifying, cavernous silence of a place that has never known a voice.

The weirdness is a physical weight. Near the center of the district, the logic of space begins to fold. Streets that appear to be straight curves into impossible geometries, looping back upon themselves like a Möbius strip of wet, slick-covered obsidian. You might turn a corner and find yourself walking on the ceiling of the street you just left, the gravity here being merely a suggestion of the heavy, hot air. Time feels looped as well; the erosion is *constant*, yet the city never seems to disappear. It is stuck in a state of terminal entropy, forever crumbling but never gone.

There is no sky to look up to. There is only the upward continuation of the wet, obsidian-slicked architecture, rising into a void of heat and darkness. To walk here is to feel like an intrusion in a digestive tract. The city is a living organ, and the rain is its sweat. It is a place of absolute density and absolute emptiness, where the only movement is the slow, grinding metabolism of the world itself, dissolving into its own floor, and rising again from the silt in a cycle that has no beginning and no end.
