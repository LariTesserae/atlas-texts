Deep beneath the crushing weight of the lightless crust, there exists a pocket of reality no larger than a cathedral nave. It is a geode of impossible mechanics, a space where the physics of the surface have been folded and pressurized into a crystalline sub-basement.

The terrain here is not stone, but a jagged, cooling architecture of self-assembling translucent glass. Because the elevation is so profound, the "sky"—the jagged, overhead ceiling of obsidian—glows with a persistent, intense luminescence, as if the planet itself were leaking its molten core into this hidden room. The light is blinding, white-hot, reflecting off the slick, weeping surfaces of the walls.

Everything is perpetually wet. Thick, heavy droplets of superheated distillate condense on the glass monoliths and slide down in slow, viscous sheets. Because there is no plant life, there is no decay; the world remains pristine, crystalline, and sterile. The moisture does not sustain organisms, but rather acts as a coolant for the ambient energy patterns that define the space. 

These energy patterns are the local "tech"—a humming, sub-molecular lattice that vibrates through the air. You can see it if you squint: fine, golden filaments of non-local force that bridge the gaps between the sparse, scattered pillars of glass. These filaments pulse in rhythmic waves, a slow, breathing cycle that dictates the room's high-temperature climate. As a pulse passes, the heat spikes, and the walls weep harder, the condensed liquid sizzling into vapor before it can pool at the floor.

It is a quiet place, though not silent. The sound is that of a glass harmonica played underwater—a delicate, crystalline ticking combined with the occasional low-frequency *thrum* of the lattice resetting its geometry. 

Fauna is nearly absent, save for the "Sifters." They are not biological in any sense recognizable to terrestrial science; they are hexagonal facets of light, tethered to the floor by thin, umbilical beams of ion-pressure. They drift in the sparse space between the pillars, moving with a jerky, rhythmic grace. They exist to maintain the structural integrity of this pocket, consuming the excess enthalpy and converting it into the faint, shimmering geometric shadows that carpet the floor. If a Sifter drifts too close to a weeping rock, there is a sharp *crack*—a sound like a splintering diamond—as it re-absorbs the excess moisture, cleaning the glass in a brief, brilliant flash of violet light.

There is nothing built here by intent; the world simply *is*. It is a self-maintaining furnace, a delicate, lonely, and brilliant accident of cosmic physics buried miles beneath the reach of any gaze. It is a place of absolute, shimmering isolation, where heat and light dance in a closed loop, forever untouched, forever beginning.
