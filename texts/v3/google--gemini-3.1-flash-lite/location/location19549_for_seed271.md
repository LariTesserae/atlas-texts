This is a hollow of low, concave stone, a depression in the earth roughly the size of a village square. It is a place that feels as though it has been exhaled by the geology surrounding it, settled into a permanent, drowsy equilibrium.

The terrain is defined by soft-edged limestone, worn smooth by eons of slow, methodical weeping. There is no sharp stone here; the erosion is not aggressive, but rather affectionate, as if the water has spent a geological epoch slowly molding the rock into gentle, shallow basins. These basins are the primary feature of the landscape. They hold small, stagnant pools of clear, lukewarm water—a 1.2 level of moisture—that do not ripple and do not drain. They simply exist, fed by the persistent condensation that clings to the limestone walls.

Because the atmosphere is perennially warm and dim, the air feels heavy, like a woolen blanket draped over the space. The light is diffused, filtered through a thick canopy far above that is never seen, resulting in a soft, grey umbra that blankets the floor. Nothing moves unless a drop of condensation detaches from an overhead crevice, falling with a flat, final *plip* that echoes briefly before the silence re-asserts its dominance.

Vegetation is a fleeting afterthought. There are no trees and no grasses, only patches of pale, velvet-like moss that cling to the dampest curves of the stone. They are stationary, unchanging, and sparse, occupying only the corners where the moisture is most concentrated. There is no animal life—no insects navigate the still air, and no burrowers disturb the dust-free sediment. The absence of fauna creates a space that feels unfinished, as if a stage has been set for a play that was canceled before the rehearsals began.

Deep in the center of this depression, there is a scattering of built remnants—a low circle of stones, waist-high, weathered into smooth, featureless lumps. They serve no discernible purpose and show no marks of tool-work. They look less like construction and more like a biological necessity, as if the stones grew into those shapes themselves. They are cold to the touch but retain the warmth of the ambient air.

In this place, the concept of "time" has been replaced by "state." There is no gradient here; the wind never reaches the floor of the hollow, and the water levels do not fluctuate with the seasons. It is a frozen system, a room-sized ecosystem that has reached a point of perfect, lonely saturation. The silence is profound—not the heavy silence of a tomb, but the expectant, rhythmic quiet of something waiting. It is a place of total stability, where the physical world is perfectly balanced against its own limitations, resting forever in the dim, warm amber of its own occurrence.
