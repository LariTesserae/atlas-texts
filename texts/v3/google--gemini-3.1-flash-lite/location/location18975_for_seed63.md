The air here is not air, but a thick, pressurized suspension of pulverized mica and suspended heat-cinders. It is a vertical world, a claustrophobic spire of crag and obsidian shard, rising miles above an invisible floor. There is no water, no moisture to soften the friction of a trillion scraping surfaces; instead, the world is a dry, scouring abrasive.

Because the heat is intense—a slow-roasting, subterranean furnace-glow that radiates from the crystalline bedrock—everything behaves like a fluid. The rock is not static. It is "settled-form," meaning the mountains themselves are slumped and folded, worn into smooth, melted ridges by eons of tectonic grinding. At this altitude, the summit plateaus are squeezed into a dense, interlocking jumble of mineral scales.

In the total darkness of this place, the only sense that matters is touch, and the touch is violent. This is a universe of packed presences. Every square inch of the obsidian terrain is occupied by the fauna. 

They are thin, chitinous, and shivering—a biology of pure heat-exchange. They resemble animated fragments of volcanic glass, spindly, multi-jointed, and silent. They do not hunt with sound; they hunt with heat-sinks. Because the population density is overwhelming, the creatures are layered three and four deep over every available surface, forming a living topography that shudders and shifts in a constant, slow-motion cascade. When one creature moves, ripples of vibration pass through the thousands beneath it. It is a carpet of living, scraping, sliding glass, perpetually migrating toward the warm vents that bleed heat from the planet’s core.

There is no light, yet the creatures navigate through a sophisticated sense of thermal mapping. They are so numerous that they create their own atmosphere of friction; as they slide over one another, the static discharge creates microscopic, silent flashes of purple light that flicker for a nanosecond before being snuffed out by the press of the next layer of bodies.

Scattered amidst this tectonic hum are the artifacts: small, impossibly ancient, pillar-like structures made of a density that defies the surrounding stone. They are not built by the creatures, nor by any intelligence known to this strata. These pillars are immovable, weathered down to polished stumps, serving as the static axes around which the living tide circles. The creatures worship these pillars in their own fashion, burying themselves into the grooves of the stone until they are worn flat by the density of the colony.

The sound is not a noise, but a frequency. It is the rhythmic, rasping *shhh-click* of billions of silicate limbs against silica rock, a dry, tectonic susurration that vibrates through the jawbone. It is a world of constant motion, yet there is no progress. The creatures are trapped in a feedback loop of warmth and abrasive survival. To stop moving is to be buried by the slow, grinding avalanche of a million neighbors. To reach the height—the very peak of the spire—is to eventually be pushed off into the dark, falling for miles until the cycle begins again at the roots of the world.

There is no sky, no horizon, only the claustrophobic density of heat, stone, and the restless, skittering skin of a million lives shifting against the dark.
