This is a world of **The Great Weave**, a pressurized, subterranean megastructure that has colonized its own geology. There is no water here; moisture is an archaic concept, replaced entirely by viscous, nutrient-rich sap and conductive resins that pulse through the infrastructure of this silent, searing furnace.

The landscape is not built of stone or steel, but of **calcified arboreal architecture**. Every surface is a thick, leathery husk—veined with glowing, amber-hued chlorophyll-circuits that function as both structural support and thermal regulation. Because the environment is so relentlessly hot—a near-blistering 2.9/3—the "plants" have evolved to be mineral-hard, woven into dense, interlocking lattices of cellulose-composite girders that form the floor, ceiling, and walls of this vast, enclosed domain.

There is no natural substrate left. The ground is a mosaic of pressurized root-plates, polished to a dull sheen by eons of friction. You are standing on a terrace carved out of what was once a grove, now fused into a single, immobile, industrial organism. There is no sky—only the interlocking canopy of carbon-fiber stalks that filter out all light. A dim, subterranean gloom persists, broken only by the rhythmic, sickly-pulsing glow of the sap-lines as they push energy into the "machinery" of the structure.

The erosion here is primordial. Everything is ground down to its absolute, final, stable form. The sharp edges of the initial growth have been smoothed away by billions of cycles of tectonic shifting and thermal expansion. It is a world that has finished its evolution; it is now merely maintaining its own entropy.

Because the density is so absolute (2.7/3), there is no space between lives. You cannot find a gap in the architecture; every millimeter of the environment is a component, a valve, a joint, or a filter. It is an industrial garden that breathes. You hear the deep, subsonic hum of the sap-pumps and the periodic *creak* of the entire facility shifting as the heat forces the organic girders to expand and contract against one another. It sounds like the groaning of a massive, wooden ship being crushed by deep-sea pressure, though here, the pressure is thermal.

There is no animal life—no heartbeat, no scuttle, no cry. Any biology that was not part of the structural weave was long ago composted into the floor-plates. You are walking inside a machine that is also a forest, a landscape that is simultaneously waiting to be born and already dead. 

The turbulence is in the hidden veins. While the surface appears ancient and quiet, the internal logistics are hyperactive. The sap-veins are a chaotic, bazaar-like flow of resources—the "anthill-energy" of a civilization reduced to its most efficient, automated metabolism. It is a place of total construction; nothing is found, everything is necessitated. You are traversing the internal organs of a world-sized loom that has long since forgotten why it started weaving, but continues to do so, consuming itself in the dark, in the heat, in the silence, to ensure that not a single cubic centimeter of space is left unexploited.
