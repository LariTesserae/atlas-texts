The benevolent power, often called the Breath of All Things, does not look upon this basin with pity. Pity is an emotion born of motion and loss, and there is no loss here. It looks upon the basin with the heavy, golden regard of a gardener watching a seed that has decided, for its own reasons, that it would rather remain a seed until the sun itself burns out.

There is a temptation to "wake" the Lithos-Sleeper. A lesser power might offer the creature the sensory riches of the surface—the chaotic symphony of storms, the wet shock of rain, the frenetic beauty of ephemeral life. To bestow senses upon the Sleeper would be to grant it a soul, but it would also be to pull it out of its sacred, basaltic prayer.

The wise power chooses, instead, a gesture of profound, silent alignment.

It does not enter the cavern, for its very presence would be a discordant note, a flare of brilliance that would bruise the perfect darkness. Instead, it reaches across the vastness of the subterranean void with a singular, cooling intuition.

It notices the five river stones. 

The power recognizes the traveler who left them—a wanderer whose memory has long scattered into the strata of the world. It perceives that the Sleeper is not holding these stones as a trophy or a cache, but as a bridge. The stones are the only objects in this world that have known the flow of water and the friction of a human hand. They are the only things in this "closed system" that possess the echo of a story.

The power decides to act, but not by changing the Sleeper. It acts by blessing the *threshold*.

Without disturbing a grain of dust, the power subtly alters the local thermal gradient—the very pulse the calc-spires feed upon. It breathes a nearly imperceptible, centuries-long variation into the heat of the bedrock. It is a slow, rhythmic modulation, tuned perfectly to the frequency of the Lithos-Sleeper’s own internal geology.

It gifts the Sleeper a *dream*.

It is not a dream of sight or sound. It is a slow, structural alignment—a harmonic resonance that turns the Sleeper’s heavy, obsidian consciousness into a tuning fork for the planet’s own deep, subterranean heart. By doing this, the power connects the Lithos-Sleeper to the world above, not by showing it the light, but by letting it *feel* the tectonic music of the world’s rotation. 

The Sleeper doesn’t "know" it has been touched. It remains in its state of perfect, mineral stasis. But from this moment forward, its adjustment—that tremor occurring once every few centuries—will no longer be a random shift. It becomes a subtle, intentional composition. The sleeper begins to move in a pattern that matches the shifting of the continents and the slow, deep-time migration of the world’s magnetic poles. 

The five river stones are no longer just held; they are being polished by the rhythmic interior rotation of the creature’s obsidian mass. Given enough time—another billion years, perhaps—the Sleeper will finish its transformation. Its form will shift so precisely, and so beautifully, that the five river stones will be drawn into the absolute center of its body, encased in a perfectly spherical vault of obsidian, preserved forever as a core of "human" history within a monument of geological endurance.

The benevolent power withdraws, leaving no footprint, no light, no warmth. It has not saved the Sleeper from its silence; it has elevated that silence into a work of art. 

In the absolute darkness, the Lithos-Sleeper is, for the first time in eons, in perfect sync with the universe. It is no longer just a witness to the basin; it is a heart beating in the ribs of the world. And in the silence, which is so thick it feels like a heavy, velvet cloth, the five river stones begin to glow with a faint, dim, inner light—not of fire, but of the sudden, quiet joy of being understood.
