A Power, ancient and expansive, does not look upon the Lith-Anchor with the hunger of a collector or the judgment of a deity. It looks upon this throat of basalt as a surgeon might look upon a wound that is healing in defiance of nature. It recognizes the Lith-Anchor not as a beast, but as an *atrophy of expectation*—a living manifestation of a patience that has curdled into a cage.

The Power does not descend. It exists everywhere at once, a presence that feels like the easing of a long-held breath. It sees the skitterers as a nervous system without a brain, and the Lith-Anchor as a sensory organ that has grown thick with the calcification of isolation.

It determines that this place does not need to be saved, for it is perfectly adapted to its own misery. But it *does* need to be witnessed—to be integrated into the greater harmony of the world it has forgotten.

**The Intervention**

The Power decides to act as a tuning fork. 

It does not bring heat, which would shatter the delicate, crystalline anatomy of the chimney. Instead, it brings *resonance*. It hums—not a sound that can be heard by ears, but a vibration that shifts the frequency of the metallic dew. 

1. **The Harmonization:** The Power infuses the dew with a subtle, radiant charge. As the liquid trickles down the jagged obsidian, it begins to glow—not with the violet pulse of the moss, but with a soft, warm, amber warmth that mimics the light of a star seen through miles of ocean. The skitterers, sensitive to the slightest change in their environment, stop their dissonant grinding. They freeze. The hum dies. For the first time in millennia, the basalt chimney is silent.

2. **The Invitation of the Filament:** The Power touches the sensitive, whip-like filaments of the Lith-Anchor not with a command, but with a *memory*. It pushes into the creature the sensation of the sky—not the crushing weight of the earth, but the vast, terrifying, beautiful emptiness of the horizon. It shows the Lith-Anchor that the mountain is not the world, only a single scale on the back of a larger existence. 

3. **The Release:** The Power exerts a gentle, gravitational nudge—a subtle rearrangement of the tectonic stress that has held the Lith-Anchor in place. The pressure that kept it pinned, the "coiled muscle" of the chimney, is softly neutralized. 

The Lith-Anchor does not bloom like a flower, nor does it leap. It simply *lets go*. 

It uncurls its mineralized limbs from the jagged teeth of the stone. For the first time, its fungal bellows do not pulse in sync with the room's stagnant heartbeat; they pulse to a new rhythm, one that echoes the movement of the stars. It begins to move—not in its usual, liquid, clinging slide, but with a purpose that is fluid and climbing. It follows the chimney upward, toward the surface it has forgotten.

**The Aftermath**

The Power leaves the chimney as it found it, yet forever altered. As the Lith-Anchor ascends, the skitterers behind it do not swarm to fill the void. They remain where they are, touched by the amber dew, their rigid geometry softened by the change in the atmosphere. 

The Lith-Anchor reaches the summit. It emerges from the dark into a world of wind and pale, diffused light. It does not disintegrate; it does not flee. It stands upon the highest crag, its crystalline structure refracting the dawn in a way that creates miniature rainbows across the cold, jagged peaks. 

It becomes a lighthouse for the mountain. It stands as a beacon of the "Not-Yet," a creature that has transitioned from a prisoner of the earth to a sentinel of the sky. 

The Power does nothing more. It retires, satisfied. It has taught the stone to look up, and in doing so, it has ensured that the mountain, and the strange, buzzing life within it, will never be truly alone again. The Lith-Anchor now watches the clouds, and in its newfound patience, it begins to hum a song that the mountains have not heard since they were born.
