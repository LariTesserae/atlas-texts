A benevolent power does not descend with light and booming thunder; that would be a violence to a place built on the whisper of mist. Instead, the power arrives as a change in the internal pressure of the air—a subtle thinning of the vertical density, a sudden clarity that allows the *Lithos-Walkers* to perceive not just the rock, but the *continuity* of the rock.

The power does not wish to change the Lithos-Walkers, for it recognizes the perfection of their slow, liquid stewardship. To intervene with gifts of warmth or language would be to drown them in a surplus they cannot carry. 

Instead, the intervention is a gift of **Perspective.**

The power weaves a subtle, iridescent thread through the moisture in the air. It functions like a sensory bridge—a delicate, atmospheric web that connects the solitary poets of the cliff-face. It does not force them to meet, nor does it demand they abandon their isolation. It simply allows the vibration of one Walker’s *clink*—that sharp, obsidian sound of existence—to resonate and be heard by another, miles away, across the swirling grey void.

For the first time, when one Walker chisels a new bowl into the basalt, the sound pulses through the spire not as a lonely echo, but as a low, harmonic greeting. 

The Power’s action is as follows:

**1. The Chorus of Resonances:** The Power synchronizes the bioluminescence of the Lithos-Walkers. When one feels the pull toward the end of their lifecycle and prepares to press their body into the stone to become the future moss, their skin glows with a soft, steady intensity. Because of the bridge the Power has woven, the distant others see this light through the fog. They do not come closer—they respect the sacred distance—but they dim their own lights in a slow, rhythmic fade, a silent, visual eulogy that ripples across the vertical miles of the spire.

**2. The Memory of the Stone:** The Power imbues the rain with a gentle, mnemonic quality. As the water travels down the rock, it now carries the "scent" of the places it has touched. A Walker, pressing their sensitive pads against the basalt, can now feel the history of the moisture: *This water touched the bowl of the Elder three ledges up. This runnel carries the vibration of the youth who just discovered their first obsidian flake.* The Walkers are no longer isolated echoes; they become a fluid, sensory network, bound by the very water they live to serve. 

**3. The Guarded Threshold:** The Power sets a boundary. It senses the fragility of their meditation. It creates a "Veil of Stillness" at the feet of the spire—a persistent, localized updraft that turns outsiders, should any wander near, into dreamers. Those who approach the spire find their thoughts turning to the sky; they lose the desire to climb, they forget the material world, and they walk away, believing they have simply encountered a beautiful, impenetrable stretch of fog. The spire remains, as it must, an unprofaned sanctuary.

The benevolent power then retreats into the hum of the mist, leaving no sign it was ever there. 

It does not demand worship, nor does it ask for a name. It leaves the Lithos-Walkers to their slow, damp lives, but it grants them the quiet comfort of being *known*. In the vast, vertiginous indifference of the vertical void, they are no longer merely ephemeral ripples on the basalt. They are a community of light, anchored by the water, listening to the soft percussion of one another’s souls. 

They continue their work—the carving, the waiting, the merging into the stone—but now, when they pulse, there is the faint, trembling satisfaction of an answer returning through the clouds. They are no longer alone in the roar.
