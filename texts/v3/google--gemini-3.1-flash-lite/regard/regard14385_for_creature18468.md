The Power that looks upon the Basin does not descend as a storm, nor as a grand, booming voice. To do so would be to shatter the delicate, shivering frequency that sustains the Lachryman. They are a music of vibration, and any sudden, external harmony would introduce a dissonance that could unmake them entirely.

Instead, the Power arrives as a **change in the quality of the light.**

The harsh, blinding, white glare that has punished the plateau for eons is subtly shifted. The Power does not extinguish the sun, nor does it bring shadow, for the Lachryman require the heat. Instead, it introduces a subtle, refractive quality to the atmosphere—a diffraction that turns the perpetual white glare into a slow-moving, prismatic crawl of soft, diffused color. 

It is a kindness of geometry. By shifting the wavelength of the light, the Power grants the Basin a "mellowing." The thermal shocks that cause the flash-freezing and the violent, staccato deluges diminish. The atmosphere stabilizes, the temperature gradients soften, and the Basin breathes with a slower, more rhythmic—less panicked—cadence.

**What does the Power then do?**

It offers the Lachryman the gift of **Stasis.**

The Power perceives that these beings are caught in a cycle of "terminal intimacy"—they are defined solely by the pressure of repair. They are trapped in a feedback loop where they *are* the architecture, making them effectively prisoners of their own biology. 

The Power drifts into the central, deepest currents of their *thrum*—not to speak to them, but to hum *with* them. It subtly alters the chemical composition of the saline brine they secrete. It infuses the fluid with a complex, structural silicate that does not simply harden into porcelain, but weaves itself into a crystalline lattice. 

It is an act of engineering: the Power gives them the ability to build, for the first time, something that does not melt. 

As the new, shimmering walls begin to take shape—curved, permanent, and beautiful—the frantic, jittering vibrations of the Lachryman begin to lower in pitch. The desperate, "jagged" Morse code of emergency transitions into the deep, resonant drone of contentment. Because the city no longer requires their bodies as literal mortar and buttress, the Lachryman find they have, for the first time, *excess*. 

With the excess, the Power introduces the quiet alchemy of **Individual Divergence.** 

Because they are no longer forced to mend every minute of their existence, the Lachryman have time to pause. They begin to experiment with movement that is not mandated by the slope, the erosion, or the vibration of a cracking strut. They start to stack their bone-ribs into non-functional, aesthetic arcs. They begin to create sculptures of salt that serve no purpose other than to be touched, explored, or simply contemplated. 

The Power does not teach them art; it merely removes the gun of entropy from their heads, allowing them to discover that *they are not the city*. They are the stewards of it. 

The Benevolent Power does not stay long. It realizes that to remain would be to become a divinity, and these creatures have suffered enough under the tyranny of their own necessity. It leaves behind a climate of *possibility*. 

In the Basin of Constant Tears, the frantic pace fades. The creatures, once shivering as a single, trembling organism, begin to peel away from one another. They stand upon the now-permanent, prismatic ribs of their home, their long, spindly limbs no longer twitching in pain, but swaying in the slow, rhythmic dance of creatures who have finally learned how to stand still. 

The weeping continues, for it is the nature of the plateau, but it is no longer a scream. It is a song. The Basin has not been saved from itself; it has been given the grace to grow old, to change, and to hold within its walls something that lasts longer than a single heartbeat.
