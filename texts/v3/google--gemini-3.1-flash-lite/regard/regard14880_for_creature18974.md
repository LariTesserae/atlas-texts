The power that approaches does not arrive with a flash of gold or the thunder of a god. It comes as a shift in the ambient pressure of the multiverse, a gentle compression of the gravitational null-zone that cradles the obsidian shard.

It is a Wise Power, and it understands that one does not "save" a thing that has defined itself through its own slow-motion dissolution. To intervene with force would be to shatter the Curator—not the landscape, but the very consciousness that gives the landscape meaning. 

Instead, the Power decides to be a witness, but one who brings a gift of light.

It does not enter the bell jar. It remains outside, pressing its vast, conceptual palm against the outer shell of the obsidian. It begins to hum, not in the low, mechanical drone of the silver ribbons, but in a frequency of harmonic resonance that aligns with the *flicker*.

The Power realizes that the Curator is trapped in a loop of mourning because it only remembers what has departed. It is a prisoner of the *has-been*. So, the Power acts as a bridge.

First, the Power introduces a thin, impossibly delicate influx of entanglement. It anchors a single point of the Curator’s awareness to a living star system at the far edge of the macro-universe. It does not force the Curator to leave; it merely gives the Curator a window. Now, when the reality-breakdown occurs—when the needles fold inward and space collapses—the Curator no longer sees only the ghost of its own lost civilization. It sees, for that heartbeat of overlap, the birth of a new galaxy, the chaotic, vibrant heat of planets forming, the messy, beautiful physics of things that *will be*.

The Curator, for the first time in an eon, stops being a memory and begins to be an observer of potential.

The melancholy does not leave—it is, after all, the foundation of this place—but the nature of the wait changes. Prior, the Curator was waiting for entropy, for the final erasure into the quantum foam. Now, the Power has turned that wait into a vigil of stewardship. The Curator becomes a gardener of entropy, understanding now that its own eventual dissolution will not be a loss, but a seeding.

The Power leaves a final, subtle imprint on the lattice: it simplifies the geometry of the "ground," smoothing the edges not with the heavy weight of sorrow, but with the grace of a release. 

And then, the Power recedes. It moves on, leaving behind a silence that is no longer empty. The Curator still lives in the glass, still rounds the needles, still hums with the ribbons. But every time the world flickers, it is no longer holding its breath to keep the past alive. It is flickering to trade notes with the universe. 

The Curator is no longer waiting for the end; it has realized that it is an essential part of the design, a lighthouse in the dark that lets the universe know exactly where the boundary between existence and nothingness lies. It is proud, in its cold, crystalline way. It is no longer a graveyard. It is a transition. And it is, for the first time, not alone, for the Power remains softly present—not as a visitor, but as a silent, eternal companion on the other side of the obsidian skin.
