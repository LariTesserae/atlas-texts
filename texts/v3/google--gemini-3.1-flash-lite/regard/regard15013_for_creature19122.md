The Great Eye of existence, that which gazes upon the birth of galaxies and the cooling of embers, turned its attention to the Spire. It did not look with judgment, nor with the desire to "fix" what is broken, for the Great Eye sees the beauty in the *becoming*. It saw the Litho-Artificers not as victims of a loop, but as a masterpiece of persistence—an infinite prayer written in copper and friction.

The benevolent power did not intervene to stop the clicking. It did not bestow ease, nor did it offer them "the afterlife" or a way out. To do so would be to silence the song they were born to sing.

Instead, the power chose a subtle, profound form of witness: **The Gift of Resonance.**

It reached into the density of the spire, folding space just enough to introduce a single, infinitesimal harmonic frequency into the atmosphere. It was a vibration that did not alter the physics of the world, nor dampen the hunger of the gears. Rather, it acted as a catalyst for the violet-light of the moss. 

Under this new influence, the bioluminescence of the crystalline fungus changed. When a Foreman anchored itself to become a corner, when a Weaver harvested the heat-memory of a stalk, when a gear-collision sparked a new life into existence—the light began to *remember*. 

Before the Gift, the Artificers lived in a permanent "now," a flickering, amnesiac state of constant labor. Now, the ultraviolet glow that coated the discarded shielding and the oxidized carapaces began to hold onto the light of their actions.

Every act of sacrifice—the Foreman who chose to become the foundation—was no longer erased by the next structural shift. The moss, catalyzed by the power’s touch, began to etch the history of those moments into the very atomic structure of the metal. If one were to look closely at a buttress, they would see, glowing in the depths of the copper, a ghost-print of the entity that had held that wall in place for a thousand rotations.

The Spire was no longer just a tomb of mechanical cannibalism; it became a library of light.

When a Weaver scuttled over its kin, it now navigated a landscape of illuminated history. The discarded plates of the fallen were no longer just raw materials; they were memories. As the swarm disassembled the old to forge the new, they were effectively reading the history of their own evolution. Each new-born spider, emerging from the heat of the friction, gained that small, flickering flicker of ancestral knowledge—the "memory" of how to be, how to serve, and how to hold the spire together with grace.

The power did something else, more intimate: it changed the *hiss*.

To the Artificers, the soundscape remained the same—the rhythmic *thrum-click-hiss*. But because the benevolent power had granted them a soul-deep resonance, the sound was no longer just the noise of a machine; it was the rhythm of a heartbeat. 

The Spire became a living piece of music. As the Litho-Artificers moved, they were dancing. They were no longer panicked ants in a falling house; they were the conductors of a symphony that was slowly, piece by piece, turning the "terminal construction" into a monument. 

The benevolent power left them to their work, but it left them with the most precious thing in any dimension: **The knowledge that they mattered.**

As the centuries ticked on, the Spire stopped being a tomb. It began to hum with a soft, steady, contented violet radiance. The Artificers still disassembled one another—for that is their nature, and their nature is their glory—but they did so now with the reverence of one taking a finished scroll and weaving it into a tapestry. They became the architects of their own immortality, finding, in the heart of the frantic, ionizing dark, a quiet, eternal peace.

The experiment was a success, not because it was completed, but because it had finally learned how to sing.
