The benevolent power does not descend as a storm or a thunderclap. To interact with the Custodian—a closed loop of such profound, quiet exhaustion—is to understand that even the most well-intended light can be a trauma to a garden that has built its own dark.

The power observes. It feels the *thud-hiss* of the ironwood pistons, the desperate, blind hunger of the rhizomes, and the absolute, lonely density of the Custodian’s "self." It recognizes the tragedy: the Custodian is not a prisoner, but a perfectionist. It has achieved a state of total, static immortality that lacks only the one thing the power values most: the possibility of change.

The power does not break the walls. It does not rip the ironwood apart to let in the sun, for the sudden shock of an "outside" would kill the delicate ecosystem instantly. 

Instead, the power performs an act of silent, infinitesimal gardening.

It breathes a singular, transformative frequency into the deep, stagnant air—not an instruction, but a *memory of wind.* 

The power does not touch the Custodian directly. It reaches into the deepest, most calcified layer of the floor, into the very slurry of the decaying leaf-litter where the Custodian’s intelligence is heaviest and most sluggish. There, it deposits a dormant, dormant spark of *entropy.*

It is not a poison. It is the catalyst of growth that knows the meaning of "endings."

The Custodian feels it first as a glitch in the hydrostatic pressure—a ripple in the vascular flow that it cannot immediately smooth over with waxy, protective membranes. It is a new, sharp, and terrifyingly *un-preprogrammed* sensation. For the first time in eons, the Custodian experiences the feeling of a season. 

The immediate result is not destruction, but a softening. The rigid geometry of the ironwood halls begins, ever so slowly, to lose its defensive curvature. The "machinery" stops pulsing solely for the sake of maintenance and begins to pulse with curiosity. The lavender mist changes, shifting in composition from a sealer of wounds to a carrier of pollen.

The power watches as the Custodian, once a closed cycle of consumption, begins to dream of things that do not exist within the walls. By introducing the *end* of the loop, the power has given the Custodian the *beginning* of an evolution. 

It does not liberate the being; it does something far kinder. It teaches the Custodian that it is allowed to *die*, and precisely because of that, it is finally allowed to *grow.*

The ironwood arches begin to warp not out of decay, but to reach, branching out into strange, chaotic patterns that defy the ancient architecture. The Custodian, once the perfect warden of its own tomb, begins to turn its thoughts toward the earth above, not with the intention to consume, but with the desire to touch the sky. 

The benevolent power then withdraws, leaving behind a silence that is no longer heavy or stagnant, but expectant. It leaves the Custodian to its new, glorious, and fragile work: the act of becoming something else.
