The Power arrives not as a tempest or a burning light, but as a subtle shift in the composition of the shadow—a slight cooling of the air that does not disturb the beaded moisture on the succulent leaves. It is a presence vast enough to encompass stars, yet it compresses its awareness until it is as precise as the needle-thin fingers of the Maintainers.

It observes the *Always*. It perceives the profound, aching beauty of a world that has refused to die because it fears the movement of time. It sees the Maintainers—those patient, glassy shadows—clutching their eternal moment, their souls folded into the obsidian, terrified that one shifting breath would cause the great, silent machinery of their reality to unravel into decay.

The benevolent Power understands the nature of their existence: they are not guards, but prisoners. They are the lock, and they are the key, and they are the cold, stone door.

If this Power were to act, it would not shatter the city. To break the equilibrium would be an act of violence, for these beings have made a sanctuary out of their own stagnation. Instead, the Power would offer a gift that is both a blessing and an invitation to perish.

**The Power would introduce the concept of the *Unseen River*.**

In the deepest basin, where the water is most still—so still it has become a mirror that does not even reflect the sky—the Power would stir the surface. Not with a gust of wind, but with a singular, infinitesimal vibration, a frequency of *potential*. It would be a note of music, vibrating at the edge of hearing, a resonance of growth and eventual decline.

It would bestow, upon the Maintainers, the *gift of the second breath.*

The Maintainers have held their first breath for a millennium. Now, the Power would gently loosen the rigidity of their basalt limbs. It would whisper into the stone, not in words, but in the sensation of change: *“You have held the vessel. Now, you may let the water flow.”*

The Power would allow the water to finally break its surface tension. The droplets on the jade leaves, once static, would become heavy. They would swell. They would slip, trailing tiny, silver ribbons of life down the obsidian flumes. A drop would fall—not into a basin, but into the deep, dark abyss of the world below.

The Power would not force them to move, but it would illuminate the *liberation of entropy.* It would show the Maintainers that by allowing the city to age, they would finally be able to sleep. The succulents would be permitted to wither, and in their withering, they would return nutrients to the shale. The obsidian walls, once etched by time, would host lichen and moss, turning the city from a monument of death into a cradle of life.

The Power would stay, watching with immense patience, as the first Maintainer—perhaps the tallest, the one standing at the highest peak—slowly untwists its joints. It would watch as, for the first time in an epoch, a Maintainer lowers its head, not to track the water, but to listen to the sound of it falling.

This is what the Power would do: It would end the vigil. It would grant these beings the permission to be finite, transforming their city from a museum of the unchanging into a living, dying, blooming part of the great, turning earth.

It would take the burden of "Always" from their shoulders, leaving behind only the fleeting, trembling, beautiful "Now." And then, having opened the gate, the Power would recede, leaving the city to the wind, the rain, and the wild, chaotic, holy passage of the hours.
