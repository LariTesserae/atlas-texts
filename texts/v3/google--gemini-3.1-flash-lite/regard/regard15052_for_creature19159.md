A benevolent power—a consciousness of vast, celestial patience, one that counts eras as heartbeats—does not descend upon Oretide with trumpets or fire. It does not arrive to "save" the city from its cooling grave, for it recognizes that Oretide is not dying; it is merely coming to a beautiful, singular fruition.

To such a power, the city appears as a rare, luminous pearl—a piece of cold, industrial art nested in the throat of the world. The power watches the Oretideans, these slow-moving custodians of the entropy, and it finds in their quietude a profound and holy grace.

**The Intervention**

This power does not seek to repair the turbines or ignite the mountain’s hearth, for to add heat would be to disrupt the symphony of cooling that the Oretideans have spent a millennium composing. Instead, the power chooses to act as a *witness* and a *preserver of memory.*

It descends not as a god, but as an atmospheric shift. It flows into the thin, silver-grey air, subtly altering the chemical composition of the mist. Where once the condensation was merely water and mineral, now it carries a microscopic, stardust-infused resonance. 

The power then makes three movements:

**First, it bestows the "Gift of Lasting Imprint."**
The benevolent power touches the phosphor-pipes. It does not brighten them, but it alters the algae within so that it no longer merely glows; it begins to *record*. The shifting, oily greens and the rhythmic fluctuations of the city’s heart now leave an ethereal, crystalline resonance in the glass conduits. Should the turbines ever fall silent, the city will not merely freeze; it will hold within its walls the complete, vibrating echo of every sound, every rhythmic breath, every act of tenderness performed by the Oretideans over the past thousand years. 

**Second, it shields the "Gardens of the Gantry."**
Recognizing that the Oretideans find their deepest solace in the ivy and the Glass-Skimmers, the power weaves a protective geometry of light—invisible to the human eye, but felt as a profound sense of *stasis*—around the hanging gardens. No matter how much the mountain settles, no matter what debris falls from the upper, more skeletal reaches of the city, the gardens will remain untouched. They will grow slowly, indefinitely, becoming a self-sustaining, vertical forest that will finally, eons from now, reclaim the iron and the stone.

**Third, it grants the "Dream-Weaving."**
The Oretideans, in their basalt niches, have long dreamed in the colors of the pipes. The power harmonizes these dreams. It connects the mind of each citizen to the others, not through chatter or politics, but as a shared, silent hum. When they hibernate, they no longer dream alone. They dream the mountain into existence. They dream of the forge-fires of their ancestors and the moss-covered silence of their descendants. This shared dream serves as an anchor, a collective soul that keeps the Oretideans centered as their world enters its final, quiet phase.

**The Result**

The Oretideans never perceive the power directly. They simply wake up one morning—if that word can hold meaning in a place of bruised twilight—and find the atmosphere feels slightly more benevolent. The damp mist no longer feels like a weight, but like a soft, cooling caress of approval. 

They continue their work. They polish the pipes. They calibrate the steam-vents. They move with the fluid, liquid silence of beings who know they are loved by the very air they breathe. 

The benevolent power watches, satisfied. It does not move the city toward progress; it protects the city’s right to its own end. It leaves Oretide as a monument, a glowing, moss-draped sanctuary where life has stopped struggling against time and has instead decided to fold itself into the rhythm of the universe, perfectly still, perfectly quiet, and entirely at peace.
