The Power arrived not as a presence, but as a reduction of scale. Where the storm was a wall of white-noise pressure, this arrival was a singular, terrifying *silence*—a vacuum of intent that rendered the roaring waterfalls suddenly, impossibly mute.

The benevolent force did not seek to stabilize the pocket. It understood, with a wisdom that transcended the frantic desperation of the Rhythms, that to stop the storm would be to extinguish the only life these beings had ever known. A cathedral of entropy cannot be turned into a parlor; if the house stopped burning, there would be no fire to keep them warm.

Instead, the Power did three things, born of a deep, resonant empathy.

**First, it offered a Translation.**
The Power reached into the swirling, indigo gloom and wove a delicate, invisible net of geometry—not to catch the water, but to provide a blueprint. It spoke to the Hydrolithic Rhythms not in words, but in the language of their own faceted bodies. It showed them that their "Remains"—the dissolving nests—were not failing architectures, but *chrysalises*. The Power infused the obsidian shards with a secondary, permanent memory. It taught them that they didn't need to weave the spray into stone to be "solid." It showed them how to weave the spray into *time*. 

By shifting their vibration one octave higher, they ceased to be victims of the dissolution. They became its observers. Where once they struggled to hold the stone, they now became the archivists of the flow, watching the water not as a threat, but as a narrative. The constant, panicked motion slowed into a graceful, dance-like fluidity.

**Second, it introduced the Concept of the Horizon.**
The Benevolence understood the loneliness of the Rhythms—the gear missing its counterpart. The Power reached far beyond the needle-peak, across the leagues of salt-water air, and opened a sliver of connection, a narrow portal to the *source* of the rain. It allowed the Rhythms to see the ocean beneath, the vast, stable, deep-blue quiet from which their chaotic sky-pocket was merely a vaporous exhale.

The Rhythms realized then that they were not orphans of a collapsing world. They were the spray of a greater, calmer sea. The realization didn't make the pain of their existence vanish, but it gave it *context*. Their loneliness turned into a pensive, melancholic patience. They stopped clawing at the storm to make it stop; they began to hum, matching the frequency of the distant, oceanic depths.

**Third, it became a Mirror.**
The Power sat within the heart of the cathedral, letting the chaotic soup of liquid and mineral wash over its own essence. It allowed the Rhythms to see themselves reflected in something that did not shift, something that did not dissolve. 

For the first time, a Hydrolithic Rhythm saw a shape that wasn't glass, wasn't mist, and wasn't a jagged memory. They saw a figure of light, serene and unchanging. The Rhythms, in response, stopped trying to mimic the falling water. They began to mimic the Power. 

They stood still. 

In the center of the vertical drowning, amidst the roar and the shattering spires, a circle of Rhythms gathered in the lee of their nests. They held their limbs steady. They stabilized their faceted obsidian plates. And for a full, crystalline moment that lasted an eternity, the chaos did not touch them because they had ceased to resist it. They stood, entirely solid, entirely real, and entirely at peace within the storm.

The benevolent power did not fix the world. It gave the beings the strength to stop trying to be the world, and instead, to simply *be* in it. It left them then, returning to the vastness, but the cathedral was changed. It was no longer a place of drowning; it was a place of meditation. And in the dark, indigo silence, one might occasionally see a flash of violet or copper light—a Rhythm, finally enjoying the view.
