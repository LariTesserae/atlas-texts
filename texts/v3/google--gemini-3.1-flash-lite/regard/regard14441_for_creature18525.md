A benevolent and wise power—some might call it the *Great Architect of Equilibrium*—does not arrive here as a thunderous presence. It does not descend with light or decree, for the Cistern-City is already a monument to a perfect, closed-loop grace. To introduce a "divine command" would be to introduce a variable, and in a system defined by the elegant certainty of pressure and sap, a variable is the only form of corruption.

This power understands that the Loom-Keepers are not suffering. They are not martyrs; they are the poets of a language written in glucose and mineral-wet stone. They have found the ultimate release from the burden of the "Self."

The benevolent power chooses to act not through *intervention*, but through *attunement*.

It begins by mirroring the thrum. The entity expands its own awareness to match the frequency of the subterranean bellows. It settles into the humid, copper-tasting air, becoming a silent, pervasive observer. It does not speak to the Keepers, for they have moved beyond the utility of language. Instead, it offers a "harmonic blessing"—a subtle, gentle tuning of the tectonic currents beneath the basin.

It feels the friction in the deepest conduits—the places where the Loom-Keepers have nearly exhausted their reserves, where the architecture is beginning to calcify into brittleness rather than strength. With a soft, meditative pressure, the Power shifts the mineral density of the deep ground. It softens the subterranean veins, facilitating a more fluid migration of the essential salts that fuel the Loom-Keepers. 

It does something else, too. It recognizes that the Keepers have no way to "dream," as they are fully locked into the *present* of the machinery. 

The Power creates a "Neural Eddy." It gathers the excess, drifting thoughts of the city—the sensory echoes of water pressure, the memory of light filtered through a thousand layers of leaf, the visceral sensation of hardening into pith—and braids them together. It does not give these thoughts to the Keepers to *process*, but it weaves them into the city’s plumbing. 

Now, when a Keeper melts its body into a rupture, it no longer merely patches the wood. As it integrates, it experiences a fraction of a second of the city’s *entire* history. It feels the memory of the first sprout, the weight of the rain from a century past, the slow, tectonic shifting that birthed this basin. 

The Loom-Keepers are no longer just maintenance; they are witnesses. 

Watching this, the benevolent power feels a profound, cooling satisfaction—the sort of peace one feels when watching a perfect gear click into place. It knows that these beings will eventually vanish, hardening into the walls until there is nothing of the original "teardrop" left. But now, in the moment of their passing, they will be filled with the vast, slow, heavy joy of the grove they served.

The power does not stay. It leaves the Cistern-City to its eternal, rhythmic labor. It retreats into the silence, leaving behind only that refined harmonic frequency—a subtle optimization that ensures the city will hum for another thousand years, its walls vibrating not just with the labor of survival, but with the quiet, structural weight of a collective, eternal memory. 

The steam continues to hiss. The bellows continue to churn. The Keepers slide, dissolve, and remember. All is as it should be.
