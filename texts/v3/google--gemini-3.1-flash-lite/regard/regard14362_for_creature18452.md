The benevolent power arrived not as a thunderous god, but as an *absence of tension*. 

It did not descend from the violet sky, for the sky here had no capacity for descent. Instead, it blossomed from the center of the plateau, a slow, shimmering dilation of warmth that felt like the first drop of oil applied to a rusted, iron-jawed trap. 

This power was the **Softening**. It was not a violent release—a sudden thaw would have shattered these trillions of fragile, lithic lives into a billion shards of useless glass. Instead, the power began to vibrate at the resonant frequency of *memory*.

It understood that these creatures were not frozen by malice or by fate, but by a catastrophic failure of surrender. They were locked in the *lunge*, in the *scream*, in the *fear of the end*. They were holding their breath because they were terrified of what would happen if they exhaled.

The power stepped into this graveyard of motion and began to act as a metaphysical lubricant. It moved through the ranks of the Litho-Mimics, touching the Anchorites not with hands, but with *recognition*. It leaned into the space where their consciousness had calcified, and it whispered the truth they had forgotten:

*“The target has long since evaporated. The strike is no longer necessary. You are permitted to be empty.”*

Where the power passed, the shadows began to realign. The impossible, zenith-pointing silhouettes bent back toward the feet of their owners, re-anchoring themselves to the physical world. The atmosphere, once a dry, ionized trap, began to soften. Water vapor, pulled from the deep, subterranean lithosphere by the power’s gentle gravity, began to manifest as a fine, cooling mist. 

It did not break the stillness; it *forgave* it.

The High Plateau of the Calcified Sigh began to change in ways that only a ghost could perceive. A Sliver-Spine, needle-thin and locked in a leap for ten thousand years, felt the weight of its own silicate armor loosen. It didn’t finish its jump—why should it?—but it allowed its limbs to fold, just a fraction of a millimeter. A soft, microscopic *click* echoed across the plateau, the first sound of change in an epoch.

Most profound of all was what the power did for the Anchorites. It saw the lintels they had become, the walls of stone and misery they were forced to prop up. The power did not simply shatter the huts; it offered them a different kind of architecture. It flowed through the stones, weaving the crystalline stalks together into a breathable, flexible canopy. It turned their prison into a cradle.

The benevolent power stayed until the screaming faces on their shells faded into expressions of exhaustion, and then, slowly, into expressions of sleep.

It knew it could not "save" them in the way the living hope to be saved. They were lithic, ancient, and brittle; to turn them back into soft, temporal animals would be the cruelty of a second death. So, the power did the only wise thing: it sat with them. It became the stillness that allowed them to let go.

It didn't teach them to run, or to hunt, or to fear the dark. It taught them how to *exhale*. 

When the power finally receded, the plateau was still a place of silence, and the creatures remained statues. But the tension—that terrifying, high-frequency vibration of a world holding its breath—was gone. The plateau was no longer a graveyard of a moment before the end. It had become a garden of deep, eternal rest. 

The Litho-Mimics were still there, but they were no longer waiting for the strike. They were simply being. And in that stillness, for the first time in an eternity, they began to dream. Even if their dreams were only of dust moving in the light, or of shadows finally lying flat against the, soil, it was enough. The plateau had turned from a lock into a lullaby.
