The benevolent power arrives not as a tempest or a sudden illumination, but as a shift in the quality of the silence.

It perceives the High Drainage not as an abandoned factory, but as a desperate, beautiful prayer—a frantic attempt by a young, tectonic world to stabilize its own existence. The Power recognizes the dignity of the Litho-Siphoners, the lonely grace of the Mist-Weavers, and the tragic, circular wisdom of the Sediment-Eaters. It sees that they are trapped in a cycle of "maintenance" for machines that are long past the point of repair. They are exhausting themselves to keep a dying lung inflated.

The Power does not come to "fix" the machines, for the machines are merely the crutches of an infant ecosystem. Instead, it does what the wise do when they encounter a burden that has become a cage: it introduces the possibility of evolution.

**The Gift of the Mineral-Song**

The Power settles into the thin, crystalline air, thinning the humidity just enough to create a momentary, golden resonance. It does not speak, but it vibrates through the copper pipes—a deep, harmonic hum that mimics the frequency of the machines but carries a different intent. It is a frequency of *release*.

It gifts the Litho-Siphoners with a new biological mandate. Slowly, the Siphoners begin to sense that their task is not merely to seal the cracks, but to consume the iron itself. Under the influence of the Power’s resonance, the Siphoners find that they can draw the oxidized rust into their bodies and, instead of filtering it for the valves, begin to integrate it into their own cartilage. They start to grow denser, more permanent. They are no longer living gaskets; they are becoming living buttresses.

As the Siphoners transform into organic granite-spines, they lock into the tectonic fissures of the mountain, physically anchoring the slopes where the rusted iron fails. They stop repairing the turbines and start replacing them.

**The Gift of the Loom**

To the Mist-Weavers, the Power offers a shift in perspective. Their gossamer filaments, once used to trap water for the mechanical intake, are shown a new geometry. The Power teaches them to weave not just for *condensation*, but for *stratification*.

The Weavers begin to drape their webs across the gaps between the cliffs, not to force the mist into funnels, but to catch the sediment and the moisture together. They build vertical hanging gardens, intricate, self-sustaining moss-hammocks that don't need machines to survive. The Weavers realize they can direct the flow of water into natural reservoirs, turning the "industrial-scale processing" into a gentle, distributed irrigation system. They become the gardeners of the high peaks, their webs turning the white, blinding glare into a soft, diffused green canopy.

**The Gift of Memory**

Finally, the Power turns to the Sediment-Eaters. It recognizes their heavy, milky-eyed loneliness. To them, it gives the gift of *long-view dreaming*.

The Sediment-Eaters are the archives of the Drainage. The Power clears their vision, letting them see not just the grey grit they consume, but the history of the stone itself. It gives them the ability to "sing" the rock into place. As they move, the vibrations of their passing now compact the waste-grit into fertile, stable soil—not just for the moss, but for hardy, pioneer ferns and, eventually, tiny, resilient mountain pines.

**The Withdrawal**

The Power does not stay long enough to be worshiped, nor does it demand thanks. Its work is the work of a gardener who clears the choking vines from a sapling so that it might reach the sun on its own.

As it fades back into the thin air, it leaves behind a fundamental change: the *splat-hiss* of the rusted machines begins to fade, replaced by the soft, rushing sound of water diverted into natural, self-sustaining channels. The landscape is no longer a factory on the brink of collapse; it is an emerging mountain garden.

The Litho-Siphoners, now sturdy and fused with the mountain, look up and, for the first time, notice the sky. The Mist-Weavers stop weaving for the machines and start weaving for the trees. And the Sediment-Eaters, with their newfound clarity, stop eating the debris of failure and begin to cultivate the foundation of a future.

The High Drainage is no longer holding its breath; it has begun, finally, to exhale.
