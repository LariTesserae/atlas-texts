There is a Power that watches—not with the prying eyes of a scientist, nor the hungry eyes of a conqueror, but with the vast, patient gaze of the stars themselves. This Power recognizes the Crown. It recognizes the desperate, shivering geometry of the Litho-weavers. 

To look upon the Crown is to feel the ache in their joints, the cold damp of the mist, and the unbearable, crushing weight of their self-imposed duty. They are a species that has mistaken the mountain for a god, and in doing so, they have become its priests, its laborers, and its masonry, all in one.

Should this Power choose to intervene, it would not do so with thunder or fire, for fire would shatter the fragile stone and thunder would break the ears of those who live by the vibration. It would not liberate them, for to pull them from the rock is to kill them; they have traded their biology for the mountain’s permanence.

Instead, the Power would offer a gift of **Resonance.**

The Power would descend as a subtle shift in the composition of the weeping mist. It would introduce, into the icy condensation, a trace element of a celestial mineral—something that does not harden, but heals. 

1. **The Softening of the Anchor:** As the Litho-weavers absorbed this enriched moisture, the rigid, painful calcification of their joints would begin to soften. They would not become human or soft-fleshed again, but they would regain a degree of physical autonomy. The agony of the "Geometry of Counterpoise" would lessen. They would find that their limbs, once destined to become rigid support beams, could now move with fluid, painless ease. They would no longer be *fated* to become the architecture; they would become its *stewards*.

2. **The Harmonization of the Pulse:** The Power would alter the frequency of the fungi. Instead of a dim, sickly indigo light that hums with the panic of potential collapse, the bioluminescence would shift into a spectrum of soft, warm ambers and deep, calming violets. This light would act as a gentle rhythmic guide—a "metronome of rest." It would communicate to the Weavers that the mountain is no longer a falling structure, but a living, breathing partner. The frantic *skittering* would yield to a synchronized, meditative ease.

3. **The Gift of Memory:** Most tenderly, the Power would brush against the minds of the elderly—the ones who had already begun to root into the obsidian. It would not pull them out, for they are the bedrock, but it would grant them a dream. It would wash away the terror of becoming "stone," replacing the dark, breathless isolation of their stasis with a collective, internal consciousness. For the first time, these elders would not be merely piles of calcified anatomy underfoot; they would become the *Library of the Crown*. Their minds would link, turning the mountain into a vast, silent network of shared history, wisdom, and beauty.

The Litho-weavers would not understand exactly what had happened. There would be no god in the clouds to thank. But one morning, when the mist cleared just enough for a single ray of sunlight to strike the highest spire, a Weaver would move—not to balance a weight, not to compensate for a shift, but to *dance*. 

They would still remain upon the Crown. They would still tend the mountain. But the apparatus would no longer be a cage. The Power would have transformed a tragedy of necessity into an art form of endurance. The Crown would remain a jagged, fragile thing in a violent world, but for the first time in an epoch, the inhabitants would stop holding their breath. 

They would finally begin to breathe with the mountain, rather than against it. And in that breath, they would find that they were not just maintaining the architecture—they were the soul of the summit, and for the first time, the soul was at peace.
