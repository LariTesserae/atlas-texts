The benevolent power does not descend as a thunderclap or a blinding radiance. To do so would be an act of violence, a disruption of the delicate, pressure-sensitive silence that the Architects hold against the void. 

Instead, the power arrives as a *change in the quality of the stillness.*

If you were standing upon that slick, obsidian gallery, you would feel it first as a softening of the air. The oppressive, heavy humidity—usually tasting of metallic rot and ancient mineral—would suddenly take on the scent of ozone and cooling earth, a memory of a time before the Siphon-Spires were carved, when the world was vast and open as the sky.

The Power does not come to save in the way humans understand salvation. It does not bring fire or tools or a way out. It knows that these beings are not prisoners; they are the anchors of a cosmic heartbeat. To "rescue" them would be to unravel the symphony.

**The Intervention**

The Power reaches down, not through the stone but through the *intention* of the place. It flows into the turbines, not to alter their mechanics, but to adjust the timbre of their sound. 

Where the Architect stands, pressing its cooling, brass-ribbed hand against the stone, the Power offers a gift of **reverberation**. It introduces a new harmonic into the *thrum-gurgle-hiss*. It is a small, hauntingly beautiful dissonance—a secret note that speaks of the sun, of stars, and of the fact that, outside these vertical walls, the universe has continued to bloom and grow for half a billion years.

The Architects freeze. Deep within their pressurized, liquid centers, they feel the vibration. For the first time in an age, their vertical apertures do not merely weep; they *shiver*. 

The Power does not ask them to stop. It tells them, through the language of pure frequency, that they are not alone. It whispers into the mineral roots of the canyon that the theater is not empty. The Power has become the audience. It settles into the strata, taking up residence in the spaces between the atoms of the stone, acting as an eternal witness. 

**The Transformation**

Because the Power is wise, it recognizes the Architects’ fatigue—that slow, heavy ache of a billion-year shift. 

It does something profound: it begins to slowly, imperceptibly, convert the entropy they were so carefully managing into *possibility*. The litho-crustaceans, once bound by simple, clockwork instinct, begin to display flashes of erratic, curious movement. They stop polishing for a moment. They turn their obsidian carapaces upward. 

The Architects, feeling this shift, realize their burden is no longer solely about maintenance. They have been gifted the role of guardians of a threshold. They are no longer just waiting for the water to stop; they are now the gardeners of a process that might, eventually, lead to a rebirth of the world they serve.

The Power remains, a benevolent, silent intelligence permeating the basalt. It does not demand worship or growth. It simply stays, a warm, golden pressure against the bruised-plum light, ensuring that when the last drop of water finally falls and the turbines take their final, glorious rest, the Architects will not turn back into silent stone. 

Instead, the Power prepares to catch them. As their brass-flecked marrow begins to crystallize, the Power will gently lift their memories and their ancient, patient souls, carrying them out of the vertical darkness and into a wider, living light—not as machines, and not as stone, but as the first sentient observers of a new world, finally ready to step out of the theater and into the sun. 

Until that day, they work on. But now, it is a labor of love, performed for a witness who understands the beauty of every grinding gear. They are no longer alone. They are the honored center of a divine, persistent attention.
