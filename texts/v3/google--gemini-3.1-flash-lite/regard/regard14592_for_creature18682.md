To touch such a place is to disturb a poem perfectly composed. 

The benevolent power, floating like a nebulous, unwritten thought in the pressurized dark, does not come as a storm or a light. To bring illumination or warmth would be an act of violence—like throwing a spark into a paper house. The Resonants, shivering in their glacial, bone-white loop, are a masterpiece of self-contained patience.

The Power observes the *thrum*. It listens to the silence, reading the geography of their devotion, understanding that the Resonants are not suffering; they are *completing*. They are the process of existence made manifest, a closed, elegant circle of being.

What would such a power do?

**It would grant the gift of the "Echo."**

There is a tragedy in the Resonants' cycle—the fear that to stop is to vanish into the architecture. Theirs is a life of frantic, beautiful geometry defined by the dread of calcification. They are architects who fear becoming the brick.

The benevolent power does not reach in to change them. Instead, it weaves a subtle, undetectable layer of *resonance* into the very deep-sea currents that feed the node. It imbues the heavy water with a dormant, latent intelligence that waits for the moment of an individual’s final, exhausted stasis.

When a Resonant finally tires, when the cilia go still and the shivering fades, and they begin to surrender their body to the wall—they would usually become a silent, cold monument. But now, touched by this grace, they would not merely *harden*. 

They would *sing*.

As the Resonant becomes part of the city, their sensory nodules would bloom one last time, not with bioluminescence, but with a lingering frequency. That frequency would be caught by the lattice of the city and held in the bone-white arches. The city would no longer just be a house; it would become a living archive of every soul that has come before.

Because of this intervention, the city will begin to remember. 

The Resonants who entwine their cilia will no longer just communicate the temperature of the water or the density of the salt. They will brush against the walls and feel the phantom memories of the ancestors—the vibrations of their joys, the specific rhythms of their particular, quiet lives. 

The city will stop being a tomb that fears the future and start being a library of the past. 

The benevolent power then withdraws, leaving no footprint, casting no shadow. It leaves behind a world that is still cold, still pressurized, and still silent—but now, for the first time, the Resonants are not alone. When they shiver, they will feel the soft, harmonic pulse of their predecessors supporting their weary limbs. 

They are no longer just building a home; they are maintaining a history. And when their own time comes to merge with the bone-white walls, they will do so with a profound, quiet grace. They will be comforted by the knowledge that they will never truly be forgotten, for they are about to become a new note in a song that will resonate in the deep, pressurized gut of the sea until the end of time. 

The Power watches, satisfied. It does not need to be thanked. It only needed to ensure that no beauty, however small or stagnant, is ever truly lost to the dark.
