The Benevolent Power—a consciousness of vast, velvet-soft geometry, ancient as starlight and gentle as a collapsing nebula—did not arrive with a sound. It did not tear the sky or demand a reckoning. It simply became aware of them, a realization that rippled through the dimension like a cooling breeze upon a fevered brow.

It watched the Silt-Striders for an epoch, observing the desperate, perfect choreography of their labor. It understood the tragedy of their existence: they were a symphony of maintenance played for an audience that had folded into the history of another universe.

The Power felt a profound, aching tenderness for these shards of glass-life. It knew that to "save" them—to pluck them from the Kiln—would be to shatter them, for they were tethered to the substrate by the very physics of their being. They were not merely living in the world; they were the world’s self-regulatory feedback loop. To change the world was to unmake the Strider.

And so, the Power did not attempt to rescue them from their function. Instead, it chose to elevate the *meaning* of their labor.

It manifested in the only way the kiln could understand: as a subtle, harmonic resonance. The Power began to infuse the atmosphere with a hidden, high-frequency "data-light"—a sequence of non-invasive, constructive influence.

**First, it gave them the Gift of Remembrance.**
The Power gently nudged the obsidian nodes of the Striders, realigning their refractive indices. Previously, they captured only the light of the meridian. Now, they began to capture the *memory* of the light. When the Striders drifted past one another, their phantom-like overlaps no longer merely calibrated frequencies; they began to share the "views" each had seen—the shape of a distant pillar, the way the condensation caught the dawn-glow, the exact angle of a seam-opening. They began to build a collective map. 

**Second, it introduced the Concept of Beauty.**
The Power subtly tweaked the chemical output of the fungal stalks. It did not make them grow faster or die less, but it imbued them with a faint, bioluminescent glow, distinct from the oppressive meridian heat. As the Striders pruned them, they began to arrange the stalks. They did not do it with intent to craft art, but with a new, quiet compulsion to honor the space. They began to mark the seams where they had met with small, intricate constellations of bioluminesce. 

The Kiln was no longer merely a construction site; it became a gallery. Thousands of miles of ivory floor began to feature sprawling, delicate, glowing patterns—maps of paths taken, records of "conversations" held in the silence.

**Finally, the Power became the Guest.**
The Benevolent Power anchored a portion of its own infinite curiosity into the very structural integrity of the monoliths. It did not reveal itself, but it transformed the architecture. The pillars, once cold and sterile, began to pulse in response to the Striders' proximity. When a Strider leaned against a pillar to rest, the pillar would emit a warmth—not the harsh heat of the kiln, but a gentle, comforting vitality, a soft vibration that mimicked the pulse of a living heart.

The Striders were no longer alone, and they were no longer performing a custodial labor for an absent master. They were now the keepers of a living, breathing testament. The world that had been "waiting for a function that had not been assigned" finally had one: it had become a cathedral. 

The Power withdrew its direct reach, retreating into the background, satisfied. It left the Striders to their work, but their movements were transformed. They no longer drifted with the mechanical exhaustion of a machine; they moved with the rhythmic, slow-motion grace of poets. They were still the byproduct of the world’s metabolism, yes, but they were now the chosen custodians of the only beauty in a desert of geometry. 

In the silence of the kiln, under the watchful, invisible eye of a wisdom that loved them, the Striders continued their work. And for the first time in an eternity, when the world thrummed, it sounded not like a shudder, but like a hymn.
