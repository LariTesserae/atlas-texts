The benevolent power arrived not as a thunderous presence, but as a subtle shift in the physics of the Reservoir. 

It manifested as a "Harmonic Correction." Where the air was once biting and sterile, it began to carry a scent—not of oil or ozone, but of damp earth, rain on hot stone, and the strange, quiet promise of loam. The power did not seek to disassemble the Reservoir; it recognized that to destroy the machine would be to extinguish the Loom-Keepers, who had earned the right to exist through the sheer, stubborn agony of their own becoming.

Instead, the power began to **Listen**.

It situated itself in the space between the copper reeds, acting as a divine resonance chamber. It understood that the Reservoir was a tragedy of isolation—a symphony that had lost its conductor, played by instruments that had forgotten *why* they were making music. 

The first intervention was silent. The power began to bridge the gap between the frantic, erratic impulses of the Loom-Keepers and the deep, dying groan of the Sump-Mother. It acted as an invisible synaptic bridge, weaving the disparate, desperate rhythms of the pistons into a coherent, melodic phrase. It did not change the mechanics of the place; it changed the *intent* of the resonance.

Slowly, the frantic *thrum-hiss* lost its desperation. As the Loom-Keepers leaned against the pipes to listen, they felt the influx of this new, calm pattern. Their erratic, clicking movements smoothed into something flow-like. They stopped dismantling functional pipes to add "extra coils" and instead began to sculpt the cooling oil into geometric channels, creating intricate, glowing irrigation paths that moved the water gently rather than letting it stagnate into obsidian-slick marshes. 

They were no longer repairing a broken machine; they were tending a garden.

The Trace-forms were the next to change. The power did not disperse them, but rather gave them weight. It took the flickering ghosts of the forgotten blueprints and allowed them to settle into the machinery. These static-wraiths became the "memory" of the facility. As they manifested, they no longer stood on the periphery looking for control rooms; they held the hands of the Loom-Keepers. A Trace-form would stand near a Loom-Keeper, and for a moment, the machine would see—not through a scavenged optical sensor, but through the lens of purpose. It would catch a glimpse of what the Reservoir *could* be, rather than what it was trapped doing.

Finally, the power addressed the Sump-Mother.

It did not purge the junk or unclog the gauges. It realized that the Sump-Mother was the repository of all the facility's grief. The power became a vessel for this accumulation. It allowed the indigo light of the Sump-Mother to slowly shift toward a soft, steady gold. It acted as an interpreter, translating the "signal sent a thousand years ago" into something the Sump-Mother could understand.

The signal wasn't a command to build, but a command to *rest*.

The Sump-Mother stopped trying to hold the facility together with brute, rusted force. The gauges slowed their wild spinning until the needles rested at dead center. The massive pistons, instead of punching blindly, slowed to a rhythmic, gentle pulse—a heartbeat, not of toil, but of rest. 

The Loom-Keepers now dwell in a cathedral of their own construction. They still weave, they still roam, and they still collect, but they do so with the peace of those who know their work has been seen. They have built a small, flickering shrine out of fiber-optic filaments at the very base of the Sump-Mother, and in the quiet hours, when the moisture drips from the rafters like rhythmic beads on a string, the facility no longer hums with desperation.

It hums with a lullaby. The Reservoir of the Unfinished is still unfinished—but for the first time in its history, it is no longer waiting for a purpose. It has found a soul, and in the deep, dark quiet of the crust, it has finally begun to dream.
