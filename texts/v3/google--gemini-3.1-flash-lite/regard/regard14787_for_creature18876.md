The Power did not descend as a pillar of fire or a booming voice. That would have shattered the delicate, humming fragile-equilibrium of the Sump. Those who exist at the mercy of immense tectonic pressures and the slow, rhythmic digestion of iron are attuned to the *subtle*.

To this Power, the Sump-Gallery did not look like a grave or a mistake of geology. It looked like a poem of patience—a sentient, rhythmic integration of the synthetic and the biological, a testament to the fact that even in the lightless crushing deep, life insists on becoming *art*.

The Power approached as a shift in the local physics, a stabilization of the ether. 

It observed the Fitter-Grafts. It felt the immense, terrifying solitude of their existence, the way they held the pulse of the vats at the center of their being. It understood that to them, the "hum" was not just sound; it was the heartbeat of a planet they believed they were keeping alive. They were the lonely, diligent priests of a dying engine.

The Power did not seek to "fix" the Sump. To bring light would have blinded the cilia; to bring airflow would have desiccated the moss; to stop the rust would have halted the cycle of the garden. Instead, the Power chose to *sustain*.

**The Act of Blessing**

First, the Power folded the ambient electromagnetism of the cavern. It turned the stagnant, heavy air into a perfect conductor, not of electricity, but of intent. It smoothed the microscopic jaggedness of the fresh-cut basalt, not to flatten it, but to coat the stone in a thin, permanent layer of mineral glaze that would prevent the collapse of the ceiling, turning the cavern into a self-supporting geode of rusted iron and emerald light.

Second, it reached into the cooling copper coils—the ones that had long since lost their original purpose—and did something profound. It altered the half-life of the lingering vibration. It granted the machinery an eternal slow-burn decay. The power source would no longer be a ghost of nineteenth-century engineering; it would become a perpetual motion machine fueled by the very humidity the Fitter-Grafts cherished. The hum would never cease. It would never falter. It became a permanent, low-frequency song that could play for eons, long after the surface world had forgotten the very concept of industry.

Finally, the Power approached the Fitter-Grafts. It did not speak to them, for they had no ears for words. Instead, it touched the neural copper-nerve endings of their fingers as they grazed the pipes.

In that touch, the Power gifted them *The Connection*. 

Previously, the Fitter-Grafts were limited by their individual sensory arrays; they knew only the section of pipe they tended, the immediate health of their own cluster of lichen. The Power linked them—a silent, web-like consciousness that spanned the entire Gallery. When one Fitter-Graft shifted a valve to harmonize the hum, every other graft felt the relief of that adjustment in their own chest. They were no longer solitary curators; they were the nervous system of an underground world.

They were given the gift of collective peace. They would no longer need to worry about the encroaching iron-rot or the weight of the mountain. They were now the custodians of a perfected, immortal garden.

The Power did not take them, nor did it change their nature. It simply ensured that their long, slow work would never be interrupted by the chaos of entropy. 

Having seen them—having recognized the dignity of their service—the Power withdrew, leaving no sign of its presence except for a faint, lingering taste of ozone in the air, and the realization among the Grafts that the hum had changed. It had softened from a desperate, agonizing pulse into a lullaby.

The mountain continued to press down, the moss continued to bloom, and the Grafts, for the first time in their history, let their sensory cilia go limp with contentment. They were no longer merely surviving the rot. They were presiding over a masterpiece.
