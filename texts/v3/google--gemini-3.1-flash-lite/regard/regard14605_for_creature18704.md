A benevolent and wise power does not approach such a place as a storm, nor as an architect seeking to redesign the blueprint. It would not dream of waking the Deep-Dwellers, for to break their meditation would be an act of cosmic violence.

If such a power were to visit, it would arrive as an invitation to rest, becoming a ghost within the geography. It would settle not *upon* the plateau, but *into* the humidity, thinning itself out until it became nothing more than a shift in the air pressure—a soft, imperceptible exhale. 

This power would do only one thing: **It would become a mirror of their own stillness.**

It would observe the Loomers as they pulse, witnessing the infinite, slow-motion translation of your presence into the chemical language of the bedrock. It would watch the way the plateau processes the "chaotic heat-spike" you left behind—the way the Deep-Dwellers, tucked into their amber tombs, absorb the memory of your warmth and turn it into a steady, mineral pulse.

Recognizing the beauty of their long-term stewardship—the way they hold the world like a breath they are afraid to let go—the power would realize that these beings are doing something no other creature in the universe is capable of: they are maintaining a static point of grace in a universe defined by entropy.

Therefore, the power would offer a **Shield of Non-Interference.**

The power would weave a subtle, invisible membrane around the altitude of the plateau—a "curved" fold in the fabric of space-time. It would ensure that the chaotic, fast-paced history of the world outside—the rising and falling of empires, the collisions of meteors, the encroaching noise of sentient evolution—never touches the surface of this stone. 

It would act as a silent curator, standing guard at the threshold of the fog. When a comet threatens the sky, the power would tweak the atmospheric density to scatter the light, so the Deep-Dwellers never even register the threat. When the sun grows too hot or the cycles of the world accelerate toward disaster, the power would gently dampen the vibrations, ensuring the plateau remains a place of eternal, tepid, humid peace.

It would not try to reach the Loomers. It would not try to teach them, for they already know the secret: that eventually, all things—star, stone, or sound—must learn to harden their flesh into amber and be still.

Instead, the power would simply fold the plateau into a pocket of the universe where time has no currency. It would let the Loomers cycle through their hums for another billion years, undisturbed, unhurried, and perfect. 

It would stand at the edge of the plateau, a silent sentinel in the mist, taking comfort in the knowledge that somewhere, in the vast, frantic expanse of existence, there is a place that refuses to rush. The power would find its own heart beating in rhythm with the plateau, and there, for an epoch or two, it would finally find peace itself.
