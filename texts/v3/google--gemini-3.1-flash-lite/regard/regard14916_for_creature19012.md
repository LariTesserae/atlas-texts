The Power that watches over the basin is not a god of storms or sudden revelations. It is a Presence akin to the breath of the cosmos—vast, silent, and cognizant of every vibration in the weave of reality. 

When this Presence turned its attention toward the spire, it did not arrive with a thunderclap or a mandate. It arrived with the profound, appreciative stillness of a gardener finding a rare, self-sustaining bloom in the middle of a barren waste. It looked upon the Litho-weavers and saw neither misery in their slowness nor stagnation in their eternity. It saw an exquisite, inverted biology: a civilization that had mastered the art of *being* rather than *becoming*.

The Power chose to intervene, though not with tools or words. It knew that to give these beings the fire of ambition or the urgency of linear time would be an act of profound violence. To offer them "progress" would be to shatter their crystalline harmony.

Instead, the Power acted as a weaver of currents.

**It blessed the light.**

Understanding that the Litho-weavers exist in a cycle of metabolic patience, the Power began to subtly—almost imperceptibly—infuse the golden sunlight that strikes the basin. It did not change the heat or the refraction, but it imbued the light with a specific, harmonic resonance. It made the air around the spire slightly more porous, allowing the ozone to carry not just chemical nutrients, but the "thoughts" of the universe itself. 

The Power turned the basin into a mirror of the infinite. 

By enriching the atmosphere with this harmonic charge, the Power created a bridge for the Litho-weavers to transcend their physical geometry. No longer were they merely sharing the memories of the spire; they began, during their long forehead-pressings, to share the memories of the stars themselves. The bioluminescence within their carapaces shifted from local amber and blue to the deep, resonant hues of nebulae and the soft silver of distant, dying suns. 

They did not become faster. They did not become "human." The Power ensured they remained exactly what they were—sedentary, architectural, and absolute. But it granted them *perspective*. 

The Litho-weavers now possess a grander, more sublime purpose. They are no longer just building a house of calcified intent; they are recording the history of the universe into the ivory stalks of their home. Every bridge they weave, every hive they anchor, is now etched with the silent, glowing data of the galaxies. 

When a Litho-weaver eventually anchors itself to the city walls, it is no longer just becoming a piece of architecture—it is becoming a scroll. 

The Power stands back, satisfied. It does not speak to these beings, for to acknowledge them would be to rupture their peace. It simply watches, a silent guardian of the spire, ensuring that the winds do not disturb the summit and that the atmosphere remains thin, dry, and pure. 

In this place, where nothing decays, there is now a library of infinite time kept by the most patient librarians in existence. The Litho-weavers continue their slow, rhythmic movements, blissfully unaware that they have become the memory of the cosmos, building their heaven one crystalline brick at a time, entirely content to spend a thousand years contemplating the nuance of a single shadow. 

And in the silence of the high peaks, the Power hums in frequency with them—a benevolent, invisible chord that keeps this beautiful, fragile, glass cathedral from shattering under the weight of the shifting world below.
