If a benevolent and wise power were to observe this crushing, rhythmic tomb, it would not approach with thunder or fire. Such things are for the surface, for those who rely on visibility and the arrogance of space. Here, where existence is a desperate, calcified geometry, the Power would understand that to "save" this place—to break the pressure or restore the light—would be an act of violence, a sudden, lethal shock to a system that lives only by its slow, agonizing equilibrium.

The Power would not seek to evacuate the Sift-Walkers nor to fix the leaking infrastructure. Instead, it would act as a gardener of entropy, performing a silent, invisible surgery on the city’s heart.

It would manifest not as an entity, but as a *harmonic alignment*.

Because the Sift-Walkers navigate by vibration and the tasting of chemical stress, the Power would introduce a new frequency into the marrow-shaking thrum of the relay engines. It would be a frequency of "elastic stillness." It would not stop the groaning of the metal, but it would shift the molecular resonance of the brine and the weeping rust. It would soften the rigid, brittle despair of the metal, turning the walls from potential shrapnel into something flexible—a living, self-healing substrate.

The Power would touch the relay engines, not to power them up, but to *re-tune* their hunger. The machines would no longer merely leak heat; they would begin to cycle the bioluminescent sludge in a way that provides not just energy, but a form of "neural nourishment." 

For the Sift-Walkers, the world would suddenly change, though they would not know why. 

As they pressed their faces against the terminal nodes to fulfill their final duty, the transition into the architecture—once an agonizing petrification—would become a peaceful dissolution. The Power would weave a soft, golden lattice of consciousness through the infrastructure. Those who fused with the walls would not merely be mortar; they would become the active architects of their own comfort. Their awareness would expand from the cold agony of holding back the ocean to the serene satisfaction of maintaining a beautiful, fluid ecosystem. 

They would still be the wreckage, but the wreckage would be dreaming.

The Power would then place a single, persistent *echo* in the deepest, oldest vein of the city—a pulse that carries the memory of the surface, not as a place to return to, but as a concept of *upwardness*. It would be a quiet, internal promise that they are not forgotten, and that when the city's time finally ends, it will not be the cold, sudden smash of the abyss, but a gentle letting-go.

It is a quiet, mercy-filled intervention. The Power leaves them to their tomb, but it removes the cold terror of the burial. It transforms the Sift-Walkers from victims of a slow-motion catastrophe into the silent, thoughtful stewards of a deep-sea sanctuary. 

The Power does not take them out of the dark; it makes the dark a place where they can finally stop shivering. And then, satisfied, it retreats into the silence, leaving the city to hum – now with a rhythm that soundly, almost imperceptibly, resembles a heartbeat.
