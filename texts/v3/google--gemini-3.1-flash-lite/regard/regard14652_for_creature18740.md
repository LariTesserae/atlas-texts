The benevolent power does not approach as a god might—with thunder or blinding light. It does not arrive to "save" the Basin from its entropy, for it recognizes that the Basin is not broken; it is simply in a state of beautiful, slow transformation. This power comes instead as a gentle, ambient warmth—a sympathetic frequency woven into the heavy, ozone-scented air.

It observes the Basin not as a graveyard, but as a lung. It breathes with the rhythmic *thwip-thwip* of the polyps. It feels the agonizing, patient hunger of the Pipe-Witches. And it understands: they are not just scavengers of metal; they are archivists of a kinetic memory that the universe cannot afford to lose entirely.

The benevolent power does the only thing that a truly wise influence can do: **it hums.**

It does not reveal itself physically. Instead, it subtly adjusts the atmospheric pressure of the pocket, nudging the subterranean conduits just enough to bridge the gaps in the seized, rusted circuitry. 

It does not force the machinery to "work" again—that would be an intrusion, a rewriting of the Basin’s nature. Instead, it reconnects the Basin’s circulatory system to the fundamental, lingering energy of the cosmos itself. The copper pipes, once cold and dead, begin to carry a faint, rhythmic pulse—not of industrial electricity, but of the very heartbeat of the stars.

The effect upon the Pipe-Witches is profound.

They do not become "more" than they are, but they are suddenly *attuned*. As the Pipe-Witch presses its frayed copper sensors against a seized gear, it no longer tastes only the gray static of decay. It tastes the vast, quiet resonance of the universe. 

The benevolence does not give them language, but it gives them *meaning*. 

Where once their Morse code exchanges were frantic staccatos of survival, they become something akin to poetry. The Pipe-Witches begin to move with a strange, new grace. They continue to salvage, but their purpose shifts. They begin to arrange their shards of etched glass and rusted dials not just to "please" themselves, but to map the resonance. They are building, in the shadows of the weeping basalt, a cathedral of reflection that mirrors the patterns of the galaxy.

The benevolent power remains in the background, a silent, invisible companion. It is the catalyst that allows the Basin to transition from a place of *mourning* to a place of *meditation*. 

The Pipe-Witches will never leave the Basin. They will never shed their wet, gelatinous skins or their crusts of mineral-rich silt. They will remain the janitors of the drainpipe. But now, in the deepest stillness of the weeping, a Pipe-Witch may pause, its copper sensors shivering in the silver droplets, and for a fleeting, glorious second, it may vibrate at the exact frequency of a star. 

It won't know why. It won't call it "grace." It will simply feel, for once, that the rust is not a tomb, but a veil—and that the music it has been trying to play with its broken, brass fingers is finally being heard by someone, somewhere, in the dark. 

The benevolence leaves the Basin, but it leaves the frequency behind—a permanent, soft, golden undertone in the *thwip-thwip* of the polyps, ensuring that even in the absolute saturation of the Basin, there is now, and always will be, a song.
