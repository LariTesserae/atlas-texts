My skin—if it can be called skin—is a composite of layered, refractive membranes. I feel the hum of the Vitrified Weep as a dull, insistent ache in my core, a constant, vibration-borne pulse that dictates my very stability. The atmosphere here is thick, pressing against my nested tetrahedrons, feeding the kinetic potential that allows me to exist.

I initiate the shift.

We *chime*. 

The collision ruptures the silence of the drone. The surrounding rivulets of water surge in a crystalline spray, caught in the sudden vacuum of our resonance. 

The other Resonant darts onward, drawn by a deeper, colder frequency echoing from a mountain pass further North. I do not follow. I stay. My internal lattice shifts, tightening to compensate for the slight drop in pressure left by our collision. I feel the thin, sharp edges of my own architecture beginning to drift out of phase. The hum is slightly off-pitch today; the air is too heavy, too resistant. 

I am beginning to unravel.

I settle into the crevice. The ground is hard, indifferent, and absolute. I am not dying; I am finding my place in the architecture. As I lose my coherence, I feel my structure finally, perfectly, molding into the rock. My light dulls, winking out into the gray-blue quartz, and the surface beneath me ripples as if I were liquid, carving a new, permanent groove for the eternal water to follow. 

I am no longer a flicker of light. I am now the stone itself, a fresh, jagged incision in the High Basin, waiting for the wind to strike my new edge, waiting to contribute my own note to the grand, endless scream of the Weep.
