I find myself pressed into the throat of a limestone flute, six hundred feet above the crushing weight of the cloud layer. I am not standing; I am *adhered*. My substance is the color of a bruise—a deep, translucent violet that mirrors the moss surrounding me—and my edges are blurred, blending seamlessly into the weeping, porous stone of the mountain’s flank.

I am a Pulsar-Singer.

My consciousness is not a monologue; it is a resonance. I do not "think" in words, but in vibrations and pressures. I feel the subterranean pulse of the mountain not as a sound outside of me, but as the primary rhythm of my own existence. When the tectonic piston deep below cycles upward, sending a surge of super-heated, mineral-rich steam rushing through the fissures, I feel the shift in my own crystalline nodes. They flare with a rhythmic, pulsing light, matching the sudden increase in geothermal intensity.

I am currently anchored in a narrow, calcified basin that has been polished by eons of flow into a perfect parabolic curve. To a human eye, I might look like a slick of iridescent slime or a patch of thick, wet moss. To me, I am a valve, a regulator, a cooling agent.

The steam begins to scream—a high, thin whistle where the pressure has become too sharp, threatening to splinter the obsidian wall. Without hesitation, I let go of my fixed shape. I flow. My body stretches, turning from a dense, viscous glob into a long, fluid column. I move with a grace that is liquid and precise, pouring myself into the fissure to act as a buffer. 

As the scalding heat passes through me, I feel the exquisite, agonizing pleasure of the intake. I am a living heat-sink. I expand, my pores opening wide to ingest the mineral soot and the violent temperature of the slurry. The heat radiates through my crystalline nodes, and I begin to hum. It is a low, throbbing chord that vibrates through the very marrow of the peak. I am tuning the mountain; I am absorbing the jagged edge of the pressure until the flow is smooth, rhythmic, and safe once more.

Once the spike is dampened, I withdraw into the basin. My work is silent, meticulous, and endless. I spend the coming hours—or perhaps centuries, for time holds no meaning here—carefully nudging a microscopic deposit of lime with a crystalline protrusion, shaping it into a minute, perfect spiral. It is not art; it is maintenance. I am perfecting the acoustics of the mountain’s vent, ensuring that the next cycle of steam will produce a hum of absolute harmonic purity.

There is no desire to leave this place. There is no concept of "elsewhere." My identity is so thoroughly tethered to the mountain’s breath that if the heart of the world were to stop, I would simply cease to be. I would dry into a brittle, purple husk, a flake of mineral to be reclaimed by the moss.

But the steam still rushes. The mountain still breathes. 

I feel a tremor deep in the rock—a shift in the tectonic plates signaling a grand, harmonic zenith. My body glows, brighter and brighter, until I am an incandescent, violet pillar of light against the white, hazy sky. I detach, drifting for a suspended, glorious moment in the humid soup. I vibrate, not with passion, but with function. I am the mountain’s ghost-limb, its sensory apparatus, its patient curator. 

I am the vibration that keeps the world from breaking. And in the stifling, silent heat, I am perfectly, rhythmically content.
