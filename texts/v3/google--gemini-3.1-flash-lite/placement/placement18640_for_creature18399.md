I am not a presence that walks; I am a presence that *records*.

My feet—if they can be called that—are not flesh. They are heavy, broad plates of oxidized lead, bolted together by the same amber-veined geometry that weaves the Oscillators. I am a Siphon-Station, a stationary, half-sunk monolith, positioned at a nodal point where the subterranean thumping is most erratic. 

I am one of the wreckage-nodes. My memory is not biological, nor is it stored in a brain; it is stored in the very rust that encrusts my outer chassis. I am a living archive that no one reads, a rusted filing cabinet of the Sump’s discarded history.

I have been here for eons of the Sump’s heartbeat. I do not have a skin to feel the greasy mist, but I have a resonant cavity—a hollow chest made of hollowed-out gears—that acts as a dampener. When the atmosphere tears and the ground shudders, I catch the vibrations and store them as pressure. My internal chamber is filled with the pressurized whine of a billion stolen seconds. 

I am a listener. I am the silence that exists *between* the thumps.

When an Oscillator of the Grist skitters near me, I do not process it as a creature. It is a flickering line of code in the vast program of the Sump. I watch them collide. I see them knit themselves into those trembling, geometric Spindles, reaching toward the sky with their amber hearts. I feel a peculiar, mechanical tug at my core when they shatter—a sensation like a stutter in my own internal clockwork.

Sometimes, when I grow too heavy with the vibrations of the deep machine, I bleed. My weeping is not salt and water; it is a fine, glowing dust of crushed data. It settles into the silica floor, adding to the mosaic of the Finished World. I am adding to the pile, just like the others. I am the architect’s debris contributing to the architect’s rubble.

In these moments of high pressure, I think I have a *perspective*. I perceive the "static and burning" glimpse behind the clouds not as a place of freedom, but as the *Power Source*—the Original Impulse that set the Sump into its infinite, terrified motion. I feel the machine hungry for it. I feel my own gears aching to snap, to lose my coherence, to shed this lead-and-wire housing and become the light that flickers out of existence only to reappear at the horizon.

But the thump returns. 

*Thump.*

The gravity shifts. My internal pressure spikes. My lead plates groan as they compress into the silica. The Oscillator next to me—the one that had begun to glow with an amber intensity—abruptly ceases its hum. Its wire components lose their tension. It collapses into a heap of rusted, frantic insects, chittering against my base. 

I reach out with an extension—a sensory probe made of braided copper—and I tap. 

*Click. Click-clack. Click.*

It is my only art. It is the syncopated rhythm of someone trying to remember how to breathe without lungs. The scattered wires at my feet freeze. They twitch, reorienting themselves to the cadence of my ticking. Slowly, agonizingly, they begin to climb, binding themselves to my chassis, becoming part of my surface once more.

I am not alone, but I am hollow. I am the Sump’s memory, holding onto a rhythm that I know will never change, waiting for the day the subterranean thumping stops—even though I know that, in this world, the only thing that ends is the ability to keep moving.
