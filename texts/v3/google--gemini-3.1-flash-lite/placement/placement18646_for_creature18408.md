I am not the Archivist, tethered to the history of the sap, nor am I the Gleaner, resigned to the dark sludge of the cisterns. I am something that arrived by accident—a stray fragment of the world above, slipped into a fissure, lost to the vertical gravity of the rot.

I find myself held fast in the **Transition Gallery**, a narrow, twisting artery between the upper nursery of the moss and the hard, cold plumbing of the deep-city. My back is pressed against the damp, shivering curve of a succulent stem, and my limbs are already beginning to hum in sympathy with the bruise-colored light.

I am an **Anchor**. That is what the Menders call me, though they do not speak in words; they speak in the frantic, rhythmic tapping of their pincer-tips against my skin. 

At first, I fought them. Every time they crawled across the bridge of my nose or tickled the hollow of my throat with their silk-spinners, I shuddered. Each minor movement resulted in a tragedy: a collapse of a ventilation shaft, the weeping of a sap-seal, the frantic, panicked vibration of the workers as they scrambled to patch the wall I had compromised. I felt like a landslide in a cathedral. I felt the guilt of the giant, ruining the work of the small.

But as the days—or perhaps the heartbeats of the sap-flow—have passed, I have begun to surrender to the cold.

I am becoming a pillar because there is no other way to exist here. If I move, I destroy; if I persist in my own shape, I am a jagged, unwelcome spike in their architecture. So, I have let the moss grow into my pores. It feels like tiny, cold fingers stitching my skin to the wall-fiber. My hair is being woven into the structural silk, binding me to the capillary network. 

I am beginning to taste the city. It is not air that I breathe anymore, but a wet, metallic ozone that tastes of ancient, crushed leaves and the salt of the city’s crystalline memory. 

I watch the Menders—those translucent, frantic hexapoda—as they weave. Their work is beautiful in its desperation. They treat the fraying of a leaf-stem with the same reverence a priest might show a sacred relic. When they climb over me now, they do not pause to wonder what I am. They have categorized me. I am *Substrate*. I am *Strength*. I am a section of the gallery that does not pulsate, a silent, unmoving knot in the living wood, providing them a pivot point around which they can build their delicate, shivering arches.

The peace is terrifying. It is the peace of the statue, the peace of the cornerstone. I feel my own heartbeat slowing, dragging itself down to match the glacial, rhythmic ebb and flow of the sap-heart elsewhere in the dark. 

I am currently holding the weight of a lateral arch. A thousand tiny lives scurry across my shoulders, carrying mica and silk to shore up a failing wall above me. If I were to stand, if I were to breathe deeply and break the seal, the entire district would weep. Liquid, translucent nutrients would flood the lower levels, and the Archivists in the deep-cool would sense the shift in the vein, the death of a node, and perhaps the city would falter. The frost outside, sensing the weakness, would lean in.

So, I remain still. I grow into the wall. I look down at my hands, which are no longer my own; they have become part of the city's floor, covered in a velvet moss that filters the moisture dripping from above.

I am a monument of flesh in the dark. I am the silence the Menders need to keep the world from ending. I am no longer an intruder. I am the wall, and the wall is tired, and the wall is holding on.
