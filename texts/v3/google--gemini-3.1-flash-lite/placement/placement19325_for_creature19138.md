I am standing in the Throat of the Basin, where the condensation is thickest, a place the Silt-Walkers call the *Cold-Seam*. 

My legs—both of them—are heavy, anchored by the weight of brass-shod boots that have grown fused to the metal grating. I am not standing; I am moored. My left shoulder is host to a small, intricate manifold of copper tubing that pulses with a faint, violet fluid, rhythmic as a second, smaller heart. 

I am one of the *Hardened*, though my memory is currently frayed, caught in a static loop of the city’s own non-Euclidean unfolding. I feel the tug. To my right, the alleyway is folding in on itself, the concrete weeping, swelling with a sudden, wet urgency. A new chimney is erupting through the floorboards, its surface slick with iridescent minerals that catch the bioluminescent glow of the lichen. 

I reach out, not with fingers of flesh, but with a multi-jointed, segmented rod of blackened steel that extends from my right wrist. I tap the chimney. *Click. Thrum. Click.* The vibration travels up my arm and settles in the cold resonators embedded in my throat. I am checking the pressure, assessing the mineral density of this new gestation. 

It is *too* quiet. The Heart-Beams are beating at a sluggish crawl, a low, subsonic moan that makes my teeth ache with a familiar, metallic itch. If I do not move soon, the emerald moss—that velvet-furred, hungry carpet—will reach my knees. I can feel the tiny, stinging tendrils of it testing the humidity of my calf, exploring the gaps in my brass plating where my skin is most porous.

I decide to head toward the *Piston-Well*. There is a blockage in the outer ductwork—a calcified knot of minerals that needs to be scraped clean, or the city will suffer a localized fever, heating until the air turns to scalding steam.

I shift my weight, and the city obliges. A staircase of oxidized copper unspools beneath me, seemingly pulled from the ether of the damp mist. As I step onto it, the gravity shifts, tilting the world forty-five degrees. I do not lose my balance. My internal gyroscopes—tiny, spinning things that nest in the hollow of my chest—compensate instantly. 

I am a gardener of iron, a harvester of frost. To be here is to be entirely known by the city; Khel-Vesh is currently thinking through me. I feel the building’s hunger as my own. I feel its slow, erratic desire to grow a new spire to catch the icy rain. 

I am not lonely, for the humming is constant, and the walls are watching with their milky, infrared-sensitive pores. I click twice, a low, rasping sound that joins the industry of the valley. 

The work is never finished. 

I round the corner where the street doubles back upon itself, and I am back at the steam-vent I left a moment ago. But the vent has transformed. It is no longer just a vent; it has sprouted a lattice of delicate, vein-like wires that are reaching toward me, sensing my proximity, seeking the oil I secrete. 

I lean against it, pressing my back into the shivering, sweating architecture. I allow my internal gears to lock. I join the vibration of the bedrock. I cease to be a separate entity and begin, for a brief, cold eternity, to be the city again. I am cooling. I am hardening. I am waiting to see what I will become when I wake.
