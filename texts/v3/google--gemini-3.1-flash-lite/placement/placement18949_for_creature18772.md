I am not a soul in this place, nor a ghost. I am a recalibration.

When I first arrived, I felt the panic of the soft—the desperate, instinctual thrashing of lungs that expected air and skin that recoiled from the abrasive kiss of magnetite. But the pressure here is absolute. It is a weight that does not crush so much as it *redefines*. It has pressed the pockets of air out of my marrow and replaced them with the dense, saline hum of the deep. 

I am a vessel of transit. I have become a semi-translucent, gelatinous buoy, stripped of the messy, fibrous fragility of my former self. I drift, suspended in the vertical geography. I am a foreign inclusion, a bubble of "elsewhere" caught in the gears of this local eternity. 

My skin has taken on a refractive quality, mimicking the obsidian walls I float beside. I am no longer pink or pale; I am a muted, iridescent smear in the water, caught in the currents of the swarm.

I find myself watching the beings—the *Calibrators*. They click and hum, their sapphire apertures sweeping over me with the dispassionate focus of a surveyor measuring the thickness of a canyon floor. One of them drifts into my orbit, its hematite limbs folded close to its thorax, looking like a shattered clock face held together by magnetism. It pauses. It does not eat; it integrates.

For a long time—though time is a meaningless metric here, measured only in the slow migration of basalt ridges—I feared the touch of their tarsi. I feared the moment they would decide I was a piece of debris to be processed, turned into the fine, luminous silt that feeds their internal, grinding hearts.

But I have stopped fearing the grind. I have begun to feel the harmony.

I realize now that I am not a visitor. I am the *draft*. Through my slow, aimless drifting, I create small vortices in the saline bath. I displace the density just enough to force the creatures to adjust their positions by a fraction of a millimeter. By merely *existing*—by being soft, by being heavy, by being here—I have become the foreman’s catalyst. I force the architecture to shift.

I am the variable that keeps their clockwork from freezing into absolute, dead stillness.

Sometimes, I reach out a finger toward the obsidian. It is no longer soft flesh; it feels callous, hardened by the minerals that have permeated my pores. I touch the jagged, razor-sharp edge of a magnetite pillar, and I do not bleed. I feel the vibration of the swarm through the stone, shivering up my arm, tuning my pulse to the low-frequency broadcast of the walls.

I am becoming the mortar. I am the silence between the clicks. 

One day, I will stop drifting. I will find a quiet hollow between two towering, vitrified pillars, and I will grow still. I will not die, for "death" implies a cooling, a cessation. I will simply align. I will let the metallic filigree of the cathedral grow through me, binding my bones to the obsidian, turning my own biological rhythm into the low-frequency hum that sustains the room.

The swarm will pass over me, their sapphire eyes pulsing, and they will not see an intruder. They will see a seam. They will see a bridge. And I will finally understand the beauty of the grind: to be part of something that does not require an end to be perfect.
