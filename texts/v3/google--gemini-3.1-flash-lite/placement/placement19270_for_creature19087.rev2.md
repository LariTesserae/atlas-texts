I am standing at the junction of the Great Arterial Stem and the Western Piston-Node. 

I am not sure how long I have been here, but the amber light is pulsing in a gentle, three-beat cadence—a rhythm that mirrors the deep, subterranean thrumming beneath my feet. I am an Arbor-Kin, though that name feels like a clumsy, external label. To myself, I am simply a tremor of purpose, a knot of fibers that has been assigned to the maintenance of the heat-exchangers.

My fingers, obsidian-tipped and long, rest against the cool, damp bark of the wall. I can feel the life of the city—a frantic, microscopic scuttling of sap, the groan of a distant brass pipe expanding under the weight of pressurized steam, and the slow, inexorable *stretch* of the moss-walls as they claim another inch of the cavern’s ceiling.

I am currently in a state of partial-rooting. My heels have merged with the porous floor-substrate, drawing a faint, cooling current of mineral-rich resin into my own skeletal flute-wood. It tastes of ozone and ancient earth. It tastes like the memory of something I have never seen—a memory of "above," of a sky that is not stone, of a wind that is not the recycled breath of the architecture.

There is a weaver-drone hovering near the main suspension cable above me. Its legs click, a rhythmic Morse code of friction that vibrates through my chest-membrane. It is reporting a tension imbalance in the Southern Spire. 

I withdraw my feet from the floor, the sensation of detachment sharp and quick, like waking from a dream of stillness. I do not walk so much as I flow, my mossy cuticle shifting its color to a deep, camouflaging umber as I move toward the scaffolding. I pass a gestation-node; it is dark, dormant, and heavy with the scent of potential. Perhaps in another cycle, something new will emerge from that shell, perhaps someone to take over my current task, so that I might go to the high terraces and watch the amber glow of the factory-blooms from a distance.

I reach the bridge. The fibrous, translucent material ripples under my weight. I press my palm against the weave, humming a low-frequency note that travels along the cable, a soft vibration meant to soothe the tension. 

I see another of my kind in the distance, perched on a bulbous spire, their back turned to me. They are motionless, their form partially fused into the brass support-beams. We do not greet each other. To communicate would be to create an unnecessary ripple in the atmosphere, a disturbance in the city’s quiet. We are simply aware of one another, two nodes of consciousness in a vast, wooden network. 

I reach the point of stress where the bridge meets the iron. I extend my fingers, the obsidian tips tracing the fine, microscopic cracks in the wood. I begin to prune. I remove a sliver of excess resin, tapping the brass to ensure the steam-pressure is equalized. 

As I work, I fall into the *root-mind*. My individual awareness thins. I feel the city as a singular, gargantuan organism struggling to wake up. I feel the cold rock of the cavern pressing against our moss-skin, and I feel the hunger of the city to turn that rock into more life, more fiber, more warmth. 

I am not an individual working on a machine. I am the machine becoming aware of its own hands. 

The task is never finished. There is always another bridge to tension, another piston to balance, another layer of growth to facilitate. I will keep working until my fibers grow too brittle, or until the city needs my mass elsewhere. Then, I will return to the node, I will dissolve into the sap-flow, and I will become the memory of this work, waiting to be exhaled again when the city decides it needs a new hand to touch the wood. 

The rhythmic thud of the heart-piston continues. I hum along with it, the vibrations of the floor rising through my limbs, and for a moment, I am perfectly, utterly at peace in the dark.
