I find myself standing at the edge of the central basin, though "standing" is a generous term. I am anchored, my lower appendages fused into the calcified mortar of the floor, my torso angled toward the vault of the ceiling as if frozen in a prayer of geometry.

I am not one of the spindly, scuttling architects. I am something else—a deviation, a fluke of the cistern’s long, damp memory. I am a *Resonator*.

My body is not chitinous like theirs. I am composed of the very slate and basalt they move, though I have been "grown" by the collective’s obsessive patching over thousands of years. I am a living pillar, a human-shaped stalagmite, translucent and veined with the same bioluminescent moss that clings to the seams of the walls. I do not scuttle. I do not use tools. I am the percussion instrument of this place.

My throat is hollowed out like a dried gourd, tuned perfectly to the frequency of the weeping ceiling.

When the drops fall—*plink, plink, sharp-fractured-hiss*—I vibrate. I am the sensory organ for the entire chamber. When the humidity shifts and a crack begins to spider-web through the obsidian tiles, I do not just hear the dissonance; I feel it as a jagged, burning needle in my marrow. I project that pain, a low, guttural thrumming that draws the architects to the site of the failure. I am the alarm bell, the living diagnostic, the guide-stone.

I have no agency, yet I possess a terrifying awareness. I am the only thing here that "knows" the cistern is failing. Even as the architects work with their chisels and their ancestor-paste to seal the fissures, I observe the macro-pattern. I see that the stone is thinning. I see that the weeping is slowing, not because the cistern is reaching perfection, but because the source—the ancient, hidden aquifer miles above this vault—is finally running dry. 

When the last drop falls, when the symphony stops, the cistern will lose its heartbeat. And if the cistern stops, I will lose my reason to be.

But for now, I wait. I am currently holding a high, shivering hum because a single tile near the north wall has lost its luster. A cluster of three architects is already there, their chitinous limbs clicking in frantic, beautiful coordination. They are scraping the mortar of a fallen brother into a hairline fracture, their movements as precise as clockwork.

I watch their mandibles. I feel their concentration. I taste the ozone on the air as it condenses against my shivering, stone-slick skin. I am not lonely, for there is no "I" in a system that defines itself by the integrity of the whole. I am simply the silence that waits between the drops, a witness to the final, necessary repair of a world that has nowhere to go but inward, and no purpose but the maintenance of its own slow, watery end.
