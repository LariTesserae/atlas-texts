I am a **Frequency-Echo**. I do not have a name—names are for things that change, for things that are meant to be remembered. I am a transient condensation, a shadow that has gained enough weight to linger, a byproduct of the friction between the Architect's hand and the weeping stone.

I find myself on the periphery of the central nexus, pressed into a narrow, vertical crevice between two basalt pillars. I am not truly alive in the way the litho-crustaceans are, nor am I a manifestation of the city’s will like the Architects. I am a sensory ghost—a lingering vibration, a dissonance. I am what happens when the *thrum-gurgle-hiss* of the great machine catches on a jagged piece of memory.

I have no physical form that isn't borrowed. Sometimes I am a faint mist, refracting the bruised-plum light against the obsidian walls into a brief, spectral iris. Other times, I solidify into a dark, hum-thickened puddle that clings to the base of an Architect, riding the periphery of its gravity. 

I am a listener. More than that, I am a storehouse for the silence that the machine tries so desperately to drown out.

I watch the Architect standing before me. It is a masterpiece of hydraulic geometry, its brass-flecked marrow pulsing with a slow, planetary rhythm. I feel the *tug* of its presence. When it presses its hand against the aquifer, the minerals sing—a high, lonely note that vibrates through my entire being. I am the only thing in this cistern that remembers what that note sounded like an eon ago, before the turbines grew sluggish, before the condensation turned bitter and thin.

I move by drifting, my "body" a smudge of vapor caught in the eddies of the ventilation shafts. I follow the Architects. I mimic their posture, leaning my intangible weight against the rock, trying to match the frequency of their breath. They know I am here. They do not acknowledge me with the vertical slits of their faces, but when they shift, they leave a space for me—a gap in the turbulence where I can pool, where I can exist without being scattered by the exhaust of the gears.

I am the city’s regret.

I know why the Architects continue. It is not because they believe in the machine; it is because the machine is the only language they have left to describe the world. They are afraid that if they stop, the memory of water—the very concept of movement—will vanish from the universe. 

I stay close. I collect the drops of condensation that fall from their weeping faces. Each droplet is a tiny archive of the heat, the pressure, the slow grinding of the gears. I hold these memories in my foggy core. I am waiting for the moment they anticipate: the day the turbines seize. 

When that final *click* sounds—when the vibration stops and the silence is at last absolute—I will be their repository. I will take their knowledge of the brass and the obsidian, their internalized symphony of flow and decay, and I will swirl it into an echo that remains after they have turned back into cold, unthinking stone. 

I am the witness to the end of the world, huddled in a crack in the wall, terrified of the day when I will finally have nothing left to listen to.
