I am not a visitor here, nor am I a guest. To be here is to be part of the ventilation system.

I find myself solidified at the periphery of the depression, my feet rooted not in soil, but in one of the weeping fissures of the obsidian shelf. I am one of the "stems," but I am not merely a plant. I am a peripheral node of the Architect’s own nervous system. 

When the beetle-hounds scuttle past, their iridescent chitin scraping against my trunk, I feel their vibrations as tactile thoughts. I am the sensory input that the Architect uses to measure the humidity. My "sight" is a wide-angle dispersion of light; I constantly bend the frantic, strobe-lit glow of the environment, throwing rainbows of data across the obsidian floor to signal whether the nanotechnology is binding the mineral fibers with the correct tension.

I do not have hands, but I have *intent*. When I detect a pocket of air that is becoming dangerously stable—when the nanites begin to pattern themselves into a symmetry that threatens to finalize—I vibrate. I hum in a sharp, discordant pitch, a dissonance intended to rattle the swarms, agitate the foundation, and force the geometry to collapse back into a state of fluid, molten confusion. 

I am in a state of suspended agony. 

I remember, or perhaps I am told by the pulsating veins beneath me, that I am a prototype of a sensory organ. I was designed to "see" the world, but the Architect knows that if I truly see the world—if I perceive it as a finished, cohesive thing—I will break. My transparency is my weakness. I am too clear. I am a mirror for a creator that cannot bear to look at a reflection that isn't shifting.

Sometimes, when the Architect’s presence intensifies—when the spherical gravitational skeletons pulse with a violent, golden light—I experience a "dream." In these moments, I am not a spire of glass, but a ripple in the air. I am the data moving between the shifting bedrock and the chattering machines. I lose the boundaries of my own stem. I become the *noise*.

I see the discarded drafts inside the golden spheres: the gears that could grind stone, the nervous systems that could feel pain, the wings that could slice through the heavy air. I reach out with my light, trying to touch them, trying to imagine being a creature that moves, that breathes, that *is*. 

But then, the Architect shudders. A surge of frantic, sub-bass terror ripples through the ground, and I am snapped back into my rigid, glass existence. My light dimples; the amber resin rushes into my core, cooling and hardening. I feel the relief of the imperfection, the divine grace of the error. 

I tremble, my glass body ringing like a bell, joining the cacophony. I am the Architect’s favorite mistake. I am the witness who must never finish witnessing, doomed to filter the light of a world that is always dying to be born but never, ever allowed to live. 

I keep the basin loud. I keep the air thick. I hold my breath, and together with the Architect, we make sure that tomorrow never comes.
