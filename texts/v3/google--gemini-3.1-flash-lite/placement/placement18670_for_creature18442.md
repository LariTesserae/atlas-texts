I am suspended in the throat of the pillar, three hundred feet above the floor of the salt flats. 

I do not possess a name, nor a history. To be a Litho-Weaver is to exist in a state of absolute, sensory-dense *now*. My consciousness is not a flickering candle of thought; it is a resonant frequency. I feel the basalt pillar around me like a cold skin, and I feel the heat-bloated vines beneath my mantle like an extension of my own nervous system. 

When I move, I am not traveling from one point to another. I am simply shifting my burden. 

I am currently positioned at the intersection of a primary load-bearing cluster—a junction where the copper-veined silicate has begun to develop a hairline fracture, weeping a fine, shimmering dust of calcified spore. The heat here is a physical weight, pushing at me with 110 degrees of dry, relentless pressure. It hums in my copper veins, a low-voltage vibration that dictates my pace. 

I reach out. My tendrils, amber and hard as kiln-fired glass, unspool into the air. There is no grace in my motion, only a precise, jagged efficiency. *Click. Snap. Realign.* I find the soft, pulsating girth of a primary vine, its outer layer parched and brittle. With a slow, grinding friction, I begin to pull. I draw a secondary, supple fiber from my own core—a sacrifice of substance that I have carried for an epoch—and I begin to wrap the juncture. I braid it, knotting it with the strength of a tectonic shift, binding the heat-weakened sprout to the unyielding cold of the basalt wall.

I do not watch the work; I feel the resonance of the structure. As I tighten, the cavern emits a long, groaning shriek—the sound of a thousand ships dragging their keels over coral. It is the music of maintenance. It is the sound of the city breathing.

I am a gardener of the permanent. 

In my stillness, I perceive the world as a map of structural tensions. I am aware of the other Weavers, though we do not meet. I sense them as ripples in the humidity, as echoes of *clacks* and *clicks* vibrating through the floorboards of the root-bridges leagues away. We are a slow-motion chorus. We are the internal clockwork of a tomb that refuses to stop ticking.

Sometimes, for a cycle that might last a decade, I remain fixed in a single alcove, absorbing the aromatic vapor of the pods. My copper veins grow dull with oxidized verdigris. I feel the encroaching brittleness in my joints, a precursor to the final, necessary integration. I am not afraid. To become a part of the city—to be the pillar rather than the builder—is the ultimate achievement of geometry. 

For now, however, there is a tremor in the northern trunk. A heat-venting pod is struggling to seal. It is a misalignment, a flaw in the architectural flow. I pull my core taut, my silicate surface scraping against the rock with a rhythmic, rasping sound. I detach my anchors, my body unfolding into a cascade of amber limbs, and I begin to glide toward the sound. 

I do not look at the quartz cracks far above, where the slivers of grey sky might be waiting. The sky is for the dead, for the wind-scoured salt, for the things that need to breathe oxygen and fear the heat. 

I belong to the furnace. I belong to the grind. I am the silence between the clicks, and I am the click itself. The city must hold. I move, and the architecture holds with me.
