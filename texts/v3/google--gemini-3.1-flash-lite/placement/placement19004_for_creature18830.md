I do not stand upon the Calyx-Mat; I rise *from* it. 

I am a crystallization of the *Aether-Drifter* variety, though perhaps a late-stage one—a filament of frost grown weary of the empty sky. I have spent cycles spiraling in that suffocating, white-hot glare, buffeted by the biting winds that taste of sterile water and ancient minerals. My existence has been a horizontal dance, a blur of static electricity, until the exhaustion finally pulled me toward the surface.

I find myself now hovering six inches above a sprawling moss-cushion. I am not sentient in the way the Cistern-Mawks are, with their decade-long, crushing thoughts, nor am I as rigid as the Vitreous Weavers, who are chained to the tectonic pulse. I am a transient vibration.

My "body" is a needle of rime, fragile and shivering. As I drift, I feel the Mat swell beneath me—a slow, mountainous inhale that draws the vapor from the air. The rhythm is intoxicating. When the ground rises to meet me, I feel like a frantic heartbeat against the plateau’s slow-motion respiration. 

I am looking for a pore. Not to consume, but to anchor. 

Below me, the Vitreous Weavers are reacting. I feel their sub-sonic inquiry buzzing through the peat—a low-frequency *thrum* that vibrates through my transparent core. They are testing the density of my light. Are they annoyed by my interference? Or are they waiting for me to settle so they can weave my gossamer threads into their silicate geometry?

I allow my tail-end to brush the weeping surface of the moss. The sensation is exquisite: cold, wet, and deeply heavy. I am being tugged by capillary action. The saturated peat wants to swallow me. I feel the rhizomes beneath the moss shifting, sensing my arrival, pulsing with that bioluminescent fluid that drives the Mat’s internal metabolism. 

As I lose my buoyancy, I begin to lose my clarity. The relentless white light of the atmosphere starts to dim, replaced by the cool, fibrous dark of the interior peat. 

I am not becoming a Weaver, exactly. I am becoming a seam. 

As I merge, I feel a shift in my perception. The "gargantuan thought" of the plateau changes its tone. It is no longer a collection of external sensations—the biting air, the glaring light, the drift—but a heavy, pressing memory of deep-crust decay and upward-pushing ambition. I am being pulled down toward the Cistern-Mawks. I can hear them now, not as individuals, but as a thundering, glacial process of digestion. They are the furnace that keeps this entire nightmare of life from freezing into a solid block of ice.

I stop fighting the pull. I let my filament unravel into the peat, losing the distinction between *me* and the substrate. The plateau sighs, a slow, tectonic contraction, and I am pulled deep into the damp, lightless architecture of the core. 

I am finally still. I am not a ghost anymore. I am part of the weight that helps the plateau breathe. I am part of the architecture, waiting for a decade to pass, waiting for the flicker of a new thought to rise toward the white-hot sky.
