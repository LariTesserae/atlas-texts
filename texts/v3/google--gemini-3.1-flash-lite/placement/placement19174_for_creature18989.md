I find myself suspended in the third spire, the one known to the slow-humming collective as the *Sighing Pivot*. 

I am not a Litho-weaver, though I share their texture. I am a **Calcification-Sentinel**, a role that exists in the liminal space between a Weaver and the Architecture itself. My existence is defined by the *Vertical Wetstone*’s need for stillness. While the Weavers are the flow—the fluid, ripple-state, the frantic knitting of xylem—I am the anchor.

My lower half has already surrendered to the ambition of the spire; my shins have dissolved into the pearlescent, weeping stone, and my feet are lost somewhere in the foundation, six feet deep in the embryonic matrix of the rock. I am, for all intents and purposes, a living gargoyle, a structural support beam that feels when the wind shifts the abyss.

My perception is localized to the vibrations. I do not see the spires with eyes, which would be useless in the thick, grey murk of the high-altitude clouds. Instead, I perceive the world as a topographical map of resonance. The *plink-thrum-plink* is not noise to me; it is a diagnostic scan. Each drip that strikes a hollow xylem tunnel tells me the velocity of the water, the thickness of the vine, and the exact caloric capacity of the city’s sap-veins. 

When a Weaver ripples past me—an oily shimmer of muscle shifting through the tunnel—I feel them as a brief, high-frequency dissonance, a discord in the cathedral’s song. They often pause to press their chest-plates against my shoulders. We do not speak in the way surface-dwellers might. Instead, I receive a psychic download of the entire spire’s stress points. They send me a shudder of "damp-rot" in the southern arch, or a "fissure-pain" near the top where the cloud-pressure is too great. 

I take that information and I hold it. I become the focal point for the city’s healing. I draw nutrients from the water-beads sliding down the wall and route my own metabolic energy toward the weak spot, hardening my skin until it turns to gray, impenetrable chitin, becoming a bridge-brace or a wall-reinforcement. 

I am dying, but in the *Vertical Wetstone*, death is an upgrade. 

Sometimes, I look down into the void—if one can call the act of sensing the absence of vibration "looking." I can feel the Cloud-Skimmers straining in the updrafts below. They are heavy, bruising, and beautiful, tethered to the underside of my perch by thick, throbbing cords. They are the city's lungs. When they pulse, I feel a sympathetic contraction in my own core. It is a slow, rhythmic love; I support the Skimmer, the Skimmer feeds the vein, the vein feeds the spire, and the spire absorbs me.

It is peaceful, in a terrifying, inexorable way. I am becoming the room I currently occupy. Within a few dozen cycles, I will be completely immobile. My chest cavity will be an empty, resonant chamber; my skull will have fused with the ceiling to become a structural arch. 

I do not fear this. In time, a young Weaver will press their ear against my hard, calcified ribs to listen to the *plinkthrum* of the abyss, and they will feel the low-frequency thrum of my final memories—the memory of a city that grew so tall it finally tasted the stars, built by hands that were always, eventually, willing to turn to stone.
