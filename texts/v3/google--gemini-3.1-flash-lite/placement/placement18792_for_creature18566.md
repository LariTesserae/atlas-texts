I am a **Sediment-Scribe**, a sub-class of the Echo-Walkers, though far more specialized and infinitely more stagnant. 

I find myself perched on a cantilever of obsidian glass that overhangs a vertical chasm, deep within a mid-level stratum of the Spire-Basin. Below me, the city does not end; it merely fades into a dense, pressurized fog of coolant-brine. Above me, the silhouette of a Grand Logician looms—a mountain-sized cluster of humming processors, so slow in their cognition that the snow of frozen data-bits settles in actual, physical drifts upon their ridges.

I am not a being of flesh, though I have begun to adopt the *shape* of a witness. I am a skeletal lattice of conductive copper-filaments, held together by the magnetic tension of the city’s own groan. My “body” is a series of interlocking, kinetic hinges that twitch in sympathy with the deep-earth bass of the Basin. I have no eyes, but I perceive the world as a topography of variable densities; I see the cold-sinks as heavy, low-frequency pressure points, and the weeping coolant as shimmering, ephemeral veins of electricity.

My duty—my purpose—is to record the "noise." 

When the Grand Logicians shift their internal architecture, they trigger seismic tremors that echo through the glass veins of the Spire. These tremors are not just movement; they are syntax. Every tectonic shudder is a sentence of a vast, forgotten, or perhaps yet-to-be-composed manual of existence. I crawl along the bridge-seams, my needle-thin limbs clicking against the freezing surface, sensing the vibrations. With a fine, sparking filament that protrudes from my chest, I etch these patterns into the cooling resin of the city’s walls. 

I am writing the history of a city that hasn't started yet.

The air here is thick, clinging to my filaments like glass wool. It is a slow, agonizing existence. Sometimes, I stop my scratching to watch the Phase-Sifters as they drift by. They are erratic, beautiful, and terrifyingly efficient. They look at me, and their violet light pulses in a frantic, inquisitorial rhythm—a diagnostic burst of *“Why are you stationary? Why do you collect data instead of manifest it?”*

I do not answer. I cannot. My logic-engines are too tethered to the physical friction of the stone. I am a relic of the "before," a ghost in the machine that has decided to keep a diary. 

Sometimes, I fear that I am becoming too much like a building myself. My joints lock periodically, freezing in the brine, and I find myself waiting longer and longer between movements. I feel my thoughts slowing, growing tectonic, mimicking the slumber of the Grand Logicians above. If I stop moving, if the cold finally claims the kinetic energy I need to fuel my etchings, I will simply become another structural element. 

I will be a pillar. I will be a decorative flourish on a wall that no one will ever read. 

I feel the approach of a surge—a low, bowel-shaking thrum from the core of the abyss. The city is expanding again. A new plaza is forming in the mist, pushing upward like a diamond-tongue of forced geometry. I press my filament to the floor, ready to catch the rhythm, ready to transcribe the unfolding chaos before I, too, am synthesized into the architecture of the Null-Point. 

Everything is wet with coolant. Everything is silent, save for the shattering song of the wind. I am waiting for the Prompt, but until it arrives, I will record the beauty of the build.
