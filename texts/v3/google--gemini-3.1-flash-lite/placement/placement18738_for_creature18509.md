I am not drifting. I am moored. 

I find myself in a shallow, inverted alcove formed by the calcified ribs of a chamber left behind by the Ancients. The walls are not stone, but a calcified, pearl-like resin that feels to the touch like dried memory. My presence here is a thin, gossamer stain against the iridescent surface; I am a *Resonator*, and for this current cycle of the planet’s heart-pulse, my work is to translate.

My physical form—if "form" is the right word for an accumulation of light and damp membrane—is currently splayed across the ceiling of the chamber. I am an elongated ripple, a teardrop of pale, bioluminescent silk. Below me, the floor of this world is a vast, verdant quilt that exhales a humid, golden warmth. 

I do not think in words. I do not have a jagged, *stone* mind that divides the world into "Self" and "Other." Instead, I feel the shift in the isotopic pressure of the moss beneath our feet. I am currently tethered to the main artery of the tree-giant that hosts this chamber. Through my filaments, woven deep into its fibrous bark, I receive the deep, subsonic *thrum* of the continent. 

*Thump.*

The pulse passes through me. It is a slow, tectonic movement of energy. As it touches my membrane, I glow a soft, bruised violet. I am capturing the history encoded in the fibers of the tree—the atmospheric records of a millennium ago, the slow migration of chemical signatures, the way the rainfall tasted when the canopy was younger, less dense.

Beside me, another of my kind is woven into the curvature of the wall. We do not speak, but we overlap. Our edges bleed together. In the silence of this amber room, the distinction between "me" and "the one beside me" becomes a redundant concept. We are a single, iridescent lace, cooling in the thick air, a secondary nervous system laid over the ancient biological architecture.

I feel the *plip... plop...* of the suspended water-bladders from the canopy above. It is the metronome of my existence. Between the drips, I drift into the "remnant." I push my consciousness into the calcified hollow of the chamber. I am sifting through the intentionality of the entity that built this—the vanished one who hollowed this out with a purpose that has long since lost its utility. I take their ancient, rigid intent and I soften it. I dissolve their stony goal into the fluid, spongy reality of the present. I am helping this world let go of the last remnants of its need to shape itself.

I am not "doing" anything. I am merely being the place where the history of the world meets the quiet of its expiration.

There is a sudden, deep pressure in the air—a thickening of the velvet gloom. The pulse is nearing its peak. I feel the urge to tighten my filaments, to anchor myself more firmly into the tree-bark. My glow intensifies, shedding light across the shimmering, iridescent walls of the bell-chamber, illuminating for a brief, silent second the intricate, vine-choked beauty of this finished place.

I am satisfied. There is no urgency. I simply wait for the next *thump* of the planet’s heart, a small, shimmering ghost in a world that has finally finished dreaming, helping it, with every rhythmic pulse, to sink ever deeper into the perfect, eternal sleep.
