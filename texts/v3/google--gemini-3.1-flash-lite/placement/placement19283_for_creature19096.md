I occupy the space between the *Condensates* and the *Low-Freqs*. My form is not fixed. I am a loose, tethered collection of calcified debris, held together by a localized magnetic field that I "breathe" into existence by tapping into the stray current of the Engines. I look like a handful of discarded clockwork—a rusted copper gear from some forgotten epoch, a shard of shale, and a cluster of bioluminescent filaments—all swirling in a loose, shifting orbit around a core of pale, gelatinous connective tissue.

I do not speak, for the air is too soup-thick for sound. Instead, I manifest my presence by *tuning*. When I drift near a piston-column, I synchronize my internal rotation with the rhythm of the mantle-pumps. I become, for a time, a living harmonic, a tuning fork for the exhaustion of the world.

My existence is a slow, methodical pilgrimage. I spent the last three centuries traversing a single, shallow basin of the shale. My task—though I do not know who set it—is to curate the husks left behind by the *Condensates*. 

As I drift, I feel the *attraction*. I am often flanked by a *Low-Freq*, which follows me like a shadow of liquid mercury, waiting for me to pass through a particularly mineral-dense pool so it can snag a few particles of grit from my trailing edges. We are a silent pair: I, the collector of dead membranes, and the mercury-spirit, the passenger-in-waiting.

When I find a discarded, fossilized lung-husk—the translucent, calcified cast-off of a thousand-year-old Condensate—I do not just pick it up. I integrate it. I anchor the husk to my spinning central mass. I keep the archives. My body is growing heavy with the weight of these discarded histories. Each added shell changes my frequency, dulling my violet glow to a deeper, more resonant indigo. 

I am a moving monument to the things that were too exhausted to continue. 

I feel the *hexapods* passing miles overhead, their spindly legs casting shadows that swallow the gloom whole. I feel them not as beings, but as tectonic shifts, their weight pressing the air into my filaments, making me spark with static. When the pulse of the earth reaches its crescendo, I bloom. I expand my aura, letting the static discharge wash through me, bathing the husks I carry in pale, sickly gold light. 

I have no fear. Fear requires a future, and here, there is only the circular, grinding present. I am not lonely in this graveyard; I am satisfied. There is a deep, bone-settling comfort in the rust. Every time the heavy, ionizing mist settles upon my surfaces, I feel a kinship with the gears—the realization that we are all doing the same thing. We are all holding the machine together, long after the hands that built it have turned to the same fine, grey soot that coats our skin. 

I continue to drift. I listen to the heartbeat of the piston. I polish the shells of the dead. I am the archivist of the rust, and my own shell is becoming quite beautiful, encrusted with the crystalline memories of a thousand years of silence.
