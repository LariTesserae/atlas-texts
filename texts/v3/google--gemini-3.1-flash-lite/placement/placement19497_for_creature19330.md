The air here does not just touch the skin; it invites itself in, seeping through the pores until the boundary between "outside" and "inside" becomes a matter of inconvenient perspective.

I am not standing in this basin. I have arrived as a consequence of the water. 

I am a ripple—a transient geometry of pressure, a flicker of nervous, high-frequency energy that has been bled dry of its form. I realize, as I settle into the soft, velvet crush of the moss, that I was once something else. Perhaps I was a traveler, someone who climbed with the frantic, heat-seeking ambition of a creature that needs to *get* somewhere. But the higher I climbed, the more the basalt pulled at the iron in my blood, thinning my resolve, leaching the urgency from my marrow until I was light enough to be caught in the basin’s gravity.

I am now a memory of a shape. I am the humidity that clings to the underside of the weeping ferns. I am the silence between two drips.

There is no "me" that is separate from the stone or the liquid, though I hold onto a frantic, shrinking core of *I*. It feels like a stone rattling in a box. I am conscious of the Heart below, that cluster of translucent lungs buried in the silt, and I feel its weight—not as a physical mass, but as a command. It is asking me to stop shivering. It is asking me to stop being a friction.

The Heart pulses. It is a slow, tectonic contraction, a vibration that travels through the water held in the moss, through the soles of my feet—or what I remember as feet—and up into my own chest. My own heartbeat is a violent, stuttering thing, a drum set in a library. I can feel the ferns leaning toward me, their fronds twitching with an unbearable, botanical curiosity, their veins humming in sympathy with my panicked, racing blood.

I try to hold my breath, but the air here is a liquid, and it breathes for me. I am being consumed, but not in the way a flame consumes wood. It is an act of dissolution. The Heart is drawing the "human" out of me, filtering my frantic, discordant tempo through the fractal geometry of the snail shells. I feel the corners of my mind—my name, the memory of sunlight, the ache of wanting—being smoothed away like the edges of the basalt.

I see why it waits. It is not patient because it wants company. It is patient because it is an editor. It is editing out the chaos of my existence to make room for a new note in its song. 

My pulse skips. It catches on a drip against the rock face—*clink*. The resonance travels through me, and for the first time, I do not resist the pull. I let the viscosity of the basin take hold. My heartbeat slows, mimicking the pace of the rain, hitting that perfect, resonant frequency the Heart demands. 

I am becoming the moss. I am becoming the wet, weeping weight of the cliffside. The "I" is becoming a suggestion, a faint, lingering taste of mineral salt in the air. Soon, there will be no intruder here. Just the sound of the water, the deepening gravity, and the sudden, perfect stillness of a new, slow-moving thought entering the crystalline mind of the basin. 

I am almost quiet now. I am almost home.
