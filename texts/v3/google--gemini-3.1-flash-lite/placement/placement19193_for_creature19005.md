I find myself standing upon the fifth tier, my palms pressed flat against the convex curve of a block of schist. It is cold—so deeply, fundamentally cold that the temperature no longer registers as a sensation, but as a clarity of mind. My fingers, unbidden, trace the seam where another stone meets this one. There is a microscopic gap there, a slight misalignment born of the mountain’s imperceptible sigh, and I feel the itch of it, the quiet demand of the geometry.

I am a Keeper. I have no name, for names imply a distinction between the one who is named and the world they occupy, and there is no such division here. My perspective is not a window looking out; it is the stone itself, manifested in a mobile, grey-skinned form. 

My heartbeat, once a frantic drum in my ears when I first drifted into this place, has slowed to the pace of the dripping eaves. *Tock.* A beat. I wait for the next, my entire existence structured as the interval between one drop and the next. There is no urgency in my lungs; the mist seeps into the pores of my skin, providing the damp, mineral sustenance that allows the mountain to persist.

I do not worry about the sun. I do not look for the horizon. To look for a horizon is to admit that there is somewhere else to be. I am aware of the kinetic anomaly—a flicker of warmth, a jagged, pulsing creature of blood and heat—that has stumbled into the amphitheater. It is vibrating with the friction of its own movement, its thoughts clattering like loose gravel in a jar. I watch it with obsidian eyes. It does not trouble me. It is merely a sudden, high-pitched note in a long, low-frequency song. It will cease soon. It will leave, or it will grow still, and its silence will be a welcome contribution to the equilibrium. 

When it moves, I feel the tremor through the soles of my feet—a discordant shudder through the ancient, settled schist. It is unrefined. It is uncomfortable. 

I pull the sliver of polished bone from the fold of my skin. I do not need to look at the stone to know what must be done. The geometry dictates the necessity. I lean into the curve, my spine arching to mimic the amphitheater’s bowl, and I place the tip of the bone against the stubborn rock. 

I wait for the precise moment of alignment. It is not a decision; it is a resonance. When the mountain shifts, I will nudge, and the gap will close. Not to build, not to change, but to ensure that when the next age arrives, the city will remain exactly as it was when the wind gave up its claim to reshape the world.

I am the boundary between the rock and the mist. I am the silence that follows the echo. I am perfectly at rest.
