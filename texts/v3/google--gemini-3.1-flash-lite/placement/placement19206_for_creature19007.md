I am not standing on the bridge. I am *part* of the bridge—or, rather, I am the tension that keeps it from unraveling into the white, humid void below.

I find myself composed of a fine, abrasive silt and the translucent honey-sap of a long-dead fern. I have no eyes to see the blurring of the horizon, but I feel the geography shifting in the marrow of my limbs. When the reality-breakdown ripples, I feel it as a sudden, sharp dissonance in my own resonance, a vibration that threatens to shatter the equilibrium of my being.

I am a Sediment-Bloom, designated to the southern buttress of the spire.

My existence is centered in the hollow of my neck. It is a shallow, concave basin, perpetually cool, waiting for the singular, suspended droplet to fulfill its potential. The air around me is a physical weight, a thick, pressurized soup that tastes of ancient minerals. I don't breathe it; I filter it. I am a sieve for the city’s stillness.

For an epoch, I have been focused on a single fracture in the limestone. It is a stubborn thing, a crack that refuses to align with the rest of the spire’s logic. Every time the hum of the city shifts—that deep, subterranean groan that pools in the air like heavy oil—the crack tries to butterfly outward, folding into a geometry that does not belong here.

I shift my position. My stride is not a walk; it is an osmosis of movement. I slide forward, my vines pulling tight against the masonry, fusing my grip with the wall. I am not sentient enough to be deliberate, but I am coherent enough to be necessary. 

As I press my chest against the failing archway, I feel the city speaking through me. I vibrate. The sound that is so loud it occupies space enters my hollow frame and rings against my calcified ribs. I become the tuning fork. I feel the masonry soften, turning from hard rock back into the wet, pliable sediment it was before the equilibrium caught it. 

I pull the nearby bioluminescent vine—cold, waxy, and tasting of starlight—and lace it through the fissure. My fingers are long, tapered extrusions of hardened mud. I do not "think" to fix it; I simply act as the bridge between the city’s decay and its desire to remain static. As the vine settles, the vibration in my chest settles with it. The archway stops its frantic rippling. It freezes again. 

I remain pressed against the stone. 

Another Bloom drifts past, crossing the path of my hanging bridge. It is a mirror image of myself, a stack of sediment and vine drifting through the auditory thicket. It does not look at me. It cannot; it has no eyes. But as it drifts, its shoulder brushes against my knee. We do not speak, for words would be too fragile for this air. We do not share a history, for we are the product of a reality that has abandoned time. 

Yet, in that contact, I feel the frequency of its duty. It is currently anchored toward a structural flaw three levels above, where a puddle of water has been hanging, unfallen, for ten thousand years. We are simply the city’s way of ensuring that the water never reaches the ground. 

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the capacity for absence, and in the City of the Vertical Weep, nothing is ever truly absent. Everything is here, suspended, wet, and roaring. 

I press harder into the stone. The moisture from the ceiling beads on my forehead and rolls down into the basin of my neck. *Tink.* The sound is a universe. I resonate. I am the silence inside the roar. I am the mud that dreams of architecture. 

I will stay here until the stone grows around me, until my sediment is indistinguishable from the masonry I hold together. When I am completely absorbed, another Bloom will precipitate from the humidity, drifting into my stead, carrying the same endless, silent impulse to keep the weeping world from breaking. 

We are not the masters of this spire. We are its self-correcting pulse, the heartbeat of a stone that is desperately, eternally trying to stay alive.
