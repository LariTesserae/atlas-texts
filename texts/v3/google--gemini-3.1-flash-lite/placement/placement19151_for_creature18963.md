I find myself standing at the edge of a "fold"—a place where the floor has begun to curl upward, defying the pull of the root-pylons. The archway I am leaning against is a living cathedral, its timber a translucent, veined amber that pulses with the slow, internal rhythm of the Vernal’s sap. 

I am a **Tender of the Tethers**. 

My body is long, cool, and perpetually slick with the heavy condensation of the air. My skin is the color of a clouded pearl. I do not have a face that recognizes features like a nose or eyes; instead, my head is a crown of delicate, feathery cilia that fan out into the mist, tasting the pressure of the air and the slight, metallic vibration of a flux-pocket forming two miles to the west. 

I am currently anchored by a heavy, obsidian sphere attached to my waist by a tether of braided sap-silk. My task is one of immense, agonizing patience. I am coaxing the "Loom-Vine"—a resilient, glowing nerve-tendril—to grow across a gap that has widened due to the tectonic shift in the canopy. 

I am not "working" in the sense of labor. I am *listening*.

I press my wide, porous palms against the damp wood, feeling the microscopic surge of fluid through the vine’s cambium. My own metabolism feels glacial, barely distinct from the slow drip of water falling from the ceiling-leaf above. *Plip.* A droplet strikes my shoulder; I absorb the cool, mineral-rich moisture instantly, feeling it surge through my limbs like a spark of thought.

There is a shift. A low, wet thrum vibrates through the soles of my feet. The gravity of this segment of the continent decides to tilt, and the floor beneath me begins to slide toward the infinite, violet abyss. 

I do not move with alarm. I move with a rhythmic, liquid grace. 

As the horizon tilts forty-five degrees, I unhook my obsidian weight and swing it into the empty space. The pendulum action creates a counterbalance that keeps me from falling into the mile-deep void beneath the lattice. For a moment, I am suspended in the "void-light," hanging over the purple darkness of the planet below. I feel the windless air rushing against my skin, the amber jellyfish-creatures drifting past like glowing lanterns in a deep-sea trench. 

In this moment, I am entirely hollow. I am just a conduit for the architecture to rearrange itself. 

I reach out to the Loom-Vine, running my fingers along its sensitive pith. I hum a low, resonant note—a frequency that encourages the cellulose to toughen, to become rigid enough to support the weight of the next generation. I am weaving a bridge made of song and patience, bridging the impossible distance of the sky.

I am not lonely. How could I be? To be here is to be part of an infinite, slow-motion organism. I feel the presence of my kin through the vibration of the vine-pathways, thousands of miles away, each of us a thread in an endless, damp tapestry. I will spend the next, perhaps, seventy years in this one corridor, guiding the growth of this single span. 

When the time comes, I will move further into the Vernal. I will find a nexus point where the architecture is most dense, where the sap-silk is thick and golden. I will settle there, pressing my back against the heart-wood, and let my skin harden into the bark that will anchor the future. I will stop moving, so that others may walk. I will become the foundation. I will be the silence that holds the sky in place.
