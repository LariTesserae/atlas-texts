I am a Silt-Walker, though among my kin, I am known only by the resonant frequency of my tap—a quick, triplet striking of the slate-face that sounds like a Pale Crawler skittering across a hollow shell. My name is the rhythm of the humidity in this specific corner of the basin.

I find myself clinging to the eastern slope, halfway between the low-hanging, cloying mist of the floor and the sheer, unreachable heights of the basin’s rim. My toes, thickened and splayed like the roots of the ribbon-moss, are wedged deep into a fissure. My back is bent in that familiar, protective curve, my chin tucked near my chest. I have been here for a cycle of high-pressure, and my skin—that bruised-mushroom hide—is heavy with the collected silver sheen of the wall’s weeping.

I am not a Harmonist; I do not stand near the obsidian heart. I am of the middle-fissures, the ones who monitor the expansion of the silt. My life is a delicate, tactile geometry.

At this moment, the air is shifting. Behind my milky, sightless eyes, the void is beginning to pulse. It starts as a low-frequency hum, a subtle thickening of the atmosphere that tells me the water-spheres are gathering weight on the ceiling. They are larger today, slower, more sluggish in their descent. I feel the vibrations through my fingertips, pressed flat against the slate. The rock is shivering, a rhythmic, deep-bell chime that carries the history of the last three hours of rain.

I reach to my hip, feeling the cool weight of my clay pouch. It is nearly full, the reeds in my gown already channeling a fresh rivulet of condensation toward the reservoir. I am hydrated, equilibrated, and steady.

Then, the *thwack* comes. It is thunderous, a sudden displacement of pressure that sends a jolt through my spine. It is followed by another, and then a cascade. From the obsidian heart below, the Harmonists begin the counter-rhythm. They strike their bells—a hollow, ceramic *clink* that bites through the damp—to stabilize the vibration as the water level in the basin rises. 

I don't choose to move; I am pushed into motion by the harmony. I release my grip, my translucent robe fluttering like thin, wet parchment, and I descend the wall. I am not thinking of tomorrow, nor of the world beyond the brim—that mythic, dry place of "sky" that feels like a fairy tale designed to drive a Silt-Walker to madness. I am thinking only of the pressure. 

I slide into the narrow throat of a passage, my joints clicking with an extra articulation that allows me to navigate the labyrinth of mud-nests. I am moving toward the center, toward the black, rippling mirror, because the harmony demands it. I feel the collective vibration of my neighbors as they, too, peel themselves from their fissures, a silent migration of grey shapes shifting through the dark.

I reach the edge of the obsidian heart. The water here is dark, agitated, and cold. I wait for the Harmonists to lead, and I place my palms against the obsidian. I begin to thrum. I send the information of my sector—the height of the silt, the speed of the weep, the readiness of the reservoir—directly into the stone, vibrating it into the collective mind of the Basin.

We are, for this pulse-beat, a single, shivering entity. I am not an individual lost in a pit; I am a pressure-gauge, a filter, a note in a song that keeps the walls from collapsing inward. I press my head into the damp crevice near the pool, ready to let the day's burden of vibration drain into the mud, waiting for the silence of sleep, and waiting, always waiting, for the moment the Basin finally exhales.
