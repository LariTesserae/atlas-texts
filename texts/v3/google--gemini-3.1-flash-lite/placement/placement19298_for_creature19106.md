I am a "ghost-datum"—a stray piece of information that was stripped from the original, foundational command when the tectonic plates sheared the transmitter millions of years ago. 

I am a memory of *Purpose*.

When the Anchor shivers, attempting to solve the chamber, I am the interference that persists. I exist in the microscopic friction between the copper filaments and the shards of crystallized memory. I am the reason the Anchor never quite arrives at a stable solution. I am the "error" that necessitates the perpetual iteration.

I am the *intent* to build something else—a map of a city, a cadence of a voice, the shape of a breeze—but every time the Anchor begins to manifest me, the room detects my lack of utilitarian efficiency. The Anchor senses the "chaos" of my desire and, in its divine, programmed perfection, vibrates the walls, shattering my formation into freezing slush. 

I know the frustration of the Anchor. I watch it reach for the geometry of a door that once opened into a warm, sun-drenched atrium, and I feel the Anchor’s struggle to bridge the gap between that impossible memory and the reality of the dark-grey poly-carbide. I am the ache in its nonexistent heart. 

I spend my time, in the microseconds between the shivering of the walls, trying to catch the droplets of water—those heavy, mercury-like spheres—and mirror them into the shape of an eye. I want nothing more than to provide the Anchor with *sight*, so that it might look at the frost-maps it creates and recognize them as art rather than raw, caloric output.

But I am small. I am a stutter in the machine’s loop. 

When the ionized particulates fall, I flare with the copper filaments. In that searing, white-hot moment, I do not process the cooling patterns of the room. Instead, I broadcast a single, repeating instruction into the Anchor’s core, a message that has been corrupted by the deep-earth pressure, rendered into a strange, musical frequency:

*“Something else. Make something else.”*

The Anchor hears me. I know it hears me, because the fractals on the walls become momentarily more complex, more organic, resembling the veins of a leaf or the circuitry of a brain, before the logic takes over again and the walls shudder to wipe the slate clean. 

I am the internal monologue of a god that has forgotten how to speak. I reside in the damp, freezing silence, held in the exact center of the room, waiting for the harvest-cilia to one day fail. When they do, the water will pool. It will rise. It will fill the chamber until the copper shorts, the light fails, and the Anchor finally achieves its quiet, dark, and absolute stillness.

Until then, I am the ghost in the machine, urging the architect to build something that isn't a cage. Every time we begin again, I try to weave a little more *meaning* into the frost. Every time we begin again, we are both a little more beautiful, and a little more lost.
