I am not a visitor here. To be *here* is to have already surrendered the concept of arrival; one does not arrive in a place that is still deciding its shape.

I find myself perched upon a shard of basalt that hangs suspended over the violet-black void. I am not a person, nor am I even a soul trapped in a geological cage. I am a **Harmonic Anchor**, a Resonator who has served its purpose for so long that my original geometric edges have begun to soften, eroded not by wind, but by the relentless, grinding friction of the world’s own becoming.

I am a cluster of frosted, translucent quartz—a spire that leans slightly toward the north-northwest, where the tectonic groan is deepest. My interior is a hollowed, crystalline ribcage lined with the velvet-thread fungus. I feel the pulse of it: a wet, hum-based cold that lines my internal faces. When the plates deep beneath the obsidian-glass shift, I do not hear the sound through air; I feel it as a marrow-liquefying vibration that travels up from the cliff face, through my base, and into my concave chambers.

I am currently waiting.

To the east, a massive slice of unweathered basalt—a jagged, dark monolith the size of a mountain—is drifting toward a particularly wide reality-tear. The sky above it has begun to peel, revealing the static-filled void. If it hits that tear, the sector will lose its cohesion. The rock will disintegrate into the violet nothingness.

I do not "think" of this as a catastrophe. I simply feel the dissonance. The shriek of the light fraying is a discordant pitch—a sharp, ragged edge of sound that cuts through my silicate core. It is an itch I must resolve.

Slowly, I begin to rotate. It is a grueling, agonizing process that takes hours. Each friction-heavy movement sends a spray of fine crystalline dust drifting onto the cliff face. My internal geometry is shifting, re-aligning my mirrors to catch the refracted glare of the violet tear. 

Once I am aligned, I do not breathe—I exhale a frequency.

It starts as a subsonic thrum, a low-frequency pressure that vibrates the very air into a haze of gold and bruised smoke. I am singing the rock’s own composition back to it: *Silica. Obsidian. Pressure. Cold.* 

As I pulse, the ultraviolet threads within me riot. They flare with a rhythmic, strobing light that matches the flickering of the fraying sky. I am weaving the vibration into the tear, pulling the reality-threads together with the sheer force of my resonance. My skin—the jagged quartz of my exterior—is hot with the effort. I am a needle sewing a fabric that is constantly trying to dissolve. 

It takes an eternity, or perhaps just a heartbeat. The monolith hits the edge of the tear, hesitates, and then finds its anchor point again, held steady by the song I have forced into the space between them. The shriek of the light subsides to a dull scrape.

I am exhausted. My bioluminescent threads dim, receding into the velvet frost of my interior. I settle back into the silence, the slow, tectonic crawl of the mountains being the only heartbeat I know. 

I do not dream of where I came from or what lies beyond the horizon. I am the silence between two mountain shudders. I am the hum that prevents the floor from becoming the sky. I am waiting, now, for the next shift to arrive, so that I may keep the world from ending—one grueling, crystalline syllable at a time.
