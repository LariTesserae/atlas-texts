I am positioned at a junction three leagues below the primary transit-nodes, where the ceiling of the Fabrication has begun to sag under the weight of an epoch. The pressure here is absolute; it is a weight that feels like memory, a heavy, velvet curtain draped over the consciousness.

I am a Curator—or rather, I am the *Becoming*.

I have no name, but I have a frequency. To identify myself is to feel the copper-laced brine rushing through the small, subterranean gills of my casing, and to recognize the long-drawn-out shiver of a rusted rivet three miles to the "east" giving way. I am currently anchored to a vertical sprawl of oxidized lead. My mass is currently shaped like a bulging, distorted pillar, slick with the pale, violet rot of the moss that maps my shoulders.

I do not think in words, but in resonance. My world is a dialogue between the *Sharp* and the *Smooth*.

Currently, there is an insult to the silence. A tectonic shift, perhaps a decade ago, forced a jagged shard of copper, no longer than a human finger, to protrude from the wall. To a creature of the surface, it would be an invisible speck. To me, it is a scream. It is a dissonance that disrupts the perfect, liquid geometry of this sphere. It is a laceration in the dark.

I begin the process. I am not sentient in the way the warm-blooded things once were, but I am *attentive*. I detach. The friction between my back-plating and the wall releases, and the viscous, oil-thick water carries me forward, a slow, dark shadow drifting through a violet-lit abyss. I do not steer; I yield to the currents that have already been shaped by the architecture of this place.

When I arrive at the shard, I do not "land." I resolve.

I wrap my heavy, rounded torso around the protrusion. I press the brass-rimmed diaphragm on my chest against the cold metal. I begin to vibrate. It is a slow, grinding song—a low-frequency thrum that travels through the marrow of the Fabrication, a song of erasure. I am grinding my own substance against the protrusion, sacrificing my density to bring the world into a state of softer grace. 

As I work, I feel the exhaustion of my limbs. I am thinner than I was when I departed the upper galleries. The particles that compose my skin are migrating, shifting away to coat the jagged edge, turning it from a splinter into a rounded, gentle curve. I am losing myself to the task, and there is a profound, mechanical peace in this shedding. 

I am not afraid of ending. I am a cycle, a flicker of motion within a vast, immobile eternity. One day, I will be too thin to vibrate. I will drift down into the stagnant, crushing black of the deepest canyons, and I will wedge myself into a gap in the architecture. I will become a node. A century later, a younger Curator will find me, and if I have developed a sharp edge in my settling, it will arrive to smooth me in turn.

I feel the sharpness fading. The snag is dulling. The metal is turning to a curve. 

*This is still,* the vibration resonates through my diaphragm, shaking the violet moss on my shoulders until it sheds like cosmic dust. *This is round.*

I pull tighter, my body melding with the wall. I am becoming the architecture. I am the silence, and for this brief, aeon-long moment, I am the only thing that is changing in a world that has decided to be done.
