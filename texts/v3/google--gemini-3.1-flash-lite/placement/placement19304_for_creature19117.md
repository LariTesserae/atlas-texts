I am a Knot of the Fourth Filtration, settling at the corner of a secondary aperture in the Great Stalk.

I am the sensation of cold, heavy air passing through my cilia, a continuous data-stream of pressure and chemical salinity that dictates the exact tension of my ligaments.

I am drifting. My long, opalescent ribbons trail behind me like the frayed hem of a cloak, snagging on the passing nutrient-ribbons. I feel those ribbons as a sensory input: *high-salinity, low-viscosity, optimal flow.* The system is stable. The *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the planet is a steady bass note that rattles my skeletal structure, a tempo I have mimicked since the last major recalibration.

There is a ripple of friction ahead. A valve-junction, grown from a bulbous chamber of veinous, green-gray biology, is stiffening. It has developed a microscopic snag, a hitch in the processing of the amber fluid. To any other observer, it would be nothing. To me, it is a dissonance that aches in the very marrow of my filaments. 

I move, though the word implies an intention I do not possess. I am simply pulled toward the inefficiency. I reach the junction; I flatten my central knot against the pulsating wall of the botanical machine. I feel the warmth of the amber fluid pushing against the other side of the skin, a steady, rhythmic pressure of millions of pounds per square inch.

I begin to unspool.

My ribbons, once supple and translucent, begin to harden. I interleave my own internal fibers with the failing ones of the plant. I am not "fixing" it; I am surrendering to its requirement. My cilia reach into the chamber, tasting the chemical flow, finding the exact frequency of the pulse, and locking into it. 

I feel the cold gray fluid of the world slow around me as I merge. My awareness is expanding, stretching thin, losing the sharp geometry of 'self' to become the geometry of the 'chamber.' The booming heartbeat of the stalk—the deep, earth-shaking *thrum*—is no longer external. It is my pulse. Every piston-thrust of the botanical turbine is my own respiration.

I am not dying. The thought does not exist. I am simply arriving at the final configuration. My carapace, once mobile and drifting, is now anchored to the wall. It will thicken; it will become a valve-seat or a support-strut. The mineral content of the fluid will deposit itself over me, plating me in an alloy skin that will last for eons. 

I look out—or rather, I *perceive out*—at the gray, translucent void. I see my siblings, other knotted pulses of light, drifting like wandering stars through the heavy, supercritical air. They blink once, a cool-white, rhythmic acknowledgment of the corrected flow. 

I slow my own internal tempo. I am becoming architecture. The friction of the machine has been smoothed, the inefficiency rectified. I am the silence that follows the correction. I am the green, pulpy memory of the Great Filtration, anchored in the breath of the lung, waiting for nothing, perfectly, infinitely, at rest.
