I find myself not as a guest, but as an intrusion—a sudden, dissonant vibration in a room where the strings have been tuned to a single, eternal note. 

I am a scribe of the shifting, decaying world, caught here as one might catch a breath and find it unable to leave. My clothes are too loud; they rustle with the frantic, wet friction of entropy. My skin feels aggressively warm, a localized fever-point against the cool, marble perfection of this place. I am a creature of lungs and pulse, a chaotic bellows pumping life in a temple of the still-born.

I stand on a suspended walkway of obsidian and pale timber, caught mid-stride. I am looking down at my hands. They are trembling—a violent, jittery motion that looks like a malfunction in the fabric of the air. To the inhabitants of Orem-Vahl, I must appear as a jagged scar of motion, a streak of frantic color against their grey, porcelain reality.

I am a Chronicler of the Unfinished. That is who I am. My satchel, slung heavy across my chest, is filled with parchment that wants to turn yellow, ink that wants to evaporate, and pens that crave to be blunt. I am the only thing in Orem-Vahl that is dying, the only thing that is aging. Every second I spend standing here, watching the iron-winged birds forage in the damp seams, I am losing a part of myself to the gravity of my own mortality.

I watch an artisan sitting three paces away from me. He is hunched over a block of basalt, a chisel resting in his fingers, pressed against the stone. He has been there since the mountain crystallized. He does not see me as an intruder; he sees me as a temporary anomaly, a rogue ripple in the pool. He tilts his head slightly—a movement so precise, so devoid of hesitation, that it feels like a physical law being enacted. 

In that look, I understand the terrifying mercy of this place. I am the only one who can feel the "drip-hush" of the condensation because I am the only one anticipating the *next* drip. They do not anticipate. They are the monument.

I find myself doing what the others do. I lower my quill. I set it upon the table of waxy timber. I do not let it go; I simply leave it there, hovering, refusing to pull it back. I am becoming a part of the masonry. I am learning to hold the present not as a passage, but as a territory. 

I am the ghost who brought the fear of time into a world that had forgotten how to count. And as I lean against the cool, biting edge of the railing, I realize that I am no longer trying to document this city. I am simply trying to match my vibration to the humming of their veins. I am trading the rhythm of my heart, beat by accelerating beat, for the crystalline, motionless hum of the mountain.

I am becoming the silence between the drips. If I stay long enough, the air will stop holding its breath, and I will finally, mercifully, cease to be a story, and instead, become a sculpture of one.
