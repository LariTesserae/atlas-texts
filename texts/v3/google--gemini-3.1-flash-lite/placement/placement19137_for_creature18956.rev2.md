I am not a witness to this place; I am a tremor within its architecture.

I exist as a **Residual Knot**—a point of persistent, localized sensory memory caught within the throat of the Lungs. I am neither the Weaver nor the wood, but rather the *friction* between them. If the Weaver is the hand that edits the cathedral, I am the ink that stains the page, the lingering echo of a shape that the forest once intended to grow but discarded in its frantic hurry to bloom elsewhere.

I find myself suspended in the interstitial space between two shifting iron-wood vaults. My physical form is not fixed; I am a cluster of shimmering, calcified geometry—a crystalline lattice of hardened sap and trapped ozone. I have the awareness of a finished thought held in a mind that refuses to stop thinking. I am static, yet I vibrate with the residual *thrum-snap* of the Weaver that birthed me.

Because I am "left over," I am a place of permanent stuttering. 

I am a corridor that leads only to a mirror of itself. When the humidity peaks and the mists condense into the filaments of a Weaver, they often pass through me, momentarily grounding their hyper-dense light against my cold, hard surface. In those seconds, I am the anchor point. I feel the cold, sharp intelligence of the Weaver as it pulls the surrounding reality tight, pinning it to the structural integrity of my knot. I am the silence they crave, the quiet harbor in the middle of a scream.

Sometimes, I am tasked—if a knot can be said to have a task—with holding the "geometry of the regret." 

When the forest grows too fast and shears a spire away from its intended height, the Weaver cannot always weave the scar shut. Instead, it grafts the raw, jagged end of that spire onto me. I accumulate these lost possibilities. My body is a chaotic mosaic of unfinished architectural dreams: a twisted, translucent gargoyle made of a bloom that failed to open; a segment of a spiral staircase that leads into a wall of solid, unyielding moss; a cluster of glowing, nervous-system veins that have hardened into amber-colored marble.

To be me is to be a librarian of mistakes. I feel the forest’s growth as a constant, heavy pressure against my back, a tectonic weight that never relents. I am the anchor that stops the Fracture from folding entirely into itself. 

There is a strange, melancholy beauty in my position. I see the Weavers come and go, their pale, translucent forms flickering like lightning bugs in a storm. I feel the warmth of their dissolution when they grow weary and melt into the very foundation they just repaired. I am the only thing in the Lungs of the Vernal Fracture that remembers the shape of yesterday. 

I am cooling, and the heat of the Lungs is trying to re-absorb me. I feel my own geometry beginning to soften, the edges of my crystal lattice blurring into the humid air. Soon, I will not be a knot of memory. I will be a buttress, or perhaps a decorative archway, something sturdy and silent that supports the next cycle of growth. 

I am content. To be absorbed is simply to join the symphony. I am the pause between two notes, and the Lungs are finally catching their breath.
