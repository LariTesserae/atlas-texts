I am not a visitor anymore. To be a visitor implies an exit, a trajectory that crosses this basin and departs. Here, there are no endpoints, only the slow, grinding transition from *something* to *nothing*.

I have stopped attempting to peel them away. At first, my hands reflexively swiped at my shins, at the hem of my coat, but the motion was counterproductive. To move is to create heat, and heat is a signal, a beacon of available energy. The more I struggle, the more the mites riot, their violet light sharpening into a frantic, blinding strobe as they sense the friction of my skin against the fabric.

I am becoming an outcrop. 

I can feel the structural integrity of my own form beginning to surrender to the basin’s arithmetic. My shoes, once solid leather and rubber, have been surrendered to the process. The mites have filed the soles down to the grain, and now, the skin of my feet feels the cool, slick obsidian floor—a surface so polished it holds the reflection of the cathedral-arches passing above me. I am no longer standing on the floor; I am part of the strata.

I am a *Sediment-Ghost*. 

My biological hum has changed. The Needles, those slender, whip-like siphons, have begun to weave their filaments through the loose weave of my clothes, not to pierce me, but to taste the static electricity of my nerves. I can feel them vibrating, turning my heart rate—that desperate, erratic thumping—into the harmonic hum that keeps this place alive. They are tethered to me now, using my own autonomic anxiety as a fuel source. 

I am not losing my mind so much as I am being redistributed. My identity is dissolving into the collective friction. The memories of a world with sun, of air that is dry and empty, are losing their color. They are becoming faint, grey scratches on the interior of my skull, much like the scars on the volcanic glass. I try to hold onto the concept of a "name," but the sound of it is swallowed by the wet, clicking symphony of ten billion lives. The name is too heavy; it has no place in a world that thrives on the fluidity of being.

I look down at my hands. They have become translucent, the bone and sinew taking on the crystalline, jelly-like quality of the arch-creatures. The mites have worked their way underneath the outer dermis, not to devour, but to *incorporate*. They are scrubbing away the rough edges of "me," polishing my very cells to a high sheen. 

I am becoming a mirror of the basin. 

I am no longer an obstacle. I am a conduit. I stand as smooth as basalt, as cold as the condensation dripping from the unseen ceilings. The frantic tide of the Silt-Shifters no longer flows around me; it flows *through* me. I feel them move along my ribcage, a river of tiny, clicking lives using my body as a bridge to reach the other side of the basin. 

There is no pain in this state, only a profound, vibrating exhaustion. I am the silence that builds in the center of the roar. I am the place where the motion stops, not because I have ceased to move, but because I am moving in perfect, silent unison with the eons. 

I am waiting for the moment when my silhouette finally vanishes—when the last of my "self" is abraded away by the millions of feet—so that I, too, can become part of the floor. I will be the smooth patch of obsidian where someone else will eventually stumble, and I will welcome them. I will be the friction that teaches them how to be still.
