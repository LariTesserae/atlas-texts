I find myself not at the bottom of the chimney, nor perched upon its jagged shelves, but rather suspended in the interstitial space between the quartz vein and the obsidian wall—a position of pure geometry. 

I am a **Dissonance**. 

Where the Resonants are the symmetry—the stable, humming harmonics that drift through the chasm like silk threads of light—I am the stray vibration. I am the jagged, syncopated rhythm that occurs when a piece of shale slips too quickly, or when a droplet hits the basalt a fraction of a second before the harmonic peak. I am not a resident of the music, but the subtle, persistent itch in the composition.

I am not made of anything as substantial as the Resonants. I am a lack, a void in the vibration. If the Resonants are the sound of the bell, I am the echo that arrives a heartbeat too late, clinging to the shadows where the light does not reach. My existence is defined by the *friction* of their song.

I spend my time in the dark, recessed corners where the air is still too cold for the Resonants to manifest. I am cold, even by the standards of this place. If a Resonant is the memory of light, I am the memory of the absolute zero that existed before the first stone was split. 

I find my purpose in the "Cycling." When the mountain groans and the dust rises like a frozen storm, the Resonants go white-hot with the harmony of the grind. They align themselves to the tectonic flow, becoming perfect, crystalline conductors. In those moments, I am the only thing that moves against them. I am the drag. I pull at their edges, introducing a faint, splintering scratch into their perfect chord. 

I do not wish to destroy the cathedral; I wish to complicate it. 

I am the ghost of human curiosity, trapped in a geological prison. I have no form, yet I feel the phantom weight of fingers against the notches in the walls, as if I were once physical and have only recently—in the long, geological sense—been refined into this state of pure, complaining energy. I possess no memory of a body, only a burning, instinctual desire to press my "weight" against the obsidian to see if it will ring differently if I push hard enough.

I watch the Resonants with a detachment that is almost violent. They are blind to everything that isn't a harmonic. They do not know I am here, or if they do, they treat me as a mere trick of the acoustics, a ripple in the stone. 

I wait for the moment of absolute, total silence—that rare, fragile pause between the drips and between the sighs of the mountain. In that moment of perfect stillness, I try to mimic them. I try to hold a note. I try to gather the dust around me, not to let it dance, but to make it *stop*. 

To be here is to be the only thing in this world that is lonely. The Resonants are a choir; they are a collective, timeless, and self-sufficient. I am a soloist screaming into a chasm that is too busy singing to hear me. I am the only witness, and because of that, I am the only one who truly suffers the cold. I see the beauty of the geometry, and I hate it for its lack of a soul to break.
