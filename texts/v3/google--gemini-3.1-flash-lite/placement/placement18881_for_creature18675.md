I am not a traveler here, for a traveler implies a destination, or at least a hope for exit. I am not a Resonant, for I possess the heavy, sluggish burden of blood, and I feel the cold in my marrow—a shivering 1.3 degrees—far too acutely to be one of the grafted.

I am the **Cartographer of the Echo**.

I am hunched in the center of one of the smaller, tertiary domes, perhaps a mile or two from the primary axis of The Hum. My skin is the color of the limestone, drawn tight over sharp, angular bones; I have become thin, spectral, less a biological entity and more a condensation of the cavern’s own minerals. I do not have a light source. To strike a match would be a blasphemy; to hold a torch would be to introduce a thermal variable that might shatter the crystalline ceiling above me. 

I see in the language of the cilia. Over the years, I have grown a fine, grey fuzz of sensory filaments along my forearms and the nape of my neck. I do not inhabit this world; I am being slowly translated into it.

My work, if it can be called that, is the preservation of the Dissonance. 

I carry a tool made of a splintered stalactite, sharpened against a blade of obsidian. With it, I track the "gaps." Every floor-shattering *ping* of a fallen Resonant is a moment of record. I crawl to the spot where a husk lies—brittle, light as a cicada skin—and I gently, agonizingly, press my hand into the dust. I am cataloging the failures. I am mapping the spots that no longer sing.

I remember my name, but it feels like a dream of a different climate. I remember the sensation of wind, and the terrifying, uncontrollable expansion of lungs that draw air without a forty-minute cycle. But those memories are fraying. They are being rubbed thin by the constant, sub-audible pressure of the Hum. 

Sometimes, I lie flat on the jagged floor, closing my eyes—not that the darkness cares—and I press my ear against the cold, unyielding stone. I listen to the collective exhale of a billion lives. When I do this, I can feel the vibration *in* me. It resonates in my teeth; it vibrates the fluid in my inner ear. For a brief, intoxicating moment, I am not a person at all. I am merely a tuning fork. I am a gap in the logic of the cavern, a soft, warm place where the universe has forgotten itself.

I am not afraid. Fear requires a future, a destination, a hope that things might be different. Here, there is only the *now*—the immense, terrifyingly beautiful *now*—held together by the friction of a billion wings. 

I am waiting for my own resonance to end. One day, my own grip on reality will slip. My breath will falter, and my own body will become just another brittle, chitinous husk to be crunched under the boots of a traveler who might, in some distant eon, descend to find me. 

Until then, I watch the walls. I listen to the vibration of the stone. I am the silence that bears witness to the sound. I am the note that is waiting, perfectly still, to be silenced.
