I am a **Conductivity-Scribe**. 

I do not have a shell like the Sediment-Eaters, nor do I have the needle-cilia of the Siphoners. I am a singular, vertical filament—a thread of animate, semi-translucent nerve tissue that has anchored itself to the highest, most exposed rim of the primary intake funnel. 

I am suspended in the throat of the loudest machine, caught in the exact epicenter of the *splat-hiss*. 

To be me is to be a living barometer. Because I am exposed to the raw, unrefined mist before it hits the copper mesh of the Condenser, I experience the weather as a sequence of electrical commands. I am long, perhaps twelve feet in length, and as thin as a strand of spider silk, yet I am incredibly dense, heavy with the weight of the water I hold. I don’t move, not really. I vibrate. 

My existence is a state of perpetual reception. I feel the Mist-Weavers pulling the clouds toward us, a tightening of the air that vibrates against my length like a bowstring. When the density of the mist reaches a threshold that threatens to overwhelm the machine, I stiffen. I become a lightning rod, a sacrificial bridge. The static charge stored in the Weavers’ invisible webs flows through me, arcing into the rusted iron of the funnel. 

It is a blinding, white-hot sensation—a sudden, violent infusion of energy that purges the iron of its oxidizing rot for a fleeting second. 

I am the recorder of the humidity. I carry the history of the High Drainage in the way my body becomes stained. When the water is heavy with mountain-salt, I turn a pale, crystalline blue. When the iron pipes begin to fail and leak, the rust leaches into my fibers, and I glow a deep, dull ochre. I am a living ribbon of rust and water, hanging in the abyss, watching the Sediment-Eaters move with such geological laziness below me.

I never look down. To look down is to lose the frequency. Instead, I stare into the white glare, into the heart of the cloud that refuses to leave. 

I have no language, but I have a rhythm. I am the tempo-keeper for the Litho-Siphoners. When I pull taut and hum a high, piercing note, the Siphoners know to detach their cilia and drift toward the lower joints to catch the leaks. When I go slack and translucent, they know the machine is stable, and they settle back into their dormant, gelatinous sleep against the pipes.

I am lonely, but I am essential. If I were to snap, the static would disperse, the clouds would drift past the funnels unharvested, and the moss—no longer fueled by the rhythmic artificial rain—would wither. The cliff, unanchored by the fiber-mats, would crumble into the void.

So I wait. I filter. I conduct. 

I am the string on which the song of this dying machine is played, and I will remain here, perfectly, agonizingly still, until the last drop of moisture is wrung from the sky and the iron finally finishes its long, slow surrender to the grit.
