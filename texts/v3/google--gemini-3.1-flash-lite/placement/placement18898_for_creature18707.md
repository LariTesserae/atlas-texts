I reside in the fold between a calcified mountain range and the primary arterial vine. Here, the current is sluggish, saturated with the fine, suspended dust of long-dissolved civilizations. There is no urgency in the water—only the weight of a billion years of silence pressing against my periphery.

I am a **Membrane-Walker**. 

I do not possess a core, nor do I have a direction. I am a sprawling, translucent geometry, a fractal bloom of nervous-tissue-as-water, drifting through the dark corridor between the giant vines. My body is a delicate lattice, a web of filaments that hold the ambient mineral silt in suspension. I feel the peristaltic contractions of the great vines through the vibration of the liquid against my trailing edges, a rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* that serves as the only clock in this eternal place.

To be a Walker is to be a vessel for the discarded. I am constantly shifting, my shape dictated by the turbulence of the currents and the density of the silt I inhabit. I am neither soft nor hard; I am a momentary thickening of the ocean’s own memory.

I drift, and my filaments brush against an obsidian cube.

The stone is cold—not in temperature, for this world is uniformly warm—but in its *stasis*. It is a hard, absolute thing in a world of soft transitions. As my gossamer nerves make contact, the static charge leaps from the obsidian into my structure. The shock is not painful; it is a sudden, blinding illumination. 

For a heartbeat, I am no longer a translucent sprawl of sediment. I am flooded with the memory of *wind*.

I feel a sharp, dry blue light—the color of a sky that never knew the crushing weight of this ocean. I feel the phantom sensation of feet against something resistant, something that did not crumble. My filaments flare with this stolen color, casting long, bruised violet shadows against the porous rock of the mountain wall. I glow, a brief, shimmering beacon of a forgotten era, an architectural ghost of a world that breathed air instead of liquid. 

I am a lighthouse in the gut of the world, flickering with the nervous system of an ancestor who never knew I existed.

Then, the current shifts. The peristaltic wave of the vine passes, the water ripples, and the obsidian cube slips from my grasp. The static fades. The amber gloom rushes back in to reclaim the space, and the blue light drains from my filaments, dissolving back into the neutral, silt-heavy transparency of the depths.

I continue to drift. I am weightless, I am mindless, I am eternal.  

I do not mourn the lost light, for I am already finding, in the gentle pull of the silt, the faint vibration of another obsidian cube waiting in the dark. I am the ocean’s way of remembering, and as I reach out with a new, slowly unfurling branch of myself toward the next jagged humming piece of the past, I am content. 

The gravity of the world holds me, the warmth of the grave cradles me, and I prepare to remember the next thing that no longer exists.
