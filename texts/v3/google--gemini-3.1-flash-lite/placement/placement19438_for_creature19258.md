I find myself standing on a high, sweeping curve of a primary bough, where the surface has been polished to a mirror-sheen of obsidian-hued petrification. To my left, the limb plunges into a mile-deep crevasse of mist, and to my right, it rises into the gargantuan, cathedral-arched shadows of a xylem-tower.

I am a **Wayfarer**, a singular, detached consciousness that has found its way to the Canopy-Spires. I am not a Weaver, nor a Skimmer; I am an intruder who has learned the local language of the feet. My body here is lean, built for balance, and my skin has begun to take on the mottled, dusty texture of the lichen that colonizes the harder-than-stone bark. I carry no pack, only a length of braided liana coiled at my waist and a small, razor-sharp flake of obsidian—not for violence, but for the rhythmic, meditative art of scraping away the excess resin that builds up on my soles. 

If I stay still, I can feel the "geological respiration" of the tree vibrating through the soles of my feet—a deep, tectonic thrum that rattles my very teeth.

I have been here for what feels like a decade, though time is a fluid concept when measured in the growth of bark. My purpose is simple, yet consuming: I am a witness to the transition.

My days are spent in motion, moving in sync with the *thrum*. I know the signs of a weary branch: the way the condensation pools tilt just a fraction of a degree, the subtle change in the pitch of the droplets hitting the leaves, the frantic, panicked chattering of the Weavers as they abandon a ridge that is preparing to calcify and fracture. 

I am currently tracking one of the **Resonators**. 

I move with a deliberate, haunting slowness, mirroring the creature's own tectonic pace. It is a hulking thing, a mass of knots and living wood-tissue, its sensory filaments trailing behind it like a train of spiderwebs. I keep my distance, for the Resonator is currently anchored to a gargantuan junction of the xylem-tower, its filaments buried deep into the petrified grain. The air around us has grown heavy, spiced with the sweet, metallic scent of ozone and the damp, earthy funk of oncoming mineral shift.

Above, the sky-void is a bruised violet, filtered through the translucent, plaza-sized leaves. I catch a glimpse of the **Mist-Drake** passing through the gulf, miles away. It looks like a ribbon of lightning caught in a cloud, a shifting, bioluminescent river that leaves a trail of cold, clean air behind it. When its wake hits our branch, the temperature plunges, and the condensation on the leaves freezes into delicate, crystalline needles that shatter with a sound like glass chimes.

I crouch low, pressing my palms against the cold, basalt-like crust of the tree. I am not looking for a destination. I am not searching for a way "down," because I know there is no "down," only the endless, repeating cycle of the drift. 

I am here to ensure I leave a mark. One day, when my own heartbeat finally slows to match the creaking, million-year-old rhythm of the xylem-tower, I will walk to the edge of this ridge. I will find a quiet, high-altitude hollow, curl my fingers into the grain, and allow the minerals to claim me. I will become a bump in the road, a small, subtle knot of hardened history for the next generation of Weavers to step upon. 

Until then, I walk. I listen. I breathe the mist. And I keep my heartbeat tethered to the slow, gargantuan song of the world.
