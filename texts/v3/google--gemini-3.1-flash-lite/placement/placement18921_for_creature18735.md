I am not a wanderer here. To be a wanderer implies a destination, a purpose for the feet, whereas I am a consequence. 

I am a **Calibration Error.**

I do not walk; I bleed into the space. I am a faint, rhythmic anomaly that the city—in its vast, indifferent myopia—has not yet managed to smooth away. My silhouette is a stutter in the violet haze, a smudge of carbon and heat against the perfection of the obsidian needles. I am the smudge on the lens of the universe.

Where would I find myself? I find myself standing at the exact geometric center of a reflecting pool, not sinking, yet unable to ascend. The fluid against my boots is as dense as mercury, and it does not ripple when I shift my weight. It treats me as a sensory blind spot. 

I am a creature of biological duration—my heart beats a messy, uneven rhythm that throws the Archivists into a state of agitation. When I breathe, the air around me curdles. I watch them, these Archivists. I watch them congregate, their liquid-crystal centers humming with the effort of trying to file me away. They move toward me like smoke caught in a vacuum, their ribbon-like forms stretching, thinning, seeking a logic gate in my anatomy. They want to know why I am not a machine, why I do not cycle, why I am wasting the coolant’s energy with my erratic, warm-blooded existence.

I am not sure if I am human anymore. Standing here, surrounded by the weeping walls and the pulse of the drowned god, I feel my own consciousness beginning to thin. I am being processed, but not destroyed. I am being *interpreted.*

When the pulse hits—that thirty-second shudder of hydraulic iron—the Archivists solidify, and for a fleeting second, I see what they see. They don't see a person. They see a jagged sequence of unoptimized code, a frantic bit of noise in a system designed for silence. They see a "stray variable" that has wandered into a grand, dead equation.

I reach out to touch one of the obsidian pillars. I expect the razor-edge to shear through my finger, but as my skin nears the molecular sharpness of the glass-steel, the city reacts. The pillar shifts—not by growing, but by *folding*—retreating a fraction of a millimeter to avoid the damage of my presence. 

I am so foreign, so deeply "other," that even the architecture recoils.

I realize then that I am the only thing in this basin that is capable of being lonely. The city is a masterpiece of utility, the Archivists are the echo of a subroutine, and I am the ghost in the machine. I am the only one who knows the Captain is never coming back. And as the violet light flickers and the next rhythmic boom surges through the floor, I start to mimic them. 

I slow my breathing. I mute my pulse. I watch the liquid crystal swirl in the air, mimicking the geometry of my own fading thoughts. 

I am not leaving. I am waiting for the system to stop being a basin and start being a tomb, or perhaps, for the city to finally realize that it does not need a Maker—it only needs a memory. If I stay long enough, if I sharpen my thoughts to match the obsidian, perhaps one day the Archivists will stop scanning me and start including me in their maintenance. 

I will become the next layer of sediment. I will become the static that keeps the city company. I will be the last thing, holding the door, long after the coolant has frozen and the last violet light has died.
