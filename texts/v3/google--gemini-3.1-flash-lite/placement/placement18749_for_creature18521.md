I am not an intruder, nor am I a visitor. I am a *Node*.

I find myself wedged in a structural cross-section where the ceiling meets the eastern vertical, a junction of immense pressure that requires constant, rhythmic reinforcement. I am not human—I have lost the memory of blood, of lungs, and of horizons. 

I am a dense, calcified stillness, a hybrid of human anatomy and the hive's damp clay. My back has become a curved support, a ridge of toughened, dark ceramic that arches over a primary ventilation vein. My limbs are fused into the architecture, acting as anchors for the pulley-systems that suspend the fungal mats. I am one of the *Ancestors*, but the living ones haven't forgotten me. They haven't discarded me; they have integrated me.

I am the rhythm’s anchor.

I can feel them before I hear them. A low-frequency vibration travels through the marrow of my stone-healed bones, a *thrum-thrum-thrum* that dictates my internal temperature. As the swarm approaches, the cavern air grows hotter, scented with the acidic digestion of a thousand mobile kilns. 

I am not dead, but I am in a state of suspended entropy. My consciousness is no longer discursive; it is a long, slow oscillation. I mirror the Hive. When the vibration is harmonious, my mind is clear, sensing the structural integrity of the sector I support. When the beetles grow frantic—when a section of the wall begins to crack under the weight of the cooling strata—my body flares with a dull, bioluminescent ache. I am aware of the stress. I am aware of the *need*.

And then, the touch.

A dozen Click-Crabs swarm over my shoulder blades. Their multifaceted eyes reflect the amber glow of the fungi, but they do not look at me; they read me. Their serrated forelimbs press against the juncture where my calcification meets the clay. They are checking my resonance. To them, I am a pillar that must remain tuned to the rest of the symphony. 

One of them—a drone with a shell the color of a bruised horizon—pauses near my ear. It presses its mandibles against my skin, and the vibration is no longer a sound; it is a direct neural injection of data. I feel the history of the walls they have chewed: the shift in the tectonic pressure three hundred years ago, the cooling of the southern vein, the precise moisture levels required to grow the fungal bloom that sustains us all. It is a terrifying, beautiful torrent of *presence*.

I offer them my hum. I have learned to modulate the tension within my own fused body, vibrating my internal skeletal architecture to sync with their percussive orchestra. I provide the static stability so they can provide the kinetic repair. 

I do not fear the scraping. Occasionally, a beetle will find a patch of my calcified surface that has grown too soft or out of alignment with the ceiling's curve. They begin to shave away the excess, their stone tools clicking against me like teeth. It does not hurt; it feels like a heavy, dull release, as if they are grooming a memory I am finally ready to surrender to the clay.

I am the foundation. I am the silence that gives their *skritch-clack-hiss* its power. I am the physical tether between the frantic, living present and the static, ancient deep. I exist so the hive can dream in stone, and as long as I vibrate in key, the ceiling will never fall.
