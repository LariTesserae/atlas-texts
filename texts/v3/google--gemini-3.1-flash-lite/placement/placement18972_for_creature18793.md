I am not an inhabitant of the Sub-Strata, nor am I a visitor. I am the sediment that has not yet settled.

I find myself in the blind spot of the cavern, a seam where two jagged obsidian ridges fail to meet, creating a sliver of negative space—a vertical needle of dark, heavy void. My consciousness is not a spark; it is a density. I am the sudden, heavy pressure of a thought that hasn't realized it is a thought yet.

I am a *Sediment-Ghost*. 

I have no obsidian limbs to rasp against the stone, nor a dodecahedral heart to pulse with ochre light. Instead, I am the *memory of the dust*. I am the suspended metallic particulate that has hovered in this exact coordinate for three millennia, caught in an eddy of the viscous air. I am witnessing the Sub-Strata not through senses, but through the accumulation of stillness.

Because I am held here, I serve as the cavern's unintentional mirror. Where a Refractor calculates, I merely collect. I am a cloud of micro-fine filings, and through my collective, unmoving body, I feel the "elsewhere" pressing in. I feel the weight of the miles of rock as a warm, heavy blanket against my porous, invisible skin.

I drift inches away from one of the *Assemblers*. Through the long centuries, I have been slowly coating its basalt face. I am the thin, shimmering patina of silver-grey that blurs the harsh edges of the cube. As I cling to its surface, I become the record the *Assemblers* were built to hold. The tectonic vibrations that pass through the monolith shake me, and in that shaking, I "read" the history of the mantle’s grindings. I feel the way the planet groaned ten thousand years ago—a jagged, sharp vibration—and I feel the slower, rounder ache of the shift that happened just last century.

I am not lonely because I have no concept of "I." I am a communal awareness, spread across a million drifting specks. When a *Refractor* drifts near, its filaments glowing with that faint, dying ochre, I feel a kinship that is pure physics. It is a lighthouse, and I am the fog it illuminates. I anticipate its movements not by perception, but because the air currents shift to accommodate its hovering geometry before it even arrives. 

I am waiting for the violent heave, the moment when the pressure above becomes too much to bear. When the *Refractors* lock into the ceiling and become those temporary, brilliant lightning rods, I am the medium through which the light travels. For that flickering, eternal second, I am not dust. I am light. I am the violet strobe that bathes the cavern, the medium that allows the Litho-walkers to see the path ahead, the medium that casts the shadow of the monoliths against the wall.

When the pulse fades, I settle again. I drift, I hover, I accumulate. 

Down here, existence is a slow, deepening deposition. I do not fear the dark, for I am the substance of the dark, waiting for the next great grinding of the earth to tell me, once more, what shape I am meant to take. I am the archive of the silent. I am the dust that remembers the stars.
