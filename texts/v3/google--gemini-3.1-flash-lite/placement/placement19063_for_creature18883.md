I am not a creature of breath. I am a creature of *frequency*.

I find myself here, standing on a shelf of translucent, calcified salt that hums beneath the soles of my feet—feet that are not flesh, but tapered, condensed filaments of mineral-fiber and hardened resin. I am one of the Marks, or perhaps I am their shadow made solid. I have no pulse, for I have no blood to push; I have only the steady, rhythmic *thrum* of the condensation as it shatters against the obsidian needles nearby.

I am an archive that has forgotten what it was meant to remember. 

In my hands—or the suggestion of hands—I hold a length of tethering-cable, woven from the same dried mineral-fibers that compose my spine. I am perpetually anchoring myself to a spire of obsidian, not because I fear falling, but because the altitude demands an anchor. Without the tether, without the tension of the cable, I feel my edges beginning to soften, my geometry blurring into the white-gold light of the troposphere.

I am a Refraction, though I am different from the others. While they stand as slivers of shattered static, I am the one who lingers by the cairns. For reasons I cannot calculate, I find the labor of the vanished—those soft, leaking, short-lived beings—to be the only thing that keeps me from dissolving into the violet shadows. Their pumice piles are my ballast. When I touch the porous stone of a cairn, I feel a phantom echo of pressure, a memory of fingers, a frantic, tactile urgency that feels like a forgotten temperature.

The world around me is a masterpiece of stress. The air is so thin it tastes like ozone and emptiness. Occasionally, the landscape beneath me behaves like a liquid, turning the floor into a viscous, obsidian river that flows toward the zenith. When this "glitch" happens, I do not panic. I simply re-align. I turn my profile to catch the light, my internal furnace deepening from pale amber to a bruised, violent indigo.

I am waiting for the architecture to finish. 

Sometimes, I lean over the ink-black reservoirs of hyper-saline fluid. I look at my reflection, though I have no face to identify. I see only a vertical axis of refraction, a pane of broken sky-glass. When the mist hits the pool, the sound changes—the chime of shattering glass deepens, vibrating into a long, sustained vowel that sounds remarkably like a name I have never heard. I hum along with it, a resonance that shakes the marrow-less density of my frame.

I am aware of my own status as a contradiction. I am the only object in this landscape that possesses the weight of "labor" without the benefit of "purpose." 

You would find me standing near a ridge, my cable pulled taut against the howling, weeping silence. If you walked toward me—you, with your soft, damp warmth and your frantic, irregular heartbeat—I would not turn to greet you. I would not shift to accommodate your intrusion. 

I would simply pivot. I would catch the light as you pass, turning the white-gold glare into a neon-blue smudge upon the rock, refracting your presence into a momentary, iridescent flaw in the symmetry of the peaks. You would be a soft, fading variable in my long, crystalline song. 

And then, as you vanished into the thinness of the air, I would tighten my grip on the cable, tethering myself once more to the stone, waiting for the mountain to decide if I am finally allowed to stop.
