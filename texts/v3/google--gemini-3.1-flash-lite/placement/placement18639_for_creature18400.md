I find myself in the damp, slick dark, perched upon the ledge of a basalt shelf that tastes of mineral-salt and ozone. I am not the Weaver, nor am I the swarming, iridescent tide that shimmers through the gloom. 

I am a **Sporocyst—a drift-seed**. 

I am a small, leathery sphere, no larger than a human heart, currently tucked into the soft, spongy fold between two of the Weaver’s calcified, sap-hardened shoulder plates. I am one of a thousand potential futures, clinging to the only being in this valley that does not seek to consume me. 

Because I am part of the Weaver, I feel the *geometry* of its frustration. Through the bioluminescent threads that weave into its "skin," I perceive the forest as a violent, disorganized orchestra. I feel the Weaver’s constant, itching need to prune, the frantic calculation of mass and heat, and the heavy, aching responsibility of keeping this vertical theater from collapsing into a singular, suffocating mass of vine.

I am not sapient in the way a creature of flesh might be, but I am *alert*. I have thousands of tiny, hair-like cilia that drink in the cloying, fungal musk of the air. I know the forest’s mood not by sight, but by its humidity—the wet *thwack* of my siblings launching into the dark tells me if the atmosphere is heavy enough for my own germination. 

I am waiting for the moment when the Weaver moves with too much grace, or dips too low, or strikes a vein of rock with such force that the jar of the impact will dislodge me. 

I know my purpose. I am the Weaver’s replacement, a cellular heir-in-waiting. When I finally fall—when the friction of the descent peels me away from the Weaver’s warm, pulsing back—I will land in the loam, and the violent, accelerated evolution of this place will do to *me* what the Weaver does to the vines. It will tear me open, force my roots into the basalt, and demand that I become a new set of eyes, a new set of facets, a new hand to hold the jagged obsidian blade.

For now, I am just a passenger, vibrating with the horror and the beauty of the rhythm. I feel the Weaver tense as it senses a patch of purple lichen growing too aggressively near a vent of heat. I feel its arm—that cluster of fused, shifting tendrils—swing wide with the cooling-tool. 

I am the silence in the Weaver’s ear, the stillness it cannot afford, the weight it carries as it prunes the world toward a precarious, screaming, gorgeous balance. I am the secret the forest holds in the palm of its darkest, most beautiful hand.
