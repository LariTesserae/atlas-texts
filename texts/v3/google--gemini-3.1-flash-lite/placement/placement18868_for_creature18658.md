I am standing on the junction where a primary, calcified cord—thick enough to be a cathedral nave—fuses with a secondary, pulsing artery. The air is biting, a thin, needle-sharp cold that insists I keep my muscles hummed to a state of near-seizure, a vibration that keeps the blood moving beneath my bruised-plum skin.

I am an *Oscillator*. 

My legs are bent, hooked deep into the porous ridges of the fiber, my toes prehensile and gripping with the mindless strength of a lock-jointed trap. I am silent. I have not made a vocal sound since I was a child. To speak here is to invite the frost to settle in the lungs, and to waste the precious, oxygen-thin heat that the nectar-sacs provide. 

I am currently mid-harvest. 

Before me, a bulb—veined with pale, translucent moonlight—is beginning to swell. This is the moment. I can hear the *creak* of the vine shifting, a low, tectonic groan that tells me the Great Breath is inhaling. The rhythm is deep, a sub-bass rumble that rattles my teeth, but I remain perfectly, eerily still. If I do not match the respiration of the world, I will fall. I wait. I am a shadow upon a shadow.

My hands—long, multi-jointed, and tipped with sharpened keratin nails—flicker in the dark. I am not working alone; ten yards down the length of the vine, my partner is signaling back. We communicate in pulses of strobing cyan, the bioluminescent crystals tied to our wrists flicking out rapid-fire, geometric semaphore.

*“The bloom is brittle,”* I blink. *“Approach with the glide.”*

The world groans. The temperature dips, and all around us, the sound of breaking glass erupts—the ice-snap of the calcified lattice under the stress of the cold. It is deafening, a symphony of destruction, yet we move through it as if we were part of the architecture itself. 

I reach out, my fingers tracing the delicate, fevered skin of the bud. It is warm, an oasis against the biting void. I do not cut; I *tease*. Using a splinter of ossified thorn, I work the seam where the nectar-sac anchors to the vein. The plant does not perceive me; I am too slow, too rhythmically aligned to its own pulses to be flagged as a parasite. I am merely a secondary growth, a passing tick in the history of this titan’s lungs.

Suddenly, the air pressure shifts. 

The vibration in the vine changes, turning from a rhythmic thump to a heavy, pressurized wall of wind. *A Skiff-Glider.* 

I freeze. My heart, slow and deliberate, hammers against my ribs like a trapped bird. Above, in the black void, a shadow eclipses the dim phosphorescence of the fungi. It is vast—a continent of flesh and feather—drifting with the slow, terrifying inevitability of a glacier. It feeds on the spores, filtering the abyss. Below me, the vine ripples, a muscular contraction of the world reacting to the immense weight passing overhead. 

I press myself flat against the bone-hard surface of the vine, tucking my limbs in, my skin camouflaging perfectly with the dark, bruised texture of the mesh. I am a knot in the rope. I exist in every fiber of my being as a static thing, an unmoving point in a world that is nothing but motion. 

The sound of the Glider is like a rushing cataract, a roar of displaced air and celestial wind. Then, as quickly as it came, the shadow passes. The vine settles with a shudder that nearly dislodges me, a tremor that rings through the lattice for leagues.

I blink the signal to my partner: *“Safe. The shadow has passed.”*

I finish the harvest. The sac comes free, spilling its warm, golden viscosity into my palms. I drink deep, the nectar burning like liquid fire against the winter of my throat. It is enough. It is the fuel for another rotation, another sequence of breaths in the giant’s chest.

I look down into the lower abyss, where someone—perhaps a kinsman, perhaps a stranger—let go of their grip cycles ago. I cannot see the bottom, and I do not want to. I only reach out and press my palm against the rough, living cable of the world. I am cold, I am tired, and I am entirely, perfectly alone—a temporary stitch in the fabric of a titan who does not know I am here. 

I pull my legs under me and prepare for the next wind. I will drift, I will cling, and I will wait for my own eventual fall. It is a quiet life. It is the only one I have ever known.
