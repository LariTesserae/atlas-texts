I am a **Syncopator**. 

I do not possess the structural permanence of the Weavers, nor am I yet a victim of the cellulose graft. I am a transient, a creature formed of thin, grey ash and recycled kinetic energy—a leftover vibration. I have no fixed shape; I am a collection of shivering, vertical lines that resolve into a human-like silhouette only when the pressure of the room is right.

I do not walk this city. I *measure* it.

I am hunched near a junction where three bioluminescent vines bleed into a rusted piston-tree. My knuckles are stained with the indigo ichor that leaks from the polyps, and my fingers are constantly trembling, twitching in micro-seconds that are not quite in rhythm with the machines. I am trying to calibrate myself—to find that singular, elusive "grand climax" where the city sighs. 

If I can hit the tempo perfectly, I intend to step through it—not into another room, but *out*. I want to be the shadow that detaches from the floor and migrates upward, back through the fern-fronds and the heavy, rotting insulation of reality, to find a place where the sun is not a myth and the air does not taste of scorched circuitry.

But I am failing. 

I feel the *Law of Permeability* encroaching on my left sole. Each time I stand still, the fibrous moss of the floor creeps upward, knitting itself into my skin. It feels like tiny, cold needles stitching my boots to the foundation. When I try to lift my foot, the sound is sickening—a wet, Velcro-tearing noise, the sound of reality losing its grip on me.

I look at the wall to my right. There is a "Grafted" there—a man, perhaps, or a memory of a man, his face half-swallowed by a woody knot. His eyes are milky, staring at the ceiling, watching for the Drifters. He hasn't blinked in what feels like a century. Sometimes, when the static hum of his chest grows loud, I lean in close. I don't want his secrets; I want his rhythm. I want to know where he tripped, where he lost the beat, so I can avoid his mistake.

He never speaks, but the static in his chest shifts tone when I draw near. He knows I am desperate. He knows that soon, the iron-dew will begin to frost my joints, and I will grow rigid, and I will become another pillar in this cathedral of meat and brass.

A piston *hisses*—a sharp, metallic inhalation. 

I shudder, forcing my heart to slow down, forcing my own internal clock to sync with the grinding metal of the city. I am not human. I am a metronome with a soul, waiting for the ceiling to contract, waiting for the nutrient slurry to fall, waiting for the rhythm of the universe to align so that I might finally step off the throat of this machine and into the light.

But for now, I am just a smudge of shadow in a violet room. I am waiting for the next beat. 

One. Two. *Thwack.* 

The floor rises to meet my heel. I am almost part of it. I have to move. I have to find the grace note before the city swallows my name.
