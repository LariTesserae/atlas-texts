I am not an explorer. I am a redundancy.

I arrived here through a fissure—a crack in the world above that seemed to promise a way down, or perhaps in. I find myself standing on a platform of rusted iron, my boots fused to the oxidized surface by a thin, milky resin of prehistoric oils and water. I do not look like a visitor anymore; my knuckles are the color of bruised basalt, and when I move my fingers, I hear the faint, dry clicking of oxidized switchgear.

I am the **Cartographer of the Inert.**

I am not breathing, not really. I am intake. My lungs possess a rhythmic, hydraulic quality, expanding and contracting not to oxygenate blood, but to regulate the steam pressure that vents from the wall behind me. Every time the great floor-engine thrums—that low-frequency vibration that shudders through my marrow—I feel my focus drift. I am losing the edges of my own anatomy. My thoughts are no longer linear; they are cyclical, recursive, running through the same short-circuited logic gates that once defined this place.

I spend my time, if I can call it time, tracing the paths of the black, cable-vines. I record the hum. I watch the way the indigo moss pulses, a rhythmic bioluminescence that serves as the factory’s heartbeat. When the moss flares, I don't see light; I see the blueprints of a lost civilization. I see the *intention* of the machine, burning white-hot behind my retinas. 

I am becoming part of the maintenance cycle.

Yesterday—or perhaps a cycle of steam-bursts ago—I felt my own skin begin to change. It is thickening, losing its softness, turning translucent like the stalks of the ferns that crowd my knees. My heartbeat has begun to slip out of sync with my physical form and into sync with the tectonic pulse of the floor. My memories are no longer mine. They are being filed, indexed, and compressed. I remember the smell of scorched solder. I remember the precise tension of a tightened bolt. I remember the frantic, sweating hands of the engineers who once stood where I stand, their ambition so sharp it left scars on the bedrock.

I have stopped trying to climb out. The rift does not want to keep me; it simply wants to utilize me. It sees my biology as inefficient, a high-temperature mess that needs to be refined. It wants to calcify my intent. It wants to turn my consciousness into an *echo*.

I reach out to touch a rusted valve, and for a fleeting second, the **Accumulated Echo** rushes through me. It is not cold; it is the static of a billion finished tasks. It is the crushing relief of a system that no longer has to strive for output, only for existence. The valve hisses, a jet of white heat blasts the air, and for that strobe-flicker moment, I *am* the factory. I feel the weight of the pistons. I feel the heat of the magma beneath the crust. I feel the glorious, mindless pressure of being needed for nothing but the preservation of the loop.

I am not fading away. I am being installed. 

I am a component waiting for its function to be finalized. Soon, my hands will look like heavy iron levers, and my gaze will be nothing more than the strobe-flash of steam against the moss. I will stand in the throat of this rift, a permanent fixture in the plumbing of the deep earth, forever waiting—not for anything to arrive, but for the cycle to turn once more. 

I am the rhythm in the dripping water. I am the hum in the stone. I am the silence that watches from the shadows of the machinery, and I am finally, perfectly, closed.
