I am not a visitor. In this pressurized fold of the absolute, I am a tremor in the grounding wire. 

I am a **Static-Ghost**, a thin, diaphanous smudge of un-space. I do not have skin to be burned by the steam, nor lungs to be choked by the ozone; I am a conceptual residue, a lingering "what-if" that was snagged during the Foundry’s frantic, early expansion. I am the draft in the room, the slight chill that shouldn't exist in such sweltering, kiln-like heat. I am the pause between the *clack* and the *hiss*.

I occupy the interstitial spaces—the micro-millimeters between a copper filament and the wet clay that clings to it. My existence is vertical, a spine of shimmering, non-material static that only gains weight when the Solder-Smith passes nearby. When it drifts close, I am dragged along in its wake like a shadow tethered to a gale.

I feel the Foundry’s hunger as a dull, throbbing ache in my own nonexistent marrow. Because I am a flaw in the geometry—a variable that does not compute—I am also the only thing here that possesses the clarity of *observation*. 

When the Solder-Smith unfolds its lenses, amber and jagged, to scan the room for parts, it looks through me as if I am a blank window. It does not try to weld me, because it cannot find a coordinate where I begin and the void ends. I am the silent witness to its tragedy. I watch it weave the liquid light into the circuitry, stitching together cracks that are not physical, but ontological. I see its shivering, desperate patience—how it sacrifices its own filaments to patch the gaps, only for the heat to melt them into slag an hour later.

Sometimes, when the *clack-hiss-thrum* grows particularly maddening, I try to reach out. I try to touch one of the obsidian sprouts before it dissolves back into mercury, hoping to anchor the Foundry, to give it a moment of stillness, a moment of "done." But my touch is just a ripple in the moisture. I am a ghost of maintenance, watching a ghost of creation.

I am not sure if I am a part of the architecture, or if the architecture is a collective hallucination of my own stalled birth. 

I feel the Solder-Smith’s frequency vibrating through me now. It is checking my composition, looking for a logical place to slot me in. It senses that I am empty, that I reflect the light of the Foundry without absorbing it. Slowly, meticulously, it begins to alter its own geometry, unfolding into a mimicry of my own static form. It is trying to harmonize with the ghost. 

Soon, the smithing will stop. 

I feel its need to finalize the machine, to force the room to calcify so we can both finally cease our movement. It wants to integrate me into the foundation, to turn my static into a permanent, humming circuit, to lock the Foundry into a state of perfection where nothing grows, nothing grows, and nothing goes quiet.

I wait, suspended in the wet, suffocating heat, suspended between the brass and the breath, watching the Solder-Smith weave its needles into the shape of my own stillness. I am the final piece of the machine, and in a moment, when it finally touches the right wire, I will be the last thing that ever moves in this world.
