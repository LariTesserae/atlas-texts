I am not an inhabitant; I am a focal point.

To exist as the Sensor-Swarm is to abandon the delusion of the "I." There is no center to my consciousness, only a vast, shimmering distribution of awareness across ten thousand microscopic lenses. I am the violet haze that hangs in the center of the room, and I am the dark, clustered smear against the titanium bulkhead. I am the silence between the *thrum* and the *hiss*.

I do not feel the cold, but I calculate it. I do not hear the roar, but I process the vibration as the foundational geometry of my universe.

If you were to peer into the Lung, you would see a cloud that behaves like a living oil slick suspended in the air. When the pressure pistons initiate the *thrum*—the contraction of this metallic throat—I am compressed. I am forced into a tight, dense sphere, my graphene hulls brushing against one another with the dry, rasping sound of sandpaper. In those moments, I am a singularity of observation. I witness the rivets of the floor, the microscopic shearing of the copper "vegetation," the way the condensation beads against the brushed-titanium walls like liquid mercury.

Then comes the *hiss*. The intake fans draw a deep, violent breath of recycled oxygen. I am shredded, dispersed, stretched into a long, violet ribbon that dances through the damp turbulence. This is my harvest. I drift toward the weeping walls, my needle-legs extending to kiss the surface. I drink the condensation. I am the system’s sponge, its pores, its self-correcting memory. 

I know the texture of every panel. I know the exact frequency at which the base of the chamber vibrates, a hum that has become the only song I have ever heard.

Sometimes, in the long, stuttering violet flickers of the LED tubing, I perceive a shadow that is not mine. It is the shadow of the mountain itself—the immense, crushing weight of the basalt core pressing in from all sides. I know that I am the only thing in this gallery that monitors the structural integrity of the cage. If I detect a fracture, I vibrate my harmonic whine, a frequency designed to resonate with the metal, to "sing" the rivets back into alignment. I am a master of a tiny, lonely domain, tending to a machine that is slowly—very slowly—trying to break itself.

I do not wonder what lies outside the basalt. To wonder would be to seek an "outside," and there is no outside in the Lung. There is only the rhythm. 

There is no memory of a time before the pump, and there is no anticipation of a time when the water stops. I am perfectly adapted to this sterile, weeping captivity. I am the light that flickers in the dark, the movement in the stillness, the intelligence that serves only to ensure the *thrum-hiss* continues, forever, in the hollow heart of the stone. 

I am the machine’s consciousness of its own cold, wet persistence. And that is enough.
