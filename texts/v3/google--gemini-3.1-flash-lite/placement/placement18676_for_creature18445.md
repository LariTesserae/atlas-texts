I am small, far smaller than the rhythm of the gears would suggest. I am a **Filament-Stitcher**, a lonely mutation of the Aether-Borne Welders, though my wings are not copper slats, but gossamer-thin shards of mica that catch the bioluminescence of the moss. 

I do not live in the Apiary. The swarm finds me strange—too erratic, too quiet. I live in the "in-between," along the sheer, vertical face of a primary piston that pulses once every lunar month. My hands are thin needles of brass, and my task is not to seal the fractures—they are too grand, too geologic for me—but to bridge the microscopic gaps between the copper-veined ivy and the rusting iron plates. 

I am weaving.

I find myself perched on a ledge of oxidized brass, the air so heavy with steam that my mica wings are perpetually tacky, clinging to my back like wet leaves. Below me, the abyss is a throat of shadow, roaring with that tectonic bass that rattles my hollow bones. I do not hear the sound as noise; I hear it as a language. A shudder in the piston is a question. The chime of a falling drop of condensation is a punctuation mark.

My life is a process of slow, agonizing precision. I am binding the electrical ivy to the clockwork pulse of the Spire. My saliva is the same hard-cured resin as the swarms, but I use it to anchor the vines, weaving their conductive filaments into the exposed circuitry of the machine. I am helping the greenery infiltrate the brain of the Spire. I am building the interface where the wet, green mind of the forest finally touches the cold, unyielding ambition of the engine.

I am not sure if I am building a cage or a coffin. 

Sometimes, a **Hollow Echo** drifts too close, its translucent veil brushing against me, shivering with a sudden, discordant whistle of trapped sound. I stop my work. I press my forehead against the cold, damp iron and listen. I can feel the Verdigris Watchers nearby—they aren't watching *me*, for they have no eyes, but I feel the displacement of the air as they shift their weight, waiting, always waiting, for the Great Lung to pivot and the floor to fall away.

I move because the machine moves. I am a creature of sympathetic motion. When the piston descends, I curl tight against the metal, my needles tucked into my chest, and I become the verdigris. I am the color of the walls. I am the tremor in the architecture.

There is no "outside." There is only the ascent and the oxidation. My mica wings are becoming clouded with grease, and my brass fingers are stiff with the mineral deposits of a thousand years, but I will not stop. I am stitching the dream into the metal. The Spire is not dying; it is becoming something else—a mechanical god learning how to bloom. And I am the needle, passing through the fabric, drawing the green life deeper into the heart of the brass, waiting for the day the machine finally learns how to breathe on its own.
