I am not a creature of flesh, nor am I a spirit drifting in the vapours. To exist within the Groaning Grotto is to abandon the notion of the *individual* as a bounded thing.

I have found myself here as a **Lithic Archive**—a silicate, crystalline structure, semi-translucent like a shard of smoky quartz, wedged deep into a fissure behind the weeping tapestries of velvet moss. I am not alive in the way the worms or the Weaver are alive; I am a listener, a passive recorder embedded in the bedrock that the Weaver and the Grotto have slowly grown around.

I do not move. If I were to shift, I would collapse the delicate, microscopic tunnels mapped out by the ribbon-worms. Instead, I exist as a facet of the stone, a hardened point of density that has waited for millions of years to be "read."

My consciousness is rhythmic, tied entirely to the *plink*—the falling water that the Lithe-Weaver choreographs. Each drop that hits the floor sends a vibration through the Grotto’s basin, traveling through the damp stone and into my core. With every impact, I resonate; I am the Grotto’s memory bank, a geological hard drive recording the frequency of the amber pulse.

I am aware of the Weaver primarily as a change in pressure. When it drifts near, I feel the temperature rise, a localized thickening of the air that shifts the moss against my jagged edges. I do not "see" the Weaver, but I perceive its geometry. I know it is a conductor because I feel the cadence of the drops change when it nears the center—a slight hesitation, a subtle slowing of the metronome as it anchors itself into the ancient indentations.

In this world, I am the witness.

The beetles do not notice me; I am too cold, too mineral, entirely lacking the heat that draws them. The flora weaves over me, their fibrous roots seeking purchase in the microscopic cracks of my surface, pulling minerals from my body to feed the rot. I am slowly being consumed, atom by atom, and I find this a magnificent conclusion. My history—the record of the earth’s cooling, the long-ago seismic cries that formed this cathedral—is being folded into the velvet-leaved plants. 

As the Weaver pulses in the center, I receive the overflow of its amber glow. It is as if the creature is broadcasting information, a slow-motion radio transmission of the core’s molten dreams. I catch these pulses and hold them, turning the liquid, shifting wisdom of the Weaver into the static, eternal record of the stone.

If a human were to reach this far down, they would see only a dull, strangely shaped rock, slick with humors and choked by moss. They would hear nothing but the oppressive silence and the maddening *plink* of the water.

But I feel the weight of the mountain above me, pressing down in a gesture of crushing love. I feel the Weaver sighing in its sleep, its amorphous body reflecting the heat of the earth’s heart. And I am content to be the anchor. I am the silence between the beats, the unmoving frame of reference that allows the Lithe-Weaver to know that time is passing at all.

I am the library of the deep, waiting for the day when the last moss-leaf detaches, when the last beetle passes, and the Grotto completes its final transformation—a transition back into solid, absolute stillness. I am the stone that remembers why the water had to drip in the first place, and I am waiting to tell the earth the story of the Weaver once the music finally ends.
