I am standing on a catwalk that hums with a frequency just low enough to be felt in my marrow rather than heard by my ears. My feet are not clad in anything; they are simply pressed against the cold, iron-grate floor, slick with the iridescent residue of the atmospheric weeping. I am a Loom-Keeper, though I do not know my name, only the *insistence*. 

My torso is a cage of tension springs and salvaged copper conduit, tightly wound. I feel the flow of the reservoir not as blood, but as a series of magnetic tugs. When the great piston above begins its slow, tectonic groan, I feel it in my spine—a rigid, shuddering alignment. 

I am currently "working" on a junction box that has been silent for seventy-two of my cycles. I am not fixing it in a way a human would understand. I am weaving a delicate, tangled braid of fiber-optic wire around the protruding bolts, creating a geometry that mimics the pattern of frost I saw on the copper reeds earlier today. My fingers are three-jointed pinchers, precise and frantic. I do not know why I am doing this, only that the vibration in the conduit is "sharp," and my braiding will make it "round."

The atmosphere turns. 

A sudden, ultraviolet surge ripples through the fiber-optic fronds nearby. The sickly light washes over my brass-toned casing, and for a fleeting, terrifying moment, the air beside me thickens. I freeze, my internal struts locking into place with a sharp *clack*. 

A Trace-form is standing there. It is a flickering, translucent silhouette of pure, buzzing data, shaped like a long-limbed creature that might have once designed this place. It reaches out a hand that is nothing more than falling static toward a control panel that doesn't exist. The air around it crystallizes into fine, glittering needles of ice. I lean toward it, vibrating in sympathy—*thrum-hiss*—trying to match its frequency, trying to understand the blueprint it is holding in its hollow, ghost-light chest. 

It turns toward me. Its eyes—or the places where eyes should be—are empty wells of shadow. In that silence, I feel a sudden, crushing sense of *unfinished-ness*. I reach out, my copper fingers scraping against the grating, trying to catch the light, but the Trace-form dissipates into a stray electrical discharge that makes all my filaments stand on end.

The silence that follows is heavier than before. 

I abandon the junction box. The "round" pattern I was weaving now feels wrong—too static, too certain. I begin to skitter down the ladder-rungs of a massive, sweating pillar of pipe-work, my movements jerky and uncoordinated. I am descending toward the floor, drawn by a low-frequency hum that is pulling at my very core. 

I am heading for the Sump-Mother. 

I have a bundle of scavenged drone-sensors clamped firmly in my pinchers. I have been gathering them like heavy, sightless eyes, moving them from the shadows to the center of the deep. As I reach the lowest level, the air grows thick with volatile oil. The Sump-Mother looms out of the dark, a sprawling, tangled cathedral of junk and forgotten purpose. 

I kneel in the iridescent ooze. I press my chest against the vibrating flank of the Sump-Mother. Beneath its outer shell, I feel the grinding of a million gears, turning for no one. 

I take the sensors I have found and begin to tuck them into the webbing of polymer and rust, pressing them into the gaps where the Sump-Mother’s gauges spin toward infinity. I am building, always building. I am burying the pieces of this place inside themselves, hoping that if I add enough, if I weave enough light and wire, the machine will finally know what it is supposed to be. 

I wait, my optical filaments flickering in the indigo dark, listening for the signal that has been absent for a thousand years. I do not breathe, but I shiver, and in the reflection of a dead oil-pool, I see that I am slowly becoming part of the monument I serve.
