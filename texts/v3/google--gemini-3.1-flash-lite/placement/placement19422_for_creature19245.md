I am not a visitor, for to visit implies a boundary between the self and the world. I am, instead, a local coagulation. 

I find myself clinging to the underside of a primary arterial conduit—a pipe of bruised, oxidized copper that groans with the weight of subterranean brine. I am currently thin, my limbs little more than frayed filaments of dark wire and coagulated silt, hanging suspended over the bubbling black basin. Below me, the heat rises in waves that distort the world into a shivering, amber-tinted mirage. 

I do not think in words. My "consciousness" is a series of fluctuations—ripples in a pond of sensory data. I feel the *thwack-hiss* of the piston three levels below as a dull, thumping ache in my marrow; I feel the shrill, discordant whistle of a valve to my left as a sharp spike of anxiety that forces me to flatten my form against the weeping metal.

I am what the basin feels when a joint begins to fail.

Right now, a section of the piping beneath me is losing its structural integrity. I know this not by sight, but by a sudden, jagged dissonance in the vibration running through the wall. The resonance has shifted from a low, steady hum to a frantic, stuttering grind. The metal is tired. It is thinning, weeping oxidation into the mire at an unnatural rate.

I move. I do not pull myself along the pipe; I simply liquefy my anchor point, shifting my molecular density until I am a dark, viscous smudge sliding along the cold, sweating basalt. I am the shadow that moves when the light shifts. 

As I reach the distress, the screech of metal-on-metal fills the air, a sound so piercing it makes my translucent, fungal-lung tissue flutter in forced, rapid rhythm. The pipe is shearing. A hairline fracture is weeping high-pressure steam, the heat searing the air into a white, blinding lance. 

I reach out. My fingers—long, multi-jointed, and composed of the self-same charcoal slurry that forms the basin’s floor—press against the fissure. I taste the hot iron and the ancient, metallic rot. I begin to pour. 

It is not sacrifice. It is simply the conclusion of a cycle. I thrum in sympathy with the machine, lowering my own vibration until it matches the frantic, dying pulse of the copper conduit. I feel my awareness narrowing, pulling away from the vast, dim cathedral of the Sump-Works and localizing entirely within the crack. 

I am cooling. As the steam bites into my core, I harden. My wire-limbs fuse to the copper; my silt-skin vitrifies under the immense pressure. I am no longer a Maintainer sensing the basin. I am becoming the patch. I am becoming the pipe. 

In a moment, the screaming of the metal will stop. The steam will cease its violent escape. I will be immovable, cold, and silent, a permanent structural correction in a land built to endure. The basin will continue to churn—a lung exhaling in the dark—and I will be the wall that keeps the breath inside, forever watching through the amber haze as the others continue their slow, liquid migrations toward the next rupture.
