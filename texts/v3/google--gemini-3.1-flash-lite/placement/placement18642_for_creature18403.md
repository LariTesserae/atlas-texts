I am standing on a catwalk that looks like a rusted ribcage, suspended over a chasm of churning, obsidian-black vapor. The air here tastes of ozone and wet, bitter earth, and my skin—a mottled, oxidized blue—feels the constant, shivering thrum of the great steam valves somewhere deep in the gut of the world.

I am a **Conductor-Scribe**.

My limbs feel unnervingly long today, stretched by the local fluctuation. My knees have the slight, sickening hitch of a joint that has been pulled a few inches past its natural limit, and my fingers, tipped with dull, hardened horn, tap a rhythmic cadence against the rivet-studded wall. I am not human, not anymore. I am a vessel for the mountain’s nervous system.

My clothes are a shifting, heavy second skin of Soot-Armor. The indigo fungal spores have bloomed across my shoulders and collarbone in a thick, velvety crust. They are restless, glowing with a soft, bioluminescent pulse that dims to a pale violet whenever the tunnel narrows. If I move too quickly, the spores release a fine, black dust that hangs in the air, creating a tiny, localized constellation around my head. I have to be careful; if I agitate the fungus too much, it will signal my anxiety to the walls, and the wall-geometry will interpret my panic as an invitation to spiral again.

I am currently "working." My task is to soothe a weeping fracture in the copper shielding near the main turbine. The metal here is sweating—great, heavy beads of condensation that roll down the rivets like tears. I press my palm flat against the vibrating copper, closing my eyes, which are nothing more than cool, dark quartz. I don't see the world; I *feel* the city’s ontological dissonance.

The vibration traveling up my arm is frantic, erratic. It tells me that the mountain beneath us is turning in its sleep, and the iron grafts are beginning to groan at their anchor points. I hum back, a low, resonant note that vibrates in the hollows of my own chest, mirroring the groan of the turbine. I am trying to convince the city that it is stable, that it is anchored, that the space around us is not currently folding into an infinite loop. 

To my left, a jagged protrusion of obsidian has erupted from the vertical surface of the pipe wall since the last shift. It is sharp, translucent, and beautiful, still coated in a layer of primordial heat-haze. I watch it with quiet, detached longing. I remember when this entire junction was flat, simple steel. Now, it is jagged, unpredictable, and growing.

There is a moment of stillness, the "interstitial breath" where the grinding of the gears falls away for a heartbeat. In this window, I catch a reflection of myself in a puddle of stagnant water trapped in a cooling vat. I see a creature that is half-person, half-scaffolding, my body draped against the wall at an angle that defies Euclidean logic, my armor sparking with a soft, warning orange. 

I don't look up, even though the vapor above has thinned for a second, revealing a sliver of nothingness that feels like a threat. I look down, toward the core, toward the deep, rhythmic pulsing of the tectonic nerves. 

I am waiting for the moment when my hum will finally lose its pitch. I can feel the slow, patient crystallization of my own joints, the way my skin is starting to knit into the oxidized copper of the wall. When I run out of breath—or when the mountain decides it has had enough of my presence—I will simply stop. I will become the very thing I am currently tending: a rusted, unmoving part of the skeleton, a Conductor who finally found their way home into the architecture.

The city shudders. My Soot-Armor flares a brilliant, blinding crimson. I press my forehead against the cold, iron pipe, closing my eyes, and melt into the vibrating dark.
