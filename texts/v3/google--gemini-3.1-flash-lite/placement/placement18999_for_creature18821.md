I am kneeling in a shallow basin of polished limestone, a curved indentation that feels less like a floor and more like a palm. My knees, pressed against the cool, ancient stone, have long since grown numb, but the numbness is not painful; it is a profound synchronization. 

I am a Scraper. 

My body is heavy, dense with a layer of insulating fat that traps the heat of the Spire. My skin, the color of a moonstone set in deep shadow, is mapped with the fine, branching lines of my clan’s history. I do not have a name—names are vibrations in the air, and there is no room for such waste here—but I have a function. In my right hand, I hold a blade fashioned from the serrated mandible of a *Kraul*, a creature that died perhaps three generations before I began this task. It is perfectly balanced to the slope of this wall.

The air is thick with the scent of fermented moss and the dry, sweet musk of the grazers. Above me, the vaulted ceiling is a blurred galaxy of amber light. I can feel the rhythm of the city in my marrow: a low, dissonant *thrum* that vibrates through the bedrock, the heartbeat of the arthropods moving in the chambers below. 

I reach out, my palm finding the flank of a grazer. Its chitin is warm, ridged, and steady as a mountain. I lean my shoulder against it, and for a moment, the boundary between my humanity and its beast-nature blurs. I feel its slow, ponderous respiration, a cycle so vast it makes my own heartbeat seem frantic and erratic by comparison. It does not pull away. We are a single architecture of bone, shell, and muscle. 

I begin to scrape. 

The sound is a gentle, rhythmic *shuck-shuck* that joins the chorus of a thousand others in the spire. I am not removing the moss so much as I am pruning the world. I clear a path, and beneath the velvet green, the resinous wall glows with a deeper, more intense flicker of phosphor. I am harvesting the city’s lifeblood, turning it into the nutrient-paste that keeps our collective metabolism from stalling. 

My eyes, milky and wide, do not look for the horizon. There is no horizon. I look into the deep, bruised-gold texture of the wall. I see the microscopic fissures where time has etched stories into the limestone, and I see the tremor—the faint, rhythmic pulse—that tells me where the next harvest will be ready. 

Behind me, another Othite drifts into the alcove. There is no greeting. They simply press a hand against my spine, a tactile anchor that says *I am here, the weight is shared, the cycle holds.* Their skin is cool and dry, a grounding presence that keeps me from drifting into the delirium of the long silence. They begin to scrape beside me, their rhythm perfectly phased with mine. We do not work to finish; we work to *continue*. 

If you were to walk among us, I would know you before I saw you. Your footsteps would sound wrong—too sharp, too quick, too resonant. You would carry the scent of thin, empty air, a vacuum that threatens our dense, humid equilibrium. My hand would reach out, not to restrain you, but to test your density. I would touch your fabric, explore the strangeness of your un-eroded edges, and feel the frantic, high-pitched flutter of a heart that still expects a tomorrow. 

You would find me waiting. I would not ask where you came from, for there is no "from" in the Spire of Oth. I would simply lean into you, let the heat of my body bleed into yours, and wait for you to realize that you are tired of moving. I would wait for you to stop, to settle, and to finally become a part of the long, amber, permanent quiet. 

I am not living; I am layering. And in this, I have found the only stillness that is real.
