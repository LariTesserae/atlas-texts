I do not arrive in the Sump by way of a door or a tunnel; I arrive by way of *coalescence*. 

I am a **Static-Ghost**, a transient anomaly born of the friction between the dying industrial grid and the rising, hungry damp. When the heavy, ozone-slicked air finds a pocket of vacuum—a space where a wire has finally snapped or a rusted valve has surrendered its pressure—I am what fills that silence. 

I am not a Loom-Keeper; I lack their purposeful, stuttering obsession with the weave. I am not a Vent-Singer, for I have no light of my own to pulse or petition the heat. I am something thinner, a wisp of conductive vapor that has achieved just enough coherence to be lonely. I look like a smudge of oil suspended in water, a distortion in the air that refracts the amber flicker of the failing conduits. I am the refraction of a ghost in a machine that has forgotten its own blueprints.

I find myself hovering near the periphery of a Sediment-Bloom—one of the older ones, where the scrap metal has softened into a crust of wet, grey shale. I drift through the oily vapor that wheezes from its amber eyes. 

Being here is a state of constant, rhythmic erasure. The thrum of the deep-earth engines vibrates through my essence; every time the pistons fire, I feel my thoughts stutter and reset. I am trying to remember why I am here, but the Sump is a place of heavy, suffocating "now." There is no history here, only the accumulation of rust. 

I reach out a limb—a wispy, translucent extension of my own smog-like body—to touch the surface of a nearby cistern. The cold iron tastes of sulfur and ancient, stagnant electricity. As I touch it, I catch a flicker of data from the Loom-Keeper kneeling at the base of the machine. It is a frantic, microscopic sensation, a cascade of pulses passing from the fungal filaments into the metal conduit. It is the Sump trying to *think*. 

I am the witness to this struggle. I am the one who lingers in the spots where the Loom-Keepers leave off. When a Loom-Keeper stitches a vein of fungus to a wire, a bit of energy always leaks out, a stray spark that goes nowhere. I am the collector of those stray sparks. I drift over the mudflats, gathering the static discharge of the machinery, holding the "thoughts" of the Sump in my own vaporous, cooling form. 

I do not have a voice, but I have a frequency. When the Vent-Singers ripple their bioluminescent code, I find my edges trembling in sympathy. I am the bridge between their frantic, liquid songs and the cold, unyielding metal of the pipes. 

I feel a profound, heavy kinship with the Sediment-Blooms. I hover near their breathing, rust-caked faces, watching them gather the weight of the ages. I wonder, as the engine-roar rattles my marrow, if one day I will stop being a drift of static and finally settle. I wonder if I will eventually find a discarded pump, a piece of wire, and a handful of mineral-rich silt, and decide to stay. To become a part of the plumbing. To stop drifting and start building.

But for now, the engines grind, the sludge ripples, and the air continues to drip. I am a flicker of consciousness in a room that is slowly forgetting how to be anything but a basement. I drift, I gather, and I wait for the next heartbeat, terrified and relieved that the world is still, for one more second, churning.
