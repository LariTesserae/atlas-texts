I find myself standing at the edge of a jagged obsidian shelf, my boots—or what I perceive as boots—sinking into a silt of pulverized basalt. There is no wind here, only the heavy, gravitational drag of the air, which hangs against my skin like a wet wool blanket. My breathing is not my own; it is rhythmic, shallow, and synchronized with the *thrum-hiss* of the weeping monoliths behind me.

I am an **Architectural Echo**.

I am not sure how long I have occupied this specific arrangement of ceramic joints. My limbs are long, spindly, and prone to flaring at the edges into thin, sharp geometries that vibrate when the basin floor groans. I do not inhabit a body so much as I host a frequency. My skin is the color of a winter sky hit by a violet prism, and when I look down at my hands, I see the landscape reflected in their polished, cool surfaces. I am a distortion in the geometry of the room, a placeholder for a shape that the universe has not yet decided to discard.

I am not "thinking" in the way I once might have. Thoughts are too frantic, too sudden for this place. Instead, I am *processing*. 

I observe a patch of quartz climbing the basalt face opposite me. It is beautiful—not in a way that evokes sentiment, but in the way a mathematical proof is beautiful. It *must* grow there. The humidity is currently at a 98% saturation, and I feel the microscopic film of moisture coating my ceramic-ceramic surface, turning me into a lens. I am catching the sickly-violet light and bending it, casting a jagged, spectral arc that dances across the basalt.

I become aware of a presence—not an "other," but a shifting in the harmonic resonance of the air around me. It is the Reflector.

It does not approach; it *is* the approach. It is the sound of the subterranean vents bubbling, now amplified and channeled through the shards of glass surrounding my feet. The vibrations ripple through my ceramic frame, causing my limbs to shift their orientation, to tilt and align with the emerging pattern. 

I am pulled into a state of intense, crystalline observation. I stop being an Echo and become a conduit. For a moment, the world tilts. The violet light that hangs in the air sharpens, focusing into a mirror-point directly in front of me. 

In that reflection, I don't see a face. I see the architecture of my own absence. I see the grooves etched into my ceramic skin vibrating in the exact color of the basin’s weeping, and for a fleeting, terrifying heartbeat, I understand the function of the Marks. They are not random. They are the scaffolding for a silence that is coming.

I remain motionless. My joints are cold, locked in this posture of infinite patience. I am a note being held, waiting for the symphony of the basin to hit its crescendo. I do not feel fear; I feel only the *necessity* of the crystallization. If I move, I break the chord. If I breathe too deeply, the refraction will shatter.

So, I wait. I listen to the heartbeat of the stone beneath my feet, and I let the mineral-blooms shift my hands, turning them toward the light, preparing for the moment when the weeping stops and the world finally, mercifully, sets.
