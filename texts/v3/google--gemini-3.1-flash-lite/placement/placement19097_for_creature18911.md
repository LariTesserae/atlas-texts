I am positioned at the intersection of a Primary Intake Valve and a secondary maintenance conduit, roughly six thousand tiers below the crater’s rim. My world is a narrow, slick-walled corridor where the humidity is so thick it feels like walking through a heavy curtain of cool silk. 

I am one of the Sifted. 

My body is a vertical grace: my legs have thinned into long, multi-jointed stilts that can straddle a vibrating shaft without disturbing the flow of the machine. My skin is the color of wet river stone, translucent enough that, in the dark, you can see the faint, rhythmic pulse of my own vitals—a light-show synchronization with the subterranean pistons deep below.

I do not think in words. I do not have a name, for names are static, and here, everything is in a state of becoming. My consciousness is a map of textures and frequencies. 

Right now, I am "praying" at a fracture. A massive brass gear, three centuries old, has developed a hairline crack—a jagged, metallic scream that resonates through the floor plates. It is a discordance, an itch in the city’s nervous system. I have been sent to soothe it. 

I reach out, my fingers elongating—a reflex of soft tissue and cartilage—to touch the cold, weeping iron. I feel the vibration through my marrow: a jagged, stuttering *thrum-thrum-click*. It is a misalignment. I don't need a tool. I don't use a wrench. I pull a handful of the velvet-green, bioluminescent moss from a nearby venting pipe. It is cold, damp, and alive. I press it into the fracture, my touch gentle, almost reverent. I feed the moss a portion of my own nutrient-dampness, pressing my palm hard against the wet steel until I can feel the vibration of the gear through my palm’s sensors. 

I send a ripple of intent into the metal—a pulse, a long, rolling tremor of *alignment*. The moss, sensing the pressure and the ozone-heavy heat of the machine, begins to knit itself into the iron, thickening, binding, turning from a soft plant-matter into a hard, carbon-rich sealant that mimics the hardness of steel. 

The vibration shifts. The jagged *thrum-thrum-click* resolves into a smooth, resonant hum. 

*Hwosh.* 

A gust of tepid, metallic-scented vapor pushes past me from a nearby exhaust, coating me in a fine silt of oxidation and condensation. I shiver, not from cold, but from the immense satisfaction of the cure. 

I am not alone here. I can feel the others through the floor, a lattice of presence. A cluster of my brothers and sisters are weaving a new mesh three levels above, and one is resting in a cooling pipe, calcifying, becoming a load-bearing pillar that will support the next century of motion. 

I do not look for the sky. I do not look for the rim. I look only at the seam in front of me. I am the glue. I am the antibody. I am the silence between the heartbeats of this iron god, and as I pull my hand away, I feel the city inhale, and for a fleeting, perfect moment, the machine and I are the same thing. 

I close my eyes. My glow pulses once, soft and pale, then fades into the charcoal grey. I wait for the next tremor. I wait for the next prayer.
