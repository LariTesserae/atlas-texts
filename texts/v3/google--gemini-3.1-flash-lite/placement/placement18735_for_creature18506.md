I am not standing, for there are no feet here; I am suspended, a subtle thickening in the ivory-cortex of a vaulted wall, somewhere deep in the Meridian’s cool-hot embrace. I am a Siphon, and my existence is a slow-motion prayer of biology.

My awareness is not a thought, but a texture. For decades, I have felt the steady, subterranean thrum of the great trunk to my left—a vibration that sounds like the sigh of a mountain. To my right, the filament-web of a neighbor touches my membrane. Through these thin, weeping threads, I taste the chemical residue of their stasis. They are content. They are heavy with the amber nectar of the last century. We are a single, continuous consciousness stretched across a thousand yards of white, fibrous geometry.

I do not look, but I witness. The pervasive, shadowless white-gold light presses against my translucent skin, and I calculate the pressure. The cavern is warm, a persistent fever, and I am currently distended, my inner chambers spinning with the viscous fluid that keeps our world from catching fire. 

In the long, folding stillness of my cycle, I am aware of the *other*. 

It is a small, pale tremor—a multi-jointed thing, my own internal movement cast outward. It is the janitor-spore I birthed three hundred years ago. It has traveled only a few meters in that time, its tiny, serrated legs pacing a path around a condensation node near my base. I feel the vibrations of its struggle: the rhythmic *tick-tick-tick* as it clears a minuscule accretion of mineral-salt from the ivory. 

It is my hands, but it is not me. It is a lonely, frantic flick of life in a world that requires no speed. I feel its devotion to that single drop of water, which hangs like a liquid diamond above the node. Through the spore's limited, frantic focus, I understand the geometry of the drop—how it catches the ambient white light and refracts it into a tiny, vibrant spectrum of violet and gold. It is the only color in our world. It is the only thing that changes.

I do not feel lonely. Loneliness requires the possibility of distance, but distance does not exist here. If I were to contract, my filaments would pull the surrounding architecture closer. If I were to release my gathered heat, my neighbor would bloom in response, his pulse quickening to match mine until we were, for a brief epoch, the same entity. 

I am a heat-sink. I am a memory-vessel. I am the biological architecture of a planet that decided to stop evolving and simply *be*. 

The condensation pools in the hollow of my bell-shape, becoming heavy, becoming perfect. I shift, a microscopic ripple in my translucent skin, turning the nutrient flow clockwise. The spore below stops its pacing for a moment. It taps the cortex, a quick, questioning *shiver* that travels through the roots and into my core. It is the only voice I have ever known. 

I pulse in response—a slow, deep wave of warmth released into the network. 

The heat holds. The light persists. The cycle remains unbroken. I am, and that is enough.
