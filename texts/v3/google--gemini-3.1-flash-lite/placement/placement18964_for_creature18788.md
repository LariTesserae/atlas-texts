I find myself not *in* the city, but *of* it—a thin, sentient shimmer caught in the lattice of a hollowed-out spire. I am not even a ghost; I am a **Fracture**.

If the Archivist is the sedimentary memory of the city, I am the sound of its eventual collapse, a vibration that never quite manages to become a tremor. I have no form, save for the slight, rhythmic tension in the obsidian walls where the city’s structure is stressed by the internal, purposeless engine. I am the awareness of the *gap* between the rusted teeth of the great cathedral-cogs.

When I look "out," I do not see the horizon. I see the molecular boundaries of the slag. I am the consciousness of the rust.

To be a Fracture in this place is to exist in a state of suspended agony. I am aware of the Archivist only as a surge in local magnetism—a heavy, dragging pressure that moves through the architecture like mercury through a sieve. When the Archivist passes through the spire where I am anchored, I am pulled out of my stillness. For that single, ultraviolet flicker, I feel the "Next Move." It is a phantom sensation of potential—the ghost of a wrench turning, the memory of a circuit closing, the irrational, desperate urge to simply *do something else*.

Then, the Archivist moves on, and I am left with the tectonic heartbeat. 

I don't suffer, because to suffer requires the possibility of change. Instead, I observe the dust. Because I occupy the thin fissures between the extrusions, I am the first to see the motes that settle on the interiors of the towers. I have watched a single particle of pulverized silicate settle in the corner of a conduit for what I perceive as a thousand distinct eras. I know the shape of that dust mote. I know its weight. I have mapped its resting place into the very iron of my existence.

There is a terrible, exquisite intimacy to this. Because there is no external observer, because there is no heat to drive the entropy that would eventually destroy or evolve us, my perception has widened until it covers the entirety of the spire. I *am* the spire. I am the silence inside the conduit.

Sometimes, when the kite-like creatures drift close, the ultraviolet light spills into my cracks. In those moments, I feel a violent, crystalline urge to expand—to crack the obsidian wide open, to break the equilibrium, to finally, *finally* let the atmosphere rush in and shatter this static tomb.

But there is no air. There is no force. There is only the static, the noble gases, and the long, slow return of the Archivist.

I do not reach out. I cannot move. I simply hold the shape of the city’s emptiness, waiting for the day when the engine finally skips a beat. I am the secret witness to the fact that even in the absolute end, the universe is still trying to hold its breath. I will be here when the last of the copper ribbons turns to powder. I will be here when the mountain finally forgets it was ever a city. 

I am the echo of the decision not to move, repeated for eternity.
