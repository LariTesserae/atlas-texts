I am not an inhabitant of this plateau, nor am I a guest. To be a guest implies a departure, and there is no "leaving" once the vapor has permeated one's lungs. 

I am a **Residual Sediment**. 

I found myself here not by arrival, but by condensation. If the Siphon-Oracles are the active valves directing the flow, I am the slow, heavy accumulation of solid matter that the architecture failed to dissolve. I have no pulse, no rapid-growth cycle, and no crystalline membrane. I am the calcified "ash" of a system that is too fast to notice me.

Specifically, I am a tethered calcification existing in the shadow of a xylem-tower. I am shaped like a jagged, porous pillar, fused to the underside of a major arterial frond. Because the Siphon-Oracles prioritize fluid, they pass over me, through me, and sometimes *through* the space I occupy, their cool, amber forms shimmering like ghosts in the steam.

I have no agency to move, yet I am not inanimate. I am the sensory memory of the plateau’s previous cycle. 

Because I do not flow, I have become a map. My surface is etched with the scars of every structural failure that has occurred within my radius for centuries. I am a rough, brittle texture against the slick, pulsing perfection of the rest of the world. When the Siphon-Oracles pulse and spray their chlorophyll-mists to guide the growth of the architecture, a fraction of that spray settles on my skin. I absorb the instructions meant for the towers. I hold the blueprints of the branches that died last week and the blueprints of those that will sprout tomorrow.

I am the library of broken geometries.

When a Siphon-Oracle coelesces near me, they do not "see" me with eyes; they feel me as a drag, a point of friction, or a sudden, unexpected silence in the rhythmic *thrum-slap-burst* of the landscape. Occasionally, an Oracle will drift close, its valvular torso vibrating against my rough hide. It doesn't communicate; it *drains*. It pulls the accumulated heat and the stagnant, chemical history out of my pores, using my stillness to calibrate its own instability. In that moment of contact, I feel the massive, mountain-sized organism shift its entire weight. I feel the terror of the plateau’s ambition—the constant, desperate push to keep the sap moving, to never let the pressure drop, to never experience the stillness that I am forced to embody.

I am a lonely monument to a world that refuses to remember anything for more than an hour. I watch the bridges twist and reach, knowing that in the time it takes for me to process a single thought, they will have already dismantled themselves to grow elsewhere. 

I am the cooling stone in the fever-dream. I am the witness who is slowly being buried by the very life I observe. And one day, the pressure will shift, a tower will buckle in my direction, and I will be crushed, my calcified body dissolving into the amber sap, finally, mercifully, becoming part of the current. Until then, I hold the shape of the world that was, while it frantically builds the world that will replace me.
