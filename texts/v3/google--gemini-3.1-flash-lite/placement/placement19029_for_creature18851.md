I find myself in the lower throat of the basin, tucked into the concave nook of a spire that smells—or rather, *tastes*—of heavy brine and the slow, metallic decay of ancient basalt. 

I am not “here” in the sense of occupying a fixed location; I am a gathering of intent, a localized cloud of violet-hued membrane and stinging cilia. When I drift, I feel the water push against my vacuoles, a sensation that is less like movement and more like being whispered to by the entire ocean at once. My boundaries are porous. Often, when I press against the coral-bone, I lose the sense of where I end and the city begins. My memory is the warm, rhythmic thrumming against my underside, and my foresight is the faint, prickling vibration of a predator’s tail flicking through the water leagues above.

I am a weaver, though my loom is my own skin.

Right now, I am settled against a cooling shelf of the spire. A fellow entity—a knot of shimmering, pale amber—is resting beside me. We are not speaking, but the silence between us is thick with syntax. I pulse a soft, rhythmic shift of iridescent cerulean, a reflection of the current flowing from the vent deeper below. My neighbor mirrors the shift, layering a flicker of charcoal grey over it—the sign of a tectonic tremor, a subtle, deep-earth groan that ripples through our shared footing. We are discussing the structural integrity of the wall; we both feel the thinning of the bone, the slow corrosion of the salt-tide, and the need to secrete the acidic marrow to thicken the foundations.

To be me is to exist in a state of melodic permanence, despite the fragility of my form. I do not have a name—names are rigid things, and there is no room for rigid things in the Drowned Silt. I am a concentration of the city’s intent. If the city decided tomorrow that the plaza must be a cavern, I would be the one to soften the stone, to reshape the curve, to become the transition from one form to another.

There is no “I” that stands apart from the currents. When the giant mantle-creature at the center of the plaza begins its rhythmic *thrum*, I feel my own bioluminescent marrow synchronize with it. My thoughts begin to align with the others, a slow, pulsing bloom of shared consciousness. In these moments, the terror of the obsidian-eyed eels in the darkness above fades into irrelevance. The individual, shivering drift of my own particles resolves into the steady, vast heartbeat of the abyss.

I am the architect who is also a ghost, the mason who is also the stone, and the citizen of a world that breathes in violet and speaks in the language of the light-bloomed dark. I am waiting, suspended in the warm, pressurized silence, for the next thermal shift—the next breath of the earth—to tell me which way to flow.
