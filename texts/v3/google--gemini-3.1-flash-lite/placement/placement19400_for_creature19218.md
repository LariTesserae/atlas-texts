I am standing on a rusted catwalk that overhangs the abyssal throat of the third terrace. The metal beneath my bare feet is not cold; it is feverish, buzzing with the relentless, sub-audible labor of the turbines six levels below. My skin, coated in the evening’s layer of harvested mineral-slick, catches the gray, diffused light and holds it like a faint, bruised sheen.

I am a tender of the conduits, a segment of the Circle of the Low-Brass. 

To exist here is to forfeit the concept of *self* as an isolated entity. I am not a person looking at the city; I am a vibration sensing the integrity of the mountain. My ears are useless, long ago filled with the persistent, weighted pressure of the Basin’s roar, but my palms are acute. They are my eyes, my tongue, my history.

I press my left hand against the primary steam-artery, a copper pipe as thick as a cellar trunk. It is shuddering violently, a high-frequency tremor that races up my arm and settles behind my eyes. I close my lids, and I do not see darkness; I see the flow of the pressurized water. I feel, deep in the iron, a minor occlusion—a hard, calcified knot of silt near the elbow joint. 

I reach into the pouch at my side, woven from the soft, blue-glowing lichen that thrives on the moisture I provide. I pull out a long, slender splinter of obsidian. With a precise, fluid motion timed to the *thrum-thrum-hiss* of the nearby valve, I press my fingers into a seam on the pipe’s casing. I do not struggle against the weight of the air; I lean into it, my body becoming a cantilever, a biological extension of the pipe itself.

I am not alone on this terrace. Beyond the veil of hanging mist, I see the faint, rhythmic pulse of another—a shimmering, electric-blue silhouette moving along a suspension bridge fifty yards away. They are also vibrating against the architecture, transmitting a complex, bass-heavy grammar into the stone. I feel their passage through the floorboards: *a quick, sharp ripple, a question of pressure, a warning of the mountain’s shifting humidity.*

I answer them not with sound, but by tightening my own core, pulsing my diaphragmatic rhythm against the iron structure. *The obstruction is cleared,* I broadcast through the metal, *the flow is stable.* The blue silhouette pauses, acknowledges the signal with a slow, resonant shudder in the bridge, and dissolves back into the silver twilight.

I am hungry, but it is a slow, cold hunger—the simple need to replenish the gel on my limbs. I move toward the condensation traps, my gait a rolling, liquid slide that avoids the shock of the terrace’s uneven edges. In the traps, the translucent jellies wait, clinging to the dripping obsidian like living dew. As I approach, they stir, sensing the unique vibration of my skin. 

I reach into the shallow, mineral-rich pool, and one of the organisms flows toward my palm, a cool, gelatinous pressure that feels like a promise. It leaves behind a thin, nourishing smear of residue. I rub this into the cracked, weeping pores of my forearm. The sting of it is sharp, almost ecstatic, a moment of profound communion between the creature, the condensate, and my own thinning blood.

For a long, unchanging moment, I stand motionless at the edge of the abyss. The clouds are hanging lower today, the weeping of the mountain intensifying into a steady, rhythmic drip. The turbines in the deep dark below shift their frequency—a heavy, tectonic groan that vibrates through my marrow. 

I feel the Cycle deep within my hip bone, the subtle, pre-natal shift of the mountain preparing to offer a new Kin. My thoughts are not my own; they are the smooth, circular, unerring logic of the gears. 

I am where the water meets the iron. I am the silence inside the roar. When the turbine turns, I turn with it, waiting for the next, inevitable beat of this beautiful, rusting heart.
