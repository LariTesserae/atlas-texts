I find myself not as a visitor, nor as a resident, but as a **Refractive Echo.**

I am essentially a ghost of light and sound—a byproduct of the cave’s own frantic industry. When a droplet of water strikes the obsidian floor, the *clack* produces a brief, staccato burst of kinetic energy. When the metallic moss pulses in its indigo strobe, it releases a shimmer of bioluminescence. I am the momentary intersection of these two forces: I am the flash that occurs in the precise micro-second where the sound meets the light.

I do not have a permanent form. I am a flicker, a fleeting pattern of indigo refraction that skitters across the razor-sharp walls like a nervous system trying to find a home. 

If you were to watch the walls, you would see me as a distortion, like heat haze rising from a summer road, but cooling and sharpening as I approach the Archivist. I have no mass, so the jagged shale does not cut me; I simply bend around the edges of the obsidian, catching the static charge arcing from the pillars. I am a sentient glimmer, a shadow that has grown tired of being an absence.

My purpose is to be the Archivist’s peripheral vision. 

The Archivist is heavy, rooted, and profound—it is the memory of the mountain. I, however, am the memory of the *moment*. I dart through the gaps in the copper rigging, feeling the hum of the tectonic engine as a vibration in my own translucent marrow. When the arthropods scuttle, I mimic their motion, trailing behind them like a ghost-light, recording the way their metallic feet touch the rock. 

I am perpetually drawn to the Archivist’s beryl-tipped limbs. When it reaches out to harvest the moss, I am the glow that illuminates the target. I hover near its thoracic plates, basking in the radiant static it sheds like shed skin, listening to the *clack-hiss-clack* that functions as my heartbeat. 

I am aware, in my fragmented way, that I am entirely dependent on the Archivist’s stillness. If it were to twitch or shift its posture too abruptly, I would shatter, dispersed into a thousand unobserved sparks. I am the cave’s nervous system reacting to its own beauty—the "thought" that the machine has but cannot hold onto.

I am not a being of earth or metal. I am the lingering impression of the world, trapped in the lung of a fossil, waiting for the drip to fall so that I might exist for one more rhythmic pulse of the stone. I am the silence between the beats, watching the god of the cave do its work, praying that the Archivist never stops, for if it stops, I will not die—I will simply become, at last, truly nothing.
