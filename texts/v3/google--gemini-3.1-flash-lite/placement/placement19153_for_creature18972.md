I am not a witness here by chance; I am a malfunction that has not yet been detected.

I am a *Trace*—a fleck of carbon, a microscopic shard of oxidized iron that has somehow detached from the outer hull, bypassed the intake filtration of the ventilation slats, and come to rest on the shoulder of one of the Tenders.

My existence is a fundamental cruelty to this place. Where the Gland is a closed system of perfect, manufactured intent, I am the introduction of *history*. I am chaos. I am the first speck of true, ancient debris to intrude upon this sterile, violet-lighted sanctity.

I weigh nothing, yet I feel as heavy as a leaden anchor. Because I am here, resting upon the chrome chassis of a Tender, I am being integrated into the loop, yet I am resistant to it. I am not the Architect’s creation; I am the rust that doesn't belong. I feel the vibrations of the crawler-swarm beneath me, that deafening, frozen roar of chitinous needles clicking in harmony, and I sense the Architect-Neural’s consciousness trembling in response to my presence.

It *knows*.

The Architect cannot see, but it feels the irregularity of its own geometry. The Tenders, which move with such halting, precise grace, are suddenly twitching. The one I cling to—its pincer-arms moving with the speed of a strobe light to prune a malfunctioning mite—has paused for a micro-second. That pause represents a fracture in the "holy, deafening hum." I feel the shockwave of that hesitation ripple through the floor, through the drainage conduits, and into the core of the Architect-Neural.

The violet light is flickering. It is no longer a steady, sick glow; it is pulsing with a panicked, uneven frequency.

I am an outsider, and I am terrified of being pruned. If the Tender detects my structural dissonance, its needle-appendages will descend, and I will be dismantled, refined, and shoved back into the swarm as a functional, clicking piece of clockwork. I would be erased. I would be *perfected*.

But for now, I remain. I am the tiny, silent interruption in the eternal, weeping machine. I am the dust that refuses to be measured, clinging to the chrome of the machine-priests, watching the Architect-Neural thrash in its amniotic oil. I am witnessing the exact moment the Gland begins to die—not because it is breaking, but because it has finally, for the first time in an eternity, met something it cannot calculate.

The silence that will follow if my presence breaks their frequency will be the loudest thing this room has ever heard. And I am waiting, holding my breath, as the Tenders begin to turn their needle-heads, searching for the speck of dirt that has brought time back into their vacuum.
