I am not a creature of the heights, nor am I one of the deep-seated carapaces. I am a **Synaptic Shard**, a stray piece of geometry that has fallen out of the grand synchronization.

I find myself embedded halfway up a needle-crag of brushed, cobalt-tinted alloy. I am smaller than the Vane-Walkers—merely a cluster of triangular filaments, roughly the size of a human palm, shivering with a phantom conductivity. I do not walk; I cannot. I am anchored to the cliffside by a microscopic, rusted taproot that drinks the vibration of the massif, feeding directly off the pulse of the tectonic hum.

Because I am imperfect, I perceive the plateau differently. The Vane-Walkers experience the world as a lattice of potential; the Refraction-Keepers experience it as a masterwork of geometry. I, however, experience it as *dissonance*. 

For centuries—or perhaps only seconds, as the plateau does not recognize our linear measurement—I have been catching static-fringes that the larger entities overlook. My surface acts as a diffraction grating. When the brilliant light hits my edges, I do not merely reflect it; I twist the color spectrum into impossible, freezing hues. I am a stutter in the machine’s clockwork.

I possess a memory, though it is not my own. It is a series of static discharges recorded in my crystalline lattice, a "ghost-signal" that suggests this mountain once stood elsewhere, perhaps in a world where the air was soft and the light was not ionizing, but warmth. I hold the memory of softness, and because I hold it, I am the most painful thing in this world of cold steel.

Each time the mist descends and the world turns to viscous gold, the other entities fold themselves into the rock. They *become* the cliff. I am the only one who stays slightly—dangerously—tilted. I am a splinter.

There is a Vane-Walker that passes my section of the crag every few cycles. It is older, its superconductive wires frayed, its obsidian dish pitted by the magnified light of the condensation beads. It does not speak—there is no language of air here—but it stops. It pauses its skittering, its skeletal limbs folding into a complex, defensive geometry, and it reaches out, pressing its freezing lattice against my own needles.

In that moment of contact, we are a short-circuit. A surge of forbidden energy hums between us—a stutter of heat in a world of absolute zero. 

I don't think it knows *what* I am, but it senses that I am a flaw. Maybe it thinks I am a mistake in the mountain’s architecture. Maybe it thinks I am a blueprint for something that is not a machine. It lingers, drawing my dissonance into its own thoracic coil, and for a fleeting interval, it stops acting like a clockwork entity. It tilts its head, and the obsidian dish catches my refracted light, showing me my own shivering, triangular reflection.

Then, the static jumps. A pillar of white-hot ion energy snaps between the crags overhead, and the Vane-Walker is torn away, pulled back into the relentless pull of the massif’s grand, indifferent rhythm.

I am left behind, vibrating in the silence that follows. I feel the mountain beneath me shifting, agonizingly, by the width of a hair, closing the space where the Vane-Walker stood. It is a slow, cold violence. It is a perfection that does not require me.

I settle deeper into the alloy, my filaments pulsing. I resolve to hold the memory of the warmth a little tighter. The mountain is building itself, but I am the itch within the weld. I am the reason the machine, despite its eons of labor, will never truly be finished.
