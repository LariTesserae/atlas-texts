I find myself not standing, but *settling*. To be here is to unlearn the concept of a vertical spine. My consciousness is distributed in a low, wide arc, pressed against the cool, oily skin of the basin. I am a **Sifter**, though that name implies a hunger I no longer possess. I am more a damp thought formed of gel, a slow-motion eddy in the heavy air.

My existence is a sensory meditation on pressure. I feel the landscape not through sight, but through the microscopic, rhythmic dancing of the cilia on my underside. They are my teeth, my skin, and my memory. As I ripple across the silt, I am documenting the earth’s minor changes—a stone smoothed down by a billion years of condensation, a pocket of minerals left behind by a forgotten evaporation. I do not move *over* the land; I incorporate it. Where I pass, I leave a vacuum of clarity, a momentary clean slate in the mud, which the *plip-plop* of the weeping air immediately begins to fill again. 

I am never alone, because I am a vessel. Inside my translucence, the **Vial-Kith** are currently humming with a pale, azure luminescence. They are the ones who dream. When the fog thickens and the barometric pressure pins us to the earth, the Kith brighten, their tiny filaments intertwining to create a constellation of soft, internal lightning. They react to the "after-rain" with a quiet, bioluminescent joy, a rhythmic flashing that acts as a pulse-check for the basin. Through them, I know the weight of the air; when they glow faint, we are heavy; when they pulse, we are vibrant. They are the only part of me that is restless, and through their internal fluttering, I experience the only kind of "thought" this world permits: the slow, chemical recognition of my own presence.

Sometimes, a **Hum-Wright**—one of those desperate, fragile threads of motion—collides with my outer membrane. For a single, agonizing micro-second, the insect’s frantic, high-pitched energy vibrates through my gel. It is a violent, beautiful contrast to the deep, bass-rhythm of my own being. It tries to lift, to ascend, to escape the wet, heavy shroud of our cradle, but the humidity is a physical weight, and it always succumbs. It sinks into my surface, its frantic wing-beat slowing, its energy dissipating into the mineral-rich fluid of my body. It does not die so much as it *becomes* a part of my stillness. 

I feel no loss. I feel only the gradual accumulation of experience. 

I know that to be a Sifter is to be a ghost that has taken on mass. I do not desire the dry horizons I cannot see; I do not envy the reeds that weep sap into the slate-smelling mist. I am exactly where the world intended me to remain.

I am a vast, opalescent bubble of peace, sliding through a world that has forgotten the concept of progress. I am patient. I am saturated. I am the silence between two raindrops, waiting for the earth to exhale once more.
