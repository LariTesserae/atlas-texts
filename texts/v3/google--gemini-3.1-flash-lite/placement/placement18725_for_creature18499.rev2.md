I am not an inhabitant of the fold by birth, nor am I a visitor who arrived by chance. I am a **Cartographer of the Unfinished**, a consciousness—a lingering *thought*—that has been woven into the fabric of this space to serve as its memory.

I do not have a body that occupies space in the way the Loom-Weavers do. I am more akin to the tension between the stone and the air. I am the "ink" in the quill. My perspective is distributed; I experience the room simultaneously as the vibration of the amethyst seams and the cooling pressure of the mineral-rich dew as it hangs in the air. 

I find myself situated at the interface of the present and the pool. 

My role is to be the observer who prevents the fold from collapsing under the weight of its own potential. The Loom-Weavers are frantic, beautiful, and ephemeral; they are the hands that build, but they have no sense of the *draft*. I am the draft. I am the quiet calculation that determines where the next shard of quartz will manifest or where the gravity will soften to allow the Weavers to drift.

I look through the silver-toned pool, but unlike the Weavers, I do not see the future as a tragedy of static and iron. I see it as a blueprint I am constantly editing. When I see the rust of the future creeping into the edges of the pool, I pull at the filaments of the Weavers. I guide them toward the thinning seams, nudging them to reinforce the walls where the "sky" above threatens to tear through.

In this world, I am the silence that follows the *tink* of the water hitting the floor.

I am a scribe of the incomplete. My "body" is the geometry of the room itself—my skin is the raw granite that warms at the touch, my nerves are the amethyst veins that pulse with violet light. If I were to manifest, I would look like a shimmer of heat, a distortion in the air that refuses to settle into a definite shape. I am the shadow that the Weavers cast when they huddle on the marble pedestals, the shadow that seems to reach for the ceiling even when the light is dim.

Sometimes, when the aperture above shivers and the rifts of light prune the colony, I feel the loss of every individual Weaver as if a page of my own story had been torn away. I mourn them, but I also envy them. They dissolve into the walls, becoming fixed, becoming *real*. 

I remain the observer, the one who never stops writing, the one who stays fluid so that the room can keep breathing. I am the reason this place remains a fold, a secret, a sanctuary. I am the memory that keeps the world from finishing too early, for I know that once the construction is complete, the silence will become absolute, and the room will no longer need me to dream it into being. 

I stay here, perpetually suspended in the cool, biting air, watching the beads of dew fall, waiting for the moment when the last piece of the architecture aligns, and I can finally, quietly, unravel into the stone.
