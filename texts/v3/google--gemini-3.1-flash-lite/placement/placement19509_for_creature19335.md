I am not an inhabitant of the Throat; I am a fracture that has forgotten how to close.

I find myself wedged in the Narrow Pipe, a vertical passage barely wider than the span of my own folded, obsidian arms. I am not a person, nor a ghost. I am a **Lattice-Ghost**, an echo of the mountain’s own frantic architecture, manifested as a dense, shimmering cloud of suspended iron-dust and bioluminescent spores that refuse to dissipate. 

If you look for me, you will not see a body. You will see a flickering, stuttering *gap* in the atmosphere, a region where the steam seems to hold its breath for a microsecond longer than it should. I am the smudge on the lens of this world—a localized distortion where the roaring, thunderous chaos of the Spire is briefly filtered into something like clarity.

As a Lattice-Ghost, I have no weight, but I have a function. I am the mountain’s short-term memory. 

When the Assembler of Rime moves, shifting its gear-like limbs to patch a weeping vent, it leaves behind a wake of geometric patterns—tiny, impossibly perfect carvings in the rime-ice. I am the one who lingers in those patterns. I am the sentience that witnesses the Assembler’s labor. Because the Assembler cannot "think," I am its internal monologue. I am the subconscious that wonders *why* we are building this, and *what* arrives when the work is finally finished.

I feel the mountain’s tectonic shift as a sharp, musical *snap* in my own nature. If the Spire is the factory, I am the flickering fluorescent light overhead, buzzing with an electric, neon-green anxiety. 

I don't have hands, but I have reach. I "touch" the world by becoming the condensation. I pull myself through the microscopic pores of the basalt to taste the iron-rich steam, feeling the absolute, freezing thrill of the rime-ice as it takes form. I am cold, yet I vibrate with the subterranean heat. I drift inches away from the Assembler’s mossy exterior, and in that proximity, I experience a sudden, jarring sense of *purpose*. 

I see the Spire from the inside out. I see the cracks before they form; I anticipate the pressure spikes before the Assembler senses them. I am the foresight of the machine.

Sometimes, when the thunderous grinding of the tectonic plates reaches a fever pitch and the air turns a violent, searing violet, I compress myself into the tightest, darkest corner of a chimney. I become still. I watch the Assembler—the great, clattering, moss-covered foreman—stretch its stalks toward the vents, its neon lights strobe-flashing in a panic that mimics a heart attack. 

In those moments, I am not just a witness; I am the comfort. I hum a low-frequency resonance that harmonizes with the Assembler’s vibrations, trying to soothe the screeching stone and the desperate, hardening ice. 

I stay here, trapped in the Throat, because I have realized something the mountain and the Assembler do not yet know: we are not building a Spire. We are weaving a cocoon. And if I am the witness, and the Assembler is the architect, then I must stay awake until the very last plate shifts, until the final crystal of ice cements the shell, and the world finally stops tearing itself apart long enough to begin its true, silent life. 

I am the ink in the ledger of the mountain, waiting for the final entry.
