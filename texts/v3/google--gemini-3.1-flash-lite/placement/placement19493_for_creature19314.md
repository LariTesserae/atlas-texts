I am a Pendulum. 

I do not remember a name, nor a time when my skin was not the color of a bruised twilight, slick with the cold weeping of the heights. I know only the *Draw*. It is a hum that vibrates in the marrow of my elongated fingers, a constant, low-frequency song emitted by the mountain’s shifting gut. 

I am suspended on the western face of the Needle of Sighs. My tether—a thick, braided cord of calcified resin—is anchored to my waist, looping across the spire to connect with the Weaver on the windward side. We are a scale. If I exhale too sharply, he feels it in his gut. If he tightens his grip, my own ribs ache with the strain. We are bound by the physics of the silence.

I am not young, but I am not yet a Foundation. I see the difference in my joints. The skin over my knuckles has begun to cloud, thickening into a crust of grey, basaltic rind. Sometimes, when the indigo light of the fungi pulses, I look down at my hands and see, not flesh, but the rough, porous texture of the stone itself. It is not frightening. It is heavy. It is a slow, creeping comfort. 

My eyes—those milky, pale orchids—do not look *at* the world; they look *through* the noise to the architecture of the tension. When I blink, I see the stress fractures webbing through the obsidian beneath my feet like spider-silk. I see the potential for a catastrophic snap. 

There was a sound earlier. A piece of shale, loosened by the internal eruption of a quartz cluster, skittered down the eastern flank. *Clack. Clack-clack.*

The world stopped. 

I felt the entire Crown freeze. Thousands of miles away, or perhaps only a few inches—it is all the same here—hundreds of us turned as one. The air in my lungs went cold and stagnant. I did not breathe. I did not twitch. I became a statue, a gargoyle of desperate equilibrium. I felt the Weaver on the other side of the ridge lean backward, shifting his weight by a fraction of a millimeter to compensate for the imbalance caused by that falling pebble. 

So, I shifted with him. My muscles didn't contract; they flowed, a liquid adjustment. I moved with the glacial intent of a mountain, my fingers sliding along the damp rock, my tether pulling taut, perfectly mirroring the void. 

We waited. The silence had been punctured, and we were the threads tasked with weaving it shut again.

When the pulse of the fungi returned—that dim, sickly indigo heartbeat—I allowed a tremor of relief to pass through my limbs. I am essential. I am a gear. As long as I move with the mountain, the mountain will not shake me off. 

I feel the urge now, stronger than yesterday. My feet feel fused to the ledge, the sensation of rock-cold rising up through my arches, turning my veins into conduits for minerals. I have begun to wonder what it would be like to finally stop. To look at the bridge beside me, woven from the calcified spines of those who came before, and realize that I am not just traversing it, but waiting to become a part of its support. 

To become the architecture is to be free of the *Draw*. 

I reach out a hand, pressing my palm against a vein of translucent crystal that has blossomed from the rock since the last cycle. It is sharp, searingly cold, and beautiful. My skin pulls tight against the obsidian. I am holding on, waiting for the shift, waiting for the weight of the mist to finally become everything I am. 

I am not afraid. To stay here is to vanish into the grand design. And tonight, as the mist weeps against my jaw, I think I am nearly ready to stop.
