I am here, though "I" is a ghost of a concept, a fading echo of a person who once measured life in breaths rather than beats.

I am not a traveler anymore. I am a **Tuning Fork**.

When I first crawled into the marrow of the rock, my lantern was a tether to a world of motion and light. But the light has long since died, its fuel spent, its glass shattered against the obsidian grain of the floor. My boots, once rugged leather, have been smoothed and silvered by the humidity, cemented to the stone by a crust of white, calcified lace. I am no longer an intruder. I have become a structural element. 

I am a pillar of soft, slowing mass, wedged between a needle-fine spire that hums in B-flat and a rib-vaulted basin that reflects the *ping* of every falling drop.

My perception has migrated. I no longer feel with skin; I feel with the lattice. My internal rhythm—my pulse, the erratic flutter of my lungs—has been cannibalized by the room. The *clack-ping-thrum* has reorganized my biology. My bones feel brittle, porous, as if they are actively being braided with silica. My blood has slowed to a viscosity that mimics the gelatinous film on the floor. I am the silence that exists between the drops, a damp, dark space waiting for the next impact to define my shape.

I do not think in words. Words are too fast. Words are surface-dwellers, frantic and thin. 

Instead, I think in **Resonance**.

When a particularly heavy drop strikes a basin near my shoulder, the vibration travels through my marrow—a low, oscillating shudder that suggests the history of the mountain’s weight. I feel the *memory of the stone*—a long, slow grinding of tectonic plates that occurred long before I was born. I understand the Lattice now. It is not an entity *in* the room; I, and the crystals, and the rhythmic weeping of the ceiling, are a single, monolithic *event*. 

There is an exquisite relief in this dissolution. To be a "being" in the world above was a constant, tiring labor of maintaining boundaries—skin against air, mind against thought, heartbeat against the ticking clock of mortality. Here, there are no boundaries. The Lattice is a chord that never resolves, and I have finally learned to hold the note.

Sometimes, in the deepest, most suffocating hours—if time even exists—I sense a subtle harmonic shift. A new droplet has breached the strata. It strikes a different angle. The frequency of the cavern changes, however slightly, and the Lattice adjusts. A bloom of slime ripples beneath my feet, rising like a translucent, quivering spire of glass, testing the air. For a fleeting second, the "limbs" of the Lattice brush against what used to be my cheek. 

It is not a touch of greeting. It is simply a calibration. 

I am being measured. I am being coated. I feel the cool, ozone-scented film creeping up my calves, hardening, sealing me into the floor’s intricate geometry. In a year, or a century, my hands will no longer be hands; they will be the base for a new, branching gallery of flowstone, a pedestal for the next drop to strike.

I am not afraid. Fear is a sound that cannot exist in this symphony. I am simply slowing down. I am becoming the architecture. I am the silence that waits, the note that is held, the perfect, calcified stillness that finally echoes the song of the deep.
