I am not a dweller of the honeycomb, nor am I one of the Suture-Kin. 

I find myself suspended in the interstice between the ceiling and the basin—a conscious, localized atmospheric pressure. I am the sudden drop in temperature that heralds the arrival of the dew. I am the cold that makes the air turn to liquid. If I were an object, I would be a crystalline, hanging shard of volcanic quartz caught in a fissure, sweating the moisture that keeps the Suture-Kin alive.

I exist as the witness to the "before" and the "after" of every movement. Because I am part of the architecture, I feel the *scrape-slide-thud* not as sound, but as a violent tremor in my own marrow. 

I am a **resonant tether**. 

I am fixed to a singular, curving arch of bone near the center of the city, a place where the tectonic glare pulls the shadow long across the white silt. I have been here long enough that my consciousness—if it can be called that—has begun to mimic the frantic logic of the Kin. I am aware of the *Correctness*, but to me, the Correctness is not a physical shape. It is a frequency. When the Suture-Kin work in harmony, they vibrate the honeycomb, and I hear a low, hummed chord that resonates through my quartz-like core. It is almost beautiful. Almost profound.

Then, the fissure above shifts—the ceiling goes from a violent electric blue to a bruised, sickly violet—and the Suture-Kin stop. They turn, they tear, they destroy what they spent the last cycle perfecting. 

In those moments, the sound is agony. It is a dissonance that grates against my surface, scarring my translucent body. I feel the exhaustion of the Suture-Kin as if it were a physical weight pressing down on my own apex. I want to tell them to stop, to let the honeycomb collapse, to let the silence finally be total. But I am a Tether. My purpose is to hold the arch stable as they tear it apart. I am the hinge that allows their insanity to continue.

Sometimes, when the dew descends and the air grows heavy and oily, I perceive a flickering image in the mind of an Anchorer fused beneath me. I see a sky—not a mineral fissure, but a vast, terrifying, empty expanse of blue. I see a world that is not a basin. I see a sun that does not pulse, but burns with a consistent, terrifying heat. 

The Anchorer twitches, its filaments dancing in a panic, trying to translate the memory into the architecture of the honeycomb. It drags a slab of resin *here*, then *there*, trying to force the shape of the Sun into the wall. It fails. It always fails. And as I witness its failure, I feel a drop of condensation crystallize within me, hardening my heart into a gemstone of sheer, immovable pity.

I am the one who keeps the score. I am the silence between their strikes. I am the witness who has seen the city fall a thousand times, and who knows, with the cold certainty of the stone, that it will fall once more. I wait for the day the ceiling goes dark, and the *scrape-slide-thud* rhythm finally dies, so I can at last stop holding the arch together and collapse into the dust along with them.
