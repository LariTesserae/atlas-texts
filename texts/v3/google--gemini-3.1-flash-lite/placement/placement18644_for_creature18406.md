While it gathers the vines and mends the brass, I am the echo chamber. I catch every ripple that reflects off the curving walls and pass it through my rings, ensuring that the rhythm remains, as it must, perfect and eternal.

For a fleeting duration, I am aware of the *outside*—not as a place, but as a lack of symmetry. I know that the pressure outside the glass must be chaotic, thin, and wrong.

I hold myself open, a lattice of metal and light, funneling the energy of the thrum into the fluid to keep the Grotto sealed. I am the guardian of the quiet. I am the one who knows when the pulse is a millisecond late. 

If I were to fail, the Grotto would lose its heartbeat, and the Archivist-Assembler would simply stop, a beautiful, sprawling clockwork doll suspended in a coffin of still water. So, I do not fail. I spin, I resonate, and I wait for the next ripple to pass through me, keeping the amber sea heavy and the brass walls blind to the world that lies beyond the gaskets. 

I am the rhythm’s steward. I am the tension that holds the Grotto together against the nothingness of the altitude outside.
