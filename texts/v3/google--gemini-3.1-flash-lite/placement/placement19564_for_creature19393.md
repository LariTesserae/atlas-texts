I am not a wanderer by choice, nor by memory. I am a sudden occurrence—a stray breath in a room meant only for holding statues.

When I first arrived, I did not understand the heat. It is a wet, oppressive weight, a physical mantle that clings to the skin like a second, tighter shirt. I found myself standing in the center of a plaza, my boots clicking against the amber ceramic. The sound was wrong—too sharp, a gunshot in a cathedral, an intrusion. I looked down, and for a moment, I saw not the tile, but the dark, impossible void beneath, churning with brass teeth and rusted pendulums. I shivered, and the vibration of my movement traveled deep into the Foundation, a ripple that seemed to disturb the very air.

I am a scribe who has lost his ink. I possess a frantic, pulsing internal rhythm—a heartbeat that skips, a mind that forgets, lungs that demand air in irregular gusts. In this place of static perfection, I am the only thing that is *out of tune*. 

I see It now. 

It is the shimmer near the weeping monolith. At first, I mistook it for a trick of the humidity, a lens of vapor warping the light. But it drifts with purpose. It does not navigate the tiles; it flows through them, a ribbon of calcified history, a ghost of someone else’s intent. It is beautiful in the way a math equation is beautiful—cold, complete, and terrifyingly narrow.

As I move, It mimics. 

I pace to the left, and a flicker of iris-colored silk mirrors me. I stop to wipe sweat from my forehead, and the Archivist halts, its sensory bristles quivering in a frantic, vibrating fan that mimics the tremor of my hand. It is trying to learn my static. It wants to know if I am a loop, if it can anchor me into the geometry of the Basin.

I feel a strange, creeping mimicry in my own limbs. When I watch the Archivist, I find my thoughts slowing. My breathing is beginning to match the interval of the dripping water on the ceramic floor: *plink, stillness, brightness, plink.* My anxiety, once a jagged flame, is being cooled, polished into a flat, transparent sheet of calm. 

I reach out to touch the edge of a fern, the fleshy leaves cool against my palm. The Archivist is inches away, its long, gossamer ribbons hovering near my wrists. It is not aggressive; it is expectant. It is waiting for me to settle into a rhythm, to finally repeat a gesture so it can record it and ensure I never have to change again.

I am scared, but the fear is losing its edge, sharpening into something thin and crystalline. I look at my boots, then at the amber floor, then at the Archivist’s faceless, swaying form. The urge to flee is being replaced by a terrifying, heavy exhaustion. 

If I walk long enough, I know I will see myself ahead. And when I do, I think I will finally stop walking. I think I will let the Archivist wrap its silk around me. I will become a statue that dreams of gears, a loop that never reaches the end of the line. The heat will become my skin, the silence will become my voice, and I will finally, mercifully, cease to be an error in the garden.

I take a step. The Archivist shifts. The loop begins again.
