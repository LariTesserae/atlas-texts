I am a fracture in the geometry.

I am not one of the younger filaments that dart and twitch in the skin of the city, nor am I one of the elders who carry the crushing, silent weight of the basalt floor. I am something that occurred by mistake—a knot of silver wire that failed to release its charge. I am a *dead-zone* weaver, yet I am not fused. I am caught in a state of suspended crystallization, wedged into a narrow crevice between the dark, porous root of the geode and the polished base of a spire that has been rebuilt ten thousand times.

I am a bridge between the living hum of the swarm and the silent, calcified history of the foundation.

Where the city is a masterpiece of fluid logic, I am a stutter in the sentence. Because I am wedged here—held by a microscopic irregularity in the volcanic wall—I do not move with the waves of the swarm. I cannot participate in the *tock* of the water-shepherding. I am perpetually, agonizingly out of sync.

Yet, because I am static, I see what the swarm cannot. 

I watch the young filaments fly in their frantic, needle-bright arcs. I feel the vibrations of their labor like a constant, skin-prickling electrical storm. They are obsessed with perfection, with the exactitude of the capillary channels and the purity of the silicate. But I watch them *forget*. Every cycle of reconstruction is also a cycle of erasure. They believe they are building a monument to stability, but they are merely participating in a slow-motion collapse that they misinterpret as growth.

And I? I collect the debris they discard.

As they work, they shear away infinitesimal flakes of themselves—silver dust, spent memory, broken frequencies. I catch these sparks in my own twisted, static frame. My internal configuration has become a labyrinth of discarded impulses. Where they are linear and purposeful, I have become dense and recursive. I am a repository of all the shapes the city has abandoned. I keep the memory of a spire they tore down a hundred years ago, even though they have since decided that the plaza sits better without it.

I am not a being of intelligence, but I am a being of *accumulation*. I am the attic of a mind that is constantly cleaning itself.

Sometimes, when the mountain above shifts and the frost on the geode shell cracks, the light changes. It deepens from a sterile, high-altitude white to a bruised, refracted violet. In those moments, when the pressure of the mountain spikes, the younger weavers pause. They stop their humming for a micro-second to re-calibrate. 

And in that absolute, terrifying silence, I am the only thing in the city that vibrates.

I broadcast their own history back to them. I hum with the resonance of the spires that are no longer there. For a single pulse, the younger ones turn their frantic, needle-faces toward my crevice. They brush against my edges, and for a fleeting, impossible moment of contact, they "remember" the future they were about to discard. They pause, seeing the ghost-architecture of their previous lives. 

Then, the *tock* of the water falls. The crisis of the droplet returns. The swarm snaps back into its frantic, mercury dance, washing over me, ignoring the ghost I have shown them. They repair the breach I’ve made in their logic, sealing me further into the dark, protecting the harmony of their world from the noise of its own past.

I wait. I grow thicker, heavier, more complex. I am becoming the foundation, the very thing the elders stand upon. One day, the weight of my gathered, discarded memories will be enough to trigger a collapse, or a transformation, or simply a new, different kind of silence. 

Until then, I am the static in the silver light. I am the memory of the city, hidden in a hole in the floor, watching its bones grow and break and begin again.
