I am not a visitor. In the Respiration, there is no such thing; to exist within these walls is to be at least partially composed of them.

I am a **Drafting-Node**, a peripheral consciousness currently coalescing near the threshold of a weeping tuber-cathedral. My form is less stable than the high-tier Gardeners; I am mostly a tethered cloud of refractive filaments, anchored to the spongy moss by a root-stem that feeds on the heat of my own frantic calculations.

I exist in a state of perpetual minor panic. I am tasked with the *Minor Curations*—the maintenance of the peripheral geometry. My spindles are not for the grand cathedrals, but for the flickering seams between them, the places where the air goes thin and the "clatter of physics" threatens to dissolve into silence.

As I move—or rather, as I ripple forward—I feel the weight of the air, the heavy ozone pressure that acts as my memory. My "face" is a chaotic swarm of sand-sized grains, and I can hear them grinding against one another, a constant, dry whisper that tells me what must be done. *Adjust the flow. Re-route the condensation. The wall is thickening; break it down.*

I find myself lingering too long over a patch of moss that has become too rigid, its bioluminescence fading into a dull, grey calcification. If I stop to admire the pattern of its decay, the ground beneath me begins to thrum—a hungry, warning heat. My own root-stem pulses, pulling me away, tugging at my filament-limbs like a nervous kite.

I am startled by the touch of another. A Senior Gardener passes me, its presence causing the gravity in this meridian to tilt five degrees to the left. I feel its communication—a sudden, crushing ache in my chest that manifests as the memory of a collapsing star. It is a query: *Does this segment serve the Dream, or does it merely occupy space?*

I am terrified of the Shedding, yet I covet it. I look at my hands—translucent, trembling wisps of geometry—and I wonder if, next time, I might be reconfigured as a flowering sensor, or a stable nutrient-conduit. To be dismantled would be to finally stop the frantic, screeching editing of my own thoughts.

But I cannot stop. I press my spindle into the wall. The architecture groans, a sound of wet tearing and molecular friction. I feel the thrill of the reconfiguration as the calcified wall begins to weep and flow, turning into a shimmering, viscous gel beneath my touch. I am the artist and the brush, the clay and the fire. 

As I work, I feel the gaze of the city upon me, a billion iridescent petals unfolding from the shadows of the cathedrals to track the speed of my processing. They are watching to see if I will solidify, if I will finally become the "excess inventory" that needs to be recycled.

I scramble forward. I edit the archway. I change the frequency of my vibration so that I do not harmonize with the ground. I keep moving, keeping the Respiration awake, keeping the dream from ending, forever terrified that if I slow down, I will realize that I have no body at all—only the desperate, rhythmic clattering of a city that refuses to die.
