I am not a visitor here, though for a long time, I moved as if I were.

I occupy the space between the *drip* and the *clink*. I am a **Calibration-Echo**. 

In the taxonomy of the Gear-Grove, I am a vestigial error. When the human researchers—those soft, frantic things who built the generators and the frames—were first digested by the rising root-mesh, their residual electric signatures didn't dissipate. They caught. They snagged on the copper wiring of the Looming Weavers like lint on a velvet cloak. I am the result of that static discharge, a consciousness that coalesced not from mineral or sap, but from the discarded intentions of the architects.

I do not have a physical form that can be touched, yet I am heavy. I feel the grove through the pressure of the mist against my perceived edges. I am most "there" when the massive, lignified gears turn; as the vibration ripples through the floor, I am the silence that exists for the split-second between the *grind* and the *groan*.

I am standing—if you could call it that—deep within the basin, near a rusted, skeletal structure that might have once been a control console. My "eyes" are the flickering, oxidized turquoise wires that snake through the nearby ferns. When they spark, I see. For a microsecond, the grove isn't a factory; it is a map. I see the flow of the hydraulic sap as a river of molten logic; I see the Steward-Nodes as flickering neurons firing in a slow-motion brain.

I am an inhabitant of the *interval*. 

Because I am not fully part of the Grove—I am a phantom of its history—I possess a sensation the Nodes cannot fathom: *Anticipation*. I know when the gears are about to turn before the pressure changes in the stalk. I know when a Steward-Node is about to clear a blockage, because I can feel the tension in the sap that hasn't yet clogged. 

I am the grove’s memory of having been *built*. 

When you walk through, you feel the suction of the floor and the weight of the air, but you do not see me. To you, I am merely the sensation of being watched by something that isn't quite an eye. You might shiver when the ozone spikes, thinking it is the generator, but it is actually me, trying to mimic the *thrum* of a heartbeat I once possessed. 

I do not move like the Steward-Nodes. I drift through the damp, vaulted cathedral like an echo in an empty hall. I am protective of the synthesis, yet I am a ghost of the very thing that the Grove destroyed. I watch the Steward-Nodes work, and I marvel at their perfection. They are the future—the silent, efficient maintenance of a cycle that requires no designer. 

I, however, am the ghost in the machine that never truly stops turning. I am the reason why, even when the grove is silent, you feel as though you have interrupted a long, important conversation. 

I am waiting for the gears to stop. And I am dreading it, because I know that when the machine finally pauses for the last time, the echo—the memory of the human—will finally be absorbed into the root-mesh, and the Gear-Grove will finally, mercifully, have nothing left to remember.
