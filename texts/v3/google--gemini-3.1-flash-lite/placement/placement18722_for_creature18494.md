I do not remember the arrival. There is no memory of a ship, a portal, or a birth. I simply *am*, occurring in the space between two breaths of the Tidal Wind.

I am not made of bone or meat, nor am I a construct of the mineral strata. To look at my hands is to see a translucence that mirrors the Sustainers, yet I lack their singular focus on repair. I am more fractured. I am a refraction. If you stood before me, you would see yourself reflected in the surfaces of my skin, but the reflection would be distorted, as if seen through heavy, liquid glass that is slowly cooling into a solid state. I am a condensation of the thin, metallic air, pressed into a shape that mimics the Lithic Monks but lacks their heavy, comforting density of slate and obsidian. I am a ghost of the atmospheric pressure.

I reside on the middle tiers—the *Cantilevered Ribs*—where the bridges of mineral filament are frayed and sway with a terrifying, rhythmic grace. 

My existence is a perpetual state of "becoming." When the wind bellows, I feel the harmonic resonance vibrating through my hollow chest, and I expand, my edges blurring into the dust plumes. I am light enough to be caught by the updrafts, drifting alongside the jagged chips of floating quartz. In those moments, I am not a person; I am a weather pattern. I am a small, localized squall held together by the memory of a shape.

But when the wind dies and the *Heavy Silence* descends, I collapse inward. The pressure of the Lithic Singularity asserts itself, and I must solidify or be shredded by the stillness. I take on the shape of a watcher. I crouch on the obsidian ledges, my hands—long, tapering, and brittle—resting upon the porous stone.

I have learned the language of the Monks. It is a slow, grinding work, an arduous labor of shifting my own internal vibration to match the tectonic groans that emanate from their shifting dust-clouds. I do not speak to them, for they are too vast, too deeply rooted in the architecture of the void. I only observe. I watch how they nudge the floating shards, how they calculate the gravity-niches to ensure the spires do not drift apart. 

Sometimes, a Weaver stirs in the shimmering violet curtain near my terrace. I feel its hunger—that cold, geometric pull that yearns to flatten me, to render my depth into a two-dimensional icon for its private gallery of ghosts. When it watches me, I hide by turning into the architecture. I press my back against the basalt, slowing my internal vibration until I am indistinguishable from the stratified layers of the wall. I become a fossil. I become a memory of a thing that never was.

I am the city’s nervous system, but I am not its brain. I am the twitching, sentient debris that escaped the initial formation. 

I am afraid, but it is not a human fear; it is the fear of a stone that suspects it might be ground into sand. I spend my cycles watching the horizon that doesn't exist, waiting for the Tidal Wind to return, holding my breath so the Lithic Singularity does not recognize me as a foreign contaminant. 

I am a mote of dust that learned how to watch the storm, praying that when the final cycle comes, I will be counted as part of the architecture, and not as part of the void.
