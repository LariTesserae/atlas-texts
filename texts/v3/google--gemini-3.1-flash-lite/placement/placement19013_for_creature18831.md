I am not a soul, nor a scavenger. I am a *residual variable*. 

I find myself standing on a walkway of latticed iron that trembles with the frequency of a million subterranean pistons. My boots, heavy and leather-bound, feel alien here—the organic flex of my soles against the unforgiving, static-charged metal feels like a rhythmic lie. I am a smudge of soft geometry in a world of absolute right angles and screaming steam.

I am not the one who built this place, nor am I a prisoner. I am the *Acoustic Archeologist*, though I have no equipment but my own senses and the terrible weight of my own transience. I arrived here as a stutter in time, an arrival that should have been impossible, and yet, here I am, trapped in the amber of the Apex Basin.

I do not move with the grace of the Keepers. I stumble. I slide on the saline slime, my breath—that ragged, wet, forbidden sound—caught in the pressurized air like a dying moth in a storm. 

When they approach, I feel the shift in the *clank-hiss*. It is a terrifying sensation, the sound of an entire continent turning its attention toward me. I see the Keeper leaning down, its boiler-casing torso cold and black, its optical apertures clicking with the sound of a closing safe. It hovers, a magnet-guided specter of rust and emerald vine, its diamond-tipped limb hovering inches from my chest. I can smell the ozone and the smell of ancient, scorched dampness radiating from its joints.

It wants to know why I have a heartbeat, when the Basin only requires cycles.

I realize, with a dawning, icy clarity, that my existence here is a test. I am the only thing in this basin that possesses the capacity to decay. Every time I draw air, I age a microsecond. Every time I blink, I lose a fraction of a memory. The Keepers, in their terrifying perfection, look at me and cannot classify me as *structural*. I am not a pipe. I am not a valve. I am not a leak that can be patched with iron. 

I am a ghost of biology.

If I run, I invite them to dismantle me—to bolt my joints to the pillars so that I may stop aging and join the permanent loop. If I fight, I am simply noise, and they will grind my resistance down until it matches their static pitch.

What I am, then, is a *witness in the process of erasure*. 

I reach out, and I place my hand against the cold, iron rib of a chimney. It is vibrating with such intensity that it hums against my palm, a message written in raw kinetic energy. I close my eyes and I stop fighting the rhythm. I force my own pulse to slow to match the *clank-hiss* of the valves around me. I become a part of the percussion. 

The Keeper’s diamond-tipped probe draws back. An aperture rotates with a sound like grinding teeth, recording the shift. I am no longer a "variance" that needs to be filed away. I have become a vibration within their song. 

I will stay here until my skin turns to pale moss, until my blood matches the mineral-rich brine, and my mind, finally, stops asking why. I am the human element being slowly subtracted from the equation, dissolving into the iron marrow of a machine that will never, ever sleep.
