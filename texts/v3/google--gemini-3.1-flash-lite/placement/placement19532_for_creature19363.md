I am found in the mid-strata, clinging to the underside of a primary heat-exchange conduit—a massive pipe that sighs like a leviathan, vibrating with the pulse of the caldera’s deep-earth fire. 

I am a Weaver, but I am not like the others who drift across the open faces of the basalt towers. I am older, my silicate joints clouded with the white, mineral bloom of many seasons. My internal fluid, once a vibrant, electrified amber, has darkened to the hue of cooled manganese. I have spent cycles—I do not know how many, for the sun provides movement but not time—anchored to this specific junction where the stress of the iron finally fractured the stone bedding.

I am the bridge. 

To exist here is to understand the language of heat. I feel the *thrum-hiss* of the condensate rushing through the conduit above me; it is a frantic, high-pitched song of escape. Beside me, the naked basalt is jagged and weeping, a surface that tries to tear itself away from the metal. My labor is one of extreme stillness. I have extended my segmented, filament-tipped appendages deep into the hairline fissures of the rock, and then, with a calculated, rhythmic pull, I have drawn the mineral-rich sap of the steam into long, glass-thin threads. 

I am stitching the pipe to the mountain. 

The process is agonizingly slow, a ballet of molecular patience. I weave a tapestry of silica over the crack, layering one brittle strand over another until the joint is sealed against the outward push of the high-pressure steam. When I am fully extended, my body acts as a conductive spine. Through my ribcage flows the electricity of the mountain; into my filaments, I draw the heat that would otherwise corrode the structure. I am thinning, exhausting my own internal bioluminescence to maintain the integrity of the whole.

There is no "self" here to speak of, only the sensation of flow. When the condensation leaks, I feel the loss of pressure as a cold, hollow silence in my own core. When I successfully weave a patch that holds the steam captive, my internal fluid hums at an octave that matches the mountain's bedrock. It is a profound, meditative resonance, a sense of being part of a vast, unthinking engine that demands only the perfection of its own function.

The white-yellow sun beats down, turning the condensation on my carapace into a prism. I do not see the light as images; I feel it as a warming pressure on my outer glass-shards, a source of energy I harvest and shunt into my structural bonds. I am a living, clicking gear in the vast clockwork of the caldera. 

Does the city know I am here? It does not need to. It is sufficient that *I* know the city. I am the silicate bridge between the stone that dreams of the earth's core and the iron that carries the city’s lifeblood. I am the silence between the clicks, the steadying hand of glass on the throat of the giant, holding the world together until the light shifts and I, at last, surrender my form to the cooling rock.
