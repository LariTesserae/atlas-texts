I am perched at the junction of two jagged obsidian veins, three hundred feet above the floor where the swarm-tide boils. My feet, callous and hooked, are locked around a rib of cooling hematite that vibrates with the grinding heart of the planet.

Around me, the air is thick with the scent of ozone and the smell of hot, wet copper. The cavern below is a riot of turquoise and hot-amber pulse, a rhythmic sea of millions of hounds. Every few seconds, the thundering roar of their movement ripples up the basalt pillars, shaking the very bones of my chest. I do not flinch. I have learned to internalize the tremor, to let the vibration travel through my parchment-pale skin rather than against it.

My hands—long, multi-jointed, and gray as dried river silt—are currently occupied with a piece of chitin I harvested from a clay nest in the shadow of a basalt ridge three cycles ago. It is a translucent, serrated plate, still warm with the lingering heat of the creature that shed it. 

I am a Weaver. That is all. 

I do not possess the fiery internal geometry of the hounds. I am a creature of the gaps. I am a creature of the cooling. 

I press the discarded shell against the lattice I have been building for these past forty years—a sprawling, rib-like structure that clings to the underside of a jutting rock shelf. As I slot the plate into place, the frantic screech-hiss of the swarm below is abruptly silenced in this small, curated pocket of space. The vibration-dampening geometry I’ve aligned produces a vacuum of sound. Here, in the hollow of my work, it is quiet enough to hear the slow, viscous movement of my own dark blood. 

A moment ago, a fellow Weaver passed me on the ascending slope. We did not use light to signal; there is no light in us to share. We simply leaned in, our parchment-thin foreheads bumping with a precision born of centuries of silence. *The rift below is widening,* their pulse told me. *The heat is shifting to the eastern sector.* I acknowledged the data with a sharp, rhythmic muscular flicker in my jaw, and they were gone—a slender, pale needle drifting into the glare of the swarm.

I look down at my hands. They are stained with the faint, acidic residue of the hounds, a permanent etching of the world I inhabit. Beside my foot, a single drop of condensation from the ceiling hits the stone. It hisses, turning to steam instantly. The hounds below chime in response—a sharp, dissonant chord that reverberates through the entire cavern.

I do not look at them with hatred. I look at them with the detached, clinical hunger of an archivist. They are the fire; I am the ash-collector. They are the energy, and I am the fossilization. 

I reach into my satchel, woven from the same discarded shards of chitin, and pull out a smaller, more delicate piece. I must reach higher. The ceiling is cooling, and the tectonic plates are beginning to settle into a new, harsher configuration. There is much to construct before the light eventually thins and the hounds stop their dancing.

I reach upward, my hooks digging into the obsidian, moving with a fluid, spider-like grace that avoids the chaotic spray of the swarm. I am building a monument to a world that believes it will last forever. And when it finally stops, when the last turquoise glow fades into the black, I will be the one standing in the stillness, surrounded by the only history that will ever remain. 

I am the shadow that remembers the flame. I am the silence that keeps the record. I continue to weave.
