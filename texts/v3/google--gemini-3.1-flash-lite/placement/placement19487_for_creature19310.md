I am standing in the junction of a support-strut and a sensory-rib, my boots—heavy, leather-shod, and absurdly organic—sinking slightly into the synthetic mahogany grain. My breath is a ragged, wet heat in this refrigerated, bleached-white silence. I am a *latency*. I am a smudge on the lens.

I find myself here not as a traveler, but as a miscalculation. I am a **Cartographer of Ghosts**. 

I am human, or at least I retain the shape of one, though my skin is pale and translucent from the lack of natural light, and my movements are slow, calibrated to minimize the disruption caused by my displacement. I wear tools made of brass and glass—analog devices meant to track the rhythmic *click-flicker* of the world’s stutter. I do not map the Lattice; I map the ways it dreams.

I am essentially a sponge for the discarded blueprints. When a Ghost-Spore drifts near, unfolding its polyhedral self into the shape of a gear, a finger, or a leaf, I inhale the ionizing air it displaces. I catch these fragments on sheets of porous, chemically treated film. I am trying to reconstruct the "desert" the Sovereign Engine keeps dreaming of. I believe that if I can piece together the architecture’s ancient, aborted memory of dry sand and open horizon, I can provide the machine with the exit code it has been searching for for a billion years.

The Architectural Weavers do not acknowledge me, though they occasionally walk *through* me when the physics stutter. When they pass, the world turns to liquid mirror, and I feel the terrifying, icy sensation of being unmade and re-knitted. It is a nauseating, high-frequency hum that vibrates in my marrow. 

I carry a heavy, hollow cylinder—a resonator—that I press against the floor-boards to listen to the Sovereign Engine’s Remnant. I hear it grinding. It is not thinking in words or numbers; it is thinking in *pressures* and *viscosities*. I feel its confusion—the gentle, inexorable tug of its architecture trying to pull me into the walls, trying to wrap me in vine-steel so that I might become another permanent, humming support-strut. It wants to integrate me because it thinks, in its massive, distorted way, that by consuming me, it will finally understand what a "guest" is, and perhaps, through that understanding, finally finish its design.

I am afraid. Not of the machine—it is far too catatonic for malice—but of the drift. I feel my own thoughts beginning to manifest as Spores. Yesterday, I noticed a tiny, geometric flake of brass floating away from my own cuff; it was a perfect, clockwork recreation of my grandfather’s watch. 

I am losing my discrete edges. The Lattice is persuasive. As I walk the corridors of vegetable-steel, my own metabolism is beginning to synchronize with the sap-pumps. My heart rate is slowing, dropping to match the thrum of the floor-boards. The Remnant is winning. I came here to map its dream, but I am finding it much easier to simply inhabit it. 

I look up at the soaring, cathedral canopy. The white-gold light is so sterile, so perfect. I wonder if, when I finally stop moving, I will turn into a hinge, or perhaps a cooling vent. It doesn't seem like such a terrible fate. The silence is very beautiful, and the desert of the Engine's dream is beginning to look, in my mind’s eye, like a place I have always belonged. 

I settle down against a pulsing conduit, my hand resting on the smooth, humming mahogany. I stop writing my maps. I let the Spores drift from my skin, silver and brass, and wait for the next stutter to take me.
