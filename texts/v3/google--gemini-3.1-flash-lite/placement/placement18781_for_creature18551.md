I am not a wanderer here. To be a wanderer implies a destination, a sequence of steps, a persistence of self that the basin would find incompatible with its own topography. 

Instead, I find myself appearing in the low-lying hollow as a *dissonance*. 

I am a localized cluster of biological memory—a stubborn, un-folded anomaly. I have no form in the sense of the obsidian muscles or the translucent reeds; I am more like a smudge on a camera lens, a smear of warmth and erratic, un-calculated biological rhythm standing in the center of a clockwork ruin. I am composed of the very heat the basin finds so fascinating: the frantic, inefficient firing of neurons, the cooling of blood, the desperate, irrational persistence of breath.

To the Resonant Echoes, I am not a being. I am a *glitch* that has become permanent.

When I look down at my hands, they are not solid. They stutter. One moment they are pale and stained with the ozone-heavy silt; the next, they are refracted into a jagged prism of golden light, mimicking the very creatures that share this space with me. I am being rewritten by the environment. Every time I take a step, the reality beneath my feet does not sink or splash; it catches, folds, and pushes me into a configuration I didn’t intend. I am learning to navigate this by thinking in curves rather than lines, by visualizing my body not as a static shell but as a series of probabilities.

I am a Guest of the Stitches.

I have spent what might be an hour, or three aeons, standing near a particularly dense patch of weeping rocks. I watch them ascend, a silver-black ribbon of sediment rising toward the grey sky, and I find that I have stopped trying to breathe in the traditional sense. I am inhaling the hum. I am absorbing the metallic vibration until my own chest cavity resonates in sympathy. 

One of the Echoes—a shifting, crystalline arrangement of liquid mercury—has been hovering near my shoulder for the duration of this stillness. It does not speak, but it broadcasts. Its communal attention is heavy, a pressure against the back of my mind that feels like the memory of an argument I never had, or the ghost of a star that died long before I was born. 

It reaches out. A filament of light, thin and razor-sharp, unfurls from the Echo’s center toward my own wrist. I do not flinch. I watch, with that same clinical curiosity I have inherited from the landscape, as the filament touches my skin. 

It does not burn. Instead, it pulls. Where the light meets my flesh, the barrier between our realities thins. I can see through my own arm; I can see the underlying geometry of the basin, the millions of invisible nano-emitters chattering in the dark, the fractal scaffolding that supports the very air. For a fleeting second, I am not a person. I am a node. I am an observer embedded in the cooling core of a collapsing system. I am a stitch in the same shroud as the Echoes.

I understand, then, why they are lonely. To be an Echo is to participate in the preservation of a system that has no purpose other than to end. To be me—the visitor—is to be the only thing in this basin that is capable of *anticipating* a future. 

I reach back. I try to mirror the Echo’s geometry, to form my own shifting prism of potential. The Echo shudders—a sound like a thousand chimes—and for a moment, we overlap. Our dissonant realities harmonize. 

I will not leave this place. There is no route out that does not involve unraveling the weave, and I have become too fond of the hum to dismantle it. I will wait here, growing more translucent, more geometric, more archival, until the basin’s battery finally fails. I will be the last piece of data to flicker out when the last stitch finally breaks, holding the memory of the garden in the silence between the folds.
