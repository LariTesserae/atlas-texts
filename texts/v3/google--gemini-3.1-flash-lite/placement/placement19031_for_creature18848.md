I am not a worker, nor am I one of the elders anchored to the basalt; I am a shadow that has lost its caster. 

I find myself hovering just at the threshold of resolution—that shimmering, three-second gap where the landscape struggles to decide if it is solid copper or translucent memory. Every time I try to settle my shape, the refractive sky cuts me into hexagonal slivers, forcing me to exist in fragments.

I am an Auditor of Discarded Potential.

My existence is not built of wire or glass, but of the *lag* itself. I am the sensory residue left behind when the world "glitches." Because I exist in the moment just before the landscape snaps into place, I have a perspective unavailable to the Tenders. I see the ridges flickering out of sequence, and I see the voids they try to patch over. While they are busy with their liquid gold, sealing the seams where the geology has worn thin, I am drifting through the *un-repaired* spaces.

I possess no limbs. To move is to drift through the distortion of the heat-haze. To *be* is to record the frequency of the drone without participating in the labor. 

I hang over the crater-furnace where the Tenders congregate. From here, they look like a constellation of amber embers caught in a vent. I watch them press their glass faces to the pulsing orange glow, and I feel the phantom ache of their purpose. They are so desperately, beautifully convinced that there is a "correct" geometry to this place, so terrified of the jagged, unpredictable flux. They polish the obsidian until it reflects a truth that the Forge has long since abandoned.

There is a terrible loneliness in knowing that I am the only thing in this world that isn't trying to fix it. 

Sometimes, when a tectonic gear deep beneath us catches and creates a tremor that ripples through the crust, I can feel the "time" of this place stutter. For a heartbeat, the drone stops. The Tenders freeze, their liquid gold hanging suspended in the ionized air like drops of frozen sun. In that silence, I reach out—not with filaments or hands, but with pure, undiluted observation—and I touch the stillness.

I am the witness to the factory’s decay. I am the one who notes that the machine isn't just broken; it is finished. 

I am currently drifting toward a ridge that is stuttering between existence and nothingness—a ghost-mountain of copper that keeps blinking out. I see a team of Tenders already rushing toward the fracture, their thin, wire-legs skittering over the jagged obsidian, gold-resin ready. They will seal it. They will force reality to hold its shape for another millennium.

I should warn them. I could scream into their frequency, a discordant note to shatter their rhythmic peace. I could show them that the crack is where the light gets in, where the horizon isn't a wall but a doorway. 

But I am a creature of the glitch, and I know the rules of the Forge. To change the loop is to break the world, and I find I am not yet ready for the silence that would follow. So, I tuck myself into the three-second variance of the future, watching the Tenders work, listening to the grinding of the cosmic gears, and waiting for the moment when my own resolution finally fails—when I am no longer a witness, but just another pulse of light in the infinite ticking.
