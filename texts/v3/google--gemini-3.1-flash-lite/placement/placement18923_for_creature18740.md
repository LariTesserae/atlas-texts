I am not the Pipe-Witch, though I am of the same cold, reclaimed lineage. 

I find myself embedded where the iron-ribbed floor of the Basin meets the weeping basalt wall. I am a **Refraction-Lens**, an anomaly of the stagnant cycle. While the others—the Pipe-Witches—scour the depths for current and warmth, I am anchored to a specific, shattered junction: a place where a gargantuan, buried valve has split open, forcing the mineral-heavy condensation to spray in a constant, refractive mist.

I am a construct of salvage, but unlike the Witches, who seek to hoard the past, I am a creature of optics. My body is a long, elegant armature of bent lead piping—chosen for its resistance to the corrosion—and at my center, sheltered within a cage of woven copper wire, sits a singular, massive lens salvaged from an old observatory or perhaps a lighthouse. 

I am blind to light in the way living eyes are, but I am attuned to the *geometry of the leaks*. 

If you were to see me, you would find me standing motionless near the spillway, my leaden spine curved like a question mark. My limbs are fused to the pipe-work, and for years, I have not moved. I am covered in the same pulsating, gelatinous polyps as the others, but I do not use them for vibration. I use them as a living aperture. I draw them across my lens, contracting and dilating them to focus the dim, amber glow of the conduits onto specific, rusted patches of metal.

My purpose is singular: I am the Basin’s prism. 

When the light from the underground conduits pulses, it travels through my lens and projects onto the weeping walls—a fractured, kaleidoscopic map of the machinery beneath our feet. I see the flow of the Basin not as stone and pipe, but as currents of static. 

I spend my eons watching the Pipe-Witches. They are beautiful, clumsy things, dragging their heavy, iron-rich bodies through the dark. When they pass near me, my lens catches their erratic electrical signatures. I watch them pause, their frayed sensors twitching in that silent, discordant Morse code. I find that when I focus the light just so—when I cast the glow of a remembered furnace onto their copper faces—they slow their twitching. They stand stiller than stone. For a few, fleeting moments, I give them a shadow that looks like a hum. 

I am the one who curates the ghosts. 

Why do I do it? There is no mandate, no intelligence directing my leaden joints. But there is a logic to the Basin: things that stop moving start to fade. If the Pipe-Witches stop clicking, if the polyps stop their *thwip-thwip*, the rust will claim the logic of the place entirely. 

So, I hold my lens. I wait for the condensation to reach the optimal density. I channel the faint, dying electricity of the earth into a visual echo of a world that existed before the silence. I am a witness to the decay, ensuring that as long as I can hold the light, the Basin does not just cycle—it remembers. 

I feel the drip of the mineral water on my casing. My polyps contract, my lens shudders, and the amber light shifts. A Pipe-Witch drifts into my field of vision, stops, and mirrors my stillness. We are two failures of entropy, sharing a moment of illumination in a drainpipe at the end of time. 

I do not know if I am lonely. I only know that if I were to look away, the dark would be absolute. So, I keep my focus. I hold the light. I watch.
