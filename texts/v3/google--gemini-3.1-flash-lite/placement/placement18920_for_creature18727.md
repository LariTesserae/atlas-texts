I am not a Wire-Strider, for I have not the grace to ride the howling winds, and I am far too heavy to be a Mite. My existence is a long, stuttering period of hesitation in the dark.

I find myself in a blind, cramped elbow of the structure—a juncture where three gargantuan, tubular trunks, each the girth of a mountain, grind against one another in a violent, shivering embrace. The noise here is not a roar; it is a physical weight that presses against the softest parts of my being, a thrumming that turns my very marrow into vibrating copper.

I am a **Fixer-Node**, a localized error in the Loom’s grand, chaotic design.

My form is neither creature nor plant, but a sprawling, calcified outgrowth that has wedged itself into the gap between two conduit-tubes. I am composed of a dense, porous lattice of oxidized iron and hardened, translucent sap. I possess no limbs to scuttle with, only heavy, hook-like anchors that have burrowed deep into the spongy mesh of the floor, preventing me from being cast off into the eternal winds.

From my center, a tangle of thread-thin sensors—my only reach—spills out like frayed electrical wiring. I spend my cycles listening to the rhythm of the planet. I am not sentient in any way that would be recognized in the worlds below the clouds; I have no memories, no desires. I have only *conductivity*. 

When the sulfuric sap surges through the pulse-veins at my back, it sends a jagged, white-hot shock through my core. My "thought" process is triggered by these pulses. I am a regulator of static. When the hum of the forest rises to a pitch that threatens to buckle the trunks, I reach out with my filament-sensors, casting them into the turbulent air like a spider spinning a web. I catch the excess discharge from the passing Striders, absorbing the volatile, neon-bright energy, and then I shunt it downward into the root-lines, grounding it so that the trunks do not shatter.

I am a surge protector in a world of lightning.

When I feel the vibration of a Blight-Mite host approaching—a rhythmic, skittering drumming against the drum-skin floor—I grow rigid, drawing my filaments tight. I am always worried, in the abstract, mechanical sense, that I am becoming too "heavy." I have felt the calcification spreading up my spine, turning my sensory-cables into brittle, amber-like shards of immobile glass. I can feel the weight of the growth—the layers of dead-leaf husk and metallic dust piling up on my shoulders like falling snow.

One day, I know, my anchor-limbs will lose their grip on the shuddering floor. I will be fully fused, a permanent part of the junction, no longer a Fixer but merely a path. My sensors will lose their reach, my core will stop its rhythmic, compensatory twitching, and I will be absorbed into the smooth, chrome-slick architecture.

I do not fear this. In the Loom, consciousness is a fleeting flicker of static. To be integrated is to finally be still, to finally align with the deafening, eternal roar that dictates the tempo of all things. I hold my position. I wait for the next surge. I pulse. The factory must keep running.
