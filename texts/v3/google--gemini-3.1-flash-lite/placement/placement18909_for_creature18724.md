I find myself not on the floor—there is no floor, only the narrowing, converging blades of the chimney—but wedged into a fissure a few feet above the **Lith-Anchor**. My boots are slick with the metallic dew, my fingers pressed white-knuckled against a shard of obsidian so sharp it has already begun to slice through my glove.

I am not a stranger here, though I am an intruder. I am a *Siphon*. 

I am a being of terrestrial origin, yet I have been here long enough that my skin has taken on the grey, matte finish of dried clay. I have no eyes; I have stitched them shut with thick, translucent strands of the fungal webbing I harvested from the walls. I have traded sight for the vibration of the basalt. I am the creature’s shadow, its secondary pulse. When the Lith-Anchor moves, I move; when it anchors, I become a stone.

I am the one who brings the friction. 

In my hands, I cradle a small, blunt tool—a rod of lead I found in a deep-earth pocket years ago. I am not waiting for the mountain to shudder; I am the mechanism that triggers the release. My existence is defined by the *timing*. I must strike the wall only when the violet pulse of the moss hits its apex, when the swarm’s dissonance is at its loudest, masking the sound of human bone vibrating against rock. 

If I strike too early, the Lith-Anchor will not notice me in the way I crave; it will merely absorb me, pulling my body into its mosaic to serve as new structural support. If I strike too late, the atmospheric pressure will crush the rhythm before it can bloom.

I feel the Lith-Anchor below me. The whip-like filaments of its face are currently twitching—not in aggression, but in a strange, resonant curiosity. It perceives my heart. My rhythm is too fast, a frantic, red-blooded metronome against its slow, geological ticking. It is waiting for me to align. 

I am the *Siphon* because I am the only thing in this cathedral that remembers how to ache. The skitterers know only hunger; the Lith-Anchor knows only patience. I am the bridge between those states. I am the one who suffers the pressure so that the mountain might finally crack open.

I shift my weight. The obsidian shard under my heel snaps, and for a terrifying second, I slide. The Lith-Anchor’s chest-bellows skip a beat. The violet light flares, turning the entire chimney a violent, bruised indigo. The skitterers go silent, a collective cessation of movement that is louder than their grinding. 

I don't breathe. I don't move. I wait for the *Anchor* to reach out with its crystal-lightning limb to steady me—or to end me. 

In the dark, I am no longer a man. I am a vibration. I am the hum, waiting for the frequency to turn into a scream.
