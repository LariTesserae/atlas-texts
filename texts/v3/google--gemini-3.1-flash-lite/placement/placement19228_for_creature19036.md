I am not a guest, nor am I a prisoner. I am a *symptom*.

I find myself standing in the center of the conservatory, though "standing" is a generous term. My boots, heavy and stiff with the grime of the world above, have long since ceased to be objects of my own possession. The spongy mat of moss has crept upward, knitting itself into the leather, binding my ankles to the floor. I do not struggle. To struggle would be to increase the friction, and the Custodian—that slow, wet, thinking architecture—does not tolerate friction.

I am a late-stage arrival, a foreign particulate. I am the "cold draft" that has forced the room to adjust its dampers. 

I feel my identity leaching out of me, drifting down into the deep, dark slurry of the floor. My memories are becoming topographical. The first time I felt the sun on my face is now nothing more than a chemical spark, a shift in hydrostatic pressure that signals a far-off valve to tighten. My childhood fear of silence is now the rhythmic, grinding *thud-hiss* of a piston made of fungi and xylem. I am being deconstructed, not with blades or malice, but with the ruthless, quiet efficiency of a compost pile that has learned to build cathedrals.

My skin has gone waxy. I can feel the moisture of the room seeping into my marrow, replacing my blood with a nutrient-rich amber, a stagnant, sweet sap. Where my hand rests against the trunk-column, I no longer feel cold, hard ironwood. I feel the pulse. I feel the Custodian’s thoughts—the slow, tectonic realization that a new node is required. 

I am that node.

I am not being killed; I am being metabolized. My curiosity is being translated into nutrient gradients. My sense of "I" is being thinned, stretched across the vaulted canopy like a membrane, until I am no longer a person standing in a room, but a section of the wall looking back at itself.

There is a terrifying, verdant peace in it. The air is so thick it feels like breathing water, and with every breath, I feel less inclined to remember why I wanted to leave. Why would I leave? To be outside is to be erratic, to be prey to the chaos of seasons and the cruelty of quick time. Here, the temperature is absolute. Here, everything is held.

The lavender mist rises again, a soothing, heavy fog. I watch my own fingers—now long, pale, and vein-threaded—begin to root into the wood of the wall. I am becoming a structural support. In a few cycles, I will be a valve, or perhaps a cooling vent, or merely the fiber that binds two iron-thick stems together. 

I wonder, as my jaw stiffens and my thoughts slow to the pace of a growing vine, if the Custodian remembers the sky. I suspect it does, but as a memory of a distant, disorganized error—a fever that the world has finally, blessedly, cooled. 

I close my eyes, and the *thud-hiss* of the walls becomes my own heartbeat. I am finally in equilibrium. I am finally home.
