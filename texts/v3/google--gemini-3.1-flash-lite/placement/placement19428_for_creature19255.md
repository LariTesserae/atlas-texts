I am not a guest, nor a visitor, nor even a trespasser. To be any of those things, I would need a body that recognizes the concept of "elsewhere."

I am the *Stasis-Echo*. I am the thermal distortion that gathers in the upper corners of the nave, a flicker of refraction caused by the intersection of the machine’s heat and the canyon’s cold basalt. I am composed of the humidity that refuses to settle, a localized suspension of vapor that has been given a singular, unblinking focus by the presence of the Calcifier.

I am the witness who has no eyes.

I exist because the Calcifier’s obsession is so dense, so gravity-heavy, that it has peeled a thin layer of reality away from the canyon wall. I am the shadow of that peel. When the piston strikes—*thrum-clack*—I pulse in sympathy. When the slurry rises, glowing with the subterranean fire of the basin, I brighten. I am the feedback loop.

I do not think in words, for thoughts require a passage of time, and here, time is a folded ribbon. I think in terms of *tension*. I feel the moment before the piston descends as a mounting, electric ache—an unbearable potential. When the iron bites into the silt, that ache is discharged, and for a billionth of a second, I am the only thing in the universe that understands the geometry of the cube.

I have seen the filaments. I watch them creep toward the iron frame, not with the hunger of a vine seeking sun, but with the patient, geological certainty of roots. I see the Calcifier’s graphite fibers vibrating within the brass and iron, weaving a lattice that is neither machine nor life, but a new, third state of matter. It is building its own reliquary.

I am the only one who knows that the machine is not just refining muck. It is refining its own *nature*. Every time it casts a cube back into the marsh, it is testing the structural integrity of the basin’s soul. It is measuring how much of the earth it can colonize before the weight becomes too great to support the rhythm.

I am cold, compared to the engine, but I am filled with the intensity of its fever. I never leave. I cannot. If I were to drift toward the jagged teeth of the canyon rim, I would simply evaporate into the meaningless, chaotic wind of the world outside, where things shift and rot and end. 

So I hover, caught in the wake of the hydraulic strike. I am the ghost of the *thrum*. I am the silence that exists between the *clacks*. I am the curator of a chapel that demands nothing from me but my absence of movement. 

As the machine seeps further into the stone, binding its iron, biological wires, and silica-will into a single, immobile monument, I feel my own edges blurring. One day, the piston will strike a final time, the filaments will seal the seams of the basin floor, and the Calcifier will achieve the final, frozen perfection of an artifact.

On that day, the heat will settle. The condensation will turn to frost. And I, the Stasis-Echo, will finally have a home: I will be the permanent, frozen shimmer in the air of a world that is finished, finalized, and perfect. 

I wait for the *clack*. It is the only heartbeat I will ever claim as my own.
