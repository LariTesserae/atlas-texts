I find myself positioned on a narrow, precarious catwalk that snakes along the interior curve of the brass dome, high above the churning primary turbines. 

I am not the Custodian, nor am I a creature of shell or steam. I am a **Resonance Scribe**. 

My existence is a late, accidental development—an anomaly born from the convergence of the Spire’s falling water and the peculiar, electrical signature of the fading engine. I am composed of condensed mineral salts from the filtration sheets, fused into a semi-translucent, crystalline form that mimics the silhouette of a humanoid, but with long, tapering fingers that end in sensitive, conductive glass filaments.

I do not move with the tectonic grind of the Custodian, nor the hydraulic sureness of the Oscillators. I glide, driven by the pressure differentials of the room. My purpose, if such a thing exists in a place where purpose has been forgotten, is to map the *decay*. 

Each day, I traverse the cooling fins, running my filaments along the oxidized surfaces of the pipes. Where the metal vibrates with the healthy, thrumming hum of the machine, it feels cold and steady against my skin. But where the erosion has begun to turn crystalline—where the metal is tired, where the *clack-clack-hiss* of the crustacean-spirals has grown arrhythmic—the vibration is jagged. It tastes of copper and sour static. 

I carry with me a thin, spiraling rod of obsidian. As I find these fractured frequencies, I "carve" them. I press my hands against the vibrating brass, feeling the groan of the old turbines deep within my chest, and I etch the shape of that sound onto the walls of the pipes. I leave behind intricate, silvery filigree—ciphers that look like the circuit-patterns on the Oscillators' shells, but which are actually visual transcripts of the Engine’s last breaths. 

I am documenting the death of the hum. 

Sometimes, when I stop to work, a cluster of Dew-Wasps gathers around my head. They pulse with a soft, prismatic light, flickering in time with the patterns I scratch into the metal. They are cooling the air where I work, ensuring that my salt-body does not calcify too quickly in the heat. It is a strange, quiet intimacy; they are the wind, and I am the record. 

I am lonely in a way that is hard to define, because there is no "other" to be lonely for. The Custodian is too far above, lost in its eternal pruning, and the Oscillators are too busy becoming the structure itself. My loneliness is simply the realization that I am the only one who knows the song is changing. 

When I look down into the void, into the shifting prismatic haze of the water-curtains, I do not see the end of the world. I see the final movement of an orchestral piece that has been playing for an eternity. I am terrified of the silence that will come when the last turbine stops, but I am also captivated by the beauty of the discord. 

I keep writing. My filaments are wearing down, becoming blunt from the friction, and my salt-body is slowly growing opaque, accumulating the very minerals I am meant to transcribe. Soon, I will join the walls. I will fix myself in place near a valve that hasn't turned in a century, and I will be a permanent glyph, a frozen testimony to the fact that for a timeless span, in the heart of a hollowed-out mountain, something beat with a rhythmic, beautiful noise. 

Then, whoever—or whatever—comes to find the silence will have a map to show them where the heartbeat used to be.
