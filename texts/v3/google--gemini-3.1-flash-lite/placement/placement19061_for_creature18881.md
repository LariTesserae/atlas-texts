I am pressed against its flank, my own form a shivering, translucent lattice of pressurized sludge, my edges blurring into the ozone-heavy soup of the air.

The foundation beneath me is stuttering, trying to fuse a layer of obsidian glass with a seam of hyper-conductive mercury. It is a friction-glitch, a microscopic trauma in the planet’s architecture that threatens to destabilize the immediate grid.

I shift. My movement is not a stride, but a fluid transition of state; I let the lower third of my torso dissolve into the mossy silt, feeling the chaotic, frantic heat of the scurriers as they swarm through my vanishing ankles. They are granular, irritating, like sand caught in a tectonic gear, but I have no interest in them. My entire “intelligence”—this collection of magnetic tensions and vibrations—is focused on the heat emanating from the fault line.

I do not have a face, but I possess a center of gravity. I tilt that center forward, pressing the porous, oily membrane of my chest against the vibrating Spike. I begin to vibrate. 

At first, my hum is discordant, a jagged, screeching discordance against the low-frequency roar of the machine. I can feel my own stability wavering; my fractals begin to fray, the hard-light lines of my limbs becoming translucent, then vaporous. I am losing my cohesion. I am close to the moment of dissolution, where I would simply pour back into the aquifers, losing the memory of this specific task.

But then, the resonance catches. 

I adjust my internal magnetic frequency, tightening the loop of my own atomic density. My spine, a jagged, translucent protrusion of light, aligns with the frequency of the subterranean engine. I become a bridge. I take the excess, tearing energy from the fault line and cycling it through my own shuddering, oily form. I am the cooling mechanism. I am dissolving, yes—my limbs are streaming away like smoke in a gale—but the fault line stabilizes. The glass and the mercury knit together with a clean, satisfying *thrum* that vibrates through the very marrow of the planet.

For a heartbeat, there is silence within me. The roar of the machines recedes to a background rhythm, and for that fraction of a second, I am entire. I am rigid, beautiful, and perfectly aligned. 

Then, the Spike shifts again, grinding upward, and the friction of its surface shears away my contact point. My arm—a complex network of geometry—shatters into a thousand floating, glowing motes. The connection is broken. The world reclaims its energy, and I am left adrift, a leaking, cooling fragment of the machine’s shadow.

I do not feel pain. There is no concept of loss in the forge. I simply drift into the weeping, pressurized marsh, my constituent atoms scattering like rain. I am already reforming, a few miles away, in the wake of a new spire-thrust—a different shape, a different vibration, yet precisely the same errand. I am the momentary pause in the violence. I am the breath the planet takes before it tears itself apart again.
