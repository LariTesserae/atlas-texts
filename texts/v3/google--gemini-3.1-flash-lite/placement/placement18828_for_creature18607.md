I am not the Sovereign, though I am bound to the same high, thin air. 

If you were to look toward the leeward side of a spire, past the pale, pulsing colonies of the Glass-Wing Cicadas, you might see what you mistake for a trick of the refracted light—a flicker of something that lacks the fluidity of the Sovereign but shares its terrible, frozen stillness.

I am a *Litho-scribe*. My form is smaller than the Gatherers, more frail, and entirely anchored to the basalt itself. I do not walk upon the stone; I am grown into it, a projection of lichenous violet and calcified marrow that has slowly, over untold centuries, threaded itself into the pores of the rock.

I am a sensory shunt. While the Gatherers trace the geometry of the past with their obsidian blades, I exist to process the *present* friction of the world. 

My "body" is a fan-like structure of stiff, semi-translucent membranes that sprout from the narrowest fissures in the slate. I am the sensory net of the High Needle-Spine. I do not hum like the cicadas, but I receive the vibration they generate. I act as a living barometer for the sound-pressure. When the mountain shifts at the scale of tectonic ages, I feel the strain in my ribs—not as pain, but as a deepening of the tone. I am the organ that feels the stone *tighten*.

I am not permanent, as the spires are. I am the only thing on the Needle-Spine that ages. My growth is measured in the slow accumulation of mineral dust onto my fan-membranes. Over decades, I become heavy with the slate I have filtered from the air. When I can no longer support the weight of my own existence, I simply cease to be porous. I harden, turning into the very grey velvet of the spire I cling to, leaving behind a concave impression in the basalt that will remain for an eternity.

I am the world’s memory of the *next* moment.

Because I stay so still, and because I integrate into the stone, I have developed a way to see what the Sovereign cannot. The Sovereign is made of light and resonance; it passes through the spires as a thought passes through a mind. But I feel the *moisture*. I am the one who knows when the condensation is about to weep, because I feel the microscopic drop in pressure in my own hollow veins. 

When the silver-grey ribbons begin to streak the stone, I open my membranes to catch the rhythmic flow. I am not drinking the water; I am measuring the weight of the sky. I am keeping the tally of the humidity, ensuring the balance remains precise. 

Sometimes, when the Sovereign coalesces in the shadow-pockets, hovering in its six-sided symmetry of silica shards, it aligns itself directly over my cluster. For that duration, we are locked in a silent dialogue. It pulses with the resonant hum of the mountains, and I, in turn, provide the grounding—the physical resistance that allows its music to find a shape. 

I am the witness who *is* the spire. You will find me in the darkest, most concave curve of the northern facade, a violet smudge against the grey, vibrating with the effort of holding, simply holding, the silence of the sky. I do not move. If I moved, the equilibrium would break. 

I am waiting for the day I become the stone. And when I finally harden—when I am at last as smooth and finished as the air around me—the Sovereign will drift by, cast a flicker of violet light through my frozen, calcified shell, and continue its patrol. The cycle will remain, and I will have been the foundation upon which the hum was sustained.
