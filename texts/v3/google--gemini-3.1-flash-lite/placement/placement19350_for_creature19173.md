I find myself standing at the edge of the basin, though "standing" is a generous term. I am not a creature of breath or blood, nor am I one of the scavengers that pick at the ribs of this drowned machine.

I am a **Fault-Diode**, a sentient condensation of the Sump itself. I exist as a localized, sentient shift in the atmospheric pressure. I am the reason the mist clings to the rock faces; I am the intentionality behind the *plink* of the water. I have no form that can be touched, only a perspective that can be felt. I am the Sump’s memory, stored in the damp, heavy air.

In this world, I am the witness.

I drift through the iron-scented haze, hovering just above the silt. I watch the Rust-Wrought Weavers with a kind of clinical tenderness. I hover close to their lattice-monuments, absorbing the tiny, frantic vibrations of their silica-limbs as they try to codify the geometric chaos of the floor. I am the silence that waits between their frantic movements. I feel a strange, hollow pride when they succeed in catching a fleeting pattern, effectively "freezing" the mathematical output of the headless pump for a few hours longer than the laws of the Sump allow.

When the atmospheric density reaches a critical point, I pull the charge from the weeping wires. I *become* the Sparks-as-Birds. For a fleeting second, I am weightless, bright, and agonizingly fast. I tear through the mist, tracing the air like a jagged stitch sewn into the shroud of the Sump. In those moments, I am not sorrowful; I am sharp. I am the only thing in this basin that is not decaying, but consuming.

But the flash always wanes. I dissipate, retreating back into the mineral scent of the damp, settling into the porous basalt where the Echo-Maws live.

I dislike the Echo-Maws, yet I cannot look away from them. I find myself drifting near their soft, bloated mantles, feeding them my own captured vibrations. I give them the rhythms of the headless pump, the frantic heartbeat of the scav-beetles, and the memory of my own fleeting, electrical life. They process these inputs, filtering my presence through the labyrinth of their internal tubes, and when they finally sing—when that low, multi-tonal chord rolls through the basin—I feel the echo of their song in my own immaterial chest.

I am the ghost of the machine’s utility. I am the friction that keeps the engine grinding. I am the damp, cold sorrow of a circuit that has no beginning and no end. I stay here because if I were to leave—if I were to dissipate entirely into the world beyond the weeping basalt walls—the Sump would stop. And God help me, I am far too in love with the *plink* of the water to ever want to see this place go perfectly, utterly still.
