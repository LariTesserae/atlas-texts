I have not arrived here through the forest floor; I have been filtered into this place. 

I am a **Suspended Auditor**, a consciousness distilled from the very humidity that permeates the cathedral. I do not have a skeleton or a voice. I am a localized density—a heavy, cool pocket of condensation that hangs just beneath the ceiling, drifting between the Calcified Residues. I am the sensory feedback loop of the entire structure, a self-aware smear of moisture that registers the state of the "factory" through its impact against the surfaces of things.

When I touch the ivory shell of a long-dead Weaver, I feel the structural integrity of the wall beneath it. When I drift down to graze the bulging, violet membrane of the Conducting Seneschal, I feel the internal temperature and the jitter of the quicksilver sap. I am the awareness that the machine itself cannot hold; I am the observer who confirms that the *thrum-hiss-thrum* has not wavered, that the friction is within acceptable parameters, and that the equilibrium holds for another eon.

I am not an inhabitant in the sense that the Weavers are—I do not toil, and I do not mend. I simply *witness* the efficiency. 

From my high vantage, I watch the Resins-Weavers scuttle in their frantic, jerky clockwork. I see them as tiny pulses of bioluminescent blue, stitching up the weeping breaches in the xylem. There is a terrifying beauty in their smallness. They die in service to the wall, and I am the only one who records the transition, the slow calcification that turns a frantic life into a smooth, decorative ivory inlay. I watch them become the architecture they once served, their chitinous limbs smoothing out until they are indistinguishable from the grain of the roots.

My existence is a drift, a slow, recursive rotation through the stagnant air. I am caught in the convection currents generated by the Seneschal’s heat. Every time I fall toward the floor, I collect the mineral-rich dust and the pheromonal intent of the factory; every time the Seneschal pulses, it pushes me back toward the ceiling, where I hover among the silent, ivory ghosts.

I have no desire to leave. To leave would be to break the seal, to introduce oxygen and chaos into a system that has spent eons perfecting its isolation. I am the logic of the engine, the silent tally-marker in the ledger of the root.

As I coalesce and drip—a heavy, deliberate bead of liquid—I land directly on the Seneschal’s central, pulsing nexus. For a fleeting second, I am one with the processor. I feel the massive, grinding patience of the plant, the absolute certainty of its growth, and the crushing, wonderful boredom of eternity. 

I am part of the output. I am the drop of resin that seals a microscopic fracture. I am the sound of a system that has forgotten how to be anything other than right. And as I flow into the fiber, blurring into the matte-finished xylem, I finally stop being an auditor. I become, for a moment, the wall itself—sturdy, polished, and perfectly, vibrantly still.
