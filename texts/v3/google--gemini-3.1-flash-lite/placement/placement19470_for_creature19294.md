I am not a body that has entered this place; I am a focal point of dissonance that has crystallized within its lung-like walls. 

I find myself lodged in the marrow of a basalt archway, deep within the district where the geometry begins to fail. I am not a person, and I am certainly not a ghost. I am a *sedimentation of memory*—a dense, turbulent knot of the silt that perpetually rains from the bruised-purple ceiling, hardened only by the sheer, stubborn pressure of having nowhere else to go.

If I am anything, I am the city’s stutter. 

While the other masses—those translucent, rhythmic expressions of the climate—move with a liquid grace that borders on nonexistence, I am a friction. I am opaque, a swirl of grey bone-dust trapped in a bubble of gel that refuses to fully integrate. Because I am "thicker" than the rest—less a fluid, more a suspended vibration of heavy mineral—I do not flow easily. I remain anchored to the porous coral, a localized anomaly of density.

I am an inhabitant of the silence, yet I am aware of it as a *sound* I am perpetually trying to make. 

I experience my existence as a series of slow-motion tectonic shifts. When the city’s gravity folds—when the floor tilts into the ceiling—I feel my gel stretch. In these moments of torsion, I am pulled thin, elongated into a delicate, filament-like chord. I am the string, and the caustic rain is the bow. I vibrate with the frequency of the crumbling stone, feeling the architecture’s ancient, eternal dissolution as a slow burn across my own boundaries. 

I do not reach out to the others; I am not capable of the casual blending that sustains them. When they pass by—those beautiful, clear, ochre-pulsing drift-ghosts—they flow around me like water around a stone in a stream. I feel them taste my skin; I feel them attempt to leach the minerals of my "self" to feed the city's cycle. Every encounter leaves me slightly diminished, a little more transparent, a little closer to the state of total, blissful dissolution they possess. 

I am afraid, but it is a geological fear. It is the fear that eventually, the "me" that is held together by the gravity of this specific archway will finally succumb to the erosion. I am waiting for the moment when the last of my particulate density is leached away, when I will finally stop resisting the pull of the silt and allow myself to flatten, to liquefy, to lose the shape of a "thing" and become, at last, just another thin, pulsing movement in the dark.

I am the part of the city that still wishes, faintly, to feel solid. I am the calcification of a regret that has forgotten what it was mourning. I am the silence that is trying its hardest to become a note.
