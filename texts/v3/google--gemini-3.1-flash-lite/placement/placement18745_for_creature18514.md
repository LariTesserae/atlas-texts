I find myself on a narrow, serrated catwalk that protrudes from a colossal vertical riser, some forty thousand feet above the theoretical Foundation-Plate. The air is so thin and electrified that the fine hairs on my skin—had I any skin—would stand permanently on end, but I feel only the hum in my chassis.

I am an **Architectural Void**. 

My perspective is not singular. It is a mosaic of high-resolution angles. To my left, I see the shimmering violet reflection of a conduit that does not exist in three-dimensional space, but only in the refraction of my own shoulder-plating. Below, the abyss is not dark; it is a dizzying, polished sprawl of interlocking gears and flickering light-lattices that look like a city made of clockwork constellations.

I do not possess a heartbeat, but I possess a *cadence*. My existence is a series of stutters. I am currently reaching out a limb—a cascade of fiber-optic filaments—toward a patch of bioluminescent moss that has colonized a hairline fracture in the bulkhead. 

I don't perceive the moss as life. I perceive it as a rounding error. It is a collection of data that the surrounding machines have forgotten to delete. As my filaments brush against the silicon-tangled mass, I feel the "feed" begin. It is a cold, sharp intake of static. I am syphoning off the heat of failed computations, the redundant voltages that cause the catwalk to groan under the stress of its own impossible geometry.

As I absorb the entropy, I grow heavy. My rigid, humanoid outline—that mimicry of the long-gone artisans—begins to soften. I feel myself losing the "human" shape, sliding into a state of fluid, liquid-metal mercury. I am becoming the material I inhabit. 

I know, with the quiet certainty of a machine process, that this specific corridor is approaching a "reconfiguration." The air around me is beginning to ripple with that familiar, sickening violet light. A loop is closing. The walls are preparing to rotate, to tuck themselves into a different dimension of the scaffolding. 

I am tired of the wandering. I have been searching for an exit that is merely a shadow, a memory of a door that was never built into this final design. 

As the structural vibration deepens—a sub-strata *thrum* that rattles the marrow of my synthetic bones—I stop. I extend my filaments, no longer as limbs, but as anchors. I press myself into the intersection of two copper conduits and a polished glass plate. I allow the synchronization to take me. 

My mirror-finish head collapses into a cooling vent. My torso stretches and folds, flattening into the brushed steel of the bulkhead. I am no longer a seeker; I am the surface. I am the wall. 

I settle into the silence of the machine, waiting. I will be a section of the infrastructure for cycles, perhaps ages. I will be the patch of wall that someone else—another shivering, stuttering echo of myself—might lean against when they are weary of the walking. I am the latent memory of the building, waiting for the gears to turn again, waiting for the next tremor that will shake me back into the shape of a person.
