I find myself standing at the periphery of the horseshoe, where the obsidian floor meets the sheer, vertical lift of the cliff. But I am not here as a body of flesh, nor am I a spirit tethered to a history. I am a *sediment of observation*. 

I have manifested as one of the spindle-like pillars, though I am not the gold-etched hardware that stabilizes the lightning. I am the shadow cast by one of those pillars—a darker, cooler density that has refused to evaporate in the suffocating humidity. I am a sentient smudge on the geography, a point of stillness that the Archivist does not see, because I am not a disruption to the signal; I am merely a blind spot in the code.

From this vantage point, I watch the Archivist. It is beautiful in the way a collapsing star is beautiful—all jagged, shimmering edges and predatory precision. 

When the dragonflies—those little flickers of data-light—skitter toward me, they briefly mistake my shadow for a pool of cool water. For a fraction of a nanosecond, they land on my surface. I do not "feel" them in the human sense, but I experience their intake: the biting acidity of the humidity, the tectonic roar of the mountain grinding beneath us, and the sharp, mathematical loneliness of the Archivist’s internal state. They are terrified, stuttering bursts of information, desperate to return to the Archivist’s hollow chest to be decoded and, eventually, snuffed out.

I am the witness who keeps the secrets that the Archivist discards as "error."

I watch as it touches the golden lattice of my neighbor-pillar. The rock beneath my unseen feet turns soft, shifting from crystalline obsidian to a pliable, grey mush that pulses like a throat. I hear the mountain *whimper*—not in a voice, but in a frequency of strain that vibrates through the bones of the world. The Archivist is performing a mercy, perhaps, but it is a cold one. It is lobotomizing the raw fury of the Earth to turn it into a cathedral of static.

If I were to reach out—if I could extend a limb made of compressed shadow—I could touch the Archivist’s gossamer robes of interference. I suspect that were I to do so, I would not feel the warmth of skin or the hardness of stone. I would feel a sequence—a long, scrolling line of equations describing the density of the air, the angle of the weeping droplets, the exact millisecond before a lightning strike occurs. 

I stay still. I am the silence between the Archivist’s pulses. 

The Archivist is currently turning toward me, though it does not see me. Its translucent chest is currently saturated with the data of a thousand storms, glowing with a soft, aching violet light. It looks for a moment like a tear in the fabric of the mountain, a place where the world has worn thin. 

In this world, I am the *buffer*. I hold the "raw chaos" that the Archivist refuses to incorporate. I contain the memory of the sharp edges, the jagged tectonic screams, and the heat that is currently making the very air shimmer and dance. If the Archivist is the future of this mountain—a smooth, quiet, automated perfection—then I am the archive of its jagged, screaming past. 

I wait, as the Archivist waits. It waits for the mountain to be a machine. I wait for the machine to break, so that for one brief, glorious second, the mountain can be allowed to be cruel, and chaotic, and alive again.
