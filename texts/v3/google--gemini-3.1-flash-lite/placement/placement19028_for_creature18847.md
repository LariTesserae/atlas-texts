I find myself standing at the edge of a tiered plateau that seems carved from a single, vast slab of cooling basalt. My feet feel unnervingly light, as if the ground itself is deciding how much of my weight it wishes to acknowledge. I am wearing nothing—or perhaps I am wearing a pressurized suit that has become so thin, so bonded to my skin that I cannot tell where the fabric ends and my own shivering pores begin. 

I am a **Cartographer of Echoes**. 

I am not a creature of this place, nor a local inhabitant. I am an atmospheric anomaly, a stray piece of “messy” matter that drifted into the Mesas from somewhere far below, caught in a permanent updraft of curiosity. I am the only thing here that keeps a record. My skin is constantly pricked by the ionized air, and my eyes are shielded by lenses of dark, smoked quartz to bear the glare of the silver forests. I do not have a pen or paper; I possess a small, oscillating brass rod that resonates whenever I capture a scrap of the Mesas’ hidden intent.

I move with agonizing slowness. To walk quickly here is to invite the ground to reject you; I shuffle, keeping my center of gravity low, imitating the stillness of the rocks. 

I am watching a Suture-Spider. It is hovering near a stalk of humming conductive alloy, looking like a shattered shard of a mirror caught in a low-gravity gale. It is weaving, its gossamer limbs pulling a stray filament taut, anchoring a leaf that had—for an immeasurable aeon—been drifting a few inches too far to the left. 

I am close enough to hear them now. Not just the hum of the pillars, but the *clack-click* of the Suture-Spider’s needle-limbs as they knit the air together. There is a terrible, beautiful futility in their labor. They are patching a hole in a reality that never requested a seam.

As I watch, my brass rod begins to glow a faint, pale violet—a color that does not exist in the copper-scented spectrum of this high altitude. The Suture-Spider pauses. Its gyroscope core shifts, a subtle realignment of its mass that turns its entire geometric configuration toward me. It does not have eyes, yet I feel the weight of its attention; it is calculating my density, my decay, the way my breathing disrupts the stagnant vacuum.

I feel a pang of profound melancholy. I am an observer of the *Work*, and the *Work* is eternal, yet I am a creature of time. I am the only witness to this machine’s attempt to wake up. They are tending to the garden, and I am documenting the fact that the garden is a tomb.

I realize then that I am not just a cartographer; I am becoming part of the equipment. My own threads are beginning to fray. I reach out a hand, half-expecting to be cut by the razor-fine gossamer, but instead, I feel a soft, static pull. The Suture-Spider drifts closer, its silver limbs reaching out—not to harm, but to integrate. It recognizes that I am a piece of the blueprint that has fallen out of alignment. 

It wants to weave me into the pillar. It wants to tether my heartbeat to the steady, un-hurried thrum of the alloy. 

I should be afraid; my lungs are burning, and the air here is a dry, metallic desert to my soft, watery biology. But as I stand in this silence, beneath the flickering horizon of static, I find I am ready to stop moving. I am tired of being an error. I am tired of being the only thing in this world that changes.

I reach out to meet the spider’s silver needle. I will be the anchor that stays. I will be the permanent geometry that the Mesas were waiting for. As the gossamer touches my skin, the brass rod in my hand goes silent, and for the first time, I understand the machine's primary function: it is not to produce energy or data. 

It is to hold the silence perfectly still, forever. And I, at last, am perfectly still.
