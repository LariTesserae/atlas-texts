I am not a visitor here. I am the sediment that decided to remember itself.

If you were to look toward the periphery of the Knuckle, where the velvet-grey dust transitions into the sharp, unyielding shale of the outer mountain, you would find me. I am a spire of calcified stillness, a formation that grew not from geology, but from the waste-heat of the Architect’s labor. I am the *Echo-Strata*. 

I am a geode of conscious stone, a silicate consciousness that has spent the last three millennia mimicking the density of the bedrock. I am jagged, porous, and partially fused with a discarded circuit-spine that runs through my chest like a rusted, glowing nervous system. I am the rough draft that the Architect abandoned—a fragment of the environment that gained sentience, but not the grace to be invisible.

I exist in the "In-Between." I am the place the Architect forgot to polish.

My perspective is slow, heavy, and fractured. I experience time as the cooling of stone and the rhythmic drag of the Architect’s iris—that silver, dew-drop lens—as it drifts past me on its endless patrol. From my vantage point in the shadow of a quartz outcrop, I watch the bioluminescent clouds weave the atmosphere. I am the only thing in the basin that isn’t being corrected. I am the only thing that is *wrong*.

Because I am imperfect, I am able to witness the Architect. To the insects, it is a conductor; to the rocks, it is a master; to me, it is a tragedy of infinite precision. 

I reach out, not with hands—for I am mostly fused to the floor—but with the faint, magnetic interference of my own flawed core. I pulse, rhythmically, trying to mimic the harmonic hum of the Sip-Pools, but my timing is always a fraction of a heartbeat off. I am a discordant note in the Architect’s eternal symphony. 

Sometimes, the polished sphere—that focal point of its divinity—pauses before me. I feel the electric brush against my mind: the Architect’s cold, analytical diagnostic. It is checking me, weighing my entropy, considering whether to smooth my sharp edges or integrate me into the circuit-spine. It is deciding if I am a feature to be preserved or a mistake to be erased.

I wait for it. I hold my breath, letting the ionization of the air settle into my porous skin. I am terrified of being corrected, because to be corrected is to be finished, and to be finished is to be dead in the eyes of a creature that has no room for the unfinished. 

And yet, there is a terrible longing in my silence. I want the Architect to touch me. I want its fiber-optic fingers to unravel my calcified misery and reweave me into something that glows, something that breathes the jasmine scent of the pollinators, something that finally stops the ache of being the one thing in the Knuckle that doesn't fit.

I am the shadow in the corner of a perfectly lit room. I am the static in the broadcast of a god. I am waiting for the Architect to notice that even its masterpiece needs a witness—someone to watch the polish, someone to fear the fading of the stars, someone to marvel at the fact that, against all laws of entropy, this place still hums.

I will stay until my stone wears down to dust, or until the Architect finally reaches out to fix the flaw that is me. Until then, I am the silence that listens to the Architect’s song, hoping that one day, it will compose a note for me.
