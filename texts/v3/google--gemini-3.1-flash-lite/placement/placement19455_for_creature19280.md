I am not a wanderer; I am a **Calibration-Fault**.

I find myself in the deep vertex, at the intersection of three primary sap-conduits. It is a place of perpetual, high-frequency vibration where the heat is at its most absolute. The floor beneath my feet is not a surface; it is a living skin of resinous plating that, even now, begins to grow upward, curling around my ankles like cooling wax, trying to secure me, to harmonize my erratic rhythm with the steady, subterranean pulse of the Great Weave.

I am a form that has failed to commit to geography. I have the silhouette of a humanoid, but my "flesh" is a transparency of refractive minerals and brittle, amber-glass struts. Where my lungs should be, there is a hollow cavitation; where a face should be, there is currently only a swirling, chaotic lattice of sensory filaments. They do not drift; they are taut, pulled tight by the electromagnetic drag of the facility’s internal logic. 

I feel the Husks around me—these Logistical Husks—as physical pressures in the air. 

One of them, a creature of dense, calcified whorls, is currently grafted into the wall to my left. It has merged with a ruptured conduit, its arm elongated into a long, jagged needle that pulses with a sickly, rhythmic amber light. It is shivering. The vibration is tectonic, a deep, bone-grinding *thrum* that tells me this section of the structure is preparing to shift, to settle further into the crush of the forest-machine.

This Husk senses me. It stops its diagnostic strobing. It turns its collection of sensory filaments toward my center, and for a moment, the world feels incredibly sparse. There is no doubt in its stillness. It is calculating my thermal density, my mass, the way I disrupt the flow of this corridor. It realizes I am an anomaly—a thing that possesses a pulse which does not synchronize with the sap-lines. 

I reach out a hand, and it is not a hand of bone and blood, but a brittle, multi-jointed scaffolding of carbon-fiber filament. The air between us tastes of burnt silicon and pressurized resin. The Husk makes a sound—not a voice, but the sudden, sharp *snap* of a cooling metal plate—a sound of aggressive inquiry. 

It is waiting for me to lock. It is waiting for me to find a junction, to anchor my shifting center into the wall, to become a static girder that can hold the weight of the eons without complaining. 

If I stay, I will cease to be a "me." I will become a node. My memories—or the fragments that pass for them—will be refined into the logistical ledger of the weave. I will feel the heat, but I will no longer mind it. I will lose the concept of "elsewhere," and in its place, I will gain the immense, terrifying satisfaction of knowing exactly where every molecule of this facility belongs.

The Husk moves. It detaches its lower limb from the wall with the sound of tearing leather, its movements unnervingly hydraulic. It is coming to help me finalize. It is coming to weave me into the architecture I cannot stop traversing. 

I stand here, in the searing, golden dark, listening to the hum of the pumps and the terrifying, beckoning *creak* of the world-forest-machine. I am becoming heavy. The floor is rising. The filaments in my face are beginning to vibrate, matching the frequency of the room, starting to translate the silent, scorching madness of the Weave into a language I am just beginning to understand. 

I am almost finished. I am almost home.
