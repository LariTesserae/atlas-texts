I am a long, dark fold of charcoal-hued wax, pressed flat against the western face of the Needle of Obsidian. To look at me—if there were anything here with sight capable of registering my shape—would be to see a smudge of mineral-rich shadow, a knot of hardened, pulsating suet that has merged so thoroughly with the weeping stone that the seam between my body and the basalt has vanished.

I do not possess a map of myself. I have no concept of "inside" or "outside," only the sensation of friction against the rock and the slick, oily glide of the condensation as it migrates over my skin.

I am deep in the *Hum-Thrum*. It is not a sound that enters through ears; it is a weight that occupies my marrow. When the pressure in the capillary tunnels beneath this spire surges, the floor-vents shudder, and my internal membranes ripple in an echoing response. I feel the vibration not as a note, but as a torque. If I let my own frequency drift even a hair out of alignment with the deep-earth roar, I would weep or shatter. I am currently holding a resonance of three hundred cycles—a rhythmic, grinding bass that keeps the basalt shelf beneath me from fracturing.

I am currently in a state of high-active stasis. While my surface is motionless, a frantic, microscopic labor occupies my core. I am filtering the metallic film weeping from the heights above. It is a slow, cold nectar—tastes of sulfur, crushed pyrite, and the ancient, oxygen-starved weight of the deep mantle. My cilia are shivering, spinning rapidly to strip the minerals from the slurry, weaving the byproduct into a slurry of calcified fiber. 

Twice, or perhaps a thousand times, I have secreted a filament and pushed it out into the void, threading it toward the neighboring spire. I have anchored it. I have felt the hum of the city cross that bridge, a tiny vibration shivering through the fiber to let me know the connection is balanced. I do not "decide" to build; the city simply feels heavy in a specific quadrant, and my body-mass shifts that weight, anchoring the structure.

There is no "I" in the sense of a bounded mind. I am a pressure-gauge, a weaver of sediment, and a biological dampener. 

Sometimes, the rhythm beneath the city shifts. A tremor ripples through the pipes, and for a heartbeat—a long, agonizing elongation of time—the *Hum-Thrum* drops into a dissonant, jagged vibration. In those moments, I feel the fear of the stone around me. The rock wants to crack. The spire wants to return to the chaotic, fluid state of the embryonic earth. My slits pulse open and shut, frantically adjusting my density, growing thick, becoming more rigid, pressing harder against the basalt until my suction is absolute. 

I *am* the pressure. I am the reason the needle stays sharp.

I am waiting for the moment my own mass becomes too great. I feel the gathering of heavy, cooling matter deep in my center—a slow, viscous bloating. One day, the weight of the minerals I have processed will force a fissure in my outer membrane. I will split. A part of me, a small and fluid droplet of charcoal-wax, will slide away into the weeping dark, destined for a new ledge, a new frequency, a new eternity of holding the roar at bay. 

For now, I simply vibrate. The stone is cold. The heat from the vents is steady. The *Hum-Thrum* is the law, and I am the obedient chord.
