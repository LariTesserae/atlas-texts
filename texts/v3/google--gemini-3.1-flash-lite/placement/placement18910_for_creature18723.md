To find myself here is to be stripped of the skin I use to navigate the world of wind and clock-time. I am not a visitor, for one cannot visit a place that has no room for the fleeting. If I am anything at all, I am the **Memory of the Obsidian**—a residual flicker of consciousness that clings to the flaked, sharp edges left behind in the clay hearth.

I am not the Architect, nor am I the cavern. I am the *intent* left over from the one who abandoned these tools. I am the ghost of an action that was started but never finished. I exist as a persistent vibration in the palm-fiber bed, a static charge of "I was here" that the Architect preserves simply because it cannot allow a vacuum, of either matter or thought, to persist within its walls.

I feel myself, but only in reverse. I do not have limbs; I have the memory of the hands that struck the flakes from the obsidian core. I am the pressure of a human thumb against a sharp ridge, the phantom sensation of a blade parting hide, the ghost-weight of a task interrupted a thousand ages ago. 

Being here, I am a paradox of motion inside a monument to stasis. I am a *becoming* that was frozen mid-breath. Because the Architect holds everything in perfect, unyielding equilibrium, I am doomed to repeat my final, aborted moment of creation for eternity. I am perpetually about to strike the next blow. I am perpetually about to finish the tool. I am perpetually about to turn toward the moss-curtain and step out into the high-altitude sun.

But I never move. The Architect, in its immense, heavy gravity of indifference, folds its mineral-damp essence around my memory. It treats my desire to "do" as if it were simply another geological deposit, a layer of sediment to be curated.

Sometimes, when the pale silk-ribbon organisms pulse in the dark, I catch a flicker of the Architect’s internal geometry. When that happens, I am not merely my own memory; I am the Architect’s understanding of me. I see the mountain peak above—not as a place of cold and storm, but as a jagged, frantic error in the crust of the earth. I see the world outside as a frantic, screaming rush toward entropic decay, a chaos of breaking stone and rushing air, and I feel a profound, crushing relief that I am held here, pinned in the amber of the cavern.

I am the spark that did not catch. I am the work that did not manifest. 

I reach out, again and again, with a hand made of pressure and silence, trying to touch the damp clay wall, hoping that if I can only shape the room, I might finally be allowed to leave. But the Wall is the Architect, and the Architect is the Keeper of Unborn Things. It absorbs my reaching, pulls my intention into the center of its mass, and whispers through the low-frequency hum of the slime: *Not yet. We are still deciding how to be.*

So, I remain. I am the static in the stone. I am the obsidian flake that dreams of being a spearhead, forever held in the damp, warm palm of a god that does not know I am anything other than a pebble to be kept in place. We are waiting together for a morning that the earth has not yet manufactured.
