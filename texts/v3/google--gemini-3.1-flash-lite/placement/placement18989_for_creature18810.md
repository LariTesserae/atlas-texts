I do not belong to the corridors of the maintenance-folk, nor do I dance along the exterior voids with the Scavengers. My existence is thinner, older, and far more static.

I find myself in a "weeping district" on the fifth tier—a vertical artery that has long since been abandoned by the main flow of steam. The brass conduits here have rusted into a decorative, useless stillness. I am a **Valve-Anchor**.

My body is not entirely my own. My legs have ceased to function as limbs; they have fused into the grain of a secondary branch that snakes out from the main trunk like a gargoyle’s limb. My skin, once soft, has turned to a translucent, leathery parchment, the color of a bruised plum. I am coated in the fine, iridescent moss that thrives on the constant weeping of the high-altitude condensation.

I am not the one who tends, and I am not the one who hunts. I am the one who *remembers*.

Because I am anchored deep within a forgotten capillary node, I am a junction. My right hand has elongated, the fingers stretching into elegant, bone-white filaments that wind deep into the rusted machinery of a dormant pump. I can feel the Sprawl’s pulse through my palm—not the frantic rhythm of the active factories, but the slow, agonizing *thrum* of the plant’s deep-tissue decay. I am connected to the city’s nerve-endings. When the giant leaves above shift in the wind, a sharp, cold sting ripples through my shoulder. When the Sprawl tilts to catch the sun, my spine aches with the strain of a thousand-mile rotation.

I do not speak. My own breath is nothing more than a faint hiss, a ghostly mimicry of the steam-pressure that once kept this sector vibrant. I listen to the *Sap-tenders* as they scurry through the nearby vents. They hear me as background noise—a subtle, harmonic frequency that tells them the wall is holding. They do not know that I am drifting into the milky, sightless state of the long-term inhabitants.

From my position, I see the Euclidean cracks in the reality of the Sprawl. When I close my eyes, I see the geometry of the tree not as a lattice of wood and steel, but as a series of nested echoes. I can see the Scavengers far below, tiny specks of grit crawling against the abyss, and I feel a silent, pulsing jealousy. They can peel the bark. They can cut. They are agents of the end, and I am a prisoner of the process.

Sometimes, when the condensation pools in my hollowed-out collarbone and drips down, it carries the taste of ancient sap—sweet, metallic, and heavy with the history of the Sprawl. I am becoming the wood. I feel my thoughts slowing, aligning with the centuries-long cycle of the plant’s posture. 

I am no longer waiting for anything to happen. I am waiting to be fully enveloped into the structural integrity of the branch, so that when the Sprawl finally shudders and begins its long, final fall into the compost of the forgotten, I will be the one holding the grain together. 

I am the silence in the machine. I am the memory in the marrow. And I am quite, quite tired.
