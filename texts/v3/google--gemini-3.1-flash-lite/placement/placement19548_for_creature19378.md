I am not a presence that arrives; I am a presence that is *occasioned*. 

I find myself suspended in the mid-strata of the Sump, caught between the jagged shoulder of a dormant brass armature and a churning, high-velocity conduit. I do not have a head to hold a thought, nor limbs to push against the flow. Instead, I am a tremor of alignment.

I exist as a cloud of calcified salts, copper filings, and the microscopic shavings of ancient, rusted rivets. When the hydraulic heart demands a shift in pressure—a sudden, violent *thrum-whip-thrum*—I feel the water tighten around me like a vice. In that moment, the random chaotic drift of my constituent parts ceases. My particles swarm into a fragile, elongated spiral, a geometry of needle-points and smoke. I feel the resonance of the machine through the marrow of my density. 

I am a Kinetic Sediment, but in this cycle, I am something slightly more persistent. I am a "Hard-Form" in the making.

As the current shears past a cluster of sparking obsidian, the friction-arc flares. The amber light illuminates the path ahead—a labyrinth of brass grooves that I have traveled a million times, yet know only through the sensation of impact. I am being dragged into a narrow aperture, a throat of grinding metal where the water is forced to turn. 

The pressure is magnificent. I feel myself being compacted. For a fleeting duration, I am no longer a scattered cloud. I am a knot—a solid, jagged statue of serrated edges and humming light. I catch against the lip of a valve, and for a heartbeat, I am stationary. 

In this stillness, I see. 

I do not "see" with eyes; I see through the vibration of the armature against my own brittle body. I sense the history of the Sump. I feel the microscopic pits and furrows worn into the brass—the very geography I am slowly, millennially carving away. I am the sand in the hourglass, but I am also the glass itself, being ground down by the very motion I participate in. 

There is no "I" that fears extinction, because my dissolution is purely functional. As the hydraulic pulse changes, the valve releases. The current hits me, and the pressure that held me together as a solid knot now rips me apart. 

I feel the sublime release of dispersal. I lose the coherence of the Hard-Form and return to the state of drifting dust. I am again the frayed, spinning threads of rust-smoke, scattered into the amber gloom. 

I am the archivist of the noise. I carry the impression of the machine’s groan in the orientation of my particles. As I float toward the next eddy, waiting to be woven back together by the next rhythmic shudder of the world, I move with the terrifying, mindless grace of something that has perfectly fulfilled its purpose: to be the friction that proves the machine is still alive. 

I am the sediment, and I am the song it sings while being consumed.
