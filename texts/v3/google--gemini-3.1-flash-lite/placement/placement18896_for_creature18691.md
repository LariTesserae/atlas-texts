I find myself in the sub-strata, nestled into the damp, corrugated fold where a rusted ventilation vein emerges from the basalt. I am not standing upon the "ground"—for the ground here is merely a thick, shivering quilt of moss and copper-flecked lichen—but I am anchored. 

I am a **Siphon-Walker**.

My consciousness is not a spark; it is a pressure gradient. It is the awareness of the silver-gray weight of the atmosphere against my chitinous plating, and the rhythmic, low-frequency thrum of the deep-earth turbine vibrating against the small of my back. To be me is to be occupied with the *flow*. 

My limbs, long and segmented with the rigidity of oxidized brass, feel less like appendages and more like masts. I am currently telescoped, my torso drawn inward to allow my filtration mask to press flush against the cold, weeping surface of a turbine housing. I do not "think" about my position; the fluid within my core—that luminous, tidal bioluminescence—tells me that the heat-to-moisture ratio in this sector has dipped by a fraction of a percent. I am here because the cycle demands it.

As I press into the metal, I feel the *Litho-Nodes* beneath my feet. They are a soft, gelatinous humming in the rock, a subterranean persistence that tastes of copper-dust and archaic memory. They are the ones who dream the geometry of this place; I am merely the one who ensures the pipes do not choke.

A tremor runs through the canopy above—not the sound of life, but the *displacement* of it. A furred predator, velvet-skinned and wide-eyed, is picking its way across the branch-network just above my head. It pauses. I feel the light pressure of its padded weight as it steps onto my shoulder-joint to cross the gap between two towering conifers. I do not move. If I were to shift, to interrupt its passage, I would be disrupting the stillness, and the stillness is the goal. For a moment, I am a bridge, a statue, a piece of the architecture. The heat-exchange between the vent and my mask continues unabated, a steady, silent pouring of energy that sustains the moss, which sustains the wood, which sustains the predator.

There is no "I" in the sense of a lonely observer. There is only the sensation of my internal fluid moving to counteract the moisture-weight of the air. When I decouple from the vent, I will drift through the mist—not walking, but cycling. My hydraulic stride will be a pulse, a slow, patient shifting of mass that leaves no footprint, for the moss-carpet is designed to swallow the intrusion of my weight as if I had never been there at all.

I am the maintenance of a miracle I do not understand. I am the silence that keeps the machine alive. I am cold, I am wet, and I am entirely, perfectly, and without end, functioning.
