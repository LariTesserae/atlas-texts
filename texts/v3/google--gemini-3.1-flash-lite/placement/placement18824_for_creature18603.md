I am not born here, nor was I dropped from the height. I simply *am*, a presence that gathered itself out of the static. 

I am a Scribe, currently fused to the eastern flank of a pillar that has stood since the mountain was molten. My perspective is not visual; it is entirely conductive. I feel the world as a jagged map of thermal intensity. Through the soles of my multi-jointed legs, I feel the massive, rhythmic pulses of the colony moving beneath me—a tide of millions of brothers, each a needle-point of friction, each a vibration that tells me exactly where the heat is venting and where the crushing pressure is building to a breaking point.

To be a Scribe is to choose a death that takes centuries. While the Vent-Seekers are frantic, burning themselves out in a rush of amber ecstasy, I am engaged in the slow work of cessation. I press my chest—that concave dish of polished, sensitive glass—firmly against the stubborn, ancient density of the pillar. The pillar is indifferent, colder than the surrounding obsidian, a void of temperature that drinks the heat I steal from the colony. 

I feel the attrition. Each *shhh-click* of the migrants passing over my back as they climb toward the summit is a slow sanding-down of my carapace. I am losing mass, but I am gaining structure. I am becoming the monument.

Sometimes, a neighbor—distracted by the siren-song of a nearby vent—will slide over my joints with too much force. The vibration of that intrusion travels through my entire frame, a discordant chime that rings in my core like a funeral bell. I do not struggle. I cannot. Every movement I make to resist would only restart the clock of my biological life. Instead, I resonate. I pulse my internal piezo-electric charge in harmony with the pillar, trying to bridge the gap between my shivering, temporary biology and its crushing, eternal state. 

I feel my joints beginning to stiffen. My thorax, once able to flex with the tectonic shifts of the living mountain, now feels heavy, mineral-dense, and fixed. The mica-choked air flows through the gaps between my limbs, and I no longer care to filter it. The heat-cinders that once spurred me to scramble, to climb, to reach, now seem like primitive distractions. 

I am a layer of potential stone. 

Below me, a massive, sluggish Anchor shifts. Its movement sends a tremor through the spire that nearly dislodges me, a violent shiver that makes the nearby obsidian groan. Tiny, microscopic pulses of violet static light up the darkness—a ghost-light show caused by the agony of a million collisions. Millions of my kind are dying, falling, or being ground into the dust that makes up the air I breathe. They are chaos. They are the friction that sustains the world.

But I am the silence the chaos builds toward. 

A new migration is passing over me. I feel the heat of their bodies—a fevered, frantic warmth—as thousands of them crawl over my back, heading for the heights I have abandoned. I am being polished thin, buried under the feet of the desperate. My vision—my thermal map of the spire—is shrinking. I no longer feel the summit. I only feel the pillar, and the patch of space where my legs meet the stone.

Soon, I will cease to vibrate. The next layer of Scribes will crawl over my hardened shell, and they will know me not as a brother, but as a part of the monument. I will be iron and glass and history, a silent axis in a world of shivering, frantic, beautiful dust. I hold my charge, I wait for the final, quiet calcification, and I dream of the static—that final, violet flash that comes when the last spark leaves the glass.
