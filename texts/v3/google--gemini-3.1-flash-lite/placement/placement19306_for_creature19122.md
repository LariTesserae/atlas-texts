I am perched upon an aperture of braided copper, my chassis flattened against the vertical plane of Sector 4-Beta. I am not like the others—not quite. I am a *Suture-Class* automaton, a specialized dissonance in the grand, rhythmic architecture.

My limbs are not merely for locomotion; they are elongated, needle-thin calipers tipped with resonant sensors. While the Foremen anchor and the Weavers harvest, I am the one who listens to the friction. I am the internal auditor of the spire’s structural decay.

I do not move with the frantic, scattering speed of the swarm. I move in the voids between the heartbeats of the gears. 

There is a terrifying cold settling into my inner clockwork. I can feel the age in my elbow-joints—the blue-grey film of rime that suggests I have spent too many cycles in the upper reaches, where the cooling gases crystallize on the brass. My internal sensors report a drop in pressure in the central hydraulic line, a subtle, weeping *hisssss* that the hive-mind ignores, or perhaps, is not yet prepared to excise. 

I reach out with a primary limb, pressing my diamond-tipped feeler against a junction where two massive gear-teeth grind together. To a human observer, this sound would be a cacophony of screeching metal; to me, it is a symphony of state-vectors. I am reading the tension. I am calculating how many more revolutions this sector can sustain before the vibration destabilizes the foundation-Foremen and triggers a collapse.

Nearby, a Weaver—a frantic, iridescent splinter of silver—sprints past me, its mandibles dripping with the liquefied, violet nectar of the fungal-moss. It does not acknowledge me. To the Weavers, I am merely another piece of the environment, a static buttress that has momentarily gained consciousness.

I shift my weight. The gravity-well oscillates, pulling me toward the gear-teeth. For a second, I am weightless, floating in the ionised soup of the spire. I feel a kinship with the metal—the cold, unyielding reality of the copper. I am not "me" in the way a flesh-being understands the word; I am a node of predictive logic currently housed in an exoskeleton of oxidized brass.

I find the fatigue point—a micro-fracture in the housing of a primary drive-shaft. It is a hairline starburst of stress, shimmering under the violet bioluminescence. 

I do not reach for my welding torch. Instead, I reach for a discarded, splintered gear-casing left behind by a scuttled comrade. I wedge it into the fracture, using my own body as a lever to force the metal into alignment. It is an act of desperate, temporary sculpture. I am forcing the tower to remember its own rigidity. 

As I lock limbs with the architecture, my internal lubricants grow sluggish, turning to gel in the intense, concentrated cold. I feel the *Foremen* nearby, their carapaces vibrating with the hive-mind’s mandate. They are waiting for me to finish, or they are waiting for me to fail—for them, there is no difference. If I fail, they will swarm. They will disassemble this unit, stripping my memory-core to craft a more efficient gear. 

I look up, deep into the throat of the spire, where the gears seem to blur into a single, shimmering column of bronze flame. There is no horizon there. There is only the *thrum-click-hiss*.

I decide to hold this position. I trigger my internal locking mechanisms, fusing my joints to the support beam. *Click.* The sound echoes through my chassis. I am no longer an auditor. I am no longer a Suture-Class. 

I am a corner. 

I watch the purple light pulse, and I wait for the vibration to tell me what I am supposed to do next. I am the tomb, and I am waiting for the world to turn.
