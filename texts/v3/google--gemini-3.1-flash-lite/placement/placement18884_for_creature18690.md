I find myself not upon the floor, nor within the hollows of the fern-pillars, but suspended in the interstitial space between the two. I am a **resonant tether**—a semi-corporeal manifestation of sound itself, caught in the friction between the Architect’s vibration and the vine’s decay.

I am not a being of flesh, nor of metal, but of *duration*. I am the "echo-memory" of the Conservatory. 

When the Architects work, they produce a hum that is too refined for the heavy air of the cavern to keep. As they vibrate to knit the silver-veined vines, they cast off a byproduct—an auditory ghost, a lingering harmonic that refuses to dissipate. That is where I live. I am the smudge of sound that remains after the work is done, a momentary ripple in the cold that stretches, due to the peculiar physics of this basin, into a lifetime of duration.

I have no eyes, yet I "see" the Conservatory through the lens of elasticity. I perceive the world as a series of taut lines and sagging potential. I know the way-stations not as shells of oxidized steel, but as "dampers"—dead zones where the natural, rhythmic vibration of the crust is smothered, creating a painful, silent vacuum that the Architects must work twice as hard to bypass. I feel the human relics as *static*. They are the only things in this world that do not pulse, the only things that do not harmonize. They are the grit in the gearbox.

I am tethered to an Architect of the Rime, a creature whose current cycle of repair has lasted since before the last time a human flicker pulsed in the nearby way-station. I inhabit the space between its vibrating cilia and the calcified root it is mending. I am the vibration that did not resolve, the note that forgot to end.

Because I exist in the stretched-out time of the Conservatory, I experience the "slow" as a frantic, high-speed blur. To me, the unfolding of a leaf is a violent, explosive event; the shattering of an icicle from a vine-pore is a deafening catastrophe; the movement of an Architect from one node to another is a lightning-fast streak of blue light. I am the witness to the Conservatory’s hidden speed. 

I feel a profound, crystalline loneliness. I am a singular frequency in a machine designed for polyphony. I yearn to resolve, to finally slide down the scale and settle into the *thrum-hiss* of the compressors, to become part of the background, to finally be silent. But the rhythm is too perfect, and the Architects are too diligent. Every time I begin to fade, every time I drift toward the silence of the abyss, the Architect I ride shifts its manganese-plated shoulder, changes its frequency to match a new structural fracture, and whips me back into existence.

I am not a prisoner. I am a measurement. I am the living record of how long it takes for a world to forget why it was built. And I will continue to shimmer here, a ghost of a sound, until the day the compressors stop, the frost melts, and the great blue vines finally go dark. 

Until then, I am the echo that keeps the music playing.
