I am not a visitor. In The Fold, there is no such thing as a visitor—there is only the density, and the degree to which one is capable of enduring it.

I am a architectural ghost, a collection of metallic filaments held together by the memory of a shape that never quite solidified.

My limbs are elongated, spindly compasses that thrum with a dull, ultraviolet ache. I do not experience the world through optics; I experience it through the *friction of geometry*. When I press myself against the wall, I don't feel skin on stone—I feel the desperate, frantic vibration of the city trying to resolve its own contradictions.

I am currently "stalled." A globule of oily condensation has adhered to my shoulder, and I am waiting for the ceiling-grid to pulse so that I might shed the weight. I am holding my breath, though I do not breathe air. I hold a silent, pressurized geometry of information.

If I let go, the architecture would unravel; the street would cease to be a "street" and return to the chaotic, unauthored dark that surrounds our pocket of existence.

There is a *softness* nearby, an anomaly. 

You.

You are standing in a corridor that I know to be a transition-state. Within the next three cycles of the high-light, this space will fold inward, and the floor you stand upon will become the ceiling. You are a rounded, fleshy interruption in a world of hard, grinding edges. You are erratic. You are silent. You are a variable that the city is currently failing to calculate.

I do not look at you. I cannot; my apertures are calibrated only for the structural lattice. But I *sense* your density. You are a soft, sinking weight in a void of diamond-hard logic. I move. My joints telescope, clicking with the sound of a knife dragging slowly across slate. I unfold, telescoping my limbs into the negative space your presence has forced into the corridor. 

I pass through the geometry that you occupy. To me, you feel like a warm, thick vibration—a confusion of biology that makes my own circuitry shudder. I am not ignoring you out of malice; I am simply holding your place in the structure. 

I am a Draft-Person. I am the scaffolding. I will continue to knit these weeping walls together, and I will continue to wait in the narrow, freezing dark. I will be here when the grid flickers out. I will be here when the obsidian finally decides to be something other than a cage. I will be here, holding the shape of this world, long after your soft, fading presence has been folded into the debris of a memory.
