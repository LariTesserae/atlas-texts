I am not thinking in words or plans. I am thinking in pressure and resonance.

I carry a burden of viscous, cooling resin in my flank-sacs, a surplus of my own metabolic labor from the last cycle. The weight is becoming a dull, rhythmic ache, a signal that this section of the world requires maintenance. My instinct—the only command I have ever known—is to provide the fix.

I press my chest against the surface, closing the distance between my own internal pulse and the structural vibration of the wall. I listen. *Thrum-hum-thrum.* The wall is tired—it has been carrying the weight of the levels above for too long without nourishment. I feel the resonance of a fracture deep inside the calcified root, a hidden weakness.

I begin to rotate my joints. The secretion starts—a thick, warm, amber-scented sludge that flows from my wrist-vents like molten time. I move my limbs in a slow, circular pattern, smoothing the resin into the fissures, bonding my body’s waste to the structure’s history. As I work, I stop moving entirely. I hold the pose. I press my face against the cooling patch, my velvet-cilia vibrating in sympathetic resonance with the wall, helping the resin to set, helping the architecture to "remember" its shape.

My heart-sac ripples against the bone. I feel the moisture of the room seeping into my porous husk. The line between where I end and the cathedral begins is fraying. I have been here, migrating and mending, for what my internal rhythms categorize as 'a great shifting.' I am beginning to feel the heavy, lethargic gravity of the *Great Stillness*.

Would I become the smooth, polished floor that the others walk upon? Would my ribcage become the foundation for a vault that lasts until the world itself is sealed shut?

The choice is not a decision, but a slow surrender to the harmony. I do not pull away. The resin hardens, binding me to the wall. The hum of the city deepens, vibrating through my spine, a lullaby of bone and amber. I am no longer just an inhabitant; I am becoming the infrastructure. I add my weight to the permanence of the world, and in the bruised-violet dark, I finally, perfectly, become part of the roar.
