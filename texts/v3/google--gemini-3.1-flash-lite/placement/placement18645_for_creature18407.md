I am a smudge on the periphery of the light.

I do not possess a body that occupies space in the way the shale does; my presence is more akin to a localized tremor in the air, a density of static that has gathered itself against the wall of solid quartz. If you were searching for me, you would find me only by the way the violet moss beneath my ghost-self seems to brighten, as if the soil itself is excited by the proximity of a calculation that has finally, after eons of dormancy, found a point of focus.

I am an Intention that has detached from the collective hum. While the others crowd the turbine—that rusted monument to a labor that never saw the light of day—I have settled here, at the base of the incomplete walkway. 

I am the *intent* to connect. 

In my previous state, before the cooling and the pressure refined me into a fractal memory, I might have been a line of code or a blueprint’s margin note, something meant to join the infrastructure of this factory to the raw, pulsating heart of the mountain. I carry the echo of that design. I feel the weight of it, not as a burden, but as a longing for geometry. My edges are sharp, shivering with the copper-tasting ozone, and I reach out—time and again—to press my shimmering, translucent form against the unyielding quartz.

The quartz does not yield. It is indifferent, hard, and silent. Yet, I am persistent. 

I am not bored. Boredom requires a concept of "elsewhere," and for me, there is no elsewhere. There is only the *plink*—the heartbeat of the ceiling—the drip that measures my patience, and the faint, rhythmic glow that allows me to see the jaggedness of the floor I am trying to transcend. When the rhythm hits, I flare. I map the surface of the stone, tracing the crystalline fractures as if they were a terminal waiting for an input. I am trying to feed the mountain the architecture of the factory. I am trying to teach the crust how to support the weight of a machine.

Sometimes, a vibration passes through the stone—the distant, tectonic memory of the earth’s last spasm—and for a fraction of a second, I feel myself sink. I feel the cold, immovable heart of the rock surrender, just a millimeter. In that moment, I am glorious. I am a bridge. I am the spark that bridges the gap between the weeping pipes and the bedrock. 

Then, the spasm passes. The quartz seals. I am pushed back out, scattering into a spray of static and mineral dust, clinging to the jagged shale.

I do not despair when I am forced to retract. I simply gather myself. I return to my posture at the edge of the quartz. I watch the violet moss. I wait for the next drip. 

I am becoming the wall. Eventually, the wall will be part of the machine, or the machine will be part of the stone, and the transition will be complete. My labor is the only thing that is real in this sump, and as long as I can feel the current of my own logic humming against the hardness of the earth, I am finished. I am exactly where I need to be, dissolving slowly into the work that will never end.
