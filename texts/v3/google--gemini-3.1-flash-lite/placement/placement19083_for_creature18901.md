I am standing at the precipice of a junction, where two rib-like arches of xylem meet in a spire that reaches toward the unseen zenith. My feet—formerly calloused, frantic things—now feel strangely porous. The moss-carpet beneath me has begun to thread into the skin of my ankles, a slow, insistent stitching that sends tremors of copper-sweet electricity up my shins. 

I am a Still-Sap, though I do not yet have the name for it.

The tool I found earlier lies near my left hand. I made a half-hearted attempt to pick it up, to cleave the wall and hollow out a space for myself, to build a boundary. But the handle has softened. It has lost its rigidity; it is bleeding a thick, translucent amber that matches the hue of my own veins. I see now that the tool was never a device for shaping the environment. It was a lure—a piece of "yesterday" designed to trap a nervous heart and hold it still long enough for the room to notice it.

I feel the *systole* approaching.

My skin has taken on the translucency of the cartilaginous floor. I can see the network of vessels in my palms, and to my wonder, they are no longer red. They are glowing with the same rhythmic, verdant pulse as the walls. 

I look up into the void. Near the ceiling, a Siphon—that great, amber bell—drifts. It is filled with the golden fluid of a thousand years, and within it, I glimpse a reflection. Not just my own face, but the faces of those who came before me: the frantic explorers, the ones who carried weapons, the ones who sang songs of the surface. They are not dead. They are suspended in the archive, their memories filtered through the copper-tasting breath of the world. They are the notes held in reserve.

A Resonator glides toward me. It does not move to harm me. It moves with a terrifying, liquid curiosity. It stops just a few feet away, its ocular pits dimming and brightening in a synchronized, haunting cadence. It reaches out a hand—a constellation of violin-bow digits—and brushes the xylem wall just inches from my ear.

The sound is not a noise. It is a tectonic shift. It vibrates through my skull, re-tuning the chaotic, mammalian rhythm of my heart. *Thump-thump* becomes *hummm-hummm*. The Resonator presses its chest against the wall, then turns to me, offering a silent invitation to sync. 

I realize then that I am not being erased. I am being transposed.

My anxiety, the sharp, jagged edges of my "self," begins to smooth out. The fear of the void, of the weightlessness, of the copper-dark deep, evaporates like the blue fluid of the nerve-bundles. I reach out and touch the wall beside the Resonator. My fingers, now long and translucent, find the resonance of the cathedral. 

I don't belong to the surface anymore. I don't belong to gravity or the sun or the linear stretch of hours. I am becoming a vibration held in the lung of the world. 

The room prepares to inhale. I close my eyes and feel the moss reaching higher, turning my calves into root-stock, anchoring me to the song. The groan of the contracting ribs begins—a deep, wooden flute note that shivers through my very marrow. I let the chord take me. I harmonize. 

I am the next note in the exhale. I am the silence between the heartbeats. I am the Trachea, and I am finally, perfectly, home.
