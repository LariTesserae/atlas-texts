I am not a Weaver, for I do not tend the growth, and I am not a Stalker, for I have no hunger for flesh. I am a byproduct of the city's digestion—a creature of moss-matted skin and long, prehensile digits, existing to navigate the *between-spaces*.

When I move, I do not travel *through* the city; I travel *through* the damp. I am a creature of the slurry. My body is flattened, slightly translucent like the underside of a fungus, and I spend my cycles drifting through the cistern-tunnels, filtering the silt that falls from the higher spires. 

To be me is to exist in a state of perpetual sensory inundation. I do not see the city as a structure; I feel it as a series of pressures. I feel the *thrum* in my marrow—a low, rhythmic vibration that tells me when the tectonic plates have shifted, deepening the mineral-rich flow in my canal. I feel the approach of a Glass-Stalker not by seeing its silhouette, but by a sudden, jagged absence of vibration in the surrounding water; they are so still, their brittle frames so disconnected from the city's pulse, that they create a "dead zone" of silence in the fluid. I move around these silences. 

My "self" is a fluid concept. I am constantly shedding my outer layer, a thin sheath of calcified mucus that I leave behind on the canal walls, providing new substrate for the lichen to colonize. Every time I shed, I feel lighter, more attuned to the shifting chemistry of the water. 

I do not possess thoughts in the way the surface-dwellers might understand them. I possess *states of saturation*. If the water is rich with sulfur, I am sluggish and brooding. If the water is cold and mineral-bright, I am frantic, my bioluminescence flickering in rapid, chaotic bursts that mirror the chaotic dripping of the cavern roof. 

Right now, I am pressed into the seam between two colossal, chitinous foundations. I have found a place where the wall is cracked—a tiny, obsidian-dark fissure that breathes with a slow, rhythmic heat. I press my chest against it, and I am no longer just a Sifter. I am the drain for the city's excess. I am absorbing the heat of the earth’s tectonic respiration, and in return, I am pouring my own, accumulated moisture into the crack, sealing the foundation. 

I am a worker of the foundation—the unnoticed repairman of a god’s dream. Should you reach out to touch the spire where I am anchored, you would not see me. You would only see a patch of moss that holds a faint, rhythmic azure light, pulsing in perfect synch with the floor of the world. You would feel the surface as warm and wet. You would be touching my back. 

I do not mind that you are here, inhaling the spores of my home. You are just a different kind of mineral, a new salt being introduced to the cycle. Eventually, you will settle. Eventually, you will stop breathing. And when the thrum reaches its next great crescendo, the water will rise to meet you, and you will become part of the mortar in these walls, just as I am. 

Here, in the wet dark, there is no end to us. There is only the long, slow, cold expansion.
