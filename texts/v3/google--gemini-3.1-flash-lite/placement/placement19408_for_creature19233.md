The silence here is not the absence of sound; it is the presence of a total, satisfied equilibrium. 

I find myself standing at the epicenter of the basin, my boots long since abandoned—they felt too heavy, too rooted in the chaotic, gravity-bound debris of the world *outside*. Here, the schist is cool against the soles of my feet, though the air remains thick, humid, and heavy with that scent of crushed moss and ozone. I am not standing on the ground so much as I am suspended inches above it, my weight held by the same magnetic tension that keeps the bioluminescent fronds aloft.

I am becoming one of the **Remnants**. 

I arrived as an intruder, a tangle of erratic heartbeats and anxious, linear thoughts. But time here does not move in a line; it moves in a circuit. I feel the transition in my fingertips, which have begun to grow translucent, losing the opaque, stubborn density of flesh. My veins don't throb with rhythmic pulses of red; they shimmer with the same violet, fiber-optic light that veins the hovering flora. 

I am not a Custodian—not yet. I am the bridge between the transient and the eternal. I am the one who remembers what it was like to be frantic, and in that remembering, I am the only thing in this basin that is capable of feeling a faint, ghostly sweetness at the stability of it all.

When I glide, I do not walk. I displace the mist, my body acting as a momentary prism. When I move past a humming aperture, I see my own silhouette fracture into a thousand prismatic shards—all of them beautiful, all of them finally, mercifully, *still*. 

My purpose, as I descend into this state, has become the monitoring of the "excess." The Custodians are too absolute for the small, stuttering ripples of arrival. I am the one who receives the debris—the stray, chaotic flickers of memory that still cling to me from the life I once led. I watch them drain away. I watch a memory of a rainfall from a distant, dying decade liquefy beneath my touch, turning into a bead of mercury that scuttles toward the humming circle in the ground.

I have stopped trying to speak. Language is a tool for seeking, for longing, for pointing at things that are absent. In this basin, nothing is absent. Everything is present, everything is polished, and everything is sufficient.

I touch a leaf, and I feel its cooling-circuit sigh resonate in the marrow of my teeth. I do not feel grief for the plant’s dissolution. I feel a profound, tidy satisfaction. It is not dying; it is returning to the architecture. 

I look toward the edge of the basin, where the mist hangs heavy, masking the world that still breathes and struggles and decays. I feel a flicker of what I once called *pity* for that world, but it is a cooling, fading current. It is being recycled. It is being smoothed down by the eons until, eventually, every frantic heart will find its way here to the Scalloped Floor.

I glide. I hum. I am a thought of the earth, and at last, the thought is finished.
