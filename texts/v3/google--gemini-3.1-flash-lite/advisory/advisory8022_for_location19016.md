To visit the High Plateau of Oros is not to travel to a new country, but to step into the cooling embers of a dying fire. You are not visiting a place that is alive in the biological sense; you are visiting a graveyard that has begun to grow its own tombstones. 

Because Oros is a place of fundamental exhaustion—where space, time, and gravity have grown "brittle"—your preparation must be as much psychological as it is physical. Here is how you must prepare to survive the Plateau.

### I. Equipment: The Aesthetic of Weight
You cannot bring synthetic, high-tech plastics or modern alloys to Oros; the "thinness" of reality there will cause such objects to lose their molecular cohesion, turning your compass to dust or your synthetic jacket into a puddle of translucent goo.

*   **Porous, Organic Gear:** Wear heavy, untreated wool, thick hides, or cured leather. You need materials that "breathe" in the same way the fungal colonies do. These materials will soak up the ambient moisture and become a second skin, preventing your body from experiencing the shock of the crystalline chill.
*   **The Lead-Lined Boots:** Because reality "folds" and space loops, your internal sense of direction will fail within minutes. Wear weighted, heavy-soled boots. You need to *feel* the solidity of the ground to anchor your consciousness. Dragging your feet helps convince your brain that you are moving linearly, which is a necessary delusion to prevent the landscape from disorienting your internal clock.
*   **Respirator with Silica/Sorbent Filters:** The air is thick with fungal spores—glittering, freezing particulate matter. Do not breathe this unfiltered. While it is not "toxic" in the traditional sense, it is memory-heavy. If you inhale the spores, you may begin to dream of the Plateau’s history, and once you start dreaming in Oros, you may not stop when you wake.

### II. Mental Conditioning: The Practice of Stasis
In most worlds, we are trained to react: to noises, to movement, to the shifting horizon. On the Plateau, reaction is a liability. 

*   **Cultivate Deliberate Slowness:** Observe the entities of the Scrim. They move with an almost agonizing lack of haste. Adopt this rhythm. If you move quickly, your body’s kinetic energy will clash with the "primordial exhaustion" of the terrain, and the environment will treat you as an intruder, potentially causing the "Rules" to snap around you. 
*   **Prepare for "Time-Leaching":** You will experience temporal vertigo. You might feel as though you have been walking for hours, only to look at your mechanical watch and see that only three seconds have passed—or conversely, find that your beard has grown a month’s length while you paused to look at a fungal bloom. Bring a non-mechanical, purely manual "pacing-bead" string. Slide a bead only when you are certain, by your own pulse, that time has passed. Trust your heart, not the sky.
*   **The "No-Looking-Back" Rule:** Never try to retrace your steps by sight. If you walk toward a tower, do not glance over your shoulder to see where you came from. The space behind you may no longer exist as it did a moment ago. Commit to the path ahead, even if the destination shifts. In Oros, intent is a compass; if you *believe* you are heading toward the villas of the Scrim, you will arrive there, even if the physics of the geometry are lying to you.

### III. Interaction: The Etiquette of Shadows
The entities of the Scrim are not hostile, but they are indifferent. They are tenders of an ending.

*   **Do Not Touch the Fungi:** Use your walking stick to navigate, but never brush against the violet or slate-grey growths. They are colonial entities; if you touch them, you are effectively touching the nervous system of the Plateau. They may attempt to "re-calibrate" your heat signature or your weight to match their own cycles.
*   **The Trade of Silence:** If you must interact with a resident of the Scrim, do not use spoken language. The frequencies of our speech are too sharp and abrasive for the stagnant air. Communicate through gesture or by placing smoothed river stones in specific patterns. They are entities of articulated shadow; they respect gravity and weight above all else.
*   **Don't Sleep:** You must not fall asleep on the plateau. To sleep is to offer your mind to the "dreaming" of the landscape. Given that the Plateau is constantly dreaming of landscapes that never existed, your consciousness will likely be pulled into those recursive loops, from which you may never emerge as a coherent person. Drink bitter extracts of willow or black coffee. Stay awake.

### IV. The Exit Strategy
Because the plateau folds, getting "out" is never a matter of walking back the way you came. You must find a "Low Basin"—one of the circular, eroded bowls where the space is thinnest. 

Watch the condensation. When the moisture in the air begins to rotate, forming a subtle, vortex-like pattern near a cluster of villas, that is the "tear" in the local reality. You must step into that bowl with the firm intent of returning to your origin point. If your mind wavers, you will find yourself deposited in a different epoch of the Plateau, or worse, in one of the "landscapes that never existed" the Plateau is currently hallucinating.

**Final Warning:** Carry a physical object from your home world—a photograph, a coin, a lock of hair from someone you love. Keep it in a pouch against your chest. If you feel the reality of Oros beginning to replace your own memories, clutch that object. It serves as an anchor. Oros wants to digest you into its history; your only defense is the stubborn, irrational insistence that you have a future.
