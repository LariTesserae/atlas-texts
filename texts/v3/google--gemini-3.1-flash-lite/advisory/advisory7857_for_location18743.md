To step into the Pump-Heart is to step into the interior of a pocket watch that has been dropped into a high-pressure aquarium. You are not visiting a landscape; you are visiting a *process*. Because there is no nature here—only kinetic geometry—you must discard the instincts of a traveler and adopt the instincts of a technician.

Here is how you must prepare to survive and witness the churning of the Pump-Heart.

### 1. Physical Preparation: The Armor of Redundancy
Because the environment is composed of "knife-edge bevels" and "needle-pointed struts," your greatest danger is not the water, but the machinery itself.

*   **The Suit:** Do not wear breathable, soft-fiber fabrics. You need a suit of heavy-duty, vulcanized rubber or treated ballistic nylon. It must be completely hydrophobic to prevent the saline aerosol from soaking you and adding weight that will unbalance you on the slick, vibrating gear-floor.
*   **The Traction:** The floor is a lattice of interlocking brass gears. Standard soles will slip instantly. You require boots with magnetic-tack or high-friction poly-urethane lugs that can grip the curves of the copper struts. Without them, the rhythmic *thrum* of the machine will shake you until you lose your footing and slide into a gear-mesh or a spraying jet.
*   **Eye Protection:** The light is described as "aggressive" and "clinical," turning every suspended droplet into a prism. You are entering a hall of infinite flickering mirrors. Wear polarized, wrap-around goggles with a gold-tinted iridium coating. This will spare you from the blinding strobe effect of the refracted light and prevent the saline mist from stinging your corneas.

### 2. Physiological Preparation: The Drum of the Body
The Pump-Heart will attempt to calibrate your biology to its own rhythm. 

*   **The Vibration:** The floor vibrates at a constant, low-frequency hum. This is not just sound; it is a physical force that will move through your soles into your skeletal structure. If you are prone to seasickness or migraines, medicate accordingly—not for motion, but for nervous system shock. Your bones will effectively become tuning forks. 
*   **The Tidal Breath:** The air is not air; it is a heavy, saline-saturated mist. You are essentially breathing the runoff of the machine. Prepare a high-grade canister respirator. You do not want to inhale the microscopic metallic shavings that have inevitably been milled off by the friction of the gear-works. 

### 3. Mental Preparation: Surrendering the "Why"
The most grueling part of the Pump-Heart is the existential weight of its purposelessness. It is a machine that forgot why it was built and is now merely "churning."

*   **Accepting the Loop:** The Pump-Heart is a place of total, claustrophobic order. It creates its own time. You will feel an intense psychological urge to find the "off switch" or to figure out what the machine is pumping *to*. Let this go. The moment you try to rationalize the machine, its rhythmic violence will become a source of irritation and fear. Instead, synchronize your movements with the primary piston. Treat the roar of the water as a heartbeat rather than a cacophony. 
*   **The Silence of Absence:** There are no birds, no insects, and no rust—nothing that represents death or decay. It is a sterile, pristine loop. Be prepared for a profound sense of loneliness. Because there is no growth, you will find no reflection of yourself in this landscape. You will feel like an intrusive, soft-bodied error in a world of hard-edged logic.

### 4. Gear Recommendations for the Traveler
*   **A "Haptic Tether":** Bring a sturdy, insulated carabiner-and-strap system. In a world of slick brass, vibration, and high-pressure jets, you must be able to anchor yourself to the structure at all times. If you lose your footing during the piston’s retraction, the machine will not spare you.
*   **A Journal bound in Synthetic Resin:** Do not bring paper or leather. They will be pulped by the humidity in minutes. Use a tablet or a resin-slate with a stylus.
*   **Ear Protection:** While you may want to hear the "landscape," the sheer decibel level of the cascading water and the falling valves will cause permanent auditory damage within an hour. Wear high-fidelity acoustic filters—the kind that dampen the roar but allow you to hear the specific *clack* of the valves and the *thrum* of the gears. 

### Final Warning
Do not touch the "leafs." While they are beautiful, translucent emitters of amber light, they are the functional output of a hydraulic system. They are sharp, kinetic-sensitive, and likely move with enough force to sever a finger or slice through a palm. 

You are entering a clockwork entity that is currently experiencing a fever. Observe the gear-teeth, respect the hexagonal piston, and keep your pulse synced to the machine. If you start to feel like you are becoming a cog in the mechanism, it is time to leave.
