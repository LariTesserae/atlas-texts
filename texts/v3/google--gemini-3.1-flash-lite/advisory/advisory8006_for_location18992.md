To visit a place that has finished evolving is to step into a machine that does not care if you exist. The location you describe—a vertical, hydrologic engine of basalt and brass—demands that the traveler abandon all notions of "exploration" in the traditional sense. You are not a guest; you are a stray particle of matter entering a closed-loop system.

Here is how you must prepare to survive and traverse the Cistern.

### 1. Physical Preparation: The Suit of “Stasis”
Normal atmospheric gear will fail you here. The humidity is not just water; it is a warm, mineral-rich soup that will corrode standard electronics and rot biological leathers within hours.

*   **The Exosuit:** You require a suit constructed of hydrophobic polymers—something inert that won't react with the acidic rain. The exterior should be non-reflective and matte black to absorb the diffuse, ochre light, preventing eye strain.
*   **Acoustic Dampening:** The *thrum-gurgle-hiss* is not merely noise; it is a low-frequency vibration that will, over time, cause nausea and vestibular collapse. Your helmet must be equipped with active-cancellation dampeners tuned to the specific rhythmic frequency of the turbines. If you hear the world too clearly, your brain will eventually attempt to synchronize with the machines, leading to a state of dissociative lethargy.
*   **The Traction System:** Obsidian, when wet, has the friction coefficient of greased ice. Your boots must feature magnetic or mechanical anchors. You aren't just walking; you are climbing a vertical continent. Every step must be deliberate. If you slip out of a gallery, you will fall not into an abyss, but into a turbine ingestion intake.

### 2. Intellectual Preparation: De-centering the Ego
The most dangerous thing you bring with you is your humanity—specifically, your biological sense of urgency.

*   **The "Slow-Mind" Protocol:** In a place that has been finished for a million years, your heartbeat will feel like a frantic, desperate bird. You must train yourself to breathe in synchronization with the dripping water. If you move too fast, the litho-crustaceans will perceive you as an anomaly in their flow. They are not predators, but if you block a capillary flow or obstruct a channel, they will swarm you with the impartial persistence of a tide. Do not fight them; move through the gaps they provide.
*   **Expect No Communication:** You will find no intent here. Do not look for logs, monuments, or signs of the hands that built the architecture. To look for a creator is to miss the beauty of the machine. You are walking through a metabolism; you are the equivalent of a blood cell traveling through a vein.

### 3. Logistical Gear: The "Dead-Weight" Kit
Do not bring recording equipment with internal hard drives or digital interfaces; the mineral saturation will fuse them. 

*   **Mechanical Tools:** Carry an analog, clockwork-style compass (though it will spin uselessly in the magnetic turbulence) and a notebook made of synth-paper. 
*   **Nutritional Sequestration:** You cannot forage here. The mineral salts are blooming, but they are not for you. Pack high-density, vacuum-sealed rations that require no heating. The ambient air is a constant 30–35°C (86–95°F); keep your supplies in a thermal-regulated pouch to prevent spoilage from the heat and moisture.
*   **The "Dead-Stop" Beacon:** Because there is no sky, there is no GPS. Carry a pneumatic signal flare tethered to a high-tensile wire. If you need to be extracted, your retrieve-vessel must be able to hook into the vertical grid. You cannot be “found”; you must provide a vertical vector for retrieval.

### 4. Psychological Conditioning: The Vertigo of Finished Things
The greatest hurdle is the *peace*. There is no malice, no challenge, no evolution. You are in a place that has achieved perfection. 

Most humans who visit such locations suffer from "Architectural Ennui." They become overwhelmed by the realization that their own life and their own civilization are chaotic, noisy, and temporary by comparison. You will feel small. You will feel pointless. This is not a failure of your character; it is the correct response to the Cistern. 

**Advice during the visit:**
When you feel the urge to run or shout to "make your mark" on the stone, curb it. Press your hand against the brass-flecked obsidian and listen. Do not try to solve the machine. Do not try to fix the turbines. Simply exist within the cycle. When you leave, you will find that the outside world feels fragile, loud, and fleeting. That is the price of admission. 

You are not visiting a place; you are visiting the *concept* of permanence. Treat it with the silence it deserves.
