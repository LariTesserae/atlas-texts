To visit the *Basin of Constant Tears* is to enter a state of perpetual, shivering dissolution. You are not visiting a destination; you are intruding upon a process. The Basin is a living organism of salt and bone, and the primary challenge of your journey is not navigation, but material survival. 

Here is how a traveler must prepare for this jagged, weeping cathedral.

### 1. Physiological Preparation: The Body as an Alien Element
The Basin will attempt to regulate your temperature and moisture to match its own. You are a warm, dry anomaly in a cold, weeping system.
*   **Thermal Insulation:** Traditional down jackets are death sentences here; they will soak up brine, lose loft, and turn into heavy, freezing weights. You require a **fully sealed dry-suit**, ideally vulcanized or made of non-porous synthetic polymers. The outer layer must be hydrophobic to an extreme degree to prevent the brined moisture from bonding with your suit.
*   **The "Squint-Factor":** The ambient glare is not optical—it is biological. The reflection off the calcified ribs will cause severe migraines and potentially burn your retinas. You need **goggles with polarized, high-density iridium lenses** (the darkest available). Do not rely on sunglasses; the brine will weep into the gaps around the frames.
*   **Tactile Defense:** You will be physically touching the geometry of the Basin constantly. Wear **gauntlets with high-friction, textured rubber palms**. The architecture is slippery; if you cannot grip the bone-struts, you will fall into the runnels.

### 2. Physical and Mental Conditioning
The architecture is designed for creatures smaller than you. To navigate, you must cultivate "low-center" mobility.
*   **Joint Flexibility:** Practice yoga or Pilates for at least three months prior to arrival. You will spend hours in a crouch. If your knees or lower back are stiff, you will fail.
*   **Sensory Overload Mitigation:** The *drip-clink-drip* is a psychological rhythmic torture. It is designed by the Basin to erode the ego. Bring **active noise-canceling earplugs** that can block mid-to-high frequency pings. You need to keep the sound of the environment out of your head, or the "frantic" pace of the inhabitants will begin to invade your own heartbeat.
*   **Vibrational Awareness:** The inhabitants communicate through the marrow-struts of the walls. If you touch the walls—which you must—do so with intentionality. If you lean too hard or carelessly, you may be signaling "danger" or "disturbance" to the colony. Adopt a light, "gliding" touch.

### 3. Equipment: The Tools of the Visitor
Do not bring heavy packs. Anything that hangs off your body will snag on the porous struts, causing you to stumble and disrupt the delicate, ongoing repairs of the residents.
*   **The Harness:** Everything you carry must be strapped tight to your torso. Nothing dangling.
*   **Nutritional Salts:** You will lose minerals rapidly in this environment. Your sweat will be drawn out by the brine, and the cold will burn through your caloric reserves. Carry high-density, electrolyte-gel pouches that can be consumed without removing your gloves or mask.
*   **The Scraper (Peace-Offering):** Inhabitants are perpetually repairing. If you accidentally erode or damage a wall (and you *will*), you will become a focus of their agitation. Carry a small, blunt **surgical-grade spatula**. If you cause a breakage, do not apologize—they won't understand—simply mend the crack with a small bit of inert, bio-compatible filler. It is the only "language" they speak.

### 4. Psychological Survival
The Basin of Constant Tears does not have a "center." It is a web of bridges and chambers that reconfigures based on the freeze-thaw cycles. **Do not attempt to map it.** A map created five minutes ago is a flat, meaningless lie now. 
*   **Surrender to the Flux:** The moment you attempt to find a "way out" or a "final destination," you will succumb to the disorientation. Treat the Basin like a meditation. Focus on the next two inches of surface in front of you. 
*   **Avoiding the Inhabitants:** If a surge of creatures comes toward you, **flatten yourself against the lowest part of the strut.** Do not stand up. You are a mountain in their city; if you remain low, you are merely a part of the terrain. If you rise, you are an obstacle to be smoothed away.

### A Final Warning: The Melancholy
The greatest risk in the Basin is not the cold or the fall—it is the sorrow of the place. It is a location that mourns its own erasure. Travelers have been known to stay too long, finding the cycle of *weep-freeze-mend* to be more "honest" than the chaotic entropy of the outside world. 

If you find yourself watching a puddle of brine and feeling a strange, hollow kinship with the salt—**leave.** The Basin will eventually make you part of the geometry, and the spiders will scrape you down until you are nothing but a smooth, white curve on a cold, forgotten wall.
