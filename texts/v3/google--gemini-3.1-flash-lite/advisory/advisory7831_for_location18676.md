To visit **Ouroboros-on-the-Spine** is to walk inside a giant, dying, yet perpetually birthing clock. It is a place where the barrier between "constructed reality" and "raw potential" has worn thin, leaving you exposed to the tectonic tremors of a planet still finding its shape.

As a traveler, your preparation must focus on three pillars: **Physical Anchorage**, **Sensory Shielding**, and **Cognitive Grounding.**

### 1. Physical Anchorage: The Suit
Standard thermal gear will fail you here. The cold is not merely a lack of heat; it is an aggressive, damp, vacuum-like presence that seeks to leech the stability from your atoms.

*   **The Weight Suit:** You must wear something weighted. The ground—and the space around you—is subject to "stretch." If your center of gravity is not anchored by leaden soles or dense, reinforced boots, you risk having your gait elongated or losing your physical orientation in the distorted geometry of the sky-bridges.
*   **Non-Porous Layers:** The air is saturated with oxidized iron dust and sulfuric-smelling condensation. Wear a suit made of treated, slick-skinned polymers. You do not want the black, industrial soot of the city to soak into your clothes; it is the physical residue of reality-fraying, and it will cling to you long after you leave.
*   **The Atmospheric Regulator:** Because the air is impossibly thin, a standard oxygen tank is necessary, but it must be fitted with an **ion-static filter**. You must avoid breathing the "black snow" (the suspended soot). If you inhale these flecks of frozen, un-realized matter, you will experience a form of psychic indigestion—hallucinations of distant, non-existent histories that play out in the back of your mind like corrupted film stock.

### 2. Sensory Shielding: Dealing with the Resonance
The constant, low-frequency thrum of the cathedral-sized gears is designed to synchronize with the mountain’s heartbeat. If your internal rhythm attempts to match it, you will lose your equilibrium.

*   **Active Noise-Cancellation:** You require high-fidelity, industrial-grade suppressors to dampen the low-frequency drone. If you listen to it for too long, you will begin to "feel" the gears moving through your own bones. It creates a hypnotic compulsion to join the slow, patient labor of the city. 
*   **Visors of Fixed Geometry:** The world here stretches and duplicates. If you look at the architecture too long, you may find your eyes tracking a street that loops into infinity, inducing severe vertigo. Your visor should have a polarized, static grid etched into the lens. This provides a "constant" frame of reference, allowing your brain to ignore the way the pipes and bridges occasionally warp and elongate.

### 3. Cognitive Grounding: The "Anchor Object"
In Ouroboros-on-the-Spine, memory is the first thing to lose cohesion. As you walk, you may find your memories of "how you got here" fraying at the edges.

*   **The Tether:** Carry a heavy, tactile object—a smooth river stone, a piece of old, rusted chain, or a physical photograph of somewhere static and familiar. Whenever the reality-shifting begins—when you see the black snow hanging in the air or the buildings start to spiral—hold the object in your hand and squeeze. Focus on the temperature and the texture. You are a stable construct entering a site of fluid potential; remind your subconscious that you are a singular, finished being.
*   **The Rule of Three:** Never look at anything in the city for more than three seconds, unless it is a machine. If you stare at the moss or the mist, you will notice patterns that suggest the city is watching you, or worse, that it is trying to absorb you into its tectonic cycles. Treat the city as a *process*, not a *place*.

### 4. Behavioral Etiquette: "The Static Scavengers"
The metallic-feathered scavengers are the only creatures that have mastered the city's shifting geometry. 
*   **Do not interact.** They feed on the electricity of the conduits to stabilize their own physical forms. If they sense you are "loose"—meaning you are suffering from the stretching of space—they may mistake your bio-electric field for a potential energy source.
*   **Pace:** Move exactly as they do: slowly, deliberately, and with long pauses. If you run, you will attract the attention of the city’s architectural stress, which tends to compress around sudden, high-energy movements. Let your heartbeat drop to match the thrum of the gears. 

### Final Warning
There is a point in every excursion where the city will exhale. The pressure will drop, the metallic shrieks will silence for a heartbeat, and the mist will clear to reveal the nothingness beneath the spire. Do not look down. If you see the mountain "deciding" what it wants to be, do not let that image etch itself into your mind. 

You go to Ouroboros-on-the-Spine to witness the labor of creation, but you should always leave with the feeling that you are *not* part of its design. **Stay heavy, keep your rhythm internal, and never, ever think about staying.**
