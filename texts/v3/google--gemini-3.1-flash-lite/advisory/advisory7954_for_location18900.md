To visit the Low Basin is to step into the digestive tract of a dead god. It is a place that does not merely tolerate human presence—it actively seeks to dissolve it. Because the air itself is a solvent of heat and moisture, and the geography is a cycle of slow, industrial decay, your preparation must prioritize **integrity, respiration, and repulsion.**

### 1. Physical Protection: The "Dry-Suit" Protocol
You cannot wear organic fibers. Cotton will dampen within ten minutes, lose its structural integrity, and become a breeding ground for the mildew that permeates this realm. Wool will become the heavy, suffocating "wet blanket" described in the atmosphere. 

*   **Materials:** Wear non-porous, synthetic polymers. A full-body, airtight tactical dry-suit, the kind used for extreme deep-sea welding, is the only viable option. Surfaces must be hydrophobic; you want the weeping walls and the dripping ceiling to bead off you rather than soak into your layers.
*   **Sealing:** Pay particular attention to the wrists, ankles, and neck. The "presence" in the basin implies microscopic dust or spores—the particulate matter of oxidized iron and pulverized basalt. If this gets inside your gear, it *will* irritate your skin, likely causing a rapid rash exacerbated by the stagnant heat.
*   **Boots:** Heavy-tread, high-traction boots with a non-porous, acid-resistant vulcanized rubber sole are mandatory. The basalt is constantly wet and slick; a slip here isn't just a physical injury—it’s an opportunity for the iron-shelled crustaceans to congregate on your stationary form.

### 2. Respiratory Survival: Filtering the Scent
The air is thick with the tang of oxidized iron and wet soot. This is not just a smell; it is aerosolized particulate saturation. 
*   **Filtration:** Bring a closed-circuit rebreather (CCR) or, at the very least, a military-grade respirator with P100/Organic Vapor cartridges. The air is stagnant; if you rely on standard air, you are inhaling the mineral exhaust of a thousand-year-old machine. Your lungs will coat with a fine silt within hours, leading to "Basin Lung"—a dry, rasping cough that mimics the clicking of the local fauna.
*   **Climate Control:** The heat is a "microclimate of rot." If you can, pack a suite of phase-change cooling vests. The ambient heat is trapped; you will overheat even if you aren't exerting yourself.

### 3. Navigation and Tools
The Low Basin is a city of "drainage logic." It was designed for water, not for people.
*   **Light:** The amber glow of the failing industrial lanterns is deceptive. It is the color of old rot. Bring a high-lumen, cool-spectrum LED lantern. You need to see the edges of the basalt clearly because they are rounded and treacherous. 
*   **The Crustacean Deterrent:** The iron-shelled crustaceans are not predators, but they are *harvesters*. They do not distinguish between a rusted pipe and a piece of equipment attached to your hip. Keep your gear sleek. Avoid hanging tools or dangling straps that could mimic the texture of their food source. If they swarm you, do not panic; remain calm and step slowly. Sudden movements provoke the "tidings" of their clicking. Use a high-frequency ultrasonic emitter; most iron-shelled organisms in subterranean environments fear vibrations that disrupt their structural frequency.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: The "Static" Mindset
The most dangerous aspect of the Low Basin is its lack of vitality. There is no wind, no weather, no change. The human mind is hardwired for transition—for dusk to turn to night, for wind to move the air. Here, the lack of circulation creates a sensory deprivation chamber of "static equilibrium."

*   **Mental Anchor:** Do not look for an exit or a goal. The Basin is a place where motion is a violation of the local law. If you walk as if you are "going somewhere," you will succumb to the frustration of the infinite, hollow plaza.
*   **The Ritual of Observation:** Treat the city like a museum of a machine that is still dreaming. Watch the water drip. Count the rhythmic click of the crustaceans. If you try to impose your "human-scale" desire for progress on the environment, you will be exhausted by the sheer, heavy weight of the place. 

### 5. Essential Logistics Checklist
*   **Desiccant Packs:** Keep these in your equipment bag. Even in your dry-suit, internal humidity will build up from your own perspiration.
*   **High-Calorie Gel:** Do not bring food that requires cooking or creates crumbs. The smell of organic matter in a place of pure mineral rot attracts unwanted attention—not just from the bugs, but from whatever "presence" inhabits the deeper, silent vaults.
*   **A "Dead-Weight" Tether:** If you are traveling as a group, stay within physical reach of one another. The acoustics of the vaults are deceptive; a sound can echo and refract, making a friend sound like they are fifty meters away when they are right behind you, or vice-versa.

**A Final Warning:** If you see a basin of water that is shimmering with an amber light that seems *too* vibrant, do not approach it. The Low Basin is a digestive system, and every stomach has its acid pools. If the iron-shelled creatures stop clicking simultaneously, stop moving. They are listening for something the city has been waiting to swallow.
