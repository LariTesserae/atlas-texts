To step into Oulo is to surrender the concept of "ground." For a human traveler, this is not merely a change in geography; it is a psychological deconstruction. You are entering a digestive, respiratory, and structural metabolism that has functioned for eons without your presence, and will not care if you choose to occupy it or drift away into the abyss.

Here is how you must prepare to survive, move, and exist within the vertical archipelago.

### 1. The Physiological Adjustment: "The Marrow-Hum"
The most immediate challenge is not the air, but the acoustic resonance. The constant, low-frequency thrum of the *Lumen-Oaks* vibrating in the cyclone will initially cause nausea. This is "resonance sickness." 
*   **The Preparation:** Spend time in an isolation tank with low-frequency sonic stimulation prior to arrival. You must train your equilibrium to stop fighting the vibration and instead treat it as a background constant, like a heartbeat. If you try to tense your muscles against the hum, your joints will ache within hours. You must learn to be "limp" in your internal musculature.

### 2. The Attire: Silk and Seal
Do not bring synthetic fibers, metal fasteners, or rigid gear. These will seem abrasive and discordant to the locals, and a sharp edge could accidentally score the living skin of a *Lumen-Oak*.
*   **The Material:** Wear tight-fitting, porous fabrics—organic linens or wicking silks. You want clothing that holds a layer of the nutrient-rich dew against your skin, as the air will leech moisture from you rapidly.
*   **The Tether:** This is your life-line. Procure—or learn to weave—a multi-strand vine-silk tether. It must be flexible, self-tightening, and fitted with a harness that distributes load across your entire torso rather than just your waist. You will spend 90% of your time tethered to a *Lumen-Oak* trunk or a bridge.
*   **Footwear:** Leave the boots. You need specialized, soft-soled slippers with high-friction, plant-resin grips. Your hands and feet must be calloused—begin an aggressive regimen of grip-strengthening today. 

### 3. The Gear: The "No-Metal" Mandate
Oulo is a biologically reactive environment. Metal is a foreign, cold, and dead element.
*   **Containment:** Any items you bring must be stored in vessels made of hardened resin or dried translucent leaf-sheaths. If you have "tech," it must be shielded in wax-sealed bark containers to prevent electromagnetic interference with the bioluminescent moss, which may react aggressively or die if exposed to alien frequencies.
*   **Tools:** Bring nothing serrated. A simple, smooth-edged piece of polished obsidian or flint is all you need for utility. You are not meant to conquer this wood; you are meant to negotiate with it.

### 4. Psychological Conditioning: The Temporal Shift
In Oulo, haste is a deformity. If you move quickly, you will disturb the local currents, create unwanted turbulence, and mark yourself as an erratic, dangerous element.
*   **The Practice:** Adopt a "slow-cadence." Practice swimming in mid-air—or diving in deep, buoyant water—to internalize the way a body moves through a viscous medium. Every motion must be deliberate. You are a guest in a cathedral that has been breathing for ten thousand years; do not startle the architecture.
*   **Navigation:** You must learn to "read the drift." The mist is not random; it has a heartbeat. Learn to notice the subtle temperature changes in the air that signal the movement of the *Cloud-Skimmers*. If you see them gathering, find a secure, hollowed-out bark-chamber. They are the heralds of atmospheric shifts.

### 5. Managing the "Lost Guest" Risk
The fear of being "lost" is the greatest psychological hurdle. To become a *Lost Guest* is to experience an agonizingly slow transition from a traveler to a drifting, oxygen-starved husk.
*   **The Sensory Anchor:** Carry a mnemonic object—a small, heavy stone from your home world. It will provide a tactile reminder of the concept of "down" and "solid mass." Whenever the buoyancy-defying pressure of Oulo makes you feel unmoored or prone to drifting, grip the stone. It is your tether to the reality of the solid world.

### 6. Interaction Protocol: The Silent Exchange
The inhabitants are lean and pale, with a range of movement that looks more like seaweed in a tide than a human walking on pavement. Do not expect them to speak in the way you are accustomed. 
*   **Communication:** They communicate through rhythmic tension in the silk-cables and modulated whistles. Do not try to shout; the wind will swallow it. Observe their fingers. When you arrive, remain still, hold your palms open and empty, and wait for them to approach. Let them touch your skin—they will be assessing your "health" by the moisture on your palms.

**Final Warning:** The dew that coats the surfaces of the city is highly nutrient-rich. Do not drink it blindly; it is designed to fuel the *Lumen-Oaks*, not human biology. Carry your own purified water source in an organic bladder, and do not attempt to consume local flora without a guide. 

In Oulo, you are fragile, you are loud, and you are heavy. Be humble. Breathe with the trees. And above all, keep your knots tight.
