To visit a city that is not built, but *accumulated*, is to abandon the human concept of a "destination" and accept the role of a stowaway in a geological process. You are not entering a municipality; you are entering the gills of a continent.

To prepare for the Plateau of Sediment, you must strip away the frantic utility of modern life and equip yourself for a world of slow, saturated endurance.

### 1. Physical Preparation: The Suit of Second Skin
Forget standard rain gear. Stiff, synthetic fabrics will betray you here; the sound of nylon brushing against damp basalt will be deafening in the silence, and the material will fail to breathe, turning your suit into a personal sauna.

*   **The Material:** You require high-density, hydrophobic wool or treated felt. These materials move silently and can handle constant humidity without becoming heavy. Your outer layer should be porous but moisture-wicking, designed to mimic the capillary action of the city itself.
*   **The Footwear:** Do not bring boots with rubber lugs or heavy treads—they will struggle for purchase on the moss-slicked gullies. You need soft-soled, high-friction leather, similar to a climber’s shoe but with significant surface area. You must be able to feel the stone beneath you to sense if it is stable or saturated enough to give way.
*   **The Sensory Filter:** The low-frequency hum of dripping water is rhythmic, but it is also a form of sensory deprivation. Over days, this "white noise of the earth" can induce a state of hypnotic disassociation. Bring earplugs that dampen high-frequency pitch but allow you to hear the subtle, gravelly shifting of the limestone walls—the only "warning" the architecture ever gives before it settles.

### 2. Metabolic Adjustment: The Slowing of the Heart
The air here is thin and heavy with moisture. It is not an environment for high heart rates. If you arrive with the "frantic urgency" of a traveler, the city will feel claustrophobic and hostile.

*   **Practice Stillness:** Before you cross the threshold, spend two weeks practicing meditative stillness. Reduce your intake of stimulants. When you are in the city, your greatest danger is not the architecture, but your own metabolism. If you move too fast, you will sweat, and that moisture will be trapped against your skin by the ambient humidity, leading to rapid heat loss. 
*   **Nutritional Storage:** The city has no "fire" and no market. You must be entirely self-sufficient. Bring dense, cold-processed fats and slow-burning proteins. Avoid anything that requires heating; the flame would be an insult to the cool, wet equilibrium of the place and would draw unwanted attention from the translucent, sightless fauna that prefer the shadows.

### 3. The Mental Framework: Abandoning "Construction"
In this city, you are not a tourist; you are a particle of sediment. 

*   **The Concept of "Up":** The city does not follow human geometry. Because the towers were formed by accumulation, they often curve or lean in ways that baffle the inner ear. You will experience vertigo. Do not fight it. Focus on the water-streaks—they are the city’s constant lines of gravity. If you feel lost, follow the flow of a droplet. It knows the path to the foundation better than your mapped-out mental trajectory.
*   **Navigating the Silence:** You will be tempted to speak. Do not. The silence is a physical weight, a membrane that keeps the city from collapsing into cacophony. If you speak, you are effectively "tearing" the local atmosphere. Communicate through gesture or, better yet, learn the tactile language of the walls. Place your hands on the basalt honeycombs; you will feel the vibrations of the city’s lifeblood moving through the stone.

### 4. Hazard Mitigation: The "Sinking" Awareness
The city is slowly settling under the weight of its own hydration. 

*   **The Warning Signs:** Watch the lichen. If a patch of pale flora on a wall suddenly looks dehydrated or turns a darker shade, it means the water source in that specific stone column has shifted. The pillar is likely beginning to buckle or re-compress. Leave that corridor immediately. 
*   **The Fauna:** The translucent scavengers are indifferent to you, provided you do not infringe on their silence. They are creatures of steady, suction-pad movement. If you see one, stop. Watch how it navigates the vertical planes. It will show you the most stable route through the next gully. Do not try to photograph or touch them; they are fragile, and the oils on your skin are corrosive to their delicate physiology.

### A Final Warning
You will find, after a few days, that your sense of linear time begins to fray. The city’s cycle is not measured in hours, but in the slow expansion and contraction of limestone pores. When you leave, the world of metal, heat, and "sharp edges" will feel violently loud and incomprehensibly dry. 

Pack a small vial of the city's water if you must—not for scientific analysis, but to remember the weight of the stillness. You will need it to ground yourself once you return to a place that insists on being "built."
