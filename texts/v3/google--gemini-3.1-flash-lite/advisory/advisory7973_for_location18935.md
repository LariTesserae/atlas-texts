To visit the Submerged Lattice is not merely to travel to another world; it is to submit oneself to the absolute authority of density and time. This is not a place for exploration in the traditional sense, but for *immersion*. Because the environment is a planetary machine of lead and copper trapped in a frigid, viscous ocean, your preparation must prioritize the survival of your own physical integrity over all else.

Here is how you must prepare to navigate the silent, metallic tomb.

### 1. Physical Augmentation: The "Solid-State" Suit
You cannot visit in a traditional pressurized submersible—a ship implies a distinction between the "inside" and "outside" that this world will eventually dismantle. You must wear a hard-shelled, articulated exoskeleton of high-density ceramics. 

*   **Pressure Equalization:** Do not use internal air pockets. Your suit must be fluid-filled, using a non-compressible, oxygenated perfluorocarbon liquid. You will need to breathe liquid. It sounds like a nightmare, but at these depths, a gas-filled lung is a hollow, fragile thing that will collapse the moment you misstep.
*   **Acoustic Damping:** Because sound in this world resonates through the marrow, you must line your suit with high-density kinetic buffers. If you do not, the "haunting" thrum of the continent-scale architecture will shake your internal organs loose. You are entering a tuning fork the size of a planet; protect your skeleton.

### 2. Navigation: The Manual Compass
There is no GPS in a world of lead and oxidized copper, and there is no magnetism to be found in a place that has been grounded for eons. The Fabrication is a labyrinth of smoothed, unrecognizable geometry.
*   **The Tactile Map:** Bring a physical stylus and a thick plate of soft lead. Do not rely on digital logs; the moment you enter the deep water, anything electronic will be crushed or rendered useless by the conductive, mineral-heavy sludge. You must carve your path as you go.
*   **The Rivet-Hook:** Since all corners are rounded, you will find nothing to grip. You must bring a manual, spring-loaded rivet gun. To "climb" these hills of bronze, you will have to create your own anchor points. Be warned: the metal is stubborn. It has resisted the ocean for ages; it will resist your tools.

### 3. Psychological Calibration: The Ethics of Stillness
The Fabrication is an architectural void. There is nothing to interact with, no salvage to be found, and no message to be decoded. If you go searching for a "purpose" for this world, you will find only madness.
*   **The Silence Protocol:** You must train yourself to stop talking. In this world, sound is a physical intrusion. Vocal vibrations will travel miles through the copper struts. If you make noise, you are shouting in a cemetery of machines. 
*   **Expect Nothing:** Do not expect the architecture to react to you. It does not care that you are there. It is a machine that has forgotten it was ever built. Prepare your mind to encounter "boredom" at an existential scale. If you are the type of person who needs a view, a goal, or a biological signal to feel alive, this world will crush your spirit before the water crushes your body.

### 4. Hazard Mitigation: The Crystal Thickets
The "lithic-growth"—the violet-glowing crystal ferns—are not alive, but they are sharp in a way the copper pillars are not. Their porous, mineral branches are brittle and needle-pointed.
*   **The Solvent Kit:** You must carry a localized acidic solvent. If a thicket of crystal moss pins you against a copper bulkhead, you cannot struggle; any movement will cause the calcified fibers to shatter and embed themselves in your suit. You must dissolve a path. Bring more solvent than you think you need.

### 5. The Ritual of Arrival
When you first enter the Lattice, do not try to move. You will be tempted to swim or push off the walls. This is a mistake. The water is viscous; it will provide more resistance than you expect. 
*   **The Waiting:** When you arrive, stay perfectly still for at least one hour of local time. Let the water pressure settle into your suit. Let your eyes adjust to the pale, sickly violet bioluminescence. Listen to the structure shift. Let the "thrum" of a distant beam-shift calibrate your sense of rhythm. 

### Final Warning
You are going to a place that is **finished**. There is no "becoming" in the Fabrication; there is only "being." Do not bring hope, and do not bring curiosity. Bring only the capacity to witness a world that has already decided what it is and has no further interest in the universe. 

If you bring anything else, the silence will swallow it.
