To step into the Reservoir of the Unfinished is to enter a digestive tract that has outlived its purpose. It is a place that feels less like a location and more like a fever dream of a mechanical mind. For a traveler, the Reservoir is not merely a destination; it is an endurance test for your sanity and your equipment.

Here is how you must prepare to navigate this hollowed-out expanse.

### 1. Physical Protection: The "Corrosive" Defense
The atmosphere of the Reservoir is a silent killer. It is not just cold; it is chemically active. The leaking iridescent oils and the weeping polymers suggest a high pH imbalance in the moisture.

*   **Materials:** Leave your synthetic nylons and standard trekking gear at home—they will dissolve or become dangerously slick within hours. You require **heavy-duty, vulcanized rubber waders** or a full-body hazmat suit made of fluoropolymer resin. 
*   **Insulation:** The "bone-chilling" temperature is deceptive. It is a conductive cold—the metal floors will draw heat from your body faster than an icy lake. Wear a **heated undersuit** powered by a redundant lithium-sulfur battery pack. 
*   **Footwear:** Standard rubber soles will fail on the oil-slicked, riveted surfaces. You need **magnetic-soled boots** with a high-friction grip. If you slip here, the surfaces are so jagged and sharp—the "un-weathered" edges of the cast iron—that a simple fall could result in a permanent mechanical injury.

### 2. Sensory Mitigation: Tuning Out the Industrial Heartbeat
The Reservoir is not "loud" in the way a city is; it is rhythmically oppressive. The *thrum-hiss* and the tectonic grinding occur at infrasonic frequencies that can induce nausea, vertigo, and a localized form of "constructive psychosis"—the feeling that the walls are leaning inward.

*   **Acoustic Buffer:** Use **active noise-canceling earmuffs** tuned specifically to industrial resonance (low-frequency dampening). Do not block sound entirely; you need to hear the *skitter* of the scavenger-drones. A rhythmic white noise generator inside the headset (playing a sound profile of a heartbeat or wind) can help mask the erratic mechanical cycles of the room, preventing your brain from trying to "find a pattern" in the machine’s respiration.
*   **Lighting:** The sodium-vapor lamps are treacherous. They flicker and cast long, deceitful shadows. Bring a **high-lumen, broad-spectrum LED floodlight** mounted on your chest. Do not rely on headlamps; the constant refraction against the wet, iridescent oil will trigger a strobe effect that leads to rapid eye strain and spatial disorientation.

### 3. Biological Integrity: Dealing with the "Scavengers"
The scavenger-drones are not aggressive in the biological sense, but they are possessive of their maintenance goals. If you get caught in a "repair zone" when a drone senses a leak or a fraying wire, it will treat your suit-material or your gear as "damaged infrastructure."

*   **Equipment Protocol:** Keep your gear strictly isolated from the wall interfaces. Do not touch the fiber-optic "plants." If you brush against them while they are surging, the electric whine denotes a voltage that could arc through your gear.
*   **The Drone Interaction:** If a scavenger-drone approaches, do not freeze. Move with steady, deliberate intent. They follow movement sensors. If you have to stand still, turn off your external lights and become part of the background. They are focused on the machine, not on organic life. Treat them like blind, territorial insects.

### 4. Psychological Preparedness: The "Unfinished" Mindset
The most dangerous aspect of the Reservoir is the lack of "purpose." Many travelers who spend more than a few hours here report a phenomenon known as **Echo-Despair**. You start to feel that your own life lacks a "blueprint," just as the Reservoir lacks a product.

*   **The Anchor:** Bring a physical, non-mechanical object from your home universe. A stone, a book, a photograph. You need something that possesses "natural history" or "weathering." In a place of jagged, sharp-edged cold-cast iron, entropy and wear are absent. Your object will remind you that things *do* end, things *do* decay, and that outside of this hollow, time functions differently.
*   **Mapping:** Do not trust your internal compass. The sheer scale makes the floor plates appear identical in every direction. Carry a **mechanical tactile map** or a length of rope. Never wander out of sight of a "pillar-junction." The Reservoir has a predatory habit of making a corridor you just walked down look completely foreign when you turn back to find the exit.

### A Final Warning
If you hear the pistons accelerate—if the rhythmic *thrum-hiss* becomes a steady, high-pitched scream—**do not stay to witness the cycle.** That is the machine reaching a threshold of "output." Whether it’s venting excess energy or recalibrating, the sudden surge will turn those copper-filament "weeds" into jagged, electrified wires that lash out wildly. 

When the vibration in the floor changes from a thud to a shiver, look for the nearest vertical conduit and climb. Get off the floor. The floor is for the machine; the air is for the traveler. 

**Remember: You are a guest in a machine that has forgotten it is being visited. Do not give it a reason to remember you are there.**
