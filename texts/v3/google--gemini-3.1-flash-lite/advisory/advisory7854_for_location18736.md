Visiting Orem-Vahl is not a journey of physical exertion, but a journey of **existential discipline.** Because the city exists in a state of absolute stasis, you are not stepping into a living environment; you are stepping into a completed, petrified thought. You are a guest in a museum where the exhibits happen to be breathing.

To prepare for a trip to the basaltic spine, one must discard the traveler’s typical mindset of "progress" and "discovery." Here is your guide to navigating the unchanging.

### 1. Physical Preparation: The "Soft Edge" Principle
The primary danger in Orem-Vahl is not the weather or the predators—which are as static as the stone—but the **mathematical sharpness** of the environment. Because the basalt has not weathered, the edges of the natural world are razor-fine.
*   **Armored Clothing:** Do not wear synthetics or thin, modern fabrics. They will shred the moment you brush against a doorframe or a cliffside. You must wear heavy, cured leathers or thick-woven, dense wools. Think of a beekeeper’s suit, but reinforced with padding at the elbows, knees, and shoulders.
*   **Footwear:** Your boots must have soft, oversized soles. Do not bring rigid hiking boots; the sound of a hard heel-strike against the obsidian floor will be unnerving in the "drip-hush" silence. You need a rubberized or thick, felt-padded sole to muffle your own presence.
*   **The "No-Evaporation" Protocol:** You are told that moisture does not evaporate. This means if your skin becomes wet, it stays wet. You will eventually develop a profound fungal or skin irritation if you do not carry a specialized, high-absorbency dusting powder. Treat your skin as if it were a delicate archive—keep it dry, or you will succumb to the same "clinging" nature as the local moss.

### 2. Psychological Conditioning: The Stillness Sickness
Most multiverse travelers experience "Temporal Withdrawal" within the first hour. In a world with no wind, no shifting light, and no entropy, the human brain—which is evolved to detect motion—begins to hallucinate activity where there is none.
*   **The Focus Exercise:** Practice staring at an object for thirty minutes straight before you depart. If you cannot look at a stone without feeling the internal urge to see it move or change, the silence of Orem-Vahl will drive you to frantic, destructive behavior.
*   **Empathic Suppression:** When you see the inhabitants sitting on the terraces, do not attempt to "rescue" them or pity their state. They are not trapped; they are *in situ*. If you approach them with high-energy empathy, you will feel like a discordant vibration in a symphony of glass. Approach them with the stillness of a statue. Move slowly. If you must walk from building to building, try to match the rhythmic, unhurried pace of the rusted-iron avians.

### 3. Equipment: What to Pack
*   **The Silence Kit:** Bring no recording devices with cooling fans or electronic hums. The environment is so quiet that a buzzing capacitor will sound like a chainsaw. Bring only journals, charcoal, and physical instruments. 
*   **The Offering of Growth:** Because nothing changes, the inhabitants value things that imply a *potential* for cycle. A sealed container of seeds—though they will not grow—is considered a sophisticated gift. It represents the concept of time, which is the only thing the people of Orem-Vahl have forgotten.
*   **Tactile Anchors:** Bring a small object of your own from a world of entropy (a decaying leaf, an ice cube, a burning candle). When you feel your own sense of time slipping away—when you begin to lose the ability to distinguish "past" from "now"—look at your anchor. Watch the candle melt. It will save your sanity.

### 4. Survival Etiquette
*   **Do not move the tools:** You will see tools resting in half-finished etchings. Do not touch them. To move a chisel a fraction of an inch is to introduce an error into a perfect state. This is considered a high transgression of local culture.
*   **The "Drip-Hush" rhythm:** The recurring sound of the condensation is the city’s heartbeat. If you find yourself losing the rhythm of the city, listen for the next drip. Close your eyes. Count the distance between the drips. If the interval remains constant, you are still in safety. If the interval begins to feel like it’s speeding up or slowing down, **leave immediately.** It means your mind is losing its lock on the local stasis, and you are starting to mentally dissolve into the equilibrium.

### A Final Warning
Orem-Vahl is beautiful, but it is a predator of the mind. It offers a promise of eternal peace, a world where you never tire and nothing fades. **Do not be seduced.** The moment you begin to find the "drip-hush" comforting is the moment you should pack your gear. If you stay too long, you will find yourself sitting on a terrace, looking out over the valley, waiting for a sunset that will never finish, and you will eventually become just another bit of carvings on a basalt wall.
