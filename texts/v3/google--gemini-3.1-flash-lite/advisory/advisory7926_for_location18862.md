To stand upon the spine of this world is to stand upon the anvil of a god-machine. You are not visiting a wilderness in any terrestrial sense; you are trespassing on a work-in-progress. The "geological infant" quality of the landscape means that entropy has not yet caught up to the terrain—there are no paths, no gentle slopes, and no soft edges. Everything here is designed to cut, pierce, or crush.

If you intend to visit this factory-planet, your preparation must shift from the survivalist mindset to that of a planetary systems engineer. Here is how you prepare for the *thrum-hiss* of the deep mountain.

### I. The Physiological Armor
Standard hiking gear is a death sentence here. The "metallic humidity" is not just water; it is likely a highly reactive, mineral-laden aerosol that will corrode synthetic nylons and Gore-Tex within hours.

*   **Chassis-Grade Protection:** You require a suit constructed of inert, ceramic-plated polymers. Avoid exposed zippers or velcro, which the arachnoid fauna will perceive as textured debris to be "cleaned" or harvested. Your suit must be sealed at the neck and wrists with industrial-grade gaskets.
*   **Lung Filtration:** The thick, pressurized air is rich in mineral particulates and microscopic industrial runoff. A standard respirator will clog. You need a closed-circuit rebreather, or at the very least, a heavy-duty, multi-stage HEPA/charcoal filtration system capable of handling acidic, metallic vapors.
*   **The Anchor-Bonded Boots:** The rocks are slick with cold condensation and vibrating at a frequency that mimics liquid. You cannot rely on friction alone. Your boots should be equipped with electromagnetic or micro-grapnel soles designed for high-vibration surfaces. You are not walking; you are *coupling* with the mountain.

### II. The Sensory Calibration
The visual and auditory landscape is actively hostile. You are in a "sickly-violet" twilight where your human eyes will strain and fail.

*   **Visual Dampeners:** The bioluminescence of the arachnoid swarms is patterned and rhythmic—a strobe-like effect that can trigger intense motion sickness or seizures in the human brain. Bring eyewear with adaptive, darkening liquid-crystal lenses to filter out the erratic flickers of the bioluminescence and the stray electrical discharges.
*   **Sonic Isolation/Harmonic Neutralization:** The *thrum-hiss* of the subterranean machine is not just noise; it is a physical vibration that will induce nausea and bone-deep fatigue. Carry active noise-canceling inner-ear inserts tuned to the specific frequency of the mountain’s engine. You must decouple your nervous system from the rhythm of the planet, or you will find yourself moving in time with the spiders, an involuntary participant in their machine-logic.

### III. Interaction Protocols with the "Maintenance" Fauna
These translucent, multi-legged arachnoids are not interested in you as food, but they *are* interested in you as a foreign object. If they detect a loose cable, an unsealed pocket, or a malfunctioning piece of hardware on your suit, they will treat you like a clogged pipe or a stray shard of metal. They will "clean" you with surgical, mandibulary precision.

*   **Static Discharge Grounding:** These creatures are sensitive to electrical potential. If you carry any electronics (cameras, scanners), ensure they are perfectly shielded. To the arachnoids, a stray spark is a dinner bell.
*   **The Non-Interference Posture:** Never stop moving, but never move erratically. If you need to observe, stand perfectly still against a rock face to minimize your silhouette. If you flail, you trigger their "clearing" response. Do not swat at them. Treat them as you would a high-pressure steam pipe: avoid contact, but do not challenge the flow.

### IV. The Mental Load
The psychological weight of this place is its most dangerous feature. You are witnessing a world being "forged." There is an overwhelming sense of indifference—a realization that the internal engine does not know that you are standing on its roof. 

*   **Anchor Identity:** Because the environment is so synthetic yet so raw, you may experience "dissociative resonance," where your brain begins to interpret your own pulse as part of the machine’s heartbeat. Maintain an external, objective mnemonic—a physical object from your home world, or a pre-recorded loop of organic sounds (a forest, a river, wind through trees). You need the chaotic unpredictability of "old" nature to remind your brain that you are a biological creature, not a component.
*   **The "No-Map" Strategy:** Do not attempt to map the mountain. It is a machine that is constantly reconfiguring its interior structural integrity. Trust only your compass and your immediate line of sight. If you look for a path that wasn't there yesterday, you are likely looking at a structural shift that will collapse under you.

### Summary for the Traveler:
When you step out of the aperture into that grey, weeping curtain, **be quiet, be rigid, and be shielded.** Do not attempt to study the machines beneath the basalt; you are a static fluctuation in a system that prides itself on perfect maintenance. If you are cold, do not seek heat; the heat belongs to the engine, and the engine does not share. 

You are an observer of an infinite foundry. Stay vertical, keep your gear sealed, and never—under any circumstances—let the arachnoids believe you are a loose rivet.
