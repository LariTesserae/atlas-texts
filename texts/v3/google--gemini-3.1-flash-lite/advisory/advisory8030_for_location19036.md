To step into the **Latticework of the Low-Reach** is to step inside a slow, cooling lung. It is not a place for the hurried, the loud, or the clean. Because the Latticework survives on a delicate equilibrium of decay and reinforcement, your presence is a foreign variable. 

Here is how you must prepare to ensure you do not disrupt the engine—or become part of its compost.

### 1. The Wardrobe: Friction and Sealing
The air in the Latticework is a saturated, cool mist. Any fabric that absorbs moisture will become a leaden weight within an hour.
*   **The Outer Shell:** Wear non-porous, synthetic materials—thin, matte-finished technical fabrics that repel water. Avoid cotton or wool at all costs. You want a "slicker" profile, something that will shed the constant condensation rather than soaking it up.
*   **Footwear:** Your boots must have high-traction, rubberized soles designed for slick surfaces. The root-bridges are polished by dampness and friction; a standard hiking boot will be like wearing skates on ice. Ensure your laces are triple-knotted and tucked, so they do not snag on the protruding copper rivets or the budding moss.
*   **Tactile Protection:** Bring a pair of thin, grippy work gloves. You will be touching brass that is perpetually wet and bark that might be soft with rot. Gloves prevent you from picking up the "living" grime of the Latticework, which may be acidic or biologically active.

### 2. The Physical Adjustment: Moving with the Sighs
You are entering a territory that is perpetually contracting and expanding.
*   **The Cadence of Movement:** Never walk with a heavy, rhythmic gait. The architecture reacts to vibration. Observe the frequency of the "Sighs"—the brass groaning—and move during the quiet intervals. If a structural groan starts, stop. Stay still. The root-bridges are essentially living suspension systems; if you disrupt their tension, the automated systems may perceive your weight as an imbalance and try to "remedy" it by shifting the bridges under your feet.
*   **Maintain the Elevation:** Do not even *consider* stepping off the bridges. The "compost" below is a nutrient-rich, semi-liquid slurry designed to break down organic matter. If you drop something, leave it. Do not attempt to retrieve a lost item, lest you sink past the canopy of your own stability.

### 3. Biological and Sensory Supplies
The Latticework is nutrient-dense but provides nothing for a human metabolism.
*   **Hydration:** The water dripping from the pipes is likely recycled through copper and botanical matter; it is laden with sap, oxidation, and mineral buildup. Do not drink it. Bring your own closed-loop hydration system.
*   **Aural Protection:** The profound quiet can be disorienting, and the sudden *plink-hiss* of the pipes can trigger a primitive fight-or-flight response. Noise-canceling earplugs are actually a liability here—you need your ears to hear the subtle creaks that warn you when a bridge is shifting. Keep your hearing sharp.
*   **Lighting:** The filtered, grey light is excellent for photography but poor for structural analysis. Bring a dim, amber-colored headlamp. Bright white LEDs will clash visually with the pale greens and bronzes, and may disturb the gelatinous insects that appear to be part of the Latticework’s maintenance crew.

### 4. Psychological Conditioning: The Observer’s Ethos
The Latticework is indifferent to you. You are a tourist in a clockwork stomach.
*   **Respect the Industrial Silence:** This is a space that has "forgotten it is a machine." If you behave like a consumer—scouting for souvenirs, trying to force open housing units, or shouting to hear your echo—you break the *gestalt*. 
*   **The "Slow-Motion" Mindset:** Adopt the speed of the environment. If you move quickly, you will be exhausted by the humidity and the tension of balancing on the roots. Move like a slow-winding gear. Breathe in time with the *plink-hiss*. 
*   **Emergency Protocol:** If you hear an alarm that isn't a mechanical groan—if the "industrial hum" shifts pitch or tempo—it means the system has identified a stray weight and is attempting to "re-purpose" the section of the Latticework you are occupying. Do not run. You cannot outrun a plant that grows faster than it decays. Instead, move toward the primary support trunks; they are the most stable "chassis" of the village.

### A Final Warning
Do not be tempted to "fix" anything. That stray wire hanging from a copper valve? That moss clogging a brass vent? **Leave it.** The Latticework is in a state of perfectly refined decay. Every bit of rust is a structural brace; every bit of rot is a nutrient cycle. If you exert your will upon this place, the engine will eventually turn its slow, uncaring automation to you, deciding that you, too, are an object in need of repurposing. 

Walk softly. Stay on the grey wood. Listen to the hum. You are not visiting a destination; you are visiting a living, ticking pause in the entropy of the multiverse.
