Visiting Ool is an exercise in profound sensory recalibration. To step into the ribcage of the leviathan is to leave behind the chaotic velocity of the multi-verse and enter a state of biological and historical suspension. Because Ool is a closed loop of equilibrium, you cannot approach it as a mere tourist; you must approach it as an intruder into a work of art.

Here is how you must prepare to ensure your survival and your welcome.

### 1. The Sensory Muting (Gear Selection)
The first thing to understand is that Ool is a place of *absolute noise sensitivity*. The city is an acoustic sponge, but it is also an acoustic mirror. The *plink* of dripping water is the rhythm of their life; your heavy boots, the rustle of synthetic fabrics, or the mechanical ticking of a standard wristwatch will sound like gunfire in that stillness.

*   **Soft-Goods Only:** Leave your nylon, polyester, and Kevlar gear behind. The friction of modern synthetics is sharp and jagged in Ool's stillness. You will need thick, felted wool or untreated brushed leather. Your clothing should be dark—not to hide, but to absorb the blinding light. If you arrive in reflective or bright modern colors, you will feel like a neon stain against the mossy ivory of the city.
*   **Silence the Movement:** Lubricate your joints or apply soft leather coverings to any metal hardware on your pack. If you carry a multi-tool or a camera, these must be wrapped in moleskin or felt. You do not just move through Ool; you must glide.
*   **The Problem of Vision:** The "unforgiving, sterile brightness" is a threat to a traveler accustomed to natural shadow. Without contrast, your depth perception will fail within hours. Bring high-quality, non-polarized amber or grey-tinted eyewear. The light refracts through the porous bone; it is physically exhausting for the pupils to remain contracted in a space with no shadows.

### 2. Physical and Biological Preparation
The air in Ool is stagnant and thick with spores from the neon-green moss and bioluminescent fungi. 

*   **Respiratory Filters:** While the scent is described as "sweet decay," the stagnant air is a petri dish of prehistoric, contained fungal spores. Carry a discreet, low-profile respirator mask. You don’t need to wear it constantly, but the moment you feel a tickle in your throat or detect that cloying, heavy sweetness, put it on. 
*   **Thermal Regulation:** Ool manages to stay temperate through divine stillness, but you are coming from the sub-zero tundra outside. The bridge between the "biting cold" and the "stagnant damp" is the most dangerous thermal shock point. Allow your body temperature to stabilize in the transition tunnels before entering the main nave.

### 3. The Etiquette of Equilibrium
In Ool, sudden motion is an existential threat. They equate stillness with stability. If you move quickly, you are perceived as a herald of disruption—or worse, of death.

*   **The "Slow-Motion" Protocol:** Practice your movement before you cross the threshold. You must be able to navigate a room without a sharp intake of breath, a sudden jolt, or an abrupt arm movement. If you drop something, do not scramble for it. Reach slowly.
*   **The Gift of Stillness:** Do not bring "modern" items intended to impress. Electricity—even a small LED flashlight—will strike the inhabitants as an act of violence against their holy uniformity. If you must have light, use a wax candle with a glass wind-shield to contain the flame. Fire is a part of nature; electronics are an invasion of it.

### 4. Psychological Conditioning: Accepting the Stasis
The hardest part of visiting Ool is the "vanishing horizon." If you suffer from claustrophobia, **do not attempt this trip.** 

*   **The Verticality of the Mind:** You will be living on a series of pulleys and ropes. Your brain will instinctively reach for a horizon that does not exist. Prepare yourself to look *up* and *down* rather than outward. Learn to appreciate the "micro." The history of Ool is carved into every square inch of bone; treat every wall as a library.
*   **The Loss of Time:** There is no ticking clock in Ool. If you bring a smartphone, leave it in flight mode and buried deep in your pack, ideally in a lead-lined bag so the light of the screen never accidentally leaks out. Rely on your own circadian rhythm, but be warned: the city *will* eventually override it. Do not attempt to force a schedule. Eat when you are hungry; settle into the *plinking* of the stalactites.

### 5. What to Bring as an Offering
The citizens of Ool revere the integrity of their skeleton. They are curators of a dead god. Do not bring money; it is worthless in a city that has ceased to grow. 

Bring a tactile, natural offering. A small pouch of polished river stones from your home world, or a bundle of dried, non-invasive aromatic herbs. Something that reflects your world without intruding upon theirs. Place these in the moss-beds, not as a transaction, but as a silent acknowledgment that you are entering their stillness as a guest, not a seeker of secrets.

**Final Warning:** If you begin to feel the urge to run, to shout, or to break the pattern—leave immediately. The city of Ool is a fragile ego-state. If the inhabitants perceive you as a catalyst for change, they will not fear you; they will simply, and very quietly, maneuver to ensure you never leave the ribcage again, becoming just another settled artifact in their eternal, white gallery.
