Visiting this realm—the "City of Frozen Flow"—is not a matter of physical endurance, but of psychological stabilization. Because this location exists in a state of absolute, static equilibrium, it functions as a sensory void. The primary danger to a multiverse traveler here is not the terrain or the climate, but the **Temporal Anchoring Effect.** 

When you enter a space where light does not shift, shadows do not move, and water does not ripple, your own biological clock will begin to protest. Without the passage of time to validate your existence, the traveler risks internal disintegration—a feeling of "unspooling" because your movements are the only things changing in a world that refuses to acknowledge change.

Here is how you must prepare.

### 1. Equipment and Gear
*   **The Metronome:** This is non-negotiable. Bring a mechanical, audible clock or a handheld metronome. You need a device that provides a steady, rhythmic ticking that you can feel in your marrow. Because the city is profoundly silent and frictionless, your brain will begin to invent phantom sounds—ringing, humming, or voices—to fill the void. A persistent, external beat will serve as the anchor for your sanity.
*   **Weighted Footwear:** The polished obsidian surfaces are perpetually slick with a thin film of, for lack of a better term, "static moisture." Standard rubber soles will slide easily. You will need boots with volcanic-grit soles or specialized crampons designed for smooth stone.
*   **Non-Absorbent Layering:** The humidity here is "saturated but not raining." It is a clinging, heavy weight. If your clothing is made of cotton or natural fibers, it will draw the moisture from the air, become heavy, and never dry. Wear hydrophobic, synthetic materials. You want the moisture to bead on your exterior, not soak through to your core. 
*   **The "Shadow-Breaker":** Bring a flickering light source—a small, warm-spectrum lantern. The hard-edged, permanent shadows of the city can be disorienting. If you look at a shadow and it stays pinned to the ground for hours, you will lose your sense of perspective. A flickering light mimics the chaotic movement of a flame, which will help your eyes process the stillness of the architecture.

### 2. Physical and Mental Preparation 
*   **Hyper-Vigilance of the "Absent Mind":** Do not touch the belongings left behind. There is a psychological temptation to interact with the bowls and mats as if this were an archaeological site. Do not. To touch and move an object in a space that has been perfectly still might make you feel like an intruder in a vacuum. You are not meant to disturb the "agreement" between the stone and the hand.
*   **The Silence Protocol:** You must cultivate an internal monologue. Because there is no ambient sound, your own breathing and heartbeat will sound abnormally loud. Do not attempt to mask it with music or talk shows—this will only alienate you from the environment. Instead, practice *audible walking*. Drag your boots slightly (if the surface allows) or tap a stone staff. You must create the sound in this world, or you will begin to feel as if you are a ghost invading a photograph.

### 3. Entering and Navigating
*   **The Gravity-Logic:** Understand that the hydraulics are the "circulatory system" of the city. Do not attempt to cross the stone arteries or climb the flumes. The water is frictionless; if you step into a basin or a channel, you will not feel a splash, but you may slip with impossible ease, as if the water were made of liquid glass. 
*   **Avoid Fixed Staring:** If you stare at the translucent succulents for too long, your brain will begin to seek patterns where none exist. You will eventually perceive the leaves as faces or shifting geometry. To avoid hallucinations, use a technique of "soft focus"—scan the horizon and keep your gaze sweeping across the terraced platforms rather than fixing on individual points of architecture.

### 4. Psychological Survival
The biggest challenge you will face is **The Impulse to Reset.** When you see a bow at a cistern, you will feel an overwhelming desire to pick it up, to move it, to "activate" the place. Resist this. The city is held in an amber-like state of perfection; it is a monument to a moment of total grace. Any action you take that signifies "change" (drinking the water, moving a bowl, climbing a new wall) will make you stand out with painful, jarring intensity against the backdrop.

**The Golden Rule:** You are not a guest in this city; you are a static anomaly. Walk, observe, breathe the mineral-scented air, but leave the geography exactly as you found it. 

If you find yourself becoming too calm—if you feel your pulse rate matching the total silence of the city—it is time to leave. That specific sensation, the "peace" that makes you want to lie down on the cool obsidian and let the mist settle over you like a shroud, is the final stage of the city claiming you as part of its permanent collection. 

**Pack your gear, keep your clock ticking, and do not let your shadow become part of the masonry.**
