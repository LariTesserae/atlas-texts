# Preparing for a Visit to the Drowned Industrial Valley

## What You Are Walking Into

This is a soft, damp, slowly moving place. The town is intact enough to walk through, but the ground is unreliable and the vegetation is in charge. Most of the danger comes from ordinary things: slick moss, sinking ground, falling masonry, and water of unknown depth. The strange behavior of the plants is interesting but not hostile. Treat it as a careful, quiet visit, not an expedition into a threat.

## Footwear and Ground

The valley floor sits barely above the water table, so expect every step to give slightly.

- **Wear tall waterproof boots with deep, aggressive tread.** Choose ankle-high at minimum; knee-high is better for the low streets and pool margins.
- **Bring a second pair of dry socks** in a sealed bag. Wet feet are the most common complaint here and they become a real problem after a few hours.
- **Do not go barefoot or in open sandals.** The paving has heaved into uneven stone plates, and moss hides the gaps between them.
- **Test every surface before committing weight.** Push a walking pole or stick into the ground ahead of you. If it sinks more than a few centimeters or the surface wobbles, go around.

Walking poles are strongly recommended. They help with balance on the moss-covered plates, they probe the pools and soft ground, and they let you lean on something without leaning on a wall.

## Water: Pools, Seeps, and Drinking

Every low place holds still water, and every still pool has a skin of pollen and leaf litter that hides its depth.

- **Never step into a pool to test it.** Assume any pool may be deeper than it looks and may have soft silt at the bottom.
- **Do not drink any water in the valley untreated.** This includes the seeps, the culverts, and the pale creeper's hollow tubes, which carry water through living channels. Bring your own water and a reliable filter or purification method, and carry more than you think you need, since the mist makes you forget how much you are sweating.
- **Keep your hands out of the water** unless you have to. Cover any cuts before you enter the valley.
- **Avoid crossing the braided channels at the water's edge** where the ground feels firm. The margins of the pools shift as the ground settles, so a path that held firm yesterday may not hold today.

## Mist, Cold, and Visibility

Cool air settles in the mornings, and a fine mist hangs at roofline height until midday. You will see little above your head in the early hours.

- **Dress in layers.** Mornings can be cold and damp, and you will warm up quickly once you start walking. A waterproof outer shell and a wool or synthetic mid-layer work better than cotton, which stays wet and cold.
- **Carry a warm hat and gloves** even in summer. The damp air draws heat from you faster than you expect.
- **Use a light with a warm, diffuse beam** so you are not dazzled by the mist. A headlamp with a red or amber setting helps you keep your night vision.
- **Do not rely on sight alone in the early morning.** Sound will be muted, so listen for the dripping and creaking to tell you where the vines and pools are.
- **Plan your major movements for midday**, when the mist thins and the light is at its best. The overcast skies still keep the lower town dim, so bring a light even then.

## Light and Shade

The lower streets stay in a permanent green shade under the vines, while the upper walls and the water tower catch brighter light. The sun appears only briefly.

- **Wear polarized or tinted glasses** if you are sensitive to glare from the wet surfaces, though you will often need them off in the shade.
- **Expect your eyes to adjust slowly** when you move from the bright upper walls to the green streets. Stop for a moment at the transition so you do not trip.
- **After dark**, the fungal mats on the cannery walls glow with a faint cold light. It outlines the brickwork but is not bright enough to read by. Bring your own light and do not rely on the glow for navigation. Its patches also shift with the wet months.

## Walls, Roofs, and Falling Debris

Nothing here looks dramatically ruined, but many walls have settled, cracked, and leaned, and some roofs have failed.

- **Stay well away from the walls.** Keep at least a few meters between yourself and any brick structure, particularly walls that lean outward or show gaps between courses.
- **Do not lean on the vine-covered walls.** The vines look solid, but the woody stems may be the only thing holding the masonry together in that spot.
- **Watch the roofs.** The fern root-mats catch rain and hold it. They can be heavy, and failed sections may come down without warning. Do not stand directly beneath a roof edge or a stretch of wall that has begun to bow.
- **If you hear a low crumbling sound**, move immediately away from the source and wait for the silence that follows. Do not go toward the collapse to investigate.

## The Plants

The plants are the main occupants of this town and they have rearranged it to suit themselves. Treat them with courtesy and caution.

- **Do not cut, pull, or burn the vines.** Several have grown into the steel of the water tower, and their trunks now brace its legs. Removing them could bring the tower down.
- **Do not damage the pale creeper** that forms hollow tubes around the old iron conduits. It carries water, and damaging it may release standing water into places you are walking.
- **Keep clear of the plant masses that open and close** with the morning damp. Their movements are slow, but they follow a rhythm, and you should not stand in the path of one as it moves. Watch the ground around them for displaced moss or fresh gaps.
- **Do not use the regularly spaced stems as landmarks or trail markers.** They look like fence posts laid out by a surveyor, and it is tempting to navigate by them, but the plants are slowly changing and the spacing will not hold.
- **Do not navigate by leaf direction.** The leaves tilt toward the cannery stack as the day warms, so they are useful for telling the time of day but not for finding your way.
- **Avoid contact with unfamiliar fungal growth.** The mats on the cannery walls are luminescent, and you should assume any fungus here is not safe to touch or eat.

## Pollen and Allergens

The valley is thick with pollen, which settles in the pools and drifts through the mist.

- **Bring antihistamines and any inhalers you normally use**, and take them before you go in.
- **Wear a dust mask or scarf over your nose and mouth** in the thickest areas, especially near the pools and the rail corridor of tall reeds.
- **Wash your face and hands thoroughly** when you leave, and change clothes if you can, since pollen clings to damp fabric.

## Fauna

Animal life is sparse, and most of it is harmless.

- **Slugs and snails** leave long silver trails over the moss. They are harmless, but the trails make the moss even more slippery, so do not step on them carelessly.
- **Small dark beetles** work the bark of the vines. Do not handle them and do not put your hands on bark without looking first.
- **The heron** stands motionless at the largest pool each morning. Give it a wide berth. Do not approach or startle it, and do not walk between it and the water.
- **Small birds** move through the fern canopy on the roofs. They are shy and you are unlikely to see much of them. Do not climb onto roofs to look for them.

## Sound and Communication

The mist absorbs most distant sound. You will hear the dripping of water from leaves, the creak of vine stems, and the occasional metallic tick of the water tower as it cools or warms. Use these sounds to stay oriented, and agree on hand signals with your companions, since voices carry poorly in the mist.

## Navigation and Returning

The town changes slowly, and a return visit may look different from your first. Walls will have leaned further, pools will have moved, and vines will have stretched toward new openings.

- **Set a fixed exit point** before you go in. High ground is best. The base of the water tower or the cannery is a good choice because they are large, easy to see at midday, and their structure changes least quickly.
- **Mark your route with something that does not rely on the plants.** Bright tape or a compass bearing works better than anything growing here.
- **Keep track of the time.** The mist lifts around midday and returns in the early hours, so plan to leave the valley floor before dusk and avoid being caught in the low streets as the light fails.
- **Assume the ground and the walls will be different when you return.** Do not count on a path that worked before.

## Before You Leave for the Valley

- Confirm your return route and exit point with someone who is not traveling with you.
- Pack a sealed bag for dry clothes and a sealed bag for food.
- Bring a first-aid kit that includes antiseptic, bandages for wet skin, and something for blisters.
- Pack more water and purification than you think you need.
- Check your boots for cracks and your rain gear for leaks.

## A Final Note

This is not a place to rush. The valley is in a slow, continuous exchange between its industrial past and its living present, and both are still doing their work. Move carefully, keep your hands and feet to yourself, and let the town show you its shape at its own pace.
