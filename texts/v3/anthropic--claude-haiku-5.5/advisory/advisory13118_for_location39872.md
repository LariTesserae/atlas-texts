# Preparing for the Hollow Cistern of White Lattice

This is a place where you cannot relax your attention without losing track of yourself. Prepare for the physical difficulty, the constant sound, and above all the stillness, which will work on your sense of time in ways you won't notice until you're already affected.

## Before You Go: Mental Preparation

The cistern does not threaten you. It does not hunger or hurry. Its danger is that it will let you stay. Visitors who lose track of time are rarely hurt by anything in the room. They simply stop counting, and then they stop wanting to leave.

Decide before you arrive what your leaving will look like. Choose a specific sign: a number of steps taken, a number of breaths, a fixed number of marks made on a surface. Do not rely on the feeling that it is time to go, because that feeling will not come on its own here. Write your exit trigger down and read it aloud at the threshold.

Expect to feel watched. The forms here have the quality of things that have been waiting a long time to be noticed. This is a feeling of the room, not evidence of anything hostile. You may acknowledge it briefly, but do not linger in it.

## Clothing and Gear

**Footwear.** This is the most important decision you will make. The terraces have ridged lips, the salt plates crack underfoot, and nothing is level in any way that your body expects. Wear boots with firm, grippy soles and a stiff heel counter so your ankles stay supported on the lips. Avoid smooth soles and anything with a worn tread. Bring a spare pair of insoles if you have them, since you will be standing still for long periods on hard mineral.

**Clothing.** The air is cool and dry, so dress in layers you can remove. Choose fabric that doesn't snag easily, because the ridges and filament-veins are sharp at the edges and will catch loose cloth. Cover your forearms and shins, since the salt can abrade bare skin when you brace against a column. Dark clothing will be dusted pale within the hour from the mineral bloom, so dress for that and expect to look like a statue by the end.

**Hands.** Wear thin gloves that let you feel the surface. You will need to grip ledges, test footing, and steady yourself on hollow spines. Thick gloves reduce your sense of how much weight a crumbling plate can take.

**Face.** The dust is fine and dry and gets into the eyes and throat. Bring a cloth to cover your nose and mouth, and eye drops. A pair of simple goggles helps in the rare spots where flaking salt rises off the terraces.

## Carrying Things

There is almost no room to swing a bag or to set anything down. Pack everything compact and close to your body, strapped so nothing hangs. Anything that drops into a seam between columns will likely be unrecoverable, so do not carry loose items you cannot afford to lose.

Bring a rope, a short length of cord, or a bright strip of cloth, something you can tie around a waist or wrist, to keep yourself and your companions connected in a space where a step can be a misjudgment.

Bring water, more than you think you need. The air takes moisture from you without any sweat or obvious sign. Dry lips and a scratchy throat are the early warnings. Drink before you enter and carry at least a full day's supply, then bring less back out than you brought in.

Bring a timepiece that does not depend on the room. A mechanical watch you wind yourself is good. A simple hourglass is better, since it measures a flow you can see. Do not trust your sense of elapsed time once you enter.

Bring something to make marks with, but do not use chalk or paint on the lattice. Foreign pigments may bond to the salt and change its surfaces permanently. Tie a knot in a cord at intervals instead, or use the cloth strip as a tally.

## The Sound

The chorus of the spines is loud and never resolves, and it will get inside your attention within minutes. Earplugs will soften it but will not remove it, and they will cut you off from the ticking of the salt, which is useful for judging when the ground is shifting. Some visitors find it helpful to hum a single steady note of their own, which gives the mind a fixed point inside the chorus. Others find that the noise becomes a kind of floor to stand on. Try both before you go in if you can.

The ticking and creaking of the salt is scattered and constant. It may sound like distant gunfire. Do not let it startle you into moving quickly, because sudden movement on the terraces is how people fall.

Your voice will be swallowed by the hum. Plan hand signals before you enter, and agree on them with your companions. You may also use taps on the salt, since each tap rings and the ring carries. Keep these signals simple: stop, come here, I am falling behind, I want to leave.

## Light and Depth

The light is even and shadowless except where lattices cross, so judging distance and step height is hard. Everything looks like it is at the same level. Move slowly, test each foot before you commit, and do not trust the apparent edge of a terrace until you have pressed on it. Close one eye for a moment if you are unsure of depth. It helps more than you would expect.

You may look up and see a ceiling so distant it resembles sky. Do not spend time staring up. Staring upward makes the stillness feel larger and makes it harder to return your attention to your feet.

## The Lichens, the Moss, and the Dark Clump

The living things here are few and careful, and they have been living in these exact crevices for as long as anyone has measured. Do not touch the parchment rosettes, the grey-green moss in the seams, or the dark needle-growth at the base of the largest spine. The moss crumbles at a touch. Scraping the lichens may damage something that cannot be replaced. The clump at the base of the great spine does not appear to spread or die, which means it has no slack to spare. Look at them, photograph them if you carry an instrument that works here, and leave them as they are.

## The Warm Shaft

The single shaft of warmer light falls through a distant opening onto one patch of floor and has never moved. It is the only warmth in the room, and it may be tempting to stand in it, to rest there, or to wait in it while you gather yourself. Be aware that it may be the place where the stillness is hardest to notice and easiest to accept. If you choose to step into it, keep your exit trigger in mind, and do not stay longer than you intended. Do not try to move the light.

## Tapping the Walls

The walls ring when struck, with a dry, glassy note that changes with thickness. It is tempting to test the room this way, and it is reasonable to tap once to learn how solid a surface is. But every strike adds a note to the chorus. Do not tap repeatedly, and do not tap the hollow spines, which are the instruments of the room's song.

## Leaving

Once you are among the columns, the entrance may become impossible to find. The forest of spines looks the same from every angle. Before you move deep into the room, mark your line of return: a knot at the first terrace, a strip of cloth tied to a lip you can recognize, a note of your intended direction. Check these markers often. Your sense of direction will drift in the stillness, and the markers will not.

Leave when your exit trigger is met, not when you feel ready. Readiness will not come, because nothing here changes to prompt it. When you leave, you may feel the room release you slowly, as though the chorus is still pressing at the edge of your hearing. This is normal. Give yourself time before you judge how long you were gone.

## In Brief

- Wear firm-soled boots, snag-resistant layers, and thin gloves.
- Carry more water than you think you need, plus eye drops and a cloth for dust.
- Pack compact and tether yourself and your companions.
- Bring an external clock, and set an exit trigger before you enter.
- Plan hand signals and taps, since voices will not carry.
- Move slowly, testing each footing, because shadowless light hides edges.
- Do not touch the living growth, do not paint or chalk the lattice, and do not tap the spines more than once.
- Mark your way back early, and check the markers often.

You are not lost here. You are only held. Prepare to be able to let go of the room when the time comes, and to recognize that time has come even though nothing in the room will tell you so.
