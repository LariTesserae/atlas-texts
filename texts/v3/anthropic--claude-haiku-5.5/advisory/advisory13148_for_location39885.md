# Preparing to Visit the Cistern Plateau

## What to Expect

The Cistern Plateau is not dangerous in the way most unfamiliar places are. Nothing here hunts you, and nothing is actively unstable. The hazards are mostly physical and mundane: slippery surfaces, sharp edges, deep water, heavy machinery, and a humid heat that wears you down without warning. The bigger challenge is psychological. The place is quiet, uniform, and unhurried, and it can make you lose track of time, distance, and your own sense of urgency. Prepare for the body and the mind.

## Clothing and Footwear

- **Wear light, quick-drying layers.** The air is near liquid, and cotton will stay wet and heavy for the whole visit. Synthetic or wool blends are better, even in warm weather.
- **Bring sturdy closed shoes with deep, grippy soles.** The grey stone is smooth where water has run across it, and the membrane lining the pans is slick when wet. Avoid sandals entirely.
- **Carry gloves, preferably cut-resistant.** The rim arrises are sharp enough to slice skin, and the condenser fins and pump housings have rivet heads and raw edges. Thin work gloves are a good compromise, since you will need to grip railings and wire lattices.
- **Protect your head from condensation.** Drips from the mesh canopy fall steadily, and a brimmed hat or hood keeps them off your face and out of your eyes.

## Health and Supplies

- **Hydrate deliberately.** Humidity suppresses sweat evaporation, so you can overheat without noticing. Drink more than feels necessary, and pace yourself on the terraces.
- **Carry your own water.** Do not drink the condensate. It drips from coils that have run for a very long time, and you do not know what the channels and pans have added to it. Your own sealed supply is the only safe choice.
- **Pack a dry bag** for electronics, documents, and spare clothing. Everything that is not sealed will be damp within the hour.
- **Bring electrolyte tablets or a salt source.** Heat and humidity deplete you faster than the sensation suggests.
- **Keep a basic first-aid kit,** including antiseptic, adhesive dressings, and a tourniquet. Cuts from the rim and machinery will be the most likely injuries.
- **Do not eat the plants.** They are cultivated, unidentified, and fed by unknown water. No animals live here to show you which ones are safe, which is itself a warning sign.

## Navigation and Time

- **Bring a dependable timepiece and set alarms.** There is no day-night cycle visible through the mesh, the light is flat and constant, and the machines never change their rhythm. Hours can pass without any cue that they have. Check your watch at regular intervals and write the times down.
- **Carry a compass and a map if you can get one,** though the terraces are arranged in an orderly descending pattern that is easy to follow. The danger is not getting lost in the sense of wandering. It is losing your sense of how far you have come.
- **Mark your path with something that will not disappear.** Cut vines regrow in the same places, and the drip channels deepen too slowly to notice. Photos and written notes are more reliable than markers.
- **Note where the rim stairs and access points are** before you descend. The stepped courses look uniform from above, and the way back up can be harder to spot than the way down.

## The Machines

- **Keep a clear distance from the pump housings and intake pipes.** The intakes are sunk into the lower basins and the suction there is strong enough to pull you under if you stand too close to an opening.
- **Do not touch the valves, gauges, or dials.** The machines have been running for a long time with a settled rhythm. You have no way of knowing what adjusting one would change, and the cistern shows no sign that it needs your help.
- **Stay out of the reach of the condenser fins and gears.** Give the moving parts a wide margin, and never reach into a housing, even when the machinery seems quiet.
- **Listen for the sighing valves.** They are the most useful audible warnings, since they signal when water is about to move between pans.

## The Water and the Plants

- **Assume every pan is deeper than it looks.** The water is very clear, so depth is hard to judge, and the root mats extend below the surface in ways that trap feet and hands.
- **Never walk on the root mats,** even though they look solid. The fibrous skin may hold your weight for a step, then give way into submerged roots and water.
- **Give the vines and ferns room.** Do not pull, cut, or train them. You will not be able to change the cistern's arrangement in any way that lasts, and the plants can tangle around a limb if disturbed.
- **Avoid standing in the drip channels.** The water there is bright and fast, and you could slip on the narrow cuts.

## The Quiet

The silence is not a void. It is filled with pump breath, dripping condensate, gurgling water, and the creak of leaves pushing against one another. Prepare to hear these sounds for a long time, and to notice when they change. Many visitors find the lack of animal life unsettling, especially the absence of insects. Expect a faint, persistent unease and accept that it is a normal reaction to a place without the ordinary signs of life.

## The Mental Approach

Visitors often want to understand the place, fix it, or find out who built it. You may be tempted to adjust a valve, clear a vine, or test the water. Resist these impulses. The cistern is an argument between people long gone and the water they tried to master, and it does not need a new participant. Observe, photograph, and record. Take nothing from the terraces. Leave the machines running as you found them.

Keep in mind that the place is slow and you are not. Plan a visit you can leave whenever you choose, and do not let the calm persuade you to stay past your own limits.

## Before You Go

- Sturdy grip-soled shoes, cut-resistant gloves, and light synthetic layers
- At least two litres of sealed water per person, plus electrolytes
- Dry bag, first-aid kit, and a reliable timepiece with alarms
- Compass, notebook, and a camera you can keep dry
- A plan for leaving: the rim access, your return time, and someone who knows where you went

The Cistern Plateau will not stop you from leaving. It will simply go on, rising by a hair each season, and you will be the only thing in it that moved quickly.
