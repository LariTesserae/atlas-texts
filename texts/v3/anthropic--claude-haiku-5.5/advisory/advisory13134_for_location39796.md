# Preparing to Visit the Hollow of Quiet Roofs

The Hollow is not dangerous in the way most places are. It will not attack you or hurt you through hostility. Its risks are quieter. It is a place that has reached a final shape and keeps it, and a visitor is the one thing in it that does not already belong. Most of your preparation is about becoming as little disruption as possible while still functioning in a wet, cold, bright, silent world.

## Understanding What You Are Walking Into

Before anything else, accept that the hollow is full. Every surface holds moss, fern, lichen, snail, frog, or thatch. Every channel has its fish or eel. Every doorway has been fitted and refitted until the wall seems grown. You will feel, almost immediately, that there is no empty place for you to occupy. This feeling is accurate. Your task is not to find a place but to pass through without leaving a gap or a mess behind.

Do not expect the hollow to notice you as a threat or a guest. It is more like a sealed room with its own rhythm, and you are an unexpected sound in it.

## Physical Preparation

**Conditioning.** The ground is soft and sponge-like, and the causeways are narrow, slick, and raised above water. Practice balance on wet, uneven surfaces. Strengthen your ankles and calves. Expect to spend long hours walking slowly and carefully, because hurrying on wet moss is the fastest way to fall into a channel.

**Cold tolerance.** The air stays cold and the water is colder. Your body will lose heat more through damp clothing than through dry air. Arrive warm and rested, and plan to keep your core temperature steady rather than trying to warm up later.

**Eyesight and light.** The sky is bright and the mist and water reflect light from every direction, so the hollow is glaring despite its humidity. Bring eye protection, ideally tinted lenses. Be aware that the near-total absence of shadow flattens depth. Stones, steps, and water edges can look level when they are not. Move slowly when stepping onto anything new.

**Hearing.** The silence is nearly complete. Your own footsteps will sound loud and wrong, and your breathing and heartbeat may become audible. This is disorienting for many travelers. Practice moving quietly in soft-soled footwear before you go, and rehearse speaking at a low volume.

## Clothing and Gear

Everything will get wet, and the mist never truly stops, so plan around saturation rather than trying to avoid it.

- **Outer layer:** A hooded, water-resistant cloak or coat in a muted color. Dull greens, browns, and grays blend with the hollow. Avoid bright colors, which look like something dropped from outside.
- **Base layers:** Wool or other fabric that retains warmth when damp. Avoid cotton, which becomes heavy and chilling.
- **Footwear:** Soft-soled, close-fitting boots with good grip on wet stone and moss. Bring a spare pair of dry socks in a sealed bag. Felt-soled or wrapped shoes are useful for keeping footfalls quiet, since the hollow's silence is easily broken.
- **Gloves:** Thin, grippy, and water-resistant. You will need your hands for balance on railings, steps, and footbridges. Bare hands will get cold quickly and slip on stone.
- **Bags:** Everything needs to be sealed. Use waterproof bags or tightly sealed containers for food, papers, bedding, and anything you value. Mist seeps into every seam over time.
- **Light:** Keep a small lantern or torch, but you may not need it. The hollow is bright. Use it only in the gaps between houses at dusk and do not leave it burning.

## Food and Water

The water in the channels looks clean and cold, but it is part of the hollow's living system. Treat it, boil it, or carry your own. Do not drink from channels where moss hangs heavily into the flow, and do not eat any fish, eel, snail, frog, or plant you find in the hollow.

Bring dried, compact, cold food: hard bread, nuts, salted meat, dried fruit. Eat it on a dry spot such as a stone bench or a raised causeway, and clean up every crumb before moving on.

Cooking is delicate. The settlement's cooking fires send pale smoke that hangs under the mist, and their presence is part of the local order. Do not light your own fire. If you must heat food, ask first. If you cannot ask, eat cold.

## Conduct in the Hollow

This is the most important part of your preparation. The hollow is kept by its inhabitants, and everything you do either fits the pattern or breaks it.

**Do not move anything.** Stone adzes, bone awls, digging sticks, baskets, and paddles lie where they were last set down. They are not clutter. Their positions are part of the order. If you pick one up to examine it, return it exactly where it was, in the same orientation. If you are unsure, do not touch it.

**Do not add anything.** Leave no trash, no tools, no gifts, no markers, and no repairs of your own. Anything you leave becomes a new object the settlement must absorb, and it will be fitted into the architecture, grown over, and maintained forever. Dropped food, broken straps, and coins are all permanent intrusions.

**Do not take anything.** Do not pick moss, fern, lichen, or flowers. Do not take stones, even ones that look interesting. Do not carry off snails, frogs, or fish, even as specimens. Every niche is filled, and removing an occupant creates a gap that will be felt.

**Stay on the causeways.** Walk where the stone paths and footbridges are. Stepping onto moss-covered floor or into a channel crushes life and disturbs the order. If the causeway is blocked, wait or go back.

**Do not touch the thatch, walls, or steps.** The plants and animals in them are not decoration. Snails coat the steps, frogs cling to the walls, and rodents move through the roofs. Let them be. Lean on nothing you do not have to lean on.

**Keep your voice low.** You may want to speak to the inhabitants or to a companion. Keep your voice soft and short. Loud sounds will carry strangely and feel violent in this stillness.

**If you meet the inhabitants,** expect them to be working. They tend roofs, channels, and walls, moving with patience and repetition. Do not interrupt them. If you want to help, watch first, then match their pace and method. Do not correct their craft, do not improve their work, and do not offer your own tools. If they set something down, leave it where it lands.

## Time and Orientation

The hollow has no visible seasons. The light is the same white at every hour except the low gold of sunset, when the mist catches fire along the edges of the eastern hills. You must bring your own sense of time.

Carry a reliable timepiece that does not make noise. Set a schedule for resting, eating, and moving, and keep it. Without it, the hollow's stillness will make hours feel like minutes or minutes like hours.

Learn your route. The bowl is small enough to navigate by its center, which is the settlement, and by the rim hills to the east and west. Mark your path mentally, not physically. Do not carve, scratch, or stack stones to mark it.

## Psychological Preparation

The hollow is calm and deeply ordered, and it can be seductive. Many visitors feel an urge to sit down and stay, to let the mist settle over them and become part of the still, green world. Plan for this. Set a departure time and keep it. Remind yourself that the stillness is not rest for you. It is the shape of a place that has finished changing.

Expect a sense of being crowded. Every surface is full, and the density of life may make you feel claustrophobic despite the open sky. Breathe slowly, find a clear spot on the causeway, and remember that the enclosure is the hollow's nature, not your own trap.

Expect the silence to stay with you. When you leave, your ears may ring in the quiet, and your voice may sound thin and far away for some time.

## Arrival and Departure

Enter and leave by the same route. Do not climb the rim or cut across the slopes; the rim is worn to its present form, and new paths would be new wounds. If you came down from a particular slope, return up the same way.

Before leaving, check your pockets, bags, and gear. Make sure nothing has fallen, and that nothing you carried has been left in the hollow. Look back once from the rim and let the place settle back into its shape behind you. The mist will close over your path within minutes.

## Final Advice

Go in quietly, move slowly, take only your own footprints away, and leave nothing behind that the hollow must learn to hold. The Hollow of Quiet Roofs will not thank you or punish you. It will simply go on being exactly what it is, and your job is to be a visitor who does not change that.
