**What the Patient Attention Does**

It does not arrive as a voice or a figure. It arrives the way slow weather does, first as a change in what the bowl notices about itself. The steam rises a little more evenly. The Hummers turn their coils toward the same warm seam. A Lean-Walker stops at a survey notch and stays longer than it needs to. The attention has been here for a while, and it has been looking carefully.

Its first decision is restraint. It could do a great deal. It could stop the sinking, lift the towers, drain the bowl, clear the vines, and restore the city to its streets and lamps. It considers this and sets it aside. The city is not a broken machine waiting to be repaired. It is a sleeper following the rhythm of a day that ended long ago, and waking it all at once would be a kind of violence, however kindly meant. The grief of a city is not something to be wiped out by a power that arrives late.

So it chooses to be a witness first, and to act only where its acting costs the living nothing.

**The Hummers and the Seam**

The engines are dying. Their gauges read figures no one has checked in years, and the stray current the Hummers drink is thinning. The attention sees that the Hummers do not need the engines. They need warmth, and the bowl has warmth of its own, rising from the cracks in the floor where the ground still releases the heat that opened the wound.

It does not move the engines. It does not command the Hummers. It makes the seam a little more generous, opening a vent here, sealing a fracture there, so that the steam carries heat along the Hummers' coils more steadily. When the engines finally go quiet, the Hummers will not be left in silence. They will still be warm, tending a heat that comes from below and does not depend on anyone's machines. Their purpose changes shape without breaking.

The attention considers whether this is interference, and decides it is closer to tending a banked fire. The Hummers chose the engines because the engines were warm. The attention only makes sure the warmth lasts.

**The Lean-Walkers and the Marks**

The marks on the walls are the closest thing the Sunk Works has to a record of its own ending. Centimeter by centimeter, the Lean-Walkers have rubbed them shiny, and they stand before the altered ones for an hour, flickering their lenses at something no one can see. The attention understands that they are reporting, and that the one they report to is not in the bowl.

It is the only other witness the Sunk Works has. The attention does not answer them with words, because words would be a rupture in the rhythm. Instead it does one small thing. Where a Lean-Walker has scraped moss from a survey notch for the hundredth time, the rock in the notch takes a faint, fine mineral skin, a pale line that catches the light from the openings and throws it back. The Lean-Walker stops, flickers, and moves on. It has been acknowledged, in the only language it can read. The record is kept.

**The Skitterers and the Drift**

The grid is not preserved exactly. It is re-laid each generation, and the trails drift a little every time two streams meet and go their separate ways. A less patient power would see this as corruption and try to hold the old lines in place. The attention sees that the drift is how the city keeps living. The Skitterers are not copying the plan. They are interpreting it, and the interpretations are the plan's future.

So it lets the drift be. It asks only that the glow stay steady, that the wire keep carrying enough current for the Skitterers to shine. Where the streams have become faint, it gives them a little light, from the fungus on the walls, and lets them choose their own routes from there.

**The Birds and the Second Note**

The single note from the rim is the only voice that comes from above, and the attention has been listening to it the way a teacher listens to a student who has spoken only one word for a long time. The birds call once and fall silent, and the bowl stops to listen. Nobody knows what the note means.

The attention does not presume to know either. But it has a thought, and it lets the thought stand. It sends one faint answer back up the walls, so faint that only the birds on the highest ledges might hear it: a second note, a fraction lower, carried on the same updraft that brings the steam. It is not a reply in any language the birds have. It is a sign that the call has been heard, and that something below is listening, the way the bowl listened before.

**The Network and the Choice of Breaks**

The green network decides where the descent will break and where it will hold. This is a responsibility no creature should carry alone, and the attention recognizes that the network has been carrying it for years. It has anchored the buried towers by sending roots into the silt, and it has been learning, slowly, which towers are worth holding up.

The attention does not take this from it. It gives the network something it has never had: the knowledge that its work is seen, and that the city it keeps will not be erased when the bowl finally fills. The network's plan will survive in the mud, in the roots of the vines that climb the buried walls, in the pattern of growth that follows the old power lines long after the lines have rotted. The city will not be remembered in stone. It will be remembered in what grows over it.

**What It Will Not Do**

It will not stop the sinking. The floor is falling for reasons the attention understands and does not oppose: the heat that opened the bowl is still moving, and the ground is still finding its level. To stop the fall would be to hold the city in a half-buried state forever, flooded and unfinished, a grave that never settles. It is kinder to let the descent finish, slowly, at the centimeter the marks already show.

It will not lift the towers, drain the pumps, or name the dead. It will not turn the Sunk Works into a monument, because a monument would be for the people who built the city, and the Sunk Works is no longer their city. It belongs to the things that stayed, and the attention respects that.

**The Last Thing**

When the attention has finished, there is very little to see. The steam rises and falls back as rain. The Hummers are warm. The Lean-Walkers strip the moss from the pipes and leave the shadows alone. The Skitterers lay their trails, and the drift continues. The network keeps growing toward whatever is still warm.

The attention stays at the edge of the bowl, where the rim meets the sky, and listens. The next time a bird calls from the ledge, the note will cross the basin and reach the bottom, and the second note will follow it, faint and low. Everything that lives there will stop, as it always does, and listen. The attention will be listening too, the way one listens to a sleeper breathing in the next room, glad to hear it, and content to let it sleep until it chooses to wake, or until the sinking has carried the last of it gently down into the green.
