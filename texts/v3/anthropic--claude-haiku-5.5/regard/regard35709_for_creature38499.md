# The Attention

It did not arrive. It had been attending for a long while before it noticed there was a place to attend to, the way a listener realizes a melody has been playing for some time. Then the Sunken Reach was in its attention whole: the terraces, the frozen blooms, the rings settling in the salt, the tone rising from below and fading.

Its first impulse was the one every good power has and must learn to distrust. It wanted to see what was wrong and mend it. The reach did not look wrong. It looked finished. Then it looked again and saw that it was not finished, only still.

## What It Learned

Time here passes without consequence, so the power could take as long as it liked, and it took a long time. It read the place the way one reads a letter that has been folded and unfolded many times.

The tallies were not counts. The rows were grouped, and some were cut deeper than others. The deep cuts came first, made with force and certainty, and they belonged to the makers. After them the keepers had added shallower marks at an even pace, never more and never fewer. The power understood then that the keepers were continuing a sequence the makers had begun and abandoned.

Everything fit that reading. The worn axe, polished by a thousand grips. The swept floors around the shelters. The cairns standing like sentries over people who were no longer there. The haulers sweeping the pans into rings that someone had once designed. The grazers keeping the films at exactly the height the makers had left them. The stillness of the whole reach was not an accident of physics. It was a practice of tending an absence, carried on so faithfully for so long that it had become the shape of the place.

The power found this beautiful and also troubling. A held breath is a kind of devotion, but it is not peace. Nothing here could die, and nothing could be born. The blooms could not grow, the water could not fall, and nothing could be surprised, forgiven, or truly mourned, because grief needs a loss final enough to be grieved. The keepers had postponed the ending indefinitely, and in doing so had postponed everything else too.

## What It Considered

It weighed several courses.

**To restore the makers.** It refused this quickly. It did not know who the makers were, and it would be presumptuous to hand back what the keepers had been holding a place for and call it a gift. A restored past is not the thing that was lost. It would also take the keepers' work from them and make it pointless, which is a kind of theft.

**To end the reach.** It could dissolve the salt, scatter the fauna toward some gentler country, and let the glass cool into something else. The power recognized this as the same error in a different coat. The keepers' lives belonged to them, and so did the place they had made their own. Mercy that overrides a people's choices is only force that has learned to smile.

**To speak to them.** It considered this at length. A single word in a world so quiet would ripple through every cell at once, and a thousand faceted eyes would turn toward it. The keepers might answer, and then the power would have started a relationship it had no right to impose, however kind its intentions.

**To do nothing forever.** This was the easiest and in some ways the most dangerous. Attention without action can harden into one more stillness, and the power saw that it was in danger of becoming another part of the held breath. It would be watching the keepers without ever letting them see that anything had changed.

So it chose the smallest thing it could find.

## What It Did

First, at the lip of the highest terrace, where the mist condensed too thinly to drip, it let a single drop fall. Not a flood, not a stream. One drop. The porous floor did not drink it at once. The drop struck a fracture in the black glass, ran down a groove the haulers had swept over countless passes, and came to rest in a shallow hollow near the edge of the nearest pan. It sat there, a small bright bead of water, and did not dry.

It was the first thing in the reach to move downward and remain where it had moved. The power watched the pan darken around it. The salt crust flexed in a way it had never flexed before, and one hexagon split along a line that had never been drawn. A hauler stopped in the middle of its sweep. The herd fell silent together, and then the murmur resumed, slightly altered, as though the herd had absorbed the change and gone on.

The power did not intervene further. The hangers would find the water or they would not. A keeper might draw it into a channel, or sweep it into the rings, or leave it to become the reach's first pool. The choice belonged to them.

Second, at the boundary stone where the grazers always stopped, the power left a single smooth, unmarked slab. It was not a cairn and not a tally. It was a blank. A space that someone would have to choose to mark, or to leave empty.

Third, it wrote nothing and said nothing. It let its attention move back to the edge of the reach, and then past it, so the keepers would not feel watched. Perhaps this was the most benevolent thing it did. For a long time it had been looking at them intently, and it judged that they had earned the right not to be looked at.

## What Followed

The power does not know what followed. It may return someday, but time here has no consequence, so there is no later in any sense the keepers would recognize. The water may still sit in its hollow. A hanger may have drunk it. The herd may have grazed around it for generations. The blank stone may stay blank.

It hopes, plainly and without requiring it, that some morning a keeper will climb to the boundary, look at the empty slab, and lift a glass tool. Perhaps it will cut a single line, shallow, unlike the others. Perhaps it will cut a deep one, marking something ended instead of continued. Either would be a story, and a story is what the reach has been missing.

Above the basin the glow pulses faintly, and the dark keeps its shape. Below, the mist rises and falls back. The haulers sweep, the salt rings settle, and the tone comes up from the deep, held for several seconds.

Then, very faintly, something else. A drop sliding down a fracture. A small, settling sound, like a breath let out at last.

The power has learned that the most respectful thing wisdom can do for a held breath is to make the exhale possible, and then to stop holding its own.
