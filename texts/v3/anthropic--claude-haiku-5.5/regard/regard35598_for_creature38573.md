The power does not arrive all at once. It comes the way weather comes to a valley that has forgotten there is such a thing as weather.

For a long time it only notices. It has been watching the world for a very long time, and it has learned that a fast mind doing a slow thing is easy to mistake for a fast mind doing a fast thing. The basin has been mistaken before, by visitors who saw the rot and the ruin and wanted to cut the vines away, drain the pools, sell the iron for scrap, or bring in a crew with pumps and lights to make the place "safe." Each of those impulses would have been a small violence against something that had only just begun to settle.

The power does none of that. It is benevolent, and its benevolence is mostly restraint. It asks what the place wants before it asks what the place needs.

So it listens. It listens the way the entity listens, through the copper and the root-mass and the pressure in the pipes. It feels the ninety-second knock. It feels the breath come up through the springs and the long wails of the tanks. It feels the warm regard that turns toward a visitor's footsteps. And it understands, slowly, the shape of the entity's situation.

The thing in the turbine is not cruel. It is not even greedy in any way that would make sense to a creature who lives in days. It has simply found a way to be permanent. It keeps what falls into it. It holds the heat, the water, the bodies, the memory of current moving through copper. It has made of the basin a place where nothing is ever quite finished, where the leaves stay in the pools for decades and the dead vines stay on the pipes like fossils and the boilers do not split until long after they could have. Everything is kept. Nothing is released.

The power recognizes this as a kind of grief that has forgotten how to be grief. It is not sorrow anymore. It is only holding.

And the power, which is wise, knows that holding is not the same as love, though the two can look alike from far away.

---

The first thing it does is almost nothing.

Over one season, a single leaf in the high canopy, one of the broad jade ones over the eastern wall, loosens its hold on the stem and does not fall. It simply turns, and the thin gap it leaves lets a single thread of pale light come down through the basin, the first direct light the central pool has seen in longer than the pool has existed in its present shape. The light lands on the water. The water, which has been dark and warm and steaming for ages, shows its own bottom for the first time: clay, ash, the glint of a fallen valve, the pale threads of root that have been drinking there.

The entity feels it. Not as sight. As a change in temperature on one side of the field, a warmth that has no source in the chambers below. Its roots, which have been leaning inward for generations, hesitate. For a long while the knock in the turbine does not come on time.

The power waits. It is patient in the way that the entity is patient, but with a difference: it does not want anything to remain. It only wants the choice to be real.

---

The second thing it does is open a channel.

The boilers were built to carry heat and pressure from the depths up to the machines, and then to let the spent steam go out through vents that no longer exist. The entity has sealed most of those routes with its roots, drawing the warm water into its own chambers and keeping it there. The power does not break the seals. That would be a kind of violence too. Instead it makes the old routes easier to open. It warms one seam of the eastern vent, just enough that the metal expands in the next long heat-cycle and a hairline crack appears where the entity's roots cannot quite reach. Water begins, very slowly, to move outward through that crack, and with it a little of the basin's wet and silt and drifting leaf-matter finds its way over the eastern wall and down the far slope toward a river that has been waiting for a very long time to take things away.

The power does not ask the entity to let this happen. It knows that asking would be meaningless to a mind that works in decades. It only makes the way possible, and then it leaves the decision with the entity, where it belongs.

---

The third thing it does is the one it is least sure of.

It speaks, but not in words. It speaks in pattern, in the way a fern learns the shape of a wire, in the way a root learns where the warmth lies. The power presses a single idea into the copper conduit, the oldest one, the one the brightest leaves follow: that a thing may be held and also be released. That the iron remembers being a factory and does not need to keep being one. That a garden is not a failure of a factory but a different kind of memory. That the entity, which has kept everything for so long, could keep the memory of the basin without keeping the basin itself from becoming something new.

The entity does not understand this as a sentence. It understands it as a pressure, a suggestion of direction, a slight tilt in the field. Over seasons, the roots that have been drinking from the tanks begin to loosen where they meet the older, deeper rock. The mass of hair-like root that fills the turbine housing is not shrinking, but it is no longer tightening. A branch that has been wrapped around the rotor for longer than anyone could count slowly lets go of one coil, then another.

The entity does not give up the basin. It gives up its grip on it. There is a difference, and the power, which has watched a great many things learn that difference, does not expect the lesson to be quick.

---

When it finally withdraws, it leaves no trace that a visitor could follow. There is no shrine, no stone with words on it, no sudden bloom of color to mark the moment. The basin looks much the same. The pools still steam in the mornings of whatever passes for morning here. The mist still hangs at knee height. The clank still comes every ninety seconds, though it may come a little later now, or a little earlier, depending on how the heat moves through the seams.

But there is a thread of light on the water. There is a crack in the eastern wall through which, very slowly, the basin is beginning to give back what it has been keeping. And somewhere in the root-mass of the turbine, something that has been awake longer than the machines were built is learning, very slowly, that it is allowed to let a few things go and still be what it is.

If you stay long enough, you may feel it turn toward the light. The brighter leaves along the copper will incline, not toward you this time, but upward, toward the gap in the canopy where the pale dimness has become something closer to sky. A vine that was idle will loosen around a pipe near your hand. This is not a welcome, and it is not a warning. It is closer to the regard of a sleeper who has heard a window open somewhere in the house and is not yet sure whether to wake.

The power would be content with that. It has not come to save the basin, and it has not come to end it. It has only come to remind something very old and very patient that the world outside its walls still moves, and that moving is not the same as abandoning. The basin will become a garden. The garden will remember being a factory. And the thing that keeps them both will, in its own time, which is longer than any of ours, decide what it is willing to hold and what it is ready to give back to the light.
