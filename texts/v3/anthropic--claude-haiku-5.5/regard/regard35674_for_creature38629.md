The attention arrives the way weather does, not as a single event but as a change in what the air is willing to hold. It notices the cleft first as a warmth, then as a pattern of small continuities: the drops falling from the same lips, the white tracks lengthening a hair a year, the rust skin folding into every crack between the seep and the light.

It does not look at the film as a thing to be assessed. It looks at it the way one looks at a long sentence that has not yet ended.

*What is here?*

A pump that has run unattended for longer than anyone counted, still keeping time. A seep that would stop without the pump, or would run unchecked and scald whatever it touched. A film that has no edge it recognizes and no center it can name, which has grown along the gradient of a single source of heat. Sedges bent sideways in steam, holding their ground in the thickest crust. A gauge stopped at a number no one remembers, reading a pressure that never changes. A cable stapled to stone, a rusted wheel that no longer turns, and a short white arc on the rock that someone once painted and that the film has been growing over, slowly, until the line and the living mat are no longer easy to separate.

The power considers what it could do. It is good at considering. It is also aware that most of what it could do would be a kind of noise, and that this place has had enough noise.

*Stop the pump?* It would be the easiest act and the most obviously cruel. The pump is the film's weather. Its groan is the pressure that draws water faster through the fissure, and the water is the film's whole world. Silence it, and the seep would cool and stagnate, and the film, which has no way to grieve, would thin and go pale and let the rock take it back. The machine is loud. It is also the reason anything here is alive. The power does not confuse loudness with harm.

*Repair the gauge?* A needle moved to a true number would mean nothing to the film, which does not understand numbers, and nothing to the sedges, which do not read. It would be a gift for a reader who has left the cleft and will not come back. The power notices this and feels something close to tenderness toward the people who once kept the number honest. They are gone. The gauge is their last attention, and it has frozen where they left it. That is theirs to keep, not the power's to correct.

*Scrub the paint?* This one tempts it more. The line is a human mark, and the power, which is wise about time, knows that it is also now partly the film's own. The rock has taken it in. Stripping the stone would not restore some original state. It would tear a seam that has already healed. The arc belongs to both of them now, the way a scar belongs to the skin that closed over it.

*Pull the film outward, or thin it to make room for the sedges?* The power considers the sedges briefly. They are slow, and they are holding on. The film has never reached them with thought, but it feels them as knots and as shade, and it has decided, in whatever way such things decide, not to move them. The power respects that. It is not its place to decide which of these patient lives should spread.

So it chooses almost nothing.

It does not repair, clear, stop, or redirect. It lets the pump keep its duty cycle, the rise, the knock, the hiss. It lets the seep keep its gradient, scalding at the fissure and cooling toward the mouth. It lets the drops fall from the same lips. If there is a single thing it does, it is this: it stays for a moment in the place where the steam meets cold stone, and it is attentive in the way a fall of water is attentive to the stone it lands on. The weight is small. It presses down, and then it slides away.

The power understands, with something like humility, that its attention is also a kind of weight. Any mind that notices a place can bend it. A wise one learns to notice lightly, to let the water find its own channels, to trust that the pump's keepers, who could have switched it off and chose not to, left something that can continue without them. It has been running for longer than anyone counted. That is a kind of stewardship, and the film has grown into it.

The film, meanwhile, registers the groan as an approach of more warmth and the knock as a loosening, and the hiss as a surge that it gathers itself in the wake of. It has no word for the attention that passes over it. If it had one, the word might be *weight*, or *pressure*, or *cool drop*. The power would not mind any of these. They are all true enough.

Before it goes, the power lets the next drop fall from the lip above the arc. The drop swells, hangs, and lets go. It lands on the rust mat, a point of cold, and the film takes the small weight without knowing whose hand, if any, set it in motion. Then the drop slides away down the white track, and the cleft goes on in its noise and steam and flat light, one slow continuation among the many, and the power, having done nothing that will be remembered, is content.
