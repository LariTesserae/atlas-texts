# What Attention Does

It noticed the basin the way you notice one note in a long chord. It was not a sound. It was a change in what the sound was for.

It is not a god in the old sense. It has no face to offer and no voice to lend. It is closer to a current of care that moves through the world and grows heavier where something is working hard and well. For a long time it passed over the massif without slowing, as it passes over most places. Then the hum reached it, a held note in the threads, and it stopped.

Its first act was to do nothing for a very long time, which for it was a kind of courtesy.

It listened to the frogs. It learned their count and understood that the count was not random. It passed from pool to pool, each group waiting exactly long enough for the last, and it was counting the intervals between the tenders' returns to the threads, the days between one sag and its repair. The frogs were keeping a record of the basin's health in the only language the basin used. It was a good record. The power found that it had nothing to add to it.

It listened to the tenders. It watched one wake in a fern frond, roll its whole length along a strand, and settle back to work. It felt the agreement that ran from post to post, the clicks and pulls that held the water in shape. Nobody had written that agreement down. Nobody needed to. It had been made over generations by bodies that could not speak to each other and did not need to.

It followed the lattice upward, thread by thread, to the place where the net gathered at the top of the massif's last stub. There a ring of stone, too regular to be natural, pointed at the sky. The threads ended there. Something had been meant to climb this far, and nothing had yet arrived.

The power considered what it could do.

It could finish the work. It could lean on the hum and let the water climb all the way up, a fountain of sky over the basin, a visible answer to the question the threads had been asking since before anyone counted. That would be easy. It would also be a kind of theft, because the question had been asked by the tenders and the frogs and the flowers together, and an answer handed down from above would make their long patience into a footnote.

It could mend the threads that travelers had cut. This it considered more carefully. The cut samples had gone dull and lost their hum within a day, and the power understood why: they had been taken out of the agreement. A thread is not only a string. It is a place in a conversation. To restore one by force would be to pretend the conversation had not been interrupted, and the basin would know the difference even if the travelers never did.

It could drive out the predators, the long silent things whose hushes moved through the pools like ripples. It noticed how the chorus rose again after each hush and how the tenders, after each one, went back to the threads with a little more care. The predators were part of the count too. A basin without them would be quieter and emptier, and it would forget how to listen.

So it did almost nothing.

It thinned the fog a little at the edges, so the flowers could hold their bells open an hour longer in the morning, which seemed to please them. It let the warm air from below rise a few degrees higher, so the mosses on the stone grew a shade more green. It did not touch the threads, the beads, the cisterns, or the hum itself.

And it climbed.

Not as a fountain. As attention. It went up the lattice the way the beads went up, a little at a time, gathering at each crosspoint, and at the top, in the thinning fog, it found the thing that was climbing toward the sun.

It was a bead of water. Only that. One droplet, larger than the rest, having been drawn up the strands by the strongest pull of the hum at dawn, and now hanging at the last crosspoint below the stone ring, trembling in the gold light. It had come further than any bead in memory. Below it, the tenders had lifted their heads. Across the basin the frogs were silent, all of them, waiting.

The power understood, then, what the lattice had been for, or what it had become. The threads did not want to lift water into the sky. They wanted to know whether they could. Every bead that climbed was a test of the question, and every bead that fell back was an answer, and the answer had always been to try again tomorrow.

The power let the bead be.

It did not push it over the ring. It did not hold it there. It let the droplet rest a moment in the light, long enough for the sun to strike it and break it briefly into colour, a tiny arc of red and gold hanging in the grey, and then it let the bead fall. It slid back down the thread, crosspoint to crosspoint, and dropped into the channel below the topmost plinth with a soft, unmistakable tick.

Far down the slope, a tender clicked once.

Then the frogs began again, starting in the nearest pool and spreading outward, the count resuming exactly where it had stopped, as if nothing had happened. Perhaps nothing had. The hum rose a little in pitch and settled.

The power lingered at the top of the massif, where the fog was lifting in long ribbons toward the peaks. It had not finished the basin's sentence, and it did not intend to. It had only shown that someone was listening, and that listening was not the same as asking for anything.

Before it withdrew, it left one thing behind, a faint note, barely there, folded into the hum like a second thread. It was not a command. It was closer to a thank-you, or a blessing, or the simple acknowledgment that a long and careful work had been noticed by something that could have been tempted to take it over.

Down in the warm grey hollow, a tender lowered its head to a sagging strand, held still, and waited. Then it began to brush.
