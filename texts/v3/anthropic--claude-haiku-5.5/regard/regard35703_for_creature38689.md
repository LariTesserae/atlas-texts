# What the Watcher Did

It did not arrive as a voice or a figure. It came the way a change in pressure comes before rain, a shift in what the basin was paying attention to. For a while it simply attended. It listened to the hush of the stream, the thousand ticks of the falls, the dry clicking of the reeds, and it listened to the tapping in the cliffs, which never sped up and never slowed down.

It understood the count first. The counters were not tallying eggs. They were tallying something the eggs stood for, a debt or a promise, and the number could not settle because one term was missing. The warm seams were the missing term. The makers had gone into the stone, and the stone was still rising, still warm, and the makers were still in it, still counting, though no longer as makers. They had become the thing the counters kept faith with.

The watcher saw that it could end the count. It could say the answer aloud, and the chain of hands would stop, the eggs would stay where they lay, and the swarm would lose its circle. It could also warm the whole basin, or cool it, or open the rim and let the sun in for the whole day. Each of these would be a kindness, and each would be a theft. The count was not a problem to be solved. It was how the Throat remembered, and remembering was not finished.

So the watcher did not solve anything. It did three small things, and then it stayed to see what they would do.

---

## First: the Blade

It went to the stream, where the flock was standing over the cold stones in their stillness. They did not move aside. They watched the watcher the way they watched each other's beaks: noticing, not changing course.

The watcher looked at the blades. They all pointed downstream, toward the bends where the flock fed, and past the bends toward the place where the braided water pooled and slid over the lip of the basin and out through the gap in the eastern rim. The makers had not set them down to be lost. They had set them facing the way the water went, which meant the makers had expected the water to carry them somewhere, and had expected someone to find them there.

The watcher lifted one blade. It was cold, and heavier than it looked, and the chipping on its edge was so fine that the watcher felt it as a kind of handwriting. It carried the blade up the wet slope, past the crawlers' furrows and the pulsing clusters, to the one ledge in the basin the sun reached each day. There it set the blade down, face up, where the light would fall on it for an hour.

The flock did not follow. One bird lifted its head, held it there for a long breath, then lowered it again.

For the first time in a long time, the shadow of a thing on the floor of the Throat did not lag. It was a thin, sharp line, moving across the stone as the sun moved, and then it was gone with the light.

## Second: the Seam

The watcher went to the warmest of the pale seams, the one the clusters pressed against most densely. It did not press itself into the rock, which would have been an intrusion. It rested its attention against the stone, the way the counters rested their foreheads against the wall after passing an egg into place. It held still for a few breaths.

Nothing answered in words. But the heat in the seam shifted, slightly, as if something inside had turned toward the attention and then turned back to its work. Somewhere in the warm dark, a count registered a new entry, one that had not been there before: *a presence held still, and was not taken.*

Above the largest pool, the swarm's column bent. It did not break. It bent the way a river bends around a stone, and reformed, and kept turning.

## Third: the Leaving

The watcher did not stay. A wise attention knows the difference between witnessing and occupying. It withdrew up the western slope, over the rim, past the notch where the sun came in, and it did not look back, because looking back would have been another kind of pressure.

But on its way out it passed a counter at a gallery mouth. The creature's feelers came out into the open air, toward the watcher, and held there, and the watcher understood that it was being tallied. It was not a threat and not a guest. It was a number that nobody had asked for, entering a process that had no need of it, and the process took it in the way the swarm took whatever fell into the pool: without asking where it came from, and without refusing it.

---

## What Remained

At dusk the chorus rose. The bats, the reeds, the crawlers' clicks, the swarm's low buzz, all of them lifted together, and the rock hummed back their doubled voices from every direction. The watcher could hear it from the rim. It sounded no different than it had before.

Except that on the ledge in the last of the sun, a cold blade lay face up in the fading light, pointing now not downstream but up toward the notch, where the makers had perhaps meant it to go all along. The flock had not yet noticed it. The counters had. Somewhere in the stone, a chain of hands passed one egg a little further along its gallery, and paused, and resumed.

The watcher had not solved the Throat of Seveth, and had not meant to. It had moved one thing into the light, warmed one seam with attention, and entered one number into a count that would never end. That was enough. The basin would go on deepening, the stone would go on rising, and the arithmetic would go on having no answer. But someone had stood in the middle of it, held still, and been counted.
