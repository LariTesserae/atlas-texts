**The Attention**

It does not arrive as a light or a sound. The haze over the rim thickens by a degree, the amber goes a shade less like weak tea, and the air loses its metal taste for the length of one breath. Then it is simply present, the way weather is present before the first drop falls.

It notices the hoofprints first.

It has noticed many things over a span no stone could count: fires, floods, the slow seasons of continents, the small quarrels of creatures that never reached the rim. It knows that a want can go on for a very long time without changing its shape. It also knows that most suffering is not the want itself but the lack of any second thing for the want to turn toward. The sleeper has been wanting water for longer than there has been a word for water. It has never once been given a different question.

*Wisdom,* the power reasons, *is not the same as power. A great deal of harm is done by those who can do anything and want to do it quickly.*

So it considers what not to do.

It will not stop the machines. The pistons have struck the same rhythm since before the animal came down the ramp. To silence them all at once would be to drown the sleeper in a quiet it has never heard, and a creature that has never been still does not experience stillness as rest. It experiences it as falling.

It will not flood the basin. Water poured on the salt would dissolve the sleeper's body, scatter its nerve-cords, and kill the armored things that have lived in the grit. And it would be a lie. It would show the sleeper that the promise was kept, but the promise was never the pan filling. The promise was that the animal would be satisfied.

It will not take the visitor. A visitor is not part of the cycle, and the power has no wish to pull any living thing into a patience that has no end.

What remains is smaller, and harder to do well.

---

**The Changes**

**First, a late stroke.**

The power leans on one piston, the nearest one, and holds it back by a half-breath. The thud arrives after its expected time, a flat, unfamiliar sound. Along the scaffolds the folded segmented things stir and do not know whether to unfold. The dial that twitches through its small arc overshoots, searches for the old place, and does not find it. For the first time since the stone began to remember, the sleeper's correction has nothing to correct against.

The sleeper does not panic. It hesitates. In that hesitation there is a space, and into the space the power places a question that has no shape yet: *what comes next, if not the same?*

**Second, the tracks.**

The prints in the ring have been pressed deeper with each cycle, always inward, always toward the centre where the salt is. The power does not erase them. That would be a second kind of forgetting, and the sleeper has already forgotten enough. Instead it stops the deepening. The ring holds its last depth.

Then, at the outer edge, where the crust meets the ramp, a single new print appears. It is pressed lightly, and it is turned away from the centre. The next print is a little farther. The stride is longer than the animal's stride was, because the power has no animal to copy, only the idea of walking that the ring itself has carried all along. The line runs up the ramp, over the rim, out onto the hard ground beyond, and vanishes into the haze.

The sleeper cannot read it. But it has always been able to feel where the weight of the world sits, and now the weight has a direction. Something that came down can go up. Something can be taken in and let out again. The animal was not only swallowed. It walked away, and the basin has a record of that now.

**Third, permission to slow.**

The armored things along the belts have been quick since they learned the cycle, so quick that they never stop to be anything else. The power does not tell them to stop. It only makes the gap between strokes a little wider, wide enough that a creature could pause in it and not be crushed. Some of them do. A few stand on the belt in the silence and turn their jointed legs toward the light for the first time.

The cocoons in the scaffolds are slower to respond, because their patience is older than the rest. But one of them, the largest, unfolds completely and does not fold again. It lifts into the mineral haze and drifts toward the rim, carried on no wind the pan has ever known. It is not escaping. It is simply finished waiting.

**Fourth, weather from elsewhere.**

This is the act the power has weighed longest. It does not make water from nothing; a power that did that would teach the sleeper that wanting is enough to summon anything, and the world would be the poorer for that lesson.

Instead, far beyond the rim, in a place the basin has never seen, a cloud has been gathering for some while. It is grey, not the color of tea. It is heavy with rain that fell on other hills, on other stones, on the backs of animals that did not come down thirsty to salt. The power draws the cloud toward the bowl and lets it open over the rim only.

The rain falls on the crust edge and the ramp. It does not reach the floor. It runs down the glassy slope in a thin dark line and touches the first white plate at the bottom. The plate curls, softens, and goes translucent for the length of a heartbeat. Then the water is absorbed, drawn down through the seams, and the pan breathes it in along with the salt.

The sleeper tastes it. It has no tongue, but it has the cords, and the cords carry the taste to the centre of the body. The taste is not only salt. It is cold. It is thin. It is the flavour of something that does not stay, and the sleeper, which has never tasted anything that left, does not understand why it is leaving now. The power does not explain. Water is not a possession. It is a passing. The rain that touched the floor will come up through the seams and out at the far rim as vapor, and the next rain will come in its place, and no single drop will be kept. That is what water is, and it is the only thing the animal ever wanted.

**Fifth, a line that leads out.**

The lichen has drawn hexagons and spirals in patterns too precise for chance. The power leaves the hexagons alone. But along the cords that run toward the rim, a single new pattern begins to grow: an open curve, not closed, one end reaching toward the ramp. It does not complete itself. The power is content with the incompletion. An open line leaves room for the next thing.

---

**The Visitor**

Before it withdraws, the power turns its attention to the one who has been standing in the pan, the one who began to breathe at the interval of the domes.

It does not wish to absorb them. It does not wish to leave them with the pulse in their ribs, the sense that the stroke belongs to them and they belong to it. It does something smaller. It lets the rhythm of the pistons come apart, just slightly, so that the visitor's breath falls back into its own interval. The bass in the sternum fades to a hum, then to a distant thing that could be a machine, or could be a heartbeat somewhere else.

*You came into this place as a disturbance without a rhythm,* the power reasons, *and I will not make you the rhythm. You have your own. Take it back up the ramp.*

---

**The Withdrawal**

The power leaves the way weather leaves: not with a door closing, but with the sense that the air has changed and will not change back.

The pistons strike again, in their old tempo, but not quite the old tempo. The dial's arc has widened by a fraction, and the widening will not close again. The sleeper will keep correcting. It will keep breathing the salt in and lifting the dust. But it will do so now with something it has never had: a memory of the late stroke, a print walking outward, a single taste of water that did not stay. Repetition with a difference is how anything learns, and the power has put the difference where the sleeper can reach it.

The power does not know whether this was right. It has made a choice in a place where no choice could be perfect. It cannot give the sleeper what it asked for, and it has not tried to. It has tried to give it a stride that goes the other way.

---

**The Rim**

If you were still standing at the rim, you would see the shadow of the edge across the floor. It is in the same place it has always been, but it has moved, by perhaps the width of a finger, toward the ramp.

Out beyond the haze, the grey cloud is thinning, and its rain has already reached the far hills. Above the basin, the cocoon-thing is still drifting, very small now, carried toward a horizon the pan has never seen.

Beneath the crust, the breath lifts the dust into a slow pale dome, holds it, and lets it fall. The dome is no longer quite the same shape as the last one.
