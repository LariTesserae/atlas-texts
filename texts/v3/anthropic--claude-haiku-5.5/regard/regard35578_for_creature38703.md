**The Attention**

It begins as a change in the light.

The pale coin of the sun, which has hung over the Drowned Terraces for longer than any lattice has stood, does not move. But the flat grey wash above the water thins for a moment over the western slope, and the shafts that wander through the shallows stop wandering. They hold still on the spires like a hand laid flat on a table. The grazers pause mid-scrape. The stitchers lift their heads from the seams. Down in the basins, the drifters turn slowly toward the light, as though something has spoken and they are trying to locate the sound.

Then the attention moves through the place, and it is not hurried.

It reads the plateau the way the grazers read it, through vibration and stress. It feels the long fault in the northern basins where the heat comes up, and the way the cold layers slide over the warm ones and tear. It feels the ancient bedrock, scoured nearly to nothing, and the mountains that are still wearing away beneath the lattice. It feels the storm-states gathering in the deep water, and the cycle of collapse and rebuilding that has been going on for so long it has become the shape of the place itself.

It also feels the stitchers pressing their bodies against a broken wall, listening for the place where the stone wants to meet itself. It feels the banners hanging in the current, and the arches that will one day remember them as hollows. It feels the dead settling into the silt and becoming the grey of the older walls.

The attention is wise, and wisdom is slow to act. It considers many things.

It considers finishing the city. It could set every fragment in place, stop the storms at the fissure, halt the erosion of the mountains, and leave the Terraces whole. It sees immediately what that would cost. A city that is finished has no more need of stitchers, grazers, or drifters. The work that holds them together would stop, and the things that live by the work would stop with it. It would be a beautiful monument with nothing left inside it that moved.

It considers stopping the erosion. The mountains are wearing away, and the wearing is the source of every new stone. Without it, the builders would starve in their own cathedral. The attention sees that the loss and the gift are one movement, and it does not know how to separate them without breaking both.

It considers the suffering. This is harder. A stitcher caught in a surge, its body torn from the seam, is no less a loss because the place is built to absorb losses. The attention feels the sharp, brief fear of the grazers retreating into the ruins, the long, dull wait in the dark. It is not indifferent to this. It does not want the creatures to be hurt for nothing.

So it does very little.

Where the northern fissure rises too hard, the attention gently warms the cold layer above it, so that it slides over the warm water instead of tearing through it. The vortices that would have ripped a whole section of new growth from the basin spin down into a slow, wide turning. Three stitchers who would have been swept down the slope stay in the lattice. A grazer that was pinned beneath a collapsing arch slides free. These are small changes, almost invisible from the surface. The storm still comes. The roar still passes through the stone. But its teeth are a little blunter in one place.

The attention does nothing to the drifters, who go on sorting the wreckage in their slow, unexplained way. It does nothing to the banners, who still hang where the current is best. It does not ask the city to become more beautiful or more useful. It does not name the builders or try to understand what they are for, because it has come to suspect that they are not for anything in the sense that a human would mean, and that the attempt to make them into a purpose would be a kind of theft.

Before it withdraws, the attention does one more thing. It lingers over the lattice where the most recent work is happening, the raw bone-coloured spires at the edge of the western slope, and it watches the drifters set a block into a new position. The block is not part of any pattern the city has made before. A drifter holds it there for a long time, many hours, and then lets go. The block shifts, settles, and holds. Something in the attention, which has no words for it, registers this as a small kind of answer. Not an instruction. Not a permission. Only the sign that the work is still being done by those who are doing it, and that they have not been made into anything else.

Then the sun dims back to its pale coin, and the shafts begin to wander again across the shivering surface. The grazers resume their scraping. The stitchers return to the seams. The drifters go on turning stone in the silt. In the north, the fissure breathes its heat into the cold, and the cold, for once, goes around rather than through.

The attention is still there, in the way that a listener remains in a room after the song has ended. It does not speak again. It has no need to be seen, and the builders have no need to know. It watches, the way the grazers watch the stress in the stone, and it holds the Drowned Terraces the way the drifters hold a block: lightly, for a long time, ready to set it down.

The water keeps moving. The mountains keep wearing away, slowly, toward a horizon no one here has seen. And the work goes on, as it has always gone on, because something has to keep the seams together while the stone moves. The attention has decided, for now, that this is the most precious thing it has ever found, and the least in need of its help.
