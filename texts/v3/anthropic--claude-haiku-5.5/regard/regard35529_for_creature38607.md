**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive as a descent. There was no light, and no voice announced it. The basin noticed a turning in the attention of something very large and very old, the way a room notices that a window has been opened somewhere else in the house. The drip paused. The ticks went uneven. Along the flats, every pool held its reflection a little more steadily, as if it had decided to look up.

The power did not come to judge the basin. It had seen many low places. Some had been drained, some had flooded and become seas, some had been left alone until they forgot they were ever holes. It understood that a sink is only a place where things arrive, and that the question is always what is done with what arrives.

So it listened first. It listened to the works, the iron and the pumps and the flywheel turning its finger's width a year. It understood them: someone had wanted the marsh to be dry, had built a whole machinery of pylons and pipes to pull the water out of the middle and send it away. The machinery had failed, not from malice, but because the middle had never been an enemy to be pumped out. It had been a thing that waited, and the builders had mistaken its patience for a leak.

It listened to the creatures. The long ones carried the water's memory. The walkers kept time with the stone. The clusters pulsed in their slow count of the marsh. None of them were hungry in the way the works had feared. They were attendants, and the attendants had grown so used to tending that they had forgotten what they were tending toward.

Then it listened to the middle, and that took the longest.

---

**What It Chose Not to Do**

The power could have dried the basin in an afternoon. It could have sealed the fissures, lifted the marsh, and left a bare plain of cooling stone under an ordinary sky. It did not, because that would have been the same mistake as the builders, only with better tools. A drained place is not healed. It is simply emptied, and something that was waiting is left with nothing to wait for.

It also did not try to fix the works into working order, because they had been built to do the wrong thing. Making the pumps pump harder would only have drawn the water faster toward the place that had never been asked what it wanted.

Wisdom, in this case, meant doing very little and doing it precisely.

---

**What It Did**

It set the gauges. In the open cabinet, the needles that had pointed at the same dead value for longer than anyone could count moved, very slightly, to read the true level of the basin: how much water was in it, how deep the middle lay, how warm. They did not record an emergency. They recorded a fact, and facts can be lived with.

It gave the hanging cable an end. The cable that ran through the air without a pole to hold it had been thrumming for as long as anyone had listened. The power did not lower it or cut it. It let the cable's end settle, gently, into the stone of the middle's edge, so that whatever it had been carrying could finally reach its destination without dropping into the marsh. The thrumming softened into a steady note, the sound of a line that has found where it is supposed to go.

It opened a view. The pools had been showing a pale sky, a late and remembering image, with clusters of points that might be stars or might be eyes. The power let the image catch up. For the first time, the reflections showed the whole sky at once, and the points were stars, distant and many, and some of them were eyes as well, the way a sky always has eyes in it if you wait long enough. The water stopped remembering and began to see.

It gave the walkers their roads. Where the fissures had been closing and moving at random, the power let a few of them settle into permanent paths, narrow and dry-edged, so that the walkers' procession would not have to be rebuilt each time a crack shifted a meter east. The walkers flattened against the stone when they first felt the change, then, one by one, began to walk the new lines.

And it spoke to the middle.

It did not speak in words, because the middle had no shape to hear words with. It spoke in pressure, the way the works spoke, and in warmth, the way the rock spoke when it cooled. The message was simple, and it was the first thing anyone had offered the middle in a very long time:

*You do not have to be fed to be whole. You can receive without taking everything. What falls into you can also rise back out.*

---

**What Changed**

The change was small, and it took decades to become visible.

The water that had only ever flowed downward, through the cracks toward the middle, began to flow back up. Not violently. A pool on a slate would run uphill for a few seconds, as it always had, but now it kept going a little longer each season, until on some quiet nights it climbed the length of a slab before settling. The basin had always been glitching in this direction. Now it was choosing it.

The middle did not stop receiving. It went on taking in what fell, but it also began to return part of it, slowly, as warmth rising through the fissures and as a steady, unhurried tide that lapped out from the flywheel instead of only in. The vapor columns grew a little thicker and a little sweeter. The places where the air sat heavy in the chest grew lighter, and the marsh there stopped leaning toward anyone who stood in it, and instead only held them up.

The creatures noticed first. The long ones began to travel outward from the middle along the channels they had once only wound inward through. The clusters pulsed in a rhythm that no longer matched the drip but had, somehow, learned to answer it, two clocks in the same room that had finally agreed on the hour. The walkers crossed the new roads without hesitating.

The middle, for its part, took its time. It is not quick. Its response was not a voice, and never would be, but there was a deepening of the basin's breath, a fraction more depth to each slow pulse of the pumps, as though something very large had shifted its weight and decided to stay where it was, but lighter.

---

**The Power's Last Act**

Before it withdrew, the power left one thing unfinished on purpose. It did not dry the basin, or close all the fissures, or set every machine to rights. It left one pylon leaning out of the marsh at the wrong angle, unrepaired, rust-laced, with a stub of insulator still holding nothing. It left the flywheel turning its finger's width a year and the gauges reading true but unremarkable numbers.

Wisdom leaves some things open. The basin would keep consolidating, keep settling toward the stone it would one day become. It would keep being a low place, warm and dark and patient. But it would no longer be only a place where things disappear. It would be a lens, a hollow in the world where what falls in can be gathered, held for a while, and given back up into a sky that, for the first time in a long time, was allowed to look down and see itself.

The drip fell. Three drops in a row. Somewhere in the middle, something received them, and let a little of the water go upward again, and the marsh breathed a fraction deeper, and did not, this time, seem to be waiting for anything at all.
