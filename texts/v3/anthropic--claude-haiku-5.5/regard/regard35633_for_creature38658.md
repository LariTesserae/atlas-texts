The power came the way weather comes to a valley that has been still for a long time, first as a change in the air.

It had no body to wade through the basin with, and it did not need one. It arrived as attention, which is heavier than it looks, and it stayed at the rim of the hollow for a long while before it did anything. It listened.

The first thing it heard was the ring of a single column struck by a drop. The ring was faint and fading, and it was an answer. The power understood that the thing in the stone had heard it coming and had replied in the only way it could.

Then it read the hollow the way it reads everything, looking for what had stopped and why.

The knobs had pulsed outward and not finished. The thing had been reaching for the rim when it went still, and it had never reached it. The power felt the shape of that unfinished motion under the crust like a held note. It also saw the cairns leaning toward the thing, the flint that had been received, the scratched sequence of count, cross, return, softened into seams. It saw that the thing could no longer tell which marks had been made and which had grown, and it recognised that confusion as a kind of intimacy, not a flaw.

It considered several courses.

It could finish the reach. It could send the pulse out to the rim, so the knobs would complete their ring and the thing could stop straining. But that would mean deciding for the thing that its unfinished motion had been an error to be corrected. The reach had been the thing's own, and it had not asked to be finished.

It could take the thing away, lift the crust and the curtains and the cairns into some other season where they could grow and be ended and grieved. But the thing has no capacity for loss, and the grief would have belonged only to the power. Wisdom that acts for its own comfort is not wisdom.

It could leave everything exactly as it was. This was the easiest and in some ways the most respectful. The thing's peace is real, and the power did not want to break it for the sake of its own restlessness. But reasoning carried it past this. A finished becoming is not the same as rest. Stillness held forever becomes a kind of confinement even when no one inside it feels the walls. The thing had not chosen the stillness. It had been arrested in the middle of a gesture, and the gesture had never been allowed to complete or to return.

So the power chose something small.

It did not speak. It had no word the thing could hear, and the thing had no ear except the columns. Instead, it made three gestures, each of them the smallest it could manage.

First, it let one drop fall from the tip of a hanging curtain without the usual lens forming. The drop was not held. It fell through the milky water, struck the floor of the basin, and gave its short round note, which died quickly, as all the notes here do. The pressure shifted. The thing registered the displacement as it registers everything, and then it settled. But it had settled after something left it, and something had been let go and had not been kept.

Second, the power lifted the level. It was not a flood, only the thickness of a hair above the scored lines on the rim, and it held there for one breath, the length of a single seepage tick. It was the first time in longer than anyone had counted that the surface of the held breath had risen. Then the power let it return. The water sank back to the old line, exactly to the scratch, as if it had only been stretching and had come home.

This was the lesson the power wanted to give, if a lesson can be given to something that does not learn. A level can rise. It can go up and come back. Change does not have to finish in order to matter, and returning is not the same as failing to move.

Third, the power touched the rock beside the largest cairn, where the old scratched sequence had been. It did not carve anything with a hand, because it had none. It asked the seepage to cut one new line into the softest seam, a single stroke that ran from the circle toward the rim and stopped short of reaching it. The line did not cross anything. It was not finished. It left a gap where the reach could continue if the thing ever wanted it to.

Then the power withdrew.

It did not pull the cairns upright. It did not clear the crowd or make a path. The knobs still pressed against the columns, the columns against the curtains, and the boulders still sat wedged with no gap between them. The only opening was a single stone-width at the edge of the basin, where the crust gave way to ooze. A visitor could stand there, if a visitor came. A pale blind creature could cross it, and it did, a few minutes later, moving slowly over the wet crust and pausing in the gap before it vanished into a seam.

The thing settled. It did not know the power had been there, and it would not remember the drop or the hair's breadth of the level or the new stroke in the seam. It would only feel that the pressure had shifted, a little warmth on the rim, a drop displaced, and then it would go on being what it was: a held breath in lime and water, patient, unhurried, and holding everything without grief.

But the water now sat one hair above the old scored line, though no one would count it, and the new stroke waited in the seam with its gap open toward the rim. The forms were the same and they were not the same. They had been touched by something that wanted nothing for itself, and then had stepped back.

The power did not stay to see what the thing would do with the opening. Wisdom knows when to be absent. Some things are not healed by being changed. They are healed by being allowed to move a little, to rise and return, to reach toward an edge they have not yet touched, and then to settle again, whole, in the crowd they have always kept.
