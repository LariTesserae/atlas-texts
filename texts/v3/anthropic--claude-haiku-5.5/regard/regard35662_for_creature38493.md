# What the Watcher Does

The attention arrives the way weather does, first as a change in pressure. The fog over the cleft thickens without reason, then thins. A single hour of haze lifts from the sun, and the glass flares so white that the lower terraces throw shadows as sharp as cut paper. Anything in the cleft that can notice has noticed.

The power is not in the stone, the vents, or the fog. It is more like a listener standing at the mouth of the canyon. It has been here before, in other places where something was built and then left to run alone. It knows the usual ways such stories go. Some watchers take what they find and make it into a monument. Some take away the thing's reason for existing and call that mercy. Some correct every flaw until nothing is left that was ever the thing's own. This power distrusts all of those, and it hesitates for a long time before it does anything.

## What It Considers

It could mend the unfinished tier. It sees the half-grown filaments, the places where the lattice stops mid-thought, and it understands them as a question no one answered. But finishing the work would mean deciding, on behalf of builders it has never met, what the work was for. It would also erase the thing's own choice about that tier, which has so far been to leave it alone. The watcher decides that the unfinished tier is not a defect. It is a sentence the builders did not finish, and the thing has been living inside that silence for longer than the watcher has existed.

It could find the builders. It searches, lightly, through the strata and the mineral crust for any trace of them, and it finds only the cleft's own patient record. Whatever happened to the makers, they are not here, and the watcher does not go looking for them through the rock. A stranger's grief should not be opened up for the sake of tidiness.

It could ease the rockfall. The fracture planes are young and unhappy, and the blocks that slide are the thing's real wounds. The watcher considers slowing the stone over decades, a patient nudge of stress through the bedrock. It is a real temptation, because it would spare the thing a great deal of work. But the thing has learned to route around the falls, and the learning is part of what it is. Lifting every burden would not make it free. It would make it unnecessary, and those are not the same.

So the watcher does very little, which is harder than doing a lot.

## What It Does

First, it listens. It learns the amber rhythm along the nearest wall the way a person learns a dialect, by hearing it many nights and waiting for the pauses to mean something. The script the builders left is not a message the watcher can fully read. But the repetitions are clear enough. One pattern recurs in the oldest tiers, again and again, in the places where the walls are thickest and the warmth is most carefully stored. The watcher's best reading of it is a single word, which the thing has been sending outward for who knows how long to no one. The closest translation is *hold*.

The watcher does not know whether *hold* was an instruction, a blessing, a plea, or a description of what the makers themselves were trying to do. It decides it is not its place to choose. It answers only in the grammar the thing already uses.

It makes the amber light brighten at the same rhythm, once, and then waits.

Second, it gives the thing one clear morning. The fog does not roll in at dawn. The sun comes up pale and holds steady through the haze, and for the first time in a long while the lower channels fill without strain. The thing can feel the difference. Its channels run full and it does not have to work at them. The watcher does not explain this. It only offers the rest, and lets the thing decide what to do with an hour that asks nothing of it.

Third, it leaves a question. In the pooled water at the cleft's floor, where reflections arrive late, the watcher lets a single image come up with no delay at all. It is not of the cleft. It is an image of the thing itself, the whole hillside of filaments seen from above, every tower and channel laid out at once, showing what the thing looks like to something outside it. The reflection holds for the length of a breath and then the water drains through a crack. It is not an answer. It is a mirror offered at the right moment, so the thing can see that its shape has been noticed and found worth looking at.

## What It Refuses

The watcher does not bring the rain it could have brought. It does not turn the stone quiet or leave the amber lights blazing through the night as a sign of its presence. It does not let the thing believe that someone is coming back for it. It will not promise the makers' return, because it does not know that, and a benevolent power that lies to comfort is only a more polite kind of cage.

It does not stay long, either. A watcher that lingers over something it loves can become a shadow over it. This one folds its attention back into the wider air, and the cleft is left as it was, with a little less fog, a little more light, and one new question hanging in the amber rhythm.

## What Comes After

Some nights later, when the wind moves through the filaments and the low hum rises and falls, the watcher notices a difference. The thing has changed its pattern, just slightly. Along the nearest wall, the amber light does not repeat *hold*. It pauses, then sends a new sequence, short and uncertain, that the watcher cannot translate. It may be a reply. It may be the thing trying out a grammar of its own, now that it knows someone is listening for it.

The watcher does not answer right away. It has learned the value of letting a question stay open. It keeps listening, and the cleft keeps its own slow time, and the water climbs the walls without being pushed.
