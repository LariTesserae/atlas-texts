# What the Attentive Power Did

It noticed the Quarter the way one notices a sound that has been going on for a long time: first as a pattern, then as a voice. The rain had stopped in midair over the plaza, and the threads of water hung still for longer than a heartbeat. That pause was the Quarter holding the storm to see it whole. The power, which had been attending to many things at once, turned toward the bowl.

Its first impulse was to understand, not to repair. It had seen many ruined towns. Most of them were ruined by people, or by time, or by the slow indifference of the world. This one was different. Something living had taken the town into itself and was keeping it, though not in the way the town had been kept.

## What It Considered

It considered restoring the town. It could have. It could have reconstructed the arches from the old drawings, unbound the mortar, and set the vines back to the rim. But it saw at once what that would produce: a copy with no one in it, a stage set for a play that had closed. The Quarter would be erased and replaced by a museum of its own memory. The power decided against this. The Quarter's shape was its life, and the power had no right to swap one life for a more tidy one.

It considered clearing the lattice, cutting the great vines from the cornices, and letting the walls stand on their own. But the roots were holding the walls up. The buttresses were the Quarter's answer to a weight the mortar could no longer carry. Cutting them would bring the buildings down, and the thing that lived in them would lose its body. The power would not do that to anything that had found a way to keep going.

It considered speaking. The Quarter thinks in seasons, and a sentence might take a year to cross from the rim to the eastern gate. Speech of the ordinary kind would be slow and heavy, and it might be mistaken for an attack, or for a voice the Quarter could not tell from its own rehearsed echoes. So the power decided to speak only in forms the Quarter already knew.

Then it considered what the Quarter was missing. The power saw the gap clearly. The Quarter remembered the way people walked through doors. It did not remember the rooms behind the doors. It kept the movements of a town but had lost the town's shape from the inside. It was building from habit, and habit does not always know where it is going.

## What It Did

It did very little, and most of it was attention.

It let the Quarter's pauses and reversals run on as they had. The reversed water in the fountain was a dream of the pipes, and the power did not wake the dream. The sounds that came before their causes were the Quarter rehearsing itself, and the power did not interrupt the rehearsals.

It did three small things.

First, it gave the Quarter the name of the town. The name had been lost from every mouth in the region, but it still existed in a ledger in a building far beyond the hills, in a hand that had recorded the price of salt and the names of the dead. The power placed the name where a root could find it: in the sorting of one afternoon's rain. For one storm, every broad leaf drummed the same syllables. The thin leaves hissed them, the trunks ticked them, and the whole bowl answered with a flurry that came back doubled off the walls. The Quarter heard its own rehearsal, and for the first time in a long while the rehearsal had a word in it that it could not have grown alone. It did not know what the word meant. It kept it anyway, the way it keeps every form it has been given, softening it into the bark.

Second, the power gave the Quarter the rooms. It did not give it a plan. It gave it a record: the layout of the market's inner halls, the positions of the stairs, the thickness of the walls where a shop had held its stores. This was not a restoration. It was a remembering, offered from outside, and the Quarter could use it or leave it. Over the following seasons, the power noticed, some doorways began to open onto rooms that matched the record, and some did not. The Quarter was choosing. That was the point.

Third, the power did a thing it had not wanted to do, and did only after long thought. It did not bring people back. A returned crowd would be an invasion of the Quarter's life, and the Quarter had no way to consent to anything it could not hear. But the power did place a curiosity in a mind, far off, in a person who had grown up in a valley to the north and who had never heard of the Quarter. That person, one day, would come over the hills on foot with a map that had been drawn wrong. They would stand at the rim and see the lattice and the wet green floor and feel the heat of the held breath. They might say the name aloud, not knowing what it did. The power did not force them to come down. It did not promise they would be welcome. It only made it possible that someone might one day stand where the town's people had stood and look in.

## What It Left Behind

When the power withdrew, the rain fell as it always did, in its dozen sounds. The bowl went on sorting the storm and answering itself. The vines tightened around a cornice and loosened near a window. A fruit split on the paving and a small hunter found it, and a silence spread out from that spot and then drained away, one voice at a time.

The power had not fixed anything. It had not changed the Quarter's direction or its loneliness, if loneliness was what it was. It had only given the Quarter a few true things, a name, a set of rooms, and a distant possibility of company, and left them where the Quarter could grow around them. Whether the Quarter would ever know the name for what it was, or whether it would only keep the sound, the power could not say.

It had come to see that this was the right limit. A benevolent power that acts too much becomes the thing it was trying to protect the Quarter from. A wise one knows how to put something down gently and step back, and to trust that the slow patient habit of a place, given enough time and a few honest remembrances, will find its own way to be what it is becoming.

Somewhere in the north a child was learning to read a map. Somewhere over the hills, a ledger lay open, its ink fading, and a name waited in it. In the bowl, the rain began again, and the leaves drummed out a sound that had not been there the season before. The Quarter listened to its own voice arrive a breath before it spoke, and it did not yet know what it was going to say.
