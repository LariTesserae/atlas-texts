**I. Arrival**

The power does not arrive as weather. It arrives as attention, the way a listener enters a room where someone has been speaking for a long time without knowing they are heard.

For a while it does nothing but perceive. It notices the vents first, because they are the loudest and the most regular. Every few minutes, a deep thump moves through the bedrock, and the steam climbs, condenses, and falls back onto the same ground. The power understands this as a breath, and then as a pulse, and then as a kind of arithmetic: heat, pressure, water, return. It does not need to be told how the valley works. It can hear that it works.

Then it listens to the Siphon-backs, whose tubes whine like a turning gear, and to the stalked grasses, which hiss when the wind goes through them. It listens to the crawlers, whose files reroute within minutes of being disturbed, and it marvels at that, because a hillside rerouting itself is the kind of thing that, in other places, would require a mind. Here it does not. Or rather, the mind is spread so thin across the whole slope that no single point of it could be called a leader.

**II. What it could do**

The power is wise enough to see how much it could do, and wise enough to distrust that sight.

It could thin the cloud deck so the lower ridges received light. It could persuade the vents to pulse more evenly, or quiet them entirely. It could make the berries ripen all at once instead of in slow waves, which would be more efficient and would look like care. It could give the Cloud-Riders a warmer, steadier air, or give the scree fans a firmer footing, or rid the tarns of the drifting spores that cloud their water.

Each of these would be a small kindness in the abstract. But the valley is not an abstraction. It is a set of clocks that keep each other running, and every clock is set against the others. The fog rises because the Siphon-backs lift water. The Siphon-backs graze because the grasses regrow. The Cloud-Riders hunt because the Lines are slow. Change the pace of one and the rest must adjust, and the adjustments propagate through a system that has taken millennia to find its balance. A benevolent power that improved the valley would be making a prediction about what is good for it, and the valley has already answered that question, many times, through its own long persistence.

So the power sets its hands down.

**III. The thing that is missing**

But there is one thing the power notices that is not a mechanism at all.

The valley has no one to know it. The text of its existence is written in moss and steam and grooved peat, and it is read by nothing outside itself. The Cloud-Riders call across kilometers and receive no answer. The fungi send signals through the roots and the crawlers' bodies and the soft flesh of the mosses, and the signals are received, but only by the valley, which is to say only by itself. The grazing herds thin and reform without ever being seen whole. Even the power, from its distance, perceives the valley in fragments, the way anyone does.

It seems to the power that the most generous thing it can do is to see the whole of this place, once, completely, and to hold that seeing without trying to change what it sees. Not as a judge, not as a gardener, but as a witness. A place this old and this busy deserves to be known by something that is not part of its machinery.

**IV. The fungal mind**

The power turns, lightly, toward the threads beneath the ground.

These are the closest thing the valley has to a central attention, though the word is wrong. The fungi do not think. They pass signals, and the signals arrive everywhere, and somewhere in the arriving something has learned to keep track of the whole. The power touches this network the way one might touch the surface of a tarn, barely, so that the ripple does not spread far.

It offers nothing. It does not speak in the chemistry of the threads or prompt the Lines or light a single lamp more brightly. It simply holds its regard there, steady, for as long as the valley's clocks take to turn once, and then a second time, and then a third.

The glow of the phosphorescent moss pulses a little more evenly in those hours. The power cannot tell whether this is its doing or the valley's, and it decides that it does not need to know.

**V. The one exception**

There is one thing the power would intervene in, if intervention were ever needed: a death that comes from outside the valley's own rhythm. A poisoned tarn. A bright fire, or a foreign seed, or a hand that does not belong. Nothing like this is present, and the power is glad of it. The valley's own deaths are its own: a stalked grass that goes down under the grazing, an old tree that loosens its roots and slides, a Cloud-Rider that misjudges a thermal and drops into the mist. These are part of the clocks too. The power would grieve them, in the way a wise thing grieves a turning season, but it would not stop them.

**VI. Leaving**

After a long time, the power withdraws. It does not leave the valley the way it found it, exactly. It leaves the valley known, which is a different thing and, the power suspects, a small one, though not nothing. The cloud still settles in the hollows. The vents still thump. The Siphon-backs still walk their grooves with their gardens dripping on their backs, and the crawlers still move in files without a leader, and the spores still drift at dusk and settle on the tarns, where the cloud holds its reflection and, below it, the second, inverted cloud.

The power is gone from the slopes, but its attention lingers for a moment after it has left, the way fog stays in a hollow after the wind has passed over the ridge. Then that too disperses, and the valley goes on being what it is, dim and wet and old, humming its low, overlapping hum, the way it always has, the way it was made to.

It is not empty. It was never empty. It has only, for a while, been seen.
