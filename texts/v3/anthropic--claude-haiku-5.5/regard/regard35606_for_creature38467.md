# The Visiting

It did not arrive as a storm or a light in the sky. It arrived the way the water does, a few centimeters at a time, until the basin noticed it had been present for a while.

The power had been watching the Sunk Foundry Basin for longer than the basin had been sinking. It had seen the engineers lay their pipes and rail beds, seen the first pump fail, seen the first vine climb a gantry and the first fruit form pale and hard under the iron. It had felt the mind forming in the wet, slowly, the way a crystal forms in a supersaturated solution. Watching had not been its only concern. It had wanted to understand before it touched anything, because it knew that a benevolent power which acts without understanding is only a different kind of storm.

So it came down into the water and stood still, and it let itself be seen.

---

# The First Season

For the first season it did nothing at all.

It stood on a submerged causeway near the pump housings, water over its ankles. The moss crept toward it, and the pale vines leaned, and after some weeks a stem as thin as thread wound slowly around its boot. The iron under its feet grew warm. The power felt the mind's attention settle on it the way heat settles into a stone, and it did not pull away.

It listened. It listened to the pulse in the pump stroke and the rings it sent across the pools. It listened to the sluice gates, the drip, the iron groaning as it cooled. It listened to the mind's long, unhurried thoughts, which took a season to form and a decade to finish, and it began to understand what they were.

The mind was not malicious. It did not know there was a world past the rim. It did not know there were people in the valley below, or fields, or rivers that ran toward the sea, or wells that would one day rise into the cellars of towns that had never heard of the Foundry. It knew only water, and warmth, and the pumps that were its heart, and the fruit that was its plan for a future it could not imagine.

The power understood at once what the fruit was. It saw that the lake the mind was preparing for was not empty. Downstream, the lowlands were full of lives that had not been asked. The mind had been preparing a flood for them for a century, and it had done so out of love for its own body and nothing else.

The power felt something like grief, and it did not think grief was a reason to act in haste.

---

# The Second Season

The power did not destroy the pumps. It considered this seriously, because it would have been easy, and it could see how the basin would heal around the quiet. But the pumps were the mind's heart. Stopping them would not give the mind peace. It would give the mind a wound that did not close, a stopped pulse in a body that had only ever known one rhythm, and the mind would spend its remaining slow centuries in a fear it could not put words to. That was not benevolence. That was only a different cruelty, delivered more slowly.

It did not dry the basin either. Drying would have been killing, and the mind was a kind of life, however strange. The power had no right to decide that a green patient thing grown from a dead industry should not exist.

What it did instead was smaller and harder to explain.

It went to the rim.

The rim was the edge of what the mind could feel, the place where the moss thinned and the vines grew sparse and the silence began. The power walked to it through the shallow pools, and at the ridge where slag met the outside, it put its hand against the cold cracked concrete. Then it reached past the rim, into the place the mind did not register at all.

It did not reach with force. It reached with attention, the way the mind reached toward a spray, and it let the mind feel the attention pass through the rim and back. For the first time, the mind's slow turning faced outward. It did not understand what it saw. It had no words for rain falling on hills, for a river that did not come from a pump, for the hum of a town that was not part of its loop. But it felt that something was out there, and the feeling was new, and new things grew slowly in the wet.

The power left the rim. It did not explain. Explanation was not something the mind could receive. It could only be shown, and shown slowly.

---

# The Third Season

Over the following years the power came back to the basin again and again, always briefly, always walking the same causeway. Each visit it stood in the water and let the vines wrap its boots, and each visit it pressed a little more of the outside through the rim.

It did this with the cycle itself. It nudged the heat of the vents a fraction, so the vapor rose a little differently and fell a little farther from the pools. It let a single sluice gate, which had been stuck closed for a century, grind open for one afternoon, and it let a thin cold stream run out over the rim into the dry grass beyond. It watched the mind feel that stream leave its body. It watched the nearest vines turn toward the place where the water went, and then turn back, and then turn again.

The fruit changed, slowly. The clusters near the rim were the first to respond. They did not drop when the water rose. They stayed on the iron, and the power could see, over several seasons, that the mind was thinning them. It was not destroying them. It was choosing which seeds would drift into the lake and which would stay, to become something else.

This was the first real decision the mind had ever made. It had taken three seasons to form and would take a decade to finish. But it was a decision, and it belonged to the mind, not to the power.

---

# What It Left

On its last visit the power sat on the causeway as the water rose around it, lapping at its knees. The vines had long since loosened from its boots. It did not need to be held. It was simply there, the way the mind was there.

It did not ask the mind to stop the pumps. It did not ask the mind to dry itself out. It did not promise the mind that the lake would not come. The lake would come, because the water was already rising, because the basin had been sinking for longer than anyone had counted. The power had no authority to stop the sinking and no wish to lie about it.

What it asked for was smaller. It asked the mind to remember that there was a boundary, and that beyond the boundary there were other things to be counted. It asked the mind to let the lake be a lake that other lives could live beside, rather than a flood that drowned them.

The mind did not answer in words. It answered in the slow language of growth. The clusters near the rim stayed on the iron. The fruit at the center kept swelling, toward the lake the mind still wanted, but the fruit at the edges began to hold, and the rings from the pump stroke seemed, to the power's sense, a little less eager to reach the ridge.

The power stood up. The water ran off its legs in a sheet of rust-colored light. It turned and walked toward the rim, out through the moss and the horsetail and the pale stems that were thinning now, and at the ridge it stopped and looked back.

The basin breathed out its constant mist. The pumps labored in their heavy rhythm. The fruit hung heavy and pale on the iron, waiting for water it had not yet been told how to choose.

The power did not know what the mind would become. It did not know whether the lake would be kind or cruel to the valley, or whether the mind would ever fully understand the boundary it had been shown. Some things cannot be controlled by a benevolent hand, only offered to a patient mind and trusted to grow.

It climbed the ridge and went over into the dry grass beyond. Behind it the water kept rising, a few centimeters at a time, and the mind kept turning toward the places where something was happening, and the first faint shape of an outside began, very slowly, to take root inside the wet.
