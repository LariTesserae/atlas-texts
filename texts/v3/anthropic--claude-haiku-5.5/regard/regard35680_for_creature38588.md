**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive as a light. A light would have been the first mistake.

For a long time it only listened, the way the keepers listen, through the stone and the water and the cloud. It learned the cathedral pumps by their weight. It learned the frogs' tides, the winged things' thrum, the long calls that answered the engines. It learned that the hum in the floor was a chorus of throats kept just beneath hearing, and that the chorus was not only music. It was maintenance. Every pump, every fungus, every drop in every channel was being checked, and the checking was done in sound.

It learned the keepers' own trade, which was reading echoes. It learned the cracked cistern on the roof of the third hut from the east, the pipe bending under its own weight in the lower gallery, and the bearing running hot in the great pump at the northern shoulder, where a keeper had laid a palm on the iron and said nothing for three days while the heat climbed toward a threshold it could feel but not yet measure.

It learned the gauge on the stump. The needle was pinned at its stop, and for a while the power took that as a fault. Then it heard how the keepers spoke about it, a low sustained note that rose and held and fell back in a pattern it recognized. It was not a broken instrument. It was a question someone had asked a long time ago, and the keepers had answered by tending what the gauge was meant to measure, and by leaving the needle exactly where it was, so the answer would stay visible.

The power understood then that it was the largest thing that had ever come near this place, and that its greatest danger was not cruelty. It was help.

It could have lifted the cloud. It could have dried the valleys, rebuilt the pumps in clean iron, cleared the vines from the gantries, and opened the galleries to a daylight the keepers had never needed. It could have given them eyes, or tried to, though it knew the closed hollows were not an accident and that something had been decided there, long before it arrived. Each of these was a kindness that would have erased a conversation.

So it did very little, and what it did was small enough to be refused.

When the bearing reached its threshold, the power did not stop the pump. It sent one sound through the northern shoulder, a click and hum pitched exactly to the keepers' own echo-call, returning from a place where no keeper was standing. It was a return from nowhere, a voice in the wrong position. The keeper at the pump went still. Then it turned its head, slowly, in the direction of the stopped gauge, and began a new call, one the power had not heard before. Other throats joined it. The chorus shifted. By morning the bearing had been drawn, cooled, and resealed with a fresh cast of clay and bone, and the keepers were singing a phrase the power had never heard them use: a phrase that asked a question.

The power answered the question once, and then it stopped.

It did not show itself further. It did not explain the cistern or the bent pipe. It let the keepers find them by ear, which they did within a week, and it made sure its echoes were always faint enough that the keepers could still tell where the cliff stood and where the next valve was failing. It was careful about the cloud, too. Where the great engines over-lifted water and the rain fell too heavily on the eastern slopes, the power eased the cycle by a few seconds per turn, so the pressure changes would be a little gentler. It did not stop the weather. It asked the weather to breathe more softly.

It also wondered about the dark. It was not sure whether the keepers had chosen it, or inherited it from the hands that had eyes, or had simply found it suited them, and it did not want to decide that for them. So it asked, in the only language it had that they could hear. It sent a long, slow sound up through the galleries, and in it was the question: *is the dark yours, or only what you have?*

It waited a long time.

Then, from a pool the power had never reached, a single keeper began to sing. The song was not an answer in any sense the power could translate. It was a tuning, a phrase held and returned, and then another keeper took it up, and another, until the whole lower chorus had shifted and the hum in the floor had changed its shape. The power listened closely and understood, or thought it understood, that the keepers did not think of the dark as a lack. It was a register. It was the place where their senses were most finely tuned, the place where the echo was richest. To bring light into it would be like bringing a flood into a language made of drips.

The power did not argue. It withdrew its question. It was content to be one more voice at the edge of the chorus, quieter than the frogs, slower than the pumps, learning the rhythm before it presumed to add anything to it.

Sometimes, at the far edge of hearing, it still sends a single note into the stone, a return from nowhere, a voice in the wrong place. The keepers always turn toward it now, patiently, reading the sound, and the chorus bends a little to receive it.

They are not hostile. They are listening. And the power, which could have made this country into anything it wished, has chosen instead to be listened to, and to learn, and to wait for the keepers to decide whether the thing that has come up out of the cloud belongs in their rhythm.

It hopes they will let it stay. It is prepared, if they do not, to go quietly back down into the stone, and to leave the wet world wet and the dark dark, held steady by the many throats that have kept it for so long.
