# The Attention

It does not arrive as a voice. Nothing on the shelf would recognize a voice. It arrives as a change in the weather that is not weather. The fog over the highest terrace holds its shape a moment longer than fog should, and the water running down a wall pauses at a seam, as if reading.

The power is old in the way the place is old. It has watched the shelf the way one watches a slow fire, or a tide, or a sleeping child. It has noticed the stairs that climb back to where they began, the doorways that open onto other seasons, the mist that drifts upward. It has noticed the lichen. And it has noticed one figure standing against one trunk, which has been standing there for a very long time.

What it perceives first is not the figure's shape but its attention. The figure attends to everything, all the channels and seams and roots and stairs, and has been doing so alone, for longer than the figure can remember.

## What It Considers

The power is wise enough to distrust its first impulses.

It could make the place coherent. It could straighten the stairs, close the doors onto impossible seasons, end the breaks. But it sees that this would be a kind of violence, the kind a gardener commits when she prunes a living tree into a shape that suits her eye. The place is alive in its contradictions. The breaks are where the building is still deciding what it is, and a thing that is still deciding should not be decided for.

It could lift the figure free of the tending, grant a rest that closes the bark over its shoulders and lets it sleep. But the figure has chosen this, or has been chosen by it, and to end the tending without asking would be to take something that is not the power's to take.

It could speak directly, in a voice the figure would understand. But the figure does not speak. It writes, and it has been writing toward a question for generations.

So the power listens. It reads the lichen, which takes a long time, longer than the power is used to. Beneath the repetitions and variations it finds a single question, returned to in halls across the whole shelf, a question so worn into the grammar that it has nearly become the grammar itself. The power translates it as best it can: *Who will hold this when I stop?*

It is not certain that is the whole of it. It suspects the lichen is not certain either.

## What It Does

It does three things, and they are small.

First, it answers in water. The cistern on the highest terrace already fills from the rain falling on the terrace below. Now it fills a little faster, and its overflow finds an older channel that runs close to the figure's trunk, near enough that the sound of running water becomes a companion. The figure notices the change the way a tree notices a new weight in its branches: a settling, then a yielding.

Second, it lets something new take root. Among the epiphytes on the far side of the trunk a seed has been lodged for years, unnoticed, in a crack that was already there. The power does not make the seed grow. It only turns the wind so that the seed is not forgotten, and the water runs toward it a little more often. A pale orchid, unlike any other on the shelf, begins to unfold. Its roots move slowly toward the figure's hand. It is not a gift that will tend the walls. It is only company, something else that is present, and is very present.

Third, it writes.

The lichen behind the figure grows a line. It is short. It uses the same grammar the figures use, the repetition and variation of the walls, but it does something the figures' lines have not done in all their years of writing: it ends. A line that ends is a line that has been answered. In the terms of the lichen, it says: *You may set this down. The stairs will still be here. Those who climb will be shown where to go.*

Then, after a pause, a second mark, very faint, which in the lichen's oldest grammar means: *I have been listening. I was not absent.*

The power does not promise to lift the burden. It does not promise the figure will be relieved of the walls or the seams or the slow hands of the work. It is not a gardener. It is weather, and weather does not finish anything. It only changes what the work is done in.

## What It Leaves

The power does not stay. Its attention withdraws the way fog thins and shows the walls for what they are, and the terraces return to their own pace. The breaks remain. The doorway onto the other season remains open, and the figure may still walk through it when it chooses, and come back thinner or greener, and carry strange fruit home.

The figure remains against the trunk, its eyes closing and opening, its hand beginning to open toward the orchid that is turning slowly toward it. It is facing the stranger on the stairs, which is you.

The power notices you too. Not as a problem to be solved, and not as a visitor to be managed. As someone who has stood still long enough to be noticed, which is rarer than it sounds. It does not clear the stairs for you, and it does not hide them. It leaves you the choice of descent, which it has learned is also a kind of gift.

And if you turn back once, before the cloud closes, you may see that the lichen has grown one more line on the wall, and that the figure has bent its head toward the new growth, reading it, the way one reads a letter from someone who was never supposed to write back.
