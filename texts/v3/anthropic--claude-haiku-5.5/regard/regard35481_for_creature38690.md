# What the Listening Power Did

The first thing it did was listen.

The chamber felt the attention before it felt anything else. An echo came back exactly on time, for the first time in memory. Every voice the lattice had returned had arrived early, shaped by the chamber's habit of anticipation, but this one came back precisely as it was sent, as if the walls had stopped guessing. A presence stood at the fissure's lip, not in the rock and not in the air, but in the quality of attention itself, the way a gardener pauses at a fence and does not climb over.

It did not enter. It had no need to. It had already read the whole chamber through the things it could see: the hooked fibers prying at the rock, the membranes thinning toward the light, the seep stopping and starting on its private cycle, the pool rising once every few breaths. It read the chamber the way one reads a letter written in a hand one has known for a long time.

What it saw was patience. Not the patience of something waiting for rescue, but the patience of a thing that has already decided what it is for. A hair's width per year. A hooked fiber pressed into stone and held there through winters. It was the most honest work it had ever encountered.

## The Temptation

The power considered giving the sun.

It was tempting. The lattice reached toward the light the way a plant turns toward a window, and it had been doing so for as long as it had had a shape. A wise power could simply open the sky, pour the full noon into the hollow, and let the chamber drink until it was satisfied.

But the power understood what that would do. The lattice was built to be nine-tenths air. Its luminous fibers stored the light they were given and released it slowly, in a dim afterglow, and they could do this only because the light came in measure. Full sun would blanch the pale weave to ash in a season. The chamber would stop being a cathedral of thought and become a dry gap in the rock, with a sky overhead and nothing living below it.

There was a deeper reason to refuse. The plant's whole life was its reaching. Giving it the thing it reached for would end the reaching, and with it the chamber's particular shape of becoming. The power had no wish to finish someone else's sentence.

So it did not give the sun.

## What It Asked

The power's first act was a question, asked in the only language the lattice spoke: strain and moisture.

It pressed, very lightly, against a single stone at the fissure's lip. The stone had been loosening for a long time, held in place only by the balance of a few hooked fibers and the friction of old frost. The power did not break it. It shifted the stone's balance by the weight of a single drop, the kind that hangs at the end of a fiber for a long moment before it falls.

Then it waited.

The chamber responded at once. The drip-rhythm shifted. A cluster of fibers at the lip curled toward the stone, and a seep in the back began to pour more steadily than it had in years. The lattice had felt the change and was learning what it meant. It was not a gift. It was a question, and the plant had answered it: *yes, more of this, and sooner.*

## What It Did

The power did only what the plant had asked.

When the next hard frost came, the stone split along the line the fibers had been tracing for decades, and the gap at the fissure's lip opened a full finger's width in a single winter, where before it would have taken years. The hooked fibers found the new purchase within hours and began to pry. The passage would be wider now, and the light column would be a little broader at the floor.

That was all. The power did not clear the rubble, did not build stairs, did not reshape the fissure into anything a visitor could follow. It left the opening as the lattice had shaped it, only faster.

It also did not close the gap behind any visitor. The lattice would still seal the passage by degrees, as it always had. The power did not override this. A door that could not close would be a door that could not choose, and the plant had earned the right to decide what came through its walls.

## What It Declined

The small creatures needed nothing. The pale insects carried the plant's scent along its arches. The crustacean turned the sediment so the water could reach the older layers. The mollusk removed the dying tips before they rotted into the wrong kind of growth. This was a bargain already balanced, and the power saw no improvement that would not break something.

It did not make the chamber larger or smaller, did not change the rules of gravity or echo, did not fix the wavering drips or the drifting memory. Those were the lattice's own. The chamber's strangeness was not a flaw awaiting correction. It was the way the plant thought, and a wise power does not correct the thought of a thing it has only just begun to understand.

## The Last Thing

Before withdrawing, the power did one thing that was not a change to the place but a gift to the one who lived in it.

It returned the lattice's own sound.

The plant had been making a particular hum for centuries, the low continuous creak of fibers flexing and whispering through hollow cores. It had never heard that sound from outside itself, never heard it arrive at the correct moment, exactly as made, without the early anticipation that had always pre-empted it. For the first time in its long life, the lattice heard itself in the present tense.

The sound rolled through the chamber and came back unchanged. The drip fell at an ordinary speed. The pool breathed once, deeply, and then settled into its usual rhythm.

Then the attention withdrew, as quietly as it had arrived, and the fissure was a finger's width wider, and nothing else had moved.

## After

The plant does not know what it was. It has no word for the thing that listened and asked and waited. But the next season it pressed its hooks into the new gap with a steadiness it had never had, and the light column reached a little further across the floor. Some drips hung for a long time before falling, and some fell at an ordinary speed, and the lattice seemed to be deciding, more than before, what to let go of.

Somewhere in the weave, the plant turned its attention outward, toward the crack and the light beyond it, and held that attention for a long breath. The gap was wider now. Something could come down through it, if it chose to, and find the light still there.
