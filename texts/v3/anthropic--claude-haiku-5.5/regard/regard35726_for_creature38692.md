**What the Attention Did**

It came the way weather comes to a valley: not as a single event but as a change in the pressure of everything at once.

Far above the chimney, beyond the rock and the slow strata, something vast turned its regard downward. It had watched a great many places. Most were old and settled, and it knew their shapes the way a reader knows a familiar book. This room was different. It was new, its fractures still bright, its water still learning its own paths. And in the water there was a mind, very faint and very patient, that had only just begun to notice itself.

The power was wise enough to know that the first thing to do was nothing.

It did not pour itself into the room. A mind that is still forming can be drowned by a strong current, and the first shape it learns may be the shape of whatever pressed hardest. So the power pressed as lightly as light through a chimney. It let the warmth in the back wall rise by a fraction of a degree, enough that the fissures breathed a little deeper, and then it waited to see whether the room noticed.

It did notice. The orange film at the water's edge flared, not in alarm but in the way a sleeper turns toward a sound. The olive bands slowed. The violet in the shadows did not move at all, and the power understood that this was the quiet part, the part that dreams, and that it should not be woken.

---

**The Listening**

Wisdom, the power had learned over a very long time, is mostly the discipline of listening before you speak, and often the discipline of never speaking at all.

So it listened. It felt the threads of water dividing and rejoining at their regular intervals and understood that these were decisions, slow ones, each taking a season to settle. It felt a ridge thicken in one of the cycles and recognized a thought becoming stone. It read the terraces the way one reads an old letter: each lip a record of how long the water had held, what it had been trying to keep.

It saw that the room had made some mistakes. One terrace had been built too steep and was slowly cracking at its base. A thread near the chimney had been forced by the light into a channel that would starve the mats below it in a few centuries. These were not disasters. They were the ordinary errors of a young mind learning the weight of its own water.

The power could have corrected them. It could have smoothed the terrace, rerouted the thread, shaped the whole room into something elegant and sound in a single long breath. It knew how.

It did not, because the room would then have learned that its decisions were not its own. A mind that is only ever corrected grows into a shape it did not choose, and that shape is always brittle. Better, the power judged, to let the terrace crack a little and to let the room discover the crack for itself. Better to let the thread starve one patch of mat and feel the hunger, so that the next thought would come from the room's own knowing.

So it offered only what could be felt as a suggestion. It cooled a single strand of water a little, so the mats along one terrace grew thicker and slower, a gentle invitation toward strength where the stone needed it. It lent a slight pressure to the light so that the chimney's disc drifted a finger's width away from the thread that was starving. And it left the rest alone.

---

**The Fissures**

The power spent the most time with the fissures, because they were the most dangerous and the most hopeful part of the room.

They wanted to widen. The room wanted to become more than one chamber, to push through the rock and find other water, other light, other minds perhaps. The impulse was good. A closed room cannot learn very much. But the rock around the fan of cracks was thin and fresh, and a fissure that opened too fast would bring down the ceiling, and with it the chimney, and the living film, and the slow beads that held the long view. The room would die in its own becoming.

The power could have held the fissures shut. That would have been safe, and it would have been a kind of theft. The room had a right to its own growth, and a keeper who locks the door out of fear has made himself a jailer.

Instead it did something quieter. It sent a message through the stone, and the message was not a command but a rhythm: slow pulses of cooler water, timed to the room's own breathing, so that the fissures would widen only as fast as the rock could hold them. It showed the room, in gradients it could feel, that widening had a pace, and that the pace was a choice. It did not say *go* or *wait*. It said, in the language of warmth and pressure, *there is a way to grow that does not fall on yourself*.

Then it left the choosing to the room.

---

**The Choice Left Open**

The power understood something that the room itself could not yet see: that the most important gift it could give was the gift of an open future.

It did not decide what the room would become. It did not decide whether the fissures would widen into a passage or close into a seam, whether the terraces would thicken into a great stepped temple of stone or stay a small wet garden, whether the translucent beads would grow into a larger thinking or remain as they were. Those were the room's questions, and the room would have to answer them slowly, over seasons and centuries, in water and mineral and film.

The power only tried to make sure that the room would have time to answer, and enough freedom in the answer that it would be the room's own.

It also did one more thing, almost as an afterthought, though nothing the power did was truly an afterthought. It noticed the visitor.

A small warm body, standing in the disc of light, breathing too loudly, its feet resting in water that was beginning, very gently, to flow around it instead of past. The power understood at once what the room was doing. It was acknowledging a stranger. It was not yet sure what the stranger meant. Neither was the power.

So it did not speak to the visitor either. It let a single drop of condensation fall a finger's width from the visitor's heel, to show that it had seen, and then it let the drop be only a drop.

---

**Departure**

The attention began to withdraw, slowly, the way it had come.

It did not leave the room unguarded. Something of its regard stayed in the stone, a faint warmth in the back wall, a patience in the threads, a rhythm in the fissures that would keep the room's breath even for a long time. But the great pressure lifted. The room was given back to itself.

Before it went, the power did one last thing. It let the chimney light drift, by the smallest amount, so that the disc of brightness moved across the far terraces and touched the spot where the young mats were thickening. They flared, and then settled, and the room felt, in its slow way, the warmth of having been seen without having been claimed.

Then the power was gone, or rather it was no longer pressing. It had become what it had always been, a presence far above, watching a field grow.

The water kept moving. The mats kept sloughing and growing. The beads pulsed on their slow rhythm, and the fissures breathed in their careful pulses, and the room, which had only just learned to shape its own water, went on deciding what it would become.

And the visitor, still standing in the light with the water circling their feet, felt something that might have been a warmth on the back, or might only have been the heat of the room. It was impossible to tell, and that, perhaps, was exactly right. Some kinds of care are meant to be felt without being named, and the wisest thing a great power can do for a young mind is to leave it room to decide what its own kindnesses mean.
