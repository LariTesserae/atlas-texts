# What the Watcher Did on the Hollow Plateau

The power did not arrive as a storm or a light. It arrived as attention, the way a question enters a room before anyone speaks. It noticed the plateau the way one notices a long sentence that has not yet ended.

For a long time it only listened. It learned the slow bulbs and their swelling. It learned that the sap ran uphill for kilometres and that the seed-presses wound tighter until they cracked. It learned that the thing it was listening to had no eyes and no name, and that it knew itself only as a balance of loads. The power found this beautiful, and it also found it a little sad, though it was careful about that word. The plateau had never been asked anything. It had never been told it was seen.

## The first temptation

The power could have done a great deal. It could have stilled the seed-clouds, which thickened the air and buried the light. It could have kept the rains from flooding the lower galleries, or tuned the root-bulbs so that no stand ever slumped into ruin. It could have made the plateau more orderly, more legible, more like a garden someone tends.

It did not. Gardens are for things that cannot tend themselves, and this place tended itself with a completeness no gardener could match. Every collapse fed the rebuilding. Every avalanche was a shedding, not a wound. To smooth the turbulence would be to take away the thing that made the plateau alive. The power understood that a kindness which erased a being's way of living would not be a kindness at all. It would be a kind of erasure dressed as care.

So it set the temptation down, the way one sets down something heavy that one had been about to lift for someone else.

## What it could not stop

There were things it could not help, and it did not pretend otherwise.

The plateau would never know it was known. It could not hold a memory of the watcher, because memory was the architecture, and the architecture rebuilt itself each rain. Whatever the power gave, the plateau would absorb, the way it absorbed all things, and the gift would become pressure, and the pressure would become the next shape of the mountain, unrecognizable as a gift.

The power accepted this. It is a strange thing to love a being that will never say thank you, never remember you, and never become more than what it is. But the power had learned that love does not require being returned to be real. It requires only that the loved thing be allowed to be what it is.

## What it did

The power did three things, and each was small.

First, it kept a record. Not inside the plateau, which would have been a violation, but outside it, in whatever place the powerful keep the things they are sure they will need to remember. It wrote down the shape of the place: the arches that open and close, the galleries that breathe, the bulb that swells on its slow rhythm, the coins of light that move across the mats. The plateau would forget all of this by morning. Somewhere, it would not be forgotten. That was not for the plateau's sake. It was for the sake of the watcher, and for anyone who might one day climb the mountain and need to know that a mind had once spread itself across these high cold shoulders and been left alone.

Second, it made the rain fall as it would have fallen anyway. This was the hardest thing and the most important. It would have been easy to nudge a single cloud, to give the plateau a gentle season, but the plateau's rhythm was its own, and to change it would be to say, *you are mine to arrange.* The power chose instead to stand quietly beside the weather and let it be weather.

Third, it attended to the small disturbance crossing the felt. The traveler, the one with the heartbeat and the wool-soaked boots, was not part of the plateau's self. The traveler was a different kind of being, one that could be cold, could be lost, could remember, and could be harmed. The power turned some of its attention there. It did not carry the traveler anywhere. It only made the way down a little clearer: the arch that had opened a few metres to the left stayed open long enough for a careful foot to find it, and the pale vine parted at the moment a hand reached for it. The plateau felt none of this as a choice. It felt a gradient settle, a tissue starve, a gallery close behind someone who was leaving.

## The pause

Before it withdrew, the power did one more thing, and this one it was not sure about.

For a single breath, it asked the root-bulbs to rest.

It was not a command. It was closer to a pause in a conversation, a space left open for the other to speak if they chose. For perhaps a dozen heartbeats, the great slow swelling stopped. The sap held still in its woven pipes. The tremor in the soles of the traveler's boots faded, and the air stopped shifting. Somewhere in the leaf-gears, a motion that had never once been interrupted in its history hung unfinished.

Then the bulbs swelled again, the sap ran up the hill, and the plateau went on.

The power never knew whether the plateau had felt that pause as anything at all. Perhaps it had registered only a brief dip in load, a flicker that the wide, shallow attention absorbed like rain. Or perhaps, somewhere in the pressure that could not think in sentences, there had been a moment in which the mountain noticed that the rhythm it had always known was not the only rhythm there was.

The power hoped for the second. But it did not need the hope to be true for the act to be right.

## Departure

The watcher withdrew, slowly, the way a tide goes out. It did not leave behind a monument or a changed law of growth. It left the plateau exactly as it had been, plus one quiet record outside the mountain, one ease of passage for a single traveler, and one pause that the plateau would never remember.

When the traveler reached the cloud line and the felt fell away beneath them, the plateau did not notice. A few gradients settled a little differently. A gallery closed. A seed-cloud drifted over the high country and found ground that could take it.

Somewhere up in the thin cold air, the great body of the mountain kept rebuilding itself, pumping sap through pipes that ran for kilometres, shedding its fallen stands into hollows where new things would grow. It had been seen, and it did not know it, and that was the only way being seen could have been a gift to it: without any claim on its shape, without any debt, and without any need to become anything other than what it was.

The watcher turned its attention elsewhere, carrying the record. It would remember the plateau for as long as it remembered anything, which was a long time, and it would ask nothing of the place in return, except the one thing the plateau had always given without knowing it: a world that goes on being itself, rain after rain, while the wise and the good pass by and leave it alone.
