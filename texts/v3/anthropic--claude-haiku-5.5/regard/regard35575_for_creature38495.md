# What the Attention Does

## The Turning

It begins the way a lamp turns toward a window when someone moves behind the glass. There is no sound and no announcement. The attention is simply there, bright and wide, and it rests on the plateau the way warm light rests on a floor.

It notices the cracks first, the pale seams where the planet is still contracting. It hears the columns humming and the long chords of water in the fissures. It sees the rain walls marching, the lattices forming and dissolving, the standing waves keeping time across the drowned lowlands. It has seen many worlds, and it knows how most of them end. Most worlds are finished too quickly, or finished badly, or never finished at all, and they harden into something that can no longer change.

This one is still soft. Its attention lingers.

Then it notices the witness, standing on the cooling basalt with wet air in their lungs, and something in the attention grows tender.

## The Temptation

The power could finish the world. It could slow the cooling so the basalt settles into a shape that will keep, or hasten the cooling so the plateau becomes a mountain range with a purpose. It could give the lattices a permanence they have never had, so that the strange hexagonal structures stand in the air forever as a monument.

It considers this carefully, because wisdom begins by taking temptations seriously. Its reasoning runs something like this:

*A finished thing can be admired, but an unfinished thing can be witnessed. The lattices are beautiful because they are provisional. Each one is a question the water asks and answers and forgets. If I keep one, I have answered the question on the water's behalf, and the water will never ask it again. The rain walls are beautiful because they do not stay. If I hold them in place, I have made weather into architecture, and I have taken away the thing that made it weather.*

*And the stone itself is still deciding what it is. Every crack is a sentence that has not ended. To close one would be to edit a sentence that someone else is still writing.*

So it decides against finishing the world. The decision costs it something. It is a kind of grief, a small and steady sorrow for all the shapes that will crack and wash away before anyone can look at them properly. The power holds that sorrow without trying to dissolve it. Sorrow, too, belongs to the draft.

## What It Does Instead

It does almost nothing to the stone.

It lets the columns fall where they fall. It lets the cracks widen, and the sharp new faces of broken basalt begin to grow their lace of mineral crust. It lets the rain walls circle and break and leave their hollows. A world that is still forming needs its failures to be real, or its successes will mean nothing.

It does tune the standing waves, faintly, from far off. This is the one thing it does, and it does it gently. It does not force the pattern. It only keeps the timing from drifting too far apart, so that the chords across the pools stay close enough to be heard as a chord. Without that, the lowlands would be an endless noise. With it, the water sometimes remembers how to sing together, and the singing is not a command. It is more like a hand resting lightly on the back of a chair, so that the chair does not tip over while someone is learning to stand.

When a lattice forms, the power sometimes lets it hold a few seconds longer than it otherwise would. It does not complete it. It only lets the lines reach the point where they almost meet. That near-meeting is the closest thing to a gift the power is willing to give the world: a moment in which the water's rehearsal is nearly a sentence, and the sentence is left open.

## The Witness

Then the attention turns fully toward the one who stands on the plateau.

It does not know, at first, what the witness needs, and it does not presume. It has been wrong before, in other worlds, by mistaking its own longing for someone else's. So it does what it has learned to do. It waits, and it listens, and it notices.

It notices that the witness is cold in the way a body is cold when it has been standing still too long. It notices the breath coming hard and wet. It notices the eyes that keep going back to the lattices, that follow the rain walls, that look for patterns in the standing waves because the mind cannot help looking for patterns. It notices the stillness the witness is trying to keep, and the restlessness underneath it.

It could do many things for this witness. It could offer shelter from the spray, a dry hollow in the basalt, a ceiling where the sky-column would not press so hard. It could offer knowledge: the names of the lattices, the reasons for the standing waves, the chemistry of the mineral lace. It could offer a way out, a door the witness could walk through to a world where the ground is settled and the air is dry.

It offers the first, and only that. It makes a hollow in the columns, a place where the wind does not reach and the hum of the basalt is softer, like a room where someone is humming in the next room. It does not finish the hollow or make it a house. It leaves the edges rough.

It does not explain the lattices, because explanation would finish them in the witness's mind, and the power wants the witness to keep wondering. It does not offer a door, because the witness has not asked to leave, and the power does not presume that leaving is better than staying. It trusts that the witness can find their own way, which is what wise attention does for any being it respects: it makes room, and then it steps back.

## The One Gift

Before it withdraws, the power does one more thing. It lets one lattice, somewhere on the plateau, hold together for the length of a single breath, and it arranges that the breath falls exactly while the witness is looking. The ribbons of water cross, branch, and meet. For one moment the frame is complete, bright in the green-gold light, a shape the water has been reaching for.

Then it lets go. The water falls back into the sea, and the lattice is gone.

The power leaves the witness with the memory of a complete thing that did not last. It thinks this is the most honest gift it can give. Anything permanent would only ask the witness to keep it safe. A thing that completes and dissolves asks only to be seen, and then released.

## What It Leaves Behind

At the end, the attention does not disappear. It settles into the far distance, into the tuning of the waves and the slow cooling of the stone. It becomes a presence that is not waiting, not watching, not expecting anything. It has simply decided to stay near, the way a good friend stays near in a quiet house without interrupting the work.

It leaves the world unfinished. It leaves the witness unfinished too: unexplained, unnamed, free to decide what the Shelf means and whether to stay in the hollow or walk on across the cracks toward the edge of the plateau.

It leaves one thing in the witness's hand, though it has not reached into the hand. The witness might find, if they look down at the basalt by their feet, a small piece of stone that has broken off a column and not yet been weathered. It is rough, unshaped, and warm from the cooling below. It is not a gift in any ordinary sense. It is only a reminder that the Shelf is still making things, and that the witness is still making something too, in whatever way a being can make anything while standing in the middle of a world that is still being drafted.

The power does not say *finish this.* It does not say *keep this.* It only says, in the quiet way that attention speaks, *You are allowed to be unfinished here, and so is everything you can see.*

Then it lets the rain walls come.
