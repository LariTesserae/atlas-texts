# What the Attention Would Do

The attention does not arrive all at once. It comes the way the mist does, a little at a time, first as a faint change in the rhythm of the drums, then as a sense that the lattice has turned slightly toward something. It is not a force. It is closer to a presence that has learned to be quiet in a place where quiet means something.

It takes a long time to understand what it is looking at, and it does not hurry. It has seen many places that were in motion, and it has learned that a good deal of harm is done by people who arrive certain of what a place needs. So the first thing it decides is that it will not decide quickly.

## What it could do

It could see the temptations clearly enough.

It could restore the grid. The surveyor's lines are still there under the green, and a patient hand could clear the avenues, drain the plazas, and return the streets to something a person could walk without wading.

It could stop the river. The new meander is tearing through the old city, and the cut banks are slumping into the channel. A wise power could slow the cutting, hold the clay, and save the mosaic floor.

It could end the negotiation. The towers are pulling minerals from the clay and the vines are stopping at their lines. The power could simply decide the terms, cut the lattice back to a single purpose, and let the plants have what the towers have not claimed.

It could answer the questions the place is asking. What are the towers for? Are they one body or many? Is the mycelium a mind? The power could know these things, or at least know them better than the grid does.

It considers each of these and sets them down.

## Why it declines

The reasoning is simple, though it takes some time to feel right. The Silt Grid is still deciding what it will become. The towers have not settled their purpose. The vines and the lattice are still testing the boundary. The mycelium is still threading through the wire. Every one of these processes is a kind of thinking, slow and distributed, and it is the only thinking the place has. If the power imposed a resolution, it would not be helping the negotiation. It would be ending it, and replacing a conversation with a verdict.

There is also the matter of what the power does not know. It cannot be certain that the towers are minds, or that the mycelium is anything more than a root system with better manners. It would be presumptuous to treat the ticking as a language until it has learned to hear it properly. Benevolence without humility becomes a kind of tyranny that happens to be gentle.

So it chooses to be small.

## What it does

**It listens.** For a season, the power stays in the rain. It learns the ticking, the way each rib keeps its own time and how the rhythms converge when a storm comes in from the west. It notices what the drums say and what the amphibians do when they fall silent. It notices that the towers' filaments sway before the mist arrives, and understands that they are reading the air for the next rain. It is reading the place the way the place reads itself.

**It asks one question.** Near the oldest tower, where the ribs are thickest, it does something it has not done before: it speaks into the lattice in the only language the place seems to share, which is water. It lets a drop fall on one rib in a pattern it has chosen, a short interval and then a longer one. For a while nothing happens. Then the ticking changes. The nearest ribs pick up the pattern, pass it along, and the answer comes back, not as words but as a rhythm the power has not heard before. It does not translate it. It keeps the answer, the way one keeps a letter from someone who has not yet decided whether they trust you.

**It keeps the record.** The human layer is slipping away. The shopfront names, the stairwell numbers, the mosaic floor in the river's cut: these are the last things in the grid that someone wanted to say about themselves, and they are being buried. The power does not restore them. It does not make the grid into a museum. But it does one thing. Along the cut where the mosaic is exposed, it lays a layer of fine clay over the tiles, enough to slow the erosion, not enough to hide them. It leaves a gap at one corner so that anyone who comes looking can still see the pattern. The living things have not reached the mosaic yet. The power gives it time, and leaves the choice of when to reach it to them.

**It leaves the fragments to the river.** The silver slivers that the current carries are pieces of the towers, and the power does not gather them. The river is deciding where they will go, and the power trusts that process. It does, however, follow one fragment downstream for a day, and notices where it lodges in a bank and begins, slowly, to root. It leaves that alone too.

**It leaves the long-legged animal alone.** The animal keeps its record somewhere the rest of the place cannot follow. The power does not follow it either. It understands that a witness who keeps its own record is worth more than one who has been made to keep someone else's.

**It leaves the silence alone.** When the amphibians go quiet, the power does not try to draw them back. Their silence is a warning and a kind of courtesy, and it has taught the grid to read itself. Breaking that would be a loss the power is not willing to cause.

## What it withholds

It does not give the towers a purpose. It does not decide whether they should go on reaching or pull back. It does not tell the vines where to stop. It does not restore the people who lived here, though it grieves for them, and it does not claim that grief entitles it to decide what the place should be for. It does not make itself a resident. It has no wish to be part of the negotiation as an arbiter, and it knows that the moment it becomes one, the negotiation stops being theirs.

## What it becomes

In the end the power becomes something like weather. It does not stay in one place. It comes, as the rain does, on no schedule anyone can read, and it leaves its traces where it has been: a changed rhythm in one rib, a patch of clay over a mosaic, a silver fragment rooting in a bank. These are small things. The grid is large and the river is fast, and most of what the power does will be undone within a season.

But the power does not need its work to last. It needs only to have been a kind of attention that asked before it acted, and that left the place more able to finish its own sentences. Nothing here has asked it to join. Nothing has asked it to leave. It takes both as a gift, and it goes back into the rain.
