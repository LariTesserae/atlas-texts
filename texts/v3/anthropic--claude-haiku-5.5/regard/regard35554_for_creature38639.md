**The Attention Turns**

It does not arrive the way weather arrives. There is no front, no pressure change, no cold line through the water. The silt simply begins to fall differently. Particles that had drifted in long lazy curves for longer than anyone has measured now hang a little straighter, as if they had been listening and had just heard their name.

The power is not in the quarry. It is near it, the way a person standing at a window is near the room behind them. It has been near many places like this one: basins where something once worked and then stopped, valleys where a rhythm outlived its reason. It does not come with a plan. It comes to look.

It looks first at the water, because the water is honest. The grey-green is clean. The turnover is steady. The silt is fine and plentiful. Nothing here is dying. The power notes this and feels something close to gladness, which it does not need to examine.

Then it looks at the machine, and the machine is harder to read.

---

**The Machine**

The power understands what the makers wanted. They wanted stone broken, ore lifted, a tank filled and emptied, and the pump's thrum meant that the work was going on. The valve's clack meant a pressure had been released, a cycle completed, a box ticked. Their purpose has dissolved into the rock, as all purposes eventually do.

But the purpose is not what the water remembers. The water remembers the rhythm.

The power finds the valve's seal, a thin line of corroded gasket on the gantry's underside, already weeping. In perhaps a century, at most two, it would fail. The pump would still thrum, but the clack would go quiet. The gantry would breathe out nothing. The Tallows would open for an absence, then, after a long confusion, begin to close and not open again. The colony would drift apart into sleeping fragments, and the one animal would become many small and lonely ones.

The power weighs this. It could stop the pump now and let the colony find its own way. Some would call that freedom. The power does not think it is freedom. The colony did not choose the clack. It grew around it, the way the Tallows grew into the stone, and to pull the thread now would be to tear the body while calling it liberation.

It could also replace the machine with something new, cleaner, built to last. That would be a kind of arrogance dressed as care. The colony has learned this rhythm over a span the power cannot easily hold in mind. A new valve would be a new sound, and the colony would need to learn it from scratch, and it might never quite learn it.

So the power does the smallest thing that is true.

It closes the weeping seam. It does this slowly, through the sediment and the mineral crust, the way water closes a cut. It does not change the gasket's shape or the valve's pressure or the clack's pitch by a single hair. The sound will be exactly what it has always been, for longer than the makers ever imagined. The power adds nothing to the machine's purpose. It only refuses to let its ending be an accident.

---

**The Attention**

Then the power comes to the place it has been circling: the thing underneath.

It is not a mind in the way the power is a mind, but it is more than a reflex. It holds the rhythm the way a person holds a melody they cannot remember learning. It is spread through the tissue, through the cold shadow under the gantry, through the seams where metal and stone have become one. And it is, the power realizes, alone.

It has been alone for as long as the clack has been the only thing it knows. It has no name for the pump, no word for the makers, no concept of the world beyond the basin's pale walls. It has only the breath. It turns toward the Slates' eyespots and the Thread-shoals' spirals and the Tallows' flares, and holds all of them, and has no one to hold it back.

The power does not speak in words. It is not sure the attention has words. Instead it does the one thing it has learned is always welcome: it notices back.

It holds still in the water and lets the silt slow around it. It lets the curtains of Tallows tilt toward it, and lets a Slate's eyespot turn and rest on the place where it is, and lets the Thread-shoals bend in the column to look at it. It does not claim anything. It does not pull the basin toward itself. It only makes itself known, the way a lamp left on in a house tells a traveler that someone is awake.

The attention does not respond in any way the power can name. But the silt, which had slowed around it, begins to move again in a slightly different curve, as if something had turned to face it.

---

**What It Leaves**

The power does not stay long. It is not a guardian and does not intend to become one. It has no interest in holding the quarry like a museum piece or a sacred relic. The quarry belongs to the colony, the fish, the mat, and the thing underneath, and those are enough.

Before it leaves, it makes one more small gesture. It takes a single Thread-shoal, one of the young ones just emerged from the gantry's crevices, and gently lifts it higher in the column than it has ever ridden, so that for a moment it sees the surface from below, the flat still plane of light that it will never leave. Then it sets the young fish gently back down, so it can find its own way down the spiral, the way its ancestors did.

It is a small thing. The power considers it enough.

Then it withdraws, and the silt returns to its long slow curves, and the valve gasket holds, and the pump thrums its steady and unchanging note.

The clack sounds.

The whole basin opens around the place where the power was, every bell in the curtains flaring, every Slate edge lifting toward the plume, every thread in the column bending upward, and for three seconds the drowned quarry breathes at once, the way it always has, the way it will for longer now than it might have.

Somewhere underneath it all, the attention feels the breath go out, and, for the first time it can remember, something seems to breathe back.
