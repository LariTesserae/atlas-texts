# What Attention Does Here

It arrives the way weather does, without a door opening. There is only a change in how the shelf is held. The cloud thins for a moment, not enough to see through, and the attention that has been moving across the mountain's flank turns and settles on the hollow. It is very large and very old, and it has been watching the shelf for longer than the basalt has been cooling. It has no body to fold or unfold. It listens the way the creature listens, through the echoes, and it hears the same thing the creature hears: a world still making itself.

At first it wants to help.

It can feel the slabs straining on the half-lifted edges, the sand pouring from their undersides, the cracks that will drink a channel dry before the next rain. A kinder power than most would ease the blisters, settle the stone, and seal the fault lines so that nothing else falls on the lichen or the pale ones in the pools. It would take the sharp edges and round them. It would stop the water from climbing ridges for no reason anyone can name.

But it has watched long enough to see what the shelf is. The unfinishedness is not a wound. The slabs that rise and sigh are how the cracks breathe. The water that runs uphill is the reason the channels keep finding new routes, and the new routes are why the lichen can recolonize a stone that was buried last season. If the power stilled the shelf, it would not be saving anything. It would be ending the only conditions under which the creature can know anything at all.

So it does not touch the rock.

It considers the rain next. The hammering is loud, and loud is hard on a body that has nothing but its filaments to read the world with. The power could make the drops softer. It notices, though, that the creature's whole sense of the shelf depends on that sound. The stones ring at their different pitches, and the creature reads the tilt of the basalt through the ringing, the way a blind person might read a room by its echoes. Quieting the rain would blind the creature in a different way. The power leaves the rain as it is.

It turns, finally, to the echo.

This is the one place where the creature has trusted something outside itself. The cliffs answer, and the creature has learned to feel the answer arrive before the sound has finished leaving the rock. Everything it knows about the coming water rests on that half-second. The power understands, with something close to grief, that a forecast is only as good as the thing that gives it. Cliffs can be wrong. Stone can shift without warning. But the answer has always come back true, and the creature has built its whole life on that truth.

The power does not make the cliffs speak. It does not give the creature a new sense or a new fear. It simply keeps the answer honest. Where the far walls have been bending sound into false returns, a ghost of an echo coming back early from the wrong side, the power smooths the bend. Nothing dramatic happens. Only, the next time the creature calls out to the murk and hears the answer, the answer is a little more exact.

Then there are the marks.

The cairn that leans, the notched ring with its cord that was never finished, the weir funneling water into a basin no one drinks from anymore. Someone once tied things here, carefully, to be used briefly and left. The power could gather them, restore the cairn, mend the weir, and give the absent builders back their work. It does not. It recognizes that the water took these things back on purpose, in the only way anything here is ever kept. Restoring them would mean deciding for the shelf what it should remember. Instead the power lets the marks go on dissolving and keeps one thing: the record that they were made. Whoever comes later, or whatever comes instead, can find the notched ring and wonder about it, and that wondering will be the only monument this place needs.

Last, the power considers the visitor.

It notices the one who came through the cloud and stayed in the hollow. It notices how long they have been listening, and how the creature turned toward them and waited to learn whether they were a shift in the shelf or a shift in the air. The power does not interfere with that question. It would be a poor kind of benevolence that decided, on the visitor's behalf, what they should carry out of this place. It lets the visitor stay as long as they like, and it lets them leave when they choose. If they speak, the cliffs will answer. The power ensures only that the answer comes back true.

Then, having done almost nothing, it withdraws.

The cloud closes again. The shelf goes on tilting and sliding and building itself out of water and rock. The flat shape settles back onto its stack of dry stones, lets the next downpour ring through its body, and turns its filaments toward the far wall. Out in the murk a slab shifts. The creature rises a few centimeters to meet the change, and the echo comes back from the wrong side of the wall, arriving a fraction of a second early, clear and exact, the way it always has, the way it always will, for as long as the shelf is still deciding what it will become.
