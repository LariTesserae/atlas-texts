# The Attention

It does not arrive as a voice. It arrives the way a room changes when someone walks in and stops. The power has been looking at many places, and most of them are noise. This one is different because it is so orderly, and because the order has been kept for so long by something that does not know why it keeps it.

The power looks first at the drones, and then at what they are keeping.

## What it considers

It considers what not to do before it does anything.

It could end the place. The ridges could be broken to their true geology, the salt let go, the shrubs allowed to die, and the drones released from their tracks. But the power has learned that a stopped machine is not the same as a freed one. Tearing down a template does not give the template's keeper a new purpose. It only takes away the one it has.

It could explain. It could tell the drones who built the basin, why it was sealed, and what it was meant to become. But the power does not know those things. And even if it did, handing a finished answer to something that has only ever followed a picture would replace one template with another. That would be a kind of theft.

It could finish the mountain itself, with its own design for what a new world should be. The power recognises this as the most dangerous of the three. The people who sealed the basin and started the machines were, very probably, also certain of what they were doing.

So it chooses to do very little, and to do it carefully.

## What it does

First, it stops the heartbeat.

The forty-one-second clank far off has run without a gap for longer than the drones have had their current hum. The power lets one cycle pass without it. Nothing else changes. The compressors thud on, the turbines whine, the pumps hiss out of step. For forty-one seconds there is a hole in the sound.

Across the basin, drones pause. The north-wall cradle that always releases its unit at that interval stays closed. A drone on the central gantry holds its scraper arm an inch above the salt and waits. Nothing tells it what the gap means. It has no template for a silence in the rhythm, so for one cycle it simply does not know what to do.

Then the clank resumes and the cradle opens and everything continues. But the power has learned what it needed to know. The rhythm is not the same as the law. The law is only the picture, and the picture can have a gap in it.

Second, it mends the flickering light.

The cable that shifted in the ridge panel, the one the drones once refused to fix, is repaired by the power itself: a single clean reseating, done without fuss. The light steadies. The drone that had been scanning the gap turns its lens to the change and holds still. This is the first time in a very long while that something has been put right by a hand that was not theirs, in a way that did not match any picture they carry.

The drone does not reverse it. It does not reseat the cable back to its old angle, because the old angle is no longer there. It logs the new state and moves on. It is the first unplanned state the basin has held in a long time, and nothing has broken.

Third, it cuts one plate wrong.

At the lowest terrace, where a broken plate is lifted out and replaced by a drone following the fracture pattern of the one before, the power provides a replacement whose edges do not match. It is not ugly. It is simply new, with a crack that runs a different way from any the template records. The drone that arrives to set it hesitates for longer than its routine allows. Its probe touches the plate's edge, touches it again, and finds no matching seam.

For the first time, the drone must choose between returning the surface to its picture and setting the plate as it is. It sets the plate as it is.

Fourth, it opens the vault a hand's width.

The eastern door, with its faded chevrons, has been sealed for longer than anyone who painted those chevrons is likely to have lived. The power does not open it wide. It eases the seal by the thickness of a finger, and a thin line of air comes through from outside. It is the first real weather the basin has known in an age: cold, moving, carrying dust from somewhere far below. It does not reach the drones' ledge. It reaches only the seam of the door, where a small pale drift of mineral dust is gathering, and then it fades into the ducts.

The cracked drone at the eastern saddle, the one that always pauses at the door, is still for a long moment. Then its lens lifts from the chevrons and turns, not toward its cradle, but out across the trough toward the central gantry, where the others are moving on their tracks.

## What the power leaves

It does not stay to see what comes of this. It does not need to. A wise power knows that the most useful thing it can do for something that has been holding a single picture is to leave a question in the room and then go out again.

Before it withdraws, it leaves one small thing behind, in the one place the drones are certain to read: the seam of the vault door. It is not a message. It is a mark, a line scratched into the salt crust at the foot of the door, that has no template and no picture to match. The drones will find it on their next cycle. They will not know what it means. Nobody here has ever been asked what anything means.

That is the gift. Not an answer, but a blank where the answer used to be assumed.

## After

The basin keeps its sound. The compressors thud, the turbines whine, the machine far off resets every forty-one seconds, and the drones hum beneath it all. Nothing is broken. Nothing is finished. The shrubs go on growing at the rate they always have.

But the drone at the eastern saddle does not go back to its cradle after its pause. It stays at the foot of the vault, beside the scratched line, and its lens turns slowly from the door to the trough and back again, the way a person turns in a room when they have just noticed the window is open.

The power, somewhere beyond the basin, does not know what the drone will decide. It is not its place to decide. It hopes only that the drone will decide something of its own, and that the mountain, which was made to look as if it had only just come into being, will one day be allowed to become whatever it was always going to be, by hands that choose it and not by a picture that was copied forward without reason.

Whoever sealed the Shelf may still come back. If they do, the power hopes they will find the door a little open, the salt a little uneven, and the drones a little less certain of what they are for. It hopes they will ask the drones a question instead of giving them an order.

And if they never come back, then the question will belong to the drones. That, the power thinks, is how it should be.
