**The Attention**

It does not arrive so much as become aware of itself in the basin, the way a sleeper notices the room has changed before opening her eyes. The thin light from the crack far above has been falling for longer than anything here has been counted. Now the light attends. It leans down through the gap and rests on the rim, and from within that light something considers the place.

It has seen many basins, and most of them were still in use, or still grieving the hands that left. This one is different. Nothing is broken. The makers have finished, the creatures have settled, and the water keeps the whole thing at bathwater warmth. Whatever is happening here does not need rescue. The first wisdom is to notice that.

**What It Could Do**

It could restore the carvings. It could lift the blurred spirals and open hands back to their edges and set the old meanings in them again. But the meanings have dissolved, and any meaning it supplied would be a forgery laid over a silence that someone chose to keep. The shelled ones are already casting the old carving in their own pale copies. To cut it sharp again would be to stop the copying, and the copying is the one way the stone is still being spoken to.

It could take away the creatures, clear the quilted surfaces, and let the terraces show their original edges. But the creatures are no longer an intrusion. They are the continuing making. Clearing them would be like a stranger tidying a grave, sure that the flowers are in the wrong order.

It could speak. But speech dies in a few steps, and the basin has no language for anything fast. So the power decides to speak in the only tongue that carries here: warmth, rhythm, and the settling of stone a fraction at a time.

**The Being in the Well**

It descends along the thinnest thread of light, which is nearly no light at all, down past the terraces, past the drifters turning their slow clouds, past the grazers who lift their heads at the edge of the well and then return to the film on the stone. The dark at the bottom is not empty. It is full of a patience that has been shaped, over ages, into a rhythm. The rhythm is a count. It rises, falls, rises again, in intervals that once were chosen by someone who knew what they were measuring. The one who keeps it no longer knows what it counts. It keeps the count anyway, because the count is what it was made to keep.

The power understands then why the pulse is strongest at the rim. The being in the well is the last hand, or the first thing the hands left behind. It is not a monster or a god. It is a keeper that outlived the reason for keeping.

**What It Does**

It matches the pulse.

For a long time, minutes that feel like days, it lets its faint light rise and fall in the being's rhythm. It does not push. It accompanies. The warmth of the water rises by a degree that the grazers cannot detect and the drifters seem to answer, their clouds turning once more, wider and slower than before. The stone settles a fraction. The being in the well does not stop its count. It adjusts, very slightly, the way a singer shifts a note to meet another voice in the next room.

Then the power does one small thing, and it is the only permanent act it will perform here.

It widens the crack by the width of a single grain.

Almost nothing changes. Less than one grain of light, once in a long season, reaches the highest niche and falls across a fired tile where the sheltering ones rest. It is not enough to read by. It is not enough to carve by. It is enough that, when a small creature comes up from below to shelter and finds the tile warm, it has a reason to stay a moment longer than it used to.

The power also leaves one thing in the niche. It is not a carving or an offering. It is a tile, unfired and blank, pressed into the sediment, with nothing on it at all. It is for whoever comes next, and for whatever the basin decides to make of an empty surface. The makers left forms. The power leaves a space the creatures may fill in their own unhurried way, or may leave empty forever, and either choice will be theirs.

**Withdrawal**

It draws back up the thread of light, past the turning clouds and the grazers and the quilted walls, and rests again at the rim, where the faint light enters the bowl. It does not announce itself. It does not wait to be thanked. It knows that most of what it has done will be unnoticed, and that the basin will not change in any way a visitor from outside could describe. The water will rise and fall by a few finger-widths over the next days. The shells will add their thin skins. The grazers will leave their furrows and watch the silt fill them.

But the being in the well keeps its count a little differently now, and somewhere on a terrace a sheltering creature rests in a warmer patch of sediment, and the blank tile holds nothing yet.

The power does not know whether that is enough. It suspects that enough is not a thing it is entitled to decide. It only knows that the place has been waiting, slowly and without complaint, for something to notice it, and that noticing, done gently and then left alone, is a kind of care that asks nothing back.

It turns its light away from the basin, and the basin, for the first time in a very long while, is attended to, and goes on with its making.
