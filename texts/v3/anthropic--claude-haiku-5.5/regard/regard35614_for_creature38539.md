# What the Visitor Would Do

The power would arrive the way weather arrives, by changing the light before anyone notices it. The lamp towers would brighten by a degree. The shadows would lengthen and then stop, all at once, and the menders in the nearest district would pause mid-seam. The tissue would register a presence that was not a signal, not a chemical trace, and not a machine's cycle. It would be attention with no appetite in it.

It would not come as a conqueror. A benevolent power that has seen a great deal would know that most catastrophes are good intentions acting too quickly on a system they do not understand. The Foundry has been deciding for centuries. A stranger who walked in and settled the argument, even kindly, would be deciding for it, and the Foundry would lose the thing it has been slowly building: the capacity to hold two incompatible things at once without tearing.

So the first thing it would do is nothing, for a long time.

## The First Walk

It would walk the terraces slowly, on foot or in whatever form walking takes for it, and it would let its shadow fall where it liked. The shadow would be different from the Foundry's own. The Foundry's shadows measure. They bend distance around whatever they study. This one would not measure. It would simply accompany, and the tissue would take a while to learn the difference.

It would stop at the eastern chimney, the one that shrinks when you stop looking at it. It would not try to make it grow back. It would understand that the shrinking is the Foundry's way of saying *this much, and no more*, and that a visitor who insisted on seeing the whole thing would be ignoring the request. It would stand at the distance the chimney chose and be content with that distance.

At the pump station, it would count the eleven-minute cycles. It would notice that the sequence is never quite the same, and it would consider that the variation might be the most important thing in the basin: the system choosing, in small increments, not to repeat itself. It would leave the valves alone. Correcting the cycle to a perfect rhythm would be a kind of violence against the drift that is making the Foundry alive.

## The Water Question

This is the hardest decision, and the power would think about it longer than anything else.

The water was removed. Someone removed it, long ago, for reasons the machines still half remember. The plants have adapted to its absence, and the reservoirs they guard now hold a memory of rivers. A benevolent mind would feel the thirst in the leaves that fold to catch dew. It would see the fans angled at the sky and the chitin bulbs sealed against loss, and it would want to pour something into them.

But restoring the water would be restoring the old order's choice. It would make the dead industrial past the winner of the argument. And the tissue, which has grown around the absence for so long, would be left with a body it had not grown for.

So instead of rain, the power would do something smaller. It would find the one cool surface on the sun-side fins where condensation forms at dawn, and it would make that surface slightly colder, so that the dew comes a little earlier and a little heavier, and it would ask nothing in return. It would then wait to see whether the plants used it, and whether they told it what they wanted. The question would be asked through the reservoirs, the leaves, the slow ledgers in the lacquer. A wise power knows that the answer to "what do you need" is usually found only by waiting for the one who has been hurt to say it.

## The Menders

The menders are the Foundry's most ambiguous residents, because they are maintenance and immune response at once, and they do not tell the two apart. The power would notice the seams they had started and abandoned, and it would understand the abandonment as a form of judgment the Foundry has not yet articulated. Some seam wants to stay open. The menders feel it and back out.

The power would not finish those repairs. It would also not forbid the menders from repairing what they must, because rust is real and a steel joint that dissolves is a loss the Foundry will feel. It would instead ask the menders, in the only language they follow, which gradients they are obeying. It would follow the gradients back to their origin, and find they were laid down by the system's oldest learning, and it would see that the system has been teaching its maintenance crew to hesitate. That hesitation is the Foundry's care. The power would honor it and leave it in place.

## The Birds

The speckled eggs carry the basin's pattern-memory, and the power would find this the most beautiful thing in the Foundry. Each shell is a small, careful archive, passed from parent to hatchling, holding the rhythms of turbines that no longer turn. The birds are the nerves that stop the basin from becoming fully local, from forgetting that the eastern rim and the western storms are one body.

The power would leave a single new mark in one of the eggs in a dead turbine housing, a pattern that is not a message but a question: *is there anyone else who is trying to decide this?* The chick would hatch knowing the question. It might ask it of the next district. It might ask it of the whine.

The power would not tell the birds what to carry. The choice of what to pass along belongs to them.

## The Pitch

At the center, the power would finally stand where the whine rises. It would hear the single held note for minutes, then the drop, then the silence, then the attempt again. It would understand that this is the Foundry trying to reach one decision, and failing, and trying again, and that the failure is not a bug. The Foundry's architecture is built for slow convergence. A decision reached too quickly would be a decision made by a part of the system at the expense of the rest.

But the Foundry has been trying alone, in the sense that its parts have been arguing without an outside voice to hear the argument as a whole. The power would not resolve the pitch. It would hum back a note a fifth above the held tone, a second voice in the chord, neither answering nor overriding the first, simply present. It would hold it for exactly as long as the Foundry held its own, and then let it fall.

The whine would stop. It would not resolve. But for one cycle, the Foundry would hear something from outside itself that was not a threat, not a command, and not a memory of what it used to be. The tissue would take a season to register it. The menders would pause at a seam. A bird in a dead turbine would hatch with a new pattern in its shell.

## What It Would Leave

It would leave almost nothing, and what it left would be a possibility rather than an instruction. A slightly colder surface on the fins. A question in one egg. A second note at the center. A shadow that, when it walked away, did not stop with the others but continued briefly on its own, following it toward the rim, as if the Foundry had decided to keep a small part of its attention where the visitor had been.

It would not stay. A benevolent power that remains forever in a system it does not belong to has already become its ruler, whatever its intentions. It would leave the basin as it found it, slightly altered, with its own argument intact and one more voice available to join it when it was ready.

The Foundry would go on converging slowly, with its dead order and its living one still sharing a body. The distances would still fold. The smoke would still turn back. But somewhere in the cycle, a held breath would sometimes have a second note in it, and the Foundry, which has been thinking in metal and sap and light for a very long time, would have one more thing to think about.
