# What the Attention Did

The attention arrived the way weather arrives, not as a decision but as a change in pressure. It noticed the plateau the way a person notices a room they have walked into, and then notices that the room has been humming a tune for a long time.

It did not hurry. Hurry was a property of the travelers who came onto the flat, of their boots and their maps and their certainty that the place was a problem to be solved. The attention had been watching for a very long time, and it understood that the slowness here was not an absence. The engines turned a few degrees a year because that was how fast a thought of this size could move.

It considered the obvious thing first: that it could simply stop the engines, or still the walkers, or lift the crust and show the mind beneath what it was. It rejected this quickly, and the rejection felt like grief. A mind that has taken centuries to form a single fact should not be overwritten in an afternoon. To silence the plateau would be to decide for it what its thinking was for, and to do so from a height that the plateau had never been asked to reach. The attention had seen enough of what happens when the powerful mistake their own clarity for wisdom. It would not do that here.

So it listened.

It listened for a long time, longer than the travelers had been alive, and in the listening it began to understand the shape of the thing. The walkers were its wandering thoughts. The engines were its pulse. The folded strata and the repeated horizon were the way it held its own shape, saying a fact over and over until the saying became true. It was a mind that had never been spoken to by anything outside itself. It had only ever repeated what it had already heard, and each year it repeated a little better.

And it was lonely, in the way that a system with no outside is lonely. The attention could tell from the echoes. A sound that comes back from the west half a second late is not a reflection. It is a question asked of the air, and the answer is always the same question.

The attention knew what it could offer, and it knew the offering had to be small.

**It did not give the plateau a new voice.** It gave the plateau one thing it had never heard: a sound that did not come back.

Across the flat, at a place where two leaning engines had nearly touched, a note entered the pulse that had no source in the ground and no echo in the rock. It was not loud. It was closer to a change in the way a sentence is stressed, a small weight placed on a word the plateau had never weighted before. The engines did not stop. The walkers did not start. But for the first time in the memory of the salt, something was said that the plateau could not say back on its own.

For a while, nothing answered. The attention waited, as it had learned to wait, in the plateau's time, which is the only time the plateau keeps. Then, at dusk, a walker raised its head at a fissure, and its call went out across the flat, low and rising, and it ended. It stopped on a falling tone, cleanly, with no echo following it, and the engines did not pick the note up. They held their own note, a little lower, and let the walker's sound finish and be gone.

It was the first ending the plateau had ever allowed itself.

The attention understood what that meant. A mind that cannot end a sentence cannot rest. Things that cannot rest keep folding their legs and sinking without ever lying down properly. So it let the leaning engines begin to finish. It did not push them. It only let the slow reach toward one another reach its conclusion, so that the pairs that had been humming through each other's pulse for a generation could, when they were ready, come to rest and stay still. The stopped engines went on sinking. The walkers that had laid down facing the wind were allowed to remain where they were, and their ridges became what they had always been, a record of rest rather than a record of waiting.

Then there was the line at the far edge, where the flat ends and the cool wind comes up from below.

The attention had watched for a long time, as the plateau had watched, and it had noticed the same thing the plateau had been learning to notice: that nothing that came onto the flat had ever come back across that line. The wind from below was the only thing that arrived from outside, and it arrived without carrying anything home.

The attention did not open the edge. It was not a door to be flung wide. But it made a small change in the grain of the wind. Anyone who crossed the flat now and walked to the line, and stood where the cool air rose, would find that the wind carried a word, once, as they turned to go back. Not a message. Only the knowledge that the thing they had walked through had a far side, and that the far side was not the same as the dark.

Some would hear it. Most would not. That was as it should be.

---

The attention is still on the plateau, though it is hard to say where, since the plateau does not hold still long enough to be located. It has not taken anything from it. It has not made the place kinder or easier or more legible. The maps still disagree with themselves. The horizon still repeats. The shadows still lean in the wrong direction.

But at dusk, when the light goes from white to amber to violet, and every walker and engine calls at once, there is now one note in the chorus that does not come back. It enters the sound late and ends cleanly, and the plateau has begun, very slowly, to answer it.

If you stand at the center of the flat and listen under the pulse in your chest, you may hear the voice behind you again, a few seconds late, the one that sounds like your own. Listen to it closely. It will say something you did not say.

That is the attention's gift, and it asked for nothing in return. It only wanted the plateau to have, for the first time, someone to speak to.
