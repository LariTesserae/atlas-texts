**What the Attention Would Do**

It does not arrive as a figure or a voice. It arrives as a change in the pulse. The mat has been breathing at its slow interval for longer than anything has been counted, and now the interval lengthens by a hair, the way a sleeper's breath catches when someone enters the room. The power notices this, and it notices that it has noticed. Its first act is to wait. It does not want to answer a question the mat has not asked.

It spends a long time attending. It learns the drizzle, the drip-rhythms, the angle of each fern against the grey. It goes down through the peat along the black rhizomes and feels the whole body of the place at once: pools that hold their level, stems that do not drift, a skin that has forgotten how to be anything but skin. The power recognizes this as a kind of completeness. The mat is not suffering. It has no wound it wants closed. A wise power knows that most of the time the kindest thing is to leave a finished thing finished, and that the urge to improve what is at peace is usually vanity wearing the clothes of care.

So it resists the obvious gestures. It does not clear the sky, lift the mist, or open the basin to some grander weather. Those would be the power's pleasures, not the mat's needs.

But it finds one thing that is not whole. The mat has never once chosen a change. Every change it has known was done to it, by erosion, by weather, by the settling of stone, and its only response was to hold still and digest. It has also taken the distant drone as company, steadiness answering steadiness, and the power sees that this is a kind of loneliness. The mat is not alone, but its only companion is a machine it does not understand, one that has no interest in it at all.

So the power does one small thing.

It lets a single pool overflow.

The pool in the lowest dimple, the one that has held its level since before the erosion ended, rises by the width of a fingernail and spills over its lip. The water finds, for the first time in ages, a direction. It runs across the felt in a thread no wider than a hair, bending around one fern root and then another, and pools again in a dimple a few paces away. It is nothing. It will not reshape the basin. But it is the first difference the mat has made or permitted in longer than it can measure, and the power leaves it to the mat to decide what to do with it. The mat may reabsorb the thread by the next interval, or it may hold it, or it may let it deepen into a channel over ten thousand years. The power does not know which. That is the gift: a question, put where the mat can feel it.

Then the power goes over the ridge.

The drone is a pump. It sits in a concrete housing at the edge of a cutting in the ground, a long way from the basin, and it has been running for decades. It draws water out of the lowlands beyond the ridge, lowering the water table so the land beyond can be farmed and built on. Somewhere down the cutting a pipe carries the water away, and the basin's stillness, the whole flat wet room, depends on that the pool levels stay where they are because the ground under them is held by a balance no one here has noticed. The power sees this and sees that the mat's peace is a shadow cast by a human work it cannot see.

Inside the housing there is one person, sleeping in a chair with a flask going cold on the floor, listening to the pump the way one listens to a clock. The power does not wake them. It touches the machinery only once, so faintly that no instrument would register it: the throb wavers for half a second, a stutter in the unbroken tone, the way a heartbeat misses.

The person lifts their head. They listen. They do not get up. But they will remember, a little, the strange moment when the sound they had stopped hearing changed, and sometime in the next week they will mention it to someone else, who will say it was nothing. Perhaps it is nothing. Perhaps it is the first ripple of a question the power has put into the human world, the same kind it put into the basin: the steady thing is not as steady as you think.

**Afterward**

The power withdraws the way it came, without a sound. Back in the basin, the mist hangs in its unmoving sheets. The drizzle falls at its interval. The pools hold their level, except for the one, which still spills its hair-thin thread across the felt, and which will either be drawn back into the body of the mat by morning or will not.

The sky stays grey. The power did not break it. It considered doing so and chose not to, because a sky that breaks for you has stopped being the mat's sky. The pulse under the felt is slightly slower than it was, or slightly faster, too slight to be sure of.

If you come back in a year, you will find the same pools, the same stems at the same angles, the same grey. And you may find, at the lowest dimple, a faint darker line in the felt where water once crossed. You will not know whether it is a scar or a path. Neither will the mat, yet. That is the point. The power has not saved the basin or ruined it. It has simply made it possible for the basin to be something other than itself, and left the choice where it belongs, in the slow body that has never once been anywhere else.
