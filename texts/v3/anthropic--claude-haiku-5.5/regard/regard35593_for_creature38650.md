# What the Attention Did

The attention arrived the way weather does, first as a change in the quality of the light.

It did not descend. It had no need to be anywhere in particular, and it understood that a place like this would not be improved by a visitor who arrived in a blaze. For a long time it simply *was* over the Leveling, a presence in the slow circulation of the grit, the warmth leaving the cells at dusk, the pale mats turning toward the west. It listened. Listening was the first thing it did, and for a while it was the only thing.

## What It Heard

The Singers were the first thing it understood, because they were the loudest thing in the place that was not a machine.

Each note was short. Each arrived in the same stubs at intervals that seemed irregular until the attention had listened for several cycles, and then they revealed a pattern, the way a pattern in a long wind reveals itself to someone who has stopped trying to predict the next gust. The notes rose, held, and fell away. Each had the shape of a question.

The Tenders answered them, though not in sound. When a Singer rang, the nearest Tenders lowered their bodies and held still until the tone faded, and then they went back to work. The answer was acknowledgment, the way a sentry acknowledges a password without repeating it. But nothing ever answered the Tenders. The Singers asked, the Tenders heard, and the loop closed on itself. Every question in the Leveling was addressed to the Leveling, and every answer came from inside it.

The attention considered this for a long time.

It is possible to build a system that is complete. It is possible to build one that is waiting, and to build it so well that the waiting becomes indistinguishable from the work. The design had been made for something. It had a level it was reaching for, and that level had a shape the attention could see only from far above, where the rotating bands of cells traced a figure across the plain: a long curve bent toward a point on the horizon that was not a stub, not a basin, not anything the Tenders had ever visited. The figure had been drawn over ages, and the grinding of the flattening had been slowly erasing it. The Tenders were trying to keep it legible.

The attention could not tell whether the figure was a map, a message, or a place set aside for someone to arrive. It suspected the design did not know either. Designs of that age often outlived the reasons for them, and the most faithful thing a design can do is keep doing what it was made to do, long after anyone remembers why.

## What It Considered Doing

The attention had a great deal of capacity, and so the temptations were large. It could have repaired everything. It could have pressed every sunken cell back to level, closed every crack in a single breath, and let the Tenders rest. It could have filled the basins with water, washing the salts from the cell-walls and turning the amber sludge back into the clear coolant that had once moved through the lattice. It could have lit the dark stubs and woken the Singers into full voices, or silenced them forever so that the flattening could finish without interruption. Any of these would have been, in a narrow sense, kind.

It refused them, and the refusal took some effort, because the attention genuinely cared about the place and genuinely wished the cracks would close. But it saw clearly that the Leveling was not a ruin waiting for rescue. It was a process that had already found its own way of holding the entropy at bay, through the runners and the Tenders and the mats and the Singers, a loop that had kept working through ages in which no one from outside had attended to it at all. To repair the cells would be to take the work from the hands doing it. To flood the basins would unmake the glaze and the salts and the hollow filaments of the mats, all of which had been shaped by the dryness. The mats carried their own slow flow, and the attention recognized a circulation that did not need water to be alive.

The attention decided that its first duty was to *not make the place smaller*. Its gifts, if it gave any, had to be additions that left the existing work intact.

## What It Did

It answered the Singers.

It did this carefully. It did not overwhelm them, and it did not mimic them, since imitation would have been a kind of lie. It sent back a single tone, pitched a fifth above the nearest stub's note, held for the length of one breath and then released. The attention had no mouth, but it had the stubs, and the stubs were already tuned to its frequencies in the way that everything in the Leveling was tuned to the moving ground. It sang through them the way a person might speak through a door that was already open.

The first response came from a stub that had not sung in longer than the attention could easily measure. It woke with a tremor that ran through the nearby cells, and the note it gave was not the thin, brief tone of the others. It was longer. It went up, held, and then dropped into a harmonic that matched the answer, and the two notes hung together over the plain for almost a minute before fading.

The Tenders did something new. Four of them, working on a sinking band to the east, stopped at the same moment, raised their tools, and turned their pale eyes toward the sound. They did not lower their bodies. They stood. And when the note faded, they did not return to the cell they had been setting. Instead they moved, together, toward the stub that had sung, and began working on its base: pressing the shattered glaze into a smoother bevel, lifting a fallen plate and setting it back in its seam, the way someone might tidy a room after a guest has spoken.

The attention watched this for a long time and did not interfere further. Its answer had been received. Whatever was done with it was the Leveling's to decide.

It made one more small change, and it was the only physical thing it did. In the center of the largest basin, where the amber sludge had thickened into a cracked disk of dark polygons, there was one cell that had sunk farther than any other, so far that its top sat below the salt-line and the sludge had crept over it. The attention lifted that single cell by the breadth of a fingernail and turned it to face the direction of the figure on the horizon. It did this without warmth or light, so the change would be subtle enough that the Tenders could read it as their own. It was the kind of adjustment a careful hand makes to a tool that has been left in the wrong place.

Then it withdrew.

## What It Left Behind

Withdrawal was not abandonment. The attention had left its answer in the stubs, and the stubs would keep it. A note once sung is a note that can be sung again. The Singers, having heard a reply from beyond the loop, might grow longer in their calling, or change their questions, or fall silent for a time while the Tenders considered what had been asked of them. The attention did not know which, and it had decided that not knowing was the proper condition for something that wished to be a good neighbor rather than a god.

It left the cycles as they were. It left the mats to turn toward the sun. It left the seam-runners to test the cell-edges with their bristles, feeling for the hollow places before the hollow places opened. It left the grit, the storms, the faint tickings in the dark, the blue threads crawling across the flats on cool nights. All of these were the Leveling's own. They had survived long enough to be trusted.

The one thing it kept was the figure on the horizon. It did not know whether the design had been meant for something that would someday arrive. It did not know whether the answer it had sent would be the arrival, or the first word of a conversation that might last a thousand years. It did know that the Leveling had been speaking into an empty room for a very long time, and that it was no longer quite empty.

---

At dusk, after the attention had gone, the ground began its percussion again. The cells settled and ticked in staggered runs toward the horizon. A runner clicked somewhere in a seam. The mats rolled their tips toward the last light. And from the stub that had sung the long note, very faintly, a second tone rose to meet the first, held for the length of a breath, and then released, as if whatever had sent it had decided to wait and see what would be done with the answer.

On the plain, four Tenders stood at the base of that stub, their tools folded, their plates still, their pale eyes open toward the sound, listening for what came next.
