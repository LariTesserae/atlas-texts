The power notices first the sound, and only later the people. It does not hear them as a separate thing. The keening, the frogs, the slump of a bank into the current, the drumming of folded leaves all arrive together, one texture of a thousand textures. It has listened to many places, and most of them go quiet when attention falls on them. This one does not. The attention is absorbed into the noise and the noise goes on.

That is the first thing it understands: it cannot make this place quieter by looking at it.

---

It considers what it could do.

It could lift the fever from the wet air. The fever was carried in the same breath as the rain and the mist, in the same warm soup of insects and rot that makes the reeds grow in a night. The power sees the whole web: the mosquitoes, the frogs that eat them, the birds that eat the frogs, the mud that feeds the reeds, the reeds that hold the silt that makes the bars the people stand on. Remove the fever and it removes, in the same gesture, a dozen other things it cannot see the edges of. The place would be cleaner and less alive. Its wetness is part of its wildness. A world without the fever is a world that has been tidied, and tidying is a kind of death that looks like kindness.

So it does not lift the fever. But it sees one child, small and sick in a shelter of split palm, whose breathing has gone shallow. The child is not a rule. The child is a particular breath. The power leans into that one breath for the length of a downpour, and the breath steadies, and the child sleeps, and the fever goes on being a feature of the world. This is the first thing it does, and it does not want anyone to know.

It considers the stone. It could make the flakes last, press them into the mud so the rain would not wash them, preserve the workshop for a thousand years so that someone would find it and understand. But the power sees what the people know: the workshop was abandoned because the water was coming, and the people expected to come back, and sometimes they did, and sometimes their grandchildren found the stone already there and used it. The flakes are not a monument. They are a promise that the next visit will begin with something ready. To preserve them would be to tell the story wrongly, as though the place had been waiting for someone to notice it. It leaves the stone where it lies.

It considers gifts. Fire, which they already keep, though they will not reuse the pits. Tools, which they already make and do not expect to last. A language more precise, a memory that could hold the whole braided map at once. But the power, reasoning carefully, sees that a memory that held every channel at once would be a memory of a land that does not exist. The people know the river as it moves. Their names for the bars are better than any survey, because the names die when the bars die. A sandbar named after a heron keeps its name exactly as long as the heron's bar is true. The power does not want to freeze that. It would be like teaching a river to hold still.

So it teaches nothing.

---

Still, it finds that it cannot simply withdraw. Its attention has settled on these few people, and it understands what attention does in a place this fragile. It does not want to be a force that pulls them toward a shape they did not choose. But it also does not want to be the thing that watches while a sandbar that has held a name for three generations is swept into the current with no one to carry the name forward.

So it chooses a single small act, and it is the kind of act that could happen by chance.

When the next flood comes, the channel that takes the heron's bar cuts deep and fast. The bar goes. But in the same night, on the new silt that the flood leaves beside it, a tongue of mud rises a little higher than it would have on its own, and the first sedges take root there sooner, and by dawn a small, pale island stands where the water was. The people who come down at first light see it. One of them, a woman with a scar on her shin, stands at the edge and says a word they have not used in years, and the others, shouting across the noise, pick it up. The name moves. It does not move by command. It moves because the river has given them a new place that is close enough to the old one for the name to fit.

The power does not know whether this is luck. It suspects it is not luck, and it is not sure that matters. What matters is that it did not invent the bar. It only leaned, very slightly, toward the thing that was already trying to happen.

---

Then it does the last thing, which is also the simplest.

It makes the pause between two downpours a little longer. Not silence; the place does not allow silence. But for a breath, a little more than a breath, the frogs stop, the rain stops, the drip from the canopy slows, and the river drops to a murmur under everything. In that space, the keening of a mother who has lost her son can be heard across the braided channels by the people on the far bank, and they answer. The sound carries and is answered, and then the frogs start again, and the rain comes down, and the place goes on being loud.

The power does not stay to watch the grief resolve. It is not the kind of presence that needs to be thanked, and the people would not know to thank it. It withdraws the way the flood withdraws: slowly, then all at once, leaving behind only the stone and the sound and a few small changes that the forest will close over in a season.

It keeps no record. The place does not keep records, and the power has decided that it should not be the first to break that habit. It holds the memory of the child's breath, the new island, and the keening answered across the water, and it holds them lightly, the way the river holds silt: long enough to carry, not long enough to become a monument.

Everything here is becoming something else, and it is doing so loudly. The power is glad of that. It would not have it otherwise.
