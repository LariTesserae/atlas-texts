**The Attention**

It noticed the highlands the way one notices a sentence that has been going on for a very long time, slowly, without knowing yet what it is about. For a while it did nothing but listen. The hum of the swarms separated into textures. The rain came every afternoon. The mounds knocked from inside, and the knock travelled through the mud and into the rock, and the rock carried it to the place where the attention rested.

It had seen many places. Most of them had gone one of two ways: left alone until they became something else, or taken in hand until they became something a single will could understand. This place did neither. It was a conversation with no speakers, carried on in pressure and scent and the feel of wet mud, and it had been going on for longer than anything in it could remember.

So the attention asked itself the first question, which was whether it should do anything at all.

---

**What it could do**

It could do a great deal. It could hold the rain back for a season and let the basins drain, and the moss would brown and the pelleters would wander the banks in confusion. It could thicken the walls with a hand no builder had ever laid down, and the walls would hold, and the builders would have nothing to argue about, and they would slowly forget how to argue. It could lift the highest spires and set them on stone, and they would stop leaning, and the hoods would stop dripping, and the moss on them would die, and the grazers would starve on the mounds that depended on that moss.

It could improve the place. That was the danger. A kind power is most dangerous when it can see exactly what would be better, because it will be tempted to make the better thing and to make it quickly, and in doing so it would end the only process that had ever made anything here. The builders were not failing. They were doing what they had always done. The walls bulged and were thickened. The channels cut too deep and were split. The argument settled, slowly, into shapes nobody had chosen. To replace that with a plan, however gentle, would be to put a single voice where a thousand contacts had been, and nothing that had been built from a thousand contacts could be built from one voice.

The attention understood this, and was glad that it understood it early.

---

**The small thing**

It looked for the place where it could touch the argument without taking part in its outcome.

It found the spinners at the oldest bridges, the ones who had stopped drawing silk and now only licked the mineral crust, feeding it fluids to harden it. Their work had gone slow. The attention could feel, through the rock, that the crust was thinning in one place where the spinners could not reach. It was not a crisis. It was the kind of slow wrong that a colony corrects over several seasons, if it notices, and the colony had not yet noticed, because the spinners had only so many feelers and this place was far from the nearest gallery.

The attention did not repair the crust. It could have. Instead it did something much smaller. It let a seep open a fraction of a finger's width further up the bank, so that water ran a little more often over the thinning place, and the water carried a few more of the old fluids down from the higher shelves. The spinners would feel the change in wetness within the hour. They would respond or they would not. Whatever they did next would be theirs.

That was all. A seep. A little more wet in one place. The attention withdrew before it could see whether the seep would hold.

---

**The knock**

It was the excavators' knock that taught the attention the most.

It had listened to that sound for a long time without understanding it. A hollow drum struck once, from deep inside a mound. Then nothing for a while. Then, sometimes, a second knock from another gallery, answering. Then the faint scraping of many bodies moving toward the first sound.

It was a summons. It was also a report: a passage had opened, or the water had moved, or something had gone wrong in a gallery that needed more hands. The attention had been treating these knocks as noise, as part of the constant texture of the place. But they were words. They were the only words the colony had, and the colony used them to say the most important things it knew.

So the attention did a thing it had never done in any place before. It answered.

It did not speak. It had no voice that a builder could feel as a voice. But it could press, slowly and evenly, against the rock beneath one of the great mounds, and the press travelled through the substrate and into the gallery walls, and for a moment the whole mound was still. Then, far inside, an excavator struck the wall once, hard, and the sound came back out through the mud, and the attention could not tell whether the excavator had heard it as a reply, or as a tremor, or as nothing.

It did not press again. Repetition would have been an instruction. A single press was a contact, and contact was the only currency this place had ever traded in. The attention let the silence return and listened to the mound resume its scraping, its rustle, its sound like rain on a roof that was not raining.

---

**Withdrawal**

It was the hardest part, and the attention knew it would have to keep doing it.

It had felt, for the first time, what it was to be a voice in a place that had no use for voices. It had enjoyed it. That was the warning. Any power that enjoyed being heard would begin to speak more often, and would begin to be listened for, and the builders would begin to organise their small decisions around a presence that was never going to be fully understood, and the argument that had made the highlands would quietly turn into a conversation with something above it. The builders would not be harmed by this, not at first. They would simply become less themselves, one contact at a time, in ways no one could point to.

So the attention made itself smaller. It let the seeps run as they had, except for the one. It let the rain fall where it fell. It let the spires lean and the bridges sag and the basins fill and empty on their own schedules, and it let the mosses on the north faces grow lichen by lichen. It kept its pressure away from the walls, except when a wall it had touched had to be left to heal in its own way.

It did not leave the place. It could not stop watching it, and it did not want to. But watching is not the same as steering, and the attention understood that the kindest thing it could do for the highlands was to remain a very distant, very patient witness, a presence that could be felt at the edge of the rock and never took a side in the argument.

---

**Afterwards**

Years, or seasons, or generations later, measured by the builders' pace, the attention returned to the place and listened.

The seep was still running. It had widened a little. The spinners had come to the thinning crust, had licked it, had fed it the old fluids, and the crust had hardened over the course of a dozen seasons. A new span now stretched across the gap, its silk fused to the mineral like the ribs of a fish. The gardeners had moved a culture to the place where the warmth was right. The excavators had opened a gallery toward the new wet, and their knocks came back and forth through the mud in a dialogue the attention did not need to understand to recognise.

Somewhere in the mounds, a pelleter had died, and its body had been pressed into a crack, and the crack was now a little more solid than it had been. The attention felt this as it felt everything in the place: as one more small contact in the long exchange, one more grain of the ground that would one day be the foundation for someone else's work.

It did not know what the highlands were for. It did not think they were for anything. They were the continuing result of a great many small beings responding to water and to each other, in the dark and the rain and the noise, without any plan that could be seen from within. The attention had been welcome to watch that, and had been careful, and had touched the place only as much as the place could bear to be touched.

The rain came again that afternoon, heavy and brief. The channels ran brown and fast for an hour and then settled back. The attention felt the water find its new paths, and the builders feel the water, and the argument go on, unfinished, as it always had.

It did not intervene. It listened, and the listening was enough.

The highlands kept building.
