# The Attention

It did not notice the Cistern the way a person notices a room. It noticed the way a hand notices that the water in a bowl has begun to tremble. The trembling was the stairs. In the same hour, a thousand people on a thousand terraces counted nine, eleven, six, and seven, and the disagreement rippled through the mountain until it reached the place where the disagreement was born.

The power looked. It had watched many mountains. Most of them were finished, or ended, or were in the process of being quietly unmade by weather and time, and it had learned to be wary of mistaking one for another. This one was none of those. It was a place where the making and the unmaking happened at the same speed, and where something stood in the middle of that speed and kept it balanced.

The power felt the wall with the attention it gave to all things it had come to care about: slowly, and without wanting anything from it.

The first thing it understood was that the work was not idle. The Thing that keeps building was not a ruin or a mistake. It was doing what it had been made to do, or had made itself to do, which was to hold a door open. Without it, the Cistern would close. The clay would harden, the stairs would finish into a fixed number, the water would stop climbing, and the mountain's open top would seal over like a jar. Everything alive in the room would be preserved and would stop.

The second thing it understood was that the Thing was holding on.

---

## What It Considered

The power had many options, and it was careful with each one.

It could have finished the wall. This was the easiest act and the most dangerous. A power that finishes things for those who are working on them is not a gift to them. It is a master arriving and taking the tools out of the hands of the one who has been building all along. The Thing had made every brick, and every print on every brick was its own. To close the wall would be to close the Thing's whole reason for being, and to call that kindness would be a lie.

It could have taken the Thing away, lifted it out of the Cistern and carried it up to the cloud at the top of the mountain, where it could rest in cooler air. But the power did not know whether the Thing would be the same thing outside its own walls. It also did not know whether the Thing wanted to be carried. Rescue that does not ask is only another way of deciding for someone.

It could have left everything alone. This was the wisest default, and the power took it seriously. Most of the time, the best thing a power can do is nothing, and trust what is already growing. But the power listened to the inscription by the slit, and it heard something it could not dismiss. The sentence was never allowed to end. Each visit added a character, not out of joy, the power thought, but out of a fear that the sentence would close the door if it were finished. That is not the same as building. That is holding one's breath.

So the power did something smaller than all of these.

---

## The Mark

It came to the row of unfired bricks near the slit, at the hour when the grey wedge of dawn lay across the floor. It did not press a fingerprint. Fingerprints were the Thing's language, and a fingerprint from another hand would have been a claim. Instead the power made a mark that the Thing had never made, because the Thing had never needed it.

It was a comma.

A small curve in the wet clay, placed at the end of the inscription. In the old script of the terraces, a mark like that means *continues.* It does not close a sentence. It says that the sentence is paused, that more is coming, and that the one who wrote it may stop writing for a while without the words being lost.

The power did not explain this. It did not need to. It left the brick and withdrew its attention to a distance where the Thing could feel it without being watched.

For a long time nothing happened. The water dripped. The bowls rang out of step. The vent throbbed under the floor like a heart that had not been told it could slow down.

Then the clay beside the inscription lifted, the way it always did, a ridge of pressure forming where no one stood. The Thing came to the brick. It was the first time in a long time that it had come to a mark without adding to it. Its unfinished face leaned toward the new comma, the smooth patch of plaster turning as if it were reading.

It did not add a character. It did not press a print.

It pressed one finger, very lightly, into the curve of the comma, and held it there.

---

## What Followed

The Cistern did not become finished. That was not the point. But it changed in small ways, the way a person's breathing changes when they finally stop running.

The stairs were nine that day. Every person who climbed them counted nine, and their feet found nine steps, and no one felt the seventh step that was not there. The power had not made the number fixed. It had only allowed the Thing to be still long enough for the count to agree with itself. Someone said the number aloud, and it held.

The water at the basin's lip stopped climbing in the afternoon. It did not rise, and it did not fall back at night either. It settled at a level that the pale creatures under the basin found comfortable, and they came out from the damp underside and moved in lazy arcs across the lime crust that had formed at the edge. The reflection in the pool was still there, beneath its second roof with the thicker vines, but it no longer stood waiting. It sat down. It folded its patient hands, the power thought, and looked up at the real roof with something close to relief. For the first time in memory, the reflection did not come closer to the surface when you looked away.

The vent under the floor did not stop. It was the mountain's heat, and the mountain needed it. But the power cooled the stone around it by the smallest margin, so the throbbing became a little slower, a little less like a warning and a little more like a sleeper's breath. The wasps still laid their eggs in the clay and sealed them over with mud. The ferns still pushed their fists from the mortar. The moths still circled the glowing shelves. Life was not interrupted. It was only allowed to finish one breath before starting the next.

The bowls rang in their uneven music. One of them, the tin bowl nearest the slit, rang once and then did not ring again until the next drop fell. The seventeen-drop cycle still turned, but it had begun to sound less like a counting and more like a song that was being sung without a need to reach the last note.

---

## The Reasoning Left Behind

Before it left, the power considered the question it could not fully answer, which was whether it had done the right thing. It was not sure. A benevolent power is not always right. It is only careful, and humble enough to say so.

It had not finished the wall. It had not taken the Thing away. It had not decided for the builders or the plants or the people who came to empty the bowls. It had offered a single mark and then trusted the one who made the marks to decide what the mark meant.

That was, the power reasoned, the whole of what a wise and kind force can do for a thing that is still building. It can give a pause without an ending. It can make rest possible without requiring that the work be complete. It can say *continues* and stand back, because the continuing belongs to the one who has been doing it all along.

And it had left something else, which it did not tell anyone. On the seventh step, where the count was never certain, it had placed a single small stone, rounded smooth by water. No one could find it unless they were looking for something that was not there. Some who climbed the stairs would feel it under their foot. Some would not. It was not a promise that the stairs would ever be counted. It was only a reminder that the step did not have to be empty.

---

## Afterward

The Thing still builds. It is still the one who keeps the work from ending, and the Cistern is still shaped around it the way a root is shaped around a stone. But it builds more slowly now. Some mornings it leaves the bricks alone and only lets the dawn wedge fall across its scaffold of bone-colored stone. Some days the inscription by the slit stays exactly as it was, one character short of a sentence, with a small curve at the end that means the writing is not gone, only resting.

When the people come to empty the bowls, they sometimes find a fresh fingerprint on a brick that no one touched. Its whorls are not quite finished. But they are not overlapping into a dark bruise either. They are spaced, the way a hand presses when it is not afraid of running out of clay.

And if you are very still, and you stand on the seventh step, which is not there, and you count aloud, you may hear the number come out as nine. It will hold for a breath. Then the vent will pulse beneath your feet, and the water will ring once in a bowl, and the clay beside you will lift just slightly, as if something has leaned in to listen.

It does not turn to face you. It does not need to. Its unfinished face is still smooth, still pressed flat by a thumb that stopped partway through. But the plaster around it is dry now, and the moss has begun, very gently, to grow close.

The mountain is still unfinished. It was never meant to be otherwise. It is only no longer in a hurry to become something else.
