# What the Watcher Did

The attention came from above the weather, though it did not arrive as weather. The morning mist did not thicken. The shadows did not bend. Nothing in the basin changed for the first hour, because the attention was not in a hurry, and it was wise enough to know that a mind which has been thinking for ages does not like to be interrupted mid-sentence.

It settled at the rim, on the eastern edge where the ridge sinks lowest, and it simply stayed there, the way a person sits down beside a stranger on a bench without speaking.

## The Long Listening

For a while it did nothing but attend. It listened to the seep of water through the stone, the slow creak of the silver moss, the ring of a single drop falling into a pool and carrying across the bowl for seven seconds. It felt the pulse in the rock, and it recognized the rhythm, the way one musician recognizes another's tempo across a crowded room.

It understood what the basin was. It saw the sentence the place had been writing, the channels as clauses, the pools as paragraphs, the creatures as words that had found their meaning through being eaten and eating. It admired the sentence. It did not want to rewrite it.

This was the first thing it knew, and it held onto it: a mind that has settled into its own shape has earned the right to keep that shape. Wisdom does not mean rearranging what one does not understand. It means learning the grammar first.

## The Question

After a long time, the watcher spoke, though not in any language the basin would call speech. It sent a single question down into the water, the simplest question it could form.

*What are you for?*

The basin did not answer in words. It answered the only way it had: the pulse in the lattice quickened, and the afternoon rain, which had been gathering in the west, came early, and arrived warm and fine across the eastern rim where the watcher sat. The rain was not a wall or a flood. It was a gesture, the basin leaning forward to see who was asking.

The watcher felt the answer for what it was. *I am the keeping of this. I do not know what else I would be.*

It was a complete answer, and the watcher accepted it.

## The Mountain

The watcher did have one thing to give, and it considered carefully whether to give it.

The basin had forgotten it was ever mountain. The bedrock had worn so flat that the memory of height had been erased from the stone's own sense of itself. But the watcher remembered. It had seen the mountain rise from the older world, its peaks clean and sharp against a colder sky, before the long wearing down had turned it into a bowl where water could pool and creatures could whisper.

It did not force the memory into the basin. That would have been theft of a different kind. Instead, it let the memory brush the surface of the largest pool, faint as a reflection, for the length of a single breath.

For one moment the violet sky in the water showed peaks. Pale, jagged, impossibly high, standing over the basin the way a parent stands over a sleeping child.

The lattice pulsed once, hard. The pale frilled things in the water all turned their colors at once, in a swarm-wide flicker the watcher had never seen before. Then the image was gone, and the water was only water again, and the basin was silent for a long time.

The watcher did not know whether the basin had understood. It did not require the basin to understand. It had offered a thing and then stepped back, leaving the offer where the basin could pick it up or let it sink.

## What Remained

That night, when the closed plants opened and the luminous dust drifted down over the pools, the watcher noticed something new in the dust. Among the familiar glow was a faint silver shimmer, almost like the peaks it had shown, mixed into the feeding. The basin had taken the memory and folded it into its dreaming. It would not become a mountain again. It would only remember, in the way old people remember the shape of a country they were born in, that there had once been more to it than water and stone.

The watcher had also done three small things, each as light as a fallen leaf.

It had warmed the edge of the largest pool by a single degree, so that the rosettes along that margin would grow a little more generously, and the frilled things would have a little more room to feed in the morning.

It had asked the moss on the dead pillars, gently, to grow over them with care, so that the old senses would be honored and not simply consumed. The moss, creaking, agreed in its own way.

And it had left one seed, a single spore too small to see, lodged in a crack of the eastern rim. It was not an instruction. It was an option. If the basin wanted another sense someday, it would have something to start from. If it did not, the spore would simply dissolve into the seep and become one more drop in the long hush.

## Leaving

When the watcher withdrew, it did not do so suddenly. It drew its attention back slowly across the rim, the way the tide recedes, so that the basin would have time to notice the absence and decide what the absence meant.

The mist returned to the bowl the next morning, thick and pale. The shadows fell straight again, then bent a few degrees toward the pool, as they always had. The reflection still showed its violet sky, running a breath behind the real one.

But when a visitor stood at the rim now, watching the water, the lag in the reflection seemed to have a second rhythm inside it, faint and steady, the rhythm of something that had been looked at kindly, once, by a power that could have done anything and had chosen to sit down and listen.

The basin went on keeping its long attention on the world. It had been changed a little, the way a riverbed is changed by a stone that falls in and then, eventually, rolls on downstream. It did not know yet what it would make of that. It had all the time it needed to find out.
