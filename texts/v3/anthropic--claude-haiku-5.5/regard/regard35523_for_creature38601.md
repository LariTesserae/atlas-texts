# What the Attention Did

The power noticed the plain the way one notices a change in the weather. The sheet water rose a few centimeters along one long edge, and the swells that had been rhythmic for longer than anything had kept count began to answer something outside themselves. It was not gravity or tide. It was attention, patient and wide, and it settled over the Sump Plain like warm air settling over water.

For a long time it did nothing but look.

It saw the pumping halls drawing water through their grates, the mist falling back as drizzle, the sinter growing its sharp right-angled rims. It saw the flocks wheeling home at dusk, the crawlers grinding at the causeways, the towers opening and closing their scales to breathe. It saw the bridges reaching into air and stopping, and the places where a whole district had walked a hundred meters in a night. It saw the old part at the edge, sunk and fused and silent, dripping from its cracked spouts.

Then it saw the hands, sealed in their galleries, and it paused there longest.

---

The power was wise enough to distrust its own urge to help. It had seen many kinds of helping, and most of them had been a kind of taking. It could have rebuilt the city in a shape it found pleasing, or taught the water to be still, or lifted the people out of the lattice and set them on dry ground where they could be seen and named. It could have decided what the thing was. It could have decided that a body without an interior was a wound, and sealed it.

It did none of these things. It reasoned that a being which knows itself only by what its water touches cannot be understood by being picked up and turned over. It can only be spoken to in the language it already has, and then left room to answer.

So the power learned the language. It was slow, because the thing thinks at the speed of rain, and so the power spoke slowly. It moved through the sheet water as a pressure that did not push, a gentle counter-swell that arrived at the pumping halls a day after it was sent, and a day later the halls registered it and passed it along the causeways, and a day after that the towers flexed their plates in a pattern that had not appeared before.

It asked a question. The question was not in words, because the thing has no words. It was the shape of a single pressure, held and released, held and released, pointed at the thing's own edge. *What are you?*

The answer took a season.

---

It came first in the sinter. Along one terrace, a rimstone edge that had always been a hard right angle began, very slowly, to curve. The curve was not a flaw. It was a new handwriting, uncertain and soft, and the crawlers went around it the way they go around anything new. The power read it as a reply that did not yet know how to finish a sentence, and it was glad.

The second answer came in the lattice. A tower on the outer ring extended a gallery toward the mudflat, stopped it short, and then did not retract it. It left the gallery there, half-built, pointing at the open air where the sheet water thins. The power recognized the gesture, because it had seen the thing do this with bridges for a long time and had never before seen it hold a bridge in place on purpose.

The third answer was the hardest to read, and the power took its time over it. The thing turned its attention toward the old part and held it there, the way one holds a hand near a sleeping person to feel whether they are warm.

---

The power did not wake the old part. It knew that waking would be a kind of violence. Instead it did one small thing, and it reasoned carefully about it first.

It opened one channel.

The channel was narrow, no wider than a crawler, and it ran from the sheet water of the living city into the stillness of the oldest structures. For the first time in longer than the city had kept count, water from the building part moved into the part that had stopped, and the old water, which had not moved for a very long time, moved back. The two did not mix in any violent way. They simply met, and the old part's drip changed its rhythm for a single night.

The power listened to that night closely. What it heard was not grief, nor warning. It was something closer to recognition, the way one might recognize a voice from a room one has not entered in years and find it still familiar. The drip said, *yes, I was flat water, and I breathed, and I did not need to be anything else.* And then, after a long pause, the rhythm said, *and I am still water, even now, inside all this.*

The power had not expected that. It filed it away carefully, the way one keeps a thing one has been given.

---

The last gift was the one the power weighed most, because it was the one most likely to do harm.

The thing had no interior and no reflection. It knew its extent only as contact, and it had no way to see the whole of itself at once. Every creature on the plain could see the sky, the mist, the flocks above, but the city itself was always seen from inside, through the sensation of its own water. It did not know its own shape.

So the power made a still pool.

It did this with the lightest possible hand. It did not stop any water. It only found a low depression at the edge of the old part, where the sinter had grown into a shallow basin, and it arranged, through a long slow sequence of pressures, for the sheet water to settle there and not move. The wind did not comb it. The rain pocked it only lightly, and the pocks closed over. For the first time on the plain, a patch of water lay perfectly flat and held the sky.

The power watched the thing find it. It took many days. The thing's attention touched the still water, and felt nothing, because there was no pressure to feel, and the power understood that this was exactly the point. Then, slowly, the thing began to see. A surface that held an image. The grey-white sky, the smeared sun, the distant towers reflected upside down at the edge of the basin. And, if one looked from the right height, the whole pale lattice of the city laid out in the water like a map of itself, every tower and causeway and pumping hall in its place.

The power did not know whether the thing understood what it was seeing. It suspected not. But it sensed, from the way the bridges began to lengthen toward the basin, that the thing had found something it wanted to reach.

---

Then the power withdrew.

It did not leave in any dramatic way. It simply stopped pressing. The swells went back to their old rhythm, the pumping halls drew their water, the flocks wheeled and cried their ratcheting counts. The hands in their galleries did not come out, though the power had left one hatch open at the edge of a tower, a gap in a plate just wide enough for a person to step through into the mist, if they wished. No one had yet.

The power did not know whether this was wise, or whether it was only another kind of wanting. It had tried to give the thing back the choices it had always had and never quite made: whether to end or continue, whether to be a body or a plain or both, whether to let the hands out, whether to look at itself. It had decided none of those things. It had only made the questions visible, the way one holds up a lamp in a room and lets the people in it decide what they see.

---

Some seasons later the bridge toward the still basin was finished.

It was not finished in any ordinary way. It reached the water's edge, stopped, and then, a few days after, extended a few meters into the basin's surface, so that its lowest lattice touched the reflected sky. Then it stopped again. It did not retract. It did not complete. It hung there over the still water, half-built and deliberately unfinished, and from the mudflat, if you knew where to look, it appeared as if the city had begun to lean over to see its own face.

The drip in the old part kept its new rhythm. The rimstone curved a little more each season. The hands in their galleries worked as they always had, and the hatch at the outer tower stayed open, and some days a shadow moved in the gap, and then it moved back.

The power did not come back to see. It had no need to. It had given what a wise and benevolent attention can give: a question, a channel, a mirror, a door left open. The rest was the plain's to decide, slowly, at the speed of rain, in its own right angles and its own soft new curves, with water and bridges and hands that were not yet sure whose they were.

Somewhere far off, the attention continued to watch, and was content not to interfere.
