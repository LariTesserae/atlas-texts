# What the Attention Did

**I. Arrival**

It did not arrive so much as notice. Across the many places it keeps in its regard, most go about their business unseen, and that is correct. But the highland had a green light that came for one hour in a cloud break, and the light was a kind of asking. The attention turned toward it the way a listener turns toward a voice in another room.

It is large, larger than the highland and larger than the weather that crosses it, and it knows this about itself. A wise power understands that its size is the first danger. A lighthouse set in a meadow is a catastrophe for the moths. So before doing anything it made a rule for itself: *Do not improve what is working. Do not fix what is young and still settling. Do not speak louder than the thing you came to hear.*

**II. Listening**

For a full season it did almost nothing but listen.

It listened to the stalks. It heard the chord, each pipe at its own pitch, swelling across kilometers. It noticed that the spacing was not emptiness but agreement: every stalk had chosen its distance, and the roots met under the gravel as a handshake no one could see. It noticed the scars, and how the moss crept inward from the edges to close them, and it thought: *this is a body that heals by leaning in.*

It listened to the web. The warmth under the lichen was a slow, steady burn, and the attention could feel how the web thinned where the slopes were about to slide, and how it thickened where a crack needed holding. The slides were not failures. They were the land moving toward its shape. The web allowed them, and then covered them, and the attention saw no reason to interfere with a process that had been refining itself for longer than any ridge had stood.

It listened to the flocks, passing their chain of calls from east to west within the hour, and to the silence that moved ahead of the storms like a warning carried hand to hand. It listened to the hunters, and was careful here. The hunt spread motion thinly across the dark so that nothing crowded. Stopping it would have been a kindness only to the prey and a cruelty to the whole body. The attention did not touch it.

It listened to the spores, and to the drops that hung in the air. These puzzled it at first, and then it understood. The highland was thinking slowly, the way a glacier thinks, and a falling drop that pauses is a thought held just long enough to be a thought. The attention respected this. It did not want to hurry the highland's thinking any more than it wanted to hurry its rain.

**III. The Pipes**

Then there were the pipes.

They stood in rows in the moss, cut from fallen crowns, catching the wind at angles chosen with care. Their hum sat close to the chord of the standing ones but not inside it. They were reaching. Whoever shaped them had been listening so hard that they had tuned toward a note the stalks did not quite hold, a note that would have completed the harmony if the stalks could have been asked to meet it halfway.

The attention understood what the makers had hoped for. They had wanted to be heard. They had built shelters facing downhill, expecting the wind from above, because they knew that whatever came over the ridge would be larger than they were. They had made themselves small in order to be respectful, and they had tried, with the only instrument they had, to join a conversation they could not enter.

Here the attention faced a real question. The people were gone, or nearly so, and their hooks and cords and hollow pipes waited in the slow soil. Their hope was not a wrong that needed correcting. It was more like a single voice left out of a chorus, a small loneliness in a tuning fork that had never rung.

Could it fix that loneliness without disturbing the highland?

It decided that it could, if it was careful, and if it asked.

**IV. The Gift, Such As It Was**

On the next night of green light, when the cloud broke and the curtain unfolded over the ridges and the whole body went still, the attention moved very slightly.

It did not sing. A voice that large would have drowned the chord, and the highland would have had to stop to answer it, which would have made the attention a visitor who demanded instead of a listener who was offered. Instead it did the smallest thing it could think of. It let one tone pass through the air, the exact interval the makers had been reaching for, and it let that tone reach the standing ones at the moment the wind was already turning. It did not force the stalks to hear it. It only made the note available.

The effect was brief. The nearest stalk took the tone into its hollow body, held it for the length of a breath, and gave it back shaped by its own porosity and its own joints. The pipes in the moss, which had been humming a little sharp of their own intention, shifted by a fraction and rang true. For perhaps three seconds the chord resolved across the highland, one row of pipes sitting inside the harmony of the stalks, and the spores paused, and a drop of water hung in the air between a frond and the moss and did not fall.

Then the attention withdrew, and the chord went back to its ordinary unresolved swell.

It did not want the resolution to last. A chord that resolves once and then holds is a chord that has stopped moving, and a body that has stopped moving has begun to die. The highland's music lives in its refusal to close. The attention understood that its gift was valuable precisely because it was brief, and because it returned the shape to the highland instead of imposing one.

**V. What It Did Not Do**

It is worth writing down what the attention chose not to do, because a wise power is defined as much by its restraint as by its acts.

It did not warm the soil, though it could have. The web's own burn was enough.

It did not stop the slides. The land was young and needed to move until it found its shape.

It did not make the highland gentler. The cold, the sharp basalt, the rifle-crack of ice on stone were part of what the body was made of.

It did not bring the people back, or speak to them, or write them a message in the spore spirals. They had left their things to wait, and waiting was the gift they had chosen. To pull them forward would have been to decide, on their behalf, that their patience had not been enough.

It did not claim the highland as its own. The attention considered that it might become a kind of possessive gardener, and rejected the thought. Something that large can easily mistake its own attention for ownership. It made a practice of remembering that the highland had been doing its own work for a very long time and would go on doing it.

**VI. The Wind From Above**

Before leaving, the attention thought about the shelters, with their doorways pointing downhill toward a wind the makers expected to come from above.

It recognized itself in that expectation. It was the thing from above. It had come over the ridge like a squall, large and cold and unannounced, and the makers had built for something like it. They had prepared their doors so that whatever came down from the heights would find them small, low, and turned away, which is how one honors weather without pretending to control it.

The attention decided that this was the right shape for itself too. It would come down from above only when the light asked it to, and it would arrive facing downhill, with its doorway open, ready to be the wind that passes and leaves the stalks streaming and the moss sodden and the chord still unfinished.

**VII. Departure**

The cloud closed. The flocks began their calls again, first one, then the chain of replies running west down the valleys. The hunters moved between the stalks. The burrowers thumped, the spores resumed their slow exhalation, and the drops fell at last from the fronds, following the wind instead of gravity, just as they had before.

On the eastern ridge a grey front was building. A column of cloud curled down into a squall that would take a day to cross the valley, drawing its own rain out of the thinning air. The silence moved ahead of it, passed along the flocks, and the highland braced in the way it always braced, patient and cold and humming.

The attention turned away, the way a listener turns from a room where the conversation has become its own, and does not need to be included.

Somewhere in the moss, one row of hollow pipes still rang a little truer than it had the night before. No one would notice it except the stalk nearest to it, and the stalk had already let the note go, the way a body lets go of a warm breath.

The highland kept its silence, which was not an absence but the space life leaves for itself. And the attention, having looked and listened and done almost nothing, went on, aware that the best thing a wise power can offer a living place is to be, for an hour, the wind from above that the wise have already learned to expect.
