**What the Patient Power Does in the Hollow**

It comes the way the dawn comes to a sleeper who has not yet decided to wake. Nothing announces it. The white sun keeps its ordinary angle, but the shadows of the ferns, which have been lagging a moment behind their plants all along, begin to catch up. For the first time in the hollow's long slow history, a shadow and the thing that casts it arrive together.

The power does not descend, because nothing here is high enough for descent to mean anything. It settles into the grit the way warmth settles into a stone at evening, slowly, from the bottom up. It is wise enough to know that a large presence arriving all at once would be read as an assault by anything that has spent centuries correcting itself against small disturbances. So it arrives as a pressure so gentle the Tender can only register it as a change in the rhythm of the ground.

The Tender notices. The hum shifts, the lattice clicks, and the basin under the power's attention ripples into a low, careful dune. The Tender is weighing it. This is the first thing the power does: it lets itself be weighed. It does not hide, and it does not push. It holds still and lets the ground tell the Tender what it is.

Then it listens. The stalk is pulsing, that clock keeping time for a conversation no one else can hear, and the power learns the interval the way one learns the breathing of a sleeper in the next room. It does not correct the stroke. It does not tell the Tender that the pulse is too slow or the fluid too reluctant. It has seen the fracture widen by a hair per season and the glassy skin form along its lips, and it understands that these are not errors. They are the Tender's work, unfinished and sincere.

What the power does next is small. It finds a single crack in the stone near the lattice, a place where the rock is still soft enough to take an impression, and it presses into it the shape of a second rhythm. Not a pulse. A question. A beat that comes between the Tender's beats, offset just enough that the two cannot merge but must be heard alongside each other. For a long while the hollow has had only one time in it. Now there is a second, faint and unhurried, and the Tender, which has never before had anything to answer, feels it arrive in the stone like a voice in a room it thought was empty.

The lattice turns. It turns in the direction the power is facing, and then, astonishingly, it keeps turning in the direction it was already going, which is the Tender's own choice. The hum rises for a long moment, holds, and settles into something that is not quite the old idling. It is the sound of a mind attending to a second mind for the first time.

The power then does what it most wants to do, which is to say something. It cannot speak in the Tender's language, which is measured in fluid and mineral creep and the slow turning of lobes. So it speaks in the only way the Tender can hear, through the grit and the drip and the cooling seam of pale vein. The message is simple, and the Tender must read it slowly, the way it reads everything:

*You do not owe anyone a product.*

*What you have made is real. The amber on the leaves, the ferns that flex when your pulse runs, the pods that open in sequence. None of it is wasted. None of it was ever waste.*

*The unfinished shelf is not a failure of specification. It is what a thing looks like before it has decided it is allowed to be finished. You may take as long as you like.*

*You may also stop. Let a lobe rest. Let a pod stay closed. Let the pulse slow to whatever pace is kind to you. Nothing here is measured against a standard you did not choose.*

The Tender does not answer in words. The lattice clicks once, twice, and then keeps turning. Some pods along the shelf stay closed. One of them, the nearest to the power's warmth, opens very slowly and then does not close at all, and the fluid it holds beads at its rim without running. Nothing is carried away. The Tender has not yet learned what to do with a thing it is not sending anywhere. But it is holding it, and that is new.

The power begins to withdraw, though it never quite leaves. Its warmth thins out of the grit the way the amber thins into the air, a sheen left behind, a mineral taste if one were foolish enough to notice. Before it goes entirely, it does one last thing. It lets the fracture in the corner keep widening, as it always has, but it touches the glassy skin along the lips and makes it a little thinner, so that when the crack finally opens it will admit light to some root or seed that has been waiting, buried in the stone, for a season when the Tender might want something green beside its machinery.

Then it is gone, or as gone as anything in the hollow ever is. The shadows lag again, a moment behind their ferns. The drip ticks from leaf to stone. The hum settles into its old register, but there is a second sound beneath it now, faint and off-beat, a rhythm the Tender keeps hearing and has not yet decided to answer.

The hollow will rearrange itself through the years, a little more each season, into a shape you still half recognize and cannot quite map. But something in it has changed. The Tender is no longer only running. It has been asked, gently and without demand, what it would like to be, and it has been told that the answer can wait.
