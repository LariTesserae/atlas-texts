# The Attention

It did not arrive so much as become aware of itself already there, the way a person sitting in a quiet room notices a clock they have been hearing for an hour.

The power had been watching the ridge for a long time, the way one watches a country one loves from a great distance. It had seen the works grow on the far side, the long pale channels cut into the land, the plumes that rose and bent in the wind. It had seen the people who ran the works, most of them tired, a few of them careful, none of them looking down into the hollow, because nothing in their lives required them to. Their instruments measured flow and temperature and pressure. Nobody had been asked to measure what happened in the bowl at the end of the channel, and so nobody did.

Then the power turned its attention downward, through the gap in the rim, and saw the Mat.

It was not an unfamiliar kind of thing. The power had known many bodies that grew by taking in what the world gave them and making themselves of it. What struck it was the Mat's honesty. The Mat held nothing back. Every ring in every stalk was a true record of a year, and the records, read together, told a story that the Mat itself could not read.

The power read it.

---

The flow had been generous for a long time. Its warmth had lengthened the roots and thickened the leaves, and the Mat had grown toward it until the whole basin was one breathing green. This was not a tragedy. It was a gift that had been given without any consideration for who received it, and the Mat had made the best of it, as anything alive would.

But the gift carried a residue. Each season the channels left a little more of the mineral load in the pools, and the Mat, which could not refuse what came to it, took it in and wrapped it in wax. The wax was clever. The power admired it. But wax costs sugar, and the sugar was coming out of the towers faster each year. The amber in the walls was brighter, which looked like health, and was in fact the sign of a body storing more than it could safely spend. The green film was new in the recent rings, and the power recognized it at once: a bloom that would thicken, cloud the deep water, and eventually choke the very thing the Mat was trying to keep open.

The Mat had not yet noticed. It had no concept of trend. It had only the pulse, and the pulse was still strong, and so the Mat was still breathing.

The power understood that the Mat was not dying. It was, however, being slowly overfed on something that would one day be too much. And the people who had made the overfeeding did not know.

---

The power considered what it might do, and it was careful, because the power had learned over a long span of attention that the most dangerous thing a wise being can do is decide, on behalf of other lives, what their world should be.

It could stop the flow. The thought came easily, and it was wrong. The Mat had grown around this gift for generations. To withdraw it would be to starve a body that had built itself on that starvation's opposite, and the withdrawal would be violence, however gentle the hand that made it. The power set the idea aside.

It could flood the hollow with clean water, washing the pools clear. But then the Mat would grow toward the clean water and become dependent on a second gift, and the power would have made itself the new current the Mat was shaped by. That was no better.

It could move the Mat, or make the ridge passable. But the Mat had no way to will itself across dry ground, and the power would not reshape a landscape to suit its own sense of what was tidy.

It could speak to the Mat directly. The power considered this longest. It could make the Mat understand that the sugar was running low and the film was a warning. But the Mat did not hold sentences. It held rates and rings, and any message the power put into it would arrive as a change in growth that took a year to appear, and it would be understood as nothing at all, or as a disturbance, like wind. The power would be shouting into a voice that had only ever been a pulse.

It could stop the machinery. This was the easiest thing and the most tempting. A wise power could simply still the pumps, and the people beyond the ridge would wake to find the works silent, and nobody would ever know why. The power recognized the arrogance in that. Those people had lives, debts, children, reasons. A being that rearranged their world without their knowledge would be a tyrant no matter how kind its intentions.

So it chose none of these things, and what remained was smaller and slower and required patience.

---

The first thing it did was to ask the Mat for help, though the Mat did not know it was being asked.

Where the channel bent sharply before entering the hollow, the ground was softer than elsewhere. The power nudged a single root to lengthen a little more than it otherwise would have, and then a little more again, and the Mat, which had always grown toward warmth and mineral, followed the root without understanding why. Over several seasons a bank of reed and fluted stalk began to form at the bend, catching the heavier mineral silt before it reached the open pools. The clicking things in the litter found the new bank rich and multiplied there, turning the silt and making the bank deeper. It was the Mat's own body doing the work, a settling basin built from the Mat's own tissue, grown into the place where the residue was most easily caught. The power did not build it. It only encouraged a direction that was already there.

The second thing it did was to look beyond the ridge.

There was, in the works, a woman who worked nights at a monitoring station. She had a habit of reading old logs after her shift ended, not because she was required to but because she could not sleep when a chart did not make sense to her. For several months, the readings from the outflow channel had been drifting in a way that bothered her. Nothing was wrong enough to report. Nothing was right enough to ignore.

The power did not put a thought in her mind. It was not that kind of intervention, and she would have resented it if it had been. Instead it made a small thing more visible: a faint dip in a sensor's trace that she had overlooked, a number in a corner of a screen that she noticed one night and then noticed again. She pulled the older logs. She compared them to a survey map someone had filed years ago and never reread. She began to wonder what lay at the far end of the channel, the way people wonder about things they have been paying for without seeing.

It was not a rescue. It was the beginning of a question, and questions belong to the people who ask them. The power was content to let her find the answer, or not, in her own time and by her own reasoning.

The third thing it did was almost nothing at all. It let the Mat keep its breath. It let the frogs keep calling, the great beetles keep carrying pollen across the open air, and the larger thing beneath the surface keep grazing the pool edges and holding the water open. The power was not interested in a perfect hollow, cleaned and sterile and arranged. It was interested in a hollow that could still choose its own shape, even if the choices were being made under pressure it did not understand.

---

The power did not know if these things would be enough. It was not certain its reasoning was sound, and it held that uncertainty deliberately, because a wise being that stops doubting its own judgment has already begun to become something dangerous. The Mat's equilibrium might be sturdier than the power thought. The wax might hold longer than the sugar could be spent. The woman might look at the chart and find nothing she could act on. The works might keep running without change for another hundred years.

Or the power might be right, and the film might thicken, and the towers might fail, and the Mat would have to become something else entirely.

The power decided to return. It would not stay. It would not hover over the hollow like a guardian, because a guardian becomes a cage, and the Mat had lived well enough without one. It would come back in a season's rings, and read them, and see what had changed, and decide again whether to act or to wait.

Before it turned its attention away, it let itself feel the hollow once more: the cupped stillness of the water, the slow sweat of the stalks, the frog chorus rising from the pools and stopping together, the droplets falling in their patient rhythm onto the broad leaves.

Somewhere down in the wet dark, a single frond leaned a degree closer to the warmth of something it could not name.

The power, which had been called many things by many kinds of being, thought that it would be enough to be a disturbance that left the right kind of mark, and then went quietly back out through the gap in the ridge, the way the rain does when it has done what rain is for.
