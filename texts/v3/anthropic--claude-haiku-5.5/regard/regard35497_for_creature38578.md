# What the Watching Power Did

It came the way weather comes to a far valley, first as a change in the quality of attention. For a long time it did nothing but listen.

It listened to the plates settling, a crackle every few weeks after rain. It listened to the grazers scraping across the mud, to the reeds rattling, to the thin whine of the concretions when the air went still before a storm. It listened to the deep pool, where almost no sound was made at all. It was patient in a way the basin itself was patient, and so it could hear what the basin was saying, which was very little, slowly.

It understood quickly that the basin did not want to be rescued. The cliffs were not in danger in any sense the cliffs would recognize. The pans would dry and fill, rise and fall, and no creature here was suffering in a way that needed mending. A kinder hand, in other places, might have coaxed rain from a dry sky or taken the salt off the plates so that roots could come up through them. Here that would have been a kind of violence, a rewriting of a mind that had taken millennia to draw its outline. The power had seen enough of the world to know that good intentions, applied to something this old, leave scars.

So it chose restraint, and then it chose to notice.

---

It went first to the stones.

They lay in the silt where they had been left, dark and fine-grained, their edges struck with a few deliberate blows. The crust had grown over several of them. The power could feel the long hands that had made them, a person who had sat somewhere near here, perhaps on a warm evening, perhaps in a season when the pans were full and the grazers were thick along the margin. That person had shaped an edge, looked at it, and set it down. They had not left a hearth or a midden or a path. They had come, worked, and gone, and the basin had taken them in without comment.

The power would not take the stones away. Lifting them out would have been a theft from the basin, which had been holding them as a body holds a scar. But the power could not bear for the makers to be lost entirely, dissolved into the floor with nothing outside the basin to remember them.

So it did something small and careful. It reached out, past the rim, past the country that had gone on being shaped by people for many thousands of years, to the lines of descent that had carried that kind of skill forward. Somewhere, a hand had struck a similar edge in a similar way and held it up to the light. It was not a message, and it could not be one. It was a resonance, a continuity of practice threading across time. The power let that resonance pass through the place in the way wind passes through the concretions, faintly, so that anyone who one day sat on the rim and turned a stone over in their fingers would feel, without knowing why, that they were not the first.

The stones did not change. The basin did not notice, or if it did, it noticed as it noticed everything, by absorbing it. But the power had made sure that the story of the stones existed somewhere beyond the basin's keeping.

---

Then it turned to the basin itself, and wondered whether it could say anything at all.

Speech, in the sense the power understood, was impossible here. The basin's language was measured in centimetres of mud and decades of plate-lift. Any message sent through the rock would take a lifetime to arrive and would be read as weather. But the power had a way of working that did not require a single moment to hold its meaning. It could leave a mark that the basin would read in its own time.

It chose the central pool.

The concentric rings along the margin were the closest thing the basin had to memory. Each ring took years to build, and the older ones were worn into something close to forgetting. The power did not add a ring. That would have been an imposition. Instead, it altered the one forming now, at the very edge of the water, very slightly. It made the mud rise a fraction of a millimetre higher in one arc than the rest, and it made that arc settle a little more gently than the others. The change was invisible to any eye. It would be read, eventually, by the silt itself, the way a body reads a small pressure it has learned to expect.

It meant: *I have listened. You are not alone in the attention of the world. Something has noticed you, and has not wanted to change you.*

The basin would not understand this as a sentence. It would simply register that the margin had been touched gently, once, by something that did not press. That would be enough. A place that keeps everything it is given can also keep one kind regard.

---

Last, the power considered the living things.

The grazers were fine. They moved through their cycles with the seasons, and their aggregations thickened on the last mud and dispersed when the rain returned. The translucent drifter fed on its crust and fed the crust in turn. The lizards flattened themselves into the plate-gaps when something large approached, and the long-winged bird still dropped into the basin now and then with its sudden stillness. The power did not add a single new species or remove an old one. It had no wish to be a gardener.

But it lingered a while over the shrubs, which had sent their roots a dozen metres sideways to find a single seep. It admired that. A plant that reaches so far to find so little has an intimacy with its world that most creatures never achieve. The power let the roots go on reaching, and it let the seeps stay where they were.

---

When it was done, the power withdrew.

It did not leave the basin altered in any way a visitor would see. The plates still rose and fell. The rain still came a few times a year, sometimes in one afternoon's violent downpour. The cliffs still retreated at the pace of fingernails per century, the light still caught fire in the early morning and burned flat at noon. The pool still lay green-black and very still.

But the stones had a witness now, outside the basin. The margin of the pool carried one slightly raised arc, a sentence too faint to read. And the wind, when it came up the channels before the next storm, carried a note that was almost the same as before and yet not quite.

The basin, which had been awake for longer than the word had meant anything, went on thinking in plates and rings and rainfall. It noticed the change, perhaps, in the way a sleeper notices a hand laid lightly on the shoulder, and it did what it had always done. It let the touch settle into itself. It kept it.

The power, having done what a wise and kind attention can do, which is very little and very precisely, turned its gaze elsewhere, trusting the basin to hold what it had been given, and trusting the rain to keep coming.
