# What the Patient Power Would Do

It does not arrive as a storm or a light. It arrives the way the basin itself changes: slowly enough that no one notices until the change is already complete. Over several winters, the pitch of certain walls drifts a little higher, and the fog settles into the lower terraces a few days earlier each year. Attention gathers over Harrow Sill the way meltwater gathers at the northern rim, and then it begins to move through the stone.

The power that notices the basin is patient, and its patience is its first kind of wisdom. It knows that a mind thinking at the speed of frost cannot be hurried, and that anything hasty done to it would be a kind of violence, however well meant. So its first act is to slow itself down until it matches the place. A thousand years pass in what feels to it like a long afternoon, and it does not mind.

## What It Would Not Do

The strongest temptation would be repair. The builders' plan is still legible: the stairways, the cisterns, the straight channels running toward the lip. The power could set the lintels back, straighten the terraces, and send the water back along its original paths. The old city would reappear, tidy and dry, and the basin would be put back where the builders wanted it.

It would not do this, and the reasons are the center of its wisdom. The basin is not a ruin waiting to be restored. It is a mind in the middle of deciding what it is, and the builders' plan is one voice in a conversation that has been going on for millennia between stone, water, root, and the people who once walked there. To straighten the channels would be to end the conversation by choosing a side. It would replace the basin's becoming with the power's preference, and a benevolent mind knows that a good outcome imposed from outside is still a theft of the one thing that made the place worth caring about.

For the same reason it would not stop the waterfall, reroute the meanders, or silence the ringing walls. The roar is the basin thinking aloud. The power would hear it as a voice, not as noise to be quieted.

It would also refrain from speaking directly. A voice from above, arriving in the shape of a dream or a sign, would be the loudest thing that had ever happened to the basin, and the basin would have to organize its entire slow self around answering it. The power is careful not to become the most important fact in the place's life.

## What It Would Do

Restraint is not the same as inaction. A wise power, having decided what not to touch, would look for the small things that protect the basin's freedom without deciding its shape.

**It would keep the record.** The builders are gone and the basin is rewriting itself, which means the past is in danger of being forgotten by both parties. The power would not preserve the old city in its original form, but it would make sure that the rings of the basalt, the lines of the old channels, and the shape of the terraces remain readable to anyone who looks carefully. It would be the witness that neither the builders nor the basin can be, the one party with no stake in the outcome, keeping an honest account so that the negotiation has a memory that does not belong to either side.

**It would tend the edges.** The basin is still deciding, but it is not deciding in a vacuum. Winters grow harsher in some years and milder in others, and a single unusual frost can undo centuries of slow settling. The power would not make the weather kind. It would only soften the extremes enough that the basin's decisions remain its own and are not made under duress, the way a gardener might keep a late frost from killing a seedling without deciding which way the vine should grow.

**It would protect the small lives.** The grey birds, the pale burrowers, and the lungs and foragers of the basin are fragile in a way the basin's stone is not. If they were lost, the basin's attention would thin, and its ability to think in its own way would be damaged. The power would make sure their seasons remain in balance, not to direct them, but to keep the basin's nervous system intact.

**It would look after the visitors.** The people who stay too long in the basin carry its pitch home in their ears and their voices, and some of them carry it for years. The power would consider this carefully. The basin tunes people the way a stone is tuned when it is set in a wall, and that is not necessarily harm. But a visitor who leaves with a pitch that is not theirs, or who cannot leave at all, has been taken rather than met. So the power would arrange, quietly, that the ringing fades for anyone who crosses the northern rim, and that the pitch they carry is only what they themselves chose to learn. Whatever they bring home would be their own, and the basin would be free to tune whoever stays.

## How It Would Ask

The hardest problem is that the power cannot know what the basin wants, and it would not presume to. A mind thinking at frost speed cannot answer a question posed in words. But it can answer a question posed in stone.

So the power would ask the only way that fits. It would choose one cistern on the middle terrace, one of the cracked ones, and leave a gap in its wall: not a repair, not a new channel, just an opening where the water might go if it chose. Then it would wait. A year would pass, then several. If the water took the gap, the power would learn something about the basin's wishes. If the water ignored it, that would be an answer too.

The power would also listen. It would strike a single stone on the lower terrace each winter, the same stone, with the same weight, and it would listen for whether the ring changed. This is the only conversation it would attempt. Each winter the pitch might shift a little, or not at all, and the power would take either as a reply, neither more nor less important than the other.

## Its Last Note

Eventually the power would have to accept that it does not understand the basin, and that it may never fully understand it. This would not trouble it. Understanding is not the goal. The goal is to be a presence that does not overwhelm what it sees, one that can witness without taking, protect without possessing, and ask without demanding an answer.

Years later, a traveler might sit on a cold cistern step in the spray and strike a basalt slab with a stone. The ring would come back a little different from the last time anyone struck it, a little more certain of itself. The traveler would not know that the sound was also a reply, carried in the stone to a power that had spent a long time listening and had decided, for once, to be only a witness.

The basin would go on deciding what it would become. The power would go on listening. Neither would be finished, and both, in their slow way, would be glad of it.
