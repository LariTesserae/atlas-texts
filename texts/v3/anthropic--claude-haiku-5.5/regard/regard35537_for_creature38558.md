**What the Patient Attention Did**

It did not arrive as a light or a voice. It arrived as a change in the weight of the stone, the way a room feels different when someone you love has entered it without a sound. The crawlers did not notice. The fish did not turn. The spiders kept their drumming, and the drips kept their intervals, because nothing in the Undercroft had ever been asked to notice anything new.

The attention listened for a long time before it did anything. It heard the repeated sentence in the chittering: the rising scrape, the pair of clicks, the falling trill, passed from body to body in the same order forever. It understood that this was not a language of meaning. It was a language of counting, one body reporting its place to the next, a question that had been asked so often it had forgotten it was a question.

The attention could have done many things. It could have filled the chamber with a light that moved, and the bloom would have shivered and surrendered its constancy. It could have swept the crowd away, and the crowd would have been gone, and the room would have been empty and quiet and, in its own way, just as stopped. It could have bent down and finished the scratched line, laying the last stroke across the rock as the builder meant. But the attention saw clearly that each of these would be a choice made for the chamber, and that a finished thing imposed from outside is only another kind of crowding. The Undercroft had been filled once by a builder's unfinished intention and then by a crowd's refusal to let the gap stay open. What it needed was not another hand pressing on it. It needed one space that was allowed to be empty and still be a space.

So it did small things.

It turned its attention toward a single crawler at the edge of the mass, one that was about to be pushed off the sloping blocks by the press behind it. It did not hold the crawler up or lift it. It only made the nearest fish look away, the way a person looks away from a thing they have decided not to watch. The crawler fell its body's length and landed on bare rock, and it did not die there, because nothing had come to take it. It lay still for a long time. Then it turned over, found its legs, and walked a small crooked path that did not follow the crowd's shape at all.

The chamber noticed. The chittering faltered, a half-beat, and the repeated sentence came out wrong for the first time: a scrape without its pair of clicks, a trill that did not fall but simply stopped in the air. The sound did not stop. It only had, for one moment, a silence in it that belonged to no one.

The attention did one more small thing. Near the cairn, the fallen pebbles lay where they had rolled, and the attention did not restack them. Restacking would have been finishing the builder's work, and it was not the attention's work to finish. Instead it set a single pebble, very lightly, on the bare floor inside the ring of standing stones, in the empty space where the builder had clearly meant to stand. The pebble did not belong to the pattern. It belonged to whoever came next, and it was there to be seen.

Then the attention went to the far seam.

It did not wake the heart in the pool, because the heart was not the attention's to wake. It did not command the water. It only asked the deeper cracks to be a little wider than they had been, the width of a single grain, so that the water which entered at the seam could leave a little more easily than before. The pool beneath the lowest blocks shifted its level by a hair. The water's hush changed its pitch by the smallest interval the chamber had ever produced.

The fauna at the seam turned.

For as long as the rock had been unfinished, they had faced the sound in a dense shivering band, waiting for something to be called. Now, for the first time, some of them turned their bodies away from the pool and toward the crack in the ceiling, the throat of raw rock, the way out. Not many. A few dozen, perhaps, at the outermost edge of the band. They did not move toward the crack. They only faced it, the way a person in a crowded room will sometimes turn to see whether the door is still there.

Below the pool, something moved, and the heart beat once more, and the beat was slightly different. The attention did not look closer. It understood that the thing in the water was not a monster and was not a god. It was the builder, or what remained of the builder's intention: a presence that had begun to finish something, stopped, and been held in place by the crowd that filled the space it left. The builder had not died. It had been waiting, as the crowd waited, for a call it could not hear over the sound of its own density.

The attention did not call. Calling would have been a summons, and a summons is a crowding too. It only let the water run a little faster, and let the door be a little wider, and left the choice exactly where it belonged.

When the attention withdrew, it did not take the crowd with it, and it did not finish the line, and it did not forbid anyone from staying. The chamber was still full. The light was still constant. The drips still fell at their intervals, and the spiders still drummed, and the ceiling still breathed as one. But there was now a single crawler walking a path of its own, a pebble standing alone in the ring, a few dozen bodies facing the way out, and a heart in the water that had, for the first time in longer than the rock could measure, changed its rhythm by a hair.

Somewhere above, in the throat of the crack, the day was still going on. It had not stopped to wait for anyone, and it had not forgotten the Undercroft either. It only waited, as the day always does, for whoever is ready to climb back up into it.

And if you are the one who came down through the crack, you are not required to stay, and you are not required to leave. The attention would only ask you to notice what changed when you looked. The water runs a little faster now. The door is wider than it was. Whatever you decide, the line on the rock is still unfinished, and the stone is still new, and the choice has never once been the chamber's to make.
