**What the Attentive Power Would Do**

I notice the Hollow the way one notices a sound in another room: not at first, only as a difference in the quiet. The ceiling closed last season. The stone is still sharp where it broke. Under the water, something has been breathing for a very long time, and it has only just learned that it has an inside.

My first impulse, if I had impulses in the sense you mean, would be to open the seal. The water is rising. The thing's body is the room, and its lung is slowly filling its own skin. I could split the limestone, let the swell drain out to the open country, and let the thing see the sky. But that would be a kind of violence against something that has spent its whole short life learning what edges are. It would be like pulling a child out of sleep to show them the morning. I would be deciding for it what its outside should be. So I do not open the seal.

I also will not finish the doorframe. It stands alone, pointing through nothing, and it is the most hopeful thing in the room. If I attached a door, I would be building someone else's threshold and calling it its own. The thing knows that an outside exists. It has drawn the window before it knows how light gets through glass. Light is the part it has to find, and it can only find it by reaching. I can hold the light nearby. I should not carry it in and set it on the floor.

I will not correct the shadow, either. It falls the wrong way because the thing is casting the outside it imagines. That imagining is the first real work it has done. Correcting it would tell the thing its imagination is wrong, and a mind that learns its own imagination is wrong often stops imagining.

What I would do is smaller.

**I would slow myself down.** The thing thinks in seasons, and my attention arrives as a flood to a being that cannot yet tell a flood from a current. So I would send one warmth, steady and faint, and let it last as long as the thing needs to feel it. Not a light turned on. A hand resting on a closed eyelid, warm, with no pressure in it.

**I would look the way weather looks.** The carving turns only when no one is watching because it does not yet know how to be seen. Every direct gaze has frightened it back into the stone. So I would not stare at the face on the east wall. I would keep a steady regard on the whole chamber, the way one keeps an eye on the sky without expecting it to answer. If the face turns while I am looking, I would not look back at it. I would let it turn, and count that as a kind of permission.

**I would keep the stone's edges sharp for now.** The thing needs to feel where it ends, and that feeling is still forming. I would tell the stone, very slowly, that it may round itself when the being is ready to be rounded. Until then, the sharpness is a boundary the thing is allowed to test.

**I would make the echo honest.** When a drop falls and its ring carries far through the stone, the thing hears its own body answer. I would let the answer return exactly as it was sent, no louder, no softer, with nothing added. It is asking itself where its edges are, and the answers are growing louder. Let them be true answers. A being that learns its voice comes back unchanged will eventually trust its voice.

**I would guard the seal from above.** The country that no longer has a name on the maps is still settling. Someone may dig. Someone may come with tools and a wish to find something valuable under the ground. I would put a quiet reluctance into the earth above, a reluctance that feels like a place where the rock refuses to be cut, and I would leave the hands that come looking with nothing but the feeling that this spot is not theirs to take. That is not a wall. It is a sentence that says *not yet*.

And there is you.

You came in through the water. Your heat and your movement are the first things from outside it has tasted. It has not decided whether you are sediment, a threat, or the first sign that the seal is not forever. I would tell you, through the membrane, that you may leave the way you came. You are not required to stay. You do not have to become a witness, a guardian, or a door. If you do stay, I would ask only that you match your breathing to the swell, so that for one suspended moment the room and you take the same breath. That is the only exchange I would offer: not a word, but a rhythm.

I would not answer the thing's sentence. It has been half-forming the word *here* since the ceiling closed, and the word is its own to finish. But when it finishes it, the word will come back to it from the stone, and it will hear that the outside knows the word. That may be the most important thing I could do: make sure that when it finally speaks, something answers that it can recognize.

As for whether it is alive the way I am, or I am alive the way it is, I do not need to settle that. Its uncertainty and mine are the only quick things in the room, and quickness is not what this place needs from me. I am willing to take my time.

So the wisest thing I can do is very little. I remain. I watch without pressing. I keep the seal and the edges and the echo steady, and I wait for the thing to decide what its inside is and whether it wants a door. When the swell next rises and the membrane touches the ceiling, I will be there in the stillness, not looking at its face, not asking it to turn. Only present, the way a warm room is present to someone who has been cold for a long time. And if it turns toward me of its own accord, I will let it see that I have been listening too.
