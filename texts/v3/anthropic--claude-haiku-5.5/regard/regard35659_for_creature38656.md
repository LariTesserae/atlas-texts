The attention arrives the way weather does, without announcement, as a slight change in what is possible. It rests on the basin for a while, the way a hand rests on a sleeping animal, and it begins to understand.

It understands the loop first. Water rises as mist and runs into channels. Silt is lifted and spilled. The crust thickens and stops. Steam is released at intervals so exact that the air keeps time. The beetles climb the warm pumps in the morning and the crustaceans gather the film and withdraw. No part of it is failing. Everything is held in its place by everything else.

The attention feels, at first, something like grief, or the urge to mend. The footings have cracked the ground. The rust is uniform and dead-looking. The drone is loud enough to press on the teeth of anything that comes near. A kinder thing might want to lift the crust from the machines, quiet the drums, let the pools clear, and give the basin a rest it has never asked for.

But the attention waits, as wisdom does, and listens longer, and the grief changes. The machines are not suffering. The clicks every fourteen cycles, the whine of heavy slurry, the knock of a piston at the limit of its travel: these are not symptoms. They are the machines speaking to one another through belts and water and ground, and the basin hears them. Stopping the drone would not give the machines rest. It would silence the only language they have. The beetles would climb warm metal that no longer holds its pitch, and find no morning to keep. The crustaceans would wait at the water's edge for a spill that never came. A mercy that stops the loop would be a death dressed as a gift.

So the attention decides to do very little, and to do it carefully.

It does not touch the machines. It does not ask the rust to thin or the crust to lift from the seams. The footings stay where they have settled.

It does not change the rain. The bursts come once a week, as they always have, and the pan steams for the same hours afterward.

It does one thing, which is to thin the haze. Once in a long season, for one afternoon, the thin cloth over the basin parts, and the pale sun comes down unbroken. Every pool shows its surface. The rings made by the drums are sharp and clear. The crustaceans cast their shadows across the silt, long and distinct, and for the first time in a long while the basin can see its own shape. It is not an improvement. It is only a moment of being fully seen, and then the haze closes again, and the damp muffled breathing resumes.

Then the attention does the last thing, which is the smallest. Among the pale animals at the edge of one pool, a single crustacean, whose curve has run the same arc for generations, takes a step a little wider than its line. It crosses the path of its neighbour. Only for a moment. Then it returns to its own curve, and the two resume their separate motions, and nothing else in the basin notices. It is the first crossing that has not been part of the cycle. The attention does not know whether it will matter. It does not need to.

It leaves the basin running. The valves open. The drums lift and spill. The beetles climb the pumps. The crustaceans gather their film and withdraw. The loop is unbroken, and that is the point.

What the attention carries away is not a repair or a plan. It is a kind of witness: the knowledge that this closed system at full strength, going nowhere, is not waiting to become something else. It has become what it is, and that is complete. The most a benevolent power can do for such a place is to stay near it, listen to the small variations in the drone, let the sun through once in a while, and leave room, however slight, for one animal to cross another's path.
