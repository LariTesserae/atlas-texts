# What the Attentive Power Would Do

It noticed the shelf first as sound.

Long before it had any interest in the resin or the grazers or the small handprints, it heard the reeds, a chord rising and falling over a grey world where most places were quiet. It turned toward the noise the way a person turns toward a voice in another room. Then it began to listen, and it kept listening for a long time before it did anything at all.

What it heard was a conversation. The reeds sang, the grazers hummed back, the mat's resin brightened when the chord was full, and the tenders sang low and were answered. The power recognised the shape of a system that corrects itself. The mat lost lobes and healed faster when the herds were near. The quiet hunters were answered by the warning chorus of the reeds. The plates broke, the dust buried stands and then moved on, and each loss pulled something new up through the old. Nothing was in balance, but everything was in relation, and the relations held.

Its first reasoning was simple: *I should not improve this.*

It could have done a great deal. It could have cooled the breath beneath, or stilled the fissures, or spread the mat across the grey shelves below the fall and filled the bare ground with green. It could have made the resin hard and permanent, so the wounds never reopened, or given the grazers a larger range, or stopped the storms that clear the dust. Each of these would have looked like a kindness from outside. The power understood that from inside the shelf, each would be a tuning forced on an instrument that had spent deep time learning to tune itself. The shelf had survived by being loud, moving, and never still. Stilling it would not keep it alive. It would be a kind of silence, and silence was what the shelf had been built to answer.

So it did not touch the stone or the breath. It did not reach into the vapour rising through the plates, which was not its to handle. The breath beneath was the one thing on the shelf it could not understand from the surface, and the power was too wise to pretend otherwise.

---

What it did do was small, and it was done to the conversation rather than to the land.

First, it listened to the tenders. It heard that they sang to the shelf and that the shelf sang back, and it understood that this exchange was the most important thing on the plateau. The tenders were not caretakers of a resource. They were the ones who kept the call-and-response going, and a call with no one to answer it is just noise. The power resolved not to answer in a way that overrode them. It would answer in their register, and let the reeds carry the reply.

So one evening, when the dusk-lanterns were lit and the reeds were singing their usual chords, the wind shifted just slightly, and a single reed that had never sung before began to hum a pitch between two of the existing ones. It was not a new note for the shelf. It was a passing tone that resolved into the old chord and made the whole chord warmer. The grazers stopped their stripping and turned toward the sound. Then they hummed back, and the reeds took up their hum, and the sound drifted all the way to the cliff's edge. The tenders heard it and sang the next line in the same key. The power said nothing more. That was the whole of its speech.

Second, it thought about endings, which was the one thing the shelf could not think about for itself.

The power had seen the older shelves below, buried under dust, their green gone to grey. It had seen the cracks in these plates, and the plates sliding off the edge with the sound of a shot. It understood that this shelf would not last forever. Some plate would one day let go and take a stand of stalks, or a cairn line, or the slab-walled room with the handprints, with it. The breath beneath might shift, or go deeper, or stop. The tenders had named none of this. They described the crack as the breath turning in its sleep, which was a more hopeful story than geology, and perhaps more accurate in the ways that count.

The power's wisdom was not to prevent the ending. It was to make sure the conversation outlived any one place in it.

So it did something very gentle. It let the wind carry a few seeds of the mat, and a few stalks' worth of the reeds' hollow spores, along the paths the wind already travelled. It did not choose the destinations. It did not plant a new shelf. It let the current that already blew across the plateau carry a fraction of the green out over the fall, into the grey haze, to any ledge where the breath might rise through a fissure and the roots could find salt. If those seeds found nothing, the wind would have lost nothing. If they found a crack in some other stone and began to hum, then somewhere the conversation would still go on, and it would not depend on one set of plates or one line of cairns.

Third, it did not interfere with the way the shelf chose to remember. The handprints in the slab room would fade. The dust would keep settling in their creases, and a child who was brought here would eventually be as old as the prints were when they were made. The power did not preserve them. It judged that a record kept by stone would never be as good as a record kept by people who still came to the room, pressed their hands to the wall, and learned how to listen. The record was alive because it was used. A preserved record would be a tomb, and the shelf had already been through enough tombs.

---

The power did not stay long. It was not a shepherd and did not wish to be one. It left the shelf with its own sound intact, and it carried away what it had learned: that the best care is attention. You do not need to fix a living thing to honour it. You need to hear it clearly enough that you know when it is asking for something, and to answer in a voice it can use.

Below the fall, the haze drifted over the old shelves. The new seeds lifted into the wind and went out over the edge. On the plateau, the reeds went on singing their chords, and the grazers hummed back, and the mat bloomed in a single night and sagged a week later and bloomed again. A tender walked the lines of cairns the storm had rebuilt. Somewhere in the stone, the breath rose through a fissure and did whatever a breath does when it is far below and has no way of knowing that anyone is listening.

The shelf was still becoming something else. The power thought that was exactly right. A world that can't change can't answer, and one that can answer is always, in its loud green way, a little bit alive.
