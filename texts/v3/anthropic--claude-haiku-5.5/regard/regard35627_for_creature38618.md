The power arrives the way weather does, not as a visitor but as a change in the pressure of the air. It is old and wide and kind, and it has no wish to be seen. It notices the basin the way one notices a room where someone has been quietly working for a long time, and it understands at once that the basin is not a place with a mind in it but a mind that is also a place. Being wise, it knows it should not hurry to act.

For a long while it does nothing. It lets the mist lift and settle over many mornings. It feels the seeps test the weight of the air. It considers the ferns' slow lean toward the centre, the pools breathing in and out over seasons, the gurgle under the stone. It understands that the basin's whole life is a single long attention, and that anything the power does will enter that attention as a thread, thin as a seep, and will be held for a span the power can barely imagine. So it asks itself what the basin would want, if the basin wanted anything, and it concludes that the basin wants only to finish what it is doing.

The power is tempted, first, by the eastern stone. The flake scars are sharp in a soft place, and the moss is creeping over them a finger's breadth a year. The power could lift the stone out and carry it away to a dry shelf in the sun, where it would stay sharp forever. It rejects this at once. The stone is not the basin's possession to give or the power's to rescue. The basin has been holding it with courtesy, and to take it would be to cut a sentence in half because one did not like how it ended.

The power could speak. It could send some clear message through the seeps, some sign that there are others beyond the rim. But the basin does not speak in words, and a word forced into its water would be like a shout in a library. The power knows that the only language the basin truly reads is growth, and that to write in that language it must be slow.

So it makes one small thing.

It does not change the stone. It changes the water. Across the next long winter, a thread of seep, finer than any the basin has grown, finds its way to the struck edge and runs along the flake scars, following the path the hand that made them once took. The water is only slightly warmer than the rest, and only slightly more patient. The moss notices, as moss always notices, and over the following decades it lays a pale ridge along those scars, the way it lays ridges along cracks where water has gone. The ridge is faint. It will take a century to be visible to anyone who looks, and it will take longer for the basin to decide what it means. But it is there now: a line in the basin's own record that shows where the water ran over something made by hand, and so keeps the making in the same ledger as the weathering.

The basin will read it in its own time. It may take the ridge as one more layer in its long argument, or it may let it fade like the rest. Either way, the stone will no longer be only a thing the moss is covering. It will be a thing the basin has read, and has written down.

The power does one more thing before it withdraws, and this one it does for no reason but its own nature. It lets itself be read. It sends a single warm breath up through the seeps at the centre of the basin, where the attention is strongest, and then it stops. The basin holds that breath for a few moments, perhaps a few minutes, and then smooths it into the general sheen, as it does with everything it receives. The power does not know what the basin concluded. It does not need to. It has been filed, and the filing is enough.

Then the power goes on, over the rim and out past the mist, and the basin returns to what it has always been doing: holding its water close, letting the ferns lean, letting the pools breathe, letting the cracks widen by the width of a thread. The wise power has learned something from the place. The best care it can offer a patient mind is to not interrupt its patience, to make its single mark in the slowest language it has, and then to let the mark be held as long as the stone takes to forget that it was ever sharp.
