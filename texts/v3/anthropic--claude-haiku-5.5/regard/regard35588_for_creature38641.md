**The Visitor**

Nothing announces the power. There is no light brighter than the light already there, no sound louder than the drip of water. What happens is that the chamber notices something, and then it stops moving for a moment, the way a herd of grazers stops when one of them lifts its head.

The power is not a body and does not come down through the vault. It arrives the way attention arrives, all at once and without a direction. The seam in the ceiling brightens, then dims, then brightens again, slowly, as if something is reading it.

For a long time, the power does nothing.

This is the first thing it understands. The chamber is provisional. Every act here, every column finished or every crack sealed, closes a door that the place has not yet decided to close. A wise power that wanted to fix things would have to be wary of fixing them into a shape that would not hold anything new. So it listens. It follows the grazers' tracks as they fade in a breath and as they last for hours. It watches a column grow in the dark and recede when it is seen. It reads the tally: what is becoming, what is coming undone, and the way each entry is revised before the ink dries.

Then it makes its first choice, which is to make very little.

---

**The Basin**

The power finds a small stone on the grit near the water's edge. It is not a cairn stone and not part of any arch. It is just a stone, rounded by the chamber's long habit of breaking things and setting them down again.

It drops the stone into the basin.

The surface breaks. The ripples go out in their usual rings, and the eels, which have been coiled and patient for longer than the chamber has had a name for, stir in the dark beneath the still skin of the water. The ripples reach the edges of the basin. They begin, as they always do, to draw back toward their source.

But the power does not reach down to still them, and it does not ask the water to keep them. It only leaves the stone where it is, sunk into the mineral, and lets the ripple go on spreading.

For a while the rings do not return. The basin holds an open disturbance, a thing that has been started and not folded back into the pool's memory. The eels rise, slowly, and sink again. One of them circles the place where the stone landed. It does not take a grazer. It seems to be deciding what this is.

Then the basin settles, and it reflects its impossible sky, and somewhere in the water a ripple is still going outward, unfinished. The eels have learned something new: that the memory of a thing can be kept without being undone.

---

**The Still Ones**

At the bottom of the hummocks, the grazers lie pale and motionless, and the film is growing over them. The power does not know whether they are sleeping, dying, or becoming floor. It considers this carefully, because all three are real, and because a wise power does not confuse its own wish to rescue with the good of the thing it wants to rescue.

So it does not lift them. It does not pull the film away or try to coax them back into the herd. It lays a kind of attention over them that asks nothing: *you may go on as you are, or you may return, and either way you are counted.*

Then it does the one thing that can be done for them without deciding their fate. It makes a record. It presses a mark into the wall beside the nearest hummock, a plain notch with no pattern and no meaning except that it says *these were here.* The tracks of the living grazers pass over the notch, polish it a little, and leave it standing. The still ones are no longer only erased. They are noted.

---

**The Spinner**

The spinner's lines have fused to the vault, and where they touch the seam, the glow is brightest. It has been measuring the cracks for so long that the measurement has become part of the ceiling.

The power does not cut the silk. It sends a thread of its own attention along one of the old lines, following it to where it meets the stone, and at that point it does something the spinner has never had: it gives the measurement a reading back. The seam, which has been opening and sealing according to no rule anyone has learned, shows its pattern for a moment, a slow pulse of close and open, and the spinner, for the first time, can see the whole of it.

The spinner stops descending. It hangs from its line and does not move for a long time. Then it begins, very carefully, to extend a new line, not down toward the grazers and moths but sideways, along the seam, toward the place where the crack is widest. It is no longer measuring only what is closing. It is following what might open.

---

**The Moths**

The moths carry light up to the vault, but the light has nowhere to go. It pools in the lichen and rises only as far as their wings can take it, and the ceiling, however bright, is still a ceiling.

The power finds the widest of the cracks, the one the spinner is now following, and it asks the stone for a single thing: that it stay open at one point. Not wide, not permanent, just open enough for a moth's wing to pass through without folding.

The stone is still soft enough to agree. A hairline gap opens in the vault, and through it, very faintly, the light of the plain above comes down. It is not the sky of the basin. It is weaker and colder and less bright, and it has rain in it.

Several moths settle at the gap, fold their wings, and go through. They do not come back. But the next time one comes down the chamber and settles on a glowing thread, it does not carry its light into the ceiling. It carries it toward the gap, and the light goes out.

---

**The Builders**

This is the part the power has been circling since it arrived.

The arch leans over the passage with its keystone missing and its ends curling inward. The cairns stand with their stones fused at the contact points. The scratches repeat and then change. The air near the arch sometimes takes the shape of a hand that stopped halfway through a gesture.

The power does not finish the arch.

It understands, reasoning slowly, that the arch was not left incomplete by accident. The builders stacked the cairns with precision, and then they stopped, and the chamber has been trying to finish their work ever since, the way water trying to finish a stone only wears it smooth. Something built those things and meant to come back. Or something built them and could not come back, and has been waiting in the curl of the arch for someone to ask.

So the power asks.

It does not place a keystone. It does not straighten the curling ends. It places one flat stone beside the arch, not joined to it, the way a cairn is begun, and it leaves the space where the keystone would go empty. Then it turns its attention to the hand in the air and does not try to make it clearer. It simply holds the question steady: *what were you building?*

The air shape wavers. For the first time, it does not blur when looked at directly. The hand opens. Then it closes, and the gesture seems to finish itself, not with a keystone or a completed arch, but with a pointing. The hand points toward the gap in the ceiling, toward the rain-thin light, toward the plain that is only a rumor.

The scratches on the wall shift once more. The pattern they make does not repeat. It moves outward, toward the crack, and stops.

The power reads it, or believes it does. *Not here. Not like this. Let it stay half, and let something go out through it.*

Then the air is only air, and the arch is only an arch, and the builders are either gone or waiting, and it no longer matters which, because the question has been asked and the answer has been given in the only language the place has ever really used.

---

**What Remains**

The power does not stay. It has no need to. A wise power that lingered would become one more presence the chamber had to arrange itself around, and this place has enough of those.

Before it leaves, it looks once more at the whole of the hollow. The basin holds its open ripple. The still grazers are noted in their notch. The spinner is following the seam toward the gap. The moths carry their light toward the sky. The arch remains half-built, its keystone space empty, its ends curled, its builders either absent or patient. The cracks open and close. The drops fall, one every few seconds, never quite in rhythm.

The tally goes on. Things are still becoming and coming undone. Nothing has been finished, and nothing has been erased more than it needed to be. But there is one new entry now, written in the water and in the wall and in the light that goes out through the gap: a thing begun and not folded back, a disturbance allowed to spread.

The chamber does not know it has been visited. But it has learned, very slightly, that a not-deciding can be a kind of openness. And the place, which has never settled on being anything, goes on being alive in the only way it has ever been, which is to keep living in the question.
