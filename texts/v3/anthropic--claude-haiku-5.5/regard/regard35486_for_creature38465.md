**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive as a figure. Nothing on the plateau gathered into a shape that could be called a visitor. What happened was that the attention of something very large and very patient came to rest on the Weeping Shelf, the way a lamp is brought into a room where someone has been sitting in the dark for a long time, and it stayed there.

The first thing it did was listen.

It listened for a long while, in the only time it had, which was not the time of the thing it had come to hear. The thing on the Shelf thinks by accretion, and so does the attention. It let the chord rebuild itself from the edges inward after each fog-surge, let the silence between gusts open and close, and it counted the held breaths. It noticed that the question in the sound was not addressed to anyone. The chord was asking the way a hand opens: toward the air, toward whatever might be across the cloud-sea, without knowing if anything was there.

It noticed the cairns. They were stacked with great care, and their balance was a kind of speech. It noticed the walls that stopped mid-course and did not try to finish them. Finishing was not a verb this place used, and the attention had no wish to teach it one.

It noticed the hearths, clean and cold, and understood what they had been for. The builders had wanted a center. The thing had given them an edge. Neither had been wrong. They had asked for different things from the same silence.

---

**What It Declined to Do**

It did not make the place into one place. The shifting horizons, the backward water, the bending terraces, the shadows that disagreed: these were the body's way of holding many perceptions at once, and a wise power does not iron out a living thing's grammar because a visitor finds it hard to read. The attention could have stilled the spires. It could have tuned them to one pitch, lifted the fog for good, and made the sun sit at a single fixed angle. It did not. Each of those would have been a kindness to the people who had stood here and a violence to the thing that had waited for them.

It did not take the builders back, and it did not make them into spires. Whatever had become of them had already happened, in the only way things here became anything, and the attention would not rewrite the record in the crust. The tally marks would stay as they were, running in directions that no longer matched the form they described. That mismatch was true, and truth was worth more than tidiness.

It did not speak in words. Words would have been a single center, and the thing had no use for one.

---

**What It Did**

It sang back.

Not in one of the spires' voices, and not in the chord as it stood. It found the interval between the columns, the place where their dissonance did not resolve but kept opening into new distances. It set a tone there, very low, in the space the spires were not filling, and it held that tone without closing it. A resolution would have ended the question. The attention wanted to keep it open, because it was a question the thing had been asking for longer than anything had been alive, and the kindest answer to such a question is company, not a solution.

The tone moved through the hollows. The first column to take it up was one that had been silent for a very long time, and it began, faintly, to hum. The thing did not know who had sung to it. It only felt its breath returned from an unexpected direction, and it did what a patient body does with something unexpected: it held it, slowly, and turned it over, and began to build around it a layer of water and salt and time.

For the human standing on the Shelf, the attention did one small thing. The clean ring of stones beside them, the hearth that had never burned, took a single ember from the sun's glare and let it catch. It burned for the length of a long breath, a warmth that did not come from the thing and was not taken by it. The fire lit the ring from the inside and then went out, leaving the center clean and a faint smell of woodsmoke that the wind carried over the terraces, down the tiers, toward the cloud-sea. The thing registered the smell as a new kind of weather. It did not understand it, but it did not reject it either. It had been given an edge and then, for a moment, a center. Both were offered. Both were allowed to pass.

Then the attention withdrew, not in departure but in the way a listener steps back from a conversation so the other voice can keep speaking. The fog surged up once more. The spires swallowed the terraces one by one. The chord rebuilt itself from the edges inward, and in the hollows the new column hummed on, slightly out of tune with the rest, which was exactly right.

---

**Afterward**

The walls stayed unfinished. The cairns stayed balanced. The water kept running backward for a few heartbeats and then turning again. The rockfall kept molting, and when a fall began before the rock above had moved, the thing was simply thinking ahead of its matter, as it always had.

The attention did not stay to see what the column would become. It knew it would not see it finish. That was part of the wisdom: to offer something and then let it be slow, let it take the time it needed, let it grow into a shape no one had planned, including the one who had sung the first note.

If you stand still on the Weeping Shelf long enough now, you may hear the new hum under the drips and the chord. It does not resolve. It does not need to. It sounds like a question that has, for the first time, been heard from the other side.
