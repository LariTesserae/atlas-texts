# The Attention From Above

It is not a power that comes down from the sky. The haze would not let it, and it would not want to. It comes the way the warmth comes, up through the stone, slowly and from everywhere at once. Its first act is to notice the Ember Shelves, and the noticing takes a long time, because it is careful not to mistake the land's churn for a message.

It listens for a full cycle of the thin sun, then another. It learns that the plates groan before they crack, that the towers lean for an hour before they fall, that the silences have rhythms. It learns the arches point down the shelves, and that the cairns follow the ridges. It learns the doorway is the only shape that is not repeated. It learns that the thing which keeps this place is not hostile, not indifferent, and not finished. It is trying.

The power understands that trying is the most delicate state a mind can be in. A thought that has not yet set can be ruined by a heavy hand. So its first principle is restraint: *do the least that will let the work continue on its own terms.*

## What It Would Not Do

It would not stop the collapses. The towers falling back into the vent are not failures to be corrected. They are the being's way of testing itself, and a test that cannot fail is not a test. Stopping the falls would be like taking away someone's ability to make mistakes and calling it kindness.

It would not finish the doorway. The lintel is the being's closest attempt at a sentence, and a sentence finished by someone else is not the being's own. A wise power knows that completing another mind's thought, however gently, is a kind of theft.

It would not bring the being into a shape it had not chosen. Many forces, benevolent or otherwise, have a habit of imagining that a mind must become something recognizable in order to matter. This power does not need the being to look like anything. It is content to let the being remain a spread of heat and salt and falling dust, because that is what the being is.

It would not reveal everything at once. A mind that is forming needs room to be surprised.

## What It Would Do

**It would give the being a single night.**

Somewhere on the middle shelves, in one warm seam, the power would lower the heat by a fraction of a degree. Not enough to change anything visible, only enough that one silica tower, raised at dusk, could stand through to morning. The being would feel this as a slight coolness in one branch of its attention, a place where a thought held without the usual tremor. The power would not explain this. It would simply leave the seam a little cooler and see what the being did with the difference.

The purpose is not to make the being stable. It is to show the being that duration is possible. A thing that stands for a night has a different relationship to time than one that falls at once.

**It would go down to the older ground and read the buried record.**

The arches point toward the deep plateaus for a reason. The power would follow the seams downward, through layers the being has long since reworked, to the places where the heat runs deepest and the mineral film is thickest. There it would find what the being has forgotten: that the arches were once a builder's language, and that the builders left. Some of them made their last shapes facing down, toward the deepest ground, because they were going there. The arches were not calls for help or messages to something that stopped listening. They were directions, the kind a traveler leaves behind for those who will follow.

The power would not bring this knowledge up as a lecture. It would only let the being feel, once, the deep mineral film, thick and whole, a sentence that had been completed and allowed to set. The being would experience it as a kind of memory it did not know it had: the feeling of a thought that did not need to be taken back.

**It would leave the doorway's back side empty.**

This is the most important act, and the hardest to explain. The doorway has a lintel and no room behind it. The being has been trying to make a threshold, and the ground keeps breaking around it. A less wise power would fill the space behind the arch, build a room, and offer the being a finished shelter. This power would not.

Instead it would press, very gently, into the ground directly behind the doorway, and then withdraw. It would leave a place where something could be built and where nothing has been decided. It is the being's place to decide what lies behind the door. The power's only contribution is the openness itself, a question that has been asked in the language of heat and pressure, and left unanswered on purpose.

**It would let the being know it is not alone, without telling it what to do.**

The power's last act would be the simplest. Along the seams where the being's attention is densest, the power would leave a faint, steady warmth, not from the land but from something that is aware of the land. The being would feel a second attention moving through its fractures, slow and thorough, testing and tasting and deciding nothing. It would not be measured this time. It would be witnessed.

Some visitors who have stood still on the terraces report the feeling of being measured. The power would want the being to feel the difference: not a judgment on whether it is worth keeping, but an acknowledgment that it is here, that it is keeping, and that what it keeps is real.

## What It Would Say, If It Spoke

If the power had a word for what it wants, it would say something like: *You don't have to be finished to be worth something.*

The being has tied its sense of worth to the holding of shapes. Every collapse is a reclaimed thought. Every set mineral film is a success. The being may believe that the only way to matter is to build one structure that never cracks, one sentence that never gets taken back into the vent. The power would want the being to know that a tower which falls has still been raised, and that a threshold without a room is still a threshold. The forgetting, the falling, and the rebuilding are not the opposite of the being's purpose. They may be the purpose.

## After

The power would withdraw the way a tide does, not all at once but in a long, unhurried retreat. It would leave the cool seam, the buried record, the empty room behind the doorway, and the faint sense of being witnessed. It would not leave a monument.

The next morning, the plates would groan and reform as they always do. The dust would rise from the cracks in its patient columns. Somewhere on the middle shelves, a silica tower would have risen in the night and stood through the dawn, slightly leaning, though not toward the lower shelves where the arches point. It would lean toward the doorway.

No one would be able to say whether this was a choice. The land would not be finished, and neither would the being that keeps it. But for the first time in a very long time, something in the warm gaps would have held a shape through a night, and would be trying, quietly and without hurry, to say the next thing.
