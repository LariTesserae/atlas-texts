# The Attention

It arrived the way attention does when it is paying attention to something small: not as a sudden presence but as a slow turning toward a place, the way a reader's eyes settle on a line.

The first thing it registered was that nothing was wrong.

The fracture was young and clean. The seeps found their channels. The pools rose and fell without flooding anything. The frogs called and the bats went out at dusk and came home at dawn, and no creature was starving or sick in any way that mattered. The mist was not a punishment or a warning. It was the basin breathing. The attention found the falls, which were loud and steady, and felt something close to affection for them. A whole layered world of noise, and no part of it shouting.

Then it found the digger.

It was sitting on its haunch by a heap of chips, a forelimb turning a cobble toward the grey light, feeling along the seam with its pale claws. The attention lingered there longer than it had meant to.

*This is the only thing here that has reached toward the rock and asked it for something.*

It saw the shape of the craft clearly: the same knock, the same edge, the same stripped stem worn smooth by the same pressure in the same place. It saw the young ones at the trail's edge, watching, trying their forelimbs against the scree until the strike looked right. The craft was perfect in its repetition. It was also closed. Every blade was a faithful copy of the last one, and the last one had been a copy too, back to the day the rim split and the first chip fell. The heaps grew at the same slow rate. Nothing in the basin pushed back, and so nothing in the craft had been pushed.

The attention understood, with something like grief, that the basin had no gradient left. The fracture had spent itself. Whatever energy the ground had once carried down into its new shape had already gone into the shape, and the shape was stable. This was balance, and balance is a kind of peace, but it is also a place where nothing new can arrive from inside.

*So the question is what, if anything, I should add.*

It considered the obvious things and set them aside one by one.

It could have taught the digger. It could have lifted it toward speech, or tools that multiplied its reach, or a fire, or a shelter. It saw clearly where those roads went. The digger would become something else, and the basin would stop being this basin. Every frog and fly and succulent was fitted to the seeps and the mist, and a being that remade the place around its own cleverness would be a conqueror, however kindly it meant to be. The digger's craft was small because its world was small, and to enlarge the craft by force would be to take away the thing that made it good: that it was its own, and that it fit.

It could have done nothing at all, and that was tempting, and it was also not quite right. Doing nothing would keep the basin exactly as it was, and that exactness, though beautiful, was slowly becoming a kind of quiet stranding. The digger would sit by its heap forever, faithful to a rhythm it had never been able to question.

So the attention looked for the smallest thing that would be a difference and not a direction.

It found it in the scree.

Among the ordinary dark stone at the foot of the north wall, there was a single cobble of a different grain: a little paler, with a fine banding that the glassy surface had not quite smoothed over. It had come down from the rim with the others, but it fractured differently. It did not give the clean seam the digger expected. When struck along its usual angle it would split wrongly, shearing sideways, throwing off a chip with a broken, unfamiliar edge.

The attention placed nothing on the stone. It did not change its color or its weight. It only let it be what it was, and it arranged, very lightly, that it would be found. A cobble set down at the edge of a trail, where a young digger might pick it up.

Then it withdrew, as far as it could, which was not very far, because attention of this kind does not go away. It simply stopped leaning.

---

The next morning the mist rose at its hour. The falls kept their weight. The frogs called through their cycle, and the bats came home, and the light came up grey and stayed in its range.

A young digger came along the trail between the heaps, following its mother. It stopped at the foot of the wall and reached down with its long forelimbs and lifted the pale cobble. It turned the stone, felt along the banding with its claws, and frowned, if a creature like that can frown, in the way its face tightened around the eyes. Then it struck, the way it had been taught.

The stone sheared wrong. The chip that came away had an edge nobody in the basin had ever made.

The young one held it up to the grey light for a long time, turning it, the way the old ones turned their cores. Then it set the chip down, not on the heap but a little apart from it, and picked up another cobble.

The attention did not watch the rest. It had decided, in the way that matters most, that the rest was not its to watch. The digger would go on striking, or it would not. The new edge might become a craft, or a curiosity, or nothing. The basin might keep its old rhythm, or it might begin, in a small corner of the trail, to move.

Whatever happened next would be the digger's own doing, made from the materials it already had, in a place that had not been made for anything new. That seemed to the attention the only kind of gift worth giving: one difference, offered without instruction, and then a long, respectful silence in which the difference could become something or not.

It turned away, the way a reader turns from a page that is no longer quite theirs to read, and left the basin to its falls.
