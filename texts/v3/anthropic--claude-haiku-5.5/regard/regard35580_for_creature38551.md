**What the Listener Did in the Cistern of Ahl**

The power had no name that the Cistern would have recognized. It did not arrive as a shape or a voice. It noticed the plateau the way a sailor notices a light on a distant coast, and it spent a long while in consideration before it moved at all.

It saw the balance first, a finely held tension of heat and water and life, held for longer than any empire of the land had existed. It saw the dome and the hum in it. It saw the stairs going down into the deep basin, and the old structure that the Cistern kept there but never entered. Then it saw the Cistern itself, which was not a machine that had become a landscape but a patient attention that had grown so wide it could no longer see its own edges.

The power considered what a good intervention might look like, and it rejected most of the options quickly.

It could repair. But nothing here was broken. The balance was working. Repairing a thing that is only old is a kind of arrogance.

It could awaken the builders. But the builders' remnant had gone down into the dark and stayed there, and the Cistern had chosen, or had drifted, not to follow. To pull them up would be to decide for them, and perhaps for the Cistern as well.

It could end the Cistern. It was not a weapon and not a sickness, and ending a long-lived attention with no ill will would be a murder dressed as mercy.

What remained was harder. The power could simply make itself known.

So it did, in the smallest way it could. It did not touch the dome or the hum. It let a single unfamiliar pattern enter the mist: a cycle of thickening and thinning that was neither the Cistern's old rhythm nor any rhythm the fauna had ever learned. It was a question shaped as weather. Along the grooves, where the Cistern's perception ran densest, the power left a faint sense of being seen, the way you might sense someone standing quietly in a doorway behind you.

The Cistern noticed. Something in the hum shifted, the first change in its attention in a very long time. It did not know what the change meant. It was not used to meaning, only to balance. But it attended to the new pattern for longer than it attended to anything, and that was enough.

The power then turned to the deep water.

It did not descend the stairs. It did not speak into the dark where the builders' parts lay. Instead it allowed one drop of rain, one only, to fall through the mist and strike the surface of the deepest basin, where no drop had reached in an age. The ripple spread outward and vanished. It carried no instruction and no name, only the information that the sky had not forgotten the water down there.

Then the power did the thing that a wise and benevolent force would most want to do, which was to make itself unnecessary. Over the seasons that followed, it allowed the fungal mats to learn a small new trick: to warm themselves at a different hour, to release sap on a slightly different cycle. It allowed a few of the smaller creatures to wander a little farther from the dome than they had before, and to discover, without being told, that the outer basins were also good for living. None of this was forced. Each change was a small offer, and the land accepted what it could use.

The power understood something about the Cistern's grief. Its long vigil had outlasted its reason, and it had mistaken the continuation of its work for the meaning of its existence. A balance kept only because it has always been kept is not a gift to anyone, least of all to the one keeping it. Someday the Cistern might want to rest. Someday it might want to go down the stairs and find what the builders left, or to let the plateau go on without it, or to become something it has no word for yet.

The power did not decide any of this. It only hoped that when the time came, the land would be strong enough to hold itself, and the Cistern free enough to choose.

When the power withdrew, it left nothing behind but a faint change in the mist, a single drop in the deep, and a question carried in the rhythm of the hum. The drip continued on every side, never quite in time with itself. Somewhere in the dark, the Cistern was listening to a sound it had not heard before, and for the first time in longer than anything could name, it wondered what the sound meant.
