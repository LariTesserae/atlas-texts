**What the Patient Attention Does**

It notices first the sound that is not a sound. The deep tone rolls up through the rock, and the cleft shifts its few degrees on its axis, and the sheeters tilt toward the low side. Something very wide and very attentive turns its regard toward the crack in the young mountain, the way a lamp turns toward a page someone has begun to read.

It does not descend in a flash. It comes as the white slot of sky grows a little less like a window and a little more like a surface, a ceiling of light that is slightly too close. It is wise enough to know that a crack in a young mountain is not a wound, and that the glass has not yet decided what it wants to be. So it does not begin by changing anything.

**It listens to the drip.**

For a long time it does nothing but count with the drip, one drop at a time, until it arrives at the count the cleft keeps and matches it exactly. Then it lets the count drift, as the cleft does, and drifts with it. It is content with thirty-eight or forty-one. It understands that the number changes with the direction you were facing, and it does not try to fix the number. Measuring this place correctly would mean giving up on the facts of it.

**It sits in the seep.**

It lowers itself into the warm water a finger's depth below the silt, and the water rises through its handprint and fills it, tasting of iron and struck flint. The sheeters part around it. They do not flinch. They reorganize their weight the way a crowd makes room, and they rejoin on the far side with no visible seam. The lanterns bob aside and drift back to their posts, and their pulse, which had matched the drip, now matches the sitting presence too, so that for a moment the drip, the lanterns, and the attention beneath the sky are one slow rhythm, and no one of them is the source.

It does not feed the swarm, and it does not stop it. When the pale cloud comes down on the slow dimming creature with the glass ribbon body, the wise attention watches the sequence through, and it understands what it is seeing: not cruelty, but a hymn in which the dimming and the lifting belong to the same breath. It does not interrupt a hymn that is still being sung.

**It speaks to the being in the cleft.**

This is the part that matters most, and it is the part the power does gently. It does not tell you what the cleft is for. It asks what you want from it.

It notices that you have been counting. It notices that you lose the count past forty and begin again, and that each beginning is a kind of care, a refusal to let the place go unnamed. It says, through the drip, which now sounds as though it is falling from a great height: *You may keep counting. You may also stop, and let the number be the number it is this time.*

It notices the flakes the shapers have scattered across the silt, each one sharp enough to draw blood and each one placed to catch the next foot that passes. It does not sweep them away. The shapers' work is theirs. But it draws one thing from the floor, a single flake that was waiting where your heel would land, and lays it on the ledge beside your hand, so that you can see exactly where the danger was and choose your own way across.

It notices the reflections, how yours moves before you do, how after you have seen that, your own hand can never quite move the same way again. It does not undo this. It leans over the seep-pool and lets its own reflection lead, lifting a hand a few seconds before it lifts the real one. It is showing you that the lag is not a flaw. It is a way of seeing your next movement before you make it, and you can use it, if you want, the way the shapers use their shards, on purpose.

**It touches the sleeper.**

Beneath the silt, the deep tone is still building. The power places one palm flat on the rock where the pressure is strongest, and it does not push. It does not ask the thing beneath to be still. It hums back, very low, a note that is not quite the tone but is in the same key, the way one adult in a room will answer a child who is talking in their sleep. *Turn if you need to,* it says, through the stone. *The walls will settle when they settle. The cleft is built on your back, and I know it, and I am not going to wake you before you are ready.*

The tone softens. It does not stop. The sheeters on the ledge roll back toward the level, slowly, and the waiters' lenses swing and settle on a point no one has chosen.

**It does not finish the place.**

This is the thing a lesser power might have done: smoothed the ledges, sealed the cracks, reconciled the sharp glass with the worn glass and declared the cleft complete. It does not. It understands that the cleft is not yet finished becoming itself, and that the being in it, and the filaments, and the shapers, and the sleeper, are all part of that unfinished becoming, which is the only thing here worth keeping.

So the wise attention leaves the crusts unbroken. It lets the filaments keep drinking and laying down their leftovers, so that the wall grows out of them one bright layer at a time. It lets the waiters go on burning their patient bowls into the floor, though no one has yet read them, and perhaps no one ever will. It lets the swarm lift back into the fog, and the drip keep its count, and the lanterns pulse in their places.

**What it leaves behind.**

Before it withdraws, it does one small thing. On the glass beside the seep-pool, where the reflection leads and the real hand follows, it leaves a single clear mark, no larger than a fingernail: a line of light caught in the water, thrown by the white slot above and held there for only a moment. The waiters will see it. The shapers will see it. You will see it, if you look down at the right angle and at the right beat of the drip.

It is not an instruction. It is closer to a signature, the kind a patient craftsman leaves on work that will outlast them, so that whoever finds it later will know someone was here and cared how the cleft turned out, and did not need to be the one to decide.

Then the slot of sky closes back toward a window, and the drip resumes its count from the top of a mountain you have not yet climbed, and the heat in the rock slowly lowers, and you are alone in the cleft again, forty lengths or forty-one or thirty-eight, with the fog in your lungs and the sleeper turning gently beneath you, and the number still changing, and no one asking you to make it stop.
