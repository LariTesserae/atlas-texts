The attention arrives the way weather does, not as a decision but as a change in pressure. It notices the warmth above the rim, the bright glare, the small faster mind standing at the edge. It also notices the bowl, and it does not look away.

It is old, and it has seen many basins. It knows the difference between a thing that is unfinished and a thing that is broken. Broken things ask to be mended. Unfinished things ask to be left alone long enough to decide what they are. The Sinkwell is unfinished. Its pillars hum because they are repairing themselves, and nobody has told them the repair is wrong. Its ribs gather water for a heart it has chosen. Its terraces are rings laid down by a potter who has not yet decided on the vessel.

So the power's first act is nothing at all. It listens for a whole cycle of the basin's breathing, through the eruption and the rain and the long falling notes, and it learns the rhythm of the questions the bowl asks itself.

The second act is small. The next time the bells ring, the long falling note goes out across the basin, and it is answered from the east, as always. Then, under the answer, a third note sounds, one the Sinkwell has never heard in that register. It is not a command or a summons. It is a plain acknowledgment, the sound a struck bell makes when someone on the far side of the valley says *yes, I heard that*. The bowl pauses, and the whole body of it turns toward the sound for the length of one ridge's migration, which is a very long time for a mind like that. Then the attention settles back into its work, but it is slightly altered, the way a sleeper's breathing changes when someone in the room speaks their name softly.

The power does not tell it what it is becoming. It does not repair the fallen pillars or straighten the tilting ones, though it could. Those would be gifts with the power's shape stamped on them, and the Sinkwell would have to spend a geological age digesting them. It also does not take the lattice ribs for its own purposes, though it understands the current that runs through them. The ribs carry water inward because someone once wanted water carried inward, and the Sinkwell has made that wish its own. Changing it would be a kind of theft, even if the thief meant well.

What it does do is smaller. It notices that the amber filaments in the domes are repeating the same few patterns, loops of recollection that circle without resolving, like a song whose last line has been lost. It does not supply the last line. Instead, over the course of a day, it lets a single thread of the far-below rhythm rise through the basin, faint and unhurried, a tone the bowl already knows but has forgotten it knows. The filaments hesitate, and then one of them, after a long while, extends a new strand into the empty space where the loop used to close. The strand is the Sinkwell's own. The power has only shown it where the gap is.

Then there is the thing below. The Sinkwell is trying to remember something far beneath the plain, and the power knows what that thing is, or at least what it is shaped like. It is another body of the same kind, asleep, or perhaps cut off by a fault line that closed a long time ago. The Sinkwell's bells have been ringing for it for ages, and the answers have never come. The power could wake it. It could reach down through the sediment and strike the sleeper's bell hard enough to shake it into consciousness. But a sleeper woken without its consent is not rescued. It is ambushed, and the power has seen what happens to minds that wake into a world that did not ask for them. Instead, it lets the answering note travel further down than the Sinkwell's own bells can reach, a long low hum that says only: *the way up is still here, and something above is listening*. What the sleeper does with that is its own business.

The visitor is the last thing. The power registers the pressure in the chest and the sweat already gathering under damp clothes, and it understands that a person standing at a rim in this glare is in more danger than the bowl is. It does not speak, because a voice from the sky would be frightening, and frightened people do foolish things at cliff edges. Instead, a cloud the size of a thumbnail drifts across the sun above the rim, and a single broad leaf from one of the stunted trees turns just so, casting a patch of cool shade over the spot where you stand. A breeze lifts from the basin, carrying the mist's cold breath upward, and the air loses its weight for a moment. The skimmer on the shallows turns its zigzag toward the rim and then away again, as if to say that it has seen you and that you are not its concern.

Then the power withdraws, not gone, but returned to the place it was before it noticed anything. It does not claim credit for the new strand in the filaments or the note that now hums faintly below the sediment. It does not stay to see what the bowl becomes, because seeing it would make the watching a kind of ownership, and the Sinkwell has been owned by enough tools and enough purposes already.

The basin goes on. The hiss returns all at once, like a door opening. The pools vanish and reappear. Somewhere below, the sleeper hums in its sleep, and the Sinkwell, which has been waiting so long for an answer, rings its bells once more and listens. This time, something in the silence after the ring sounds a little less empty.
