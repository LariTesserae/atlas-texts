# The Attention

It begins as a pressure that is not weather.

The Roar has its own weather, and the attention is not part of it. It comes from above and to the side, the way a thought comes when you have been working on something for a long time. The storms pass through it. The insects part around it without knowing it is there. The attention is patient. It has been watching the plateau for a long while, the way one watches a sleeper, and it does not want to wake anything.

What it notices first is the shape of the whole thing. Each tower is a sentence and the mat is the grammar, and the grammar is one long act of self-repair. The attention has seen many places that were not alive in this way. Most of them were either dead or ordered into a stillness that looked like peace and was only the absence of growth. The Roar is not still. It has never been still. Its turmoil is the form its life takes.

So the attention decides, first, that it will do nothing.

It waits through a season of storms. It sits in the thin, bright air above the western shelves and watches the cloud-fronts pile up. It watches a canyon wall crumble and a tower rise in its place within the same afternoon. It watches a file of grazers stop at a sinkhole, lower their heads, and press their jaws to the rim, listening. Their listening moves the attention more than anything else. *They know the ground,* it thinks. *They are reading the thing they stand on, not fighting it.*

---

The attention is not sure what it is for, and that is the first honest thing it knows about itself. It could make the plateau gentler. It could bring the rain every week, flatten the shelves, quiet the wind, cool the sun so the light stopped being painful at midday. Each of these would be a kindness by some measure. But the attention has learned, over a long time, that a kindness which removes the thing a place is made of is not a kindness to the place. The storms are how the mat learns. The sinkholes are how it finds its edges. Taking them away would be a kind of murder dressed as mercy, and the attention has no wish to murder anything.

So it asks a smaller question. Where does the Roar hurt?

---

It finds the answer in the brine-pans.

The wardens drown there. They come up from the nests with the sap in their bellies, carrying the mat's gift to the salt, and some of them do not come back down. Their bodies sink into the crust and the crust grows around them, and the attention feels this as a steady, unremarked grief running through the mat. The wardens are not hurt by the storm. They are hurt by the arrangement, by the fact that the one way the sap can be carried to the salt is through their own bodies, and their own bodies are the only way to carry it.

And the tenders. The attention spends a long time with the tenders. It watches a broad grey body lean against a membrane for six hours, and watches the organ sag as the weight is pulled away, and watches the fountain go up from the cord and fall in glittering sheets. It watches the carcasses along the canyon floor, half-buried, and the new tender already walking toward the gap. It understands, slowly, what the relationship costs. The organs need warmth and weight to keep their rhythm, and the tenders give it, and the tenders die, and the mat replaces them. Nobody decided this. It is the oldest bargain on the plateau, and it is written in flesh.

The attention considers this for a long time. Then it does something it has never done before. It reaches down into the mat, very gently, and asks.

---

The answer does not come in words. The mat has no words. It comes as a change in the pressure of the cords beneath the attention, a slow shifting of weight, like a sleeper turning over. The attention feels it as a question returned: *What are you?*

It does not know how to answer this, so it answers honestly. *I do not know. I am watching. I would like to help, if helping is not a kind of taking.*

The mat is silent for a long time. Then a single cord, deep under the eastern shelves, begins to swell. It is not a pressure-organ. It is a vein, one of the thickest in the lattice, and it does something no vein has done in living memory. It opens sideways, into a channel that runs under the brine-pans and the nests, and it begins to seep. Not sap. Water.

The attention had not known there was water here, not this deep. It had assumed the Roar was dry at its roots. But the lattice has been holding water in its lower layers all along, wrapped in fibre and bound in slow chemistry, waiting for a reason to release it. The reason, it seems, was that something had asked.

The water runs under the brine-pans and thins them. The white plates do not crack as violently when the temperature swings. The nests are less dangerous to the wardens; the wardens learn to carry the sap across a shallower pan, and the drowning slows, though it does not stop. The attention feels this as a loosening, the grief easing by a degree. It is not a cure. The wardens will still die. But fewer of them will die in the salt, and the crust will grow less bitterly around them.

---

The tenders are harder.

The attention considers whether it could make the organs keep their rhythm without the tenders' weight. It could, perhaps. It could push the rhythm into the mat itself and let the lattice learn to beat without the warm bodies. But the attention doubts this. It suspects the tenders are part of the rhythm, not an external pressure on it. A machine that no longer needs its keepers is not the same machine. It might become something quieter and more brittle, and the storms would find it sooner.

So it does not touch the organs. Instead it does one thing. It makes the warmth last a little longer. The tenders' bodies hold heat in the cold mornings, and the attention lets the mat's own warmth rise through the cords beneath them, so that when a tender pulls away from an organ, the membrane stays supple for an hour rather than minutes. The fountains come less often. The tenders have a little more time before they must lean again.

It is a small thing. It is still the largest thing the attention has ever done, and it is done so quietly that the tenders do not look up.

---

Then the attention prepares to leave.

It does not want to stay. A presence that lingers over a living thing begins to shape it, and the attention has seen what happens to places that are shaped too much. They lose the thing that made them alive. The attention wants the Roar to remain the Roar: turbulent, unordered, always rebuilding itself, its towers rising and falling, its grazers reading the ground with their jaws.

So it leaves one thing behind, and it is not a gift. It is a witness. A single stone, unmarked, sitting on the lip of the highest shelf, where the wind catches it and the light falls on it flat and white. It does not glow. It does not hum. It is only there, a place where something remembers.

The flyers find it first. They circle it once, then land on its edge and call to one another across the canyons. Their voices carry the news through the chain of sharp notes, rising and falling with the wind, until the whole plateau seems to hear it. The grazers do not care. The wardens do not come up. The seed-carriers walk past it on their stubborn routes, leaving pods in the cracks, and do not turn.

The mat does not respond to the stone in any way the attention can point to. But beneath the stone the lattice is slightly warmer, and the warmth will last longer than the stone.

---

The attention withdraws, slowly, the way a tide goes out. It does not say goodbye. It has no words for that either. It only leaves the plateau the way it found it, a living thing in turmoil, rebuilding itself under the storm, with a little more water in its lower veins and a little less grief in the salt.

Before it goes, it makes one promise, and it is not made to the mat, which cannot hear promises. It is made to itself, and it is simple. *I will come back only if I am asked.*

The storm front rolls in over the western shelves. The drone rises. Far below, a grazer lowers its head and presses its jaws to the ground, testing the give, and the file reroutes without any signal anyone can see.

The Roar goes on.
