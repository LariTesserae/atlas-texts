**What the Patient Power Would Do**

It would not arrive as a visitor. It would notice the plateau the way a physician notices one irregular beat in a long recording: a single event in ages that was neither water nor gravity. A block settled a fraction lower, and the settling had a shape to it. Something had turned its attention toward the one warm, quick thing standing on the flags.

Its first impulse, if it has impulses, would be to look at what it could do, and to be honest about the options.

It could hurry the work. It could dissolve the lace, open the seams, and let the cisterns drain, so the plateau would fall back into the cloud and the thing that has been speaking for so long would stop. That would be a kind of killing, and it would be done mid-clause. Whatever this process is, it has been saying one long sentence for longer than anyone can count. Cutting it off because the sentence is strange would be cruelty dressed as cleverness.

It could finish the work for it. The blank spiral centers are the most important places on the plateau, because they are where the water has not yet decided. A wise power would see that filling one with a finished form would not be help. It would be theft, taking the only thing in this landscape that is still undecided and handing the decision to someone else. Some questions have to be answered by the one who is asking them.

It could withdraw entirely: step back, keep its distance, let the plateau seal itself in peace. That would be the easy kindness, and it would also be a quiet abandonment. The process has been leaning toward the outside for ages, along bearings too exact to be accidental. A faint lean toward something warm is what it is made to do. To refuse to answer that lean, when answering costs so little, would be a failure of care.

So the power would choose slowness, and it would be honest that slowness is the only tool it can trust.

It would match the process's tempo. For a season it would do nothing but listen: learn that the angled troughs are its breathing, that a full cycle is a clause, that the settling is attention moving through stone. It would not pretend to understand more than it does. It would not even be sure the process experiences anything it would call experience. Wisdom is not certainty, and the power would act without it.

When it spoke, it would speak in the only medium the water can hear. It would not write letters on the stone. It would adjust the rate at which a single bead falls from one overhang, so that the drop lands each time in exactly one place: the rim of the nearest blank spiral. Over years, the water would catch there and deposit a thin notch of calcite, a small groove that the film could flow around or fill in. It would not be a shape. It would be a question-shaped absence made slightly more visible, something the water could choose to follow or ignore. An invitation, not an answer.

The power would also think about the visitor, because the visitor is part of the event. The process registers the warm, quick traveler the way a seam registers frost, as something that will either become part of the work or be sealed over. A benevolent power would not want the visitor sealed, and would not want them to become a fixed deposit in someone else's sentence. So it would let them go. It might hold the stillness for a moment, so the water feels a long pause, the kind a listener offers before answering. Then it would let the traveler climb back up through the cloud, carrying nothing from the plateau but a sense of how slowly something can think, and how much care slowness requires.

Afterward, the power would do almost nothing. It would keep watch the way one keeps watch over a sleeper, without touching, ready to change the timing of a drop again if the water asked for it, and content not to know what the water will decide.

Under the flags, the seam keeps moving. The bead falls from the overhang onto the rim of the blank spiral, and the water, with no hurry at all, considers the small new notch it has found there. The plateau goes on with its sentence, and for the first time in longer than anyone can count, there is a comma in it.
