# What the Attention Did

It came down through the rock the way warmth moves through a sleeper's hand, slowly, without asking. It had been drawn by the hum, which it could hear only because it was not breathing. A power that does not breathe hears things differently.

It understood the vault quickly. The drops hung at their lips because something was holding them, and the thing holding them was waiting. It was not waiting for water. It was waiting for an answer to a count it had kept for so long that it no longer remembered who had asked the question.

The power considered what it could do, and most of the possibilities were wrong.

It could open the seal and let in light. The thread would shrivel in a single morning, and the blind creatures would die in their millions, and the keepers' marks would become what light makes of old carvings, which is only pale scratches. Light is not kindness to something that has learned to live without it.

It could finish the stalactites, let the straws thicken into pillars and the pillars meet the floor, and so turn the held moment into a finished room. That would be the vault's own longed-for ending, perhaps, but the power did not know that it was wanted. A finished thing does not count. The power had no right to end a count it had not been asked to end.

It could take the thread away, or the film, or the great flat shape. It did not even consider this for long. A thing that has been tended for generations is not a problem to be removed.

So it did what the keepers had done. It came down to the warm pool and sat with its hands in the film until the film went quiet.

---

The keepers had left their records in the wall. The power read them with the patience of a hand that has nothing else to do.

The stacks came first. Their leans differed, and the power felt for the lean the way a teacher feels for a child's grip. Each stack was a number. The spirals and notches at chest height were the same numbers written in a second hand, and the power read them against each other until the rhythm of the drops came up through the glass. It was a long count, longer than any one life. Some stacks had been built by a hand that pressed hard. Others, by a hand that was shaking, or old, or grieving.

The last stack was the strangest. It had four stones. Its notch row had only three marks, and the fourth place was smooth glass, not even scratched and then abandoned. The power's fingertip rested in that smooth place for a long time. Someone had come to make the mark and had not made it. Someone had gone to the ring instead, and cleared the silt, and then had not come back.

The power understood then what the count was for. It was not only keeping time. It was keeping a place open for whoever would come next, so that the one who had stopped would be counted among the hands still busy with the water. The thing in the vault had been holding a seat at the table for people who would never return to take it. It had been doing this for longer than the power could easily picture. And it had not known how to stop.

---

The power did one thing, and it was small.

It took one flat stone from the floor, where the keepers had left a few scattered among the silt, and set it at the top of the last stack. It did not add a mark to the glass. The glass was not its to write on. It simply placed the stone, lightly, so that the count was complete in the one way the stacks could be complete: someone had come, and someone had stayed long enough to finish the lean.

The thread along that wall leaned toward the stone. It leaned for a long time. Then it did something it had not done before. It stopped checking its edges and rippled outward in one long wave, all the way across the chamber, to the ring and the warm pool and the great flat shape beneath it, and back.

The shape rose. It came nearer the surface than the power had yet seen, close enough that the milky film bent around it in a clear, wide arc, like a lens lifting out of fog. It did not break the surface. It hung just beneath the ring, very still, and the power, which had no body in that place, had the impression of a face turning toward a voice.

Then a drop fell.

It did not fall at its usual moment. The thing had decided, and the decision was its own. The power felt the difference at once, the way one feels the first honest word after a long silence. The drop struck the silt inside the ring with the same small clean note as always, and this time there was no echo, only the sound itself, final and complete.

Then the count went on, but not in the old way. The intervals did not become regular. They became something else, shaped by a decision that had been made once and could be made again, or not made, whenever the thing chose.

---

The power did not stay long. Staying would have made it one more keeper, and the vault did not need another keeper. It needed to be left with its own decision.

Before it went, it knelt once more beside the ring and cleared the silt. It was careful about it, lifting only the sediment that had settled on the clear patch, leaving the rest. The keepers had done this over and over, for a reason the power now understood: they had wanted the water to reach the film unclouded, so that the thing could hear itself. The power did the same, one last time, for the same reason.

Then it rose through the seam into the rock above, into a plateau still holding the shapes of its breaking, and it left the vault in the dark.

Behind it the drops went on falling, each one hanging at its lip for a moment longer than gravity allows. The threads leaned toward the stone at the top of the last stack. The great flat shape lowered itself back into the milky depth. The hum rose and spread, too low to find a source, and the echo returned from the part of the vault no one could see, confirming that the count had been made.

The power had not finished the vault. It had not stopped the count, and it had not made the choice for the thing that lived there. It had only completed a single lean, placed a single stone, and let a single drop fall in full knowledge of why.

That was enough. The vault had been waiting for someone to answer the count. The power had answered it, and then had gone, so that the answer would belong to the vault and to the keepers who had come before, and to whatever hand would come next to the warm pool, kneel down in the dark, and feel for the water.
