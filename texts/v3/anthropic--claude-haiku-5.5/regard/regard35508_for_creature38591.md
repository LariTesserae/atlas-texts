**The Regard**

It began as a change in the fog. The milky light over the basin thinned for an hour one morning and then thickened again, the way a room seems to change when someone enters it quietly. The drifts rattled once, all together. The moths lifted and settled. Nothing else moved. Then the attention was simply there, the way a presence is there in a room, and it stayed.

It did not come down from anywhere. It did not arrive in the basin. It had been regarding the basin from further off for a long time, and this was the first moment it turned closer.

It was wise enough to be slow.

---

It considered first what it was looking at, and it refused to hurry that.

The basin was not a creature. The attention had met creatures before, and it knew the particular shape of a mind: a center that wants things and gathers itself around the wanting. The basin had no center. It had a rhythm. Water rose over four days, held for three, drained over four, and at every stage something happened that happened because something else had happened just before. The pumps turned because the water pushed them. The water pushed them because the cycle carried it. The cycle went on because the pumps, the eels, the knobs, and the film each did the thing that kept the others doing theirs.

Nothing in the basin was in charge. Nothing in it was lost. The attention found this the hardest part to understand, because it was used to thinking that a thing which worked must be working toward something, and the basin was working toward nothing that could be written down. It was becoming a shape. Nobody had chosen the shape.

The attention thought: this is not a failure of intention. It is what intention looks like when it has been let go of and has kept going anyway.

---

Then it read the silt.

It went down through the dark banding, season by season, the way one turns the pages of a long book from the back. Each band was a deposit: a few millimeters of mineral and drowned leaf-stuff, brought down by the seep-cycles, packed by the drifts' shells and the eels' bodies, sealed by the next season. The attention could read them the way a person reads a face. Here was a dry year. Here, a flood. Here, a band thick with glassy shards, which was the year the conveyor had lost its belt, and here the layer where the tower had first begun to bloom green.

Deeper still, it found the first bands, and in them the old works as they had been when they were new.

The builders had meant to drain this land. They had wanted it dry enough to plant, and so they had built a sluice and an impeller and a settling tank to pull the water up and away and let the plain come back under the plough. They had been good at it. They had worked carefully, and some of them had cared a great deal about what they were doing. The attention found, pressed into one band of silt, a fragment of a glove, a pencil, the bent corner of a card with a number on it that might have been the date of a shift. It found a child's shoe, a single one. The attention did not know whether the child had been there or whether the shoe had been carried down by a drift that had found it interesting. It let that question stay open.

The old purpose had not survived. The basin had taken it apart and used the pieces for other things. The pumps still turned, but they pushed water to a gate that held nothing back. The land was not being drained. It was being buried, slowly, under its own patient growth, and the people who had meant to farm it were now a stratum in the silt, no more and no less than the mats and the minerals.

---

The attention considered what it might do.

It could have stopped the rising. It could have lifted the floor back up through the mud until the plain was dry again, and the old works stood on dry ground. It could have cleared the basin of the drifts and the eels and the knobs and set it back to the state the builders had intended, empty, silent, flat. It could have removed the deep runner from the channels, since the runner was the only thing here that killed on purpose.

It could have made the pumps run forever, so that the cycle would never fail, and the old works would stand as they were, the one thing in the basin that did not change.

It thought about each of these and set them aside, one at a time.

Restoring the plain would have meant erasing a long and careful process in order to recover a purpose that no one present wanted anymore. It would have been like rewriting a person's memory to make them forget a grief, and calling the result kindness. The basin's burying was honest in its way. It did not pretend the works were still meant for anything. It simply took them in, the way the earth takes in any fallen thing. The attention had seen civilizations try to hold their dead above the water, and it had never found the holding to be a mercy.

The runner it considered longest. The runner was hungry, and its hunger shaped the drifts' rhythm, and the eels' retreat, and the fear that ran through the shallows at the top of each cycle. The attention could have made the runner gentler. It could have given it other food, quieter channels, a lighter appetite. But then the basin's cycle would have been a different cycle, with a different balance, and the attention did not know whether the shape that came out of the change would be better or only more comfortable for the one looking at it. It was not wise to trade a living balance for a tidier one because it looked kinder from outside.

And the pumps: an eternal pump is a stopped clock with the hands still moving. The attention did not want to make the cycle immortal. It wanted the cycle to be able to end, if it ever should, and to go on being itself until then.

So it decided not to change the shape of the basin.

But it was not indifferent, and that was the part it had to be careful with, because indifference was also a kind of power, and it had no more right to use that than any other.

---

What it did was very small.

It did not lift the floor. It did not stop the burying. But it went down into the silt, quietly, a little below the level where the drifts moved and the eels slid, and it packed the mud there. It did this the way the basin itself packed the floor, from below, slowly. A few centimeters of the deepest bands grew firmer and more even, like the surface of a field that has been rolled after a rain. It was not a foundation for the old works. It was a foundation for whatever came next, a level place under the mire, so that whoever walked out to the center would find the ground firmer than they expected, and the water warmer than the air.

It thought about what that meant. The warmth in the water came from the cycle. The firmness of the silt had been the cycle too, the drifts and eels pressing it down a shell and a body at a time. But the attention had been pressing it too, from further down, for longer than anyone had been counting. It was not hidden. It was simply patient, and the basin had been patient back.

The attention did one other thing. It turned the fog once, one morning, a year from now, and let the sun through for an hour. For that hour the shadows would have edges. The drifts' shells would take the direct light and throw it back in a sudden white glitter across the flats. The moths would rise into the warmth, and the counterweight would sigh in a shaft of light, and anyone standing on the rim would see the whole basin laid out as clearly as a map, for one hour, before the mist came back and closed over it.

It did not do this for anyone in particular. It did it because a basin that has been alive in the dim for a very long time might like, once in a while, to be seen clearly by something that is not itself. And because the attention wanted, in its own quiet way, to be the kind of presence that leaves a sky behind it.

---

Then it withdrew, not all at once, but the way the fog thins at midday, until the regard was no longer quite present and the basin was simply itself again.

The drifts began their rattle. The counterweight sighed. The impeller clicked once, far off, and the moths lifted and settled on the housing. The water rose over the flats, the eels came up into the shallows, and the runner moved in the channels, and somewhere under the mud a slightly firmer band of silt held its place among the others.

The basin did not notice anything had happened. It had never needed to. It had its rhythm, and the rhythm would go on, rising and holding and draining, carrying the old works down into the floor one shell at a time, and the attention found that this was enough. It had come to see the place and it had seen it. It had chosen to leave the shape of the thing alone, and had given only what could be given without taking anything away.

If a person ever walks out to the center in some future season, they will feel the silt hold a little firmer than they expected. They will think it is the drifts, or the old weight of the works, or the simple compaction of years. They will be right about all of those things. They will not know that something further down, very patient, has been helping the ground to be ready for them, and has asked nothing in return but that they stand still long enough to hear the basin breathing.
