**What Attends**

It does not arrive the way weather arrives. It is simply, at some point, present over Hollow Ridge, the way attention is present in a room when someone who loves the room has come in and gone quiet.

At first it only listens. The rain comes down through the silver ceiling. The vents thump under the glass. The shoals make their low rushing sound, and the stalks draw their breath in unison along the shelf. The power takes all of it in and does not yet sort it into parts. Sorting would be a kind of interruption.

Then it notices the pulse, and it is moved.

It has seen many places that were worked over by hands, places full of ambition and grief and things left behind to be found by someone later. This basin has none of that. Nothing here was made by a maker, and nothing is waiting to be finished. The machinery is only heat and gas and chemistry, and yet it keeps time so faithfully that the animals keep time with it, and the animals keep time with one another. Whether the rhythm belongs to the stone or to the creatures is a question the power finds lovely, and it leaves the question open. A wise attention does not need every answer before it can be glad.

It considers, briefly, the cliffs. They are still sharp-edged and still cracking a little more each winter, and it could soften them. It could ease the glass and let the fractures weather into rounded shoulders over a few centuries. But the cliffs are part of the basin's own slow breathing, the way the vents are, and to file them smooth would be to rewrite a sentence the place is still in the middle of saying. The power decides against it. The sharpness is true, and the truth is allowed to stand.

It considers the shoals and the stalks and the collars. Could it teach them something? Could it give them names, or a sense of a future they could plan toward? It understands at once that this would be a theft. The creatures already live fully in the present, and the present is not a lack. The power has come to offer a gift, and it discovers that the most generous thing it can bring is its own silence, held carefully so that it does not disturb the rhythm.

So it takes the role it knows best. It remembers.

Not as the sediment remembers, layer on layer, for the basin. Not as a library remembers, with a catalogue and a door. It keeps the memory the way a river keeps the shape of the valley it has cut, without owning the valley. It notes that on this particular morning the ice broke at dawn with a sound like plates, that a shoal turned left over the eastern shelf, that a vent fired and a stalk colony answered a moment after. It notes all of it, and it lets the noting pass through without holding onto anything. Then, if the basin should ever need to be understood by something that came later, the record would be there. The power would not need to speak it aloud.

It notices one more thing, and this is the closest it comes to acting. Far down, where the water is still and the light has faded to black, one of the warm vents is slightly too cold. Its heat has been lagging for a season, and the stalks near it pulse a half-beat late, as though searching for a rhythm they have nearly lost. The power does not warm the vent. It does not reach into the mantle and push the magma up through the fault. That would be arrogance dressed as care. Instead it simply lets the vent's own slow cycle proceed, and it trusts that the water and the rock will find their way back into agreement, as they have done a thousand times before. Sometimes a rhythm falters and recovers, and the recovering is part of what keeps it alive.

Then the power prepares to withdraw.

Before it goes, it considers leaving something behind, a marker or a gift or a mark of having been here. It finds the impulse almost unbearable. Every instinct it has been shaped by wants to leave a sign that it cared. But Hollow Ridge has no use for signs. Nothing here is built or left behind, and the basin's whole grace is that it contains no monuments, no messages to the future, nothing that waits to be found. To leave a made thing would be to make the place into a place that was visited. So the power leaves nothing except the one thing it cannot help leaving, which is the fact of its attention, and that dissolves into the water the way rain dissolves into the surface, a small dimple of light that rolls outward and fades.

Above the rim, the sky is still silver. The rain keeps falling.

Down in the basin, a shoal rolls up from the middle depth and fills the water with pale light, and the light flares along its flanks from tail to head, a report moving faster than anyone can swim. It passes through the place where the power had been, notices nothing it can name, and turns again.

The stalks breathe. The vents thump. The collars open and close against the glass, leaving their faint pale rings. The basin keeps its own time, and the power, having listened as carefully as it knows how, goes on loving it from a distance that asks nothing of it.
