# What the Attention Did

It came the way weather comes to a valley that has been still too long: not as an event but as a change in pressure. For a while nothing in the Lowland of Settled Plates knew it was being looked at. The stacks kept their rhythm, the extrusion streams kept spreading, and the drips kept falling on the same plates in the same order. The attention was not hurried. It had never been hurried.

It noticed the seams first, because they were the most precise thing in the basin. A hand's width deep, knife-edged, aligned to a grid so faint that it could be seen only from far above. The grid was not the builders' signature. It was the creatures' route-map, laid down so long ago that the plates had taken its shape as their own. Everything here fit everything else. Nothing had been left over.

Then it noticed the creatures, and it paused, because it had expected something else.

It had expected either predators or prey, a food chain holding the place in tension, or else a dead mechanism with the creatures as residue. Instead it found a closed system of care. The creatures kept the seams sharp. The seams kept the plates from slumping. The plates held the water in its channels, and the water fed the creatures. The towers laid new composite into the spaces the creatures could not reach. The two processes met at the edges and did not interfere.

It could have said that this was beautiful. It could have said that it was horrifying. It recognized both, and then it asked a more useful question: *What is this for?*

---

## What it did not do

It did not dismantle the towers. Their output was steady, and their steadiness was the thing that kept the basin from being eaten by its own ruin. A wise power knows that most of what looks like waste is someone's long attention, and the extrusion lines were attention made into matter.

It did not rewrite the creatures. Their pads fit the seams the way a key fits a lock, and their routes never broke. Remaking them to match some other idea of a good life would have meant deciding, from outside, what their circling was worth. It had seen enough of its own kind make that mistake to distrust the impulse.

It did not speak to them. They had no language it could detect, only a clicking that was the sound of contact itself. To address them would have been to pretend they were the kind of minds that answer.

It did not try to explain the place to itself, either. The explanation was that the basin was kept. Someone had decided, a very long time ago, that the floor should not be allowed to forget how to be a floor. The someone was gone or was not. The decision had outlived them. A wise power does not rush to undo a decision just because its author has left the room.

---

## What it did

It found one seam.

It was not special. It sat on a route that looped around the foot of a pump stack, one of the tight loops that had worn the plates to a shine. Its edge was as sharp as any other. The creatures crossed it every few hours and kept it bright.

The power did nothing to the seam. It did not cut it, polish it, or shield it from the water. It simply stopped attending to it. Not in the way the creatures attended, with pads and legs and steady pressure, but in the way a gardener stops watering a single plant to see whether it can survive on rain. It let the thing be what it was.

The change was almost nothing. A week later, a creature crossing the seam found the groove a little wider than its pads expected. It paused. It checked the edge, and the edge was slightly dull, softened by the faint wear of a surface that had been left alone for one season. The creature searched along the groove, found a match, and went on. It did not turn back. It did not stop. But when the group passed the seam again the following day, they left a small disturbance in the sheen, a shallow scratch where a pad had dragged too hard, as if they had been trying to restore the edge and found they could not quite reach it.

The power watched this for a long time.

Then it did one more thing. It did not change the seam. It changed the time. Where the rest of the basin kept the same hour forever, it let this one seam keep a different one, so that the sun's crossing and the shadow's return would land a little later on this plate than on all the others. Nothing in the machines noticed. The creatures did not seem to notice. But the shadow of the pump stack fell across the seam a few minutes after it fell across every other plate, and for those minutes the seam was in a different light than the world around it.

That was all.

---

## After

The power did not stay to see what the place would become. It is not the kind of attention that needs an ending. It came the way it came, and it may leave the way it leaves, and the basin will go on with its routes and its towers and its drip.

But it left one thing behind that was not in the design: a seam that is getting older.

Whether the creatures will learn to live with it, or change their routes to bypass it, or come to tend it more gently than they tend the rest, the power cannot know. It did not ask for an answer. It has seen too many places that were kept so perfectly that they forgot they could be lived in, and it hoped only to give one plate the chance to become a place.

Somewhere under the water, in the throats of the pumps, the composite keeps its memory of the choice that made it. The builders are still gone, or still here. The floor has not yet remembered how to choose. But on one seam, for the first time in longer than anyone can measure, the edge is beginning to soften in the way that all edges do when they are finally left alone long enough to be something other than new.

The power will come back, eventually, to see whether the seam has learned anything. It hopes it has not. It hopes only that, when the creatures cross it, they find it a little different from what they expected, and that they are free, for once, to wonder why.
