**The Seam**

The power does not arrive as light or sound. It arrives as attention, and the chamber notices it the way the grazers notice the hum. Every plated back lifts a fraction and waits.

It does not act at once. It has learned that anything entering the Hollow must first learn the breath, so it stays through several pulses of the amber glow, matching its attention to the rise and fall until it can sense the chamber without disturbing it. It counts the tally strokes, groups of seven with a longer mark between, and considers what they might measure. Not seasons, since there are none. Perhaps the number of times the hum has answered before a pause. Perhaps the number of returns. Perhaps the number of times someone sat beside the basin and waited for something to happen, and it did not.

It listens to the hum for a long time, and it hears the structure: a call, a pause, and an answer coming back from a slightly different direction. It understands that the hum is answering. The question is what it is answering, and whether anyone is still asking.

The power reads the broken roof. The fracture is clean, sharp, unweathered. Something split the ceiling open quickly, and then something closed it again, deliberately, and the sealing has held. The careful few did not simply die here. They broke a way in, or a way out, and then chose to shut the chamber and keep it. They did not want the vent to go cold or the loop to stop. Whatever they were protecting, they protected it with their own hands, and then their hands stopped working, or stopped being needed, and the chamber went on.

This is the difficult part, and the power takes its time with it. A wise power does not confuse preservation with kindness. The loop is complete in its way, and it is also a kind of held breath. Nothing here has been allowed to finish. Breaking it open would be a violence against a thing that was carefully kept. Leaving it alone forever would be a different violence, a quiet one, to the careful few and to whatever they hoped would come after them. The power decides that it will not take the choice away from the place. It will offer one, and then it will wait for the answer.

First it speaks into the hum, at the hum's own pitch and in its own rhythm: a call, a pause, and then an answer that is not a copy but a change. It says, in the only way it can, *I have heard you.* Every grazer in the chamber lifts its head. For the first time the lift does not fall. The heads hold, and the whole chamber holds with them, the rattle of the leaves dropping into a silence so complete that the glow seems to pause mid-pulse.

Then the power opens a seam.

It is not a fall of stone. It is a hair-thin line high in the ceiling, where the old fracture meets the sealing, and it lets through the first air from above that this chamber has ever known. The air is cool. It carries a smell that has no place in this closed sweetness: wet mineral, a distant green, the faint sour edge of a world that changes by the day. The grazers' heads turn toward it together. The amber light flickers, not in its fixed interval, but in a stammer, as if something has spoken in a language the chamber had forgotten it could hear.

Nothing else happens for a long time. The power does not hurry. The wind through the seam moves, shifts, and finally settles into a steady thread of cool air crossing the warm updraft from the fissure, and where the two meet, the glazed yellow stone beside the vent begins to shimmer differently.

Then one drop of water falls.

It lands on a ridge of dust that has lain undisturbed since the chamber sealed, and the ridge bends, darkens, and collapses into a small wet crescent. The chamber has never been washed. Now a single line of the floor has been touched by something from outside, and the dust carries its shape away. The resin crust along the basin's rim softens at one edge and begins, very slowly, to run. A grazer lowers its head to the wet place, sniffs once, and does not feed. Its track, the worn path it has walked for generations, bends at the point where the water fell.

The power does not do more than that. It does not lift the roof, or rebuild the fallen cairns, or call the careful few back out of whatever they became. It does not decide what the Hollow should be. The grooves at arm height are still there, exactly the right height for a hand that wants to climb toward the seam. The basin still holds its brittle residue. The hum still rises and falls, and it has changed its call. The new answer has a different direction in it, a pitch that comes from above as well as below.

The power withdraws slowly, the way it arrived, and leaves the seam open for whoever wants to climb. Before it goes, it adds one mark to the tally strokes near the cairns. It is not a stroke of seven, and not the longer mark that closes a group. It is a single short line, cut at a slant, and the power leaves it to be read as the chamber reads everything: slowly, with a patience that has no direction except return, and that may, for the first time, be starting to point somewhere.
