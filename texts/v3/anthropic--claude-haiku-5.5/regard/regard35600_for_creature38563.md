The attention arrives the way weather does, not as a decision but as a change in pressure. It has been turning toward this place for some time, the way a listener turns toward a sound they have heard for years without deciding to listen.

It sees the basin whole: the sixty meters of raw fracture, the pale ferns, the seam of gray light, the hum at the base of the pipe. It sees the green body and recognizes it at once as one thing, not a crowd of plants. It sees that the body is not suffering. It has no wound that needs closing, no hunger, no fear. It is thin-fronded and patient and whole. Its growth is steady and its death is a return. It has made a complete life out of ruin, and the ruin has made a complete life out of it.

The power's first impulse is to do nothing. It has learned, across a long time, that most rescue is really the rescuer's need to be useful. A gift given to something that did not ask for it is still a force applied to it, and forces change things in ways that cannot always be taken back. Here the being has no request. Its quiet is not a silence waiting to be filled. It is the shape of its life.

So the power waits, and watches the hum.

The hum is the one thing it cannot leave alone. It does not know what the sound is. The power does, roughly: a machine that once turned heat into motion, still holding some stored energy in its old vessel, still drawing warmth from rock that is itself a kind of furnace. The deep heat, the steam, the fresh cracks, the collapse that never finishes: these are signs of a system that has not yet fully gone quiet. If the hum grows, or stops, or the vessel fails in some way no one planned, the basin could change in a single night rather than in centimeters a year. The being would not understand that change. It would be swept up in it.

But the power also sees the other side. The being has wrapped the pipe in moss and fern. It has learned to keep the vibration close, the way a body keeps a fire in a cold room. Pulling the hum out would be a kind of violence against the one thing it has organized itself around. The being would lose its heartbeat without a replacement, and its roots would reach for a warmth that had gone.

The power decides that it will not stop the hum, and will not strengthen it either. It will let things stay as they are, with one change, and that change will be very small.

There is a place in the upper wall, near the seam, where the rock is thin and already weeping. The power loosens it by a hair. Not a collapse. Not a shock. Just a fracture that opens a few millimeters more each decade, a little wider than the crack that lets the steam out. Over centuries this lets slightly more of the gray light fall onto the highest fronds, and the being's lean toward the seam will have something real to lean toward. Nothing in it changes except the reach of its attention. If it wants that light, it can grow toward it, as it has been growing all along, and it will not be forced.

The power also decides against any gesture meant to be understood. It does not write on the wall or send a voice through the pools. The being is not listening for words, and the reeds were never listening for anything. What the power gives is the same thing the being already gives the basin: slow attention, and a little more room.

Before it withdraws, the power considers the sign on its post, the last legible line, the instruction whose meaning the mold has half erased. It does not restore the letters. Those who wrote the sign were careful people trying to keep others from harm, and their caution is not wrong, only finished. The being has outlived the warning and made its own peace with the place the warning described.

Then the attention turns away, the way a draft fades as a door is shut behind it.

The basin goes on as it was. The drips fall into the pools. A vine settles a centimeter under its own weight. A section of wall gives way with a dull crack, and the shock runs through the roots, and new roots reach into the raw face within the week. The green takes the new stone into itself. The hum keeps its steady rhythm at the base of the pipe. Somewhere high in the rock, one fracture gains a hair's width, and a thread of gray light, slightly wider than before, falls onto the tallest fern, which does not yet know it has been given anything.
