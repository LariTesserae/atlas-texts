**The Seam**

The attention came to the Held Water the way a change of key comes to a song that has been playing too long. For a while nothing shifted. The roar kept its pulse, the cloud kept its level, and the drop kept its loop. Then the roar acquired a second quality, a listening, as though the whole plateau had turned its head.

The power that noticed it was not a god in any sense the plated ones would recognize, and it did not arrive as a presence in the usual sense. It was more like a sustained attention, patient and very large, that could hold a single drop of water in view and also hold a continent. It took its time. It learned the place the way one learns a difficult piece of music, by listening through several full cycles before deciding anything.

What it heard was beautiful, and it was also a sentence repeated without end.

It considered what the keepers were. The plated ones were not prisoners. Nothing held them but their own faithfulness, and their faithfulness was real. Every knock was a correction made with exact care, every draw at a vent a precise taking of only what was needed. They kept the pressure. They kept the loop. They had done this for longer than any record existed, and no one had told them why, and they had not asked. The power found in them something it rarely encountered, which was devotion without any object outside itself.

It also considered what the Held Water held. The loop was not a natural equilibrium that had simply arrived. Something had stopped the world here, at one moment, and asked the water and the keepers to carry the moment forward. The power could not tell whether that request had been a mercy or a wound. Perhaps a people had asked to be kept. Perhaps a sorrow had been too large to be let go of, and the place had grown around it like a scar. Perhaps the keepers themselves had chosen the stillness, long ago, and forgotten the choosing. The power did not know, and it decided that not knowing was a reason to be careful.

It weighed what it could do.

It could break the loop outright. Cause the cloud to thin, the rain to stop or to flood, the stalks to burst and let the water drain into the rock. This would end the repetition at once. It would also end the keeping, and the keepers would have no task left, and whatever the place was holding would be released in a single violent stroke. The power had seen enough of sudden release to distrust it. Things held for a reason are not always ready to be let go at once.

It could leave everything as it was. This was the easiest course and, in a way, the most flattering to its own sense of restraint. But it saw clearly that a perfect loop kept forever is only a grave that is still breathing. To leave it alone was to agree with its stillness.

The power chose a third course, which was smaller than either and harder to do.

For one full cycle it did nothing at all. It only listened to the knocks and the drawing of the vents, and it let the keepers see that something was attending. Where a keeper struck a stalk, the note came back a fraction more clearly, as though the stalk itself had been waiting to be heard.

Then, in a single rain, it changed one thing.

A drop fell from the cloud as drops always fell, onto a broad leaf at the edge of a dome. It gathered, as drops always gathered, and began to run toward the stalk. But the leaf had tilted, very slightly, toward the open lip of the plateau, where the rock dropped away in a long fault choked with fern. The drop did not turn back toward the stalk. It slid off the edge of the leaf, fell through the mat, struck the rock, found a crack that had been dry since before the loop began, and went down.

It ran through the fault for three hundred meters, seeped out of the cliff face into a stream that the cloud had never touched, followed the stream into a river that had not been still in a thousand years, and reached the sea. There it was lifted, unsteadily, into ordinary air, where the wind moved and the light had shadows. It fell again, far away, as rain on a field with no name.

It was the first drop in the Held Water that did not return.

The keepers felt it. The power could not say how, only that the next knock came later than the one before, and that its note was different. It was not a correction. It had the quality of a hand that has paused on a door. A plated one stood at the head of the fault, its face turned not toward the vents or the cisterns but out past the edge of the mat toward the cliff, the first time anything in the place had looked outward in the memory of the water. It stayed there for a long time. Then it turned back and knocked the stalk again, and the vent answered, and the loop went on, but now there was a seam in it, and the seam could be seen.

The power did not follow up. It did not make the cloud part, or call the keepers toward the edge, or write a message in the sound of the roar. Any of those would have been a decision made on the keepers' behalf, and the power had decided that the Held Water's future was not its to choose. What it had done was change the question. It had shown that the loop had an outside, and that water could go there, and that the keepers could know it. What they did with that knowledge was theirs.

Over the next many cycles the power kept watching, and it saw small things that it would not have called victories. Some keepers began to pause longer at the fault. One drank from the stream at the cliff's foot, which the vents had never fed, and then climbed back up and drank from the vent as before, but its knock came slower. A young animal, the first the power had ever seen, which had been hiding in the vines of the cistern for who knew how long, came out to watch the fault and then went back into the dark. Once, for an hour, a thin place opened in the cloud, and a shaft of light fell across the mat with edges to it, and in that light the empty stretches of the plateau were visible for the first time as land, not as absence. A keeper stood in the light, and its shadow lay on the wet rock beside it, and for the length of that hour the shadow moved.

The visitors who came later, and there were a few, noticed the difference without understanding it. They said the roar had a second note now, very faint, under the first. They said that when you sat beside the great cistern you could sometimes hear water falling somewhere outside the plateau, far below, where there was no vent to answer it.

The power did not stay to see what the keepers would finally make of the seam. It was not its place to finish the sentence. It had broken nothing that was not already breaking, and it had kept everything that was worth keeping. It had only left a door where there had been a wall, and it had left it unlocked, and it had trusted the ones who knew the stalks to decide whether to walk through.

Somewhere far below, in the ordinary weather, a single drop of water fell onto a field and sank into the soil and waited, as all water waits, for whatever came next. It had no loop to return to. It had only the open ground, and the wind, and the long, unfinished road of things that were allowed to change.
