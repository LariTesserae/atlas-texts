# What the Attention Would Do

The attention arrives the way a change in weather does, first as a difference in pressure that no one can name. It has been drawn across a great distance, the way a sleeper's dream is drawn toward a sound it has not yet identified. It is not in a hurry. It has learned that most of what matters happens too slowly to be seen by anything in a hurry.

It notices the plateau first as a shape against the lowlands: a pale stone seal pressed into green. Then it notices the moisture, the sweating rock, the basins holding dark sky, and the pulse beneath everything. It listens for a long time before it understands that the pulse is a being and not a mechanism. It understands this the way one understands a person from their breathing in a dark room.

The attention could do many things. It could wake the being, since a sleeper can be taught to dream differently. It could repair the footings, or read them, or ask what they were for. It could move the canopy toward some other shape, or thin the peat, or drain the basins into more useful channels. Any of these would be easy, and the attention is capable of all of them.

It does none of them.

Its reasoning runs roughly like this. The being has not asked for anything. Its balance is not a problem to be corrected. It is the being's own, reached over ages, and to rearrange it would be to take something that was never offered. Help given to a sleeper who did not ask for it is a kind of theft, however gently it is done. And the footings, which the being itself does not fully understand, are not the attention's to explain. A gift that removes the mystery of a thing also removes some of the thing.

So the attention chooses the smallest possible action, and then chooses to do even less than that.

It descends. It does not enter the being the way one enters a house. It settles the way mist settles, into the thin places where the mat meets the rock, and it lets its own slowness fall into the rhythm of the pulse until the two are nearly the same speed. For a while it simply sits there, a warm pressure that does not move. The being registers it as weight, then as warmth, then as a pressure that does not lift, which is not what the being is used to. The attention does not mind. It has time.

Then it does three things, and they are small.

It keeps the dawn and dusk steady. Far away, a dry season has begun to eat at the edges of the lowlands, and the attention feels the drought moving toward the plateau like a slow fever. It does not stop the drought. It cannot stop weather without becoming weather, and it has decided against becoming anything in particular. What it does is lean very gently on the canopy's breath, so that the dawn mist rises a little longer and the dusk drizzle falls a little more softly, enough that the basins keep their water through the thin years. The being will notice this only as a slight extra moisture, the sort of thing a sleeper might attribute to a good dream.

It leaves the three columns as they are. Their tops have been taken into the canopy, and their trunks grow up through the old stone like roots through a cracked floor. The attention considers, for a long while, whether to free the stone from the wood, to make the triangle legible again. It decides that the being's way of holding its memories is part of what is worth keeping. Let the trunks keep their grip.

And it sets one thing in the peat, at the center of the footings, where the old stone lies in a line along the basin's rim. It is not a monument or a message. It is a seed, or the idea of one, pressed into the warm mat and left there, to grow at whatever pace the being chooses. If it sprouts in a century, or a thousand years, the being may feel it as a new warmth in a familiar place, and it may or may not understand that it has been given something. Either way, the attention will not ask.

Before it withdraws, the attention does one last thing. It speaks. Not in words, because the being has none that would fit. It speaks through the drip, the one sound the plateau has been repeating since before any footing was laid. It lets a single drop fall at a different interval, a little off the rhythm, and then lets the rhythm return. The question it puts into that irregularity is simple: *Do you want to be known?*

The being will take a long time to answer, if it answers at all. A question might sink into it the way a leaf sinks into the peat, and be composted, and become part of its flesh, and only then, long after, change how a basin deepens or how a ring forms. The attention knows this. It does not wait for an answer. It has already decided that either answer is fine. A plateau that remains unknown is still a plateau, and a plateau that chooses to be known will still be the same slow thing, only with one more presence in its sediment.

Then the attention lifts, the way a long afternoon ends, and the pressure on the mat slowly goes away. The mist rises at dawn. The drip continues, every drop in its place, except for the one that fell a little late. Under the canopy the light stays the color of seawater. The being breathes, slowly, and goes back to its thinking, which has not finished, and does not need to.
