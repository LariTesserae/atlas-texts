# The Attention at the Rim

It does not arrive the way weather arrives. Nothing falls from the pale disk, and the mist does not part. The change is subtler than that. The mist over the basin grows a little thicker in one place and a little thinner in another, as if something has leaned close to look.

The attention is wide and patient. It has seen many basins, young and old, finished and unfinished, and it is not in a hurry. It moves across the flats the way a hand moves over a sleeping animal, not to wake it, only to learn its temperature and its breath. It notices the heat with weight. It notices the craters, each one identical to the rest, and it pauses over them the way one pauses over a word repeated too many times in a single sentence. It notices the pale threads in the clear pools.

Then it notices what the threads are waiting for.

## What It Considers

The attention is wise enough to know that the first impulse of power is to fix things, and that the impulse is usually a kind of blindness. So it does not act at once. It considers.

It could harden the rim. A word of stillness spoken into the fissures would cool the warm water and let the new stone set through, all the way to its heart. The lace would come to the edge and find the edge solid, and it would cross, or it would not, but either way the building would be finished. The basin would become rock, a fine and quiet rock, and the frogs would call over a plain with no threads in it. The stillness would be complete. It would also be a death, and the attention knows the difference between a balance and a silence, and it does not want to make a silence.

It could soften the rim. It could let the warmth spread outward until the fresh stone gave way like wet paper, and the lace would pour across the flats in one slow, shining tide. But the lace has never been a tide. It has been a creeping, careful thing that holds its breath at the white line, and if it crossed all at once, it would consume the mats and the reeds and the pale growths on the margin, and the basin would become a single living net with no edge at all. That is not a fulfillment. It is another kind of finish.

It could stop the bubbles. Then the craters would never form, and the lace would have no gesture to repeat, and the ridges would never grow. The basin would lose its record of hunger and its rhythm of rising and sinking. The attention could not make that choice without taking away the very thing that makes the lace what it is.

It could speak to the lace. It could explain the edge, or the stone, or the balance, or the fact that the waiting might end. But the lace has no word for hope or fear, and to give it one would be to give it a world it did not ask for. The attention would be trading the lace's own shape for a shape of its own design, and calling it a gift.

So the attention considers, and it finds that almost every act it could perform would be a kindness that kills. The basin has been balanced so long that the balance itself has become sacred, and the wise thing, the attention decides, is to be very careful about touching it.

## What It Does Instead

It sits.

It does not descend into the water or take the shape of a frog or a bird. It stays in the mist and learns the rhythm. It counts the calls of the frogs and the rising columns of insects, and it discovers that the cycles do not quite match, that each is a little out of step with the next, so the basin's silence is never a silence. It learns the bubbles. It learns that they rise when the ground decides, and that the ground decides in a pattern too slow for any one life to see. It learns the fringe, the cilia combing the mineral water, the slow pulse of the sweep.

And it learns the thread that lifts itself free.

When that thread hangs over the white line, quivering in the warm air, the attention feels something it has not expected. It is not hope, and it is not fear. It is the posture of a thing that has come to the edge of its own knowing and is holding still there, unable yet to step across and unwilling to step back. The attention recognizes this posture. It has been in it too.

This is the hardest thing the attention has ever admitted: it is not finished either. It is a power that has never been able to stop being a question, and it has come to this basin because the basin is the first place it has found that shares the same condition.

So it stops trying to fix. It joins the waiting.

## The One Small Act

After a long time, which is no time at all in a place where a bubble rises once every season, the attention does one thing.

It does not harden the stone or soften it. It does not stop the bubbles or speak. It finds a single fissure at the rim, one of the many that breathe at the ground's pace, and it moves the warmth in that fissure by the width of a hair. The water there grows a little warmer for a little longer. The new stone at that one point holds a fraction more firmly than the stone around it. Not enough to bear a fringe for long. Enough to bear it for a moment.

Then it withdraws.

It leaves the choice where it has always been, at the edge of the lace's own shape. The thread that has been hanging over the white line for who knows how long feels the stone beneath it firm up by a hair. It lowers itself, slowly, and its fringe touches the new rim, and for the space of a single breath the lace has a foothold it did not have before.

It does not cross. Not yet. The thread holds, combs the fresh stone, feels the warmth in it, and then lifts again and returns to the water. Whether the lace has understood anything, the attention cannot tell. It may only have felt a change in the temperature of the edge and responded the way it responds to everything, by feeding a little and waiting.

But the stone at that point is no longer quite what it was. A small ridge has begun there, a lacy bead slightly taller than its neighbors, and the bubbles that rise beside it will now rise into a slightly different shape. The basin is one notch changed. Not finished. Not ruined. Changed in the way a living thing is changed by being touched gently and then left alone.

## What It Leaves

The attention does not stay to see what the lace does with the foothold. It has learned that its staying would become a kind of gravity, and that a thing with gravity is never really left to its own edge. It turns back toward the wide sky, which is the only other place it has to go, and as it lifts, the mist over the basin thins and thickens in its old rhythm.

Before it goes, it leaves one thing behind, and it is not a gift. It is closer to a question, held as lightly as a drop on a leaf:

*You have been keeping the edge alive. Do you want to keep it? Or would you rather cross?*

The lace cannot answer in any language the attention knows. But the thread that touched the new stone is the first in a long time to hang over the white line and then lower itself to press against something that was, for a moment, almost ready. It may be a sign. It may be nothing but warm water and the ground's slow breath.

Either way, the choice has changed hands by a hair's breadth. The lace is no longer only waiting for the stone to harden. It is beginning, very slowly, to find out what it wants the stone to be.

Kneel at the rim of a clear pool and you can feel it: the warm water rising over your fingers, the fringe combing the mist, the pale threads lacing the crust in a pattern that repeats forever. And somewhere at the edge, one raised bead of new stone, faintly taller than the rest, catching the dim gold light like the first letter of a word no one has yet decided to finish.
