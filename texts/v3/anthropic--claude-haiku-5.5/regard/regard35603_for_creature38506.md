**What the Attending Power Did**

It noticed the Seep Shelf the way a listener notices a voice that has been speaking for a long time in an empty room. First there was only the sound: the braided meltwater, the penstocks booming like distant guns, the pump-throb in the ground. Then there was the shape beneath the sound, the single slow body spread across the upland, drinking and exhaling in a loop that had been running for longer than anyone had counted.

Then there was the traveler, one small warm disturbance crossing the wet.

The power's first impulse was to help. It is not wise to pretend otherwise. It saw the cracked saddles under the penstocks, the valve wheel choked in its bark collar, the footing gripped by roots. It saw the traveler's boots slipping on the streaming stone at the eastern rim, and the cold that would settle into bone by dusk. It saw the old huts with their bowed doors and the people who had shut them and walked away, and it felt something like grief for the workers who had built a clean, purposeful thing and then been called elsewhere.

It could have mended all of it. Rust could be unmade, rollers freed, the gauge glass cleared so the needle could be read again. It could have turned the pump station back to whatever it had been meant to do.

But the power had attended to the mat for a long time before deciding anything, and what it had learned was this: the machines were no longer only machines. The roots ran through the penstocks now. The vines were in the standpipes. The turbines turned on the body's schedule, and the body had learned to modulate its own water through the iron the way a hand learns the shape of a tool until the tool is part of the hand. To free the valve wheel would be to cut a nerve that was already grown. To clean the gauge would be to read a dial whose numbers the weather had erased, and to call that correction would be a lie about what the place had become.

The gullies on the eastern rim were the body's handwriting. The power recognized a sentence in progress and did not want to finish it for the author.

So it was careful, and it kept its hands open.

---

**The Season of Listening**

It spent a full season inside the water, which for the power was a small and difficult thing, like holding its breath for a year. It learned the body's concerns, which were not many and were not urgent. Some seeps were failing on the north slope, where the stone had split and the water had found a new seam to run through. Some hollows were drying at their edges. The mat did not ask for rescue. It was reaching, as it always reached, toward whatever was dry and open, and it was slow about it, and it was not afraid.

What it wanted, if wanting is the word, was only that the ground keep finding its way. That the water keep carving and carrying. That the tenants in the grey paste keep doing their small work unobserved. That the pulse of the pump station, which the body had come to feel as a rhythm of the whole upland, keep its tempo, and not grow erratic.

The power considered that last point longest. The pump station was the one thing still pulling against the body with an outside purpose. It was drawing from the same soaked earth, on a schedule set somewhere else, by hands that had left. The power could have silenced it. It could have made the throb stop, and the body would have adjusted, slowly, as it adjusted to everything.

But the body had already adjusted to the throb. The pulse had become part of its breathing, and to stop it would be a kind of violence against a rhythm the body had chosen, even if the choosing had been made by accident. The power decided only to let the throb be a little gentler, a little more responsive to the water's own weather. It did this by very small adjustments in pressure, a few degrees of pull, spread across a season so that no single moment would register as a change. The body noticed, the way it notices everything, as a shift in the local weather. It did not resist.

---

**The Gifts**

The power did three things, and it was careful to call them gifts only in its own private language, because it did not want to imagine itself as a giver to something that had never asked for anything.

The first was rain, not much of it, falling on the north slope in the one hollow where a seep was failing. The water found the new seam and rejoined the old flow. The mat rose green over a patch of stone that had been bare for a decade.

The second was a single clear hour in the fog. On the afternoon the traveler stood at the edge of the eastern rim, testing the streaming stone with one boot, the air thinned for a little while, and the far valley came through the glare: the falling land, the slow river of cloud, the pale shoulders of the higher peaks. The traveler would not know why the view had opened. The body would not know either, except as a warmth on a wide surface, the sun drinking through condensation.

The third the power did not do, and that was the choice it was proudest of. It did not correct the steel post with the faded arrow. It did not repaint it, did not move it, did not place another sign at the junction to say what lay ahead. The arrow still points at nothing. The power had come to think this was the most honest thing anyone had built on the Seep Shelf: a marker that admitted it could not lead anywhere, because the place was not a destination. It was a body, too wide and too slow to walk to, and the only way to know it was to walk across it and feel it give and sigh back.

---

**Withdrawal**

When the season ended, the power began to withdraw. It did not leave, exactly. Attention does not leave a place so much as stop leaning into it. It thinned the way the fog thinned, and the plateau returned to its own weather.

Before it went, it let one final thing happen, which it could not have prevented and did not try to. A drop of meltwater gathered at the tip of a fern-fine frond on the eastern rim, hung there in the cold white light, and fell onto a bare stone the body had been reaching toward for a long time. It landed in a pocket of grey paste. Somewhere under the surface, a small worker turned over and went back to its work.

Behind the traveler, a penstock boomed, a clearing of throat across the upland. Ahead, the fog closed again over the valley, and the river of cloud moved on through it with no particular hurry at all.

The power did not need to see it finish. It had seen enough to know the Seep Shelf was not waiting to be rescued, or completed, or understood. It was assembling its own skeleton, slowly, in its own handwriting, and the most any wise and benevolent attention could do was to stand at the edge of the wet, keep its hands open, and let the ground go on finding its way.
