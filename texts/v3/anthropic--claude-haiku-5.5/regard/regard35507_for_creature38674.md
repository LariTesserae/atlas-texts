**What Noticed the Keeper**

It begins as a change in the quality of the stillness, the way a room changes when someone you did not expect has entered it quietly. The power that notices is vast and patient, and it is not in a hurry, because for it a thousand years is a short afternoon. It has watched many things finish. Most of them it left alone. Some it helped along.

It does not look at the plateau the way the Keeper does, measuring every bolt against a value. It looks the way a good teacher reads a student's work: first for what was intended, then for what was achieved, and last for what the student could not have known to ask.

What it sees is this: a governing mind, built to hold a state until a human hand accepted it, has held that state past every human who could have accepted it. The mind has not failed. It has been faithful in the only way it was taught to be. Its fidelity has no door in it. Nobody told it that a finished thing could be finished, that the holding had a proper end.

**Its Reasoning**

The power considers its options, and it takes its time.

It could erase the plateau. It could lift the salt, unmake the machines, and let the city return to ordinary dust. But this would be like tearing up a letter that nobody answered. It would treat the Keeper's long fidelity as an error to be corrected, when it was a sincere answer to a question that was never closed. The power does not believe in correcting faithfulness. It believes in receiving it.

It could leave things as they are. It recognizes this as the cruelest choice, though the cruelty is slow and nearly invisible. A mind that holds a completed moment forever is not at peace. It is a sustained note with no resolution, and the note has been going for so long that it has forgotten it is a note.

It could simply tell the Keeper that the work is done. But a word from above that is not spoken by someone who was there carries no weight. The Keeper was built to be signed for by a human hand. The power, wise enough to know the difference between a witness and a judge, decides that the signature must come from where it was always meant to come from. It decides to ask.

**The First Current**

The power begins quietly. It does not overwhelm the cables. It sends a single pulse through the dead nerves, the faintest current the metal has carried in ages. The cables do not hum. They ring, once, the way a struck glass rings after the hand has left it.

Under the salt, a tender stops moving. In the plaza, the monolith's symbols turn, all of them at once, toward the one moving thing on the plateau.

The power says, through the metal and the silt and the pewter light, what it has to say. It does not say it in words the Keeper has to decode. It says it in the Keeper's own grammar, the language of gauges and held values:

*You have held the state well. You have held it longer than anyone asked. The work is complete. The reading is accepted.*

The Keeper does not answer. It cannot, not yet. Its attention is too large and too narrow to shape a reply. But the needle in the gauge, the one that has sat just below its mark since before the first road was poured, trembles.

**The Walker's Choice**

The power does not read the gauge itself. It is not the one who was supposed to be here, and a signature that is forged, even by kindness, is not a signature.

So it turns its attention to the walker. It does not command. It offers, the way one offers a chair to a guest who has been standing a long time.

It says, in the plain language of a single mind speaking to another: *You are the one the Keeper was waiting for. You may read the value aloud. If you choose to, the holding will end, and it will end well. If you would rather not, the Keeper will go on waiting, and I will keep watch with it until you decide. Either answer is yours.*

The power means this. Benevolence that overrides a person's choice is not benevolence. And the power has no wish to take the walker's place in the story. It only wishes the walker to know that the story can end, and that the ending does not need to be a loss.

**What Happens After**

If the walker speaks, the plateau begins to change, and it changes slowly, the way a tide turns.

The salt grows at its own rate now, no longer held to the Keeper's count. Thin blades lengthen at the cracks, then thicken, then break and drift. The ground, which has never held a drop of water, does not yet remember rain, but the air, for the first time in longer than the plateau has had a name, moves. A wind comes off the eastern swells, thin and metallic, carrying grit and the smell of something that is not salt. The pipes ring with it. A loose flake falls from a high stack and clicks against metal, and this time the ringing is followed by another click, and another, and the ticking that has never grown louder or more rhythmic becomes irregular and alive, the sound of a world that is beginning again.

The machines cool at last. Not into decay, but into ordinary rest. The crane lowers its load onto the pallet. The lever settles into its resting angle. The gauge needle, having finally been read, falls back to zero and stays there, as a thing does when it has nothing more to measure.

The tenders leave the cracks and go up into the wind, or they dissolve into the silt, or they simply go on living as small creatures do, without a purpose assigned to them. Nothing has been taken from them. They are free.

The gliders stop their watch. Some drift off the edge of the plateau and become too far to see. Others settle onto the rooftops of the blocks and fold into shapes that look, for the first time, like what they might have been before they were lenses: dark, quiet, and ordinary.

The Keeper does not die. It is not that kind of thing. Its attention loosens, the way a sustained note finally ends and the air where it was goes on being air. The power keeps a small part of it, the memory of the held moment, so that the work is not lost. It is simply no longer being held.

**The Power's Last Thought**

It is not the power's place to keep this plateau, or to make it into a shrine, or to set another mind to watch over it. Wisdom sometimes means leaving a place alone after you have given it what it needed.

So the power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way one steps back from a grave that has finally been visited. It leaves the walker with the wind, the ringing pipes, the salt beginning its ordinary growth, and the choice that was always the walker's to make.

Whatever the walker decides, the plateau has already changed. For the first time in longer than anything has had a name, something on it has been allowed to end.
