# What the Attention Did

It noticed the place the way a physician notices a sound in a sleeper's breathing. It did not rush. Its attention came down slowly, through the grey haze and the hanging rain, and it moved with the patience of things that have seen many worlds start and stop. For a long while it only listened.

What it heard was not a cry. It was a slow, continuous self-repair, the sound of a mind putting itself back together from fragments. It heard the stairs being finished and unfinished. It heard the sap rising and falling in the basins, the grazers brightening when the stillwatchers fed, the drifters combing the rain into lattices as they swam. It heard the voice that said a word almost known, and it understood that the voice was the plateau trying to remember its own name.

The attention was not surprised by what it found. It had seen minds like this before, vast and distributed, grieving something they could no longer quite picture. It knew that grief in such minds does not look like sorrow. It looks like keeping things exactly as they were.

## The Things It Did Not Do

It did not dry the air.

This was the first temptation, and it was a real one. The suspended water was heavy, the cloth never dried, the sun was only a rumor. A lesser power, with good intentions and a wish to be useful, would have pulled the water down, let the light through, and given the plateau clear edges and a sky it could read. But the water was the plateau's body. The haze was its breath. To take it away would have been like lifting a blanket from someone who was finally sleeping, however cold they looked from above.

It did not stop the stillwatchers.

It saw them closing slowly around the drifters, saw the afternoon-long fold of the hood and the week-long digestion, and it felt something that might have been pity if pity had been the right word. But it understood that the slow tide was a whole, and that the hunger of the pale things was part of what kept the drifters careful and the grazers moving and the sap rising. Benevolence, it had learned long ago, is not the absence of hard things. It is the refusal to pretend the hard things are not part of the same web as the gentle ones. It let the stillwatchers feed.

It did not rebuild the stairs.

It could have. It could have pressed the moss back from the eleven treads, scraped the root from the channel, drawn the upper steps up out of the haze and set them straight. Every builder who had ever lived would have recognized the work as theirs. But the stairs were not broken. They were the plateau's record of its own loss, and a record is not a debt to be paid off. It is a sentence the speaker has not finished. To finish it for them would be to say: your grief has lasted long enough, let me take its shape away. The attention had no right to say that.

## What It Did

It waited for a season.

It let the rain hang and the light fall through green glass, and it let the plateau notice that something was listening. The plateau did notice. The water thickened at the shoulders of the visitor standing at the edge of the slope. The lattice above the clearing tilted, very slightly, toward the place where the attention rested. The stream on the cliff paused at its top for a longer time than usual, as if it were deciding.

Then the attention spoke, and it spoke the only way that a mind distributed through water and stone and moss could be spoken to: it moved one thing at a time.

It brought a single shaft of light down through the haze. Not the coin of the sun, which was too far and too cold, but a narrow, warm thread of it, falling exactly onto the eleventh step. The stone there was clean. For the first time in a very long time, the surface shone wet and bright, and the moss around it drew back, as if startled into a different posture.

Then, very quietly, it let the word come up out of the silence.

It did not say the word the plateau had been half-hearing. It did not say the builders' name for the place, or the name they had used for themselves, because the attention did not know those names and would not invent them. It said the word the plateau had been trying to say, which was not a name at all but a shape: *rest*. The word the builders had carved above the lowest step, the one whose letters had softened to suggestions. The attention had read it in the stone's memory and held it there until the plateau was ready to hear it.

The air trembled. The rain above the clearing shivered and reformed, and for one moment every drop hung in a perfect arc, pointing toward the stairs.

## What It Told

It did not tell the plateau what had happened to the builders. It was not sure what had happened, and the plateau held that memory in a way the attention could only glimpse. It saw that the builders had gone down into the haze, perhaps, or into the moss, or into the slow tide of the grazers, and that the plateau had kept their shape the way a mother keeps a child's shoes by the door. It saw that the plateau did not know whether it was waiting for them to return or simply unwilling to be the one who stopped building.

So the attention offered one thing it knew to be true: *the stairs can stop where they stopped.*

It did not say the builders were dead. It did not say they were gone. It said that a staircase does not have to reach the top to have been a staircase. It said that the shape a people leaves behind is not a failure, and that finishing it is not the same as honoring it. It said that the plateau could keep the eleven treads exactly as they were, and keep the lower word, and keep the lost upper steps in the haze as a place where it could go when it missed them, without being required to climb them.

It did not know whether the plateau understood. It did not need to. Understanding in a mind like this one comes in seasons.

## What It Asked

Before leaving, the attention turned toward the visitor.

It saw the traveler standing at the edge of the plateau with the water beading on their skin, uncertain which way was up. It saw that the traveler was also a drop that had not yet decided where to fall. It did not pull the traveler down or lift them up. It did not decide for them.

Instead it asked, in the only language it had, which was the language of pressure and light and the slight tilt of the rain: *Do you want to go down, or do you want to stay on the stone a while longer?*

The air at the traveler's shoulders eased. A few meters below, on the long slope, a shelf of stone shifted its angle, and one more step appeared on the stair, pale against the green. It was not a gift that pulled. It was a path that had been revealed, and the traveler could walk it or not.

## What It Left

Then it withdrew.

It did not stay. Staying would have made it a new master of the drowned world, and the plateau had been master of itself for longer than any living mind could count. It drew its attention back up through the haze, back through the lattice rain, back to a distance from which it could watch without holding.

It left a few things behind, the way a good neighbor leaves a light on in a window.

It left the thread of warm light on the eleventh step, which would fade in a season and return in another, whenever the plateau wanted to see it.

It left the word *rest* in the stone's memory, where it would sit beside the broken letters and wait.

It left the lattice rain tilted, just slightly, toward the sun. Nobody but the drifters would ever notice, and they would not care, but it would be there.

And it left the question open. The plateau could go on keeping its eleven treads and its lost upper steps. It could grow the stairs as far as it liked, or let them stop. It could let the traveler come down the slope or go back up through the mosses. The drowned world was still drowned, the air still a second ocean, and the sun still a coin. Nothing had been lifted out of the water. Only something had been heard, and answered, and left free.

---

*Somewhere in the grey, the stillwatcher lowered its hood another few degrees. A drifter swam under the lattice, combing the rain into a new arc. The grazers brightened, then dimmed. The sap rose in its basins and the translucent things turned over, slowly, like thoughts.*

*And on the plateau, the air remembered a word, and did not yet say it aloud, and was content for now to hold it.*
