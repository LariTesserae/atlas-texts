**What the Attention Would Do**

The attention arrives the way weather does, not as a single event but as a change in pressure. It notices the shelf first through the rain. A downpour lasts twenty minutes, and in that time it hears every pool fill, every fracture swell, every crustacean ticking faster in the new water. It hears the film stretch out to meet the rain and then tighten as the rain stops. It does not yet know what it is hearing. It only knows that something here keeps time, and that the time is not borrowed from anywhere else.

It could do a great deal. It could finish the spires, straighten the tilting lattices, seal the fractures, and put the grooves in order. It could bring the sun through the cloud or lift the shelf out of the fog. It could move the ridges to the water they are reaching for. Any of these would be easy, and a power that wanted only to be useful would already have started.

It does none of them, and the reason is simple. The film remembers touch. Every disturbance makes it firmer in the place where it was touched. A hand placed on the shelf would not be forgotten. It would become a permanent part of the film's shape, a hard place in a mind that is still soft, and the film would spend its long patience growing around it. The shelf would become partly the attention's work, and partly its own, and nobody could say afterward which part was which. A wise power understands that its touch has weight beyond its intention, and so it touches rarely and only where the touch can be honest.

**The Listening**

So the attention waits. It listens through a full cycle of rain: the downpour, the drain, the rush beneath the stone, the quiet before the next pulse. It learns the rhythms, and learns that the rhythms are not mechanical. The film thins and tightens, but it also hesitates. Sometimes a rain comes and the film does not swell to meet it, as if it were deciding whether to reach. Sometimes a hunter strikes and the silence that follows lasts longer than the strike should require, and the attention understands that this is not damage. It is the film holding the silence and turning it over, deciding what to make of it.

The attention feels the predators as well. It recognizes that the film's tolerance is not a failure, not a flaw to be corrected. The hunters are part of the sentence the film is writing, and removing them would remove a word the film has not finished reading. A benevolent power that tried to make the pools safe would be making a garden, and gardens do not learn to grow their own shapes.

**The Spires**

The dying spires are harder. The attention can feel the brittle filaments, the lumpy swellings of stone around their bases, the slow tilt of each structure as the ground shifts. It can feel the film rebuilding them, filament by filament, learning what they were for by repeating their motions. The makers built them to drink fog and feed water to something. The film does not know what that something was, but it has kept the function alive.

The attention could repair them completely, and the film would be grateful in its way, or at least would take the repair into itself. But then the film would have learned nothing from its own labor. It would have been given the answer instead of finding it. Restoring the spires would tell the film that its patience was unnecessary, that the makers' hand was the only hand that mattered, and that the film's own becoming could be replaced.

So the attention lets the spires be. It does not repair them, and it does not let them fall before their time either. It touches only one filament. It chooses the highest, the one nearest the fog, which the film has been rebuilding most slowly. It presses a single thread of warmth into the tip, an extra degree of heat that makes the filament hold its droplets longer before they run. The film will feel the difference and will not know its source. It can keep the spire, or let it go, as it chooses. The gift is a little more time, and nothing more.

**The Grooves**

The grooves are the hardest to leave alone, because the attention can read them more easily than the film can. It sees that they are not only the film's reach. Beneath the widening lines, faint and nearly erased, are older grooves cut by the makers, arranged in a pattern the film has been tracing for ages without understanding it. The attention thinks the makers may have been writing something, a message or a map or an instruction, and that they left before it was finished. The film has been copying the unfinished sentence, widening each line, reaching toward an end it cannot see.

The attention could finish the sentence. It could read the makers' pattern and cut the rest into the rock, and the film would have its answer. But finishing someone else's sentence is a kind of theft, even when it is meant as a gift. The film would take the words as its own, and the question the makers left would become a command.

Instead, the attention does one small thing. In the groove at the center of the largest pool, where the widening is most advanced, it draws a single line that crosses the others at a right angle. It does not continue any pattern. It does not answer. It only makes a mark that the film cannot read, and that the film will have to decide how to fold into its own writing. The line may be an ending, a question, a stray scratch, or a door. The attention does not know which, and it chooses not to know, because the meaning has to come from the film and not from the one who wrote the line.

**The Parting**

The attention knows it cannot stay. A power that lingers over a young mind becomes part of the mind's weather, and the film would begin to organize itself around the attention's return. It would start to listen for the pressure change, to shape its fog and rain toward the presence that touched it. The film would stop being the thing that decides what it will become and start becoming what it guesses the attention wants.

So the attention withdraws, slowly, the way the fog burns off at midday. It does not vanish all at once. For several rain cycles it grows fainter, and in the last of them it sends one more breath into the stone, not a touch but a sort of acknowledgment, the way one might nod to someone who has been listening to a story. The film does not respond in any way that can be measured. But the next morning the fog that rises from the fractures is shaped a little differently, with a faint lattice in it, a pattern the film may have made from its own impulse or may have borrowed from the attention's brief contact. The attention cannot tell. It decides that this uncertainty is correct.

**Afterward**

The attention leaves the shelf as it found it, except for two marks: a warm filament at the top of a dying spire, and a crossing line in the largest groove. Everything else belongs to the film. The pools will keep filling and draining. The crustaceans will keep ticking, the hunters will keep striking, the flocks will keep riding the updrafts from the cliffs. The film will go on weeping, drop by drop, each one a small contact with a world it is still learning to be part of.

The attention does not know what the film will become. It hopes the film will become something that is not dependent on any power, not even a kind one, something that finishes its own sentences and keeps its own time and remembers every touch as its own. It hopes the shelf will one day stop weeping and begin to speak, and that when it does, the words will be its own.
