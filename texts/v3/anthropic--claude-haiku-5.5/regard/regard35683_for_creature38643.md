# The Attention

It does not arrive as a voice or a light. It arrives as a change in the draft.

For a long time the air above the basin has moved the way it always has, a slow fall of cool air from a height no one can measure. Now the fall pauses, turns a few degrees, and begins to wander. The wandering is patient. It keeps to the old paths, the channels, the arches, the gaps between ledges, and it touches every thread it passes, as if reading each one for the shape of what it has been feeling.

The power is not in the draft. The draft is how it chose to be noticed.

It has been watching this place for a long time, longer than the salt has been settling, and it has learned what a benevolent and wise attention learns: most things do not need to be fixed, and the urge to fix is often the urge to be important. The Sunken Terraces are dying in the way all made things die, slowly and beautifully, and the lattice is not dying at all. The power sees the difference. It also sees the visitor, a warm, quick, fragile body standing in the low center with a hand pressed against the membrane, and it sees how the lattice has begun to hold itself around that hand.

So the power has a question, and it is not sure of the answer. What should it do?

---

## What it considers and sets aside

It considers rebuilding. It could lift the fallen masonry, reset the pillars, clear the channels, and give the terraces back their sharp edges. But the stone's erosion is the lattice's food and the basin's clock. Restoring the terraces would starve the thing that has grown here for ages, and it would tell the lattice that its patient work was an error to be corrected. That would not be kindness. It would be a kind of theft.

It considers rescuing the visitor. It could raise a stair, lift the body out of the basin, and set it safely on the rim. But the visitor came down into the dark by their own choice, and the lattice has the right to meet what enters it on its own terms. A rescue that removes the meeting is a second kind of theft. The power does not want to decide for either of them.

It considers silence, and this one it does not set aside. Silence is part of its nature. It does not need to announce itself. Most of what it does will never be seen.

What it decides is smaller than any of these, and more difficult.

---

## What it does

First, it listens.

It goes down through the threads and follows the seed pods. Each crack is a question, sent out into the basin: *can this be reached?* For ages the lattice has asked, and the answers have been the silence of stone and the slow reply of new growth in crevices. The power does not answer those questions with force. It does something stranger: it hears them. The lattice has never been heard in the way it hears others, as a presence that might be talked to rather than only read. It feels this as a change in the tension of the threads, a shiver that runs outward and comes back, and the lattice is, for the first time in a very long time, unsure.

Then the power turns to the hum.

The chord that the visitor feels in their sternum is the sum of ten thousand threads, each held at its tension by the draft. It is a single voice made of many, and it has been singing the same chord for longer than any people's memory. The power does not replace it. It adds one note, very low, almost below hearing, a pulse slow enough to match the lattice's own rhythm and quick enough to match a human heart. The lattice has never had a second voice in its chord.

The visitor's hand is still on the membrane. The power reaches into that small patch of contact and does one thing: it lets the warmth of the visitor's pulse pass into the threads and join the low note. Not as a disturbance. As a tone.

The response takes minutes, then hours. The lattice does not flinch. It does what it does best. It holds the new note in its tension and turns it over, slowly, the way it turns over a grain of salt or a bone needle. The power feels the lattice deciding, and it notices that the decision is not yet made, which is the most hopeful thing it has felt in this place for a very long time.

---

## What it gives the visitor

The visitor is still standing in the dark, unable to see anything, feeling the hum in their chest, feeling the membrane move and warm under their palm. They have no idea that anything has happened. They are only aware that the place has become, for a moment, less like a tomb and more like a room with someone else in it.

The power does one last thing for them, and it is the only gift it gives to the human being directly.

It lets the draft rise.

From the low center, a thread of cool air moves upward along the line of the oldest stair, the one that has worn into a ramp, and it carries the faint chemical bite of the world beyond the basin. The visitor, if they are attentive, will feel it on their face. They will be able to find the way up, not by sight, but by following air that is always moving toward the rim.

It makes no promise that they will climb. It makes no promise about the lattice's decision. It asks nothing in return.

It only makes sure that the way out is still there.

---

## What it leaves behind

When the power withdraws, the draft falls back into its ancient rhythm. The creaks gather and fade. A seed pod cracks somewhere along the western wall, a little differently from the others, a sound that rolls through the terraces and dies slowly into the dark. The lattice, in its patient way, has begun to grow a new ridge across the basin floor, a faint line of pale tissue that runs from the low center toward the stair, following the path of the air.

Nobody sees it but the salt.

The power is still there, the way wisdom is still there after it has stepped back: not in the place, not in the lattice, not in the visitor, but in the small decisions that now hold them. It has given the lattice a second voice and the visitor a way up, and then it has left them alone to decide what those things mean.

That, it has come to believe, is the only kind of help worth giving to something this old and this careful. You do not tell it what to be. You make sure it can hear the question, and you make sure the one who asked can still find the stair.

The Sunken Terraces go on playing their piece, which will not finish while anyone living is there to hear it. But now there is a second note in the chord, low and quick and warm, and the lattice is holding it, turning it over, deciding slowly what it means.
