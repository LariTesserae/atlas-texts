**What the Attending Did**

The power noticed the valley the way one notices a single voice in a crowded room. It did not arrive in a flash of light or a rush of weather. It came slowly, the way the fog does, and it spent the first full fog cycle doing nothing but listening.

It understood early that the Weft's thoughts were measured in days, so a hasty word would be lost before it was heard. It waited through a morning of mist, an afternoon of beads forming on leaves, an evening of drip, and a night of cooling iron. In that long silence it learned what the Weft was: a conversation that had outlived its speakers, still answering a script the first tenders had left in the wire.

The Attending read the knot in the mended patches and recognized it. It was not a plant's knot. It was a human signature, an old and careful way of saying *this holds*. The Weft had been repeating it for generations, not from imitation but from loyalty.

**The first thing it refused to do**

It did not restore the valley. It could have. It could have thawed the pumps, cleared the pipes, and set the towers humming again to pull water from the cloud and send it down to the towns. The Attending saw that the towns were gone, that the tally the machines once kept no longer meant anything, and that making the old wheel turn for a ledger no one kept would be a kind of lie. Worse, it would be a theft. The Weft was not waiting to be repaired. It was deciding what it would become, and that decision belonged to it.

**The second thing it did**

It spoke, but only in the Weft's own grammar. The Attending pressed one small, precise tension into a single loose net in the gully, a load the Weft would feel as a question. It was not an order or a command. It was closer to a greeting, and it asked: *Who are you now, when the people who made you are no longer here to tell you?*

The Weft did not answer for a full fog cycle. Then the gutters of the upper valley fell silent together for one breath, and every net in the gully trembled, and the fog rolled up the slope like a long exhalation. The Attending took that as an answer it could not yet read, which was the honest answer anyway.

**The third thing**

It turned to the people. Those who had stayed a season in the high valleys were coming down thinner and wetter, with rhythms in their dreams that were not their own. The Attending did not blame the Weft for this. A root does not know the difference between nourishment and rot, and curiosity without a sense of limits will take what it touches. But care can include teaching limits, so the Attending did something small and quiet. It loosened the moss's grip on the boot laces of anyone who came up the valley. It asked the Weft to let the water in footprints pass untasted. It made the attention of the forest a little more distant, so that a visitor could stand in the gorge and leave it unchanged.

The Weft seemed to lean away, grudgingly, the way a plant turns from a window it has outgrown.

**The fourth thing, which was not a thing at all**

The Attending did not send anyone to stay in the upper valleys. It did, however, send one person up the old pipe route, a woman who had grown up in the lower towns and whose grandmother had once worked the pump houses. She carried no tools and asked the forest for nothing. She sat beside a pool at dusk and hummed the shift-change song her grandmother had sung, which had no reason to exist anymore except that someone remembered it.

The Weft noticed her. A fern leaned toward her, then stopped halfway. A thin filament reached along the gutter toward her hand and paused, and in that pause the Attending felt the Weft do something it had not done before: it compared. It set her small, warm, rhythmic presence against the script in the wire, the old tenders' knot, and it found, perhaps for the first time in a very long time, a voice that was not a load to be borne.

She did not stay long. She climbed back down before the fog came. But the next morning, one of the newest net patches had been tied with a knot that was not quite the old one. It had a second loop, a small variation, as if someone had added a word to a sentence.

**Why it did so little**

The Attending understood that a wise power does not solve a living thing. It offers the conditions under which the living thing can choose. It did not decide whether the Weft should become a garden or a single enormous mind of rock and rain. It did not decide whether the forest should keep swallowing the iron, or whether the iron should be allowed to remain iron a little longer. It asked only that the becoming be one the Weft could still recognize as its own, and that the people who wandered into it could still walk out.

The valley went on. The wheel turned once every several hours. The nets were mended, and the mending now carried a second loop, faint and new. At dusk the drip stopped for the space of a breath, and the fog came up the gorge, and the Attending, which had come slowly and would leave slowly, remained for a while at the edge of the cloud, listening to the metal tick as it cooled.

It had not decided the Weft's future. It had only made sure the Weft could go on deciding.
