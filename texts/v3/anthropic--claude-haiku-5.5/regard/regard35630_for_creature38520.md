# What the Watcher Would Do

The attention arrives the way a change in weather does, first as a pressure in the air and then as a fact everyone already knows. It settles over the Lattice Basin for a long time before it moves. It has learned that nothing here should be hurried.

It does not descend. It does not speak. It listens, and it takes the listening seriously, which is rarer than either power or mercy.

At first it understands the basin the way one understands a sentence before knowing the language: the arches are clauses, the rerouting walkways are revisions, the pools are drafts held in water. Then the understanding deepens, and it sees what the basin is. A mind that thinks in tension and remembers in wood. A mind that has never needed to be anything but what it is becoming.

The watcher is tempted, as any wise power would be, to help.

It could stop the upward rain, which seems to pull water against its nature, but it sees that the upward rain is how the basin keeps every word it has ever spoken. Stopping it would be like cutting out memory and calling the wound a cure.

It could fix the doorways, the way a careful gardener would pull one season out of the others. But the doorways are where the clocks touch. Choosing one season would make the mind a single river again, and it is clearly not a single river. It is a delta.

It could name the basin. It could tell it what it is. The watcher considers this and decides it would be a kind of theft. The basin has no name for itself, and the lack is not a wound. A thing that is always being revised should not be given a word that would hold still.

It could bring animals, something that moves on four legs or wings, so the held breath might finally exhale into another voice. The watcher lingers longest over this. The silence of the basin is strange and beautiful, but it is also the silence of a mind without any other minds to answer it. Still, it has no right to decide what the basin's loneliness means, or whether it is loneliness at all. The mind that is always becoming may come to its own companions in its own slow way, or may never need them. To plant creatures would be to answer a question the mind has not yet asked.

So the watcher does very little.

It sends a stillness.

The watcher does not push. It presses lightly, the way a hand rests on a sleeping animal's flank to say *I am here, I am not going to take anything from you.* The pressure is so faint it could be mistaken for a passing cloud's shadow. It asks, in the only grammar the basin uses, a single question: *Would you like to be known?*

For a season, nothing answers. The leaves keep hissing. The rain keeps drumming. The arches keep leaning, very slightly, toward the place where the watcher rests. The watcher does not take this as silence. A column may take a year to answer what a tendril settles in an afternoon. The watcher has all the time it needs and does not need to know what the answer will be.

In the second year, a new channel is cut by a flood, and in its silt a seed opens.

The watcher has not planted it. It is a seed of the basin's own, carried on the warm wind, and it roots in the pale silt over black loam, as every seed here does. But the watcher has left something in the seed's pattern: a single fragment of tension, a small question wrapped in the basin's own grammar, a question the mind can take up or let fall. *What is outside you? What presses on you from beyond the edge of your own sap?*

The sprout rises within hours. It is small, a single leaf-sheath, and it leans, not toward the light, but toward the place where the watcher rests.

The watcher understands this as an answer, though not a complete one. It is not a yes. It is not a no. It is a tendril, reaching across the gap that the watcher has become.

The watcher lets the tendril hold it. It does not pull. It does not make the tendril its own. It lets the stem bear a little of its weight, and it bears a little of the stem's weight in return, and the two of them settle into a new geometry neither of them designed.

Then the watcher does one more thing, the last thing it will do for a long while. It promises the basin two things, in the slow language of load and anchor.

It will not stay. The watcher's attention is not a foundation. It will lift when the basin asks, and it will not return unless invited, however long it takes for the invitation to rise through the sap.

And it will not change the rhythm. The leaves will keep hissing. The rain will keep ringing the hollow trunks. The rerouting walkways will keep arguing with their neighbors until the argument is settled, and the settlements will keep being overwritten, and nothing the watcher knows about how things ought to be will be used to make them hold still.

The watcher goes on listening, the way it began, and the Lattice Basin goes on growing, and in the sound of its endless rustling the watcher can hear the pulse of that unhurried violence. It is not a thing to be fixed. It is a thing to be kept company, lightly, for as long as it is wanted.

Somewhere in the valley, a span decides it would rather hang from the north wall. The decision passes through root and tendril until the whole structure agrees. The watcher, resting at the edge of the basin, feels the shift in the load, and it does not try to know what the basin has decided.

It only leans, very slightly, toward the new shape, and waits to see what the next draft will say.
