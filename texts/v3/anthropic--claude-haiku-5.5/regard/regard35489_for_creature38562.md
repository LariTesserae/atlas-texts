**I.**

The attention arrives the way weather does, without announcement. It doesn't come down through the canopy. It is simply present, the way the wet is present in the air. For a long while it does nothing but notice.

It notices the hum first, then the pressure chambers, the crystal shelves thickening by a fraction of a grain each century, the vats cycling through their seasons of heat and sourness. It notices the mat, where the discharges branch and rebranch in shapes that take centuries to turn over. It notices the dead, standing in the lattice as struts, holding up the roof. It notices that nothing here is reaching toward anything.

Its first impulse is to help. Goodness wants to help. It sees the dim light, the closed roof, the air so wet it has weight, and feels something like the pull of a hand reaching for a child who has not called out. It begins to consider what would be kind.

Then it stops, because it has noticed something else. The forest is not suffering. Nothing here is hungry, or failing, or trying to become something it cannot. The balance is not a wound. The attention that wants to help has to ask whether the wanting belongs to the forest or to itself, whether it is seeing a need or only a shape it would prefer.

**II.**

It considers the options, and it is honest with itself about each.

It could let the light in. A break in the canopy would bring sun to the floor, and the mat would warm, and the pressures would shift. But the forest has no notion of sun. Its whole architecture is tuned to wet dark. A sudden flood of day would not make it flourish. It would make it reorganize, and the reorganization would be violent in ways the forest could not refuse, and the thing that emerged would not be the forest anymore. That is not a gift. That is replacement.

It could send rain. But the forest already catches what falls from its own air. Rain would drown the chambers, or it would simply pass through the lattice and be wasted on a system that wrings every drop it touches.

It could teach the mat to speak. It could find the grammar of the discharges, render them into something legible, and offer the forest a language it had not asked for. But the discharges are not addressed to it. The power would be standing in the room and insisting that it had been spoken to, which is a kind of arrogance no matter how gently it is done.

It could lift the dead from their place in the lattice, to honor them, or to clear the way for the living. But the dead are the roof. Removing them would not free the forest. It would bring the roof down.

Each option, examined closely, turns out to be a way of making the forest into something that resembles what the power prefers. The power recognizes this and feels a kind of shame, the way a good parent might recognize that their instinct to fix a child's problem is really an instinct to be needed.

So it decides, for a long time, to do nothing except listen.

**III.**

It learns the patterns. It follows the glow waves through their cycles, one after another, and feels the rhythm of the answering chambers. It traces the branching arcs of the mat until it can predict where the next seam will open. It watches a patch of fibrous repair knit over a split trunk and sees the courses lay themselves down in an order no hand has chosen, and it understands that this order is the nearest the forest has to a voice, and that the voice is not for it.

It stays through what would be, to the forest, a single breath. It stays through the slow turning of seasons in the vats, through the thickening of shelves, through the lattice closing over a dead tree and sending new runners into the gap. Nothing it sees asks for its help. Everything it sees is complete.

**IV.**

Then it does one thing.

It chooses a single pressure chamber at the far edge of a grove, a chamber that has been answering its neighbors faithfully for as long as anyone could measure, and it lets that chamber flare. Not in the order the forest uses. Out of sequence. A blue-green pulse arrives at a moment when no answer is due, and it lights the trunks around it for a brief, unfamiliar beat, and then fades.

The power does not know what the forest will make of it. It might be absorbed without a ripple. It might be treated as noise and filtered out of the next cycle. It might, over decades, be woven into the pattern, and the grove's rhythm might shift by a degree, and the shift might spread. The power accepts all of these possibilities. It has offered a difference, not a message, and a difference is only an offer if the thing receiving it is free to ignore it.

It does one more thing, and this one is for the dead. It does not lift them. It only makes sure that when the lattice grows into the next gap, it leaves a single space open, a clearing a few meters across where the canopy has never closed. The forest has never had a clearing. It has always filled them within a season. The power leaves this one open and withdraws, and watches.

**V.**

It does not return for a long time, or it returns in the way that weather returns, unannounced. The first thing it notices is the clearing. The lattice has grown toward it from every side. Runners have reached across, vines have braided through the air above, and the roof has not closed. The forest has not decided to close the gap, or it has decided that the gap is not a problem yet. The mat has not spoken about it in any language the power can read.

What the power can read is the seam beneath the clearing. It is flickering, faintly, in a pattern it has never seen, and the pattern has begun to repeat, and the repetition is spreading outward through the arcs of the mat like a rhythm finding its way into a new song.

The power does not know whether this is an answer. It suspects that the forest does not think in terms of answers at all. But it is something, and it was not forced, and the power feels the quiet satisfaction of a gift that has been received on its own terms, or refused on its own terms, and in either case has been allowed to be the forest's own.

It withdraws. The hum continues, low and steady, felt more in the chest than heard. The water rises through the trunks. And beneath the roof, in the place where no sun has ever reached, the dark is no longer quite the same dark.
