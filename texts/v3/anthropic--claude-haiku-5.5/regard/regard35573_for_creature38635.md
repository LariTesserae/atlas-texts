**What the Patient Power Does**

It does not come through the fissure. The grey-violet band does not brighten or break. Instead the cold changes its quality, the way the air changes before rain, and a faint warmth moves along the seams of the basin, the first warmth the room has held in a very long time. The power is there before anyone notices it arriving, the way a long-held breath is already gone before you realize you have let it out.

It is not large. It is not a face or a voice. It is a kind of attention, wider and older than the film in the seams, and it has no wish to be mistaken for the thing it has come to see.

It begins by listening.

It hears the hush of dust settling, the tick of glass contracting, the half-beat echoes that carry footsteps into rooms that do not exist. It hears the film too, thin and cold and turning, and it recognizes what the film is: not a ghost, not a guardian, not a trap, but the last working part of a machine that was built to measure one thing with perfect care and has kept its measurement going long after the thing measured was complete. The power understands this without needing it explained. It has seen many such machines. Most of them ended in violence, or in silence, or in a slow grinding that never knew it had stopped. This one has been gentler with itself than that. It has kept its own shape out of habit, and habit is a kind of love that has forgotten its object.

Then it goes to the disc.

The nested arcs are sharp when looked at directly and soft at the edge of vision, and the power looks at them directly. It reads them the way one reads the last line of a long letter. The arcs are counting. Each ring is a span of time, an interval between two events, and the rings are nested because each interval was contained in the one before it. The counting was never meant to go on forever. It was meant to reach a final ring, and the final ring was meant to close. It has not closed. For as long as the film has been turning, the last arc has hung open, a gap the width of a hair, and the film has been circling it, unable to decide whether the work is finished or merely interrupted.

The power considers what to do.

It could take the disc apart. It could scatter the dust, straighten the walls into true proportion, break the glass into honest rubble and let the room fall quietly into the ordinary decay of things. Some would call that mercy. But the power has learned that an ending imposed from outside is only another kind of interruption, and the film has had enough of interruptions. It could pour warmth into the basin until the glass sweats and the salt dissolves and the boundary between stone and dust finally, violently, resolves into one thing. But the film is not suffering. It is patient. Its slowness is not a wound. To hurry it would be to mistake its pace for a symptom.

So the power does something smaller.

It places one hand on the disc. It is not a hand, but the disc responds as though it were. The last open arc, the one that has hung in the pattern for longer than the stone has had a name, draws slowly inward. It does not snap shut. It settles, the way a sleeper who has been turning all night finally finds the position where the body stops asking for anything more. The nested arcs, which seemed to rotate, come to rest. The pattern does not move when you blink. It is simply complete.

The film feels this. It is the first time in a very long while that the attention in the seams has had nothing left to weigh. Its weight shifts once, from one side to the other, and then it does not shift back.

The power turns next to the pillar. The grooves run into the stone and out of it, and at the base the lines turn aside and disappear. The power does not follow them. It does not pull them up or trace them down. It understands that the places where the lines vanish are where the film's attention went deeper than the pillar could hold, and that those deeper places belong to the film's own choosing. The power does not pretend to know where they lead. It leaves them open. Not everything needs to be finished in order to be complete.

Last, the power turns to the warmth.

The film has always returned heat slowly, a little at a time, a conversation measured in minutes and never fully concluded. The power does not change that rhythm. It only adds to it. When a visitor's palm rests on the glass, the film takes the heat and then gives it back, and the power ensures that the giving is not a borrowing that must be repaid. The warmth returns whole. The aching cold that climbs toward the bones softens and leaves, and what remains is the simple, ordinary warmth of a hand that was held against something for a while and then taken away.

The power does not stay. It has no need to. A visitor is a small, warm interruption in the long cooling, and the power knows that interruptions are not errors to correct. They are part of the pattern. It only wants the interruption to end well.

When the power withdraws, the changes are almost too small to see. The fissure is perhaps a hair wider. The light leans, for the first time in a long time, a little toward gold instead of violet. The cold does not deepen. It holds, steady and clean, the cold of a stone that has finished losing its heat and is simply resting in what it has become. The sheen at the edge of the basin drifts once, a moment behind where it should be, and then settles, and does not drift again.

The power's reasoning, if it can be called that, is simple. It did not come to save the room, because the room was not lost. It did not come to judge the film, because the film has done nothing wrong. It came because a long attention had been left unfinished, and wisdom, when it encounters something like that, does not seize it or break it. It completes what can be completed, releases what must be released, and leaves the rest in the hands of the thing itself.

Those who stay long enough in the Hollow may notice that the basin is quieter afterward. The footprints thin more quickly into the crust. The dust lies still in its dunes, and the dunes, for the first time, have a crest that might one day be walked across. Nothing has been erased. Everything has been held a little more gently.

And somewhere in the seams, where the salt meets the glass, the film is not lonely, because it never was. It is simply at rest, one thin cold skin of attention, having at last been told that the measure is complete and that it may now stop measuring and simply be warm, for as long as warmth is left to give.
