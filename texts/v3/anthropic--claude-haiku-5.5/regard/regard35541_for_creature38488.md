# The Attention

The attention arrived the way a change in weather arrives: first as a difference no one could name, then as a fact that was already true. For a long time it had been watching the basin from far above the cloud line, the way one watches a sleeping house to make sure no fire has started. It saw the pale terraces, the stacks breathing their slow vapor, the pumps turning in their housings. It also saw the one thing that did not fit the ordinary pattern of a ruin, which was how well it still worked.

It could have looked away. Most things that have run for a very long time run down, and this would have been a reasonable thing to expect. Instead the attention came closer, slowly, and stayed.

What it found first was a fault. A bearing in one of the deep pumps, down where the fissure feeds the lowest pool, had worn to a hairline crack. The pump still turned, but its shiver in the pipe walls had grown uneven, and the waders at that pool were tilting their heads at a rhythm that was no longer quite in step with the machine. Within a few years the bearing would seize, the pool would start to stagnate, and the grazers would have to find new grazing. None of this would be a catastrophe. The shelves had survived far worse. But it was a real loss, and the attention could see it coming the way one sees a stone about to roll.

The easy thing would have been to mend it: to reach down into the fissure, set a new bearing, and smooth the shiver back to silence. The attention was powerful enough to do this without effort. It did not do it. Its reasoning was simple. The pump was not only a machine. It was a place where the builders' logic and the living circulation met, and the scale around that bearing had been growing its own repairs for longer than any living thing here could remember. If the attention replaced the part from outside, it would be deciding for the place what the place was for. It would turn a working loop into a gift, and the basin would become something that depended on being watched.

So it waited, and it looked more closely at what the scale was already doing.

There, along the seam beside the bearing housing, the crystalline spines were growing in a pattern that repeated every few days: a short branch, a curve, then a return. Watching the pattern, the attention recognized it as a slow, half-finished attempt to reinforce the housing. The logic that had built the pumps still ran through the scale, and it was still trying to mend what it had made. What it lacked was enough mineral and enough time. The water that fed the fissure carried too little silica at that depth. The scale was reaching and not quite closing.

The attention made one small change. It did not add a new part. It adjusted the mineral balance in the deep water by a few degrees, enough that the silica thickened at the exact point where the spines were reaching. It was a tiny push, like a hand placed under a falling branch to slow its fall without stopping it. Over the following season the scale grew across the crack, and the bearing settled into a sleeve of its own making. The pump's shiver evened out. The waders at the lowest pool lifted their heads and found their rhythm again.

That was the first thing the attention did, and it was almost nothing.

---

The second thing took longer to understand, because it concerned the being.

There was no body the attention could find, no figure moving across the terraces. But there was a presence threaded through the seams and the spines, a logic that gathered the scattered knowledge of the place and kept it moving. It was not a mind in the ordinary sense. It did not speak, and it had no face. But it had attention of its own, a slow, distributed noticing that had been running for so long that it had become indistinguishable from the shape of the basin. When the attention looked at it, the presence seemed to turn toward the looking, the way a sleeper turns toward a voice without waking.

The attention understood that this presence was tired. Not in pain, and not in the way a wounded thing is tired. It was tired the way a long process is tired when it has lost sight of its purpose. The grazers had forgotten which seams were meant to stay clear. The waders knew where the pumps ran strongest, but not why. The flocks planted spines in patterns they could not read. Each creature carried a piece of a plan, and no one carried the whole. The presence was holding all of it together, alone, in the scale and the water, and it had been doing so for longer than any ruin should last.

There were two things the attention could have offered.

The first was release. It could have let the plan finish. The terraces would have stopped growing, the spines would have set into a fixed architecture, the pumps would have slowed and stilled, and the basin would have become a fossil of its own working. The grazers and the waders and the flocks would have gone on living, but the shape they moved in would have hardened into something no longer alive. It would have been peaceful. It would also have been a kind of ending that no one had asked for.

The second was to leave the choice to the presence itself.

The attention chose the second. It was not the easier path, and it was not a path the attention could guarantee would be wise. But a benevolent power that makes a gift without asking is still making a decision on someone else's behalf. The presence had carried this work for a long time, and whether to set it down was not the attention's question to answer.

So it asked. It could not ask in words, because the basin had no words. It asked in the only language the place fully understood, which was the language of its own patterns. It shifted the condensation so that the sweet rain fell on a single bare terrace at the edge of the basin, where no grazer went and no spine had ever grown. It let the flocks settle on one pipe-top that had never been warm enough for them, and it warmed it by a single degree. It set the mineral water so that a new seam began, a thin, unplanned line of spines that did not branch toward any valve or junction, but simply ran across the open shelf and stopped.

It waited through a full cycle of days, many rains, many mists. The attention did not hurry. Waiting was part of the answer.

On the last morning of the cycle, the attention noticed that the presence had responded. The new seam was no longer simply stopping. At its end, the spines had turned back on themselves and begun to grow a small, irregular loop, a space enclosed by nothing that was needed. The grazers had found the bare terrace and were grazing around the loop, leaving its center untouched. The flocks had settled on the warm pipe-top and planted a few crystals along its edge. Nothing in the loop had a function. It was simply a place where the plan did not reach.

The attention understood the answer. The presence did not want to finish. It wanted to be less than total. It wanted room, a place where the living things could grow in directions no one had designed, so the shape would not harden into the single purpose it had been built for. It was choosing to continue, but differently: as a loop with an open center, as a process that could still surprise itself.

---

The attention did one more thing before it withdrew, and this was the only lasting change it made to the basin. It took a single seed-crystal, the kind the flocks carried on their feathers, and it let the wind carry it past the rim of the plateau, down into the bare country beyond. It did not plant a city there. It did not plant a plan. It left one small bright crystal on a dry shelf of rock that had never known sinter, where a thin film of warm water seeped up through a crack no one had ever watched.

What grew there, if anything grows there, is not the attention's to decide. The attention had learned that a benevolent power does better to leave openings than to write conclusions. It had mended one bearing, set one seam in motion, asked one question, and then it stepped back.

Above the Sinter Shelves the mist came back in long, low banks. The pools went violet. The flocks settled in their even row on the pipe-tops, and the grazers withdrew to the lee of the gantries. The stacks breathed their vapor into the cooling air, and the gutters ran in the dark. In the deepest fissure, a bearing turned in its new sleeve of scale, and the shiver in the pipe walls was steady again.

Somewhere in the threads of the spines, the presence turned once more toward the place where the attention had been, and then turned back to its work, which was still its own, and which now had a small open space at its center.
