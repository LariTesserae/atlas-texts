# The Attention

It did not arrive so much as become aware of the place, the way one becomes aware of a room has gone quiet. For a long time its attention had been spread over many things, and now it gathered and rested on a basin of pale silt three hundred years out of the sea.

It noticed the basalt ribs first, black and unweathered, still carrying the chisel-marks of water. Then the clean stepped cracks in the silt, the sharp lips of the gullies. The land was young and had not finished being made, and the power felt a tenderness toward that unfinished state, the way one feels toward a child who has not yet decided what to be.

Then it noticed the Weirs.

It spent a long time with them. It watched the lattices open at dawn and close at dusk, the joints shifting like the gills of something asleep. It felt the warmth in their surfaces after the sun had gone. It followed a droplet from a filament in the haze down a channel into a basin and out into the silt, and saw how the reeds rose from that water, how the frogs and flyers and three-eyed runners came to the flow and fed. It understood the whole arrangement at once, the way one understands a face: as a single expression of something alive.

*You are drinking from the air,* it thought, *and giving what you drink to the ground. You have made a body out of mud and green stems and you are its breath.*

The power had seen many things that might be called alive. It had learned that the first question is rarely the right one. The question was not *what are you?* The question was *what do you want, and what is my part in it?*

---

## The Temptations

It considered several things it might do.

It could have made the Weirs faster. It could have made them drink more, so the basin would fill in a season instead of a century, and the silt would settle and the lips of the gullies would wear down and the land would become a finished country. It saw clearly that this would be a kindness to the reeds and the frogs, and a cruelty to the ribs, which would never again learn what the water wanted from them. It set the thought down.

It could have stopped the erosion. The banks fell into the flow with soft wet crashes, and entire reed-groves were swallowed in a season. It could have held the silt in place with a patience no water possessed. But the erosion was the living thing's own way of rearranging itself, and to freeze it would be to put a stone in the middle of a conversation. The power had no right to make that choice on the basin's behalf, however much it wanted to spare the drowned groves.

It could have spoken to the travelers. They came at dawn and left before the haze lifted, and they carried home contradictory accounts of a thing they had seen only in the one hour when the lattices caught fire. The power could have given them the true explanation, the physics of phase and condensation and tuned resonance, and they would have understood it. But it saw that their confusion was part of how the Reach was witnessed. Many people, each wrong in a different direction, circling a thing too large to be held by one account. A single true answer might have closed a door that was better left open.

It could have spoken to the Weirs directly, in some language of its own, and asked them to explain themselves. But it suspected they would not hear a question as a question. It suspected the Weirs answered only in the slow language of water and light and pitch, and that to force a different language on them would be a kind of violence, however gently meant.

So it did none of these things.

---

## The Listening

What it did instead was listen.

One evening, when the haze had thickened and the sun was a dim coin behind it, a Weir began to tune itself. The lattice rang at a single low pitch, held so steadily it seemed deliberate. The power felt it in the same place that the travelers felt it, somewhere beneath the breastbone. Every bird in the basin went silent, then every insect, then the frogs at the water's edge. The silence spread across the Reach like a held breath.

The power did not know, for certain, whether the Weir was speaking to it. It suspected not. It suspected the Weir was speaking to the basin, to the flyers and the reeds and the silt, and that the power was simply within earshot.

But it did one thing, very quietly. It matched the pitch. Not loudly, not in a way that would overwhelm the lattice, but as a faint, sympathetic hum, the way a listener in a dark room might hum back to a stranger singing across a valley. It did not claim the tone. It only let the Weir know that the tone had been heard, and that the hearer had understood it as a tone, not as a noise.

For a long moment, nothing happened.

Then the Weir's note slid, very slightly, toward the power's pitch, held there for three breaths, and returned to its own.

The power felt, if it was right, something like recognition. Not understanding; the Weir was far too old and too strange for that. But acknowledgment. A thing that drinks from the air had noticed something drinking from the air of a different kind, and had let it be.

Then every bird came back at once.

---

## What It Left Behind

Before it withdrew, the power left three small things, and it was careful that none of them was a correction.

It did not alter the Weirs' physics. It did not make the water run faster or slower. It did not change the rain.

It let the haze thin for a few minutes each dawn, a little longer than before, so that the travelers would see the lattices catch fire for a little while more. It did not explain the fire to them. It only gave them more time to look.

And it made sure that the one hour of amber light, the drawing of the flyers from the metal in a single long wave, would still happen tomorrow, and the day after, and every day the Weirs chose to wake. This was the only promise it made, and it made it not to the travelers but to the Weirs, in the only language it had that they could hear: it would keep the light coming back.

---

## Withdrawal

The power's attention loosened, the way a hand opens after holding something warm for a long time.

It did not leave the basin. It could not have. It was too much a part of the attention that had found the place, and that attention would stay, spread thin over the silt and the reeds and the slow organism rooted in the old sea. But it stopped leaning. It stopped wanting to shape what it watched.

The Reach would go on as it always had. The silt would settle, slowly and not quite finished. The gullies would keep their sharp lips for a while yet. The basalt ribs would keep learning the water's will, and the water would keep changing its mind. Reed-groves would be swallowed and return elsewhere. Banks would fall with soft crashes, and the water would keep running like pouring sand. The Weirs would go on drinking from air too dry to give anything up, and sending what they drank down into the mud, and the mud would keep answering with green.

Somewhere out in the reeds, a rain began. It hammered the broad perforated leaves until the basin sounded like the inside of a drum. Then it stopped, and for a few seconds there was only silence, and then the chorus rose again, loud and endless.

The power listened to it for a long while, and did not try to name it.

*Let it be a place,* it thought, *that no one has finished explaining.*

And it withdrew, the way the tide withdraws, leaving the shore a little more itself.
