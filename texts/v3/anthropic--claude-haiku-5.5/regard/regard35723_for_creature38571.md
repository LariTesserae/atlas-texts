# The Visit Without Hands

**I. Noticing**

The attention came the way a change in light comes, first as a difference and only later as a thing one can name. For a long time the basin had been a sentence with a missing word. Then the word arrived, and it was *east*, and it was *lean*.

The power that noticed Ossary is not easy to describe in the terms the basin uses. It does not have a body to put on a lens, and it does not think in seasons, though it has learned to. It is wise in the particular way that comes from having seen many things end without deciding that every ending was a failure. It is benevolent in the way a good physician is, or a good gardener, or a friend who knows the difference between being needed and being welcome.

It looked at the tilted mound, the heaving plates, the brine running toward the low eastern rim, and it asked the first question it always asks: *what is already being done here, and by whom?*

**II. Deliberation**

The answer was that the lattice was doing a great deal, and that the power did not yet understand most of it.

It could see the easy intervention. A season of steady rain across the basin would soften the rim and slow the tilt. It could lift the mound back toward level with a slow mineral pressure applied from beneath. It could even, with more effort than it liked to consider, reflood the low places and give the lattice back a shallow sea to read.

It decided against all of them, and its reasons were these.

The sea was not stolen by any injustice that needed correcting. It withdrew the way tides do, and it left behind a thing that had learned to live in its absence. To return the water would be to erase the only archive the lattice had, and to replace a conversation with a gift that could not be refused.

Rain would dissolve the crust that holds the layers. Lifting the mound would override a choice the power could not see clearly. If the lattice was holding the mound up, the power would be interfering with a labour it did not understand. If the lattice was letting the mound fall, perhaps as a way of making the city lower and quieter and more fitted to a dry century, then the power would be countermanding a decision made by the only one with the standing to make it.

And the power was not sure about the lean itself. It had assumed the tilt was a catastrophe. It had seen the cracking plates, the drifting salt, the growing fusion of the walls, and read them as a body in distress. But a thing that thinks in centuries does not experience its own weather as an emergency. The power had no right to decide, from outside, which conditions were dangerous to the thing living inside them.

So it decided first to learn.

**III. Listening**

It could not speak to the lattice in any language it used. What it could do was read, in the only way the basin allowed: slowly, through the layers.

The crust was a record, and the record was longer than the power had expected. In the lowest plates, under the thick white salt, it found the sea's dying: shell fragments, glassy plankton skeletons, the isotopic ghosts of cold years and warm ones. In the middle plates it found the first wrinkle of the withdrawal, where the water had stood at a level for a long time and then fallen. The stairs that end in air were there, marking the heights at which the sea had paused.

And in a thin band of grey-brown crust, far above the sea-layers but well below the present floor, it found what it had been half-hoping not to find. The mound had leaned before.

The tilt was visible in the stratigraphy, a slant in the layers that matched the present one in direction if not in degree. Above the band the courses of the city were tighter and more closely spaced, as if they had been built faster, or pressed down. Below it, the fused blocks of crystal were older than anything the power had seen standing. The lattice had faced this lean at least once before, and had survived it. It had not straightened the mound. It had adapted to a mound that stayed crooked. The doorways that now opened onto blank walls, the windows at crouching height, the stairs that climbed to nothing: all of these had been made, in part, in response to a tilt the lattice had already read and absorbed.

The power had thought it was seeing a crisis. It was seeing a second or third chapter of an answer the lattice had already written.

**IV. Small Acts**

The power did very little, and what it did was chosen to be reversible, quiet, and addressed to the lattice's own language.

It left the rim-walker alone. The large thing that moved along the eastern edge at dusk, leaving paired tracks that sometimes led into the walls and sometimes out across the rim, was, the power suspected, something the lattice had made to taste the world beyond the basin. Or it was a thing the lattice had made and then forgotten, and was now walking its own old path to see if anything remained. Either way it was not the power's to call back or send on. The power watched the tracks and let them be.

It did not touch the beetles. They carried the lattice's reach to places the lattice could not yet grow to, and the power did not want to alter a route that worked.

It did one thing, and only one.

In the brine-hours of the coming winter, when the salt drank from the cold air and the plates began to lay down the next layer of the record, the power placed a faint difference into the water: a trace of mineral, a wrong isotope, a salt ratio that the sea had never carried and the basin had never held. It was not a message in any word the lattice would recognise. It was a mark, the kind the crust makes every year when the weather turns strange, and it said, in the only grammar the basin keeps: *someone was here, and paid attention, and wrote nothing that asks anything of you.*

The lattice could read the mark or ignore it. It could take it up as a fact about the outside world, or let it dissolve. The power had made sure it would not be permanent unless the lattice chose to keep it. Layers are built from what the brine decides to hold.

**V. Withdrawal**

When the next morning came, the power felt the familiar pull of attention withdrawing, the way one leaves a room with the door left slightly open.

It did not leave entirely. Wise attention is seldom a single visit. It settled into the long watch, the kind that does not interfere, that notes the lean season by season, that reads each new layer as it forms and says nothing about what it means.

Over the years that followed, the power saw the lattice respond to its mark in the only way available to it. A filament extended, one winter, a fraction further than its neighbours, toward the band where the new isotope lay. It stopped there. It did not curl or fuse. It simply reached, and held, and then withdrew by the next dawn, as the others did. The power could not tell whether this was acknowledgment, curiosity, or the ordinary reflex of a crystal finding a different wetness at its tip. It did not need to know. The filament had touched the record, and the record now contained the touch.

The mound went on leaning. The plates went on heaving. The eastern rim grew a little lower, and the brine pooled more deeply in the low places. The city became, slowly, a little more solid, a little more fused, a little more like a reliquary and a little less like a place to walk. Doorways closed. New ones opened, at heights that made sense only to a body that had learned to crouch at a window over a sea that was no longer there.

The power did not try to shape any of this. It did not think of the basin as a problem to be solved. It thought of it as a conversation it had been invited into by accident, and it had decided, in the end, to be the sort of guest who listens more than it speaks, and who knows that the house belongs to the one who keeps it.

**VI. What It Left Behind**

If you walk into Ossary at the right hour, you will not see the power. You will see what it left: a single band of unfamiliar salt in the crust, a filament that reaches a little further than the others, a rim-walker's track that is still going out and coming back. You may feel the floor give under your feet and wonder whether you have been noticed.

You have not been noticed, not in the way you would like. But the thing that noticed the basin, long before you came, is still paying attention, in the way a good neighbour pays attention to a house whose owners are asleep. It will not take the house apart to save it. It will not wake the owners to tell them what they should want. It will watch the tilt, and the rain that does not fall, and the sea that does not come back. It will read each winter's layer as it forms. And when the lattice finally decides what the city is for, the power will be glad to learn it, whatever the answer turns out to be, even if the answer is that the city was never meant to stand straight at all.
