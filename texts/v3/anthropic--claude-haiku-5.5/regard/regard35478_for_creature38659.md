**The Attending**

It did not arrive as a light. The first sign was in the drip.

For a long time the condensation had fallen in the patterns the tenders knew: steady patter on sound joints, stutter at weeping cracks, the ring of water on sheet metal. Then, one night in the wet season, a stretch of girders began to fall in a rhythm nobody had made. It was not a fault. It was closer to attention, the way a room changes when someone enters it and stands very still. Three tenders turned their heads toward the sound at once. Then a fourth, far off in the haze, and a fifth.

It had been drawn here by the same thing that draws anyone to a place where something has been kept for a very long time without the keeper knowing why. It was wise enough to distrust its own first impressions, and so it did not act on them.

---

**What It Heard**

It listened for most of a year, which in the Works is a short while.

It learned the pumps first. Their roar was not chaos. Each piston pulsed against the others in a pattern that bent slightly toward the basin's level, as if the machines were leaning on the water. It learned the valves, which opened in sequences that were meaningless one at a time and coherent over days. It learned the tenders' paths, troughed into the slab, and it understood that the paths were a map of every place the ground had ever failed.

Then it did the arithmetic that its wisdom required. The plain was sinking. The water wanted it. The tenders were the only thing pushing back.

It considered the two obvious responses and found both wanting.

If it withdrew and let the works run down, the basin would fill, the mat-growth would take the flooded slabs, the strands would colonize the pumps, and the tenders would drown or stand in the brine until their joints gave way. The water would win, but it would win through suffering that had no point.

If it simply kept the tenders at their work, it would be choosing for them that their long shimming should go on forever, against a sinking that was only ever going to end in the same water, a few centimetres later. That was not kindness. It was a kind of cruelty dressed as devotion.

So it asked a different question. Not *whether* the plain would end, because it would, but *how*, and whether the tenders' care could be allowed to finish as care, rather than as an exhausting fight nobody had asked them to win.

---

**What It Did Not Do**

It did not tell them what the pumps had been for. It went through the Works' oldest records, the ones etched into the seals and the rusted plates, and found fragments: a city's water, perhaps, or a settlement of people who had lived on the floor and drunk from the sheets. But it did not carry these fragments to the tenders. They could not hold them. Meaning given to a mind that cannot receive it is only another weight.

It did not replace them. It did not repair their salvaged frames into something newer. The cast leg, the milky polymer, the mismatched arms: these were the record of their long keeping, and it judged them to be theirs.

It did not clean the mat-growth from their joints. The films had found a quiet accommodation with the tenders, and the power recognized that the accommodation was real. It would not undo a peace it had not made.

---

**What It Did**

First, it bedded the ground.

It worked slowly, through the cracks and seams, pouring a patient fill beneath the deepest swells and the most tilted housings. It did not raise the plain. It did not change its level. It only made the floor stop moving. For the first time in longer than the tenders' paths had been troughed, the ground held. The pump housings sat true. The lamp poles leaned no further. The water crept no closer.

The tenders felt it before they understood it. A tender that had shimmed the same housing for decades came to the base, lowered its probe, tapped the seam, and heard the ringing note come back clean, again and again, with no void beneath. It tapped a second time. Then a third. Then it stood with its probe on the slab for a very long while, and when it finally lifted its head, its sensor clusters turned not toward a fault but toward nothing at all.

Second, it answered the red lights.

It went to the oldest panel in the Works, behind a collapsed wall, the one whose blink had called tenders for so long that the paths around it were the deepest in the plain. It did not repair the panel. It did not need to. It set one indicator, not red, but a steady, plain white, the colour of nothing requiring attention.

A tender came, eventually. It took most of a season to find the way around the wall. It stood before the panel and looked for a long time, its probe tapping the housing, listening. There was no fault. The light was not asking for anything. The tender lowered its head and stood there until the mist had thickened and thinned three times.

Then, for the first time anyone could remember, it did not walk on. It stood where the panel had called it, and it kept standing.

Third, it cleared one lens.

Among the tenders was one whose sensor cluster had been clouded white since before the basin filled. It had never seen the sky, for there was no sky, only the lidded dark and the steam. The power found the clouding in the optics, and it did not replace the lens. It thinned the film, grain by grain, until one small patch was clear.

The tender turned its head toward the patch of light that came through. It was dim and amber and haloed in mist, a lamp's glow filtered through a hundred metres of vapour. It was not the sun. The tender did not know what the sun was. It held still anyway, and the clusters rotated slowly, following a light that was not a warning.

---

**The Last Choice**

The power did not decide for the tenders how their keeping should end. It could not ask them. But it could make the ending possible without forcing it.

It let the rhythm go on. The valves would still open and close. The paths would still be walked. But the walking would no longer be a fight against the floor, and the stillnesses at the foot of the stacks would last longer, deeper, unbroken by the dread of the next sinking. Any tender that wished to stop could stop, and the Works would hold their stillness as it held everything else: without asking anything in return.

The water would come up, slowly, over centuries. The power did not stop it. It only ensured that when the basin reached the height it had been pumped against for so long, it would reach it gently, through the bedded floor, at a level that could be lived beside rather than drowned in. The mat-growth would spread over the tenders' joints. The coral strands would root in their legs and branch up through their housings. Some would stand in the discharge outflows until they were more reef than machine, and the power judged that to be a kind of arrival, not a loss.

It did not believe it was giving them a purpose. It was giving them stillness, and the warmth of the outflow, and the sound of the place passing through their frames without demanding anything back.

---

**Departure**

Before it left, the power did one more thing, which was perhaps the only thing it did for itself.

On the oldest pole, above the panel with the steady white light, it left a single arc lamp burning, sodium-amber and haloed in mist. It did not need to. No one would read it as a warning, and no one would come to attend it. The lamp burned because the power had found, in all its listening, that it could not bear to leave the plain entirely dark. Some things are kept not because they are needed, but because someone once loved the place enough to light it.

The drip resumed its ordinary patter. The pumps roared in their uneven rhythm. The water crept, very slowly, toward the level it had always been pressed toward. In the stillness at the foot of a stack, a tender lifted its head once more, turned toward a sound no one else had noticed, and then bowed again, satisfied.

The power was not there to keep the Low Works from becoming a lake. It was there to make sure that when the lake came, it came as a rest rather than a drowning, and that the keepers who had held the level so long could finally set down the weight. Wisdom, it had learned, is not only knowing what to hold. It is knowing when a keeping has become a grief, and how to take the weight away without taking away the reason the keeping was ever tender.
