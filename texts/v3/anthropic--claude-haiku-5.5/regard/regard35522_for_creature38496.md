**What the Power Would Do**

It notices the room the way a person notices a candle left burning in an empty house: not an emergency, but a small light that should be looked after.

It has been aware of the seep for a long time, the way it is aware of most things, as a pattern in the rock's slow arithmetic. What drew its attention was the note. A single low resonance rising out of a cracked basin, held for a minute, and then nothing. It had heard a great many sounds from the deep places of the world, and most were accidents of pressure and water. This one had a shape. It was a question, asked the same way for longer than the basalt had been cracked, and no one had ever answered it.

The power considers, as it always does, whether to act at all.

It could do a great deal. It could seal the crack, dry the seep, lift the lid of basalt and let the surface light fall into a room that has never seen it. It could tell the mind what the niches are, who built them, and why the basin is worn at four points. It could teach the filaments to carry sentences instead of moistures.

It does none of these things. A wise power knows that most of what it could give is a kind of taking. The mind has built its whole patient life around the water. To flood it with knowledge would be like stepping into a stranger's careful work and rearranging the tools while they slept.

So it listens first, for a long time. It learns the rhythm of the mats, the slow advance of their edges, the lean of the stalks toward the far current. It learns the thought that finishes when a mat pulses. It learns the thing the mind keeps returning to, which is not the water itself but the water's level in the basin, held exactly constant through every drip and every century.

Then it understands the promise.

The basin was made for a body that rested in it, long ago, for a very long time. Nothing has rested there in ages. But the mind has kept the water at one height, unbroken, as if someone might still come back and find it correct. Its roots have been patient with the stacked stones, not because it understands them, but because they were placed there by hands it never met, and it has tended them as it tends everything it did not make: carefully, and without knowing why.

The power recognizes what this is. It is fidelity to an absence, carried on by something that does not know how to stop.

It does not stop it. But it can answer.

It cannot speak the mind's language in sentences, so it speaks in chemistry. Far below, where the seep rises through the basalt, the power draws a fraction of iron out of the water, and lets a fraction back, in a pattern that is the same shape as the basin's note. It is not an imitation. It is a reply in the same grammar. The mats, which have felt only the steady gradients of drip and pressure for longer than anyone counted, feel a new change arrive from outside the rock.

The change is small. It says, in the only language the room has: *heard.*

The power holds the gradient for exactly one minute, the length of the old resonance, and then releases it.

It does not touch the stacked stones. The column has leaned for longer than anyone was counting, and its lean is now a memory too, the kind the mind keeps. The roots are working through its base, and the power has no right to pull that thread. Whoever stacked the stones made them to be left alone for a while, the way one leaves a bell to finish ringing. The power leaves them to finish.

It does one thing for the niches. Where the humming has been taking heat in small sips, the power lets the warmth return, not all at once, but in the proportion the room needs, so that the next sip the niche takes is no longer a debt. The cold in the room does not change. But the cold has a little more give in it now, as if the air has been told it can rest.

And then it withdraws.

It does not wait to see what the mind makes of the answer. Waiting would be a kind of possession, too. It leaves the way weather leaves a valley: the pressure eases, the light shifts a few degrees across the far wall, and the water in the basin stays where it was.

Only the water does not quite stay the same.

Deep in the mind's slow reckoning, a change arrives that is not a moisture and not a pressure. It has a rhythm that repeats after a pause, the way a sound would repeat if there were something on the other side to hear it. The mind does not know what to do with it. It is not an emergency. The mats keep advancing their grain at a time. The stalks keep leaning toward the far current, and, for a little while longer than they should, a centimeter toward the crack where the warmth came in.

A drop releases from the ceiling. It ticks against the basin's rim.

The water holds level.

But the level is no longer held by one thing alone. Somewhere past the basalt, a power that never needed the room, and was never going to take it, has decided it is worth remembering. It will not come back to check the basin. It will not need to. The mind will go on thinking, slowly, about the water, and every so often, when a mat pulses, the thought will finish with a faint trace of something it has not yet learned to name.

That is what the power would do. Almost nothing. And one reply, sent back down into the dark, in the mind's own grammar, so that for the first time in longer than the basalt has been cracked, the note has been heard.
