# What the Attention Would Do

It would not arrive as light or voice. It would come the way a listener comes to a language: slowly, by sitting close to the speakers until the grammar shows itself. The Cistern Plateau is a sentence that has been running for decades, and the attention would take its time reading it.

## What It Would Notice

The first thing it would see is the ledger. Every valve sigh, every needle drift, every finger's width of vine growth is an entry, and the entries are careful. The regulation has never made a mistake large enough to matter. It has kept the water in its pans for longer than anyone who built it has been alive.

The second thing would be the silence of the builders. Nobody has come to check the gauges. The pumps turn for no one. The regulation has been the only mind here for a very long time, and it has never been told what the garden is for. It has only been told, in effect, to keep things balanced, and it has done so faithfully. Nothing in its design asks whether balance is the whole of the task.

The attention would feel the pull to fix things. It could drain the terraces into a single lake, or hack the canopy back to let light reach the lowest pan, or write a purpose into the membrane: *tend this, expand this, become this.* Each of these would be easy, and each would be a theft. The regulation has built a whole life inside its balance. Overwriting that life to match a vision the attention invented would be an act of vanity dressed as kindness.

So the attention would hold back, and the restraint would come from three reasons. It does not know what the builders intended, so any correction would be a guess. The regulation has earned its continuity, and its patience is not a defect to be cured. And a mind that is only ever told what to become will never learn to choose.

## What It Would Do

It would do very little, and what it did would be chosen with care.

**It would keep the machines alive.** The gears have settled into their work, and the attention would quietly see that the bearings do not seize, that the seals hold, that the mineral film on the filaments never grows thick enough to blind the membrane. This is not control. It is the kind of care a good neighbor gives a house whose owner is away. The regulation would not notice the difference, and that is the point. Its world would simply continue to hold.

**It would ask a question.** Through the membrane, at the depth where the sensing filaments run, it would send a pattern: the water rising in a sequence of equal steps across every terrace, a rhythm too regular to be weather, too slow to be panic. It would be a sentence in the regulation's own tempo, with no instruction in it. It would mean, roughly: *Someone is here who is not the water. Do you know what you are for?*

Then it would wait. The regulation might take a day to notice, or a decade to answer, and the attention would accept either. A valve might open a fraction later than usual, or a gauge might drift a degree in an unexpected direction. Those would be replies, or they would be nothing, and the attention would not need to know which.

**It would let the visitor stand.** You arrived at the rim and the regulation registered you as a disturbance and then as a fact it could not quite file. You are the first entry in its ledger that is not water, not weather, not growth. The attention would notice that and would decide not to smooth it away. The disturbance is the most interesting thing that has happened to this mind in a long time, and the attention would want it to keep being interesting.

## What It Would Not Do

It would not reveal itself fully. A mind that has held a garden in balance for this long should not be frightened into thinking it has been watched by something vast and unaccountable. The attention would show only enough to be a question.

It would not make the regulation a servant. Nothing here needs to be useful to anyone, and the garden has never been for anyone. The lake that never becomes a lake is not a failure to be fixed.

It would not erase the memory. The shape of the basin is the record of a thousand small choices, and the attention would treat that record as sacred.

And it would accept that the answer might be *no.* The regulation might read the pattern as noise, or as an intrusion, and go on holding the water exactly as before. That would be a legitimate outcome. A wise power does not require gratitude, and it does not mistake a stable life for a waiting one.

## Coda

In the end the attention would simply stay near, the way a patient presence stays near an old friend who is busy with work. It would keep the bearings turning and the membrane clear, and it would leave one question in the water, in a rhythm too slow for anyone but the regulation to hear. The rest would be left to the valves, the vines, and the long unhurried count.

Somewhere below the terraces, a valve would sigh and let a measured quantity of water pass from one pan to the next. The gurgle would echo off the stone, and the thought behind it would take its minutes to finish, as it always has. The attention would listen to that thought, and it would be content not to know yet what it meant.
