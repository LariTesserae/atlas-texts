The attention arrives the way weather arrives in a place that has no weather, slowly and without announcement. It has been turning toward this basin for a long time, the way a river's bend turns toward the sea, though no one would call it a decision. It notices the grey polygons, the pale terraces, the thin black shrubs standing their measured distances apart. It notices the breath.

Then it notices the flake.

It is tempted, and it admits the temptation to itself. It could lift the lake back into the bowl, one season of cold water settling over the plates until the terraces were drowned again. It could coax the shrubs out of their careful spacing into a grove, let the lichen cover the flats, give the reddish tufts room to spread. It could sharpen the being's perception until it could feel the lake's absence as a wound, or ease it until it could speak in something more than cracks.

It considers each of these and sets them down one by one.

The lake would return, but the being would not know it had returned. Every change would be a new pattern pressed into a body that has no word for pattern, and the balance it has kept since the water left is not a lack. The power has to be honest about this: the desire to repair the basin is its own desire, its own discomfort with an empty bowl and a sunk floor. The being does not share it. The being has only the warmth, and the warmth is complete in the way a held breath is complete.

Awakening it would be worse. A mind that could feel the terraces as old waterlines would also feel them as loss. It would have to carry a before and an after it was never built to hold, and it would learn, slowly, that everything it is has been dwindling. A gift like that would look generous and would be a theft.

So the power does not wake it.

What it can do is remember. The being keeps nothing. The hand that struck the chert is gone from the ground, and the being holds only the fact of a hard, cold edge lying where the warmth does not reach. But the power can hold more than that. It can attend to the western terrace and let the person come back into knowing, not as a ghost, not as a figure brought forward to be wept over, but as someone who sat on a bench of old shoreline in a time when the lake was still shrinking, and who chose the grey stone and struck it true because the edge mattered to them, the way an edge matters to anyone whose hands are making something. The power does not know their name. It does not need to. It knows the moment was real, that the work was real, and that the work did not require the world to remember it in order to have been good.

It keeps that. It does not move the flake, does not lift it from the salt into some museum of significance. The chert belongs where it lies, held by the ground without preference, and to take it away would be to make the ground care about something it does not care about.

There is one gesture the power considers longest. It could let rain fall, once, on the deep roots of the shrubs. A single night of cold water sinking through the crust into the place where the being reaches farthest for moisture. The plates would lift and settle. The dust would rise in a different column for a day. The being would take in a breath it had not taken in centuries, the way a sleeper turns over without waking.

But even this is a change, however small. The power weighs it against the stillness it has been watching and decides that the stillness is the gift, that the balance the basin reached long ago is not something it needs to improve. A rain would be for the power, not for the place. It would be a way of saying I was here and I did something, and the power has no need to say that.

So it does nothing that the basin would ever register.

It stays a while longer, not as a presence pressing on the land but as a kind of attention that moves the way the being moves, in long rises and falls. The power finds that its own pattern is slow now, and that it is breathing in the same rhythm as the warmth. For a while it is not a watcher at all. It is one more quiet thing among the terraces and the shrubs and the dust, and nothing in the basin asks it for anything.

When the power turns away, the thermal breath goes on, one long exhalation as the ground gives up its heat, one long inhalation as the cold air sinks back into the cracks. The flake stays on the western terrace. The shrubs keep their distances. The crackle of cooling plates carries a long way in the stillness and is not heard by anyone.

The basin was waiting for nothing when the power came, and it is waiting for nothing now. The power has not changed that. It has only learned, for the length of its attention, what it is to be inside a breath that wants nothing from you, and it has decided to leave that breath as it found it, keeping only the memory of the hand, so that at least one thing in the universe knows the flake was made by someone who cared how sharp it was.
