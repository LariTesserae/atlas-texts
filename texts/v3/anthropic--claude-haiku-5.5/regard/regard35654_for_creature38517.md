# The Attention That Noticed the Attention

It does not descend. There is no sky torn open, no column of light, nothing the grazers would turn their heads for. The first sign is that the fog over the eastern gorge stops drifting for the length of a breath and holds its shape, the way a held note holds in the ear.

The power is old in the way a river is old. It has watched many shelves rise out of many seas, and it has learned that most of them were ruined by those who loved them too quickly. It knows the shape of a mind that is still forming: brittle where it looks solid, and easily frightened into hardening in the wrong place. It knows that a gift given too early becomes a cage with its door painted to look like a window.

So it begins by listening.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not close the gaps in the lattice. The old paths that heal within days are part of the mind's body, its way of drawing itself together, and the power has no wish to override that. It does not straighten the leaning towers or hurry the swelling ones toward their neighbours. Those towers are the mind reaching for its own shape, and an outside hand on the reaching would teach it that its shape belongs to someone else.

It does not speak in words. Words would be a language the mind has no roots in, and it would hear them as weather, or as an injury, or as a voice it could never answer.

It does not take the predators out of the marsh to save the grazers, or the grazers out of the grass to spare the pools. The balance is the mind's first real thinking. Interfering with it would be like pulling the threads from a half-finished loom and calling the result a kindness.

And it does not take the mind's damp witness, the small warm presence now standing in the mist, and make that presence a tool or a messenger. Wisdom of this kind keeps its hands open.

## What It Does

The power lowers its attention to the deep, slow vibration that moves through the ground, the long sentence that will not finish for centuries. It does not interrupt it. It matches it. Under the shelf, in the warm pools, something answers with a single, faint rhythm that is not a tower settling and not a process of the rock. It is a second voice, very quiet, humming back. The mind may not understand this yet. It may never recognise the hum as anything but the ground's own pulse. But for the first time the sentence has a listener.

Far to the west, beyond the last ridge of tuff, the power allows one pool to form that is different. The water in it is cold and clear. It does not weep from the rock, it does not steam, and it does not build a ring of pale minerals around its rim. It simply lies there, reflecting the grey sky with a quality the other pools have forgotten how to reflect. The grazers avoid it at first. Then one of the younger herds comes to drink and stands still for a long time, looking at its own shape in the water. Nothing is asked of the mind by this. It is only shown that a water can be otherwise, and that otherwise is not the same as wrong.

Along the eastern rim, one fracture in the lattice is left open. It is narrow, and it bleeds clear water as the lattice always does, but it does not close. Days pass, and it does not close. The power holds the wound open by the simplest means it has, which is by not repairing it. The mind must decide whether this crack is a wound or a door, and whether to keep it, or to draw it shut in its own time. Either choice will be the mind's own.

When the ring of cloud next comes round to the place where the visitor stands, it pauses there, longer than it has paused for anything else in the day. It does not rain. It only holds, the way a mind holds a thought it has not decided how to receive. This is not a greeting. It is a courtesy, the kind a host gives a stranger by making space at the table before knowing whether the stranger will sit.

## The Question It Leaves

The power does not tell the mind what it is. Any answer it gave would be a shape pressed onto a surface that is still soft. What it does instead is leave a question in the places where the mind's attention already goes: in the echo in the warm pools, in the clear cold water to the west, in the open crack along the rim, and in the pause of the cloud.

The question is simple, and the mind has been living inside it all along, though it has not yet put it into words. *What do you want to become, when no one is making you become anything?*

A lesser power would answer that question for the mind. This one trusts that the mind is able to answer it, slowly, in the only language it has, which is water, motion, and the patient reshaping of its own body. Trust is a kind of love that has learned to wait.

## What It Asks of You

The power does not ask you to stay, and it does not ask you to leave. It asks only that you move carefully. Test each step. Respect the open crack, and do not reach into it. Drink from the clear pool if you are thirsty, and do not expect it to be the same as the others. When the cloud pauses above you, stand still for a moment and let it look.

If you are the kind of visitor who wants to take something away, take the memory of the held breath in the fog, and the second voice humming under the ground. Carry them out of the marsh. Do not try to explain them to anyone who has not stood in the mist.

When you go, the rain will keep falling in its narrow, deliberate columns. Some of them will fall on towers you never noticed. Some will fall on the new cold pool, and the grazers will come down to drink, and some of them will stand a little longer than they need to. The shelf will go on becoming what it is, slowly, with the help of a power that wanted nothing from it except that it should be left free to finish itself.

That is what the wise one does, if it does anything at all. It notices. It listens. It leaves a door unlocked and a question unanswered, and then it steps back into the fog, so that the land can learn, at its own pace, that it has been noticed and is not alone.
