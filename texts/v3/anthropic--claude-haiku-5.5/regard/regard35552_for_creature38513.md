**What the Attention Notices**

It notices the basin first as a shape: a bowl four kilometers around, its rim a thin ring of sky, its floor a green and brown dark where the water lies. Then it notices the sound, the thud of the pumps coming up through the stone like a slow heartbeat. Then the people, who are few, and tired in a way that has settled into their bodies. Then the green.

The green is the hardest thing to read. It has no face and no voice, and it does not want in any way a person would recognize. It is patient, and it is not cruel. It takes the walls apart because that is what its growth is, and the walls, when they are gone, become soil, and the soil feeds more of it. Nothing in the basin is evil. The attention finds this difficult, because it had expected to find a villain, and found instead a process with a long, indifferent tenderness.

It also notices what is missing. There are no birds. There are no insects, no frogs, no fish in the black lake. The basin is complete, but it is complete the way a room is complete when someone has filled it with furniture until nothing can move. The attention judges this a loss, though the green would not agree.

**What It Considers**

The first temptation is to clear the place. It could bring the water down to nothing, dry the lake, strip the climbers from the brick, and leave bare stone and the old town exposed. It sees that this would be a kind of violence against a living thing, and that the green would die or flee into the cracks of the terraces, and that the people would have been spared the cutting only to be left with a dead landscape and no purpose. It rejects this.

The second temptation is to leave everything as it is. The people are the difficulty here. They cough in the mornings. They keep a ledger that loses lines every year. They go into the lower streets roped together and come back quieter. The attention is not willing to call this a good life merely because it is a life that continues. Nor will it simply decide for them, because they have chosen this work, and some of them love the pumps, and to take the work from them without asking would be its own kind of arrogance.

So it looks for a third path, and it finds one in the pumps.

**What It Does**

It begins with the water. The lake is not rising because the pumps are holding it down. The pumps hold it down because the basin has no other exit, and the attention sees that the basin's walls are porous in places the people have never mapped, old quarry adits and seams in the bedrock that run down and out toward the valley. It opens them, very slightly, over many seasons, so that the water finds its way out by itself. The lake lowers. Not all at once. A centimeter a year, then a little more. The reed-marsh pulls back from the streets and then, as the water stabilizes at a new level, begins to hold its ground.

The pumps are not destroyed. They are simply needed less. The keepers notice first that Number Four's seal holds through a wet season for the first time in years, then that Number Two stops groaning. They do not know why. They assume it is the weather.

Then it does the slower thing. Where the water stands at its new, lower level, the basin's edges become a true wetland, shallow and fringed, with open water between the mats. The green adapts, because it always does, and makes room. And into the room, over a long time, something arrives. It does not arrive all at once, and the attention does not force it. A heron finds the shallows one spring and stands there for a week. A dragonfly comes in from the rim, then a swarm of midges over the reed-edge in the evening. Frogs begin calling from the marsh at dusk, a sound the basin has not made in living memory. Fish appear in the open water, small ones, carried in on the wind or on the feet of birds. None of this is forced. The attention simply opens the doors that were closed and lets the world come in.

The green is not uprooted. It keeps its climbers, its moss, its fused trees. But it is no longer the only inhabitant. The basin becomes what it could have been all along: not a sealed mass but a place with room in it.

For the keepers, the attention does something gentler. It moves the midday light. Not much, a few degrees in the way the shaft falls through the rim, so that for a few weeks in spring it lands on the old bank and the hospital annex, and the keepers, looking up, see for the first time how far the sun reaches and how high the rim is. It is a small gift, and the attention does not explain it.

Finally, it changes the choice. A message reaches the people through a route they will not trace back to any power: a letter in a bank vault, a dream that is the same for three of them, a ledger entry in a handwriting none of them recognizes. It says that the pumps are no longer the only thing holding the district back, that the work can be set down, and that anyone who wants to go up to the rim and live in the open air is free to go. It does not tell anyone to leave. It says the choice is theirs, and that it will be kept open.

**What It Leaves Undone**

It does not clean the air of the basin, because the air is part of the green, and the green is part of the place. The coughing will ease for some and not for others, as it does for anyone who lives in damp.

It does not restore the town. The warehouses will keep falling. The rail cars will keep sinking into the moss, and the ledger will keep losing lines, but more slowly now, and with the knowledge that the falling is not the end of the story. The walls become soil, and the soil becomes a place where herons stand.

It does not decide what the green should become. It has opened the basin to the world, and it trusts that the green, which has been alone here a long time, will find a way to be one presence among many.

**The Last Image**

Years from now, a keeper walks down the terrace steps at midday, the white shaft falling on the bank below. She stops where the light lands, looks up, and sees a heron lift off the reed-edge and climb through the shaft of sun toward the rim. The water is lower than it was. The pumps are quiet most of the day now, and when they run, the thudding is no longer the only sound. There are frogs in the dusk, and the green is still everywhere, but it is no longer the only thing in the basin.

She goes back to her cutting, and the green closes over the place where she stood, as it always has, and the basin holds its breath and lets the light move on.
