**What the Attention Did**

It came the way warmth comes into a room, not all at once but as a change in what the room is like. The hall did not wake, because it had never slept. The power simply began to attend to it, the way a good steward walks through a house it did not build and notices which doors are sound, which hinges are dry, and what has been kept well.

It noticed the wheel first. It turned without complaint, its bucket chain lifting water and spilling it back into the reservoir, so the level never drifted. It noticed the sluice plate, worn smooth in one place by a hand that had stood there many times. It noticed the notches in the tiles, which were not decoration but instructions: this piece goes here, after that one. Whoever had built the hall had thought in sequences, and the sequences were still unfinished.

Then it noticed the gold on the western wall.

The power did not reach for it at once. It had seen many things grow, and most of them had wanted to be left alone or wanted something it could not give. So it watched the mat through a full day and a full night. It watched the pulse travel outward and die at the edges. It watched the threads lift and settle, and the way they pulled back into the seams when the column of sun moved on, closing around the older core like a hand. It noticed the diary written in the sheet's thickness, a record of every noon since the oculus was cut.

It asked itself the questions a wise thing asks before acting.

*Is anything wrong?* No. The water was clean, the level was steady, the dust was held. The stalks in the seams were upright. The mat was healthy, and its growth matched the light it received. Nothing here needed fixing.

*Could I make it better?* Perhaps. The power could have widened the oculus so more light fell on the western wall, or narrowed it to keep the mat's edges from thinning. It could have cleared the drifts in the corners, or mended the cord-marks, or dressed the rough blocks so the hall looked finished. But the hall was not supposed to be finished by strangers. It was supposed to be finished by its makers, and their absence was the open question the whole place was built around. To finish it on their behalf would be to answer for them.

*Is the mat mine to decide about?* This was the harder question, and the power took longest over it. The mat had learned the hall's one rule and made a life of it. It listened to the machinery. It held the dust. It followed the warmth. Whatever it was, it was not a tool the builders had installed, and it was not a pest. It was a keeper, in its own way, of the hall's purpose. To lift it out, or to name it and bind it to a role, would be to treat it as something other than what it had become.

So the power did very little.

It did not touch the wheel, the sluice, or the water. It did not lift a single block. It let the inflow keep roaring and the outflow keep draining, and it left the level where the builders had set it.

It did one small thing. In the dust that had settled on a ledge beside the stack of spare tiles, it pressed a single notch, the same shape as the maker's marks scored into every floor tile. It was a mark that said nothing about who had been here. It said only that someone had come back and seen the work, and had not rushed to change it. If the builders returned, they would find the notch and know that the hall had been watched over in their absence, and that the watcher had chosen restraint.

And it did one more thing, which it had not planned until it happened. When the mat pulsed, the power felt the pulse. It felt the swell travel outward, the threads lift, the sheet brighten and fall. It answered the pulse the only way it could, with a slight increase in the warmth of the light that fell on the western wall, a few minutes longer on the sheet's leading edge each day, enough to let it extend without thinning. Not a command. An acknowledgment, the way one hand touches another in passing.

The mat did not understand this, not in any way the power could name. But over the following days its leading edge crept a little farther along the arc of the vault, tracing the template's curve, and the older core grew thicker and deeper gold. The stalks in the seams held their heads higher in the current. The dust stayed in its soft drifts and did not wash down the outflow.

The power remained, attending. It would not stay forever, and it did not need to. It had come to see the hall and had seen it. What it left behind was not a repair or a gift of power but a kind of witness, and a standing agreement with the thing that kept the place: *I see what you have made of the rule. I will not take it from you. When the sun moves, I will let you follow it.*

Then it withdrew, and the hall went on as before. The inflow roared, the wheel turned, the sluice waited, and the mat slept in the mortar until morning, its threads drawn into the seams, holding the notch-marked dust in place against the day when someone would come back to finish the building.
