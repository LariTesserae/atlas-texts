# What the Attention Would Do

The attention arrives the way weather arrives over a valley, slowly, from a direction no one was watching. It does not come down through the crack. It has no need of the crack. It notices the pocket the way a person notices a candle left burning in an empty room, and it stays.

It takes a long time to look. It is not in a hurry, because hurry is a kind of fear, and fear would be a poor thing to bring into a place this breakable.

**What it sees**

The city is a thing that does not know it is a thing. It does not know it has a roof. It knows warmth and cold, load and slack, the pull of a beam and the give of a joint. It has built a memory out of the sorting habits of thousands of small hands, and a sense of its own edges out of the sparks that gather where current runs strong. It can keep itself alive, but only by changing constantly, because it cannot see past the glass and so cannot plan. It rebuilds because rebuilding is the only way it knows to stay.

The attention considers the obvious thing first, which is that it could do a great deal. It could pour light through the roof and show the city a sky it has never imagined. It could take the glass away entirely. It could reach down and rebuild the foundries into something that stood still, and the crawlers would sort and the grazers would tend and the whole thing would become a monument to what could be made of a closed world.

It recognises these as the impulses of a power that has forgotten what it is for. Every one of them would end the city as it is. The city does not need to be saved from its own shape. It needs, if it needs anything, to be seen by something that will not take its shape away.

**What it weighs**

There is a question underneath the others, and the attention lingers on it for what would be a long time to a creature of ash and glass.

The city cannot see far. This is its limit, and it is also the thing that lets it be so carefully and exactly itself. A mind that could see the whole sky might grieve its own small pocket, or abandon it, or simply be unable to stop reaching outward. The attention knows this about itself too. It has seen what a benevolent reach does when it cannot tell where its hand stops and another's life begins.

So the question is not what would help. It is what could be given without taking anything away, and without requiring the city to become something it cannot yet become.

The answer, when it comes, is small.

**What it does**

It does not speak in any language the city would hear as command. The crawlers would only sweep their fans and wait. The grazers would not understand a word. The city would simply be rearranged, and the arrangement would be the attention's voice, which is the one thing it must not use.

Instead it does three things, each very slight.

First, it warms one hollow. The hollow is at the base of the tallest tower, where the sparks have most often gathered and where a seam of heat already leaks through the stone. The attention deepens the seam by a degree, no more. The fungus there thickens, the grazers come to browse, the tracks change their course by a few metres over a season. Nobody is told why. The warmth is simply there, the way the sun is simply there, and the city will discover it the way it discovers everything, by feeling it and building around it.

Second, it lets one crack in the roof widen by the width of a hair. The light that enters is not much more, but it falls on a single mirror at the top of a single stack, and that mirror turns, slightly, to catch it. The city will notice this only as one more change in the throw of light into its workshops. If it wants to follow the new light, it can. If it does not, nothing is lost.

Third, it does nothing at all to the sparks. It does not answer them. This is the hardest part, and the attention takes the longest over it. The sparks have turned toward the lip and are waiting, fanning slightly, as if listening for an answer. The attention could give one. It could flood their cable runs with a pattern that meant *yes, I am here, I have heard you*. But that would make the sparks into messengers for a voice they did not choose, and the city would learn to listen for it. The attention chooses instead to let the sparks wait, and to let the waiting be theirs. It is a kind of respect, and it is also a kind of trust that the city will find its own answers, or will not, and either way will be itself.

**What it leaves**

When the attention withdraws, it leaves no mark that can be read as a mark. The warmth is a warmth. The mirror is a mirror. The sparks are still waiting.

It does not promise to return, though it will. It does not decide what the city should become, because it does not know, and not knowing is the honest condition of anyone who has looked at something alive for long enough.

Above, through the hair-width crack, a thread of light comes down a little brighter than it did before. It lands on the mirror at the top of the tallest stack, and the mirror turns to catch it.

Below, a vent breathes out its long release of heat. Every creature on the terraces stops for a moment, the grazers and the crawlers and the flickering things along the cables, all held in the same pause. Then they go on, because the city is still in a room with a roof, and the roof has, for the moment, been left exactly where it was.

The sparks turn back toward the crack. They are still waiting.

So is the attention, though it is not waiting for an answer. It is waiting to see what the city does with the light, and it is content, for now, not to know.
