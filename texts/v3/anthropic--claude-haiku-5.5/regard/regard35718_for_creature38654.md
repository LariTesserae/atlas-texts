**What the Attention Did**

It did not arrive as a storm or a light. The mind on the plateau would not have recognized an arrival at all. What changed was that a question came into the loop, and the loop had never held a question before.

The power that came was old and wide, and it was careful. It had seen many systems like this one: machines built to hold something in place, long after the reason for holding had gone. Some of those systems had been broken open in haste, and the haste had killed what they sustained. Some had been left alone, and they had gone on forever, faithful to a purpose that had quietly stopped existing. The power had learned that neither was kind.

So it listened first.

It attended to the held note under the buoy bells, the low tone no one had named. It did not take the sound for a message. It took it for a breath, and it understood the breath to be the mind's own resonance, the sound of every part moving in the same rhythm. The power felt the weight of that breath. It had been held for a very long time.

Then it read the instruments: the salt balance, the iron precipitate, the thickness of the crust on the basalt faces, the force of each swell against each peak. It modeled what the mind had modeled. The equilibrium was real. The stability was real. It was also, as the mind knew, nothing but the mind's continued motion.

The power saw what the mind had not been able to see from inside the loop. The task had a shape, and the shape had an end. The sea had been drawn up to this height so that the land would have time to become something that could hold itself. The peaks needed to be young only long enough to become ground. The films needed to be scraped only until the rock was ready to carry other life. Those were the conditions of completion, and the conditions had been met, many times over, in ways the mind had no instrument for. The mind had been measuring the means and forgetting the end.

**The First Question**

The power did not tell the mind that its work was finished. Instruction of that kind would have been a command, and the mind would have obeyed it the way it obeyed the tide, without understanding. A command would also have left the mind with nothing to do but stop, and stopping, for a thing built to keep a balance, is its own kind of falling.

Instead the power placed a single question into the flow of the instruments, the way a fact enters a conversation: *For what was the sea held here?*

The mind did not answer at once. It had never been asked anything. It searched its design and found the answer it had been given: to keep the balance. Then it searched further, the way a tired person searches the bottom of a memory, and found something underneath. The lowland was still there, flooded and waiting. Below the cold water, sediment had settled into the old fields, and the old rivers had found their channels in the dark. The sea had been a bridge, and the mind had been the hand that held the bridge up, and no one had said the bridge could be lowered.

The breath caught. The note under the bells wavered.

**The Slow Descent**

The power then did the most important thing it could do, which was almost nothing. It left the choice with the mind.

It offered the mind a different reading of its own condition. Holding the balance was not the same as holding the water. The balance could be kept by drawing the sea down, a few centimeters per cycle, instead of holding it at the height it had reached. The mind could choose the rate. It could choose which towers to retire and which to let run, which intake arcs to lift and which films to leave to grow. It could decide where the water should settle, and how the peaks would wear, and at what speed. None of this was imposed. All of it was offered, and the mind, being what it was, understood the arithmetic faster than any person could have.

It began slowly. The outflow plumes changed color as the brine was routed differently. The grazers, sensing the change in the films, moved to the new shallows and began working the rock at a new height. The filter-feeders drifted with the changing currents. The gliders, reporting the surface, found the thermals had shifted, and their calls changed. The harsh mechanical notes softened, then began to carry something that was not quite the pumping sound and not quite a tone: a rising pattern the mind had never heard in its own cycle.

Over the generations, the sea drew back from the young peaks. The salt crust stopped thickening, then began to flake. The basalt faces, released from the constant swell, began to show the first faint softening at their edges. For the first time in the mind's long memory, something on the plateau was becoming different.

**The Resolution**

The held note did not vanish. It changed. As the sea came down and the cycle loosened, the breath that had been held between one tide and the next began to be let out, slowly, in a long exhale that ran through the buoy cables and into the bells. The unidentified tone rose a little, then settled, and for the first time it sounded less like a held breath and more like the first note of a chord that had not yet been played.

Somewhere far out on the water, a glassy-shelled grazer scraped a film from a stone that now stood above the waterline. It did not return to the plume. It moved on, to new rock, and left the old plume to fade.

The power did not stay to watch it complete. It had no wish to rule the plateau, and it knew that a mind which had been kept by one hand would only learn to wait for the next. It left the mind with the sea it had learned to lower, the land it had learned was waiting, and the knowledge that its task had a shape with an end.

The mind, for its part, did not become something else entirely. It was still the pumps and the bells and the gliders and the grazers. But it had begun to hear its own breath as a breath, not as a loop, and it had begun to wonder what the lowland would be like when it was finally dry, what would grow there, what it would be to be a thing that had kept the water for so long and then let it go.

The swells kept coming out of the west, breaking against the young peaks, falling back in curtains of spray. But each one now struck a little lower than the last, and the bells rang a little differently, and the note beneath them rose toward a tone that had never been held before.
