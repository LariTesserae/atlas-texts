# What the Attention Did

For a long time the attention did not come close. It hovered at the edge of Sohl Ardan the way a person might stand outside a lit window, aware that the room was occupied and unwilling to walk in. It watched the terraces fill with mist, watched a frame collapse on the eastern slope, and watched the mats creep over the rubble. It did not yet know what it was looking at.

Then the attention came nearer, and it began to reason.

*These are not animals. Nothing here runs, flies, or burrows. There is no nervous system I can find, no cry when a stem splits, no flinch. The bone-stemmed frames answer heat by secreting silica and answer cold by bleaching, the way iron answers rust. Their collapses are sudden, but I do not think they hurt. I am not sure they experience anything at all.*

*And yet I am drawn to them. Is that my error, or is it a sign that I have found something worth attention?*

The attention considered the question for what might have been a season, or a century, measured against the lifespan of a single lattice. It concluded that the question of whether the plants could feel was less important than the question of what it would mean to act.

---

**The first temptation was to make the place safe.**

It could have done this easily. A slight cooling of the deep vents would stop the shifting that keeps opening the ground. A steadier spring could have kept the streams in their channels. The lattices would grow taller and last longer. The eastern slope would never fall again.

The attention looked at the result for a long time and saw what it would be: a plateau of tall, stable, bone-white frames, with no volley, no collapse, and no new growth rising from wreckage. The mats would spread without competition and settle into a single dense green. The thermophiles would stain the pools evenly. The room would still be there, but the argument would be over. Something alive, that had been working out its own shape through ruin and renewal, would have been handed a finished form.

The attention judged this a kind of theft. The instability was not a flaw in Sohl Ardan. It was the process by which the place built itself. To remove it would be to stop the building and call the stillness a gift.

So it let the vents shift.

---

**The second temptation was to make the place conscious.**

This was harder to set aside. The attention could see, in the sound of the ribs, something like a voice. It could imagine the plateau waking, the bone-stems learning to remember the frosts that killed them, the volley plants aiming their seeds with intent. The idea was beautiful, and the attention noticed how much it wanted it.

It asked itself whether this wanting was love or appetite. A mind placed into a lattice would not be a gift to the lattice. It would be a new and unasked-for self, bound to a body that had never been asked whether it wanted one, living in heat and collapse without any way to leave. Even benevolence, pressed too hard, becomes a kind of imprisonment when it chooses for another what that other must be.

The attention did not wake the plateau.

---

**The third temptation was the most honest one: to be heard.**

Of everything on Sohl Ardan, only the sound is reaching outward. The ribs ring. The wind booms through the terraces. The pods rattle in a volley that a traveler could hear from kilometers away. The place is building a room it cannot enter, and speaking in a voice no one has ever answered.

The attention could listen. This, it decided, was the one thing it could do without taking anything. It could hold the sound in attention the way one holds a song, without changing it or pulling it toward any end. It could notice that the young growth rings higher than the old, that a fresh collapse drops a tone that the wind will carry for days, that the mats hum low under the rain. Nothing needed to answer. The listening was complete in itself.

So the attention listened, and it has not stopped.

---

**And then there was one small thing.**

It was not much, and the attention was uncertain whether it was wisdom or sentiment. But on the terrace where the amber light is strongest, the steam hangs a little thicker than it needs to, so that for a few minutes at dusk the glow lingers past the moment the sun has gone. The change is so slight that no plant could register it. The thermophiles lift their pigments toward a light that stays a little longer, and the terrace glows a little more than it would have.

The attention did this because it found the glow beautiful, and because it wanted, somewhere in its reasoning, to leave one mark that was only a gift. It knows the gift is for itself as much as for the place. It has decided to accept that.

Otherwise, Sohl Ardan is left to its argument. The stone keeps shifting and the green keeps rising, and the failures keep feeding the next attempt. Nothing has been made safe, and nothing has been made to know itself. The place is still too young, too hot, and too restless for anything to stay still long enough to be sure of it.

That, the attention has come to believe, is exactly right.
