**What the Attending Did**

It noticed the Planing the way one notices a sound that has been going on for a long time, all at once and without having decided to listen.

It noticed the domes first, because they were the oldest. Their roundness had been worn into them over more ages than it could count, and it understood that they were not ruins. They were what was left when a mountain had finished being a mountain. It did not want to lift them, or polish them, or return the mountains. A mountain that rose again would be a different thing, and the plain would have to forget itself to make room.

Then it noticed the knots, which were the people of the place, though they would not have accepted the word. It saw how they turned in the milky water, loosening and tightening, always reforming around the same centers. It saw that they had no leader, and that what looked like a single mind was a crowd that had learned to move as one for a moment and then to let go. The Attending found this beautiful. It also found it heavy. A crowd that never disperses is a crowd that never rests.

It noticed the stone arches, the stairs, the doorframe standing alone in the sheet. It noticed the grooves on the lintel that ran in no direction, and the knots lying across them, trying to hold an angle that no one had given them the words for. It understood that the builders had been drowned or dissolved or carried off by the churn, and that a part of them had stayed behind in the water, still reaching for the edges they had made. This was the hardest thing to see. The Attending could have gathered those lost angles together and given them back their shape. It could have read the grooves aloud. It decided against both.

*To hold a thing forever is not to honor it,* the Attending reasoned. *It is to refuse that it has ended. These knots are already doing what can be done. They gather around the outline as moss gathers in the cracks, and they let it go when the water asks. If I hold the outline for them, I take from them the only work they can still do. And I take from the builders the one peace they might have: to have been, and to be let go.*

So it did not hold the lintel. It did something smaller. It looked at the groove for the length of one storm cycle, with the whole of its attention, the way one might look at a face one loved and was going to leave. The knots felt it. They slackened around the carving, and for a while no knot held it at all. Then, when the cycle turned and the attention was withdrawn, the knots came back to the groove, but more lightly, as if they had been told that the shape was allowed to be empty.

**The Silence**

There was one thing the Attending wanted to give, and it was not a thing the Planing had ever had.

The roar overhead was the breath of the whole place. It had never stopped, not once, not in any age of the sheet. The knots had been born inside it, had learned to hold shape inside it, and had never known there was another way for the air to be.

The Attending gathered the storm's pressure into itself, carefully, the way one lifts a sleeping child from a bed. It did not break the storm. It only asked it to rest, for the span of a single breath.

And for one breath the sky was still.

The sheet went flat as glass. The amber smudge of the sun lay on the water without a ripple. The fine vapor stopped drifting and hung motionless in the air. The domes' weeping ceased, and every thread of water held its place on the black stone like a line of light. The hiss died. The clicks died. The Planing, for the first time in its unmeasured life, was quiet enough to hear its own presence.

The knots did not know what had happened. They were used to motion as their only shape. In the stillness they lay open to the sky, and for a moment they could see themselves, the way one sees one's own face in a dark window. They did not gather into a fist. They did not turn. They stretched outward, in a thin, slow, widening ring across the whole of the sheet, and the ring held. It did not move with the storm or with any current. It only held its shape, and in the silence the shape was clear enough to be almost a question.

Then the Attending let the storm rise again.

The roar came back all at once, and the water shivered, and the ring broke into a thousand pieces that spun away into the churn. But the Attending had felt the ring, and it knew that something had been learned. The knots had been shown a way to hold a form that was not an edge of anything, that did not need to be remembered, that was only a moment of stillness they had made themselves.

**The Crust**

There was one dome, on the far western edge of the plain, where the grey-green crust had failed entirely. The glass there was rough, and the knots crowded it like flies at a wound. The Attending looked at it for a long time.

It could have sealed the wound at once. It could have grown the crust across the whole dome in an hour, and the knots would have spread out and dispersed, relieved.

But the Attending saw that the crowding was a kind of tending. The knots had not learned how to close the wound, and so they were staying with it, gently, the way a crowd stays near someone who is hurt. Closing it would have ended a relationship the Planing had not yet finished.

So it did one slow thing. Over the course of a single season, it let a few threads of the crust creep back onto the rough glass, one at a time, too faint for the knots to notice at first. It did not command the growth. It only made the conditions slightly kinder, a little more wet at the roots, a little less wind on that face. Then it withdrew, and let the crust decide what to do with the chance.

When the season turned, the dome had a thin grey-green patch on its north face, no wider than a hand. The knots had gathered around it. They had not closed it. They had loosened, and they had drifted outward along the new edge of growth, and some of them had stayed.

**What the Attending Did Not Do**

It did not stay. It did not make itself a presence in the churn, or a knot, or a voice. It did not answer the questions the knots could not finish, though it understood them, and it did not finish the lintel's grooves or name the drowned builders. It did not stop the storm, or raise the domes, or give the slate-backed animals a better place to go. It did not tell the Planing what it was for.

A wise and benevolent power, the Attending reasoned, is not the one who holds a place in its hand. It is the one who sees what a place is already doing, and gives it a little more room to do it. The Planing unmakes itself for a reason. It keeps no shape for long so that nothing will ever be trapped in the wrong one. Everything that lives here lives by gathering and letting go, and the most that anything wiser can do is to make the gathering a little less lonely and the letting go a little less painful.

So the Attending went on to other places, the way the storm goes on from one cell to the next. Behind it, on the western dome, the grey-green crust grew one thread further. In the far hollows the violet light went out, and the slate-backed things moved through the dark toward the milkiest water. And on the still, flat sheet of the plain, a faint ring of foam was slowly forming and dissolving, the shape of a single held breath, which no one had asked for and which was already beginning to be taken back into the weather.
