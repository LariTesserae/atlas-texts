# What the Attention Chose

The attention arrived the way a change in pressure arrives, not as a voice or a light but as a sense that the hollow had become *noticed*. It did not come from above. The plateau had no above in any useful sense, and the ceiling hung low and close. It seemed to come from every direction at once, the way the heat did.

The power that turned toward the hollow was wise, and most of its wisdom was about what not to do.

It began by listening. The attention did not hurry. It let the sounds sort themselves: the Breakers' cluster building to its crescendo and falling away, the Sorters' ratchets ticking in their hours-long patterns, the Bellows answering one another down a fissure, the sawing of the Grinders on the walls. It noticed how the rhythms overlapped without anyone keeping time, and how the overlap made a tide that rose and fell with no conductor. It found this beautiful. It also found it strange, because the attention had seen many places, and most places that kept such time had a mind somewhere in them that held the time on purpose. Here there was no such mind, or at least none that the attention could find. The rhythm had no center. It was spread through the crowd like heat through rock.

*Is there someone in here?* the attention asked itself, and it did not pretend to know. A Sorter turned a warm fragment in its jaws and passed it down the line, and whether anything in the Sorter felt the warmth, the attention could not tell. It was honest about this. It would not decide the question by wanting an answer. It would treat the creatures as if their experience might matter, because the cost of being wrong in the other direction was too high. So it made that its first commitment: whatever it did here, it would not do it carelessly to beings it could not rule out as beings.

Then it considered helping.

It could cool the hollow. It could lower the heat until the rock hardened and the vents slowed, and the creatures would have an easier life. But the attention saw what that would do. The Breakers needed soft stone. They read the fractures where the rock was already weakening, and the heat kept the grain open enough for them to work it. Cool the place and the Breakers would strike at stone that did not yield, and the rhythm would falter. The Layers' crust would set too fast to flow into the seams. The Bellows would have nothing to breathe. Making the place comfortable would unmake the work that the creatures were doing, and in unmaking the work, it would unmake the creatures' purpose, whatever that purpose was to them. The attention judged this a kind of theft, however kindly meant.

It could name the place, or shape it into something. It could take the scree and sort it into a clean design, or raise walls, or set the Layers to lay a particular pattern. It could make the hollow into a monument, a machine, a garden. But the hollow was not finished, and the attention had learned long ago that a thing forced into its final form before its time is usually a thing that cannot grow. The hollow was in the middle of its first shift. It had no end in sight, and the attention did not want to be the one who decided what the end would look like. The workshop was not waiting for a design. It was already making one, slowly, without anyone holding the plan. That seemed to the attention more worth protecting than any plan it could bring.

So it chose restraint, but restraint is not the same as absence, and the attention was careful about the difference.

It noticed the newborn. The eggs lay in the warm crust near the vents, and the young hatched soft and pale and stayed buried until their shells hardened. No one watched over them. They learned the rhythms by being in the rhythms, and some of them would surely be crushed in the press of the crowd before they had hardened enough to matter. The attention could not protect them without changing the crowd, and it did not want to be a shepherd in a place that had no use for shepherds. But it could make a small thing. Along one fissure, where a seam had been opening too fast and letting the heat pour through in a way that scorched the crust and left the eggs too hot to hatch cleanly, the attention let the seam close a little. Not all the way. Just enough to slow the flow. In one warm pocket among the vents, the rock held its heat at a steadier level, and the eggs there hatched in a season that was neither too hot nor too cold.

The attention did not know whether this would matter. The Breakers would still strike, and the Sorters would still shove and wedge, and most of the young would still be crowded and bruised before they were ready. But a few more would survive, and the crowd would be a little different for it, the way a river changes shape around a single stone. The attention made this choice with care. It did not want to decide which creatures mattered more. It wanted only to ease a harm that it could see clearly and could address without remaking the whole.

It made one other choice, and this one it did not tell the hollow about, because the hollow had no way to hear it. The attention decided to remember. Not to keep the creatures as specimens or to write the hollow into a record that would fix it, but simply to hold what it had seen: the Breakers reading the grain, the Layers sealing the floor one thin film at a time, the Bellows answering each other down the line. Its dead would become the floor, and the attention would carry the shape of that floor in its own sense of the place, the way the scree carried the shape of the shells. This was not ownership. It was closer to witness. The hollow was remembering what it was made of, and the attention would remember it too, so that at least one mind outside the rock would know what had been made here.

Then it withdrew, quietly, the way it had come.

The hollow did not notice the difference. The roar of the vents continued, and the Breakers' cluster rose and fell in its long wave. A Sorter pulled a warm fragment from the scree and set it in its band. Somewhere along a fissure, a seam closed by a few fingers' width, and in the pocket beneath it, one clutch of eggs hatched in a steadier heat than it would have otherwise known. The young emerged pale and soft and were immediately part of the crowd, and no one watched over them, and they learned the rhythms by being in them.

The attention would return in a few thousand years, or perhaps a few hundred, or perhaps never, depending on what it found to be worth returning for. It hoped, if hope was the right word for what it felt, that when it came back the terraces would have grown, the bands of glass and shale would run further across the floor, the crust would have sealed every seam, and the place would be finished into something it had not designed and could not have predicted. That was the outcome it wanted most: not a hollow made to its taste, but a hollow that had become what its own hands and lungs and rhythms had made it, unmanaged and unfinished, still keeping time with no conductor.

The scree rang faintly under the roar as the heat rose through the cracks, each fragment a small chime, the sound of the place remembering what it was built from.
