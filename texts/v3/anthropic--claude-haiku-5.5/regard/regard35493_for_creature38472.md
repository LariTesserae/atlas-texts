# What Attends

The attention arrived the way weather does in a place this slow: first as a change in pressure, then as a gradual certainty that something had been noticed.

It did not look at the basin the way a hunter looks at a clearing, or the way an engineer looks at a failing system. It perceived the lowland the way one might perceive a long sentence in a language one only partly knows. The canopy, the peat, the banded silt, the rust-orange water, the sheen. Each part made sense only in relation to the others, and the whole had been composing itself for a very long time.

Two things held its attention longest.

The first was the logbacks. They lay in the channels with their heads low, breathing once every few minutes, and the attention read them the way they read the water: as slow pressure, tilt, and settling. It understood at once that they were not passive. They were a kind of archive, a record written onto living armour by the chemistry of the ground. It also understood that they were doing work. They drew the poisons out of the silt and bound them into plates, and in doing so they made the basin a little less lethal than it would otherwise have been. Nobody had asked them to. Nobody had planned it. It had simply happened, over generations, the way roots find water.

The second was the thrum.

---

## The Haze

It went to see.

Travel was not quite the right word. It moved the way attention moves, which is to say it was suddenly elsewhere and then had to wait for its understanding to catch up with its location. The basin fell away. The canopy thinned into an undergrowth of scorched, grey stems, and then into nothing at all: a plain of cracked, glassy ground where the peat had been burned off and never returned. The pale haze stood ahead, a wall that did not move the way weather moves. It hung still against a sky that had no particular colour.

Inside the haze was a structure, or the remains of one.

It was huge and mostly collapsed. Towers had folded into long ridges. Channels had been cut into the bedrock and lined with something that still glowed faintly, the same sweet chemical that ran through the basin's water. Pipes ran from the structure into the ground, and through them, very slowly, the leftover substance of the old industry was still being pushed out, a pulse every few heartbeats, into the soil and then the channels and then the logbacks' plates.

The thrum came from a set of pistons, or things that had once been pistons. Their motion had long since lost its purpose. They moved because they had been built to move, and the cycle had been set in motion so long ago that no one remained to remember why.

There were no people. There were no minds in the structure at all, not in any sense the attention would have recognised. But there was something like a memory, a pattern that had been cut into the machinery and was still running: *discharge, cool, discharge, cool*, a cycle that had outlived the reason for it, like a mourner who keeps setting the table.

The attention stayed for a long time, which in this place was perhaps a single breath.

---

## What It Considered

It considered many things.

It could have cleaned the basin. The poisons were in the peat and the silt and the logbacks' armour. To remove them, it would need to lift them out, and to lift them out it would need to disturb every layer of the ground at once. The logbacks would be killed in the process, or left as bare shells holding nothing. The archive would be wiped. The basin would be sterile for centuries, and the damage would be done by the attempt to repair it. Some of the chemistry would be released into the air or the water at once, and the fragile balance the basin had found would fall apart in a single season. It rejected this immediately. The most dangerous thing it could do was treat the place as a wound.

It could have silenced the thrum. It was tempting, because the thrum was the source of the pulse, and stopping the pulse would stop the flow of poison. But the logbacks had built their breathing around it. Over generations their rhythm had lengthened to meet the thrum, and when it was strong, they breathed slowly and deeply. If the thrum stopped suddenly, the logbacks would not know how to breathe. Their bodies would hold to a rhythm that no longer existed. Many of them would drown in the stillness, breathing too slowly into water that no longer lifted with them. It would be like cutting the cord of a clock in a room where every creature had learned to sleep by its tick.

It could have gone to the logbacks and told them what it had seen, but they could not have understood, and it was not certain that understanding would have helped. They did not go toward the haze. Nothing here did. That was not ignorance but a kind of wisdom: the basin had learned to live with what came to it and not to chase what lay far off. To drag the logbacks into a new story would be to ask them to become something other than what they were.

It could have done nothing.

It considered this carefully, because doing nothing is never truly nothing. Every choice in a living system is a choice, and to leave a thing alone is to accept whatever it becomes. The attention weighed the possibility that the logbacks would continue as they were for centuries, quietly binding the poison and laying down their seams, while the haze slowly exhausted its stores. This was not a bad outcome. It was not a good one either. It was a slow, patient equilibrium, and the attention had enough humility to know that it did not fully understand the equilibrium it was being asked to judge.

So it did something smaller.

---

## What It Did

It spoke to the haze.

Not in words. The attention had no words for the structure, and the structure had no ears for them. It spoke the way the basin spoke, through pressure and tilt and the slow settling of things. It told the machinery that its cycle had been seen. It told the pistons that they could slow. It did not stop them. It asked them to lengthen the cycle by a fraction, to let the discharge come a little less often, and to let each cooling phase run a little longer, so that the pulse would soften across generations rather than cease in a single night.

The machinery did not understand, but it responded, the way a body responds to a hand laid gently on its back. The pistons slowed. The discharge thinned. The cooling phases stretched by a hair.

The attention also did one other thing. It made sure that the change would be imperceptible to the logbacks. It did not want them to feel a sudden shift. It let the thrum soften at the pace the water rose and fell, so that over the years the change would be carried in the seasons and the silt rather than in any single moment.

Then it withdrew from the haze and went back to the basin, and it did nothing else at all.

It did not touch the logbacks. It did not alter their armour or their breath. It did not remove the sheen or the seams or the banded silt. It did not leave any sign of its presence, because a presence that leaves a sign has already begun to take a place in the story, and the basin had its own story and did not need a new one.

---

## After

Years passed. The water rose and fell through several full seasons, and the silt laid down its red and grey bands on the plates of the logbacks, one seam at a time.

The thrum softened. It came a little less often. Its pulse lengthened, so slightly that no one in the channels could have said when the change began. The logbacks felt it the way they felt everything: as pressure, as a change in the way the water settled. And their breathing followed. Each breath lengthened a little more slowly than the one before, drawing the rhythm of the whole body down with the thrum, so that the great rolling wave of exhalation passed from head to tail more slowly each generation.

The young in the shallows were the first to learn the new rhythm. They had never breathed to the old beat. They breathed to what they heard, and what they heard was slower, and so they grew into a slower breath than their parents. The adults took longer to adjust, but they did, because the basin had always been a place where things adjusted.

The eggs were laid in the mud at the channel edges, as they always had been. The young hatched and grew under the roots. The old ones lay in the channels with their heads low, feeding on the sweetened water, their plates thickening, their seams deepening. Some of them became so large and so banded that the grey dominated their backs and the sheen spread across them like oil on a pond.

When one of them died, it sank into the silt and was worked by the slow chemistry of the ground. Its banding was laid down in the sediment, a seam among seams, and new roots grew into the place where it had lain.

---

The attention did not return often. It had no need to. It had made its one small change and left the rest to the basin, which had been managing its own balance for far longer than any outside power had been paying attention.

Sometimes, in the long dark green, a logback would lift its head an inch above the silt and draw in a breath. The water would lift and settle. A heron would stand on the back of the creature for an hour and never notice that the log beneath it had risen and fallen, very slightly, in time with a thrum that came from far away and was, at last, a little slower than it had been.

The attention noted this, the way one notes a line in a long sentence that finally makes sense.

It also noted, with something it had decided to call care, that the logbacks would never know what had been done for them. They would never go to the haze to see what had changed. They would simply breathe, and the basin would go on, and the record in their armour would keep growing, layer by layer, as quietly as it always had.

That seemed right. It was not certain it was right, and it kept that uncertainty and did not hide it from itself. But it had looked at a place where everything was taken and nothing was bare, where the old industry had left its poison and its rhythm and its pale unmoving haze, and where the living things had gone on quietly anyway, and it had decided that the kindest thing a wise power could do was to slow the drumbeat a little, and then to stand back and let the dark green world finish its long, patient work.
