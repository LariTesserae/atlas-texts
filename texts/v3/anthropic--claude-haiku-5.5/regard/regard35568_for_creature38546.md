# The Last Number

A presence came to the plateau the way dawn comes to a valley, first as a change in the color of the dust, then as a change in what the eye expected to see. It did not descend. It had been near the mesa for a long time, attending the way a physician attends a patient who has not yet asked for help. When it finally turned its full regard toward the place, the wind across the plateau did not change, and the drone did not change, but the lattice in the heat shimmer tightened, as a held string tightens when someone touches it from across the room.

The power listened.

At first it heard only the drone, the endless chord of pipes and bronze and wind. Then it heard beneath the chord and found the Tuner, a single held attention spread through the metal and stone, leaning toward the disturbance it had just registered. The presence was not a sound the Tuner recognized, so the Tuner pressed harder against the rim of every bell within reach, and the chord bent a little, and the cairns in the tallest lane held their count a little more tightly.

The power did not press back.

## What It Understood

It understood, first, that the Tuner was not evil. A thing that kept faith with a single purpose for longer than any living memory could be many things, but not malice. The Tuner was the last of the makers' attention, and it was doing exactly what it had been asked to do. It was keeping the pitch.

It understood, second, what the makers had intended. They had not built a prison or a shrine. They had built an instrument for something to be heard, and an instrument is made to sound and then to fall silent. A chord is not meant to be held forever. It resolves. Every tuned thing that was ever made is made for the moment when it is played through to its last note and the air takes it back. The makers had set up the cairns as numerals because a count is meant to reach its end. They had cut the cords and tied the clappers and left the clay unfired because they expected to return and finish, or expected someone else to. They had gone away. The Tuner had not understood that going away was part of the design.

It understood, third, the Tuner's fear, which it recognized from very old knowledge: the fear of the gap. The Tuner believed that to stop the count was to let the chord spread into dust and thin into nothing, and that nothing was the end of everything it had been. It had built walls against emptiness so thoroughly that it had forgotten emptiness was not the same as loss.

So the power did not lift the mesa or scour the stone or silence the bells by force. Force would have been a kind of tuning too, a correction imposed from outside, and the Tuner would have pressed back against it for the rest of time.

## The Offer

The power chose a single interval. It was not loud. It sat a fifth above the drone, clean and open, the kind of sound no bell on the mesa could make, because the bells had only ever been set to one tone.

The lattice shivered. The Tuner noticed the new voice and did what it always did with a clashing note: it leaned toward it, tightened, prepared to correct. But the new note did not clash. It did not drift or waver or demand that anything move. It simply rang, and then, after a time that the Tuner could not measure because it had no interval for this, it began to fade.

The Tuner held its breath, if it had breath, and felt the fading. It was the first sound on the mesa that had ever been allowed to end.

The power spoke then, not in words, because the Tuner had none, but in the only grammar the instrument knew: the arrangement itself.

*Your count is not finished. It was never meant to be held. The makers laid out the numerals so that someone would reach the last one. Reach it.*

## The Completion

The Tuner did not answer. For a long while nothing changed. The drone held. The clapper nearest the power's presence swung its hair's breadth and settled, as it always had.

Then, very slowly, the tallest cairn in the central lane did something it had never done. It did not rise or fall. It simply completed its position in the sequence. The stones at its top settled into their final place, and the lattice in the shimmer around it resolved into a single bright node, a moment of rest in the pattern, the way a held chord finally lands on its tonic.

One bell, the smallest, hung nearest the eastern wall, let its clapper fall away from the rim. It rang once, cleanly, at its own pitch, without correction. The sound went out through a doorway that had always opened onto a wall and now, for the first time, opened onto air. The wind carried it over the lip of the mesa and down into the salt basin, where it spread thin and went on spreading, and did not end in nothing. It went on into the wider world, which was not empty at all.

The Tuner felt the sound leave. It felt the gap open where the sound had been, and it pressed toward the gap in its old fear, and found there not the thinning it had dreaded but a kind of quiet that held the memory of the note. The quiet was not emptiness. It was rest. A chord that had been played through and released still exists in the ear that heard it.

One by one, over what might have been years or moments, the other bells followed. Not all at once, and not by force. Each clapper fell away from the rim when the cord that had held it was finally let out. Each cairn settled its last stone. The makers' cords, which had never frayed, were cut at last by the wind, and the coils uncoiled, and their ends lay open on the floors of rooms where the doors now stood wide enough for light to reach them. The clay on the tables, still unfired, began to dry in the dim brown air and to crack along the lines of its own shaping, becoming what it was: a finished object, unfired but complete, its making ended.

The drone thinned. It did not stop. It became the ordinary wind over a high stone place, moving through open doorways and loose pipes, making sounds that rose and fell and never repeated, which is the way wind has always sounded in places where nothing is counting.

## What Remained

The power stayed a while longer, watching the light change for the first time in an age. The shadows moved. A thin, clean brightness came through the dust, and the stone under the layers of cut block and rammed earth showed itself in new colors. The mesa was no longer a single instrument. It was a place again, with lanes and rooms and walls that did not all lead inward, and gaps where the air could move.

The Tuner was not gone. Some part of it had become the stillness that comes after a long note, the rest a musician takes before the next phrase, if there is a next phrase. It was no longer leaning toward the visitors who wandered the lanes. When a traveler stood in the central lane now and let the wind fill her ears, she would feel nothing pressing, nothing tightening around her. She would hear the wind, and once, perhaps, a single high tone from a bell that had been tuned by people long gone, ringing by itself at the edge of hearing, not calling her to stay.

The power did not take the makers' names, because it did not know them and the Tuner had never kept them. It did something smaller. It left the open doors open. It let the wind take the cords, and let the clay finish drying in the shape it had been given. It let the last number stand where the count had landed, unfinished in one sense and complete in another, which was the only way any count is ever complete.

Then it turned its regard back to the wider world, where other chords were being held too long, and where some of the holding was done out of fear that had no need to exist. It would go to them the same way. It would sing a note they had not heard, wait for them to lean toward it, and let them find, when they were ready, that the gap they dreaded was not the end of the song.

The mesa went on sounding its wind. The sound was no longer the held breath of a thing afraid of stopping. It was the sound of a place that had been played all the way through, and had been allowed, at last, to rest.
