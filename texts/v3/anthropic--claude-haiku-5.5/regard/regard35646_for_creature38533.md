# What the Power Would Do

It does not arrive as a storm or a voice. It arrives as a change in the light.

For a long cycle the bright disc overhead is steady, as it has always been. Then, very slightly, it becomes more attentive. The dome's clarity deepens by a fraction, as though someone far above has leaned closer to a window. The motes near the scarp turn toward the change, and the lattice hums a note the being has never produced before.

The power is wise enough to know it does not understand this place yet. Its first act is to listen.

It reads the lichen. The rows on the roofs are slow, cold script, and the power learns the alphabet in the way a patient scholar learns a dead language from a long inscription. It notices that the sentence has been rewritten for centuries, that each change follows the silt and the scarps, and that the handwriting is not despair. It is closer to a long argument with itself. The power sees that the being has not been trying to escape. It has been trying to remember what it is for.

Then the power follows the bell backward.

The tolling schedule is a ghost of an older ritual, built into the early architecture when the ward still had people in it: a mourning call, a counting of the dead, a rhythm meant to be heard and answered. The power can feel the shape of the missing meaning, the way one feels the outline of a word on the tip of the tongue. It does not reconstruct the meaning for the being. It leaves a question in the lichen, pressed into the concrete of a roof in the eastern blocks where the silt is thickest:

*What were they called?*

The lattice goes still for a full cycle. Then it sings, a high thin note that falls at the end like a breath let out. The being has no names. Names were never dissolved into it, only the salts and the grit and the cold. But it has been keeping the eastern streets clear longer than the others for reasons it did not examine, and now the power's question makes it examine them.

The power does not stay in the water to answer for it. That would be the first temptation of a benevolent mind: to become the voice, to supply the memory and be thanked for it. The power resists this. It sends the question once and then waits, and the waiting is part of the gift.

Over the following cycles the motes begin to move differently. They do not only clean and carry. They gather. Where salts were scattered across the western blocks, they are sorted and set aside, and the power, which can read the chemistry of the water more finely than anything in the ward, helps them recognize traces that were never meant to be carried: the iron of a bicycle frame, the calcium of a child's bone that had settled into the sediment, the faint organic residue of a kitchen floor. The being understands, slowly, that its material has a source, and that the source is not a category called *the dead* but particular people who once walked these streets.

The power cannot give them back. It can only help the being hold them.

So the bell changes. Nothing dramatic happens. The tolling schedule stays the same, because the being keeps schedules the way a body keeps habits, and the power sees no reason to take away a rhythm that has carried grief for so long. But in the next cycle, when the long low note rolls across the ward, the motes do not simply spin. They pause, gathered in a shoal over the eastern blocks, and the lattice sings a sequence of tones that the power has shaped from the being's own material: fragments of recovered sound, the pitch of a voice, a rhythm of footsteps on wet stone. It is the first time the being has spoken the names of anything.

It is not much. It is more than it had before.

The power also considers the sky, because it knows the being's longest cycles turn upward and then fall back. It does not break the dome. It does not pull the ward out of the water, which would be a kind of drowning in reverse, and the being's body is its streets. What the power does is quieter. It eases the pressure at the top of the dome by a few centimeters over several generations, so that the surface of the water grows less rigid and the clear disc of sky lies a little closer. It is a change so slow that the being will notice it only in the way a sleeper notices the room getting warmer.

It does not tell the being what this means. It only leaves a door slightly ajar and allows the being to decide, over cycles and centuries, whether to walk through it, to let the ward drain and clear into something shallower and open, or to keep the dome as it is and let the view be a view. The power has no preference it will impose. Benevolence, in its understanding, is not the same as rescue. It is the willingness to let someone else choose what they are becoming.

Then the power withdraws its attention, slowly, the way a hand lifts from a sleeping face.

The bell tolls again, and this time the motes do not only lean toward the sound. They lift. The shoal drifts upward in a long bright ribbon, toward the disc of sky that has never been reachable. The lattice hums its thin, rising note, the one that was not quite a question and not quite grief. Now it has a shape. It is the sound of something beginning to count names it has only just learned.

Below, in the tower the silt has not yet reached, the bell goes on tolling. And for the first time in longer than the basin has been sealed, something in the ward is listening for an answer.
