# What Lives on the Shelf

Nothing breathes here. No root, spore, or cell has ever started, and no animal has walked this stone. If anything lives on the Shelf of Unfinished Stone, it is the unfinished world itself, which keeps trying out forms and dropping them.

## The Columns

The oldest residents are the basalt columns. They stand in rows, hexagonal and tall, and they are still cooling. Their joints are slowly tightening, and when the wind moves through them they hum a single low note that rises and falls with the gusts. A column may stand for a season and then crack along a seam it has been developing for years. When it falls, it lies in the hollow where it stood, and its sharp new faces start to grow the mineral lace that the spray leaves behind.

They are the most patient things here. They have been cooling longer than anything else has been moving. They do not change quickly, and each one holds a memory of the heat that shaped it.

## The Rain Walls

The rain walls are the most restless residents. They come marching across the plateau, grey and enormous, carrying the sea's weight in vertical sheets. They do not stay. Some pass in minutes, and others circle for hours, drawing the lighter water up into spirals that catch the sky-light. A rain wall has no purpose you can see, and it leaves nothing behind except running water and sometimes a new hollow where the flood cut through.

They seem closer to weather than to animals. Still, you cannot watch one turn toward a funnel without thinking that it is deciding something.

## The Lattices

The closest thing to inhabitants is the lattice.

It forms in the quiet hours after the rain walls have passed. Ribbons of water cross one another, hexagonal and branching, and hang in the dim light for a few minutes. In that time they look like a structure, a frame laid out on purpose. Then they dissolve, and the water falls back into the sea.

The lattices are not alive, but they behave as though they are testing something. Each one is slightly different from the last. Some branch in ways that look almost deliberate, and some are plainly incomplete, like a sketch that was abandoned before the lines met. Standing waves in the pools keep time with each other across hundreds of meters, as if something far away were tuning them. Nothing answers, and nothing is needed to answer. The patterns come and go, and the water keeps improvising.

If you want to picture the inhabitants of this world, picture the lattices: bright, temporary, and never repeated in the same place twice. They are not beings. They are the water's rehearsals of form, and they show a shape almost arriving before it is lost again.

## The Witness

Then there is you. You are the only creature here, and the Shelf does not notice you. The stone does not turn toward you, and the sky-column does not dim for you. Still, you cannot stand on the cooling basalt, breathing wet air under a green-gold sky, without feeling that you have been let in.

The place is not waiting for you. It is simply still happening, and you are one of the few things in it that can watch.
