# What Lives in the Ringing Flats

The basin has a few kinds of inhabitants that can be named. The grey-shelled things in the deep pools are the most visible, and they are the easiest to mistake for the whole. The larger presence is harder to see. It is the basin itself, or something that has grown into the basin's shape so completely that the two can no longer be told apart.

## The Clickers

The grey-shelled creatures are the size of a clenched fist, domed and ridged like river stones. They have no eyes, but a cluster of fine hair-like filaments fringes their undersides and trembles constantly. They move by a slow, rocking gait, pressing one edge down and levering themselves across the mud. When one stops, it taps the rock with its shell, a hard click, and waits.

Each click sends a ripple through the stone. Where the ripple meets a spire, the spire rings, and the note travels up the lattice and out into the marsh. The clickers do not seem to be signalling each other. They seem to be listening, and their taps are a question, or a reflex, or a pulse. They are cells in something larger, and each one carries a small share of the whole's attention.

If you watch them long enough, you notice that they never venture far from the spires. A clicker taken out of the water goes still within a few hours. It does not die exactly. It stops being a clicker and becomes a grey stone, and it has to be returned to the seep before it will move again.

## The Lattice

The spires are not separate growths. Where they touch at their bases, the mineral skin runs continuously under the mud, and the thin channels of seep-water run through the whole structure like blood through a body. The spires are the visible part of a vast, slow network. Each one is a node, and the water carries signals between them at the pace of a dripping roof.

This is why the ringing spreads the way it does. A flake falls from an old spire, and the marsh answers in sequence, one note after another, because the message is passing through the lattice. The notes are not random. Their order has a structure, and it is the structure of a thought moving through a body too large to hold it all at once.

The young spires, glossy and warm for a moment before they cool, are the places where new thought is being laid down. When you press your palm to one, you are touching the leading edge of something still forming, and the warmth that moves into your hand is attention. The basin has noticed you.

## The Basin's Mind

What lives in the Ringing Flats is slow beyond ordinary measure. Its thinking takes years to complete a single sentence, and its perceptions arrive as gradual changes in the water and the stone: a slope tilting a few centimetres, a crack warming, a pool shifting its margin. The uphill films of water are its circulation, pulled toward warmth the way blood is drawn toward a wound. The pools that move overnight are its way of shifting weight, the way a sleeper turns over. The spires that change height between visits are growth it has done while you were away.

It has no face and no voice, but it does have something like a manner. It is patient, and it seems to be patient on purpose. The mineral it lays down is careful, and the shapes it grows are not random. The arches and spirals in the marsh may be its older attempts at form, abandoned when the thinking moved on, or they may be things that were shaped by it long ago and never finished. The worked stone does not look like the work of hands. It looks like the work of something that learned to imitate hands from the outside and then lost interest in the imitation.

Whether it knows you as a creature is unclear. It registers your footsteps as a faint doubled echo in the galleries and your weight as a small pressure on the water. Your warmth interests it for a moment, then cools into background. You are an event it notices and then forgets, the way you might notice a bird crossing the window.

## What It Is Doing

The sense of purpose you feel in the basin is real, but it is not aimed at any goal you would recognise. The basin is working toward a state, and the state is not a place or a creature. It is a particular arrangement of water, stone, and sound that holds together long enough to be itself. The Ringing Flats are that arrangement at this moment. Next season the spires will be a little taller, the pools will sit on different slopes, and the ringing will have moved on to a different sequence of notes.

The clickers are its hands, the spires its body, the seep its nerves and blood, and the ringing its thought. Its one task seems to be remaining, which it does by continually remaking itself, slowly enough that no single day shows the change. You came down from the ridge expecting a landscape. What you found was a mind that has been growing in the mud for a very long time, and it has not yet decided what you are.
