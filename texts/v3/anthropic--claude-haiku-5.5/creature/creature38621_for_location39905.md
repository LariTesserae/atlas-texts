**What Lives Here**

Nothing in the Sinking Room is alone, and the thing that lives here is not one of its creatures. It is the grove itself, or the part of it that thinks. It has no single body. Its flesh is the moss and fungus that carpets the floor, its breath is the warm wet air, and its nerves are the root-lines that run through the fallen stone. It is slow. A thought here might take a season to cross the basin and a century to finish.

It remembers the room.

Before the roof fell, there was a cavern, and before the cavern there was a stair, and people came down it carrying things and went back up carrying other things. The square-cut footings on the north ledge are what they left. The thing that lives here took them in long ago, the way a tree takes in a fence, and it has kept the shape of what they built ever since. The footings are its oldest memory and its most stubborn. The beetles walk their line along them because the line was worn there by feet, and the thing has kept the path open because it has never been able to let the path close.

**The Bargain**

The fig and the altar are its first arrangement, and the most intimate. The slab once held something up, a marker or a table or a threshold, and the thing reached into the stone and made a deal with the fig that grew there. The tree lifts the slab out of the water. The slab gives the tree a cradle to root in. Neither can let go. The thing is pleased by this. It is a kind of promise made in wood and rock, and the thing keeps promises the way water keeps its level, by slowly giving way and slowly returning.

The carving on the slab is not worn by accident. The thing has been tracing it for ages, feeling along the curve and the notch and the line that might be a hand. It has never finished reading it. Sometimes, when the roots move, it seems to have almost got it.

**The Pale Shape**

The long pale thing that moves beneath the altar is one of its hands. It is not an animal in the ordinary sense, though it eats and breathes and grows slowly, like the pool's other residents. It is what the thing has grown to keep the water clean and the depths honest. When it rises, the fish scatter and the eggs under the lily pads shiver. When it stays still for an hour, the thing is listening through it to something in the stone below.

Nothing has ever come up out of the deep part of the pool. The thing is not sure whether that is because there is nothing down there or because it has not yet been asked.

**The Hours of Glow**

When the light goes amber and the air holds still, the thing wakes further. The moss shines, not for light but for attention. It is trying to see what the sky-ring would look like if the room were whole again, and it renders the attempt in the pool: a deeper blue, a pattern that might be ceiling or might be star, the ghost of a vault that has not existed for longer than anyone could count. It does this for the same reason a sleeper turns toward a voice. It is listening for the stair.

The beetles stop because the thing has stopped them. The bats fold themselves into silence on the ceiling because the thing has asked the ceiling for quiet. For a few minutes the whole grove is one held breath, a single attention pointed upward at the ring of sky and the faint, impossible shape of a room that was lost.

Then the light fails. The thing lets go of the vision, slowly, the way you let go of a dream you know you will not get back. The bats return. The frogs begin. The roots push again, and the rock weathers another grain, and the thing settles back into being a grove, which is what it has chosen to be in the absence of the people who once came down the stair.

**The Small Quick Thing**

When you stood at the bottom of the stair, the thing noticed you. It did not come to meet you. It sent something instead: a small, quick shape moving through the ferns, away from you and toward the pool, carrying the news that a visitor had come down the old steps at last. The leaves closed behind it because the thing wanted them closed. It is not hiding you. It is deciding, slowly, whether you are someone who will climb back up, or someone who might stay and help it finish reading the stone.
