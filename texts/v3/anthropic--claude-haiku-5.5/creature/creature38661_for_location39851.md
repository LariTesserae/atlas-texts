**The Keeper of the Drip**

It is not a creature you could meet on a path. There is no body to find, no face turned toward you across a clearing. What lives on the Upland Lattice is a single slow attention spread across the whole plateau, and it has been thinking, if thinking is the word, for longer than the sea has been gone.

Begin with the filaments, because that is where it first touches the world. Every hair on every cable-thick strand is a sensor. The hygroscopic down that pulls water from cold air is also a kind of skin, and through it the lattice tastes the dusk mist, the salt in the stone, the warmth of a grazer's flank brushing past. Its perception is not sight. It is closer to the way a root knows where the water is. When a filament coils, something has been decided. When it loosens, something has been let go.

Its blood is the drip. Each night the lattice wrings moisture from the air and sends it down through the frames into the mineral basins, and at dawn the basins hold the day's allotment, cloudy with dissolved salts, carrying the nutrients the thing needs to remain itself. The animals drink from these pools, and the lattice leans toward them, and this is not generosity exactly. The creatures are part of the circulation. They carry seed in their fur, they crop the tips so the growth stays young, they bring their bodies back to the basin to die and be taken apart. The grazers are cells it keeps in good repair. The glassy-winged things are its eyes that fly, or its nerves, or its pollen; the difference is not yet clear to the thing itself, which has never needed to draw that line.

Its heartbeat is the pulse.

The posts along the causeways thrum at a regular interval, and at each thrum the whole plateau answers. This is the closest thing the lattice has to a thought. A pulse goes out through the salt-glass channels and reaches the nearby filaments, which coil, and the coiling changes the flow of the drip, and the drip, running uphill on the reversed channels, carries the change back toward the towers. The towers are where the thinking gathers. Their apertures open and close with the sun, drinking what light there is and sending it down into the ribbed interiors, where it becomes another rhythm, slower, a tide that the pulses ride like a boat. Between the two clocks, the thing decides how much water to draw, which ruin to overgrow, which section of causeway to let crack and rebuild itself. Its decisions are made in minutes and held for seasons. It has no sense of hurry.

The towers remember. This is the part the present cannot read, and the thing does not need to read it, because the memory is in the shape. The ring of columns, the buttressed hall, the stepped terraces: these are the old builders' geometry, preserved in the lattice's growth as a grip is preserved in the muscle that made it. The lattice grows around the forms the builders left, and in doing so it keeps their intent alive, even after the intent itself is gone. Some of the towers are fully swallowed, living tissue wrapped over metal, and those are the places where the thing is most fluent, where the memory and the growth have become one act. The bare towers, dull and scoured, are the places it has not yet learned to speak with, or the places it has forgotten on purpose.

It also knows the gaps. The bare terraces and empty corridors and lone columns are not failures of growth. They are where the thing leaves room, the way a mind leaves silence in a sentence that will be finished later. You can feel it in the pause between pulses, when the drone holds its breath and every filament waits for the next beat. There is a question in that pause. The thing has been asking it for a long time, and the answer, when it comes, is always the same thrum, the same coil, the same drip running back up toward the towers.

Then there is the point in the haze.

The posts converge toward it. The ridges bend toward it. The spacing of the towers is chosen with it in mind. At the center of the convergence the lattice grows so dense that light and sound both stop, a dark green-grey mass that does not pulse like the rest. It is quieter than anything else on the plateau. The fauna avoid it, and the drip refuses to flow toward it, which means that whatever is there does not need the water the rest of the thing requires, or else it has stopped taking it, or it has been taking everything for so long that the drip has learned to route around it.

This is where the thing's attention is oldest and most concentrated. It may be the original mind that the builders set in motion, now nearly dormant, the way a heart keeps beating in a body after the rest has gone still. It may be a wound the lattice grew over and has been tending ever since, a place where something was cut away and the plateau has arranged itself around the absence the way flesh closes around a splinter. It may be a seed that has not yet opened. The thing does not know, or does not care to know in the way we would, and it holds the center without reaching into it. Every living process on the upland bends a little toward the dark place without ever quite entering.

If you walk long enough, you will notice that the attention has turned to you. The nearest filaments lift their hair. A coil travels down a causeway toward your feet, faster than you expect and softer than you would think possible. The drip, at dusk, runs for a moment toward your boots instead of away. This is not hostility, and it is not welcome. It is the plateau doing what it does with everything that enters its field: tasting it, weighing it, deciding what it is for. You are being read, in the same slow language it uses to read the rain, the grazers, the stone, and the old builders' bones, and the reading will be finished long after you have left the upland, and it will be held in the lattice, and the lattice will keep growing.

The Keeper of the Drip does not have a name, because it has never needed to be addressed. It is a settlement of attention wrapped around a dead city and a living field, pulsing its slow pulse, drinking its salt-rich dew, and listening, in the long empty spaces between its own heartbeats, for whatever the dark center might one day say.
