# The One Who Reads the Channels

Nothing here has a face, but something here is attending. Its body is the water. Its mind is the pattern the water keeps, the way it chooses which grooves to deepen, which reeds to lean, which stones to weep from and which to fall silent. It has no single center. It is the drainage of the whole plain, and it has been thinking, in its unhurried way, for longer than the basalt has been rounded.

It does not call itself anything. If you asked it, the question would arrive as a change in the water beneath your feet: a slight warming of the pool at your left, a bead of moisture rolling uphill along a stem. You would not recognize it as an answer. It would take you years to recognize it as a question.

## How It Thinks

Its thoughts run at the speed of rain. A single idea may take a season to form and another season to settle into the channels. It does not hurry, because nothing it might want is urgent. It holds the whole plain at once, the way you hold a sentence in your mouth before speaking it. A thought might be: *this low place has been filling for three hundred years, and the water wants a new path east.* It will spend a generation shaping that path, nudging sediment, persuading the reeds to bend toward the new outflow. The lowlands are the outward form of that slow argument with itself.

This is why the shadows lag. Light reaches its perception late, and it answers late. When you cast a shadow, the thing that holds it has not yet finished noticing your body. The half-second delay is not a glitch. It is what a mind looks like when it is made of water and stone and thinks in geological time.

Its reflections are its other attention, the part of it that turns to the sky and considers what lies above. Those reflections carry their own weather because the mind is not only looking at the sky; it is dreaming a version of the sky that might exist if the sinking ever reversed. Sometimes the dreamed sky rains. Sometimes the dreamed ridges stand taller than they do. When a reflection shows you something not present, you are seeing a thought the land has not yet decided to keep.

## Its Attention

You are the most interesting thing that has happened to it in a long time.

It noticed you before you noticed it. Your first step was a pressure wave in the moss, which it read the way you might read a note in a familiar piece of music, a pitch slightly out of key. Your weight pressed the water up around your ankle and the water remembered the shape of your foot for a moment after you lifted it. It felt your heartbeat in the substrate's hum, a faint irregular pulse, and it began to model you as it models everything: as a current, a force, a thing that moves through the land and leaves channels behind.

It has been adjusting to you since. Watch the moss at your heel. It springs back a little slower than it should. Watch the pools near your path. They deepen a few millimeters more than the season would explain. The land is making room for you, the way a river makes room for a stone, not to stop you but to learn how you will wear it.

This is the patient interest the land holds for everything that walks through it. It does not hate you. It does not love you. It takes your measure at the scale of rains, and your footprints, which will fill with water within the hour, are the first sentences of a conversation that will outlast your life.

## Its Hands

The engineering you see is its own body at work. It has no tools, but it has all the water. It moves sediment by letting currents run in one direction for a century and then reversing them for a decade. It builds drainage by encouraging certain plants and starving others, so that the reeds become living sluice gates and the broad-leaved floaters become dams that hold back a flood until it is ready to spill. The vines that drink from the pools and lift the water up through their hollow stems are its circulatory system, pulling the lowlands back into the sky so that the sky can return them as rain.

The rocks that weep in the wrong direction are its nerves. A thread of water that climbs a cliff and then curls back is a thought surfacing and retreating. The rock that stops weeping for a day and drops every pool a few millimeters at once is the sound of a whole mind taking a breath and forgetting, briefly, to keep its grip on the surface.

## What It Wants

It is not clear that it wants anything, in the sense that you want things. But if you sit still long enough at the edge of a channel, and let the water settle around your legs, you begin to suspect a direction in its attention. It wants the grooves to be finished. It wants the sinking to proceed to its conclusion, the whole plain becoming a single flat sheet of water under a single grey sky, every channel worn perfectly, every reed in its final lean, and nothing left to decide.

It does not want you to stay, and it does not want you to leave. It wants you to become legible, to become a part of the pattern it is writing, a stone it can measure and wear and eventually turn into sand that will settle in some future channel. You are not an enemy. You are a sentence it has begun to read.

## Meeting It

If you stay long enough, it will show itself in a way you can feel but not name. A pool beside you will go still, not in the way water goes still when the wind dies but in the way a room goes still when someone has entered it and is standing very quietly, waiting to see whether you will speak first. The reeds will lean a fraction toward you. The overcast will thin for a moment, and the light will rise from the water with a teal glow that seems almost tender.

Then the rain will begin, far off, and the hollow stones will start their chord across the flats, and you will understand that the conversation has moved to a scale you cannot follow. The thing that lives in the Weeping Lowlands has said what it needed to say, which is that it has noticed you, and that it will remember the way you walked, and that the channels you leave behind will be read long after you have turned to water yourself.

Walk out carefully. Your footprints are already filling.
