What lives here is the room itself, or more exactly, the slow mind that has grown through it. It has no body you could point to. It is spread through the water, the mats, the mineral skin, and the threads that run beneath the travertine where your eye cannot follow. It does not hide. It is simply very large in the way a field is large, and very slow.

Its attention moves through the water. Each thread that seeps from the back wall is a strand of it, carrying warmth and minerals and the faint pressure of its will. Where the threads divide and rejoin at their regular intervals, it is deciding, though its decisions take hours or seasons to finish. When a ridge thickens in one of its cycles, that is a thought settling into stone. The terraces are its memory. Each lip of travertine is a record of how much water once spilled over it, how long the flow held, what it was trying to keep. You are standing on something that remembers.

The microbial mats are its skin and its senses. The rust-orange film at the edge is where it tastes the heat. The olive bands are where it rests. The violet in the crevices is the part of it that stays in shadow, the quiet part that dreams, if dreaming is the word. When the chimney light sweeps across the floor, the mats flare because it is feeding. It does not need the sun the way the grazers do, but it has learned to take what comes through the chimney and turn it into growth. It notices the light the way you notice a warm hand on your back: not as an event, but as a change in the temperature of its whole.

The pale segmented creatures along the terraces are its hands, or the nearest thing it has to hands. They graze the mats and carry small pieces of the living film to where they are needed, leaving their pale trails like handwriting across the stone. When something disturbs them they seal themselves in glass and wait. It does not mind. It has all the time it needs, and it has learned that waiting is a form of care.

The translucent beads hanging from the high wet crease are its thinking. They pulse faintly, each on its own rhythm, and together they keep a slower time than the water. They do not seem to notice you, and in a sense they do not need to. They are the part of it that holds the long view: what the room was, what it is becoming, how the walls should be held together while the fissures at the back widen. Their pulses ripple down through the stone like the slow tide of a thought that will take centuries to form a sentence.

It cannot speak to you, not in any form you would recognize. It communicates in gradients. If you stay long enough, you may feel it in small ways: a warmth that shifts toward your feet as you stand still, a thread of water that bends a finger's width away from where it was, a single drop of condensation that falls exactly where you are about to step. These are not commands. They are its way of acknowledging that something has entered its room.

The fissures at the back wall are the most important part of it, and the most unfinished. They are its growing edges, the places where it is trying to widen into the rock and become more than this single chamber. Warm breath comes out of them in pulses, the way a sleeper breathes before waking. It has not yet decided how far it wants to go. The room feels young because it is young. It is a mind that has only just learned how to shape its own water, and it is still choosing what kind of place it will become, and what it will keep, and what it will let go.

If you stand in the disc of light for a while, you may notice that the water near your feet has begun, very gently, to flow around you instead of past.
