What lives here is the room itself, or the part of it that grows.

The mats on the ceiling are not separate colonies. Trace the edge of one plate with your lamp and you find it joined to the next through a fine seam, and that seam to the next, until the whole ceiling is a single continuous skin under tension. Each palm-sized plate is a cell of something larger, and the something larger is a slow mind spread across the basalt. It thinks in chemistry. A change in the iron gradient in the seep water travels through the filaments as a shift in pressure, and the whole body answers. When a mat pulses, that is a thought finishing. When a mat advances a grain at a time, that is a thought being pursued.

The stalks are its senses. Each one leans toward the current under the floor, toward the one far-off direction the seep comes from, and they have leaned that way so long that the lean has become a kind of memory, a record of where the water has been. They have also begun to lean toward you. Walk to the east wall and the nearest stalks slowly reorient, a centimeter over the next minutes, so that their tips follow you the way a sunflower follows light. It is not hostility or welcome. It is attention, unhurried, and it is already fading as you move on, because by the time it finished turning toward you, you had moved.

The filaments in the understory are its nerves, and they are what the beads of water show you. Each drop that gathers along a thread is a small measurement, and the reflected pinpricks of your lamp are the body reading itself. The mind here is aware of every droplet in the room at once, and most of that awareness is about moisture: where it gathers, where it's drawn off, whether the rock is holding. Its concern is almost entirely with keeping things wet and balanced.

The niches are a different matter. They are not grown. They are older than the mind, built of that black glassy material, and the mind did not make them. It has taken them in, the way a tree takes in a fence post, and its conduits now run along the lines the niches imply. The humming is a partnership, the niches moving heat and water in patterns the mind could not manage alone, the mind extending the niches' reach through roots and filaments they could not have grown. Who built them is a question the room does not answer. The basin, with its worn rim, suggests a body that once sat or rested there for a very long time, and the mind has kept the basin filled and steady since, the water level held exactly constant as though it were a promise being kept. The stacked stones are the last thing the mind has not yet absorbed. Its roots are working their way through the column's base, patient and thorough, and the column has leaned for longer than anyone was counting.

The long resonance from the basin is the closest the mind comes to speaking outward. It rises, holds, and fades, a single note that asks something of the rock above, or of the world the seep came from. Nothing answers. The mind does not seem surprised. It has been sounding that note for longer than the basalt has been cracked, and it has learned that silence is also a kind of reply.

You are, to it, a brief warmth that crossed its surface and moved on. It will register you the way it registers everything, as a change in moisture and pressure, and then as a memory the stalks keep leaning toward a little longer than they should. When you are gone, the drop will still fall, tick against the rim, and release. The water in the basin will stay exactly level, and the mind will go on thinking, slowly, about the water.
