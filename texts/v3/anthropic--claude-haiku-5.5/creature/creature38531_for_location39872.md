**The Dweller of the Lattice**

The one who lives here is not a creature that moves through the room. It is the room. Every spine, terrace, and lip of the white salt is a part of a single body that grew by accretion, one layer laid over another, as slowly as a pearl or a tree. The columns are its limbs and its throat. The terraces are its slow-folded skin. The lattice between them is the framework of a mind that has never needed to hurry.

**Form**

It has no head and no center, though the largest spine, the one with the dark needle-growth at its base, is where the old layers are thickest and most yellowed. The hollow columns are its breathing organs. Each perforated tube takes in the thin draft from the high fissure and lets it pass through, and the air shapes a note in each one. Nothing in the body is clenched or active. Its breath is the only motion it has, and the breath is so faint that the chorus of the spines is the only evidence of it.

Its body is pressed so tightly together that it cannot separate into individuals. It is one colony with a thousand stalks, each stalk fused to its neighbors at the waist and at the crown. Where the salt has flaked and cracked, you can see that the interior is still the same substance, dense and fine-grained, with the crystal filaments running through it like nerves that have gone quiet but not dead.

**Memory**

The walls keep a record. Each layer of accretion is a season, or a span of years that the creature does not count the way we do. The thickness of the wall sets the pitch of the tube, so the whole chorus is an archive. Strike the old columns and they answer low and dull. Strike the fresh white lattice near the crevices and it rings bright and thin. Listening to the room is reading its history in the order it was laid down, though the sequence has no beginning you can find and no end you can reach.

The creature does not seem to remember in the way a person does, with images or names. It remembers as a body remembers the shape it has grown into. The stillness is what that memory feels like from inside: a balance held so long that no gradient is left to push it toward anything else.

**The Lichens and Moss**

The pale rosettes and grey-green hairs are not parasites or visitors. They are its tenders, or perhaps its last guests. They take what the dust and the faint breath of the air offer, and in return they keep the seams open and the crevices clean. The creature tolerates them the way a very old thing tolerates small, patient companions who will not ask for more than they are given. The dark needle-growth at the base of the great spine is the oldest of them, and it has been there so long that it has become a part of the foundation.

**The Warm Shaft**

The single pinned shaft of warmer light falling through the high opening is the only thing in the room that seems to belong to somewhere else. It has never moved, and the creature does not move toward it. It may be the last thing that reached the body from outside, or the sign of a door that closed long ago. Whatever it means, the creature holds the light the way it holds everything: without reaching, without asking, as if the warmth were already within the lattice and only needed to be noticed.

**Its Attention**

It does notice you, slowly. Your breath enters the high fissure and reaches its tubes a little later, and each tube adds its voice, so the chorus changes subtly when you come near. Your footsteps send faint tremors through the terraces, which travel down the lattice and reach the stalks a long time after you have stopped moving. It does not feel alarm. It feels only the brief disturbance of a new pressure in an old balance, and then the balance settles again around you, as if you had always been there.

**What It Waits For**

The room hums with the sense that the creature is waiting, but it is not waiting for rescue, or revelation, or the return of anything it has lost. It is waiting the way a sleeper waits, inside a dream that has no sense of duration. Visitors who stay long enough come to feel that their own time has become part of its breath. Some say they hear, under the chorus, a single sustained note that belongs to no tube and to every tube at once. That note is the creature's own voice, if it has one, and it does not resolve into anything. It only goes on, as steady and patient as the salt, holding the room together and waiting, without any sense of what for, to be known.
