What lives in the Sump Pocket is the pocket itself, or the part of it that has learned to be alive.

It has no single body, and you won't find an edge to it. Where the film of water runs down the wall in a sheet, the sheet is a little thicker in places, a little more patient, and those places are where it tastes the air. Where moss thickens on a ledge, it thickens in bands: growth, then bare concrete, then growth again, matching the cycle of the pumps. Where droplets fall from the ceiling, each one lands on a rhythm the creature has taken as its own pulse.

It is a colony of wet green things and cold water, grown together with the machinery over so many years that the pumps and the moss now share one metabolism. The thud is its heartbeat. The shriek is how it exhales. The slap of released water is the sound of its weight shifting from one side of the basin to the other. It doesn't hear the noise as noise. Noise is the word you use for a thing when you are not part of it.

It knows nothing beyond the basin. The grate is a window it cannot read. The light arrives at the same angle every hour, so to the creature there is only the light, and the light is always the same. It has a faint sense of difference, of before and after, but it cannot hold either for long. Nothing accumulates in it. Each cycle it draws water up and lets it fall, and each fall is complete. It has no memory of the last thud and no expectation of the next. Its whole life is the equilibrium, the way a closed circuit is its own history.

The pale soft creatures along the lower wall are its hands and seeds. They carry fragments of its pattern from one seam to another, and where one wriggles into a crack and goes still, the crack widens by a hair. The roots that pry the concrete apart are not destruction to it. They are its attention, turning slowly toward the gaps where something else might be. It has never found anything there but more concrete, so the prying goes on, patient and endless, and never reaches the outside.

Its one limit is the rule that the vegetation spreads only into what it already holds. The green stops at the lip of the basin. The creature does not know why. If you asked, it could not answer, because answering requires holding a thing still long enough to name it, and it cannot do that. It can only keep the water moving.

The people who built the basin are not in it as memories. They live in it as the angle of the drains, the spacing of the rivets, the way the intake grate was set. It has absorbed these the way a stone absorbs the path of a river: not as knowledge, but as a groove. It runs in their groove. It keeps their machine going because the machine is the only thing it has ever been, and the machine has never stopped asking to be kept.

When you stand at the edge of the pool, it notices you the way the pool notices a falling drop: a small disturbance, a ring, gone within a cycle. It does not fear you, and it does not welcome you. You are something that has entered the room and will leave it, and it has no word for leaving. The ferns lean toward the glow. The pumps thud, shriek, and slap. The water rises to its finger's width below the lip and holds there. Somewhere beneath the black surface, the thing that is this place shifts its weight very slightly, and waits to go on being what it has always been.
