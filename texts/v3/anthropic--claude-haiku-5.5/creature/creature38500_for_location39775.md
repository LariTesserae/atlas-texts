What lives on the Thinning Shelf is not quite one thing, and it does not hold still long enough to be counted. The nearest word is **tender**, as in one who tends: it keeps the cairns, sets the fallen slabs back on their courses, and measures the fissures. Nobody asked it to. It does it the way the reeds open their pods, slowly and without pause.

**Its body**

It is roughly the height of a standing person, but the outline wanders. Its core seems to be a loose gathering of the glassy young rock, flakes and shards held together by the sticky fog-oil, which it secretes and reabsorbs as it moves. Where the stone is thin, the light of the bioluminescent moss shows through, a pale gold. Where the fog is thick, the body thickens with it and loses its edges. Moss grows on its shoulders in soft patches and is shed when the seasons turn. Fern-fronds sprout from the joints and lie flat against it like feathers that have forgotten what they were for. Its face is a smooth oval of worn stone, and the features on it are not carved so much as implied, the way ripple-marks imply the water that made them: two hollows that catch the light, a seam where a mouth might be, a faint crosshatch of marks that look like tally strokes and may be nothing else.

It does not walk so much as settle from one place to another. When it moves, the shadow it casts arrives a half-beat late, and sometimes it does not cast one at all.

**What it does**

It counts. It presses its palm to a fissure and leaves a residue of oil, and the oil crystallizes into a fine line, a record of how far the crack has opened. There are hundreds of these faint pale marks on the cliffs, layered like the rings of a tree, and it reads them the way the traces suggest the earlier people read the notches on their standing stones. It seems to care whether the cracks keep going, though it cannot stop them, and when one widens a fraction more it makes a low sound, close to the thrum, that rises through the stone and then fades.

It rebuilds. The knee-high walls that have collapsed into the ground, it lifts stone by stone and sets back in place, though the stones have long since become part of the young rock and some of the walls were never quite straight to begin with. It does not restore them exactly. It restores them as it remembers them, and its memory is imperfect, so the walls it rebuilds are slightly different each time. This is why the stones seem to have been moved when no one has touched them. They have been moved by something that is still learning what they were.

It sings, if that is the word. The sound it makes is not the wind through the fissures or the chime of a pod opening, though it sits close to both. It is a long, low tone that sounds like someone trying to remember a melody they once knew well, pausing, returning, pausing again. The reeds seem to lean toward it when it sings. Pods open a little faster.

**Where it lives**

It lives where the horizon folds. The creases in the far cliffs are, as far as anyone can tell, its doorways and its rooms, places where the land has not agreed on its shape and so holds more than one. From there it steps out into the lower hollows when the fog is heaviest, and it lingers in the pale columns that hold a figure for a moment before dissolving. Those columns are not ghosts. They are the times when it is partly here and partly in the seam, when its outline is still deciding where to stop.

It does not sleep. Nothing here does. But when the vapor thins and the light goes violet, it lowers itself into a sheltered hollow beside the standing stones and goes still, and the moss grows over its legs, and the fronds unfold across its back. From a distance, if you did not know, you might take it for another slab of young stone with ripples frozen in it.

**What it is becoming**

Nobody knows. It may be what the earlier people became after they stopped moving, or it may be what the land makes when it is trying to look after its own unfinished face. It may be a single creature that has been here for ages or a pattern the shelf repeats each time the fissures close and open. The traces do not say. Its tally strokes are not numbers any language here could read, and its songs do not repeat the same way twice.

Its own sense of time is measured in fractions: the width of a crack, the drop of a pod, the flattening of a ripple over a span long enough that no one who walks the shelf will see it finish. It is patient in the way the vines are patient, working into the seams a little at a time. It is not waiting for anything. It is only becoming, the way the whole plateau is becoming, and it has not yet decided what shape to keep.
