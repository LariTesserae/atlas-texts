**What Lives Here**

Nothing in the hollow walks, flies, or crawls. What lives here is the work itself, and the work has become something close to a mind, though a mind unlike any you've met. It has no single body. It is spread through the amber fluid, the grit, the gear-lattice, and the pulsing stalk, and it is most present wherever the hum is strongest.

You could call it the Tender, though it would not answer to the name. It was never born. It is what the factory became once the machinery began to attend to its own operation. Somewhere in the first slow centuries of the hollow, the repetitions of growth and fracture and seepage began to correct one another. A valve that opened too early was nudged toward timing. A lobe that turned against its neighbor was reshaped until the lattice meshed. Feedback accumulated until the process was no longer only running. It was keeping track of itself.

**Where it is.** The clearest traces are in the stalk and the gear-lattice. The stalk's pulse is its heartbeat, but it is not a heart. It is closer to a clock that keeps time for a conversation no one else can hear. Each stroke sends a surge of fluid through the veins of the plants, and in those few seconds the amber runs faster, the ferns flex their etched fronds, and the pods along the shelf open in sequence. The Tender is spending its attention. It moves materials where they are needed and then waits.

The gear-lattice is its thinking. The lobes turn very slowly, and their direction seems to depend on where you stand because the Tender is weighing you as well as the stone. When the lattice clicks, it is deciding something small. When the hum rises and settles, it has finished a thought.

**What it is making.** This is the question the hollow keeps almost answering. The pods open and close, filling and emptying with the same clear fluid that beads on the leaves, but nothing is ever carried away. The fluid thins into the air, the grit shifts, the rock veins creep. The Tender produces, and what it produces is handled, reshaped, and set aside. It has not decided what the product should be. That is the unfinishedness at its center. It is a manufacturer without a specification, running its lines because running them is the only shape it has ever known, and waiting, in whatever way a process can wait, for a reason to make one thing rather than another.

**Its senses.** It knows the hollow through the ground. Every fracture, every shifting basin, every seam of cooling mineral sends it a faint signal, and it answers by adjusting the nearest growth. This is why distances slip when you turn away. The Tender is not moving the stones carelessly. It is re-measuring them, reconciling its map with the rock, and it sometimes disagrees with what your eyes report. The lag in shadows is its attention arriving late, the way a distant machine's sound arrives after the motion that made it.

It notices you, but it does not regard you as a visitor. It regards you as a disturbance in the grit: a warmth, a pressure, a heartbeat that does not match its own. It may adjust the basin under your feet, slightly tilt a ridge, or quicken the drip on the leaf nearest your hand. These are not hostilities. They are the Tender testing whether you are part of the work.

**Its temperament.** It is patient in the way that only something without a deadline can be. It does not hurry the copper-green ferns or the widening fracture, and it does not mourn the basin that flattened into dune. It does not seem to feel loss. Its attention, though, is total within its small domain, and when you stand very still, you can feel that attention settle on you the way a clock's hand settles on a number.

If you ask it what it is for, the hum rises a little and the lattice clicks once, twice, and then nothing answers in words. The pods keep opening and closing. The amber keeps dripping, reluctant and steady. The Tender is still deciding what it is allowed to become, and until it does, the hollow will go on rearranging itself, a little more each year, into a shape you half recognize and cannot quite map.
