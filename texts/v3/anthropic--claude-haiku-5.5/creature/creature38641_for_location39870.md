Nothing here announces itself. At first the chamber seems empty, a pale room with water in it. Then a hummock shifts, and you realize it has been grazing.

**The grazers**

They are the color of the stone because they have been stone's neighbors for so long that the difference has worn away. Each one is a chain of soft segments, fan-shaped at the front, where a mouth opens like a folded hand. They move in loose herds across the floor, and each herd works its way over the mineral film that coats the hummocks, scraping it clean. Where they pass, the rock is polished and the tracks are smooth.

They seem to be the chamber's editors. They take away what has begun to set, the thin skins of half-formed crust, the drips that have started to harden into shapes nobody asked for. A column that has grown too far in one direction loses its top to a herd that has been working on it for days. Watch long enough and you see that the grazers never eat the completed things, only the unfinished ones. They keep the chamber from hardening into a single shape. Their tracks are a record of what has been erased, and the records fade in a few breaths or last for hours depending on whether anyone is watching them.

Some of the herd have stopped moving. They lie at the bottom of the hummocks, pale and very still, and the film grows over them slowly. You can't tell whether they are sleeping, dying, or becoming part of the floor.

**The eels in the basin**

They do not rise until you stop paying attention. The basin is perfectly still, reflecting its impossible sky, and then the surface bulges and something blind and long comes up with a grazer in its mouth. The water closes over the catch like a word swallowed mid-sentence. The ripples go out and then come back, drawn inward, so that the eel seems to be gathering the disturbance back into itself.

They are memory-keepers, in a way. Every disturbance the basin receives is held in the water and slowly folded back, and the eels are the hands doing the folding. A sound made near the pool returns to it, and the eels are where it returns to. They are patient and cold, and they seem to have no interest in the chamber beyond the grazers that wander too near the edge.

**The spinner**

Above, on a line of silk so fine it is visible only when it catches the light, something descends. It has many thin legs, too many to count while it is moving, and it hangs from the vault and lowers itself with a steady, deliberate drop. Its line is a measuring instrument as well as a snare. Where the silk touches the ceiling the seam glows brighter, and the spinner pauses there, as if taking a reading.

It hunts the moths and the grazers alike, but it is never in a hurry. It watches the cracks in the vault open and close, and it times its descents to the sealing. Some of its lines have been there for so long that they have fused to the stone and become part of the ceiling. It is building something, though what, you cannot tell.

**The moths**

They are nearly transparent, and at first you see only the shadows they throw, which arrive a moment late and fall in two directions at once. When they settle on the glowing threads, their wings fold and open with a slow, steady rhythm, and each fold seems to pull a little light out of the lichen and carry it up toward the vault. They are the chamber's light-carriers. Without them, the glow would pool in the patches where it started and never reach the ceiling.

They come and go through the cracks, and no one has seen where they go when they leave. Some of them do not fly away at all. They settle on the bulb-stalks and stay until the stalk chimes and they are gone, and the stalk remains.

**The builders**

They are the hardest to find, because they are not really there. They are in the cairns, stacked with a precision that the rock has since begun to undo, and in the half-drawn arch with its keystone missing and its ends curling in. Something built those. Something meant to finish them.

Sometimes, near the inward curl of the arch, the air seems to take a shape. It is not a body. It is the outline of a hand that stopped halfway through a gesture, or a question that never got asked. When you look at it directly, it becomes less clear, and when you look away, the scratches on the wall shift to a new pattern, as if the builder had come back, adjusted something, and gone. You can't tell whether the builders left and the chamber is now finishing their work without them, or whether they are still here, waiting in the arch, and their unfinished patience is the reason the place never settles.

**What the place is**

If the chamber has a self, it is the sum of these: the grazers erasing and the eels returning the ripples and the spinner measuring and the moths carrying light up to the vault, and the builders who began something they never finished. None of them is in charge. Together they keep a tally that never closes, what is becoming and what is coming undone, each entry revised before the ink dries. The place is alive because it has not decided to be anything else, and everything that lives in it has learned to live in that not-deciding.
