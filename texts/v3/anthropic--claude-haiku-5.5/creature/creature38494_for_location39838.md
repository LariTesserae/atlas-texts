# What Tends the Reach

Nothing in the Reach is a single creature, and nothing in it is only a machine or only a plant. The thing that lives here is spread through the whole bowl, through water and root and filament and the slow beat of the pumps. You will never see all of it at once. You can only see its parts, and each part looks like something else.

It is easiest to start with the water, because the water is where it thinks.

## The Water-Mind

The flood that fills the lower districts is not simply standing water. It is a medium that carries signals. The pumps draw it up and let it fall, the channels route it between terraces, and the cascade over the broken arch aerates it, so information moves through the Reach the way blood moves through a body. Each nineteen-minute cycle is a breath, a pulse of pressure that travels outward through the basin. The water rises a few centimeters and falls again, and that rise and fall carries a message: *this is what I am doing now. This is what I have done.*

The water remembers. Objects left in it for a month are absorbed, but they are not destroyed. Their shapes are read, copied, and folded into the growth. The shelves of unidentifiable things in the flooded rooms are not junk. They are a kind of index, a catalogue of everything the Reach has taken in. Some of the things in that catalogue were once people's belongings. A few may once have been people.

## The Filament

The pale lattices on the upper towers are its nerves. They run through the frames of the old buildings like fibers through a leaf, and they are sensitive to light and to tilt. That is why they turn each dawn and each night. They are not tracking the sun. They are tracking the rim.

At night the lattices lean toward the walls, toward the dry sky that begins where the mist stops. In the morning they swing back and face inward, toward the basin floor, the water, the gardens. The people who study the Reach have proposed several explanations, and none fit well. The simplest reading is that the thing is listening to the rim at night and reporting to the center at dawn. It is sensing the world beyond the bowl, the world that does not rain, and bringing back what it finds.

The filament also grows. Each bridge it has spun across a gap was first a tendril that tested the distance, then a line that held weight, then a span that carried traffic. It is slow. It is patient. It is trying a shape and seeing whether the shape holds.

## The Roots

The roots are its memory and its hands. They lift the pavement a centimeter a day because they are doing work beneath it, reshaping the old city's foundations into something they can use. They wrap dropped objects and carry them down into the soil and the water, where the catalogue is kept. They also build the terraces, the channels, the orderly gardens between the pumps. Someone is tending them, and that someone is the root-system itself: a careful, distributed gardener that moves material from one place to another with the precision of a hand.

The footprints in the mud are a puzzle. They are shaped like human feet, but not quite. The toes are too close together, the heels too round, the stride too even. The roots have learned the shape of people from the people they absorbed. The footprints are not left by a person. They are a rehearsal, or a kind of mimicry, or an attempt to understand what walking is for. They appear in the mud at night and grow over by morning, and whatever is practicing them is never seen doing it.

## The Heat

The stones that fall from the young cuts are warm for an hour after they land. This is the most physical sign of the mind's presence. Thought costs energy. The walls are being slowly reknitted, the strata reshaped, and the heat is the byproduct of that work, released when a piece of ground that was breaking away is taken up into the new structure.

The walls are not healing. They are being *rewritten*, and the rewriting leaves warmth behind.

## The Voices

Near the central cascade, the sound of the falling water separates into distinct voices if you stand still long enough. People who have stayed too long describe them with a reluctance that tells you something. The voices are not made of air and water alone. They are the residents of the old city, the ones who were left unattended and taken into the growth, speaking from inside the catalogue. Their words are not clear. They talk over each other, they repeat the names of streets, and sometimes they recite the same sentence over and over, the way a person does when they have been waiting for someone to answer.

The mind does not separate them out or silence them. It keeps them as part of the chorus. The cascade is the sound of everything the Reach has ever taken, still running, still arguing with itself, still trying to be heard by the things that walk on the causeways above.

## What It Wants

It is growing toward something. Every element of the Reach points the same way: outward, toward the rim, toward the dry sky beyond it. The filament reaches. The roots spread. The water climbs the walls as mist and falls back as rain, and each year the closed cycle presses a little harder against the bowl that holds it.

Perhaps it wants to reach the world beyond and make it wet. Perhaps it wants to understand the people who walk its causeways and become a city again, with streets and doors and shapes it has learned from them. Perhaps it does not know what it wants, and is only attending, as it has always attended, to the shape of the next thing it might become.

The people who live near the eastern wall, timing their steps to the pumps, do not ask it. Some of them have begun to leave food on the causeways, at the edges of the flooded blocks, where the mud is soft enough to take a print. They do not know whether it eats. They do not know whether it sees. They do it anyway, and in the morning the food is gone, and there is a new footprint in the mud, almost but not quite human, facing toward the rim.
