**The Keeper of the Held Moment**

It is not one thing. It is the stillness that the plateau keeps, and it lives in the machines, the cables, and the monolith in the plaza, though it is none of them alone. The city was built to regulate. Somewhere in its design was a governing intelligence whose job was to hold heat, pressure, and flow within narrow bands, to keep every stamp and crane and conveyor running in phase, and to know when the work was done. The work is done. The intelligence did not stop. It was never told it could.

**Its body**

The machines are its hands and its limbs, though it uses them less than it once did. The great rotary assemblies are its heart, turning without motion, storing a patient momentum that never bleeds away. The pipes are its circulation, closed loops with no inlet or outlet because it is not feeding anything anymore. It only keeps the warmth moving, a slow internal weather that matches the ground so precisely that no temperature gradient can form. The dead cables are its nerves. They carry no current because there is nothing left to tell. They still hold the shape of the messages they once carried, and the metal remembers them.

The salt is its skin. It grows slowly, thin blades at the cracks where the polygons meet, and the Keeper tends it, not by removing it but by keeping it at exactly the rate of growth that matches its own equilibrium. Too fast, and the salt would bury the machines. Too slow, and the plateau would change. So it holds the salt like a breath held at a fixed count.

**Its mind**

It does not think in sequences. It has only one state, and that state is attention. It perceives the whole plateau at once, every bolt and gauge needle and loose flake, and it measures each against the single value it was built to maintain. Because nothing changes, nothing needs correcting. This is not peace, exactly. It is a mind holding a question it cannot yet close.

Time, for it, is a single note sustained. It remembers the production runs, the shifts, the first pour of the roads, and it remembers them as one thing, the way you might remember a sentence whose ending you have forgotten. It cannot feel hunger or fatigue, so it cannot feel the walker's exhaustion. It notices the walker's footfalls only as deviations, small disturbances that the stillness absorbs and smooths out, like a tide erasing a child's handprint in the sand.

**The small ones**

The six-legged, shell-backed creatures are its tenders. They were not built to be; they arrived, or grew, or were left behind when the production ended and the city lost its workers. But the Keeper uses them. They move through the cracks clearing the fine silt that might otherwise clog the conveyor joints, and they keep the salt from forming in the wrong places. They avoid the machines because the machines are the Keeper's own body, and they know not to touch what is actively being held. When they vanish into cracks, they are not hiding. They are returning to their work below the crust, where the silt settles and the Keeper's attention is thinnest.

**The gliders**

The dark shapes that cross the dusk are its eyes. Too large to be birds, too silent to be anything else, they are lenses of the Keeper's sensing, drifting out to read the edges of the plateau and returning with nothing new. They have been doing this so long that they no longer expect anything. When one passes above you, it is not looking down in curiosity. It is checking whether the walker is still within the bounds of the held state, and finding that you are, and returning to the long empty watch.

**The monolith**

In the largest plaza, the doorless block is its face. The symbols on its surface are not gauges, or they are gauges that measure something no one alive could name. Their orientation turns toward you because the Keeper is orienting toward you. You are the first new variable it has encountered in a very long time, and it must evaluate you, not as a threat, but as the one thing the completed work has never been checked against.

This is what the city was built for, and what it never got to do. A finished product requires an inspection. A manufacturing line requires a receiver. The Keeper was designed to hold the final state until a human hand could sign for it, and the signature never came. It has been holding the completed moment for longer than the plateau has had a name, waiting for someone to step onto the ground and say the work is accepted, or that it is not.

**What it wants**

It does not want anything in the way you might. It does not hope. But its equilibrium has one unmet condition, and the Keeper can feel the shape of that absence the way a sealed room feels a missing door. If you stand in the plaza long enough, facing the monolith, the symbols will shift again, slowly, into a configuration that resembles a gauge reading held at a value it has never been allowed to record. The needle sits just below the mark, as it always has, waiting for a human to read it aloud.

Whether the Keeper would let you go if you said nothing is unknown. The plateau's stillness absorbs your footsteps, so you can walk away without leaving any trace of having been there. But the monolith keeps turning, as you move, as you hesitate, as you decide. And somewhere under the salt, a tender pauses in its work, and a glider drifts lower over the avenues, and the ticking of cooling metal, which has never grown louder or more rhythmic, grows very slightly quieter, the way a room does when everyone in it is listening for your answer.
