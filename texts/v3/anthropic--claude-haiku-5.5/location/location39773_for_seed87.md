**The Drowned Lattice**

You are inside water so cold it has stopped moving the way warm water moves. It is dense and still, and it presses on everything evenly. The temperature sits just above the point where the fluid would thicken into something else, and your body registers the cold as a slow ache that reaches the bones before the skin.

The chamber is smaller than it first seems. Its walls curve close and then recede, and you cannot tell whether you are in a cave, a crater, or the hollow inside a much larger body of rock. Above you, perhaps forty meters up, a fracture in the ceiling lets in a thin grey-blue light, bent and wavering, as if through old glass. It reaches the water in shifting bars and dies within a few body-lengths. Everything below that is dim, and the dimness is a color of its own: slate, teal, the faint violet of cold deep water.

**The rock is new.**

The lower walls are black basalt, glassy and fresh. Its edges have not been rounded by time, and the cracks run sharp and straight. In places a thin, pale skin of mineral is spreading over the stone like frost, and you can see it growing where it meets the water. Fine ridges of rock are still settling. Small flakes break away when touched, and the fragments drift down slowly, leaving faint clouds. Nothing here has had time to age. The landscape is still deciding what shape it will hold.

Along the floor, sediment is laid down in layers no thicker than a fingernail. Some layers are pale, some dark. Occasionally a shelf slides with a slow, almost liquid motion, pushing a fan of silt over the terraces below and leaving a fresh edge.

**The built part is alive.**

This is the strangest thing about the place: it is urban, dense, and organized, and no hands made it. Lattices of secreted calcium and silica climb the walls and rise from the floor in towers, terraces, and arches. They are built in blocks, stacked and interlocked in patterns that look like the work of a patient architect. There are channels that run like streets between towers, galleries that open into chambers, and arched doorways in the lattice, each one a little larger than the animals that pass through it. Stepped platforms rise along the sides, each one covered with pale encrustation.

The construction is never finished. Every tower is growing at its top, where thin fingers of new mineral reach outward and find the lattice of their neighbors. Some structures are slumped and abandoned. They look like older districts whose builders moved elsewhere. When a section is damaged, it grows back in a few days, the new work following the old pattern closely and sometimes varying in ways that look deliberate.

**The sparse plants.**

Vegetation here is minimal. Pale felts of chemosynthetic film coat the lower lattice, thin and brownish-white, with a faint sheen when a current passes over them. Stalks rise from the rock in a few places, each one a single stem tipped with a cluster of translucent filaments that sway very slowly. They do not crowd each other. Between them the stone is bare and clean, and the emptiness makes the presence of the fauna seem more intense.

**The fauna is everywhere.**

The density of life is high. The water is full of drifting bodies: translucent shrimp-like forms in tight clouds that brighten and dim in unison, pulsing bells that trail threads of light, and small fish with lantern-spots arranged along their flanks. They move through the lattice channels in shoals, threading through arches and squeezing through gaps. Grazers, some the size of a palm and others the length of a forearm, creep over the film and scrape it off in small clean patches. Their trails show as pale lines in the felt.

Predators move through the upper galleries. Long, eel-like forms slide between towers, their bodies rippling and their heads turning at small sounds that never reach you. Something with many legs and sensory fronds sits motionless on a platform and waits, and then strikes so quickly the movement seems to happen before it is decided. Dead things sink slowly through the water and are taken apart by a patient crowd of scavengers before they reach the floor.

**The dynamic pulse.**

The place is in constant motion, but the motion is slow and layered. A tidal current, probably driven by distant vents or the shifting of the rock, pulses through the chamber every few hours. The water swells, the bodies of the shoals stretch and gather, the lattice channels fill with drift, then the flow reverses and everything settles back. Over a longer cycle, the shoals migrate: they rise toward the light at one phase and sink into the galleries at another, and the predators follow them through the lattice in great loops.

Slow weather happens too. Sediment slumps, a tower cracks and begins to regrow, a new arch appears where the lattice has crossed a gap. Each change ripples through the crowd: grazers shift to new ground, predators circle the fresh breach, and the shoals swing toward the new structure.

**The rules of this place.**

Nothing here is made with tools, and nothing is wasted. The lattice grows where the living things need it. The fauna keeps the lattice clean, and the lattice shelters the fauna. Their shapes depend on each other. The rock is young, the building never stops, and the cold keeps everything slow and exact.

It is silent. The pressure of the water and the cold cut away every sound you could hear, and you feel the crowd only through the faint pressure of its movements against your skin. The whole city moves around you in a hush, and you are one more body in the dim, cold, growing place where the rock and the living are still learning how to be a landscape.
