**The Sunk Works**

You stand at the lip of a bowl about a kilometer across, a wound in the ground that is still raw. The rock walls are unweathered, their fracture faces sharp enough to cut, and they step down in ledges of pale basalt and rust-colored tuff. The bowl is not old. Something opened it recently, and the sides still shed grit when the heat cracks them. At the bottom, the city.

It was built into the sink. Towers of poured grey concrete rise from the floor in rows, their upper stories cut off at the level of the rim, as though the ground rose and swallowed them to the third or fourth floor. Their windows face the sky and hold nothing but green. Pipes as thick as a man's body run between the buildings on iron trestles, many of them bent and sagging. Above the streets, cooling towers stand half-submerged, their fans stopped, their louvres choked with vine.

**Heat and water**

The air is thick enough to chew. Temperature sits high and steady, and the bowl holds it like a pot. Water is everywhere but never in open sky. The rock walls sweat. Condensation runs down the stone in threads and collects in gutters cut by the rain of the last few seasons, and the rain itself comes in short, heavy bursts that turn the streets to warm brown rivers for an hour, then drain away through old storm culverts with a sound like something swallowing. Between storms the air hangs wet and still. Your shirt is damp within a minute. Your breath fogs faintly, though it is not cold.

Steam rises from fractures in the basin floor, where the ground still releases heat from whatever opened it. The steam drifts up the walls, meets the canopy, and drips back down. The whole bowl is a slow still-life of condensation.

**Vegetation**

The plant life is packed. Nothing here has room to be alone. Broad-leaved climbers, some with leaves the size of doors, have grown over every surface, pulling down the lines of rusted wire and stretching across the streets in a continuous roof. Above that roof is a second layer of fleshy, fern-like things that grow on the pipes and balconies, and beneath everything the ground is a mat of creeping moss that swallows footsteps and holds the damp.

The growth is not random. Vines follow the old street grid, so that the green roof above you has dark corridors where the streets used to be, and bright openings where the squares were. Along the pipes, moss grows in bands exactly the width of the pipe's shadow. Something in the ecology keeps the plants where the city's lines were. Some of the trees have bark that has grown around old steel railings until the metal is hidden inside them. When you cut a branch you find wire.

**Light**

Light is dim, filtered through the canopy and the mist. The sky is a grey-green slab far above, visible only in gaps. Shafts of light fall through the openings in patches the color of weak tea. In the deepest corridors it is nearly dark, and the lamps that still hang from the towers flicker or are dead. Some of the fungal growths along the walls give off a faint blue-white glow, the only light that does not come from above.

**Machines**

The industry here is not gone; it is stalled. Large pump engines sit in their housings at the bottom of the bowl, their drive shafts furred with moss, their gauges still reading figures no one has checked in years. A conveyor runs along a trestle for a kilometer, carrying nothing, its belts rotted to ribbons, its rollers still turning very slowly as the sinking ground tilts them. Generators in concrete bays hum at a frequency so low you feel it in your teeth rather than hear it. Some of the machines are still warm.

**Sound**

The place is mostly quiet. You hear dripping, always, from a thousand points, and the soft hiss of steam. Rain brings a roar that fills the bowl and then fades. Wind does not reach the bottom. Birds are rare here, and when one calls, it is a single note that carries a long way before it stops. The few animals you see, small skittering things in the moss and a pale four-legged creature that watches from the high pipes, move silently.

**Strangeness and slowness**

The sinking is continuous. Measurements taken at the rim show the floor dropping perhaps a centimeter each year. The towers are tilting slowly into the bowl, and their upper floors are being pulled down past the rim as the basin deepens. No one has ever seen it happen. Over generations, the city will go under. Its streets will fill with silt, the vines will climb the sides of the buried towers, and the steam will keep rising through the canopy and falling back as rain. The change is patient and nearly invisible, measured only by the marks on the walls and the slow lean of the pipes.

Some of the strangeness lies here. The vines keep to the old street lines. The moss keeps to the pipe shadows. The steam finds its way up the same shafts that the elevators once used. The city, sunk and overgrown, is still running a kind of plan, faintly, under the green, the way a sleeper still follows the rhythm of a waking day.
