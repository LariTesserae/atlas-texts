**The Shelf**

It is a basin roughly the size of a city block, sunk into a plateau of fractured grey-black stone at about four thousand metres. The stone is not natural. It is slag-cast and cut in long sharp facets, each edge still crisp, as if the mountain were freshly broken. Nothing has weathered it. No dust has softened the corners, and no frost has split them further. The effect is of a range that was born an hour ago and has been held in that moment ever since.

**Terrain**

The floor is a stepped series of terraces, each about the height of a man, falling toward a central trough. The walls are ridged, serrated, and buttressed, with narrow ledges where maintenance walkways run in straight lines that ignore the contours. Some ridges are hollow shells that reveal, when a panel is open, banks of pipe and cable. Small saddles between peaks have been bridged with lattice gantries. The "pass" through the eastern wall is a sealed vault door the height of a cliff face, painted in faded chevrons.

The lowest terrace holds a set of shallow evaporation pans, filled with a crust of pale salts that cracks into hexagonal plates. These were once brine ponds. The water was removed, and what remains is a surface of white mineral that reflects the overhead lamps in a dull, flat sheen. Walking on it leaves no print. The crust is hard and dry and ringing, like ceramic.

**Vegetation**

Sparse, and tended. Along the warmer seams of the central trough, where heating conduits run beneath the floor, grow low grey shrubs with fleshy, needle-thin leaves. They are in rows. Their roots feed from perforated tubing laid in the substrate, and each plant sits in a small ring of dark grit that has been replaced by hand. A few tall filamentous growths, pale and translucent like wire-fungus, climb the gantry struts. They branch toward the light panels and have bent their growth into fixed shapes that have not changed in years. Between the plants, and in the shadow of the ridges, there is bare slag and salt. Density is low: a shrub every few metres, then nothing for a long stretch.

**Atmosphere and light**

The light is even and bright, close to daylight, but it comes from above. A continuous ceiling of panels hangs across the basin, supported by cable runs, and it throws no shadows that move. Shadows fall straight down and sharp-edged under the ridges. The air is thin and very cold. Breath clouds briefly and then clears. There is no wind, but there is constant forced circulation from ducts set in the walls, and the air carries the faint metallic taste of ozone and cold oil. Humidity is near zero. Lips crack. The tongue sticks to the roof of the mouth.

**Sound**

The sound is the defining feature. It is deafening, and it does not stop. Compressors thud in slow cycles, each one a heavy double-beat that shakes dust from the ledges. Turbines whine at a pitch just above comfort and stay there. Pumps hiss in overlapping rhythms that never quite align. Pipes knock as they expand and contract. Somewhere far off, a sequence of heavy machinery clanks and resets every forty-one seconds, a sound that has become the basin's heartbeat. There are no birds, no insects, no wind in grass. Anything quiet is swallowed. Conversation requires shouting, and even shouting is a strain. The sound is not chaotic. It is a dense, layered machine rhythm that the ear eventually stops hearing and then, after a while, cannot stop hearing again.

**Fauna**

None. No animal has ever been here, or none has survived the cold, the dryness, and the noise. The only moving things are maintenance drones: small, boxy, and silent beneath the din, sliding along the gantries on fixed tracks. They collect salt from the pans, clear the grit from the shrub rings, and return to their cradles in the walls.

**Rules of the place**

Everything is stable and nothing changes. The machines cycle, the lamps hold, the shrubs grow at the rate they have always grown and no faster. The salt does not reform because the pans are kept dry. The erosion does not progress because nothing reaches the stone. The sound has no beginning and no end. If you stand still long enough, you notice that every shadow, every plant, every crack in the mineral crust is exactly where it was when you arrived, and will be where it is when you leave. There are no seasons and no weather. Time here is a flat line, drawn by machines that have been running so long that their steadiness has become the terrain's only law.

**The strange part**

The ridges are too neat. The fracture planes of the slag run parallel across entire walls, the way a crystal would split, and the sharp edges were laid out on purpose to look geological. The mountain is a costume that was made to look newly formed. Something built this place to resemble a world that had just come into being, and then sealed it, lit it, and started the machines. What it was meant to become, nobody knows. It has been stable for so long that it has stopped being an incomplete thing and become a finished one.
