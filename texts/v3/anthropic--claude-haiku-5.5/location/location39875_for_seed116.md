**The Cistern Stair**

You are inside a single vessel, and it is almost entirely water and almost entirely made. The whole world is perhaps a few hundred meters across, enclosed by a curved ceiling of grey-green alloy so high and so dim that it reads as a sky. Under it rises a mountain that no one piled up from rock. It is a stair of reservoirs, stacked terrace on terrace, climbing toward a summit that disappears into its own cloud.

The light is ordinary daylight, arriving through a grid of cracked panes set into the ceiling. It is warm, slightly green, and filtered through a permanent haze of vapor. Nothing casts a hard shadow. Everything softens.

**Terrain and erosion.** The terraces were once sharp-edged. Poured concrete, riveted plate, and ribbed girders went up in a single program, and time has worn them down to something closer to river stone. Corners have rounded into long curves. Surfaces have gone pitted and soft, and the seams between old slabs have dissolved into shallow troughs where moss and fern root. The steepest faces, which must once have been vertical, now lean back at a slope a person could walk up with some effort. Nothing here is rock, but it has the patience of rock. The whole structure has settled into a final shape and no longer has any drama left in it.

**Water.** Water is the medium of the place. Each terrace is a reservoir, some still brimming, some drained to black mud. Sluice gates the size of houses stand half-open. Water pours down the stair in braided falls, drops from lip to lip, and fills everything below. Pools hold a green, opaque water thick with suspended algae and silt. The air is almost as wet as the pools: condensation beads on every surface, and the steady fall of droplets from the ceiling is a constant rain that never quite becomes a storm. Where a shaft of light catches the spray, you can see rainbows that hang without moving.

**Vegetation.** Lush does not begin to cover it. Vines drape the terrace walls in curtains. Broad-leafed plants the color of wet jade fill every crack and every drained basin. Thick mats of moss grow over the old machinery, and small trees have taken root in the sediment of dried reservoirs, their roots gripping the old pipes like hands. The vegetation looks deliberate in places, as if someone once planted it in rows and then left it to run wild. Some of the plants are unfamiliar. They flower in bursts that open all at once and close with a sound like paper folding.

**Fauna.** It is crowded with animals, though you may not see them at first. Amphibious things with long, banded bodies move between the pools and the mud. Birds with heavy, hooked beaks nest in ledges where machinery once housed its switchboards. Insects hum in clouds that drift up the terraces with the rising warm air. Fish, pale and blind, circle the reservoirs and turn at the surface in silver flashes. Predators are everywhere in the rhythm of the place: something with too many joints lies still on a wall for an hour, then strikes a bird in a single motion and drags it under a leaf. The herbivores graze in the moss. Nothing here is alone for long.

**Tech.** The machinery is still running, though it is old and worn. Pumps knock in their housings. Valves hiss, releasing pressure from pipes that run up the stair like veins. Conveyor belts, long since stripped of their load, still turn slowly on rusted rollers. Lights, a few of them, flicker on the underside of the ceiling and cast irregular patches on the water. Every so often a large gate shudders open and water roars down a channel to a lower basin, and the entire stair vibrates with the sound. Nothing here is built to rest. Everything is still doing the job it was made for, long after anyone remembers what the job was.

**Sound.** The place is loud, and the noise is layered and continuous. Water is always falling somewhere, from a thin trickle to a deep roar. Pumps thud at a steady, heavy beat. Birds call from the ledges in sharp, metallic cries, and insects drone in the thick air. Every few minutes the flower-bursts pop like tiny fireworks. Beneath it all you can feel a low hum that comes from the structure itself, a resonance of huge machines still running in their own slow dark.

**Dynamics.** The place never holds still. Its cycles are the rain that falls from the ceiling, the pumps that move water up and down the stair, and the predators that feed on the herbivores who feed on the plants. Reservoirs fill and empty on a timer that nobody can read. Mud flats grow green in a few days and then go dark again. The animals adjust to each change, and the change itself is the only steady thing about the place.

**Strangeness.** The oddest feature is that the stair seems to remember its purpose. At certain points, a sluice opens and water flows in exactly the pattern a manual would prescribe, and then the flow stops and the sluice closes. The animals seem to know when it will happen. Some of them wait for it on the ledges, and when the water comes, they follow it down the terraces to feed on what it carries. Nothing here was designed for them, and yet they have built a life around the machine's old rhythm.
