**The Sweating Chamber**

You are inside a pocket of rock, not a cave in any grand sense. It is a room about the size of a sleeping hall, roofed by a shelf of pale, newly fractured stone that still shows the sharp angles of its breaking. The break was recent, geologically speaking: the edges have not yet been softened by water or time. Where the ceiling meets the wall, the fracture lines are still bright and clean, like the inside of a snapped bone.

Light comes from above. A single chimney, perhaps a meter across, pierces the ceiling at an angle, and through it falls a hard white column that is almost painful in its brightness. It lands on the floor in a slanted disc and spreads across the wet stone. Everything in that disc glares. Everything outside it sits in amber half-shadow, lit by the glow of the mineral skin on the walls.

The floor is shallow water over a slick pavement of cream-colored travertine-like rock, ridged into low terraces that step down toward the room's far side. The water is warm, almost hot, and it moves. It seeps from cracks in the back wall in thin continuous threads, pools in the terraces, and spills over their lips in glassy sheets. The sound of it is the only consistent noise in the room: a soft, constant patter and trickle, a sound like rain heard from indoors. Otherwise the room is quiet. Your own breathing seems loud.

The air is thick and humid. Condensation beads on the ceiling and drips in slow, irregular plinks. Steam rises in faint wisps where the warm water meets the cooler air near the chimney, and the wisps curl upward into the column of light and vanish. The heat is steady and heavy, the kind that makes the skin feel tight.

The living things here are sparse and simple. There are no plants, no roots, no leaves. Instead, the walls and terraces are coated in mats of microbial film, layered in bands: rust-orange near the water's edge, a deep olive where the flow is slower, and a faint bruised violet in the shadowed crevices. These mats are slightly rubbery to the eye and glisten. Where the light strikes them they seem to shift color, though this may be the water moving over them. Some mats have formed low fringed ridges that wave gently in the current, the way seaweed does, though they are firmer and more mineral in texture.

Fauna is rare, and what exists is small and slow. Along the terrace edges, pale segmented creatures about the length of a fingernail graze the mats, leaving pale trails. When disturbed they pull into hard, glassy casings and wait. High on the ceiling, in a wet crease, a cluster of translucent organisms hangs like clustered beads, pulsing faintly. They do not seem to notice anything outside their own slow rhythm.

Something about the channels is odd, though not in a way that breaks the laws of the place. The threads of water run in remarkably even paths, dividing and rejoining at regular intervals. The terraces are stepped with a precision that does not match the rough fracturing of the walls. Certain ridges in the travertine seem to thicken and thin in cycles, as if the flow is being gently redirected by something beneath the surface that you cannot see or quite explain. It is not a machine, and nothing here is built. It is simply that the water seems to know where it is going.

The room is alive with process, though it is a slow kind of life. The water is always moving. The mats are always growing or sloughing. Condensation gathers, falls, and gathers again. The chimney light sweeps a little across the floor as the sun above shifts, and the disc of brightness creeps toward the far terraces by degrees. Each time it moves, the mats in its path seem to flare and then settle, as if they are drinking the light, or rather as if the light is part of the cycle they are part of.

Near the back wall, where the threads of water emerge, the rock is cracked in a fan of fine fissures, each one narrow and dark. Warm air breathes out of them in faint pulses. They look as though they could widen, though they have not yet. The room feels young and unfinished, like a place still deciding what it will become.
