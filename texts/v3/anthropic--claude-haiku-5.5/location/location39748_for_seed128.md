**The Weeping Cistern**

You are inside a vessel that was never meant to be seen from outside. It is a hollow about forty meters across, cut into the flank of a plateau so high that the air is thin and cold at night, yet the stone here holds a warmth like a sleeping animal's. The plateau itself is not rock. Its surface is a terraced skin of dull grey material, fused and seamed, with the faint ridging of something that was once extruded in layers by machines now gone quiet. Nothing here is natural substrate. Even the dust is ground from the same made stone.

The light is low and comes from above, a slanted shaft far up where the rim opens to a sky the color of pewter. Below that shaft the vessel descends in stepped galleries, each ledge wider than a road, each wall pitted with alcoves and hung with curtains of dripping filament. The dimness is not total. Patches of faint blue-green glow pulse slowly in the crevices, a bioluminescent film that spreads over the older surfaces and retreats from the new, as though following a tide no one can see.

**Water.** Everything here weeps. The walls sweat beads that gather, slide, and merge into threads that run down the terraces into channels cut to exact gradients. Some of these channels still carry water in precise lines; others have dried into pale crusted seams. The condensation is constant, a fine breath of mist that never quite becomes rain. Some beads hang in the air for a heartbeat before falling, as if the weight of the water arrives late. Pools collect in shallow basins at each level, mirror-still in the corners, churning where the threads pour in. The surfaces of the pools are alive with skaters, larvae, and the slow bodies of amphibians with pale, translucent skin.

**Sound.** This is the first thing that hits you and the last thing you forget. The vessel is a resonating chamber, and it fills everything with noise: thousands of drips striking different tones depending on the basin they fall into, a rising chorus of insects whose wings beat in a shared rhythm, the clicking and whistling of birds nesting in the wall-cavities, and beneath it all a low hum from deep in the stone, a pump or a heartbeat that you feel in your teeth. Calls bounce from wall to wall and return a fraction late, so that every sound seems to come from two places at once. Speaking here requires shouting. Silence is a memory.

**Vegetation.** Plant life is sparse and clings to the margins. Pale, fleshy lichens crust the edges of the basins. Thin reed-like stalks rise from cracks where the seams have worn open, their tips heavy with seed-pods that rattle in the air currents. A fungus grows in soft white shelves on the shaded walls, and where it grows the stone is cool and damp to the touch. Nothing grows far from water.

**Fauna.** This is the dense heart of the place. Flying things fill the middle air: moths the size of hands, dragonfly-like forms with stained-glass wings, small bats that roost in the alcoves and drop in flickering swarms at dusk. Birds nest in the vertical seams, their young peering out from gaps lined with feathers and bright fragments of old, unidentifiable material. On the ledges, slow-moving reptiles with plated backs bask in the faint warmth of the stone. Below, in the pools, a dense life of tadpole-like forms, filter-feeders, and crustaceans moves in restless clouds. Predation is constant but unhurried: a heron-like thing stalks the shallows, and a coiled, pale creature drops from the ceiling onto anything that stays still too long.

**Rules.** The vessel seems to have been built to gather water from the air, and something of that function still operates. The stone draws moisture from the sky and channels it downward; the channels still run, if erratically. Certain surfaces are warm only where a conduit once passed beneath them, so the heat map of the place is a faded diagram of its old plumbing. The filaments hanging from the upper rim sway in a breeze that cannot be felt below them. Parts of the structure repair themselves slowly: a crack sealed overnight, a broken channel bridged with fresh grey extrusion that is still soft to the touch. The place is not dying. It is in a long, unhurried equilibrium, a machine that has forgotten its purpose and become an ecosystem instead, one that drips, sings, and feeds itself in the cool dim air high above the world.
