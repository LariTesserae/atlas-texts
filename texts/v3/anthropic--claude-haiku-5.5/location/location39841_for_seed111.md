**The Throat of Seveth**

You stand in the floor of a cup-shaped basin, about two kilometers across, at an altitude where the air is thin and clean and the breath comes short. The walls rise on every side, raw and unweathered. They are not old mountains worn down by ages. They look like they came up out of the ground recently and have not yet been softened. Their faces are knife-edged, and the ridges still show the fracture planes where the rock broke open. Some towers stand as detached needles, leaning against each other like the teeth of a comb. The stone is dark gray, nearly black where wet, with pale seams of mineral that catch what little light reaches the floor.

The sky is a narrow slot, a jagged strip of pewter cloud overhead. The sun reaches the basin for perhaps an hour a day, slanting through a notch in the eastern rim, and even then it lights only the highest ledges. Most of the floor sits in a cool, wet dusk that holds through the day. Your eyes adjust slowly. Everything is blue-gray, and edges blur into the damp.

**Water**

Water is everywhere, but it arrives in small and irregular ways. It condenses on the overhangs and falls as a steady patter, so the ground beneath the cliffs is always dark and slick. Thin threads run down the rock faces and collect in cracks, where they form pockets of standing water, clear as glass and cold enough to ache the teeth. A meltwater stream crosses the center of the floor, shallow and quick, braided through boulders the size of cottages. Its sound fills the basin: a continuous hush under everything else. Where the stream slows, it spreads into peat-dark pools with mats of pale algae drifting on their surfaces. Rain comes in brief, heavy bursts without warning, then the air clears and everything drips for an hour.

**Ground and vegetation**

Little grows here, and what does grows low and spread thin. Crusts of gray-green lichen cover the rock in rings and rosettes. Between the boulders lie low cushions of moss, dense and springy, the color of wet slate. A few stiff reeds, hollow-stemmed and rattling, line the stream. Their tips are pale and bristly, and they make a dry clicking when the wind moves through them. Nothing here is tall. The only true shelter comes from the stone itself.

**Fauna**

This is where the place becomes overwhelming. Life crowds every surface that holds moisture or shelter, and the density is startling. The cliff faces are stippled with burrows and nests, and the rock appears to breathe as thousands of small bodies move in and out. Insects with translucent wings hang in swarms above the pools, forming shifting columns that drift and reform. Long-legged crawlers with carapaces banded like slate work the moss, turning it over in patches. Clusters of soft-bodied things cling to the undersides of ledges, pulsing slowly. Something like a flightless bird, with a heavy, flat head and a fringe of feathers that lie close to its body, moves along the stream in tight flocks, dipping its beaks into the shallows, each one watching the others.

Bats, or creatures that fill the bat's niche, wheel through the gloom in numbers that darken the slot of sky at dusk. They vocalize in a fine, high chatter that the cliffs send back in layers.

**Sound**

The basin is loud, and the noise has layers. At the bottom is the stream's constant hush. Above it, the dripping of a thousand thin falls ticks like rain on a roof. Then come the animals: the chittering of the cliff dwellers, the buzz of the swarms, the rattle of reeds, the periodic clack of hard shells on stone. Sounds echo off the walls and return doubled, so every call seems to come from several directions at once. At dusk the creatures synchronize. For a few minutes the whole basin takes up a single resonant trill, low and rising, and the rock seems to hum with it. Then it stops, and the silence afterward is strange.

**Strangeness and rules**

Several things here do not behave the way they should.

Shadows lag. When the rare sunlight crosses the floor, shadows of stone and creature stay behind for several seconds before they move, as though the ground is slow to take up the light.

The swarming insects never settle, even at night. Their columns circle a single point over the largest pool, and anything that falls into that pool is absorbed into the algae within a day, leaving the water a shade lighter afterward.

The pale seams in the rock are warm to the touch, though nothing else in the basin is. Animals gather along them and sleep pressed against the stone.

The cave-dwelling creatures do not come out in the open at all. Their nests in the cliffs seem to contain more eggs than the creatures could possibly keep track of, and the adults shift them constantly, as if in a slow, endless count.

Nothing built has ever been made here, yet in the shallows of the stream lie rough, hand-chipped stone blades, all oriented in the same direction, downstream, as though they were set down there long ago and the water has been slowly moving them. No one is visible. The blades are simply there, and they are cold.

**The feel of the place**

The Throat of Seveth is young and sharp and loud, full of things that crowd each other in the damp dark, but the crowding is slow. Growth and decay here happen over seasons and decades. The stone is still rising, the basin is still deepening, and the creatures are still counting their eggs. You get the impression that you have stepped into a process that began before you and will continue long after you leave, and that it has no particular interest in whether you stay.
