**The Seep Chamber Beneath the Shelf**

You arrive by a crack that is not quite a passage. The rock above is a lid of old basalt, thick enough that nothing from the surface reaches you. The air is cold, a steady cellar cold that doesn't change with the hours because there are no hours here. Your breath hangs in front of you and does not drift.

The room is small, perhaps the size of a wrecked cistern, but it is full. Every surface is furred. Pale fungal mats cover the ceiling in overlapping plates, each one the width of a palm and faintly translucent at the edges, like wet paper held up to a candle. Thin stalks rise from the floor and bend toward the walls, all leaning the same way, as if the whole room were listening to one far-off current. Between them, a dense understory of filaments grows in threads so fine they catch the eye only when moisture beads along them and each bead briefly holds a pinprick of reflected light from your lamp.

There is no light source. Your lamp is the only light in the chamber, and it seems to be drinking itself, its beam thinning out a few meters off into a grey that does not quite end. The vegetation does not need light. It feeds on the slow chemistry of the seep: mineral water rising through the basalt, carrying iron and sulfur and some dissolved sugar nobody has named. The mats pulse faintly, a rhythm slower than breathing, and when you look at one long enough you can see the edges advancing by a grain at a time.

**Water**

Water is everywhere but rarely in motion. It condenses on the ceiling and gathers in the furrows of the mats, and every few minutes a drop releases and falls with a sound you feel more than hear: a soft, close tick, then nothing. Along the far wall, a thin sheet of water slides down a smooth face of rock, so slowly it looks frozen until you watch a single drop race another for a finger's width. There is a channel under the floor, audible if you kneel and press your ear to the stone, a faint continuous murmur like a crowd very far away.

**The Built**

Something made this room, or made part of it, long before the fungus arrived. Set into the walls are small, squared niches, each lined with a glassy black material that is neither ceramic nor metal. Hairline seams run through it in patterns that repeat but never quite align. In the center of the floor is a shallow basin, cracked across one side, its rim worn smooth at four points as though something rested there repeatedly for a very long time. Water collects in the basin and does not overflow, though the crack should let it drain. You notice that the level stays exactly constant even as drips fall into it. Nearby, a short column of stacked stones leans at an angle that should have toppled decades ago, but has settled into the floor, its base grown around by fine white root-threads.

The machinery, if that is what it is, shows itself only in details. The niches hum at a pitch just below hearing, and the furred mats around them grow in lines that follow invisible conduits in the rock. When you hold a palm near one niche, the cold lessens for a moment and then returns, as if something had briefly taken a sip of heat and set it down again. You cannot tell whether the engineering is meant to move water, to keep it still, or to tend the growth. It seems to do all three without ever finishing.

**Sound and Stillness**

The room is mostly quiet. The drips are the only regular event, and between them there is a silence so deep that your own heartbeat becomes audible. Occasionally a mat shifts with a dry, papery rustle that carries across the chamber. Once, a long, low resonance rises from the basin, lasts perhaps a minute, and fades. Nothing answers it.

**Time**

Nothing here moves quickly. The stalks have grown a few centimeters since whatever last disturbed them. The seep's chemistry changes over centuries, the mats advance over decades, the basin's stillness is maintained by processes that operate on timescales you cannot easily measure. You get the sense that the room is simply continuing, as it has for a very long time, and that your presence is a minor, brief event it has absorbed without comment.

Somewhere far beneath the basalt, the murmur of the channel continues. The drop from the ceiling releases, ticks against the basin's rim, and the surface of the water, unbroken, holds steady.
