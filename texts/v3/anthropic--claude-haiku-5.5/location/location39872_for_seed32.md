**The Hollow Cistern of White Lattice**

You stand in a room no larger than a village square, though it feels smaller, because every inch of it is occupied. The floor is not floor but a stepped mass of pale mineral terraces, each one edged with ridged lips like the ribs of a shell. Columns rise from them so close together that they touch at their waists and fuse at their tops, forming a forest of hollow spines. There is nowhere to put a foot that is not already a surface, a shelf, a pillar's base, or a crowded ledge of smaller forms growing out of larger ones. The air has no gap in it. Everything is pressed against everything else.

**Material**

The whole place is made of a single substance, a gypsum-like salt that is milk-white where it is fresh and dulls to the color of old bone where it has been exposed longest. It is not carved. The surfaces show no tool marks, only the grain of slow accretion, layer over layer, like the inside of a pearl or the growth rings of a fallen tree. Some of the columns are hollow tubes, perforated along their length with rows of round apertures, and the walls have a fine lacework of ridges in which thin crystal filaments run like veins. Tap any of it with a fingernail and it rings faintly, with a dry, glassy note that changes pitch depending on how thick the wall is.

Nothing here is wet. There is no moisture in the air, no pooling, no sheen. The salt has a faint dusting of mineral bloom, like frost that never melted, and in places it has crusted into flaky plates that crack underfoot.

**Light**

The light is bright and even, without a visible source. It seems to come from the crystal itself: the walls hold a diffuse glow, as though each layer were a lamp dimmed by its own thickness, and the brightness is strong enough that shadows are thin and sharp-edged only where one lattice crosses another. There is no sun, no dusk, no change. You can look up and see a ceiling so distant and so faintly luminous it might be sky. A single shaft of a different, warmer light sometimes falls through an opening far above, but it does not move. It has always been there, pinned to the same patch of floor, and it has never crept.

**Vegetation**

The plant life is sparse and careful, like a few survivors in a crowded city. Pale, flattened lichens spread in the crevices where the terraces meet the walls, forming thin rosettes the color of old parchment. Where two columns almost touch, a fine, grey-green moss grows in the narrow seam, hair-thin and brittle, and it crumbles when touched. Near the base of the largest spine there is a single clump of stiff, needle-leaved growth, dark as wet slate, which does not seem to spread or die. Nothing here is lush. Everything is patient and minimal, taking only what the crevices offer.

**Sound**

The room is loud, though nothing in it appears to move. The primary sound is a continuous low chorus from the hollow spines. Somewhere high above, a current of air enters through a narrow fissure and passes through the perforated columns, and each tube sings a slightly different note. The result is a dense, humming cloud of tones, like a pipe organ with a thousand stops pulled at once, and it never resolves into a melody. Under it, the salt itself ticks and creaks as it settles, a dry, scattered crackle like distant gunfire that never comes closer and never ends. There are no animal calls, no insect buzz, no rustle of life. The noise is pure mineral and draft.

**Ecology and Rules**

There are no animals here, not even the smallest. Nothing feeds, nothing hunts, nothing moves through the spaces between the columns, because there are no spaces between the columns. The lichens and mosses are the only living things, and they seem to live off the mineral itself, drawing what they need from the dust and the faint breath of the air. Their presence is a quiet fact rather than a process. They do not spread, decay, or respond to anything.

The strangest rule of this place is its stillness. You can wait for hours and nothing will change. The light stays the same. The sound stays the same. The crystal does not grow or erode. The shaft of warm light does not drift. It is as though the place was finished at some point and then set aside, held in a perfect balance where no gradient remains to drive any change. Visitors have been known to lose track of how long they have stood there, not from fatigue but because time seems to have no grip on the room. There is no sense of before or after, only now, and now, and now.

**Atmosphere**

The feeling of the place is one of dense, crowded silence broken only by its own endless singing. It is cool, dry, and perfectly calm at the surface, but the chorus of the spines and the ticking of the salt give it a constant, pressing presence. It feels less like a cavern and more like a mechanism that has stopped mid-cycle, still humming, still poised, never quite finished and never quite falling apart. You are not lost here. You are simply held, among a great many forms that have been waiting, without any sense of what for, to be noticed.
