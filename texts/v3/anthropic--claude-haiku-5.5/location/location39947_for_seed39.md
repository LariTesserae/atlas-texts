**The Stepped Plateau of Arrin Tahl**

You stand on a plateau that has only recently finished rising. The stone under your boots is pale, fractured, and unweathered, with sharp edges that have not yet been rounded by time. Along the cracks, thin rust-colored seams show where iron-bearing water has seeped through the rock and dried. Nothing here is old. The land is still arguing with itself about what shape it will take.

**Terrain and erosion**

The plateau is a broad, tilted shelf at roughly the height where the air starts to thin and every breath comes slower. Its surface is a mosaic of raw bedrock slabs, loose scree fans, and shallow pans where meltwater collects and freezes into dull plates. Young channels are cutting into the shelf. Some are only a finger's width deep, scoured through the stone in a few seasons. Others are ankle-deep gullies with sharp, undercut walls that drop away as the water works at their bases. Fresh landslides lie where the lips of the gullies have collapsed, their debris still stacked in angular heaps, not yet softened by lichen. The valleys ahead have barely begun to form. In some places the land has only a suggestion of a valley: a faint trough where the water has decided to run.

**Water**

Water is everywhere and nowhere settled. Fog rolls in from the lower basins and condenses on every surface, so the rocks weep constantly. Droplets gather on the edges of stone, run in thin beaded lines, and drip into hollows that have become tiny tarns. The ground is spongy in low spots, a marsh-edge of dark mud and blue-green meltwater, skinned with ice at dawn and open by midday. After each rain the whole plateau steams faintly as the sun works on the wet stone. The air always feels saturated, and your clothes stay cold and damp no matter how you move.

**Atmosphere and light**

The light is dim and diffuse, filtered through a thick, low overcast that rarely breaks. The sun is a pale disc that never quite clears the cloud, so shadows are soft and the color of everything is muted: grays, slate blues, the dull ochre of exposed iron, lichen gone the color of old bruises. Near dusk the cloud takes on a faint orange cast from something burning far to the east. The air carries a flat metallic taste, and at certain times a fine grit settles on the snow, dark and glittering, which the locals in no settlement would ever call dust because there is no settlement here.

**Industry beyond the horizon**

Nothing built stands on the plateau, but the effects of distant machinery reach it. To the east, across a wide basin that drops away into haze, a column of industrial smoke rises and bends slowly on the high winds. At night the smoke's underside glows a dull amber. Its source is never visible, only its breath. Sometimes the wind brings a low, rhythmic pulse through the rock: the thudding of heavy presses, or compressors cycling, felt more than heard. Sometimes a sharper, electrical whine rises for a few seconds and fades. Static builds in the air before storms, making hair lift and small metal objects tick. The ground hums faintly where the grid's current runs somewhere below the crust.

**Sound**

Most of the time the plateau is quiet, the fog absorbing sound the way wool does. Water drips and trickles. Loose stones tick as they settle in the cold. Wind moves across the bare rock with a thin, whistling edge. The industrial pulse arrives unevenly, so the quiet is always broken by a faint, distant thudding that you feel in your sternum before you hear it. When the fog thins, birdcalls carry from the ledges.

**Fauna**

Animal life is abundant but spread thin. Each creature keeps its own territory, so you rarely see many in one place, but you see many kinds across the plateau. Ledge-nesting birds with dark wings and pale, reflective breasts crowd the cliffs where the shelf meets the drop. They wheel out into the fog and return with small, pale prey. Ground-dwelling burrowers with thick, heavy fur push up mounds of gritty soil near the tarns, and their tunnels collapse in the thaw. Rock-grazers, low and broad, scrape lichen from the stone, moving in slow, spaced lines. Lean, pale predators hunt along the gully edges, ambushing the grazers at the water's edge. The predator-prey pattern repeats across the whole shelf: birds feed on insects that hatch in the tarns, burrowers feed on the roots of the sparse growth, and the predators follow whichever population is ascending. Carcasses are rare because so much gets eaten. Bones that do survive are picked clean, bleached, and slowly crushed into the grit.

**Vegetation**

Plant life is sparse and clings to the cracks and edges. Low, hard cushions of moss grow in the lee of boulders. Gray-green lichen spreads over the stones, slowly, as though it has all the time in the world. Scattered tufts of a wiry grass fill the gullies, pale at the tips and dark at the roots. Nothing grows in the open stone, which is still too new and too unstable for roots to take hold.

**Rhythm and change**

The plateau is always in motion. Fog arrives in the early morning and burns off or thickens by afternoon. Rain or sleet can fall without warning, and each downpour cuts the gullies a little deeper, shifts the scree, and sends a fresh sheet of water sliding over the bedrock. Ice forms and breaks each night. Animals move with the rhythms of feeding and hunting. The smoke on the horizon shifts as the wind turns, and the pulse of distant machinery swells and fades in a cycle no one on the plateau controls. The land is still forming, and the processes that shape it, weather, water, life, and whatever industry hums in the basin below, all run at once, each reshaping the others.

If you walked here, you would notice how little of the plateau is finished. Every stone is raw, every channel is still lengthening, and every creature is adapting to ground that has only just decided to exist.
