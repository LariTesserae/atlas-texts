**The Sunken Mat**

You come down into a basin so shallow it hardly deserves the word. The land has been worn so long that no slope remains that could carry water anywhere, and the water here simply stays. A pale ridge, low and rounded like the back of a sleeping animal, rims the far edge. Beyond it, you can't see anything, only hear it.

**Ground**

The ground is not ground. Everything you might call soil, rock, or bedrock lies under a continuous living skin of moss, liverwort, and a grey-green felted lichen that has grown over every surface for longer than anyone could count. Where the skin lifts, you can see that it is several hands thick in places, and that it is laced through with black, cord-like rhizomes. Underneath is a dark, spongy peat. The mat gives under your boot with a wet, springing resistance, and every step leaves a slowly refilling print. Small pools sit in the low dimples of the mat, their surfaces skinned with pollen and fine green dust. Nothing here has been hard for a long time.

**Vegetation and air**

The plants are everywhere and yet the space they occupy is strangely thin. The mat is total, a blanket of life across every horizontal surface, but upward the world empties out. A few tall stems rise from the mat: fern-like fronds the color of old brass, slender reed-horsetails jointed like bamboo, and a scattering of gnarled, lichen-cloaked trunks that look more like antlers than trees. Between them are wide columns of nothing, air so still and cool that the mist hangs in long unmoving sheets. You can walk for minutes without brushing a leaf. The place feels like a room whose furniture was placed a very long time ago and never moved.

**Atmosphere and temperature**

The air is cool and close with a faint sweetness, like decaying fruit left in a cellar. Condensation gathers on everything: on the fronds, on your sleeves, on the gleaming skin of the lichen. Every few minutes a fine drizzle drifts through without seeming to fall, and then stops, leaving the leaves beaded and the mat darker. Light is ordinary and diffuse, coming through a thin grey overcast that never breaks and never thickens, so that the shadows are soft and the color of everything is slightly washed.

**Sound**

This is the loudest place you have ever stood, though nothing is shouting. Insects fill the mat in a dense, layered chorus of clicks, hums, and wet rasping. Frogs or something frog-like call from the pools in deep, resonant tones that carry across the basin. Water drips constantly from the canopy of stems in a thousand overlapping tick-tock rhythms. Birds, if they are birds, call in bright, fragmented phrases from somewhere in the mist. And beneath it all, from beyond the ridge, comes a steady mechanical drone: a low, unbroken throb, the sound of engines or machinery running somewhere far off, too distant to see, too constant to stop hearing. It never rises or falls. It is simply the background of the world.

**Fauna**

Life is abundant at the surface and in the mat's depths. Beetles with segmented, mineral-looking shells crawl over the lichen, leaving trails of pale residue. Thin-bodied, many-legged things move through the fronds. Larger creatures, slow and sheathed in the same grey felt as the ground, graze along the pool edges, nosing the surface film. Nothing here seems to hunt quickly. Movement is patient, almost liturgical, as if every creature knows the arrangement is fixed.

**Built and made**

There is nothing built here. No road, no fence, no marker, no shaped stone. Yet the machine-drone from over the ridge suggests something is working out there, and occasionally, when the wind shifts, you catch a faint smell of oil or hot metal, quickly swallowed by the sweet rot of the mat. Whatever industry exists is elsewhere, and this basin has been left alone by it, as by everything.

**Rules of the place**

The basin does not change. The mist comes and goes in the same pattern, the drizzle falls at the same intervals, the pools hold the same level. Plants do not seem to grow or die so much as persist. Fallen fronds dissolve into the mat without leaving a visible trace. If you return after a year, you will find the same pools, the same drip-rhythms, the same stems at roughly the same angles. The ancient erosion has taken away all the features that would have made the place different from itself, and what remains is a perfect, quiet equilibrium: a flat wet room under a grey sky, full of noise and nothing else.
