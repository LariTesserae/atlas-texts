# The High Terraces of Vessan Mar

The land rises in stepped shelves, each one a long level of pale, weeping stone, and the whole arrangement looks built. Nothing here was quarried or laid, though. The plants made it.

## Substrate

The bedrock is young. It is a fresh, vesicular stone, still spongy and black at the core, with glassy sheens where the surface has only recently cooled. Wind and rain have not yet had long enough to carve it, so the shelves have crisp edges and the cracks are sharp. Where the rock has begun to weather, it crumbles into a grey grit that the plants immediately seize.

Over that raw foundation the vegetation has built everything else. Thick, fibrous stems, some as wide as a cart wheel, rise in close-set columns and lean together to form arches, walls, and roofs. Their bark is banded in ochre and rust and has the grain of woven cane. Between the columns, lattices of climbing vines run from pillar to pillar and thicken into beams. The paths between the columns are floored with a dense mat of moss and low fungal shelf, springy underfoot and faintly warm.

Everything has the look of deliberate work: colonnades, galleries, stepped terraces, vaulted nave-like hollows where a crown of leaves has closed overhead. None of it was built by a hand, and none of it is ever finished. Each form is in place, and it is also still plainly in the middle of becoming something.

## Water

Water is everywhere but never pools. The high air is thick with cloud that does not quite rain. Droplets gather on every surface, beading on the stone, running down the columns in fine threads, and collecting in shallow cups where the stems have knotted into bowls. Rock faces weep continuously, leaving dark streaks and mineral crusts that glitter in the grey light. Some of these crusts are being laid down in thin, banded travertine, pale ribbons that slowly build small terraces lip by lip along the edges of the shelves.

The ground is always soft. Step off a moss mat and your boot sinks into cool, dark mud that smells of iron and sweet rot. Small channels run under the vegetation, gurgling quietly, and they feed a few still, ochre-colored tarns at the lower edges of the terraces. The water in these is clear as glass but stained red at the bottom.

## Air and Temperature

The climate is warm, but at this altitude the warmth is damp and close, like the air inside a greenhouse that has been left to breathe too long. The light is soft and even, diffused through the high cloud, with no harsh shadows. Colors look slightly saturated, as though the air carries a fine pigment.

## Sound

The landscape is loud, but almost all of the noise is continuous and natural. Water is the main source: a low, unbroken murmur of seepage, dripping from leaf tips into hollow stems, which then resonate like pipes. Wind moves through the open colonnades and produces a deep, hollow moan, and the stems knock together with a wooden clatter when gusts are strong. In the closed vaults the sound pools and echoes.

Occasionally a pressure-pop comes from somewhere within the vegetation, a sealed seed pod or bladder bursting, followed by a rustling cascade.

## Fauna

Animal life is scarce. A few species persist here: small, dark, slow insects that cling to the undersides of leaves, and an occasional pale bird that calls once from high in the canopy and then goes silent for long stretches. Their absence is noticeable. The silence between their calls is filled with the constant sound of water and wind, which makes the place feel even more self-contained.

## Rules of the Place

The strangeness here lies in how little changes. The terraces do not grow taller, the travertine ribbons do not visibly thicken, and the columns do not lean any further. The water keeps the same rhythm, and the mist comes and goes in the same patterns. Everything is in a state of suspended construction, like a city whose builders paused decades ago and never resumed, yet whose walls still stand and whose channels still run.

Nothing here is dying, and nothing is finishing. It is a settled equilibrium that seems to be waiting, though for what, no one can say.
