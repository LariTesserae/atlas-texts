**The Hollow Under the Plateau**

You are inside a pocket of the world, a hollow in a high, raw plateau. The ceiling hangs low, perhaps forty meters up, and the walls curve in on themselves so that no horizon is visible. The place feels closed, like the inside of a kiln or a cupped hand. The air is thick with heat, and the thin atmosphere at this altitude makes every breath feel shallow.

**The ground**

Nothing here has weathered yet. The floor is fresh basaltic rock, still fracturing as it cools, with edges so sharp they look wet. Plates have slipped and stacked into terraces. Between them lie drifts of black glassy scree that ring faintly when stepped on. Some stones are still warm to the touch, and a fine ash sifts down from the seams, settling into grey crusts that crack in the heat and curl up at the edges like old paint.

There is no soil. No silt, no humus, no clay. Dust and fragments accumulate, but nothing binds them. Where a vent exhales, the ground is cemented white with sulfur and salt, crystals grown in fans and needles that break under weight with a sound like dry sugar.

**Heat and light**

The temperature is high and constant, and it does not rest. Heat rises in shimmering columns from the fissures, and the rock radiates it back, so the air trembles everywhere. The light is dim, close to darkness. What little there is comes from the rock itself: a dull cherry glow in the deeper cracks, orange at the edges of the vents, and occasional pale flares where gas ignites in a split and dies again. Shadows are deep and seem to move when the glow shifts. You see mostly by the heat-glow and by the sparks of fauna that flash and fade in the dark.

**Sound**

The loudest thing here is the machinery of living things. The place is never silent. There is a low, continuous roar from the deep vents, a sound like a furnace breathing. Over it comes a dense, rhythmic percussion: hard shells striking rock, many at once, in slow clusters that build and fall away. Some creatures grind. A rasping, sawing sound comes from the walls, where armored bodies drag across stone. Others click in patterns that sound almost mechanical, ratchets and escapements repeating for hours. Gas hisses, scraping and clanking echo from the vaults, and the whole hollow amplifies it all. It is loud enough that you feel it in your teeth.

**Fauna**

The creatures are abundant, crowded almost shoulder to shoulder. Nothing here is empty. They cover the walls in layers, clinging to fissures and ledges, and they pour across the floor in slow, dense currents. Many are plated and segmented, built like crushers, presses, and bellows. Some carry ridged, toothed limbs that work like gears. Others hold their bodies over the vents and pump heat or gas through their own chambers, exhaling in regular puffs that match the surrounding roar. Some seem to be the industry of this place. They break rock, sort fragments, lay down crusts, and feed on the minerals they release. Their bodies are the machines here, and they do not need buildings. The hollow is their factory, and its work is constant.

You cannot easily walk without touching them, and you learn to step carefully. Their movement is slow and deliberate, but there are so many that the floor seems to shift under your feet.

**Vegetation**

There is none. No roots, no stems, no leaves, no spores, no moss on the stone. Life here is entirely animal and mineral. Any growth you think you see is crust, a crystalline mat or a sulfur bloom, and it does not spread.

**Constructions**

Nothing here was made. No walls, roads, or tools. Everything is either bedrock, vent, or creature. Even the grinding and pressing has no maker apart from the animals themselves.

**Dynamics**

Change is slow. The plates shift over decades, and a crack may widen by a finger's width in a lifetime. Crusts thicken over centuries. The creatures seem to follow long cycles: they gather at the vents, work through their rhythms, then disperse and return, with the whole population swelling and thinning over seasons you cannot quite perceive. Nothing here happens quickly, but everything here is always happening.

**Rules of the place**

Water is absent. Chemistry runs on heat, gas, and sulfur. The physics is ordinary, but the scale and density make it feel unfamiliar. The hollow appears to be in the early stages of becoming something, a place still forming, its raw rock still hot from being made. It is crowded and loud and bright only in fits, and it feels like a workshop in the middle of its first shift, with nothing yet finished and no end in sight.
