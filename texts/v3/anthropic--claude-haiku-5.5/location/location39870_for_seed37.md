**The Undecided Hollow**

You are beneath a plain that doesn't know it's a plain yet. The ground above is only a rumor here. What holds you is a chamber still in the middle of becoming, a cavity the rock has not finished deciding to be. Its walls are raw and unfinished, sheets of pale mineral that look soft, like cooling wax, and in places they run in slow ripples as if the stone were still settling. Nothing is quite sealed. Gaps open and close in the ceiling, and a seam of cracked, glassy material runs across the vault like a healing scar.

**Terrain and materials**

The floor is a shelf of loose grey grit over bedrock, sloping gently toward a basin in the middle. The basin is not a pool so much as a lens of water held in a shallow bowl of crusted mineral, some of it hard and white, some of it translucent as old fingernail. Around it the floor rises in low hummocks, each one a half-formed column, some pulled up from below and some dripping down from above, meeting nowhere yet. Some are pillar-thick; others are only knuckles of stone. Shards of older formations lie among them, broken and rounded, as if the chamber has been rebuilding itself for a long time and keeps discarding its attempts.

The air is cool, around the temperature of a cellar in early spring, and it moves in slow exhalations from cracks you can't see. Every surface is faintly wet. Droplets gather on the ceiling, swell, and fall with a small, deliberate sound, one every few seconds, never quite in rhythm.

**Light**

The light is bright, surprisingly so, and it has no source you can point to. It comes from everywhere at once and nowhere in particular, the way light sometimes fills a room with frosted windows. Shadows are present but confused. They lean away from objects, or stretch toward the vault, or fall in two directions at once. Some seem to arrive a moment after the thing that casts them moves. Looking directly at the seam in the ceiling makes it brighter, as if it were watching you back.

**Water and moisture**

Water here is a conversation between states. Condensation forms on the walls as thin skins that slide down in beaded threads, pausing mid-run and sometimes reversing briefly before continuing. The basin holds still water that reflects a sky that is not overhead: a pale, moving cloudscape that doesn't match the vault. When something disturbs the surface, the ripples spread and then, after a moment, begin to gather back toward their source, so the pool seems to remember every disturbance and undo it slowly.

**Vegetation**

Plant life is sparse but persistent. Pale threads of something between lichen and fungus creep across the walls in patches the size of hands, taking the bright light and holding it as a faint glow. Upright stalks rise from cracks in the floor, thin as grass and tipped with translucent bulbs that chime softly when the air shifts. There are no leaves. Everything that grows does so on mineral and moisture alone, and it grows only in the places where the stone is still soft enough to accept it.

**Fauna**

The chamber is crowded with animals, though you may not notice them at first because they are small and pale and the same colour as the stone. Segmented grazers with soft, fan-shaped mouths move across the floor in loose herds, scraping the mineral film that coats the hummocks and leaving behind clean, polished tracks. Above them, moths with almost transparent wings drift in the air and settle on the glowing threads, feeding with a slow, rhythmic fold of their wings.

Predators are part of the same ecology. Blind, eel-like things lie coiled in the basin and rise without warning, latching onto grazers, then sinking back into the water with their catch, the surface closing over them like a swallowed word. Something with many thin legs hunts along the ceiling, descending on a silk line that catches the light as it drops. Predator and prey move at the same pace, a steady cycle of feeding and fleeing, and the chamber carries the rhythm of it everywhere.

**Built marks**

There are signs that someone has been here, or that something built the way a nest is built. Cairns of flat stones stand near the edges of the basin, each stacked with precision, but the stones are fused now at their contact points, as though the stacking happened long ago and the rock has been slowly merging them. A low arch of pale material leans over one passage, half-formed, its keystone missing and its ends curling inward like a half-drawn circle. Scratches in the wall form patterns that repeat and then change, as if the marks are being rewritten by water or by time. None of it is finished. It looks abandoned in the middle of construction, or constructed by something that never intended to finish.

**Sound**

Sound is quiet and sparse. Water dripping. The chime of the bulb-stalks when the air moves. Small scrapings where the grazers feed, and the soft wet sound of an eel-thing surfacing. Every sound seems to arrive slightly after its source moves, and occasionally an echo comes back before the original noise has finished, so that you hear your footstep a fraction of a second before you take the step.

**Rules of the place**

Things here are unfinished, and the chamber behaves as if it is still deciding what is allowed. Water flows toward the basin and sometimes away from it. Columns grow when no one is watching and recede when they are observed. Time does not pass evenly: the grazers' tracks fade in a few breaths or persist for what feels like hours, depending on whether anyone looks at them. The rules of cause and effect are soft. Disturbance spreads outward and then returns, cracks seal and reopen, and the built marks change in small increments that you cannot catch happening.

The chamber is alive, but its life is provisional. Everything in it is partly born and partly undone, and the place keeps its own quiet tally of what is becoming and what is unbecoming, never quite settling on either.
