# The Unfinished Basin

The first thing you notice is the heat, and the second is that the heat has weight. The air is thick and warm as breath, and it presses wet against the skin. Everything here is dripping, or about to drip, or has just stopped. Nothing is dry, and nothing has been dry for as long as anyone could say, because there is no long enough to say it in.

The light is dim, as if the sun is behind a sheet of wet muslin. A pale disk hangs overhead, bright enough to be seen but not to cast shadows. Everything sits in a soft, even gray-gold, with no edges sharp enough to cut. Colors are present but muted: olive, bruise-purple, the sallow green of wet paper, and here and there a gleam of mineral white where the ground is still forming.

## The Ground

This is a basin that has not finished being a basin. Low and shallow, it spreads for miles in every direction, and it is raw. Sediment is still settling. Mud lies in soft grey sheets that tremble when you step on them and hold your footprint with a fine sheen of water that fills it slowly, from below. Where the mud has begun to harden, it forms a crust like cooling bread, cracked into plates that curl at the edges.

Rock is coming into being here, and you can watch the process in its earliest stages. Warm, mineral-heavy water seeps from fissures in the ground and spreads in thin films across the flats. As it evaporates, it leaves behind calcium and iron in soft, lacy deposits. The deposits stack and build, and they have been doing so for a long time, yet they have not grown any taller. The rims of the forming ridges are sharp and new, and they are still soft enough to scratch with a fingernail. The ridges look like frozen ripples, or like the folds of a cloth that was lifted and set down. Their surfaces are pocked with small craters where gas bubbles rose, burst, and left their rings behind, and each crater is perfectly equal to the others, a pattern that repeats across the whole basin.

There are no mountains, no cliffs, no scarps. Nothing in the landscape is steep enough to fall. Everything is a gentle slope or a flat, and the water finds its way across all of it in shallow, glittering sheets.

## The Water

Water is everywhere, at most a hand's depth. It is warm as bathwater, faintly sweet and sulfurous, and it has a slow, oily shimmer on its surface where films of organic matter float and break apart. Here and there, pools open out in clear turquoise, and you can see the mineral crust glowing pale beneath them. The pools are where the freshest growth is, the thin lacy rims creeping outward from the edges at a pace too slow for the eye to catch.

The air is always condensing. Mist rises from every surface at dawn and dusk, but there is no dawn or dusk in the sense that marks the passage of hours. The mist simply thickens and thins in a steady rhythm, and it hangs in the low places like a second layer of water. Drops collect on every leaf and every stone and slide downward in small glittering runs. You are never fully dry, and after a while you forget what dry would feel like.

## The Growth

The vegetation is lush, almost overwhelming, but it is not tall. It is dense, and it spreads. Reeds grow in tight, bristling stands, and their stems are thick and fibrous, filled with a pale milky sap that beads at the cut edge. Broad, flat-leafed plants with waxy surfaces cover the wetter ground in mats so tight that you cannot see the mud beneath them. Their leaves are large enough to shelter a person from rain, and in fact they catch the rain perfectly, funneling it toward a central pool at their base. Some of these pools are small enough to hold a single frog; others are deep enough to hold a whole ecosystem.

Among the reeds and mats, there are fleshy, bulbous growths that look like fungus and also like coral. They are pale pink and cream and bruise-violet, and they are slick to the touch. They exhale a faint, sweet, rotten smell when pressed. They have grown in these exact shapes for as long as the basin has existed, and their branching patterns are symmetrical in a way that seems deliberate.

Where the mineral deposits are fresh, the growth is thinnest. Nothing has managed to take hold on the newest rock, and it stays bare and white, a clean rim of new stone that the green presses against and never quite crosses.

## The Fauna

The fauna is overwhelming. Life here is not scattered. It is layered and crowded, with many presences in every cubic yard of air and water. You cannot take a step without disturbing something. The reeds shiver with the movement of small bodies. The mats hum. The surface of every pool is broken by rising shapes and the rings of things that have just gone under.

Insects fill the air in a constant drifting haze. Some are the size of a fingernail and iridescent, with wings that throw off sparks of blue and green in the dim light. Others are larger and slower, with bodies like folded paper and wings that make a dry, rustling sound. They gather in columns above the water, rising and falling in unison, and the columns are so dense that when you walk through one, the air goes thick and buzzing and you taste it on your tongue.

Amphibians cover every surface that is not water or plant. Frogs of every size press themselves into the mud and mats, and they are pale, speckled, and glossy. Some have throat sacs that swell to the size of a fist, and when they call, the sound is enormous, a single bass note that carries across the whole basin and then stops abruptly, as if a switch had been thrown. Then the chorus rises from another direction and the cycle begins again, so that the basin is never silent.

Birds are in the canopy of the broad leaves and the tops of the reeds: long-legged, slow-moving, with plumage in dull browns and slate and sudden flashes of red. They wade through the shallow pools with deliberate care, lifting each foot slowly and setting it down without a splash. They rarely fly. When they do, a dozen take off at once, and the air fills with the sound of wings like heavy fabric being shaken.

Beneath the surface, the water is crowded with life you can only infer. Shapes slide past the edges of pools, sinuous and fast. Things surface, breathe, and vanish. The clear turquoise pools reveal, at certain angles, a tangle of pale bodies in the mineral crust below, moving slowly, feeding on the films and the deposits. They seem to be building as well as eating: their movements leave trails of fresh white lacework that join the existing ridges.

## The Sound

The sound is loud, and it has no gaps. There is the constant hum of insects, the rising and falling of frog calls, the dry rattle of wings, the wet slap of things moving through mud. Water runs in a thousand tiny channels, gurgling and trickling, and bubbles break on the surface with small crisp pops. Over all of it, from far away, there is a deeper sound, a low resonance that might be wind through reeds or might be the basin itself, the slow grinding of mineral forming and dissolving.

It is not unpleasant, exactly. It is simply total, the kind of noise that becomes the air you breathe, and after a while you stop hearing it as separate sounds and hear only the single, rolling texture of the whole.

## The Strangeness

The strange thing about this place is that it does not change. The rock does not grow taller. The ridges stay the same height. The bubble craters are identical from one end of the basin to the other, and they were identical yesterday and will be identical tomorrow. The frogs call on the same cycle, the insect columns rise and fall in the same pattern, the mist thickens and thins without ever gathering into rain or clearing into sun. The pale sun hangs at the same angle.

The basin is building itself, and yet it has been building for so long that its building has become a kind of stillness. Every process here is balanced against an opposite: deposition against dissolution, growth against decay, birth against death, so that nothing accumulates and nothing disappears. The place is always becoming, and it has never become anything.

If you stand still long enough, you can feel this. The heat presses, the mist drifts, the frogs call, and the rock beneath your feet is soft and new and somehow already old. The whole basin seems to be holding its breath, forever on the edge of finishing and never finishing, a landscape suspended in the moment just before it becomes itself.
