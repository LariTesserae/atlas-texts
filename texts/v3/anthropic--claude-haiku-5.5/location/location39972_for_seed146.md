# The Sunken Reach

You stand at the bottom of a basin that is not a basin. The floor sinks away from you in long, stepped terraces, and the walls rise on every side into a dark that has no visible top. Some distant part of that dark holds a faint, uneven glow, like frost on a window seen from the outside. It is the only light. Nothing here burns, and nothing here grows.

**Terrain**

The ground is young rock, still sharp. Fractures run through the terraces in straight lines and clean angles, the way stone breaks when the pressure that made it is recent. The faces of the breaks are black and glassy and cut the skin like paper. Where a shelf has collapsed, the rubble is still angular, with no rounded river-stones and no sand, only shards that clatter and then lie still. Nothing has had time to wear them down.

Between the terraces are salt-crusted pans, pale grey and cracked into hexagons. They are dry. Their crust is hygroscopic and pulls moisture out of the air, and in some hollows it has formed brittle white fingers and blooms, frozen mid-growth, the way a crystal stops when its solution is removed. Every bloom is in the same position it was in when you first looked at it. None of them grow, and none of them dissolve.

**Weather**

The air is warm, close, and heavy, and it moves. Thermal columns rise along the walls where the rock holds heat, carrying fine salt dust upward into slow spirals. At the top of the nearest column the dust condenses into a fog that hangs in layered sheets and does not drift. Mist sits on the ledges and beads on the edges of the glass, forming drops that gather, swell, and hang. Then the drops stop growing, because the floor is porous and drinks them away before they can fall. The wet is always there, but it never collects, never pools, never makes a stream. It is a weather that goes through its motions endlessly and comes back to the same place, like a clock whose hands turn without ever counting a new hour.

Lightning does not occur here, and rain does not fall. What falls is a dry and gentle sift of salt grains from the high ledges, which pattern the ground in fine concentric rings. The rings have been there as long as the reach has existed.

**Fauna**

The place is crowded. The fauna are dense and mostly small, and they live in the rock itself. Their burrows and cells are cut into every ledge and wall, so the cliff faces look like honeycomb or the layered shell of some vast colonial organism. Dozens of openings sit within a hand's width of each other, and their occupants watch you from them with pale, faceted eyes.

The creatures are segmented and chitinous, pale as bone or dark as wet slate, with many legs folded tight against the body. Some crawl across the terraces in loose herds, grazing the pale mineral films that coat the stone. Others hang from the undersides of ledges, feeding on the suspended salt-fog. A few species are larger and move slowly among the pans, dragging broad flat bodies that leave long grooves in the salt. Nothing here has a plant to feed on, so everything eats minerals, films, dust, or one another. The food chains are short and dense and tightly woven, and they hold still.

**Built Things**

Someone was here, and left faint marks. Small cairns of flaked glass stand on the terraces, stacked in careful spirals about a forearm high. Their builders are gone, or perhaps they never existed in any form you would recognize. Some of the cairns have fallen, but the fallen stones lie in patterns that suggest the work was intentional, not accidental.

There are low shelters made of slabs leaned against one another, roofed with flat plates and open on the windward side. Inside, the floors are swept clean of salt, and you can see the grooves of scraping tools in the dust around the entrances. The tools themselves are rare: crude hand-axes and scrapers chipped from the same black glass as the terraces, a few with handles of bound bone or fused chitin. One of them has been used so often that its edge has rounded, the only object you have found here that shows any wear.

On a wall near one shelter, someone has scratched a long series of marks, tallies or a notation of some kind, in rows that run the length of the stone. The marks are old enough that their edges have been softened, but not so old that the lines have filled with salt. You cannot tell whether they count something or record something.

**Atmosphere and Sound**

The reach is mostly quiet. The fauna rustle and click, the glass ticks as it cools, and the mist makes a faint hush when it moves along a ledge. Occasionally a deep, slow sound rises from somewhere below, a single resonant tone held for several seconds and then gone, which may be the breathing of some large creature or the settling of the rock. Sounds carry strangely. A whisper from across a terrace can seem to come from directly beside you, and a footfall can echo for a long time without ever really fading.

The light is poor. It comes from the glowing patches on the high walls, which pulse very faintly, but the pulse never quite reaches the floor. The shadows are deep and sharp, and they hold their shapes. A shadow cast by a cairn in the first hour remains in the same position every hour afterward, because nothing moves the sun or the glow that casts it.

**Rules of the Place**

Everything here is in equilibrium. The weather cycles without changing. The fauna feed and rest and feed again, and their populations stay steady. The salt blooms hold their shapes. The cairns and shelters remain as they were left, slowly softened but never worn away. Time passes, but it passes without consequence, the way a held breath passes through lungs that never empty. Whatever brought the reach to this state, it has settled into a configuration that does not drift, and it will be the same when you return, and the time after that.

You can leave the reach by climbing the terraces, though the ascent is long and the glass edges cut. Some of the fauna will follow you to the lower ledges and then stop, as if they have reached the boundary of the place they belong to. Above you, the glowing patches recede into the dark. Behind you, the mist keeps rising and condensing and falling back, and the salt rings settle into the same pattern they have always made.
