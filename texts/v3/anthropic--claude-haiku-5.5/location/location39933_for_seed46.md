**The Hollow Above the Unfinished Shelf**

You are in a pocket about the size of a large room, sunk into a high plateau. The air is thin and hot, like the air over a stone that has been in the sun all day, though the sun here is ordinary and white and sits at a plain, believable angle. Nothing in the light is strange. What is strange is what it falls on.

**Ground and stone.** The floor is raw rock that has not finished deciding what it is. Some of it is still soft enough to take a fingerprint and harden around it. Ridges are sharp-edged and unweathered, as if the wind has not had time to reach them. Veins of pale mineral run through the dark stone like wet solder, still cooling, still moving very slightly over long spans of time. In one corner a fracture is widening by a hair per season, and a thin skin of glassy crust is forming along its lips. The rock makes no promise of permanence. Shelves break off and reform. A basin that was a pool of fine grey grit yesterday is now a low, ribbed dune, and tomorrow it will be something else.

**Water, or its absence.** There is no water here, and nothing in the hollow behaves as if water were the usual medium. Moisture gathers, but it is viscous and faintly oily, a clear-amber fluid that beads on leaves and drips with a slow, almost reluctant tick. It pools in shallow dishes of stone and does not evaporate so much as thin away into the air, leaving a faint sheen and a mineral taste on the tongue if you were foolish enough to taste it. The ground is dry to the touch but holds this fluid in its pores, and when you press a palm into the grit, it comes back cool and faintly tacky.

**Vegetation.** This is the surprise. The hollow is lush. Ferns with fronds like hand-pressed sheet metal uncoil slowly from the cracks, their surfaces etched with fine repeating lines that look almost machined. Broad, thick-leaved plants hang from the walls, their leaves ribbed like heat exchangers, and their undersides glisten with the amber fluid. Vines of braided fibrous material climb the stone, and where they meet a ridge they split into finer strands that wrap the rock and seem to draw something from it. The plants are dense in patches and bare in others, and the spaces between them are wide enough that you can walk through without brushing anything. The growth is green in the way that old copper is green, a tarnished bloom over something that looks like it was made to a standard.

**Industrial residue without built things.** There are no buildings, no walls, no doorways, and nothing standing that was set down by hands. Yet the place carries the logic of a factory. Along one wall runs a seam of fused, gear-toothed growth, a lattice of interlocking lobes that rotate, very slowly, when the air shifts. A thick stalk pulses at a regular interval, a rhythm like a pump's stroke, and the amber fluid beads and runs faster for a few seconds after each pulse. Small cylindrical pods line a shelf in a row, each opening and closing its lid in turn, as though a process were being carried out by a sequence of valves that nobody installed. It is as if the machinery of some industry was never built, only grown, and the growing has taken its own shape: the efficient repetition of a production line, expressed entirely in living tissue and stone.

**Sound.** The hollow is mostly quiet. The loudest thing is the tick of fluid dripping from leaf to stone, and the soft grate of grit sliding in a shallow basin. Under those sounds there is a low, steady hum, felt more in the teeth and sternum than heard, like a machine idling a long way off. Occasionally a sharp click comes from the gear-lattice, and the hum rises for a moment, then settles again. There is no wind, no birdsong, no insect hum. Nothing calls out.

**Weirdness.** Reality here is loosened at the edges. Distances shift when you are not watching. A stone you passed a minute ago seems now to sit a little farther to the left, and when you walk back to it, it has moved again. Shadows lag slightly behind their objects, so that when you lift your arm, the shadow of your arm lifts a moment later. The gear-lattice appears to turn in a direction that changes depending on which way you are facing. If you hold very still and listen, you may hear the hum change pitch in step with your breathing. The place does not feel dangerous. It feels unfinished, and the unfinishedness has leaked into the rules: cause and effect come slightly out of order, as if the hollow is still deciding what it is allowed to do.

**Time.** Everything here moves, but slowly. A fracture widens over seasons. A vein of mineral creeps a finger's width across a century. The gear-lattice completes one turn in what might be a whole day. The amber fluid collects and thins in cycles too long for a single visit to reveal. You can stand for an hour and see almost nothing change except the drip, the hum, and the faint shift of shadows. Come back in a year and the hollow will have rearranged itself into something you half recognize and cannot quite map.

**Overall.** It is a small, hot, high place, green with a copper-tarnished lushness, sealed off from water and from any living animal, full of the quiet mechanics of growth that behaves like a factory and cannot be entered as a building. It is a corner of a world still being made, where the rocks are soft, the plants keep production hours, the air is thin and oily-sweet, and the physics bend a little whenever you look away.
