**The Held Basin**

The land sits high, a bowl of fresh rock in a cloud-cap, perhaps a few kilometers across before its walls rise into fog. Everything here looks newly surfaced. The cliffs are not yet weathered. Their edges are sharp enough to cut a finger, and the basalt columns still have the glassy sheen of cooling. Fissures run across the floor like unhealed seams, and the stone is wet along every one of them. Water seeps from the rock face in thin sheets, beading and running down in threads that never quite pool. The whole basin feels like it was lifted out of the earth a moment ago and has not yet decided what shape to keep.

The cloud is low and constant, and light comes through it gray and thick, a permanent late afternoon with no sun to point to. Shadows have no direction. Colors are muted except where something is wet, and then they flare: green moss goes nearly black-emerald, copper-brown lichen on the stone gleams like oil. The air is cool and saturated. Breath fogs. Surfaces are always slick, and a faint mist rises from the ground at every hour, so distant objects dissolve into pale gray at a few hundred meters.

**Vegetation**

The growth is dense and wildly lush, though not in the way of a tropical jungle. These are cloud-forest plants pushed to their extremes. Tree ferns as tall as houses lean over the streams, their fronds uncurling slowly, and their trunks wear shelves of moss and small orchid-like plants that hang their blooms downward to catch condensation. Between them grow mats of sphagnum and thick cushions of moss, so spongy that stepping on them releases a puddle. Vines drape the cliff faces and drip. Nothing grows tall in the open meadows; instead there are fields of low, fleshy plants with translucent leaves that hold water like lenses.

Everything here is saturated. Leaves carry a constant film of water, and the drips collect into rivulets that find the fissures and run downhill to the basin's center.

**Fauna**

Animal life is abundant, though the bodies are hard to see in the mist. The air carries a constant chorus of insects, clicking and humming, with the rhythm of wings that never stops. Birds with long, curved beaks move through the fern canopy, calling in short repeated phrases. There are amphibians the size of cats, slick and dark, that sit in the shallow runs and emit a deep resonant croak, and crawling things with segmented shells that climb the wet cliffs in slow, steady columns. Some animals seem to move only when no one watches. A few moss-colored shapes hold still on the rock and are only recognizable as animals when one of them blinks.

**Sound**

The basin is loud. The water is loud, with thin falls drumming the stone and streams rushing through the fissures, and the insects and frogs fill every gap. Over all of it, from the center of the basin, comes a steady mechanical sound.

**The Machinery**

At the bottom of the bowl, where the fissures converge into a shallow, black-watered lake, stand the engines. They are industrial in scale and purpose: pumps the height of cathedral towers, long piston arms, gears as broad as threshing floors, pressure vessels with gauges, belts stretched between drive wheels, conduit pipes running in every direction. They hum, knock, and hiss. Their rhythms are heavy and regular, a thudding, cycling roar that could power a factory floor.

But nothing here was built. There are no joints, bolts, or seams that a human hand would make. The machinery is grown or extruded from the stone itself. Pistons are fluted columns of dark basalt that move in grooves polished by water. Gears have teeth of stone and bark, and their axles are woven fern-stems hardened to iron-brown. Pipes are roots that thicken and branch as they reach the lake, and they pulse faintly, as if carrying a fluid other than water. Moss grows on the machines and is pumped along with the water, and the engines seem to draw life from the basin as they work.

**Weirdness**

The place does not obey its own rules consistently. Water in some of the fissures runs uphill for a few meters before turning, then flows back to where it started, forming a loop. Reflections in the lake lag behind their objects by a heartbeat, so that when you look down you see yourself a moment late. Sound carries oddly: a shout from the cliff face arrives at the lakeshore before the voice leaves the mouth, and the engines' rhythm sometimes comes from directly beneath your feet. Sometimes a fern frond unfolds in reverse, retreating into its bud, and then unfolds again. Distances shift when you are not looking at them.

**Stillness**

The most unsettling thing is that nothing advances. The engines run through the same stroke forever, the piston at the same height, the gear at the same tooth. The water falls in the same arcs and never reaches the basin floor, held in suspension where the cliff meets the sky. Insects stay in the same positions in the air. The frogs begin the same croak and never finish it. A fern begins to uncurl and does not complete the motion. The basin is a single moment, held in place by the grinding machinery, endlessly running and endlessly the same.
