# The Hollow Under the Lattice

You come down through a fissure no wider than a shoulder, and the rock gives way to a chamber that is almost entirely plant. The walls, floor, and ceiling are one continuous living substrate: a pale, fibrous weave that the locals of this place would never call "ground." It gives slightly underfoot, like wet felt over springy wood, and it is warm, about the warmth of a sleeping animal's flank.

Nothing here was built. There are no joints, no seams, no sign that anything was ever set in place. The chamber simply grew into its shape, and it keeps growing.

## Terrain and Structure

The space is pocket-sized, perhaps forty paces across at its widest, but it does not read as small. The lattice rises in tiered arches, each one thick as a tree trunk and hollow at the core, so the walls are less solid than they look. Tap one and it rings faintly, like a drum with a skin of paper. Between the arches the weave stretches into thin membranes, some translucent enough to show shadows moving on their far side.

The density here is strange. The plant mass is total in the sense that every surface is covered, but the matter itself is sparse. Roughly nine-tenths of the volume is air. You walk through a cathedral of fibers, and the fibers are spaced so loosely that your body could pass through most of them without touching anything. The effect is of standing inside the skeleton of a thought, all structure and very little substance.

The floor slopes gently toward a central depression where the lattice thickens into a shallow basin. The basin holds a pool of clear water that is not quite still. Its surface rises and falls in slow pulses, about once every few breaths, as if the whole chamber is breathing through it.

## Water

Moisture is everywhere but never settles for long. Condensation beads on the upper arches and runs down in threads that catch the light and carry it downward. Every so often a bead swells past its hold and falls, and the drop lands with a small, precise *tick* on the floor or in the pool. In the back of the chamber a seep emerges from between two roots and pours in a thin ribbon, then stops, then pours again, on an irregular cycle that seems to follow some internal pressure rather than any weather above.

Intermittent rain does reach this place, though it comes indirectly. Once in a long while the ceiling sags and a broad sheet of water pours through the weave like a curtain being drawn, filling the basin in minutes and draining it in an hour through channels the plant opens and closes. During these events the membranes swell and sag, and the whole chamber seems to hold its breath.

## Light

Light is ordinary daylight, which surprises most visitors. It comes from the fissure above, a crack that reaches up toward the surface, and it falls down in a single slanted column. The column is bright enough to read by. Dust motes, pollen, and spores turn slowly in it.

The light does not stay where it lands. The lattice carries it. Fibers channel the beam sideways and downward, so that light appears in places it has no business reaching: pale stripes across the ceiling, a glow leaking out of a wall that should be dark. Some fibers are luminous, but only in the light's wake, as though they absorb sunlight and release it later in a dim, greenish afterglow. Stand still long enough and the room seems to exhale brightness after you step out of the column.

## Sound

This is the loudest place you will ever stand in quietly.

The drips are the foreground: sharp, bell-like, scattered at irregular intervals across the chamber. Beneath them runs a low, continuous hum, the sound of the lattice itself. The fibers creak as they flex, pop when tension shifts, and whisper when air moves through their hollow cores. At certain points, especially near the arches, the sound rises into an actual roar, the rushing of wind through a hundred passages that do not seem to connect to any outside air.

Echoes behave badly here. A sound you make returns to you a fraction of a second early, as though the walls anticipated it. A dropped stone may strike the basin before you hear it leave your hand. Voices sometimes come back in pitch-shifted tones, lower and slower, as if the chamber is repeating you through a body much larger than its own.

## Ecology

The plant that makes this place is the only truly dominant organism. It is a single, interconnected entity that has grown into every corner, and its growth is visible if you watch long enough. Tips of fibers creep across the floor in slow arcs. Old sections darken and dissolve into a fine, gray dust that the basin absorbs. New shoots push up from the dust in pale spirals within a few hours.

Fauna is rare. The few creatures present are small and seem drawn to the plant's byproducts. Pale, eyeless insects with long antennae move along the fiber surfaces, feeding on the sugared film the plant secretes. Something that might be a crustacean lives in the basin, moving in tiny jerks and leaving spiral tracks in the sediment. You may also see a larger, slow creature, a kind of cave-dwelling mollusk with a shell shaped like a cupped hand, grazing on the lower arches. These are the predators and prey of this system, though the predation is quiet and mostly unseen. The chamber's activity is biological in pace: the lattice grows, decays, and regrows over days, and the small life around it moves in cycles that match.

## Tools and Traces

The primitive tech here is not made by any inhabitant of the chamber. There are no tools, no fire rings, no marks on the walls. The only artifacts are the ones the visitor brings. But the plant does seem to use a crude toolkit of its own. Some fibers have hardened into hooks, spikes, or levers, which it uses to pry apart rock at the fissure's edges and widen the passage a little each season. Others have curled into hollow tubes that channel water exactly where the plant needs it. If there is any craft in this place, it belongs to the lattice.

## Rules of the Place

The chamber does not obey its own geometry consistently. Some of the stranger rules seem to be the following, though they may not hold:

- **The room is larger inside than the fissure suggests.** Walking to the far wall takes longer than the paces should allow, and walking back takes less.
- **Gravity wavers.** Near the arches, dropped objects sometimes fall toward the ceiling for a moment before correcting. Near the basin, the water sometimes rises a finger's width up the side of a stone and then sinks back.
- **Time pools.** Drips fall at varying speeds. Some seem frozen midair for a long moment, then rush to the floor.
- **Echoes arrive first.** As above, you hear a thing before you make it, or sometimes just after you hear it begin.
- **Memory is loose.** Visitors often find that the chamber's shape is different when they return from the basin, as if the plant has redrawn the walls while their attention was elsewhere.

None of this is hostile, exactly. The chamber does not seem to care whether you are there. But it responds to you, subtly, the way a living thing responds to a touch: a fiber curls toward your hand, the drip-rhythm shifts when you hold your breath, the light column brightens when you look up. It has no mind that you can point to, only a pattern of attention spread across the whole of itself.

## Leaving

The fissure is still there when you turn to go, but it is narrower than it was. The lattice has grown a little while you stood in its hollow, and it will keep growing after you leave, sealing the passage behind you by degrees, until the chamber is once more closed, warm, breathing, and full of its own loud, sparse, endless life.
