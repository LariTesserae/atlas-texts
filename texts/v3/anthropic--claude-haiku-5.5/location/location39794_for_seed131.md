**The Sump Pocket**

You are at the bottom of a basin that was dug, poured, and fitted by people who have been gone for longer than the concrete has been setting in its own slow way. The place is roughly the size of a large room, perhaps forty paces across, and its floor sits lower than anything around it. Everything you can see was made. Nothing here is rock, soil, or bedrock. The walls are poured concrete gone soft-edged with age, rounded at the corners by centuries of water, so the original angles have worn down into gentle slopes. The whole structure has settled toward a final shape, nearly flattened, like a stone step worn by feet that stopped walking long ago.

**Water**

Water is everywhere, and it is always close. The basin holds a shallow, still pool, black-green where the light touches it and brown-clear at the margins. Its surface sits a finger's width below a lip of corroded iron. Water weeps from every seam and rivet. Thin films run down the walls in sheets that never quite become streams, and the air is saturated enough that the ceiling beads with droplets which fall at a steady, unhurried rate. Everything is damp to the touch. Cloth left here would never dry. The atmosphere is a warm, close condensation, like breathing the inside of a greenhouse lid.

**Vegetation**

The plant life is lush and has claimed the built forms completely. Ferns grow in the joints of the wall panels, and their fronds spill over the ledges in tiers. Broad-leaved plants with thick, glossy leaves cover the upper curve of the basin in a dense mat. Moss thickens every horizontal surface, and fine green filaments hang from the lower rungs of an access ladder that has not been climbed in generations. Vines have found the iron railings and wound through them, pulling the rusted bars into tangled cages. Some plants have grown through cracks in the concrete, and their roots have opened the seams further, so the vegetation is slowly prying the structure apart in the same ancient, patient way that water shapes stone. Yet the plants are sparse in the open air. The basin's volume is mostly empty, and the green is clustered at the edges, leaving the center to the pool, the air, and the sound.

**Sound**

The sound is the dominant fact of the place. Somewhere in the walls, or beneath the floor, large pumps run without pause. Their rhythm is a deep, labored thud, followed by a rising mechanical shriek and a wet slap of released water, repeating every few seconds with no variation. The noise fills the basin to its corners. It vibrates through your ribs and the soles of your feet, and it makes the droplets on the ceiling tremble. Speech is almost impossible here. You learn to read lips or wait for the gaps between cycles, which are short and never quite silent, because the surface water still hisses against the intake grate. The noise has no beginning and no end. It has simply been going on for so long that it has become the place's shape.

**Light**

The light is ordinary, unremarkable daylight, which is strange, given how sealed the basin feels. It enters through a single grate high in the far wall, a rectangle of corroded lattice that admits a steady, even glow. The light falls at the same angle every hour, never warming or cooling, and it lays the same pattern of shadows across the water and the plants. Dust motes hang in it, but they do not drift. The beam is fixed, like the gravity that holds the water in its bed.

**Fauna**

Animal life is rare. Once in a while a small, pale creature moves along the lower wall, a blind, soft-bodied thing that smooths itself into a seam when disturbed. Insects are few. Their drone is lost beneath the pumps. You may see a single brown insect crawling on a fern, and you will not see another for a long time. Nothing here seems to depend on animals. The ecology is built from water, plants, and machinery, and the animals are only occasional guests.

**Rules of the place**

The world here follows a few constant rules. The water level never rises or falls. The light never shifts. The pumps never stop, and their cycle never changes its tempo. The vegetation grows, but only into spaces it has already occupied; it does not spread beyond the basin's edges. Things that move here move in place: water circulates, droplets fall, and the pumps push and drain in an endless loop. Nothing accumulates, and nothing is lost. The whole pocket is a closed equilibrium, a machine that keeps itself running in a world that has forgotten what it was for.

You stand at the edge of the pool, where the concrete curves down into black water. Overhead the grate lets in its even light, and the pumps thud, shriek, and slap in their unchanging rhythm. Droplets fall, and the ferns lean toward the glow. Nothing here will change while you are here, or after.
