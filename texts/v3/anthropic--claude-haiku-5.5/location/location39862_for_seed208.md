The land is old enough to have forgotten its shape. Mountains went down here so long ago that what remains is a low, rolling expanse of bedrock, rounded like river stones and polished in places to a dull sheen. Nothing rises more than a few meters above the general level. The horizon is close and flat, and the sky seems to sit on it like a lid.

**Terrain and materials**

The ground is mostly exposed stone: grey-brown sandstone worn into shallow basins, dark ironstone that rings faintly underfoot, and pale quartz veins standing proud of the surrounding rock like the ribs of something buried. Where the stone has crumbled, it has become a fine silt that holds water and gives under weight, so every step leaves a print that fills slowly with seep. Low ridges, barely noticeable at first, run in long parallel lines, as if the land once had a grain and has been sanded almost smooth.

There is no soil in the usual sense. The darker basins hold a black, spongy mineral mat, several centimeters thick, that gives off a sulfurous, mushroomy smell when disturbed. It is closer to crust than to growth. It flexes, cracks in polygons when it dries, and peels in translucent sheets that curl at the edges. Nothing grows in it, but it moves a little: the sheets shift and reform with the seasons of wet and dry.

**Water**

Water is the defining element. Shallow pools lie in nearly every hollow, often joined by thin sheets that glint across the flats. The pools are clear at the edges and dark in the middle, and the water has a faint bluish tint that deepens with depth. Rain falls often, and in the wet season it seems the land is only half above water. Morning brings a thick condensation that clings to the rock, so every surface beads and drips. Small seeps weep from cracks in the cliffs and at the edges of the basins, leaving pale mineral stains like tear tracks down the stone. Where the stains have built up over ages, they form terraced lips and rimstone pools, stepped and luminous.

The air is always damp, and the cool keeps the mist low and close. It hangs at knee height in the mornings and thins to a haze by midday, though it never fully clears.

**Light**

The light is dim and diffuse, filtered through a heavy overcast that is rarely broken. Shadows barely exist; everything is lit evenly from above and from the reflections in the water, so the land seems to glow faintly from within. Colors are muted: greys, browns, slate blues, and the occasional bruised violet where the mineral mats catch the light. At dusk the light drains slowly and does not truly go out, leaving a long grey twilight that lasts most of the night.

**Sound**

This is a loud place, and the noise is continuous. The fauna is abundant, and much of it is vocal. Layered choruses rise from the pools and reeds of stone (rigid, fluted growths that are not plants but carved-looking mineral spires, hollow and resonant). Some creatures click and whistle; others produce low, pulsing drones that vibrate in the chest. Their calls overlap and build into a roar at dusk and dawn, then ease to a steady, hissing hum through the day. Water adds to it: drips strike rock like a rhythm section, seeps gurgle, and sheets of runoff rush across the flats after rain. Wind moving through the hollow spires produces a sustained, flute-like note that rises and falls. Between these sounds there is almost no silence, only brief lulls when the choruses shift.

**Fauna**

The animals are plentiful but mostly small or middling, and many are hard to see. Low-bodied, many-legged creatures move through the wet mats, leaving trails that look like stitched lines. Larger, slow grazers, heavy and armored, graze the mineral crust, scraping it with broad, flat mouthparts. Above, flocks of winged things circle the pools, dipping to skim the surface and sending out ripples. Among the spires live tall, thin-legged forms that stand motionless in shallow water, their heads turning in time with the sounds, as if listening. Predators are scattered and quiet, moving between the calls. The whole system seems to run on sound: creatures call, respond, and track one another by pitch and rhythm.

**Built remains**

People, or something like people, were here, but they left little. There are cairns of flat stones stacked at intervals along the ridges, some collapsed, some still tall, marking what may have been paths or boundaries. Hand-cut channels run from the seeps toward the pools, lined with rounded stones, now half silted over. Near some of the old basins are rings of blackened stone where fires were lit, and fragments of worked bone, flint flakes, and cracked clay vessels lie in the mud, half sunk. A few upright slabs, carved with shallow spirals, lean at angles, eroded nearly to nothing. None of it is recent. It has been worn to the shape of the land, and in places it is hard to say whether the marks are made or natural.

**Rules and oddities**

There are strange regularities. The spires hum at different pitches depending on the depth of water at their base, so the sound map of the land changes with the rains. Rimstone pools, when disturbed, release a faint pale vapor that smells of iron and cold stone, and creatures seem to avoid it. In the deepest basins, the water sometimes turns briefly milky, then clears again, as though something beneath is breathing. Light in the wet season has a slight green cast that disappears when the pools dry, though no green things grow here. Creatures that die on the flats are dissolved within days, leaving pale, lace-like patterns on the crust that the others step around.

**Overall atmosphere**

It feels like a world that finished its major work long ago and is now only maintaining a fragile, noisy balance. Water moves through it constantly, wearing it, feeding it, and carrying life through a landscape that offers almost nothing green. Traces of people are faint and nearly erased. The dominant feeling is of a vast, wet, patient country, loud with unseen activity, sitting under a low sky and waiting for the next rain.
