**The Resin Shelf**

You stand on a high, broken table of rock, roughly the size of a village commons, bounded on three sides by cliffs and on the fourth by a fall into haze. There is no river here, no lake, no standing pool. Water has never pooled on this shelf, and the ground shows it. The stone is pale and fissured, cracked into plates that curl at the edges like dried bread. Dust lies in the hollows, fine and grey-gold, and when the wind moves it hisses across the plates.

Yet the shelf is green. It is almost violently green. Vegetation covers nearly every surface that isn't bare rock, and it grows thick, glossy, and fast. The dominant form is a low, many-lobed mat of broad leaves, each lobe gleaming with a skin of amber-brown fluid. That fluid is not water. It is a thick, slow resin with a sweetish, metallic smell, and the plants seem to live on it. Where the mat is cut, the wound beads and hardens within minutes into a translucent cap. Above the mat rise stalks like reeds, hollow and jointed, some as tall as a person, some tipped with fan-shaped crowns that open and close. Their hollows whistle when the wind enters them.

The plants do not drink from the ground. Look closely at the cracks in the stone and you will see faint crusts of salt and a pale, sticky dew that forms at dusk and sublimes away by midday. The roots reach into the fissures and draw from the cracked rock itself, and from a slow, invisible breath of vapour that rises through the plates from something far below. The lushness is an inheritance from deep time, a long, patient harvest of what the stone exhales. It is a green that has learned to grow without rain.

**Light**

The sky is a low, bruised ceiling of amber and grey. The sun is a dull disc behind a permanent scrim of fine dust, and the light that reaches the shelf is thick and soft, without edges. Shadows barely exist. Colours seem muted until you notice that every leaf and resin cap throws back a small glow of its own, a faint bioluminescence that pulses in patches when the sun dips. At dusk the shelf brightens in a strange way, from within. The resin lights first, then the stalks, and the whole place becomes a field of dim lanterns for a short while before it fades again.

**Heat**

The air is warm and close, though not oppressive, since the altitude keeps it from settling into stillness. Heat comes off the stone in the afternoons and the rock ticks as it cools at evening. The wind is constant but gusty, arriving in shoves that bring gusts of dust, spores, and small flying things, then drop to an eerie lull.

**Sound**

This place is loud, and the loudness is its defining quality. The reed stalks whistle and hoot in the wind, each at a different pitch, so that the gusts produce chords that rise and fall like organ pipes. Insects fill the gaps: clicking beetles with ribbed wing-cases, buzzing swarms that rise from the mat in clouds, and a species of cicada-like thing that produces a rasping, rhythmic drone that comes from every direction at once. Beneath it all, the stone creaks and pops as it expands in the heat, and every few minutes a plate lets go with a crack like a rifle shot and slides off the edge. The cliffs send echoes back, so that the noise never quite ends.

**Fauna**

Animals are abundant here, and most of them are busy. Six-legged grazers the size of goats move along the stalks in herds, stripping the resin-lobes with thick, ridged mouthparts and leaving behind pale, scarred stumps that heal within a week. Their hides are armoured with overlapping plates that catch the dim light. Smaller, quicker forms dart between the leaves: skink-like things with fringed frills, and flying creatures with translucent wings that drone in loose, erratic spirals. Predators are low to the ground and rarely seen; their presence shows only in the sudden silence that falls over a patch of mat, then breaks in a scatter of frightened clicks. Many animals are tied to the resin itself. They drink it, bathe in it, or cocoon in it, and some carry sticky amber globs on their backs like gifts or wounds.

**Structures**

Human presence here is faint, scattered, and old. Some of the cracked plates have been stacked into low cairns, each the height of a knee, arranged in lines that might be boundaries or might be the remains of a trail. Near the cliff's edge stands a single small structure, a wall of mortared slabs enclosing a space the size of a bedroll, its roof collapsed and half-buried in resin that has hardened over it like glass. Inside, if you look carefully, there are scratched marks on the inner stones: spirals, notches, and a row of tiny hand-prints, some faded, some fresh. Someone still comes here. A few wooden hooks hang from a stalk nearby, carrying bundles of dried mat and small amber cakes wrapped in leaves. Tools are simple: flaked stone blades, bone scrapers, hollow reed pipes, and a few clay vessels stopped with wax.

**Weirdness**

Something in this place doesn't quite obey the expected rules. The resin appears to respond to sound. When the whistling stalks reach a certain pitch in unison, the resin on the nearest leaves begins to bead faster, and the sticky caps on the cut stumps grow in a few hours instead of days. The old people who tend the cairns say that the shelf "listens and answers," and the fauna behaves as if this is true: herds move toward the loudest reeds, and the grazers hum to each other in a low register that the stalks echo back. Nothing here is still for long. Patches of mat bloom in a single night, flush with new growth, then sag and blacken within a week as their resin drains back into the stone. New stalks push up through old collapsed ones. The cairns are rebuilt every season from the plates the wind has moved. The shelf is always becoming something else, and always through noise and motion.

**Dynamic**

The turbulence of the place runs through every scale. Dust storms rise from the edges of the fall and sweep the shelf clean for an hour, then drop their grit in thick drifts that bury whole stands of stalks. Plates break and tumble off the cliffs with frequent thunder. Herds migrate in restless loops, swarms rise and settle, resin floods and hardens, and the sun's dim disc drifts through its slow arc behind the haze. Nothing stays fixed, yet the whole shelf holds together, a loud, green, shifting equilibrium balanced on the edge of a long drop into grey, where the bones of older shelves lie buried under the same dust.
