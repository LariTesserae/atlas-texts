**The Hollow Under the Ridge**

You are inside a space about the size of a large room, or a small cathedral, though its walls are not quite walls. The ceiling is a broken shelf of dark stone, and a fissure no wider than an arm lets in the only light: a thin, grey-violet glow that seems to come from somewhere much higher than the fissure should allow. The air is very cold and very dry. It doesn't smell of anything. Your breath doesn't fog, and you have the sense that the cold is coming from the stone itself, which has been losing its heat for a very long time.

**Terrain**

The floor slopes gently toward a central depression, a shallow basin about twenty paces across. It is paved with plates of black volcanic glass, some as smooth as poured oil, others weathered to a pale, pitted matte. The glass has worn into long, low ridges, like the ribs of a sleeping animal, though nothing sleeps here. Where the plates have separated, fine grey dust has collected in the seams and drifted into shallow dunes that never quite move. Their crests are sharp and unmarked. Nothing has walked across them.

The walls rise on three sides and curve inward, becoming a slanted ceiling on the fourth. The stone is layered in bands of ochre, rust, and a bleached white, like the strata of a cliff face, but the bands do not meet at the corners. They bend away from one another and resume on the far side at a slightly different angle, as if the room were drawn by a hand that forgot its own proportions.

**Materials**

The mineral palette is limited and worn. Glass, pale feldspar-like rock, and a fine crust of salts that crunches softly underfoot and leaves no trace. Nothing organic exists here: no roots, no lichen, no bone. Where the salt crust meets the glass, the boundary has a faint iridescence, a shimmer that changes when you move your head, as if a thin film of something else is caught there. You can't scrape it up. It is only a sheen.

The cold lives in the surfaces. Touch the glass and it burns your fingertips with a clean, aching cold. Hold your palm still against it for several seconds and the cold reaches your bones. Hold it longer and you feel the faintest warmth returning, as though the glass has borrowed heat from your hand and is giving it back in a slow exchange.

**Light**

The light is dim and does not come from a single source. The fissure admits a grey-violet band that falls across the basin and stops at its edge. Beyond that band the shadows are deep, but they are wrong in small ways. A shadow falls a fraction of a second behind the object that casts it. Your own shadow, when you step into the light, arrives after you have already moved, then slides into place like a reluctant guest. The light itself has no clear color; it shifts between grey, violet, and a pale green-gold depending on where you stand, and the boundaries between these tones are not sharp but slightly out of focus.

**Sound**

There is almost nothing. A faint, continuous hush, the sound of very fine dust settling in the seams. Occasionally a plate of glass ticks as it contracts, a sound like a fingernail on a teacup, too distant to locate. Your own footsteps come back to you a half-beat late, and the echo sounds slightly lower in pitch each time, so that a few steps seem to carry you into a deeper room that isn't there.

**Built remains**

Scattered across the basin are a few structures, or what might once have been structures. Each one is small, no larger than a bread oven or a standing human figure. The nearest is a pillar of dark metal, though it might be a fused stack of glass. Its surface is covered in shallow grooves that run in lines too regular to be natural. The lines do not quite finish. Where they should meet at the base, they turn aside into the stone and disappear, and you cannot tell whether they go into the ground or come out of it.

Further off, a flat disc lies half-buried in the dust, its face etched with a pattern of nested arcs. The arcs seem sharper when you look directly at them and softer at the edge of your vision, and when you blink, the pattern has moved a fraction of a width, as if it is slowly rotating in a direction you cannot see.

There are no tools or inscriptions anyone could read. Only the sense that something here was built to do a single thing very precisely, and that the thing was finished long ago, and that the machinery is still maintaining its own shape out of habit.

**Rules of the place**

The world here keeps its own accounts loosely. Distances stretch when you are not watching them: a stone you saw three steps away may be four when you look again, and you cannot be sure you walked at all. Time moves at an uneven rate. A single breath can seem to last the length of a long exhale, and then the whole room seems to jump forward, the dust having drifted further than it should in the moment you were not counting.

Nothing is permanently lost. An object you set down can be found later in a slightly different place, slightly more worn, its edges softened as though it had been there for centuries. A footprint you leave remains for a while, then thins to a faint outline, then becomes simply part of the pattern of the salt crust. Nothing is ever wholly erased or wholly preserved. It is held in a state of very slow transition.

**Dynamics**

The change here is glacial and nearly invisible. The glass cracks along lines that have been slowly lengthening for longer than the stone has had a name. The dust moves one grain at a time, its dunes drifting a finger's width per century. The basin deepens by a fraction of a hair each year, and the fissure above it, if you measured it carefully, is slightly narrower than it was when you arrived, or perhaps slightly wider. The room is in balance, a balance so patient it has become indistinguishable from stillness, though it is not still.

If you stay long enough, you may notice that the ribs of glass on the floor are not parallel to the walls anymore. They have rotated, a few degrees, while you weren't looking. You will not be able to say when it happened. You will only know that the room is slightly different, and that it has been moving toward a configuration it has not yet reached and perhaps never will.

The cold deepens by a degree every long while. The light shifts a shade toward violet. The shadows fall a little further behind the things that cast them. Nothing here is hurrying, and nothing here has ever needed to.
