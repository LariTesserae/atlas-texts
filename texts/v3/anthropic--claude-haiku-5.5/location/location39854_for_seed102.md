**The Hollow Under the Ash-Crust**

You are inside a pocket of earth that never reached the surface. The ceiling is a shelf of pale, fractured mineral, about the height of three people standing on each other's shoulders, and it has not weathered. The breaks are raw and glassy, edges still sharp enough to cut, as if the roof split open only a few seasons ago. Dust lies in fine ridges where it settled and has never been disturbed by rain, because there is no rain here. There has never been water in this chamber. Nothing has washed it.

The air is warm and close, around the temperature of a body resting in the sun, and it carries a faint sweetness. The heat comes from below, from a fissure in the floor that breathes slow warm gusts through the stone. Where the fissure opens, the mineral is glazed and faintly yellow, and the surface wavers as if seen through heated glass. Nothing condenses. Your skin feels dry even while you sweat.

Light is dim and comes from no single source. A low glow seeps from the vegetation itself, a pale amber bioluminescence in the sap-filled tissues of the plants. It rises and falls on a slow rhythm, like breathing, and it throws everything into soft gold and shadow. The corners of the chamber stay dark.

**The vegetation** is lush in the way a crowded greenhouse is lush, though the species are strange. Everything grows thick and succulent, but nothing here drinks water. The plants store a viscous amber resin in swollen trunks and bulbs, and their metabolism runs on it. Some look like upright ribbed columns with fringed crowns; some sprawl across the floor like coiled, fleshy ropes. The largest, a shape like a crouched bundle of reeds, grows from the ceiling downward, its tendrils hanging nearly to the floor, where they tap the dust and draw up the faint minerals they need. Their leaves are thin, translucent, and rattle when the warm wind moves through, a dry clicking that sounds like many small shells knocking together.

Where the resin has seeped out and hardened, it forms glossy honey-colored crusts on the stone. Some of these crusts have been scraped away in neat patches, exposing the cut-grain of older surfaces underneath.

**The fauna** is abundant and noisy. The chamber is never silent. Insect-like creatures with segmented, glassy bodies cling to the columns, and their wings are thin as burnt paper. They click their mandibles against the stone in a restless, overlapping chatter. Larger animals move along the floor in low, heavy bodies, something like a cross between a beetle and a dry-bellied lizard, with plated backs that catch the amber light. They feed on the hanging tendrils, tearing them and sucking up the resin, and their chewing makes a wet, grinding sound that carries far in the stone. Somewhere deeper in the fissure, a low pulsing hum rises and falls, possibly the animals' calls, possibly the vent itself resonating.

The sound is loud, layered, and continuous: clicking leaves, chattering insects, the grinding of feeding, the deep hum from below. Speech is hard to hear over it, and you find yourself speaking loudly without meaning to.

**The built traces** are small and scattered, and they are old by the look of them, though nothing here has decayed much. Someone or something worked this chamber with simple hand tools. There are shallow grooves cut into the walls at roughly the height of a reaching arm, spaced evenly, perhaps for hanging or gripping. A few low cairns of stacked mineral plates stand in the open floor, each no taller than a knee, with a single flat stone laid across the top. Some of the cairns have been knocked over, and the plates lie scattered as if they were once used and then forgotten. In one corner, a shallow basin has been hollowed into the stone and lined with resin crust, its rim worn smooth. It holds a dried, brittle residue. You cannot tell whether it was a container, an altar, or a place where something was crushed.

Inscriptions, if they exist, are faint: short, repeated marks scratched in rows near the cairns, like tally strokes. Nothing here is grand. It is the work of hands that were careful and few.

**The rules of the place** are not written anywhere, but they are visible in what happens. Nothing moves in the ordinary sense of seasons. The fissure has breathed at this same warmth for as long as the marks have stood. The plants grow and cycle in an unbroken loop of storing resin, feeding, and shedding. The animals return to the same tendrils on the same rhythm. The amber light pulses at a fixed interval that never drifts. The chamber has no weather, no erosion to speak of, no change of season to mark time. It is a closed equilibrium that has held its shape indefinitely, and the only thing that changes is you, the longer you stay in it.

The roof's fresh breaks are the single exception, and even they are old enough now to have stopped crumbling. Whatever split the ceiling did so suddenly and then stopped. The fracture lines are sharp and unweathered, and they tell you that this is a place that was opened once, and then sealed again, and has kept its shape ever since.
