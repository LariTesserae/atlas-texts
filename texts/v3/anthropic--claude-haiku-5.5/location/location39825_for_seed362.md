# The Verdant Foundry

The land is flat, or it was made flat. From the edge of the basin the ground runs out in terraces, each one a shelf of compacted slag and fused grey aggregate, stepping down a few meters at a time toward a center too distant to see. Nothing here is natural bedrock. The terraces were laid by machines, and the seams between the slabs are still visible, though they are now hard to find under the growth.

The growth is everything. Plant tissue covers every surface, but it does not sit on top of the built world like a skin. It has grown through the structure. Pale green stems climb the lattice of old gantry towers and split their steel girders. Roots, thick as cable, run along conduit trenches and push the floor plates apart. Where a pressure pipe once ran, a ropy vine now coils around it, its bark dense with a sheen of dark lacquer that might be resin or might be something the plant secretes from metal it has dissolved. The soil is not soil. It is a laminated mat, centuries deep, made of stems, spores, and mineral residue, layered like peat and printed with the faint outlines of the machines it has buried.

## Water

There is no standing water anywhere. The air is cool and dry, and it carries a fine metallic dust that catches the light. Water has been removed from the system, and the plants have adapted to its absence. Their leaves are broad, waxy, and folded in fans that face the sky to collect what little condensation forms on the cooling fins of the old refineries at dawn. Some plants have grown bulbous reservoirs at their bases, sealed in greenish chitin. When a reservoir is cracked open, the fluid inside is clear, faintly sweet, and tastes of solder. It is the only liquid in the basin, and the plants guard it.

## Light and Air

The light is strong, almost white, and it does not come from a single source. A sun hangs high and pale behind a thin sheet of cloud, but a ring of tall lamp towers also stands at intervals across the basin, some still burning, others guttering or dark. Their light overlaps the sun's, and shadows fall in several directions at once, so that a single tower may throw three shadows that fan out over the terraces like the spokes of a broken wheel. Walking across the basin, your shadow sometimes leads you and sometimes trails behind, and occasionally it stops moving entirely while you keep walking.

The cold has a dry bite. Frost forms on the sun-side of the metal and never melts on the shaded side, so the surfaces of the structures show a marbled pattern of white and bronze. Breath clouds, and the clouds hang in the air longer than they should.

## The Weirdness

The distances do not hold together. A chimney stack on the eastern rim looks close enough to touch, but walking toward it for an hour leaves it no nearer, and when you stop, it has shrunk to half its size. Some of the terraces appear to fold back on themselves at the horizon, so that the far edge of the basin meets the near edge in a seam of rust-colored smoke.

Machines still run, though nobody has tended them for a very long time. A pump station on the central plateau cycles through the same sequence of valve openings every eleven minutes, and the sequence is not quite the same each time. A conveyor belt carries nothing but carries it anyway: slag blocks appear at one end, pass through a dark hopper, and emerge at the other as fragments of leaf. A smokestack emits a column of pale vapor that rises straight up for two hundred meters, then bends sharply and drifts back toward the factory it came from, as if the air itself were reversing course.

Some of the plants grow in shapes that echo the machines. A cluster of spiral vines at the base of an old turbine mimics its blade housing, its ribs thick and ridged. Whether the plant copied the machine or the machine's designers copied the plant is not clear, and the plant does not say.

## Sound

Silence is the default. The basin is a place where sound seems to be absorbed. The hum of the machines is felt more than heard, a low vibration in the soles of the feet and the bones of the jaw. Leaves tick as they cool. Water drips from a cracked reservoir into a metal pan, one drop every few seconds, and each drop rings faintly. Sometimes a distant whine rises from the center of the basin and holds a single pitch for minutes before dropping away, and no one has ever found its source.

## Fauna

Animal life is sparse. A few insect-like forms move through the vines, bodies armored in brushed alloy, that seem to repair the plant tissue they pass over. Small birds nest in the gear housings of the dead turbines, and their eggs are speckled like circuit boards. They are rarely seen and never in groups. The basin does not seem to need them, and they do not seem to need it.

## Dynamics

Everything here is changing slowly and at once. The plants are growing into the machines, and each season some valve or piston seizes under a new coil of root and must be replaced by the plant's own tissue, which hardens into a fair imitation of the original part. The machines respond by adjusting their cycles, and the adjustments ripple outward through the system in ways that are not predictable. Dust storms form on the western rim, carrying fine salt and rust. They roll east across the terraces in a wall of brown haze that lasts a day, then collapses into dry drifts that the vines immediately start to bind.

The basin is not ruined. It is in balance, though the balance is one no designer intended: a dead industrial order slowly being rewritten by a living one, and in the process generating a new kind of weather, a new kind of ground, and a set of rules that bend the light and the distance into shapes that do not quite close.
