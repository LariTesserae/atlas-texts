**The Sluice Basin**

From the rim, the land looks like a dish someone pressed into the earth a very long time ago and then forgot. The basin is perhaps four or five hundred meters across, and its floor sits barely above the water table. Nothing here has risen in a long while. The bedrock has been worn so smooth by water that it reads as glaze, grey-green and faintly iridescent where it shows through. Where it doesn't, the ground is mud, black and fine as cocoa powder, and it takes a boot print slowly, the way warm wax would.

The air is thick and warm, like the inside of a mouth. Water gathers out of it constantly. Droplets bead on every surface and run in threads down the rock faces, and the whole basin seems to sweat. Standing pools lie in the low places, flat as mirrors, and some of them give back reflections a beat late, so that you see yourself finish a movement after you've made it. The sky is a bright, even white, normal daylight through cloud that never quite breaks. Nothing casts a hard shadow.

**The growth.** Plant life is not a feature of this place. It is the place. A single living mat covers almost every surface: vine-trunks as thick as a person's waist, braided and ribbed, climbing over one another in lattices that form ceilings, walls, and floors across the basin. Between the vines, fern-analogs unfurl in slow spirals, and their fronds are translucent, carrying veins that pulse faintly with fluid. The mat roots into the rock and into the rubble, and it has grown through the built things so completely that in many places you can't tell where the plant stops and the structure starts. Doorways are filled with living tissue. Columns are sheathed in bark that has the regular tooling marks of masonry. Stairs have become root-steps, worn into smoothness by generations of something climbing them.

The growth is not uniform. Dense knots of it, dark and fibrous, cluster around the old structures like coral around wrecks. Between them are wide clearings of water and reed-like stalks that chime when the wind crosses them. The density of the whole place is low, though it's hard to say so given how much is alive. It is less that things are crowded than that each presence is a separate island, with long wet gaps between.

**The built layers.** Beneath and through the growth lie the remains of a settlement, or something settlement-shaped. Terraces step down toward the center of the basin. Arched galleries run in rings. Channels cut into the rock form a grid, and water still moves through some of them, sluicing from one level to another with no visible pump or mechanism. Engineering of this kind doesn't obey the physics you'd expect. Some of the structures are clearly flowing, the way wax flows, and some hold shapes that look sculpted in the moment you look away. A cluster of tall, slender towers at the basin's center tilts gently and returns, over periods of hours, like a breathing thing. Their surfaces are a mesh of fine hexagonal cells, each cell faintly lit from within in a cool blue that flickers, not like a screen, more like something thinking.

Some of the cells are assembling. Others are dissolving. At any moment there are patches of the lattice that have lost their structure and gone to a soft grey sludge, and beside them, patches where new cells are extruding out of the plant mat itself, sprouting like buds and locking into geometry within minutes. Standing in the basin, you can watch a wall go from sludge to lattice to vine and back again in a single afternoon.

**The fauna.** The basin is crowded with life that moves. Amphibious creatures, long-limbed and bright-throated, wade through the reed pools and call from the ledges in a chorus that never really stops. Insects hum in the lattices, many of them metallic-bodied, and some seem to carry fragments of the old machinery in their mandibles, tapping them against the rock as they go. Winged animals the size of hawks glide between the towers, and they carry seeds and small cells in pouches under their bellies; wherever they land, something sprouts or starts to build. Nothing here seems hunted or hunting. The animals move through the place like workers through a busy market.

**Sound.** The basin is loud. The amphibian chorus rises and falls in waves. Water is everywhere, dripping, running, gushing through the channels, and the cavities of the old galleries turn each splash into a resonant tone. The reed clearings chime in the wind. The towers groan as they tilt, a long low note that you feel in your teeth, and the lattice cells click and hum as they reconfigure. Underneath it all runs a steady metallic ticking, like a hundred clocks slightly out of sync.

**Rules and oddities.** Nothing here decays cleanly. Dead matter is absorbed into the mat within days, and its nutrients reappear as new growth or new lattice. Fog settles each evening and lifts each morning, and the doors that open in the towers seem to answer to the fog, sliding aside only when the air is saturated and closing again when it clears. Sometimes a path that was open at dusk is blocked by dawn, and the lattice on the far rim has rearranged itself into a new route. Visitors who sleep here report waking with a faint green staining on their skin that fades over a week, and a sense that the basin has been counting them.

The place doesn't feel finished. It feels like something very old that has learned to keep rebuilding itself so that it never has to become anything else. Everything that is here is partly ruin and partly seed, and the water keeps moving through it all, slowly, toward a center that no one has ever reached.
