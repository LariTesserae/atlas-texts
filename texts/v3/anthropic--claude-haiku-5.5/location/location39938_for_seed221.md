**The Weeping Hall**

The place is a pocket inside a machine that was built to pull water out of the air. It sits in the flank of a bare mountain at high altitude, where the thin cold air holds little heat and the clouds snag on the ridge. Nothing grows on the slope above it except a grey lichen the color of old ash, which clings to the poured concrete of the intake louvers. The vault itself is a low, ribbed chamber, perhaps the size of a chapel, cut into the rock and lined with grey concrete and a lattice of corroded steel. Its roof is a honeycomb of condensation plates. Air is drawn in through the louvers, chilled against the plates, and the water falls out.

**Materials and surfaces**

The walls don't stay dry. Every surface is slick with a skin of water that runs in thin sheets and beads into rivulets along the seams. Where the water sits longest, the concrete has begun to change. Its lime leaches out and recrystallizes into pale flowstone, soft ridges and crusts the color of old candle wax. These mineral forms are young and fragile. They are the only part of the place that is not made by hand, and they are forming so slowly that a visitor would not see them grow in a lifetime. The floor is a shallow, black-green pool fed from a dozen drains, its surface crazed with the constant impact of drops. Steel gratings cross the pool on legs that have grown thick with rust-colored crust. Some of the crust has taken on a glassy, almost mineral sheen, as if the iron were turning back into stone.

**Atmosphere and light**

The light is dim and mostly comes from sodium lamps set high on the ribs, their amber glow bleeding through a heavy mist. Condensation makes the air feel close and heavy despite the cold. Breath fogs at once. Everything is damp to the touch, and metal tastes sharp on the tongue. The cold has a quality of settled stillness, and the mist hangs in layers, thicker near the floor, thinning toward the roof where the plates glow faintly with frost.

**Sound**

The sound is the defining fact of the place, and it is nearly unbearable. Water falls everywhere at once: a constant rain from the plates, a spatter on the pool, a trickle in every seam, a hiss where drops strike the hot pipes. Beneath it run the machines. Two great pumps cycle in a fixed rhythm, a grinding thud followed by a pneumatic sigh, roughly every eleven seconds. Valves click open and shut on the same timing. Over all of this is the drone of the fauna. Their wings and bodies beat against the mist in a hum so dense it becomes a pressure in the ears. At certain moments a chorus of clicks and whistles rises from the rafters, layered like a crowd, and then falls back into the general roar.

**Ecology**

The fauna are overwhelming in number. Most are small, pale, many-legged things that cling to the plates and the rib-beams in clusters. Their bodies are translucent, and they drink from the falling water, collecting it in their gut-sacs and then spitting it into their building material: fine silt and the crushed lichen from outside, bound together with a secretion that hardens quickly. Over the years they have built hanging nests and lace-like tubes that drape from the ribs and link the plates into a web. Some of the tubes run into the pools and draw from them. The larger creatures are rarer, long-legged and blind, and they move through the mist like slow shadows, tending the nests and sometimes taking the falling drops straight from the air on their fore-limbs. Nothing here eats anything that isn't water, mineral, or another of the same. The system is closed and nearly complete. Its only inflow is the air the machine pulls in, and its only outflow is the water that drains away through the floor.

**Rules of the place**

Time does not seem to move here. The pumps cycle in the same rhythm, the water falls at the same rate, and the flowstone grows so slowly that its shape at any hour looks like its shape at every other hour. The fauna's population holds steady, with births and deaths balancing, so the nests look the same from one visit to the next. The place is in a frozen equilibrium: everything is in motion, and nothing changes. It is the kind of place that seems to have been waiting for someone to notice it, and once noticed, it seems to resist being left.
