# The Weeping Basin

The land here has been worn down so long that it has forgotten it was ever mountain. It lies in a shallow bowl perhaps two hundred paces across, rimmed by a ridge of stone so eroded it reads more like a long sigh in the ground than a wall. The bedrock is pale, banded, and nearly level. Its surface has the smoothness of old riverbed, pitted with thousands of small hollows where water has pooled and drained for longer than anyone could count. Nothing sharp survives. Even the cracks have rounded edges.

Water is everywhere, but not as flood. It seeps. Every seam in the stone beads with moisture, and the rock itself seems to sweat, so that the whole floor shines faintly under the sun. Thin sheets of water slide across the flats in no particular direction, then gather into a dozen shallow mirror-pools joined by channels no deeper than a finger. The air is saturated. Every surface carries a fine film of condensation, and in the early morning a pale mist lies over the basin like a held breath. By midday it burns off, and by afternoon the sky clouds over in a slow, deliberate build, releasing a warm rain that lasts an hour and leaves the stones steaming.

## Light and Air

The sun is high and fierce, and the light has a quality of being reflected from everywhere at once. Standing water throws it back up under the leaves, so the shadows are thin and bright-edged. Shade is rare and precious, and the creatures seek it with the same urgency as anything else here.

The heat is steady, not punishing. It is the warmth of a greenhouse or a tropical lowland, thick and humid. Breathing feels like drinking.

Odd things happen to the light. Near the largest pool, shadows fall a few degrees off from where the sun should cast them, as if the pool is pulling them slightly sideways. Reflections are wrong too. The water shows a sky that is a shade darker and more violet than the real one, and in it, sometimes, something moves that is not in the basin at all. People who have watched this for long say the reflection lags by a breath, like a second observer slightly out of step with the first.

## Vegetation

The plant life is lush and low. Nothing here stands tall. Broad, fleshy leaves lie flat against the rock in overlapping rosettes, each as wide as a shield, their surfaces ridged to channel water toward the center where it collects in a small reservoir. Between them grow fern-like fronds with translucent tips that glow faintly in the morning, and mats of something like moss but silver-green and faintly ridged, which spread across the wet stone and creak softly when stepped on.

The vegetation is dense where water collects and thins to bare stone where it doesn't, so the basin is mapped in greens and grays, with the living parts clustering around the pools like rings on a tree stump. Where the leaves have grown over the edge of a channel, they form natural bridges, springy and treacherous.

Some of the plants are closed to the sky and open only at night, when their inner surfaces release a faint luminous dust that drifts down and settles on the pools, feeding something beneath.

## Fauna

This is the most crowded part of the place, and the most silent.

Creatures fill every gap. Small, soft-bodied things move through the leaf litter in their thousands: segmented crawlers the color of wet clay, flat things like thrown cloth that slide over the rock, six-legged skitterers that leave faint trails of luminous slime. In the pools, pale shapes hang suspended, fanning themselves with translucent frills. Above the leaves, winged things drift in slow spirals, their wings so thin they seem to be made of the same mist as the morning.

Nothing here is loud. The creatures move like wet fabric and feed on each other without a sound. Their communication appears to be chemical or visual; they flicker in pulses of color along their bodies, and whole swarms shift hue at once as if passing a message along. When a predator strikes, there is no cry, only a sudden stillness spreading outward from the point of attack, then the scatter.

Predators here are patient. Some lie flat under the leaves and wait with their frills spread, indistinguishable from the stone. Others are plants, rosettes whose inner reservoirs close around anything that touches the water they hold, and digest slowly over several hours. The basin's economy is a long, quiet chain of ambush and absorption, running continuously.

## The Built Things

Human-scale ruin is absent, but other traces are present, and they are not quite like anything a builder would make.

Along the rim, roughly every thirty paces, stands a half-sunk pillar of pale metal-like material, its surface dimpled with thousands of tiny hexagonal pores. They look like condensers. Air moves through them slowly, and where they stand, the moisture in the basin is thicker. Some still work: their pores exude beads of water that run down the outer face and feed the channels. Others have clogged over with moss and no longer do anything except slowly rot into the stone.

At the basin's center, almost submerged in the largest pool, is a structure like a flattened lattice of fine filaments, each one thinner than hair, arranged in patterns that repeat at increasingly small scales. It does not reflect light properly; it looks dull and dark even in full sun, and the water around it is perfectly clear and cold, several degrees cooler than anywhere else in the basin. Nothing grows directly on it. Creatures avoid it. The filaments seem to pull water inward and downward, and sometimes, on still afternoons, a faint vibration can be felt through the soles of the feet, slow and regular, like a heart somewhere deep under the rock.

## Rules and Patterns

A few things seem to hold constant here, though nothing is written down.

Water moves toward the center. Whatever the lattice does, the seeping and channeling all resolve inward, so the largest pool is always the deepest and the warmest in the morning.

Time runs at a pace that feels slightly stretched. Growth is visible within days. Predation cycles are measured in hours. But the stone itself barely changes, and the pillars along the rim have been the same height for as long as anyone has noticed.

The afternoon rain is not random. It arrives within a narrow window, and its intensity follows the lattice's faint pulse. When the vibration in the rock quickens, the clouds come sooner.

And the shadows, the reflections, and the slight lag in the water do not change at all. They are simply part of the place, the way the color of the sky is part of the sky.

## Sound

Almost nothing. The loudest sound is the seep of water into the stone, a soft continuous hush like breath through cloth. Occasionally a drop falls from a leaf into a pool and rings faintly, and the ring carries across the basin for several seconds before fading. Footsteps on the moss creak. Otherwise the silence is thick enough to feel, and crowded with things that are moving constantly and making no noise at all.

---

It is a small place, a room-sized world with its own weather and its own slow machinery, full of life moving in silence over stone that has been worn almost flat by time. Standing at its rim, you feel that you are looking at a complete system that has been running, unhurried, since before there was anyone to notice it, and that it will keep running after the last of the watchers has left.
