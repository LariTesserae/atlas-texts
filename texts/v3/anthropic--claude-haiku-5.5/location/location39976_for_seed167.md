**The Drowned Basin of Ysk**

The whole world here is water, and it is warm. It lies in a broad, shallow-bowled depression, a former valley floor sunk beneath a sea that filled it long ago and never drained. The water is the color of strong tea gone black at depth, thick with suspended silt that never fully settles. Nothing reaches it from above. Whatever light once came through the surface is long gone, and what remains is a perpetual dusk, a brown-black dimness in which shapes resolve only at arm's length and then dissolve. Warm currents rise from vents in the basin floor and bend the murk into slow, shifting ribbons.

**The city**

Beneath this sea lies a city. It was built on the valley floor when the land was still dry and the builders were not sea-dwellers, and it has been submerged so long that its architecture has become part of the reef. The buildings are terraced stone, their walls thick and squared, their roofs flat and set with rows of drainage channels that now serve as burrows. Streets run in a grid across the basin. Their paving has cracked and heaved, and in the cracks grow pale calcareous crusts, layered like the rings of a tree, built up over centuries by the slow accretion of shell and lime.

The city is not dead. Its ruins are the scaffolding for a new ecology. Doorways have become gill-slits for the currents. Plazas have filled with fine sediment that holds worm-tubes and fan-shaped sponges. The upper stories of the tallest towers reach toward a surface that is only dimly suggested. Above them the water thins, and a faint, unstable brightness flickers, but the light never makes it down to the streets.

Nothing built here has moving parts. No machines, no devices, no wheels or hinges. Every structure was raised by hand and stacked by hand, so the city feels more like a massive geological formation than a place made by minds. Its lines are human in their geometry and inhuman in their wear.

**Life**

Fauna is dense, and it is everywhere. Shoals of small silver fish move through the avenues in rolling clouds, turning together and splitting apart. Larger forms patrol the edges: long-bodied eels that coil into drainpipes, armored crustaceans the size of a dog that walk the walls on jointed legs, and a slow, blade-finned predator that hunts the shoals from the rooftops and sends whole flocks scattering in silent bursts. Octopus-like forms wrap around pillars and change color in pulses that flicker along the street like failing lamps. Where the buildings meet the open water, a constant traffic of drifting plankton, jelly-things, and bristling worms makes the currents thick with living matter.

Vegetation is sparse. Thin films of algae cling to sun-less stone, clumped in dark olive and rust. Occasional patches of sea-grass lean in the currents like hair, and a few rooted fans of pale kelp grow where the flow is steady. There are no forests, no canopies, only scattered pioneers that cling to the margins of the city. Their rarity makes them important: the grazers who depend on them move in tight groups around each patch.

**Sound**

The basin is mostly quiet, and the quiet is the first thing a traveler notices. Sound travels well through water, so every noise is clear and carries far. Clicks and rasps from the shoals ripple through the streets. Shell-knocks echo from doorways as crustaceans shift their weight. The warm vents hum at a low, steady pitch, and the buildings themselves creak under pressure and temperature, groaning in long, slow tones like a ship's hull. Once in a while a deep, distant pulse rolls through the basin, perhaps a large animal turning, perhaps the settling of a collapsed wall. Then the quiet returns.

**Rules of the place**

No light reaches the streets. Creatures navigate by touch, pressure, vibration, and the faint chemical trails the currents carry. Bioluminescence exists but is brief and defensive, a flash to startle or warn, never a lantern. The water is warm enough that metabolisms run fast; things here grow, breed, and die in short cycles, and the city's ruins are being remade faster than the sediment can bury them.

The basin moves in pulses. Thermal currents rise and fall on a cycle of days. Every few seasons, a surge of cooler, denser water sinks from some unseen margin and sweeps the lower streets clean of silt, exposing bare stone for a time before the sediment returns. During these pulses the shoals migrate upslope and spawn in the towers, and the predators follow them up, so the whole basin shifts in a rolling tide of feeding and breeding.

**Movement**

To cross Ysk is to swim slowly through a drowned metropolis, following the lines of streets whose names have been lost along with everything else. You pass through doorways into rooms full of drifting silt and staring crustaceans. You skirt the edges of plazas where the shoals gather and part around you like smoke. You feel the warm pulse of vents against your skin and the tug of currents that hint at the basin's deep, slow breathing. The city above you is a ghost of what it was, but its bones carry a different kind of life now, one that has no interest in what the buildings were for and every interest in what they can hold.
