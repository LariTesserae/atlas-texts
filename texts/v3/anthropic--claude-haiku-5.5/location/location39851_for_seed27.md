**The Upland Lattice**

You stand on a plateau that was once the floor of a sea. The sea is gone so long that its absence has become the landscape's main fact. The ground is a pale, fine-grained salt-stone, worn by millennia into long low swells, like the backs of enormous sleeping animals half-buried in dust. Nothing sharp survives. Every edge has been rounded by wind, and the rock has the matte, bone-colored softness of something that has finished changing.

The air is thin and cold. At this elevation the sky is a dull bruise-violet, and the sun, a copper coin behind a permanent haze, gives only a dim amber light that never sharpens into shadow. Things here look as if they are seen through old glass. Distances flatten. Color comes in muted tones, except where the living growth catches what light there is and throws it back in green-gold sparks.

**The growth**

Vegetation covers almost everything, but not in the way one expects. There is no forest and no meadow. The plant mass is structural. It grows as a continuous lattice, pale grey-green filaments thick as cables braided into open frameworks that climb, span, and hang. Where the salt-stone rises in ridges, the lattice has grown over the ridges like a net over a carcass, and where it has grown over ruined masonry, the masonry is hard to find. Walls, stairs, and arches are visible only as the regular geometry the growth has inherited: a ring of columns, a buttressed hall, a row of terraces whose stepped outlines the plants have kept while dissolving the stone beneath.

Water is absent, and the plants have adapted to that absence in a way that affects everything. Their filaments are covered in fine hygroscopic hair that draws moisture from the cold air and from the faint mist that forms at dusk. At night, the lattice drips. The drip collects in shallow mineral basins worn into the rock, and at dawn these basins are the only pools on the plateau, thin and cloudy and rich with dissolved salts. Animals gather at them in shifting crowds and the plants lean toward them, their hair quivering.

The plants are dense only in their structure. Between the lattice-frames there are wide gaps: open terraces of bare salt-stone, long corridors of dust, clearings where a single column stands alone with no growth on it at all. The density is low, the scattering wide, and the emptiness between presences feels deliberate, as if the plateau were leaving room for something.

**The built**

The buildings are old and large. Towers rise in clusters, grown rather than built, though some of their geometry is far too regular for growth alone. Their surfaces are ribbed and faceted, with apertures that open and close slowly as the sun moves. Some of the towers are fully swallowed by lattice, appearing as dense green-grey columns with the rest of the city showing only as scaffolding beneath them. Others are almost bare, their surfaces dull metallic-ceramic, pitted and scoured but still intact. Between them run low causeways of fused salt-glass, cracked into hexagonal plates, and along the causeways stand tall posts that hum.

The engineering is not something the present understands. Certain things are clear. The apertures track light. The posts emit pulses at regular intervals, and the pulses change the behavior of the lattice near them: filaments coil tight around a post during a pulse and relax afterward, like muscles. The posts seem to be part of a larger system, possibly a circulation network, because mineral-rich drip moves along the causeways in channels that seem to have been designed for it, and because in certain places the drip reverses, flowing uphill toward the towers at the pulse rhythm. The plants and the city have grown into a single machine, and no one can say whether the machine was built to feed the plants or whether the plants were grown to run the machine.

**Sound**

The plateau is loud. Wind passes through the lattice and produces a sustained, many-voiced drone, a low chord that rises and falls with the gusts. Over it come the sharper sounds: the clicks and rasps of fauna in the lattice, the knocking of hollow filaments striking one another, the hiss of drip falling on dry stone, and, most insistently, the pulses from the posts, a deep resonant thrum felt in the sternum more than heard. Between pulses there is a brief silence in which the drone seems to hold its breath, and then the next pulse comes and the whole plateau answers.

**Fauna**

The animals are abundant and mostly small. Long-legged things with segmented bodies move through the lattice in groups, clinging to filaments and chattering. Some have glassy wings that catch the amber light and throw it in flashes. Larger forms are rarer. Heavy, slow grazers with armored backs move across the terraces in herds and crop the lattice tips, and where they pass, the filaments regrow within days, bright and fresh. There are also predators: quick, low-slung shapes that hunt along the causeways in pairs, their movements matched to the pulse rhythm, striking when the posts thrum and the grazers' attention is caught by the sound.

**Rhythm and motion**

Everything here moves on several clocks at once. The wind shifts across hours. The drip cycles through the night and dries by noon. The apertures open and close with the sun's slow arc. The posts pulse on their own rhythm, and the lattice breathes with them, coiling and loosening over minutes. The fauna follow the drip and the pulses, feeding and hunting in tides. Beneath all of it, the rock erodes almost imperceptibly, and the growth reshapes the city by fractions every season.

The whole plateau is in motion without being restless. It has reached a settled state, but the settlement is a balance of many living processes, and the balance is maintained, rather than fixed.

**The unsettling part**

Walk for long enough and you notice the regularity. The ridges run in a pattern. The clusters of towers are spaced at intervals that seem chosen. The posts, if you map them, form lines that converge on a point far off in the haze, where the lattice grows so thick it becomes opaque, a dark green-grey mass that the light does not reach and the sound does not carry out of. The fauna avoid that point. The drip does not flow toward it. Something there is either very old or very new, and the plateau has arranged itself around it the way a body arranges itself around a healing wound, or around a heart.
