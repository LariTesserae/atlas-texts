The basin lies below the country around it, a long trough about the width of a river valley and several times its length, sunk into a plateau that has only recently started to come apart. The fracture is still visible. The rim on the east side stands in raw steps, and every ledge is a clean edge. Nothing has had time to round off. The stone is a dark, vesicular volcanic rock, cooled from flows that poured across the land in some earlier age and then split when the ground dropped beneath them. Its surfaces are glassy where they broke, and pale where a thin crust of lichen has started. Blocks lie at the foot of the walls in heaps, angular and unweathered, with sharp corners that still catch the light.

The air is cool and always a little wet. It doesn't rain so much as hang. A fine mist settles in the lower part of the basin in the early hours and stays there, thickening along the floor, and by midmorning it has mostly lifted into a grey overcast that never breaks into full sun. The light is dim and even, without shadows of any strength. Everything looks slightly underwater. Colors come through muted: the rock is charcoal and rust, the lichen is a dull silver-green, the water is the color of pewter. Distant walls fade into a haze that makes them seem farther away than they are.

Water is everywhere, but in pockets and threads rather than as a single river. Seeps come out of the cracks in the basin walls at many levels and run down in thin, clear sheets across the stone. Where they meet a flat bench they gather into shallow pools, some no wider than a cart, some long and narrow, stepping down the basin floor one into another. Some of the pools have no visible outlet; they simply sit, slowly rising and falling into the rock. A few drain through channels worn into the lava and vanish underground, and their sound, a hollow gurgling, comes up from below your feet in places where the stone is thin. There is a larger sheet of water at the far south end where the basin narrows, and it falls over a lip of black stone in a steady veil. The roar of that falls carries the full length of the valley and never stops, and it is the floor under every other sound.

Vegetation is sparse but tenacious. It grows where it can get purchase: in the joints of the broken rock, on the wet faces of the seep-lines, along the margins of the pools. Low scrub with grey, wiry stems and small leathery leaves forms patchy mats. Thick-bodied succulents with pale, translucent tissue crowd the cracks and store water, their surfaces beaded with condensation. Mosses blanket the north-facing walls in a soft, dark green sheet. There are no trees. The basin is too young and too exposed to the seepage's shifting edges for anything tall to root, and the wind that funnels through the fissures keeps the growth low and flattened. Between the plants the bare stone shows through, glittering wet in the gloom.

The fauna is the most astonishing thing about the place. It is overwhelming in number, and it fills every layer of the basin. The air above the pools is crowded with insects: midges in columns, gnats in clouds, long-legged flies skating on the surface film. The water itself teems. Amphibians with pale, mottled skins crowd the shallow edges in their hundreds, calling in a nearly continuous chorus that rises and falls but never quite stops. Under the surface, in the clear pools, small fish and crustaceans move in dense shoals, and the bottom is littered with their shells and the husks of the things that eat them. Along the walls, bats hang in the fissures in huge clusters, and at dusk they pour out in a dark ribbon that circles the basin and comes back before the mist has finished rising. Birds are everywhere, mostly small and grey-brown, flocking through the scrub and perching on the ledges in long rows. Their calls mix with the frogs and the insects into a dense, layered noise.

The sound of the place is loud, but it is a steady, layered loudness rather than anything sudden. The falls form the base. Above it, the frog chorus and the insect hum and the bird calls braid together, and seeps drip and trickle and splash in a hundred small voices. Occasionally a stone breaks loose from the rim and comes clattering down the steps, and the echo bounces along the walls for a long time before fading. Wind moves through the fissures with a hollow, breathy note. Nothing in the basin is quiet, but nothing shouts over anything else either.

There are signs of tool use, though no makers. Among the scrub and at the edges of the larger pools, there are small piles of flaked chips of the dark rock, some with a crude edge still on them, as if something has struck stone against stone for a purpose. In one place a split length of stem lies beside a shallow hole dug into the mud, and the end of the stem is worn smooth from use. The animals here seem to be the ones doing this. Some kind of grey-furred burrowing creature with long forelimbs has been seen, in the sense that its trails run between the piles and its diggings appear beside them, and the tool-marks look like its work. It is not clear whether the chips were made to be used or are only the litter of its habits. Either way, the nearest thing to human craft in the basin is the animals' own.

Nothing built, nothing made by any hand that shaped a permanent thing. The stone walls are the only architecture, and they are the work of the earth's breaking. There are no paths cut through the rock, no marks of fire, no abandoned shelters. Everything here is as it came out of the fracture, and it is how it has been for as long as anyone who might have passed through would have known.

The basin does not change. The mist comes at the same hour and lifts at the same hour. The bats pour out at dusk and return at dawn in the same pattern. The seeps run at the same rate through the same cracks, and the same pools hold the same depth. Light comes up grey in the morning, dims toward evening, and never gets any brighter or darker than its normal range. The frogs call through the same cycle, the birds rest on the same ledges, the falls keep their steady weight. Whatever happens here, it happens inside a balance that does not drift, a frozen equilibrium in which the sunken fracture has settled into the particular shape it holds now, and every gradient in it has already been spent.
