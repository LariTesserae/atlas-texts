# The Weeping Highlands

You stand on a plateau that rises out of cloud, though the cloud is not overhead. It is around you, and it moves through the trees at walking pace. The land climbs in long stepped shelves toward ranges so far off that their outlines blend into the grey. Everything here is wet. The air holds water the way a sponge does, and each surface gives it back.

**Water and rock.** The bedrock is a dark, fractured basalt, laid down in ancient flows and then cut into terraces by rivers that have long since learned their courses. Water seeps from nearly every face. The rock weeps in thin sheets that run down the stone and gather in shallow, mirror-still pools on each ledge. The pools overflow into one another in cascades so gentle they make no sound, and the whole plateau is veined with threads of runoff that braid and rejoin across the land like the veins of a leaf. Where the seepage is heaviest, the stone is slick with a green-black film, and the drops that fall from the overhangs keep a steady, patient rhythm, the only regular beat in the place.

**Vegetation, packed to the edges.** The forest is layered so thickly that there is no ground you can see. Tree-ferns rise in colonnades, their crowns thirty meters up and dripping. Below them stand giant horsetails in dense stands, hollow-stemmed and ridged, with branches whorled like green fireworks. The understory is moss, and the moss is itself a habitat: cushion mosses, feather mosses, and mats of liverworts thick enough to hold a footprint for hours before slowly rising back. On every trunk and branch, epiphytes crowd for space: filmy ferns, bromeliad-like rosettes cupping their own small ponds, lichens in rust, ochre, and pale blue-grey, and fungal shelves in tiers like stacked plates. Nothing is bare. Where one organism ends, another begins before the first has finished.

**Atmosphere and weather.** Cloud is the weather here, and it has its own life. Banks of fog move through the valleys in slow, deliberate currents, pooling in hollows and lifting off ridges like breath. Every few hours the cloud thickens and the land is rained on, a soft, steady downpour that drums on the broad fern fronds, then clears in a sudden breath of sunlight that turns every leaf to glass. The light is ordinary daylight, but the water in the air diffuses it, so shadows are soft and colours deepen: greens go almost blue in the far distance, and the rocks take on a sheen. Cool air sinks into the valleys at dusk and the cloud thickens into mist that beads on everything and drips for hours after the sky has gone dark.

**The engines.** Here the strangeness begins. Scattered across the plateau are machines: enormous, slow, and industrial in their scale. They are not mounted on foundations and they leave no roads, no cuts, and no buildings. They are huge hulks of riveted metal, pumps and condensers and rotating drums the size of houses, and they move. They crawl on articulated legs or broad treads through the moss in deliberate loops, their intakes drawing in cloud and their outflows spilling water into the ground. Over time, moss has grown over their plating, ferns have rooted in their seams, and fungus has colonised their interiors. Some have been so long in the forest that they are indistinguishable from the rock except by their rectilinear outlines. They do not seem to be building anything. They seem to be grazing on the fog, working the air for water and returning it to the soil. Their hum is so low it is felt in the chest before it is heard, a slow tidal thrum that fades in and out as the machines pass.

**Fauna, abundant and layered.** The animal life fills every level. Frogs the size of a fist sit in every pool, their calls a patient chorus in the grey mornings. Small, many-legged arthropods with shell-like backs graze the moss in tight herds. Among the tree-ferns, flocks of pale, long-necked birds feed on the fungal shelves, moving in sweeps that rustle the canopy and then fall silent. Insects hang in thick clouds above the water. Larger creatures move through the undergrowth: heavy, slow, grey-hided herbivores with ridged backs that browse the horsetails and leave long channels through the moss. Predators follow them, low and quiet, using the fog as cover. Every surface is contested, and every movement is someone's meal or someone's threat.

**Sound.** The place is quiet, but never silent. The drip of water, the patter of rain on fronds, the soft suck of mud under a heavy foot, the distant frogs, and beneath everything, the low breathing of the engines. Sound carries strangely in the cloud, muffled close up and oddly clear at a distance. You may hear a flock wheel overhead long before you see it.

**Rules of the place.** Water is the currency and the medium. Everything here is shaped by its movement: where it pools, where it runs, where the fog settles and the sun breaks through. The engines are the strangest feature, and they are part of the ecology, not above it. They drink the fog and return it to the soil, feeding the forest that feeds the animals that feed the predators. Nothing is built to last here, and nothing needs to be. Things rot, regrow, get swallowed by moss, and the plateau keeps all of it in a long, wet balance that has been running for ages and shows no sign of ending.
