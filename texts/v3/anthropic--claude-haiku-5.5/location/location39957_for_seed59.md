**The Shelf of Weeping Stone**

The land rises in a bowl of grey andesite, a cirque about four hundred paces across, open only on its eastern lip where a river has cut a notch and falls away into cloud. Everything here is at altitude. The air is thin and cold enough that breath shows in the mornings, and the ground never fully dries. Water seeps from every joint in the rock. The cliffs weep in long dark streaks, and the terraces are green-black with standing wet.

**The terraces**

Long ago people cut the bowl into steps. Eleven terraces stack down the inner wall like the rungs of a broken ladder, each a retaining wall of fitted stone with a level strip behind it. The stone has weathered into soft rounded edges, and moss has filled every seam. Nobody has rebuilt a wall here in living memory. The terraces are older than the people who use them, and they are settled in the way old things are: they bulge, sag, and lean, and the balance between wall and pressure has held for generations.

**Plant as architecture**

The vegetation is total. You cannot tell where the town ends and the forest begins, because the town is built out of the forest. Trunks of a tree with pale fibrous bark have been trained into posts and beams, and their living crowns have been grown into roofs. Lateral roots of a broad-leafed vine have been guided across the gaps between terraces to form footbridges as thick as a man's thigh, and they thicken a little every year. Houses are living lattices of woven stems with a clay skin. Ladders are made of climbing shoots that have been pruned into rungs. Between these structures, the ground is a dense carpet of ferns, lichens, creeping herbs, and fungal shelves, and it rises as vertical walls of epiphytes, orchids, and moss hung from the cliff face. Everything is growing into everything else. Trimming holds it back, but the plants always push back.

**Fauna**

The fauna is overwhelming. The air is full of wings. Small nectar birds with iridescent throats dart at every flower. Broad-bodied moths the size of palms rest on the stone walls, and they open and close their wings slowly as if breathing. Frogs live in the water-filled hollows of every tree and in the cracks of the stone, and their calls are layered, rising and falling in waves. Tree-climbing animals with long prehensile tails move along the bridges in small crowds and shake the canopy as they pass. Leeches gather on the wet paths in the morning. Something with many legs is always moving under the litter, and the fungal shelves are alive with grazers that scatter when you approach. Insects are everywhere: beetles that glow faintly green in the shade, hovering flies, ants running in lines along the stems, and clouds of tiny midges that rise at dusk. You learn to walk with your arms slightly raised and your mouth closed.

**Sound**

Sound is loud and layered. Water is the base layer: springs trickle from the walls, the river roars over the notch, and every hollow has a drip. Over that come the frogs and birds, then the insects, then the clatter of people. Wooden mallets knock on stone and fiber. Voices carry across the terraces, and the calls of traders and herders bounce off the cliff. Every few minutes a large limb cracks and falls somewhere in the canopy with a heavy wet sound, and the animals scatter in a burst of noise before settling again.

**Light and atmosphere**

The light is ordinary daylight, diffuse and grey-white most of the time, as cloud sits in the bowl through the morning. When it breaks, shafts fall through the canopy and the wet surfaces flare. The air is saturated and cold. Mist collects at the terrace edges by dawn and burns off by midday, only to return in the afternoon as the valley cools. Everything smells of wet bark, rot, bruised leaves, and wood smoke.

**Life and tools**

The people here work with what is at hand. They make stone axes, fiber cords, bone needles, and clay pots fired in pits. They have no metal. They dry fish and fungus on racks, weave baskets from split stems, and carve tools from hard wood. Trade is constant. Along the lowest terrace, a market forms each morning on the flat stone where three bridges meet. Goods come up on the river raft and go down on the backs of carriers. Bargaining is loud, quick, and sometimes violent. The town never stops moving. Plants grow a finger's width every day, walls are patched, houses are re-woven, fungus is harvested and replanted, and animals wander through the market and are shooed out again. The place is in constant small motion, and it has been this way long enough that the motion is its stability.

**Rules of the place**

Water is never far away, and whoever controls the springs controls the terraces. Wood that is cut must be replaced by planting, or the bridges fail. A walkway that is not walked on is taken back by the forest within a season, and a house that is not tended collapses into the undergrowth. The fauna is dangerous in its numbers but rarely in its individuals. Moths and birds do not hunt people, but a swarm can strip a person in minutes, so people carry smoke-pots and move in groups. Every new season brings a storm that rips down a few limbs and carries off a bridge or two. The people rebuild before the next storm arrives. The place endures because it is always being remade.
