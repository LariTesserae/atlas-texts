**The Planing**

The land here has been worn down for so long that it no longer has a shape of its own. Mountains once stood where this plain now lies, and rain took them grain by grain until only their roots remained. Those roots are domes of black-glassed bedrock, rounded like the backs of sleeping animals, and they rise a few meters out of a sheet of water that covers everything. The sheet is never more than a hand's depth. It doesn't drain, because the stone beneath is so smooth and so old that nothing soaks into it. The water simply lies there, a skin of standing light over a floor that has forgotten how to be rough.

The air is saturated. Fine vapor hangs in it at every height, and it condenses on every surface, so the domes weep constantly in threads that run down their flanks and join the sheet. It almost rains, but never quite. Each breath tastes of iron and something sweet, like cut green stems. Everything you touch is slick, cold at the surface and warm beneath, as if the land is slowly exhaling.

**Light**

The sun is there, but it is a smudge of pale amber behind a ceiling of its own vapor, a layer so thick and so constantly churning that it never clears. The light falls evenly from everywhere and casts almost no shadows. It is dim enough that distant domes lose their edges and merge into the haze, and it reflects off the water so that the ground seems to glow faintly from below. In the deeper hollows between domes the light goes violet and then nearly gone, and things there are only shapes until you are close.

**Sound**

The plain is loud in a way that feels physical. The weather overhead is a single system the size of a continent, and its roar never stops. It is a low, continuous sound that you feel through your sternum before you hear it. Over it come the nearer noises: water sliding off glass in sheets, thousands of small clicks and tinkles as droplets strike the sheet, and a dense, crackling hiss that rises from the water itself. Sound travels strangely here. Echoes return from the wrong directions, sometimes before the sound that caused them, so that you hear a step and then hear its arrival behind you, as though the air is remembering the sound and sending it back out of order.

**The Weather That Is the Land**

The storm overhead is not separate from the ground. Cells of pressure form and dissolve across the plain within minutes. A rise of wet air will lift the sheet into a standing column of water, a cylinder kilometers tall that hums and then collapses into a fan of spray. Where a column falls, the bedrock is briefly exposed and gleams black, then the sheet closes over it again. Domes are sometimes buried and sometimes emerge in new places as the water shifts. Maps made here age in days. The land is always arriving and departing, and nothing holds its place for long.

**The Packed Presences**

The water is not empty. It is dense with motes, filaments, and tiny clustering bodies, too small to see individually but visible as drifting smoke when they gather. They form into knots, split apart, and reform into different knots, always moving, like a crowd in a market that never disperses. Where they thicken near the surface, the water turns milky and the hiss grows louder. Everything that lives here is part of this churn, and the churn fills all the space between things. There is no empty room in the air or under the water. Every gap holds something moving.

**Sparse Growth**

Plants are few and strange. Low crusts of grey-green mat cling to the north faces of the domes, where the weeping is steadiest. They are tough and dense and they hold the rock together in patches. Occasionally a stalk rises from the sheet, pale and jointed like wet bone-reed. It grows fast, flowers in a few hours, and then folds into the water and dissolves, leaving a faint ring of foam on the surface. Nothing here is meant to last.

**Fauna**

Animals are rare, and you mostly see them as wakes: long, slow V-shaped ripples moving across the sheet against the wind of the storm. Once in a while a shape rises close enough to see, a broad, flat back the color of wet slate, and then it sinks and the surface closes without a splash. Their size is hard to judge in the dim light. They seem to avoid the columns.

**Ruins Without Makers**

Some built things remain, but they are not the work of anyone who used tools. They are stone, arranged in ways that suggest deliberate placement long ago: a line of pillars half-sunk so that only their capitals show, a stair descending into the water with the steps worn to smooth curves, a doorframe standing alone in the sheet with nothing on either side, its lintel carved with grooves that run in no clear direction. Some of these face one way, and some are angled as though the ground has turned under them. The stone is also weathered almost flat, its carvings reduced to faint suggestions. Whoever shaped these places left no clear sign of who they were, only the sense that the place once had more edges.

**The Rules Here**

Things here do not hold their shape for long. Water becomes solid for a moment and then liquid again. Sound arrives out of order. Domes move slightly every time the sheet shifts, so that a landmark seen at dawn is somewhere else by noon. Distance is unreliable, because the haze and the echoes make far things seem near and near things seem far. Time moves in cycles that line up with the storm rather than the light. Traveling through the Planing means accepting that you will never find the same place twice, and that the land is busy making and unmaking itself around you while the roar of its weather fills every breath.
