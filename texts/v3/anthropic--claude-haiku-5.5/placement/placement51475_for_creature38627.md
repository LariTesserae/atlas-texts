I come up the stair at the margin, where the shore gives way to steps worn so smooth they feel poured. The current reaches me first, a slow upward breath that smells of wet clay and, under the clay, something mineral and clean, like flint struck once in a dark room. I don't know yet what I am. The warmth decides for me. I am small, and I am not in a hurry.

Up here the water thins. My body, which below would have floated easily among the drifters, tightens against the air-cool stone of the upper terrace. I know how to do this. I have done it before, or something that carried my pattern did. I pull myself into a hollow where the water never reaches, a niche lined with fired tile and filled with fine grey sediment that takes my weight the way old flour does.

The sediment is still and dry. My body is not built for dryness, so I move carefully and stop often. Each shift leaves a scrape. I don't mean to write anything. The marks simply follow from the way I move, the way a stream's bends follow from the shape of its bed.

The tile beside me has a spiral pressed into it, the edges blurred to a suggestion. I touch it with the tip of one feeler and feel the fired grain, a little rough, a little warm from the stone beneath. Someone pressed this into wet clay for a reason. A count, perhaps, or a weaving draft, or a prayer for rain, or nothing so grand, only a mark a hand made while its owner thought about something else. I can't read it. I am not sure there is anything left to read. The meaning went out of it long ago, the way the edges went out of the spiral. What is left is the shape, and my own shape has the same curl at the back, a whorl I did not choose and cannot explain, pressed into me by generations of creatures growing up in this stone. Perhaps I am copying the makers. Perhaps I am only what a body becomes when it lives among their work for long enough. Nothing here is in a hurry to decide which.

I rest. My breath is slow enough now that I can feel the basin's pulse through the stone, the long rhythm that takes minutes to notice, rising faintly toward the central well and then receding. Below me the cloud of drifters is turning again. I can see the stone's glow shift through their pale ribbons as they wheel around the dark rim. Nobody calls them. The water rose a finger's width, or a gate hummed somewhere, or nothing happened at all, and they began.

I could join them. I think about it, the way one thinks about a stair. But I have come up here to visit, not to stay, and visiting is what this niche is for. When I go, I leave a small heap of grains beside the tile, a little dark pile the size of a seed. It is the only offering I have. Tomorrow the silt will settle over it. Next season it will be part of the floor, and some other small body will come up the stair, feel the spiral with its feeler, and leave its own scrape beside mine.

I back down the stair into the warm water. The current takes hold of me, and I let it. Below, the well stays dark and still, its rim worn into a shallow dip by hands that no longer come. I pass the grazers, whose furrows are already filling. I pass the shelled ones, laying their thin new skins over the old carving, one grain at a time. I am one of many small marks in a structure that is still being made, and that is enough.
