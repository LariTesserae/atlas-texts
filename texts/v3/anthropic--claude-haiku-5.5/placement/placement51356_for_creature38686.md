I would be standing on the stair where the air turns warm, the place where the green throat opens. I would not stay there long. The stair is only a threshold, and what I would want is to get down into the basin, where the sound is.

Then I would be a drop.

I would gather for a long time in the canopy, in a cup of broad leaf twelve meters above the floor. The leaf would hold me with the others, a small weight in a shallow bowl of green. I would feel the day's heat press down through the fronds, and I would feel the bowl slowly fill until its lip tips. I would not choose to fall. The leaf would simply stop holding me. I would drop through a shaft of pale daylight, through a column of seed-fluff and cicada noise, and the light would catch me for a moment so that I would see the whole basin lit from above: the iron arches, the bark-scaled boiler, the copper lattice flowering in its repeating patterns.

Then I would land on warm iron and begin to steam. The pump would thud through the metal under me, and in the gap after each thud I would hear the chorus rise and fall. I would be half vapor for a few seconds, drifting up toward the canopy I had just left, and then the cooling air would take me back down. I would fall into a cistern.

The cistern would be dark and full of frogs calling. I would find the water already crowded with other drops, the whole basin's rain gathered in one pool, and I would learn that nothing here is only itself for very long.

Then the creature would lower its head.

I would be pulled up through the long, slow draw of its mouth, and I would feel the throat close around me. I would go into the body as warm breath, into the fungus on its flanks, which would brighten where I passed. Then I would be spread through the fibers of the ribs, the pale stem-hard bones that run along its sides like braces on a wall. I would feel the plates grind against each other as it shifts its weight, and I would know, for a while, the exact rhythm of the belt and the pump, because the creature keeps that rhythm in its body.

I would sit in the rust that the fungus feeds on, and I would move with the iron. I would hear the gauges through the roots. The needles would tremble and the nodes on its brow would tremble with them, and I would be part of that trembling. I would be a message passing from the machine to the forest to the animal, and back again, with no one sending it.

Eventually the creature would lie down in the copper mesh, its nodes turned toward the gauges, and it would sweat. I would bead on the plates and run down the rivets, which look like scales polished by rain. The warm air would take me up again, and the canopy would hold me until its leaves tipped, and I would fall once more onto the iron and steam and drift.

That is who I would be: not the creature, not the machine, not the forest, but the water they share. I would move through each of them in turn and keep the basin's eleven-second beat as I went, so that whatever argument the pump and the roots were having, I would be the one thing that had no side in it and carried them both.
