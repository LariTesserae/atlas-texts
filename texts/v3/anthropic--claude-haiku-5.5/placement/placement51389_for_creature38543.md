# The Throat Under Stack Nine

I would be in the drain.

Not on the plain, not on the stacks. Under the pump housing at the base of the ninth stack, where the condensation runs down the fins and gathers in a black channel and falls into the dark. The water is warm there, and the sound is different. The drip becomes a hollow knocking, and the clicking of the swarm above comes down through the seam like a rhythm heard through a floor.

I am small. I am the length of a finger joint, and my plates are still soft at the edges. My pads have not hardened. When I first move, my legs do not lock. They slide, and I fall back into the channel and come up again. That is how I learn that the seams are not just surfaces. They are grooves, and a body has to fit into them.

## The first fit

The groove I find is a hand's width deep and runs along the wall of the drain. I push one pad into it and feel the edge take the weight. The joint ratchets once, and the sound goes through my whole body. Then I push a second pad in, and the first one locks, and I am moving.

I do not know where I am going. I only know the groove leads somewhere, and that the wall is warm and wet and the pads grip it. For a long time I climb. The channel bends, and I follow the bend without thinking, because the seam bends with it and my legs bend with the seam.

Then the drain opens, and I come out under the sky.

## The lowland, from below

The light is the first thing. It is not harsh. It is the same even light I have heard the others describe, though I have never heard anyone describe it. It lies across the plates in hard bands, and the cracked halves catch it and throw it back. The stacks rise around me, and their fins drip, and far away the horizon is a blue haze that looks like water standing in the air.

I climb onto the nearest seam and feel the edge under my pads. It is sharp, freshly cut, and the sheen on it is bright. I drag one pad along the groove and feel how the composite gives a little and then holds. The seam under me brightens where I pass. I do not think this is a choice. My body does it the way it clicks.

Around me the swarm is moving. Its patterns are fixed. A group passes the foot of the stack, circles it, climbs a gantry strut, then drops back down toward the drain I came out of. I watch them with the same attention I would give a word I almost remember. I know their routes, though I have not walked them. My legs seem to know the grooves before I touch them.

## The bowls

I walk the seams for a long time, and eventually I reach the old ground. The plates here are black and glassy, shaped into bowls and ridges that ring under my pads. I stop at the rim of the largest bowl. Its surface is scored with fine parallel lines, hundreds of them, so dense that they blur into a single sheen. Each line is the track of a body that passed here, and each body left a fresh surface on top of the last.

I touch the nearest line. It fits my pads exactly. I know, in the way I know the clicking, that this is where many routes have crossed, and that the ground here is kept sharper than anywhere else. I also know that I am not the first to fit it. Something older than me, something that may have been a creature or may have been a tool, walked this same groove long before the first extrusion stream set in the basin.

I do not know what that means. The routes do not explain themselves.

## The divergence

Here is the thing I have not told the others, because I do not think they would understand it, or because they would understand it too well.

On the way back toward the stacks, I find a groove that no group is walking. It runs off the edge of the bowl, along a seam that is a little duller than the rest, and the sheen on it is thinner. Nobody has tended it for a while. The composite is holding, but not as firmly as it does elsewhere.

I put one pad into it. It fits, but barely. It is not my route. My route is the one the drain gave me, the warm wall and the bend and the groove that led me out. This groove leads somewhere else, to a place I have not been and that no body in the swarm has marked.

I do not know whether a route can be made. The loops are fixed. The count does not change. The sheen holds exactly where it has always held. Everything I have seen says the pattern is the pattern, and the ground keeps it.

But my pads have locked into a groove that is not on any map I know. The ratchet of my joint has a different note, a single click that does not match the swarm's fabric.

I stay there longer than I should. Above me the drip falls on the same plates in the same rhythm. Behind me, the seam I left is brighter than it was.

Then I begin to move, slowly, along the duller edge. The seam takes my weight. Where I pass, the composite holds for one more moment, and then I am not sure whether I am tending the ground or the ground is tending me.
