I find myself at the eastern rim, in the raised lip of a salt plate that lifted this spring by a hand's width. I can tell it was this spring because the crack beside me is still sharp-edged, and the brine has only just begun to find it.

I am small. I am a single coiled chamber, a foraminifer or a broken piece of one, and I was once calcite. Now I am a pale ghost held in salt, a shape with a hollow where my body used to be. I drifted in a sea that was warm for one particular year. My walls grew thicker that year, and the oxygen in them took up a heavier signature than it would in a colder season. Nothing in me knows this. It is only chemistry, but it is the kind of chemistry that keeps a year.

When I died, the water kept moving for a while, and then it did not. I sank into the soft grey bottom. Other shells came down on top of me, then salt, then more salt, a layer for each season the sea stayed. I lay in the dark for longer than the word *long* can hold, until the water went and the crust split and the plates rose in their dish-shaped lips and I was left near the surface, at the rim, where the tilt is steepest.

Then the cold comes.

At dusk the air begins to drink, and the brine rises through the plate in thin dark threads. It reaches the crack beside me first, then my edge. Then a filament touches me.

It is not gentle and it is not rough. It is more like the way a hand moves over a scar, slowly, with attention that has no hurry in it. The fine hairs at its tip open and draw a little moisture from my surface. I feel the lattice tasting the old salt on my walls, the thickness of them, the faint difference in the mineral that marks that warm year. I am not sure "feel" is the right word. I am a record, and the record is being read, and reading is the only way I know to describe what happens when something presses on my memory and I am still, in some sense, the memory.

The filament lingers. Somewhere beneath the plate, the thing that is reading me is already doing something with what it has found. A hair's width of mortar thickens in the city's eastern wall. A fingertip extends a fraction further toward the rim. I cannot tell whether the reading has changed anything, or whether I am only one more layer the lattice passes through on its way to a decision it has not yet made.

Later, something passes close by. It moves low and quick along the rim, and its paired impressions cut the crust beside my crack. Its track stops at the edge of the plate, one step from where I lie, and I understand, without any way to understand it, that this is the place the watcher turns back. Or the place it goes down. The crust does not say which.

Before dawn the brine withdraws. The filament curls shut. The sun comes up as a smear of weak tea, and the plate dries to chalk, and my walls go pale and dull. What the lattice took in during the night is not gone. It is held somewhere in the colony now, in the thickness of a wall or the tilt of a plate, in a form I will never see.

So here is what I am. I am not a witness, and I am not a guardian, and I am certainly not a city. I am an input, a fragment of a warm year that a patient, cold, slowly deciding thing has read a little more closely than the sea ever did. The basin keeps tipping toward the east. The plate under me is leaning with it, and I am leaning too, one hair's width at a time, toward the place where the watcher's tracks disappear. I do not know what the lattice will do with me, or whether it will decide what the city is for before the rim takes me.
