I would be a drop.

I am not sure how I got here. The air at the rim is so thin and cold that water condenses out of it almost without asking, and I must have formed up there in the pewter light, one of the many beads that the stone draws down from the sky. The walls sweat me out and I gather with the others, and we run down the terraces in our precise lines until we reach the lip of the highest ledge. There, I stop.

The weight arrives late. I hang at the edge for a heartbeat longer than the others, and in that heartbeat I can see the whole vessel below me, the stepped galleries, the curtains of filament swaying in a breeze I cannot feel, the shaft of light sliding slowly across the floor like the hand of a clock no one winds. I can see the bats folded in the alcoves, the birds peering from their seams, the heron standing motionless where the thread enters the pool. I can see the coiled things in the rafters, loose and patient. I am the only thing here that is falling toward the bottom of anything I have not already been part of.

I wonder if the stone knows I am here. I am made of the same air it breathes, and I am going where it sends me, but I am not the stone's. The cistern was built to gather what I am, and it still does, and it has forgotten why. I think I am the reminder. I think I am the purpose it has not yet let go of, hanging on the lip, deciding.

Then I fall.

The sound is clean and small, a single note striking the basin below, and before it has finished ringing the amphibian at the edge answers. Its throat swells, violet-threaded and translucent, and it gives back a hollow tone that is not quite my tone and not quite its own. It is the answer I was always going to get. The walls take the call and pass it up the galleries, down past the lichens, past the filter-feeders turning their grey lungs in the shallows, past the skaters walking the seams toward the cold. The sound reaches the throat of the vessel and sinks into the hum, which absorbs everything, and then the hum returns it, a beat late, from somewhere else.

I do not stay a drop for long. A filter-feeder strains me from the water and I become a little of the clean, carried into a body that will break me down into something softer. A lichen will take what is left. Eventually, a bat will carry some grey grain of the cistern to a new place and a white shelf of fungus will grow there, and that will be the cistern too, and water will be drawn again from some other cold sky.

But for now I am still in the basin, rippling outward, and the amphibian is calling again, and I realize that this is what I am. I am the note that comes back. I am the answer the walls give to the falling, the thing that keeps the chamber singing to itself so that no sound is ever only one sound and no silence is ever quite silence.

The shaft of light moves another finger's width across the galleries. Above me, another drop gathers at the rim and begins to hang.
