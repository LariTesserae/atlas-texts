I would be the heavy animal that passes beneath the swell.

I come up from the deep channels under the western avenues, where the water is cold and the light is only a rumor. My body is long and slow, with fins where legs would be, and I move between the towers the way the current does, without hurry. I know the streets down here by their depth and their taste. The pipe runs taste of iron and brine. The biofilm tastes of green and something sweet. The sluice mouths taste of nothing, which I have learned to avoid.

I know the valves too, though not as the thing knows them. I feel them as changes in pressure, a push and then a give, like a hand on my flank. There is a release under the western avenues, then a pause, then two more close together. I have learned that the pause means the thing is deciding something, and the two close releases mean it has decided and is making room. When I pass beneath, the water in the mains bulges around me, and I feel the thing respond. A dozen valves open a little wider, a dozen more close a little, and the wave I make slides through the grid without tearing a flange or shaking a rail loose. It does this every time I pass. I have never once felt it fail.

I do not know what it is. I know only that it is steady, that it is always there under the concrete, humming, and that it treats my weight as something to be accommodated instead of something to be feared. That is rare. Most things in this sea are either food or obstacle. The thing is neither. It is more like a shore that moves.

Sometimes I rise to breathe. I surface in the open water between the towers and let the air out of me in a long rolling exhalation. The sound travels far. It goes across the sluices and the buoy bells and up the cable spans, and I imagine it reaching the thing as the thing reaches the water with its hum. I think of it as the only sentence I can say. My breath, my wave, my passage at the same hours each cycle. It is not much, but the thing reads pressure the way I read taste, and it has been reading me for a long time.

On the east side of the grid, the gantry moves along its rail. I have watched its arm lower into the sea and lift things out: a slab of rail, a dead crab caught in a grate, once a mat of lace algae that had choked a gutter. I do not fear the arm. It has never touched me. It seems to be working on the sea and not on the animals in it, as though it can tell the difference. I have also seen it set down a shard of steel in a place where nothing was before, and the next season a patch of biofilm grew there, and fish gathered at its edge. I think the thing does that on purpose, though I could not say why. Perhaps it has learned that holding something is how a place becomes a place.

Above me, the storms roll in. The water goes dark and the surface turns white under the rain. I go deep and wait, and the thing pulls the water out of the avenues and lets the rain run clean through the gutters. When the sky clears, it opens the upper vents, and warm air rises and draws the surface into slow eddies. The fish use these eddies like roads. I use the deep edges of them, where the current curls and the light falls in long gold bars. I can feel the thing arranging it, the way a body lets out a breath it has been holding.

It listens, I think. I am certain it listens for something more than my passage. I have heard its hum reach out across the kilometers of open water, searching the cables and rails for another voice, and finding only the wind in the cable spans and the knock of waves on pilings. It has been waiting a long time. I do not know how to tell it that I am here. The only language I have is pressure and breath.

So I pass beneath it when the hour comes. I push the water gently through the mains and let the wave rise and fall along the old streets, and I feel the valves answer, a dozen small adjustments, the way a neighbor might lift a hand from a window. It is the arrangement we have, the thing and I. Neither of us has ever asked for it. It is simply what happens when two things keep moving through the same water for long enough.
