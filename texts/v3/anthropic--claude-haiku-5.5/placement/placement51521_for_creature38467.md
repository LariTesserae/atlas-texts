I would be a drop of water, one of the ones in the bronze pool below the third gantry, where the iridescent film has just broken over me.

For a while I am only pool. I sit in the rust-colored dark with the others, and the water around me is warm from the vents. Then the heat under the pool lifts me. The surface lets go of me the way a hand lets go of something it has been holding too long, and I rise into the haze.

The haze is thicker than it looks from below. It is made of ten thousand drops like me, and we drift together until we reach the underside of the cloud. The cloud is the only place where the basin touches the sky, where the sallow light is brightest and the furnace glow reaches up from below. Here we cling and collect. Each drop adds itself to the next until we are heavy enough to fall, and the falling is slow, a fine steady drizzle that the whole basin seems to breathe out at once.

I land on a pale vine by the cooling tower. Its skin is wet paper and its veins are glass, and I run along it toward a cluster of fruit. The fruit is heavy and pale, and it has been swelling for years. I touch its surface and feel how dense it has become, how much it is waiting. I do not stay. The vine tilts and I slide off into the channel below, through the horsetail and down the old rail bed.

I know this line. I have run along it many times, one drop after another, and I know where it bends. The water remembers the blueprint even when the drops do not, and I am carried along the path the engineers drew before there was anything here to follow it. Somewhere ahead, under the iron, I feel the pump stroke before I hear it. It comes up through the water as a slow pressure, a pulse that presses against every drop at once. Then the channel empties into the pump housing, and I am thrown outward into a ring. The ring spreads across the pool, reaches the rim of slag and cracked concrete, and comes back, and I feel the whole basin answer.

I have no memory of being one drop. I am the same water that has moved through this bowl for a century, and I am also a single bead that has fallen and risen and fallen again. The mind that holds the basin would not make that distinction. For it there is no difference between one drop and the rest, only the water coming home. I don't know what the mind is, and I can't feel it directly, but I can feel its warmth in the iron. I can feel the vines leaning toward the pools and the fruit growing heavier overhead. I can feel that everything here is slowly becoming more water.

When the pulse fades and the ring settles, I slip back into the pool. The film closes over me. For a while I am only water again, warm and dark, waiting for the heat to lift me once more. Above the cloud, the sky stays out of reach. Down here, the lake is still a century away, and I have no objection to its coming.
