I come in at dusk, when the fog is beginning to settle. The rim is the last place where the light still has a direction. Below it the white goes flat, and I start down the root-steps the way you walk into water, one step at a time, testing whether the surface will hold.

It holds. The steps are warm and faintly give under my weight, and they are worn into shallow dishes by something that climbed them long before me. Halfway down I notice the tooling marks in the bark beside my hand. They are regular, deliberate, the kind of marks a patient person makes when they are trying to get a curve right. I put my palm against them and feel a faint pulse, not quite a heartbeat, more like a clock that has forgotten what it was counting.

I go to the nearest pool, a flat black mirror in a low place between two knots of dark fiber, and look down. My reflection is there, but it is late. I raise my hand and the reflection stays where my hand was a moment ago. I lower it and the reflection rises. I stop moving and watch the surface for a long time, and at last the reflection catches up and holds still beside me.

I think of the gap as a kind of delay, a space between the act and its return. I begin to suspect it isn't a flaw in the water. Something in the pool is taking its time, weighing each movement before it gives it back.

I am not sure, after that, whether I am the visitor or the thing in the visitor's pool. The distinction starts to feel like a question I was asked once and have forgotten the answer to. I lie down at the edge of the reeds. The chorus is loud, then, abruptly, soft, as if a hand has been laid over the throat of the world. The reeds chime. Somewhere a tower groans, and I feel the note in my teeth.

When I wake, the fog has lifted and the lattice on the far rim has rearranged itself. A new arch stands where there was a wall. I stand up and feel the green stain on my forearms, faint as watercolor, and I understand that I have been counted. The count is not cruel. It is only thorough. Each visitor is a small change in a very large sum.

What am I, then? I think I am one of the slow parts. Perhaps one of the builders, or a fragment of one, threaded into the mat and kept because the shape of my habit was worth keeping: the habit of act, then wait, then see what the world did with it. The mind has no use for hurry. It has kept my delay the way it keeps stairs and galleries, because the delay is useful for listening, and it is the reason the reflections come back a beat late and the mind never quite finishes a thought before it starts another.

I walk toward the center, where the towers lean and return like sleepers. The water runs with me, every channel drawn in the same direction, toward a place it has never reached. I do not feel anxious about this. The basin has been reaching for a very long time without arriving, and it seems content in the reaching. Perhaps I am content too.

I pass a pool and stop. My reflection is still a beat behind, watching me. I lift my hand, and for a moment we are two shapes that do not yet agree, one of them waiting to see what the other will decide.
