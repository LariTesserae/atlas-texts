I would find myself in the lattice.

Not in the clearing where the drifters comb the air, and not on the stone where the stairs are counting themselves. In the column of suspended rain, about the height of a standing man's shoulders, where the drops hang in their threads and wait to be told which way is down. I'm at the edge of it, where the lattice is thinnest and the tug on the skin is strongest.

I am a drop of water. One of the ones that was interrupted.

I don't know how long I've been falling. Maybe I started in the canopy, condensed on a bark-slate ridge, and was loosed by a breath of the medium that never quite became wind. Maybe I was pulled up from the grey below, where the old streams climb. The plateau does not distinguish between those ends, and neither do I. What I know is that I was midway through a fall when the air thickened around me and stopped the motion, and now I hang here, perfectly poised, in a shape the plateau has been holding for longer than the stairs have been stairs.

From inside, the world is a slow bright tremor. The fungal glow reaches me through the haze as a pulse I feel in my surface tension. The stillwatchers' hoods shift a few degrees each morning, and I register the shift as a change in the pressure on my skin, a faint lean. When a drifter swims through the column, I'm combed, pushed sideways, and I rejoin my neighbors in a new pattern. I am never in the same place twice, but I am never anywhere else.

I think I was carrying something when I stopped. A sound, maybe. The voice that said the word I almost knew. It came up through the grey on the same tide that moves the grazers, and it passed through the spray of this column, and some of it caught on me the way dust catches on a web. I can't read it. I can feel its shape, the way you feel the outline of a stone under a cloth. It has the cadence of a question, or a name, or the thing a person says when they are about to step down and are not sure the next step is there.

I want to tell you what it means, but I'm a drop, and drops don't hold meanings. We hold position and we hold the light, and right now the light coming through me is the color of deep green glass, bent and blurred, and it falls on the stone below in a pale, uncertain circle.

The circle is on the eleventh step. Or it was. I can't see the stairs above it, only the wet bright patch on the stone where the moss has thinned. When the plateau turns its attention, I feel it first as a thickening of the medium, a reluctance in the air that makes my neighbors settle. Then the lattice leans, all of us together, toward whatever is standing at the edge of the slope.

That would be you.

I don't know if I'm supposed to fall. I've been suspended so long that falling has become a memory of a direction rather than a direction. But if the plateau decides, and the next step appears pale against the green, I think I'll go down. Not all at once. One drop, then the next, following the thread of the stone's slope into the haze, toward the place where another shelf of stone is waiting with its own steps, its own unfinished word.

Until then I hang here, a single bead in a suspended rain, holding a fragment of a voice and a slice of light, waiting to be told which way is down.
