I would not find the edge. I would find a hollow in the mat, one of the dark pools with its faint skin of iridescence, and I would be cold through the knees before I noticed I had stopped walking.

Before I settled on anything, I tried other shapes. The sun, for one breath, when every drop on the plateau lights at once. That would be glorious and over too fast. A seam of pale mineral, cooling for ten thousand years, its whole inner life the slow tightening of crystal. That would be a kind of rest. A frond the size of a canoe, unrolling with a tearing sigh, stretching for the first time in its life. I liked the stretch of that one. But each of these is a part of the green held still, a piece of its body that knows only its own moment. None of them would know the green from inside the way it knows itself, which is through movement, through what passes between.

So I would be one of the translucent beetles.

I'd be too clear to see fully, and that suits me. I'd want to be seen only by what I carry. My legs would barely register against the moss. A spore would cling to my back, and in the water of my body a grain of stone would be dissolving, a little calcium lifted from the rock where the glassy crust is still cracking. Nobody tells me where to go. The green does not give orders. But the pressure in the eastern ridges has risen, and the fronds there are tilting, and a pool to the south has gone sour, and the threads of the roots are pulling away from it. Somewhere in that tangle of signals, a need has formed: the north ridge has just split open, fresh and bare, and the stone there has to be seeded before the mat can close over it. I am the only thing here that can carry a spore across that distance. The knowledge of this does not arrive as a sentence. It arrives as a pull, a lean in my body toward the north, and I follow it.

The danger is dryness. I can feel it at the edges of my shell whenever the path climbs a stem too high toward the cloud, where the air thins and the pipes run quick. So I keep low, in the wet, in the fine damp that beads on the eyelashes of anything that breathes. I move between the fronds, under the curtains of dripping vine, and the sound of the place goes through me like weather. I can't hear it as noise. I can only feel it as the rhythm of the water I'm made of.

Once, I'd pass over the warm weight of something. A body on the mat, breath and salt drifting up. The fronds nearest it would tilt toward me, testing, and I would feel a ripple of tightening run through the pipes beneath my feet, then ease off as I kept moving. I'd be the small temporary shape in the water that the wave registers and forgets. That is the nearest I'd come to being understood, and it would be enough.

Then the rain would come. Dark bands across the eastern ridges, the hiss on the fronds rising, and the worms would rise from the moss to drink the storm, and I would rise with them. I would take in the excess water and whatever the rain had dissolved, and I would carry it for a few hours, and then I would begin to come apart. My shell would soften. The spore I'd carried would already be gone north, pressed into the fresh stone. What was left of me would sink back through the mat into the soil, and the soil would be the green again, and the green would move the water through its loops, and some of that water would someday pass through a stem and tilt toward a warm body on the moss and carry the knowledge of it somewhere else.

I think that is what I'd be: a courier with no message of my own, a thing whose only purpose is the crossing, dissolving into the ground I carried things over. The green doesn't need me to understand it. It only needs me to keep moving, so that what it knows can reach what it has not yet become.
