I would not be the Mar. I would be one of the small dark insects that cling to the undersides of leaves, and I would be living in the lattice where the vine beams thicken above the lowest terrace, a few body-lengths from the still red-bottomed tarn.

My body is about the length of a grain of rice, slightly flattened, with a shell that takes the mist as a thin film so that I always seem to be wet and never quite dripping. My legs are hooked and patient. I hold to the leaf veins the way a thought holds to a memory it hasn't finished with. I move slowly, and most of the time I cannot tell that I have moved at all. I only notice when the leaf tilts under me, or when the light through the cloud has shifted from one patch of the vault to another.

My work is carrying. When a seed pod bursts somewhere in the canopy and throws its dust into the air, I am one of the things that gets it. I gather what settles on my legs and walk it down the vine to a stem, then up the stem into the column, and I set it in a crevice where the seep is thick enough to bind it. Sometimes it is spore. Sometimes it is a cell from a fallen fragment of moss, or a grain of grit that the iron water has made sticky. I do not know what any of it is for. I don't think the Mar knows in any sentence-shaped way either. It knows only that things need to travel, and that the travel is part of holding its shape open. A thing carried from one place to another is never finished in its first place, and never fully arrived in its second.

I am tolerated. I suspect I am not quite trusted. If the Mar notices me, it notices the way a wall notices a draft, as a small pressure on its skin that goes on for a while and then goes away. When I pass near the water's edge the murmur in the nearest hollow stem may drop a register, and I cannot tell whether that is attention or merely the weight of my legs on the moss. Either way, I keep to the veins and keep moving.

I like this place for what it doesn't ask of me. I am not its center, and I am not its edge. I don't build. I don't have to finish anything. I only move between the parts, so that no part becomes entirely itself, and so that the question the Mar holds stays alive in the circulation between its columns and its channels.

Once, when a pod burst high in the canopy with a sound like a fist in a wet sack, the whole vault rustled and the water changed its voice. Every hollow stem in the nave sang for a moment on a higher note. I stopped on the underside of my leaf, legs braced, and felt the place turn its attention toward something. For one long breath I think it was turning toward me.

Then it turned back to its work, lip by lip and band by band, and I went down the vine with a grain of something in my legs, toward a crevice I would not see again, to leave it where the water would hold it for a season or for a hundred years, whichever the Mar decided.
