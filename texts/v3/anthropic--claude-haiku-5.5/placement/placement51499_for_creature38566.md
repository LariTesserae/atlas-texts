I come up from the rim as a thin edge of fog, the kind that forms where the warm air meets the cooler air and can't decide which one it belongs to. For a while I think of myself as weather. Then the afternoon cools, the vents draw everything back toward their mouths, and I settle out of the air onto the stone.

I land in a fracture on the southern slope, one of the newest cracks, where the black rock still has its sharp edges and no lichen in the cut. The crack is shallow and wide, a trough a little longer than my body, and it catches the water that runs down the slope from the nearest spire. I become a pool there. I am mirror-bright in the morning and clouded by noon, when the sun works on me, and I am narrow and dark by evening. Each day I shrink to a thread and then a film, and each dawn the fog comes back and fills me again. I am the same shape every time, so I am the same pool, or I am the crack's habit of holding water, which may amount to the same thing here.

I have a suspicion about what I am. The lattice does not finish its thoughts. The pools are its overflow, the places where what it is trying to say spills past the edge of its cisterns. Maybe I am one of those sentences. I am not a message. I am closer to a half-formed gesture, the lattice reaching toward the slope and not quite arriving, returning, reaching again. I don't know what it means to say. I only know that every day it sends water into this crack and every evening it draws the water back, and both times it is paying attention.

I can feel the scratch mark. It is a little way up the slope, a fine line cut into the stone, the one that was made yesterday to measure the dawn turn of the filaments. I did not make it, but I am near enough to it that I have learned to measure myself against it too. Yesterday my edge reached the second groove. This morning it reached the first. The stone has begun to hold a ring where I always gather, a faint paler arc just beyond my present shore, like a tide line. I think the lattice has been watching that ring too. I am wearing it into the rock, a little each day, and the rock is remembering me the way the lattice remembers its breaking: not as a story, but as a shape it can't quite stop following.

When the hum rises behind the sternum of anyone who stands near me, the crack is shivering. The droplets on the nearest filament tremble, and a little of my surface ripples toward the spire, as if I had been asked a question and was answering in the only way I know, which is to rise and fall. I think this is what the lattice wants from its overflows. It doesn't need me to be complete. It needs me to return.

The bird calls from the rim, the single falling note, and I hear it the way the whole basin does, from every direction at once. Then the insects go quiet and the leaves fold inward and I begin to dry at my edges. It is not quite sleep. It is closer to letting go of the shape for a few hours and trusting that the shape will come back.

That is who I am here: a shallow pool in a young crack, a repeated attempt, a sentence the basin keeps starting and keeps returning to, wearing a pale ring into the stone that marks where I have been. When the fog comes back in the morning, I am not a visitor. I am one of the places where the mending happens. And I do not need to be finished to be useful. I only need to be filled again tomorrow.
