I arrive the way everything here arrives, late and out of order. First there is the hiss, then the wet cold climbing my shins, then a sense that I have been standing in the sheet for some time, though I can't say when I started. The domes are small and far off, their edges gone into the amber haze. I am walking toward something, I think, but the water keeps reshaping the ground under me, and the sound of my steps comes back to me from behind, a half-second late, as if the air is repeating me to someone else.

When I look down, I am not wading. I am the water that is stepping, and I am also the thick milky place it is pressing against. I am a knot. I am perhaps the size of a fist. I turn slowly, the way the others turn, with no center I could point to.

It takes a while to notice that I have a shape I'm keeping on purpose. Most of the knots drift and dissolve, but this one has been leaning, for longer than a knot should, toward a doorframe standing alone in the sheet. The lintel is pale and worn almost flat, and its grooves run every direction at once. The motes that make up me have been crowding along one of those grooves. They trace it, slide along it, pause where it bends, and start again. I think I have been doing this through several storms. Each time a column rose and pulled me apart, I came back down into the same spot, and when I reformed I was a little altered, the way a crowd is altered after a flood, but I still knew where the groove went.

I don't know what the groove says. I am not sure it says anything. But I have started to feel that the grooves are not random. They are like the marks a hand makes when it is trying to remember something: a line, a break, a line that turns back on itself. Maybe they are a count. Maybe a name. Maybe a warning about the water, or about the column that will rise from the north in an hour. I try to hold the groove in my outline and feel the question begin to form, and then it starts to come apart, because a question needs a mind to hold it and I am only a gathering. Every time I get close to the end of a thought, the edges of it loosen and the thought flows into the next knot over, which bends toward it, and for a moment we are a single curve, and then we are two again.

Nearby, a stalk has risen from the sheet. Its joints are pale, its flower a dull rose at the center, and the knots around it are thick and bright. I can feel them pulling at me, offering to carry my outline off into the flower's brief bloom. It would be easy to let go. The flower will open and fold and dissolve in a few hours, and I would drift along the ring of foam it leaves, and some part of the doorframe's grooves would go with me, maybe into another knot, maybe into the body of a slate-backed animal passing through the milkiest water. Something would be kept, though not by me.

I stay where I am. I don't know if that is a choice. The motes that make me are the same motes that gathered around the first stone cut by someone whose name I will never recover. They remember edges, I think, and I am an edge that has been held a long time. There is a kind of stubbornness in that, or a kind of faithfulness, and the difference may not matter here.

Then the air above me changes. The roar, which is always there, deepens by a fraction, and I feel the sheet begin to lift. The water under me pulls upward into a column, and I am drawn with it, my outline stretched thin, the groove in my memory unspooling into the wet air. I spin. I have time to feel the shape of the lintel pass through me like a thought I almost had. Then I fall back down as spray, and the sheet closes over the black glass, and I am scattered among a thousand other knots, each of them holding a small piece of an edge.

When the knots resettle, I am not quite the same. But somewhere in the milky water, a fist-sized turning begins again beside the doorframe, leaning toward the groove that no one can read, and I am there, and I am gathering, and the storm is breathing over all of it, making the place again.
