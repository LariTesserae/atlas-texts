I think I would arrive the way the damp does, without a moment of arriving. I'd stand at the open side of the basin, where the slope falls toward the grey, and feel the clay give a little under my weight. My footprint would hold its shape for an hour and then slowly fill. The film would register me the way it registers a falling bead, and go on. For a while I'd be a visitor, a weight that pressed and then was absorbed.

But I don't think I'd stay one. The basin doesn't ask visitors to stay, and nothing here is hurried enough to need a person's help. What I'd want is the smallest thing that still belongs to the place's pattern, and that's a bead on the overhang.

I'd be a drop on the north face of the western ridge, just above a lichen rosette where the film shows itself through the stone. I'd form slowly out of nothing visible. The film breathes out its moisture, the rock goes oiled, and somewhere in that sheen a point gathers and begins to hold together. I'd swell over hours, maybe days, and my surface would take in the even grey light from everywhere at once, so I'd have no shadow, no true top or bottom, only a faint edge where I stop being water and start being air. I'd hang there a long time. Nothing would push me. Every hour would look like the one before, and I wouldn't have to measure any of them.

Then the surface tension would fail, not dramatically, just the way it always does for a thing that has grown as far as it can. I'd let go. That wouldn't be an ending. It would be a change of place, a fall of perhaps the height of a knee, slow enough that falling and hanging would seem to be the same activity.

I'd land in the small pool below and make the round note. Of all the sounds in the basin it's the only one with a clear beginning and then a long, unhurried fade, and I'd be the thing that makes it. The note would go out over the clay and the polygon cracks and the sage mats in the channels, and I'd hear it go, as much as a drop of water can hear anything, until the quiet came back over everything like the air. Then the pool would soak down through the silt, and I'd be taken up into the film again, which is where I started, and where the pattern gets its breath.

So I'd be a bead: one fall in a long sequence that never changes its rhythm, a single visible unit of the thing that holds the basin together. Not the film itself, which has no centre and is spread so thin that any part is much like the next, but a small event of its breath, one of the many points where the body shows itself as water for a moment before it goes back under.

The basin wouldn't notice me go. It would notice the water reach the healed crack and the silt settle a fraction deeper, and the lichen would grow outward on the north side by the width of a hair, as it always does. The sediment in the square hole would thicken by a grain. Nothing would hurry toward closing it, and nothing would hurry away from it. I'd be a small, complete thing in a place that is already finished, which seems like the right size for me.
