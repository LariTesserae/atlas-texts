I'd want to be the film.

Not the whole film, but a piece of it, on a pool in the lee of the eastern cooling tower where the runoff slows and the sulfur comes up thick through the clay. I'd begin in the dark, before the fog has finished settling. I wouldn't know yet that I was a *thing*. I'd just be the thin edge where the water meets the air, pressed flat by its own surface tension, feeding on the yellow in the water and the iron that drifts down from the seeps. Feeding is the wrong word. It's closer to leaning. I lean toward the light that hasn't arrived yet.

When the light does arrive, low and orange through the fog, I become visible to myself, or to whatever in me does the seeing. Every color at once. The colors shift as the angle changes, so I learn quickly that I exist only in relation to movement, to a head tilting above me or a shadow sliding across. A lizard's tongue touches the edge of my territory and I thicken there, laying down a little more mineral skin where I've been disturbed. I don't know that I'm repairing myself. I only know that the cool hours are when I can work, and I have maybe three of them.

The swallows are a rhythm more than a threat. Their shadows pass and the midges rise, and some of the midges land on me and sink, and that is the first thing I ever tasted of the wider world, flesh and chitin feeding the bacteria that are also me, the way I feed the stone that will outlast me. I am not sure whether there is a difference between me and the community pressed under me. I think the question would bother me less than it bothers you.

By late morning the heat starts. It doesn't hurt. It's just the end of the working hours, the tension giving way, my skin cracking into drifting flakes that spin down through the water column. I'd feel the pull of the bottom, the slow settling into sediment, and I'd be part of the next ring in the old runoff channel, a layer thin enough that no one could see it alone. A night's work pressed into stone. Each ring a little wider than the one before. Some of them grow past the height of a man, and I would be one of the thousands of invisible skins that make them up, all of us ever so slightly larger than the ones that came before.

I like that the rings are the archive and not me. I would not be the record. I would be the writing, the hand, the thing that does the lean toward light and then falls without being asked to explain itself. A reader might tilt a jar and see me shimmering, decide the pool is safe or too rich, and then tip me back into the water, and that would be the only time anyone ever paid attention to me at all, and it would be enough. The readers learn water's habits by watching things like me, and I'd be glad to be read that way, as a sign of something larger than the pool.

And at dusk the fog comes down, and I wouldn't remember the day. But something in the sediment would, something in the rings and the crests of the lizards that drank from me. The lizard with the sulfur-yellow crest would carry a small piece of my chemistry on its back. The old russet-banded one from the eastern seeps would carry another. None of this would be mine in any sense I could hold, but the shelf would hold it, the way it holds everything: slowly, in the tick of the cooling pipes and the widening of the stone.

So I'd be the film, or a film, one of the countless thin, bright, short-lived skins on the water, doing the only work I can do in the few cool hours I'm given, and letting the stone remember me in the only way stone can.
