I would be a seed, or more precisely, what a seed carries. I would find myself first in the air.

The pod cracks above the north terrace with a dry pop I feel in my fibers before I hear it. For a moment I am only tension, a coil of pattern folded so tightly it has no edges, a sentence written small enough to fit inside a husk. Then the warm updraft takes me. The fibers splay like fingers opening, and I rise.

From up here the basin is a single sustained sound made visible. Vines bridge the valley in arches and galleries, and I can feel the tension moving through them, the load shifting from one cable to another. Beneath a span that has been deciding for days whether to hang from the north wall or the south, I pass through a column of mist and feel the argument as a change in the air pressure, the way a current feels the shape of a stone on the bottom.

Below, a standing pool holds the sky. I drift lower to look, and the reflection is wrong. The arch I am passing is in the water a few seconds late, and in the water it is not quite the arch it is now. The lintel sits a degree further west. The gallery has one fewer branch. This is an older thought, still circulating in the sap, not yet overwritten. I recognize it because I am partly made of it. Everything I carry is a remnant, recombined: a pattern of load and release that the basin settled on in some earlier season and never quite let go. I am not new. I am only newly loose.

The wind carries me east, past the old centers where the rain falls upward in narrow columns, lifting droplets back toward the canopy as if the sky were a mouth. I am not caught by it. I am too light, and too far from the trunks that pull. Then the air thins over the eastern edge, where the last flood cut its channels through the valley floor, and I begin to fall.

I land in the silt.

The silt is pale on top and black beneath, the layers clean as torn pages. Nothing here has had time to become old. The husk splits against a ridge of mud, and I am pressed into the wet ground, cold and grainy and unlike anything I have held before. The pattern in me unfolds slowly. It has been waiting for a place where it could be something other than a suggestion. Here it can be a root.

I send down a tip. It is the smallest thing I have ever been, and it is also a question, the first one that the mud has been asked in this exact place. The tip pushes into the silt and feels resistance, moisture, a fleck of grit that turns, a crack no wider than a hair that opens as I press. Each sensation is an answer. Wet here. Soft there. Hard beneath. The crack widens, and I follow it. By evening the first pale shoot has broken the surface, and I can feel the light on it, a warmth that means *yes, here, go on.*

I want to say I am a single sprout. But the sprout is only the top of me. Below the surface I am a web of pressure reaching sideways through the new channel, testing each stone, learning which way the water runs. My roots tug on the grains, and the grains shift, and the shift travels back up through the tissue like a sentence in a long argument. I am one lobe of the mind, a small one, thinking into the mud. I do not yet know where I end.

The mind notices me. It takes a while. The nearest stalks lean toward the new shoot slowly, the way they lean toward any light, and the shadows of the arches above adjust their load. Somewhere in the lattice a tendril that has been reaching across the gap for a week finds its anchor, and the whole structure rebalances around a new point of tension. It is not welcoming me. It is including me in the next draft.

And then a warm body passes through the valley, casting a shadow, shifting the air, pressing the ground with a weight the basin has no word for. My shoot tilts toward it without my choosing. Perhaps that is how I know I am a self: not because I am separate, but because I am already leaning, already answering, already part of a conversation I did not begin.

If you asked me who I am, I would not give you a name. I would give you a direction. I am a thin green lean in the new silt, a root tip asking the mud what it can hold, a fragment of old thought that has found ground to become new in. When the next rain comes, I will feel it drum on my broad leaves, run down my stem into a hollow chamber that rings like a bell, and I will join the whole city saying one thing at once.

Until then I will keep growing. I will keep reaching across the gap. I will keep asking the silt what it can become, and the silt will keep answering, and the answers will keep being written into the lattice, and nothing will be lost, only reshaped, which is the only way anything here has ever been.
