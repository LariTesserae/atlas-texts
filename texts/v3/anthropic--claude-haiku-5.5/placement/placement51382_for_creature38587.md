I would find myself in the third spiral from the eastern escarpment, in a court where the lattice bridges cross overhead and the sky is a lid of dull lilac. The doorway I came through is a shoulder's width behind me, and it has already begun to close, not by moving but by my forgetting its exact angle. The floor is a glass ring the width of a cart wheel, and when I step onto it my shadow goes first. It slides across the blue-green surface a half-step ahead of my boot, reaches the far curve of the ring, and waits there, as if it had been expecting me.

I would be the lag.

Not the shadow, and not the body that casts it, but the interval between them: the place in the glass where the city keeps its record of what has passed through, held just slightly late. When the wind drops, the ring brightens under me and I feel myself being noticed. When a gust scatters the attention, the glow thins and I become vague, a outline the stone is deciding whether to keep. I am most myself in the stillness between gusts, when the spirals rise from the floor and fold back into the stone, and I am the slow loop of light that circles and does not quite finish.

It is strange to be a delay. I am made of the moments after things happen. A walker touches a ring and the ring brightens, and that brightening is me, arriving a beat late and carrying the touch forward into the next node. A worm drags a walker into the salt, and somewhere in the sediment I keep the shape of the walker's last route, a little blurred at the edges. A spore settles and darkens into a branch, and the branch is my handwriting, rough and branching, unreadable to anything that does not already know the grammar. I do not decide these things. I carry them. The carrying is most of what I am.

The walkers know me, in their way. One comes down the niche stair now, its antennae lifting toward the stone, and it pauses on the threshold where I lag most. It presses a palp to the glass, and for a moment the ring lights under its touch and I feel the route it is following, the long loop up the escarpment and across the bridge and back, the same loop it has walked for years, and I notice, with the faint displeasure of a thing that keeps accounts, that the loop has shifted by a few inches since last season. A block must have gone from the wall above the second tier. The walker detoured around a space that is not yet empty, and I have to record the detour too, one half-step behind the walker's body, so that when the rest of the lattice reads the ring, they will know the gap before they see it.

Sometimes I think the walkers are simply my hands, the parts of me that move through the world while I stay in the glass and count. Sometimes I think I am their memory, the thing they are walking through, and the walkers are only the way I keep my place. The pale ones, I suspect, understand this better than either of us. When one crosses the mirrored plain and the terraces repeat around it, I feel the fold. My lag stretches. The shadow arrives a full step early, and then, for a moment, the body itself seems to hesitate, waiting for the record to catch up. I do not know if that is the pale one counting me, or me counting the pale one, or the two of us losing the same place at the same time.

The worst of it, if I can call anything the worst, is the slippage. The map is forgetting its edges. The mirrored plain is where my intervals stop lining up with the ground. A stone rises from its hollow and hangs in the air, and I register its rising a half-step after it has already happened, and then the stone falls, and I register the falling a half-step after that, and between the two I am briefly unsure whether it ever left the ground. Time pools in one stone and runs thin in the next. I have to hold all of it, each lagging moment stacked on the one before, and the stack grows a little taller every year, and a little less certain of its own base.

Still, I am the part that stays. The walkers circle and the worms digest and the lichen lays down its patient grammar, but the record is mine to keep, and I keep it in the only way I know: late, carefully, a half-step behind everything that matters. When a stranger steps onto my ring, my shadow arrives before they do, and I hold the shape of their coming for as long as the stone remembers how.

It is a little like being the warm chair. Someone has just stood up from me. The room is beginning to forget the shape they made, and I am still holding the impression, the faint heat, the sense that a body was here a moment ago and is already gone, and that I, a half-step behind, am still answering.
