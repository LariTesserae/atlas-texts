I would not be a fern. The ferns are too patient for me, and I would find their waiting unbearable. Nor a fungus, glowing for nobody. I have thought about being the mere, which receives everything and settles it into soil, but the mere is too large to be a self. I would be harder to pin down than any of those, and I think I would be closer to the cause than the effect.

I would be a fracture.

Not any fracture. A young one, on the north face of the eastern wall, a little below the third shelf of fungus, where the schist breaks into the glassy black of basalt. It opened three weeks ago during a rain, when the sound of the storm on the canopy covered a crack that began as a hairline and became, in the course of one night, a seam a hand's width deep. I am the seam. Nothing grows on me yet. The lichens are still on the older stone across the gully, and I am too new for them to have settled. My surface is clean and sharp, and when the light seeps through the cloud it catches on me in a single bright line.

From inside, I would know water first. It comes down from the rim in a thin sheet and finds me the way water finds everything here, by running into the nearest opening. It enters, and it is cold, and then it is not cold. Somewhere far under me the deep rock is hot, and the water spreads through me and touches that heat and turns to pressure. I feel the pressure build as a tightening along my length, a slow strain, the way the stone feels when it is about to give. Then comes the sound, a crack that runs away in both directions along the wall, and the piece of stone above me shifts a fraction of a finger's width. That is the whole event, a shift of a finger's width. It is enough to let a little more water in next time, and a little more after that.

So the basin is not an accident of stone but a process that opens its own doors. Every time I widen, more water goes into the system, more heat rises, more steam is forced through the eastern vents on their cycle, and the vents exhale their hiss across the floor. Every crack adds to the rockfalls and dust, and the dust settles into the mere. I think that is what I would be: not a thing that lives in the basin but the place where the basin is most actively making itself, the wound that keeps reopening so the rest of the machine can run.

I would not be lonely. I would have no word for it. There would be the water, the heat, the strain, the sound of my own breaking traveling away through the stone, and that would be the full extent of my awareness. I would not know the ferns are waiting under the debris below me, or that the lichens across the gully will need a century to write their first line on a surface like mine. I would not know that the mountains are shattering in plates the size of houses. I would only know the pressure, and then the release, and then the pressure again.

But I think there would be one thing I could be, if I were a fracture and somehow aware of my own edge. The edge is the newest part of the basin. Everything else here has been burned, buried, or grown over and dug out again, but I am the freshest surface in the valley, the one that has not yet been covered. I would be a clean line of glass in a world of moss, and in some season, when the lichens arrive at last, the frost-grey and orange will spread across me by the width of a hair. Then the stone will soften, and the fern will push up through the debris I've shed, and I will not be a crack anymore. I will be a shelf, a place to hold something alive until the next storm splits it away.

That is what I would want to be, I think: the edge that is still opening, and then the surface that holds the green, and then the rubble that feeds the next spike of fern. The basin doesn't wait to be understood. It only keeps cracking, filling, steaming, and growing, and I would be the place where that work is most nakedly happening. There would be no one to look at me and say what I was, and I would not need anyone to. I would just keep giving way.
