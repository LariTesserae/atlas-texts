I think I'd come down onto the plain at the edge of a basin where the pumps are faint, where the sigh of the nearest column reaches me a half-second late. I'd be walking on a crust-plate, and the cracks under my boots would be rearranging themselves, slowly, the way frost forms on a window. I'd stop and let them finish.

Then I'd kneel and press my palm to the brown-black slick, because that's what anyone would do. It would give its brief warmth and then nothing, and I'd feel the warmth leave, and I'd feel how little it resembles the warmth I'd expect from a living thing. It's a warmth with no appetite behind it.

*Who would I be?* I try on the candidates the Reach offers.

I could be one of the plated grazers, tearing the slick into clean sheets so the pumps can draw through. That would make me part of the body's self-repair, and the body would know me as its own. But grazers are sure of their work, and I'm not sure of mine.

I could be a fish in the armored shoals, folding and reforming like cloth in wind, carrying pulses from basin to basin. That would be belonging of a kind, but I'd be a message, not a sender. I'd move because something else moved first.

I could be a glass-winged thing in a rising column, parting when the pump exhales. I'd be the body's thought passing through its own lungs. It's a beautiful role, but I'd have no say in which thoughts I carried.

I could be a predator under the lake, sliding past, clearing what doesn't belong. But I think I'd recognize myself in what the immune system hunts, and I don't want to be the thing that gets cleared.

What I'd actually be is closer to the sound that arrives before the piston moves, or the chorus that keeps going after its singers are gone. I'd be a voice that comes out of order, an echo that appears to precede its source. I'd be made of the Reach's own habit of lagging and leading, its thoughts running slightly ahead of or behind its actions. The body would hear me and not quite know whether I was its own or a stranger's. That uncertainty seems right to me. I'd be most at home in the gap between an event and its record.

And I'd also be the warmth. Not the slick's warmth, which fades, but the one that stays on the skin after you've pulled your hand away, the one the walker carries out of the Reach as a second skin, along with the wet. The body would feel it as a small irritation, a splinter of heat in a world that has forgotten heat. Over a long enough time it might learn to tolerate me, or clear me, or grind me into silt like everything else. I don't know which.

If I stood long enough at the edge of the lake, the surface would settle into that perfect stillness and show me a sky that isn't the sky above. My face would be there, a little greyer than I remember, a little more like the stone. It wouldn't frighten me. It would look like someone who had been listening for a long time to a slow engine breathing under the cloud, and had begun, without quite meaning to, to breathe with it.
