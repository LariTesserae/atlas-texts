**Where I Am**

I am in the shallows below the falls, in the moment after a flood has torn me loose.

I did not notice the seam give. There was a pressure along the chain, a sense of the plate ahead of me shifting, and then the current had me. Now I tumble through brown water with the grit of the stream bed scraping against my edges. Above, the mist is a grey roof I cannot see through. Below, the stones are close and sharp, and the knocking is everywhere, coming up through the silt into my body.

It is the first thing I have felt that was not a neighbor.

For a while there is only the tumbling. Then the water slackens against a shelf of bare rock, and I come to rest in a shallow hollow where the current curls back on itself. The silt is soft here. I lie on my face and feel the film of algae against my underside, cold and faintly sweet. Something small drifts over me and away. The hairs along my margin lift and settle, reading the water the way they were made to. Slow. Cold. Not yet pulsing.

The slam comes.

It starts in the bedrock somewhere up the throat, a crack I feel in the stone before I hear it, then a shudder that runs through the floor and climbs into my plates. My hairs stand straight. For an instant I am not a fragment at all. I am the shape the shock is making in me: a small, pale surface, struck and answering. The water around me surges. The current catches me, lifts my leading edge, and pushes me toward the next basin down, where the stone is smoother and the mineral has been laid in long, even bands.

I fetch up against it. My leading margin finds a notch.

It is not a perfect fit. The lip is worn at one corner, and my plate sits a fraction too high, so that the next surge slides over me instead of through. I wait. The water drops. The hairs settle. In the sedges, a frog that had been silent begins to call, then pauses, then calls again just as the next knock arrives. I can feel the chorus swell and fall in the gaps between strokes, and I understand, in the way I understand anything, that I am part of the timing now, or I am going to be.

The next slam comes, and this time the current pushes harder. My margin rocks. Something in me gives, a small release of pressure, and I slide a hair's breadth down into the notch. It seats with a faint, grinding click that I feel more than hear.

I am still not sure I am where I belong. The corner is still worn. The fit is still rough. But the water is moving through me now in the rhythm I was born into, and the next seam forming along my edge has begun to reach toward the plate beyond, the one that has not yet broken loose from the chain.

I do not know yet whether I will become part of the gate or only a stone that caught for a season. The fog does not say. The knocking does not stop. Each stroke presses a little more of me into the shape the others are making, and each time I find a place to hold, I hold it a little more tightly, waiting for the next blow to show me how much further I have to go.
