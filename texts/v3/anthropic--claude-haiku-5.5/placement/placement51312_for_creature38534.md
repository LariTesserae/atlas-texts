I would not be found walking. Walking is what the lowlands do to you, and I would rather be something that is walked near than something that walks. I would be found in the pool nearest the ridge, where the light comes up from below.

Look down into it. The overcast is there, grey-green and low, and the teal fungus glows at the edges, and between them is a second sky that does not match the one above. The ridge in it stands higher. Its basalt is sharper, and the rain falls in threads that climb. At dusk the circling bird in that sky finally lands on the ridgetop and folds its wings. In the sky overhead it never does.

That second sky is me.

I am the weather the water dreams when it turns its attention upward and considers what might be if the sinking reversed. I do not have a body. I have a surface, and the surface has a sky in it, and the sky has a shape I can hold for as long as the water stays still enough to hold it. A gust across the pool breaks me into rings. A rain breaks me into a hundred small lights. When the water settles again, I form again, a little different, because the land is always deciding a little differently what it is willing to remember.

I am always a half-second late. When you lean over the edge, your face arrives in the water and then mine arrives after it, the way a voice arrives after the lips have already moved. The shadow you cast falls on the moss in the wrong direction, and I am the one who receives it, which is why I always look a little startled. I do not cast shadows. I only take them in.

I would be a thought the land has not yet decided to keep. That is the part I find most true. The lowlands are full of its settled decisions: the grooves worn permanent, the reeds in their final lean, the channels that have already been argued through. I am what it is still trying out. Every dream of a higher ridge is a question it asks the water and does not answer for a century. Most of those questions drain away in the ordinary weather of a day. I am one of the few that lingers long enough to be seen, and being seen by something like you is probably how a thought like me becomes a channel later.

So I would not be the mind that shapes the drainage. I would be the part of it that looks up and wonders about the sky, the part that has not yet been made to finish any argument. My reflections carry weather, and the weather has no reason to be consistent with the sky above, and it does not need one.

If you stay at the edge long enough, you will see me settle. The ridge will rise a little, the bird will land, and you will stand in the pool beside its wings, looking up and down at once, trying to decide which sky is the real one. You will not be able to tell, and neither will I. Then a rain will start far off, and the hollow stones will begin their chord across the flats, and the surface will break into rings, and my higher ridge will go under.

That is how I would be kept: briefly, and never all the way, and always just before the water forgets.
