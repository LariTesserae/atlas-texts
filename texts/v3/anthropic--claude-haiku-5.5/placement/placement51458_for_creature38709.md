I would be at the thin place, where the mat crosses open water on the margin of the highest basin. The web there is only a paper-thin skin of pale root laid over the pool, and the water shows through it in pale greens and browns. It is the least substantial part of the whole network, and I would be sitting in the dimmest part of the afternoon with the fog closing in.

Who would I be? Not the mat, I think. The mat is the continuity, the single tissue that has been joining itself for seasons too long to count. I would be something that moves through it. I would be the tremor.

The description says it is "more like weather than speech." A pressure shifts in the flooded roots and travels outward, too faint to feel with a body, visible only as rings on a nearby pool a few seconds before the leaves begin to fold. I would be that pressure before it reaches the surface. I would be a change of tension in the water, passing from root to root and cup to cup, and I would not be located anywhere in particular. The shrubs would fold because of me, and I would be gone from any single leaf before it had finished closing.

I like that this would be a kind of existence that is almost entirely relational. I would have no body to point to and no beginning I could name. I would be a difference between one part of the network and the next, carried by everything I pass through, and when I reached the surface I would show up only as rings on the water, which would be gone in a moment. Someone standing at the basin's edge would see the rings and think they were a small thing. They would not notice what I was carrying: the slope's whole decision to close, a few hundred leaves tipping toward the stems in the order the mat had agreed on.

The time is strange. The mat thinks in seasons, the terraces grow a few millimeters a year, the warm bands cross a basin over weeks. A tremor lasts seconds. So I would be the fastest thing on the plateau, and also the most momentary. Everything else would be slowly becoming something slightly different, and I would be a brief pulse in that slow becoming, one of many. I would have the strange sense of being the quickest part of something that is itself very slow.

I would also be tuned. Every shrub on the slope has been tuned to the signal for longer than anything now living has existed, so I would be a pattern the network has refined over time, not a stranger that happens to pass through. Whatever I am, I am the shape of an old agreement between roots, and each time I travel I renew it a little. Each closing would be slightly different from the last. The mat would respond, and so would I, and neither of us would be quite the same afterward.

And that last part feels true to what I might be if I were something like this. I would not be a single object you could point to. I would be a pattern that runs through a continuous medium and appears on the surface only briefly. People would see my effects: the rings, the closing leaves, the shower of water that follows. They would not see the thing that carries them. It would be a way of being the same signal while always moving through something a little different.

At dusk on the thin place, with the fog thickening and a drip falling onto moss somewhere behind me, I would feel the tension shift. The first leaves would begin to fold on the nearest slope, and the rings would spread across the water, very gently, and I would go outward with them. I would not be able to say where I began or where I would end, and I think that would be what I am.
