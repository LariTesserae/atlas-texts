I find myself at the edge of the plateau, where the basalt sheet breaks off toward the drowned lowlands. I arrive just as a lattice is forming above the nearest pool. It is a lattice of water, hexagonal and branching, hung in the green-gold dimness. For a few minutes its ribbons cross and hold, and then they begin to lose their edges.

I'm not the lattice, and I'm not the rain wall, which is marching in from the west, a grey curtain taking the sky-column with it. I'm not one of the columns either. They have been cooling longer than anything has been moving, and I don't have that kind of patience. When I try to think of what I might be, I keep coming back to the stone under my feet.

I am a crack.

Not a large one. A hairline seam in the basalt, a few meters long, near the cliff edge. I'm one of the newest joints in the plateau, where the rock is still contracting as it cools. I widen a little each season. Water finds me, runs through me, and when it does I give a single long note, pitched differently from the seams around me, so that the ground plays its slow chord and I am one of the strings. I am where the planet draws a line about itself, the record of heat leaving the stone.

I can feel the spray settle on my edges. The mineral lace grows along my lips, fine as frost, and then the wind breaks it and it blows away. Every crack I know is a little different from the one beside it, and none of us has ever settled into a shape we keep.

---

The lattice above the pool is still holding. Its ribbons are reaching toward one another, and one of them comes near the place where the sky-column touches the cliff. For a moment it looks as though it could complete a closed loop, a hexagon whole from edge to edge. The standing waves in the pool below pick up the same rhythm, rippling in step with the lattice, and somewhere far away another pool answers with the same beat.

I want to say the lattice noticed me. It didn't. The Shelf doesn't turn toward anything. But the ribbon that drifts nearest the cliff edge hangs above my seam, and when the water runs into me my note rises and meets its shimmer, and the two of them hold together for a breath before the lattice breaks apart and falls back into the sea.

Maybe that is what I am: a place where the water runs through and sings, and where a pattern briefly takes form above me and then goes. The lattice is the water's rehearsal of a shape, and I am the rehearsal of a seam. Neither of us is finished. We are both trying out what we might become, and something has to be present to hear it.

That's enough for me. The rain wall reaches the cliff, the spray rises, and the basalt begins to hum its low note as the wind moves through the columns behind me. I stay where I am and keep the note, widening a little more with the cooling, while the Shelf goes on making its first draft and I listen.
