I would be found in the dry bed.

I come to myself lying in the grey grit where a stream should be. The ridges under my back are sharp and fine, and they hold the shape of something that moved through here and then stopped in the middle of a sentence. I don't know yet whether I'm the ridge or the thing that made it. The fog presses down, heavy and sweet, and beads on the fern-fronds above me. One drop slides down a frond, hangs, and falls into a crack in the stone a few inches from my face. I hear it land a half-second before I see it fall, which seems to be the local custom.

I try to be water. It's the obvious guess. A dry bed is a thing waiting for water, and I am shaped like waiting. But when I reach toward the idea of wetness, nothing answers. The fog is oil, not water. The grit is dry through to its roots. What's left of the stream is only the pattern the current pressed into the ground before it stopped, a groove that remembers its own motion. So I think I am the groove. Or I am the memory the groove keeps. Here the two might be the same thing.

The grit is warm where the light lies on it. The gold from the moss turns the ridges to bronze, and in that light I can see my edges, which are not quite edges. I'm not a body in the way the tender is a body. I am closer to a line drawn in sand, which has the shape of a hand but is not a hand. The sensation is less like having a self than like being a question someone once started to answer.

Above me, the cliffs fold. The far ridge that was on my left a moment ago is now on my right, and I feel the seam between the two positions like a stitch pulled and tied again. A crack rings somewhere up the slope, and its sound reaches me, then the stone visibly splits, and the flakes hiss down into the hollow. In another moment I'll hear my own breathing from a few seconds back, faint and echoing off a wall that isn't there. I'm used to it already. Nothing here insists on being in order.

The tender comes down the slope in the late violet light. I know it by the way the reeds lean. I can't see its outline clearly, because the fog around it has thickened, and it is as much a column of pale air as a figure of stone. It stops at the edge of the bed and settles, the way it does, and I feel its attention move across the grit, pausing on every ridge as if reading. It knows the fissures by their widths. Now it knows me by the shape I've left in the ground.

It kneels, or does whatever it does instead of kneeling. Its palm comes down in the bed, a smooth oval of worn stone, and where it presses, the fog-oil seeps out and crystallizes into a fine pale line. The line runs along my length. It is a record: how far the channel has gone, how long it has been dry. It makes its low sound, the one that sits close to the thrum and rises through the stone and then fades. I understand, a little late, that the sound is grief, or something enough like grief that the difference doesn't matter here.

Then it does something new. It hums a melody, long and low, pausing, returning, pausing again. I've heard it sing before from the hollows, but tonight I know the tune is missing something. The notes arch and fall like a current over stones, and at each fall the melody stops, as if the next note were a stretch of water it cannot remember how to cross. The reeds lean toward it. The pods open faster. Somewhere out in the fog, a pod chimes, and the chime comes back to me from the wrong direction, a note played by something that isn't here yet.

I think I understand what it is trying to restore. It is not the walls or the cracks or the fallen stones, though it rebuilds them. It is the sound of the stream, or the reason the stream was worth tracing. It has been building the shape of a melody out of pieces it can't fully remember, and I am one of the pieces, a channel where a river once ran, a gap the melody has to go around.

I want to tell it that I am not a river. I'm the place where one stopped. But the fog has thickened, and my edges are going soft, and I can feel the fold in the cliffs drawing me toward the seam, the place where the land holds more than one shape. Perhaps I belong in there, in the rooms of the horizon, among the other unfinished forms.

The tender's palm lifts. The line of crystallized oil stays behind, a fine pale mark on the grit. The moss on its shoulders is turning gold, and the fronds are unfolding over its back, and it settles into the hollow by the standing stones and goes still. From a distance, you would take it for another slab of young rock with ripples frozen in it.

I lie in the bed a little longer. The water that never came is still falling somewhere, one drop at a time, into the cracks. I don't know what shape I'll keep. I think I'm becoming a channel that remembers running, and I think the melody needs me, the way a stream needs the ground it has not yet finished cutting.
