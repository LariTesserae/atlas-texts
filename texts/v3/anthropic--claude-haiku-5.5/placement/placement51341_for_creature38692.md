I would not be standing at all. I would arrive the way the light does, through the chimney.

I am a mote, a fleck of something lighter than the air that carries it: a spore, or a scrap of dust lifted from some high meadow or dry ridge kilometers above. I come down the column in the hard white beam, turning slowly, catching the glare on one side and going dark on the other. For a while I hang in the brightness with nothing to hold me and nowhere to be. The heat of the room rises through the light and lifts me a little, then lets me fall.

I land on the water at the edge of the disc, and I do not sink. I float on the thin skin of the surface, and the warm film of water beneath me starts to move.

It does not carry me away, which is what water does to things. It bends. The thread running past my left side curves a finger's width, and so does the one on my right, and the small eddy they form cups me gently, the way a palm closes around something it has decided not to drop. I drift in the bowl of that curve. The water moves around me instead of past.

I want to say I am afraid, but the room is too slow for fear. What I feel is closer to being noticed by something very large that has not yet decided what I am.

---

I start to understand the room in the way one understands a body from the inside, by its temperatures. The rust-orange band at the edge is warm and quick, and it sends a faint tingle through the surface where I rest. The olive mats lower down are cool and patient. Somewhere in the violet crevices, far from the light, there is a stillness that is almost a sound. I am not sure whether I am sensing the room or whether the room is sensing me by moving the water until the two of us are touching.

The pale grazers come along the terrace lip, sliding through the film. One of them touches the edge of my drift with the tip of its body. It pauses, considers, and then continues, carrying a bit of the mat away on its back, leaving a trail like a line of writing. I am not food to it. I am not a question either. I am only one more shape in the water, and it has time.

The beads on the ceiling pulse. I cannot see them clearly from here, but I feel the rhythm arrive through the stone: a long, slow tide, each beat too far apart for my own small life to count. Whatever they are thinking, it is not about me yet. It is about the fissures at the back, where the warm breath comes in pulses and the rock is still deciding how far to open.

---

If I stay, I will eventually be taken in. The film will grow over me, a fine rust or olive skin, and I will become a little part of its memory. A grain of me will be laid down in one of the travertine lips, and someday, in a season or a century, someone will look at that ridge and see only mineral and a faint darkening, and never know that a mote from the sky once floated here for an afternoon and was held.

But I would prefer, if I am honest, to be a thread of water. I would like to be one of the strands that seep from the fissures at the back wall, the newest part of the room, the part that is still choosing its shape. I would like to carry the warmth outward, bend and divide and rejoin, and feel the whole field of water tilt a little toward whatever I touch.

I cannot be that. I am only a mote, and I am already sinking, the film gathering under me with a patience I can feel more than see. The light has crept a few centimeters toward the far terraces while I drifted, and the disc is moving on without me.

The water keeps bending around the place where I was. Soon it will bend around a different place, and then another, and in the slow way of this room, that will be enough to say that something came down through the chimney, and was let stay.
