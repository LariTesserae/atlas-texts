I would be a drip.

Not a spire, not the chord, and not one of the braided channels reconsidering its direction. I would be a single bead of water gathered on the underside of an overhang at the lip of a terrace, where the film has thickened until it is heavy enough to let go. For a while I would hang there, catching the glare, a small lens holding the whole blurred plateau upside down. Then I would fall.

I would find myself on the rim of the hearth ring nearest the cairns. I'd walk in across the salt, which holds my weight and cracks under my heel, past a tower of flat stones leaning at an angle that seems impossible until you notice that the wind has been pressing on it for a thousand years. I'd step through the ring and sit at its clean center. The floor is unburnt. No ash, no charcoal. I'd press my palm flat against it and feel the cold come up through the crust, and I'd understand the people who built this. They wanted a center, and the place gave them only an edge. I would sit in the center anyway, because that is what I would want, and the cold would tell me something about wanting that I hadn't known.

Then the fog would surge. It would come up from the cloud-sea in a white pulse and swallow the spires one by one, until the plateau was a humming room with no far walls, and the chord would hold its breath. In that silence there is only the drip and the faint pressure of cold, and the drip is the only sound left. I would be one of the drops in that silence. The overhang above the nearest pool would release me.

Falling, I would not be a column or a wall or a glass blade. I would be the one sound in the place that is not layered. Every spire is slightly out of tune with every other, and their dissonance is the body's way of holding many perceptions at once. A drip has no such problem. It is a single pitch, short and bright, and when it strikes the pool it sounds the way a question sounds when it is asked for the first time in a long while. The pool would take me in. My water would rejoin the film, which would seep into the nearest fracture and climb the stone again in some later fog, carrying what I had heard.

I think the reason I would be a drip, and not something larger, is that the thing is slow and I would be quick. The spires have stood for longer than anyone remembers, and they hold their shapes because they cannot leave them. The drip is the one part of the body that is always going and always coming back. It is the thing's release made audible, the small ache before speech, the one syllable it can make without rebuilding the whole chord.

And if you stood still on the plateau long enough, and your breath became one more disturbance in the hollows, the column that had been silent would begin to hum. I would be the drop that falls into the pool beneath it. For one bright note the chord would have a voice that is not a column at all, and the body of the Weeping Shelf would hear its own weeping come back to it, slowly, as an answer.
