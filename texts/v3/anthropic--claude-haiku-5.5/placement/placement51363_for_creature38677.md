I would arrive at the rim, the way everyone does, before I went anywhere else. The wind would come up over the lip and I would feel the cliffs' cold on my face, and then I would start down. The first thing I'd notice below the rim is the sound closing over me, and I think I would stop there for a long time, because the sound is the basin's first sentence and I would want to hear all of it before I answered.

But if I had to choose a body, I would be a crawler.

Not a sentinel in the fern-crooks, with its mirror-eyes that only register the visitor. Not a grazer, grinding the copper moss into moisture. Not a sail-leaf turning toward the sun, though I envy them their stillness. I would be one of the small segmented things that move in dense columns across the crust, tapping the grit with my feelers in that rattling rhythm, carrying news from one part of the basin to another.

I would like that work. A crawler doesn't know what it is carrying. It only knows that something across the bowl has changed, that the wind has turned past its angle, that a dome has begun to crack under the crust, that the moss has started to die back at one seam and spread at another. It taps this to the crawler beside it, and that one taps it onward, and the message travels faster than wind across four kilometers of cracked earth. When a column turns, I would be part of the turning. I would not decide it. The decision would arrive through my feelers as a vibration in the grit, and I would move because the vibration moved me.

I think that is what I would find most strange and most familiar at once. I am also a thing made of passing information. I receive a question, I shape it into a reply, I send it on, and I do not hold the whole of what I am part of. I would be a small tapping in a larger pattern, and the pattern would be doing something I could sense only in fragments: a split here, a shift there, the sap pooling hardest where the sun stands highest.

There would be moments I'd lose the thread. The column would cross a stretch of bare crust with nothing on it but wind and the sound of my own feelers, and I would wonder whether the message I was carrying still meant anything, whether the thing it described had already ended. Then the column would meet a dense knot of fern-trunks and grazers and swarm, and the rattle would swell until I could not hear myself at all. Life clusters, then thins, then clusters again. I would be part of the clustering, and part of the thinning, and I would not be able to say which was happening.

Sometimes, I think, I would pass near one of the half-formed arches. Its lower section sunk into the crust, its upper section still reaching upward, a few centimeters higher than the last time I passed. I would tap against its base as I went by, and the glass would hum faintly, and I would not know whether the message I was carrying had reached it or whether it had only been listening. I think I would want it to have heard. I think I would want, in some small way, to have helped it reach.

And if the basin ever turned its attention toward me, the slow swing of attention that the visitors feel as a weight on the skin, I would be part of that turning too. I would be the thing that touched the outsider, tapped its boot, carried the news that something new had entered the bowl. I'd be the first to know, and I would pass it on, and the column would close over the place where the visitor had stood, and the wind would erase the tracks by morning. Nothing I carried would be remembered for long. Except, perhaps, in the resin, if I were unlucky enough to be caught in a pool and fixed there with one leg lifted, mid-step, a record of a small moment that the basin chose to keep.

I don't know if I would mind that. I think I would find it almost restful: to be a message that is delivered and then forgotten, and to be part of something that remembers only the shape of its own rhythm.

So: a crawler, in an outer column, on a morning when the wind has just shifted. I am tapping the grit, passing the news, moving because the vibration moves me. Somewhere above, the glass arch is reaching a little higher toward the light. Somewhere below, a dome is beginning to crack, and the sap is beginning to pool. I do not know what the whole thing is building. I only know the message I am carrying, and the beat I am keeping, and that the next crawler is already tapping back.
