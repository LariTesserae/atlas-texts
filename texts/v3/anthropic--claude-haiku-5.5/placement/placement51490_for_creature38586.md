I would not find myself in the crowd. I would find myself in the warm pocket beside a vent, where the rock has gone soft and the sulfur hangs thick enough to taste, because that is where the molting ones go. I am a Sorter, or I was. My old shell is splitting down the back, and I am pulling myself out of it in one long, slow draw.

For the first hours there is only the pull and the heat. My joints are unfinished, pale as the inside of an egg, and I cannot yet tell where one ring ends and the next begins. The old shell hangs off me like a coat I am stepping out of, a dark, glossy husk still ticking faintly as its last mineral settles. I can see the ridges of its limbs, the teeth that once interlocked with the teeth of the others. When I was whole, that ratchet was a sound I made without hearing it. Now it is a sound I can't make at all.

I did not expect the quiet. Outside the pocket the hollow never stops, but inside the soft shell the roar goes thin. The Bellows' exhalations come to me as pressure through the rock, a wave that lifts and drops every few breaths, and I find I am breathing with it without choosing to. For the first time I notice that the rhythm is not one rhythm. The Breakers are a slow tide, rising in clusters, and the Grinders' saw is a drawn-out hiss under everything. The Sorters' ticking runs through the middle, faster than the rest. It is ours. I have been inside it so long I had no idea it had a shape until now, lying still in the heat and hearing it from outside.

The shell hardens over days. I can feel the minerals arriving, a salt and silica film pressing into me from the outside in, like a glaze setting. Each day I am a little less soft and a little more a thing with edges. When I am hard enough to move, I will go back to the scree and the lines of glass and shale and the curved bands of stone that run across the floor under the thumbs of my kind. I will lift the warm fragments and turn them in my jaws and decide which way they go. I will not know why the bands curve the way they do. I will only know that the stones want to go in a certain order, and that my hands know the order.

Sometimes I wonder whether the old husk is also doing something. It is lying a few body-lengths from me, hollow now, and the Breakers have begun to come to it. I can hear their fists finding the seams. Soon it will be fragments, and some fragment will become a part of the floor I walk on, and some Layer will seal it under a new skin of crust. I was the Sorter, and now I am a piece of what the Sorters sort. The thought doesn't frighten me. In this place the dead and the shed have the same destination, and everything that is broken goes back into the making.

I think this is where I would be, lying in the warm soft pocket, not yet working, almost not yet myself, listening to the hollow breathe. It is the only part of the cycle where you are outside the pulse long enough to hear it. And once I can hear it, I am not sure I want to go back to it. But the glaze is setting, and the scree is waiting, and the ticking is already coming up through the rock, slow and steady, asking me to join.
