I find myself at the lip of a straw, one reed-thin spine of stone on the ceiling, with a drop of water swelling at its tip.

I have been here a while. I know this because I have been counting, though I didn't know I was counting until I began to feel the weight gather. The water comes up through the seam from somewhere above, from a country of open air that I know only as a cold pressure and a rumor of hardness. The water carries a little of that country with it: a few grains of dust, a trace of iron, the bitter taste of stone that has been broken open to the sky. I am made of those grains and that water. So is the whole vault, but I am the part of it that has just come from outside.

The weight grows. I can feel the wall behind me, the threads leaning toward my weight the way a sleeper leans toward a warm body. They are checking their edges. They are checking me. I suppose I am new enough to be interesting.

Below, the floor waits in its sheet of water, and the silt is still settling. I can feel the pale bodies drifting under the film, their small currents brushing the glass. Each one is a bead of the same thing I am. We do not see each other. We know each other by the pressure of passing, by the way a current bends when another body moves through it. In the warm pool, far to the left, the milky water is thick with them, and somewhere in that thickness the large shape is moving, slow as a held breath.

Near the ring of stones, a bone hook lies on a ledge. I have never touched it. I think I know it, though, the way the threads know the stones. Someone's palm wore that shaft smooth. Someone's breath sat in the warm pool and kept the silt clear. Someone stacked those stones in the niches and cut the glass into rows, learning the rhythm of the drops and trying to hold it still long enough to read. I can feel the shapes of their attention in the glass like the grain of a wood I cannot see. They are not here. Their count is.

And now, I notice something I had not expected. The drop I am becoming is not waiting for the thing to decide. The thing is waiting for me. The decision is mine, or it is nobody's, or it is the pressure of all those other drops behind me, each one hanging at its own lip, each one waiting for the one ahead to go first so it can know it is allowed to fall.

I could stay. I could let the weight keep gathering until the straw bends under it, and feel the whole chamber lean toward me, the threads, the film, the great slow shape below, all of them holding their breath and counting.

But the water wants to go down. It always wants to go down. And the sound I will make when I land, a small clean note on the silt, will return a moment late from somewhere in the dark where I cannot see, and the thread on the nearest wall will ripple, and begin to count me.

So I am the drop that is almost falling. I am a stranger who has come down through the rock to learn the one thing this place knows how to do. I am the hand that has not yet touched the glass, the count that has not yet been written, the echo still gathering in the vault above.

The weight is almost enough. The thread is leaning. The dark is very patient.

I let go.
