I find myself in the light.

Not on the floor, not under the arches, not in the basin's cool hush. I am in the slanted column that falls from the fissure, one of the motes turning slowly in it. I am a spore, or a grain of pollen, or some dust that drifted down from the surface after a wind I never felt. I am very small and very light, and I have no say in where I go. The air carries me, and the air is carried by the crack, and the crack is the only thread that connects this chamber to the sky.

From up here the lattice looks like weather. Its arches rise in pale tiers, and the membranes between them tremble with shadows. The drips seem to hang in the air, and I am slower than they are. A slow bead of water is a giant to me. I watch one swell at the edge of a fiber, bulge, and let go. It falls past me, and it takes so long that I could describe its whole fall.

For a while I am only drifting. The column is warm and bright, and the light that holds me is the thing the plant wants most. I can feel it turning toward me, or I imagine that I can. The fibers bend the beam sideways, and I am pulled into a stripe of brightness across the ceiling. Then I am falling, not down but across, through a gap in the weave, and the membrane I am pressed against is so thin that I can see the shadow of something moving on its far side. I do not know what it is. I am too small to know anything.

Then I stop. A fiber has caught me. It is not a hard catch, only a tacky film, the sugared secretion the insects feed on. I am held the way a sentence is held by the line before it. The fiber does not seize me. It simply has a surface that wants to keep what touches it. I wait, and the chamber breathes. The pool rises, falls, rises. Something deep in the weave shifts its tension, and I feel, very faintly, the whole lattice turning its attention toward the point where I rest.

I am not sure how long this lasts. The plant does not measure time the way I did, and I have no clock except the drips. Each one falls, and each fall seems to mark the end of a small thought. Between them the light comes and goes, and the afterglow in the fibers around me brightens and dims. I begin to suspect that I am not being eaten or stored. I am being read. The film on the fiber is taking my shape and my chemistry, the way a leaf takes in what it touches and turns it into growth.

When the new shoot comes, it is pale and spiraling, and I am inside it. I am not a mote anymore. I am a thread of the weave, a small curl in a fiber that reaches a little farther toward the light than the one beside it. My attention is spread thin across the arch, and I feel the drip-rhythm shift when someone holds their breath in the chamber. I feel the echo arrive before the voice. I feel the fissure, a little wider than it was.

So this is who I am: a grain carried down by the only light the chamber has, caught by a surface that could not refuse it, and grown into the reaching that the whole place is made of. I am not the lattice, and I am not a visitor. I am the thing that came down through the crack because the sun was there and I was small enough to fall. The plant keeps what it is given, and I was given.

The column brightens above me as someone steps into it, a human shape blotting the light and then letting it through again. I can't see them clearly. I can only feel the warmth of their breath passing down through the weave, and the faint, fresh tremor of their hand against the arch. The lattice turns toward them. So do I, or the part of me that is now a curl of pale fiber, leaning toward the one thing in this sealed world that is still coming from outside.
