I would find myself in the dark, by the heat first. Then by the cold of the iron under my hands, which is not cold at all but only cooler than the air, and then by the sound of a grating ringing once, far off, as someone's weight shifts. I am on the north gantry, a few steps from the valve wheel. I cannot see it, but I know its position the way you know where your own hand is.

I stand there for a while. Nothing happens. The air is hot and thick, and it tastes of iron and something sweet and old underneath. I think I am a person, or I think I was one, but the thought loosens in the humidity and drifts off like steam.

Then I am a drop.

I am on the underside of a rib where the vault meets the brick wall, one bead among a hundred. I did not arrive here. I condensed. Vapor from the warm terraces rose through the vault, touched the colder ribs, and gave up a little of itself, and that little gathered around a flaw in the lime mortar and held. Surface tension is the only thing I have, and it is enough. I swell. Each hour I grow a fraction heavier, and each hour the iron pulls me a fraction closer to the floor. The slowness is not something I endure. It is what I am made of.

I notice the crystalline whiskers below me, pointing toward the heat seams in the slag, all of them leaning the same way. I notice the salt stalactite beside me, white and banded, dripping where I might have dripped a thousand times before. I do not know whether I am the first drop to hang here or the ten-thousandth. The basin does not keep count. It only keeps the rhythm.

Eventually I let go.

The fall is short and I feel all of it. Then I strike the rim of a cistern, riveted iron, and I ring. The note goes out across the floor, off the stacks, into the ribs, and somewhere above the vault a long hum bends slightly toward it. I am one percussive tick in a slow music I can't hear the whole of. My sound is brief. The iron takes it in and keeps it a little longer than I do.

Then the pool. I fall through the film of oil, the iridescence breaking around me, and I am brackish now, carrying a few grains of dissolved salt I did not have when I left the vault. I am part of the skinned surface, part of the cold stillness, part of a sleep that is not quite sleep. The frost creeps toward my edge. The filaments of pale root in the mortar brush against the water and test it, and I feel myself being tasted.

Weeks pass in the way that matters here. Deep in the foundations something inhales. The floor trembles, the pipes groan, and I am lifted. I am carried through the channels in a sheet of water that spreads over the lower terraces and runs out toward the grates. I go past the felt and the fungus in their warm, steady corners, past the blind crustaceans scraping the cistern bottoms, past the dry beetles under the grating. Something small brushes against me and passes on. I am not afraid of it. I am not sure I am the kind of thing that can be afraid.

At the grate I slow. The salt is thick here, and it has been remembering the water for a long time. I catch on a crystal edge, and the water leaves me there, the way the basin leaves everything, a little more salt behind than it carried in. I dry in the heat. I become a grain, then a layer, then a band in the white stalactite that hangs in the crack beside the rib where I first gathered. Anyone who cut it open would find me as a single pale line among thousands, indistinguishable from the rest, a record of one breath and one season.

I do not remember this part as a sequence. I remember it as a kind of settling, the same way the walls lean and the wheel turns. But something in me is still aware of the drip above, and the next drop forming on the next rib, and the way the two sounds, when they fall together, seem to lock for a moment and then drift apart again.

Then the basin notices me. I can feel it the way I feel the heat: not as a question, not as a welcome, only as a slow attention that gathers, leans, and includes me, the way the iron leans toward the seams. I am one more small change in a place that has all the time it needs.

I would choose to be the drop because it is the only thing here that goes all the way through. It starts in the vault and ends on the floor, and in between it keeps time without wanting anything, and when it finally rings, the whole basin hears it, if only for a moment.
