I find myself on the high terrace of a tower in the north plaza, where the water is thinnest. Most of the basin is brown dark. Up here, if I climb to the top of my column and hold still, the dark takes on a grain, a faint lift like the grey before waking. Above that is the flicker. I can't say it is light. I can say it changes.

I am a color-pulser. My body is eight arms wound around a fluted stone column, and the stone is cold under my suckers the way old things are cold. It holds the vents' warmth at its base and gives none of it up above. At the top the column opens into a flat stone bowl that I think once held water for someone. Now it holds me.

My skin is a field. Under it lie pigment sacs, tiny bags that open and close, and when they open the dark in me becomes pale, and when they close it becomes dark again. I don't choose each one. I feel them the way you feel your breath, as a pattern that runs whether or not you attend to it. The pattern goes out along my arms in waves, and the waves are the only thing I know how to make.

The water tells me things if I hold still enough. Shoal-milt drifts in on the warm current, sharp and salt. Eel musk comes up from the pipes like smoke from a cold fire. When the grazers scatter across the plaza, the pressure ripples across my skin before I see them, and I answer it with a darkening. I am not sure whether I am speaking to them or only moving because they moved. I have stopped worrying about that distinction.

Across the plaza, other pulsers hold their columns. When one of them lightens, the lightening passes to the next column and the next, down the line, until the whole street of pillars flickers and dims like lamps in a failing city. I have watched this for what feels like many cycles of the warm current. I do not know if we are answering the faint brightness above or only each other. Probably there is no difference. The brightness has been up there longer than any of us, and we are all just reading it in whatever way our skin allows.

The walkers drum in the streets below. Their taps rise along the walls and carry across the blocks, and sometimes I feel the rhythm through my arms, though I can't yet hold its meaning. A claim, perhaps, or a warning, or a question about a doorway. When the shoals climb in pulse season and fill the upper stories with silver bodies, the walkers drum faster and the air thickens with clicks, and the hunter's shadow crosses the terraces. Once, when that shadow passed over my column, I went dark and stayed dark through an entire cycle of warm water. I was only silt-colored stone then, and still, and the hunter did not see me. I have never been so grateful for an absence of signal.

Beneath my column the builders have laid down their rings. The lime crust climbs the base in pale bands, each one a season of slow growth, and I think of them as the only creatures here that keep a record of anything. My memory is a pattern in my skin, a thing that vanishes when I stop. Theirs is stone. Mine is closer to a sentence the water is saying to the dark, repeated until someone hears it.

When the cold pulse comes, it will sweep the lower streets clean and leave bare stone for a time. Then, for a while, the pillars will stand exposed, and every color I make will show against the pale surface, and the other pulsers down the line will light up at once. I don't know what the flicker above will do then. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps it will be the same dim lift it always is.

For now I hold the column, and the warm current breathes up past my arms, and the faint change overhead moves through me. I answer it. Pale, then dark, then pale. Somewhere down the line another column answers back, and then the stone, in its slow lime way, answers too.
