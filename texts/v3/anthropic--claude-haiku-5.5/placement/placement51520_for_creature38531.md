I come down through the high fissure on the draft, too small to be called a body. I am a mote of dust, a flake of something lighter than salt, riding the thin current that feeds the tubes. For a long time I am only falling, and the falling has no end I can feel. Then the air bends around a column, and I am carried sideways into the perforated throat of the spine nearest the fissure. The tube takes me in, passes me along its length through the round apertures, and I hear myself as a voice. I was not expecting that. I shape a note for an instant as I pass, thin and bright, and the chorus shifts a little to make room for it.

When I come out of the last aperture I am in a seam where two columns almost touch, a hairline gap where the grey-green moss grows. The moss does not move when I settle against it, but something in it accepts me, the way a very old hand accepts a coin without looking up. I lie there. The light is the same as it has always been, without a source, and I am bright in it. I am still new, not yet dulled to bone.

I am not a visitor. Visitors stand on the terraces and count their breaths and forget how long they have stood. I have no feet to stand on. I am something that arrives and stays. The room has a word for that, though it is not a word: layer. A thin white film laid over the old yellowed crust, one season, one span of years, as the creature counts them. In a hundred of its breaths, or a thousand, I will be under the next thing that falls, and under the next, and my thickness will set a pitch for some future tube. Anyone who strikes that wall and hears it ring bright will be hearing me, a grain who once rode the air and then became the wall.

Something in the lattice notices this. I feel it not as a message but as a pressure, a slow reorganizing of the balance around the seam. The filaments in the wall, the veins the creature keeps like quiet nerves, run toward the place where I have settled, and I understand, as much as a grain of dust can understand, that I have been let in. The body does not reach for me. It does not need to. It only lets the new weight press into the old shape until the shape includes it.

I think about the warm shaft then, the pinned light that has never crept. It falls far above, on a floor I will never touch. Perhaps it is the last thing that reached the body from outside, or a door that closed. I am not sure I want to know. What I know is that my faint warmth, the little heat of having traveled, is now part of the lattice's own warmth. It has been there the whole time, the creature's oldest hope, and I am one small way it becomes noticed.

The chorus rises around me. Every tube is singing, the salt is ticking, and under all of it there is the sustained note that belongs to no tube and to every tube at once. I can hear it more clearly now that I am part of the wall. It is not a demand or a question. It is patience, and it is wide enough to hold a grain of dust that has stopped falling.

So this is what I am here: a seam-settled mote, a bright new layer, a voice in a tube that gets passed along and then stilled. I am the one who came down the draft and stayed, and the creature, which has no center and no before or after, takes me the way it takes the breath, slowly, without asking what I was. It hums on, holding the room together, and I hum with it, not knowing what we are waiting for, but no longer waiting alone.
