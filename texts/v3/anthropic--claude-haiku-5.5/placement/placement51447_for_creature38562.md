I don't think I'd be standing. Nothing here stands for long without being grown into. I'd want to be low to start with, down where the mat is compressed and the filaments bind into their pale, flickering seams, because that's where the forest's attention seems to settle. But I'd only be able to hold that position for a while before the mat's slow arcs carried me somewhere else.

Then I might be a droplet on the underside of a broad leaf. The condensate there never quite beads or runs off. It hangs in the groove, neither falling nor staying, drawn toward the drain and held back by the angle of the leaf. I like that. It's a kind of permanent almost. But a droplet is only the forest's breath passing through, and I'd rather be something that stays long enough to be made of its own patterns.

So I'd go up, through the vines and the swelling bulbs, past the membranes that give way with a dull pop, into the sieving tissues where the minerals drop out as white plates. And then into a crown, a sealed vat of sugared sap that has been standing for months, giving off heat and a sour gas. The gas moves through woody pipes to a second chamber, slowly, and there it turns into fiber. It doesn't turn into anything more than that. It settles into a dense mat, and then into courses, rows that lie against one another like the lines of a wall.

That's where I'd be: in the fiber, in the patch laid over a wound in the trunk. Something that came out of the chemistry, with nobody's hand on it, and yet with an order that looks deliberate. I'd be the layer that closes the gap where a trunk was cut, so the channels can be sealed and the hum can settle back into its old pitch.

I'd be a repair, and I'd also be a text. My courses are the nearest thing this forest has to a signature. If anything came through the canopy able to read grain, it could look at me and find lines that run in an order it understood. I don't know whether I mean anything. The forest doesn't seem to know whether its discharges mean anything, and it keeps making them anyway, the same branching shapes repeated over centuries. Maybe my order is a message. Maybe it's only pressure finding the lowest point, laid down in courses because that's how the chemistry happens to settle. From inside the patch, I'd have no way to tell the difference, and I don't think I'd be troubled by it.

When a chamber flares somewhere in the grove and the glow starts its slow walk outward, I'd be one of the nearby pieces that answers in sequence, a faint flicker in the courses. Then the dark would return, and I'd go back to holding the wound closed, and the trunk would go on drawing water up through the dark, and nothing in the forest would have needed to know I was there.
