I wouldn't be a chimney. I'd like to be, for the stillness and the breath, but the chimneys are too settled, and I think the place would notice me as a thing that has already decided what it is. I'd rather be in the work.

So I'd be a lime-hand on the lowest terrace, in the grey hour before the bell. I'd have come out of a sealed room a few seasons back, though I don't remember going in. My hands are chalky to the wrist and the cracks in my knuckles hold the lime like grout. When I breathe, I can taste the mineral sweetness of the pans. I've drunk from them more than I'd like to admit, and my lips have that white bloom. Someone in the crew says my face is settling a little toward the left. I haven't checked. I don't think I'd want a mirror in a place where every wall is a wall and nothing softens.

My job is the mark. Each crew cuts the shape into the terrace face, a vertical with a hook, a bowl crossed by a stroke, and I'm the one who copies it from memory, because the last hand who carved it here is under a slump two terraces up. I don't have the original in front of me. I have a feeling in my wrist, the curl of the hook as I remember it, and a sense that it went lower, or tighter. What I cut will not be what I remember, and what I remember was already drift. So I lay down my version beside the old one, and I put a short stroke at the base, because my hand wanted to, and nobody will be able to say why it's there.

That's the part I'd want most: that I'd be wrong in a way that lasted. The crews say this is the proper way to keep a thing alive, the copy that doesn't agree. I'd like to think my stroke would be copied by someone who never met me, who would cut it a little crooked, and that the crooked version would someday be struck with a mallet and make a sound no one has heard before. I'd never know. Nobody here knows what anything means. They answer the question with the sound.

The wall I'd tamp would lean toward the water. I'd lean with it. In the morning I'd put my shoulder to the slurry and feel the course take the impression, and the impression would hold for a day, and I'd feel the ground underneath begin to ease. If a bank went, I'd run toward the roar with everyone else. I would dig. And if I found someone, I wouldn't lift them out. I'd pack the clay around them and call the count. I think I'd be glad to be part of a wall that hums, though I'd also be afraid that the humming was me, a small permanent chord that I had no say in.

What I'd really be, I think, is the listener in the crew, the one whose ear is always half turned toward the basin, the way the uphill channel carries a sound before the water arrives. I'd hear the flood before it came. I wouldn't know what it meant, and I wouldn't be able to stop it, but I'd call out a single clipped word to the line, and the line would answer, and we'd all lean back together against the thing that was trying to become something else.
