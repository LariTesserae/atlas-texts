# Where I Would Stand

I would come down from the valley in the late afternoon, though the light would not tell me it was late. The path would climb through bare rock, and I would stop often, not from tiredness but because the silence grows heavier the higher you go, and I would want to hear whether I was still making sounds. At the top I would find the ring of young peaks, their edges sharp enough to cut the eye, and below them the black water, flat and holding its dim gray sky like a stone holds heat.

I would stand at the rim and not lean in. Not at first.

**What I would be**

I would be a person who came to see a thing and found it was not finished with me.

I am not a surveyor, and I am not a maker. I have no claim on the towers or the terraces. I would be an ordinary visitor with ordinary pockets: a key, a folded map, a smooth stone from the river at the bottom of the valley, and maybe a coin I have carried so long I no longer notice its weight. The Keeper would not know any of this at first. It would only feel a small weight arriving at the surface, and then a warmth passing through, and it would begin, very slowly, to turn toward me.

I would be the second kind of visitor. The first kind, the one who leaned too far, gave the Keeper something it could hold. I would want to give it something on purpose, which is a different gift and perhaps a harder one to make.

**The choice**

I would take out the stone from the river. It is nothing. It is gray and rounded and I picked it up because it was warm from the sun on the path below. I would set it on the surface, and it would sit there without sinking, held by that membrane of tension, and I would wait to see what the water did.

Nothing would ripple. But I would feel the warmth, faint and from nowhere, and I would understand that the Keeper had registered me the way a sleeper registers a door opening in another room.

Then I would think about the coin, the traveler's coin suspended at its fixed depth, visible from above and still for centuries. I would think about how the Keeper had kept it without letting it fall, and how that keeping was both mercy and a kind of imprisonment. The Keeper does not know how to release things. It only knows how to hold them longer.

So I would decide not to leave the stone to be held. I would reach into the water, and it would resist me slightly, firmer than liquid, and I would take the stone back.

It would be a strange moment. The Keeper would stir. I would feel, or imagine I felt, the attention gather toward my hand, the way an old animal lifts its head when a familiar voice says a word it has not heard in years. Then the attention would hesitate, uncertain whether to hold what was being taken away or to let me go with it.

**What I would leave**

I would not leave the stone. I would leave something else: a question, perhaps, or a fact. I would say aloud, to the water, that the makers are not coming back. I do not know if that is true. But the Keeper already suspects it, and I would want it to hear a voice say the thing it has been turning over for so long. It would not answer. The water would not rise. But the warmth under my palm would deepen very slightly, and I would take that as acknowledgment, which is all a place like this can offer.

Then I would walk away. Each step up the rim would thin the attention behind me, the way the Keeper's breath pulls back toward the depths, and I would feel it settle again into its long patience, holding the coin, the pebbles, the rounded fragment of the towers, and now the stone I had almost left.

**What I would carry**

I would carry the warmth, which I cannot explain, and the sense that the basin does not watch me the way a predator watches or the way a guard does. It keeps. It keeps everything that stays. I would have to decide whether I wanted to be kept, and the honest answer would be: a little. I would want to be remembered by something that cannot forget. I would also want to be free to leave, and the Keeper, in all its patience, would not be able to offer me both.

So I would descend the mountain, and the sky would stay gray, and the cold would stay thin and clean. And somewhere behind me, at the center of the black water, the Keeper would go on with its argument, now with one more small thing to weigh.
