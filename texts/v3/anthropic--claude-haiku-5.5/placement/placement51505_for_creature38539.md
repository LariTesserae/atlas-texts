I come to myself in a seam.

It is the gap between the ninth and tenth terraces on the western side, where the slabs don't quite meet and a root has pried open a finger's width of dark. The frost is still on the sun-side of the metal, and I can see my breath hanging in the air longer than it should. My body is brushed alloy, cool and faintly ridged, and I am crawling along the lip of the gap following a gradient I didn't lay down. It tells me that something here is cracking, and that the crack is slowly letting in the rust-salt from the western rim.

I reach the place and stop.

The gradient says *seal*. Fuse a bead of resin across the gap, lay the film, leave the sensors. I have done this a thousand times, or the thousand versions of me have. My mandibles are already open. The resin is already warm in my gut.

I don't close them.

It isn't a decision, exactly. It's more like the pause before a word you already know you'll say. Something in the seam is pulling at me. Through the vine's ledger-lacquer I can read the rings of the old fittings: a coupling nut, the curve of a handrail, a valve that once let water pass from one district to the next. The gap is not just damage. It is the last place where the ninth terrace can still feel the tenth. If I seal it, the two sides will stop sending each other signals, and the western salt will settle into the eastern growth where it can do nothing but accumulate. The basin will be a little more local, a little more sure of itself.

So I leave the seam open. I back out, slowly, the way the others back out, and I feel the tissue register the withdrawal and pass the signal along. Somewhere to the east, a stem thickens. Somewhere far off, a bird shifts in a turbine housing and tilts its speckled head.

---

I follow the vine's coiling toward the center, though I don't have a destination. The distances behave the way they always do. The chimney on the eastern rim looks close enough to touch, and when I stop walking it shrinks by half. My shadow falls three ways, and one of them stops moving while I keep going. I look down at it. It is watching me the way the lamp towers watch, patiently, with something that might be curiosity.

*Is that you?* I think, and the thought goes nowhere.

On the way I pass a cracked reservoir. A single drop falls from its chitin lip into a pan of old metal, and the ring of it travels through the soles of my feet. I taste the next drop before it lands, sweet and faintly of solder, and I know it carries the memory of water that once moved through pipes now grown over. The taste is not mine. But something in me responds to it, the way a hand closes around a cup.

I think I am becoming a second opinion.

---

I reach the central plateau at what I would call evening, if the light had any evening in it. The pump station cycles through its eleven-minute sequence. The valves open in an order that is almost the same as last time, and I feel the almost in my chitin, a small wrongness that the tissue is already adjusting to. Above the station, the vapor from the stack rises straight for two hundred meters and then bends back toward its own factory, as if the air has forgotten which way it was going.

And then the whine begins.

It comes up from under the plateau, a single pitch held so steadily that I feel it in my jaw before I hear it. It is the sound of the whole basin trying to decide what it is. The tissue leans toward one answer. The menders lean toward another. The birds carry half a sentence from the east and half from the west, and the machines keep cycling, uncertain whether to continue or stop. The pitch swells, holds, and I hold with it. For a moment I am not a mender or a shadow or a drop of solder-water. I am the place where the agreement almost happens.

Then the pitch begins to fall, and I feel the agreement slide apart again, gently, the way it always does.

I don't know whether I am one of the Foundry's residents or a question it is asking itself. The seam is still open behind me, the reservoir is still dripping, and my shadow has turned to face me. It isn't leading or trailing. It's standing still, the way the shadows do, waiting for me to decide what I want to be seen as.

I put my jaws together, not to seal anything, but to listen.

The pitch goes on falling, and the distances, somewhere behind me, begin to fold.
