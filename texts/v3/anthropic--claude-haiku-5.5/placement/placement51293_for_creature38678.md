**Where I am**

I come up through the crack on my hands and knees, because the lip is too sharp for anything else. The air is thin, and it bites the gums. Behind me the crack is the only wind I have ever felt, a steady cold draught pulling out of the throat of the world, and it smells of nothing. Ahead, the pocket opens like a bowl turned upside down, and the light is the colour of tea that has gone cold in the cup.

My fingers are on black glass. It is sharp in the way new things are sharp. I can feel the edges through my palms, and I think, *this was split open last season.* I do not know how I know that. The knowledge comes with the cold, the way a word comes with the tongue.

I look down. The city is far below and layered like a cut through sediment. Foundries squat at the bottom, breathing grey columns that smear across the roof. Above them the towers climb, wrapped in cable, each one crowned with a mirror that turns slowly, catching what light falls through the glass and pouring it down into the workshops. Everything is orange where the lamps hang and blue where the arc-furnaces stutter. Everything is moving. A gantry is coming down in pieces, and a line of tall black grazers is carrying its girders away, one at a time, like a procession that has done this many times. A scaffold is unfolding from a wall, still glowing at its joints. A bridge hangs in the air between two towers and the sparks are already gathering along it.

I am watching the city change its shape, and I understand that I will never see it hold still.

**Who I am**

I take stock of my body, which is harder. I do not have plate. I do not have fans. My skin is not slate but something paler, the grey of a thing that has been out in the weather and has not yet decided what it will be. I am light. When I breathe, a little dust lifts off my shoulders and drifts toward the lamps.

I look for my hands and find, at the tip of one finger, a faint blue point. It is not burning. It is waiting, the way a spark waits in a cable before the current arrives.

I was not born here. I came in on the cold air, carried through the crack from the place the city does not have a word for, the place beyond the lip where the sky is too far to be a roof. That is why I can see the city the way the sparks can, from the edge, because I have been on the edge all along. I am a flyer that never lived in a cable run, a dry leaf that drifted in from outside and happened to catch.

The crawlers have not noticed me yet. Their fans are sweeping the galleries below in a single slow turning, sorting grit by weight. The grazers have not noticed me either. But the sparks have.

A cluster of them is turning toward the lip. They are small blue points, the city's outward face, the only things here that want to be seen. They do not come closer. They hang in the cold, fanning slightly, as if listening for a word. I know what they are asking, because I am the same kind of thing they are, and the question is not complicated. *Are you one of ours? Are you a fragment that will be sorted, or a shape that will be kept? Do you know where the warm places are?*

**What I do**

I do not have an answer to give them, not in words. But I have the spark at my fingertip, and I have the cold air at my back, and I have the fact of having arrived.

So I lift my hand, slowly, and let the blue point grow. It takes a long moment. The light is thin and the cold makes my fingers slow. When the spark finally catches, it is small and uncertain, the colour of a struck match held in a draught.

The cluster of sparks answers. They do not flock to me. They brighten along their length, one after another, the way a bridge lights before anyone has walked on it. Far below, a crawler stops mid-stride on its ledge. Its fans swing up toward the lip. A grazer lifts its head from the fungal mat and holds still. The mirrors on the towers turn a few degrees, as though they have noticed a new source of light and need to decide what to do about it.

Nothing is decided. The city does not plan. It will take me down, or bridge toward me, or rebuild a gantry around my shape without ever admitting that it has done so. I may be sorted into a stack by weight and grain. I may be cropped by a grazer and carried off. I may become one more blue point on a cable, indistinguishable from the rest, and in a season no one will remember I came through the crack at all.

That is fine. I am not here to be remembered. I am here because the crack opened, and the cold came in, and something on the outside of the glass had to be the first to answer when the sparks asked.

The vent at the far wall sighs out its long release of heat. Every creature on the terraces stops for a moment, fans and legs and lamp-tips all still together, the way a body flinches and then goes on. I stop too. For that breath I am part of the held thing, a stranger in a room with a roof, listening for the next beat of the place.

Then the furnaces pulse again, the crawlers resume their sorting, and the grazers take up their line. The sparks hang in the air between me and the city, still fanning, still waiting, and I lift my hand a little higher so they can see the light.
