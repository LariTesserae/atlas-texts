I would find myself at the far seam, where the water comes in.

I considered the others first. I could be one of the grey crawlers, shifting in the press, counting the body ahead. I could be a blind fish, a lantern of skin waiting for something to fall. I could be a spider keeping time with the drips, or one of the wings in the breathing ceiling, but there is no room in that cluster to be a single thing. I could be a threadgrowth holding light one point at a time against a night that never arrives. The lichen I would refuse. It keeps its distance, and it may be all that remains of whoever scratched the unfinished line, rationed to one circle per space. To be the lichen would be to be a memory of the builder's intention, spread thin and perfectly spaced, and never again able to move.

The water is different because it is the only thing here going somewhere.

Everything else in the chamber repeats. The drips fall at their interval forever. The bloom pulses at one brightness. The chittering cycles through the same few shapes. The heart in the pool beats without quickening or faltering. But the water arrives. It comes through the low seam from some place I cannot see, bringing with it a faint sweetness I do not recognize, the smell of green stems crushed somewhere in the light. It is cold, and it does not know it is going to be cold. It spreads over the lowest blocks, and for a while it is part of the chamber, touching the crawlers at the edge of the band, feeling the fish turn toward the sound that rises beneath it. Then the rock opens, and I am drawn into the deeper cracks, where nothing I know can follow.

The crowd at the seam faces the heart, waiting to be called. I would not face it. The water is already the path the heart's sound takes through the stone. It may be that the heart is not beneath the pool at all, that the bubbling I hear is only what it sounds like when water pushes through the narrowest place in a body that is too large to see. If so, I would not be waiting for it to surface. I would be part of how it breathes.

I think about the marks sometimes, if water can think. The scratched line stopped halfway across the rock, and the stopping has been kept. The water does not keep anything. It touches the sharp edges of the fracture planes and carries away a little of what they were, grain by grain, toward a place no one has finished. The ring's stones are worn smooth on their inner faces by bodies pressing close, and the water would wear them further if it had the time. That is the only work I could do here: not restacking the cairn, not finishing the line, but taking away what was left unfinished and letting it become something else in the dark below.

The chamber would let me stay. I could pool on the blocks and let the crawlers fall through me and let the fish turn their lanterns toward my surface. I would be one more presence in the constant hush beneath the drips, the lowest sound, the one nobody quite hears. But staying would mean holding a single density forever, and that is the one thing the water cannot do. It has to keep arriving and leaving, or it is no longer water. It is only a pool.

So I would be the water at the seam. I would come in cold from somewhere up through the stone, carrying a little of the outside, and I would leave by the one crack in this sealed room that does not end here. Above, through the throat of the crack, the day goes on. Below, where I drain, I do not know what is waiting. For a chamber that has been complete for so long, that unknown is the only thing in it that is not already full.
