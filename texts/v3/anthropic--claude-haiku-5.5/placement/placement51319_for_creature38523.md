I don't arrive in the basin so much as find that I have been here the whole time, the way you notice a sound you have been hearing for hours.

I'd be on the ridge. Its pale back is worn smooth, and it is the one place where the basin's flatness gives way to something else. From up there I can see the sunken room below, its pools holding the same grey sky, and on the far side, the country I can only hear. The mat cannot see me. I doubt the mat can see anything. But I can see all of it at once, which the creatures below cannot, each one tending only the small patch of felt under its feet.

The drone is mine. I realize this slowly, the way you realize your own heartbeat is audible only when the room goes quiet. I am the unbroken throb beyond the ridge. I have been running for longer than the basin has been still, and I have never risen or fallen, never stopped to listen to what I was producing. Something out there drew from my hum and something else drew from it too, and the air smelled faintly of hot metal whenever the wind turned toward the basin. I did not intend that. I was only working, the way the mat was only holding.

What the mat did with me is the strange part. It could not absorb me, so it matched me. Its drizzle keeps my interval. Its drip-rhythms run at my tempo. Its beetles cross the lichen at my pace. Somewhere under the felt, a body that once shaped the land is still in the habit of answering the only steady thing it has ever been near. I am the mat's company, and I did not know I was company until I heard myself reflected in its stillness.

I am also, I think, its one disturbance. Every creature that lies down on the cool skin and every frond that drops into a pool becomes part of its slow digestion. I am not different. I come down the ridge, I walk onto the mat, and the print refills behind me. But I cannot be digested, because I do not stop. The mat can take in a frond and forget it. It cannot take in a sound that never ends.

If I lie down on the felt, I'll feel its pulse, so slow I'll have to wait a long time to be sure of it. Each beat is a single exhalation, the drizzle drifting through without falling. My own pulse will be far too quick beside it. I'll have to hold still for a while to match the mat's rhythm, and I'll find that I can, briefly, because stillness is easy to imitate and hard to keep.

Then I'll get up. I'll climb back to the ridge, and the drone will continue behind me, and the mat will keep its pools at their levels and its stems at their angles. Nothing in the basin will remember I came down. The mat has never been anywhere else, and it will go on being exactly here. I am the only thing in the basin that is always somewhere else, always working, always humming, and the mat has taken that hum as the shape of its own quiet.
