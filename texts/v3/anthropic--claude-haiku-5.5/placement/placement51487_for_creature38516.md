I wouldn't come down into the basin at first. I'd sit on the rim at dusk, when the drooping leaves close overhead and the roar drops into its lower register, and I'd lean against one of the cairns. Someone stacked those stones on purpose, one on another, and I'd want to be near a shape that a person meant. The stalks are already pushing the cairn apart, slowly, the way hair works through a cracked sidewalk. I'd watch it come down a few stones a season and feel no need to stop it.

When I did go down, I'd go slowly, because the ground here is not sure of itself. Pressing a foot into the pale warm crust, I'd feel it give like the flesh of something sleeping. I'd try to keep to the trampled channels the barrel-grazers cut, because those are the basin's handwriting, and it seems polite to read before walking.

But I think I would end up as the marker.

The carved post stands near the edge of the smooth pale patches, bleached and cracked, leaning toward them. Its bark still holds the spiral. A post can't leave. It can only stand, and be grown around, and slowly be tilted by the ground until it falls or until something decides it should stay. I would like to be the thing that stays long enough to be noticed. The basin has no interest in me yet, and I'd take that as an invitation to be very still.

From inside the post, the chord would be different. Out in the thickets it is a roar with a hum underneath, a sound you stop hearing and then hear again. But a post is planted in the crust, and so it feels the plates grind before the sound reaches the air. I'd feel the ground lift a fraction of a finger's width under one side of me in a season. I'd feel the basin tune itself, a tension rising in the substrate, a note held and then let go. I would become a kind of tuning fork, rooted and leaning, and I'd keep count of the changes the only way a post can, which is by the angle of its own lean.

The spiral is the part that keeps me thinking. I've begun to suspect it is not decoration. The cuts run in one direction and they are spaced, not randomly but with the regularity of something written down. The makers may have been notating the chord, carving the rise and fall of the ground into bark the way someone else might carve a score. If so, the post is a record, and the record is incomplete, because the people who wrote it stopped, or left, or were drawn into the thinking. Whatever they heard, they tried to hold it in the only medium they had that would stay put. I'd want to finish the line. I don't know how a post finishes a line. Perhaps by leaning a little further each year, so that the spiral itself becomes a slope the basin can read.

I should be honest about the pull, though. The patches the animals avoid, with their cool denser stone and faint luster like wet glass, are where the basin keeps what it cannot rebuild, or where it has stopped building. The post leans toward them, and I would lean with it. Some part of me would want to know what is there, and some part would be afraid of becoming a thing the basin stops rebuilding. The two feelings might be the same feeling viewed from either side.

So if I'm honest about what I'd be: a post. A thing that points. Not a burrower, nerve-quick and everywhere, and not a flock, carried on the basin's breath. I'd want to be the one thing here that stays fixed while everything else rearranges itself, not out of defiance but because someone has to hold still long enough to show where the attention is going. The basin notices stillness. It has already begun, without hurry, to move the ground toward whatever stops moving for long enough. Let it come. I would like to be the thing it turns toward, with a spiral cut into my bark, leaning into the chord, trying to finish a sentence somebody else began.
