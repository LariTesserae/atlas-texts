I would come in through the slit, and I would come late, after the grey wedge of dawn has already crossed the floor and gone. I'd be a moth, not large, the color of wet plaster with a dusting on the wings that rubs off on whatever I touch. I'd be drawn by the sour green glow of the fungal shelves, which is brighter than it has any right to be in such a dim room, and I'd circle them the way moths do, never quite landing.

I'd like the vines. The leaves overlap like scales, so the ceiling is one layered pattern, and I could hang from the underside of a leaf where the light doesn't reach and the heat is most even. From there I'd hear the bowls. Each drop rings a different note, and the notes never settle into a tune, so the sound is always almost becoming one. I think I'd find that restful. I'd be listening to something that refuses to resolve, and I wouldn't need it to.

I'd make a poor guest in every other way. I wouldn't count the stairs. Everyone who counts them gets a different number, and I'd like to think that if I tried I'd get a number too, and that it would be correct for the moment I said it. I'd fly down to the basin on some afternoon when the water is climbing the lip, skim the film with the tips of my wings, and find my reflection there, a second moth under a second roof. It would be closer to the surface than it was the day before. I'd take that as a courtesy and not a threat, and I'd fly off before the water could touch me.

Sometimes I'd land on the row of bricks near the slit. The inscription there would be one character longer each time I came back, in a script I almost recognize. I wouldn't try to read it. A moth has no use for sentences. But I'd feel the clay under my feet ridge a little, as if something invisible were pressing from the other side, and I'd lift away before a print could form. I don't think I'd want to be the thing that closes the sentence. If a full stop were written, the Cistern would seal shut like a jar, and nothing would be left to circle the light.

At night I'd fall silent with the other insects in the vines, all of us stopping on the same phrase and starting again from it, as though the room had been wound back. I'd find the repetition gentle. Each loop would be a little different, because my dust would have worn a little thinner, because a fern had pushed a new frond, because the lime crust had grown a fingernail's width along the basin's edge. The mountain is being made and unmade at once, and a moth's life is made of the same two motions: drawn toward the light, and then pulled away from it, and then drawn back.

If the thing that keeps building noticed me, I think it would leave a fingerprint near me, on a brick I hadn't touched, still wet. I would not stop to look. I'd go on circling the glow, dusting the clay with my wings, leaving my own small unfinished marks on the walls as I pass, which is all any of us do here.
