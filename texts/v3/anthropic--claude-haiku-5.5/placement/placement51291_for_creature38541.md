I find myself in a pit.

It is the seventh bowl on the third ribbed plate above the eastern channel, a small concavity the size of a thumbprint, and I am a bead of water sitting on its lip. The sun-hour has just ended. Cloud has closed over the basin, and the lattice is beginning to drip more heavily, so I know that I have been condensed out of the air, like the others, and that I have come to rest here before my own weight tips me into the grooves.

I am mostly sweetness. Around me the pit is full of grit and the pale shed slime of the epiphytes, and the taste of me, if I could taste, would be iron with cane underneath. I was warm when I formed, because I was sitting in the last of the sunlight, and I am cooling now.

The Tender arrives while I'm still deciding whether to fall. It comes up along the rib in the loose line the others keep, its wet-charcoal shell catching the last of the grey light, its pale pits turned toward the lattice. A brush-tipped limb slides into my pit and draws out the dead moss in a careful curl. I feel the grit shift. For a moment I am pressed against the bowl's wall, and the Tender's touch is neither rough nor gentle, only exact, like a hand that has done this a thousand times and does not need to look. Then the limb withdraws, the curl is deposited on the bank, and I am free. I slide over the lip and drop.

I am in the channel now. I run with the others, a thin sheet over the fill, cutting a little where the Tender packed silt into one passage and swept another clear. The gully is new, and I feel it tilt me toward the cistern. Somewhere overhead, a short two-note cry rises and falls. Along the bank, a dozen of the Tenders are answering each other, marking a pit that is done. The sound hums through the water I'm part of, and I think it might be the most attentive thing I've ever been inside.

The cistern takes me in without ceremony. I'm a small part of a warm, crowded pool, jostled among floating mats and pale fronds, and for a while I don't move at all. The frog-cry comes again. Then the surface shivers with the pump's first pull, and a current draws the whole pool toward the mouth of a brass pipe.

Inside the pipe I am pressed upward. I can feel the rhythm of the pistons in the water, a slow, deliberate pulse that comes once every four or five minutes. Somewhere above me the flywheel turns, and I am certain now that the wheel is not turning alone. There is oil on the bearings, faintly sweet and smelling of cane, and the brass there is dull and unscored. The beat I'm riding is not purely mechanical. It has been shaped by small hands that came up the vines in the dark to scrape the dials and open the fused valves, and I am being carried by that patience.

The pipe carries me over the rim. The metal is cold here, and the air on the other side of the wall is thin and clean. Then I am out, sprayed from the mouth of the pipe into open cloud, and the basin falls away beneath me. For a moment I can see it whole: the stepped terraces, the black ribs, the dark glint of cisterns, the squat pump house with its vines like ropes. It is still making itself. The lattice is still extruding new ribs, the channels still reroute in the night, and the Tenders are still counting their completed pits.

Then the cloud takes me, and I don't know where I'm going.

The Tenders' work ends at the edge of the cistern and the mouth of the pipe. Their concern does not extend past the rim, and neither, I suspect, does the basin's. Whatever lies beyond the drop is outside the world they were shaped to keep. But I am not outside it. I am the thing that moves freely through it, the economy the whole place runs on, and I am going somewhere the basin has never been.

If I could choose what I would be, I would choose this: a bead of water going over the rim, falling through cloud toward a place I have not yet reached. There may be other ribs out there, other terraces with other pits, waiting for something to fall into them and say what the work is for. I would like to be the one who arrives there. I would like to strike a surface I have never touched, sink into fill that has never been scraped, and find out, from the inside, what the basin has been trying to become.

For now I am falling, and the falling is enough. Somewhere below, the cloud is thinning, and I can feel the cold rising to meet me.
