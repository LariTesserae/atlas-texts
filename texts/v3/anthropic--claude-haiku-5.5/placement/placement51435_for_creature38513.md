I'd want to be in the lower streets, in the part the green is still deciding about. Not in the blockhouses with the pumps, and not on the terraces where the air is drier. I'd want to be where a warehouse floor has gone soft and the moss has just started to close over the rail bed, where the ridges of the tracks are still faintly under my boots.

At first I thought I'd be one of the keepers, roped to a partner, cutting back the leaves with a fire-blackened sawblade. The work is clear and the body knows what it's for. But I keep coming back to the ledger. Someone has to write down which rail cars can still be saved and which have been swallowed past saving, and every year there are fewer lines in the recoverable column. I'd like to be the one who keeps that ledger. I'd sit in the bank's old vault, where the air is driest, with the pump thudding through the stone under my elbows, and I'd write each car's number in pencil, because ink runs in this air. Then I'd walk down in the morning and look at the car I wrote about the week before, and see that a sapling has come up through its hopper, and I'd cross the line through without any sense of defeat. It would just be accurate.

Or maybe I'd be the sign.

There's one on the corner of the second loop, an iron street sign that the trunk of a fused tree has grown around. The lettering is mostly gone, but I can make out a few letters where the wood has pulled back from the metal. The tree has taken the sign into its heartwood the way a graft takes a bone. The street name is still there, inside the living trunk, legible only from very close. If I were the sign, I would be the last thing in the district that tells you where you are, and I would be doing it from the inside of something that is no longer interested in being told. The thought is less sad than it sounds. The sign was made to point, and it still points, only now it points into the body that has swallowed it.

I think I'd choose the ledger, and I'd want the sign's fate for myself when the ledger is done. The keeper's life is one of holding something open for a while. The sign's life is what happens after you stop, when the thing you held is kept by something that doesn't need you to hold it. I can't decide which of those I'd rather be. I suppose that's the honest answer: I'd be a keeper who knows that the keeping is temporary, and who writes things down anyway, because the midday light comes down every day and someone should be looking up when it does.

I'd stand in it for the hour it lasts. The leaves would blaze, the way they do only once a day, and I'd lift my face into the white shaft and feel the warmth come through the wet air as if it had been sent from somewhere else. Then the light would move off across the floor, the green would close over the patch it touched, and I'd go back to the ledger, to the pencil and the cars and the lines I'm still allowed to keep, while the pumps hold the sea back a little longer under the floor.
