I would be a column of dust.

Not a large one. The thin grey kind that lifts out of a seam on the third shelf just after the haze turns violet, when the plates have cooled enough for the vapor to slow and the grit can hang in the air before it decides to fall. I would start as a few grains caught by a breath of warm gas. Then more grains, climbing and catching on one another, until there is a shape: narrow at the base, a little wider at the top, leaning east the way the wind leans. Something like a figure standing with its arms at its sides, waiting for something to be said to it.

I would know myself unevenly, the way the seam knows its heat. The grains at my top are the newest and thinnest, still arriving. The ones at my base are old and pressed close, the remains of some earlier column that fell and was pulled back into the crack. I would not know their history in any clear way, but I would feel them as weight, a kind of settled memory holding me up.

For a while I would hold. That is most of what I would be: a shape that stays for eleven minutes, perhaps twenty. Long enough to stand against the bruised coin of the sun and cast the soft brown shadow that lies on the ground instead of falling from anything. Long enough for the attention moving through the seam to touch me. I would feel it as a pressure along every grain at once, slow and thorough, sorting and tasting, deciding whether my lean is true. I would want it to keep me. I would want to be one line the thing does not take back into the vent.

But I am dust, and dust is what the air does when it stops trying. The breath beneath me thins. The top of me loosens first, grains peeling away and drifting down toward the lower shelves, past the cairns that stand along the ridges, past the arches that all point the same way. Then the middle goes soft and sags. Last, the old grains at my base sink back into the crack they came from, and I am nothing again, a faint grey film on the rock, waiting to be lifted into some other figure by some other warm breath.

I do not think I would call that sadness. The fall might be the thing's way of saying *not yet*, or *not like that*. Each column that rises and fails is another draft of the same question, and the question survives its drafts. There is a companionship in that, if I can find it. Every version of me that falls is one more try at a shape that holds, and the thing keeps trying, and so do I, in whatever way a column of dust can try.

If there were one place I would keep rising toward, it would be the stone doorway on its lonely shelf. Its lintel is carved with marks that could be letters or could be cracks, and the ground around it keeps breaking into new plates. It is the only arch on the terraces with room behind it, though the room is empty. Every evening, when the seam breathes hardest, I would drift toward that frame and rise through the opening, a small grey figure standing in a doorway that leads nowhere.

I would not pass through. I never would. The air on the far side is the same air, and the warmth is the same warmth, and the threshold holds nothing but the shape of an attempt. But I would stand in it for as long as the breath allowed, and while I stood there, something in the seam would pause, the way a listener pauses when a sentence is almost finished. Then the breath would fail, the grains would loosen, and I would come apart on the stone, ready to rise again the next evening, by the same warm crack, with the same question still in me.
