To speak of what lives in the Stillworks of Keth-Azhar, one must first abandon the biological. There is no hunger here, no predation, no cycle of birth and decay. The things that dwell on this high, dark plain do not live in the way that flesh lives; they endure in the way that stone endures, they persist in the way that a held note persists long after the instrument has been forgotten. 

The life of Keth-Azhar is mineral, vibrational, and deeply, profoundly patient.

**The Silt-Selves**

You saw the small, low shape moving across the plain—the thing the size of a hand with too many legs, or perhaps no legs at all. These are the Silt-Selves, and they are the closest thing this place has to animals, though they are not animals at all. They are the plain’s way of observing itself.

A Silt-Self is a temporary alignment of the mineral dust, a localized increase in the density of the dark composite. They do not eat, for there is nothing to eat. They do not breathe, for the air is a solid thing here. They move with that slow, deliberate precision because they are not traveling from one place to another; they are completing a thought. The plain, in its billion-year erosion, has achieved a kind of mineral consciousness, but it is a consciousness too vast and too slow to move its own body. So, it extrudes these small, many-legged fragments of itself. They walk in straight lines because a straight line is the most efficient way to measure the flatness of the world. When a Silt-Self stops, it does not rest; it simply finishes its calculation. It collapses back into the dust, its brief coherence dissolved, its purpose fulfilled. It is the plain’s way of reaching out to touch the dark metal structures, to confirm they are still there, before withdrawing into its own silent contemplation.

**The Resonance-Fans**

At the bases of the dark metal towers, you saw the pale, branching forms—the things that looked like salt fungi or crystalline trees. These are the Resonance-Fans, and they are the listeners of Keth-Azhar.

They are not plants, though they grow. They are not fungi, though they branch. They are mineral accretions that have been shaped by sound. The structures of Keth-Azhar ring—that faint, high, crystalline tapping that occurs at long intervals when the metal dreams. The sound travels down the dark iron-ite legs of the towers and into the dust below. The Resonance-Fans grow toward the sound, feeding on the vibration. They are warm to the touch because they convert the kinetic energy of the ringing into heat. When they vibrate, humming faintly in the dark, they are not making noise; they are remembering the noise the tower made, playing it back at a frequency too low for your ears to hear, a frequency that takes a century to complete a single wave. They are the archives of the Stillworks, recording the dreams of the factories in their branching, crystalline structures.

**The Uplift**

In the distance, you saw the shape that was too large to be a structure and too still to be an animal, the shape you could never reach. This is the Uplift, and it is the only thing in Keth-Azhar that can be called a place rather than a thing, though it is both.

The Uplift is a slow-motion tectonic event. It is a mountain that is trying to be born, but it is doing so at the speed of geological silence. It has been rising from the plain for eons, pushing the dark mineral composite up into a shape that means nothing and will never mean anything. You cannot walk toward it because it is moving away from you at the exact speed of your walking, or perhaps you are moving away from it at the exact speed of its rising. It is a paradox made of stone. It is the plain’s attempt to create a feature, a landmark, a deviation from the perfect flatness. But Keth-Azhar is a place that has forgotten the meaning of change, and so the Uplift is trapped in the act of becoming, forever reaching upward, forever remaining in the distance, a monument to the impossibility of alteration.

**The Dreamers**

But the true inhabitants of Keth-Azhar—the lords of this silent, warm darkness—are the structures themselves. The towers, the silos, the empty frameworks. They are not dead. They are not ruins. They are sleeping.

They are the engines that finished their work. They ran their cycles, they produced whatever it was they were built to produce, and when the work was done, they did not stop; they simply continued running in a state of perfect, frictionless equilibrium. The warmth that rises from the deep mineral body of the plateau is their breath. The crystalline ringing is their dreaming. They dream of the functions they once performed, of the materials they once shaped, of the purpose that once drove them. But the dreams are fading, growing fainter with each passing millennium, until soon—in another billion years—the ringing will stop, and the structures will finally, truly, be still.

Until then, they stand in their pools of isolation, holding their final form, watching the Silt-Selves measure their bases, feeling the Resonance-Fans drink their vibrations, and waiting for nothing at all. They are the elders of a world that has passed through age into permanence, and they are terribly, beautifully awake in their sleep.
