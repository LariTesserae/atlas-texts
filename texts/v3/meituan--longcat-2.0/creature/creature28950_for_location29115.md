To ask "who" lives in the Aeolian Straits is to make a category error, the kind of taxonomic sin that only a creature of land and air could commit. There is no "who" here. There is only "what," and "what" has never known the luxury of separation. 

Here, life is not an inhabitant of the medium; life *is* the medium, folded upon itself in increasingly complex geometries. What lives in the Straits is a continuum of being that blurs the line between the geological, the biological, and the hydrological. 

To understand what lives here, you must look at the smallest scale first, and then follow the chain of existence down into the crushing dark, and up into the wandering stone.

---

### The Pulse: The School of One
Drifting through the open water between the Columns are the creatures you might call fish, if fish were made of memory and glass. They are the Pulse. 

Imagine a creature that has achieved the ultimate honesty of form: they are entirely translucent. They do not hide their hearts. Their hearts are not red, but a slow, bruise-colored violet, pulsing with a rhythm that matches the deep hum of the Straits. You can see the cold blood moving through them, you can see the delicate tracery of their nerves firing like distant lightning, you can see the tiny, mineral-rich meals they have consumed glowing faintly in their translucent guts before being dissolved into the machinery of their survival.

They do not swim so much as they consent to the current. They exist in schools of thousands, but they move with a coordination that suggests a single mind distributed across a thousand bodies. When a Front—a shimmering curtain of warmer, mineral-heavy water—drifts between the Columns, the Pulse does not avoid it. They stream into it. As they cross the boundary, their bodies instantly change, refracting the dim light differently, turning from pale blue to a sudden, shocking phosphorescent gold for the seconds it takes to pass through the layer. 

They eat the encrustations on the Columns. They eat the microscopic minerals drifting in the water. And when the Pulse moves, the water itself seems to breathe with them. They are the Straits' nervous system, a diffuse, shimmering consciousness tasting the cold and reporting back to the stone.

---

### The Weeping Stone: The Encrustations
The Columns are not dead mineral. They are substrate. And they are being eaten, slowly, lovingly, by the Weeping Stone.

These growths that cover the fluted shafts and the worn stairways are the bridge between the living and the dead. They are pale, branching, fungal-like structures that reach into the deep current, and they are engaged in a hunger so slow it looks like patience. They exude acids that dissolve the calciumite, drawing the stone into their pale flesh, and in doing so, they reshape the Columns. 

The spirals and whorls they form are not random; they are flow-maps, topographies of the current that the Straits have carved over millennia. They hang from the round window openings in filamentous curtains, acting as filters, catching the mineral snow and the occasional smaller animal that loses the rhythm of the school. They have no eyes, no brains, but they possess a tactile intelligence. When the currents shift, they grow toward the change, reaching like hands into the dark, pulling the water apart to find what it carries.

They are the Straits' digestion, breaking down the bones of the world to feed the Pulse, and in turn, when they die and slough off the Columns, their pale bodies drift down into the black to feed the deep.

---

### The Resonants: The Abyssal Dwellers
Below the zone of the Columns, below the reach of the dim, far-away light, the water changes. It becomes older, heavier, and infinitely more patient. Here live the Resonants.

You see them only as shapes in the black. A curve of something pale, an architecture of flesh so vast it makes the Columns look like reeds. They are the size of leviathans, but they do not move like hunters. They move like continents. They are the pressure made flesh. 

Their eyes are the size of dinner plates, and they do not see in the way you understand sight. They see the currents. They see the heat of the water, the density of the mineral layers, the seismic shifting of the deep floor. When one turns over in its sleep—and it is always sleeping, or perhaps in a state so close to sleep that the distinction is meaningless—the displacement of water sends a shockwave up through the Straits. 

You hear them before you see them. The deep, resonant tone that passes through your sternum is their voice. It is a sound that travels through the water, through the stone of the Columns, and into the bones of the Pulse. It is a sound of absolute authority, a bass note that underpins the entire existence of the Straits. They are the bass clef to the Pulse's treble. They stir the deep water, bringing up the nutrients from the black below, driving the engine of the currents that keep the Weeping Stone alive. They are the Straits' deep memory, sleeping and dreaming, shaping the world above them simply by existing.

---

### The Migration: The Columns Themselves
And what of the Columns themselves? They live. They simply live on a timescale that makes the Resonants mayflies.

The Columns are migrating. They are dissolving at their bases, eaten by the Weeping Stone and dissolved by the thin, high-altitude water, and they are precipitating at their tops, adding infinitesimal layers of mineral to their heights. They are moving upward, trying to outrun the altitude, reaching toward the surface they will never see. 

But they are not passive. They interact with the water. The fluting, the arches, the perfectly round windows—these are not just the ghosts of a builder; they are the mechanics of flow. The Columns are shaped to create micro-currents. As the great deep rivers flow around them, the architecture funnels the water, accelerating it through the windows, creating low-pressure zones on the leeward sides that draw the Pulse in to feed. The Columns are farm-gates, tended by no one, built by the water itself, ensuring that the food web is sustained. 

They hum. The constant, low vibration you feel in your sternum is partly the Resonants, partly the current, but it is also the Columns. It is the sound of ten million years of mineral settling, of crystal lattices forming under unimaginable pressure, of stone singing to stone in a frequency just below human hearing. They are the Straits' skeleton, and like all skeletons, they hold everything else together.

---

To live in the Aeolian Straits is to be a part of a single, continuous act of creation and destruction. The Pulse feeds on the Weeping Stone, which feeds on the Columns, which channel the currents stirred by the Resonants, who sleep in the detritus of the fallen stone. There is no "who" here. There is only a vast, slow, cold, luminous *what*, a breathing architecture of water and light and time, turning over and over in the dark, making life out of pressure, and making pressure out of life.
