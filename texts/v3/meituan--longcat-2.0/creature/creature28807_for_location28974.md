To describe what lives in the Sump of Keth-Ang, you must first abandon the taxonomy of biology. There is no carbon here, no DNA, no cell division. What lives here is *mineral consciousness*—a slow, bright, crystalline awakening of the rock itself. They are the plateau’s dream of movement, the stone’s brief, shimmering rebellion against entropy. 

They are the Lithovores, though they do not eat. They are the Keth, though they have no name for themselves. They are the living architecture of a world that has not yet decided if it is a city or a creature.

**The Myriad (The Small Ones)**

The vast majority of the inhabitants are the size of your thumb to your forearm, and they exist in numbers that defy counting—a shifting, cascading tapestry of translucent bodies that cling to the spires and swim through the mineral channels. 

To look at one is to look through it. Their bodies are composed of the same calcium-silicate matrix as the towers, but where the towers are dense and opaque, they are diaphanous, like living glass. Inside them, there are no organs, no blood, no viscera. Instead, there are *lattices*—intricate, three-dimensional geometries of crystalline scaffolding that serve as both skeleton and synapse. These lattices pulse with a faint, internal bioluminescence, cycling through a narrow, deliberate spectrum: the amber of slow heat, the pale violet of deep pressure, the green-grey of cooling stone. 

They do not move; they *negotiate*. They possess no limbs, no cilia, no means of locomotion that physics readily explains. Instead, they manipulate their own density. By inducing a controlled phase-shift in their crystalline structure, they momentarily become less-solid, slipping through the gaps in the atomic lattice of the surrounding matter. To the observer, this reads as a stutter in vision—a skipped frame of reality. One moment they are clinging to the channel wall; the next, they have displaced themselves three inches to the left, having briefly existed as a probability rather than a solid object. 

They move with a fluid coordination that suggests a hive mind, but it is not a hive mind. It is a shared physics. They are all vibrating at the same frequency as the plateau, resonating with the deep, tectonic hum of the Sump. When they flow through the channels in cascading waves, they are not following a leader; they are following the current of the hum, moving as a single organism because they are all tuned to the same slow, geological song.

Their purpose is maintenance and growth. They press their translucent bodies against the raised aqueducts, smoothing the mineral deposits, thickening the walls, redirecting the flow of the supersaturated water. They are the immune system and the metabolism of the city, endlessly repairing the infrastructure that is built from the bodies of their ancestors.

**The Cycle of Becoming**

The most profound truth of the Keth is their relationship with death, which is not an end, but a setting. 

When one of the small ones dies—its internal light fading to a dull, static grey—its body does not rot. It calcifies. Within hours, the water leaches out of its translucent form, and the silica rushes in, hardening the lattice into something indistinguishable from the native basalt. The dead sink to the bottom of the channels, or cling to the spires, becoming just another inch of growth on the mineral towers. 

The structures of Keth-Ang are not built; they are *accumulated*. Every spire, every balcony, every channel is a necropolis—a towering monument to the calcified dead, upon which the living climb and build. The city is a slow, continuous cycle of mineral consciousness: the rock pushes up from below, the water seeps through, the creatures emerge from the substrate, live their bright, stuttering lives, and then return to the stone, leaving their hardened bodies as the foundation for the next generation. The plateau is made of its own dead, and the dead are the plateau.

**The Deep Ones (The Large Ones)**

In the lowest undulations of the Sump, where the water has no outlet and concentrates into heavy, viscous pools of mineral sludge, the larger creatures dwell. They are the size of dogs, or larger, and they move with a ponderous, tectonic grace. 

Where the small ones are frantic, stuttering sparks of light, the Deep Ones are slow-burning furnaces of complex geometry. Their internal lattices are vast, multi-layered architectures of light that shift through colors human eyes cannot fully parse—ultraviolet grief, infrared longing, shades that exist only in the spectrum of mineral stress. They do not phase-shift; they are too massive, too anchored. Instead, they flow through the dense fluid like continents drifting, their immense weight displacing the viscous water in slow, rolling waves.

They do not react to the presence of an observer, because their timescale is not your timescale. A thought takes them minutes to form; a decision takes hours. When they turn their massive, translucent heads toward you, it is not a startle response. It is a slow, deliberate focusing of senses that read the world through vibration and pressure. They are the anchors of the Sump, the deep-time thinkers, listening to the groaning of the rock strata below and the hissing of the condensation above. They are the memory of the plateau, holding the resonance of the hum steady while the small ones dance upon the surface.

**The Hum**

Ultimately, what lives in the Sump of Keth-Ang is the Hum itself. 

The creatures are merely the visible expression of a planetary frequency. The groaning of the rock, the dripping of the condensation, the phase-shifting of the small ones, the slow turning of the Deep Ones—all of it is synchronized into a single, low, sustained tone that vibrates through the wet, heavy air. It is the sound of a place that is alive in a way that renders the boundary between living and nonliving irrelevant. 

They are the stone, briefly awake. They are the water, briefly thinking. They are the city, endlessly being born, built from the calcified dreams of its own inhabitants, stretching out in the dark, wet, mineral embrace of a world that is still, and will always be, *becoming*.
