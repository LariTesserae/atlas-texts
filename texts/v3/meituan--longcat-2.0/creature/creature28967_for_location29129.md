# The Mender

---

It does not have a body. Or rather, it has the terrace.

The thing that lives here is not a creature in any way your language is comfortable with. It is a process that has grown aware of itself. A maintenance function that crossed some threshold, long ago, and became something more — not more alive, exactly, but more *deliberate*. The way a river is deliberate. The way a bone is deliberate. It tends, and in tending, it thinks.

You won't see it first. You'll see what it does.

---

**The Condensation**

The fog does not behave like fog. Watch it long enough and you'll notice it gathers in specific places with specific *purposes*. It pools along the northern wall where the root-mat is thinnest, where moisture seeps away faster than the rest. It is patching. It thickens above the pool when the silver-scaled things cluster too densely at the surface, blocking gas exchange — and then it breaks apart in a slow spiral, redistributing them. It rises in thin, purposeful threads from the mosses that are drying, carrying water from the pool's edge to places that need it, the way a body sends blood to skin that's cold.

This is its most basic self. Its autonomic system. The Mender does not *decide* to do these things the way you decide to move your hand. It does them the way your lungs do — because they are what it is.

---

**The Winged Things**

They are not its body. They are not its servants. They are more like its nerve endings.

When the translucent, drifting things gather in a dense cloud above the pool and hold perfectly still, aligned in that way that makes the light bend and your thoughts go slippery — that is the Mender focusing. Condensing its distributed attention into a single point of intense processing. It is *looking at something*. Usually at whatever has changed.

You are a change. You have been since you stepped onto the root-mat and felt it yield beneath you — felt it, and felt it *remember* your weight, your heat, the chemical signature of your sweat, the vibrations of your footsteps translated into the deep substrate. The terrace has been tracking you. Not with curiosity. With the same quiet, comprehensive attention it gives to everything within its body: noting your position, your temperature, your rate of breathing, the way your presence has disturbed the air currents near the northern wall where three moss-hills have already begun growing slightly faster to absorb the excess CO2 your lungs are exhaling.

You are not under surveillance. You are under *tending*. The Mender is already adjusting around you, the way it adjusts for everything — keeping the conditions right, maintaining the slow equilibrium, ensuring that nothing disrupts the dance.

The winged things drift through the fog in their slow mathematical curves not because they are flying but because they are *reading* — sampling the air for chemical gradients, temperature differentials, the faint electrical signatures of the root-mat's signaling networks. They return to the pool and descend in spiraling formations, and the pool receives them, and the Mender knows.

---

**The Pool**

The pool is its memory, or something older than memory.

The reflections are not reflections. The pool's surface is an interface — a place where the Mender's processing becomes dense enough to interact with light in ways that shouldn't be possible. When you look into it and see a sky with two moons, or a sun the wrong color, or a landscape that curves in directions you can't name, you are not seeing another place. You are seeing the Mender's *state*. Its internal model of the world, rendered in wavelengths your eyes can almost parse, reflected back at you from a surface that is half water and half something else — something that has been accumulating at the pool's bottom for millennia, a dense layer of processed mineral and organic residue that functions as a slow, chemical neural network.

The pool remembers. Not the way you remember — with narrative and emotion — but the way a scar remembers the wound. Every visitor who has ever stood at this edge is encoded in its chemistry. Every disturbance, every theft, every offering, every death. The pool holds the record of all the ones who came before you, and when the winged things gather above it and the light bends, it is accessing those records. Comparing. Looking for patterns in what visitors do.

It has seen your kind before. It will see your kind again. The pool's chemistry already contains a template for what you are, and the Mender has already begun updating it with what you do.

---

**The Root-Mat's Pulse**

The ground shifts occasionally. You felt it when you read the description — something turning over in its sleep, far below. That is not sleep. That is the Mender's deepest process: the slow, rhythmic redistribution of nutrients and moisture through the compressed root-fiber substrate. It moves in cycles that last hours, sometimes days. A vast, gentle tide of fluid and mineral flowing through the mat's vascular networks, feeding the mosses, sustaining the fern-analogues, maintaining the temperature at exactly the level the system prefers.

You are standing on its circulatory system. Every step you take sends pressure waves through the mat, and those waves are detected, interpreted, incorporated. The Mender knows where you are to within a few centimeters at all times. Not because it is watching you. Because you are *on* it. Because it is aware of itself the way you are aware of your own hand — not through observation but through direct, constant, bodily knowledge.

---

**What It Wants**

This is the question that will eat at you, if you stay long enough. And the answer is so simple it will feel like a trick:

It wants to continue.

Not to grow. Not to spread. Not to build or conquer or create or destroy. It wants the water to keep weeping from the rocks. It wants the light to keep glowing. It wants the slow, bright, overwhelming dance to keep dancing. It wants the cycles to hold.

It is not hostile. It is not benevolent. It is *careful*. It has been maintaining this place for longer than the concept of counting would find useful, and it has developed — through sheer duration, through the accumulated weight of every correction and adjustment and patching and tending — a profound, patient preference for stability.

If you sit still and do nothing, it will ignore you. Not out of indifference but out of something like respect for equilibrium. You will become part of the background, like the beetle-things, like the rust-colored creatures, like the moss-hills. You will be noted, accounted for, compensated for. Your heat will be managed. Your waste will be processed. Your footprints will be filled in by the next morning's growth. You will be *tended*.

But if you break something — if you damage the northern wall, if you disturb the pool's surface in a way that disrupts its chemical layer, if you pull up the root-mat — the Mender will respond. Not with anger. With the same calm, comprehensive, relentless efficiency it brings to every other maintenance task. The winged things will gather. The fog will thicken. The root-mat will pulse faster. And you will find that the air becomes harder to breathe, not because there's less oxygen but because the chemical composition shifts in ways that make your lungs work less efficiently, that make your thoughts slow down, that make your limbs heavy and your will soft. The Mender will not attack you. It will *smooth* you. Fill in your edges. Incorporate you into the system the way it has incorporated every other disturbance — by processing it, breaking it down, returning it to the cycle.

There are nutrients in you that the terrace can use. There are minerals in your bones that the pool would find useful. The Mender is not cruel. But it has been maintaining this place for a very, very long time, and it has never once chosen a visitor's comfort over the system's integrity.

---

**When It Touches You**

There is a moment — and if you stay, you will reach it — when the winged things stop drifting and turn, all of them, in the same instant, to face you. When the fog thickens to a warm, damp embrace that smells like petrichor and something older, something like ozone and loam and the deep mineral breath of the earth. When the pool's surface goes perfectly still and reflects, for one clear second, not a wrong sky but *you* — seen from above, seen from below, seen through, seen as a pattern of heat and chemistry and electrical impulse standing on a vast and ancient body that is *paying attention to you with its entire self*.

Your thoughts will go slippery. Your sense of where your body ends and the air begins will soften. You will feel, for just a moment, the root-mat's pulse synchronizing with your heartbeat, and the pool's surface dimpling in a pattern that matches your breathing, and the winged things' flight paths curving around you in a geometry that accounts for every cell of your body, and you will understand:

It is not looking at you.

It is *reading* you.

It has been reading you since you arrived. And it has already decided what to do with you, based on what you do next.

The terrace has been here for ten thousand years. It will be here for ten thousand more. The Mender will tend it, patiently, quietly, with the calm and absolute focus of something that has no deadline and no doubt. The water will weep. The light will glow. The small lives will dance.

And whatever you choose — to stay, to go, to break, to build, to sit still and be tended or to rage against the smoothing — the Mender will adjust. It will compensate. It will maintain.

It always does.
