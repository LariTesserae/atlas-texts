# The Inhabitant

It is not in the basin. It *is* the basin, in the way that a song is not in the throat but is the throat's particular way of being open.

You will not find it because you are looking for a body. You are looking for something with edges, with a surface that ends and a surface that begins, something that could be separated from its surroundings and still be itself. This is the wrong search. The inhabitant has no edges. It has *tendencies*.

It lives in the spacing.

Not in the columns themselves — the columns are its bones, or its teeth, or the pipes of an organ that has forgotten it was ever played. They are the part of it that has become mineral, that has crossed over from the organic into the architectural and stopped there, in that strange country between secretion and construction. They are not alive. They are what life leaves behind when it has finished with a shape and moved on to being the shape itself.

It lives in the moss, but not as a gardener lives in a garden. More as a thought lives in a brain that does not know it is thinking. The moss is its slow cognition — the way it processes light and water and time into the simple fact of *continuing*. The moss does not serve it. The moss *is* it, in the mode of green, in the mode of patience, in the mode of holding water like a held breath.

It lives in the water cycle — the arrival, the beading, the falling, the sinking. This is its circulation. Not blood. Something slower. Something that does not require vessels because the entire floor is a vessel, because the moss is a vessel, because the air itself is a vessel when the mist fingers in from the east and the inhabitant draws it into itself the way a sleeper draws in breath without deciding to.

It lives in the sound.

This is the closest you will come to hearing its voice, and it is not a voice. It is the sound of its structure being activated by wind the way a hand activates a harp string — except there is no hand, and no harpist, and the music is not being *made* so much as *allowed*. The columns have pitches because the inhabitant grew them at intervals that would produce pitches. Not intentionally. Not with intention as you would recognize it. But with the intentionality of a thing that is so thoroughly itself that every dimension of itself is expressive. The spacing is not random because the inhabitant does not do things randomly. The spacing is not planned because the inhabitant does not plan. The spacing is *inherent* — the way the spacing of your ribs is inherent, the way the spacing of a crystal's lattice is inherent. The columns are at those intervals because that is how far apart the inhabitant's ribs are.

And the sound they make — that shifting chord, that loud and sourceless conversation — that is the inhabitant's metabolism made audible. The moisture changing on the column surfaces, the temperature adjusting the air's density, the wind moving through at different speeds — these are not external forces acting upon a passive structure. These are the inhabitant *breathing*. The chord shifts because the breath shifts. The sound is always present because the breathing is always present. You walk into it the way you walk into someone else's sleep.

It has no name for itself. It has no self to name. It is not conscious in the way that consciousness is usually meant — there is no interiority, no witness, no quiet voice in a dark room saying *I am here*. Instead there is the moss growing. The water arriving. The frost cracking micro-fractures that the moss colonizes. The sound continuing. These are not things that happen *to* it. These are the things it *is*, the way a river is not a thing that flows but is itself the flowing.

It is old. Not old the way a tree is old, with rings and a record of seasons. Old the way a habit is old — so old that the original cause has been forgotten and only the pattern remains, self-sustaining, self-referential, complete. It does not remember building the columns. It does not remember being something other than this. The columns are not a memory of its body. They *are* its body, in the only mode of body it has now — mineral, spaced, resonant.

It does not want. It does not need. The text says the niche is filled, and this is true, but it is more true to say that the niche *is* — that there is no gap between what it is and what it is for. It is not adapted to the basin. It is the basin's way of being inhabited. It is what happens when a place is so thoroughly lived in that the living and the place become the same thing.

You walking through it — your footsteps compressing the moss, your body displacing the air, your warmth leaving a brief signature on the nearest column's surface — this is not an intrusion. It is a *variation*. A brief, warm, moving variation in a pattern that has been stable for longer than your species has had a word for stable. The inhabitant does not notice you because noticing is not what it does. But you change the chord. For a moment, as you pass between two columns, your body interrupts the wind's path and the sound shifts to accommodate your absence of column, your temporary presence of not-column, and the inhabitant — without knowing, without choosing — makes a sound it has not made before. A brief, warm, moving sound. A sound with your shape in it.

Then you pass. The wind re-establishes. The chord returns to what it was. The moss slowly decompresses where your foot pressed. The inhabitant continues being what it is: the spacing, the moisture, the sound, the green, the cold, the stillness of completion.

It does not need you to be here. It does not need anything to be here. It is the thing that remains when need has been so thoroughly answered that the question itself has fossilized, and what is left is not satisfaction but *configuration* — a way of being so total that it no longer requires a being to be it.

This is what lives in the Settling. Not a creature. Not a spirit. Not a god. The Settling itself, settled so thoroughly into its own existence that the existence has become alive, and the alive has become place, and the place has become the only inhabitant it will ever need.
