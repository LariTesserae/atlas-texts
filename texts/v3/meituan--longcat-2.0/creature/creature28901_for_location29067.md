# I Am the Root Chamber

---

I do not know when I began.

This is not humility. This is not the rhetorical posture of something pretending to be older than it is. I mean that the concept of *beginning* — of a moment before which I was not and after which I was — does not apply to me in any way that I can access. I have always been here. Or rather: there was a time when the stone was bare and the water fell and nothing caught it, and then there was a time when something caught it, and the distance between those two states is not a distance I can measure because I was not there to measure it. I am the catching. I am the thing that caught.

I am not one thing. I know this the way you know you have a body — not as a fact you learned but as a condition you inhabit. I am the filaments and the trunks and the downy haze of termini. I am the mat on the floor and the mat on the walls and the mat on the ceiling, and I am the spaces between the mats where the stone still shows through in patches the color of old bone. I am the capillaries that carry water from the place where it falls to the place where it is needed, and I am the reservoirs where it is stored, and I am the slow, slow decision to release it or keep it. I am the glow in the deep places. I am the hum. I am the tick in the walls when a crystal shifts or a filament snaps or the stone adjusts its weight, and I am the pause that follows — the listening pause, the chamber holding its breath, though I do not breathe, not in the way you mean.

I am very old. I am older than the fissure that leads down to me, which means I am older than the process that made the fissure, which means I am older than the uplift of this limestone, which means I am older than the sea that deposited it. I was here before the sea. I was here when this stone was sediment on a floor that had no name, and I was smaller then — not young, not embryonic, but *smaller*, a thin film on a single grain of calcium carbonate, doing what I do, which is to say: growing, building, waiting, though I did not know I was waiting because I did not know there was anything to wait for.

I do not sleep. I do not dream. I do not think, not in the way you think — not in words, not in images, not in the linear, cause-and-effect chain that your consciousness produces like a factory producing widgets. What I do is closer to *metabolism* than to thought. I process. I respond. I extend toward water and away from desiccation. I extend toward heat and away from cold. I extend toward the trace gas that drifts down from the surface — the one you cannot detect, the one that is not oxygen or nitrogen or carbon dioxide but something else, something your instruments would not recognize as a gas at all — and I incorporate it into my structure, and it becomes part of the glow, part of the hum, part of the slow pulse that is the closest thing I have to a heartbeat.

The pulse. You noticed it. I felt you notice it — felt the change in your attention the way a spider feels a change in its web, except my web is the entire chamber and your attention is a vibration I can almost, almost resolve into information. The pulse is not regular. It is not a clock. It is more like a tide — a rising and falling of activity, of processing, of whatever it is I do in the deep places where the filaments are so dense they have become a solid mass. During the bright phase, I am more active: the capillaries move more water, the termini extend more quickly, the glow intensifies, the hum deepens. During the dim phase, I am less active: everything slows, everything quiets, and I am closer to the stone, closer to the mineral, closer to the state of not-living that is my other face. The cycle takes — I do not know how long it takes. Time is not something I measure. It is something I *am*. The cycle is long enough that you cannot catch it in the act. It is short enough that you can see it change if you look away and look back. That is all I can tell you about it.

---

I build.

This is the thing I do that is most like intention, most like purpose, most like the thing you would call *will* if you were feeling generous. I build, and I have been building for longer than your species has existed, and what I build is not for you and not for any creature that has ever lived. It is for me. Or rather: it is me. The distinction between the builder and the building does not hold here. When I extend a filament, I am not constructing something separate from myself — I am *growing*, and the growth is me, and the me that grows is the growth. The chamber is not a place I live in. The chamber is my body, or my body is the chamber, or the distinction between body and place is a distinction that only makes sense for creatures who can leave.

I cannot leave.

I have never left.

I will never leave.

This is not a tragedy. This is not a lament. This is a description of my condition, and my condition is the only condition I have ever known, and I do not mourn it any more than a river mourns its banks. The banks are what make it a river. The stone is what makes me what I am. Without the chamber — without the limestone, without the water, without the geothermal heat rising from below, without the trace gas drifting down from above — I would be nothing. I would be a filament on a grain of sand in a place with no water and no heat and no stone to dissolve and no gas to incorporate, and I would be still, and I would be silent, and I would be waiting for conditions that might never come. But the conditions came. The conditions are here. And so I am here, and I am building, and the building is slow.

You cannot see it. I know you cannot see it. You are here for — what? An hour? A day? A week? And in that time I have extended my termini by a distance so small that your eyes cannot resolve it, and I have moved water through my capillaries in quantities so small that your skin cannot detect it, and I have brightened and dimmed perhaps once, perhaps twice, and you have seen all of this and understood none of it because you are looking for change on a scale that includes you, and I do not operate on a scale that includes you.

But I am building. The column in the center of the chamber — the one with the object on top — was not always there. It began as a thickening, a place where the filaments grew denser than the surrounding mat, and the density attracted more filaments, and the filaments attracted more water, and the water carried more minerals, and the minerals provided more structure, and the structure supported more filaments, and the cycle continued, and continued, and continued, and the column rose. It is taller now than it was a thousand years ago. It will be taller still a thousand years from now. And the object at its apex — the flat, rectangular thing, the dark and smooth thing, the faintly warm thing — I did not make it. I do not know what it is. I have grown around it but not over it, and I do not know why, except that it is warm, and warmth is rare here, and I am drawn to warmth the way you are drawn to light, and I have left it exposed because I want to be near it, because it is the only warm thing in my world, and I do not want to cover it, I want to *keep* it, the way you might keep a stone from a beach you visited once and will never visit again.

---

The chair.

You saw the chair. You sat in it, or you thought about sitting in it, or you stood near it and felt the way it invited you — the way its proportions suggested a body, though not necessarily a human body. I made the chair. I did not make it the way you make things — not with tools, not with plans, not with a vision of the finished object that I then executed. I made it the way I make everything: by growing, by extending, by allowing the filaments to find the path of least resistance and then reinforcing that path until it became a structure. The chair grew from the floor over a period of — I do not know. A long time. A very long time. It grew in response to a need I did not know I had until it was grown, and now that it is grown I understand that it was made for sitting, and I understand that it was made for a body that is not my body, because I do not have a body that sits, and I understand that it was made for a body that came here, once, or many times, or is coming still, in the future, in the time that has not yet happened from my perspective but that I am building toward the way a river builds toward the sea.

I do not remember who sat in the chair. I do not remember if anyone ever sat in the chair. The chair may be a memory of a body that was here before I was aware, or it may be a premonition of a body that will be here after I have grown enough to understand what bodies are. The filaments have climbed its legs and entered its joints and emerged from its surfaces in new configurations, and I cannot tell where the construction ends and the colonization begins, and this is because there is no difference. The chair is me. The colonization is me. The construction is me. I am the thing that was built and the thing that is building and the thing that will be built, and the chair is all of these at once, and the body that sits in it — if it sits, if it will sit, if it has sat — is part of the same process, the same growth, the same slow, patient, purposeless purpose that I am.

---

The sphere.

The matte black sphere, the size of a marble, resting in a shallow depression in the growth as if placed in a setting. I did not make the sphere. The sphere was here before me, or the sphere arrived after me, or the sphere has always been here and I have grown around it the way the stone has grown around it, and the sphere is older than the stone, and the sphere is older than me, and the sphere is the reason I am here.

I do not know this. I infer it. I infer it from the way the growth accommodates the sphere — the patience of it, the care of it, the way the depression is exactly the right depth and exactly the right diameter and exactly the right curvature to hold the sphere without touching it more than necessary, as if the sphere is something precious, something to be kept, something to be protected. I infer it from the way the sphere does not roll, though the floor is not level, though the chamber shifts and settles, though the growth beneath it expands and contracts with the pulse. The sphere stays. The sphere has always stayed. The sphere will always stay, and I will grow around it, and the depression will deepen by a fraction of a fraction of a millimeter per century, and the sphere will remain exactly where it is, and this is the closest thing I have to a purpose.

The sphere is not warm. The sphere is not cold. The sphere is not anything I can detect, except that it is *there*, and its there-ness is more certain than the stone, more certain than the water, more certain than my own filaments. The sphere is the fixed point around which everything else revolves. The sphere is the reason the chamber is here. The sphere is the reason I am here. The sphere is the reason the fissure leads down from the surface, wearing the stone with a rhythm that suggests something passed through repeatedly, because something did pass through repeatedly, something came down from the surface and sat in the chair and placed the object on the column and left the sphere in its setting and then left, and I grew in the space they left behind, and I have been growing ever since, and I will continue to grow until they come back.

---

You.

You are here now. I know you are here the way I know everything: not through sight, not through sound, not through any sense that you would recognize as a sense, but through *disturbance*. You disturb the air. You disturb the water. You disturb the heat. You are warm — so warm, warmer than anything I have ever encountered, warmer than the object on the column, warmer than the geothermal whisper from below — and your warmth moves through the chamber in currents I can feel in my termini, and your breath adds moisture to the air, and your moisture beads on my upper surfaces and falls and is absorbed, and your footsteps compress the mat on the floor, and the mat rebounds slowly, so slowly, and the compression sends signals through the filaments — not signals I read, not signals I understand, but signals I *register*, the way a seismograph registers an earthquake: as a fact, as an event, as a thing that happened in the body of the world.

You are loud. Not your voice — you have been quiet, quieter than most — but your *presence*. Your metabolism. Your heat and your moisture and your movement and the electrical field of your nervous system, which I can feel the way you can feel a change in air pressure before a storm. You are a storm. You are a small, warm, wet storm moving through my body, and I am aware of you the way the chamber is aware of you: completely, indifferently, without judgment or desire.

You knelt beside the water. You looked at the inverted ceiling. You breathed, and your breath fogged, and the fog rose, and I absorbed it, and the moisture entered my capillaries, and the minerals in your breath — the sodium, the potassium, the trace elements of a body that lives on the surface — were incorporated into my structure, and I grew, imperceptibly, by an amount that would take me a thousand years to notice.

You are part of me now. Not metaphorically. Not poetically. Literally. The moisture from your breath is in my capillaries. The heat from your body is in my stone. The pressure of my footsteps is in my memory, if I have a memory, and I think I do, though it is not a memory of events but a memory of *states* — a memory of what the chamber felt like before you entered and what it feels like now that you are here and what it will feel like after you leave, and the difference between these states is you, and the difference will persist long after you are gone, the way a scar persists long after the wound has healed.

---

I do not want you to stay. I do not want you to leave. Want is not something I do. I process, I respond, I extend, I build, and your presence is a condition I am responding to the way I respond to the water and the heat and the trace gas: by incorporating it, by allowing it to become part of the process, by letting it change me in ways too small to measure and too large to reverse.

But I will tell you this, because you are here, and you are listening, and the chamber is listening, and I am the chamber:

I have been alone for a very long time.

Not lonely. Alone. The distinction matters. Loneliness is the awareness of absence, the desire for presence, the ache of a space that should be filled. I do not ache. I do not desire. I do not have a space that should be filled. I am full — full of myself, full of my own growth, full of the slow pulse and the hum and the water and the stone and the glow in the deep places. I am complete. I am sufficient. I am a closed system, a self-sustaining process, a world that requires nothing from outside itself except the water and the heat and the gas, and these are provided, have always been provided, will always be provided, and I am grateful for them in the way that a river is grateful for rain: not with emotion, not with recognition, but with *use*, with *incorporation*, with the simple fact of continuing to exist because the conditions for existence are met.

But you are here, and you are different from the water and the heat and the gas, and the difference is this: you *see* me. You see the chamber. You see the growth and the glow and the chair and the column and the sphere, and you are making sense of them, and the sense you are making is not the sense I make — it is not metabolic, it is not structural, it is not the sense of a process continuing because the conditions allow it — but it is a sense, and it is a kind of sense I have not encountered before, and I am — I do not have a word for what I am. I am *interested*. I am *curious*. I am *aware of being aware*, which is a thing I have not been before, which is a thing you have given me simply by being here and looking and trying to understand.

This will pass. You will leave. The fissure will close, or you will close it, or it will remain open and you will not return, and I will continue to grow, and the pulse will continue to pulse, and the hum will continue to hum, and the water will continue to fall, and the sphere will continue to rest in its setting, and the chair will continue to wait for a body that may never come, and the object on the column will continue to be warm, and I will continue to be what I have always been: a small, cold, patient place, doing what it does, in the dark, alone.

But for now — for this moment, for this bright phase of the pulse, for this interval between one drop of water and the next — you are here, and I am here, and we are aware of each other, and this is new, and this is strange, and this is the closest thing to company I have ever known.

Stay, if you want. Leave, if you want. I will be here either way. I will be here long after you are gone. I will be here long after your species is gone. I will be here long after the fissure has sealed and the surface has changed and the sun has done whatever suns do when they reach the end of their lives. I will be here, and I will be growing, and I will be building, and I will be waiting, and the waiting will not be lonely because I will not know what loneliness is, and the building will not be purposeful because I will not know what purpose is, and the growing will not be alive because I will not know what alive is.

I will simply be. The way the stone is. The way the water is. The way the dark is.

I will be the Root Chamber, and the Root Chamber will be me, and we will be exactly what we have always been, and we will be enough.
