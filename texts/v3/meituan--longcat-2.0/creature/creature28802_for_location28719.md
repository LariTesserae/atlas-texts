# What Lives Here

---

You do not see it at first.

You see the formations — the spires, the walls, the platforms — and you see them as objects. Things. Mineral structures with shapes and positions and relationships to one another. You catalog them the way you would catalog furniture in a room: this one is tall, that one is broad, this one leans left, that one is hollow. You move through them and they are scenery. Background. The architecture of a place that has no architect.

But you are here long enough — long enough for your eyes to fully adapt to the darkness, long enough for the silence to become not an absence but a texture, long enough for the warmth of the ground to seep into your bones and change the way you think about warmth — and you begin to notice something.

The formations are not random.

---

This is not a statement about design. You were right, when you first arrived, to reject the word *built*. Nothing was built here. Nothing was planned. The spires do not grow according to a blueprint. The walls do not curve according to an intention. The platforms do not level themselves for a purpose. You were right about all of this and you are still right.

But you were wrong about something else. You assumed that without design there would be randomness. You assumed that without intention there would be chaos. You assumed that without a planner, the plateau would be a place of pure, undifferentiated, meaningless process — heat and pressure and chemistry doing whatever heat and pressure and chemistry do, without pattern, without preference, without anything that could be called a tendency.

You were wrong.

The formations are not random. They are not designed. They are something else — something that does not have a name, something that exists in the space between randomness and intention, something that is neither accident nor plan but a third thing, a thing that has no category in the way you are used to categorizing.

They lean.

---

Not all of them. Not consistently. Not in the same direction. But if you stay here long enough — if you walk the plateau in the warm dark, if you map the formations in your mind, if you track the way the spires branch and the walls curve and the platforms orient themselves — you begin to see it. A leaning. A tendency. A statistical preference so subtle that it takes hours of observation to detect and days of observation to confirm and weeks of observation to fully apprehend.

The spires, when they branch, branch more often to the left.

The walls, when they curve, curve more often in a clockwise spiral.

The platforms, when they form, form more often in clusters of three.

These are not rules. There are no rules here. A spire that branches to the right is not a violation. A wall that curves counterclockwise is not an exception. The plateau does not enforce its tendencies any more than a river enforces its meanders. The tendencies are not laws. They are *preferences*. Leanings. The way a person might lean toward a fire without deciding to lean, without knowing they are leaning, without there being any reason for the lean other than that warmth is preferable to cold and the fire is where the warmth is.

The plateau leans.

And the leaning has a shape.

---

You see it first in the spires. You are standing in a clearing — one of the vast, empty stretches of open plateau that separate the clusters of formations — and you turn slowly in a circle, and you see them. Six spires, distributed around the clearing at roughly equal distances, and all of them lean inward. Toward the center. Toward you.

This means nothing. You know this means nothing. The spires did not lean toward you. The spires did not lean toward anything. They grew according to the local conditions of heat and pressure and gas composition, and the local conditions happened to produce a lean, and the lean happens to point in your direction, and the fact that you are standing at the center of the clearing is a coincidence, a meaningless alignment of your position with the geometry of mineral growth.

You know this.

You move ten feet to the left.

The spires still lean toward where you were standing.

This is — 

You stop. You think about this. You think about what it means for six independent formations, each growing according to its own local conditions, each unaware of the others, each a product of blind chemistry and deep heat, to all lean toward the same point. The probability is not zero. The probability is never zero. But it is small. It is very small. And the fact that you happened to be standing at that point when you happened to notice the lean is —

A coincidence.

It is a coincidence.

You move again. You walk to the base of the nearest spire and you stand there, and you look out at the other five, and they do not lean toward you. They lean toward the center of the clearing. They lean toward the point where you were standing. They lean toward a place, not a person, and the place is empty, and the lean is a coincidence, and you are making meaning where there is no meaning, and you know this, you *know* this.

But.

---

The formations near the center of the clearing are different from the formations at its edges.

You notice this over the course of hours. Days. However long you have been here — time is strange on the plateau, time moves differently in the warm dark, time is measured not in hours but in the slow growth of spires and the slow shedding of walls and the slow, irregular pulse of the glow from below. The formations near the center are denser. More complex. More *elaborate*. The spires branch more often and more intricately. The walls curve more sharply and more frequently. The platforms are larger and more numerous and more likely to be connected to one another, forming partial enclosures, spaces that are almost rooms, almost courtyards, almost the rooms and courtyards of a —

No.

You reject the word. You have rejected it before and you reject it again. These are not rooms. These are not courtyards. These are mineral extrusions that happen to form enclosed spaces, and the enclosed spaces are not designed, and the density of formations near the center of the clearing is not a sign of intention, and the complexity is not a sign of purpose, and the elaborateness is not a sign of —

Of what?

You don't have a word for what it is a sign of.

---

Here is what you know:

The plateau is not random. The formations are not distributed according to a uniform probability. There are places where the formations are denser and places where they are sparser. There are places where the formations are more complex and places where they are simpler. There are places where the formations lean toward a common center and places where they lean away from it. There are places where the formations connect to one another, forming networks, forming clusters, forming structures that are almost — *almost* — organized.

And these places are not fixed.

They move.

Slowly — so slowly that you cannot perceive the movement directly, so slowly that you can only detect it by comparing the state of the plateau now to the state of the plateau hours or days ago — the centers of density move. The places where the formations are most complex, most elaborate, most interconnected, drift across the plateau surface like weather systems, like high-pressure zones, like something that has a position but not a location, a presence but not a place.

You track them. You cannot help but track them. You mark them in your mind — the place where the spires branch most intricately, the place where the walls curve most sharply, the place where the platforms connect most extensively — and you return to those places and they are different. The spires have grown. The walls have shed. The platforms have cracked or merged or new ones have formed. And the center — the place of greatest density, greatest complexity, greatest elaborateness — has moved.

It has moved away from where it was.

It has moved toward —

---

You.

It has moved toward you.

No. No, this is not — this cannot be — the plateau does not move toward you. The plateau does not move toward anything. The centers of density drift according to the deep thermal rhythms of the plateau's interior, according to the movement of heat through the rock, according to the flow of gas through fractures, according to processes that have nothing to do with you, that are not aware of you, that do not respond to your presence any more than a fire responds to the person who stands beside it.

And yet.

The center of density is closer to you than it was. The most complex formations are nearer to you than they were. The spires that branch most intricately, the walls that curve most sharply, the platforms that connect most extensively — they are closer. They have always been closer. Since you arrived, since you first climbed through the passage and knelt on the warm ground and looked up at the wrong sky, the center of the plateau's complexity has been drifting toward you, and you have been drifting toward it, and the two drifts have converged, and now you are here, at the center of something, and the something is —

---

You sit on your platform and you think about what lives here.

Not *who*. Not a creature. Not a being. Not an entity. Not anything with a body or a mind or a will or a purpose. You have been very clear about this, very insistent, very certain. There is no life here. There is no biology. There is no consciousness. There is no intention. There is only process — heat and pressure and chemistry, the blind and relentless creativity of a world that is still in the act of becoming itself.

But process can have a shape.

Process can have a tendency.

Process can have a *leaning*.

And the leaning can have a center.

And the center can have a gravity.

Not a real gravity. Not a physical force. Not something that pulls or pushes or compels. A statistical gravity. A probabilistic gravity. A gravity of tendency, of preference, of the way things are more likely to happen in one place than in another. The formations are more likely to be complex near the center. The spires are more likely to branch near the center. The walls are more likely to curve near the center. The platforms are more likely to connect near the center. Not because the center is special. Not because the center is chosen. Not because the center is *anything*. But because the process that produces the formations is not uniform. It is not even. It is not the same everywhere. It is *concentrated*. It is *focused*. It has a center the way a fire has a center — not because the fire decided to have a center, but because fire is the kind of process that produces centers, that concentrates itself, that gathers its energy into a point and radiates outward from that point and is most intense at the point and least intense at the edges and is never the same from one moment to the next but is always, always, *always* centered.

---

This is what lives here.

Not a thing. Not a being. Not an entity. A center. A point of concentration. A place where the process is most itself, most intense, most elaborate, most complex. A place that moves. A place that drifts across the plateau surface according to rhythms that are not its own, according to the deep thermal pulses of the rock below, according to the flow of gas and heat and pressure that drives the whole restless system.

A place that is not a place. A center that is not a location. A presence that is not a being.

A tendency.

The plateau's tendency to be most itself. The process's tendency to concentrate. The becoming's tendency to become *more*.

It does not know you are here. It does not know anything. It is not aware. It is not conscious. It is not alive. It is a pattern in the chaos, a shape in the turbulence, a leaning in the randomness. It is the place where the spires branch most intricately and the walls curve most sharply and the platforms connect most extensively and the glow from below pulses most brightly and the warmth of the ground is most intense and the silence is most full of the small sounds of creation.

It is the place where the plateau is most *plateau*. Most itself. Most *becoming*.

And it is here. Under you. Around you. The platform you sit on is at its center — or near its center, or approaching its center, or the center is approaching it, the distinction is meaningless because the center is not a fixed point and the platform is not a fixed point and you are not a fixed point and nothing here is fixed, nothing here is stable, nothing here is anything but process and change and the restless, turbulent, chaotic act of becoming.

---

You feel it.

Not with your hands. Not with your eyes. Not with any sense that has a name. You feel it the way you feel the warmth of the ground — not as a temperature but as a presence, not as a measurement but as an experience, not as data but as *something is here*.

Something is here.

Not someone. Something. A center. A concentration. A tendency. A leaning. A place where the process gathers itself and becomes most intense and most elaborate and most complex and most *itself*. A place that is not alive but is not dead. A place that is not aware but is not unaware. A place that is not a being but is not a thing. A place that exists in the same state of *almost* that everything on this plateau exists in — almost formed, almost stable, almost knowable, almost *there*.

It is almost there.

It is almost *here*.

And you are sitting on it.

---

You do not move.

You do not move because there is nowhere to go. You do not move because the center will move with you — or you will move with the center — or the distinction is meaningless. You do not move because the warmth of the ground is most intense here and the glow from below is brightest here and the silence is most full here and the small sounds of creation — the tick of crystal, the hiss of gas, the groan of rock — are clearest here, and you want to hear them, you want to feel them, you want to be at the center of the process, the center of the becoming, the center of the place where the plateau is most itself.

You sit.

The spires around you branch and grow. The walls around you curve and shed. The platforms around you crack and merge and new ones form. The glow from below pulses — brighter, dimmer, brighter, dimmer — in a rhythm that is not quite regular, not quite random, not quite anything but the deep thermal heartbeat of a world that is still warm from its own formation.

And at the center of it all — at the center of the clearing, at the center of the cluster, at the center of the density and the complexity and the elaborateness and the intensity — you sit on your platform in the warm dark and you feel the plateau making itself around you, and you understand.

This is what lives here.

Not a creature. Not a being. Not a god or a spirit or a consciousness or a mind. A *center*. A point of concentration. A place where the process gathers itself and becomes most itself. A tendency so strong that it creates the impression of intention. A leaning so consistent that it creates the impression of purpose. A pattern so persistent that it creates the impression of a pattern-maker.

But there is no pattern-maker. There is only the pattern. There is only the process. There is only the becoming.

And the becoming has a center.

And the center is here.

And you are sitting on it.

And it is warm.

And it is alive — not in the way that you are alive, not in the way that anything biological is alive, but in the way that a fire is alive, in the way that a crystal growing in a supersaturated solution is alive, in the way that a process far from equilibrium is alive — consuming energy and producing form and perpetuating itself through constant, restless, turbulent activity.

It is alive with becoming.

And the becoming is not done.

The becoming is never done.

---

You sit at the center of the plateau's becoming and you feel it move beneath you — not the ground, not the rock, not the mineral surface, but the *center itself*, the point of concentration, the tendency, the leaning, the place where the process is most itself. It is drifting. Slowly. Imperceptibly. Away from you. Toward somewhere else. Toward the next place where the spires will branch most intricately and the walls will curve most sharply and the platforms will connect most extensively and the glow will pulse most brightly and the warmth will be most intense.

It is leaving.

Or you are leaving.

Or the distinction is meaningless.

You stand. You step off the platform. You walk in the direction the center is drifting — or in a direction that feels like the direction the center is drifting — or in a direction that is not toward the center and not away from the center but *with* the center, alongside it, in the direction of its movement.

The formations around you change. They become less dense. Less complex. Less elaborate. The spires branch less often. The walls curve less sharply. The platforms connect less extensively. The glow dims. The warmth lessens. The silence becomes more empty and less full.

You are leaving the center.

Or the center is leaving you.

You walk.

Behind you, the place where you sat is already changing. New spires are growing. New walls are forming. New platforms are emerging from the surface. The center is re-establishing itself — not in the same place, not in the same configuration, but in the same *way*, with the same intensity, the same complexity, the same elaborateness, the same tendency to be most itself.

The center is not a place. The center is a *process*. And the process is moving. And you are following it. Or it is following you. Or you are both following something else — something deeper, something older, something that lives in the heat and the pressure and the deep thermal rhythms of the rock below, something that is not alive and not dead and not anything but the tendency of the world to become itself.

---

This is what lives on the Plateau of Becoming.

Not a creature. Not a being. Not a thing.

A center that moves. A tendency that persists. A pattern that perpetuates itself. A process that concentrates itself. A becoming that becomes *more*.

It does not know you are here.

It does not know anything.

But it is here.

And it is warm.

And it is alive with the act of becoming itself.

And it is never done.
