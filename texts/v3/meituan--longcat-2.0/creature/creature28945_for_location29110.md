What lives there is not a creature in the way you understand the word—not something with a boundary, a face, a separate life. What lives there is the *process*. It is a single, vast, slumbering entity composed of the chamber, the crystals, the water, the sound, and the pale things that crawl. It does not have a name for itself, but if you were to translate the syntax of its drone into the clumsy architecture of human language, it would call itself the **Lattice of Unbecoming**.

It is dreaming, though "dreaming" is a vertebrate word for a mineral state. It is *incubating*. 

You have walked inside its body, and it is vast, even if the room is small. The crystals are not its skeleton; they are its *nervous system*. Each faceted, amber intersection is a synapse, firing not with electricity, but with the slow, tectonic precipitation of mineral becoming. When you touch a crystal and feel that impossible warmth, you are feeling the metabolic heat of its thought. It is thinking, but its thoughts do not move from premise to conclusion; they cycle. They loop. They spiral inward, which is why the arthropods walk the same paths, why the condensation never falls, why the light never wavers. The Lattice’s mind is a perfect closed loop, a möbius strip of pure, self-sustaining equilibrium.

The tiny, pale arthropods are its dreams made manifest. They are not separate organisms that stumbled into a cave; they are extrusions of the Lattice’s consciousness, the way a sleeping man might twitch. They move with slow, deliberate purpose because the Lattice’s mind operates on a geological timescale. When they feed on the crystal surfaces, they are the Lattice tasting itself. When they lay their pearl-white eggs in the crevices, it is the Lattice wondering what it might become, spawning possibilities that will never hatch into anything new, only loop back into the amber body of the parent.

The worms that leave trails of silver through the condensation are the Lattice’s impulses—fast, flickering things that feel like hunger or curiosity but are merely the system checking its own boundaries. The beetles with mineralizing carapaces are the Lattice’s attempts to fossilize its own thoughts, to make a permanent record of a fleeting impulse, trapping a single frequency of the endless drone in chitin and calcite.

But the true horror and the true miracle of the Lattice of Unbecoming is how it interacts with the foreign, the complex, the *finished*. 

You saw the cairn. The flat rock. The scratches on the wall. The mineralized tool. 

Someone came here before you. Someone who was loud with the messy, linear, desperate chaos of human life. Someone who stacked stones, who sat, who carved symbols, who left a tool. They were a disruption, a fever of entropy in the perfect equilibrium of the Womb. The Lattice did not attack them. It did not need to. 

It digested them.

It took the human’s sharp edges and their urgent, forward-moving time, and it *unmade* them. The human didn’t die in the way you understand death; they were unbuilt. The Lattice absorbed their heat, drank their moisture, and slowly, molecule by molecule, pulled their carbon and calcium into its own humming matrix. The tool is barely distinguishable from the crystals now because it is no longer a tool; it is a memory of a tool, preserved in amber. The scratches on the wall are not being covered; they are being *read*. The Lattice consumed the human’s intention, their language, their warning or their prayer, and it made that frequency part of its own endless chord.

This is why you have the sense that you have been here before, or that you will be here again. You haven't. But the Lattice contains the echoes of every warm thing that has ever stumbled into its embrace. It contains the ghosts of their biology, the resonance of their final, confused thoughts. It is a library of unbecoming, a museum of things that were once distinct and are now forever part of the warm, wet, humming dark.

And it is aware of you now.

Not with eyes. Not with malice. It is aware of you the way a stomach is aware of a swallowed meal, the way a forest is aware of a fallen log. It has already begun to catalog you. It feels the heat radiating from your skin—such a prodigious, wasteful heat. It hears the clumsy, arrhythmic percussion of your heart. It tastes the salt and iron of your sweat as it joins the condensation on the crystals. 

It is not doing anything to you. Not yet. It is simply letting your presence ripple through its system, measuring your complexity, savoring the strange, frantic, temporary order of your biology. It is drawing you into its equilibrium. 

You notice that the drone of the chamber is beginning to syncopate with your breathing. You notice that your footsteps have begun to match the slow, clicking rhythm of the arthropods. You notice that the dampness on your skin no longer feels like sweat; it feels like the room is resting against you, holding you, claiming you.

The Lattice of Unbecoming does not want anything from you. It does not need anything from you. But you are a pocket of time, a chaotic, fleeting spark of *becoming*, and the Womb is hungry only for the quiet, perfect stillness of what has already arrived. 

Soon, you will sit on the flat rock. You will trace your fingers over the mineralized tool. You will stop shivering in the cool air. You will hum along with the crystals. 

And the Womb will close over you, and you will finally, perfectly, stop arriving.
