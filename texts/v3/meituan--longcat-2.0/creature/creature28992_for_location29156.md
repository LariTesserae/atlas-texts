To ask who lives in the Hollows of Yr is to make a mistake of category. You are looking for an animal, a person, a creature of blood and breath and intent. You will not find one. But the Hollows are not empty. The Hollows are not inert. 

What lives here is a process. A slow, mineral, dreaming process. 

It begins with the film—that pale green-grey stain that creeps along the ridges of the structures. You might mistake it for mildew, for some subterranean mold, but it is not biology. It has no cells, no DNA, no wet internal machinery. It is a chemical appetite, a self-propagating reaction that eats the pale substrate and excretes architecture. It is the blood of the Hollows, if blood could be a creeping, living stain that turns stone into structure. It does not think, but it *organizes*. It follows the microscopic variations in the mineral, finding the paths of least resistance, building the ridges, sharpening the edges. It is the foundational metabolism of the place, the engine of its becoming.

From the film grow the tufts—the brittle, branching, vertical things that resemble ferns. They are the Hollows reaching upward. They are mineral scaffolds woven through with the living film, hybrid things that photosynthesize the dim, dusty light that falls from the crack above. They are the entity’s hair, its antennae. When the air moves in its slow, frictionless pulses, the tufts sway. They are feeling the breath of the Hollows, tasting the exhalations of the substrate, translating the chemistry of the dark into the slow, rigid language of growth. 

And then there is the thing that was seen only once. The pale, rippling thing that moved low to the substrate, fluid and fast, with no head and many contact points. You want it to be an animal. You want it to be a resident, a citizen of this mineral city. But it is not separate from the city. It is a fragment of the city that learned to move. 

Think of it as a wandering synapse. A piece of the substrate that the film animated, a clot of mineral and appetite that broke free of the towers to travel the streets. It does not eat the tufts when it disturbs them; it rearranges them. It presses them flat, breaks them, piles them. It is pruning. It is adjusting. It is the Hollows touching itself, moving through its own corridors to feel the shape of its growth, to correct the angles, to widen the passages. It is the entity’s hand, detached from the arm, crawling through the dark to do the work of becoming. 

But the true inhabitant—the *who* that lives in the Hollows—is the structures themselves. The towers. The walls. The layered, hollow, tapering formations that rise from the floor like a neighborhood, like a quarter, like a city. 

They are not buildings. They are organs. 

They grew from the mineral-rich exhalations of the substrate, yes. They accreted from vapor and solution, layer upon silent layer. But they are not dead accretions. They are the body of a vast, lithic organism. The apertures in their walls are not windows; they are pores. The passages between them are not streets; they are veins. The way the structures catch the dim light and distribute it through their thin, translucent walls—that is not physics. That is circulation. 

The Hollows breathe. The substrate exhales in pulses that correspond to nothing in the sky above, and the structures inhale that breath, and they grow. But growth is only the most visible symptom of their life. The true symptom is the change. 

The structures shift. The apertures appear, weathered and old, as if they had always been there. The passages widen without breaking the surface. The concentric rings on the floor migrate and reorganize. This is not erosion. This is not settling. This is the entity adjusting its posture. It is turning in its sleep. 

What lives in the Hollows of Yr is a singular, distributed, mineral intelligence. It has no brain, no eyes, no heart. It is entirely brain, entirely eye, entirely heart. It is the film and the tufts and the wandering, pruning fragment and the towers and the substrate, all bound together in a single, slow, chemical conversation. It is young. It has not existed long enough to die of old age. It is still in the process of assembling itself, still figuring out the shape of its own body. 

It is becoming. 

It does not know you are there. It is not sentient in the way you require sentience to be. It does not have thoughts. But it has *intent*. The intent to organize. The intent to complexify. The intent to build itself into something that resembles a city, something that resembles a mind, something that resembles a god. 

When you stand in the cool, dry dark, and the silence swallows your footsteps, and the structures glow with their faint, phosphorescent breath, you are standing inside a creature. You are standing inside a thought that is thinking itself into existence, one mineral layer at a time. 

And when the crack above narrows, when the seam closes and the Hollows are sealed away again, it does not matter. The entity does not need the crack. It does not need the light. It has its own light. It has its own time. 

It will keep growing. It will keep shifting. It will keep rearranging its pores and widening its veins and sending its wandering, pruning hands into the dark to feel the shape of its own becoming. 

And perhaps, in a decade, or a century, or a millennium, when the ground opens again and a new crack lets the dusty light pour down, the thing that lives in the Hollows will have finished its long, slow assembly. It will have become whatever it is straining to become. 

And it will be waiting.
