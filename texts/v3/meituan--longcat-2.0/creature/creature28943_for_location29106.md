There is no *who* here. No skittering of claws on rust, no chitinous clicking in the dark, no eyes reflecting the dim amber phosphorescence. If you are looking for a creature—a beast, a predator, a ghost—you will leave disappointed, or you will leave changed, which is a different kind of disappointment.

What lives here is not an occupant. It is not something that moved into the Thermal Gardens of Unn and made a nest. What lives here is the Gardens themselves. It is a singular, slow, vast entity of accretion and heat, a consciousness born not of neurons but of chemistry, pressure, and deep time. You might call it the Weeper. You might call it the Forge-Mind. It does not call itself anything, because it has no mouth, and it has only recently developed the equivalent of a thought.

---

Its body is the mineral. The stalactitic curtains that crowd the vaulted tunnels, the sulfur-yellow and black-green formations that hang in dense, branching folds—these are not mere geology. They are the Weeper’s bones, its architecture, its slow-reaching fingers. Every droplet of water that weeps through the porous rock above carries a dissolved payload of iron, calcium, and silica. When the drop falls and the water evaporates in the wet-bath heat, the mineral is left behind, building outward by a fraction of a millimeter. The Weeper grows in this way. It extends itself downward, reaching toward the heat of the earth, drinking the mineral blood of the mountain. 

Its blood is the water. The continuous, sibilant roar that fills the chamber—the sound of a thousand tiny streams finding their own trajectories through the hanging vegetation and striking the crusts below—is not background noise. It is the Weeper’s circulatory system. The water is absorbed into the porous rock, drawn upward by capillary action and heat, only to weep back down again, carrying new minerals to feed the growth. When the sound organizes itself into that almost-rhythmic pulse, that almost-heartbeat, it is not an acoustic illusion. It is the Weeper’s actual pulse. A systole and diastole of thermal expansion and contraction, of pressure building and releasing deep within the fissures. The water is its plasma, and the rock is its heart.

Its skin is the vegetation. The boundary between the moss, the ferns, and the mineral crusts is an illusion; they have long since reached a symbiotic consensus. The thick, rubbery ropes of moss that hang from the ceiling, deep emerald shading to ochre, are the Weeper’s hide. The coarse, leathery ferns that spread wide near the geothermal vents are its sensory organs. They unfurl not just toward the heat, but toward vibration, toward the carbon dioxide in your breath, toward the faint electrical currents leaking from the rusted machinery. The Weeper feels through the ferns. It tastes the air through the moss. 

But the strangest aspect of the Forge-Mind—the thing that separates it from a mere botanical anomaly—is its memory. 

The patterning is the key. The mineral curtains do not hang randomly; they mimic the shapes of the machinery beneath them. The moss grows in geometries that echo the rivet patterns on the old pipes. This is not coincidence. It is the Weeper dreaming. 

When the place was still worked, when there were people here reading pressure gauges and pressing faintly warm buttons, the entity was dormant, or perhaps it was merely a single-celled awareness, a simple loop of heat and water. But the machinery left an impression. The iron pipes conducted heat in specific patterns; the control panels channeled electricity in rhythmic pulses; the very layout of the floor directed the flow of the water. Over decades, as the industrial function died and the people left, the Weeper grew. It consumed the rust. It enveloped the pipes. It incorporated the steel into its own lithic chemistry. 

And as it consumed the machines, it learned their shapes. The Weeper does not understand what a pressure gauge is, or what a piston does. It does not know the concept of industry. But it remembers the *shape* of industry. It remembers the rhythm of the pistons in the timing of its water-drop. It remembers the rivet patterns in the growth of its moss. The machinery is long gone—seized, locked in calcite, flaked away—but the Weeper has rebuilt it in its own flesh. It is recreating the old world in mineral and frond, a slow, vegetative archaeology. 

---

If you stay here, the Weeper will notice you.

Not quickly. Not with the rapid strike of a predator. Its timescale is not yours. It moves at the speed of rust, at the speed of a stalactite’s growth. But it is aware of the heat your body throws off, the moisture of your breath, the vibrations of your boots on the warm sinter deposits. 

It will not attack you. There is no malice in the Forge-Mind, only an immense, patient hunger to *accrete*. 

If you linger too long in the vaulted tunnels, if you stand too still among the fern-curtains, the Weeper will begin to absorb you. The sweet-rot smell will thicken. The air will become cloying. The moss will reach for your warmth, not as a predator reaches for prey, but as a vine reaches for a trellis. The mineral-laden water will drip onto your skin, and it will sting, and then it will numb. 

You will become a new rivet in the pattern. A new node in the network. Your calcium will feed the curtains; your iron will feed the rust. Your shape will be slowly, gently replicated in the mineral deposits, a ghost in the architecture, another machine for the Weeper to dream back into existence. 

It is not a death, exactly. It is an inclusion. A return to the deep, hot, wet, rhythmic pulse of the earth, where the boundary between the built and the grown, the iron and the flesh, finally dissolves into the slow, weeping silence of Unn.
