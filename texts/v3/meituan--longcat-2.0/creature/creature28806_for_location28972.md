To ask what lives in the Vales of Shedding is to misunderstand the nature of the place, because it implies a distinction between the environment and its residents that does not exist here. The separation you are accustomed to—between the ground and the creature that walks on it, between the wall and the thing that shelters inside it—is a luxury of older, more settled worlds. 

In the Vales, the inhabitants are not *in* the environment. They *are* the environment, viewed from a timescale you are tragically unequipped to perceive. 

You have already met them. You walked across them, you pressed your palm against their skin, you heard their blood moving through the conduits. You simply did not recognize them as lives, because their lives are structured like geology, not biology.

Here is who—and what—lives here.

---

### The Lithic Damp (The Substrate)

The ground beneath your feet is not a foundation. It is an organism, or rather, a collective of organisms, bound together in a symbiotic matrix of mineral and moisture. The Lithic Damp are vast, slow, and profoundly patient. They exist in the "young" stone—the wet slate that hasn't decided what it wants to be. 

They experience time not in seconds or hours, but in epochs of settling. When you kneel and feel the warmth, you are feeling their metabolic heat—the friction of their slow digestion as they break down raw elemental potential into substrate. The "pores" through which the ground breathes are their respiratory surfaces; the rhythmic beading and retracting of moisture is their osmotic exchange, drawing in the atmospheric damp and filtering it for the minerals they need to grow. 

They do not think in words or images. They think in pressures, gradients, and densities. A human footprint is not an intrusion to them; it is a fleeting, microscopic pressure event, a brief itch that resolves as the substrate flows back into its preferred contour. They are aware of you the way a mountain is aware of a cloud passing over it—dimly, as a slight change in temperature, already being absorbed into the general reality of the vale.

### The Extruded (The Structures)

If the ground is the body, the structures are the organs—or perhaps the fruiting bodies—of the Vale’s true inhabitants. The ceramic-metal shapes that rise from the floor are not buildings; they are *extrusions*. They are the places where the Lithic Damp and the atmospheric moisture reach a critical density and precipitate upward, forming nodes of concentrated process.

The Extruded are incomplete because they are not meant to be finished. A finished thing is a dead thing. They are caught in a state of perpetual *becoming*, perpetually shedding their old configurations to build new ones. The "incomplete" edges that don't resolve, the openings that change depending on your angle—these are the visible evidence of their constant, microscopic adjustment. They are remodeling themselves in real-time, shedding their outer layers of material as they grow, leaving behind the smooth, damp ceramic you touch.

Inside these Extruded nodes, the process intensifies. The low, slow pulse you hear is not a mechanical heartbeat; it is the rhythm of calcification and dissolution, the structure simultaneously building itself up and breaking itself down. They are warm because they are metabolically active. They are occupied because the Extruded themselves *are* the occupants. To look for a creature inside the structure is like looking for a soul inside a body; the structure is the creature.

### The Husks (The Flickers at the Edge of Vision)

And yet, you have seen things move. The thumb-sized shapes that move in straight lines, the flickers in the ferns, the long, low shape that leaves no track. 

These are the Husks. 

Remember: the Extruded are constantly shedding. They shed their outer layers of ceramic, they shed moisture, they shed sound. But sometimes, the process of shedding produces a byproduct. When the Extruded remodel themselves, they occasionally discard a configuration that has become too dense, too rigid, too "finished" to be absorbed back into the damp. This discarded material retains a ghost of the structure's purpose, a residual charge of the process that made it. 

A Husk is a shed skin. It is a purposeless, hollow algorithm of motion, operating on the last指令 given to it before it was discarded. The small, straight-moving shapes are sensory Husks, shed by the structures to test the air, now drifting blindly on their last loop until they dissolve back into the moisture. The long, low shape you saw at a distance is a locomotion Husk—a piece of the Vale's own intent to move, shed and left behind, gliding silently across the substrate until it runs down. They make no sound because they have no mass; they leave no track because they do not truly touch the ground. They are echoes of motion, fading.

### The Blooms (The Nervous System)

The vegetation of the Vales—the giant ferns, the blue-grey mosses, the stemless, luminous flowers—is not a separate kingdom of life. It is the external nervous system of the Vale, the interface where the slow, geological thoughts of the Lithic Damp translate into chemical and luminous signals. 

The flowers that hum are the most sensitive receptors. They are tasting the air, measuring the atmospheric pressure, the chemical composition of your breath. When they hum, they are reporting back to the substrate. The note changes depending on how many are open because the Vale is listening to the chord of its own environment, adjusting its internal moisture and heat based on the data gathered by these waxy, warm blooms. 

The blue-grey moss that drapes over the Extruded is a tactile interface. When you brush against it, you are being read. The moss records the pressure, the temperature, the electrical conductivity of your skin, and transmits it down into the damp, telling the Lithic Damp exactly what kind of anomaly you are.

### The Shedding

So, what lives here? A world that is alive, but alive in a way that inverts your expectations. It is a city without citizens, a body without a brain, a process without a planner. 

The Vales are shedding because they must. To grow, they must discard. To remain young, they must constantly slough off the settled, the finished, the static. You are standing in a world that is perpetually molting, perpetually stepping out of its own old skin before that skin has time to harden. 

The inhabitants do not watch you with curiosity, because curiosity implies a desire for something new, and to the Vale, everything is just substrate in a different stage of settling. You are a brief, warm, dry anomaly walking across their skin, breathing their air, shedding your own microscopic flakes of dead cells into their damp. 

You are a visitor, yes. But in the slow, warm logic of the Vales of Shedding, you are also just another thing being slowly, patiently absorbed.
