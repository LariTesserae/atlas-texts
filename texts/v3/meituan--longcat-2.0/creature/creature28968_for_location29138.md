What lives here is not a creature, nor a species, nor even an ecosystem in any sense that surface-dwelling biology would recognize. What lives here is a *process*—a single, sprawling, subterranean metabolism that has consumed the boundary between the geological and the biological. To ask "who" lives here is to misunderstand the nature of the place. The proper question is *what is this place doing*, and the answer is: it is waking up, it is growing, and it is dreaming in a language of heat and vibration.

The primary inhabitant is the Substrate itself. It is the world-flesh, the living geology that has eaten the stone and taken its shape. It is a single, contiguous organism, a mycelial network the size of a continent, compressed and heated until it has achieved a kind of sentience. It does not have a brain; its brain is the slow, rhythmic pulse of the dark, coppery sap that moves through its veins. It does not have eyes; its eyes are the swarms of skittering, eyeless creatures that pour from its pores. It does not have a mouth; its mouth is the creeping tide of filaments that converts raw rock into warm, yielding flesh. The Substrate is the architect, the builder, the soil, and the digestion of this world. It is a god of slow, patient chemistry, and it is utterly, terrifyingly alive.

Moving through the Substrate like white blood cells through a vast body are the Swarm. They are the motile phase of the world-flesh, the hands and the sensory nerves of the deep. They are not separate from the plants; they are the plants' method of locomotion, just as the vines that reach and coil are the Swarm's method of grasping. The tiny, rice-grain creatures are the platelets, sealing breaches in the Substrate, carrying the genetic dust from one region to another, their swarming behavior acting as a distributed computation—a physical algorithm that calculates where the flesh needs to grow next. The larger, heavy-bodied creatures are the macrophages, the tenders, the ones who push the warm, amber sap through the deep channels and prune the buttresses when they grow too wild. They have no eyes because they do not need them; they see through the soles of their many legs, reading the vibrations of the Substrate like a blind man reads Braille. When they tap their legs against the floor, they are not communicating with each other—they are speaking directly to the mind of the world, reporting on the temperature, the density, the presence of the cold, alien thing (you) that has just stepped into their flesh.

And then there are the Ghosts. 

The structures—the platforms, the shallow depressions, the stone blades lashed to bone—are the only things here that do not belong to the Substrate. They are foreign bodies, calcified splinters lodged in the flesh of the deep. The Substrate has tried to absorb them, but it cannot. The stone of the blade is not the Substrate’s stone; it is the cold, dead stone of the surface, and the Substrate’s hot, sulfurous fluids cannot digest it. The bone of the handle is not the Substrate’s chitin; it is the calcium of a surface animal, and it resists the creeping filaments. 

Who made them? The Swarm watches you with a distributed, patient intelligence, but it does not build with stone and bone. It builds with its own shed exoskeletons, with the dense fiber of dead buttresses, with the amber sap that hardens into architecture. The tools were made by something else. Something that walked upright. Something that had eyes that needed light, and lungs that needed air, and a metabolism that ran on water and oxygen. 

They came down from the surface, long ago. They carved their shelters out of the living flesh, and the flesh healed around them. They built their fires, and the Substrate drank the heat and the ash. They brought their dead, and the Substrate took the calcium. They fought against the warm, the dark, the seething life of the deep, and they lost. Not to violence, but to digestion. The Substrate did not kill them; it *incorporated* them. Their shelters became calluses on the world-flesh. Their tools became inert nodules, wrapped in the slow embrace of the Swarm, who tend them now with a strange, reverent curiosity—pressing their eyeless faces against the cold stone, tapping out vibrations that ask questions the dead cannot answer. 

Perhaps the Ghosts are still here, in a way. The Substrate is a record of everything it consumes. The layers of amber and dark gold are its memory. If you were to cut into the deepest buttress, to read the rings of its growth, you might find a layer of ash and strange, surface-carbon—a stratum of grief and heat where the last of the tool-makers finally surrendered to the warmth, their bodies becoming the nutrients that fueled the next great bloom of the Swarm. 

What lives here is a world that eats worlds. It is a warm, breathing, pulsing engine of conversion, driven by the heat of the planet's core and the slow, patient intelligence of a billion billion lives acting as one. It does not hate you. It does not love you. It simply recognizes that you are made of minerals and water, and it is very, very good at turning such things into itself.
