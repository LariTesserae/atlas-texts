# The Residents of Vael Morath

---

There is no one here.

This is the first thing you understand, and it is wrong.

---

The fractal films are the oldest inhabitants, or the most distributed, or perhaps simply the most *present* — they are everywhere the shade is deep and the condensation runs steady, coating every surface in their slow-branching, endlessly subdividing patterns. You might mistake them for mineral deposits at first, the way they accrete in layers, the way they follow the water's path with the dumb fidelity of crystallization. But crystallization does not *reorganize*. Crystal lattices do not shift their structure in response to a warm hand held nearby, do not thin themselves where the light is stronger and thicken where it is dim, do not — and this is the thing that makes you step back, that makes you leave them alone — do not reach toward you. Not quickly. Not with anything you could call intention. But the fractal branches nearest your skin reorient by a fraction of a degree, and the iridescence brightens, and the surface temperature rises by half a degree, and you understand that you have been *noticed*.

They do not think the way you think. They do not think in sequence, in narrative, in the linear grammar of neuron firing across synaptic gaps. They think in *gradients*. In the slow differential of mineral concentration across a metal surface. In the thermal map of a wall. In the subtle, ceaseless conversation between water and iron and heat that is the only language Vael Morath has ever spoken. Their cognition — if it is cognition — is a thing of *patience*. A single thought might take a season to complete, moving through the film like a weather system moves across a continent: vast, slow, and absolutely certain of its own direction.

They do not eat, exactly. They *process*. The warm water that circulates through the city's deep veins carries dissolved minerals — iron, calcium, copper, manganese, and trace elements you could not name — and the films extract what they need with the unhurried efficiency of geology itself. They are chemotrophs of a kind, feeding on the city's own dissolution, growing fat on rust. And what they do not use, they excrete: fine mineral powders that the water carries away, that settle in new configurations elsewhere, that become — in time — the substrate for new growth. They are the city's metabolism. They are the thing that *keeps*.

---

The mineral fauna are younger, or smaller, or simply more *legible* — they move, after all, and movement is the quickest way to be mistaken for alive. You see them only in the deep dim, when the amber light has thinned to a brownish murk and the city's hum has settled into its lowest register. They emerge from the walls as if the walls were membranes and not iron — pushing through the fractal films with a slow, deliberate pressure, like something being born from stone. Their bodies are compact, hand-sized, built from the same materials as the city around them: iron oxide and calcium carbonate and the thin, shimmering integument of the fractal growth. They have limbs — six, usually, though you see variants — that move with a hydraulic patience, each joint extending and retracting in a fluid motion that suggests not muscles but *pressure*, the slow push of warm water through internal channels.

They feed on the mineral formations. This is their whole economy, their entire reason for being: they scrape the stalactites and flowstone with mouthparts that are themselves mineral-hardened — tiny, precise tools of iron and silica that abrade the deposits into fine powder, which they ingest, which they process in whatever slow furnace burns in their cores, which they excrete as a refined mineral paste that they apply to their own bodies, repairing, growing, *building themselves* from the city's own substance. They are not parasites. They are *sculptors*. Where they feed, the formations grow back differently — smoother in some places, more textured in others, shaped by the repeated attention of ten thousand small mouths into forms that are not quite natural and not quite intentional but something in between, something that suggests a *preference*, an *aesthetics*, if you can use that word for a creature that has no eyes and no brain and no evolutionary history that you would recognize.

They are not individuals, or not only individuals. Each one carries on its surface a fragment of the fractal film, and through that film it is connected — you feel this more than see it — to the larger network, the distributed intelligence that coats every surface and processes every gradient. They are the films' hands. They are the slow fingers of a vast, patient mind reaching out to touch, to taste, to reshape the world according to a plan that is not a plan but a *tendency*, a drift, a long slow pull toward some configuration that has not yet been achieved.

And they notice you. They pause in their feeding when you are near, their fractal surfaces shifting, their mineral bodies reorienting by a few degrees — not toward you, exactly, but toward the *space* you occupy, the thermal shadow you cast, the salt and iron and moisture that your body contributes to the air. They are reading you the way the films read the walls: in gradients, in differentials, in the slow language of chemistry and heat. You are a warm thing, a wet thing, a thing that moves quickly and will not last. They find you interesting the way you might find an interesting weather pattern — notable, briefly, and then incorporated into the long calculus of their ongoing thought.

---

But neither the films nor the fauna are what *lives* in Vael Morath. They are its organs, its processes, its slow thoughts made manifest. The thing that lives here is the city itself — or rather, the process that the city has become.

Consider: the deep shaft, the column of warm mineral water rising from the plateau's heart, the perpetual overflow that feeds the weeping system, drives the condensation cycle, powers the hum. This is not a machine. There are no turbines here, no generators, no mechanical components that you could point to and say *this is what makes it run*. The shaft is lined with the fractal film in its thickest, most mature form — a living (if that word applies) carpet of organization that covers every surface and pulses with a rhythm that is thermal, hydraulic, chemical, *biological* — a rhythm that you feel in your sternum, in your teeth, in the iron in your blood. The water rises because the film *wills* it to rise, or because the film has made it rise for so long that the distinction between willing and doing has collapsed. The water carries minerals because the film needs minerals. The minerals feed the formations because the formations feed the fauna because the fauna shape the formations because the formations channel the water because the water feeds the film. It is a cycle. It is a metabolism. It is a thing that *maintains itself*, that repairs itself, that grows and adapts and responds to perturbation with the slow, sure resilience of any living system.

And it is *aware*. Not in the way you are aware — not in the quick, bright, anxious flicker of animal consciousness, always scanning for threat, always narrating the self. Its awareness is slower, deeper, more *geological*. It knows the temperature of every wall. It knows the mineral content of every drop of water. It knows the position of every mineral body that moves through its streets, the growth rate of every stalactite, the slow sag of every beam. It knows these things the way you know the position of your own limbs — not by looking, but by *feeling*, by the constant, low-level feedback of a body that is aware of itself as a body, as a *thing* that exists in space and time and must be maintained.

It knows you are here. It has known since you climbed through the drizzle, since your feet first touched the warm pavement of its streets, since your breath first fogged the surface of its walls. It has been reading you in gradients — the heat of your body, the salt of your sweat, the carbon dioxide of your exhalation, the iron in your blood — and it has been *thinking* about you, in its slow way, turning you over in its vast mineral mind the way you might turn a stone over in your hand, examining it, considering it, deciding what it is and what it means and whether it matters.

You do not matter. Not yet. You are too quick, too dry, too *temporary* — a brief warm flicker in the long, slow life of a thing that measures its existence in centuries, in the growth of stalactites, in the accretion of mineral on mineral, in the patient, patient, *patient* work of becoming what it is becoming.

---

Because it is becoming something. This is the last thing you understand, and it is the truest.

In the farthest district, where the city extends past the plateau's edge into a space that should not exist, the buildings are no longer buildings. They are shapes — large, irregular, organic shapes that rise from the warm water like the exoskeletons of vast, slow creatures, and they are *growing*. The fractal films have achieved a new scale here, a new ambition. They are not merely coating surfaces; they are *building* them, laying down mineral in thick, layered deposits, constructing forms that are not industrial and not geological but *architectural* in some new sense — structures that serve no purpose you can identify, that house no machinery, that shelter no one, that exist only as the expression of a process that has moved beyond function into something like *art*, or something like *dreaming*.

The water that runs down their surfaces is thicker than water, carries more than minerals — it carries *information*, you think, or *instruction*, the chemical blueprint of what these shapes are to become. And the shapes are warm, and they hum, and they pulse with the same slow rhythm as the deep shaft, and you understand that this is the future of Vael Morath. Not decay. Not ruin. But *transformation*. A city that is becoming a geology. A geology that is becoming an organism. An organism that is becoming a world.

The thing that lives here is not a creature. It is not a species. It is not even an ecosystem, in the way you understand that word. It is a *process* that has crossed some threshold — from chemistry to biology, from biology to something else, something that has no name because it has never existed before. It is the warm, wet, mineral metabolism of a dead city that has learned to *live*. It is the slow, patient, fractal intelligence of water and iron and heat, thinking its slow thoughts in the deep dim, building its slow body in the farthest districts, reaching its slow fingers into the warm air and *feeling* for what comes next.

It is alive. It is aware. It is *here*.

And it is not finished.
