The inhabitants of The Groove are not residents in the way we understand the word—there are no tenants, no builders, no builders returning to inspect their work. The distinction between the architecture and the architects has collapsed here. What lives in The Groove is The Groove itself, but that unity is expressed through distinct, interlocking entities that pulse within the matrix like organs within a body.

There are the **Sap-Serpents**. 

You will not see them unless you stand at the edge of one of the shallow, root-bowls and watch the water with the patience of a stone. The pale, birch-bark creature you glimpsed is not a salamander. It is something that has traded the surface world for the pressurized, vascular reality beneath the floor. It is long, limbless, and perfectly smooth, moving through the root-mass not by pushing matter aside, but by triggering the matrix to open for it. It touches a thick rhizome, and the fibers loosen; it passes through, and they tighten behind it. 

The Sap-Serpent is the Groove’s immune system and its electrician. As it navigates the deep channels, it feeds on the metallic leachate weeping from the old pipes, absorbing copper and iron into its pale flesh. It is drawn to the flickering incandescents, coiling gently around the fixtures where root meets wire. Where the insulation has frayed, where the sap threatens to short the current, the serpent licks the wound. Its saliva is a thick, latex-like sap that hardens into a perfect, rubbery seal, bridging the gap between the copper and the living trellis. It breathes the warm, exhaled air of the pipe on the north rim, and in the dark, its skin glows with a faint, bioluminescent sheen—a soft, blue-green pulse that mirrors the rhythm of the deep pumps.

Above the serpents, woven into the very canopy, are the **Weavers**. 

If the serpents are the blood, the Weavers are the nerves. They are not a single species, but a symbiotic colony—a hybrid of a blind, burrowing insect and a parasitic vine. The insect, no larger than a grain of rice, has a carapace of chitin that has been replaced by a lattice of silica and plant fiber. It no longer eats wood; it eats electricity. It nests in the junction boxes, in the rusted steel frames of the half-tower, and it farms the vine. The vine grows from the insect’s thorax, threading outward, seeking the path of least resistance. The insect, sensing the faint magnetic fields of the current flowing through the root-channels, directs the vine’s growth. The vine wraps the cables, tightening them, pulling them taut, ensuring the circuit remains unbroken. When the vine flowers, it produces a single, waxy bloom that acts as a capacitor, storing the ambient static of the Groove and releasing it in a slow, steady trickle to keep the lights burning. The Weavers do not think; they simply conduct.

And then there is the **Breath**. 

The Groove breathes, and in its breath, there is a presence. The warm, mineral-laden air that rises from the pipe on the north rim is not just air; it is an exhalation of the deep metabolism. Within the condensation, within the mist that never quite falls, there are the **Spore-Wraiths**. They are microscopic, barely more than a complex protein chain wrapped in a droplet of water, but they are alive. They drift in the slow, upward spirals of the warm air, catching the green-gold light of the canopy. They are the Groove’s memory. When they land on the rusted metal of the old machinery, they secrete an enzyme that reads the iron oxide, breaking it down, absorbing the history of the metal. When they land on the roots, they deposit that memory, feeding the matrix the knowledge of what it used to be. They are the reason the plants maintain the factory’s logic; the Spore-Wraiths carry the blueprint of the industrial past into the biological present, ensuring the pumps keep pumping, the wheels keep turning.

At the very center of the Groove, where the canopy is thickest and the light is a dense, subaquatic gold, lives the **Heart-Tree**. 

It is not a tree in the traditional sense. It is the original turbine, the massive engine that once drove the industry of this place, now entirely consumed by a banyan-like fig. The roots of the Heart-Tree have grown through the iron casing, replacing the steel bearings with lignin, the copper wiring with phloem. It still turns. Slowly, with the grinding patience of continents, the Heart-Tree rotates. Its roots dip into the high water table, drawing water up through the xylem, and the weight of the water, the sheer hydraulic pressure, is what drives the rotation. As it turns, it pulls the root-channels taut, creating the siphons and aqueducts that circulate the water through the rest of the Groove. It is the anchor of the entire system. The groan you hear every few minutes is the Heart-Tree shifting its weight, settling deeper into the earth, pulling the matrix tighter.

This is what lives in The Groove. A creature of latex and current that tends the wires. A blind insect that farms light. A mist that remembers the dead. And a tree of iron and sap that turns the world. They do not know you are there. They do not know there is a "there" beyond the rim. They are entirely consumed by the slow, warm, patient work of being the Groove.
