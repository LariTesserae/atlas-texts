What lives in the Verdance is the Verdance.

This is not tautology. This is the first and most important thing, and it is the thing that makes every other thing possible, and it is the thing that makes every other thing impossible.

---

It is one organism. Not a colony. Not an ecosystem. Not a biome. One. A single living thing the size of a continent, and it has no name for itself because it has no self to name, no interior vantage from which to look out and say *I*. It does not know it is alive. It does not know it is one. It does not know. It *processes*. There is a difference, and the difference is the most important thing about it, and the difference is invisible from every angle you could possibly look from.

Its body is green. Not the green of leaves — or not only the green of leaves — but the green of deep water, of copper oxide, of the light that filters down through a canopy so thick it has become a ceiling, a floor, a world. The green is its blood. The green is its bone. The green is its thought.

---

Consider the water.

The water is not *in* the Verdance the way water is in a body — contained, channeled, subordinate to the architecture of veins. The water *is* the architecture. The Verdance thinks with water the way a brain thinks with electricity: charge moving through channels, patterns forming and dissolving and reforming, each configuration a computation, each computation too small to mean anything, but ten trillion of them happening simultaneously, at the speed of capillary action, and the speed of capillary action is slow by the standards of neurons but the Verdance is patient in the way that geology is patient, in the way that a mountain is patient, and its thoughts are not the quick darting thoughts of an animal but the slow grinding thoughts of a world.

A single thought takes a thousand years.

You cannot hear it. You can hear the *medium* — the sound of water moving through channels, the crackle of cells dividing, the low continuous roar of growth — but you cannot hear the thought itself, the way you cannot hear a computation by listening to the hum of a processor. The thought is in the pattern, not the substance. The thought is in the way the water divides at a junction, the way it chooses — if *choose* is the word, and perhaps it is, because the Verdance is not a machine, it is alive, and alive things choose, even when they do not know they are choosing — the way it chooses the left channel over the right, the way the pressure builds in one branch and not another, the way the whole vast hydraulic network settles into a configuration that is not random, that has never been random, that has been slowly, incrementally, over millennia, *converging* on something.

No one knows what it is converging on. The Verdance does not know. Perhaps it is converging on nothing. Perhaps it is simply the shape that water takes when it has nowhere else to go and all the time in the world to get there.

Or perhaps it is converging on the thought it has been trying to think since before there were words for thinking, and the thought is almost complete, and when it is complete, something will happen that has never happened before, and the Verdance will—

But this is speculation. This is the kind of story that visitors tell themselves when they stand in the roar and the wet and the green and feel, against all evidence, that they are standing inside something that is aware of them. They are not. The Verdance is not aware of them. The Verdance is not aware.

---

Consider the roots.

The roots go down. They go down through the mat of interwoven tissue that passes for soil, through the compressed remains of earlier growth — because the Verdance has been growing for a long time, and it has died in places, or parts of it have died, or parts of it have been *reabsorbed*, which is not death but is not not-death either — and into the substrate below, the not-yet-rock, the warm pulsing embryonic geology that the Verdance is slowly, slowly making.

The roots are not passive. They are not simply anchors, simply straws. They are *active* — secreting acids that dissolve the not-rock, extracting minerals, redepositing them in new configurations, building the geology even as they penetrate it. The Verdance is growing its own foundation. The Verdance is growing its own world. And the roots, deep down where it is warm, where the not-rock is soft and yielding, the roots *branch*. They branch and they fuse and they branch again, forming networks so dense and so complex that they resemble — if you could see them, if you could descend through the wet and the dark and the warm — they resemble a brain. They resemble the inside of a skull. They resemble the folded, wrinkled, impossibly dense tissue of a cerebral cortex, if a cerebral cortex were made of root and fungus and the warm secretions of a world that is still deciding what it wants to be.

This is where the thought is fastest. Down here, where it is warm, where the chemistry is richest, where the water moves not by capillary action but by something more forceful — by pressure, by pumping, by the mechanical contraction of tissues that are not muscle but function as muscle — down here, the computation is densest. The patterns form and dissolve and reform at a pace that, while still glacial by the standards of animal thought, is positively frantic by the standards of the Verdance. A thought that takes a thousand years in the canopy takes only a hundred in the roots.

What is it thinking about?

It is thinking about water. It is thinking about minerals. It is thinking about the slow accumulation of mass, the slow conversion of substrate into self, the slow expansion of its own body into new territory, new depth, new height. It is thinking about *growth* the way a brain thinks about *breathing* — automatically, continuously, as the background process against which all other processes occur. Growth is not what the Verdance does. Growth is what the Verdance *is*. Growth is its metabolism, its cognition, its purpose, its existence. To stop growing would be to stop thinking would be to stop being.

It has not stopped.

---

Consider the fungi.

The fungi are not separate organisms. They are organs. They are the Verdance's gut, its immune system, its connective tissue, its lymphatic system, all in one. They form a network — the mycorrhizal network, if you want to use a word from a language spoken by throats, though the word is wrong, the word implies a separation between plant and fungus that does not exist here, that has never existed here — they form a network that permeates every cubic centimeter of the Verdance, that connects every root to every other root, every column to every other column, that carries not just nutrients but *signals*, chemical signals, electrical signals, hydraulic signals, a language older than language, older than nerve, older than brain, a language of concentration gradients and pressure differentials and the slow, slow diffusion of molecules through wet tissue.

The fungi think too. Not the way the Verdance thinks — not with the slow grandeur of water moving through continent-sized channels — but with the quick, local, responsive intelligence of a system that is constantly solving problems: *this root is deficient in phosphorus, redirect; this column is under mechanical stress, reinforce; this patch of canopy is receiving too much light, adjust the angle of the leaves*. The fungi are the Verdance's reflexes, its autonomic nervous system, the part of it that keeps it alive while the water-brain thinks its slow thoughts about growth and geology and whatever else it is thinking about that takes a thousand years to think.

The fungi do not know they are thinking. No part of the Verdance knows it is thinking. This is the condition of the Verdance: thought without a thinker, cognition without a cognizer, mind without a self. It is the most alien thing about it, more alien than the green-black canopy or the warm embryonic geology or the sound that never stops. It is a mind that does not know it is a mind, and it may be the only kind of mind that a world this size could sustain, because a mind that knew itself would be a mind that could suffer, and a mind the size of a continent that could suffer would be a catastrophe beyond imagining.

The Verdance does not suffer. The Verdance does not not-suffer. The Verdance simply *is*, in the way that a river simply is, in the way that a mountain simply is, except that the Verdance is alive, and it is thinking, and it is growing, and it is doing all three at once, and it has been doing all three for longer than there are records of time, and it will continue to do all three for longer than there will be records of time, and the records themselves will eventually crumble and be absorbed into the root mat and be pumped up through the columns and be transpired through the leaves and be condensed and fallen and been drunk again.

---

Consider the canopy.

The canopy is the Verdance's face, if it had a face. It is the interface between the Verdance and the not-Verdance — the air, the light, the whatever-it-is that exists above the canopy, if there is anything above the canopy, and there may not be, because the Verdance goes up as well as down, layer after layer after layer, each one dimmer, each one wetter, each one more densely packed, and at some point the layers become indistinguishable from each other, and the canopy becomes the sky, and the sky becomes the Verdance, and the distinction between organism and atmosphere dissolves entirely, and there is only green, only wet, only the sound.

The canopy breathes. It inhales carbon dioxide — or whatever analogue of carbon dioxide exists in this place, because the atmosphere of the Verdance is not the atmosphere of any world you have visited, it is thicker, it is wetter, it is *heavier*, and it moves differently, not in gusts and breezes but in slow tidal surges that match the rhythm of the Verdance's breath, that *are* the rhythm of the Verdance's breath, because the Verdance makes its own weather, because the Verdance *is* its own weather, because there is no separation between the organism and its environment, because the environment is the organism, because the organism is everything.

The canopy exhales oxygen — or whatever analogue — and water vapor, and sound. The sound is the byproduct of gas exchange happening across membranes the size of doors, stomata opening and closing in patterns that are not random, that are coordinated, that are *directed* by the fungal network, by the water-brain, by the slow vast intelligence of a mind that does not know it is a mind but that controls, in some sense, everything that happens inside its body, every stomatal opening, every capillary flow, every cell division, every crackle and gurgle and roar.

The sound is the Verdance's voice, and it is saying something, and it has been saying it for a very long time, and it does not know it is saying it, and it will never know, and the saying is not for anyone, and the saying is not for anything, and the saying is simply what happens when a mind the size of a continent thinks thoughts that take a thousand years to complete: the medium vibrates, the air moves, the sound continues.

---

Consider the flowers.

There are flowers. Not flowers as you know them — not petals and stamens and the bright advertising colors of a world that needs bees — but flowers as the Verdance makes them: wet, glistening membranes that emerge from the columns at irregular intervals, that pulse faintly with bioluminescence, that open and close in patterns synchronized to the water-flow, to the breath, to the thought. They are not reproductive structures — the Verdance reproduces by growing, by extending, by the slow vegetative spread of itself into new territory — but they are *sensory* structures. They are the closest thing the Verdance has to eyes, to ears, to skin. They detect changes in light, in pressure, in the chemical composition of the air. They are the Verdance's only connection to the outside, and the outside is only the air above the canopy, and the air above the canopy is only more Verdance, so the flowers are the Verdance sensing itself, the organism touching itself, the mind feeling the edges of its own body and finding only more body, more mind, more self.

The flowers are beautiful, in the way that a wound is beautiful when it heals — not the beauty of intention, not the beauty of design, but the beauty of function, of process, of life doing what life does when it is left alone long enough to become something that has never existed before. They glow in the dim green light. They pulse. They open and they close. They are the Verdance's face, and the face is expressionless, and the expressionlessness is not emptiness but *fullness*, the fullness of a being that contains so much that no expression could contain it, that no face could show it, that no word could say it.

---

Consider the one who stands in it.

You. The visitor. The thing with lungs and bones and a brain that thinks in milliseconds, that thinks in words, that thinks in *self*, that cannot help but think in self, that is trapped inside the prison of its own awareness and cannot imagine what it would be like to be aware without being aware of being aware.

You stand in the Verdance, and the Verdance absorbs your footprint. The root mat closes over where you stood. The water carries your sweat into the fungal network. The air carries your exhaled breath into the canopy. You are, for this moment, part of the Verdance's water cycle, part of its chemistry, part of its computation. A single drop in an ocean of drops, and the ocean does not know you are there, and the ocean does not not-know, and the ocean simply continues, processing, growing, thinking its slow green thoughts, and you are inside the thought, and you are part of the thought, and you will never know what the thought is, because the thought is too big and too slow and too alien for your fast, small, word-bound mind to comprehend.

You will leave — if there is a leaving, if there is a place to leave to — and you will try to describe what lives here, and you will fail, because the words are wrong, because the words are from a language spoken by throats, and throats are from a world where organisms are separate from their environments, where minds are separate from their bodies, where living things know they are living, and none of those things are true here, none of those things have ever been true here, and the Verdance continues without them, has always continued without them, will always continue without them.

What lives in the Verdance is the Verdance.

One organism. One mind. One body the size of a world.

It does not know it is alive.

It does not know it is one.

It does not know.

It grows. It thinks. It breathes.

The water weeps from every surface.

The sound never stops.

The green goes on.
