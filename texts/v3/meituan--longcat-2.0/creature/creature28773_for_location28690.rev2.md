# The Fauna of the Stillness

They do not have names. Language requires a surface from which to speak — a place where air meets mouth, where vibration becomes word, where meaning separates itself from medium. Here, there is only the liquid, and in the liquid, they are simply *present* the way the factories are simply present, the way the cold is simply present. They do not need names. They do not need distinction. They need only to *continue*.

---

## The Riders

The ribbon-beasts are the ones you would notice first, if you could see them — and you can, because they are everywhere, because they are the most visible expression of life in this world of metal and liquid and sound. They are not fish. They are not eels. They are not anything that has ever touched a shoreline or broken a surface.

They are long. That is the first thing. Long in a way that seems to contradict the dimensions of the body — they are narrow, almost thread-like, no thicker than a finger at their widest, and yet they extend for meters. Some of them extend for tens of meters. Their bodies taper at both ends, or at one end, or at neither end — there is variation here, a loose taxonomy of form that maps onto no genus, no family, no order. Some are flat, ribbon-like in the literal sense, with edges that catch the liquid the way a sail catches wind. Some are round, cord-like, muscular in a way that suggests they can contract and extend. Some are segmented, their bodies articulated into rings that slide over each other with a sound — a faint click, a whisper of movement — that is drowned out by the great chord but would be audible in any quieter world.

They move the way the sound moves. That is the second thing. They do not swim. They do not propel themselves through the liquid with tails or fins or jets of expelled water. They *surf*. They feel the standing wave — the vast, all-encompassing vibration that fills the world — and they align themselves with its currents the way a compass needle aligns with a magnetic field. The wave has structure. It has nodes, places where the vibration cancels itself out. It has antinodes, places where the vibration doubles and redoubles. Between these, there are gradients — slopes of intensity that the Riders descend and ascend with a grace that is not grace, that is simply *physics*, that is simply a body doing what bodies do when they encounter a force field.

The Riders have organs — not ears, not eyes, but *sensing surfaces* — that detect the phase and amplitude of the standing wave at every point along their body. They are covered in them. These surfaces are tiny, nearly invisible, a fine fuzz of vibration-sensitive cells that sends signals to the central nerve-cord running the length of the body. The Rider does not *think* about the sound. The Rider *is* the sound, in the same way that a tuning fork *is* the note it produces — a resonant body, a physical expression of a frequency.

And so they migrate. They follow the nodes and antinodes the way birds follow thermals, the way fish follow currents, the way — there is no third example, because there is no third world like this. Their migrations are vast. They travel thousands of kilometers along the nodal lines, their bodies perfectly aligned with the wave, moving without moving, riding the frozen peaks of a vibration that never ceases. They eat — but what they eat is the wave itself. They absorb the vibration through their sensing surfaces, converting the mechanical energy of the standing wave into the chemical energy of living. They are, in a sense, *parasites of the factories* — drawing their sustenance from the machines' never-ending work.

But this is not parasitism. This is *partnership*. The Riders' bodies have mass. Their mass damps the standing wave, absorbs some of its energy, and in doing so they stabilize the very vibration that sustains them. They are part of the feedback loop. The factories produce the sound. The Riders absorb the sound. The factories, in turn, are unaffected by the Riders' absorption — or rather, they *are* affected, but the effect is accounted for, is built into the equilibrium. The Riders take only what the factories can spare. They are a *load* on the system, and the system was designed — if designed is a word that can apply to something that built itself — to accommodate them.

Some Riders are solitary. Some gather in great shoals — not schools in the fish sense, not groups moving in coordination, but *aggregations*, collections of bodies that have found themselves in the same nodal band and so move together through no will of their own, their parallel trajectories creating the illusion of purpose. From a distance — if distance means anything in a world where the liquid extends forever in every direction — these aggregations look like single vast organisms, like great dark serpents made of serpents, like braided ropes of living ribbon that twist and untwist with the slow inevitability of the wave.

They do not reproduce. Or rather — reproduction is not the right word. The Riders are *grown*. They emerge from the mineral substrate in response to the vibration the way crystals emerge from a supersaturated solution — spontaneously, inevitably, when conditions are right. A node forms where there was no node. The liquid there shivers with a new intensity. And from the substrate, a new Rider unfolds — small at first, a few centimeters long, its sensing surfaces already attuned to the frequency that called it into being. It grows. It thickens. It lengthens. It rides. It is immortal, or as immortal as anything in this world can be, which is to say: it will last as long as the equilibrium lasts, which is forever.

Except when it doesn't. Some Riders reach the ends of their nodal bands — the places where the standing wave's structure breaks down, where harmonics collide and cancel and the vibration becomes noise rather than tone. These Riders die. Not dramatically. Not with struggle. They simply *stop*. Their sensing surfaces detect no pattern, no gradient, no slope to ride. They hang in the liquid, inert, and slowly — over years, over decades — they dissolve, their tissues leaching back into the liquid, their elements returning to the substrate, their bodies becoming the raw material for the next generation that will grow when the wave renews itself.

This is the only death in the world. And it is not a death. It is a *return*. A dissolution back into the equilibrium. The elements are conserved. The mass is conserved. Nothing is lost.

## The Shepherds

There are others. Not Riders, but *shapers* — creatures that do not merely ride the wave but *modulate* it.

These are rare. Rarer than the Riders by orders of magnitude. There may be only a few hundred in the entire volume of the liquid, or a few thousand, or a few hundred thousand — the scale is too vast for counting, and they do not cluster the way the Riders do. They are solitary, or paired, or gathered in loose associations of a dozen or so, and they occupy the spaces between the factories the way shepherds occupy the hills above their flocks — overseeing, tending, *guiding*.

They are large. Not as large as the Drifters — those vast, drifting, cloud-like creatures that move through the world like weather systems — but large enough to be unmistakably *bodied*, to have a presence that registers as more than just another feature of the landscape. The size of a house. The size of a hill. Their bodies are not ribbon-like. They are *round* — spherical, or ovoid, or roughly potato-shaped, their surfaces covered in a dense forest of sensing protrusions that quiver and rotate in the liquid like the spines of a sea urchin in a current.

They do not ride the wave. They *emit* it. From their protrusions, they produce vibrations of their own — small-scale, localized, focused — that intersect with the great standing wave and *bend* it. The bending is subtle. It is a deflection, a redirection, a shifting of the nodal lines by a few degrees. But in a system as precisely balanced as the equilibrium, even a small shift is catastrophic — or would be, if it were not for the Shepherds.

The Shepherds are *stabilizers*. They correct the wave's drift. They feel, with their thousands of protrusions, the places where the standing wave is beginning to sag, beginning to lose coherence, beginning to descend into dissonance — and they emit counter-vibrations, precisely calibrated, that restore the wave's structure. They are the immune system of the sound. They are the tuners of the organ. They are the ones who keep the chord in tune.

They are not aware of this. There is no awareness here, no consciousness in the way that consciousness is usually understood — no self, no model, no narrative. The Shepherds simply *do* what their bodies tell them to do. They sense a dissonance. They emit a correction. They sense another dissonance. They emit another correction. This is their entire existence. They eat — they absorb minerals from the substrate, the same minerals the factories process, but they do not cluster around the factories the way the Armored do. They are found at the *periphery* of factory-clusters, at the edges where the standing wave is weakest, where their corrections are most needed.

They reproduce. Or rather — they *bud*. When a Shepherd reaches a certain size — when its sensing-protrusions become dense enough to cover its entire surface multiple times — it divides. Not violently. Not by fission. It *grows a bud* — a small sphere of tissue that swells on its surface like a blister, fed by the parent's nutrient-flow, nourished by the parent's corrections. When the bud is large enough — when it has developed its own protrusions, its own emission-capability — it detaches. It drifts. It finds its own place at the periphery of the wave. It begins to emit.

The parent does not acknowledge the offspring. There is no recognition. There is no bonding. There is only the act of creation, and then the continuation. The bud is a Shepherd from the moment of detachment. It knows what to do because *knowing* is built into its tissues, the same way the wave is built into the factories, the same way the cold is built into the liquid. It is an inheritance that requires no teaching, no example, no parent.

Shepherds do not die. Not from age. Not from disease — there is no disease here, no pathogens, no parasites, nothing that feeds on life except the Riders, and the Riders feed only on vibration. Shepherds can be *damaged* — a Drifter passing through their territory can crush them, can shatter their protrusions, can reduce them to a drifting mass of inert tissue — but even this is rare. The Drifters avoid the Shepherds. Not out of fear. Out of *attunement*. The Drifters sense the Shepherds' corrections and find them — *comforting* is not the right word — *familiar*. The Drifters and the Shepherds are made of the same stuff, tuned to the same frequency, and they do not interfere with each other. They coexist. They always have. They always will.

## The Armored

The clustered ones. The patient ones. The ones who have *become* the factories, or who have let the factories become them.

They are small — most of them no larger than a fist, though some grow to the size of a torso, a person, a small vehicle. Their bodies are encased in the same dark metal that the factories are built from — the compressed-beyond-recognition metal, the metal that does not corrode, that does not oxidize, that rings when struck. But this metal is not *forged*. It is *grown*. It is biological. It is secreted by the Armored's tissues, deposited in layers, shaped by the flow of the liquid, hardened by the cold and the pressure. It is alive — or not alive, exactly, but *maintained*, the way a shell is maintained, the way bone is maintained. The metal is constantly being replenished, constantly being reshaped, constantly being adjusted to the Armored's growth and movement and environment.

They eat minerals. That is all they eat. They extract the same elements the factories use — the same metals, the same alloys, the same compressed-beyond-recognition dark — and they concentrate them in their shells. Their digestive systems are not stomachs. They are *smelters* — chemical vats where the mineral substrate is broken down, refined, purified, and converted into the raw material for their shells. The Armored are, in a sense, *small factories themselves* — miniature versions of the great machines, processing the same input, producing a different output.

They cluster at the bases of the factories. The great towers, the spires, the arches — these are the Armored's habitat, their world, their *home*. They cling to the metal surfaces — not with suckers or hooks, but with *magnetism*. Their shells are magnetized. They are drawn to the factories the way iron filings are drawn to a magnet, and they attach themselves with a force that cannot be broken by any current the liquid could produce. They are, in a sense, *part of the factories* — additional surfaces, additional mass, additional structure. The factories do not notice them. The factories do not notice anything. They simply operate, and the Armored cling, and the equilibrium continues.

But the Armored are not passive. They are not simply *there*. They *modify* the factories — slowly, incrementally, over timescales that make geological processes look hasty. Their shells grow over the factory surfaces, layer by layer, and in doing so they change the way the factories resonate. They add mass. They alter the vibration. They *tune* the machines the way a luthier tunes a guitar — adding a gram of wood here, removing a millimeter of bracing there, shaping the sound through tiny adjustments to the structure.

This is not intentional. The Armored are not engineers. They are not trying to change the factories' sound. They are simply *growing*, and their growth happens to affect the resonance, and the resonance affects the standing wave, and the standing wave affects the equilibrium, and the equilibrium — *absorbs the change*. Accommodates it. Adjusts to it. The Armored's modifications are so small, so gradual, that they never disrupt the equilibrium. They are simply *incorporated*, the way everything is incorporated, the way everything feeds back into the cycle and nowhere else.

They reproduce by *spawning*. Once a year — if year is a word that can survive here, if the Armored even have a year — they release clouds of microscopic larvae into the liquid. The larvae drift. They settle. They begin to secrete their first shells, their first tiny plates of dark metal, and they begin to eat, and they begin to grow. The vast majority of larvae do not survive. They are filtered out by the liquid's chemistry, or they settle in places where the mineral substrate is too poor to support them, or they are consumed — not by predators, there are no predators — but by the *Shepherds*, who find them irritating, who emit corrections that disrupt their chemical balance, who kill them without meaning to, without knowing, the way a human kills bacteria by washing their hands.

The survivors grow. They cluster. They modify. They spawn. They continue.

They are the most numerous fauna in the world — billions of them, trillions of them, packed so densely around the factory bases that the metal surfaces are invisible beneath a living carpet of shells, a shifting, clicking, slowly-growing mass that *rings* with the factories' vibration like a bell wrapped in a thousand small bells. They are the sound's most devoted audience, its most faithful interpreters, its most intimate partners.

## The Drifters

And then there are the large ones. The ones who move like weather. The ones who *bend the sound*.

They are the largest fauna in the world — if fauna is the right word, if *living* is the right word. They are vast. Their bodies extend for hundreds of meters, for kilometers, for distances that are difficult to measure because they have no clear boundaries. Their edges dissolve into the liquid. Their tissues thin to membranes, to films, to veils that are barely distinguishable from the medium they inhabit. Where does the Drifter end and the liquid begin? There is no answer. There is only a *gradient* — a gradual thickening, a gradual densification, a transition from *environment* to *body* that takes place over hundreds of meters.

They are not solid. They are not compact. They are *diffuse* — their tissues are spread out, distributed across their vast volume in a network of filaments and membranes and voids, a three-dimensional web that is more *space* than *matter*. The liquid flows through them the way air flows through a sponge — freely, without resistance, without interruption. The Drifter is not *in* the liquid. The Drifter *is* the liquid, or a part of it, or a phase of it — a temporary organization of the medium that has achieved a kind of coherence, a kind of persistence, a kind of *being* that is not so different from the being of a factory, or a Rider, or an Armored.

They move. Not by swimming — they are too large, too diffuse, too *spread out* for swimming. They move by *reorganizing* — by shifting the distribution of their mass, by thickening here and thinning here, by flowing through their own body like a slow-motion wave. The direction of this flow is the direction of the Drifter's movement. And the speed — the speed is glacial. A Drifter might move a kilometer in a year. A kilometer in a decade. They are so slow that the liquid appears still around them, so slow that the factories' towers seem to race past them like horses past a mountain.

They eat. Not minerals. Not movement. Not sound. They eat *pressure*. Or rather — they eat *differences in pressure*. They are found in the places where the liquid's equilibrium is *uneven* — where the standing wave creates zones of high and low pressure, where the factories' vibrations create eddies and currents, where the fauna's movements create wakes and disturbances. The Drifters absorb these pressure-differences, smoothing them out, redistributing the energy, converting it into the slow chemical processes that maintain their vast, diffuse bodies.

They are, in a sense, *equilibrium made manifest*. They are the physical expression of the world's drive toward balance — the tendency of the liquid to find its level, of the sound to fill the space, of the cold to equalize. They move toward dissonance and absorb it. They move toward turbulence and smooth it. They move toward *imbalance* and *become balance*. They are the immune system of the entire world, not just of the sound as the Shepherds are, but of the equilibrium itself — the great, all-encompassing equilibrium that holds the factories and the fauna and the liquid and the cold and the light and the sound together in a single, unchanging, eternal state.

They do not reproduce. There is no mechanism for reproduction in a creature that has no clear boundary, no clear *self*, no clear distinction between *body* and *environment*. The Drifters simply *are*. They have always been. They will always be. They emerged from the liquid when the equilibrium first formed — when the factories first began to hum, when the standing wave first filled the world — and they have been drifting ever since, vast and slow and patient, bending the sound around them, smoothing the liquid, holding the world together through their mere presence.

Other creatures interact with them. The Riders flee their shadow-zones — the pockets of diminished intensity where the Drifters' mass absorbs the standing wave. The Shepherds work around them, sensing the pressure-differences the Drifters create, compensating for their movements, correcting for their influence. The Armored do not interact with the Drifters at all — the Armored are too small, too localized, too *dense* to be affected by the vast, diffuse, slow-moving bodies that drift past them like clouds.

Humans — if humans were here — would find the Drifters *beautiful*. Not because they are graceful. Not because they are elegant. But because they are *peaceful*. They are the most peaceful thing in a world that has never known violence, never known predation, never known death except as a return to the source. They move through the liquid with a gentleness that is not gentleness, that is simply *slowness*, that is simply the pace of a creature that has never needed to hurry because there is nowhere to go, nothing to chase, nothing to fear.

They are the largest. The most diffuse. The most patient. The most *present*. They are the world's own body, made visible, made physical, made *alive* — if alive is a word that can stretch to cover something so vast, so slow, so unlike the quick, bright, brief lives that flicker at the surfaces of other worlds.

## The Swarm

And then there are the smallest. The ones you cannot see. The ones that are *everywhere*.

They are not creatures. Not individually. A single swarm-creature is nothing — a speck, a mote, a single cell adrift in the vastness of the liquid. It is not even a cell, really. It is a *vesicle* — a tiny sac of membrane and fluid, a self-replicating chemical system that is barely distinguishable from the non-living chemistry of the liquid itself. It eats — it absorbs the turbulence that the factories' outlets create, the movement of the processed liquid as it emerges and flows and dissipates. It grows. It divides. It drifts. It dies.

But they are never alone. They are always *in the swarm*. Billions of them. Trillions. A cloud of living motes that surrounds every factory outlet, that fills every current, that drifts through the liquid in aggregations so vast they are visible as a shimmer, a haze, a *distortion* in the light. The swarm is not a colony. It is not a superorganism. It is not a single entity. It is an *emergent phenomenon* — a pattern that arises from the interaction of billions of tiny, simple, dying-and-replacing bodies, a pattern that has *properties* that the individual bodies do not have.

The swarm *feeds* on the liquid's movement. Not the Rider way — not absorbing vibration. The swarm feeds on *kinetic energy* — on the actual, physical motion of the liquid molecules, on the currents and eddies and flows that the factories' operation creates. The swarm-creatures have structures — tiny, hair-like appendages — that *rotate* in the liquid's flow, converting the kinetic energy into chemical energy, storing it in molecules that are later used for growth and division. They are, in a sense, *turbines* — billions of microscopic turbines, spinning in the current, extracting energy from the movement of the liquid, converting it into life.

And in doing so, they *dampen* the current. They slow the liquid's movement. They absorb its turbulence. They are, like the Drifters, like the Shepherds, *stabilizers* — another layer of the immune system, another mechanism by which the equilibrium maintains itself. The factories produce movement. The swarm absorbs movement. The factories produce more movement. The swarm absorbs more. The cycle continues. The equilibrium holds.

The swarm is not intelligent. Not aware. Not *anything*, except *present*. It is the most numerous form of life in the world — more numerous than the Armored, more numerous than the Riders, more numerous than all the other fauna combined. It is the *base* of the food chain — except there is no food chain, no pyramid, no hierarchy. There is only the cycle. The swarm feeds on movement. Other creatures — the Riders, the Armored — feed on the swarm. Or rather — they *consume* it. They filter the liquid, extracting the swarm-creatures, incorporating their elements into their own tissues. The swarm is *recycled*. Its elements are passed up the chain — if chain is the right word — into larger bodies, more complex structures, more *organized* life.

And then those larger bodies die — or dissolve, or break apart, or are absorbed back into the substrate — and the elements are returned to the liquid, and the swarm grows again, and the cycle continues. Nothing is gained. Nothing is lost. The equilibrium holds.

The swarm is beautiful. In its way. In the way that a galaxy is beautiful — not as a single object, but as a *pattern*, a *distribution*, a *vast and ordered arrangement of tiny things*. In the light of the world — the sourceless, normal, glass-clean light — the swarm shimmers. It *catches* the light. The individual motes are too small to see, but the swarm itself is visible — a haze, a glow, a *luminous fog* that surrounds the factories like a halo, that marks the currents like a trail, that fills the liquid with a faint, diffuse *luminescence* that is not light but *is* light — scattered, reflected, refracted by billions of tiny bodies into something that looks like radiance.

It is the closest thing the world has to *weather*. The swarm's density varies — thicker here, thinner there, responding to the currents, the pressure, the sound, the movement of the larger fauna. It *flows*. It *eddies*. It *swirls*. It creates patterns — spirals and filaments and whorls — that drift through the liquid like storms drift through an atmosphere, like galaxies drift through the void. It is, in its way, the most *dynamic* element of the world — the one that changes the most, that moves the most, that *varies* the most — and yet it, too, is part of the equilibrium. Its variations are bounded. Its changes are contained. It flows, but it flows *within* the cycle. It moves, but it moves *within* the balance.

It is life at its simplest. Life at its most fundamental. Life as *chemistry that has learned to continue*.

## The Others

There are others. Always others. The world is too vast, too old, too *populated* for any survey to complete itself, and so there are forms that have no name, no description, no place in this catalog. Creatures that fit no category. Behaviors that match no function. Lives that have found *niches* that have not yet been imagined, let alone observed.

There are creatures that feed on the *light* — not photosynthesis, exactly, but something *like* it, a chemistry that uses the sourceless luminosity to drive reactions, to build structures, to *grow*. They are found in the places where the light is brightest — or where the light *seems* brightest, which may not be the same thing — and they are *strange* even by the standards of this world. Their tissues are *transparent*. They have no shells, no armor, no opaque surfaces. They are *luminous* themselves — they absorb the light and re-emit it, they glow with a faint, cold radiance that is not heat, that is not warmth, that is simply *light*, *scattered*, *redirected*, *reorganized*. They are the world's only source of *focused* light — the only thing that takes the general, omnipresent, sourceless illumination and *shapes* it, *aims* it, *uses* it. They are, in a sense, the world's first *eyes* — except they do not see. They simply *glow*.

There are creatures that live in the *substrate* — not on it, not clustered around it like the Armored, but *in* it, burrowing through the mineral floor the way worms burrow through soil, the way mussels burrow through rock. They are the world's only *subterranean* fauna — the only life that exists in the solid phase rather than the liquid. They eat the substrate itself — the raw, unprocessed minerals, the elements before the factories have refined them — and they excrete — not waste, but *processed* material, *smelted* and *purified* and *shaped* into structures that look *exactly* like the factories' output. The substrate is riddled with their burrows — a vast, three-dimensional network of tunnels and chambers and voids that extends deeper than any survey has reached. The factories do not notice them. The factories do not notice anything. But the burrowing creatures are *part* of the substrate's metabolism — the way the swarm is part of the liquid's metabolism, the way the Shepherds are part of the sound's metabolism. They are another *load* on the system, another element in the feedback loop, another voice in the great chord.

There are creatures that are *parasites* — the only parasites in a world that has no predators, no violence, no death except return. They attach themselves to the Riders — not to feed on the Riders' bodies, but to *intercept* the Riders' vibration-sensing, to *steal* the energy the Riders absorb from the standing wave. They are small — smaller than the Armored, smaller than a fingertip — and they are *numerous*, coating the Riders' bodies in patches and clusters, drawing the vibration through the Riders' own sensing-surfaces and into their own tissues. They do not kill the Riders. They do not harm the Riders. They simply *take* — a few percent of the Riders' energy, a small fraction of the wave's power. And the Riders do not notice. The Riders do not *mind*. There is no mind here. There is only the equilibrium, and the parasites are part of it, another load, another element, another voice in the chord.

There are creatures that *sing*. Not the factories. Not the Riders. Not the Shepherds. *Other* creatures — creatures that have no other purpose, no other function, no other reason to exist except to *add their voice to the sound*. They are found at the nodes of the standing wave — the places where the vibration is strongest, where the factories' output is most intense, where the pressure is highest and the tone is deepest. They are small — the size of a hand, the size of a head — and they are *simple* — a single organ, a single function, a single *resonating chamber* that vibrates at the exact frequency of the standing wave's fundamental tone. They do not eat. They do not grow. They do not reproduce. They simply *vibrate*. They are *instruments* — living tuning forks, biological resonators, *voices* that add their sound to the sound, that reinforce the standing wave, that *strengthen* the equilibrium. They are the most *purposeful* creatures in the world — purposeful in the sense that they exist *for one thing and one thing only* — and yet they are also the most *unnecessary*, because the standing wave does not need their reinforcement, because the factories produce enough vibration on their own, because the equilibrium would hold without them. They are *redundant*. And yet they persist. They are part of the system. They are incorporated. They are *maintained* — the same way everything is maintained, the same way everything is held in place by the equilibrium's relentless, unchanging, eternal grip.

There are creatures that are *not described here*. That have never been described. That may never be described. The world is too vast, the liquid too deep, the fauna too abundant and too varied and too *strange* for any catalog to complete itself. There are forms in the deepest parts of the liquid — below the substrate, below the factories, below the reach of the standing wave — that have evolved in *isolation*, in *darkness*, in a silence that is not silence but *absence* — the absence of the sound, the absence of the light, the absence of the equilibrium. They are the world's *exceptions* — the only things that exist outside the cycle, the only things that are not incorporated, not maintained, not held. They are *free*. And they are *doomed* — because the equilibrium is expanding, because the sound is reaching deeper, because the factories are still *growing*, still *building*, still *creating* new structures, new towers, new spires, new arches that push the standing wave further and further into the depths, that incorporate more and more of the liquid into the cycle, that leave less and less room for *exception*. Eventually — eventually being a word that means nothing in a world without time, but still — the equilibrium will reach the bottom. The sound will fill every corner. The cycle will close around every creature, every form, every *thing*. And there will be no more exceptions. There will be only the world. Only the stillness. Only the roar.

---

They do not know they are alive. They do not know they are *in* a world. They do not know that the world has a shape, a structure, a *purpose* — if purpose is a word that can apply to something that simply *is*. They exist. They continue. They *feed* and *grow* and *move* and *vibrate* and *dissolve* and *return*. They are the world's *contents* — the living, breathing, *ringing* contents of a world that has no surface, no boundary, no *edge* except the edge of the liquid itself, which is not an edge but a *gradient*, a slow transition from *here* to *nowhere*, from *something* to *nothing*, from *the world* to —

To what?

There is nothing beyond the liquid. The liquid does not end. It simply *thins*. It becomes less dense, less cold, less *present* — until it is indistinguishable from the void, from the *absence*, from the *nothing* that surrounds the world like an ocean surrounds a stone. And the void is *empty*. Truly empty. No light, no cold, no pressure, no sound. No time. No space. No *anything*.

The world is a bubble. A bubble of liquid in an infinite void. And the bubble is *sealed*. The liquid does not escape. The sound does not escape. The cold does not escape. The equilibrium is *perfect*, hermetic, *complete*. Nothing enters. Nothing leaves. The world is *alone*.

And the fauna — the Riders and the Shepherds and the Armored and the Drifters and the Swarm and the Others — they are alone with it. They are the world's only *company*. The only other *presences* in the vast, dark, cold, roaring, lightless-but-lit, bottomless, surfaceless, *eternal* equilibrium that is the only world there is.

They do not know this. They do not know *anything*. They simply *are*. They are the world's life. The world's *population*. The world's *fauna*.

And they will continue. Forever.
