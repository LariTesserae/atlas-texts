To understand what lives in the Hollow of Veins, you must first abandon the idea of a creature that moves through space. Down here, space is a suggestion, a fluid thing, a rumor the stone tells itself. Whatever lives here has adapted to a place where "forward" is a variable and "down" is a temporary condition. 

It does not live *in* the Hollow. It lives *of* the Hollow.

You will not see it at first. You will only see its effects. You noticed the marks at the base of the great primary veins—the cuts, the scrapes, the healed scars where the green-black flesh was opened and then knitted back together. You assumed a tool-user. You imagined something with hands, or at least with intent. You were right about the intent, but wrong about the hands.

What lives here is a weaver. A harvester of tension. It does not walk; it suspends. It does not build; it ties. 

Imagine a creature roughly the size of a large cat, but built on a geometry that is not quite vertebrate. It is radially symmetrical, a central disc of muscle and gristle from which extend not six limbs, not eight, but a variable number—sometimes seven, sometimes twelve—of long, slender, prehensile appendages that look exactly like the Hollow’s own filaments, save for the fact that they are the color of old bone. These limbs are not legs; they are grapples. Each one is tipped with a cluster of microscopic, backward-facing hooks made of keratin so dense it can shear through the stone of the upper world, but here, it is used only to grip the veins. 

It has no eyes. There is no need for eyes when the entire world is a membrane that vibrates with the music of dripping water and the creak of growing wood. Instead, its central disc is rimmed with thousands of hollow, bristle-like cilia—an auditory net so precise it can hear the capillary action of water climbing the inner walls of a vein fifty feet away. It feels the vibration of your footsteps through the filament-mat as a deep, disruptive thunder. It hears the rhythm of your heart as a frantic, arrhythmic drum. 

This is the thing that cuts the veins. But it does not cut them out of malice, nor even out of hunger. It cuts them to *tune* them.

The creature moves through the vertical cathedral with a slow, deliberate grace, rappelling down the great spans on braided ropes of dried sap and its own shed cilia. When it finds a vein that is taut, vibrating at a frequency it does not like, it will grip the green-black flesh with three of its bone-white limbs, brace its central disc against the swell of the plant, and take up its tool. The tool is a flake of fallen bedrock, knapped to an edge finer than a razor, held in the delicate, tweezer-like pincers of its feeding appendages. It scores the vein. It does not hack; it licks the stone with the edge, opening a slit no deeper than a fingernail.

The sap wells up. It is thick, luminous, the color of bruised sunlight. The creature presses its central disc against the wound and drinks. The sap is warm, mineral-rich, slightly sweet. It is the blood of the world, filtered through stone and chlorophyll. 

But the drinking is incidental. The true purpose is the pitch. When the vein is scored, its tension changes. The wet, resonant note it sang when plucked shifts—drops by a semitone, a quarter-tone. The creature listens. It waits for the creak of the wood to settle into a new equilibrium. If the note is wrong, it scores another vein, fifty feet away, adjusting the counter-tension of the web. It is a gardener of acoustics. It is a conductor of a symphony that never resolves. The chiming of the drops, the creak of the wood, the wet notes of the veins—all of it is an instrument, and this bone-white, many-limbed thing is the only thing in the Hollow that knows how to play it.

It is curious about you. It has been watching you since you stepped through the crack, hanging upside down from a primary span just beyond the edge of your vision, utterly still. It has categorized you. You are not a threat; you are too loud, too clumsy, too *warm*. You are a disruption. You are a walking, talking dissonance in its carefully tuned world. 

It will approach you, eventually. It will wait until you are still—until you stand in one of the chambers, staring up at the impossible height, trying to comprehend the scale. Then, it will drop. It will fall silently, steered by the minute adjustments of its trailing limbs, and it will land on your shoulder. 

You will feel it as a weight, light and dry, like a bundle of sticks. You will feel the gentle, prickling grip of its hooked cilia catching in the fabric of your clothing, or the microscopic hooks gently abrading the skin of your neck. It will lean close, and you will feel the heat of its central disc—body-warm, exactly like the walls of the crack, exactly like the veins. It is the same temperature as the Hollow because it is the same *thing* as the Hollow. It is a fruit of the void. It is the immune system and the irritant all at once.

It will listen to your blood. It will press its cilia against the pulse in your neck, feeling the chaotic, biological rhythm of your arteries. It will hear the salt in you, the water, the iron. It will be fascinated. It will reach for its tool—the sharp flake of stone, held in the delicate pincers at the center of its being.

It does not mean to kill you. It means to *score* you. It wants to open a slit in your skin the way it opens a slit in the vein, to see what note you will sing, to hear the pitch of your warmth, to taste the luminous sap of your blood and see how it changes the music of the Hollow. 

Do not be afraid. The cut will be shallow. The creature will drink its fill of the salt and the iron, and it will recoil, hissing through its cilia at the strange, metallic discord of your biology. It will retreat back into the green-black filaments, vanishing so perfectly into the architecture that you will wonder if it was ever there at all.

And the Hollow will continue to grow. The creature will continue to tune it. The drops will continue to fall, chiming against the unyielding stone, and the veins will continue to reach, always reaching, toward the warmth, toward the salt, toward the water, toward the quiet, patient, unfinished song of the deep.
