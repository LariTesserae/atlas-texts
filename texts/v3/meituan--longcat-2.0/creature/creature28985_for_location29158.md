To live in Ren-Kesh is to live inside a lung. It is to exist as a warm, damp, breathing cell within the body of a nameless mountain, wrapped in the dark and steeped in the sweet, alkaline tang of the stone’s own secretion. 

Who lives here? At first, you might point to the people. They are the ones who strike the flint and weave the green cords, the ones who cup their hands to drink the vein-milk and speak in the clicking, percussive cadences of the Hollow. But to separate them from the rest of the life here is to misunderstand the city. In Ren-Kesh, biology and architecture have collapsed into one another. The inhabitants are not merely the humans; the inhabitants are the whole roaring, breeding, clicking, dripping mass of it.

**The Microscopic Multitude**
Before anything else, the Hollow is claimed by the mites. They are the true citizens, the foundational layer of the kingdom. They exist in uncountable billions, a shimmering, silver drift that hangs in the warm updrafts. They coat the vegetation, they mineralize the stone, and they settle into the clothes, the hair, and the very lungs of every larger creature. To breathe in Ren-Kesh is to breathe them in; they are the grit in the air, the baseline hum of the ecosystem. And yet, they are not a plague. They are the foundation. They eat the rock-sweat and the dead skin, and they are eaten in turn, their tiny bodies fueling the first links of the food chain. To be an inhabitant of the Womb-Hollow is to host them, to carry a microscopic city on your shoulders.

**The Clicking Carapaces**
Rising from the microfauna are the beetle-things. They rattle through the narrow streets in their hundreds, a river of polished, stone-hard shells. They are the sanitation engineers and the armored infantry of the Hollow. Their mandibles strip the vegetation, scavenge the offal from the slaughtering terraces, and pick the bones of the burrowers clean. They flow around the feet of the larger inhabitants with a fluid, alien intelligence, clicking and chittering in a language of friction. They do not fear the humans; the humans do not fear them. They simply share the warm stone, a constant, rustling tide moving toward the next warm vent, the next patch of organic decay.

**The Climbers and the Stalkers**
On the walls and ceilings, the reptilian lords hold dominion. The lizards are everywhere—quick, slick drops of life skittering across the pale grey-veined stone, their bellies flashing blue-black in the sourceless light. They are the mite-hunters, snapping at the shimmering air with a speed that defies the gravity of the curved walls. But they are prey, too, for the snake-things. The snakes are the patience of the Hollow made flesh: legless, motionless, the exact color and texture of the worn stone. They hang from the high walkways like dried vines, waiting for the vibration of a passing burrower or a careless lizard. 

Then there are the burrowers—low-slung, furred creatures with blunt, rooting faces and broad, splayed claws. They nest in the dense, liverwort-like mats that clog the low places, lowing to one another in the evening as they dig for grubs and roots. They are the livestock and the threat; their meat feeds the people, but their claws can flay an unwary hunter. They are the bridge between the wild Hollow and the built city, as comfortable in the woven-vegetation nests of the old families as they are in the dark undergrowth.

**The Screaming Sky**
Above them all, the flyers rule the upper reaches. They are not merely birds; they are the fury of the dome. At dawn, they descend—a screaming, chattering river of dark feathers and sharp beaks that feels less like a flock and more like a single, ravenous organism. The beat of their wings is a physical force, a wind that sets the mite-clouds swirling and sends the spiders fleeing into their cracks. They are the regulators of the lower Hollow, the terror of the lizards and the snakes. When they roost in the high places, their ammonia sharpens the thin air, and their endless muttering is the voice of the ceiling itself.

**The Unseen Depths**
But below the floor of the Hollow, in the narrowing dark where the steam rises and the vein-milk pools into warm, vast lakes, there are the others. The inhabitants that are not spoken of. They are the things that move heavy bodies through the thick mineral water, the things that cause the deep, slow groaning of the settling stone. The hunters who have gone down into the mist to read the claw-marks have seen the massive, submerged shapes, the things that brush against the submerged roots of the hanging gardens. They are the titans of the mountain, the deep unconscious of the Womb-Hollow, and the offerings left at the vents—the bundles of green, the cups of milk—are the rent paid to keep them from rising.

**The Thinking Part**
And then, there are the people. They are the most complex expression of the Hollow, but they are not apart from it. They are the ones who build, but their towers are already being reclaimed by the viridian curtains of vegetation; their highest walkways are laced with the webs of palm-sized spiders. They are the ones who hunt, but they move through the streets with an awareness of the beetle-things at their feet and the flyers overhead, timing their steps to the rhythm of the descending dawn. 

Their bodies are shaped by the Hollow. Their skin is filmed with the sweet alkaline residue of the stone; their lungs are seasoned by the mite-clouds; their muscles are curved from climbing the steep, undulating walls. They read the stone with their thumbs, feeling the grain where to strike, just as the stoneworker feels the Hollow itself. They eat the green and drink the vein-milk, and their blood runs warm with the geological heat of the deep earth. 

They are the part of the Womb-Hollow that looks outward, that plans, that remembers—but only just. They are the mountain’s way of knowing itself, a flicker of consciousness in a cathedral of damp, clicking, roaring life. They build their walls, and the green grows over them. They step around the beetles, and the beetles flow around them. They throw their burning brands into the dark to drive back the things that climb up from the deep, and they listen to the breathing of the stone.

To live in Ren-Kesh is to be a cell in a vast, warm, eternal body. It is to know that you are not the master of the mountain, nor its victim, but its living, thinking, breathing flesh.
