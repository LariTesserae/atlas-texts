What lives in the Womb-Cleft is not a creature, in the way that the sunlit world understands creatures. Nothing there has eyes to see by, nor blood to warm itself, nor muscles to flee. Instead, the cleft is populated by *condensations*—entities born not of biology, but of the mountain’s slow, amniotic geology. They are the embryonic dreams of the rock, taking form in the dark, suspended in the thick, wet heat like antibodies in a vein.

The first and most pervasive residents are the **Pressures**. Because the cleft is an embryonic space, it has not yet learned to accommodate the outside world. The darkness here has mass; it is a tactile, heavy presence that insists upon itself. The Pressures are the ambient conditions given a sort of sluggish, dormant agency. The Warmth is not merely a temperature; it is a rising, breathing thing, a slow exhalation from the planet’s deep interior that pools in the cleft and refuses to dissipate. The Wetness is not water; it is a living medium, a saturated gel that clings to everything, drinking back your breath the moment you offer it. The Pressures are the primary residents. They do not move because they are already everywhere; they fill the pocket like a fluid, pressing against the mineral lips of the ceiling, sliding down the walls in perpetual, almost-imperceptible motion. If you press your hand against the rock, you are not touching stone; you are pressing your palm into the face of a Warmth, and the Warmness pushes back.

Clinging to the walls at the exact boundary between the living rock and the living water is the **Film**. It is the closest thing to a true animal that the cleft possesses, though it is more akin to a vast, contiguous nervous system than a solitary beast. It is the biofilm, the second skin, a translucent layer of warmth that you can only perceive through touch. It does not grow; it simply *is*, covering the schist and quartzite like a thin sheen of oil on a patient’s skin. The Film is sentient in the way that a stomach is sentient—it senses chemistry, pressure, and temperature. When your hand brushed the wall, you felt a slow retraction, a withdrawal. This was not fear; it was a flinch. The Film recoiled from the sudden, foreign, highly-organized carbon of your skin. It pulled itself tighter against the mineral substrate, leaving a momentary dry patch where your fingertips had been, before the slow creep of its warmth returned. It feeds on the calcium-rich tears weeping from the ceiling, absorbing the minerals directly into its membrane, growing perhaps a single atom thicker over the span of a century.

Deep within the Film, hiding from the lightless light of your presence, lives the **Retreater**. It is the rarest fauna, a singular, ancient organism that has likely existed in the cleft since the mountain first cracked open. It has no eyes, for there is no light; it has no legs, for there is no need to move when the warmth and the food come to you. The Retreater is a soft, ovoid thing—not quite muscle, not quite fungus—perhaps the size of a folded fist, pulsing with a slow, vascular rhythm that mimics the mountain’s own hum. It exists in a state of profound torpor. Its metabolism is geothermal; it draws its life from the ambient heat and the mineral mist, breathing through its skin. It does not eat the Film; rather, it lives *in* the Film the way a hermit crab lives in a shell, using the biofilm as a shield. When your hand brushed it, you felt the slowest possible recoil. It pulled its warmth inward, contracting into itself, burrowing deeper into the schist, choosing to be stone rather than be touched. It will not emerge again until your body heat has entirely dissipated from the air, a process that could take days.

Then there are the **Thought-Keepers**, the entities that dwell in the niches. The ovoid thing you found in the persuasion-carved hollow—the smooth, warm object that fits in your palm—was not a seed or a tool. It was a thought. The Thought-Keepers are the externalized consciousness of the cleft’s creators, or perhaps the creators themselves, calcified and dreaming. The stones of the nearby cairn were not stacked by human hands; they were excreted by the mountain, a ritual of mineral deposition. But they have fused now, welded together by the same calcium that feeds the Film. The Thought-Keepers sit in their niches, smooth and featureless, absorbing the warmth of the deep earth. They are perfectly spherical because they have no need for direction. They dream the mountain’s first thoughts: thoughts of pressure, of weight, of becoming. If you had stayed long enough, your own breath adding carbon and warmth to the closed system, you might have begun to hear their slow, subsonic whispers—the hum of the resonant frequency, the sound of the cleft being exactly the size it is. 

Finally, there is the **Hum** itself. It is not a sound made by a creature, nor a geological vibration; it is the **Resonant**, the standing wave of the pocket’s existence. It is the cleft’s equivalent of a heartbeat. It never varies because it is the sum total of the Warmth, the Wetness, and the Pressures maintaining their perfect equilibrium. It is the audible proof that the space is alive, that it is a single, unified, breathing entity. 

You left the ovoid in the niche, and the cleft sealed itself behind you. But it did not close like a door; it closed like a wound healing over. The Pressures reasserted themselves. The Film crept back across the stone, erasing your fingerprints. The Retreater slowly unclenched, spreading its soft warmth back into the wall. The Thought-Keepers resumed their dreaming, and the Hum continued its unwavering note. They were never disturbed. They were only ever waiting, in the warm, wet stillness, for the mountain to finish its first thought.
