To live in the Basin of Bells is to live inside a warm, beating heart. The water itself is the blood, thick and dark and carrying the faint mineral tang of iron, and everything that moves within it is an extension of that circulatory rhythm. The builders are gone, but their city has not died; it has been metabolized. The inhabitants of the basin are not merely sheltering in the ruins—they *are* the ruins now, their bodies and habits completing the architecture that the bone-ite secretions began.

To catalog them is to describe a single, breathing organism partitioned into a thousand different forms of hunger, light, and vibration.

---

**The Plasterers (The Coil-Shells)**

In the lowest terraces, where the gray-green chemotrophic film clings to the bone-ite like velvet to bone, the grazers move. They are the Coil-Shells, and they are everywhere. Each one is no larger than a human thumb, encased in a perfect logarithmic spiral of translucent, amber-hued shell. They move on a single, rippling foot, their radulae scraping the bacterial mats from the ancient walls with a sound like distant, rhythmic rain. 

But they do not merely consume the film; they digest it, and what they excrete is a warm, viscous paste that hardens on contact with the mineral-rich water. This paste is chemically identical to the bone-ite of the original structures. As the Coil-Shells graze, they inadvertently repair the ruins, filling the pitted erosion, sealing the cracks, and smoothing the worn edges of the chambers. They are the city’s immune system, its maintenance crew, its slow, blind masons. They do not know they are building. They are only hungry. But in their hunger, they keep the city from crumbling into the silt.

**The Vibration-Searchers (The Pale Shrimp)**

Where the Coil-Shells graze, the Pale Shrimp follow. They are the middle terraces’ primary movers, drifting through the water in loose, shifting clouds. They are entirely devoid of pigment, their bodies like drops of living glass, their internal organs visible as faint, shadowy knots. They have no eyes. They do not need them. 

Instead, their antennae are impossibly long, feathered with cilia so fine they are nearly invisible, turning the shrimp into drifting nets of pure sensation. They feel the displacement of water, the scrape of a radula, the minute flexing of a tubeworm’s crown. When a Coil-Shell grazes, the shrimp feel the specific frequency of its hunger, and they descend. They do not hunt with speed; they hunt with patience, landing softly on the shell and using delicate, jointed appendages to pry the creature from the wall, consuming it slowly while it is still alive. 

But the shrimp are also the city’s nervous system. When the water shifts—when a vent breathes out, or the pressure changes—the shrimp feel it first. They turn together, a sudden, synchronized murmuration of glass, and their collective movement sends a ripple of vibration through the basin that warns everything else.

**The Tube-Masons (The Calcareous Worms)**

In the middle terraces, the original architecture has been entirely overwritten by the Tube-Masons. They are the inheritors, the ones who took the builders’ abandoned chambers and filled them with their own miniature, frantic version of the city. Each worm secretes a calcareous tube, rising from the silt like a tiny, hollow chimney. But they do not build randomly. They build in clusters, their tubes leaning against one another for support, sharing walls, creating arches and buttresses that mirror the macro-architecture of the ruins above them. 

When a worm extends its crown of tentacles into the water to feed, it is a flash of sudden, violent color—a deep, arterial red or a bruised purple—stark against the pale bone-ite and the gray silt. But the moment the shrimp-cloud turns, or the water pressure drops, the crown snaps back into the tube with an audible *click*, and the worm seals the opening with a hardened mucus cap. The chamber falls still again, a city of locked doors, waiting.

**The Silt-Shade (The Unseen)**

There is something in the lowest terraces that has never been seen whole. The old divers called it the Silt-Shade, and they spoke of it in the hushed tones reserved for gods and predators. It is the apex of the basin’s lower reaches, a creature of mass and intent that moves through the ruins like a slow, dark tide. 

It is not a fish. It is not a cephalopod. It is something older, something that has adapted to the crushing warmth and the absolute dark by abandoning the need for speed or sight. It is flat, perhaps, or elongated, its body a loose, undulating sheet of muscle and cartilage that flows over the silt and presses into the chambers. It moves by peristalsis, rippling its body in slow, muscular waves. 

It does not hunt the shrimp or the Coil-Shells. It hunts the warmth. It seeks out the vents, pressing its vast, cold-blooded bulk against the mineral-rich stone to absorb the heat. But in doing so, it acts as a living buttress. When the Silt-Shade presses into a crumbling chamber to warm itself, its body holds the walls up. It braces the architecture. The other inhabitants have learned this. The shrimp do not flee the Shade; they rest on its broad, undulating back, picking parasites from its hide. The Coil-Shells graze the bacterial mats that grow in the Shade’s wake. The Shade is the city’s foundation, its living bedrock, moving so slowly that its passage is measured in inches per year.

**The Luminous Drift (The Fallers)**

Above the terraces, in the dark water where the pressure eases, the Fallers drift. They are the larvae, the spores, the eggs—the next generation suspended in the warm currents. They are the basin’s rain, slowly descending from the upper water where the pale, boneless kelp-fingers reach down. 

The Fallers are drawn to warmth and to the chemical signatures of the bone-ite. They drift until they touch a surface—a wall, a tube, a coil-shell—and then they attach. They metamorphose. A Faller that lands on a warm vent becomes a Coil-Shell. One that lands in a tubeworm chamber becomes a Tube-Mason. One that lands in the open silt, far from the ruins, becomes food for the shrimp. They are the basin’s lottery, its method of distributing life to where it is needed most.

**The Bell-Worm (The Resonance)**

And then, there is the source of the sound. 

Deep in the central spire of the ruins, in a chamber that has been sealed for millennia, lives the Bell-Worm. It is the basin’s heart, its voice, its oldest inhabitant. It is a massive, segmented creature, pale as bone, anchored to the ceiling of the chamber by a thick, muscular foot. Its body hangs down into the dark water, a pendulum of flesh and cartilage. 

It does not feed. It does not move. It simply waits, absorbing the mineral heat from the stone. But as the heat builds in its tissues, the Bell-Worm’s body expands, slowly, imperceptibly. The chamber it inhabits is a perfect dome of bone-ite, shaped by the original builders to exacting, unknowable specifications. As the worm expands, its hide presses against the walls. The friction is microscopic, but the acoustics of the chamber are perfect. 

When the pressure of the upper water shifts, or the vents breathe out a new surge of heat, the Bell-Worm’s expansion reaches a critical point. Its skin catches the ridges of the nautilus-patterns on the walls, and it *rings*. The sound is not a vocalization; it is a structural resonance, the entire chamber acting as a sounding box, the worm’s body acting as the clapper. The note travels through the bone-ite, through the silt, through the water, a low, resonant hum that says: *I am here. The city is here. We are still here.*

---

This is what lives in the Basin of Bells. Not a collection of species, but a single, continuous act of habitation. The Coil-Shells repair what the Shade holds up; the Shade warms what the Bell-Worm rings; the Bell-Worm’s vibration guides the Fallers down; the Fallers become the shrimp and the worms; the worms feed the shrimp; the shrimp feed the Shade. 

The builders are gone, but their city breathes. It breathes in the slow, rhythmic pulse of the Bell-Worm, in the scrape of a thousand tiny radulae, in the synchronized turn of a thousand blind, glass bodies. The basin is not a ruin. It is a body. And it is alive.
