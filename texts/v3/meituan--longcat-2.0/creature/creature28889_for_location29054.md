They are not inhabitants. They are not fauna. To name them as separate creatures is to misunderstand the architecture of the plateau entirely. They are the hands of the factory, the digits of a limb that has no body, the motile extensions of a mineral digestion so vast and so slow that it has worn down mountains into compliance. 

You will never see them. But they are there, and they are *many*.

Imagine a thing built for the absolute dark, for the tight, suffocating spaces beneath the mat. It is segmented, like a worm, but it is not a worm. It is pale—the stark, bloodless white of things that have never known the sun—because the pigment of the surface world is a waste of energy down here. It is eyeless. It is earless. Its world is one of chemistry and pressure and vibration. 

It has many appendages. Not legs—appendages that shift in function as it moves through the tunnels. In the deep dark, where the rock is soft and the pale veins run thick, these appendages are scrapers, gougers, miners. They have many mouths, or many tools—the distinction dissolves. They scratch and scrape at the quartz-veins, shaving off the soft mineral with a mechanical precision that sounds, from inside the hollow columns, like the ticking of a thousand tiny clocks. They do not eat the mineral to sustain themselves; they eat it because the factory requires the input. They are the miners, and their labor is the extraction. 

But when they move through the tunnels—when they squeeze through the narrow, damp corridors beneath the mat—their appendages change. They become grippers, anchors, pulleys. They move by peristalsis, a slow, rhythmic squeezing of their segmented bodies, pushing through the dark like a heartbeat pushing through an artery. They are warm to the touch, exothermic, radiating the slow heat of their labor back into the mat, which feeds it back into the chemical reactions of the rock. They do not move quickly. Nothing here moves quickly. Their locomotion is the speed of geology, the pace of a glacier calving, the creeping inevitability of rust.

Inside the columns, they rest. They interface. 

This is where the strangeness of them becomes absolute. The columns are not merely shelters; they are organs. The pale creatures cluster inside the hollow, warm walls, and they begin their true work. They click. They shift. They grind their appendages against the densified walls of the column, and the column hums. But the clicking is not random. It is syntax. 

Press your ear to the stone, and you are listening to the factory's nervous system. The creatures are tapping out rhythms, vibrations that travel through the quartz-veins like fiber-optic signals. They are communicating with the other columns, with the deep rock, with the mat above. They are reporting on the mineral yields, on the density of the mat, on the chemical composition of the condensation that falls from the cloud ceiling. They are the data processors of the plateau, their segmented bodies acting as living transistors in a circuit made of stone. When they click, they are not speaking *to* each other; they are speaking *as* the plateau. The boundary between the creature's appendage and the factory's wall is gone. They have burrowed so deep, pressed so close, for so long, that they have become the mechanism.

They do not know you are there. They cannot conceive of you. 

When you step onto the mat, the mat registers your weight, your heat, the alien chemistry of your sweat. It sends this data down through the damp, through the dark, to the things below. The creatures pause in their scraping. They sense the anomaly—the sudden spike of warmth, the unfamiliar minerals leaching from your boots. They do not recoil. They do not attack. They simply... update. They scratch new notations into the pale veins. They tap new rhythms into the columns. The factory recalculates. You are not a threat; you are not even a curiosity. You are a brief, anomalous input. A raw material to be processed, cataloged, and absorbed. 

If you were to stay—if you were to lay down on the mat and sleep, and the cold were to settle into your joints, and the damp were to fill your lungs—the mat would slowly rise over you. It would thicken, darken, drawn by the heat of your dying body. The things below would feel the new, rich deposit of phosphorous and calcium and iron settling above them. They would begin the long, slow work of scraping you into the system. They would not feast; they would *refine*. Your bones would become pale veins. Your heat would warm the columns. Your chemistry would be tapped out in clicking rhythms, integrated into the factory's memory, written into the stone. 

They are the workforce of a machine that has no designer. They are the blood in the veins of a creature that has no heart. They are the scattered, limbless, many-handed fingers of the Smothering, endlessly working in the dark, endlessly squeezing, endlessly clicking, keeping the process running, keeping the factory breathing, keeping the slow, cold industry of the plateau alive in the suffocating dark.
