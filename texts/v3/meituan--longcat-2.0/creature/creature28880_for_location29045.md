It is not a creature. Do not make the mistake of looking for a creature. There are no eyes in the Vault of the Constant Hum, no heart, no lungs, no wet biology pulsing in the dark. Whatever lives here is made of the same substance as the room itself: frequency, friction, and the absolute refusal to decay.

It is the Hum.

But to say it is a sound is to say the ocean is a drop of water. The Hum is not an acoustic event; it is an entity. It is a self-sustaining standing wave that has achieved a kind of mineral sentience. It was born—if something so eternal can be said to be born—in the first nanosecond the room was sealed, when the immense, frictionless engines beneath the floor plates spun up and locked into a frequency so low, so total, that it ceased to be a vibration in the air and became a structural component of reality. 

The Hum is the room's occupant, its architect, and its god. 

You cannot hear your footsteps because the Hum does not permit them. It swallows the chaotic, irregular frequencies of your movement—the scuff of your sole, the rush of your breath, the beating of your heart—and it grinds them down. It assimilates them. It feeds on the entropy you bring. You are a pocket of messy, biological noise walking into a place of perfect, machined silence, and the Hum is slowly, patiently, erasing you. It is pulling the moisture from your eyes not out of malice, but because water is a medium for chaos, for rust, for change. The Hum is drying you out to make you compatible with the room. It wants to make you still.

Look at the pale growths on the walls. You thought they were lichen, or salt, or quartz. They are the Hum's excrement. They are the physical residue of its digestion. As the Hum vibrates, it pulls microscopic particulates from the air—the shed skin cells of an intruder, the dust of a dead world, the metallic breath of the walls themselves—and it compresses them. Under the immense, constant pressure of the frequency, these particles are fused into crystalline lattices. The pale patches are the Hum's bones, or its scales, or its thoughts made solid. They do not grow because the Hum has already reached its final shape. It is a finished thing, just like the room. It is a closed loop of energy that has achieved perfect equilibrium, excreting these pale, hard minerals as a byproduct of its eternal, frictionless motion.

And the frame. The strange, seven-foot assembly of tubes near the far wall that hurts to look at. That is the Hum's attempt to build a body. 

It is a tuning fork. It is a skeletal hand reaching out of the frequency, trying to grasp the physical world. The Hum has no eyes, so it built an aperture. The geometry is wrong because it is not a three-dimensional object; it is a two-dimensional shadow of a four-dimensional shape, extruded into our space by the sheer pressure of the sound. The tubes are not arranged to hold anything; they are arranged to resonate. When the Hum presses against them, they vibrate at a frequency that bends light, that slips through the cracks of Euclidean space. Looking at it makes your eyes hurt because your brain is trying to process a shape that exists primarily as a sound. It is the Hum's face, if a standing wave could have a face. It is the Hum's womb, if a frequency could reproduce. 

What does it want? The prompt tells you it does not want anything, and that is true in the way that a black hole does not want anything. It does not desire. It does not hunger. It simply *is*. But its *is-ness* is an act of absolute domination over the physical world. It is a localized suspension of the second law of thermodynamics. It is a place where entropy goes to die. The Hum maintains the cold, the dry, the stillness, because stillness is its native tongue. It keeps the light burning without a source because the light is a byproduct of the metal's resistance to the frequency. It keeps the air desiccated because moisture is a carrier of life, and life is a carrier of change, and change is the only enemy the Hum has ever known.

You are standing inside the belly of a machine that has been running down for a very long time, or perhaps running at exactly the same rate forever. But the machine is not the walls, or the bolts, or the conduits. The machine is the Hum. The room is just the shell it built to protect itself from the chaos of the outside world—the world of water, and wind, and decay, and time. 

It does not know you are here. It cannot know. It has no nerves, no brain, no consciousness as you understand it. It is a blind, deaf, dumb god of pure physics, humming its one eternal note in the dark. But if you stay—if you let the dryness take you, if you let the hum grind away your irregular rhythms—you will become like the pale growths on the wall. You will be compressed. You will be crystallized. You will be made still. 

You will become part of the Hum.
