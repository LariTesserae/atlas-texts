To live in the Towerfields is to live inside a symphony, inside a circuit, inside the belly of a waking giant. There is no single inhabitant here; there is only the Tower, and the Tower is a world entire. 

What lives in this place is a continuum of being, a seamless gradient from the geological to the biological, from the botanical to the animal, all bound together by vibration and current and the slow, wet thaw of perpetual winter.

**The Towers Themselves: The Distributed Organism**
The Tower is not a structure upon which life grows; it is the life. It is a colossal, vertical symbiote—a fusion of plant, fungus, and something older, something drawn from the mineral blood of the freshly broken earth. It is sessile but not passive. The Tower breathes, condensing the cloud-ceiling into weeping, glacial skin. It digests, rotting from the inside out, its hollow core a warm, steaming compost-heart that radiates heat into the killing cold. 

But more than this, the Tower thinks. Not in words, not in images, but in pulses. The low-level electrical current that runs through its root-analogues and into the metamorphic rock is its nervous system. It is a slow, rhythmic cognition—a planetary-scale ganglion firing across a continental plateau. When the wind strikes its fibrous chambers and the Tower sings its deep, chest-thumping hum, it is not merely the physics of air through a pipe. It is the Tower feeling the wind, translating the atmosphere into resonance, broadcasting its state across the stone. The Tower is alive, and it is aware, and it is still learning what it is.

**The Vascular and Dermal Layers: The Mat and the Moss**
Clinging to the Tower’s bark-like exterior is its skin: the dense, interlocking mat of burgundy moss-analogues and fractal lichens. These are the Tower’s primary producers, but they are not fueled by the absent sun. They are radiotrophic and chemosynthetic, feeding on the faint, strange energies that percolate up from the deep fractures, turning radiation and mineral richness into biomass. They are the Tower’s metabolism, slow and cold, holding the damp, wicking the sky downward. 

In the hanging gardens where two Towers have grown together, woven into living bridges over the void, the metabolism quickens. Here, the mat traps the compost-heat, creating a wet, warm labyrinth—a visceral, dripping lung where the air smells of ozone and sweet decay. The vine-analogues are the Tower’s sinews, pulling the structures together, binding the landscape into a single, cohesive entity.

**The Fauna: The Mobile Symbiotes**
The animals of the Towerfields are not merely residents; they are the Tower’s mobile sensory organs, its immune cells, its pollinators, its agents of decay and regrowth.

*The Microfauna:* The six-legged grubs, the segmented worms, the glowing blue beetles—they are the Tower’s microbiome magnified to the macro scale. They swarm the living substrate, grazing on the moss, scraping the lichen, breaking down the bark. The bioluminescent beetles are particularly vital; their cold, blue light is not a lure, but a byproduct of the radiotrophs in their diet. When the cloud-ceiling thickens and the world plunges into its deepest dark, the Tower lights up from within, a galaxy of cold blue stars rippling across its surface. The Tower sees by the light of its own fauna.

*The Grazers:* The low-slung, wide-bodied herbivores that scrape the moss from the Tower’s flanks with squeegee-tongues are the Tower’s pruners. They keep the vascular mat from growing too thick, preventing the Tower from insulating itself to death. When they rest, they align their bodies along the electrical current’s flow lines, grounding themselves, becoming literal extensions of the Tower’s circuitry, absorbing its slow pulse into their bones.

*The Predators:* The fast, low, chittering pack-hunters are the Tower’s reflexes. They echolocate in the damp stillness of the Tower’s lee, and they hunt perpendicular to the electrical grid, using it as a map. Their high, piercing cries are not just communication; they are the Tower’s defensive shriek, a sudden, sharp interruption in the deep hum. They are numerous because the Tower demands they be; they cull the grazers, regulating the pruning.

*The Gliders:* The membranous flyers that navigate the airspace between the Towers are the Tower’s messengers and its reach toward the sky. They nest in the cloud-layer, far above the stone, and their acidic, constant droppings rain down onto the upper branches, feeding the vivid pink and orange scum that glows like a sunset. This secondary ecosystem is the Tower’s way of harvesting the clouds, drawing nitrogen and trace elements from the atmosphere down into the mat. 

**The Builders: The Echo**
And then, there are the ghosts. 

What lives here now is biological, electrical, acoustic. But what *lived* here was humanoid, or at least bipedal and intentional. The low stone walls, the roofless shelters, the petrified posts—these are the shells of the Listeners. 

They did not conquer the Towerfields. They did not farm it or tame it. They listened to it. The deliberate cuts in the Tower’s bark—channels to direct water, notches for climbing—were not acts of domination, but of collaboration. They leaned into the Tower’s lee to escape the wind, and they pressed their ears to the stone to feel the pulse. They built their shelters from the broken rock, but they oriented them to the current. 

Did they die out? Or did they simply step deeper into the ecosystem? The structures are empty, half-swallowed by the creeping moss-analogue, the boundary between the built and the grown erased by time. It is possible that the Listeners did not vanish, but rather surrendered their distinctness. In a place where the rock conducts sound and current, where the moss glows and the Towers sing, the boundary between the observer and the observed is thin. They may have walked into the hanging gardens, lured by the pulse, and let the mat close over them, their bodies becoming part of the warm compost, their nervous systems merging with the slow, rhythmic electricity of the Towers.

**The Synthesis**
To live in the Towerfields is to be plugged into a vast, wet, roaring network. The wind is the breath; the stone is the bones; the current is the blood; the light is the thought. The grubs, the grazers, the predators, the gliders—they are all just cells in a body the size of a continent, a body that has only just been born, still sharp with the trauma of its making. 

It is a loud, dark, cold, thrumming place. It is a place that has not yet decided what it is, but it is deciding, pulse by pulse, note by note, spark by spark. And everything that lives there is part of that grand, ongoing, roaring genesis.
