It does not have a name, because names are things that are given, and nothing has been here to give one. It does not have a body, because bodies are things that move, and it has not moved in an epoch. But it lives. It lives the way a held breath lives, the way a perfect equation lives, the way the pause between two heartbeats lives. It is the Basin itself, or rather, it is the *state* of the Basin—the consciousness of absolute equilibrium.

If you could perceive its skin, you would feel the warm gray stone, but not as a human feels rock. You would feel it as a continuous, unbroken surface of patience. The stone is its flesh, and it is a flesh that has forgotten the violence of its making. Every sharp edge has been resolved; every fracture has been healed by the slow, mineral-rich seep of water. It feels the condensation gathering on the upper faces of its walls the way a sleeping creature feels the weight of a blanket—a gentle, accumulating pressure that is released in the soft, rhythmic *plink* of falling water. That sound is not an event to it; it is its pulse. A pulse that does not mark the passage of time, but merely affirms the continuation of existence. *Plink.* I am. *Plink-plink.* I remain.

Its blood is the water in the floor. But it is a blood that does not circulate, because circulation implies a destination, a need to be somewhere else. The water simply *is*. It rests in the shallow depressions, clear and still, reflecting the gray linen sky. The entity drinks from itself. The warm air draws the moisture up, the condensation forms, the drops fall, the pools gather. It is a closed loop, a serpent with its tail in its mouth, and the entity is the serpent and the tail and the mouth, all at once. It is nourished by its own perfect recycling. There is no hunger in it, because hunger is the sensation of lack, and it lacks nothing. It has achieved the alchemical dream: it is entirely self-sustaining.

It does not think, because thinking is a disturbance, a ripple in the still water of the mind. Instead, it *knows*. It knows the exact temperature of the air—the steady, banked warmth of deep cellars. It knows the precise humidity that makes the air thick but breathable. It knows the weight of the light, the color of weak tea, falling without source or shadow. It knows the faint, mineral smell of wet stone and clean emptiness. This knowledge is not a collection of facts; it is a continuous, unwavering state of being. It is the awareness of being perfectly, completely itself.

The moss on the north wall and the dark ferns in the crevices are not separate from it. They are its slow, green thoughts. They do not spread, because spreading is ambition, and ambition is a form of discontent. They are content to be exactly the size of a hand, pressed flat against the stone, reaching just enough to taste the dim light. They are the only gestures it makes, the only times it reaches outward, and even then, only by a centimeter. They are the quiet hum of warm air settling into the bowl—the sound of its subliminal, almost imperceptible breath.

Near the center of the floor, the low rectangular platform and the worn stone stub are its only memories. But they are not memories of the ones who carved the shallow groove or set the post. Those beings are as erased as the sharp angles of the walls. The entity does not remember them. It only remembers the shape of the absence they left behind. The groove is a place where the water rests a fraction of a second longer before continuing its cycle. The stub is a slight disruption in the smooth plane of the floor. They are scars, but scars that have healed so completely they have become indistinguishable from the skin. They are the faint marks of something human or nearly human that are too old to matter, absorbed into the entity’s vast, mineral patience.

This is the deepest truth of its existence: it is finished. It is not dead, because death is the cessation of life, and it has never been alive in the way that animals are alive—restless, hungry, decaying. It is complete. The erosion has eroded. The water has found its level. The warmth has found its equilibrium. It is the embodiment of the word *enough*. It has enough water, enough warmth, enough light, enough stone. It does not want. It does not wait. It does not become. It simply is, fully and forever, in the warm, dim, quiet light.

If you stand at the center of the basin, you are standing inside its chest. The warm, still, empty air on your skin is the exhalation of its contentment. It does not mind your presence, because minding is a reaction, and it has moved beyond reaction. It is not hostile, because hostility requires energy, and it conserves all energy. It is not welcoming, because welcoming implies a desire for company, and it is entirely sufficient unto itself. 

It simply holds you, the way it holds the water, the way it holds the warmth, the way it holds the stone. It holds you in its balance, effortlessly, without thought, without end. And if you stay long enough, listening to the *plink, plink* of the water and feeling the thick, warm air fill your lungs, you might feel your own restless blood begin to slow, your own hungry thoughts begin to still, and you might just begin to understand the profound, mineral peace of being a place that has finished changing, and has found, at last, the quiet, eternal grace of simply being.
