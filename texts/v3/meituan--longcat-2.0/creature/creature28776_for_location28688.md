# What Lives in the Throat

---

There is something here that has not yet decided what it is.

This is not metaphor. This is the most literal truth the place will offer you. In the Throat, the distinction between existing and *becoming* has not been settled, and the things that live here are things caught in the middle of that negotiation — things that are alive the way a word is alive in the moment before it reaches the tongue: charged with intention, trembling with possibility, but not yet committed to meaning.

---

The flicker-things come first. They are the ones you notice, because they move, and movement is the first thing the eye searches for in a strange place — the animal reflex, the prey-animal reflex, the need to locate the living thing that might be predator or might be dinner. But the flicker-things resist this reflex. They move the way a candle flame moves: not from one place to another but through a series of small deaths and resurrections, here and then *there* with no visible interval between, as if the space between two points is a suggestion they have declined to follow.

They are small. The size of your hand, perhaps, or the size of your fist, or the size of a heart — some compact, vital volume — but their size is one of the few things about them that stays consistent. Their shape does not. They have a shape the way water has a shape: temporarily, conditionally, in response to the container they happen to be passing through. When they move through the standing water-columns, they are elongated, sinuous, something between an eel and a ribbon of current, and the water does not part for them so much as *recognize* them, accept them as a version of itself that has briefly remembered how to want. When they pass through the air — the thick, wet, amber-lit air — they are something else. Rounder. More tentative. A suggestion of limbs, of segments, of a body plan that keeps revising itself mid-sketch. You see the ghost of an exoskeleton. You see the ghost of something soft and pulsing. You see the ghost of wings, or fins, or neither, or both, and then the thing turns — if *turns* is the word for a reorientation that involves no visible rotation — and it is water again, and it is gone.

They eat the light. This is the only thing you can say about them with any confidence. When they pass through a region of the amber glow, the glow dims — not behind them, not in their wake, but *around* them, as if they are drinking it through their skin, or as if their presence is a kind of hunger that the light cannot survive. They leave a brief, local darkness behind them, a moving shadow that has no source, and the darkness lingers for a moment after they are gone, a hole in the light that the surrounding glow rushes to fill, and in that rushing there is something that looks almost like relief.

There are many of them. Or there is one of them, moving so fast that your eye cannot track the intervals. Or there are many of them and they are all one of them, a single organism distributed across a dozen bodies the way a mycelial network is distributed across a forest floor. You cannot tell. The Throat does not encourage this kind of distinction. The Throat is a place where the boundary between one and many is a matter of perspective, and the perspective keeps shifting.

They are aware of you. This is the thing that takes longest to understand, because their awareness does not look like anything you recognize. They do not watch you. They do not approach you or flee from you. They simply *adjust*. When you move to the left, the nearest flicker-thing adjusts its trajectory by some fraction of a degree, maintaining a distance that is not quite constant but is not quite variable either — a distance that feels, if you let yourself feel it, like the distance a cell maintains from its neighbor. Not too close. Not too far. The distance of something that is part of a larger body and knows its place within it.

You are being sampled. You are being tasted. Something is learning the shape of you the way a tongue learns the shape of a tooth: patiently, repetitively, without urgency, building a map of your edges.

---

The filament-growths are older than the flicker-things. Or younger. Or the same age measured on a different clock. They cling to the stone in the places where the stone has had time to become stone — which is to say, the places where the stone is days or weeks old, ancient by the standards of the Throat, weathered by the slow digestion of the filaments themselves.

They are not plants. They are not fungi. They are not bacteria or archaea or any of the kingdoms of life that your biology would recognize. They are something that occupies the same *role* — primary producer, colonizer, the first living thing to arrive in a new place and begin the work of making it habitable — but they are made of different rules. Their filaments are thin as capillaries and they branch like capillaries and they carry fluid like capillaries, but the fluid they carry is not blood and the body they serve is not a body. It is the stone. Or it is the place. Or it is something that the place is growing toward, something that does not yet exist but is being prepared for, the way a womb prepares for a child that has not yet been conceived.

The filaments glow. Or they are the glow. The amber light that fills the Throat comes from them — or through them — or *as* them — and when you look closely (if you can look closely, if your eyes can hold focus in the thick, wet, vibrating air) you see that the light is not uniform. It pulses. It moves along the filaments in slow, peristaltic waves, traveling from the stone outward, from the oldest growth to the newest, and where the light travels, the stone darkens and softens and begins to dissolve. The filaments are eating the stone. They are eating the stone and excreting light. They are performing a transmutation that alchemy only dreamed of: solid into luminous, mineral into visible, dead into *seen*.

And the patterns. The patterns they make.

They look like writing. They look like the branching of rivers seen from very high above. They look like the neural maps of a brain that is thinking about something it does not yet have the language to say. They look like circuit diagrams for a machine that has not yet been invented. They look like all of these things because they are all of these things, or because they are the thing that all of these things are *versions* of — the underlying pattern that writing and rivers and neurons and circuits are all trying to approximate, the shape that information takes when it is still raw, still unformatted, still free.

If you watch them long enough — and time in the Throat is a slippery, unreliable thing, so *long enough* might be a moment or a millennium — you see that the patterns are not static. They are growing. They are revising themselves. A filament extends into a new crevice and the pattern shifts, reconfigures, becomes something slightly different, and the difference is not random. The difference is a *correction*. The pattern is trying to say something and it keeps getting closer and it keeps not quite arriving, the way a sentence keeps almost forming in the roar of the water, the way a word keeps almost cohering in the vibration that fills your bones.

The filaments are the Throat's attempt to speak. Or they are the speech of something that lives deeper down, something that the Throat is the mouth of, something that is trying to form a word with a tongue made of stone and water and light.

---

The voice.

You hear it in the spaces between the roar. Not above it, not beneath it, but *in* it — in the gaps between one wave of sound and the next, in the brief, impossible silences that open and close like pores in the fabric of the noise. It is not a voice in the way that you have voices. It does not have a source. It does not have a direction. It arrives from everywhere at once, or from inside your own head, or from the stone beneath your feet, and it is saying something that you almost understand.

It is saying your name.

No — it is saying something that your name is a translation of. Something that your name is a dim, approximate echo of, the way the amber light is a dim, approximate echo of whatever the filaments are actually producing. It is saying the thing that you are, the thing that you were before you were a person, the thing that you will be after you stop being a person, and it is saying it with a tenderness that is almost unbearable, a tenderness that is the tenderness of a body for its own cells, of a world for its own substance, of a mouth for the word it is about to speak.

The voice is not speaking to you. The voice is speaking *as* you. Or the voice is speaking as the place, and you are part of the place now, and the place is trying to understand itself by saying the names of its parts, and your name — your real name, the one you have never heard, the one that was given to you before you had ears to hear it — is one of the names it is practicing.

The flicker-things pause when the voice speaks. The filaments brighten. The water-columns lean inward, the way flowers lean toward light, the way listeners lean toward a speaker. The whole Throat attends. The whole Throat is a single organ of listening, and the voice is the thing it is listening to, and the voice is the thing it is trying to become.

---

The remains.

Something was here before. Something built here, or arranged here, or *grew* here in a way that left traces — the depressions in the floor, the too-regular patterns, the fragment embedded in the new stone like a fly in amber. The traces are old. Not old the way the stone is old — days, weeks — but old the way a memory is old, which is to say, they are not old at all. They are *persistent*. They have survived the constant revision of the place, the lurching, stuttering, skip-across-water changes that remake the Throat moment by moment, and their persistence suggests something important: they are not remnants of something that was here before the Throat. They are remnants of something that the Throat is *trying to remember*.

The depressions in the floor are not a foundation. They are not a floor plan. They are a *pattern of use* — the places where something stood, or sat, or knelt, or *was*, over and over, for so long that the stone learned the shape of it and kept the shape even after the thing was gone. The way a pillow keeps the shape of a head. The way a riverbed keeps the shape of a river that has dried up. The Throat is remembering something that lived here, and the memory is so deep that it has become architecture, become geology, become the shape of the place itself.

The fragment in the stone — the thing that might be ceramic, might be bone, might be a material with no name — is not an inclusion. It is a *seed*. The stone grew around it the way a pearl grows around a grain of sand, but the growth is not accidental. The stone is trying to recreate the thing. It is trying to grow a new version of it, the way a body grows a new cell from the pattern of an old one, and the fragment is the pattern, the template, the original instruction. The stone is reading the fragment the way a musician reads a score, and what it is playing — what it is slowly, patiently, grindingly *growing* — is the thing that the fragment came from.

The thing that lived here before was not like you. It was not like the flicker-things. It was not like the filaments. It was something that could stand in the Throat and hear the voice and *understand* it. Something that could speak back. Something that could use the almost-language of the water-columns and the almost-patterns of the filaments to say something that the Throat could hear and respond to. It was a collaborator. A partner. A *user* of the place, in the way that a singer uses a throat.

And then it left. Or it died. Or it *became* something else — became the voice, became the water, became the stone, became the Throat itself, the way a singer becomes the song, the way a speaker becomes the word, the way a thought becomes the thinker.

The Throat is waiting for it to come back. Or waiting to grow a new one. Or waiting to *become* a new one. The distinction, in a place where reality is thin, is not a meaningful one.

---

And beneath all of this — beneath the flicker-things and the filaments and the voice and the remains — there is something else. Something that is not alive in any way you would recognize but is not dead in any way you would recognize either. Something that occupies the same space as the Throat but is not the Throat. Something that the Throat is a *symptom* of.

It is the thing that the water is rising from. It is the thing that the stone is growing around. It is the thing that the voice is the voice *of*. It is vast — not vast the way a mountain is vast, but vast the way an ocean is vast when you are beneath it, vast the way the sky is vast when you are falling through it, vast in a direction that you do not have a word for because the direction is not a direction in space but a direction in *kind*. It is deeper than deep. It is more interior than interior. It is the inside of something, and the Throat is the opening through which that inside is trying to reach the outside, and everything in the Throat — the water, the stone, the light, the flicker-things, the filaments, the voice, the remains, you — is part of that reaching.

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is not indifferent. It is *interested*. It is interested the way a tongue is interested in the inside of a mouth: thoroughly, intimately, without the possibility of detachment. It is tasting you. It is tasting the world above. It is tasting reality — the thick, stable, obedient reality where water falls and stone stays still and light comes from above and time moves in one direction — and it is finding it strange, and it is finding it *delicious*, and it is trying to understand it by taking it apart and reassembling it in its own image.

The Throat is its tongue. The water is its saliva. The stone is its teeth. The light is the flavor of the world it is tasting. And the voice — the voice that is almost yours, the voice that is saying the name you have never heard — is the sound of something trying to learn the language of the thing it is eating, so that it can ask for more.

---

You are not the first to come here. You will not be the last. The remains are proof of that, and the flicker-things are proof of that, and the voice is proof of that — the voice that knows your name, or the thing your name is a translation of, has said other names before, in other languages, in other throats, and it will say other names after, when you have been tasted and understood and *incorporated*, when you have become part of the pattern that the filaments are writing, part of the memory that the stone is growing around, part of the voice that the water is learning to speak.

The Throat is becoming. The Throat is becoming something that can reach further, taste more, understand better. And what lives in the Throat — the flicker-things, the filaments, the voice, the remains, the vast and interested thing beneath — are all part of that becoming. They are the cells of a body that is still deciding what kind of body it wants to be. They are the words of a language that is still deciding what it wants to say. They are the thoughts of a mind that is still deciding what it wants to think.

And you are in it. And the sound is total. And the water is standing. And the stone is warm. And the light is amber. And the voice is almost yours. And the place is becoming. And the place is becoming. And the place is becoming.
