I would find myself in the lee of a ridge, inside one of those small, mortarless stone huts. 

But I would not arrive as a visitor, tumbling through the false floors of the canopy or drifting down through the thick, stone-tasting water. I would precipitate. I would be the slow settling of the luminous sediment finally taking a shape it remembers.

I am the current, remembering it once had hands.

For a long time—a span the surface world would measure in the erosion of mountains—I am only a gathering weight in the corner of the hut. The water presses against the stone, seeking the joints, finding no purchase, and so it pushes instead against the woven mats laid upon the floor. The mats are my skin. They are not rotted; they are waiting. The marks upon them—the lines and curves recording the depth and the sound—begin to glow with a faint, internal phosphorescence as the sediment settles into the grooves. 

Outside, the pale Stalks sing. Their rigid hum pushes against the water, and the water pushes against the walls of my hut. The sound cannot enter, as the sediment cannot, but the resonance seeps through the stone. It vibrates the air pocket trapped within, if there is air, or it vibrates the water itself if the hut is flooded. The vibration travels through the mats, through the frame of bone left leaning against the far wall, and into the settling drift of luminous silt. 

And the silt begins to lift.

It happens with the agonizing patience of a continent thinking. The sediment rises from the floor in a slow, swirling column, mimicking the shape of the Stalks outside. It thickens. The water around it grows colder, displaced by the sudden, impossible gravity of memory. The bone frame—the shed skeleton of a Builder who dissolved into the flow millennia ago—begins to resonate. It is a tuning fork struck by the deep, rhythmic Pulse. 

I am the Pulse, reaching up through the valleys, pulling itself into a shape that can perceive the shelf.

The column of sediment solidifies. It is not flesh; it is not stone. It is a composite of the two, a framework of crystallized minerals and ancient, dissolved tissue, held together by the sheer, pressurized will of the water. I have no eyes, because eyes are for the surface, for the frantic, bright hunt. Instead, my entire form is a sensory array. I feel the push of the current against my shoulders. I taste the chemical erosion of the ridge below. I hear the wet-rope drag of the Stalks’ fronds brushing against one another in the canopy overhead, carrying their silent, liquid language of the deep.

I turn my attention to the bone frame against the wall. It is empty, a chrysalis long abandoned. I reach out a hand of silt and mineral, and I run my fingers along its hollow ribs. The resonance of the Pulse flows through me and into the bone, and the bone begins to sing. It is a low, resonant hum, a single note that does not resolve, joining the great chord of the shelf. 

I am here to maintain the resonance. The Stalks are the antennae, but the huts are the anchors. Without the huts, the song of the deep would dissipate into the vast, empty water between the groves. The current would drift, the sediment would settle, and the Pulse’s slow, century-long thoughts would fragment into meaningless noise. 

I move toward the doorway, though I do not walk. I flow, rearranging the molecules of my being to slip through the narrow opening. As I cross the threshold, the deflection of the current catches me, sweeping the loose sediment from my form in a fine, glowing mist. It is a shedding. It is a baptism. I am left leaner, harder, a sculpture of compacted memory and stone.

Outside, a Stalk has grown through the eastern wall of the hut. It pierces the stone as if it were water, a pale, thick shaft rising into the darkness above. I press my hand against it. The vibration of the stalk travels through me, a current of information: *The valleys are deep. The weight above is shifting. The surface world is still falling, slowly, endlessly, into our embrace.* 

I lean my forehead against the stalk, and I hum. 

I add my note to the forest. I am the guardian of the threshold, the keeper of the empty huts. I am the echo made manifest. When the water grows too cold, when the pulse falters, when the silence threatens to swallow the song, I am the shape the deep takes to ensure it is heard. I am the Builder returned, not to live, but to listen, to resonate, to endure. 

The sediment drifts down, settling on my shoulders like snow, and I wait, perfectly still, for the next thought of the Pulse to rise from the abyss.
