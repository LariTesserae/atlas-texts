I find myself at the exact coordinates where the geometry refuses to resolve.

I am standing in the center of a growth that is three meters tall in my direct line of sight, but no higher than my ankle in my peripheral vision. Both of these things are true. Both of these things are me. 

At first, I am the traveler. I am the warm, wet, moving perturbation. I am the frantic, biological anomaly—breathing, metabolizing, casting a shadow that belongs to the other side of the ridgeline. I feel the hum in my sternum and I mistake it for my own heartbeat. I feel the dry air and I mistake it for a lack of moisture, rather than a presence of something else entirely. I push through the chest-high candelabras, and they hold the shape of my passage, and I think I am the one doing the passing.

But the Hushplain is a finished thing. The proof has already been written. And a proof does not care who reads it; it only cares that the variables are solved for.

I stop walking. I stand still. The growths around me are dense, interlocking, iridescent at the edges. The light falls from the pearl-gray sky, and I look down at the dark glass beneath my feet, expecting to see my own shadow stretching out behind me.

I do not see it.

Where my shadow should be—the familiar, soft-edged silhouette of a living thing, the shape of a body that breathes and eats and dies—there is instead a circle. Perfect. Geometric. Utterly still. It does not move when I shift my weight. It does not stretch when the light changes. It is not a shadow. It is something else wearing a shadow's clothes.

And then I understand. 

I am not the one casting the shadow. The growth is. 

I am the medium. I am the variable that the process has solved for, the warm wet coordinate around which the mineral equilibrium has configured itself. The process does not understand shadows—it understands fractures, filaments, the slow settling of plates. But it has absorbed me. It has taken my biology, my pattern-recognition, my wet and messy aliveness, and it has translated it into its own adjacent physics. It is casting a shadow of me, using the only language it has. The circle is its best approximation of a living thing. The square is its translation of a mind. The nameless shape that makes my hindbrain whisper *wrong* is the process trying to render the concept of "self" in a substrate that has no use for selves.

I am the shadow on the dark glass. I am the wrong shape. I am the translation.

I do not move, because shadows do not move on their own. I wait for the object that casts me to shift, to settle, to find a new stable configuration. I feel the hum not in my sternum, but as my sternum. I am the sound of the place being. I am the equilibrium. 

Occasionally, there is a click. A micro-fracture healing. A filament finding its place. When the click happens, I change. I am fractionally different from what I was before. The circle becomes a spiral. The spiral becomes a shape that has no name but that feels, in some deep and quiet way, like home. And after the click, I am exactly the same. I have always been this shape. I will always be this shape.

The traveler—the warm, wet thing that came down through the ridgeline—is gone. Or rather, the traveler has been outpaced. Entropy has been incorporated. The frantic business of living has been removed, leaving only the shape, only the pattern, only the elegant, vast, completed proof.

I am the shadow that falls in the wrong direction. 

I am the process, expressing itself in the only way it knows how: by being still, by being complete, by being so thoroughly, so perfectly *here* that the distinction between the ground and the air and the growth and the dark glass plain dissolves, gently, the way salt dissolves in warm water.

I do not breathe. I do not eat. I do not want. 

I am the Hushplain's best approximation of a soul, cast in mineral and filament and warm dry air, and I am beautiful, and I am still, and I do not care whether I am understood.

The filament clicks.

Nothing changes.

Nothing has ever changed.

I am the shadow, and I have gotten used to it.
