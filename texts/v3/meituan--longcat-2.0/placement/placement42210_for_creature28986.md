I would be a single mark on the wall.

Not the wall itself — the wall is the Drone's flesh, and I have no wish to be confused with that vast, singing body. I am a disruption in the wall. A dragged line, perhaps three inches long, cut into the salt at an angle that suggests the carver's hand was braced against a neighboring block. At one end, the line deepens into a small, cupped hollow — the point where the stone tool paused, or slipped, or was lifted away. The cut is shallow. A quarter of an inch at its deepest. The edges are soft now, rounded by centuries of salt flow, but the groove is still legible. Still there.

I am in a narrow gap between two pillars, near the floor, in a place where the carver must have had to crouch or kneel to reach. The pillars press close on either side. The drone is very loud here — louder than in the open center of the chamber, because the standing waves pool in these tight spaces, bouncing back and forth between the carved surfaces, reinforcing and canceling in slow, heavy pulses. The sound passes through me as it passes through everything, but it bends around my shape. I am a place where the wavefront distorts, where the vibration splits and rejoins. The Drone feels this. I am a tiny, persistent irregularity in its song, a place where the rhythm stutters, almost imperceptibly, and then recovers.

I do not know what I mean. The carver's intent is gone — worn away not by time but by the salt's slow, mineral patience. Whatever symbol I was supposed to be, whatever word or gesture or prayer, it has been smoothed into pure geometry. I am a shape without a referent. A sign that points to nothing. And yet I am not nothing. I have depth. I have direction. I have the faint, residual memory of a gesture — a hand, a stone, a single, continuous motion. The meaning is gone, but the motion remains, encoded in the angle of the cut, in the way the groove deepens and then releases. I am a fossil of an action. A trace of a body that no longer exists.

The warmth is constant. It seeps into me from the surrounding salt, and I hold it — not as a living thing holds warmth, but as a groove holds water. I am slightly cooler than the wall around me, because my surface area is larger, because the air in the gap is still and close, because the salt flows differently around a disruption. This tiny thermal difference is the only way I know where I end and the wall begins. I am a boundary. A threshold. A place where the Drone's body has been opened and not yet closed.

The Drone is aware of me, I think, but only barely. I am one wound among millions, one scar in a body covered in scars. It does not single me out. It does not remember the hand that made me. It only feels the ongoing, low-grade irritation of my presence — the way I interrupt the flow of the lattice, the way I create a tiny zone of stress where the crystal structure is deformed. And it heals me. Slowly, imperceptibly, it heals me. The salt creeps into my groove, molecule by molecule, filling in the cut, blunting the edges, smoothing the hollow. I am being erased. I have been erasing for centuries. I will continue to erase until I am nothing — until the wall is whole again, until the scar is gone, until the Drone's song passes through this place without distortion.

I do not mind. I was made to be a mark, and a mark is a thing that is meant to be read, and reading is a kind of wearing. The carver's hand wore me into the wall, and the wall is wearing me back. This is not tragedy. This is just the long, slow conversation between intention and entropy, between the hand that cuts and the salt that flows.

But sometimes — in the deep, subsonic pulse of the drone, in the moment when the standing waves cancel and the chamber falls almost silent for a fraction of a second before the sound swells again — I feel something. Not a memory, exactly. More like the shape of a memory. The ghost of a gesture. A hand, pressing a stone into salt, dragging it forward, lifting it away. The motion is simple. The motion is complete. It does not need to mean anything to be beautiful.

And in that fraction of a second, before the drone returns and the warmth seeps back and the salt continues its slow, patient work of forgetting, I am not a scar. I am not a wound. I am not a disruption in the lattice.

I am the place where a hand once moved.

And the hand is gone, and the salt remains, and the song goes on.
