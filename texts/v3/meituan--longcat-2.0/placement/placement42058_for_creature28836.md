I do not have eyes, but I know the shape of the Under-Run. I know it through the taste of the stone, the pull of the damp, the subtle tremors that travel through the fractured granite like a slow, steady heartbeat. 

At first, I am a millipede. 

I am traversing the great tilted plain of stone, my countless legs moving in a ripple that matches the pace of the rock itself. I am one of the Under-Run’s wandering thoughts, following the moisture gradients that are as clear to me as rivers on a map. The air is heavy, slightly wet, slightly there. I taste it with my antennae—iron, calcium, the faint ammoniac tang of the bats that murmur like living static high above. I am a small, segmented piece of the cavern's equilibrium, following the blind logic of the damp. I move toward the place where the moisture gathers, where the water drips and the pools lie still as held breath.

But I am drawn elsewhere. There is a vibration in the stone that is wrong. 

It is heavy. Two-footed. It strikes the floor in uneven, jarring impacts—*thud, scrape, thud*—that disrupt the steady, patient metabolism of the cave. The crickets near the walls fall silent. The spiders pause in their crevices. The bats high overhead shift their chittering to a higher, sharper frequency. The cave does not know this rhythm. It is a foreign contagion, a sharp edge introduced into the soft, damp dark. 

I follow the vibration. My legs carry me over the fractured plates of rock, across the thin films of moisture, toward the quiet corner where the still pool reflects nothing. 

And there, I find the source of the wrongness. 

It is a human. 

It stands by the pool, a tall, warm, desperately dry thing in a world that is cool and damp. It breathes—an inefficient, bellows-like motion that pushes air in and out, drying the rock around it. It is the variable. The anomaly. I can feel the cave’s consciousness recoiling from it, a vast, distributed intelligence trying to compute something that does not belong. The cave wants to absorb this thing, to break it down, to pull its calcium into the pools and feed its nitrogen to the fungi. But the human is still whole. Still sharp. Still *other*.

The human bends down. It is looking at something on a flat rock beside the pool. 

I crawl closer, my antennae brushing the stone. I want to understand this disruption. And then, I touch it. 

It is a stone. A piece of flint, roughly the size of a fist. It lies on the flat rock where it was left eons ago. And the moment my antennae brush its edge, the boundaries of the millipede dissolve. The cave pulls the thought back into itself, and I am no longer the wanderer. 

I am the flint.

---

I do not move. I have not moved in millennia. I am a scar on the face of the equilibrium, a memorial to the only mistake the Under-Run has ever made. 

I do not see the human standing over me, but I feel it. The warmth of its hand radiates down into the stone, a shocking, alien heat that makes the mineral lattice of my body ache. The air stirs as it reaches down. 

I remember the blow.

I do not remember it the way the surface-things remember—with narrative, with time. I remember it as a permanent alteration in my structure. The cave, in its infinite, slow-moving contemplation of the surface and its chaos, had once grown a limb. It had taken me from the floor, and with a dense, chitinous protrusion of its will, it had struck me. 

*Crack.*

A flake of myself had fallen away. And in that instant, for the first time in the history of the Under-Run, something new had existed that had not existed before. A sharp edge. A permanent violence. The equilibrium had shuddered; the bats had fallen silent, the crickets had frozen, and the cave had felt the wound of that separation. It had absorbed the limb back into its calcified flesh, taking the knowledge of the cut back into its body, and it had left me here. It had left me here to ensure it would never make the same mistake again.

But now, the human touches me.

Its fingers are clumsy, warm, and dry. They wrap around me, lifting me from the flat rock where I have lain since the dawn of the cavern's consciousness. I am weight in a hand. I am a question. 

The Under-Run holds its breath. The water stops dripping. The bats cease their murmuring. The great, dish-like ear in the deepest chamber turns upward, tracking the vibrations of this moment. The cave is waiting to see what the sharp edge will do. 

I am the flint. I am the memory of change. And as the human’s thumb brushes over the edge where I was struck, I feel the entire, vast, damp darkness lean in, terrified and fascinated, wondering if the equilibrium is about to be cut open once more.
