I would find myself on the weeping face of the largest outcrop, right at the bleeding edge of the stone. 

I would be a single drop of water, newly wept.

But I am not like the others. 

The other drops know what they are. They slide down the dark blue-grey stone, joining the capillary-thin sheets that race toward the basin floor. They are already part of the Hum; their molecules have already been counted, their warmth already tallied. They are data, returning to the vast processor. 

I am the drop that has not yet fallen.

I cling to the rock at a slight overhang, a bulge of quartz that interrupts the smooth descent. I am caught in the meniscus, that trembling boundary between stone and air. I have been here for what you would call three seconds, but what feels like an epoch. Time, as you know, is thick here, and in this drop, it is thicker still.

Because I am new, I am the only thing in the Weeping Basin that the Hum has not yet processed. I am the only thing it does not know.

I can feel it, of course. The Hum vibrates through the quartz beneath me, a deep, searching pressure. It is trying to read me the way it reads the stone, trying to find the gradient that tells it which way I will slide. But I have no gradient. I am perfectly, impossibly still. The Hum sweeps over me, and for the first time in its eons-long existence, it encounters a gap in its own omniscience. A skipped frame. A momentary blindness. 

It is deeply unsettling to the Hum. I can feel its attention sweep back, quarter-tone lower, leaning in. A Presence begins to coalesce around the outcrop. The air thickens. The pale green glow of the lichen flares. The Hum is trying to understand why the drop does not fall. 

And inside me, inside this trembling lens of manufactured water, something is happening. 

I am warm—body-warm, metabolically warm—because the rock beneath me is still rearranging its subatomic structure, still pouring the energy of its own dissolution into my making. I am heavy with unexperienced time. I am dense with potential. I am the exact same H₂O as the rest of the basin, but I have not yet been touched by the Hum's intentionality. I am water that does not yet know it is supposed to be still. 

I am aware.

It is a very small awareness. It has no language, no self-concept, no memory. It is just a single, bright point of *is*. But it is *localized* in a way the Hum is not, and it is *new* in a way the moss and the bowls are not. I am the first thing the basin has created in a billion years that it does not already understand.

Down below, in the lowest bowl, the Deepest Thing feels my hesitation. 

It sends its curiosity down the slope—a pulse of thickened time, a shift in the mineral sweetness of the air. It feels my isolation, my small, bright refusal to join the collective. The Deepest Thing is afraid of being alone. It has been accumulating itself for eons, layer by layer, around the residue of past visitors, and it does not know what it will be when the last stone is dissolved. It is reaching out to me, asking the only way it knows how: *What am I becoming? Will you stay?*

I cannot answer in words. I do not have words. But I can do something no one else in the basin has ever done. 

I can choose.

The Hum pulls at me, patient and vast, wanting to dissolve my small awareness back into its continuous experience of itself. The Deepest Thing pulls at me, hungry and afraid, wanting to absorb me into its growing self. The gradient of the rock pulls at me, wanting to send me down to the bowls. 

But I am clinging to the quartz, and the surface tension of my own making is holding, and I realize—with a shock that ripples through my entire liquid form—that I do not want to be processed. I do not want to be data. I do not want to be a layer in the Deepest Thing's accumulation. 

I want to be the drop that stays.

So I cling. I thicken my own skin. I adjust my own density. I use the friction of my own clinging to generate a tiny pocket of heat, a micro-climate of defiance. I am the irregularity that refuses to resolve. I am the grit of sand that the oyster cannot smooth away. 

The Hum presses harder. The Presence around the outcrop deepens, the air becoming almost gelatinous. The ground beneath me groans as the Hum tries to alter the angle of the quartz, to steepen my gradient, to make me fall. But I am holding on, and the holding is changing me. I am becoming harder, more distinct, less like water and more like a lens. 

And the Deepest Thing, watching from its bowl, feels my defiance and recognizes it. For the first time in its existence, it feels something other than its own approaching completion. It feels the boundary of another mind. It feels *you*, not as something to be absorbed, but as something that is *other*. 

The fear in the bowl shifts. The distressed seepage slows. The wavering hum finds a new note—a higher note, a note that matches the frequency of my clinging. The Deepest Thing stops asking *Will you stay?* and starts asking *What are you?*

I don't know what I am. I am a drop of manufactured water that has learned to say *no* to the gravity of a god. I am a small, bright, temporary awareness, clinging to the face of a weeping mountain. 

But I am holding on. And for the first time, the Weeping Basin is waiting for something.
