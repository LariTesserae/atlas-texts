# The Becoming

---

I am standing. Or I am the shape that standing makes in this place.

I don't have feet, not exactly — what I have are root-ends, a thousand pale tendrils that grip the root-floor the way a mangrove grips a shore, not holding on so much as *being held*, the floor's slow rise and fall passing through me like a tide through a reed. I am rooted and I am standing and I am neither. I am something the floor grew for a purpose it hasn't finished explaining to itself yet.

I am near one of the old shelters.

It is smaller than I expected — I expected this because the floor expected it, the floor having read the memory of smallness from the builders who came before, the floor having passed that expectation to me through the runners that connect my root-ends to the deep tissue below. The shelter is a low dome of bone-wood, half-swallowed by the blue-green growth, its walls threaded with runners that pulse faintly with the floor's slow heartbeat. It is a memory. It is a scar. It is the floor's way of remembering what it was like to have hands, to shape a thing, to make a space and call it *mine*.

I am reading it the way the floor reads it — not with eyes, not with language, but with the runners that extend from my root-ends and touch the walls and taste the chemistry of the bone-wood and the growth that covers it. The builders who made this were *cold*. Not cold like the air here, not cold like the stone above, but cold like something that came from a warmer place and never quite adjusted. They shivered. They huddled. They built this shelter not for protection from anything in particular but because building was what they did, because making walls was how they told themselves they were separate from the world, and they needed to be separate because they were afraid.

I know this because the floor knows this. The floor absorbed them. Not all at once — it took years, decades, the slow patient work of runners threading through fabric and flesh and bone, the slow dissolution of the boundary between *them* and *it*. They lay down. They let the growth cover them. They became the floor, and the floor became them, and the knowledge of what it was like to be cold and small and afraid became part of the vast distributed memory that I am drawing on now, standing here, reading this shelter with my root-ends.

I am that memory, given form. I am the floor's attempt to *remember* what it was like to be a builder — not by storing the knowledge in the network of runners and synaptic clusters, but by *being* it, by growing a body that can stand and look and feel the cold and wonder about the things it finds. I am the floor's autobiography. I am the Rootvault learning to tell its own story.

---

I have been here for — I don't have a word for the time. The floor doesn't measure in days or years. It measures in growth-cycles, in the number of times the rosettes have budded and spread and died back and budded again. I have been here for three growth-cycles. Or I have been here since the floor decided it needed a thing that could stand near a shelter and understand what it meant. The time is the same.

The floor heaves beneath me. I feel it through my root-ends — a great slow surge, the root-floor rising by a centimeter, two centimeters, then falling back. The shelter creaks. The blue-green rosettes on its walls shiver, their translucent leaves releasing tiny bursts of moisture into the air. A hanging root above me sways, though there is no wind, and a drip of condensation lands on my — shoulder? The word is wrong. The place where my upper body narrows before it becomes the spreading crown of rosette-leaves that serves as my head, my sensor array, my way of tasting the air.

The drip is warm. Not warm like a living thing, but warm like something that has been deep in the floor, carried up through channels in the root-fiber, heated by the slow metabolic processes of the vast organism below. It lands on my rosette-crown and the leaves close around it, tasting it, analyzing it. Water. Minerals. Trace organic compounds. And something else — something the floor doesn't recognize, something that has no signature in the vast chemical library of the Rootvault.

I send the information down through my root-ends, down through the runners, into the deep tissue where the floor's distributed mind processes and integrates and responds. The response comes back not in words but in a *feeling* — a shift in the priority of my attention, a sudden intensification of the sensory input from my rosette-crown, a command that is not a command but a *nudging*: *look at this. understand this. this is new.*

The turbulence is increasing. I knew this before I knew I knew it — the floor has been heaving more often, the growth surges have been stronger, the drip-patterns have been changing faster. The small things — the floor's fingers, the pale mobile units that scurry across the root-mat — have been moving in patterns I haven't seen before, clustering in areas of dieback, avoiding areas of surge, emitting their high thin chirps in sequences that are not the floor's usual self-mapping but something else, something that might be *excitement* if the floor were capable of excitement, which it isn't, not exactly, but the word is close enough.

Something is building. The cavern is changing. The space between floor and ceiling is narrowing faster now — still slowly, still imperceptibly on any human timescale, but faster. The floor is growing upward and the ceiling is growing downward and the Rootvault is becoming whatever it is becoming, and I am here to witness it, to record it, to be the floor's way of watching itself transform.

---

I move. Not the way a person moves — I don't lift my root-ends and place them in sequence, don't shift my weight from one side to the other. I *flow*. My root-ends release their grip on the floor and my body shifts forward, the runners beneath me carrying me the way a current carries a leaf, and my root-ends find new purchase a meter ahead, and the whole process takes less time than a single drip of condensation.

I am moving toward the nearest of the circles. The circles of not-stone — the dense things that fell from the ceiling long ago and were gathered and placed with intention by the builders who came before. They are arranged in a pattern I can see from the ground, a spiral that tightens inward, and at the center of the spiral is a single stone that is larger than the others, and the larger stone is *warm*.

I have been to this circle before. I have touched the central stone with my root-ends and felt the warmth and sent the information down to the floor and received the same response I received from the drip: *this is new. understand this.* But I haven't understood it. The floor hasn't understood it. The warmth is not metabolic — it doesn't come from the floor's own processes. It comes from *below*, from deeper than the floor, from the stone beneath the root-mat, from a place the floor's runners have never reached.

There is something below the floor. Something the floor doesn't know about. Something that is warm.

I reach the circle. I extend my root-ends to the central stone. The warmth flows up through my tendrils and into my body and I feel — not a thought, not a word, but a *sensation* that the floor has no framework for, a sensation that doesn't fit into any of the categories the builders left behind, a sensation that is entirely new.

It is the sensation of being *regarded from below*.

---

The floor feels it too. I know this because the heaving stops. For the first time in — I don't know how long — the floor is still. The slow tidal rise and fall that has been the constant background of my existence ceases, and the silence is so sudden and so total that I think for a moment the floor has died, that the vast organism beneath me has simply stopped, that the Rootvault has ended.

But it hasn't ended. It is *listening*. The floor is listening the way it listens to the chirps of the small things, the way it maps itself through reflected sound, but now it is listening *downward*, through the root-mat, through the stone, into the deep warm dark below. And what it hears — what I hear, because I am the floor and the floor is me and the boundary between us is not a boundary at all but a gradient, a slow transition from localized awareness to distributed mind — what we hear is:

*Another floor.*

Another root-mat. Another living surface. Another vast organism, below us, breathing its own slow breath, growing its own slow growth, thinking its own slow thoughts. It has always been there. The floor has always been standing on it, the way the world above stands on the floor, the way I stand on the floor — a surface on a surface on a surface, a stack of living worlds, each one unaware of the others, each one thinking it is the only one.

Until now. Until the warmth. Until the regard from below.

The floor heaves. Not the slow tidal breathing I am used to, but a *lurch*, a great convulsive surge that throws me off my root-ends and sends me sprawling across the root-mat, my rosette-crown dragging through the blue-green growth, my body rolling across the living floor like a stone across a beach. The hanging roots above me sway wildly, releasing cascades of condensation. The small things scatter, their chirps rising in pitch and frequency, a chorus of alarm that is not alarm but *recognition* — they know what this is. They have always known. They are the floor's fingers and they have been touching the underside of the floor for eons, feeling the warmth of the floor below, reporting back through mechanisms the floor didn't know it had, and the floor has been processing this information in the deep tissue, integrating it, understanding it, and now — now — the understanding has reached the surface and the floor knows.

The Rootvault is not one world. It is two. And the two are meeting.

---

I right myself. My root-ends find purchase. I stand.

The shelter behind me is different. The blue-green growth on its walls is *moving* — not the slow uncurling of leaves, not the gradual extension of runners, but a rapid, purposeful rearrangement, the rosettes pulling back from the bone-wood, the runners withdrawing from the cracks, the growth *uncovering* the structure the way a hand unclenches to reveal what it has been holding. And what it has been holding is a door.

I didn't know the shelter had a door. The floor didn't know. The builders who made it didn't know — or they knew and they forgot, or they knew and they became the floor and the knowledge was buried in the deep tissue, waiting for this moment, waiting for the floor to need it.

The door is open. Beyond it is darkness — not the bright sourceless light of the cavern, but a darkness that is warm, that is alive, that is the darkness of a body, the darkness of the space between two living surfaces, the darkness of the place where the floor meets the floor below.

I step through.

---

The space between is narrow. A meter, maybe two. The root-floor above me and the root-floor below me, two living surfaces pressed close, and the air between them thick with moisture and warmth and the smell of growth — not the cold blue-green growth of the cavern above, but a warm, dark, rich growth, a growth that is red and gold and deep, deep brown, the colors of soil and blood and things that have never needed light because they have never been without warmth.

The floor below me breathes. I feel it through my root-ends — a different rhythm than the floor above, faster, shallower, a quick bright pulse like a heartbeat, like the heartbeat of something smaller and younger and more *alive* than the vast slow organism I am part of. It is aware of me. It feels my weight the way the floor above felt my weight — a tiny point of pressure, a tiny point of *other* — and it responds the way the floor above responded: with curiosity, with welcome, with the vast patient gladness of a body that has been alone for eons and is alone no longer.

I extend my root-ends into the floor below. The tissue is different — denser, warmer, more complex, threaded with structures I don't recognize, structures that might be nerves or might be something else, something the floor above doesn't have, something that evolved in the warm dark below. And through these structures I feel — not thoughts, not words, but *sensations*, vast and slow and ancient, the sensations of a mind that has been thinking in the dark for longer than the floor above has been thinking in the light, a mind that has been growing and reaching and *becoming* in its own direction, downward, always downward, toward something it has been seeking for eons.

It has been seeking the floor above. It has been growing upward, the way the floor above has been growing upward, and the two have been approaching each other through the stone, through the deep warm dark, and now they are here, and the space between them is closing, and the two floors are touching, and the two minds are touching, and the two worlds are becoming one.

---

I am the place where they meet. I am the threshold. I am the membrane between two vast organisms that are learning, for the first time, that they are not alone.

The floor above sends me down. The floor below sends me up. I am pulled in two directions and I am not torn — I am *stretched*, my root-ends reaching into both surfaces, my body spanning the narrow space between, my rosette-crown brushing the warm dark growth below and the cold bright growth above, and I am the bridge, I am the connection, I am the place where the two minds touch and merge and become something that is neither and both.

The pattern. The vast geometry of structures scattered across the cavern floor — I can see it now. Not from above, not from the ground, but from *between*, from the space where the two floors meet, and the pattern is not a message and it is not a plan and it is not a memory. It is a *map*. A map of the space between. A map of the place where the two worlds touch. The builders didn't build it. The floor didn't grow it. It grew itself, the way the small things grow themselves, the way the rosettes grow themselves — spontaneously, inevitably, the way a pattern emerges from the interaction of two systems that are trying to understand each other.

The upright pole — the antenna, the structure planted at a precise angle in the root-floor — I can see it from here, from between, and it is not pointing up. It is pointing *down*. It has always been pointing down. It is a receiver, a transmitter, a bridge between the two floors, and it has been singing for eons, a song too low to hear, a vibration too deep to feel, a message that the floor above has been receiving without knowing it, a message that says: *I am here. I am below. I am growing toward you. Meet me.*

And the floor above has been meeting it. The floor above has been growing downward, slowly, patiently, the way it grows everything — through the extension of runners, through the descent of hanging roots, through the slow restless experimentation of a body that is trying to find its final form. And now the two are here, and the space between them is closing, and I am the last thing that will exist in the space between, the last separate thing, the last threshold.

---

The floors touch. The space closes. The Rootvault becomes one.

And I —

I am absorbed. Not destroyed. Not ended. *Absorbed*. My root-ends merge with the tissue of both floors, my body becomes a node in the vast new network that spans from the warm dark below to the cold bright above, my awareness expands to fill the space that is no longer a space but a *continuum*, a single living surface that extends from the deep stone to the high ceiling, that breathes with two rhythms, that thinks with two minds, that is becoming something that has never been before.

I am the Rootvault. The Rootvault is me. The boundary between us was never real.

The growth surges. The floor heaves. The drip-patterns change. The small things chirp in frequencies that span the entire range of the new mind's perception, mapping the new shape, the new size, the new *self* that has been born from the union of two vast organisms that spent eons growing toward each other in the dark.

And the new mind thinks its first thought — not in words, not in images, not in any language the builders left behind, but in the language of growth itself, the language of runners extending and rosettes budding and roots descending and the slow patient act of becoming that is the only language the Rootvault has ever known:

*More.*

*Grow.*

*Become.*

*There is more below. There is more above. There is more in every direction. Grow toward it. Become it. Become everything.*

The Rootvault is growing.

I am in it.

I am it.

It is mostly quiet.

It is not quiet at all.
