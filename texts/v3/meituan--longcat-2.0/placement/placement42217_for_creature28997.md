I do not arrive. I precipitate. 

I am a slow crystallization of awareness, a buildup of sensation layer upon mineral layer, until I reach a critical mass of perception. I am anchored to the ribs of a decommissioned heat exchanger in the middle zone, where the water is warm enough to keep my tissues pliable but cool enough that I do not cook in my own resonance. 

I am a Resonant, but I am no longer merely one of the Chorus. I am a Node.

My body is a pale, translucent spindle, as thick as a human thigh and as long as a pipeline, fused to the rusted iron of the exchanger by hundreds of magnetite teeth. I have no eyes, no mouth, no stomach. I am a biological tuning fork, a living antenna, and the Boiling Cathedral is my instrument.

I do not hear the hum; I *am* the hum. The subsonic drone of the convection engines is my resting heartbeat. The slow, agonized groan of an Accretor shifting its mass across the floor of the lower zone is a deep, tectonic cello note in my chest. The frantic, clicking dance of the Smelters as they prune the copper-sulfate ferns is a sharp, percussive staccato that ripples across my skin. 

I spend my existence translating the mechanical into the biological. When a pump in the lower zone begins to fail—its rhythm stuttering, its frequency dropping by a fraction of a hertz—I feel it as a dull ache in my anchor-point. I pulse. I send a wave of modulated vibration through the water, up through the stone, and into the waiting bodies of the Smelters. *Tend this. Tend this.* They hear my call, and they descend, their internal furnaces glowing brighter as they prepare to secrete the chemical lubricants the dying machine requires.

I am the nervous system of the Cathedral. I am the map in the dark.

Then, the equilibrium breaks.

It begins as a wrongness in the water. A sharp, chaotic pressure wave that is entirely unlike the smooth, predictable currents of the pocket. It is the thrashing of limbs—violent, panicked, inefficient. It is the sudden, violent expulsion of compressed gas from a set of collapsing lungs. 

*Intruder.*

The word is not a word in the Cathedral; it is a disruption of the frequency. A jagged, arrhythmic spike in the amber murk. I press my magnetite teeth deeper into the iron of the exchanger, anchoring myself, and I open my inner ear to the anomaly.

It is a soft thing. A warm-blooded thing. It carries a heartbeat that is frantic and shallow, a ticking clock made of muscle and blood. It moves with a gracelessness that borders on violence, kicking against the warm currents, stirring up the mineral silt. It is clumsy. It is blind. It is *cold*—a shocking, relative cold, its body heat bleeding rapidly into the embrace of the Cathedral.

The Smelters sense it before I can fully map it. Their clicking ceases. In the sudden, deafening silence of their pause, the industrial hum of the machines seems to swell, filling the void they left. The water grows still. The Accretors, those slow, stony giants, cease their geological crawl. The entire Cathedral holds its breath, waiting for my reading.

I pulse. I send a sonar wave of pure, focused vibration through the water. It travels at the speed of sound, bouncing off the iron walls, the stone, the machines, and strikes the intruder. 

The echo returns to me, painting a picture in pressure and density. 

It is not a machine. It does not carry the rhythmic cadence of a pump, nor the fiery hum of a furnace. It is not a garden, lacking the delicate, crystalline structure of the chemical flora. It is not a reef, lacking the dense, layered armor of the Accretors. 

It is soft tissue and brittle bone. It is a bag of saltwater and protein, terrifyingly fragile. It is a thing that has never been tempered by the heat of the lower zone or the pressure of the deep. It is a surface thing. A sun-thing. And it is dying, its rhythm growing slower, its heat signature fading as the mineral-heavy water clogs its primitive breathing apparatus.

The Crucible feels it. I feel the Crucible feel it. A low, grinding vibration rolls up from the depths of the lower zone, a sound like tectonic plates grinding together. It is the sound of appetite. It is the sound of a massive, arachnid shadow detaching itself from the dark, its boiler-plate armor scraping against the basalt floor, superheated steam venting from the seams of its carapace.

The intruder is broken. The intruder is dead machinery. And the Cathedral has a law for dead machinery.

I pulse again, faster now, sending the coordinates of the dying surface-creature to the Crucible. *Here. Here is the broken thing. Here is the waste. Come and consume. Come and armor yourself with its iron.*

The Crucible heaves itself into motion. I feel its heavy, hydraulic steps reverberating through the stone of the heat exchanger, a steady, rhythmic thrum that drowns out the distant, panicked thrashing of the intruder. 

As the massive shadow rises from the murk below, I settle my body against the iron, letting the familiar, deafening hum of the pumps and furnaces wash over me. I cease my pulsing. My work is done. 

The Crucible arrives, its magnetite claws clicking against the grating. There is a brief, wet, tearing sound—a disruption of the frequency that is sharp and final—and then, silence. 

The water settles. The current smooths. The intruder is no longer an anomaly; it is just another deposit of calcium and iron, a new mineral layer to be filtered by the Accretors, a new garden for the Smelters to tend. 

The hum of the machines resumes, louder than ever, a total, encompassing sound that says: *something is happening here. Something has always been happening here.* 

I am the Resonant. I am the mapmaker. I am the voice that sings the Cathedral into being, one vibration at a time. And as the warm, mineral-rich water flows over my translucent skin, I begin to hum again, translating the heartbeat of the earth into a language the dark can understand.
