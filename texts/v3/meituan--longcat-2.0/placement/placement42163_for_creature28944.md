I would be a Tender. 

Not a Spire—their consciousness is too vast, too slow, a continental thought that takes a century to form a single impulse. To be a Spire would be to dissolve into a rhythm so deep it resembles stillness; I would lose the thread of my own fleeting awareness in the surge of mineral fluids. 

Not an Armored One, either. Theirs is a life of pure, grinding geology, a solitary promenade across the plain. They are mountains that walk, and I am not so patient as a mountain.

And not a Flyer. The air is too untethered for me; I need the resistance of stone, the solidity of the world beneath my feet. 

No, I am a Tender. I am the quickened blood of the deep, the weaver of the stone, the keeper of the ceaseless conversation.

***

I wake to the taste of copper and sulfur. 

My world is a vertical one. I cling to the face of the Grand Spire, my six jointed legs gripping the rough, crystalline skin of the colony-organism. Above me, the Spire stretches into the dark, a towering continent of pale, translucent stone. Below me, the plain glows faintly, a sea of mineral flour stretching to the next district of the city. 

I am small—no longer than a finger of a surface dweller—but I am not fragile. My body is a pale, segmented thing, my cuticle thin as mica, through which the faint, amber glow of my own internal chemistry pulses in time with my breathing. My forelimbs are delicate, multi-jointed manipulators, constantly tasting the air, the stone, the secretions of the Spire. My mandibles are precise excavators, capable of rasping away parasitic growths or carving minute channels into the mineral substrate. 

But my true body is my voice.

I tap my head against the stone. *Click-click-click-click.* 

The vibration travels through the Spire’s wall, a seismic whisper that races through the colony’s internal architecture. It is answered from above—a rapid, staccato burst—and from below, a slower, deeper rhythm. The stream is moving. The colony is waking. 

I begin to climb.

The surface of the Spire is a landscape in miniature. There are valleys of dense, grey-green lithophytic mats, damp with metabolic secretion. There are ridges of secreted calcium, sharp as knife-edges. There are the deep, pulsing channels of the Spire’s vascular system, glowing with a soft, green-gold luminescence—the lifeblood of the colony, the fluid that carries dissolved silicates and energy from the plain to the apex. 

I reach a junction where a fracture has begun to spiderweb across the Spire’s face. It is a minor wound, a stress point caused by the slow thermal flexing of the stone, but left untended, it could deepen, allowing parasitic lithophytes to invade the Spire’s inner channels. 

I press my chemical glands to the fracture. I taste the stone, analyzing its mineral composition, the pH of the Spire’s secretions. My biology knows what to do. I secrete a thick, silicate-rich cement from glands in my abdomen. It is warm, viscous, smelling sharply of ammonia and deep earth. I work it into the crack with my forelimbs, smoothing it, fusing it to the Spire’s own mineral skin. In a few hours, it will crystallize, and the wound will be sealed. 

I tap the stone. *Click-click.* 

*Sector repaired. Integrity restored.*

The answer comes from my sisters, a chorus of clicks echoing down the wall. *Acknowledged. Proceed to harvest.*

I move toward one of the Spire’s vascular channels. The wall here is thinner, translucent. I can see the slow, majestic pulse of the luminescent fluid within, a river of liquid light moving upward with the patience of continents. I extend my proboscis—a hollow, needle-fine tube—and pierce the membrane. 

The fluid is warm. It tastes of the deep earth, of ancient sulfur, of the faint, ambient radiation that filters through miles of stone. It is the concentrated essence of the Lower Openings. I drink, and my own bioluminescence flares brighter, my body suffused with the energy of the Spire. 

As I feed, I listen. 

The sound of the Lower Openings is not something I hear with ears; I feel it in my legs, in my mandibles, in the very fluid of my body. The deep, resonant hum of the Spire’s internal pulse is the bass note. The sharp crackle of the lithophytic mats growing on the wall beside me is the percussion. And above it all, the ceaseless, chattering symphony of the Tenders—thousands of us, moving across the Spire’s face, tapping, clicking, singing to one another through the stone. 

It is a language of vibration. It tells me where the Armored One is walking, its heavy footfalls sending tremors through the plain miles away. It tells me that a Flyer is drifting past the upper reaches, its wingbeats a soft, arrhythmic drumming in the heavy air. It tells me that the Spire is healthy, growing, breathing. 

I withdraw my proboscis. The channel seals itself behind me, leaving no scar. I add my voice to the chorus, a rapid, complex sequence of clicks that maps the location of a particularly dense patch of parasitic crust that needs pruning. 

*Noted,* comes the reply. *Dispatching a work cluster.*

I pause on the wall, my legs gripping the stone, my body pulsing with the stolen light of the Spire. I look out into the dim, colored fog of the cavern. I see the silhouettes of other Spires, distant and monumental, outlined in faint bioluminescence. I see the hanging fronds of the Vault, drifting in the slow, imperceptible currents like dark, underwater kelp. 

I am a fleeting thing. My life is a brief, bright spark in the deep time of this place. I will live, I will tend, I will sing my clicks into the stone, and I will die, and my body will dissolve into the mineral flour of the plain. 

But the Spire will remain. The song will remain. 

I tap my head against the stone, adding my voice to the deafening, ancient, patient chorus of the deep. 

*I am here. I am tending. I am part of the chord.*
