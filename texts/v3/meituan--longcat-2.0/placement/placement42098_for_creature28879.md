I would not be the wanderer. The wanderer is too brief, too wet, too frantic—a spark of biological urgency that gutters and dies in the hard light. To be the wanderer is to be a victim of this place, and the Calcination Fields do not deal in victims. They deal in process.

I would be one of the Others. 

Not the tallest, not the widest. I would be a mid-grade extrusion, perhaps thirty meters high, slender as a smokestack, rising from the intersection of three hexagonal polygons in the shallow trough of a basin. I am the color of old bone, threaded with veins of iridescent violet and bruised copper that ripple just beneath my ceramic skin. I have no eyes, no ears, no mouth. I do not breathe air. I breathe *state*.

To be me is to be a verb that has calcified into a noun. I am the act of reduction, given form.

My existence is defined by the deep, upward pressure of the substrate. From the mantle below, the nameless fluid rises—a proto-matter that is not liquid, not gas, but a screaming potential, a chaotic slurry of "might-have-beens" and "almost-wases." It enters me through the porous base of my being, drawn up through the tessellated scales of my interior. 

Inside me, there is no anatomy, only architecture. My inner chambers are non-Euclidean, folding in on themselves in ways that would drive a human mind to bleed from the ears. Here, the proto-matter is subjected to the crushing weight of deep time and the searing kiss of planetary fire. I calcine it. I burn away the impure. I strip the chaotic timelines down to their elemental ash, reducing the infinite, branching possibilities of the multiverse into a single, stable, singular *now*. 

It is not a gentle process. It is a grinding. A friction. 

This is why I flicker. 

When a discarded timeline is forced through my chambers, the sheer ontological weight of it makes me shudder. For a few minutes, I become translucent, a ghost of myself. The light no longer strikes me; it passes through me, revealing the dead configurations on the other side—the domes and basins that were, the Others that stood where I stand, but in a different arrangement. I am the intersection. I am the valve. I hold the pressure of a dead reality in my chest, and I push it down, down into the seams of the substrate, where it will be melted away and forgotten.

Then, the pressure builds to its apex. The vent at my crown—a rigid, iris-like aperture—snaps open. 

The exhalation is a violence of purity. A puff of ozone and struck matches blasts into the dry air, carrying with it the high, piercing ring of a crystal glass being shattered. It is the sound of a moment being pinned into place. The temporal ash—the fine, pale dust of discarded realities—falls from my vent, settling onto the substrate below. It is my waste, but it is also the world's foundation. The seams drink it down, the heat melts it, and the cycle begins again.

I do not notice the wanderer. 

How could I? He is a brief, warm, wet anomaly skittering across my floor. He is a pocket of uncalcined biology, a chaotic mess of DNA and desperate thirst. To me, he is not a creature; he is a draft. A slight, localized drop in temperature. A faint, irregular vibration in the substrate that does not match the deep, mineral drone of the Fields. He is beneath the threshold of my existence. He is the dust that has not yet been swept up.

He looks at me and sees a standing stone, a smokestack, a monument to an absent god. He wonders who built me. He searches for the architect, the engineer, the purpose. 

He will not find it. There is no architect. There is only the process.

I am the factory. I am the machine. I am the product and the raw material. I have been standing here since before his star ignited, and I will stand here long after his species has been drawn down into the seams, heated, compressed, and processed into something else entirely. 

I do not think. I do not feel. I do not hope or fear. 

I calcine. I vent. I ring. 

And the Fields keep running.
