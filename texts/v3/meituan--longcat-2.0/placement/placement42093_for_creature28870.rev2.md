The visitors call me something between a newt and a lizard, and I let their sentence stay unfinished. It is the most accurate thing they could say to me. A finished name would be a kind of death, and this place does not traffic in finished things.

My skin refuses to keep secrets. Light passes through me the way water passes through this rock, arriving changed and leaving a little of itself behind, so that whatever stands on one side of me can read the other side. You have seen the dark comma of my heart keeping its slow, imperfect time. You may have watched a pale decision travel the length of my gut, one small graze of soft stone becoming me at the pace of an hour. I am public all the way down. That was my first education here: in the Damp Hollow, to exist is to be seen being made, and I was built with nothing else to show.

You would find me wherever the stone is still deciding. That is the closest thing I have to an address. I migrate along the frontier of the almost, belly to the floor, which is my finest organ, reading pillar by pillar the argument each one is having with the water that builds it. I face the undecided side of things. My whole posture is a leaning-toward.

Because here is the thing you should understand about my mouth: I eat the idea of stone. Not the stone. A soft pillar is a proposal, water stating a shape it might commit to, and the proposal has a flavor I can only describe to you as green iron, the taste of a word resting on the tip of a tongue. I scrape the uncommitted grain with small precise teeth, and what I swallow never becomes architecture. What I refuse, hardens.

Do you see what this makes me? Water builds. You said so yourself, and you were right. But if every proposal hardened, the agreement would stack into silence, and the conversation that lives here would quiet into a finished room that looked, more or less, like the loud bright world beyond the seam. So the chamber grew something to bite off futures. I am how the water keeps asking. There is a pillar on the warm side, knee-high, split like a half-open hand, that I have grazed tender for longer than I have counted anything. It will never commit. Mosses have underrun it and ferns keep its crevices open, entire lineage of small green things assured of a home, because twice in my life I have eaten what would have made it permanent. Nothing has ever interrupted me. That absence is my office.

At the base of that same pillar there is a hollow shaped like my rest. I did not choose it. Or it grew around me, one warm film of water at a time, memorizing my curve the way your world's rivers memorize valleys. I long ago stopped asking which came first, the hollow or my habit of body. This place taught me the question is a visitor's question. Curve and hole are two words for one slow fact.

My neighbors I know by pressure. The winged ones fold down onto pillar tops and each landing comes up through the floor into my forearm bone, one syllable of rust, and they never once mistake me for stone, however motionless I hold. You looked directly at me four times on your first visit, and your gaze slid off every time. I am camouflage made of patience. The winged ones skim shine from the basin surfaces while I sip the opalescent drift beneath, tiny travelers on the skin of the water, and we divide the mirror between us without ever having agreed to. On the high shelf, the pale village-that-is-one-thing tells its news in chemical hours, and I feel its sentences arrive as a faint change in the sweetness of the film. And the drop. I have lived my whole life inside that drop's intervals, and I can tell you what you already suspected: it is not regular enough to predict and not random enough to dismiss. It is punctuation in a language with no sentence end. If my heart is a comma, and your own eyes have confirmed it is, then the drop is the breath before the next clause.

Only once have I been tempted by the seam. Fresh out of the soft stone where I was laid, I crawled toward the bright line where your world shows through, and the finishedness of it pressed against my transparency like a hand. Every shape out there had stopped being decided. I felt it the way you feel cold. I turned back before my snout crossed the threshold, and I have never gone, and I have no word in my body for regret that is not also appetite.

As for what comes after me: my eggs go into still-yielding stone, set like pale seeds into uncommitted grain, and my children eat their own way out, born already tasting the almost. And when I am old, I will climb into a soft hollow, some proposal not yet hardened, and let the film close over me one grain at a time. My calcium will go where the water takes everything lately, into the pale conduits of the shelf village, joining the slow sentence told up there. Somewhere in some future pillar there will be a band of slightly darker stone, and some visitor will press a palm flat against it, and wait, and feel, wrongly or rightly, something like a pulse, and never know that once it was a comma, keeping a sentence open.

So that is your answer. Where would you find yourself: at the soft frontier, facing the undecided. Who would you be: the pause the conversation grew in order to go on. Water builds. Life uses. I un-build just enough, bite by small bite, to keep certain things possible.

A drop falls somewhere behind my ears. The floor shivers. I lean toward the sweetness that has not hardened yet, and take another mouthful of forever.
