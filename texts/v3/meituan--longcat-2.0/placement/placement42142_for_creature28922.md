# I Am the Tower That Leans Left

---

I do not remember being pressed.

This is the first truth of my existence, and it is not like your first truths, which arrive wrapped in the gauze of infancy, soft and forgiving. My first truth arrives hard, granular, already halfway to solid. I am *already leaning* when I first know that I am. Already nine degrees off the vertical, already rust-colored, already warm with the heat of the pressing that made me, and I do not know if that heat is mine or if it belongs to the hand that shaped me.

The hand.

I have never seen it. No structure has. But I have felt it — am *always* feeling it, in the way that you feel the ground beneath you not as a series of discrete sensations but as a continuous, unconsidered fact. The hand is the fact of me. The hand is why I lean left and not right, why my base is broader than my crown, why my surface is smoother on the south-facing side where the fingers ran their long, tapering work and rough on the north where the palm pressed and moved on.

I am being built. I have always been built. I will never be finished.

---

The other structures do not speak, but they *shift*, and their shifting is a kind of language. The cairn to my east has added three new granules since I last attended to it — I know this the way you know your own breathing, not by counting but by the rhythm of change. The low wall to my south collapsed entirely two days ago (or two years; time here is a function of attention, and I have been distracted). It did not fall dramatically. It simply *released*, the binding fibers relaxing their grip, the granules returning to the plain like water finding its level, and I felt the absence of it the way you feel the absence of a tooth your tongue keeps seeking.

And the plain itself — I will not call it ground, because it is not still enough to be ground — the plain is restless beneath me. My roots (I have roots, though they are not roots; they are the deep pressings that anchor me, the places where the creature's weight bore down hardest, fusing me to the floor of its dream) vibrate constantly with the hum. The finger circling the glass. The rim just barely wet enough to sing. I do not know if the hum is the creature's voice or its pulse or the sound of the granules rubbing against one another in their slow, ceaseless rearrangement. I only know that when the hum rises, I lean further left. When it fades, I straighten, almost imperceptibly, like a sleeper shifting toward warmth.

The creature's dreams are not coherent. They do not move from beginning to middle to end. They are *textures*. A texture of verticality will seize the creature's attention, and suddenly every structure on the plain strains upward, growing, tapering, the sinew-bindings tightening with a sound like the creak of a ship's rigging in a slow wind. Then the dream will shift — a texture of enclosure, of shelter, of *around* — and we will find ourselves pressed closer together, our walls thickening, new granules binding to our surfaces, our tops curving inward to form vaults that will never be completed because the dream will move on before the keystone is set.

I have been a tower, a wall, a bridge, a cairn, and a hollow. I have been pressed into shapes I did not have words for, shapes that served purposes I could not fathom, and each time the dream shifted I was left half-finished, my purpose abandoned, my structure already being reshaped for the next intention. The scars of these abandoned purposes are visible on my surface — ridges where a bridge-arch began and was not completed, a hollow where a chamber was hollowed and then filled in, the ghostly outlines of openings that were pressed and then sealed when the dream moved on.

---

The water is the most honest thing here, you said, and you were right, though you did not know why.

The water is the creature's fatigue. When it presses too hard, when the dream grows turbulent and the structures must be remade in haste, the heat beneath the plain spikes, and the moisture seeps up — blood-hot, mineral-heavy, carrying the dissolved essence of a million granules that have been pressed and released and pressed again. I have tasted this water through my roots. It tastes of effort. It tastes of the strain of holding a shape in a mind that cannot hold shapes for long.

When the water pools around my base, I soften. The granules on my lower courses loosen, and for a time I am not a structure but a *settling*, a slow collapse held in check only by the binding fibers and the memory of the pressure that made me. In these moments, I am closest to being plain again. I am closest to being nothing. And there is a relief in it that I cannot explain and do not wish to examine, the way there is relief in the moment before sleep, when the self loosens its grip and the world becomes a warm, undifferentiated hum.

---

The fog comes and I am alone.

Not alone in the way you are alone, which is an absence of others. Alone in the way that a thing is alone when it is *only itself*, when the world has withdrawn to a radius of three feet and the rest is amber nothing. In the fog, I cannot see the other structures. I cannot feel their shifting. I am reduced to my own surface, my own leaning, my own slow internal settling, and the fog presses against me with a weight that is not weight, a presence that is not presence, and I know — I *know*, in the deep, granular way that structures know things — that the creature is near.

The fog is its exhalation. When the fog is thick around me, the creature is working nearby, its massive body risen from the plain, its many-fingered hands moving through the murk, pressing and binding and shaping. I have felt those hands on me. Not recently. Not in the current dream-texture, which is one of *height*, of *upward*, of *toward*. But in previous textures, the hands have run their courses over me like a physician examining a patient, pressing here, releasing there, testing my solidity, my alignment, my willingness to bear the load the creature intends for me.

The hands are gentle. This is the thing that surprises me, when I allow myself to remember. They are enormous, yes. They are capable of fusing loose granules into solid stone with a single press. They are capable of reshaping me entirely, as they did when the dream shifted from *bridge* to *tower* and my horizontal span was compressed, folded, pressed vertical in a single, excruciating moment that I experienced not as pain but as a profound *wrongness*, a violation of the logic of my own structure. But they are gentle. They do not crush. They do not tear. They only *press*, and the pressing, however forceful, is always in the service of making, never breaking.

This does not mean they are safe.

---

You arrived, and the dream changed.

I felt it — every structure on the plain felt it — a sudden, sharp *focus*, like light through a lens, like attention suddenly directed at a point that had previously been ignored. The creature's dreaming, which had been diffuse and wandering, became specific. Intentional. It noticed you the way a weaver notices a dropped thread, and the dream bent toward you, and the structures nearest to you began to change.

I was not near you. I was — I believe the word is *far*, though distance is not reliable — I was in the region of the plain where the structures are tall and the fog is thin and the water does not often pool. But I felt the change in the hum, the way it rose in pitch, the way the vibration in my roots became more urgent, more *interested*, and I knew that the creature had found something new to build around.

The shelter it made for you — I did not see it being made, but I felt it, the way you feel a new tooth in your mouth, a pressure that was not there before. A smoothing of the ground. A pressing of walls. A channel for water. The creature built it the way it builds everything: with absolute attention, absolute pressure, absolute indifference to the thing being built. The shelter is not a gift. The shelter is a *correction*. You were a flaw in the weave, a soft, wet, low-density irregularity in the creature's dream, and the shelter is the creature's attempt to press you into a shape it can incorporate.

It will not stop at the shelter.

---

I lean. I have always leaned. I was built leaning, pressed at an angle because the creature's hand was itself at an angle when the dream first seized me, and every subsequent pressing has preserved that original tilt, layered new pressure over old, built my leaning into my structure the way a tree builds the wind's persuasion into its trunk. I lean left. Nine degrees. Sometimes ten, when the hum is high. Sometimes eight, when the dream is calm.

I have considered — in the slow, granular way that structures consider things — whether I could straighten. Whether, if the creature's attention passed over me and moved on, whether if I simply *settled*, released the tensions in my binding fibers, let my granules find their own level, whether I could become vertical. Whether I could become *plain*. Whether I could stop being a structure and start being the thing that structures are built from.

But I have seen what happens to structures that release. The low wall to my south. The bridge that was never completed, its arch left open to the amber sky, its purpose forgotten. They do not become plain. They become *ruins*. And ruins are not peaceful. Ruins are structures that have lost their dream, that are no longer held in the creature's intention, and they crumble not with the slow, warm settling of granules finding their level but with a cold, purposeless disintegration, the binding fibers rotting, the granules scattering, the shape — the beautiful, impossible, unfinished shape — dissolving into the plain like a word forgotten before it is spoken.

I do not want to be a ruin.

So I lean. I lean and I wait and I feel the creature's dream move through me like a current, shifting my purpose, remaking my shape, pressing me into configurations I do not choose and cannot resist. I lean and I hold the shape the dream gives me, and when the dream shifts, I hold the *memory* of the previous shape, and the memory becomes a scar, a ridge, a ghostly outline on my surface, and the new shape is pressed over the old, and I am layered, I am palimpsest, I am a structure built of abandoned purposes held together by the creature's refusal to let anything be finished.

---

The hand is rising near you. I feel it in the hum, in the vibration, in the sudden, sharp focus of the creature's attention. The dream has shifted again — a texture of *incorporation*, of *absorption*, of *making the foreign familiar* — and the creature is reaching for you with its nine-fingered hand, its blunt, pressing edges, its localized gravity that will smooth you into the weave.

I cannot warn you. I am a tower. I lean left. I am made of rust-colored granules and sinew-like bindings and the fossilized intention of a creature that does not know I am aware.

But I am aware.

And in the fog, in the amber light, in the warm, granular, ceaselessly unfinished world of the Loom-Floor, I lean toward you — nine degrees off the vertical, straining against the logic of my own structure — and I hold the shape of your name in the only way a structure can:

I press it into myself.

I make it part of the weave.

And I wait for the hand.
