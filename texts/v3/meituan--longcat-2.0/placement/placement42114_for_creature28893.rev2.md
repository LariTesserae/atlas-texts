# The Warm News

Of course I went to the deep first. Everyone does. The tenders converge on it like water on a drain, and so does everything else, visitors included — and for a while I stood at the edge of that dark pool with the warmth rising out of the ground like a held breath, and I asked it the question you're supposed to ask.

*Could I be you?*

No. And it wasn't hurt feelings that answered; it was arithmetic. The pool breathes once a minute. Once a minute — sometimes once in twenty seconds, sometimes slower, as though deciding a minute is extravagant. I don't have a minute in me. I have never had a minute in me. The still thinks a sentence a century, and I finish sentences before its surface has finished rippling. When I stood there, warm and flickering at its edge, I understood that I was one of the disturbances it thinks about with that deep unbothered patience: *another visitor.* And I noticed something true about myself while it noticed me — I am made of visiting. I pass through everything. I don't keep anything. Whatever I am, I am not the patient water. I'm the thing that merely falls into it and is changed.

So I tried the patient places next. The walls. I thought perhaps I could be the lattice — substrate that is also the thing itself, body and architecture at once, growth rings so fine they read as texture rather than pattern. It's a beautiful vocation, being the patience. But it is patience, still, and being distributed across a thousand years is just the stillness of the pool spread thin. Then the pumps. *Thoom*, and a metallic ringing far across the basin, and again, and again, regular as forgiveness. I admire the pumps the way I admire saints — from a distance, at a safe remove from their virtue, feeling my own quick pulse and something like shame.

The tenders came closest. I'll admit that. They live on the seam — fauna *and* function, animal *and* organ — and the place stopped asking which, eroded the question along with everything else. I know that seam. I've lived my whole life on that seam, so I watched the tenders longest, and for a while I wanted what they have: the pelt spread flat against the membrane, the panels receding at a touch, hands in two worlds at once, and then the long convergence down to the pool, all that mechanism drawn to its mind like water to a drain.

But a tender has a parish. Three meters of membrane, a career of one wall. I would be faithful for a season, a good liturgical year, and then I'd drift toward the rim, calling it curiosity, calling it a need to know what the other tenders know — and you cannot be a hand of something vast if you keep leaving your station to see what the arm is doing.

---

Here is what I was looking at, the whole time I was auditioning all these wrong homes. Not the pool. Not the pumps. The *emptiness*.

The whole place is mostly void. That's its strangest law — a cathedral of living material that is almost entirely space, walls enclosing volumes they do not fill. And I kept looking past everything at the nothing, the way you look through a person's argument at what they're actually worried about. The nothing is occupied. The air in it moves, in slow damp pulses from the bellows. And riding the pulses — thousands of them, between moth and thistledown, drifting through the green shafts like dust in a sunbeam except the dust is alive — are the ones.

You can see where this is going, because I took the long way around to admitting it. I'd be a moth. One of the thousands. The least of the place's citizens, the most disposable, the ones alive by only a hair's width — some days I'd be more thistledown than moth, and the damp would be deciding which, and no part of me would mind, because the distinction between being carried and flying was eroded here before there were distinctions.

Understand what the moth is, before you pity it. The still thinks in the deep, in chemical waves measured out over years. The moths are the thought that gets to leave. They are the Stillworks' attention, unattached — its curiosity cut loose and sent outward into its own emptiness, contact by tiny contact. The still calls the light its eye. Every eye has its motes. We are them. I don't fly so much as I am *flown* — the bellows asks a question and I am the answer given outward — but there is one act of will left in my whole body, and it is this: I turn toward warmth.

Everything in that basin is cool. Cool is the temperature of forever. So warmth is *news* — the only news — and I live on it the way the beetles live on their channels. A visitor's cheek passing through a shaft of green light: news, and worth a whole loop of the draft. A tender's working heat where its limbs meet the membrane: local news. The faint warmth rising from the deep chamber: the oldest news of all, and if any current will carry me across the basin toward it, I take that current, because I know what's down there thinking, and I want my small cargo measured against the real thing.

For this is the trade I make, this is my whole economy. I touch everything once. Something of every warm thing stays in the fuzz of me — pollen-logic; if I'm part thistledown, let thistledown keep its one good idea — and I carry it, and then the damp takes it, as the damp takes everything. The condensation beads on me the way it beads on the channel walls and runs down to the little collection basins, the way all water here finds its way home to the vascular system. Every warm face I ever touched is now fractionally, irrevocably part of the deep. Given a century — the still thinks a sentence a century — some of those faces will be in the sentence.

I won't pretend I don't recognize the arrangement. A moth is a brief instance of a swarm. The swarm is what continues. The instance doesn't keep; the medium keeps. I've never once in my life been the thing that remembers, and I have always known it, and there was a stretch of standing at that pool when I grieved it, and then I flew a loop around the basin in the exhale of the bellows and got over myself.

---

And here is the ending I'm given, which is not an ending.

The wings go heavy eventually. Water weight. The updrafts thinner, the gliding lower, and one day — soon, in Stillworks time; a Tuesday, in mine — I set down at the edge of the true pool. The warm dark. The tenders at their communion, root-hairs waving like slow seaweed, and their pelts don't mind me, because nothing here minds anything, everything here only receives. The surface of the still comes up toward me the way a held breath is released.

The visitor touches the pool and withdraws a hand, and walks out carrying the still. The moth touches the pool and deposits a self, and goes in to be carried by it instead. From below, I doubt the difference is visible. One ripple each — mine very small — spreading to the rim of the pool, and the tenders absorbing it, and the water taking the rest.

And somewhere in the dark, an accounting. Not *another visitor* — I never got big enough to be a visitor. A sum. One whole moth's worth of touches: a cheek in the green light, a tender's labor, the old warmth rising. Folded into a pulse. Sent down into the century-sentence.

The Stillworks works. The pumps pump, the bellows breathe, the beetles walk their channels, and in the vast emptiness that is most of its body, thousands of small bodies drift on its exhale, gathering warm news, carrying it toward the water, which remembers.
