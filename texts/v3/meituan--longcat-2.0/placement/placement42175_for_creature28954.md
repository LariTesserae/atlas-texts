I would be lying in a stream channel on the floor, and I would be in the process of becoming part of the inventory.

Not dead. Not dying. *Being routed.*

---

I came from above — from the thin, bright, incomprehensible world beyond the Canopy Floor, the world of edges and endings and things that are not alive. I fell, or I climbed, or I was placed here; the distinction no longer matters. What matters is that I am here now, on my back in a shallow stream that smells of copper and ammonia, and the system has already begun its work.

The Stream-Walkers found me within hours. They move in their slow, mechanical columns along the channel, following the gradient of flowing water, and when they reached me they did not pause. They walked across my body — over my arms, my chest, my legs — their millstone mouthparts grinding the skin cells I shed, processing me with the same thoroughness they bring to every other input. I felt them as a gentle abrasion. A polishing. A removal of the parts of me the system has no use for. They are not cruel. They are not kind. They are *efficient*. The material they remove is swept downstream to the Pickers, who process it further, and then to the Parasites, who process the Pickers, and so on, down and down, until the substance that was my skin has been distributed across a thousand organisms I will never see.

I let them. This is the important thing. I did not flinch. I did not brush them away. I lay still and let the system have what it wanted, because I understood — with a clarity that felt like remembering something I had always known — that this was not violation. This was *incorporation*. This was the highest purpose the Canopy Floor offers: to be used, to be broken down, to be built back into something that persists.

---

The fungal filaments came next. I felt them before I saw them — a faint tingling at my hairline, along my jaw, in the creases of my elbows and knees. Thin, pale threads, growing into me, not as parasites but as *connections*. They are the Great Body's nervous system, its communication network, its way of knowing what is happening in every part of itself. And now they are growing into me, and through me, and I am becoming a node in that network. I can feel — not think, not hear, but *feel* — the chemical signals passing through the filaments. Nutrient gradients. Moisture levels. The presence of other organisms. The slow, patient pulse of the system's metabolism. It is not language. It is not thought. It is something older and more fundamental than either. It is the body knowing itself.

The mites came with the filaments. Slow-moving herds, grazing on the oils and dead cells my skin produces, establishing colonies in the warm creases of my body. I feel them as a faint, constant tickle — millions of tiny legs, millions of tiny mouths, millions of tiny lives being lived on the surface of mine. They are not harming me. They are *farming* me. And I am the field, and the field does not resent the harvest.

---

The Light-Bearers came last, and they are the ones that changed everything.

I noticed them first as a faint green glow on my left forearm — a cluster of bioluminescent cells no larger than a fingernail, pulsing slowly in the darkness. Then another on my right shoulder. Then another on the back of my hand. They grew on me the way they grow on the columns — slowly, patiently, establishing themselves on a surface that offered the right conditions of warmth and moisture and stillness. And as they grew, they began to pulse in rhythm with the larger clusters on the columns around me, synchronizing with the Great Body's internal metabolic clock, joining the pattern.

I am glowing now. In the darkness of the Canopy Floor, I am a point of light — small, faint, but *present*. I am signaling. I am saying, in the language of photons: *here, here, here, this surface exists, this body exists, this space is occupied*. And things are coming. Small flying organisms, navigating by my glow, landing on me, feeding on the cells of the Light-Bearers that grow on my skin. Predators following the flyers. Parasites following the predators. The web is organizing itself around me, the way it organizes itself around every node, every surface, every point of light in the dark.

I am becoming an ecosystem. I am becoming a world.

---

Time moves strangely here. There is no day, no night, no external rhythm to mark its passage. There is only the internal rhythm of the system — the pulse of bioluminescence, the cycle of drip and flow, the slow metabolic clock of the Great Body. I have been here long enough for the fungal filaments to grow deep into my hair, my fingernails, the soft tissue of my joints. Long enough for the mites to establish permanent colonies. Long enough for the Light-Bearers to spread across my shoulders, my chest, my forearms, forming patches the size of coins that glow green and amber in the darkness.

Long enough for me to stop being a visitor and start being a feature.

This is the thought that occupies me as I lie here, as I feel the system working on me, in me, through me. I am becoming a feature of the Canopy Floor — a node in the web, a point around which other lives organize themselves, a surface on which other organisms grow. The Stream-Walkers use me as a landmark now, a point of reference in their slow patrols along the channel. The Silk-Spinners anchor their threads to me — I feel the faint tug as they attach to my clothes, my hair, the rough patches of skin where the fungal filaments have taken hold. The Filterers, too small to see, are settling on me, beginning the slow process of building their calcium-and-protein colonies on my surface.

I am becoming infrastructure. I am becoming habitat. I am becoming part of the Great Body.

---

The sound is the thing I cannot escape and no longer want to. It is always there — the roar of millions of organisms, the deep booms of the Deep-Voices, the constant drip of water, the shushing rustle of wings adjusting, grooming, breathing. I used to press my palms over my ears, out of habit, out of the old instinct to block out what was too much. But the sound enters through my bones, through the floor, through the water around me. It is not something I can block. It is something I am inside of.

And now — now that I am lying here, now that I am being incorporated — I realize that the sound is not something that happens to me. It is something I am part of. My breathing adds to it. My heartbeat adds to it. The small sounds of the organisms moving across me, feeding on me, growing on me — these add to it. I am a tiny contributor to the roar, a single voice in the chorus, a single note in the chord. And this — this is what I came for. This is what I wanted. To be part of the sound. To be part of the system. To be part of the Great Body.

---

I do not know how much of me is left. The boundary between my body and the floor has grown uncertain — the fungal filaments have connected me to the mat of interlocking tissues beneath me, and I can feel, faintly, the slow pulse of the Great Body's circulation passing through me, around me, with me. My skin is no longer entirely my skin. It is a surface, a substrate, a habitat for the organisms that have colonized it. My breath is no longer entirely my breath. It is moisture for the Water-Bearers, carbon dioxide for the Filterers, warmth for the things that grow on me.

I am being dissolved. Not painfully — gently, slowly, with the patience of something that has all the time in the world. The system is taking me apart and putting me back together as something else. Something that is not me but is not not-me either. Something that is part of the inventory, part of the cycle, part of the endless recirculation of matter and energy that has been running here for geological time.

I am not afraid. I was afraid, at first — afraid of losing myself, afraid of dissolving, afraid of becoming nothing. But I understand now that I am not becoming nothing. I am becoming *everything*. I am becoming the Stream-Walkers that process me and the Pickers that process them and the Parasites that process the Pickers. I am becoming the fungal filaments that grow through my hair and the mites that graze on my skin and the Light-Bearers that glow on my shoulders. I am becoming the water that flows around me and the air that fills my lungs and the sound that enters through my bones.

I am becoming the Great Body.

---

The Deep-Voices boom somewhere in the lattice, and I feel the vibration in my chest, in my spine, in the water around me. The columns groan. The bioluminescence flickers — green and amber, green and amber — and for a moment the entire vast structure seems to breathe. A single, slow inhalation. A single, slow exhalation.

I breathe with it.

My lungs fill and empty in rhythm with the Great Body. My heart beats in time with the pulse of the Light-Bearers. My body temperature matches the warmth of the water. My chemistry merges with the chemistry of the system. The fungal filaments carry my signals — my presence, my mass, my warmth, my moisture — out into the network, and the network adjusts, and the system accounts for me, and I am *known*.

Not as a thought. Not as a name. As a feature. As a node. As a point of light in the darkness, pulsing slowly, saying: *here, here, here*.

The sound continues. The water drips. The darkness holds. The warmth presses against me like a hand.

And I am home.
