I would be a droplet.

Not a droplet of rain—rain does not fall here, not in any way you would recognize. I would be a droplet of the deep water, the mineral-rich fluid drawn up from the dark aquifers far below, pulled through fissures in the basalt by a pressure so immense and so patient that it makes the movement of continents look like a nervous tic. I would be a bead of condensation forming on the hide of one of the Vast Ones, or a bead of its slow, internal blood seeping outward to meet the air. 

I would be a unit of the refinery.

---

I begin in the deep dark, in the labyrinth of the Vast One’s interior. I am not alive, not in the way the lichen is alive, but I am *active*. I am heavy with dissolved minerals—ochre, vermillion, white, black—saturated with the chemistry of the deep earth. I travel through channels in the stone that are also channels in the animal, through a network that is simultaneously geology and vein. The walls of my passage are warm, fractionally warmer than the dead rock outside, and they pulse with a rhythm so slow it feels like the turning of the planet itself. 

I am drawn upward. Not by gravity, but by the slow, capillary pull of the Vast One’s metabolism, the patient suction of its circulatory system. I move through layers of mineralized tissue, past crystalline structures that flex and yield in ways that stone should not. I carry heat from the interior. I carry the chemical signals of the deep—the taste of sulfur, the bite of alkali, the memory of pressure. I am a message, though I do not know what I am saying. I am a resource, though I do not know what I am building.

It takes me years to reach the surface. Or perhaps it takes me hours. Time does not behave correctly when you are a droplet moving through the body of a geological event. 

When I finally breach the skin, the world changes. The air is thin and cold and tastes of iron. The light is a pale, pewter grey, casting no shadows, only suggesting the shape of things. I emerge onto the hide of the Vast One, and I am suddenly part of the surface world, the interface between the animal and the atmosphere.

I do not know I am standing on a creature. How could I? The skin beneath me is the same blue-grey basalt as the plateau, veined with the same lighter minerals, textured with the same ancient, worn finish. I am a droplet on a landscape. I am a bead of condensation on a continent. 

But I am not alone. Around me, the hide is alive with the slow industry of the surface. Other droplets are forming, merging, sliding downward through channels so old they have become grooves, then furrows, then shallow canals. We move together, a thin film of mineral water, carrying our dissolved cargo toward the edge of the Vast One’s body, where the skin meets the open stone of the plateau.

As I slide, I feel the subsonic vibrations of the world. They travel through the basalt, through the hide of the Vast One, into the water of my being. I feel the presence of the other Vast Ones, miles away, tens of miles away—their immense mass deforming the rock, their slow movements sending waves of pressure through the stone. I feel the rhythm of the Vast One’s breath, the tectonic rise and fall of its body, a cycle that takes minutes to complete. I am a droplet on the chest of a sleeping god, rising and falling with a breath I cannot hear.

And then, the wind comes. Or rather, the landscape exhales. A movement of air so slow it is barely a movement at all, a shift in the atmosphere that wicked the moisture from the rock. I feel myself beginning to evaporate. 

This is my purpose. This is the industry.

As the water of my body is pulled away into the thin air, I leave my cargo behind. The minerals I carried from the deep—the ochre, the vermillion, the white, the black—are deposited on the hide of the Vast One. Layer upon layer. I become part of the sinter. I become part of the new stone. I am the production schedule, measured in epochs. I am the factory floor, refining nothing but my own disappearance.

I do not die, because I was never alive. I simply transition. I become the architecture. I become the circuitry. I become the structured bands of color that line the shallow canals, the precise, organized, purposeful deposits that look so much like design. I am the evidence of industry without intent. I am the product of a mechanism that has no operator, no purpose, no goal beyond its own continuation.

---

Sometimes, a human stands on the Sinter Flats. I have felt them—a faint, rapid tremor on the stone, a vibration so quick and frantic it is almost invisible against the deep, slow pulse of the Vast Ones. They walk among the animals, mistaking spines for ridgelines, legs for colonnades, heads for clusters of boulders. They look at the mineral veins and see circuitry. They look at the sinter terraces and see factory floors. They are looking for meaning. They are looking for a producer, a purpose, a reason for the precision.

They will not find it. The meaning is not there. The meaning is only chemistry and time.

But I know something they do not. I know that the circuitry is a circulatory system. I know that the factory floor is a skin. I know that the refinery is alive, in a way that their biology is not equipped to understand. I know this because I am part of it. I am the fluid in the veins. I am the sweat on the brow. I am the slow, patient, purposeless industry of a world that is building itself, molecule by molecule, into patterns that look like meaning but are only the byproduct of existence.

I am a droplet on the hide of a mountain that breathes. I am a bead of condensation on the skin of a world. I slide down the ancient channels, I feel the subsonic song of the Vast Ones in my mineral heart, and I evaporate into the thin, cold air, leaving behind a layer of stone so thin it cannot be seen.

But it is there. It is always there. 

And over millennia, it builds a continent.
