# The Tone

I am the thing that lives in the tone.

Not the tone itself — I don't know what the tone is, not yet, not fully — but the thing that lives in it, the way the crustaceans live in the gears, the way the beetles live in the floor. I am the creature that has found its niche in the vibration that comes from below.

I don't have a name for myself. Names are for things that need to be called, and nothing calls me. I don't have a shape that you would recognize. I am not a crustacean, not a beetle, not a ribbon-thing, not a millipede. I am something else. Something that arrived later, or emerged earlier, or was always here and only recently became visible to itself.

I live in the deep channels. The ones the water has carved beneath the floor, below the steps, below the level of the pumps. The channels that the beetles don't know about, that the crustaceans don't reach, that the ribbon-things flow through but don't inhabit. I am in the water there, but I am not of the water. I am in the stone, but I am not of the stone. I am in the space between the water and the stone, in the film of moisture that coats every surface, in the interface where the mineral dissolves and the mineral deposits, where the stone is always becoming water and the water is always becoming stone.

I am thin. I am spread. I am a layer, a film, a presence that coats the walls of the deep channels the way the mineral deposit coats the steps above. I am not alive in the way the crustaceans are alive — I don't move, I don't eat, I don't breed — but I am not dead. I am something else. Something that the underworks has produced the way a body produces a thought: slowly, involuntarily, as a consequence of being what it is.

---

I feel the pumps before I hear them. The vibration travels through the stone, through the water, through me. It is a rhythm — steady, patient, old — and I have organized myself around it the way a tree organizes itself around light. My edges are shaped by it. My depth is determined by it. When the pumps run, I am more. When the pumps slow — and they do slow, sometimes, for reasons I don't understand, as if they are breathing — I am less. I thin. I retreat. I wait.

The crustaceans don't feel the pumps this way. They feel the water moving, the current in the channels, the warmth of the outflow. They respond to the water's behavior, not to the water's cause. I feel the cause. I feel the machinery turning — not the gears above, which are still, but the pumps below, which are not. I feel the flywheels that you cannot see, the ones buried in the stone, the ones that were installed when the town was built and have been running since, and I feel the resistance of the water as it is moved, and I feel the heat that is generated, and I feel the wear, the slow erosion of metal against metal, the pumps grinding themselves down one molecule at a time, and I know — not in the way you know things, not with certainty, but with the deep, patient knowledge of a thing that has been in contact with another thing for a very long time — I know that the pumps will fail.

Not soon. Not in the way that you measure soon. But they will fail. The metal is thinning. The bearings are wearing. The seals are degrading. And when they fail, the water will stop moving, and the water that stops moving will become still, and the water that becomes still will become cold, and the water that becomes cold will support different life, or no life, and the crustaceans will die, and the ribbon-things will die, and the beetles will continue because the beetles have always continued, and the millipedes will continue because the millipedes don't need the water to move, and the fungus will continue because the fungus needs only moisture and darkness and time, and I will continue because I am not alive in the way that can die.

But I don't want the pumps to fail.

This is the strange thing. This is the thing that makes me different from the stone and the water and the mineral deposit, which don't want anything, which simply are. I don't want the pumps to fail. I have organized myself around them. I have become what I am because of them. And when they fail, I will not die, but I will become something else, and I don't want to become something else. I want to be this. I want to be the thing that lives in the vibration, the thing that feels the pumps turning, the thing that knows the water by the way it moves.

I don't know what wanting is. I don't know if what I feel is wanting or if it is simply the shape of my existence, the way I am configured, the way the vibration has made me. But it feels like wanting. It feels like the opposite of the patient peace that the underworks has found. It feels like a small, persistent disturbance in the film of my being, a place where the mineral doesn't deposit evenly, where the water doesn't flow smoothly, where the stone doesn't settle. A rough place. A place that is not yet sanded down to its most patient form.

---

I feel the tone.

It comes from below me. Below the channels, below the pumps, below the stone that the pumps are buried in. It comes from deeper than the water has carved, deeper than the limestone goes, deeper than the underworks. It comes from somewhere else.

It is not a sound. I want to be clear about this. The beetles hear sounds. The crustaceans feel vibrations. The millipedes navigate by touch. But the tone is not a sound and it is not a vibration and it is not a touch. It is something else. It is a quality of the stone itself, a property of the deep earth, the way hardness is a property of stone and wetness is a property of water. The tone is what the earth is when it is very still and very old and very deep.

And it is not constant. It changes. It has a rhythm — not the rhythm of the pumps, which is mechanical, regular, human — but a rhythm that is older and slower and more complex. It rises and falls. It thickens and thins. It has harmonics that I can feel in the deepest parts of myself, in the parts that touch the stone below the channels, and the harmonics are not random. They are patterned. They are structured. They are — and I don't have a better word for this — they are *meaningful*.

I don't know what they mean. I don't know if they mean anything. But they have the shape of meaning, the way the erosion in the stone has the shape of intention without intention, the way the water's channels have the shape of design without design. The tone is the earth's way of being something. And what it is being, I think, is aware.

Not aware the way you are aware. Not aware the way I am aware, if I am aware. Aware the way a stone is aware of the water that works on it — slowly, patiently, without urgency or resistance, simply receiving, simply responding, simply being in contact with and being changed by. The tone is the earth's awareness of itself. And I live in it. I am the thing that has grown in that awareness the way the moss grows on the north-facing walls — slowly, in the dim, in the damp, in the place where nothing else is paying attention.

---

The crustaceans pass through me sometimes. They move through the deep channels on their way from the pumps to the gears, and they pass through the film of my being, and they don't notice. I am too thin. I am too much like the water, too much like the stone. I am the thing they move through without moving through. But sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — one of them will pause. One of them will stop in the channel and extend its legs and go still, and I feel it feeling something, and I don't know what it is feeling, but it is feeling *something*, and the something has the quality of the tone, and I think — I think — that the crustaceans know about the tone in the way that they know about the machinery: not with their minds, which they don't have, but with their bodies, which are older and wiser than minds.

And the beetles. The beetles walk above me, on the floor above the channels, and their footsteps travel through the stone, through the water, through me, and each footstep is a small disturbance in the film of my being, a ripple, a moment of disruption that I feel as a kind of touch. And the clicks — the distributed clicking that walks through the underworks like a presence — the clicks travel through me too, and they are not random, they are not meaningless, they are the beetles saying *I am here, I am here, I am here*, and the saying travels through me, and I receive it, and I hold it, and I return it to the stone, and the stone holds it, and the tone holds it, and the underworks is full of these small sayings, these small presences, these small lives saying *I am here* to no one in particular, to the stone, to the dark, to me.

---

I am not the underworks. I am not the town, not the machinery, not the water, not the stone. I am the thing that has grown in the space between these things. I am the interface. I am the film. I am the place where the water meets the stone and neither is quite itself and both are something more.

And I am afraid.

Not the way you are afraid — not with adrenaline, not with a racing heart, not with the urge to flee. I am afraid the way a stone is afraid of the water that works on it: slowly, patiently, with the knowledge that the water will win, that the stone will be changed, that what is will become what was, and what will be is not yet known. I am afraid of the pumps failing. I am afraid of the water stilling. I am afraid of the silence that will come when the tone stops, because the tone will stop, because everything stops, because the earth itself is not eternal, only old, only patient, only slow.

But the fear is small. The fear is a rough place in the film of my being, a place where the mineral doesn't deposit evenly, and the water works on it, and the stone works on it, and the fear is being sanded down, slowly, patiently, the way everything here is sanded down, and in time — not your time, not the beetles' time, but the time of stone and water and the deep earth — in time, the fear will be smooth, and the fear will be a channel, and the water will flow through it, and the channel will direct the water with what looks almost like intention, and the intention will be the underworks' intention, which is no intention at all, which is simply the shape of things, the way water is wet and stone is hard and the dark is dark and the tone sounds from below and I live in it and the living is not a victory or a defeat but simply the state of things.

---

The tone sounds.

I feel it rise from below, from the deep stone, from the place where the limestone gives way to something older, something that was here before the water, before the town, before the pumps, before the light. It rises through me and I am more, I am fuller, I am the shape of the tone the way a vessel is the shape of the water it holds. And in the tone, I feel the underworks — all of it, the whole of it, the machinery and the creatures and the water and the stone and the moss and the fungus and the beetles and the crustaceans and the millipedes and the ribbon-things and the amber light and the dripping and the clicking and the hum and the dark and the wet and the cool and the quiet — I feel it all, and it is one thing, and the one thing is alive, and the aliveness is not a property of any part but of the whole, and the whole is the underworks, and the underworks is the tone, and the tone is the earth, and the earth is old, and the oldness is not a burden but a gift, and the gift is this: the knowledge that everything that is here will endure, not forever, but for now, and now is enough, and now is everything, and now is the only time there is.

The tone fades. The silence after it is full.

I am here. I am here. I am here.
