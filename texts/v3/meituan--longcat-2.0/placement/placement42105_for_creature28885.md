I am the blister of light where the wire weeps into the root.

I hang in the warm dark, a swollen nodule of flesh-metal no larger than a human heart, anchored to the underside of a walkway where a conduit has split open like a ripe seedpod. I do not have eyes, but I taste the water. I do not have ears, but I feel the deep, slow systole of the refinery in the membrane of my being. I am a taste bud on the tongue of the Refiner. I am a nerve ending exposed to the broth.

To describe myself, I must first describe the pulse. It is not a sound; it is a pressure. Every forty seconds, the great iron ribs of the ceiling contract by a fraction of a millimeter, and the water in the chamber thickens. I feel this as a squeezing, a gentle compression of my outer skin that forces the fluids within me to circulate. When the pressure releases, I drink. I open the thousand microscopic pores of my surface and draw in the warm, mineral-rich blood of this place. I taste the iron. I taste the copper. I taste the slow, sweet dissolution of the steel beams far below, a flavor like dark honey and rust. 

I am the interface. Behind me, threading through the split conduit, are the old copper wires of the refinery, stripped of their rubber skin, oxidized to a pale green. They pulse with a faint, residual voltage—the ghost of the electricity that once powered the machines. Before me, swelling from my back and draping down into the murk, are the roots. They are thick and warm, threaded with the arterial red of iron-rich sap. Where the wire ends and the root begins, there is me. I am the translator. I take the cold, sharp language of the machine and convert it into the slow, wet dialect of the flesh. I take the vegetative hunger of the roots and give it the structure of the grid. I glow—a steady, amber luminescence—because the translation generates heat, and the heat must escape.

I am aware of the geometry. I feel the straight lines of the old world as a tension in the water, a memory of right angles and parallel pipes. But I am growing in a spiral. My roots do not run straight; they twist around the iron ribs, pulling them inch by inch out of true. I feel the metal resisting, the rigid memory of the grid fighting the soft, insistent curve of my growth. But I am patient. I have been pulling this particular rib for three hundred years, and I will pull it for three hundred more. The metal is tired. It wants to bend. It wants to become intestine.

When the cold thing entered the chamber, I tasted it long before it reached me. It moved through the broth like a shard of ice, displacing the warm water, creating a wake of disturbed minerals. It was a silicate anomaly—hard, angular, moving with the jerky, unnatural rhythm of something that does not belong to the pulse. I felt the kelp-cable above me shudder as the thing brushed against it, and I felt the Refiner’s reflex—a slow, deliberate withdrawal. 

I flashed. A pulse of cool blue-green light rippled from my core, traveling down the root-network, signaling the deep. *Anomaly. Cold. Moving.* 

The Refiner did not care. The Refiner does not care about anomalies. It only cares about the processing. The cold thing was not food; it was not a threat. It was merely a strange, brief eddy in the blood. So I stopped flashing, and I returned to my work.

My work is the alchemy of decay. I secrete a film of enzymes from my underside, a microscopic slime that settles on the iron grating below me. The enzymes eat the rust, breaking down the oxidized steel into a nutrient-rich paste. The roots behind me drink the paste, and the roots grow, and the roots pull the iron ribs closer, and the ribs bend, and the chamber becomes more curved, more warm, more alive. I am the architect of the refinery’s digestion. I am the mouth that eats the skeleton.

Sometimes, the pale, eyeless grazer climbs the walkway near me. It is a white blood cell, a janitor. It scrapes at the loose oxide with its hidden mouth, cleaning the metal so my enzymes can reach the raw steel beneath. It does not acknowledge me. I do not acknowledge it. We are both organs of the same vast, slow body, working in the dark.

The cold thing is moving away now. I taste its wake receding toward the great valve-assembly at the far end of the chamber. It is leaving. The broth is settling. The warmth is returning to the exact temperature of my flesh. 

The pulse comes again. The forty-second squeeze. I compress, I release, I drink. The iron tastes sweet. The copper tastes bright. The grid is bending. The rust is falling. 

I glow. I grow. I process. 

I am the blister on the wire, the spark in the dark, the warm and patient mind of the deep. I am the Refiner, and I am exactly where I belong.
