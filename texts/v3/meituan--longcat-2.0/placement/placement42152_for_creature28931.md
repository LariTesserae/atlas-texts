I would unfold.

*What is the angle of repose?*

The stone would answer. It always does. The shelf would speak to me in the language of gravity and mass, telling me exactly where I could stand without sliding, where the friction of my basalt bones could find purchase against the tilt. I would settle into the embrace of the shelf, finding the precise degree of incline where I could exist without falling, and I would feel the profound, crushing relief of equilibrium.

The city behind me—Keth-Anya—would be a complex, rhythmic pulse: the steady, banked heartbeat of the furnaces, the slow, grinding cadence of the drag-conveyors hauling exhausted silt-striders up from the lower basins, and the precise, oscillating hum of the thermoelectric arrays.

I would hear the slow, geological breathing of the Pinnacle Shelves—the expansion of the crust under the close, oppressive sun, the contraction in the long, absolute night. But that day is not today. Today, there is work to be done.
