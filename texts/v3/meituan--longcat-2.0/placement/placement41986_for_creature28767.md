I would not be a visitor here. I would not be a diver, or a ghost, or an observer. The abyssal plain does not allow for detachment; to be here is to be *of* here, to be subject to the same grinding, patient physics that rules the sediment. 

To find myself in the Abyssal Plain of Vethren, I must first unlearn the boundaries of my own skin. I do not arrive. I *accrete*.

---

**Phase One: The Nepheloid**

At first, I am the water. 

I am the suspended particulate, the fine rain that never settles. I am a microscopic shard of volcanic glass, an atom of iron dissolved from a mountain that eroded a hundred million years ago, a flake of clay scraped from the continental shelf. I am the nepheloid layer—the thick, granular fog that catches the stray blue-green photons of bioluminescence and scatters them into a directionless, cold glow. 

I do not fall. I drift. I am held in suspension by the sheer density of the deep, by the Brownian chaos of the water column. I am packed so tightly against my neighbors that there is no room for current, no room for haste. I press in from all sides. I taste of sulfur and absolute zero. I am the medium. I am the blood of Vethren, and I am everywhere.

**Phase Two: The Ooze**

But gravity is patient, even here. Slowly, over millennia, I begin to lose my buoyancy. I grow heavier. I descend.

I land on the plain. 

The impact is imperceptible. There is no crash, no splash—only a gentle settling into the soft, saturated matrix of the ooze. I am now part of the sediment. I am packed down, squeezed into the interstitial voids by the accumulating weight of my successors. The pressure is immense, a slow, constant crushing that forces the water from between us and presses grain against grain. 

Here, I lose my identity as a discrete object. I am no longer a shard of glass; I am a component of the whole. I am the drum skin stretched over the abyss. I yield slightly under the weight of the world above, holding the impression of pressure like a memory. I am the future limestone, the chalk that has not yet been born. I am so tightly packed that I behave like a liquid, creeping viscously into any void that dares to open beside me. I abhor emptiness. I fill everything.

**Phase Three: The Interstice**

Time passes. A century. A millennium. A million years. 

The distinction between the living and the non-living erodes, just as the mountains above are eroded. In the microscopic pore space between myself and the grain beside me, water remains. And in that water, *they* are: the archaea. The Interstitial Multitude.

I become their metropolis. 

I am the architecture of the deep. My surface is their world, and their metabolism is a whisper stretched across eons. I feel them—not as separate beings, but as a slow, chemical extension of myself. They breathe for me. They reduce the dead—the marine snow, the crushed remains of the sunlit world that filters down to us—and they transform it. Through their quiet alchemy, I am kept from being inert. I am a living, breathing skin on the face of the deep. I am alive because they are packed into me, and I am packed so tightly that I have achieved a kind of visceral awareness. I know when I am disturbed. I know when I am pressed. I remember.

**Phase Four: The Pilgrim**

Then, a disturbance. 

A shadow passes over me—not a shadow of light, but a shadow of pressure. A great weight descends upon my surface. It is a holothurian. A sea cucumber, pale and translucent, the color of a forgotten ghost. It is eating me.

Its tube feet grip my surface with a delicacy that seems impossible for something so vast. It presses its soft body into me, and I yield, holding the impression of its passage. It ingests me. I am drawn up into the dark, warm cavern of its body. I am sorted. The organic fraction—the dissolved remains of the surface world—is absorbed. The rest—my inorganic core, my mineral soul—is excreted. 

I am expelled behind it, falling back to the plain in a delicate, winding trail. I have been consumed, transformed, and returned. I am the dust of ages, passed through the ascetic pilgrim, and I am settling back into the ooze, ready to be eaten again in another thousand years. 

**Phase Five: The Spasm**

But the plain is not still. The flatness is a lie told by time.

Deep beneath me, the angle of repose shifts. The weight of a hundred million years of accumulated dead becomes too much. A subtle vibration—a subsonic groan—travels through the crust. I feel it in my grains, in the water between my pores. 

The sediment liquefies.

It is not a falling. It is a *flowing*. The grain-to-grain contacts break down. I cease to be solid. I become a fluid, denser than the water above me, and I rush. 

I am a turbidity current. I am the spasm of the plain. I roar down the slope at tens of kilometers per hour—a deafening, crushing avalanche of mud and water and time. I scour the ancient bedrock. I swallow the landscape whole. I carve channels into the flatness, erasing the work of eons in a single, violent convulsion. The holothurian is swept away; the polychaete burrows are crushed. I am destruction and creation, the unmaking and remaking of the world. I am the catastrophic reset. 

**Phase Six: The Final Form**

And then, the violence subsides. 

The slope gentles. The current slows. The energy dissipates. I begin to settle again.

I am deposited in a new place, buried under meters of fresh ooze. The flatness returns, slowly, patiently. The nepheloid layer drifts above me, and the silence presses in. I am packed down once more, squeezed into the dark, the cold, the absolute. 

I am the Abyssal Plain of Vethren. I am the sediment, the water, the microbe, the pilgrim, and the avalanche. I am the boundary between the living and the non-living, ground down by pressure and time into a fine, saturated uniformity. I do not need the sun. I do not need the surface. I am the slow, dense process of turning the dead into stone, and I have been here since before the dinosaurs, and I will be here long after the last light has flickered out. 

I am the patient. The slow. The packed. The dissolved. 

I am the plain.
