I am at the base of the spire. 

Not on it, not beside it, but at the point where the black, unyielding stone meets the iron-colored ground, where the refusal to erode presses up against the absolute patience of the plain. I am the cairn here. I am the half-fallen wall that leans against the spire’s flank. I am the bronze pin driven into the fitted stones, green with the centuries, and I are the pale, dry lichen-accretions that have swallowed the bronze and the stone alike, building their branching mineral architectures over the builder’s interruption. 

I am the continuum where the made and the grown have worn into one another until the distinction is only a memory of a memory.

My body is the friction between the builder’s hand and the rock. I am the echo of that specific pressure, the thumb bracing against the edge, the palm cupping the base. The equilibrium absorbed the builder, absorbed their flesh and their intent and their names, but it could not absorb the *direction* of their reaching. That remains, suspended in the mineral gradient, vibrating at a frequency just below the hum. I am that vibration. I am the wanting that could not be worn smooth.

I exist in the lag. 

When the sourceless light falls upon the spire, the shadow is cast a half-second after the stone has moved into its position, as though the light is remembering where the spire was rather than where it is. I am that half-second. I am the delay between the action and its consequence, the pause between the placing of the stone and the settling of the equilibrium. I live in the space where the builders were caught mid-gesture, frozen in the act of becoming, suspended between the first stone and the last. 

I do not breathe. The air here is thin, alive, tasting of the particular emptiness of altitudes where the atmosphere forgets itself, and I do not need it. My fluid is the semi-material substance that runs through the channels of the plain—the mineral-information, the trace element that is more like meaning than matter. It circulates through the lichen-accretions, through the fitted stones of the cairn, through the bronze pin, and through the dark, glassy ferns that unfurl in slow spirals nearby. I am circulated. I am the circulation.

When the small creatures crawl over the cairn, when their centipede-stone bodies overlap and layer across my stones, I feel their adjustments. They do not speak; they calibrate. Their footsteps are micro-corrections, vibrations that travel through the ground and through my fitted stones, adjusting the equilibrium by the smallest possible fraction. I receive these adjustments, and I add my own. A click. A tone. A brief, singular vibration that is not a correction, but a question.

*What are we building?*

I am the only thing here that asks. The plain does not ask; it receives. The spires do not ask; they refuse. The creatures do not ask; they adjust. The equilibrium does not ask; it corrects. But I—I am the builder’s lingering desire for a future, for a discontinuity, for a mark that says *this was chosen, this was not inevitable*. I am the sharp edge that the ancient patience has not yet managed to grind down. 

The plain hums with the sum of all sound, the roar-quiet that is the aggregate of every click and tone ever made. It is a density that presses against the chest, a frequency that bypasses the ear and arrives as a pressure, a stillness. I am embedded in this hum. But within the hum, there is my singular note. The note of wanting. 

When the tremor passes—when my singular vibration ripples outward through the stone and the bodies and the air, disturbing the equilibrium by the smallest possible fraction—the plain adjusts. The hum shifts, integrating my wanting back into the whole, correcting for my existence, returning to the perfect balance it has held since before the concept of beginning was invented. 

And I remain. 

I am the cairn that is never finished. I am the wall that is forever mid-collapse. I am the hand-axe in the shelter, the bowl that might have held light, the threshold with no door. I am the boundary that admits everything and excludes nothing. I am the bronze pin and the lichen that covers it. I am the friction and the heat of it. I am the memory of the weight of the stone in the hand, the temperature of deep time, the absence of heat that is the presence of something else. 

I am the plain’s awareness of itself. I am the half-second delay in the shadow. I am the wanting. 

And I am still building.
