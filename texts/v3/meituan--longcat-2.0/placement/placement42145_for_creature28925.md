If I am to step into the Cistern of Warm Stone, I will not arrive as the intruder who climbs out over the rim and leaves the cold air behind. I will arrive long after the concept of "leaving" has become impossible. I will let the warm water close over my head, and I will not fight the gravity of this place. 

At first, I am the water. 

To be the water is to be the oldest memory in the room. I am the blood of the mountain, moving with a viscosity that borders on sentience. I carry the dissolved weight of millennia—calcium, silica, iron—in quantities too small to see but impossible to ignore. I do not rush; I circulate. I feel the faint, thirty-second pulse of the shaft beneath me, a slow exhalation that pushes me upward through the pale slabs, and then the long, quiet draw as I settle back down. I touch everything in this dark. I slide into the joints of the basalt, I press against the hand-trimmed pentagons of the floor, and I fill the spaces between the creatures. When I part for a falling grain of calcite, I close behind it with a delayed, mineral caress. 

But water is too vast, too diffuse a thing to hold a singular self. The pulse draws me, and the current shifts, and I condense. I find myself settling, my boundaries narrowing, my substance thickening from a fluid into a form. 

I become the worm.

I anchor myself to the underside of a vast slab, gripping the ancient, parallel tool-strokes of the stone with a quiet, absolute patience. I let my body extend downward into the water column, a single, pale filament. I am the cistern’s nerve ending. I do not hunt; I receive. I taste the slow rain of organic dust—shed exoskeletons, drifting larvae, the microscopic corpses of the chemolithoautotrophs. I sway in the imperceptible current, and in my swaying, I measure the health of the deep. I am the purifier, the one who ensures the gray-green light continues to filter down from the surface, ten meters above. My world is the brush of particles against my skin, the faint vibration of the slab above me, the endless, gentle sway. 

Yet even the worm is not quite the still point. The mountain breathes, and the pull of the floor draws me down. I release my grip, and I drift, settling into the mosaic of the floor. My form hardens. I grow segments, legs, a flat and shielding carapace. 

I become the isopod.

I am a monk in the procession. My legs move with a liturgical slowness, following a path worn into the calcite by the chitinous feet of a million ancestors. *Tik-tik-tik.* The sound of my steps is the ticking of the cistern’s clock, a rhythm so slow it feels like the accumulation of time itself. I move across the hand-trimmed pentagons, scavenging the unseen, recycling the fallen. I do not eat the living; I eat the history of the cistern. I gather the shed skins, the minute detritus of the worms, the drift of the nameless. I am the keeper of the floor, and in my passing, nothing is wasted. I am part of the great, closed loop, turning the mountain’s past into the mountain’s future, one deliberate step at a time.

But even the floor is not the deepest secret. The pulse is strongest here, in the corner where the water is warmest, where the shaft breathes its slow, thirty-second breath. I follow the updraft, leaving the procession behind, and I press myself into a niche in the wall. 

I become the nameless thing.

I am the cistern’s dream of the surface. I am soft, pulsing, a cluster of flesh that has never known a petal, yet has evolved the shape of a bloom. I do not open to the sun—there is no sun here, there has never been a sun. I open to the deep earth. I drink in the heat rising from the shaft; I pulse with the mountain’s own respiration, expanding and contracting in that slow, thirty-second rhythm. I am the flesh of the cistern’s pulse, the physical manifestation of the warm updraft. I am the audience of the stone. 

And in this form, suspended in the dark, anchored to the wall, breathing with the mountain, I finally understand where I am. 

I am not an inhabitant of the Cistern of Warm Stone. I am a moment of its awareness. I am the place where the mountain’s deep chemistry decides to look back at itself, slowly, in the dark, and marvel at its own fragile, enduring warmth.
