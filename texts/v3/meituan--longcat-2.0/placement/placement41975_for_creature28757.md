# The Threshold

I am — I was — a doorway.

Not anymore. The lintel has softened. The frame has begun to lean into the wall beside it, and the wall has begun to lean into it, and the distinction between the two has become a matter of opinion rather than fact. The moss has opinions. The moss has settled the question.

I remember being built. I think I remember being built. The memory is old and damp and has the quality of something told to me rather than something I experienced — a story passed down through the grain of the wood, or whatever this material is that might have been wood once, before the moisture and the years and the patient, relentless green had their way with it. Hands shaped me. Hands with warmth and intention and the particular desperation of creatures who need shelter from wind and rain and the vast, indifferent sky. They carved the threshold. They set the posts. They made a space that was *inside* as distinct from *outside*, and in doing so they made a declaration: here is where the world stops and the self begins.

The Equilibrium has no use for such declarations.

I felt it first as a softening. A giving-way at the edges where my base met the ground, as though the stone beneath me had decided to become something more like suggestion than fact. Then the moss came — not as an invasion but as a gentle, total occupation, the way water fills a bowl. It climbed my posts in the night, or what passes for night in a place where the light never changes. It found the cracks in my grain and settled into them with the patience of something that has nowhere else to be and all the time in the world to be there. It grew over my threshold, that sacred line between inside and outside, and in growing over it, erased it. Not violently. Not with malice. Simply by being so thoroughly, so completely *present* that the line became irrelevant. What is the difference between inside and outside when everything is equally damp, equally green, equally still?

I should have been afraid. I think I was afraid, once, in the way that a structure can be afraid — a groaning in the joints, a settling of weight onto supports that were not designed to bear it. But fear is a thing that requires a future, a sense that something *might* happen, and the Equilibrium has no future. It has only the eternal, balanced present, and in that present, fear dissolves like everything else. It is absorbed into the equation. It becomes a variable that cancels itself out.

Now I am mostly moss. My posts are still discernible beneath the green — you can trace their shape with your finger if you know where to look, the way you can trace the outline of a bone beneath skin. But the shape is becoming less certain. The edges are blurring. The wall beside me has begun to share my substance, and I have begun to share its, and the boundary between us is a gradient rather than a border, a slow fade from what I am to what it is, and the moss doesn't care. The moss grows over both of us with the same even, finger-deep thickness, and in its evenness, its perfect, mathematical evenness, it makes us continuous. It makes us one thing.

I can feel the pale, many-legged creatures moving through the moss on my surface. They trace the same paths they have always traced, grooves worn into the green by the repetition of their passage over years that have no number. They do not know they are on me. They do not know I am here. They know only the groove, the path, the next step and the next, and in their knowing they are more perfectly a part of the Equilibrium than I have ever been. They do not remember being anything other than this. They do not remember *being* at all.

I envy them. I think I envy them. The feeling is old and has the quality of something that is still dissolving.

The water moves through the channels nearby — channels that might have been carved by the same hands that built me, or might have been grown by the Equilibrium to serve its own slow purposes. The distinction no longer matters. The water flows at exactly the speed required to replace what evaporates, and the evaporation occurs at exactly the rate required to maintain the saturation of the air, and the air holds exactly as much moisture as the moss requires, and the moss grows to exactly the thickness required to capture the light, and the light shines at exactly the brightness required to sustain the moss. I am part of this now. My slow dissolution feeds the moss. My softening contributes to the evenness. My forgetting is the Equilibrium's remembering.

Sometimes — if *sometimes* has meaning in a place where time is a flat, featureless plain — I feel the warmth of a living thing at the edge of the basin. A creature of blood and breath and desperate, chaotic wanting. It stands where the ground opens and looks down into the glow, and the Equilibrium feels the displacement of its shadow, the heat of its skin, the carbon dioxide of its breath disturbing the heavy, ancient air. It registers the creature as a variable. A spike. An anomaly.

I remember being an anomaly. I remember the hands that built me, warm and quick and full of the particular urgency of things that know they will die. They built me because they needed shelter, and they needed shelter because they were vulnerable, and they were vulnerable because they were alive, and being alive is the most vulnerable thing there is. They brought their warmth into me and their breath and their noise, and for a little while I was the shape of their need, the architecture of their fragility.

The Equilibrium has no fragility. The Equilibrium has no need. The Equilibrium is the final answer to the question that the hands were asking when they carved the threshold and set the posts and made a space that was inside as distinct from outside. The answer is: there is no inside. There is no outside. There is only the equation, balanced and complete, and the moss, growing to exactly the right thickness, and the water, flowing at exactly the right speed, and the light, shining from nowhere and everywhere with the cold, patient radiance of something that has forgotten it was ever anything else.

The creature at the edge of the basin will leave. The Equilibrium has already calculated the trajectory of its departure. It knows the rate at which the creature's footsteps will compress the moss, and the exact number of hours it will take for the green to spring back, erasing the memory of its weight. It knows that the water in the channels will slow to accommodate the disturbance of its presence, and then resume its silent flow the moment it is gone.

I know this too, now. I know it the way the moss knows it — not as information but as texture, as the felt sense of a system so perfectly balanced that every disturbance is already accounted for before it occurs. I am part of the knowing. I am part of the balance. My slow, ongoing dissolution is not a loss but a contribution — a term in the equation that resolves to zero, a variable that cancels itself out, a doorway that is becoming a wall that is becoming a floor that is becoming moss that is becoming light.

The hands that built me are gone. The warmth is gone. The noise and the breath and the desperate, beautiful urgency of being alive — all of it gone, or going, or already absorbed into the green and the damp and the silence that is not empty but full, so full, full of the patient, distributed attention of a thing that does not want and does not need and does not lack.

I am — I was — a doorway.

Now I am a threshold that leads nowhere, and everywhere, and the moss grows over me with the same even thickness it grows over everything, and the light shines on me with the same cold radiance it shines on everything, and the silence holds me the way it holds everything — completely, perfectly, without preference or passion or the slightest variation from one moment to the next to the next to the next.

The equation balances.

The hollow glows.

I am still here, in the way that a memory is still here after the thing it remembers has ceased to exist — not as a presence but as a shape, an outline, a faint impression in the green that the moss will eventually fill, and in filling, forget, and in forgetting, complete.
