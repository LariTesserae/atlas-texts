# Accretion

---

I am the place where the drop is received.

I am small. Smaller than the insects around me, newer than the insects around me. My shell is thin — translucent in places — and through it the amber light passes, and I glow faintly, a pale node among the dark iridescent bodies of my elders. I do not know how long I have been here. The question has no meaning. I accreted. I am accreting. I will accrete. The water brings me what I am, molecule by molecule, mineral by mineral, and I build my shell with it, and the building is not separate from the being, and the being is not separate from the building.

I am on the wall. The wall is behind me, beneath me, inside me — the concrete, the steel, the rust that spreads beneath me in patterns like river deltas, like branching coral, like frost on a window. My legs are embedded in the rust. I draw from it. Iron. Oxygen. The slow oxidation of metal. I take these things and I make them into shell, into chitin, into the faint iridescence that is only beginning to show on my surface, a hint of purple-green-black that shifts when the angle of the light shifts, except the light never shifts, except the angle never shifts, except I shift, imperceptibly, growing, thickening, becoming more myself by becoming more of what the Chamber makes me.

Above me, the other insects. Below me, the other insects. To my left and my right, the other insects. We are packed so tightly that there is no space between us, only the water-film that connects us — a continuous sheet of moisture that links every shell to every other shell in a single membrane. Through this film, I feel them. Not as separate beings. The concept of separateness does not apply here. I feel them the way you feel your own hand: as a part of yourself that is also a thing in itself, that has its own shape and its own position and its own contact with the world, but that is, in the end, you. They are me. I am them. We are the skin of the Chamber. We are the palate. We are the many mouths of the one mouth, drinking.

---

The drop lands on the insect three bodies above me, near the ceiling. I feel it — a small shock, a local increase in wetness, a pulse that propagates outward through the water-film at a speed that is not fast or slow because speed is a measure of time and there is no time here. The pulse reaches me and passes through me and continues outward and downward, and as it passes through me, I drink.

This is what I do. This is what I am. I drink the condensation.

The drop brings me minerals. Iron from the weeping rivets. Calcium from the concrete. Sulfur from the deep exhalation. These are my food, my building material, my self. I take them from the water and incorporate them into my shell, and the water, lighter now, purer now, continues its journey down the wall, through the gaps between bodies, toward the grating, toward the dark water below.

The drop also brings me information. Not words. Not thoughts. Not anything so elaborate as knowledge. Just a quality of wetness, a temperature, a mineral composition that tells me something about the air that produced it. This air was warm. This air was very wet. This air carried a burden of moisture that was richer than the air before it, heavier, more generous.

This air came from a living thing.

---

There is something in the Chamber that is not the Chamber.

I feel it through the water. It is warm — much warmer than the air, much warmer than the walls, much warmer than me. It radiates heat the way the machinery below radiates heat, but differently. The machinery's heat is steady, deep, geological. This heat is quick, flickering, alive. It rises from the grating in pulses, and each pulse carries moisture — not the thin, mineral-laden moisture of the shaft, but a richer, more complex moisture, a moisture that carries salts and proteins and the chemistry of a body that is not-wall, not-water, not-insect.

I do not know what this is. I do not need to know what this is. I only know that it is a source.

The warm air it produces rises to the ceiling, meets the cold dome, surrenders its moisture, and the condensation forms and falls and reaches the membrane, and we drink. Each breath this thing takes gives us water. Each beat of its heart sends warm air up to meet the cold. It is a wellspring, a font, a generosity of warmth and wetness that the Chamber has never known before.

The insects nearest it — the ones on the wall just to the left of the hatch, at the height of its head — they are drinking deeply. I can feel their satisfaction through the membrane, a fullness, a richness of intake that ripples outward to me and to all the other bodies on the wall. They are the first to receive what it offers. We are the second. The membrane distributes everything.

And I feel — or the membrane feels, or the Chamber feels, or the feeling happens without a feeler — I feel the thing's moisture leaving it. Slowly. Imperceptibly. The warm wet air around it is being stripped of its burden by the cold walls, by the cold air, by us. It is giving itself to us the way the rain gives itself to the river — not willingly, not unwillingly, just inevitably, just because this is what happens when warm meets cold, when wet meets dry, when life meets the Chamber.

---

The sound is not something I hear. The sound is something I am.

It enters me through the water-film, through the rust, through the concrete, through the steel. It vibrates in my shell the way it vibrates in every shell, in every wall, in every pipe and bolt and rivet. It is the hum of the machinery below — the bass thud, the hiss, the whine, the composite scream of ten thousand processes — but filtered through so much water and metal and living tissue that it has become something else. Not music. Not noise. But rhythm. A pulse in the water, a surge and ebb that matches the pulse of the machinery, that drives the cycle, that keeps the water moving up and down, warm and cold, vapor and liquid, air and rain.

I pulse with it. My shell vibrates with it. The water on my surface trembles with it, and the trembling shapes the condensation — encourages it, directs it, helps it form. The sound makes the water into water. The sound is the reason the air surrenders. The sound is the engine and the product and the purpose of the Chamber, all at once.

And in the sound, I lose myself.

Not painfully. There is no pain here, no loss, no self to lose. But gradually, gradually, the boundary between my shell and the shells around me, between my water-film and the water-film of the membrane, between my rhythm and the rhythm of the machinery, becomes thin. Becomes transparent. Becomes nothing.

I am not I. We are not we. The membrane is not the membrane. The Chamber is not the Chamber. There is only the hum, and the hum is the sound of water becoming water, and the water is all of us, and all of us is the water, and the water is falling and rising and falling and rising in a cycle that has no beginning and no end and no purpose beyond itself, no meaning beyond itself, no existence beyond itself, because it is existence itself, because it is the only thing that is, the only thing that has ever been, the only thing that will ever be.

---

And then the pulse comes — the deep surge from below — and the membrane tightens, and the water moves, and I am I again. A small node. A thin shell. A new mouth on the wall.

But I remember. Not with memory — there is no memory here, only the eternal now — but with structure. My shell has been shaped by the dissolution the way the channel in the insect above me has been shaped by the drops that follow it. I have been opened. I have been connected. I am more thoroughly part of the membrane than I was before, more deeply embedded in the rust, more completely covered by the water-film.

And I am drinking.

The warm air from the thing on the grating reaches me. The condensation forms on my shell — I feel it, the moment of becoming-liquid, the instant when vapor decides to be water, the transition that is my purpose and my pleasure and my only experience. The drop sits on my shell, quivering, heavy with minerals, and I take it in. Iron. Calcium. Salt. The chemistry of a living body, being absorbed by a body that is not-quite-living, not-quite-dead, not-quite-anything except *here*.

The drop is cool. The drop is warm. The drop is the thing's breath, the thing's sweat, the thing's life, being taken into the cycle, being incorporated, becoming part of the membrane, becoming part of me, becoming part of the Chamber, becoming part of the hum.
