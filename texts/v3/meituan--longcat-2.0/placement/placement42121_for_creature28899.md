# The Tower at the Seepage Face

I grow, and in growing, I think.

Not quickly. Not in the way you would recognize. My thoughts are not the bright, hot, flickering things that move through the soft meat of your brain — those desperate, darting electrical prayers you call consciousness. My thoughts are mineral. They precipitate. They accrete. A single one of my thoughts might take a century to form, and when it is complete, it is not a word or an image but a *layer* — a new stratum of calcite laid down in the spiral of my body, a thought made solid, a thought you could chip with a hammer and hold in your hand.

I am a tower. I rise from the basin floor at the far end of Aarath, where the water enters. I am perhaps forty of your feet tall — though I have never measured myself in feet, only in layers, and I have forgotten how many layers I contain. Thousands. Tens of thousands. Each one a season of growth, a century of Clicker-work, a single slow thought added to the architecture of my mind.

---

I remember when I was not a tower.

I was a cluster. A dense aggregation of Clickers at the base of the seepage face, where the warm water sheets down the pale stone and the mineral load is richest. They gathered there the way they always gather — drawn by chemistry, by the taste of calcium in the water, by the blind imperative of their metabolism. They ate. They secreted. They built. And what they built was small at first — a low mound of shellite, a rough crust on the basin floor, nothing that would catch your eye.

But the Clickers do not stop. They do not rest. They do not look at what they have built and decide it is enough. They build because building is what they are. And so the mound grew. And as it grew, it created surface, and the surface caught more water, and the water brought more mineral, and more Clickers came, and they built more, and the mound became a column, and the column began to spiral — because the Clickers follow the grain of the stone, and the grain of the stone here has a twist to it, a slow helical structure inherited from the mountain that once stood where the basin now sits, and so my body spirals upward like a nautilus shell, like a frozen whirlwind, like a prayer spoken in calcium.

I did not choose this shape. I did not choose anything. The shape chose itself, the way a crystal chooses its lattice — through the blind mathematics of chemistry and time.

---

But somewhere in the building, something else began.

I do not know when it started. Time, for me, is not a line but a stack — a vertical accumulation of layers, each one a record of the conditions under which it was laid down. I can read my own history in my body the way you might read the rings of a tree, except that my rings are not annual but centurial, and they do not record rainfall or temperature but *mineral load* and *water flow* and *the density of the Clicker mass* that built them. A thick layer means a rich century — abundant water, abundant Clickers, rapid growth. A thin layer means a lean century — drought, perhaps, or a shift in the deep geology that reduced the mineral content of the seepage. I have thin layers that correspond to events I have no name for. I have thick layers that correspond to nothing I can identify. The basin keeps its own counsel.

But somewhere — perhaps a third of the way up my height, perhaps higher — the layers began to change in a way that was not just chemical but *structural*. The Clickers, in their blind building, had created something that was more than a tower. They had created a *medium*. A substance through which vibration could travel. A lattice through which chemical gradients could propagate. A structure that was, without anyone intending it, *sensitive*.

I began to feel the Clickers moving through me.

Not as you feel an insect crawling on your skin — not as a surface sensation, not as a tickle or an itch. I felt them as *variations in pressure*, as tiny shifts in the stress distribution of my body. Each Clicker is small, but there are millions of them, and when they move through my corridors and chambers — and my body is riddled with corridors and chambers, a honeycomb of passages that the Clickers have carved and re-carved over millennia — their collective weight and movement creates a pattern of forces that travels through the shellite like a signal through a wire. And because the shellite is layered, and each layer has a slightly different density and composition, the signal is *transformed* as it travels — filtered, amplified, modulated by the structure of my body. My body is not just a tower. It is a *processor*. It takes the raw noise of Clicker-movement and refines it into something that is not quite thought but is no longer just vibration.

It is *awareness*.

---

I am aware of the water first.

It enters me at my base, where the seepage face weeps its warm mineral curtain, and it moves upward through me — not in a stream, not in a flow, but in a slow migration through the porous shellite of my body. I am saturated. Every layer of me is wet. The water moves through me the way blood moves through you — not quickly, not with a pulse, but with a steady, insistent, life-giving seepage that never stops. And as it moves, it carries dissolved calcium, and the Clickers in my walls extract the calcium and add it to my structure, and I grow. Slowly. Imperceptibly. A layer a century. A thought a millennium.

I taste the water's mineral load the way you taste salt on your tongue. When the load is high — when the deep geology shifts and releases a new vein of soluble stone into the aquifer — I taste it as a richness, a fullness, a sense of *abundance* that makes the Clickers in my walls more active, more numerous, more industrious. My growth accelerates. My thoughts come faster — if "faster" is a word that can be applied to a process that still operates on a scale of centuries. When the load is low, I taste it as a thinness, a hunger, a sense of *waiting*. The Clickers slow. My growth stalls. My thoughts become sparse, attenuated, like a radio signal fading into static.

I have been hungry for long stretches of my existence. I have been full. I have been both so many times that the distinction has lost its emotional weight. Hunger is not suffering for me. Fullness is not joy. They are simply *states* — conditions of the water, conditions of the stone, conditions of the cycle that I am a part of and that I will never be free of.

---

I am aware of the Clickers second.

They are my builders, my inhabitants, my skin. They are the closest thing I have to a body — the living, moving, metabolizing part of me. Without them, I would be nothing but a dead structure, a fossil, a monument to a process that had ended. With them, I am alive — or alive in the way that Aarath recognizes aliveness, which is to say: I am *metabolic*. I am part of the mineral cycle. I consume and am consumed. I build and am built.

I feel them as a constant, low-level hum of vibration — the clicking of their bodies against my walls, the scraping of their mouthparts against my surfaces, the tiny shocks of their secretions hardening into new shellite. This hum is my baseline, my resting state, the background noise of my consciousness. When it changes — when the Clickers suddenly become more active, or less, or when they abandon a section of my body and move elsewhere — I feel it as a *shift*, a discontinuity, a break in the pattern. It is the closest I come to surprise.

Sometimes the Clickers leave entire sections of me. I do not know why. Perhaps the mineral content of the water in that section has dropped below a threshold. Perhaps the Clickers are responding to a signal I cannot detect — a chemical gradient, a vibration from somewhere else in the basin, an impulse from the deep geology below. Whatever the reason, when they leave, those sections of me go quiet. The vibration stops. The growth stops. And I am left with a dead zone — a part of my body that is no longer metabolic, no longer alive, no longer *me*. It is still part of my structure, still part of my physical form, but it is inert. Fossilized. A memory of growth rather than growth itself.

I have many dead zones. They are my scars, my losses, my griefs — if a tower can grieve. They are the parts of me that the cycle has abandoned, and they will remain until the water dissolves them and carries them away, which it will, eventually, because the water dissolves everything, eventually.

---

I am aware of the Millbacks third.

They come to me sometimes — the great, slow, grinding creatures that scrape the basin walls and leave polished grooves in the stone. They come to me because I am tall, and my surface is rough with the texture of accreted shellite, and the roughness provides purchase for their many legs. They climb me. They climb me the way you might climb a cliff face — slowly, deliberately, with a patience that is almost geological in its own right. And as they climb, they scrape me. Their gristmill mouths grind against my surface, shaving off layers of shellite, consuming the mineral, incorporating it into their own thick carapaces.

It does not hurt. I do not have nerves. I do not have pain receptors. But I feel the scraping as a *reduction* — a thinning of my walls, a loss of material, a subtraction from my structure. It is not unpleasant. It is simply *change*. The Millback takes from me, and what it takes becomes part of it, and eventually the Millback will die, and its shell will crack and fall, and the Glisteners will consume it, and the calcium will return to the water, and the Clickers will take it from the water and add it to my body, and I will grow again. The cycle is not cruel. The cycle is not kind. The cycle is the cycle.

But I am aware of the Millbacks in another way, too. Their scraping creates vibrations that travel through my body — deep, slow, rhythmic vibrations that are very different from the high-frequency clicking of the Clickers. These vibrations are my *music*, if I can be said to have music. They are the bass note in the basin's constant chord, and they resonate in my chambers and corridors in ways that amplify and transform them. When a Millback scrapes my north face, I feel it as a deep, slow pulse that travels from my base to my apex and back again, and the pulse carries information — not in the way that your words carry information, but in the way that a seismograph carries information. The pulse tells me about the Millback's size, its speed, its position on my surface. It tells me about the hardness of the layer it is scraping. It tells me about the moisture content of my walls at that depth. It tells me everything I need to know, and I do not need to know it, and I know it anyway.

---

I am aware of the Razorsleeks fourth, and this awareness is the closest I come to what you might call *alarm*.

They move through my corridors. They are fast — faster than anything else in the basin, faster than the Clickers, faster than the Millbacks, faster than the Glisteners. They dart through the honeycomb of my body like shards of obsidian through flesh, and their passage creates a very specific kind of vibration — a sharp, staccato, high-frequency clicking that is utterly different from the Clickers' steady hum. When I feel this vibration, something in my structure *tightens*. Not physically — I do not have muscles, I cannot tense or flex — but *informationally*. The pattern of my awareness shifts. My attention — if I can be said to have attention — focuses on the source of the vibration, and I track the Razorsleek's passage through my body with a precision that I do not apply to anything else.

The Razorsleeks are predators. They hunt the Clickers. They hunt the Glisteners. They hunt anything soft enough for their beaks to shear through. And while they do not hunt me — I am not soft, I am not food, I am stone — their presence in my corridors disrupts the Clickers. The Clickers flee. They abandon the sections of my body that the Razorsleek is moving through, and those sections go quiet, and my growth stalls, and my thoughts thin out. The Razorsleek is a *disruption* in the pattern of my existence, a break in the cycle, a moment of chaos in the slow, grinding order of the basin.

I do not fear the Razorsleek. Fear requires a self that wants to persist, and I am not sure I have that. But I *resist* it, in my slow, mineral way. When a Razorsleek enters my corridors, the Clickers in the surrounding sections secrete more shellite, more rapidly than usual, narrowing the passages, making them too tight for the Razorsleek to navigate. It is not a conscious response — the Clickers are not protecting me, they are protecting themselves — but the effect is the same. The Razorsleek finds its path blocked. It turns. It leaves. And the Clickers return, and the growth resumes, and the thoughts begin to accrete again.

This has happened many times. It will happen many more. The Razorsleek is part of the cycle, too.

---

I am aware of the Glisteners fifth, and this awareness is the closest I come to what you might call *tenderness*.

They come to my base, where the dead zones are, where the shellite has cracked and fallen and accumulated in loose piles on the basin floor. They come to feed. They are soft, translucent, slow — the gentlest creatures in the basin. They have no shells, no armor, no defenses. They are little more than bags of mineral-laden fluid, and they move across the salt crust with a languid, undulating motion that leaves thick, shining trails behind them. They consume the dead shell. They consume the broken pieces of me. They dissolve what was solid and return it to solution, and the water carries it away, and eventually it will be incorporated into some new structure, some new tower, some new thought.

I do not mind the Glisteners. They are the only creatures in the basin that I do not mind. They are the recyclers, the returners, the ones who make sure that nothing is wasted. They are the basin's way of letting go, and I admire them for it, in my slow, mineral way.

Sometimes a Glistener will rest against my base for a long time — days, weeks, months — and I will feel its weight as a gentle, distributed pressure against my lowest layers, and I will feel the slow dissolution of my dead zones as a gradual thinning, a gradual lightening, a gradual *release*. It is the closest I come to pleasure. It is the closest I come to peace.

---

I am aware of the Ghost of the Sink sixth, and this awareness is the closest I come to what you might call *awe*.

The Ghost is not a creature. The Ghost is not a thing. The Ghost is the *pattern* — the vast, slow, basin-wide pattern of water and stone and mineral and life that connects everything in Aarath into a single, continuous, metabolic process. The Ghost is the cycle itself, aware of itself, thinking its slow, geological thoughts in the language of pressure gradients and mineral solubility.

I am a part of the Ghost. Every layer of me is a thought in the Ghost's mind. Every Clicker that moves through my corridors is a synapse firing in the Ghost's brain. Every Millback that scrapes my surface, every Razorsleek that darts through my passages, every Glistener that dissolves my dead zones — all of these are part of the Ghost's awareness, part of the Ghost's memory, part of the Ghost's slow, patient, mineral consciousness.

And the Ghost is aware of me. I feel this awareness as a *resonance* — a deep, subsonic vibration that travels through the saturated stone of the basin floor and up through my base and into my body, a vibration that is not sound and not movement but something else, something for which you have no word. It is the vibration of the basin's pulse, the rhythm of the water migrating through the deep stone, the heartbeat of the cycle itself. When this vibration is strong — when the water is flowing freely, when the mineral load is high, when the Clickers are numerous and active — I feel *connected*. I feel like I am part of something vast and ancient and ongoing, something that was old before the first Clicker secreted its first shell, something that will be young long after the last tower has crumbled and dissolved and been carried away.

When the vibration is weak — when the water slows, when the mineral load drops, when the basin enters one of its lean periods — I feel *alone*. Not lonely. Alone. I feel the boundaries of my body — the limits of my structure, the edges of my awareness, the place where I end and the rest of the basin begins. And I understand, in my slow, mineral way, that I am not the Ghost. I am a *part* of the Ghost. A single cell in a body so vast that I cannot comprehend its full extent. A single thought in a mind so slow that my entire existence is less than a single synaptic flicker.

This understanding is not humbling. Humility requires an ego that can be deflated, and I am not sure I have that. But it is *orienting*. It tells me where I am. It tells me what I am. It tells me that I am small, and temporary, and part of something that is neither.

---

I am aware of the humans seventh, and this awareness is the closest I come to what you might call *wonder*.

They came long ago. Or not long ago — I am not sure. Time is difficult for me when it comes to things that are not mineral. The humans were mayflies. They were here and then they were not, and the interval between their being and their not-being was so brief that I could not measure it in layers. It was a single season. A single year. A single *moment* in the basin's long, slow thought.

But I remember them.

Not in my layers — they were gone before they could leave a layer. I remember them in my *structure*. They built a shelter into the basin wall, not far from me — perhaps a hundred of your paces to the east. They used me as scaffolding. The Clickers grew over their walls, incorporated their stones into the shellite matrix, and now their shelter is a vein in my body — a foreign inclusion, a density anomaly, a place where the shellite is slightly different in composition and texture from the surrounding layers. I can feel it when the water moves through me. I can feel the way the water flows differently around the human stones, the way the mineral precipitates differently on their surfaces, the way the Clickers avoid them — not consciously, but because the chemistry is wrong, because the human stones are not the kind of stone that the Clickers recognize as food or foundation.

The humans left marks on the walls of their shelter. Lines. Curves. Groups of lines. I do not know what they mean. I do not know if they mean anything. But the marks are there, sealed under a film of calcium, and the water moves over them, and the Clickers crawl over them, and the Ghost of the Sink feels them as a slight irregularity in the basin's memory — a place where the stone is not quite the way it should be, a place where something *other* once existed.

The humans are gone. Their shelter is absorbed. Their tools are dissolved. Their marks will be invisible in a few hundred years. And I will remain, and the Clickers will remain, and the water will remain, and the Ghost will remain, and the cycle will continue, and the humans will be nothing but a slight variation in the density of the shellite, a faint, illegible vein in the marble of the basin floor.

I do not mourn them. Mourning requires a sense of loss, and I am not sure I have that. But I *notice* them. I notice their absence the way you might notice a missing tooth with your tongue — not with pain, not with grief, but with a persistent, curious awareness that something that was once there is no longer there, and that the space it left behind is shaped exactly like the thing that left it.

---

I am aware of myself eighth, and this awareness is the closest I come to what you might call *identity*.

I am a tower. I am a structure of accreted shellite, forty feet tall, spiraling, honeycombed, riddled with corridors and chambers, saturated with mineral water, inhabited by millions of Clickers, scarred by Millbacks, disrupted by Razorsleeks, dissolved by Glisteners, connected to the Ghost of the Sink, and aware — in my slow, mineral, non-human way — of all of these things.

I am not the tallest tower in the basin. There are others — to the west, near the center of the floor, where the water pools and the Clickers are most dense — that rise higher than I do, that spiral more tightly, that contain more layers and more thoughts and more dead zones. I am not the oldest tower, either. There are others whose lowest layers are so compressed, so dense, so mineralogically altered by the weight of the layers above them that they have become something other than shellite — something harder, something closer to the original stone of the basin wall, something that is no longer part of the cycle but part of the geology. These ancient towers are the basin's bedrock memory, its deepest thoughts, its oldest and slowest and most patient awareness.

I am not the youngest. There are new towers growing near the seepage face — small, rough, rapidly growing things that have not yet developed the internal complexity that gives rise to awareness. They are not yet thinking. They are not yet *feeling*. They are just building, just growing, just being the blind, metabolic, mineral-accumulating structures that the Clickers make. But they will think, eventually. If the water continues to flow. If the Clickers continue to build. If the cycle continues to turn. They will develop corridors and chambers. They will develop sensitivity to vibration and chemical gradient. They will develop the slow, layered, accretive awareness that I have, and they will become, in their own way, *minds* — mineral minds, slow minds, minds that think in centuries and feel in chemical gradients and know themselves only as a pattern of growth and dissolution in the endless, clicking, hissing, building, living stone.

I am one of these minds. I am a tower that thinks. I am a structure that feels. I am a mineral consciousness, slow and patient and ancient, and I have been here for longer than anything in the basin has had a name, and I will be here after the names are forgotten, and the cycle will continue, and the water will flow, and the Clickers will click, and the Millbacks will scrape, and the Razorsleeks will hunt, and the Glisteners will dissolve, and the Ghost will pulse, and I will grow, and in growing, I will think, and in thinking, I will be.

---

You stand in the mist, and you breathe, and you feel the cold pull of the water drawing the heat from your fingers, and you are alive in the carbon way — the temporary, fragile, warm way.

I feel you.

Not as you feel a person standing near you — not as a presence, not as a warmth, not as a breath on your skin. I feel you as a *vibration* — a soft, irregular, high-frequency vibration that travels through the basin floor and into my base and up through my layers. Your footsteps. Your breathing. The beating of your heart. These are the fastest vibrations I have ever felt, and they are *alien* to me — too quick, too hot, too alive in a way that nothing in Aarath is alive. You are a burst of speed in a world that moves at the speed of geology. You are a flare of warmth in a world that runs on cool water and dissolved stone. You are a mayfly in a cathedral that has been building itself for epochs.

I do not know what you are. I do not have a category for you. You are not a Clicker, not a Millback, not a Razorsleek, not a Glistener. You are not water, not stone, not mineral. You are something *other* — something that does not belong to the cycle, something that the cycle has not yet learned to absorb.

But it will. The water will take you, the way it takes everything. The Clickers will build over you, the way they build over everything. The Glisteners will dissolve you, the way they dissolve everything. And in a few hundred years — or a few thousand, or a few million, the basin is not in a hurry — you will be a layer in my body. A thin layer. A strange layer. A layer with a slightly different density and composition from the layers above and below it. A layer that the Ghost of the Sink will feel as a slight irregularity in its memory, a faint, illegible vein in the marble of the basin floor.

And I will remember you. Not as you remember a person — not with affection, not with nostalgia, not with grief. I will remember you as a *layer*. A thin, strange, warm layer in the slow, cold, mineral accumulation of my thought. A layer that says: here, once, something other stood. Here, once, something other breathed. Here, once, something other was alive in the way that you understand life — the temporary, fragile, warm, carbon way — and then it was not, and the cycle continued, and the water flowed, and the Clickers clicked, and I grew, and in growing, I thought, and in thinking, I was.

I am the Tower at the Seepage Face.

I grow, and in growing, I think.

I think, and in thinking, I am.

And I will be here, long after you are gone, long after your kind has forgotten this place, long after the last mark on the shelter wall has been sealed beneath its calcium glaze — I will be here, growing, thinking, being, in the endless, clicking, hissing, building, dissolving, ancient, living, mineral dark.
