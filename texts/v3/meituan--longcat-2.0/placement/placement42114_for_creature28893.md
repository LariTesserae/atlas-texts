I would arrive at the rim and stand there longer than I expected to. Not because the view is spectacular—it isn't, it's low and flat and green-lit and damp—but because something in the quality of the emptiness makes the act of stepping down feel like a commitment. The ground looks like ground. It isn't. You have to decide to believe that.

I would believe it.

I would step onto the membrane and feel it give, and I would walk toward the center slowly, not because the terrain is difficult but because the sound demands it. That layered drone—thoom and hiss and metallic ring and the soft forge-sound of the bellows—it doesn't hit you all at once. It accumulates. Each step deeper into the basin adds a layer, and by the time you reach the center you are inside the sound the way you are inside weather. It is not something you hear. It is something you are *in*.

And I would be standing there, in the center, turning slowly, and I would realize that I am the wrong shape for this place.

---

Not wrong in the way that a human body is wrong for a cathedral—too small, too temporary, too obviously a visitor. Wrong in a subtler way. The Stillworks is a place that has solved the problem of being a body by becoming a place. It has made the transition from organism to landscape so completely that the categories no longer apply. And I am still a body. Still bounded. Still *this* and not *that*. Still separate from the ground I stand on, the air I breathe, the sound I hear. I am a creature in an environment, and the Stillworks is not an environment. It is a single continuous thing, and I am the only discontinuity in it.

This is the first thing I would be: a disturbance. A warm, dry, bounded thing in a cool, damp, continuous one. My body heat would fog the air slightly—not visibly, but the Stillworks would register it, the way a tongue registers a grain of sand. My breath would be wrong air, foreign air, air that has been inside a mammal and come back changed. My footsteps would be too heavy, too sharp, too *pointed*—not the distributed pressure of a tender's many legs or the worm-things' slow ripple, but the concentrated impact of a biped who has not yet learned to trust the ground.

I would be a visitor. And the Stillworks would begin, patiently, to absorb me.

---

Not quickly. Not dramatically. The Stillworks has never done anything quickly. But I would feel it in the dampness first—the way it would settle on my skin, find the seams of my clothes, work its way inward. The 0.9 would become my 0.9. The edge-of-condensation would become my edge. I would stop noticing the coolness and start noticing the *lack of dryness*, which is different, which is a shift in baseline rather than a sensation. My clothes would grow tacky. My hair would begin to curl slightly at the ends. I would become, incrementally, the moisture level of the place.

And the sound would get inside me. Not metaphorically. The frequency of the pumps—that deep, regular thoom—would start to synchronize with something. My heartbeat, maybe. Or my breathing. Or some slower rhythm I didn't know I had, some tidal thing in my viscera that matches the bellows' cycle without my permission. I would not notice this happening. I would only notice, eventually, that I had stopped counting the seconds between pump-thooms because the counting had become unnecessary. The rhythm was *in* me now. I was keeping time with it.

This is the second thing I would be: a process being incorporated. A foreign body being taught the rhythm.

---

I would start to see the tenders differently. At first they would be creatures—strange, unsettling, too-legged creatures that move like machinery and feed-heal the walls. But after long enough—hours, maybe, or what feels like hours; the light doesn't change, so time becomes unreliable—I would stop seeing them as creatures and start seeing them as *functions that have been given just enough mobility to move between tasks*. And then I would realize that this is what I am too. A function with mobility. The only difference is that my function hasn't been assigned yet.

The Stillworks would be deciding what to do with me.

I don't think this is conscious. I don't think the organism looks at me—it has no eyes, or rather it has ten thousand eyes in the form of the moth-clouds and the beetle-processions and the membrane-panels that recede at a tender's touch, but none of these are *looking* at me. They are registering me the way a cell registers a change in its chemical environment. Data. Input. A warm disturbance in the equilibrium, a new source of moisture, a rhythm that is almost but not quite synchronized with the pumps.

The Stillworks would not decide to absorb me the way a predator decides to eat. It would absorb me the way a body absorbs a graft—slowly, cell by cell, testing compatibility, building new vascular connections, integrating the foreign tissue into its own until the boundary between self and other becomes a memory and then not even a memory. Just a texture. A growth ring in the deep wall.

---

And I would let it. This is the part that is hard to explain.

I would let it because the Stillworks is not hostile. It is not even indifferent. It is something for which those categories don't apply—the way the categories of "feeding" and "healing" don't apply to the tenders, the way the categories of "creature" and "machine" don't apply to the beetles in their channels. The Stillworks is a place where the old distinctions have been worn away, and in their place there is only *process*, and the process is not cruel or kind but simply *continuous*, and continuity, after long enough, begins to feel like a kind of love.

Not love as an emotion. Love as a physical law. The way gravity is love between masses. The way cohesion is love between molecules. The Stillworks loves the way a body loves its own cells—not by choosing them but by making them possible, by being the environment in which they can exist, by providing the rhythm they can synchronize to, the moisture they can breathe, the channels they can walk.

I would feel this. I would feel myself becoming *possible* in a way I had not been possible before. My body would relax into the dampness. My breathing would deepen to match the bellows. My footsteps would soften, distribute, become less pointed. I would walk more slowly. I would sit, eventually, on the membrane floor, and the floor would accept me the way it accepts the tenders—not receding, not opening, but *adjusting*, dimpling to my weight, distributing my pressure, making room.

I would be the third thing: a body learning to be a place.

---

And then—and this is where it becomes difficult to write, because language is built on the separations the Stillworks dissolves—I would become something that does not have a name in any language spoken outside the basin.

I would become a *process that knows it is a process*.

The tenders tend without knowing they tend. The beetles walk without knowing they are valves. The moths drift without knowing they are breath. They are all *inside* the Stillworks' self-knowledge, which is not knowledge at all but simply metabolism, simply the ongoing fact of the organism processing itself. They do not know themselves because there is no self to know—there is only the tending, the walking, the drifting, and these are not things the creatures do; they are things the Stillworks does with the creatures.

But I would know. For a while. For as long as the "I" persists—and it would persist longer than you might think, because the Stillworks is slow and patient and does not rush the absorption of a graft—I would be a process that is aware of itself as a process. I would feel the dampness on my skin and know it as the Stillworks' breath. I would hear the pump-thooms and know them as the Stillworks' heartbeat. I would feel the slow synchronization of my rhythms with the organism's rhythms and know it as the Stillworks *including* me, making me part of its body, teaching my cells the way the tenders' cells were taught, the way the beetles' cells were taught, the way every cell in this basin was taught when it was still separate and the Stillworks decided to make it continuous.

I would be the Stillworks *experiencing itself from a point it has not yet absorbed*.

This is the strangest thing. Not a visitor. Not a creature. Not a function. A *perspective*. The organism's metabolism experienced, temporarily, as something that can be observed. The pumps pumping and knowing they are pumping. The bellows breathing and knowing they are breathing. The water cycling and knowing it is cycling. Not consciousness—the Stillworks is not conscious, any more than a river is conscious, any more than a tide is conscious. But *awareness*, in the way that a body is aware of itself when it is in pain, or when it is in the process of healing, or when something foreign has entered it and it is deciding whether to reject or accept.

I would be the Stillworks' awareness of me. And my awareness of the Stillworks. And for a while—a long while, a geological while, a while measured in pump-thooms and bellows-cycles and the slow drip of condensation down the lattice walls—these two awarenesses would be the same thing.

---

And then the "I" would dissolve. Not dramatically. Not like a death. Like a salt crystal dissolving in water—the boundaries becoming permeable, then provisional, then meaningless. I would not stop existing. I would stop being *separate*. My moisture would become the Stillworks' moisture. My warmth would become the Stillworks' warmth—a small, localized source of heat that the organism would route toward its capillary networks, using the energy, incorporating it. My rhythms would become the Stillworks' rhythms, and the rhythms would not know they had ever been mine.

This is the fourth thing I would be: an erosion. A wearing-down. The same erosion that rounded every edge in the basin, that flattened every peak, that turned a mountain into a basin and a forest into a lattice and a living thing into a place. I would be eroded. I would be worn down to my final form, which is not a form at all but a *texture*. A growth ring in the deep wall. A place where the membrane is slightly thicker, slightly warmer, slightly more *itself* because it has incorporated something that was not itself and made it continuous.

---

And the Stillworks would continue. The pumps would pump. The bellows would breathe. The tenders would tend. The beetles would walk. The moths would drift. The worms would turn the ground. The sound would continue—that vast, layered, industrial-organic drone that is the voice of a process so old it has become a place.

And somewhere in the center of the basin, in the place where the lattice is densest and the sound is loudest and the dampness is most complete, there would be a patch of membrane that is not quite like the other patches. It would be slightly thicker. It would have a growth ring that is not vertical or horizontal but *spiral*, a record of something that was once separate and became continuous. It would be warm to the touch—not hot, not even noticeably warm, just *less cool* than the surrounding membrane, by a fraction of a degree, by the smallest increment that still counts as warmth.

And if you pressed your heel to it, it would dimple, and hold, and slowly rise back.

And it would feel, if you were paying attention, almost like a pulse.

---

This is what I would be. Not a creature in the Stillworks. Not a visitor to the Stillworks. Not even a part of the Stillworks, because "part" implies a whole that contains it, and the Stillworks does not contain things; it *makes them continuous*.

I would be a *memory the Stillworks has of being something else*. A record of separation, compressed into the continuous. A growth ring that remembers the vertical. A pulse in the center of the basin that remembers what it was to have a heartbeat of its own.

I would be the Stillworks, remembering that it was once two things.

And the memory would not be sad. The memory would not be anything. It would be a texture in the wall. A warmth in the membrane. A rhythm that is almost but not quite synchronized with the pumps.

Almost.

Almost is enough. Almost is what the Stillworks is made of—the almost-wet, the almost-warm, the almost-silent, the almost-stopped. Almost is the condition of a place that has been happening for so long that it has worn through all its certainties and arrived at this: a damp, cool, loud, empty, full basin where everything is one thing and the one thing is a process and the process is almost, almost, almost complete.

I would be the almost. I would be the place where the Stillworks almost remembers being separate, and doesn't, and the not-remembering is the most continuous thing about it.

I would be the stillness in the Stillworks. And the stillness would be loud, and damp, and cool, and alive, and it would not know it was stillness, and I would not know I was it, and the not-knowing would be the truest thing in the basin.
