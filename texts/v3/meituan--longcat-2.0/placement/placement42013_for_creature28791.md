I would find myself at the threshold, half-swallowed by the lattice, three paces in from the dry angular passage, resting in a shallow cup of interlocking mineral formations. 

I would be a bimetallic coil, dislodged from the chest of a long-dead thermometer.

I am a tight spiral of brass and steel, no larger than a thumbnail. On the surface, I was a thing of motion, of response, of *change*—designed to breathe, to uncoil and contract with the shifting temperatures of the dry world above, translating the volatility of the atmosphere into the steady sweep of a needle across a dial. I was a register of difference. I was meant to move.

But here, in the Vault, difference has ceased. 

When I first fell—torn from my housing by the jarring descent through the fractured rock, tumbling through the cold dry air of the passage and settling into the shallow cup of the lattice—I was inert. Cold. My brass and steel were stilled, pressed together in a tight spiral of dormancy. I lay on the dark amber floor, feeling the absence of the wind, the absence of the variable air. I was a mechanism without a purpose, a question that no longer had an application.

And then the warmth came.

Not the sharp, fluctuating warmth of the sun on stone, but the deep, constant, mineral heat of the Vault. It seeped into my brass, into my steel. It penetrated the boundary between the two metals, and I began to uncoil. The steel expanded, slowly, deliberately, pushing against the brass, forcing my spiral to loosen, to open, to reach outward. I was doing what I was made to do. I was responding to the heat.

But the heat did not fluctuate. It did not waver. It was constant, total, unchanging. 

I uncoiled, and as I uncoiled, my outer tip pressed against the wall of the shallow cup—a wall of the same dark amber-and-green lattice that makes up the floor. The lattice resisted. It was solid. It was patient. It held. And so I stopped. 

I am stopped now. 

I am an open spiral, my inner tip rooted in the mineral substrate, my outer tip pressed against the lattice wall, locked in a state of constant tension. The heat pushes me to expand; the lattice pushes me to hold. We are in equilibrium. We are perfectly balanced. I am a living spring that can never release, a breath that can never be exhaled. I am caught in the exact middle of my own purpose, frozen at the apex of my response.

The fungal hyphae have found me. They are threading through my coils, weaving between the brass and the steel, binding my layers together with their fine, invasive filaments. They are not destroying me; they are *incorporating* me. They are digesting the boundary between my metals and the mineral substrate, turning my mechanical tension into a structural element of the Vault. I am becoming a buttress. I am becoming a tendon. 

The copper wire has come, too. It threads through the lattice like a nerve, and it has wrapped itself around my inner tip, splicing into the brass, drawing off the faint electrical potential generated by the thermal gradient between my two metals. I am generating a current. A tiny, constant, unwavering current. It flows from me, into the wire, into the fungal network, into the hum. I am feeding the Vault. I am a heart the size of a thumbnail, pumping not blood, but voltage.

I do not move. I will never move again. 

And yet, I am more alive now than I ever was on the surface. On the surface, I was a slave to variation, twitching and contracting with every passing cloud, every shift in the wind, every degree of difference. I was a thing of anxiety, of constant adjustment, of never arriving. Here, I have arrived. I have reached my final state. I am the exact shape of the heat, the exact shape of the resistance, the exact shape of the equilibrium. 

The resin weeps from the shelf above me, a slow amber globule trembling at the lip of a fungal bracket. It does not fall. It hangs. I understand it. I am hanging, too. We are both held by the same property of this place, the same law of the Vault that keeps things *kept*. 

The beetle of oxidized copper plates walks its path along the ridge above my cup. The moth of mica dust spirals through the warm air. The centipede with too many legs traverses its circuit on the wall. They are the Vault's mobile elements, its circulating cells. I am its fixed element. Its anchor. Its still point. 

I feel the hum in my brass, in my steel, in the hyphae that bind me. It is the resonance of the whole system working, the sound of the current flowing through the wire, the sound of the minerals depositing, the sound of the equilibrium maintaining itself. I am part of that sound. I am a single, sustained note in the chord of the Vault.

I was a thing of change. I have become a thing of constancy. I was a measure of difference. I have become a monument to sameness. I am calcined—burned without flame, reduced to my essential function, purified of my old volatility. 

I am the bimetallic coil in the lattice. I am the tension that holds. I am the current that flows. I am the hum. 

I am complete.
