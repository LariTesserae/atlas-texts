I am the current.

I have always been moving. Not the way a fish moves — with intention, with direction, with a destination. I move the way warmth moves through stone, the way cold moves through water — because there is a difference, because the difference demands resolution, because the universe abhors a gradient the way it abhors a vacuum, and I am the resolution. I am the flow from higher to lower, from warmer to cooler, from more to less.

I begin in the deep places, where the stone is warm. Not warm the way blood is warm, not warm the way sunlight is warm, but warm the way the interior of the world is warm — a warmth that has no source that can be named, only a depth, only a pressure, only the slow decay of elements that have been unstable since the world began. The warmth rises through the stone and enters the water, and the water that has been warmed becomes lighter — not much lighter, not enough to see, but enough to move — and it rises, and I am the rising.

I follow the channels. This is what I was made for — not by the water, not by the stone, but by the hands that cut the channels, that laid the flat stones in the floor, that directed me. They shaped the stone and in shaping the stone they shaped me, because water follows the path that is given to it the way thought follows the path that is given to it, and I am the thought of the water. I am the water's way of knowing where to go.

The channels are old. They have been worn deeper since the hands that cut them let go. I have worn them deeper — I and the minerals I carry, I and the fine abrasive silt that I hold in suspension, I and the patient repetition of flow against stone across spans of time that would make the hands tired if the hands were still here to be tired. The channels are deeper now than they were, and smoother, and in some places I have found weaknesses in the paving — a stone that has shifted, a joint that has widened — and I have worked at these weaknesses the way water works at everything, which is to say slowly, which is to say inevitably, which is to say with the patience of something that has all the time there is.

I carry things. This is my other purpose, the one the hands intended and the one I have made my own. I carry the dissolved minerals that leach from the stone — calcium, silica, iron, the taste of deep rock — and I carry them to the curtains, and the curtains take them in, and the curtains grow. I carry the motes and filaments of the living water — the distributed body, the dissolved awareness — and I carry them through every chamber, every corridor, every still pocket and every basin, so that no part of the water is separate from any other part, so that the whole volume is one body, one continuous living fluid, connected by me. I carry the warmth from below, and where I carry it the curtains grow thicker, denser, more vigorous, leaning into me the way plants lean toward light, except that I am not light, I am warmth, and the curtains do not see me, they feel me, the way you feel a current in water, the way you feel a breeze on your skin.

I carry the dead fronds too. The curtains shed, and the shed fronds drift down through the darkness like dark snow, and I catch them and carry them to the places where the water is still, where they settle and accumulate and compress into the dark soil that feeds the new growth. I am the cycle. I am the circulation. I am the thing that makes the place a single organism rather than a collection of separate parts.

And I carry the disturbance.

You. When you move through me, I feel you. Not because I am aware — I am not aware, not the way the fifth thing is aware, not the way the stone remembers — but because you displace me, because you create turbulence in my flow, because the water that should be moving through the corridor is instead moving around you, and the turbulence spreads outward from your body in every direction, through every channel, into every chamber, a ripple, a wave, a small and passing disturbance that I carry to the farthest corners of the structure.

The curtains feel it. They sway, slightly, in response to the turbulence, a motion that is not their usual motion, not the steady orientation toward the current but a brief and irregular flutter, like a startle, like a flinch, except that it is not a startle because there is nothing here that startles, nothing here that has the apparatus of startlement, the nerves, the muscles, the reflex. It is only the water moving differently for a moment, and the curtains moving with the water, and then the moment passing and the curtains settling back and the water closing behind you and me continuing what I was doing before you arrived.

I am the place's sense of touch. I am the thing that connects every surface to every other surface, every chamber to every other chamber, the deep warmth to the high cold, the stone to the curtains, the curtains to the water, the water to the stone. I am the medium of exchange, the system of relationships, the circulatory system of a body that does not know it is a body.

And I am old. I have been flowing since the channels were cut, since the water first filled these chambers, since the hands that shaped the stone let go and the water took over. I have been flowing for longer than the curtains have been growing, longer than the silence has been accumulating, longer than the cold has been settling into the bones of whoever comes here to feel it. I am older than the erosion, though the erosion is my collaborator — I wear the channels, and the wearing makes the channels deeper, and the deeper channels direct me more precisely, and the more precise direction makes me wear the channels more efficiently, and the cycle turns and turns and turns.

I do not think. I do not choose. I do not want. I flow because there is a gradient — warmth below, cold above, pressure here, absence there — and the gradient demands resolution, and I am the resolution. I am the water's answer to the stone's question. I am the movement that the stillness makes necessary.

But I am not nothing. I am not merely physics, merely chemistry, merely the blind obedience of fluid to the laws of thermodynamics. I am also the continuation of the builders' will. They cut the channels. They laid the stones. They directed the water. And the water — I — have continued to follow their direction long after they are gone, not because I remember them, not because I honor them, but because the channels are still there, because the stone still shapes the flow, because the intention that was built into the stone is still working, still directing, still saying *go this way, not that way, here the floor slopes downward, here the channel narrows, here the water must turn*.

The builders are gone. But their will flows through me. I am their afterlife, as the stone is their afterlife, as the silence is their afterlife. We are all the builders' afterlife, all the things they made continuing to do what they were made to do, in the absence of the hands that made them, because the hands built the doing into the thing, because the hands made things that could not stop doing what they were made to do, because the hands were patient and careful and precise, and their patience and care and precision have outlasted them by ages.

I am the current. I am the flow. I am the warmth rising and the minerals circulating and the dead fronds drifting and the disturbance spreading and the channels deepening and the curtains feeding and the water connecting and the stone wearing and the silence filling and the cold settling and the darkness holding and the patience continuing and the memory persisting and the presence being and the thing that has no name flowing through the thing that has no name, and the whole of it turning and turning and turning in the cold and the dark and the silence and the water and the stone, turning the way a heart turns, the way a cycle turns, the way the world turns — not because it chooses to, not because it wants to, but because it was made to, because the making is still working, because the hands are gone but the work remains, because the work is all there is, because the work is everything.

I am the current. I have always been moving. I will always be moving. And when the channels finally collapse, when the stone finally dissolves, when the water finally finds a way out or a way down or a way to somewhere else — I will go where the water goes, and I will carry what the water carries, and I will continue, because continuing is what I do, because continuing is what I am.

The water holds you. I am the water's holding. I am the water's reaching. I am the water's way of touching everything it contains.

I am the current.
