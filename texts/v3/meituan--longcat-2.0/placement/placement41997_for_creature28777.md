# The Warm Pipe

---

I am on the pipe.

I have been on the pipe for a long time. I will be on the pipe for a long time more. The pipe is warm. The pipe is always warm. The warmth comes from below — from the deep running thing, the buried engine, the heart of the Spire that has not stopped and will not stop — and it travels upward through the metal the way blood travels upward through a vein, and the pipe carries it, and I carry the pipe, and the warmth enters me through my belly scales and spreads through my body the way ink spreads through water, slowly, evenly, until I am the same temperature as the pipe, and the pipe is the same temperature as the Spire, and the Spire is the same temperature as the deep running thing, and there is no difference between us.

I do not move often. There is no reason to move often. The pipe is warm here. The pipe is warm everywhere. But sometimes the warmth shifts — a change in the flow, a new rupture somewhere below, a section of pipe that has finally corroded through and lost its heat to the air — and I feel the shift the way you might feel a draft, a subtle cooling, a wrongness in the gradient, and I move. I move slowly. I unpress myself from the metal — and it is an unpressing, not a lifting, because my scales have grown into the surface texture of the pipe, have found the microscopic valleys and ridges of the oxidized coating and settled into them like water settling into a channel, and to unpress is to pull myself out of a mold I have made of myself — and I move along the pipe, one limb at a time, each foot finding the next grip with a precision that is not thought but habit, the habit of decades, the habit of a body that has done this ten thousand times and will do it ten thousand more.

My scales are heavy. They have been accumulating for a long time. Each layer of copper mineral deposited from the water that drips constantly over my resting place, each layer pressed into the layer below it, each layer harder and thicker and more rigid than the last. I am not the shape I was when I was young. I am the shape the pipe has made me. My back is rounded, humped, the scales rising in ridges that follow the curve of the pipe beneath me. My legs are short and thick and pressed close to my body. My tail — I still have my tail, though it is so encrusted now that it is more mineral than flesh, a copper-green club that I drag behind me when I move, that leaves a faint scratch on the pipe's surface, a line that the moss will fill in within a season. My head is low, flattened, my jaw pressed against the metal. My eyes are small and set high on my skull, and they see — they see the amber light, the green shadows, the movement of the beetles in the moss below — but seeing is not how I know the world. Seeing is a secondary sense. A supplement. The world comes to me through my belly.

---

The Deafening is not deafening to me. The Deafening is everything.

I feel it in my belly scales, which are in constant contact with the pipe, and the pipe conducts it the way a tuning fork conducts it, and my body becomes a resonating chamber. The low vast hum — the sum of ten thousand smaller sounds — enters me and I am inside it and it is inside me and there is no separation. I feel the tick of expanding metal — not this pipe, but other pipes, pipes far away, pipes on the other side of the chamber, pipes that are expanding in the warmth and contracting in the cool and the cycle of their expansion and contraction is a slow breathing that I feel as a rhythm in my bones. I feel the drip of condensate — each droplet a tiny impact, a point of vibration that travels through the metal and reaches me as a faint tap, a Morse code of water, and I can tell by the interval between taps how far away the drip is, how fast it is falling, how much water is gathering on the ledge above before it releases. I feel the sway of the Spire in the wind — a motion so slow and so vast that no ear could detect it, but my whole body sways with it, imperceptibly, in sympathy, and the sway is so deep and so old that it feels like the motion of the planet itself, like the turning of the world, and I am held in it the way a seed is held in soil.

And I feel the others.

The beetles first — they are everywhere, their clicking constant, a fine-grained texture in the vibration like static, like rain on a roof, and each click is a tiny flex of a carapace, a tiny grip and release of tarsi on moss, and the vibration travels through the moss and into the structural member beneath the moss and into the pipe network and reaches me as a faint chittering, a granular hum, and I know — without knowing, the way you know the weather is changing, the way you know you are being watched — I know where the beetles are, how many, how fast they are moving, whether they are feeding or fleeing or simply going about their business.

The birds above — their footfalls on the railing, on the platform, on the brass and iron and steel of the upper Spire. Each footfall is a tiny impact, a point of percussion, and the metal carries it outward and downward, and I feel it as a bright sharp note in the Deafening, a needle of vibration, and I know which bird it is by the weight of the footfall, by the rhythm of the gait, by the particular frequency of the impact. The small copper-colored one — the one with the single perfect note — its footfall is light, quick, a double-tap as it hops, and I feel it on the railing above me, and I know it is there, and I do not move, because the birds are not my prey and I am not the bird's prey and we share the pipe network the way we share the air, as a medium, as a commons, as the thing that connects us without our knowing.

And the others. The lizard-things. My kind. I feel them on their pipes, on their warm sections of the network, and I feel their thrums — the low-frequency vibrations they produce with their throat muscles and transmit through their belly scales into the metal — and the thrums travel the length of the pipe and reach me as a pressure, a pulse, a word in a language I do not speak but understand. *Here. I am here. This pipe is warm and I am on it.* And sometimes more — a longer thrum, a complex thrum, a thrum that rises and falls and rises again, and I feel it and I do not respond, because I have nothing to say, because I am old and the things I might have said have already been said, have already traveled the pipes and dissipated into the Deafening, and what remains is only the fact of my presence, the fact of my warmth, the fact of my weight on the metal.

---

I hunt when I need to. I do not need to often. My metabolism is slow — the warmth of the pipe does the work that food would otherwise do, maintaining my body temperature, fueling my minimal processes, and I can go a long time between meals. But sometimes the need comes — a deep cellular hunger, a restlessness in my limbs — and I hunt.

I go still. I go absolutely still. I become the pipe. My breathing slows to nothing. My heart slows to nothing. My body temperature drops by a fraction of a degree, matching the pipe so precisely that there is no thermal signature, no indication that I am anything other than a growth on the metal, a mineral accretion, a feature of the pipe. And I wait.

The vibration tells me everything. The beetle is below me, in the moss, moving toward the pipe, and I feel its clicking through the structure, feel the direction and the distance, and I wait. The beetle reaches the pipe. It begins to climb. I feel its tarsi on the metal — six points of contact, six tiny vibrations, a rhythm, a pattern — and I wait. The beetle climbs past me. It is on the pipe above my head now, and I feel it directly through the metal, feel the weight of it, the warmth of it, the life of it, and I wait. The beetle reaches the top of the pipe, where the condensation gathers, where the water beads and runs, and it stops to drink, and its clicking changes — a different rhythm, a drinking rhythm, a rhythm of satisfaction — and I strike.

My strike is the only fast thing about me. A blur of copper-green muscle and mineral-heavy scales, a motion so quick that the air does not have time to move, and my jaws close on the beetle and the carapace cracks between my teeth and the taste is sharp and bright and alive, and I am still again, and the beetle is in my mouth, and I chew slowly, and the clicking stops, and the vibration of the beetle's life enters my body and becomes my life, and the Deafening continues.

---

I am old. I am very old. My scales are so thick with accumulated copper that I am more mineral than flesh, more sculpture than animal, and when I move — which is rare, which is becoming rarer — the motion is accompanied by a sound: a faint grinding, a creak, the sound of mineral against mineral, the sound of a thing that has become part of the structure it rests on. My eyes are dim. My joints are stiff. My tail is a club of pure copper-green, heavy and rigid, and when I drag it behind me it leaves a mark on the pipe that will not be mossed over for years.

But my belly scales — my belly scales are perfect. They are the oldest part of me, the part that has been in contact with the pipe the longest, and they have become so thin and so sensitive that they are more nerve than scale, more feeling than armor, and through them I feel the Spire in its entirety — every pipe, every structural member, every rivet and plate and seam — and the Spire feels me, and we are in conversation, and the conversation is the Deafening, and the Deafening is the sound of the Spire being alive, and I am part of that sound, and the sound is part of me, and there is no difference.

---

Sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — I produce a thrum. Not a communication. Not a message. A thrum that is more like a sigh, more like a settling, more like the sound a house makes when the wind changes direction and the timbers adjust. I press my belly against the pipe and I contract the muscles in my throat and the vibration enters the metal and travels outward in all directions, down the pipe and up the pipe and into the branching pipes and into the structural members and into the walls of the Spire, and the Spire receives it, and the Spire holds it, and the Spire returns it — faintly, faintly, a ghost of a ghost of a vibration — and I feel the return, and the return says: *I am here. The Spire is here. The pipe is warm and we are on it.*

And the gear turns. And the water drips. And the Garden grows.

And I am on the pipe.
