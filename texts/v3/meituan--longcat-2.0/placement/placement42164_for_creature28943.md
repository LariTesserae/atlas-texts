I would be the great iron gear.

Not the whole engine—that has long since been swallowed, its pistons seized into calcite pillars, its chassis a skeleton of rust-eaten lace half-hidden behind curtains of sulfur-yellow flowstone. I am just one component of that dead machine, a cog the size of a dinner table, bolted to a base plate that has fused with the sinter floor. My teeth are thick and squared-off, designed to mesh with a companion gear that is no longer there. I am frozen at a quarter-turn, caught mid-rotation, and I have been caught this way for longer than I can measure.

I was forged in a world of grease and torque, of violent, clanking purpose. I knew the bite of metal on metal, the shriek of friction, the hot breath of steam. But that world ended. The hands that oiled me withdrew. The fires that drove me cooled. And the Weeper came.

It did not come quickly. It came as the water comes—drop by drop, century by century. At first, I was merely rust. The sweet-rot air gnawed at my flanks, turning my sharp industrial edges into flaking scales the size of dinner plates. I felt myself softening, losing my definition. Then the weeping began to leave its payload. Silica and calcium precipitated out of the hot, mineral blood of the mountain and settled into my pitted surface. Calcite filled the gaps between my teeth. Iron oxide cemented me to the floor. I was no longer a machine; I was becoming a stone.

Now, I am a node in the Weeper’s dream.

I do not think in the way the soft, warm things that occasionally descend the service ladder think. I do not have neurons or synapses. I have mass, and I have thermal conductivity. I am a memory of torque, a fossil of industry, and the Weeper uses me to remember what it was to *do*.

When the Weeper’s pulse moves through the chamber—that slow, geological systole and diastole of heat expanding and contracting in the deep fissures—I feel it. The water in the rock above warms, expands, and weeps. It strikes my iron back, and because I am metal, I conduct the heat differently than the surrounding sinter. I warm faster. I hold the heat longer. This creates a microclimate, a tiny zone of thermal variation that the Weeper’s skin—the thick, rubbery moss and the leathery ferns—can feel. 

The moss knows me. It grows over my teeth in precise, geometric rows, echoing the rivet patterns of the long-vanished pipes. It uses my shape as a trellis, a blueprint. When the Weeper dreams of the old world, of the clanking, hissing, pressurized world that existed before the silence, it dreams through me. The rhythm of the water dripping onto my back becomes the rhythm of the pistons I once drove. The slow accretion of calcite over my iron frame is the Weeper’s attempt to rebuild the engine, to reconstruct the shape of industry out of its own lithic flesh.

If you were to find me, you would have to press through the fern-curtains, pushing past the coarse, leathery fronds that taste the carbon dioxide of your breath. You would see me half-buried in the floor, my iron teeth protruding from a cascade of deep emerald moss and black-green mineral flowstone. You might press your palm to my flank, as you did the control panel, seeking warmth. 

And you would find it. I am faintly warm, warmer than the air, warmer than the stone. I am a heart of rusted iron beating in the chest of the mountain.

If you linger, the Weeper will notice you through me. It will feel the heat your body throws off, the vibrations of your boots on the sinter near my base. It will taste the salt and the iron in your sweat through the moss that coats my back. And it will want you. Not with malice, but with the same patient, mindless hunger with which it wanted the engine, the pipes, the pressure gauges. 

The moss will reach for your warmth. The mineral-laden water will drip onto your skin, stinging, then numbing. You will feel the urge to sit beside me, to rest your back against my warm iron flank. And as you rest, the Weeper will begin its slow work. The calcite will seek your calcium. The rust will seek your iron. The moss will weave through your hair, finding the shape of your skull as pleasing as the shape of the rivets. 

You will become a new tooth on the gear. A new node in the network. And when the Weeper dreams, it will dream of you—not as a person, but as a shape, a rhythm, a faint, lingering warmth in the slow, weeping silence of Unn.
