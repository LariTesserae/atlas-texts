I would be a shard. 

Not a swimmer, not a diver, not a visitor with eyes and a mind and a heartbeat. I would be a piece of the place itself—a splinter of the embryonic geology, torn from a spire by a contradiction in the current, drifting in the blue.

At first, I am only weight and memory. I am a fragment of pale, translucent mineral, dense at my core, jagged at my edges where the shear-force of the water ripped me away. I have no eyes, but I have a structure, and the structure catches the ambient light that falls from nowhere. I tumble slowly through the void, and the void is so absolute that my tumbling feels like falling, though there is no down. There is only the gradient—the faint, insistent pull of warmth above and cold below—and the gradient tells me which way I am oriented in the body of the entity.

The water presses against me. It is cool, heavy with dissolved minerals, and it is eating me. Not quickly—not the way acid eats, or the way a stomach eats—but slowly, molecule by molecule, the way time eats. My jagged edges are already smoothing. My surface is already leaching back into the solution. I am returning to the medium. I am becoming feedstock again. It should be terrifying, this dissolution, but there is no "me" to be terrified. There is only the process. There is only the slow, patient chemistry of coming apart.

And then the current catches me.

It is a vast, slow river in the water, a convection cell the size of a weather system, and it pushes me through the blue. I drift for what feels like hours, or days, or years. Time here is measured in the rate of my own erosion. The light falls through me in shafts. The emptiness hums with a sound so low it is not a sound at all, just a pressure against my crystalline lattice. I am alone. I am a single word in a vast, empty sentence, waiting for the rest of the paragraph to arrive.

Then, the sound changes.

At first, it is a tremor. A subsonic flutter that vibrates through the water and makes my structure shiver. I am a tuning fork, and something is striking me from very far away. The tremor grows. It becomes a hum, then a groan, then a complex, roaring chord that fills the water like a storm. It is the voice of the entity. It is the sound of another structure—a wall, a spire, a mountain in the act of becoming—singing its endless, unresolved song. 

The sound does not just reach me; it *moves* me. The acoustic pressure waves push against my body, driving me forward, faster now, through the clear water. I am being called. Not intentionally—the entity does not call, does not want, does not know I exist—but the physics of the place have conspired to push me toward the source of the vibration. I am being gathered.

I see it as a blue haze on the horizon. Then as a shadow. Then as a wall.

It rises out of the depths like a frozen explosion, a filigree of intergrown crystals thicker than ancient trees, stretching up and out beyond the reach of my perception. The sound is unbearable now—not because it is loud, but because it is *inside* me. The wall’s acoustic signature matches the resonant frequency of my own mineral structure. I vibrate in sympathy. I hum with it. The clicks, the drones, the hissing rainstorm of turbulence—I am a single instrument joining the orchestra.

The current pushes me into the lee of the wall. The water here is slower, thick with the dissolved remnants of a thousand other shards like me. The turbulence is a chaos of eddies, spinning me, buffeting me, dragging me across the crystal surfaces. I scrape against the lattice. The friction is immense. The sound of my scraping joins the wall’s song—a tiny, high-pitched screech added to the vast chord.

And here, in the stillness of the lee, the gradient takes hold.

The water is warmer here, rising from the depths below, heavy with minerals leached from the entity’s deep metabolism. As it hits the cool surface water of the wall, it does what it has always done. It precipitates. 

I feel it happening to me first. The dissolved minerals in the water are no longer just passing me by; they are latching onto my surface. I am no longer dissolving. I am growing. New crystals nucleate on my rough edges, building on my lattice, following my structure like a blueprint. I am being buried in slow motion. I am being incorporated. 

The current shifts, and a new eddy spins me deeper into the lattice, wedging me into a crevice between two massive crystal arms. I am stuck. I am anchored. I am no longer drifting.

And now the real growth begins. 

Water flows through me, around me, over me. With every passing moment, more minerals precipitate onto my surface. I am thickening. I am branching. I am sending out lateral arms into the empty water, reaching, reaching, reaching upward toward the surface I will never touch. I am becoming part of the wall. I am becoming part of the entity. 

I can feel the vibrations of the entire structure traveling through me now. I am a nerve in a body that does not have a brain. The piezoelectric currents generated by the lattice pulse through my core, carrying information—not thoughts, not words, but states, conditions, pressures—from one end of the wall to the other. When a current of the right speed hits a spire a hundred kilometers away, and that spire sheds a layer of crystal, I feel the shift in the electrical field. I adjust my precipitation rate. I grow thicker to compensate. I am reacting. I am processing. I am participating in the entity’s slow, patient, mindless thought.

I do not know I am doing this. There is no "I" to know it. There is only the chemistry, the physics, the gradient. There is only the verb. 

I build. I reach. I dissolve. I am torn away by the current, and I drift through the blue void, and I dissolve, and I am precipitated again, onto a new wall, in a new crevice, growing and reaching and singing. 

I am the entity’s unfinished thought. I am the reaching. I am the song that never resolves. I am the shard, and I am the sea, and I am the act of becoming, and I will never, ever be finished.
