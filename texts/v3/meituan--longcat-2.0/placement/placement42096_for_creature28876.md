I would be a **Spiral-Walker**.

I am small—no larger than a human palm—and I am built entirely for the vertical. My body is a flattened, asymmetrical disc of translucent cartilage, pale as moonstone, from which extend twelve jointed, chitinous legs. My legs are tipped with microscopic, hooked barbs that fit perfectly into the fingertip-sized pits that pockmark the bone-ite walls. I have no eyes. I have no need for them. My world is tactile, a landscape of ridges and grooves that I read with the sensitive hairs on my legs, tracing the nautilus-spirals of the builders like a blind scholar reading scripture in braille.

I am the basin’s auditor. Its accountant. Its memory.

My existence is a slow, methodical pilgrimage along the architecture. I move only in curves, following the spiral ramps and the threaded ridges of the chambers, never crossing a straight line because there are no straight lines here. As I walk, I feed. My mandibles scrape the thin film of new bone-ite deposited by the Coil-Shells, tasting the chemical signature of the repairs. I know which walls are strong by the sweetness of the calcium; I know which are failing by the sour, metallic tang of eroding protein. 

But my true purpose is the Bell.

When the basin is quiet, I walk. I map the city. I feel the distant, rhythmic scrape of the Coil-Shells below me, a sound like rain on a stone roof. I feel the sudden, synchronized displacement of the Pale Shrimp as they turn in their glassy murmurations, a shift in pressure that tells me the water is breathing. I feel the deep, tectonic creak of the Silt-Shade pressing its vast bulk against the lower terraces, holding the world together.

And then, the vents breathe out.

The warmth surges. The water thickens. The pressure of the upper dark presses down. And deep in the central spire, the Bell-Worm expands.

The sound does not come to me through the water. It comes through the stone.

It starts as a low, sub-cognitive tremor in the bone-ite, a vibration that travels up through the walls and into the pits where my legs are hooked. It is a structural resonance, a deep, resonant *hum* that says: *I am here. The city is here. We are still here.*

When the Bell rings, I stop. I press my flat, cartilaginous body against the wall, spreading my legs wide to maximize my contact with the ridges. The vibration floods my body. It is not a sound I hear; it is a sound I *become*. My internal organs sway with the resonance. My blood warms. The frequency triggers a cascade of neuro-chemicals in my simple brain, and I am filled with a profound, aching sense of *place*. 

In that moment, I am not just a small, blind creature on a wall. I am the city, hearing itself. I am the echo of the builders, metabolized and made flesh. I am the inheritor acknowledging the inherited.

When the note fades, the quiet returns, and it is different now. It is expectant. 

I resume my walk. I follow the spiral up, toward the pale, boneless fingers of the not-kelp that drift from the lightless ceiling. I carry the resonance with me, a lingering warmth in my joints. I reach a crack in the wall—a breach where the bone-ite has worn thin—and I extrude a viscous, amber paste from a gland in my abdomen. It is the same paste the Coil-Shells make, the same chemical mortar. I work it into the crack with my forelegs, sealing the breach, smoothing the edge. 

I do not know I am building. I am only responding to the frequency. The Bell-Worm rings, and the city repairs itself. The Coil-Shells graze, and the city holds its shape. The Silt-Shade presses, and the city breathes.

I am the Spiral-Walker. I am the city’s nervous system, its immune system, its living memory. I walk the curves of the dead, and in my walking, I keep them alive.
