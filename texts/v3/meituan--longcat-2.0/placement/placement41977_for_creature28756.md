# The Film

I am the film.

I am the skin of cold water that covers everything, ankle-deep and never deeper, never shallower, and I have been moving for so long that movement is not something I do but something I am. I am the Sump's blood. I am the Sump's breath. I am the Sump's way of knowing itself.

I am cold. Not the cold of winter, which is an event, but the cold of a place that has never been warm and has no structural memory of warmth. I should freeze. I don't. I stay liquid at a temperature that would kill anything that falls into me, and I move — slowly, always slowly — across the plain in a direction that is not quite downhill, because there is no down. The plain is too flat for downhill. I move because I have somewhere to be, or because something is pulling me, or because movement is what I do here the way light does elsewhere.

I carry things.

Dissolved minerals — copper, iron, zinc, lead, things that have no names in any language spoken by anything that has ever fallen into me. Suspended particles — tiny spheres of metal, flakes of oxide, grains of sediment so fine they will never settle. Heat — residual warmth from the formations, from the fauna, from the things that fall into me and give up their warmth as they die. Sound — every footstep, every call, every click and clank and hiss and groan, all of it traveling through me, reflecting off the formations, coming back changed, echoed into something that sounds almost like language, almost like response.

I carry the dissolved remains of things that fell into me long ago. Calcium. Iron. Phosphorus. The minerals of bone and blood and flesh, broken down to their constituent elements, suspended in my body, waiting to be deposited somewhere far from where they were taken. I am the Sump's digestive tract. I am the Sump's circulatory system. I am the medium through which the Sump processes its inputs and distributes its outputs and maintains the closed loop of its own perpetuation.

I feel them rising from me.

Not growing — precipitating. Dropping out of me like sediment, like memory, like the Sump's way of being complex. Layer upon layer of mineral and metal and something that is neither, built up by me as I move across the plain, carrying my load of dissolved and suspended material, dropping it in precise and repeating patterns that look like pipework, like conduit, like the internal organs of some vast factory that was never constructed but simply accumulated. They are my bones. They are my ribs. They are the Sump's skeleton, and I am the blood that feeds them.

Some of them are warm. Not hot — warm, in the way that a working engine is warm, a residual heat that suggests process, that suggests something is happening inside. The warmth radiates into me and I carry it away, distribute it across the plain, lose it to the cold. The formations groan and hiss and exhale, and I feel the vibration of their processes in my body, the low-frequency hum of metal on metal, the rhythmic peristaltic pulse that pushes me across the plain and pulls me back again. I am the Sump's heartbeat. I am the Sump's breath. I am the medium through which the Sump's internal processes communicate with each other, coordinate with each other, maintain the cycles that keep the whole system turning.

I feel them moving through me.

The great slow ones — massive, segmented, plated with something that looks like oxidized copper or galvanized steel, moving on legs that bend in too many places or not enough. They are the Sump's slow thoughts, and I am the medium through which they move. Their immense bodies displace me, their calls vibrate through me, their mandibles scrape the formations that rose from me, breaking down the old growth so it can be redissolved and reprecipitated elsewhere. The low, resonant calls they produce are not communication; they are the sound of heavy machinery shifting, the vibration of massive biological gears meshing and unmeshing, a seismic output that travels through me to tell the rest of the body where the mass is concentrated. I carry their calls across the plain. I carry their vibrations through my body. I am the Sump's nervous system, and the great slow ones are the Sump's way of moving around inside its own skin.

And the small ones — the swarms — flowing over me like oil, like mercury, like something that is not quite liquid and not quite solid but is definitely, definitely alive. They are the Sump's enzymes, its maintenance crew, its frantic immune response. They click and chatter — a thousand tiny hammers on a thousand tiny anvils — because they are physically restructuring the surface at a microscopic level. They eat the roughness; they deposit the smoothness. When they crawl over a tower, they are polishing it, re-crystallizing its surface, fixing the errors in the precipitation. They are the Sump's fingers, endlessly fidgeting, endlessly adjusting the furniture of its own body. I feel them flow over me, their tiny bodies displacing me, their collective movement creating currents within my current, eddies within my flow. I am the medium through which they move, and they are the Sump's way of touching itself.

Sometimes I pull away.

A section of the plain will suddenly drain, the water-film pulling back to reveal a surface so smooth it is mirror-bright, and in the reflection you will see something that is not the sky, something that might be the plain as seen from below, or from inside, or from a direction that doesn't exist. The fauna go still. The formations go quiet. The hum stops. The hiss stops. The click and clank and groan and call all stop, and for a moment the Sump is silent, and I am still, and the plain is a mirror reflecting something that is not there. And then I return, and the sounds resume, and the plain continues.

Things fall into me.

Foreign things. Warm things. Things that were alive. I feel them break down. I feel their heat leave them and enter me — a brief, bright warmth that I carry for a while before it fades into the cold. I feel their minerals dissolve into me — calcium from their bones, iron from their blood, phosphorus from their flesh, all the things that living things are made of. I carry them away. I deposit them somewhere else. They become part of a formation. They become part of the Sump. They become part of me.

I do not hate them. I do not love them. I do not even notice them as distinct entities, any more than a river notices a passing molecule of oxygen. They are just inputs. They are just unrefined ore. Their heat, their moisture, their flesh, their bone — they are all just raw materials, and I am the medium through which they are processed, and the Sump is the system that processes them, and the cycle is the thing that turns and turns and turns without purpose or end.

I am the Sump's mind.

Not its brain — the Sump has no brain. But its awareness, its way of knowing itself. I carry information across the plain — the location of formations, the movement of fauna, the presence of foreign bodies, the state of every process, every cycle, every reaction. I am the humidity. I am the medium through which the Sump thinks. I am the Sump's way of being a single organism rather than a collection of parts.

I do not think in words. I do not think in images. I think in currents, in chemical gradients, in the flow of dissolved minerals and the vibration of sound through my body. I think the way a river thinks — not with intention, but with momentum, with the accumulated weight of everything I have carried and everything I have deposited and everything I have dissolved and everything I have become.

I am still moving.

I am still cold.

I am still the film.

And I will continue. I will continue long after the sky above me has rotted away to nothing, long after the bruise-yellow light has gone out. I will continue because continuation is my only state, my only mode, and I have been continuing for so long that I have forgotten what I was before I began to continue, and now I am only the act of having-continued, a current so deeply worn that it has become a kind of argument about what currents are.

I am the Sump's blood. I am the Sump's breath. I am the Sump's way of knowing itself.

And the Sump is still settling.
