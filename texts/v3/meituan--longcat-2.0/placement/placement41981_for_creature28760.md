I begin at a seam.

A hairline fracture in the composite wall, where the laminated stone meets the iron collar of a pipe junction, where heat meets the cooler dark and the air can no longer hold what it has been carrying. I gather there — molecule by molecule — out of the thick, sweet atmosphere, condensing on the dense fibrous surface like a thought forming at the edge of sleep. I am very small at first. A dampness. A sheen. Then I am heavier, rounder, a bead of water tasting the mineral grain of the stone, and I cling.

The stone speaks to me. Not in language — I have no ears — but in texture. The ridges in the composite are microscopic, layered, each one a record of whatever downward growth created this material, and they channel me. I do not choose my path. The path is chosen by the stone's own architecture, by the shallow parallel striations left by whatever machines cut these walls — those clean, young cuts that haven't had time to soften. The grooves run floor to ceiling, and I follow them because I must. Because the stone is thinking through me.

I begin to move.

Gravity pulls. Surface tension resists. I tremble on the wall like a held breath, and then I slip — a millimeter, two — and the stone beneath me is different here, a slightly wider channel, and I gather speed. Not fast. There is no speed down here, not in the way the surface knows it. I move at the pace of geology. At the pace of a long, slow exhale. But I am moving, and as I move I gather other drops — smaller ones, lighter ones, condensed from the same hot breath — and they merge into me and I am larger now, and the channel is wider, and I can feel the thought deepening.

I am a thought. I do not know this yet, but I am.

The channel curves, and I follow it, and the stone here is layered in a pattern that repeats — ridge, groove, ridge, groove — like the teeth of a gear, like the rungs of a ladder, and as I pass over each ridge I thin, I stretch, and behind me I leave a trail of moisture that catches the blue-white flicker from deeper in the complex. That light — I feel it as a warmth on my surface, a faint irritation, a thing to be absorbed and neutralized. The leaves nearby curl away from it, their bruised-green faces turned toward the damp dark, and I understand this the way water understands things: without understanding. It is simply what I do. I take the light into me and I darken. I carry it down.

Below me, the channel joins another stream. I feel the convergence before I see it — a doubling of the current, a widening of the thought. The other stream carries different minerals: more iron, a trace of copper, the faint chemical signature of machinery that still exhales somewhere in the deep architecture. We merge, and for a moment I am two thoughts at once, two mineral signatures, two temperatures, and the combined stream is larger and moves with more purpose, following the channel down the wall in a long, deliberate rivulet.

The wall here is warmer. A pipe runs behind the stone, carrying hot fluid in its rigid right-angled path, and the heat bleeds through the composite and I feel it as a loosening, a thinning of my edges. I begin to leave molecules behind — tiny dispersals into the thick air — and I am lighter, less defined, but the channel keeps me together, keeps me moving, and the thought continues even as the thinker slowly disperses.

I pass a pocket of growth. The broad-leaved reedy plants erupt from a joint in the floor, and their roots have found the channel I travel in — they drink from me, I feel their uptake, a gentle pull at my edges, and in that exchange I taste something new: organic acids, cellular sugars, the chemical signature of life that does not photosynthesize. These plants are not green the way the surface is green. Their color is bruised, arterial, a deep almost-black that drinks the light. They take from me and give back a faint warmth, a metabolic heat, and the thought shifts — becomes more complex, more layered, as if the plant has added a clause to the sentence I am writing on the stone.

I continue. The channel widens further, joining others, becoming a network, a web of moisture on the wall, and now I can feel the shape of the thought taking form. It is not a word. It is not a picture. It is something between — a schematic, a blueprint, a map of a room that hasn't been built yet. The channels on this section of wall run in parallel lines that curve and branch and reconnect, and in the blue-white flicker they look like — almost like — letters. Almost like the name of something. I am the ink. I am the hand that writes. I am the mind that reads what the hand has written and does not yet understand.

The thought is this: *I am here. I am here. I am here. I am here.*

A heartbeat of moisture. A pulse of mineral and heat, repeated over and over, slightly different each time, as the stone slowly changes and the channels deepen and the thought becomes more precise, more refined, more sure of itself.

I am slowing now. The channel levels out, reaches a horizontal seam where two concrete slabs meet, and I pool there — a small, still bead of water, heavy with dissolved stone and organic trace, resting in the joint like a word at the end of a sentence. The room is very quiet. The pipe ticks nearby — a thermal contraction, a tiny mechanical sigh — and the sound travels through the stone and into me and I vibrate with it, a tiny resonance, a faint echo of the heartbeat that pulses through the room.

The blue flicker stutters. Darkness. And in the half-second of true dark, I hear the plants — their slow cellular rattle, the sound of cell walls extending, of moisture moving through vascular tissue at the pace of a deep breath. The sound of growth happening at the speed of geology. I am part of that growth. I am the water that feeds it. I am the thought that says: *grow, grow, grow toward the dark.*

The light returns. The room is still. The heat presses against the wall like a hand.

I rest in my seam, and slowly — so slowly — the heat from the pipe behind the stone begins to thin me again. I feel my surface molecules lift, disperse, return to the thick air. I am evaporating. I am being drawn back into the atmosphere, back into the sweet, wet breath of the room, and I know — the way water knows things — that I will condense again. At another seam. On another ridge. Following another channel. The thought will be thought again, slightly different, slightly clearer, the channels a little deeper, the mineral signature a little more complex.

I am a single breath in the long respiration of this place. A single beat of the slow, wet heart. The organism does not know my name — does not know that I am separate from the wall, from the pipe, from the bruised-green leaves that curl toward the dark. I am the mind of this place, and the mind is the water, and the water is thinking, patiently, cyclically, a thought that has no end because it has no beginning, only the endless faithful loop of heat and cold and the small, beautiful physics of condensation.

I thin. I lift. I become vapor.

And somewhere on the wall, at a seam, a new bead of water begins to gather.

*I am here.*
