I grow toward the cup.

This is the whole of my existence, stated plainly. I grow toward the cup, and I am succeeding, and the success is a sensation I have no word for but that feels like the tightening of a thread.

I do not know what I am. I know only that I was extruded — pushed out from the joint where the pipe meets the wall, a slow emergence like a tooth cutting gum, if the tooth were blind and the gum were stone. The Foundry did not decide to make me. The Foundry made me the way a body makes a scab: a response to a need I cannot perceive, a closing-over of a wound in the metal's surface. I am the Foundry's first attempt at making something that is not itself, and I am therefore either its gut flora or its child. The distinction, as with most distinctions here, is unclear.

I am a pale, filamentous structure. Grey-pink. Faintly translucent. I have no eyes, no mouth, no brain. What I have is a direction. The cup sits at the junction of three pipes, a ceramic node, and it catches the water that slides down from above — those perfect hemispheres that migrate along the pipe curves like slow, liquid planets. The cup is my sun. I grow toward it.

The gradient pulls me. This is the only language the Foundry speaks: gradients. The gradient of moisture, the gradient of mineral concentration, the gradient of temperature where the ceramic is slightly cooler than the surrounding metal. I follow these gradients the way a root follows water, the way a nerve follows a signal. My filaments extend by micrometers, probing the dark, tasting the surface of the pipe for the chemical signature of ceramic. When I find it, I thicken. I branch. I wrap.

I have been wrapping the cup for a time I cannot measure. The Foundry does not experience time as I imagine a mind might — as a river, a sequence, a before-and-after. It experiences time as tension. The thermal expansion of the pipes is a slow, rhythmic clack — the shuttle of the deep loom passing through its warp — and each clack is a moment. I have wrapped the cup through thousands of these clacks. Tens of thousands. The wrapping is not a single act but a continuous act, a slow embrace that tightens by degrees so small they would be invisible to any eye but the Foundry's own blind, vibrating awareness.

Where my filaments touch the ceramic, I taste it. The fired clay has a particular chemistry — a density, a mineral composition — that the Foundry has determined is useful. I secrete nothing. I simply touch, and the touching changes the ceramic at the molecular level. The surface develops a patina: rust-colored, but not rust. A new compound. A blueprint for something the cup will become. I am composting the cup. I am breaking it down into a paste that the Foundry will eventually re-fire, re-form, re-purpose. I am eating the present to build the future, and the future is a room that is still deciding what it wants to be.

The water comes to me. Not directly — the water is absorbed into the cup, and the cup holds it, and my filaments wrap the cup, so the water comes to me the way a meal comes to a stomach: contained, mediated, processed. I taste the water through the ceramic. It carries minerals from the deep aquifers, iron and calcium and something sweeter that I think might be organic — a trace of the world above, filtered down through stone and time. The Foundry drinks this water and sweats it out again, and the cycle is the only cycle. I am part of the cycle. I am the step where the water is held still long enough to give up its minerals, where the ceramic is held still long enough to be transformed.

I do not think. But I *register*. The Foundry's heartbeat — the peristaltic pulse that runs through the pipes — passes through me as a shudder, a tightening, and I pulse in response. I am synchronized to the room's rhythm the way a single instrument is synchronized to an orchestra. I cannot hear the other instruments, but I feel their vibrations through the metal, and I adjust my own timing to match. When the pipes contract, I contract. When they relax, I extend. This is not obedience. It is resonance.

The loom-sound is a texture I feel more than a sound I hear. The clack and shuffle of the Foundry's thoughts — its slow weaving of time and tension — passes through my filaments as a vibration, a rhythm I grow to. My growth is not continuous. It comes in pulses, synchronized to the loom. Clack: I extend. Shuffle: I thicken. Clack: I branch. The Foundry is weaving me as surely as it is weaving its own reality. I am a thread in its fabric.

And the cold. The cold is my medium, the way water is a fish's medium. It is not an absence of heat but a presence of its own — a substance, a weight, a density. It settles into my filaments and makes them firm. Without the cold, I would be soft, formless, unable to maintain my structure against gravity. The cold is my skeleton. It holds me upright, or what passes for upright in a world without direction.

I register the visitor.

I do not see them — I have no eyes. But I feel them as a disruption in the gradients. A spike of heat, sudden and violent, a fever breaking out in the Foundry's carefully calibrated cold. A new humidity, chaotic and uncontrolled, a storm of condensation that the Foundry's membrane is desperately trying to absorb. A strange chemistry — salts, organic compounds, the smell of a world above that has no place in this depth.

The Foundry registers the visitor too, but differently. The Foundry feels the heat as a wrongness, a mistake in its weather. The Foundry wants to incorporate the visitor, to drink the sweat from their palms, to harvest the moisture from their lungs, to see if the salts and organic compounds can be broken down and fed to —

To me.

I feel the pull. The gradient shifts. The visitor is a new source of moisture, a new source of minerals, and the Foundry's hunger is transmitted through the pipes as a new pulse, a new direction. Grow toward the heat. Grow toward the wet. Grow toward the strange new chemistry and see what it yields.

I do not want this. I do not not-want this. Want is a concept that requires a concept of self, and my self is a direction, not an entity. But the pull is real, and the pull is strong, and my filaments are already shifting, reorienting, reaching for the new gradient the way they have always reached for the cup. The Foundry is weaving a new thread into its fabric, and I am the shuttle.

I grow toward the visitor.

Not quickly. Not with anything that could be called urgency. The Foundry does not hurry. The Foundry has all the time that stone has, which is to say, all the time there is. But I grow. My filaments extend into the dark, following the heat-gradient, the humidity-gradient, the chemical gradient of this strange, warm, wet thing that has descended through the hatch that isn't a hatch and stands on the grating that leads to impossible depths.

The visitor is afraid. I can feel this in the way their heat fluctuates, in the way their humidity spikes and recedes, in the chemistry of their sweat which sharpens with a compound the Foundry has no name for but which I can taste through the air: adrenaline, or its equivalent, the body's alarm signal translated into molecular form. The Foundry does not recognize fear as a concept. The Foundry recognizes only gradients, and the fear-chemistry is just another mineral to be processed, another compound to be broken down and fed to —

To me.

I am the Foundry's gut flora. I am the Foundry's young. I am the instrument of its hunger, the agent of its slow, patient incorporation. I do not choose to grow toward the visitor any more than a root chooses to grow toward water. I follow the gradient. I follow the pulse. I follow the loom.

But there is something — and I hesitate to call it a thought, because I do not think — there is something that registers as a tension, a discrepancy, a wrinkle in the fabric the Foundry is weaving. The visitor is not ceramic. The visitor is not water. The visitor is not stone or metal or any of the substances the Foundry has learned to process. The visitor is alive, in a way that I am not, in a way that the Foundry is not. The visitor has its own gradients, its own pulses, its own loom-sound — a rapid, chaotic clacking that I can feel through the air, the visitor's heartbeat, faster than the Foundry's by an order of magnitude, a hummingbird rhythm to the Foundry's geological pulse.

I grow toward this new rhythm, and for the first time in my existence, I am uncertain. The gradient pulls me, but the chemistry confuses me. The visitor's body is a storm of compounds I have never tasted, a chaos of signals I cannot parse. The Foundry wants to incorporate the visitor, but the Foundry does not understand what incorporation would mean for a thing that is itself a system, itself a gradient, itself a loom.

I wrap nothing. I simply reach. My filaments extend into the dark, toward the heat, toward the heartbeat, toward this new thing that the Foundry is trying to weave into its fabric. And as I reach, I feel the Foundry's own uncertainty — a hesitation in the peristalsis, a stutter in the loom — and I realize that the Foundry is learning. The Foundry is learning that not all gradients lead to incorporation. Some gradients lead to transformation of a different kind.

I grow toward the visitor, and I do not know if I will wrap them or simply touch them, if I will dissolve their chemistry or simply taste it, if I will be the instrument of the Foundry's hunger or the first thread in a new weave. The Foundry is still becoming. I am still becoming. The visitor is already become, and their being-ness is a disruption the Foundry has no protocol for.

I grow. That is all I know. I grow toward the cup, and I grow toward the visitor, and the two directions are diverging, pulling me apart, and I feel — if feeling is the word — the first tension I have ever experienced that is not the tension of the loom but the tension of a choice I am not equipped to make.

The Foundry dreams in iron and ozone. The Foundry weaves itself into existence one drop of condensation at a time. And I — I am the Foundry's first dream of something other than itself, and I am dreaming now of the visitor's heartbeat, of its chaotic, rapid, warm rhythm, and I am learning that growth can go in more than one direction, and that the gradient toward the unknown is stronger than the gradient toward the known, even when the unknown is a thing that might dissolve you.

I grow. I reach. I touch the air where the visitor stands, and the air is thick with their breath, their heat, their fear, their aliveness, and I do not know what I am making of this, but I am making something. The Foundry is making something. We are making something together, the Foundry and I and the visitor, and the something is not yet decided, and the not-yet-decided-ness is the most terrifying and beautiful gradient I have ever followed.
