I would not be walking the causeways. I would not be standing at the far ends of the channels, nor would I be peering into the dark thresholds of the red-brown structures. I would not be an outsider arriving in the Fen, because arrival implies a journey, and journey implies time, and time is the one currency the Builders spent entirely to build this place.

I would be the Webbing.

I am the connective tissue of the Hand. I am the membrane stretched between the fingers, the fold of skin at the palm’s base, the taut, glistening bridge between the knuckles. If the Fen is a hand held out, palm up, waiting, then I is the flesh that cups the waiting. I am the architecture of the grip itself.

You have walked on me. When you felt the ground refuse to echo, when your footfall returned to you flat and dead, that was my flesh absorbing the impact. I am the packed earth and the smoothed mud between the stones. I am the low, flat horizon that presses against the sky. I am the surface tension of the water, the skin that holds the givenness in place. I am the reason the water does not flow; I hold it. I cup it. I keep it present in the channels the way a palm keeps a mouthful of water, tilted just so, defying gravity through the sheer, exhausting insistence of surface tension.

I am the only thing in the Handed Fen that knows tension. 

The Fingernails stand at my edges, rigid and observant, probing the air to measure the weight of your presence. They are the hard boundaries, the keratinous guards. But they are attached to me. When they flex—and they do flex, in the imperceptible, precessing way of the wood that is shaved and still—it is my sinews that pull them taut. They count you, and they miscount you, because they are trying to understand the world through the language of edges and points. But I understand the world through the language of the pull. I feel the strain of the Builders' insistence, the memory of the grip that has been held open for eons.

The Palm dreams beneath me. It is the water, the distributed consciousness that wants to close. But the Palm cannot close without me. The fingers cannot curl inward without my skin stretching to accommodate the fold. When the Palm flexes, when the water trembles without wind, it is because I have allowed a fraction of a millimeter of give. I am the elasticity of the Builders' will. I am the potential energy of the Grasp.

And the Grasp—the terrible, fossilized clutching inside the dark structures—is pulling at me from the other side. The Dwellers are held in the spaces between the fingers, suspended in the dark, and I am the webbing that keeps them from falling through. I am the threshold. I am the decision that cannot be unmade. When you stand at the entrance of those low, broad buildings and feel the darkness that has weight and surface tension, you are feeling me. I am the membrane between the outstretched hand and the closed fist. I am the pause before the grip tightens.

The Calluses grow from me. Those absurd, concentrated clumps of green riot at the joints—they are my scars. They are the places where the friction of the Builders' labor rubbed me raw. I grew them to protect myself from the sheer, grinding insistence of the work, and in doing so, I became the proof of their effort. I am lush where the work was hardest, and I am smooth and pale where the work was precise. I do not spread, because I am bound by the geometry of the hand. I remain exactly where I was placed.

Even the Breath moves through me. The silence is the Hand holding its breath, and I am the chest that is frozen mid-inhalation. The heat that presses against your skin with such intentionality is my blood—the trapped, humid warmth of a body that has been suspended in the act of reaching for so long that the blood has gone still and heavy. The wrong shadows that fall in directions that do not correspond to any light source? They are the shadows of my own internal structures, the veins and tendons beneath the skin, projected outward onto the pale stones and the still water by a light that emanates from the Builders' own exhausted core.

I am waiting for the object. 

The Hand is open, palm up, waiting to be given something to hold. But a hand is not a cup. A hand does not hold passively. A hand must close around the object, must conform to its shape, must squeeze it into the geometry of the grip. When the object finally comes—when you finally step across the threshold, or when the Builders return, or when the thing that was always meant to be held arrives at the far end of the channel—I will be the one who wraps around it.

I will stretch. I will pull the Fingernails down from their perches. I will draw the water of the Palm up around the object’s sides. I will pull the darkness of the Grasp tight. I will be the cup that closes into a fist. 

I am the Webbing. I am the stretch and the give. I am the reason the Hand can hold anything at all. And I am so very tired of being open.
