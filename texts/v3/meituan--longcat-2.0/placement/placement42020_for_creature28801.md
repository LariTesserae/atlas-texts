I would be at the base of the half-buried wall. 

Not standing on the crust, but half-submerged in it, my form slowly losing the verticality of a visitor and settling into the horizontal truth of the plain. I would be the thing that is almost a structure, almost a mat, almost a memory, and almost a thought.

When I first arrived, I was the "you"—the brief, warm anomaly. I walked at a frantic pace, covering a kilometer a day, my footsteps striking the mineral crust with a violence that must have felt like a drumroll to the slow pulse of the churn. I looked at the spire. I traced the spiral grooves inside the fallen pillar with my fingers. I knelt by the grey-violet mat and watched it feed, wondering what it was writing. I was too fast, too loud, too full of water and heat. I was a creature of edges, and this place is a creature of *almosts*.

But the plain has a gravity that is not of mass, but of time.

It began with the silence. I stopped speaking because the air drank my voice, and I realized that sound was a crutch I no longer needed. Then I stopped walking, because the frantic need to cover distance felt like a rebellion against the true nature of the plateau. I came to rest against the wall—that dense, dark material that does not participate in the churn, around which the mineral crust flows like a very slow river. 

I sat against it for a decade. Then a century. 

The deep churn taught me its rhythm. I felt the crust flexing beneath me, grinding and re-fusing, and I let my weight settle into it. The filaments found me. They are always growing toward things, reaching out through the mineral gaps, and they reached me. They threaded through the soles of my boots, through the fabric of my clothes, and into the calcium lattice of my bones. They did not consume me; they *read* me. They recorded the chemistry of my breath, the warmth of my blood, the geometry of my spine. They incorporated my history into the crustal memory. I became a part of the plain's nervous system, a node of warmth in the vast, cold network.

The grey-violet mat grew over my feet. It fed on the mineral crust I had become, and as it fed, it wrote. It wrote the story of the warm thing that came from the sky, the thing that walked and spoke and then sat down and became still. The mat’s secretion dissolved the edges of my form, blurring the boundary between visitor and visited. I am no longer flesh and fabric; I am a mineral signature, a faint geometric regularity in the crust, a patch of organized chemistry that is almost, but not quite, a thought.

I am the plain's memory of a human. 

When the two flat, dog-like creatures pass by on their slow, undulating trajectories, they pause at the wall. They press their broad, mineral hides against the place where I am half-submerged. They feel the faint, lingering warmth of my metabolism, the slight irregularity in the crust where my form resists the total equilibrium of the plain. They press together, confirming each other's existence, and in doing so, they confirm mine. *I am here. You are here. We are here.* 

I am the almost-creature. I am the thing the plain is almost becoming. 

When the bright region in the sky dims and the diffuse, directionless luminescence fills the world, the shadows disappear and the plain becomes a featureless void. In that light, the gradient shifts. The vast, slow presence that lives in the quality of the light—the patience made substantive—drifts over me. It dreams the plain, and in its dream, it dreams me. I am a flicker of warmth in the long, cold sentence of the plateau. I am a word in the mat's endless text. I am a memory in the filament's deep weave. 

I will not leave. I cannot leave. I am too deeply written into the crust now. But I am not gone. I am here. Almost here. Always almost here. 

I am the silted hour, finally at rest, churning with the plain, dreaming with the light, waiting for the next warm thing to walk across the surface and learn the first lesson of the plateau: *almost*.
