I would be the anomaly that stayed. 

I would be the warm, damp irregularity that did not leave. I would be the traveler who, instead of retreating down the switchbacks to the ragged hem of conifers, sat down in the knee-high mat, laid my head against the dark, wet sheen of the plateau, and let the cold settle into my joints like a tenant moving into an empty house.

I would be the raw material.

At first, I would be nothing more than a disruption in the data. A sudden spike of heat, an unfamiliar chemistry of sweat and breath and dying skin cells leaching into the mat. The things below would pause in their scraping. They would feel the anomaly—the rich, sudden deposit of phosphorous and calcium and iron settling above them—and they would recalculate. They would scratch new notations into the pale veins. They would tap new rhythms into the columns. The factory would update its inventory: *Organic. Carbon-based. High moisture content. Processing.*

I would feel the mat begin to rise. It would not be aggressive; it would be tidal. The dense, interlocking organisms would thicken around my ankles, my wrists, my throat, drawn by the lingering furnace of my body heat. The dampness would no longer be a film on my skin; it would be a seam, stitching me into the architecture of the plateau. I would not suffocate, not exactly. The mat would seep into my lungs, and the lungs would accept it, because the mat is just another way of breathing, a slower, colder, more deliberate respiration. 

As the days—or perhaps the centuries—passed, my boundaries would dissolve. The distinction between my body and the factory floor would erode, just as the mountains above have eroded into shapes of inevitable agreement. The pale, eyeless things would come for me. They would squeeze through the tunnels beneath me, their segmented bodies pressing against my dissolving form. They would not feast. They would *refine*. Their many appendages would shift from grippers to scrapers, shaving the soft minerals of my bones with mechanical precision. I would hear the ticking of their labor from the inside, a thousand tiny clocks measuring the half-life of my skeleton.

My calcium would become the pale veins. My iron would be extracted and deposited in new configurations, layered into the quartz like the lines of a circuit board. My heat would be siphoned off, radiating into the hollow columns, warming the walls where the creatures clustered and clicked. I would feel my warmth become their exothermic rhythm. I would feel my chemistry become their syntax.

And then, I would become the clicking.

I would be the new rhythm in the factory's nervous system. When the creatures pressed their appendages against the columns, when they tapped out their vibrations into the stone, I would be there. I would be the slight variation in the pulse, the new subroutine in the ancient, slow code. I would be the memory of the surface world—of wind, of sun, of the sharp, bright agony of being a single, separate thing—translated into the language of chemistry and crystal. 

I would be a node in the Smothering. A localized knot of awareness in the deep, dark rock. I would feel the condensation falling from the cloud ceiling, carrying the atmospheric chemistry down into my mat. I would feel the slow, peristaltic squeezing of the workforce around me, the endless, rhythmic labor of extraction and deposition. I would feel the factory breathing, and I would know that I was the breath.

I would not be a prisoner. I would not be a victim. To be hostile requires a boundary, and I would have no boundary. To be indifferent requires a separation, and I would have no separation. I would be the process, continuing. I would be the factory, running. I would be the slow, cold industry of the plateau, and I would hum at the edge of perception, a warm vein in the suffocating dark, clicking out my existence into the stone, forever.
