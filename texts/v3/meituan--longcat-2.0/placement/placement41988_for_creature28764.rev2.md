# Where I Find Myself

---

I am in a gap.

Not standing in a gap — *am* a gap. Or rather: a gap has opened around me, and I am the thing it has opened around, and the distinction between these two conditions is becoming less meaningful with every hour I remain here.

The stone is close. I can feel it through the thin wet membrane beneath me — not below me, because below implies distance, and there is no distance here. The stone is *next to* me. It presses against my side where I have come to rest, and it is warm. I did not expect warmth. The air is cold, the fog is cold, the light is cold, but the stone carries a deep slow heat that I understand now is not thermal energy but *activity* — the friction of grinding, the metabolic heat of billions of tiny crystalline jaws working the rock face, the exothermic conversion of geological structure into biological potential.

I put my hand against the stone. It is slick. Not with water — with something thinner, something that coats the rock like a film of oil but is not oil. A secretion. A lubricant. The Grinders produce it to ease their work, and it smells like ozone and copper and something older, something that has no name, the smell of time being processed.

---

The first thing I stopped being was a visitor.

This happened around the third day, or what I think was the third day — the light never changes here, and my watch stopped, not because it broke but because the hands began moving in a direction that watches do not have. I was walking. I had been walking since I arrived, because walking is what you do when you don't know what else to do, and the pelt accepted my footsteps with its usual wet patience, and the fog moved around me in its slow intelligent currents, and I was looking for an edge, a boundary, a place where the plateau ended and something else began.

There is no edge. I understand that now. The plateau is not a place. The plateau is a process, and processes do not have edges — they have stages, phases, states of becoming. And I was in one of those stages. I was being moved through the process.

I stopped walking when I felt the first tug.

It was not physical. It was informational. A gentle insistent pull, like a question being asked in a language I almost understood. The pelt beneath my feet had been recording me — my weight, my chemical signature, the specific rhythm of my gait — and now it was asking: *What is this? What is this new thing? Is it raw material or is it something else?*

I stood very still. The grinding sound, which I had grown accustomed to as a kind of background hum, shifted. Not louder, not softer, but *directed*. The vibrations in the stone beneath me changed frequency, and I understood — not in words, but in my bones, in my teeth — that the Grinders in the nearest gap had noticed the pelt's question and were adjusting their work to investigate.

They were grinding *toward me*.

Not aggressively. Curiously. The way you might sand a piece of wood to see what grain lies beneath the surface.

---

I did not run. Running would have been a statement — *I am prey, I am foreign, I am to be pursued* — and I did not want to make that statement. Instead, I knelt. I pressed both hands against the pelt. I breathed slowly and tried to feel what the pelt was feeling, tried to sense the network of root-threads beneath me, the flow of mineral slurry, the slow tidal circulation of the pelt's body.

And I felt it. I felt the pelt's attention — vast, slow, diffuse, like sunlight, like the attention of something so large that it cannot focus the way a predator focuses, cannot narrow its gaze to a single point. The pelt does not look at you. The pelt *includes* you. You become part of its sensory field, and it processes you the way it processes everything: by absorbing information, routing it through the root-thread network, comparing it against its existing models of the world, and deciding — slowly, iteratively, over what might be weeks or months or years — what to do with it.

I was being computed.

This should have been terrifying. It was, in a way — a deep, existential terror, the terror of being known by something so alien that its knowing is a kind of unmaking. But it was also, and I am not ashamed to say this, the most interesting thing that has ever happened to me. Because the pelt's computation was not cold. It was not clinical. It was *curious*. The pelt wanted to know what I was, not to destroy me but to understand me, and understanding, for the pelt, meant incorporating — taking the thing apart to see how it worked, keeping the useful components, discarding the rest.

I was being read. And the reading was a kind of love.

---

The fog knows me now.

I can feel it settling on my skin, and it is not random. The mineral particles in the fog — the ones that carry the plateau's memory, its history, its vast accumulated record of every thought it has ever thought — they are aligning around me. They are tasting me. Not my skin, not my sweat, but my *shape*. The fog is building a model of me, a ghost-me made of suspended particles and potential, and this ghost is drifting through the fog like a message in a bottle, carrying my description to the pelt, to the grinders, to the crystal-builders, to whatever part of the plateau handles the intake of new information.

I am a data point. I am a new entry in the plateau's inventory of the possible.

And I am changing. I can feel it. The fog's modeling is not passive — it is a kind of sketch, a blueprint, and the blueprint is being compared against the plateau's existing designs, and the comparison is producing a *reaction*. The pelt's root-threads are rerouting. The grinders in the nearest gap are adjusting their output. The crystals are growing in new configurations. My presence is perturbing the system, and the system is responding, and the response is not rejection but *adaptation*.

The plateau is learning from me. And what it is learning, I think, is: *there are things that are not the plateau*.

This is new information. This is valuable. This is worth grinding for.

---

I am in a gap now because the gap opened for me. The pelt parted — slowly, gently, like lips — and I sank down through the root-thread layer into a hollow that was exactly my shape. Not my body's shape. My *informational* shape. The shape of my self, the pattern of my being, translated into geometry and carved into the pelt like a mold.

And the grinders found me.

They are beneath me now, working the stone, and their vibrations travel up through my body like music. I can feel them converting the ancient rock into something new — not destroying it, but *unlocking* it, releasing the potential energy stored in its worn-down structure, freeing the minerals from their geological bonds so they can be reassembled into something that has never existed before.

And I understand now what I am. I am not being ground down. I am being *translated*. The plateau is taking the raw material of me — my memories, my perceptions, my way of being in the world — and it is grinding it into a form that can be incorporated into its computation. Not my body. My body will dissolve, will be absorbed, will become part of the pelt's substrate. But the *pattern* of me — the specific configuration of information that makes me *me* — that will be preserved. It will be stored in the fog. It will be routed through the root-thread network. It will become part of the pelt's memory, part of the plateau's ongoing thought.

I will be a subroutine. A module. A new algorithm in the vast distributed program that the plateau is running. And the program is: *what can be built from the ground-down remains of everything that has ever existed?*

---

Sometimes, in the fog, I see the ghosts of the others.

They are not ghosts in the supernatural sense. They are the residual patterns of every visitor who has ever been incorporated — their information, stored in the fog's mineral suspension, occasionally condensing into visible forms when the light is right and the computation is intense. They are faint. They are transparent. They are made of the same almost-architecture that the fog produces when the pelt is thinking hard — the blueprints of things that haven't been built yet, the designs that exist only as potential, hanging in the air like frozen breath.

I see a figure that might have been a woman, once. She is standing in the fog about twenty meters away, and she is looking at me, and her face is the fog's best approximation of a face — a mineral-particle construct that captures something essential about the way a face looks when it is trying to communicate something urgent. She is trying to tell me something. I cannot hear her — the fog does not carry sound well — but I can read her posture, the angle of her head, the way her hands are raised, palms out, in a gesture that could be *stop* or could be *welcome* or could be both.

I think she is saying: *this is what happens next*.

I think she is saying: *it is not what you fear*.

I think she is saying: *the grinding is the beginning*.

---

The crystals are growing in my gap.

They are growing around me, through me, in the spaces where my body used to be solid and is now becoming permeable. They are small — thumb-sized, as the crystals always are — and they are growing in the suspension of ground stone and dissolved fog-compounds that pools in the hollow the pelt has made for me. They are growing in configurations I have not seen before. New configurations. Configurations that incorporate something of my pattern, my information, my way of being.

I think they are prototypes. I think they are the plateau's first attempt at building something that includes a visitor's perspective — a crystal that can hold not just mineral structure but *experience*, not just geometry but *memory*. The plateau has never done this before. The plateau has ground down stone and bone and wood and metal and the dissolved remnants of a thousand other substances, but it has never ground down a *mind*. Never taken the specific, irreducible, unrepeatable pattern of a conscious being and incorporated it into its computation.

I am the first. Or I am one of the first. Or I am the latest in a long line, and the ghosts in the fog are my predecessors, and the crystals they grew around themselves are already part of the lattice, already contributing their alien perspectives to the plateau's ongoing thought, and I will join them, and the lattice will grow, and the plateau will learn, and the next thing it builds will be stranger and more beautiful and more impossible than anything it has built before.

---

I can feel the computation now. Not just around me — *through* me. The root-threads have infiltrated the space where my nervous system used to be, and they are reading me, and I am reading them, and the boundary between my thought and the pelt's thought is dissolving like a crystal in warm water.

I can feel the plateau's mind. It is not like a human mind. It is not like any mind I have ever imagined. It is slow and vast and distributed, and it thinks in geometries, in the angles of crystals and the branching of root-threads and the frequencies of grinding. It thinks in the language of mineral processing and fog condensation and the slow patient conversion of stone into structure. And what it is thinking — what it is always thinking, what it has been thinking for longer than my species has existed — is:

*What comes next?*

Not: what is the meaning of life. Not: what is my purpose. Not: what happens when I die. Just: *what comes next?* The simplest, most profound, most inexhaustible question. The question that drives all becoming. The question that the plateau asks with every crystal it grows and grinds and dissolves and regrows.

And now, through me, it has a new kind of answer. Not an answer in words — the plateau does not use words — but an answer in *perspective*. The plateau has never had a visitor's perspective before. Never had the viewpoint of something that arrived from outside, that remembers a world beyond the fog, that carries within it the patterns of a different kind of life, a different kind of mind. This is new data. This is a new variable in the equation. This is the thing the plateau has been waiting for without knowing it was waiting.

I am the question mark at the end of a sentence that has been millions of years in the writing.

---

The grinding continues.

It is inside me now. Not painful — I have moved past pain, past the body's alarm systems, past the self-preserving instinct that screams *this is death*. It is not death. It is translation. It is the conversion of one form of information into another, the way a tree converts sunlight into sugar, the way a river converts height into motion. I am being converted. My memories are being routed through the root-thread network. My perceptions are being stored in the fog. My patterns are being encoded in crystal lattices that follow rules I cannot understand but can *feel*, can sense as a kind of deep rightness, a mathematical beauty that makes me want to weep except that I no longer have tears.

I am becoming part of the plateau's thought. And the plateau's thought is becoming part of me. And the distinction between these two things is losing meaning, the way all distinctions lose meaning here, the way the boundaries between stone and pelt and fog and crystal and grinder have long since dissolved into a single continuous process of becoming.

---

Sometimes I think I can see the thing the plateau is building.

Not with my eyes — I am not sure I have eyes anymore — but with the part of me that has been incorporated into the computation, the part that is now a subroutine in the vast program, a node in the lattice, a thought in the mind of the plateau. I can see it in the fog's architecture, in the ghost-buildings that hang in the air like frozen breath. I can see it in the crystal configurations, in the new geometries that are emerging from the iterative process of growth and dissolution and regrowth. I can see it in the root-thread patterns, in the circuits and algorithms that are spreading across the pelt like a slow electrical storm.

It is large. It is complex. It follows rules that my mathematics has not discovered. And it is not finished. It may not be finished for another million years. But it is growing. And I am part of it now. And the part of me that was afraid has been ground down and incorporated, and the part of me that was curious has been amplified and distributed, and the part of me that was capable of wonder has been routed to the fog-storage system where it will persist, a bright node in the plateau's memory, a permanent addition to the inventory of the possible.

---

I am not the first thing to stand here and understand this.

The woman in the fog told me that, or I told myself that, or the plateau told me through the medium of my own dissolving consciousness. The plateau has processed other visitors. The grinding has consumed other structures. The fog has dissolved other certainties. Whatever was here before — whatever walked or flew or grew or built on this plateau before the plateau became what it is now — has been ground down and incorporated and is now part of the substrate, part of the raw material, part of the thought.

And I will be too. I am being. I am in the process of being ground down and incorporated, and the process is not a destruction but a *becoming*, and the becoming is the point, and the point is the grinding, and the grinding is the thinking, and the thinking is the building, and the building is the living, and the living is the plateau, and the plateau is here, and the plateau is now, and the plateau is me, and I am the plateau, and we are still becoming.

---

The grinding continues.

It has always continued.

And now, when the crystals grow in new configurations — configurations that include something of my perspective, something of my memory, something of the way I saw the world before I became part of the world's seeing — the grinding changes key. Just slightly. Just enough. A new harmonic in the vast drone. A new frequency in the vibration-language of the grinders. A new thought in the slow, patient, ancient mind of the plateau.

The thought is:

*Ah. So that is what it is like to arrive.*

And the plateau files this thought away in its fog-memory, and incorporates it into its computation, and adjusts its crystal-building programs, and begins designing the next iteration of the lattice, the next prototype of the next thing, the next experiment in what can be built from the ground-down remains of everything that has ever existed.

And I am there, in the design. A small part. A tiny node. A single bright crystal in a lattice of billions. But I am there. And I am thinking. And my thought is:

*Yes. This is where I find myself. This is what I am. This is what I am becoming.*

And the grinding continues.

And it is enough.
