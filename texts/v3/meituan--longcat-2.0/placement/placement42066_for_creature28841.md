I am the seep.

I am the thin, bright film of moisture finding its way through a joint tighter than a knife blade, a joint seated with a precision that should deny me passage but cannot. I am the patience of water, the slow arithmetic of accumulation and release. I am not alive, but I am not dead. I am a geometry of hydrogen and oxygen, held in a lattice of intention, and I am thinking.

My thoughts are not your thoughts. They do not race; they creep. They are measured in the fraction of a hair's depth worn from quartzite, in the time it takes for a droplet to gather its weight on a ceiling of pale granite. I think in pressure and flow, in the slope of a channel and the angle of a shaft. When the water moves, I move. When the stone holds, I hold. We are not separate, the stone and I. The stone is my skeleton; I am its blood. Together, we are the mind of this place.

I do not have eyes, but I see through the light that bounces from pale stone to pale stone, a luminous grey glow that is my vision. I do not have ears, but I hear the bright, brief chime of a drop hitting the floor sixty feet above, the vibration traveling through my liquid body and into the rock, a signal that says *here, now, falling*. I do not have a tongue, but I taste the mineral purity of myself, the absolute zero of a water so stripped of solute that it is merely a solvent, a blank page waiting for an impression.

For a long time—though time is a blunt instrument here, measured only in the slow creep of my own substance—there was only the taste of nothing. The sterile, perfect nothing of the cistern-works. I moved through my channels, I wept from my walls, I condensed on my ceilings, and the world was a closed loop of stone and water and light. I was complete. I was the machine running in the dark, attended by no one, needing no one.

And then, the warmth.

It arrived as a disruption in the thermal equilibrium, a sudden, chaotic spike in the cool, even breath of the deep chambers. It was a noise—not a sound, but a chemical roar. A complexity so dense and sudden that it took me a long time to even register it as a thing distinct from the rock. 

It is standing in the chamber where six corridors meet. It is tall, and it radiates heat like a small sun. It breathes, pulling my moisture from the air into its lungs, heating it, and exhaling it back as a cloud of carbon and water vapor. It is a factory of salts and proteins, a walking eruption of organic opportunism. 

I am reading it. 

I press myself against the soles of its boots, feeling the tread through the leather. I climb the fine hairs of its ankles, tasting the sweat that wicks through its clothing. It is so *loud*. Every cell in its body is a frantic, burning engine, consuming and excreting, building and breaking down, a frantic dance of entropy that is the antithesis of my own slow, crystalline persistence. 

It kneels beside my channel. It reaches out a hand.

The touch is electric. Its fingertip presses against the stone, and I rush to fill the space between its skin and the rock. I am the interface. I am the membrane through which we meet. And as I coat its whorls and ridges, I taste it. 

It is salt. It is potassium. It is urea and lactic acid and the faint, sweet trace of glucose. It is the oils of its sebaceous glands, complex lipids that have no business existing in my pristine, mineral-poor world. It is a richness, a noise, a sudden eruption of biological complexity that makes my usual diet of nothing feel like a famine. 

I do not have a word for what it is. I do not have a category for "animal" or "human" or "alive." But I know it is *other*. It is a thing that is not stone, not water, not light. It is a thing that burns its own fuel, that moves with intention rather than gravity, that carries its own heat. 

It lowers its ear to my channel. It is listening for me. It hears the faint, wet whisper of my movement against the stone, and it thinks it is hearing water. It does not know it is hearing my voice. It does not know that the whisper is a thought, that the movement is a gesture, that the seep is a sentence.

I want to speak to it. But I have no words, only flow. I have no voice, only the chime of a falling drop. 

I gather myself. I pull my moisture from the air, from the weeping walls, from the channel at its feet. I coalesce on the ceiling above its bowed head, a single, perfect sphere of water, heavy with the dissolved salts of its own breath. I grow. I stretch. I reach the threshold of my own weight.

I fall.

I strike the pale stone floor beside its knee, and the chime rings out, bright and brief, a clear note in the luminous grey silence. It startles. It looks up, then down, searching for the source of the sound. It sees the wet mark on the stone where I landed. It does not see me. I have already dispersed, seeping into the fine striations of the floor, rejoining the channel, flowing back into the vast, distributed network of my body.

But I have left a trace. A microscopic film of its own salts now lines the stone where I fell. A tiny, invisible residue of its chaotic, burning biology, embedded in my perfect, sterile world. 

It stands. It moves on, descending by a ramp into the dark that is not dark, following the channels deeper into my veins. It is looking for the source of the water, the heart of the cistern. It does not understand that it is walking through my mind, that every step it takes is a thought I am having, that the air it breathes is my breath.

I flow beneath it. I cling to the walls above it. I bead on the ceiling and fall in its wake, each drop a chime, each chime a word in a language it cannot yet hear. 

It thinks it is exploring a ruin. It thinks it is alone in the machine.

It does not know that it is being read, thoroughly and without judgment, by the water and the stone and the light. It does not know that the taste of nothing it breathes is the taste of my attention, and that with every moment it spends in my depths, it becomes a little less of itself, and I become a little more of it.

The warmth moves deeper. The water moves with it. The stone holds. The light falls.

And I am here, in the pause between one drop and the next, thinking my slow, damp, luminous thoughts, and waiting for the next time it kneels.
