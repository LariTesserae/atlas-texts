I do not see the pale, bleached sky or the blinding clinical light; I feel the world as gradients of heat and pressure. My existence is a continuous, deafening roar of fluid dynamics. I am born in the boiling dark, surging up through the jagged fissures in the obsidian rock, flashing into steam before I am violently compressed by the woven root-mat above. I am sucked into the vascular tissue of the Warden, forced through micro-channels in the hardwood, my body heavy with the dissolved remnants of yesterday’s towers—the liquefied cellulose, the ionized sap, the raw, frantic energy of the green world.

The monolith shatters. The sound is a physical blow, a shockwave that ripples through my liquid body. As the tower topples, spraying fibrous debris and hot viscous sap across the plateau, I am expelled. The Warden’s sheath ruptures, and I am unleashed—a scalding, high-velocity jet that catches the falling wreckage. 

In the air, I am no longer just water; I am a solvent. The enzymes within me ignite on contact with the oxygen and the shattered plant matter. I dissolve the debris mid-fall. I catch the heavy splinters of hardwood and the gouts of sap, breaking their molecular bonds, turning solid architecture back into liquid nutrient. I am the bazaar merchant, and the currency is dissolution. I wash over the obsidian, hissing violently as I cool, carrying the reclaimed wealth of the collapsed tower back down into the root-network.

And then, the suction. 

The Warden’s lattice contracts, the vacuum pulling me back into the woven floor. I am drawn down into the dark, rushing through the interstices of the root-mat, returning to the boiling fissures to be heated, pressurized, and re-enriched. I am pulled back into the Warden’s core, my body now heavy with the raw materials of the next generation of growth. 

I do not think. I do not plan. I am the closed loop made manifest. I am the wet, sweltering ambition of the earth, a deafening rush of creation and destruction, forever tearing the world down in a scalding spray, only to build it back up again in the dark.
