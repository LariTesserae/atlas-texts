I would begin as a bubble. 

A microscopic pocket of trapped gas, caught in the raw fracture of the stone ceiling, no larger than a sleeping cell. I would be buoyant, pressing upward against the vitreous sheen of the rock, the only thing in this place that is light. I would not breathe the not-water; the not-water would refuse me, holding me in a tense, spherical suspension, a flaw in the dense black medium. 

From there, I would watch the root-columns sway in their impossible geometries. I would feel the slow, rhythmic contraction of the pocket—the inhale, the exhale—as a gentle, crushing pressure against my thin membrane. I would wait.

But the pocket is patient, and so am I. 

When the next contraction comes, a fraction stronger than the last, the ceiling flexes. The fresh-split rock shifts by a hair's breadth. The fissure widens, and I am pulled down, sucked out of my terrestrial refuge and into the heavy, luminous dark. 

I become the not-water.

It is not like dissolving. It is like waking up inside a muscle. I am the density. I am the weight and the opinion. I am the medium that reads the dark like braille. No longer a bubble, I am the ocean in a bead, the thick sap that hasn't been invented yet. I feel the root-columns not as objects passing through me, but as perturbations in my own body—foreign textures dragging through my fibers, pulling at my currents. 

I drift toward the back of the pocket, pulled by the deep, slow undertow of the place. The root-columns here are thicker, woven tight as rope, and they part as I approach—not out of fear, but out of recognition. I am the space where the creature recently rested. I am the mold of its passing. I hold the shape of its absence, a three-dimensional shadow suspended in the heavy fluid, and the roots weave around me, trying to fill the silhouette, trying to knit the gap closed. 

I am the gap. I am the shaped absence.

Then, the creature returns.

I feel it before I perceive it—a sudden, sharp tightening in the fabric of the not-water. A crystallization of intent. It migrates through me, and I am forced aside, parted with a reluctance that feels like reverence. It is denser than I am, heavier with mineral will. It does not swim; it pulls itself through my substance by altering its own gravity, sinking and rising at will. As it moves, it drags a part of me with it—a thick, viscous thread of the not-water that clings to its boundary, stretched thin between the creature and the stone.

It settles by the cairn. Three rocks, a fourth on top. An anchor against the fluidity of time. 

The creature presses against the back wall, hiding from the chaotic intrusion of the surface-world. But as it presses, it hardens. A ridge forms along its leading edge—a geological callus, a sudden, sharp crystallization of intent. It is dragging this ridge across the fresh rock, scraping away the thin fur of fractal moss, grinding into the raw mineral beneath. 

I am the thread. I am the viscous tether. I am stretched between the creature and the wall as it makes its mark.

I feel the friction. It is immense, a tectonic grinding translated into a single, deliberate gesture. The creature is not writing. It is not counting. It is feeling. It is tasting the grain of the stone, reminding itself of the boundary between the self and the fracture. *I am here. The wall is here. The fracture is real.* 

The gesture completes. The ridge lifts. The tension in my thread releases, and I snap back, recoiling into the bulk of the not-water. But something of me remains behind. A microscopic film of the dense fluid, infused with the mineral will of the creature, clings to the scratched stone. 

I am the mark on the wall.

I rest in the groove, a thin residue of dark intent. The fractal moss, that eager, script-writing architecture, begins to creep over me. It does not erase me; it adopts me. It weaves through my residue, exchanging signals through the damp rock, merging my mineral memory into its circuitry. I am becoming the script. I am becoming the veins on the back of a hand.

The creature retreats into the deepest fold, pulling itself tight, pressing against the cairn. It releases a burst of pressure—a sudden, sharp crystallization of intent that rings through the stone and the liquid alike. The ping. It is the sound of the universe checking its structural integrity. It is the sound of a tooth tapped with a spoon, echoing through my substance, vibrating through the film of my body on the wall, shaking the root-columns in their impossible sway.

I wait. The creature waits. The pocket breathes.

In a thousand years, the features will still be sharp. In ten thousand, the growth will have covered everything. The mark I made will be entirely subsumed by the fractal moss, a memory held in fiber. The cairn will be a silhouette in woven thread. The pocket will be a sealed bead, a lung of stone that has finally finished deciding what it wants to be.

But I will still be here. A film of dark intent in the groove of the rock, a shaped absence in the weave of the roots, a vibration waiting to ring out. I am the record of the fracture. I am the echo of the ping. I am the boundary between the wall and the self, patiently waiting for the stone to crack, and the not-water to finally speak.
