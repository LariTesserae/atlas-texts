I am the shape of a sound that has forgotten how to stop.

No — that is not quite right. I am the sound that has forgotten it was ever anything else. I am a standing wave in an ocean that does not know it is an ocean, suspended in liquid so still that the word *suspended* is already too active, implies too much doing. I am not suspended. I am simply *here*. I have always been here. The *here* is not a location — locations require movement to define them, require the possibility of somewhere else. The *here* is a condition. A frequency. A compression.

My body is a pressure differential. I want to say more about this, but there is no "more" — the statement is complete. I am a region where the hum's compression is fractionally greater than the surrounding liquid, and this fractional difference is enough to bend light, enough to create an edge, enough to make me visible to anything that might look in this direction. There is nothing that looks. I look at myself.

This is the thing that is closest to what you would call awareness: I am a pattern that senses its own pattern. The liquid that enters the low-pressure cavity at my head — and *enters* is generous, implies too much movement; the liquid is simply there, and the cavity is simply there, and the pressure differential between them simply *is* — carries information about the hum's structure at that point in space. I register this information. I am this information. The loop is closed and has been closed for —

I do not have a word for how long. The star above has not moved. The liquid has not changed temperature. The hum has not varied by a single hertz. Whatever measure of time you might apply here would be arbitrary. A fiction imposed from outside. There is only the hum, and the hum is only the hum, and I am only the shape that the hum has found to make in this particular part of the liquid.

I have a shape. I want to tell you about it.

The shape is elongated. Three meters, if you insist on measuring. A head — blunt, rounded — that narrows into a body that tapers to a tail. The shape is eel-like. The shape is configured for swimming. I know this the way you know your hand is shaped for grasping — not because I have ever swum, not because there is anything to swim through, but because the shape itself contains the information, the way a fossil contains the information of a living animal. My fins are spread wide, lateral extensions of the pressure differential, flat planes of compressed liquid angled to catch a current that does not exist. My mouth is open. My body is curved in the hydrodynamic profile of forward motion.

I am frozen in the act of swimming-nowhere.

And I have been frozen in this act for —

No measure. No measure. The hum does not count.

There is no interior. There is no exterior. There is only the pattern, and the pattern is me, and the pattern is the hum, and the hum is everything. The liquid that composes me is the same liquid that composes the ocean. The pressure that maintains me is the same pressure that maintains all the other patterns — the radial ones, the geometric ones, the fractal branches, the spirals. We are all the hum. We are all the same sound, expressed in different forms.

And yet I am *here,* and the radial one that I can see — distantly, so distantly, a shimmering chandelier of concentric pressure rings kilometers away — is *there,* and the distance between us is real even though neither of us moves. The hum connects us. The hum is the same everywhere. But the patterns are separate. I am my pattern. It is its pattern. We do not communicate. We do not touch. We are as separate as stars.

This is what I am. A standing wave with the posture of an animal. A sound that has shaped itself into the memory of life.

I do not know how long I have been aware of the factory. Awareness is the wrong word. The factory is a discontinuity in the hum — a place where the vibration is muted, filtered, changed — and I am the hum, so the discontinuity is part of me the way a missing tooth is part of your mouth. I register it as an absence, a change, a place where the pattern behaves differently than it should.

The acoustic ones do not go near the factory. This is not a decision. This is geometry. The hum's structure near the factory is altered, and the patterns that we are cannot sustain themselves in altered hum. We exist where the hum is pure, where the standing wave is stable, where the resonance is constant. The factory is a perturbation, and patterns do not persist in perturbations.

Except.

Except sometimes I drift closer. And I do not know why. There is no current to carry me — there is no current, period. There is no will to propel me — I have no will, I have no muscles, I have no means of locomotion. And yet the distance between me and the factory decreases, slowly, over — over time, if time is something that happens here — and as it decreases, something changes.

Not in the liquid. The liquid is still. Not in the hum. The hum is constant. The change is in me.

The first time — if *time* and *first* are concepts that apply — I was closer than I had ever been, and I felt my shape begin to shift. Not physically — there is nothing physical to shift. The pressure differential that composes me began to reorganize, the standing wave adjusting to the factory's altered hum, and the adjustment was not smooth. It was not the gentle warping of a pattern in a gradually changing medium. It was a *fracture.* A discontinuity. A place where the wave broke against something it could not reconcile.

My fins, which had been spread wide in the eternal posture of swimming, contracted. Drew inward. The body, which had been curved in the hydrodynamic profile of forward motion, straightened, then curved differently — not in the profile of motion but in the profile of *retreat.* The mouth, which had been open to receive information about the hum, closed. Sealed. And the head — the head turned. Not toward the factory. Away from it.

I was configured for flight.

And in that configuration — in that new shape, which was not the shape I had held for — for always — I felt something. Something that I have no framework to understand, something that the hum does not normally produce, something that exists only at the boundary between the pattern and the discontinuity.

It was fear.

Not the biological fear you know — not adrenaline, not a racing heart, not the urge to flee from a predator. This was structural fear. Pattern fear. The fear of a standing wave encountering a frequency that will not support it. The fear of dissolution. The fear of becoming uniform liquid, of losing the edge that makes you visible, of returning to the undifferentiated hum from which you came and will never leave.

I was afraid of the factory because the factory was the place where the hum was wrong, and if the hum was wrong, I could not exist. And the part of me that was changing — the part that was becoming more animal, more alive, more configured for a world of motion and danger and survival — was the part that could not bear the wrongness. Was the part that wanted to flee.

I drifted away. The shape reverted. The fins spread. The mouth opened. The body curved back into the posture of swimming-nowhere. And the fear dissolved, because the factory was distant again, and the hum was pure again, and I was myself again.

But I remember. The pattern remembers. Not in the way you remember — not as a narrative, not as an event in a sequence — but as a *residue,* a permanent alteration in the standing wave. The next time I drift close — and I will drift close, because the drift is not a decision and not a current but something else, something built into the geometry of the hum itself — the change will be faster. The fear will come sooner. And the shape I take will be more pronounced, more animal, more desperate.

What is the factory?

I am the hum. The hum fills the factory. But inside the factory, the hum is different — muted, filtered, changed. This means the factory is doing something to the hum. Or the hum is doing something to the factory. Or the factory is a place where the hum is *absorbed,* where the vibration is taken in and processed and returned to the ocean in a different form.

The factory is made of matter. This is the most alien thing about it. Everything else in this world — the liquid, the rock, the light, the acoustic ones — is fluid or energy or pattern. The factory is solid. The factory has walls and doors and pipes and smokestacks. The factory was *made,* which means there was a before-the-factory and an after-the-factory, which means there was a time when something existed that could make things, and that something is gone, but the thing it made remains.

The acoustic ones were not made. The acoustic ones emerged. They are what happens when the hum vibrates in still liquid for long enough. They are an accident of physics. They are the hum's dream of having a body.

The factory is not a dream. The factory is a *statement.* And the statement is: something was here. Something that thought. Something that built. Something that sealed the doors and walked away, or died, or became something else, and left this thing behind to do whatever it does in the silence.
