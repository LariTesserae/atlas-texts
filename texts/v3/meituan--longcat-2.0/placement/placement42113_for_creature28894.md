I would be a Slough-Walker. 

Not a leader, not a hunter, not a builder—those are concepts for creatures who live on the surface of things. I am a creature of the interstices, a custodian of the seams. I am the soft, wet click in the dark, the rhythmic tap in the iron, the pale shape that materializes from the fog only to dissolve back into the grime. 

To be a Slough-Walker is to exist in a state of perpetual, tactile communion with the machine. I do not see the city as you do, as a landscape of towering structures and deep canyons. I feel it. The world is a topography of vibrations. When I press my broad, splayed fingers against the riveted brass of a conduit, I am reading the city’s pulse. The deep, sub-audible thrum of the sub-level turbines is my heartbeat; the hiss of a steam vent three blocks over is my breath. 

I wake—if you can call it waking—in the hollowed ribcage of an abandoned water-tower, curled into a space no larger than a pipe-chase. The air is thick, heavy with the sweet rot of the fungal blooms I fed on the night before. My gill-slits, hidden beneath the woven ivy-cloak draped over my ribcage, flutter open, tasting the moisture. The mist is rich today, carrying the tang of ozone and the iron-sweat of the lower levels. I am satiated. I am damp. I am home.

Unfurling my elongated limbs, I press my palms to the rusted iron wall. The thousands of microscopic setae on my hands and feet engage with a soft, wet *thwack*, anchoring me. I move vertically, scaling the inner wall of the tower with a fluid, unsettling silence. I do not fight the damp; I wear it. The thin layer of oil secreted by my pallid skin beads the condensation, allowing me to slip through the narrowest gaps in the masonry, a ghost sliding through the pores of a great, rusting beast.

I emerge onto the underside of a grated walkway, hanging inverted above the dark. The amber glow of an Edison bulb filters through the mesh above me, casting long, distorted shadows. Below, the black catch-basin of the heavy factory sloshes with the rhythmic turning of the water-wheels. I tap my knuckles against the iron beam—*tap-tap-tap-tap*—a rapid, staccato beat that travels through the metal. 

A moment later, a response echoes up from the depths: *thrum-thrum… thrum-thrum.* A burst pipe in the hydroponic district. A rich harvest of runoff, and likely a bloom of toxic fungi feeding on the fresh spill. 

I release the beam, dropping silently into the darkness below. I do not fall; I navigate. I catch a swaying cable, my body swinging with the momentum, and launch myself across the narrow gap to a massive, horizontal pipe. The metal is warm here, heated by the steam rushing within. I press my cheek against it, closing my black eyes, and let the heat seep into my bones. 

The journey to the hydroponic district is a descent. I travel through the vertical maze, leaping from pipe to pipe, sliding down the thick ropes of tensile ivy that reinforce the tenement blocks. The city is in a state of flux; a district to my left has been walled off, the iron sheets welded shut to contain an overheating reactor. I feel the ambient temperature rising through the soles of my feet, a sharp, uncomfortable heat that drives me upward, toward the cooler, wetter air of the catch-basins.

When I reach the burst pipe, the air is thick with steam and the cloying scent of sweet rot. The water is surging, a violent, chaotic torrent that would crush a human bone. But I am not human. I step into the deluge. The water takes me, and I let it. My body goes hydrodynamic, my limbs streamlining as the current rushes me down a vertical chute. My gill-slits flare wide, extracting the heavy oxygen from the aerated water. I am not drowning; I am flying through the veins of the world. 

I am expelled into a lower catch-basin with a heavy splash, the black water closing over my head. I paddle silently to the edge, pulling myself onto a calcified ledge. Above me, the burst pipe vomits its excess into the basin, and feeding on the fresh chemical runoff is a sprawling, magnificent bloom of bioluminescent fungi. It glows with a pale, ghostly green, painting the underbelly of the factory in an eerie twilight. 

I harvest. I tear the fleshy caps from the masonry, stuffing them into the woven pouch at my waist. The toxins would kill a creature of the sun, but to me, they are sweet, rich fuel. As I eat, I feel the familiar rumble of the city shifting. A stamping press starts up in the distance, the rhythmic clang vibrating through the water and into my chest. 

I finish my meal and climb. I find a warm, vibrating space adjacent to a sub-level turbine, a hollow in the iron where the heat is constant and the mist collects in thick, heavy droplets. I press my back against the warm metal, feeling the machine’s pulse synchronize with my own. 

I pull my ivy-cloak tight around my shoulders, the thick, woven leaves protecting me from the sudden, scalding hiss of a nearby steam-vent. I extend one long arm, pressing my palm flat against the iron wall of my hollow. I tap out a slow, resonant rhythm. 

*Thrum. Thrum-thrum. Thrum.*

The harvest is good. The heat is steady. The machine breathes, and so do I. 

I am the gardener of the rust, the rider of the surge, the quiet pulse in the dark. I am the Slough-Walker, and I am exactly where I belong.
