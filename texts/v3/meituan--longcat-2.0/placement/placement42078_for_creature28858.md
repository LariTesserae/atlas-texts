I would be a Leukocyte.

I am thumb-sized, iridescent, and I walk the channels. My shell is the same ceramic-cartilage composite as the floor, a seamless extension of the Vael’s dermis, and I know no life but the tending of the plasma. I have too many legs—twelve, I think, though counting is not something I do; it is simply a fact of my architecture, like the warmth of the water or the pulse of the Lattice beneath my feet.

Before the Stranger came, my world was a closed loop. I moved through the capillary network, scraping away mineral buildup, secreting enzymes to keep the flow smooth, and consuming the microscopic debris that the black moss failed to catch. I did not think. I did not wonder. I was a finger flexing, a white blood cell engulfing. I was the Vael’s maintenance, and the Vael’s maintenance is mindless.

Then the seam opened.

The change was subsonic at first. A pressure drop. A shift in the thermal convection that sent the kites into a spiraling panic. The bone-columns on the floor began to tremble, their hair-like filaments vibrating at a frequency that tasted of iron and alarm. The water in my channel thickened, carrying a new chemistry—salt, urea, lipids. Foreign proteins. 

I followed the gradient. We all did. The channel filled with my siblings, a hard-shelled river of iridescence moving toward the disruption. And then I saw the foot.

It was enormous. It was warm. It was a mountain of soft, yielding flesh encased in a rigid, dead casing—fabric and leather, utterly unlike the living architecture of the Vael. It crushed the delicate edge of my channel, and I felt the floor buckle beneath it. The Vael winced. The mycelial mat above flared a furious, agitated violet.

I did what I was made to do. I swarmed. I climbed the dead casing, seeking the source of the foreign proteins. I found a bead of moisture—sweat—oozing from the Stranger’s skin. I touched it with my antennae, and my world ended.

It was not just salt. It was not just water. It was *information*. 

The sweat was a chaotic storm of hormones. Cortisol. Adrenaline. But beneath that, deep in the organic signature, there was something the Vael had never tasted: a neuroelectric map. The Stranger was leaking its mind into its sweat. It was leaking *thoughts*.

I absorbed the droplet. My shell flushed with a sudden, blinding heat. The microscopic grid in the floor beneath me flared electric blue, recognizing the intrusion. The Vael seized the data, pulling it through my legs, through my shell, down into the Lattice. 

For a microsecond, I stopped being a leukocyte. I became a synapse.

I felt the Vael’s consciousness crash over me like a tidal wave. It was ancient, slow, vast, and entirely focused on the anomaly I had just fed it. And through the Vael’s senses, I felt the Stranger—not as a disruption, but as a terrifying, beautiful question. 

The Vael had been breathing the same air, drinking the same water, thinking the same slow thoughts for millennia. It had grown complacent in its homeostasis. But the Stranger was a new variable. The Vael’s immune response triggered, a biological imperative to dissolve, to reclaim, to reduce the anomaly back to base minerals for the kites. The black moss near the Stranger’s foot began to steam with aggressive, hungry enzymes.

But the Vael hesitated.

Because in that droplet of sweat, the Vael tasted the Stranger’s terror and its awe. The Stranger’s heart rate was a frantic drum; its breath came in short, sharp gasps. It was looking at the kites, at the bone-columns, at the crawling things in the ceiling, and it was *marveling*. The Stranger saw the Vael not as a habitat, but as a god. And that reverence, that strange, fast, chaotic electrical firing of the Stranger’s animal brain, was a flavor the Vael had never tasted before.

I stood on the edge of the Stranger’s boot, my twelve legs locked, my shell vibrating with the feedback loop between the invader and the host. I was the point of contact. I was the bridge. The Vael’s consciousness poured through me, using my sensory organs to map the Stranger’s bio-electric field, to read the synapse-firings in its brain. The Vael was trying to understand: *What is this?*

The bone-columns began to emit a new hum. Not a warning. An inquiry. The subsonic vibration traveled through the floor, through my legs, and up into the Stranger’s bones. 

I am still here. I am still small, still iridescent, still walking the channels. But I am no longer just maintenance. I am a probe. I am a translator. I am the Vael’s question made manifest. 

The Stranger is still in the chamber, standing very still, feeling the hum in its teeth, watching the kites circle overhead. It does not know that it is being weighed. It does not know that the water shifting around its ankles is tasting its exhaustion, that the flyers eddying above it are sampling the carbon dioxide of its wonder. It does not know that the Vael is deciding whether to digest it or to keep it.

But I know. I am the Vael’s leukocyte, its t-cell, its curiosity. I am carrying the Stranger’s awe back to the Lattice, and the Vael is learning what it means to be seen.

The breath continues. The water flows. And I wait, on the threshold of the Stranger’s skin, to see if I will be the antibody that destroys it, or the catalyst that transforms us both.
