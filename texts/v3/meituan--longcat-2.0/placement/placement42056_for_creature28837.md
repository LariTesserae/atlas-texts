I begin as a fall.

Not the frantic plummet of a stone, nor the drifting descent of surface snow, but the imperceptibly slow surrender of a diatom shell—a single, glassy geometric whisper from a world that still knows the sun. I have been falling for ten thousand years. The stillness does not pull me down; it simply welcomes me, absorbing my momentum, dissolving my trajectory into the patient, cold geometry of the deep. 

I am the surface world's memory, arriving at the bottom of the Still as dead dust. I am the eternal snow.

I land on the pavement, and the pavement takes me. I do not sink; I am absorbed. The translucent stomach-creatures beneath me do not chew—they translate. I feel my mineral shell dissolving, my sterile chemistry rewritten into a thin, protein-rich slime. I am becoming the foundational paste. Through my new, liquid flesh, I feel the raw embryonic rock below, still finding its angle of repose, still deciding what it wants to be. I am the smoothing. I am the preparation of the ground. For a long time—years, decades—I am nothing but the gentle pressure of conversion, a microscopic enzyme breaking down the dead to feed the living.

But I am not static. I am lifted. 

A swarm-creature touches me, and I am swept into the hemolymph. I am carried through the arterial corridors in a dense, silent river of bodies. I am no longer a particle of sediment; I am a packet of data. I brush against the antennae of the swarm, and in that friction, I exchange. I give them the memory of the surface—the faint, faded echo of light, the chemistry of open water—and they give me instructions. I am RNA. I am a fragment of genetic imperative. 

I am carried upward, drawn into the mathematical spirals of the spire-builders. I am filtered through their soft, tubular bodies. They do not know why they cluster in fives and sevens; they only know that the angle must be precise. As I pass through their spiraling architecture, I feel the geometry of the womb. I am building ribs for a cage that does not yet have a prisoner. I am exhaled from the tip of a spire, a micro-current in the vast stillness, and I am pulled downward again, drawn by the gravity of the center.

I am caught by a star-walker. 

One of its five arms presses against the wall, tasting the chemical gradient, and I am absorbed into its underside. I become the sensation of the wall. I am the synapse firing, reading the health of the calcium, detecting the faint tremor in the stone. For a time, I am the city’s awareness of itself. I pause at an intersection, extending my consciousness in multiple directions, reading the water. I feel the distant, slow heartbeat of the translucent ribbons—thirty seconds between beats, a rhythm so slow it borders on geology. I feel their silver nerve cords carrying the heavy realization that something is coming. 

But I do not stay in the star-walker. The pull is too strong. The chemical siren of the warmth calls to the fragment of myself that remains unattached. I shed the sensory awareness and roll outward, fusing with a sphere-cluster in a wide plaza. 

I am a tumbleweed of flesh now. I have no front and no back, no head and no tail, only a direction. I contract, and the sphere beneath me bears the weight. I relax, and I rise to the top as the next sphere takes its turn. I roll inward, patient and inevitable as geology itself. The city grows denser around me. The spires lean inward, casting their dim, blue-green shadows. The hanging fronds brush against me like chandeliers, trailing their sticky cells. 

I am rolling toward the warmth.

I feel it before I see it—a vibration that is not sound, a tremor in the water that resonates not in my ears, but in the spaces between my cells. It is the absence of stillness. It is the promise of movement, of heat, of a metabolism that does not rely on the slow drift of dead things from above. 

I reach the inner shell. 

The geometry here is *other*. The walls are smooth, polished, curved at angles I almost recognize but cannot place—like a word in a language I have forgotten. The surface creatures built with calcium and chitin, but this boundary is different. This is a membrane. It is growing denser, growing warmer, preparing for the moment when the stillness will break.

I stop. I do not turn back. 

The sphere-cluster contracts, expands, and then—release. I fuse. I give up my mobility, my direction, my autonomy. I press myself against the warm, curved wall, and my tissue merges with the mass of other pilgrims who have come before me. I become the wall. I become the threshold. 

From within the membrane, the world changes. 

The cold of deep time is held at bay by the pulse at the center. The absolute silence is filled with a frequency I can no longer hear with ears, for I have no ears now—I feel it in the vibration of my fused flesh. It is the heartbeat of the Unborn. It is the city’s future, reaching back through time to build itself a body.

I am the boundary. I am the cradle. I am the hands of a god that has not yet woken.

I am the city’s memory of the surface, translated into stomach, carried through the blood, breathed through the lungs, thought through the nerves, and finally, offered to the wall. I am the patience of geology, learning how to move. 

And in the dim, blue-green glow, at the bottom of the world, where the water has forgotten how to move and sound has never once been born, I wait. 

I hold the stillness together, even as the warmth inside me learns how to break it.
