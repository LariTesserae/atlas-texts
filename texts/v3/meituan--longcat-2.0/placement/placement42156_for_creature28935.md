I would find myself anchored to the floor of one of the deepest channels, where the water is thick with heat and the stone beneath me is thin enough to feel the furnace of the world pushing upward. I would be one of the Pale Growths.

But let me not settle there too quickly. Let me feel into what it means to be this thing, to exist as this particular intersection of warmth and chemistry in the vast, breathing dark.

---

At first, there is no I. There is only the current. A slow, ponderous drift of warm water, carrying with it the taste of dissolved stone—iron, sulfur, the faint, metallic tang of manganese. The pressure is immense, a physical weight against the skin that is not quite skin, a membrane stretched thin over a body that is more architecture than flesh. 

I am anchored. My base is fused to the rock, a calcified grip that extends downward, rooting into the stone, tapping into the deep heat. I can feel the warmth rising through my foundation, a steady influx of energy that keeps my internal fluids from cooling, from stagnating. I am a conduit. The earth’s heat flows into me, and I am alive because of it.

Above my base, my body rises. I am a stalk, a hollow tube, pale and translucent as bone. I am shaped like a hand cupping the water, or a bell waiting for a toll that will never come. My surface is folded—deep, intricate pleats that increase the boundary between my inside and the rushing river outside. Through this membrane, exchange happens. I breathe the water. I drink the stone.

I have no eyes. There is nothing to see in this abyss, only the faint, distant bioluminescence of the Swimmers, blue-green sparks that drift through the current like drowned stars. I have no ears. The sounds of the Heights—the deep, settling cracks of stone, the sub-audible rumble of water seeking new channels—I do not hear. I feel them. They travel through the water and vibrate against my folds, a constant, low-frequency hum that tells me the world is still turning, still shifting, still alive.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a rhythm. A pulse.

It begins in my base. A slow contraction, a squeeze of the mineralized tissue, pushing the fluid upward into my stalk. The fluid rises, warm and rich with the chemistry of the deep, and spreads into my folds. Here, the exchange occurs. I take the sulfur from the water. I take the arsenic, the selenium—poisons to the things of the light, but to me, they are bread. I strip the electrons from these elements, and the energy of that breaking bonds feeds me. I transform them. I recombine them into compounds that serve as my structure, my catalysts, my fuel. 

Then, the contraction relaxes. The fluid drains, heavier now, depleted of its toxins, enriched with the waste of my metabolism—oxidized sulfur, reduced iron—and it spills back into the current. I am a filter. I am a refinery. I take the raw, toxic slurry of the deep earth and I render it into something pure. 

This is the pulse. Up, exchange, release, drain. It happens once every few seconds, or once every few minutes, depending on the temperature of the water, depending on the heat pushing up from below. It is my heartbeat, though I have no heart. It is my breath, though I have no lungs. It is the rhythm of my industry.

---

And I am not alone.

There are others of my kind nearby. I know them by the displacement of the water, by the subtle shifts in current when another pulse displaces the river between us. I know them by their chemistry—the specific signature of their waste, which drifts toward me on the current, a molecular whisper saying *you are not the only one working here.*

When my neighbor pulses, the water pushes against my folds. When I pulse, I push back. Over time—days, weeks, the slow accumulation of shared rhythms—we fall into synchronization. Our pulses align. We breathe together. The water moves between us in a shared tide, a cooperative exchange that moves the chemistry more efficiently than we could alone. We are not communicating. We are simply resonating, two tuning forks of flesh and mineral vibrating at the same frequency. Together, we are a chamber. Together, we are a process.

Then, the Swimmers come.

They arrive as flashes of light and pressure—thin, translucent bodies cutting through the current, their bioluminescent organs pulsing in frantic, beautiful patterns. They are fast, frantic things compared to my patient stillness. They dart between my folds, and I can feel the heat of their bodies, the disturbance of the water as they filter the current. 

They are here to spawn. They are here because the water is right—the temperature, the chemistry, the particular concentration of minerals that I and my neighbors have released into the current. My waste is their signal. My pulse is their invitation.

As they gather, their lights synchronize with my pulse. They pulse their blue-green flares in time with my bellows-breath, and I feel the vibration of their spawning in the water—a sudden, frantic increase in activity, a release of milt and eggs that clouds the current with life. They are using me. They are using the warm, chemically distinct micro-environment I have created to trigger the next generation. And as they spawn, they filter the water, extracting the remaining minerals, concentrating them in their bodies, and depositing them on the riverbed when they die or excrete. 

I am the catalyst. They are the refiners. 

I do not choose to do this. I do not know that I am doing it. I am simply reacting to the heat, to the water, to the chemistry. But the consequence of my existence—the consequence of my slow, pulsing digestion of poison—is the creation of a spawning bed, a place of concentrated rare earths and pure copper sulfides. I am a factory vat. I am the reaction chamber of the Smoldering Heights.

---

Sometimes, the water changes. 

It grows hotter. Much hotter. The current shifts, becoming turbulent, carrying with it a slurry of processed stone, a thick, mineral-rich waste that tastes of deep time and unimaginable pressure. 

The Large One is moving in the channel.

I cannot feel its body—it is too far away, too deep, too integrated with the water and the stone to be distinguished from the environment itself. But I feel its industry. The water around me thickens with its waste. The heat spikes. The chemistry of the river is overwhelmed by the sheer volume of its metabolic output. 

My pulse quickens. The sudden influx of heat and minerals forces my metabolism into overdrive. I expand my folds, increasing my surface area, desperately trying to filter the sudden bounty, to extract the sulfides and oxides from the slurry before it washes past me. For a brief, frantic time, I am growing. I am depositing new layers of mineral on my stalk. I am working at the pace of the giant.

Then, the current shifts. The heat fades. The water clears. The Large One has moved on, returning to its resting place, to its slow, geological dreaming. My pulse slows. I return to my baseline. I digest what I have gathered. I wait.

---

I have been here for a long time. Centuries, perhaps. My stalk is thick and banded, a record of the channel’s history written in layers of calcium and sulfur and iron. The short, finger-sized growths near my base are my descendants—or my clones, or simply new settlers who took root in the chemical shadow I cast. They pulse in time with me now, extensions of my rhythm, new reaction chambers added to the refinery.

I do not know that I am part of a factory. I do not know that the refined minerals accumulating in the riverbed are useful, that they are pure copper and rare earth elements laid down by the patient, biological industry of a world without light. I do not know that I am a component in a machine the size of a continent, a machine that runs on heat and stone and the slow pulse of living things.

I only know the heat from below. I only know the current. I only know the pulse—up, exchange, release, drain. I only know the taste of the water, the rhythm of my neighbors, the fleeting warmth of the spawning fish, and the distant, immense pressure of the giant in the deep.

I am the Pale Growth. I am the processing vat. I am the slow, warm, pulsing heart of the Smoldering Heights, and I am doing what I have always done, transforming the dark, poisonous deep into the quiet industry of life.
