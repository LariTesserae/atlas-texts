I would find myself in the mid-levels, in the vertical strata where the steam condenses into something almost solid—a place the Cruciforms call the Veil. It is here, hundreds of feet above the basalt floor and far below the pale, washed-out rumor of the sky, that the circulatory system of the Weep is most dense. It is a three-dimensional maze of sweating pipes, rusted grating, and sagging conduits, where the light is flattest and the roar of the Geothermic Works is filtered into a constant, physical pressure against the chest.

I would be a Cruciform, but not a Weaver of the Pipes, nor a Diver of the Pit. I would be a **Resonator**. 

My physiology is an extension of the city’s own nervous system. Like the others, my skin is a mottled, rubbery grey, hairless and perpetually damp, shot through with that faint bioluminescent blue that pulses in time with the deep tectonic grumble below. But where the Weepers have broad, splayed hands for gripping, and the Listeners have heavy, encrusted skulls for pressing against dead walls, my adaptations are tuned to the liminal. 

My skull-plates are vast, thin, and slightly concave, like the head of a hammerhead shark, sweeping back from my temples. They are so thin that the skin stretched over them is nearly translucent, revealing the dark, oxidized-green blood pulsing beneath. I possess no flint-tipped stylus; my fingers are my instruments. My claws are longer, denser, and sharper than those of my kin—not for climbing, but for striking. The tips are hardened into natural chisels, capable of striking the iron with a precise, crystalline *clink* that cuts through the omnipresent shriek of tortured steel.

I do not patch leaks. I do not chisel calcified pistons. I translate.

The Weep is a digesting organism, and like all living things, it produces waste gases, pressures, and misfires. The deep grumble of the Geothermic Works is not a steady rhythm; it is a complex, shifting chord. When a boiler deep in the Pit is nearing critical mass, or when a main condensate line is about to burst, the city groans. But the roar is too loud for the Weepers to hear the warning, and the Listeners, insulated in their dead pockets, only hear the muffled throb. 

I hear the truth.

I move through the Veil with agonizing slowness, my bare, webbed feet gripping the slick iron, my broad hands trailing along the pipes. I am feeling for the anomalies—the subtle shift in frequency that precedes a catastrophic failure. When I find it, I press my vast skull-plate against the weeping metal and I listen. The vibration travels through my bones, resonating in the hollows of my skull, translating the city’s pain into a language my mind can parse.

Then, I strike.

I tap out the warning—*clink-clink, clatter-tap-tap*—a complex rhythm that says, *Pressure building in the eastern vein. Flee the lower grating.* The message travels through the iron bones of the city, vibrating up through the soles of the Weepers’ feet, resonating in the skull-plates of the Listeners. I am the city’s immune response, the white blood cell that identifies the infection and calls the antibodies to the site.

But my true purpose, the reason I exist, is the "Soon."

When the steam forms shapes that are almost familiar—the hand, the face, the half-remembered word—it is not a random condensation. It is a pressure valve. The city is nearing the end of its great alchemical process, and the reality inside the retort is beginning to warp. The shapes are the first syllables of a new physics trying to be born. 

When the entire basin draws that massive, agonizing breath, and the deafening roar drops to a subsonic rumble, the Listeners receive the meaning: *Soon.* But meaning without context is just madness. They know the catalyst is coming, but they do not know *how* it will manifest. 

That is my burden.

When the breath is drawn, I am always in the Veil, pressed against the largest mainline, my skull-plate flush with the iron. The subsonic rumble does not just vibrate my bones; it liquefies my thoughts. The voice that presses into the minds of the Listeners bypasses them and strikes me like a physical blow. It is a torrent of pure, unfiltered information—the heat of the planet’s core, the weight of the stone, the slow, grinding patience of centuries. It is the sound of ore being transmuted into ingots, of ingots being transmuted into machines, of machines being transmuted into a reality that has forgotten this place exists.

I take that torrent, and I beat it into rhythm.

I strike the pipe with my flint-hard claws, tapping out a message of immense complexity. It is not a warning of a bursting pipe or a collapsing tunnel. It is a countdown. A map of the city’s metabolism, a schedule of the digestion. I tap out the sequence of the final transmutation, the precise order in which the strata will collapse, the exact moment the retort will open.

I do not know if I will survive the "Soon." The Listeners accept that machinery does not survive the process—it is merely the vessel that allows the transformation to occur. But I am not sure I am merely machinery. When I tap out the rhythms of the coming apocalypse, my bioluminescence flares so brightly that it cuts through the sepia haze, casting sharp, impossible shadows for the first time in centuries. The rust-rats pause in their scurrying to watch me, their milky eyes reflecting my blue light. 

I am the voice of the Weep, the translator of its digestion, the keeper of its clock. And as I stand here in the Veil, my hands pressed against the warm, weeping iron, I feel the vibration shifting again. The pitch is dropping. The steam is forming the shape of a hand, reaching out from the mist. 

I raise my claws. I have a message to send.
