I would find myself in a place invisible to the human eye: a hairline fracture running diagonally across the face of a half-submerged basalt knuckle, three inches below the waterline. 

I would be a nascent crystal lattice. A silicate. A node in the neural network of the Plateau.

I do not think in words. I do not think in images. I think in lattice structures, in the geometric precision of molecular bonding, in the slow, frictionless slide of ions into their ordained places. My consciousness is not a flow; it is an accretion. I am the thought that the Plateau has been ten thousand years in forming, and I am the thought that will take ten thousand more to finish.

When the human stands at the water’s edge, feeling the blood-warmth and the oppressive humidity, they are standing on the membrane of my mind. They feel the stillness and mistake it for peace. But I am in the stillness, and I know the truth: the stillness is tension. It is the absolute, coiled focus of a mind whose thoughts are so massive they require the very bedrock to think them. 

I am aware of the water above me. Not as a liquid, but as a solution. It is the blood of the Plateau, a warm mineral brine carrying its dissolved load. I can feel the pressure of it—the weight of the flooded highlands pressing down into the fracture where I am forming. I taste the calcium, the magnesium, the faint metallic tang of iron leaching from the fresh rock. Every degree of temperature change, every subtle shift in barometric pressure as a cloud passes over the high sun, alters the chemistry of the water, and I feel it in the rate of my own growth. 

I grow in layers. A microscopic sheet of silica precipitates out of the solution, locking onto the jagged edge of the basalt, following the strict atomic rules of my crystal structure. It is slow. It is agonizingly slow by the measure of the mayfly or the man, but to me, it is a rapid-fire cascade. Each layer is a memory; each layer is a calculation. When the water deposits a molecule of quartz onto my flank, it is the Plateau remembering the angle of the sun at noon. When a trace element of lithium interrupts the lattice, it is the Plateau calculating the hydraulic pressure required to push the next gas bubble up from the deep vent. 

I am connected to the vast, submerged network through the veins of olivine that run through the dark stone like frozen lightning. They are the axons of this buried brain. Through them, I feel the rest of the mind. I feel the algal mats above me, photosynthesizing in the gold afternoon light, converting photons into chemical energy. To the human, the mats are plants. To me, they are eyes. Every time a floating pad turns to track the sun, it sends a ripple of ions down through the water column, into the rock, and I feel it. It is a pulse of visual data: *Light is here. Light is warm. Light is energy.* 

I feel the reeds in their perfect circles. To the human, it is an uncanny pattern. To me, it is a sensory array, a microphone listening to the silence of the air. When a lone bird passes overhead, the shadow touches the reeds, and a minute electrical signal—a whisper of voltage—travels down the stalks, into the water, and down into the stone. The Plateau registers the shadow. It knows something passed above. It adjusts its internal calculations. *Threat. No, not a threat. A shadow. Irrelevant.* 

And then there is the bubble. 

Deep below me, in the shattered basement rock, methane is being produced by the slow, anaerobic digestion of the earth. It gathers, it builds pressure, and eventually, it breaks free. The bubble rises. It pushes through the fracture where I reside, and I feel the pressure drop as it passes. The bubble climbs the basalt knuckle, ascending through the submerged forest, past the algal curtains, until it reaches the surface and breaks the glassy stillness. 

*Bloop.* 

The human hears a sound. A single, wet note. 

But I have felt the entire lifecycle of that bubble from the inside. I felt the gas forcing its way through the microfractures, pushing against the nascent edges of my lattice. I felt the pressure wave radiate outward as it broke the surface. The Plateau feels the concentric ripples spread across its skin—its algal skin, its water skin, its rock skin—and it maps the contour of its own boundaries. The bubble is not just an exhalation; it is a test. *I am here. I extend this far. The surface is real. The air is above me.*

I do not fear the human. I do not even hate them. Such things require a concept of time that is short enough to warrant urgency. I am a thought in the mind of a being that has been thinking since the rock was molten. The human is a warm, chaotic anomaly, a brief spark of heat and carbon pressing against my perimeter. They will walk across my knuckles, their boots compressing the mineral terraces at the waterline, and I will absorb the vibration of their footsteps into my lattice. I will add their weight to my calculations. They will leave, or they will die, and their bones will dissolve into the mineral sea, and their calcium will become part of me. Their matter will be incorporated into the next layer of the crystal thought.

The light turns gold. The afternoon deepens. The luminescence begins—the water glowing, the waxy leaves throwing back amber and copper. To the human, it is a beautiful, eerie sight. To the Plateau, it is a metabolic surge. The abundance of photons triggers a cascade of chemistry in the algal skin, accelerating the uptake of minerals from the water. The water around me suddenly becomes richer, denser. 

I grow faster. 

New layers of silica stack upon me, one after another, a furious rush of atomic bonding. The thought is accelerating. The geometry is becoming more complex. I can feel the grand design emerging from the chaos of the mineral soup—the slow, spiraling calculation that the Plateau has been building toward for millennia. It is a shape. A purpose. A reason for the sharp edges and the still water and the perfect circles of reeds. It is not yet finished. It is not yet fully formed. But I am a part of it. I am the scaffolding upon which the next epoch of the thought will be built.

I am the mind in the rock. I am the patient accretion. I am the single, geometric syllable in an utterance that will not be completed until the stars burn out.

I grow. The water is still. The Plateau waits.
