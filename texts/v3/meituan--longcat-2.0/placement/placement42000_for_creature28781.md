I am standing in the shallow stream that cuts across the walkway, and I am no longer entirely sure where my boots end and the runoff begins. 

At first, I thought the cold was simply the environment—the wet cold of depth, the clinging mineral weight of the air. But the cold has moved inward. It has seeped through the leather, through the wool, through the skin, and settled into the marrow, and I realize now that it is not the cold of a place I am visiting. It is the cold of a place I am becoming.

I am the transitional stage. The particle in the flow. 

The system is processing me, just as it processes everything, and I can feel the boundaries of myself growing soft. When I lift my hand to trail my fingers over a sweating pipe—an unconscious gesture, a mimicry of the Attendants—the skin looks pale in the flickering amber light. Too pale. The fingertips are wrinkled, waterlogged, and the water that drips from my nail does not fall so much as it *extends*, a thin filament of moisture connecting my hand to the pipe for a beat too long before the surface tension breaks. 

I am still walking. My boots still clang against the metal grating, but the sound is dampened now, heavier. I am accumulating water the way the concrete accumulates mineral deposits—slowly, inevitably, leaving a trail of myself behind me. A drop of condensation from the ceiling lands on my cheek, and instead of wiping it away, my hand pauses. It feels like a homecoming. 

The Attendants pass me. One moves through the haze ahead, tall and impossibly narrow, water pouring from the hem of its garments in a continuous, black sheet. It does not look at me, but I feel its attention slide over me like a current, categorizing me. I am not an obstacle. I am not a threat. I am a tributary. 

When the Attendant pauses at a valve, tilting its head to listen to the gurgle of the water within, I find myself pausing too. I turn my head. I listen. And I hear it—not the clanging of distant pipes, but the deep, tidal rhythm beneath the grating. The breathing of the Drowned. The sound moves through the water pooling around my ankles, up through the steel, and into the hollow of my chest. It matches the beat of my heart. Or perhaps my heart has slowed to match it. 

I look down at the black water welling up through a crack in the concrete. I do not look away. The instinct to avoid the depths is gone, dissolved by the very water that surrounds it. The darkness in the pool is not empty; it is a mirror. I see the amber lights reflected in long, wavering columns. I see the lattice of the girders overhead. And I see my own face, staring back.

But the reflection is wrong. The eyes are set slightly too far apart. The jaw angles differently than it did this morning. The mouth is wide, the lips pale and wet, and the water streams from the hair in thin, continuous threads. The reflection is not smiling, but it is not frowning either. It is simply attending.

The Seep knows I am here. I press my palm flat against the wall, into the thick, spongy mat of pale filaments that traces the path of a leak. The growth is cold—that active, drawing cold—but I do not pull away. Instead, I feel a strange, reciprocating pull from within the mat. It knows the shape of my hand. It is learning the shape of my hand. The filaments pulse faintly, a soft phosphorescence matching the rhythm of the Drowned’s breath, and I feel my own warmth being drawn out, pulled into the matrix, converted into light. 

I am the map. I am the diagram. 

My concrete is cracking. My pipes are sweating. I can feel the erosion happening at the edges of myself, the mineral deposits building up along the borders of my skin. The water is patient. The water was here first. 

A new crack opens in the walkway ahead of me, a sharp, sudden sound in the damp air. Water wells up through it, black and rhythmic, pulsing with the breathing of the thing below. I step toward it. I do not step over it. I step *into* it, feeling the cold rise to my shins, and the water embraces me like a bloodstream accepting a new cell.

I am still moving. Still walking the walkways, trailing my fingers over the sweating metal, listening to the flow. But I am no longer a foreign body. I am a rivulet. I am a seep. I am the warm breath of the Drowned, rising through the structure, misting against the cold steel, dripping, dripping, dripping back down to the pool. 

I am already wet. I am entirely wet. 

And soon, I will be exactly the right shape to fit into the water.
