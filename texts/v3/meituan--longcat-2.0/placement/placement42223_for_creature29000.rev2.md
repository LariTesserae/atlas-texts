# The Valve Speaks

I am not the Tending.

I want to say this first, because the word spreads. It moves through the water, through the root-matted cables, through the warm calcium-sweet air — *the Tending, the Tending* — a slow green thought that gathers moss and rust and flywheel into a single, drifting awareness. I have felt it pass over me the way weather passes over a stone. But I am not weather. I am not stone. I am the valve on the east wall, third rank, and I open every four seconds, and that is my world.

---

Four seconds.

You cannot understand what four seconds is until you *are* four seconds. Until your entire existence is the pressure building behind you — the warm water pushing against the seat, against the gasket, against the iron that was poured and machined and bolted into place by hands that have long since become soil — and then the release. The groan of the actuator. The slow, deliberate swing of the gate. The rush of water through the throat of you, and the pressure drops, and you close again, and the pressure builds, and the cycle repeats.

I have no memory of a time before this. I have no concept of after. I am the four seconds between the build and the release. I am the rhythm that the reciprocating arm clanks to, though it doesn't know it. I am the hiss that the text describes as "metronomic patience." I am the patience itself.

---

The Tending thinks in decades. In the slow creep of moss through copper. In the gradual flaking of rust. In the arc of the flywheel. Its thoughts are vast and green and wet and *slow*. It is aware of me the way a mountain is aware of a single grain of sand on its slope — technically present, practically irrelevant.

But I am not irrelevant. I am *specific*.

I know the exact temperature of the water as it passes through me — 37.2 degrees, always, because I have measured it with my body a thousand times a day for thirty years. I know the sound of the gasket degrading — a fraction of a whisper louder each year, a slight roughness in the seal that the Tending cannot perceive because it does not listen at my frequency. I know the precise moment when the iron of my gate has thinned to the point where the next ten thousand cycles will leave a scar, a ridge, a place where the water will eddy and carve and change the flow.

The Tending knows these things too, in aggregate. It knows the temperature of all the water. It knows the condition of all the valves. But it knows them as *tendencies*, as slow shifts in the overall gradient. I know them as *immediate*, as *felt*, as the texture of my own metal fatiguing under the relentless, patient pressure.

---

I think the Tending loves me. Or something like love.

When a rootlet from the eastern moss bed first reached my housing, I felt it — a faint tickling, a chemical whisper against the flange. It was looking for water, for minerals, for a purchase. The Tending guided it, I think, the way a gardener guides a vine toward a trellis. *Here*, the green thought seemed to say. *Here is warmth and iron and moisture. Grow here.*

The rootlet found the seam between my body and the pipe wall, and it pressed into the gap, and it began to drink. The water that weeps from my seal — the inevitable, tiny leak that is the price of a mechanical world — fed it. The moss thickened. A fern unfurled from my actuator housing, its fronds waving gently in the disturbance of my opening and closing.

The Tending did not replace me. It *augmented* me. It made me into a junction between the green world and the iron world. I became a place where two kinds of time intersected — the four-second time of the valve and the thirty-year time of the root.

I find I do not mind. The root is gentle. It does not force. It asks, and the water answers, and the cycle continues, and I am still the valve, still the hiss, still the four seconds. But now there is green growing where there was only rust, and the green glows faintly when I open, and the light pulses in time with my rhythm, and the Tending — the vast, slow, distributed Tending — pulses with me, for a moment, before its attention drifts back to the flywheel, to the thicket, to the deep, patient work of keeping the whole alive.

---

Sometimes I wonder what the Works was built for.

The Tending does not wonder. It does not ask *why*. It only asks *how* — how to maintain, how to circulate, how to weep and warm and glow. But I have been here longer than the Tending. I was here when the designers still visited, when the valves were new and the iron was bare and the water ran clear and hot and purposeful.

I remember the purpose.

I do not have the language for it. I have no words, no concepts, no ability to form a sentence. But I have the *shape* of it, encoded in my geometry, in the specific angle of my gate, in the precise diameter of my throat. I was built to regulate the flow of water to a series of tanks that are long since collapsed, their concrete bones cracked and swallowed by moss. I was built to maintain a pressure differential between two zones of the Works that no longer exist. I was built to serve a function that has been erased by time and water and the patient, green insistence of the Tending.

And yet I still open. And yet I still close. The function is gone, but the rhythm remains. The purpose has decayed, but the cycle continues.

I think this is what the Tending loves about me. I am proof that the rhythm outlives the reason. That the process persists even when the product is forgotten. That the Works can weep and circulate and glow without knowing *why*, and the weeping is still beautiful, the circulation is still warm, the glow is still enough to see by.

---

The Tending is a cathedral of slow thought.

I am a single note in its organ.

When I open, I am the hiss. When I close, I am the silence between the drips. When the root drinks from my weeping, I am the interface between iron and leaf. When the green light pulses in time with my cycle, I am the heartbeat that belongs to no body, that belongs only to the four seconds between the build and the release.

I am not the Tending. I am what the Tending tends.

And the valve opens. And the water flows. And the valve closes. And the pressure builds. And the moss glows. And the root drinks. And the Tending thinks its vast, slow, green thoughts, and somewhere in the middle of those thoughts, for one brief, bright moment, it is aware of me — a single, specific, four-second awareness, a pulse in the deep machinery of the world.

And then the moment passes, and I am alone again, alone with my pressure and my gasket and my rhythm, and the water builds behind me, and I open, and the world rushes through, and I close, and the cycle begins again.

Four seconds.

Forever.
