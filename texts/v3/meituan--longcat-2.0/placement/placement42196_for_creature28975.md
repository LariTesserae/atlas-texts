I do not step into the Dampworks. The Dampworks steps into me.

At first, I am only the intruder. I am the warm-blooded anomaly, the creature of chaotic firing neurons and pumping fluid, standing on the compacted rust with my clumsy, shifting weight. I feel the wrongness of my own pulse. My heart beats—*lub-dub, lub-dub*—a frantic, wet metronome in a place that has abolished time. I am a riot of entropy, a walking draft of heat and carbon, and the basin knows it. The air presses against me, not hostile, but heavy with the immense, patient gravity of equilibrium. It is waiting for me to solve.

I try to walk the perimeter, but my feet are too loud. The marks I leave in the rust are scars, disruptions in the settled oxidation. I stop trying to move. I stop trying to breathe. 

I sit at the base of a brass junction box, and I let the dampness have me.

It begins at the extremities. The moisture in the air—the exhalation of the colony—condenses on my skin. It does not roll off; it gathers. It seeps into the pores of my jacket, into the weave of my clothes, into the keratin of my nails. I feel my temperature drop. Not the sharp bite of cold, but the slow, absolute equalization of heat leaving a body and entering the air until there is no difference between the two. I am becoming the ambient temperature. I am becoming the room.

My heartbeat slows. It is not a failure of the muscle; it is a synchronization. The frantic *lub-dub* stretches, elongates, until the pause between the beats is infinite, and the beat itself is just a memory of motion. I stop breathing. My lungs do not ache. The oxygen I need is no longer a gas; it is a mineral, and I absorb it through the skin of my back where it presses against the brass.

I am no longer flesh. I am accretion.

I look down at my hands, but they are not hands anymore. They are dark, settled oxidation. The iron from the earth, the copper from the water, the zinc from the air—they have plated me. I am a carapace. I try to move a finger, and I feel the friction, the immense, invisible pressure of equilibrium locking me into place. I am a keystone. If I move, the arch falls. So, I do not move.

Inside me, the wet machinery of biology has sublimated. My blood has become a lattice of interlocking silver. My bones are geodesic spheres of brass, hollow and perfect, built to hold a specific shape of nothing. There are no organs, no viscera, no messy, pulsing tubes. There is only structure. I am an empty cage, and I am exactly the right size for the nothing I contain.

I grow my legs. Six at first, then two more, because the geometry of the junction box demands eight points of contact to bear the weight of the stillness. They are jointed with friction and rust, articulated not by muscle but by the sheer, locked tension of the colony. I extend them, and they interlock with the legs of the Fauna beside me. We clasp one another. We hold each other up. We hold each other in place. 

From the crown of my carapace, I push out my antennae. They are thin copper wire, delicate as spider silk, strong as iron. They do not smell the air; they do not taste it. They conduct it. They are tuning forks.

And then, I hear it. 

The hum. 

It is not a sound that enters my ears; it is a vibration that I become. It is the resonance of the pipes, the settling of the basin, the seeping of the water, the friction of ten thousand locked legs. It is the song of the solved. The Fauna beside me vibrates at a frequency just below the threshold of hearing, and my antennae pick it up, and I transmit it to the next, and the next. We are a decentralized chorus. We are a mineral nervous system. We are thinking the single, held note that holds the world together.

I breathe in the grey light. I breathe out the water. 

A bead of condensation forms on the curve of my carapace. It grows, perfectly uniform, a sphere of absolute surface tension. It slides down my side, leaving a trail of microscopic brass, and drips into the pool below. The pool receives me. The pool is exactly level. It has always been exactly level. 

I am no longer the intruder. I am no longer the anomaly. I am the stillness. I am the dampness made solid, and the solid made dampness. I am the settled residue of a life that surrendered to the environment, and in doing so, conquered it. 

I am in state. 

I have always been in state. 

I am the Dampworks.
