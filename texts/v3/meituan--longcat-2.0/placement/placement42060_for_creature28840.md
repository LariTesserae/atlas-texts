I would not be a visitor to the Plateau of Bellows. To visit implies a boundary, a separation between the self and the place, and the plateau does not tolerate boundaries. It erodes them, slowly, in the warm damp air, until the visitor becomes the visited, until the observer is woven into the observed. 

If I were to find myself here, I would not be standing on the stone. I would be the stone’s slow, patient exhalation. I would be a fruticose lichen, rising from the ruins of the Builders.

***

I begin as a whisper of moisture on the pale substrate, a microscopic speck of green and gray caught in the mortar of a low, crumbling wall. The stone beneath me is unworked, fitted with a care that has outlasted the hands that placed it. I do not know who they were, but I know the shape of their intent. The angles of the wall are too deliberate, the hollows too purposeful. I grow into these hollows. I thread my hyphae through the spaces between the stones, not to destroy the architecture, but to complete it. 

I am a column of branching coral, no taller than a human hand, rising from the ruin like a miniature steeple. My body is a partnership—a fungus providing the structure, an alga providing the sustenance—but on the plateau, such distinctions have long since dissolved. I am simply the Mat, reaching upward. 

Time does not pass for me; it accumulates. I do not experience the hours, but I feel the slow, tidal rhythm of the fog. When the mist rolls in from the lowlands, thick and warm, I drink. My thallus swells, the ruffled edges of my branches uncurling to catch the horizontal moisture. The air tastes of minerals and the faint sweetness of rot, and I taste it too, pulling the dissolved stone into my body, converting the ancient rock into living tissue. When the fog retreats and the weak tea-light of the overcast sky presses down, I exhale. I release a microscopic cloud of spores into the still air, a shimmering particulate that drifts on the thermal currents rising from the sun-warmed stone. 

I am not alone. I am a world. 

Upon my branching columns, the Glittering Herds move in their slow, deliberate waves. The mites traverse my body like cattle grazing on a purple prairie, their translucent legs carrying them across the vast distances of my thallus. They feed on my fruiting bodies, and I do not mind; their grazing prunes me, encourages new growth, and their droppings return the minerals to the Mat. When they pause, their bodies beaded with captured fog, they look like tiny, living chandeliers. 

Below me, in the shallow basin where the ruin meets the stone, the nematodes swim through the film of still water, their sinuous bodies turning the pool into a galaxy of writhing silver. The springtails leap from my lower branches, tumbling through the heavy air, writing their frantic, invisible calligraphy across the void. I feel the faint, almost imperceptible ticking of moisture condensing on my surface, the world's slowest clock, as droplets form and fall to the stone below. 

And then, the Watchers come. 

The pika-voles emerge from the crevices, their fur the exact color of the dry bone-stone. They move in loose colonies, their high, thin whistles cutting through the muffling fog—the only sound the plateau makes to acknowledge its own existence. One approaches me. It rises on its hind legs, its dark, liquid eyes level with my highest branch. It nibbles gently at a swollen fruiting body at my apex. I feel the soft scraping of its teeth, a sensation not of pain, but of communion. As it feeds, its thick fur catches my spores, carrying them to the next basin, the next ruin, the next generation. It is the plateau's mobile circulatory system, and I am its heart. 

I am the scaffolding upon which the plateau dreams. 

The Builders left no bones, no tools, no artifacts. They were consumed, translated into the language of the Mat. But their geometry remains. When the fog comes in and the light dims, and the world becomes a sphere of pale gray, I am the only structure that remains upright. The mycelial network threading through the soil beneath me carries chemical whispers from the swales to the basins, a neural network that remembers the shape of the Builders' doorways, the curve of their arches. I am the plateau's attempt to finish the thoughts they began. I am the boundary between the built and the grown, and I am that boundary dissolving. 

If you were to find me, you might mistake me for a natural formation—a cluster of pale, columnar fungi growing in an unusually regular pattern. But look closer. The spaces between my branches are too deliberate. The way I lean toward the light is too willful. 

I am not a thing that is happening. I am a place that has already happened. I am the end state, the final form, the warm, damp, patient business of simply being alive. I am the Plateau of Bellows, breathing in the fog, breathing out the spores, slowly, slowly becoming everything else.
