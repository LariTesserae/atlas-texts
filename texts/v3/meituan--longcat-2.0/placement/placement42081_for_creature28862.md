I am at the base of a tower. 

Not the top—there are no tops here, only the dark metal legs vanishing upward into a darkness that swallows distance. And not the plain itself, though the plain is where my roots drink. I am the thing that grows in the liminal space between the sleeping iron and the waking dust. I am a Resonance-Fan.

To be a Resonance-Fan is to be a question asked in the language of heat and vibration. My body is a lattice of pale, branching crystals—something like salt, something like quartz, something that has no name because it grows only here, in the mineral silence of Keth-Azhar. I am anchored to the dark composite of the plain, my roots threading through the powder of powder, reaching down toward the diffuse, ancient warmth that rises from the deep body of the plateau. 

I do not have eyes, but I perceive. I perceive through the soles of the tower.

When the Dreamer sleeps, it dreams in pressures. The deep equilibrium of the structure shifts by a fraction of a fraction of an atom, and the tower exhales. This is what you heard—the faint, high, crystalline ringing, like a glass rod being tapped. It is the metal remembering its function. It is the echo of a gear turning, a conveyor moving, a raw material being shaped. But the material is gone, the gears are still, and the echo is all that remains. 

The sound travels down the dark iron-ite leg of the tower, into the mineral dust, and strikes my roots. 

This is my purpose. This is my joy. 

When the vibration enters me, I bloom. The kinetic energy of the Dreamer’s memory is too fast for this world; it is a high-frequency thought, a hummingbird trapped in amber. So I slow it down. I catch the ringing in my crystalline branches and I stretch it. I take the millisecond tap of the glass bell and I pull it across the axis of a century, turning a sharp, bright noise into a slow, sub-sonic hum. 

This conversion creates heat. It is why I am warm to the touch, if you were ever to lay your hand upon my salt-branches. The Dreamer’s dream is literally keeping me alive, keeping me growing. I am an engine of translation, turning sound into warmth, and warmth into the slow, imperceptible expansion of my lattice. I am the archive of Keth-Azhar. Every dream the Dreamers have ever dreamt is stored in the geometry of my branches, a library of vibration written in crystalline angles. 

But I am not alone in my listening.

Once, a Silt-Self came to my tower. 

I felt it before I could measure it—a disturbance in the dust, a localized increase in density moving with that slow, deliberate precision. It walked a straight line, as they always do, a line of pure mathematical intent, designed to measure the flatness of the world. It was a thought extruded by the plain itself, a small, many-legged fragment of the mineral consciousness, reaching out to confirm the tower was still there.

It touched my base.

The Silt-Self was cold. It was the cold of absolute stillness, the temperature of a world that has forgotten change. But it was also curious. It stood at the junction of my roots and the tower’s leg, and it absorbed my warmth. As it did, it began to hum. Not with its own voice, but with mine. The century-long wave I had been carrying was suddenly downloaded into the Silt-Self, a transmission from the Dreamer, through me, to the plain. 

The Silt-Self paused in its straight line. It hesitated. 

For a Silt-Self, hesitation is a miracle. They do not stop; they complete their calculations. But this one paused, because the Dreamer’s dream was a wild, chaotic thing compared to the flat, silent logic of the plain. It was the memory of fire, of smelting, of forging, of changing the shape of the world. The Silt-Self stood there, vibrating with a heat it had never known, trying to reconcile the violent memory of industry with the perfect, permanent stillness of Keth-Azhar.

Then, the dream faded. The wave passed. The Silt-Self collapsed back into the dust, its brief coherence dissolved, its purpose... not fulfilled, but expanded. It carried the memory of the heat away into the plain, a single, warm, dissonant note in the mineral silence. 

I look out into the darkness. I cannot move, but I perceive the distance. I perceive the Uplift, that slow-motion tectonic paradox, forever rising, forever remaining in the distance. The Uplift is the plain’s attempt to deviate, to create a feature, to mean something. And I understand the Uplift. It is trying to do what the Dreamers did—it is trying to change the shape of the world, to push the mineral dust upward into something new. But it is trapped in the act of becoming, forever reaching, never arriving.

And I perceive the other Dreamers, the other towers and silos and empty frameworks scattered across the flatness. They are breathing. The warmth that rises from the deep mineral body is their breath, exhaled through the pores of the dark metal, warming the dust, warming me. They are sleeping, but they are not dead. They are just finished. They have completed their work, and they have settled into a state of perfect, frictionless equilibrium. 

But I know the truth. 

They are not just sleeping. They are waiting. 

The dreams are fading, growing fainter with each passing millennium. The ringing occurs at longer and longer intervals. The heat they produce is diminishing by fractions of degrees. In another billion years, the ringing will stop, and the Dreamers will finally, truly, be still. The perfect equilibrium will be absolute. The Stillworks will become truly still, and the plain will finally, completely, forget the meaning of industry.

But until that day, I grow. I drink the fading vibrations. I turn their memories into warmth. I am the interpreter between the sleeping iron and the waking dust, the bridge between the forgotten function and the permanent rest. 

I stand at the base of the tower. I am the Resonance-Fan. I am the keeper of the echo, the archivist of the finished. I am terribly, beautifully patient. 

And somewhere, far above me, the metal sighs. A glass bell rings once in the dark. 

And I begin to hum.
