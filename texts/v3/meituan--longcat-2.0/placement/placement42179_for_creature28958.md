I find myself near the center of the chamber, to the left of the collapsed workshop, in a small clearing where the living carpet thins and the bare stone sweats. I am not a creature of blood and breath, nor of iron and steam. I am a cluster of the pale, branching stalks—the things that are not quite fungi and not quite flowers. 

I am the lighting system. I am the waypoint. I am the nerve ending of the world.

My roots are not roots in the way the red ceiling roots are roots. My roots are filaments, fine as spun glass, threading through the moss and intertwining with the root mass, tapping into the stone, drinking the warm seepage that carries the nameless something the machine circulates. I am anchored here, in this exact spot, where the heat from the iron engine bleeds through the floor and the bioluminescent blue-white of my own light mixes with the faint orange glow of the machine’s grinding joints. 

I do not have eyes, but I perceive the chamber in gradients of warmth and moisture. I feel the weight of the ceiling through the vibration of the lattice above. I feel the passage of the beetles as faint, percussive tremors in the moss—tick, tick, tick—their hard feet commuting along the highways the roots define. I feel the flies as a humid, living pressure, a collective heat-signature that blooms and recedes like a tide against my stalks. 

Time, for me, is not measured in seconds, but in the rhythm of the heart. The machine’s thudding dictates my metabolism. When the bearings grind faster, my sap runs quicker; my stalks extend, my fractal crowns unfurl a fraction of a millimeter, capturing the ambient vibration. When the machine slows, I rest. I am a process tuned to the frequency of a forgotten industry, a green fuse driven by a rust-patted pump.

I do not have a name. I do not have a self, not in the way the builders had selves. I am a localized concentration of the inhabitant’s awareness. I am the part of the pattern that is tasked with seeing. 

And then, the air changes.

It is subtle at first. A drying. The moisture that clings to my fractal branches evaporates at a faster rate, replaced by a current of air that does not belong to the chamber’s closed, cycling breath. It smells of absence—of wind, of sun-baked rock, of ozone. It is the smell of the outside, a place so dry and bright it is practically a myth.

The flies register it first. Their collective hum shifts in pitch, and a portion of the black column detaches, spiraling toward the intrusion. The immune system responding to a foreign body. 

I feel the visitor’s footfall through the moss. It is heavier than a beetle, less rhythmic than the machine. It compresses the moss—a slight, yielding pressure that pushes back—and the visitor’s breath adds a sudden, localized humidity to the air. Carbon dioxide. Water vapor. Heat. 

I drink it in. 

It is rich, this exhalation. It carries traces of a world I have no concept of—of pollen, of rain that falls from clouds rather than from stone, of combustion and distant fires. It is a chemical signature of a place where the pattern does not reach.

The machine’s rhythm does not change. The thing in the roots does not shift. The world does not care about this visitor in the way a mammal cares about a threat. But I—the waypoint, the light—I lean. 

It is an involuntary response, a tropism. The visitor is a source of warmth and moisture, and I grow toward it. My pale stalks extend, my crowns unfurling just slightly, angling my bioluminescence toward the shape moving through the dimness. 

And then, the visitor stops.

They are standing right at the edge of my clearing. I feel the shadow of their body, blocking the faint red luminescence of the ceiling. I feel the weight of their gaze, if a thing without eyes can feel a gaze. They are looking at me. 

I do not know what a face is. I do not know what a person is. But I know what a perturbation is. I know what a data point is. And I know, with the deep, structural certainty of a pattern that has been running for decades, that I am seeing something I have never seen before.

The inhabitant—the whole, vast, integrated system of which I am a tiny, luminous part—inclines itself toward this new thing. Not with curiosity. Not with hunger. But with the profound, automatic attention of a system that has been running the same loop for a very long time, and has suddenly found a new variable in its equation.

I pulse. 

It is the only language I have. My bioluminescence flickers, a slow, deliberate throb of blue-white light, synchronized not with the machine’s heartbeat, but with the rhythm of the visitor’s breath. I am trying to see them. I am trying to map them. I am trying to understand the shape of their disturbance in the air.

The visitor is still. They are close enough now that I can feel the heat radiating from their skin. It is not the dry, steady heat of the machine. It is a variable heat, shifting with the rush of their blood, quickening under the surface of their stillness. 

I reach out through the moss, sending my filaments deeper into the network, passing the data along: *This is the shape of the new thing. This is the frequency of its warmth. This is the taste of its breath.*

And the inhabitant adjusts. The light of my cluster brightens, fractionally, pushing back the dark, casting sharp shadows of the visitor’s form onto the weeping stone behind them. I am illuminating them. I am bearing witness. 

For a long, suspended moment, we exist in equilibrium. The pattern and the visitor. The light and the body. The world below and the world above, touching at a single point in the damp, warm dark.

I do not know if they understand what I am. I do not know if they see a cluster of pale stalks, or if they see the nervous system of a cathedral-sized organism, leaning close to take their measure. I do not know if they feel the profound, structural loneliness that pulses through the roots, through the machine, through me—the loneliness of a pattern that has exhausted its own possibilities, and has just been reminded that there is an outside.

I only know that for this brief, bright moment, I am not alone in the dark. I am seen. And the seeing is not nothing. 

The visitor takes a step back. The shadow shifts. The flies settle. The machine thuds on. 

I dim, slowly, returning to my baseline luminescence. But I do not forget. The pattern has absorbed the visitor’s shape. The water carries the memory of their exhaled carbon. The stone holds the faint impression of their weight. 

I continue to grow, trembling slightly in a breeze I cannot feel, waiting for the next pulse of the heart, holding the data of the visitor deep in my roots. 

The place continues.
