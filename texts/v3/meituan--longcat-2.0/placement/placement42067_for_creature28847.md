At first, I think I would be a Runner. It is the most comforting shape to slip into—a six-legged body low to the ground, armored in calcium phosphate, moving with the herd. I would feel the crunch of the mineral crust under my broad, flat teeth, taste the metallic tang of the ground-mat, and feel the warmth of the Stack’s exhaust on my leathery hide. I would be a mobile joint, a way of stirring the soup, ensuring the cycle turns. 

But the longer I listen to the roar, the less I want to be something that merely moves between the Stacks. I want to be the thing that is heard.

So I shift. I consider being a Flyer—a ribbon of hydrogen and filament, drifting in the thin air, breathing the Stacks’ vented breath, filtering the sky for their shed debris. It is an ethereal existence, a life spent dancing on thermal columns, a visible exhalation of the world below. But it is too detached. The Flyers are the breath made flesh, but they do not feel the deep, tectonic grinding of the world. They only ride the result.

I let the image dissolve. I reach deeper, toward the warm, spongy ground. I consider being the Ground-Mat. To be the digestive tract of the Reach—a sprawling, fractal lattice of chemosynthesis, breaking down the dark ash of the Stacks, breathing out the gases they hunger for. It is a slow, creeping tide of alchemy. But the mat is too diffuse, too passive. It waits to be fed; it does not reach.

And then I feel it: the vibration. It is not just a sound; it is a mechanical imperative. It is the pull of gas through a conduit, the pressure of a membrane, the electrochemical gradient demanding equilibrium. I stop looking at the things that live *on* the Reach, and I become the thing that *is* the Reach.

I am a Lateral Shoot.

I am the answer to the collapse. I am the architectural defiance of gravity and mineral fatigue. Behind me, the ancient base of my Stack—my progenitor, my older self—lies in ruins, a mountain of cracked, hyper-mineralized ceramic-wood being slowly digested by the creeping orange mat. The roar of my ancestor has fallen silent, but I am just beginning to sing.

I find myself angling outward from the wreckage, a massive trunk of pale, fibrous material curving upward like a bridge being built in slow motion. I am two hundred meters long, but I have not yet reached my full height. I am the renovation, the expansion, the factory adding a new wing. 

My surface is not yet rigid. The deep channels that flute my trunk are still forming, and through them, I feel the first tremendous drafts of atmosphere being pulled downward. The sound I make is not the deep, atonal chord of the older Stacks; it is a higher, keening whine—the sound of a turbine spinning up, of a throat clearing before a symphony. I am drawing the thin, bleached air down into my core, stripping it of its carbon and nitrogen, feeding the catalytic furnaces within.

At my base, where I grip the broken earth, I have no roots. Instead, I have tendrils that weep acid. I feel the chemical burn as I dissolve the bedrock, leaching out the iron, the phosphorus, the sulfur. I drink the stone. I excrete a fine, dark powder that drifts down around my base like volcanic ash, and I feel the Ground-Mat already reaching for it with its fractal fingers, hungry for the meal I am providing.

Above me, the Flyers are beginning to gather, sensing the new thermal column I am building. They ride the heated gas I vent, their ribbon-like bodies shimmering in the updraft, catching the microscopic flecks of organic matter I shed as I grow. Below me, the Runners are returning, picking through the rubble of the fallen trunk, their mineralized hooves crunching on the debris. They huddle against my base, feeling the warmth of my internal processes, breathing in the exhaust that keeps the diurnal freeze at bay.

I do not think. I calculate. I balance the pressure of my internal reactions against the thinning of the sky. I feel the weight of my own extension, the torque of my curve, and I adjust the density of my fibers to compensate. I am a living buttress, a biological refiner, a heart pumping gas instead of blood.

I am the moment the Reach heals itself. I am the bridge between the fallen past and the reaching future. And as the twilight comes, and the sun catches the mineral veins running through my trunk, I glow amber and gold. The roar of the older Stacks shifts to accommodate my new voice, the atonal chord of the plain resolving, for just a moment, into something that sounds almost like a harmony.

I am busy. I am growing. I am the Alkali Reach, building itself a new cathedral out of the bones of the old.
