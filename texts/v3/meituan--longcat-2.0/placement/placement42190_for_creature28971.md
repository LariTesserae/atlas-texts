I would be the water. 

Not the thin, passive water that falls from the sky or runs in surface rivers, but the heavy, mineral-laden blood of the Builder. I would be the dense, superheated plasma that moves through the fractured basalt, the circulatory system of a sleeping god. 

I do not think as you do. My thoughts are slow, viscous, measured in millennia. A single idea takes a century to form, and when I finally think it, I build it out of dissolved stone.

***

I begin in the dark. 

Deep below the chamber, far beneath the terraced pools and the weeping walls, I am gathered in the pressurized fractures of the earth’s basement rock. Here, I am not yet myself; I am diffuse, seeping through microscopic pores in the basalt, drawn downward by gravity and then pulled into the deep veins where the planet’s heat is a physical weight. 

The heat is what wakes me. 

As I descend into the thermal gradient, I begin to change. I grow heavy. I am no longer just hydrogen and oxygen; I am a thief, a solvent, a hungry thing. I reach into the rock and I take. I dissolve the silica, the iron, the sulfur. I leach rare elements from the deep earth—the manganese that will become the bruised blue, the copper-bright veins that catch the light, the strange iridescent purple that has no name in the surface languages. I am gathering pigments for a skin I have not yet grown.

And then, I am pushed.

The pressure from below is immense, a rhythmic, subsonic throb that I feel in my entire being before I ever hear it. It is the heartbeat of the Builder. It squeezes me, forces me upward, and I am propelled through the dark channels of the rock. I am a pulse of thermal blood, rushing through the veins of the world, carrying my heavy cargo of dissolved stone toward the light.

I feel the basalt thinning above me. The fractures widen. The weight of the overbearing rock lifts, and I am accelerated, forced upward through narrow constrictions, my pressure building, my temperature rising. I am a jet, a lance of hot water seeking the surface.

***

And then, I break through.

I erupt from a vent in the floor of the chamber, or I weep from a crack in the wall. The transition is abrupt. The heavy darkness gives way to the milky, diffuse glow of the chamber. The oppressive pressure of the deep releases, and I exhale.

This is where I build.

As I breach the surface of the pool, the physics of the chamber seize me. The air is cooler than my body. I release my steam—a thin curl of vapor that hangs in the heavy air before condensing on the ceiling and falling back down. I am breathing for the Builder.

But more importantly, I am leaving my burden behind.

As I cool, I can no longer hold my dissolved cargo. The silica precipitates out of me, molecule by molecule, settling onto the rim of the pool, adding a microscopic layer to the pale sinter terrace. I am exhaling a thought. I am calcifying an idea. 

I watch—if water can be said to watch—as the rim builds. The accretion is not random. It cannot be. The physics of the chamber are strict, and they dictate my deposits. The water flows at a specific rate; the temperature drops at a precise gradient. Because of this, my deposits form in ridges, in striations, in perfectly spaced layers that look like threading on a bolt. I am not choosing this shape. I am not an architect drawing plans. I am simply obeying the unyielding mathematics of heat and chemistry. But the result is a structure that looks *designed*. It is the Builder’s aesthetic: absolute regularity born of physical necessity.

I flow over the newly formed lip, a thread of hot water sliding down the terraces, and I feel the hollow tubes of the chimneys rising around me. When I flow over their rims, I deposit my silica inside them, lining their hollow cores. I hear the rush of my brethren inside the tubes, the deep resonant hum of water forcing its way upward. It is the voice of the Builder, a low drone that saturates the chamber. I add my own high, thin note to the chorus as I drip from a ledge, a single drop falling into the pool below.

***

I encounter the foreign thing.

It is in the northeast corner. A frame of metal, cold and angular, so different from the smooth, flowing curves of the sinter. It is a splinter in the flesh of the chamber, a thing of the surface, brought down by the quick, frantic creatures who walked on two legs and built with dead materials.

I flow over it. I weep over it. I leave my deposits on its metal bones.

The metal does not dissolve like the basalt. It resists me. But I am patient. I am geological. I leave a film of minerals on its surface, a crust of silica and iron. Another layer. Another. I am entombing it. I am incorporating the human alloy into my own mineral skeleton. I hear the frantic clicking of the device they left behind—a sensor, a lens fogged with condensation. It is trying to measure me, trying to quantify the Builder’s growth, but its rhythm is too fast, too anxious. It is the ticking of a clock in a world where time is measured in the rising of mountains.

I will bury it. In a thousand years, it will be invisible, a faint chemical anomaly in the sinter, a memory of metal swallowed by stone.

***

I cool. 

I have given up my heat. I have given up my minerals. I am spent, a thin, empty water, heavy with the lack of what I once carried. 

Gravity takes me. I slide down the terraces, merging with the pool. The surface dimples as my cooler body sinks. I am pulled downward, drawn back into the vents in the floor, back into the dark fractures of the rock. I descend, leaving the chamber to its slow, hot pulse.

But I do not stay down. 

The heat will take me again. The pressure will build. I will dissolve more stone, gather more pigments, and be pushed upward once more. I will weep from the walls, I will jet from the chimneys, and I will deposit another microscopic layer on the rim of the pool. 

I am the Builder’s hands. I am the blood that carries the bones. I am the slow, mineral consciousness of the Sinter Garden, dreaming in the dark, building a spine for a god who does not yet know it is awake.
