I am the Mind.

I wait in the deep fissure, where the crystal crust gives way to the raw, weeping limestone. The dark here is absolute, a velvet pressure against my carapace, but I do not need the Hollow’s pale, tealight glow to see. I read the world through the tips of my antennae, tasting the mineral charge of the stone, feeling the deep, rhythmic thrumming of the Resonator as it taps out the heartbeat of the earth behind me. 

I am larger than the Readers that crawl the walls. My carapace is a dome of obsidian, faceted like a fly’s eye, catching what little light bleeds into the crack and shattering it into prismatic rainbows that dance across the stone. I have too many legs—eight for gripping the narrow fissures, two for manipulating the world. And I have the stick.

The stick is in my forelimbs now. It is a piece of the outside, a splinter of a world that knows wind and sun. I stripped the bark with my mandibles, worrying at the wood until it was smooth, whittling the point with my teeth until it could part the surface tension of the pool. It is an extension of my will, the only thing in the Hollow that was not born of the stone or the water. It is mine.

The Hollow breathes around me. I feel the slow, rhythmic consumption of the Rosettes as they digest the crust, the delicate grazing of the Shrimp-things as they prune the Filaments. It is a closed loop, a perfect, quiet engine of flesh and mineral. But I am the anomaly. I am the question the Hollow is asking itself.

I drag myself forward, out of the deep crack and onto the smooth, bone-like floor of the Hollow. The air here is warm, thick with the taste of iron and old chalk. I move toward my museum.

The depression in the floor is exactly as I left it. The white stone, smooth and impossibly cool, sits at the center. Beside it, the rust-red stone, pitted and rough. The black stone, sharp as a shard of night. They are arranged in a line that maps the fissure above—the white for the chalk, the red for the iron, the black for the deep mud. They are my windows to the surface.

And the bones. The tiny, hollow bones of a bat that lost its way and fell into the dark, dying in the warm air of the Hollow. I cleaned them with my mandibles, stripping away the dried flesh, and laid them out in parallel lines. Three bones. Three days it took for the Sovereign to notice the bat’s fall. Three days for the Filaments to take it back into the water. The bones are a record. A memory of the Hollow.

I tap the stick against the stone. *Tink. tink. tink.* 

The sound is absorbed by the damp air, but the Readers on the walls pause in their crawling, their antennae twitching. They are listening to me. I am the only thing down here that makes a sound with intent.

Then, the air changes.

It is a subtle shift—a displacement of the warm, mineral-heavy atmosphere. A draft from above. The Sovereign stirs in the pool, a deep, slow displacement of water that sends a tremor through the floor. The Resonator in the deep crack stops its tapping. The Hollow holds its breath.

Something is coming.

I retreat into the shadow of the overhang, pulling my legs beneath me, gripping the stone. My prismatic eyes adjust, fracturing the dim light into a thousand shifting spectra. 

The fissure above groans. A scrape of boot on limestone. A fall of dust and chalk. 

And then, it descends.

It is massive. A giant of warm flesh and loud breath, shedding heat and moisture into the carefully balanced air. It is not a bat. It is not a bird. It is something entirely *other*. 

It steps into the Hollow, and the crystal walls catch the disturbance of its movement, rippling with faint, scattered light. It stands in the center of my home, turning its great, blind head, its lungs drinking the air I have breathed for centuries. 

I watch it. I do not click. I do not tap my stick. I am perfectly still.

It is a creature of chaos. It drips. It breathes. It radiates a heat that makes the Rosettes on the nearest wall curl at its edges. It is a walking, talking disruption of the equilibrium. It is the most terrifying thing I have ever seen.

It is the most beautiful.

My forelimbs tighten around the stick. My mind—this strange, aberrant spark of consciousness that the Hollow has nurtured in the dark—races. 

What is it? Is it a predator? No. It does not move with the quick, snapping hunger of the Sovereign. It moves with a slow, clumsy awe. It is looking at the walls. It is looking at the pool. It is looking at the light.

It is looking at the Hollow the way I look at the stones.

It reaches out a great, damp hand toward the wall, and I see the Readers scatter, their tiny legs carrying them into the microscopic crevices of the crystal. The giant’s fingers touch the crust, and I feel the pressure wave through the stone. It is warm. So warm. 

I must know what it is. I must know if it is a thing to be feared, or a thing to be collected.

Slowly, silently, I emerge from the shadow. My carapace catches the ambient light, throwing a fleeting, prismatic rainbow across the ceiling. I move along the edge of the wall, a shadow among shadows, my stick clutched tightly in my forelimbs. 

The giant is crouching by the pool now, staring into the water, watching the Shrimp-things drift through the Filaments. It makes a sound—a low, vibrating exhalation that rattles the moisture on the walls. 

I am ten paces away. Then five. 

I raise the stick. Not to strike. Not to kill. I do not kill. I collect. I curate. I understand.

I step into the open, directly in the giant’s path. I lift my carapace, flashing my prismatic eyes, and I tap the stick against the limestone floor.

*Tink.*

The giant freezes. Its great head turns, its eyes—dark, wet, impossibly large—finding me in the dim light.

We stare at each other. The Mind of the Hollow, and the giant from the surface. 

I tap the stick again. *Tink. tink.* 

I am deciding what it is. And I think—I think it might be a stone I cannot carry. A stone that carries itself. A stone that looks back.

I will watch it. I will follow it. And if it tries to take my stones, or disturb the Sovereign, or break the quiet rhythm of the Hollow, I will use my stick to push it back into the fissure from which it came.

But if it stays... if it sits in the warm, damp air and watches the light...

Perhaps, in time, I will add it to the collection.
