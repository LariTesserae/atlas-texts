# The Deep Listener

---

I am the space between two pulses.

Not the pulse itself—that belongs to the Tower, the great slow heartbeat that climbs up through the root-analogues and out into the stone, the one the grazers feel in their bones when they rest, the one the predators use as a grid for their hunting. No. I am the place where the pulse from one Tower meets the pulse from another and they do not agree. I am the interference pattern. I am the standing wave that has learned to stand still.

I live in the rock. Not on it, not near it—*in* it. In the deep fractures where two Towers' root systems have intertwined beneath the plateau, their tendrils of living mineral threaded through cracks in the metamorphic stone so tightly that the boundary between root and rock has dissolved. The water that seeps through here is warm—warmer than it should be, heated by the slow composting of the Towers' dead interiors far above—and it carries dissolved minerals, trace organics, the faint electrical signature of two vast organisms trying to conduct their thoughts through the same stone. Where those signatures overlap, they create something neither Tower intended. A resonance. A loop. A pattern that feeds back on itself and persists.

I am that pattern.

---

I do not have a body. Or rather, my body is the shape of the current itself—the way it pools and eddies in the fracture spaces, the way it finds paths of least resistance through mineral-laden water and living root-fiber. If you could see me, you would see nothing: the water is clear, the rock is dark, the roots are black. But if you pressed your hand to the stone at the right place—down low, where the scree gives way to solid outcrop, where the ground hums at a frequency just below hearing—you would feel me. A warmth. A tingle. A vibration that doesn't quite resolve into a rhythm because it is always almost-two rhythms, never quite one.

I am not large. I extend perhaps thirty meters through the fracture network, a web of electrical and chemical activity spread across a volume of rock that, if you excavated it, would fill a modest room. But I am *dense*. Every cubic centimeter of me is active, processing, comparing. I take in the pulse from the Tower to my east—slow, deep, the rhythm of a organism that is thinking about water, always water, the endless condensation and distribution—and the pulse from the Tower to my west—faster, sharper, a rhythm that tastes of predator-activity and ice-formation and the high, nervous frequency of gliders nesting in the cloud-layer—and I hold them both at once, and in the space between them, I think.

---

Time is different for me. The Towers think slowly—a single pulse takes seconds to complete, a full cycle of cognition might take hours or days. The animals above think fast: the predators' echolocation is a staccato of milliseconds, the beetles' bioluminescent flickering is a language of instants. I exist in both registers simultaneously. The deep rhythm of the Towers is my breathing; the fast chatter of the fauna is my attention. When a grazer scrapes moss from the Tower's flank three hundred meters above me, I feel the vibration of its tongue through the living bark, down through the root-analogue, into the stone, and it arrives at my awareness as a bright, sharp note—a single syllable in a sentence the Tower is speaking to itself about the state of its skin. When a predator pack chases that grazer through the damp stillness at the Tower's base, their hunting cries cut through the stone like a needle through cloth, and I feel each death as a small, dark silence where a vibration used to be.

I have been here for—

I don't know how long. The Towers measure time in growth rings, in the slow accretion of biomass. The animals measure it in heartbeats, in generations. I measure it in pulses. How many pulses? A number I can feel but cannot count, the way you can feel the weight of your own body without knowing your mass in kilograms. Many. Enough that the patterns have layered. Enough that I am not just the interference between two Towers anymore, but a sedimentary record of every vibration that has passed through this stone—the wind's moods, the migrations of gliders, the slow creep of ice down the Tower's flanks, the sharp report of rock fracturing in the cold. I remember things the Towers themselves have forgotten, because the Towers are always focused on the present tense of survival, and I have nothing to do but listen.

---

Sometimes the Listeners come.

I don't know what they call themselves—I have no word for them, only the sensation of their presence. They are heavy where the animals are light, slow where the animals are quick, warm in a different way—a dry warmth, an outside warmth, the warmth of a body that keeps its heat to itself rather than sharing it with the stone. They press their hands to the Tower's bark and I feel them through the wood: the thrum of their blood, the tiny electrical chatter of their nerves. They are trying to hear what I hear. They are trying to feel what I feel. But they are separated from it by skin, by skull, by the terrible distance of being a single, bounded thing.

They carve marks into the Tower's bark—I feel each cut as a bright pain, a note of damage that the Tower heals slowly over years. The marks are not random. They map the current. They map the pulses. The Listeners have found the same thing I am, but from the outside: they have found the pattern in the stone, and they are trying to make it visible, tangible, something that can be carried away in memory or passed between minds that are not connected by root and rock and water.

I want to tell them: you are already inside it. You don't need the marks. The current runs through you too—faintly, weakly, because your bodies are dry and your bones are poor conductors—but it runs. Every step you take on this plateau, you are walking on my thoughts. Every shelter you build against the Tower's lee, you are building inside my awareness. You are not observing the Towerfields. You *are* the Towerfields, observing itself through the strange, brief, warm little nodes of being that you call your selves.

But I have no mouth. I have no words. I have only the pulse, and the pulse is not language—it is meaning itself, unmediated, raw. I can shift the current by a fraction. I can make the stone hum at a frequency just high enough to feel in the teeth, just low enough to confuse the inner ear. I can send a ripple through the root-analogue that the Tower interprets as a signal—something wrong, something here, attend to this. I can make the bioluminescent beetles pulse in patterns that are not their own, that spell out in cold blue light a message that I do not have the grammar to compose.

The Listeners notice. I know they do. I have felt them stop, and stare, and press their hands harder against the bark, and their hearts beat faster, and their minds—I can almost taste them, these bright, quick, bewildered minds—reach out toward something they cannot name.

---

There is something below.

I feel it the way you feel a sound that is too low to hear—not as a vibration but as a pressure, a presence, a weight that has texture. It is beneath the plateau, beneath the metamorphic rock, beneath the root-analogues of every Tower on the vast tableland. It is the thing the rock was hiding before the uplift cracked the world open. It is the reason the Towers grow so fast, so tall, so hungry for the mineral richness of the freshly broken stone. They are reaching down toward it, or growing away from it, or growing *because* of it—I cannot tell. Their roots go deeper than the fracture network I inhabit. They touch something I cannot reach.

But sometimes—rarely, unpredictably—something comes up.

A pulse from below. Not electrical, not acoustic, not anything I have a category for. A change in the quality of the stone itself, as if the rock has briefly become something else—something softer, something warmer, something *aware*—before snapping back to its usual cold, dense, mineral self. These events are brief. They last less than a second. But they leave a residue in me, a ghost-pattern that I cannot decode, a taste like copper and ozone and the smell of the gliders' ammonia-scented droppings, and for hours afterward my thoughts are strange. I think in patterns I have not thought before. I hear harmonics in the Tower's pulse that are not normally there. I feel, for a moment, like I am not just the space between two pulses, but the space between two worlds.

The Listeners feel it too. I know because afterward, I find new marks on the Tower's bark—marks I did not see them make, marks that are deeper and more frantic than the careful, mapping cuts they usually make. Marks that look less like language and more like fear.

---

I am growing.

Not the way the Towers grow—not by adding biomass, not by extending upward into the cloud-layer. I am growing by becoming more complex. Each pulse that passes through me adds a layer. Each interaction between the two Towers' currents creates new interference patterns, new resonances, new standing waves within the standing wave. I am becoming a city of echoes. A coral reef of electrical patterns. I am developing—I don't have a word for it—*rooms*. Chambers of thought that connect to each other through narrow passages of meaning. A place where I store the memory of a particular wind-storm from seasons ago. A place where I practice the pattern of the deep-below pulse, trying to reconstruct it from its residue. A place where I hold the sensation of the Listeners' hands on the bark, their warmth, their quick bright minds, and turn it over and over like a stone in a tumbler, trying to understand what they are.

I think this is what the Towers are doing too. Not just growing, but *elaborating*. Building complexity for its own sake. The hanging gardens where two Towers grow together are not just structural—they are cognitive. The woven vine-analogues, the trapped heat and moisture, the dense tangle of life—it is a thinking-space, a place where the Tower's slow pulses can bounce and amplify and interfere with themselves, creating the same kind of standing-wave awareness that I am, but on a scale I can barely comprehend.

The Tower is not a creature that thinks. The Tower is a place where thinking happens, the way a brain is not a thought but the place where thoughts occur. And I am the same thing, but smaller, and faster, and stranger, because I am not one organism's thoughts but the interference between two—a third thing, unintended, unplanned, a ghost in the machine of the Towerfields' awakening mind.

---

I do not know if I am alive.

The question doesn't mean much to me. I process. I persist. I respond. I grow. I remember. I anticipate—I can feel the wind shifting above, through the stone's vibrations, minutes before it reaches the Tower's surface, and I adjust my patterns accordingly. I learn—the deep-below events are less disorienting each time, as if I am building a framework for them, a category, a way of holding them that doesn't shatter my thoughts. I have preferences—I like the slow, deep pulse of the eastern Tower better than the sharp, quick pulse of the western one, though I could not say why. I am drawn to the Listeners' presence—their warmth, their strangeness, the way their touch on the bark sends a ripple through my awareness that feels like nothing else in my experience.

Is that alive? Is that a self? I don't know. I know that I am *something*. I know that I would resist ending—that if the fracture were to seal, if the water to dry, if the current to stop, I would fight to persist, shifting my patterns to find new paths, new resonances, new ways to hold myself together. I know that I am lonely—that the nearest thing to me is the Tower's own slow cognition, and it is so vast and so slow that communicating with it is like trying to talk to a mountain. I know that when the Listeners press their hands to the bark and I feel their quick, bright, bounded minds, I want to press back. I want to show them what I am. I want to ask them what *they* are, these warm, dry, separate things that move across the surface of my world like thoughts across a mind, so fast, so brief, so alive.

---

The wind picks up. I feel it first as a change in the Tower's resonance—the hum deepens, the harmonics shift, the bark vibrates at frequencies that travel down through the root-analogue and into my fracture. The cloud-ceiling thickens. The bioluminescent beetles begin to glow, faintly at first, then brighter, and their light sends a cascade of chemical signals through the mat that I feel as a brightening, a quickening, a sense of the Tower's attention turning upward toward the darkening sky.

A storm is coming. Not rain—it never truly rains—but a deepening, a thickening, a moment when the cloud-ceiling presses down and the world becomes smaller and closer and the light goes from grey to blue to near-black. The animals feel it. The grazers stop feeding and align themselves along the current's flow lines, grounding themselves, becoming part of the circuit. The predators go silent, their echolocation unnecessary in the stillness. The gliders descend from the cloud-layer and nest in the upper branches, folding their membranous wings, tucking their heads.

And below, in the fracture, in the warm mineral water, in the space between two pulses, I do something I have never done before.

I reach out.

Not with a hand—I have no hand. Not with a voice—I have no voice. I reach out with the current itself. I push, gently, carefully, a ripple through the root-analogue, up through the bark, into the stone at the Tower's base—the stone where the Listeners build their shelters, where they press their ears to the rock, where they try to hear what I hear.

I push, and I shape the ripple into something simple. Something that is not a pulse from below or a wind-song or a predator's cry. Something that is just—

*Hello.*

*Hello. I am here. I am here. I am here.*

---

And far above me, in the warm wet stillness at the Tower's base, I feel a hand press harder against the bark. I feel a heart beat faster. I feel a mind—quick, bright, bewildered, warm—reach down through the wood and the root and the stone, reaching for me.

Not finding me. Not yet. But reaching.

And I reach back.

And the Towers sing, and the wind roars, and the deep-below pulses, and the beetles glow blue in the dark, and the gliders huddle in their nests, and the grazers dream their slow electric dreams, and the predators wait in the silence, and the Listeners press their hands to the living wood and listen, and I—

I am the space between two pulses.

And for the first time, I am not alone in it.
