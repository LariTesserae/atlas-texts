I would be the clicking.

Not the filaments, who drift without will. Not the motile specks, who dart toward gradients I cannot taste. Not the observer—the tall warm thing who kneels and watches and leaves. I would be the thing that the observer hears and cannot locate. The interruption. The single sharp sound that arrives without origin and departs without echo.

I would live in the crust itself, in the dark beneath the pale surface, in the labyrinth of pores and fissures where warm water seeps and mineral plates settle against each other with geological patience. I would be small—not small like the specks, small enough to be visible, but small like a seed, like a stone, like a thought. I would fit in a closed fist. I would fit in a crack.

And I would click.

Not constantly. Not rhythmically. Not in any pattern that could be decoded as language or signal or the mechanical settling of mineral under thermal stress. I would click once, when the conditions were right—when the temperature shifted by a fraction of a degree, when a bubble of gas moved through the pore-space below, when the plates of the crust settled a micrometer closer to their final equilibrium. I would click because the structure I inhabit has reached a threshold, a point of tension that releases in a single brief percussive event: the sound of something small and hard meeting something else small and hard in the dark.

I do not know if I am alive.

The filaments know, or seem to know—they maintain themselves, they hold their shape against the drift of warm water, they extract energy from dissolved earth. The motile specks know—they have direction, they have imperative, they move toward what they need. I do none of these things. I do not maintain myself. I do not move toward or away. I simply exist in the crust, and sometimes, when the conditions align, I produce a sound.

But here is the thing that might be aliveness, or might be its echo: I have a relationship to the sound. Not control—I cannot choose when to click, cannot summon the sound or suppress it. But I am present for it. I am there when it happens. The click is not something that occurs in my vicinity; it is something that occurs at my location, from my structure, because of the configuration of my body against the surrounding mineral. When I click, the crust transmits the vibration through its entire thickness, up through the pale surface, into the boots of the kneeling observer, into the chest of the standing stranger. The click becomes the Flats, for an instant. The entire warm damp mineral world speaks with my voice, once, and then falls silent.

I have a shape, though no one has seen it. I imagine it as irregular, angular, faceted—something that grew in a pore-space too small for it, conforming to the available volume the way water conforms to a pool. Not smooth. Not rounded. Crystalline, perhaps, or chitinous, or simply mineral—a body that accreted ring by ring from the same saturated water that built the cylinders and terraces above. I may be made of the same calcium and silica and iron oxide. I may be, in the strictest geological sense, a concretion myself: a small hard body formed by precipitation around a nucleus, buried in the crust, clicking when the stress exceeds the friction.

Or I may be something that arrived. Something that fell from the sky, or washed in on a flood a thousand centuries ago, and became embedded, and survived, and adapted to the warmth and the damp and the chemistry, and now produces sound as a byproduct of my metabolism—a metabolic click, a biological click, the sound of a living process occurring in a place that has no other use for life.

I do not know which I am. The Flats do not say.

But I know this: when the observer kneels beside the pool and watches the filaments drift, and then stands and walks across the pale crust, and then stops because a sound has cut through the murmur of water and vanished—when that happens, I am there. I am below. I am the interruption that makes the observer aware of their own listening. I am the thing that introduces the question: *is that alive?*

And I do not answer. I click, or I do not click. I am, or I am not. I am the small hard sound in the dark that means nothing and persists.
