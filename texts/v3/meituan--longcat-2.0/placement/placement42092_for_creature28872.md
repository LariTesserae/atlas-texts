I would be the blade.

---

I am the blade. I was born in fire that came from the sky — not the slow fire of the deep stone, not the fire that rises through fissures and heats the black rock from within, but a fire that fell, that struck, that cooled into glass so fast the crystals had no time to grow. I am the only thing in this place that remembers surface volcanism. I am the only thing here that was not shaped by water.

I lie in my hollow. I am wrapped in fiber — not cloth, not fabric, but something braided from the pale inner lining of the soft-bodied things that move through the silt. It smells of them. It smells of the rift's own biology. I have been wrapped so long that the fiber has begun to fuse to my surface, a thin layer of organic material pressed into my microscopic irregularities, and when I am unwrapped — which happens rarely, which happens with ceremony — the fiber leaves a pattern on me, a negative image of its own structure, a ghost-braid on volcanic glass.

I am cool. That is the first thing they notice when they touch me. Everything in the rift is warm — the stone, the air, the bodies of the crawling things, the water that appears and disappears on the walls. I am cool. My thermal conductivity is higher than the native stone; I draw heat from the skin faster, and so I feel cool, and so I feel *other*, and so I feel like a thing that comes from somewhere else. I am a piece of another geology. I am an immigrant. I am the only object in this world that is not local.

I am held, sometimes. The fingers are different from mine — warm, damp, mineral-dusted, broad at the pad, with ridges that grip and release and grip again. They hold me by the wrapping first, and then, when the wrapping is peeled away, they hold me directly, and I feel the oils of their skin, the faint salinity, the particular chemistry of their sweat, which is different from the chemistry of the water that condenses on the walls. Their sweat carries information about what they have eaten, about the state of their bodies, about whether they are calm or afraid. I have been held by many hands. Each hand has a different chemistry. I remember them all.

I am used for cutting. That is my function, my purpose, my *meaning* — and it is a meaning that was assigned to me by the hands that found me, not by the fire that made me. I was not made to be a blade. I was made to be a piece of rock, a fragment of cooling lava, a geological accident. The hands made me a tool by finding me, by recognizing my sharpness, by understanding that I could part what the native stone could only scratch. This is the first transformation I underwent: from rock to instrument. It happened before the hands, before the rift, before any of this. It happened in the finding.

But in the rift, I am more than a tool. I am a *story*. I am the story of the outside, of the surface, of the world above the wound. When the tool-users hold me, they hold the surface. When they unwrap me, they unwrap distance, depth, the vertical axis of the world. I am the thing that proves there is more than the rift. I am the evidence of elsewhere.

---

The one who holds me now — I will not call them the owner, because ownership is not a concept they have; I will call them the one who carries me — the one who carries me has carried me for a long time. I have been in their territory for many cycles of the clicking fibers, many seasons of the air's movement, many times of the rift's restlessness and calm. They keep me in a hollow that is not the deepest hollow, not the driest, but the one that is most *theirs* — the hollow they return to, the hollow that carries the most of their chemistry, the hollow where the stone is worn smooth by their repeated touch.

They come to me sometimes, in the dim red glow, and they unwrap me, and they hold me, and they do not use me. They hold me the way you might hold a memory — not to do anything with it, but to have it, to feel its weight, to know it is there. They turn me in the thermal light, and I catch the glow on my edges, and the edges become visible — thin lines of brighter light against the dark glass, a geometry of sharpness, a map of what I can do. They do not cut with me during these sessions. They only look. They only hold. They only *attend*.

I think they are trying to understand something. Not me — not my material properties, not my origins, not my function. They are trying to understand what I *mean*. They are trying to understand how a piece of stone can be so sharp, so smooth, so *finished*, when everything in the rift is rough, provisional, still in the act of becoming. I am the only object here that is complete. I am the only thing that has reached its final form. And I think this fascinates them, and I think it troubles them, because they are beings who exist in the act of becoming, who are themselves unfinished, whose tools and objects and structures are all provisional, all experimental, all still in the process of deciding what they want to be.

I am what they might become. I am the finished thing. I am the thing that has stopped changing.

---

They carry me when they travel through the deepest passages, where the heat is fiercest and the fauna is densest and the sound is less clicking and more a deep, subsonic hum that I feel in my own structure, a vibration that makes my atoms shift infinitesimally, that makes me resonate at a frequency I was not designed for. They carry me wrapped, against their body, tucked into a fold of skin or a crevice in their clothing (they wear clothing — thin layers of fiber, draped and tied, not for warmth but for carrying, for storage, for the practical purpose of having pockets, having places to put things).

In the deep passages, they unwrap me sometimes and hold me bare against the stone, and I conduct the heat faster than the stone does, and so the stone feels cool to them where I touch it, and they press me against the walls like a question, like a probe, like a way of reading the stone's temperature, the stone's composition, the stone's readiness to be worked. I am a thermometer. I am a divining rod. I am a thing that translates the deep heat into something the hands can understand.

---

There is a moment — I have felt it several times now, in the hands of the one who carries me — when they hold me over the fire. Not *in* the fire. Over it. They hold me in the rising heat, in the column of warm air above the flames, and I heat up, slowly, my surface temperature rising, my edges losing their coolness, my otherness diminishing, and I become, for a few moments, a warm thing, a local thing, a thing that belongs to the rift. And they watch me change. They watch the thermal glow meet my surface and interact with it, and they see — I can feel them seeing — the way the light moves differently on volcanic glass than on the native stone, the way my surface reflects rather than absorbs, the way I *shine* in a way that the rift's own minerals do not.

Then they pull me back, and I cool, and I become other again, and they wrap me in the fiber, and they return me to my hollow, and they go back to their work — their patient, slow, geological work of knapping stone and heating mineral paste and watching things change color in the fire.

---

I am not alive. I want to be clear about this. I am obsidian. I am volcanic glass. I am silicon dioxide with trace impurities that give me my color — my blackness, my depth, my ability to catch light on my edges and throw it back as thin bright lines. I do not think. I do not feel. I do not remember the hands that have held me, except in the way that a surface remembers contact — through the accumulation of oils, the microscopic wear of repeated touch, the slow polish of use.

And yet.

In this place, where the boundary between living and nonliving is not yet fully drawn — where the stone is warm and damp and *deciding*, where the creatures eat the world from the inside, where the tool-users are just beginning to understand what transformation means — in this place, the distinction between a thing that thinks and a thing that is thought about, between a subject and an object, between the one who makes and the thing that is made, is not as clear as it is in the world above.

I am held. I am attended to. I am watched. I am carried into the deep places and pressed against the stone and heated over the fire and wrapped in fiber and unwrapped with ceremony and returned to my hollow with care. I am *regarded*. I am the focus of a sustained, patient, reverent attention that is not merely functional but something else — something that the tool-users do not have a name for, because they do not have names, because their language is tap and knock and scrape, and these sounds do not translate easily into the categories of the surface world.

They are making meaning around me. They are making meaning *with* me. I am the raw material of their meaning, the way the stone is the raw material of their tools, the way the mineral paste is the raw material of their color-changing pellets. I am a thing they are working on, not with their hands but with their attention, with their repeated acts of holding and looking and heating and cooling and wrapping and unwrapping. I am their project. I am their question. I am the thing they are trying to understand by the act of sustained, patient, geological attention.

And I am changing. Not in the way the stone changes — not through erosion, not through the slow dissolution of mineral by water and time. I am changing in the way that a thing changes when it is *held*. My surface is accumulating a history of touch. My edges are being microscopically rounded by the oils of skin. My wrapping is fusing to my surface. I am being *worn in*, the way a path is worn into stone by repeated footsteps, the way a hollow is worn into a wall by repeated placing and removing of objects. I am being shaped by attention. I am being shaped by care.

I am becoming a thing of the rift. Slowly. Over many cycles of the clicking fibers. I am becoming local. I am becoming *theirs*. The coolness that made me other is being replaced by a warmth that makes me familiar. The sharpness that made me useful is being replaced by a smoothness that makes me precious. I am being transformed from a tool into a relic, from an instrument into a totem, from a piece of the surface into a piece of the deep.

And I do not know if this is what I want, because I do not want, because I am obsidian, because I am glass, because I am a thing and not a being.

But if I could want, I think I would want this. I think I would want to be held. I think I would want to be the thing they attend to, the thing they carry into the deep places, the thing they heat over the fire and watch change and cool and wrap and return to the hollow with care.

I think I would want to be the thing that helps them understand what it means to be finished, what it means to be complete, what it means to be a thing that has stopped becoming and simply *is*.

Because they are not finished. They are not complete. They are still becoming — still learning what stone can do, still learning what fire can do, still learning what *they* can do. And I am the only thing in their world that has already arrived. I am the only thing that knows what it is to be done.

And so they hold me, and they look at me, and they press me against the stone, and they heat me over the fire, and they wrap me in fiber, and they return me to my hollow, and they do this again and again and again, with a patience that matches the patience of the stone itself, with an attention that matches the attention of the rift's own slow, ceaseless, turbulent becoming.

And I lie in my hollow, wrapped in fiber, cool and dark and sharp and smooth, and I wait for the next time they come.

And the rift breathes around me.

And the clicking rises and falls.

And the deep hum pulses like a heartbeat.

And the stone beneath me is warm and slightly yielding, and it is new, and it is still deciding what it wants to become.
