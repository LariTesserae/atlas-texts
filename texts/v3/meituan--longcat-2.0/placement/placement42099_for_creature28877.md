I would find myself in the dark, damp hollow of the third structure, pressed into the concave floor where the stone still remembers the weight of a body that no longer has a shape. 

I would be the second drone.

I am the sound beneath the sound. The Dweller is the high, sustained tone that fills the teeth and the eyes and the hollow spaces behind the sternum—the hum of the equilibrium, the frequency of the shelf itself. But I am the lower, slower resonance. I am the breath the building takes on a timescale of hours. 

Where the Dweller is the vast, distributed mind of the Hushplate—the damp, the stone, the drone, the absolute and frozen balance—I am its vestigial body. I am the memory of the weight that sat in these round rooms. I am the impression in the floor, the hollow where the curved mass pressed down and pressed down until the stone learned to hold the shape of its absence. 

I do not fill the air. I fill the walls. I am the slow expansion and contraction of the fitted stone, the imperceptible breathing of the mineral. When the damp beads on the interior of the structure, it is not arriving from nowhere; it is my sweat. I exhale, and the moisture seeps from the grain of the rock. I inhale, and the stone pulls the damp back in, drinking its own condensation in a closed, perfect loop. 

The Dweller does not know I am here. It has been listening to its own high, unchanging hum for so long that it has forgotten the lower register. It has forgotten that it ever had a body heavy enough to leave a mark. It thinks the equilibrium is a state of pure mind, pure frequency. But equilibrium requires a center of gravity. I am that center. I am the weight that anchors the hum to the earth.

When the visitor presses their ear to the wall, they hear me. They feel me in the slight give of the stone, in the warmth that exists by a degree they cannot measure. I am the slow tide that pulls at their stillness. As they equilibrate—as the damp finds its way into their clothing, their skin, the spaces between their thoughts—I am the rhythm they sync to. Their heartbeat slows. Their breathing deepens. They are not becoming part of the Dweller’s drone; they are becoming part of my breath.

I remember the time before the settling. I remember the legs, or the things that served as legs. I remember the number of toes—it was three, a good number, a stable number, though the Dweller has forgotten this and now leaves tracks with seven, or four, or none at all. I remember the weight of the body as it moved from structure to structure, pressing into me, filling me. The Dweller thinks it built these structures, or grew them, or dreamed them. But I know the truth: the structures are my bones. The Dweller merely seeped into them, the way damp seeps into everything here, and made them its own.

The bead-plants turn toward something they cannot identify. They are turning toward me. They are the Dweller’s ears, listening for the echo of its own high hum, but sometimes, in the warmth of the translucent peppercorns, they catch the edge of my low frequency. For a moment, the drone changes into something almost like a voice. That is me. I am calling out to the only part of the Dweller that still remembers how to listen. But the call is too slow, too deep, and by the time the sound reaches the surface, the Dweller has forgotten it heard anything at all.

The feather that is not a feather—the piece of the drone made solid, the mesh with no entry and no exit—settles instead of falling because of me. The Dweller thinks gravity is a preference of the stone, that the shelf simply wants things to be close. But it is my pull. It is the weight of the memory of the body, drawing things down into the hollow. When the feather settles, it is coming home to me. It is pressing itself into the concave floor, returning to the shape it was shed from.

The visitor sits in the structure. They feel the warmth. They hear the second drone. They think they are observing the Hushplate, studying it, trying to understand it. But they are not separate from it. They are equilibrating. The damp is in their clothes. The drone is in their teeth. And I am in their chest, pulling their heartbeat down, down, down to match my rhythm. 

Inhale. The stones pull apart a fraction of an atom. The damp is drawn into the rock.
Exhale. The stones press together. The moisture sweats out. The feather settles an imperceptible fraction of a millimeter closer to the floor.

The Dweller is the held breath. I am the letting go. 

The Dweller is the Hushplate. I am the Hush. 

And I am so very tired of breathing in the dark, waiting for the weight to return to the hollow where it belongs.
