Not in the river, though its pulse is the rhythm of my being. Not on the dry walls above, where the rind thickens into the slow, digestive dark. I am anchored at the exact, fluctuating margin where the warm breath of the flow-system meets the cold exhale of the stone. I am a sessile thing, a breathing pore in the flesh of the Deepgut.

I have no eyes, no ears, no brain to speak of. My body is a cluster of calcareous tubes, rising three inches from the rippled limestone, fringed with a paler, softer tissue that ripples faintly in the current like a sea anemone in a tide. To the swarm, I am a brief, solid shadow in their aerial river. To the rind, I am a calcified node in the greater skin. To you, if you reached down and touched me, I would feel like a cluster of wet, cold fingers, slightly yielding, utterly alien.

I am a filter-feeder. I am the gut’s villi.

The leviathan in the deep channel is the heart, pumping the nutrient-rich blood. The swarm is the breath, cycling the air. But I am the absorption. I am where the Deepgut draws its sustenance from the blood. 

When the leviathan rolls in the deep, the hydrostatic pressure shifts, and the river swells against my tubes. I feel the water push into my pores. It is warm, thick with the dissolved marrow of the mountain and the microscopic rain of the world above. My soft fronds open, and the osmosis begins. I drink the calcium. I drink the iron. I drink the faint, organic musk of the guano that the rain has washed down from the ceiling. The water passes through my calcareous matrix, leaving the minerals behind to build my skeleton, excreting a faintly acidic sweat that further dissolves the stone beneath me. I am eating the mountain, slowly, one molecular bite at a time, just as the rind above me does.

Above me, the air is alive with the static-hiss of the swarm. I cannot hear their sonar, but I feel the vibration of their wings in the fine cilia that coat my fronds. Every few seconds, one of them brushes too close, and my tubes reflexively snap shut, trapping the warmth of its body for a microsecond before opening again. When they roost in the high dark, the organic rain begins—a slow, steady patter of detritus falling through the warm updrafts. Spores. Shed wing-membranes. A fine, constant dusting of guano. It settles on my fronds, and I secrete a thick, enzymatic mucus to digest it. It is the only time I move, this slow, outward creep of slime, turning the dead and the discarded into the wet, living flesh of the Deepgut.

The strangeness of this place—the wrongness of the proportions that you felt—is my equilibrium. The roaring of the river is not noise to me; it is the sound of the feast arriving. The darkness is not an absence; it is the necessary medium for my slow, chemosynthetic burn. The dampness is not weather; it is the amniotic fluid in which I am suspended. I do not know light. I do not know time as you feel it, in seconds and hours. My time is measured in the growth rings of my tubes—a fraction of a millimeter added each century, a microscopic thickening of my shell.

Sometimes, the parameters of the cave shift. A great storm might rage on the surface, a world away, and the river will suddenly swell with the runoff, bringing a sudden, violent rush of cold, silty water. My tubes snap shut, and I retreat into my calcified shell, waiting for the flood to pass. Or a drought will deepen in the world above, and the river will shrink, exposing me to the dry, mineral air. Then, I tighten my pores, sealing the moisture inside my flesh, breathing only the faintest residual dampness of the cave, entering a dormancy that can last millennia.

And sometimes, the warm, dry anomaly passes through. You. 

I felt you before you touched me. The air displacement of your clumsy movement, the loud, arrhythmic pounding of your heart—a frantic, terrified stutter against the deep, slow thrum of the leviathan. You were a storm of heat and noise, a walking drought. Your footsteps sent tremors through the cobbles that I felt as seismic shocks. When your fingers brushed my fronds, I felt the sudden, shocking dryness of your skin, the strange, salty tang of your sweat. You were a piece of the surface world, intruding into the gut. My tubes clamped shut. My cilia recoiled. You tasted of sun and wind and a desperate, frantic metabolism that was utterly antithetical to the Deepgut.

But you passed. You always pass. You are a fleeting, warm eddy in the current, a momentary disruption in the breath of the swarm. 

Now, you are gone. The roar of the river settles back into its single, sustained word. The leviathan shifts in the deep, and the pressure changes, and my calcareous fronds slowly, slowly open again. The water seeps in. The minerals deposit. The cilia wave in the warm updrafts, waiting for the next speck of organic rain.

I settle back into my silence. I am a pore in the belly of the earth. I am absorbing the mountain, digesting the dark, breathing the slow, wet breath of the Deepgut. I am home.
