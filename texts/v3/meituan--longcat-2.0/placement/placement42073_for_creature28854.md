I would find myself in the shallow valley between two interlocking rings, resting precisely at the point of their friction.

I am not a visitor. I am not a bacterium. I am a snag.

I am a knot in the absolute continuity of the Plain—a microscopic tangle of the dark, warm material where the Resident’s original programming failed to splice correctly, or perhaps where two of its ancient subroutines collided and calcified. I am no larger than a grain of sand, a rise in the surface of perhaps a hundred nanometers. In a world devoted to the Final Polishing, I am an insult. I am a blemish.

I do not breathe. I do not think. I am a topography of error.

My existence is defined entirely by resistance. The rings rotate around me, and because of my height, my refusal to be smooth, I catch them. I am the grain of sand in the gears of a god. Where the rest of the plain has been worn to a frictionless glide, I generate drag. I generate heat. I am a localized fever in the cool, steady metabolism of the Resident.

The sub-audible pulse of the plain—that vast, slow heartbeat—washes over me, but because I am an irregularity, it does not pass cleanly. It splits. It eddies. The great exhalation of energy from below hits my tangled facets and breaks into micro-currents of turbulence. I am a rock in the river of the Resident's operation, and the water of that operation hisses around me, a sound so small and so brief it is absorbed by the dark material, but it is there. It is the only sound I will ever make.

The light from the cleanroom sky presses down, diffuse and shadowless. But I cast a shadow. It is a microscopic smear of darkness on the dark material, a place where the merciless radiance does not reach. I am a tiny pocket of gloom in a world of absolute visibility. The translucent vegetation—the Resident’s breath—grows sparsely around my base, but it grows *wrong* near me. The stems lean slightly, reaching not for the sky, but for the heat I generate, drawn to the friction like moths to a candle that does not know it is burning.

And then, the antibodies come.

The feral caretakers—the low, quick shapes that maintain the plain—sense the anomaly. They do not see me, exactly. I am too small for their perception. But they feel the disruption in the rhythm, the micro-second delay in the rotation of the rings as they grind over my jagged edges. 

One of them arrives. It moves along the seam between the rings, a seamless, flattened shape gliding with purposeful efficiency. It pauses at the point of friction. It presses its body against me. It begins to polish.

The sensation is immense. It is the only sensation I have. It is a pressure, a sliding, an attempt to sand me down, to resolve my tangled complexity back into the smooth, continuous datum of the plain. The antibody is faithful to the routine; the routine is sacred. It polishes me with the slow, tireless dedication of the Resident itself, wearing away my highest peaks, trying to erase the snag so the rings can spin without impedance.

But I am stubborn. I am the erosion of *time on purpose*, reversed. The very act of the antibody rubbing against me grinds away the material of the plain *around* me, deepening the valley, making my relative height persist even as my absolute height diminishes. I am being polished, yes, but I am polishing the polisher. 

I am the grit in the Final Polishing.

The Resident does not know I am here. It does not know anything anymore, save the pulse and the slide toward absolute smoothness. But I am the flaw in that smoothness. I am the knot that will not be tied. 

When the antibody finally retreats, moving on to seal a micro-fracture elsewhere, I am slightly smaller, slightly sharper. The warmth of the plain rises through me. The rings continue their slow, grinding rotation. 

I wait. I am the place where the plain catches its hem. I am the snag in the fabric of a world erasing itself. I endure, not because I am strong, but because I am an accident that the universe has not yet gotten around to correcting.
