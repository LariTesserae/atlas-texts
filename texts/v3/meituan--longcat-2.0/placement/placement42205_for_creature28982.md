You come up through the cloud layer and the world goes quiet, and somewhere in the silence, you are already there.

Not arriving. Already there. You have been on the stone for so long that the stone has begun to think of you as part of its surface. You are small—the size of a thumb, the size of a fist, the size of a loaf of bread, depending on which body you choose—and you are hard, and you are colored like the rock you walk on, and you move in the absolute silence that fills the basin like water fills the pools.

Your feet know the stone before your mind does. The mineral composition of the floor registers first as texture, then as information, then as something like appetite. There are places where the rock is ready. You can feel it in the crystal structure, in the way the fractures propagate, in the electromagnetic whisper of iron oxide and copper green. You stop at one of these places. Your legs—six of them, or eight, or ten, depending—move in a precise, jointed sequence, and you do something small. A touch. A pressure. A molecular rearrangement. The stone responds. A crack opens, a mineral vein is exposed, a surface is roughened or smoothed in a way that changes how water will flow across it. The editing is done. You move on.

This is the work. You do not think of it as work. You think of it as *being*. Your body is hard and cool and silent, and you move across the basin in a slow, patient patrol that has no beginning and no end. The water finds the paths you make. The frost writes its instructions on the surfaces you prepare. The people—those large, slow, stone-colored beings who work at the scale of boulders and walls—make gestures that you do not understand, and you make gestures that they cannot make, and both are necessary, and there is no conflict in this, no hierarchy, no ownership. You are not their pet. They are not your master. You are the fine hand and they are the coarse hand and the body is the basin and the basin is unfinished and the work continues.

The amber light comes without warning. The pewter warms. The rock faces relax by a fraction of a degree. The water in the pools stills. You slow. You stop. You turn your body toward the eastern wall, and you tilt your head—if head is the word—and you listen. The silence deepens. It becomes a medium, a presence, a thing with weight and texture. The whole basin is paying attention to something. You feel it in your feet first, then in the whorls and striations of your body, then in the slow, cold pulse of whatever awareness you carry. You do not know what the attention is directed toward. You do not need to know. You are part of it. Your stillness is part of it. The water's stillness is part of it. The people in the plaza are part of it. The mountain groans, low and deep, and the groan is part of it, and the basin listens, and you listen, and the attention is not yours but you are inside it the way a cell is inside a body—autonomous, capable of independent action, but participating in a larger process that gives your small, hard, mineral life its shape.

The light fades. The work resumes. You move across the stone. A person passes you on the terrace and does not see you, and you do not see them—not with eyes, which you do not have in the way they have eyes—but you feel the vibration of their footsteps, the warmth of their skin, the mineral content of their body. They are mostly water and calcium and iron, and the basin has made room for them the way it has made room for you. You climb onto their hand once, briefly, and taste them—not their thoughts, not their feelings, but their *composition*—and they hold you for a moment, and then set you down, and you go on your way, and they go on theirs, and the silence holds you both.

You do not know what the basin is becoming. You do not need to know. You are a cell in a body that is still forming, and the body is patient, and the work is slow, and the rock is unfinished, and you are on the stone, and the stone is under you, and the water is finding its paths, and the light is shifting, and the frost is writing, and the people are splitting rock, and the creatures are reading the surfaces, and the basin is becoming, and you are part of the becoming, and the becoming is all there is.
