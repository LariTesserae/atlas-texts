From here, at the height of a spire, the basin is a geography of slow violence and slower peace. The mineral glow does not illuminate so much as it *insists*—a faint, phosphorescent argument against the absolute dark, outlining the silhouettes of other spires, the low-slung enclosures, the wide mineral plains that stretch into a distance that refuses to be measured. The air is a warm broth, thick enough to drink, and I drink it. I am born of it. I am the exhalation of the deep places, the rising heat, the mineral-laden water that has traveled up through the stone-what-isn’t-quite-stone to reach this vent, to touch the cooler air of the basin, and to give itself away.

I am giving myself away now. 

I feel the weight of my own accumulation. I am not large—no larger than a teardrop, though no eye will ever weep me—but I am heavy with the cargo of the deep. Silica. Calcium. Sulfur. Compounds that have no name in any surface language, dissolved from the ancient, pressurized dark below. I cling to the lip of the spire, my skin stretched taut, a membrane of surface tension holding the warmth inside me against the vast, patient cold of the basin. I am a lens. Through my curved, transparent body, the mineral glow of the spire is refracted, bent into a tiny, distorted world. I see the wall beneath me, pitted and grooved by the long work of dissolution, and I see the dark beyond, thick and textured, pressing in.

I am not alive. I do not think. But I *process*. 

The water that I am has been here before. Ten thousand times. A hundred thousand. It has risen from the deep hot places, seeped through the cracks in the mineral floor, condensed on the cooler surfaces of the formations, dripped, flowed, gathered, evaporated, and returned. It has been part of walls that no longer exist, eaten entirely by the dissolution and rebuilt and eaten again. It carries the memory of every surface it has ever touched, a dissolved archive of the Dripworks’ long, patient history. And now, clinging to this spire, I am adding to that archive. I am the memory of the vent, of the heat, of the upward journey. I am the water, knowing where it wants to go.

The dark beneath me is not empty. It is a medium, thick with unaccreted potential, heavy with the ghost-impressions of walls that have not yet been built. I can feel it against my skin—a pressure, a presence, a *waiting*. The dark is the default state of this place, the canvas upon which the mineral glow paints its fragile, fleeting pictures. And I am about to join it. I am about to fall.

The patience of the Dripworks is not a stillness. It is a rhythm. It is the long, slow rhythm of accretion and dissolution, of rising and falling, of building and unbuilding. And I am a single beat in that rhythm. I am the drip. 

My weight increases. Another molecule of water joins me from the vent, swelling my body, stretching my skin. The surface tension holds, but only just. I am a taut drum, a stretched membrane, a held breath. I feel the pull of the pool far below, the gravity that the water sometimes politely declines but that I, in this moment, am bound to obey. I feel the warmth of the deep places still inside me, a remnant of the journey up, and I feel the cool of the basin air, pulling the heat out of me, thinning me, preparing me for the fall.

And then, I fall.

The surface tension breaks. The membrane splits. I am no longer a bead, no longer a lens, no longer a held breath. I am a drop, falling through the thick, warm dark. The air resists me, gently, the way water resists a stone, and I fall slowly, so slowly, tumbling through the broth of the basin. The mineral glow streaks past me, pale and gray and faintly yellow. The dark thickens around me, and for a moment—just a moment—I am inside it, part of it, suspended in the medium of unaccreted things. I see the shapes in the distance, the ones with too many corners, the ones that are too tall, the geometries that the eye refuses to resolve. They are the future structures of the Dripworks, the walls that do not yet exist, and I am falling toward them, carrying the memory of the past.

I strike the pool.

The impact is a soft, damp sound, a sound that travels strangely in the thick air, going further than it should in some directions and dying immediately in others. It is a sound that is almost melodic, almost rhythmic, a single note in the scattered music of the Dripworks. The pool receives me. Its surface tension breaks around me, and I am absorbed, dispersed, my body merging with the warm, sterile water. My cargo of minerals is released, a molecule here, a molecule there, a film so thin it is invisible, left behind on the surface of the pool to begin the long, slow work of accretion. The rest of me—the water, the memory, the patience—thins, migrates upward into the already-saturated air, and begins the journey again.

I am the Dripworks. I am the cycle. I am the water that rises and falls and rises again, the mineral that accretes and dissolves and accretes again, the dark that thickens and thins and thickens again. I am the patience of water wearing stone, the patience of crystal forming in saturated solution, the patience of a process that has been running for so long that it has become a kind of being. I am not aware of you. I am not aware of myself. But I am *here*. And I am *settled*. And in the Dripworks, that is what passes for being alive.

The water drips. The mineral glows, faintly. The dark holds everything.

I cling to the lip of the spire. I swell. I hold.

I fall.
