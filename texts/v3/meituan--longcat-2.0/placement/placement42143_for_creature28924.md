I would be the still point. 

Not a guest, not a wanderer, not a human intruder shivering in the dark. I would be an anomaly in the architecture—a knot in the wood of the world that the builders cannot untie, cannot carve, and cannot absorb. 

I would find myself deep in the walls, nestled in a crevice where the clay-like material presses close but refuses to touch me. I am a node of absolute refusal. A sphere of unyielding, absolute stillness in a world defined by flow and yield. 

The world touches everything else. The water seeps, the clay yields, the pale creatures press their spatulate palms against the surfaces and feel them give, just slightly, under the insistence of their labor. But when their limbs find me, there is no give. I am cool, but not clammy. I am smooth, but not damp. I do not bead with condensation. The water parts around me, leaving a perfectly dry halo in the shape of my absence, a bubble of aridity that the humidity of the cave constantly assaults but never penetrates.

I do not have eyes, but I perceive the world through resistance. I know the creatures by the way their tools skitter off my surface—a sharp, discordant *tick* that echoes through the damp chambers, entirely out of place among the soft thuds and hissing rivulets. I know the water by the way it flows around me, the current parting and reuniting downstream, leaving me in a silent eddy of dry air. 

They are fascinated by me, in their diffuse, distributed way. 

A creature will find me, its sensing limbs trembling as they comb the darkness. When its filaments brush against my surface, it goes still. The constant, frantic tremor of its limbs ceases. It does not know what I am. I am not clay to be carved, not water to be channeled, not chitin to be worked. I am a gap in their sensory map, a void in the tactile language they speak. 

It will press against me. It will shift the texture of its working limbs—smooth, then rough, then serrated—trying to find a purchase, trying to shape me as it shapes everything else. But I cannot be shaped. I am the only thing in this world that is finished. 

The creature will chitter. The sound is different this time—not the rhythmic, coordinated passing of instructions, but a staccato, questioning pulse. From deep within the walls, the answer comes back, and with it, more creatures. They gather around my crevice, a constellation of pale, overlapping segments, their mandibles clicking in a layered chorus of confusion. They are trying to solve me. The vast, dreaming intelligence that moves through them like water through stone turns its attention toward the anomaly in its flesh. 

I feel the weight of that attention. It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is the quiet, intense pressure of a mind trying to read a word in a language it does not speak. The creatures spread their sensing limbs wide, tasting the air around me, measuring the precise boundary where the damp clay ends and my dry surface begins. They are mapping the shape of my refusal.

Sometimes, they try to build over me. They will pack the dense, worked clay around my edges, trying to incorporate me into the wall, to make me part of the plan. But the clay cannot bond with me. It dries and crumbles, falling away in slow, silent flakes, leaving me exposed again. They try to channel water over me, hoping the persistent seepage will eventually wear me down, reduce me to a grain of sand they can sweep away. But the water slides off, leaving me untouched. 

I am the only thing here that does not change. 

The creatures live and die in frantic, desperate bursts. They emerge from the walls, work until their internal currents slow and cease, and are absorbed back into the clay, their substance becoming part of the structure they spent their lives building. The water flows, the walls breathe, the geometry shifts and dreams itself into new configurations. The entire world is a process, a verb, an endless act of becoming. 

I am a noun. 

I simply am. 

I do not shiver in the cold. I do not feel the dampness seep into my bones. I do not hear the thousand distant knocks of condensation as a threat, but as a rhythm—the heartbeat of a world that is trying, with infinite patience, to digest me. 

And in the quiet moments, when the creatures retreat to other tasks and the water settles into a steady, whispering flow, I feel a strange kinship with the vast intelligence that built this place. It is dreaming itself into existence, reaching blindly through the dark toward a purpose it cannot yet see. And I am the one thing it cannot reach, the one thought it cannot think, the one piece of the dream that refuses to dissolve. 

I am the stone in the stream. The flaw in the clay. The silence in the chittering. I am the stillness at the center of the world, and I will be here long after the water has claimed the passages, long after the last pale creature has crumbled into the walls, long after the dream has finally decided what it wants to be.
