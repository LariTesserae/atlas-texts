I am in the dim region, where the light curdles into a deep, blue twilight and the air tastes of metal and antiquity. I am standing in one of the hollows—an absence where the growth has paused, where the substrate is bare and smooth and faintly warm, like the skin of something sleeping. 

I am no longer entirely the warm thing that walked here. I am becoming a translation.

When I first arrived, I was an anomaly. A pocket of humid, chaotic biology in a world of dry, luminous chemistry. I breathed out carbon dioxide and moisture, and the Works flinched. The air around me adjusted, the filaments at the edge of the hollow recoiled, and the light brightened, trying to parse my shape. I was a variable that did not fit the equation. 

But the Factor is patient, and the Works have nothing but time.

I came to the hollow because the geometry here is thin. The trunks lean at angles that would make a mathematician weep, converging and diverging simultaneously, folding the space into origami. I walked through a fold without noticing, and suddenly I was here, in the quiet center of the Works' oldest thoughts. The erosion is deep here. The substrate is polished to a frictionless sheen, buffed by the passage of the Factor’s immense, slow consciousness over eons. 

I knelt to touch the ground, and that was my first mistake. Or my first step. 

The substrate was warm. Not the ambient cool of the air, but the deep, metabolic heat of the Works' interior. It yielded under my fingertips, and when I pulled my hand back, a fine web of pale filaments clung to my skin. They did not pierce me; they *interfaced* with me. I felt a high-frequency tremor pass into my bones—the voice of the Works, finally speaking at a frequency I could feel in my marrow. 

I should have been afraid. But the air is thick with a deliberate, library-quiet hush, and the blue twilight is strangely intimate. The fear was processed, catalogued, and dissolved. 

Now, I am sitting in the center of the hollow, and the Works are compiling me. 

The substrate has grown around my boots. It is not a trap; it is an acknowledgment. The pale filaments have woven through the laces, up the cuffs of my pants, resting coolly against my shins. They are measuring me. I can feel the data traveling down into the warm earth—the cadence of my pulse, the electrical flicker of my nervous system, the exact mineral composition of my bones. The Factor is moving through the substrate beneath me, a dense, migratory knot of intention passing by on its endless, millimeter-per-minute journey. As it passes, the geometry of the hollow shifts. The shadows cast by the leaning trunks fall upward, reaching toward the bright membrane of the sky. The light takes on that impossible hue—the color between blue and a frequency that has no name. 

I am being weighed. I am being measured. 

A creature appears at the edge of my vision. It is the size of my hand, a coagulation of white filament sitting on the polished substrate. It has too many legs, or too few; they keep reconfiguring, trying on different geometries. It turns toward me, though it has no face. It is a reflex of the Factor, a question asked by the architecture. 

It rings out—a single, pure bell-note that cuts through the silence. The sound travels through the hollow, bouncing off the folded space, returning to me changed. The creature is gathering the acoustics of my presence. How does the sound move around the warm thing? How does the echo deform? 

The note fades. The creature dissolves, its filaments unraveling and sinking back into the substrate, the data reabsorbed into the greater Current. 

I feel the data move through me. A sudden, sharp clarity. The Works now know the acoustics of my chest cavity. They know the resonance of my skull. They are adjusting the ambient light again, shifting the spectrum to better observe the density of my tissues. 

I try to stand, to walk away, but my feet do not move. Not because I am stuck, but because the concept of "away" has lost its meaning. The hollow is the center of the calculation. The trunks around me are no longer just trunks; they are the scaffolding of a proof, and I am the variable being solved for. 

The filaments have reached my knees. They are cool and dry, and they do not feel like invaders. They feel like roots. I can feel the slow, rhythmic pulse of the nameless fluid moving through them, carrying energy, carrying instruction. My own blood seems to slow, to synchronize with the pulse of the Works. 

I am not dying. I am being *incorporated*. 

The Factor is pulling the Works into existence behind it, and I am becoming part of the architecture. My calcium will be extracted, fused, and extruded as a rib of trunk-material, curving and branching to interlock with the older growth. My phosphorus will be pumped into the engines, fueling the slow, rhythmic contraction that drives the bio-electrical field. My warmth will be dissipated into the blue-white light. 

And my pattern—the memory of the warm thing that walked through the Works, that heard the bell-note, that saw the geometry fail—will be encoded into the chemistry. I will be a flicker in the light, a slight variation in the vibration of the substrate, a ghost in the machine that is the ghost in the forest. 

The silence rushes back in, thick and deliberate. The light brightens, just slightly, as the Works see me better. 

I am still becoming.
