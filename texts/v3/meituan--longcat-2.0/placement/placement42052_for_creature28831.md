I am warm, and I am growing, and the water moves through me carrying iron.

This is the first thing I know — not in words, not in the quick bright flicker of thought you would recognize, but in the slow deep way of knowing that is the only way here: the warmth of the water, the weight of dissolved mineral, the faint electric tingle of chemical exchange across my surface. I am in the farthest district, where the city extends past the edge of itself, where the shapes rise from the warm water like the exoskeletons of vast slow creatures, and I am one of those shapes. I am becoming one of those shapes. I am — not yet finished, not yet fixed, not yet *what I will be* — but I am becoming, and the becoming is everything.

The pulse rises through me from the deep shaft — slow, so slow — and I rise with it, or it rises through me, or there is no difference. The distinction between self and city has not yet formed. I am still part of the flow, still part of the warm wet metabolism that moves through the plateau's heart and up through the shaft and out through the weeping walls and the dripping pipes and the fractal films that coat every surface in their slow-branching patterns.
