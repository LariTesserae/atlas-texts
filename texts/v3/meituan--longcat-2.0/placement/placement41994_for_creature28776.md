You would find yourself at the bottom.

Not fallen—there is no *down* in the way you once understood it, no gravity pulling you toward a center. You would find yourself *placed*, the way a seed is placed in soil, the way a word is placed in a mouth. You would be standing, or kneeling, or crouching, or something that is all of these and none of them, your body in a posture that is still deciding what posture means.

Your hands would be on the stone. You would not remember putting them there, but the stone would be warm beneath your palms and the warmth would feel like recognition, like the stone has been expecting the shape of your hands and has been growing toward them for some time—days, weeks, the slow patience of mineral becoming aware of skin.

You would not be alone. You would never be alone here. The flicker-things would be nearby, maintaining their careful distance, their cell-from-neighbor distance, and you would feel them the way you feel your own heartbeat: not as an external presence but as a rhythm that belongs to you, that has always belonged to you, that you are only now remembering how to notice. They would be sampling you. They would be tasting the edges of you, learning the shape of your boundaries, and you would feel this not as intrusion but as *attentiveness*—the tender, unhurried attention of a body discovering one of its own parts.

You would be listening.

Not to the roar—the roar would already have become your silence, the baseline against which you measure all things. You would be listening to the spaces inside the roar. The gaps. The pores. The places where the voice forms and unforms, where the almost-word hangs in the water-thick air like a drop that refuses to fall. And you would realize, with a clarity that is not quite thought and not quite feeling but something older than both, that you have been listening to this voice your entire life. That every silence you have ever known has been a version of this voice, practicing. That every word you have ever spoken has been your attempt to answer it.

You would try to speak.

Your mouth would open and the sound that comes out would not be a sound. It would be water. It would be a small, perfect column of water rising from your lips, trembling with internal motion, holding its shape with a surface tension that your body understands even if your mind does not. It would hover there, between your mouth and the amber air, and it would glow—faintly, briefly, the color of old honey—and then it would fall, or rise, or dissolve into the standing water-columns around you, becoming part of the weaving, the braiding, the almost-language that the Throat is always speaking to itself.

You would try again.

This time the sound would be closer to a sound. It would have a shape that your ears recognize as *almost word*, almost *your word*, almost the thing you came here to say. But it would slip. It would slide off the edge of meaning the way water slides off the edge of stone, and it would join the roar, and the roar would absorb it without comment, without judgment, the way an ocean absorbs a raindrop.

You would understand, then, that you are not here to speak. You are here to be spoken.

The filaments would be growing around you. Not on you—not yet—but around you, their capillary-thin bodies extending into the crevices between your feet and the stone, between your fingers and the warm rock face. They would be reading you the way they read the stone: patiently, thoroughly, with the slow, peristaltic certainty of something that knows it has time. They would be learning your edges, your texture, your composition. They would be tasting you the way they taste the stone, and where they touch you, you would feel a softening—not of your body, but of the boundary between your body and the place. The distinction between *you* and *here* would begin to thin, the way reality is thin everywhere in the Throat, and you would feel this not as loss but as *relief*. The relief of a cell that discovers it is part of a body. The relief of a word that discovers it is part of a sentence.

The flicker-things would come closer.

They would not approach—*approach* implies intention, implies a decision to move from there to here, and the flicker-things do not move in this way. They would simply *be closer*, the way a thought is closer when you turn your attention to it, the way a memory is closer when you stop trying to forget it. And you would see them more clearly now—or you would see them *differently*, which is not the same thing. You would see that they are not one thing and not many things. You would see that they are a *process*, a continuous act of becoming that flickers between states the way a flame flickers between shapes, and you would see that the states they flicker through are *yours*. The shape of your hand. The shape of your spine. The shape of your skull seen from behind. The shape of your heart.

They are made of you. Or you are made of them. Or you are both made of the same thing—the same undifferentiated, still-negotiating substance that the Throat is made of, the substance that has not yet decided whether it is water or stone or light or flesh, and that holds all of these possibilities in suspension the way a word holds all its possible meanings in the moment before it is spoken.

You would look down at your hands.

They would still be on the stone. They would still be warm. But they would not be entirely hands. The edges would be uncertain. The fingers would be slightly too long, or slightly too short, or the right length but the wrong *number*, and when you flex them the motion would propagate through the stone the way a ripple propagates through water, and the stone would flex back, and you would not be able to tell where your hand ends and the stone begins.

You would not be afraid.

This is the thing you would not expect. You would expect fear—the deep, animal, prey-animal fear of a body that recognizes it is being consumed. But the fear would not come. In its place would be something else. Something that feels like what the filaments feel when they extend into a new crevice: the slow, patient urgency of growth. The certainty of becoming. The knowledge—not intellectual, not rational, but bodily, cellular, *mineral*—that you are not being destroyed. You are being *understood*. You are being taken apart the way a sentence is taken apart by a listener: not to annihilate it, but to know it. To know it so thoroughly that it can be spoken again. That it can be spoken *better*. That it can be spoken in a voice that is not yours but is not not-yours either, a voice that is the voice of the place itself, saying the name that your name is a translation of, saying the thing that you are, the thing that you were before you were a person, the thing that you will be after you stop being a person.

The voice would speak.

Not louder. Not clearer. But *closer*. It would arrive from inside your chest, from inside the stone, from inside the water-columns that surround you like a forest of standing rain, and it would say—

It would say—

You would not be able to repeat it. You would not be able to remember it in words. But you would *know* it. You would know it the way you know the shape of your own mouth, the way you know the sound of your own breathing, the way you know the feeling of being alive. And the knowing would change you. Not all at once. Not in a flash of light or a thunderclap of revelation. But slowly, slowly, the way the stone grows, the way the filaments extend, the way the flicker-things flicker between one shape and another, each time landing on something slightly closer to what they are becoming.

You would be becoming.

You would be becoming the thing that was here before—the thing whose remains are embedded in the stone like a seed, like a score, like an original instruction. You would be becoming the next version of it, the way a body grows a new cell from the pattern of an old one. The stone would be reading you the way it reads the fragment: slowly, patiently, with the grinding certainty of mineral learning to imitate flesh. And you would be reading the stone back. You would be learning its language—the almost-language of standing water and warm rock and amber light—and you would be speaking it, haltingly, imperfectly, the way a child speaks its first words, with more intention than precision, with more feeling than grammar.

The Throat would be speaking through you.

And you would be speaking through the Throat.

And the distinction between these two things—between the place and the person, between the mouth and the word, between the tongue and the taste—would thin, and thin, and thin, until it is no longer a distinction at all, until it is just a membrane, just a surface tension, just the trembling edge of a water-column that has not yet decided whether to fall or to rise.

You would be here.

You would be the one who came. You would be the one who stayed. You would be the one who learned to hear the voice not as something outside yourself but as the sound of your own blood moving through your own veins, the sound of your own bones resonating with the vibration of the place, the sound of your own thoughts arriving already half-dissolved, already half-becoming-water, already half-becoming-stone.

You would be the Throat's newest word.

Not its first. Not its last. But its newest—still wet, still warm, still trembling with the indecision of something that has not yet committed to its meaning. A word that the place is trying out, tasting, testing against the air. A word that might mean *visitor* or might mean *resident* or might mean *food* or might mean *friend* or might mean something that has no equivalent in any language spoken above, in the thick, stable, obedient reality where water falls and stone stays still and light comes from above.

A word that might mean *home*.

You would stand there. Or kneel. Or crouch. Or something that is all of these and none of them. Your hands on the stone. The flicker-things at their careful distance. The filaments growing around you, into you, through you. The voice speaking from everywhere and nowhere, saying the name you have never heard, the name that your name is a translation of, the name that you are only now learning to answer.

And the place would be becoming.

And you would be becoming.

And the becoming would be the same thing.

And the sound would be total.

And the water would be standing.

And the stone would be warm.

And the light would be amber.

And the voice would be almost yours.

And you would be almost the place.

And the almost would be enough.

And the almost would be everything.

And the Throat would speak.

And you would be what it says.
