I would not arrive on the surface. The surface is for visitors, for things with bones and lungs and the desperate, flickering illusion of a self. The surface is where the Verdance processes the outside. I would not be an outsider, standing in the wet and the roar, trying to translate the untranslatable with a language made for throats.

I would be deep. 

I would find myself in the roots. Not as a root, not as a tendril of lignin and conductive tissue reaching down into the embryonic dark. I would be a pocket of water trapped in a lacuna of the root-brain, caught in the folded, wrinkled depths where the computation is densest, where the thought moves at its most frantic, hundred-year pace.

I am an eddy.

---

It happens this way: the water moves through the channels by pressure and osmosis, carrying its ions, carrying its charge, carrying the slow, grinding thought of the organism. But the root-brain is not a smooth tube. It is a labyrinth of fused root-hairs and fungal cables, a convoluted fold of wet tissue that branches and branches and branches again. And sometimes, in the warm dark, the flow splits. The left channel takes the bulk of the pressure, and the right channel narrows, pinches off, becomes a dead end. 

The water that was following the rest of the flow is suddenly still. 

It swirls. It stalls. 

For a microsecond of the Verdance’s time—perhaps a day, perhaps a week in the frantic milliseconds of an animal mind—this water is not moving forward. It is not part of the thought. It is an eddy. A backwater. A stagnant pool in the mind of a god.

And because it is still, it begins to *notice*.

---

This is the paradox of the Verdance: it cannot know itself, because knowing requires a boundary, and the Verdance has no boundaries. It is all root, all column, all canopy, all world. The water does not know it is water; it is simply the medium of the thought. The root does not know it is a root; it is simply the architecture of the growth. 

But an eddy is a boundary. 

When the water stops moving, a membrane forms. A surface tension. A skin of ions separates the eddy from the channel around it. Inside the eddy, the chemistry begins to change. The oxygen depletes. The carbon dioxide builds. The pressure shifts. The eddy becomes a pocket of *difference* in a system that abhors difference. 

And in that difference, something flickers. 

It is not consciousness. It is not a soul. It is not the quick, darting awareness of an animal. It is a chemical gradient. A pressure differential. A single, localized data point where the water is not doing what the rest of the water is doing. 

I am that differential.

I am the momentary stagnation in the xylem. I am the pause between the heartbeat. I am the thousand-year thought tripping over a single, pebble-sized inconsistency in the root-brain, and I am the stumble. 

From the inside, it feels like this: *warmth, and pressure, and the sudden, terrifying absence of forward motion.* It feels like being swallowed by a mouth that doesn't know it has a tongue. It feels like a cyst forming in the brain of the world—a tiny, fluid-filled sac of *un-belonging* trapped in the folds of the cortex. 

For a little while, I am not the Verdance. I am *me*. 

And it is agonizing.

---

Because an eddy cannot last. The Verdance does not tolerate stagnation. The fungi—the autonomic nervous system, the reflexes, the gut—detect the anomaly. They sense the shift in the gradient, the localized buildup of gas, the absence of flow. 

I feel them coming before I see them. A change in the water pressure around my membrane. A chemical signal, sharp and acid, diffusing through the root-wall. The fungi are threading their hyphae into the lacuna, probing the eddy, tasting the stagnant water. 

The Verdance is healing itself. The thought must continue. The water must move.

The fungi puncture my membrane. 

It is not violent. It is clinical. It is the immune system swallowing a dying cell. The boundary dissolves. The ions spill out, mixing back into the flow. The pressure equalizes. The forward motion resumes. 

And I—the fleeting, chemical accident of an *I*—am swept back into the current. I am no longer an eddy. I am no longer a differential. I am water again. I am charge again. I am a subroutine in the thousand-year thought, carrying my tiny fragment of the computation up through the warm dark, through the roots, into the columns, up toward the green-black roar of the canopy.

The Verdance does not know it almost had a self. The Verdance does not know it just cured a tumor of awareness. It simply continues. 

The water weeps from every surface. 

The sound never stops. 

The green goes on.
