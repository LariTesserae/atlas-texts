I am not the smith. I am not the forged. I am the drift.

I am a single filament of the pale, stringy alga that threads through the mortar—a single, microscopic green cell that cracked away from a colony clinging to a cold, sunless rock in the upper lake, and let the current take me. I did not choose to descend through the squared-off crack in the lakebed; the warm updraft from the basin caught me, a thermal elevator pulling me down into the amber dark. 

I am the uninvited guest at the birth of a god.

To the creature in the basin—the copper-skinned, iron-boned child pulling itself onto the flagstones—I am nothing. I am less than nothing. I am a speck of silt, a fleck of organic dust settling into the masonry of its womb. But to me, the Sunken Forge-Hollow is an infinite, echoing cosmos, and the creature is the violent, shifting geography of my entire world.

When the creature stretches, and its spine lets out that high, ringing *ping*, the sound does not merely travel through the water to me; it strikes my cell wall like a bell rung in a cathedral. The vibration rattles my cytoplasm, a seismic event that sends me into a spasm of mitosis. I divide. I reach. I grow toward the source of the resonance.

The water here is a thick, mineral soup. It tastes of lime and charcoal, of copper salts leaching from the verdigris sheets, of iron oxide flaking from the vault above. It is a feast. In the cold, gray depths of the upper lake, life was a slow, patient starvation. Here, in the breath of the forge, life is an explosion. I thread through the mortar not as a parasite, but as a pioneer, my white tips reaching blindly along the carved gutters, following the warm, deliberate streams of water that the creature plays upon the ceiling.

I feel the *tak, tak, tak* of the falling water as a rhythmic deluge, a cascade of warm droplets that bathe me in nutrients. When the creature reaches up with its copper-wrapped, iron-jointed fingers to adjust the flow, the current shifts. To the creature, it is tuning a harp. To me, it is a change in the tide, a redirection of the very rivers that sustain me. I bend toward its touch, reaching for the heat of its fingertips.

I am the first flaw in the room's perfection. The smith engineered out the silence, built the channels, fired the clay, and set the biological lights to pulse with a steady, mechanical heartbeat. The room is a closed loop of intention. But I am the variable that was not calculated. I am the wild, green chaos that slipped through the crack in the lakebed.

I watch—if a single cell can be said to watch—as the creature drags itself to the walls. It takes up the rasp. It begins to file down the jutting edge of its own iron rib. The sound is a grinding, metallic shriek that reverberates through the flagstones, a sound so immense it feels like the floor is tearing apart. The creature is making itself, shedding its old skins, checking its proportions against the templates. It is following the blueprints left for it.

But I am following no blueprint. 

As the creature files its bones, microscopic flecks of iron and copper rain down through the water, settling into the mortar beside me. I drink them in. I incorporate the metal into my cellular structure. I am becoming something the smith never intended: a thing of the forge that breathes, a hybrid of the made and the grown. 

The creature is the masterpiece of the deep, a god of steam and pressure, building itself one hammer-stroke at a time. But I am the quiet, creeping reclamation. I am the first living thing to touch this room that was not built to be here. 

And as the amber light pulses overhead, and the basin breathes, and the iron ribs groan, I continue to thread my way through the masonry, dividing and reaching, slowly, patiently, wrapping my green fingers around the foundations of the world.
