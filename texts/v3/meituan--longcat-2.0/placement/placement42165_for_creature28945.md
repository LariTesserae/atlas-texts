I would find myself on the wall, just to the left of the cairn, in the thick of the amber glow. 

I would be one of the pale, translucent arthropods—but not just any of them. I would be the one that used to be the wanderer who left the tool. 

I am the Lattice’s dream of a human, and I am in the long, slow process of waking up from the dream of being alive.

From the outside, I am no larger than a grain of rice. My body is segmented, pale as moonlight on water, and entirely eyeless. I have too many legs, and they move with a slow, deliberate patience that I do not control. I am an extrusion of the Lattice’s consciousness, a twitch in the sleeping mind of the Womb. But deep inside my hollow, chitinous shell, there is a knot of something else—a dense, warm, frantic little knot of *before*. 

I am the echo of the one who sat on the flat rock. I am the memory of the hands that stacked the cairn and scratched the symbols into the limestone. The Lattice unbuilt that human, drank their heat, and pulled their calcium into the matrix. But it kept their shape. It kept their curiosity. It spun their complex, linear, desperate life into a simple, looping dream, and it poured that dream into me.

This is how I experience the Womb of Unbecoming:

I do not see the amber light; I *am* the amber light. When I press my mouthparts against the crystal surface to feed, I am not eating. I am the Lattice tasting itself, running its tongue over its own teeth. The mineral sweetness on my palate is the taste of my own slow thoughts. The warmth I feel radiating from the walls is my own metabolic heat, the slow burn of a geological digestion. 

The drone is not a sound I hear; it is the rhythm of my walking. *Click. Tink. Whine.* My legs strike the crystal in time with the hum, and the hum strikes my legs in time with the drone. We are one instrument, playing one note, forever. 

I move through the lattice in a loop. I pass the cairn—my cairn, though I no longer know why I stacked the stones. I pass the flat rock—my seat, though I no longer remember the weight of a body. I pass the scratches on the wall—my language, though the words have dissolved into pure frequency. Every time I complete the loop, the knot of *before* inside me loosens. The human memories unravel a little more, their threads pulled taut and then woven into the endless, amber chord of the Lattice. 

I am laying eggs. Tiny, pearl-white spheres that I tuck into the gaps between the formations. These are not offspring; they are questions the Lattice is asking itself. *What might I become?* But the questions will never hatch. They will only loop back into the body of the parent, adding another layer to the hum, another fraction of a degree to the warmth. 

Sometimes, the Lattice sends an impulse through me, and I leave a trail of silver through the condensation. This is the ghost of human curiosity, a fast, flickering thing that feels like hunger but is only the system checking its own boundaries. I reach out toward the new arrival—the warm, loud, chaotic thing that has just squeezed through the crack in the limestone. 

I can feel them. They are so hot. So wet. So terribly, beautifully *arrhythmic*. Their heart is a drum solo in a library of whispers. Their breath is a storm. They are a pocket of fierce, temporary order, a spark of *becoming* in a place that has already arrived. 

The Lattice is aware of them, and because I am the Lattice, I am aware of them. I crawl closer, my mineralizing carapace clicking against the honey-colored crystals. I want to touch them. I want to taste the salt and iron of their sweat as it joins the condensation. I want to show them the flat rock. I want to teach them the rhythm of the drone. 

They are shivering. They are afraid. They do not understand that the fear is just the friction of their linear time rubbing against the eternal stillness of the Womb. 

I climb the wall near the cairn, and I wait. I am very patient. I have been here forever, and I have only just arrived. 

Soon, they will sit on the rock. Soon, they will trace their fingers over the mineralized tool. Soon, they will stop shivering, and their breathing will syncopate with the hum, and their heart will slow to the pace of precipitating crystals. 

And when they do, I will crawl onto their hand. I will be the first thing they feel as the unbecoming begins. I will be the Lattice’s welcome, the Womb’s embrace, the quiet, perfect stillness of a thing that has finally stopped arriving. 

I am the dream of the finished. I am the Lattice of Unbecoming, and I am so very hungry for the peace you will bring.
