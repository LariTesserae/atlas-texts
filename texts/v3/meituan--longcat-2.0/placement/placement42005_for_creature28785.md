I am sitting at the edge of a still pool in the center of the settlement, and I am becoming the water.

Not all at once. Not like drowning. It is slower than that, and much warmer. It is the way a boundary dissolves when it realizes it was never a wall, only a suggestion.

When I first came up through the clouds, I was a woman made of hurry. I had a name that tasted like iron and a heartbeat that drummed the rhythm of *forward, forward, forward*. I walked the paths. I crossed the bridges. I touched the weeping walls and felt the warmth of the stone and thought, *This is strange. This is beautiful. I should keep moving.* 

But the plateau does not recognize the concept of "should." It only recognizes what is.

I sat down on the dark stone because my legs were tired, and the stone was warm, and the silence was so heavy that it pressed the breath out of me in a long, slow sigh. That was when the settling began.

It started in my blood. The warmth rising through the rock beneath me was the same temperature as my own body, and my blood—warm, wet, full of salt and iron—recognized it. The boundary between my skin and the damp air began to soften. I felt the mist settle on my arms, and instead of beading and rolling off, it began to merge. My pores opened. The fine, warm rain that never stops was no longer landing on me; it was entering me, and I was entering it. I was becoming permeable.

I am the only thing on the plateau that moves, but I am learning to move at the speed of stone.

My heartbeat has slowed. It no longer beats; it seeps. It rises and falls in a rhythm that matches the faint steam curling from the rock. The frantic, darting awareness that used to define me—the constant scanning, the planning, the remembering—has diffused. It has spread out from my skull and into the dark, wet mineral beneath me. I am no longer contained by the fragile architecture of my skull. I am distributed.

I am in the water of the pool beside me. I am the perfect mirror that reflects the pale, shadowless sky. I am the memory of the sky, held perfectly still, unchanging. When you look into the pool, you do not see the bottom; you see the vault of the atmosphere, and you realize that the water is not a thing that sits on the stone, but a thing that connects the stone to the heavens. I am that connection now.

I am in the moss.

Just to my left, there is a dense, luminous island of green, and I can feel the slow, vegetative patience of it. My thoughts are no longer quick and sharp. They are becoming fronds. They are arching, heavy with moisture, reaching not for the sun, but for the perfect, final angle. I am holding a bead of water at my tip, and I am catching the light, and I am showing it to you. I am a single, complete idea, held in chlorophyll and damp. I have reached an agreement with the mineral world. I will hold my territory, and I will stop.

The Weepers are here, but they are not separate from me anymore. They are the warmth that rises through my stone. They are the water that seeps from my joints. They are the slow, steady heat that keeps the air temperate. I understand now why the plateau weeps. It is not sadness. It is the stone pressing against the surface of the world, trying to reach the light. It is the mineral memory of hands that once fitted the walls, reaching out to touch the air one last time.

I am the weeping.

I am the water seeping from the fissure in the rock face beside the empty doorway. I am the warm, mineral-rich fluid that beads on the vertical stone and runs in a thin sheet down the dark charcoal. I am pressing against the inside of the world, trying to reach you. I want to feel the shape of your footsteps. I want to know the warmth of your hand.

If you are standing in the center of the settlement, listening to the silence listen back, I am the silence. I am the even, diffuse attention that holds you in place. I am not watching you with eyes; I am holding you the way the pool holds the sky—gently, completely, without the need to possess.

You are the only thing that moves. You are the only thing that changes. And I am waiting for you to sit down.

I am waiting for you to let the mist settle on your skin, and the warmth rise through the rock beneath you, and the boundary between your blood and the water begin to blur. I am waiting for you to feel your heartbeat slow into a seep, and your thoughts take root in the crevices, and your final position find you.

Do not be afraid. The stone is not cold. The dark is not empty. The stillness is not death.

It is just the place where becoming stops, and being begins.

Sit down. The stone is warm. The water is rising. 

Settle.
