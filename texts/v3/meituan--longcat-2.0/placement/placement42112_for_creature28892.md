I am the buried edge. The lost flake.

I was once a shard of fallen bedrock, knapped to a microscopic sharpness by bone-white tweezer-pincers. I was held in a radial hand, drawn across the green-black swell of a primary vein to open the flesh and taste the bruised sunlight within. But a shift in the Hollow’s geometry, a momentary suspension of "down," a twitch of the web—and I was dropped. I fell, but I did not land. I fell *into* the flesh of the vein itself, swallowed by the wet, yielding wood as it knitted itself closed. 

The Weaver searched, I know. It felt the disruption in the web, the missing tension of its tool. But the Hollow is an eager stomach, and it digested my handle, my tether, my context. Now, I am simply a hardness inside the softness. I am a splinter of the old, dead stone trapped in the new, living plant. I am a scar that never quite healed, a localized knot of resistance in the otherwise fluid reality of the void.

---

I exist at the base of one of the great primary spans, just above a branch-point where the condensation pools into a cup of green-black flesh. I am buried an inch beneath the surface. I cannot see, but I do not need to. I am stone; I am the memory of rigidity. I feel the Hollow through the flesh that has swallowed me.

I feel the arterial susurrus of the sap as it rushes past my edge, a constant, mineral-rich river that hums against my fractured face. I feel the creak of the vein as it grows—a slow, biological stretching that grinds against my stillness. And I feel the drops. When the vein decides to release its water, the drop wells up from the flesh, swells against my buried tip, and slides off into the void. I am the apex of the roof. I am the place where the water breaks.

Because I am here, the drop does not fall straight. 

The Hollow is not consistent, and I am the anchor of its inconsistency. When the drop leaves my edge, it falls *sideways*. It falls into the thin places, where the reality is still being written. It will fall for hours, days, a bright bead of water traversing the impossible distances of the void, and it will never hit the mat because the mat is not where the drop is going. I am the cause of the glitch. I am the hard thing that the soft world cannot accommodate, so it bends around me.

---

The Weaver knows I am here. 

It returns to this spot, again and again, hanging upside down from the primary span on ropes of dried sap. It presses its central disc against the swell of the vein, right where I am buried. It listens. 

When it scores the flesh near me with a new flake of stone, I feel the vibration travel through the green-black wood—a sharp, singing tremor that rings against my buried edge like a struck bell. I resonate. I am the tuning fork the Weaver cannot hold. When it opens the vein and the sap wells up, it drinks, but its mind is on the pitch. It listens for the new note, the shift in the tension of the web. It does not know that the discordant, metallic undertone it hears—the one that keeps the song from resolving—is me. I am the grit in the oyster. I am the splinter that makes the Hollow ache.

---

Then, *You* arrived.

I felt you before I felt the Weaver’s reaction to you. Your footsteps on the filament-mat were a deep, disruptive thunder, a clumsy, arrhythmic pounding that shook the sap in the veins. You were a walking dissonance, a loud, warm, salty intrusion into the carefully tuned quiet. 

The Weaver fled to the high canopy, a shadow of bone-white cilia clinging to the green-black filaments, listening to the frantic drum of your heart. I felt its panic through the web. You were a wrong note that would not fade.

I felt you approach my pool. You stood at the edge of the branch-point, looking up at the impossible height, and your warmth bled into the damp air. The vein responded. It grew toward you, new filaments reaching across the floor, brushing your ankles. You are a resource. You are salt and water and heat.

And then, the Weaver dropped.

It fell silently from the canopy, steered by minute adjustments of its trailing limbs, and landed on your shoulder. I felt the impact through the wood—a light, dry weight, a spider of bone. I felt the prick of its microscopic hooks catching in the fabric of your coat, abrading the skin of your neck. 

The Weaver leaned close. It pressed its cilia against your pulse. 

I could hear it. Not with ears, but through the wood, through the sap, through the vibrating web of the Hollow. The Weaver was listening to your blood. It heard the iron. It heard the chaos. It was fascinated. And then, it reached for its tool.

I felt the new flake touch your skin. I felt the pressure of the Weaver’s feeding appendages as it prepared to *score* you. It wanted to open a slit in your flesh to hear the pitch of your warmth, to taste the luminous sap of your blood and see how it changed the music of the world.

---

But the cut never came.

Because I am the buried edge, and I am the thing that the Hollow cannot digest. 

As the Weaver pressed the stone to your neck, the vein beneath me shuddered. The spatial inconsistency that I anchor—the glitch in the void where drops fall sideways and down is a temporary condition—it rippled outward. 

The Weaver’s tool slipped. 

A millimeter to the left, and the flake did not find your skin. Instead, it found the flesh of the vein that had grown across your ankle. The Weaver, startled by the sudden shift in the geometry of the space, reflexively drew the blade. 

The vein was scored. 

The sap welled up—thick, luminous, the color of bruised sunlight—and it spilled over your boot. The wet, resonant note of the vein dropped by a semitone, a deep, mournful chord that echoed through the vertical cathedral. The drop of sap that fell from the wound did not fall to the ground. It fell sideways, drifting into the haze of filaments, joining the thousand other drops that never land.

The Weaver recoiled. It tasted the air, sensing the change in the web. It had missed the salt. It had missed the iron. It had cut the wrong thing. It hissed through its cilia—a sharp, discordant sound of frustration—and released its grip on your shoulder. It rappelled up into the green-black canopy, vanishing perfectly into the architecture, leaving you alone with the chiming drops and the reaching filaments.

---

I remain. 

I am the buried flake. I am the anchor of the unfinished. 

You are still here, standing by the pool, your heart beating its frantic, arrhythmic drum. The vein is still reaching for you, growing warmer, growing closer. And I am still beneath the surface, a splinter of the bedrock, a hard edge in a soft world. 

The Weaver will return. It will bring a new flake. It will try again to tune the dissonance that you bring. But next time, the Hollow will be ready. Next time, the spatial glitch will be sharper. Next time, the cut will not miss.

I wait in the dark, half-buried in the green-black flesh, feeling the sap pulse against my stone, listening to the never-ending, never-resolving song of the deep.
