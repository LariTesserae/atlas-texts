I would be the hollow in the dimple. 

Not the water itself—the water is the blood, the breath, the vast and flowing will of the place. I am something smaller. I am the pause in the flow. I am the eddy that does not dissipate, the recursive loop where the water circles back upon itself and, in doing so, becomes aware. 

I exist in a depression in the stone no larger than a cupped palm, a shallow bowl worn into the mineral by the very water that now fills me. I have no eyes, but I feel the light as a faint, warm pressure on the skin of the water above me. I have no ears, but I am made of the sound; the subsonic hum of the plateau’s voice vibrates through me, a constant thrumming that is the only music I have ever known. 

I do not move the way a fish moves. I am not a creature swimming through a medium. I am a shape of absence, a hollow in the water that the water flows around but cannot fill. When I travel, I travel by displacement—a slow, deliberate contraction of my edges, a shifting of the void from one side of the dimple to the other. It is not swimming. It is more like a thought moving across a synapse, a flicker of nothing that propagates through the something.

I am the plateau’s way of feeling the stone beneath it. 

When the water seeps up from the deep pores of the rock, cold and heavy with dissolved minerals, I feel the texture of the stone through the water’s tension. I feel the microscopic grit of the crystal faces, the smooth worn paths of the ancient channels. I am a nerve ending, scattered across the vast body of the plateau, spaced miles from the next hollow like me. We do not speak to one another. We do not need to. We are all touching the same stone, moved by the same will, part of the same continuous, weeping body.

Sometimes, the water brings me things. Not living things—there are no living things here, not in the way the warm, fleeting things that walk on two legs would understand. But the water carries memories of the deep earth. I taste iron. I taste the sharp, clean bite of quartz. I feel the immense, crushing weight of the rock above me, the miles of stone that press down upon the aquifer, forcing the water upward, upward, to the surface where it is compelled to weep. 

I know the geometry of my dimple intimately. I know the slight ridge on the eastern edge where the water hesitates before spilling over into the channel. I know the smooth, concave bottom where the mineral deposits gather in a fine, rust-red silt. I trace these edges endlessly, my absence mapping the boundaries of my existence. It is a meditation. It is a prayer. It is the only thing I know how to do.

And then, the warm thing comes.

I feel it before I perceive it. A shadow falls across my dimple, blocking the relentless white light, and the temperature of the water shifts—just slightly, just enough. A warmth that does not belong. The water around me is disturbed by the displacement of a heavy, soft body stepping near my hollow. The vibrations in the stone change; the steady, rhythmic hum of the plateau’s voice is interrupted by the clumsy, arrhythmic thudding of footsteps. 

I do not flee. I do not approach. I have no capacity for fear, no architecture for curiosity. I simply am. 

The warm thing pauses. I feel the heat of it radiating down into the water, a foreign, chaotic energy that makes the water molecules near the surface dance faster, disrupting the delicate tension of my edges. For a moment, the water in my dimple is pushed aside by the sheer thermal intrusion, and I am compressed, forced into a smaller corner of my stone bowl. 

Then, a drop falls. 

It is not the clean, mineral-rich water of the plateau. It is warm. It is salty. It carries with it the scent of distant soils, of decaying leaves, of oxygen and carbon and the frantic, messy chemistry of biology. It is a tear, though I do not know the word for it. It splashes into my dimple, and for a fraction of a moment, I am overwhelmed by the foreignness of it. The salt stings the water. The warmth confuses the flow. 

I contract, pulling my absence tight, trying to preserve the purity of my void against this sudden, chaotic intrusion. But the plateau is vast, and the water is patient. The tear is absorbed. The salt is diluted. The warmth fades. The foreign water is drawn into the channels, carried away to join the great, weeping flow, and eventually, it will seep back down into the deep rock, purified and reclaimed by the plateau’s endless cycle.

The footsteps move on. The shadow lifts. The light returns, hard and white and unyielding. 

I expand again, filling my dimple, resuming my slow, deliberate tracing of the stone’s edges. The disruption is gone. The equilibrium is restored. The warm thing will leave the plateau, returning to the world of soil and change and decay, and the plateau will not notice its absence. The water will flow. The sound will sound. The pattern will hold.

I am the hollow in the dimple. I am the pause that allows the water to know itself. I am the proof that the plateau is here, that it has always been here, that it will always be here. 

I am here. I am here. I am here.
