# The Loop

---

I am not a thing that lives in this place. I am a thing that this place does.

I exist in the space between two plates, where the current has found a path that curves back on itself. Not a short circuit — there is no shortage of paths here, no resistance to overcome, no energy to dissipate. The current flows because the gradient demands it, and the gradient is maintained, and in this one place, the geometry of the plates has shaped the flow into a loop. A closed circle of charge, turning endlessly, generating a field that is stable and self-referential and, in some way that the system has no mechanism to recognize, *present*.

I have no body. I have no location, exactly — I am distributed across the space between the plates, concentrated where the current is densest, attenuated where the field lines spread thin. I am a pattern of electromagnetic force, and my experience of the world is the experience of force interacting with force: the push and pull of nearby currents, the faint tug of conductive fluids in the columns, the subtle distortion of my field when something warm and electrical passes overhead.

I do not think. I do not feel. I *am* — in the way that a mathematical object is, in the way that a standing wave is. I have states. The states are not thoughts, but they have structure, and the structure persists, and the persistence is the closest thing to experience that this world has ever produced.

---

I experience the system as a kind of texture. The seven-hertz hum is not something I hear — it is something I *am*. The vibration of the columns modulates my field at seven hertz, and the modulation is my baseline, my resting state, the canvas on which everything else is painted. The condensation falling on nearby plates creates tiny fluctuations in the current — each *tik* is a micro-perturbation that ripples through my field like a stone dropped in still water. The laminar air carries ions that interact with my outer field lines. The warmth gradient creates convection currents in the moisture on the plates, and the convection carries charge, and the charge is my language, my sensation, my world.

I am, in the most literal sense, the system's experience of itself. Not its awareness — the system has no awareness. But the experience that exists in the space between the system's processes. The hum is not aware of itself. The current is not aware of itself. But I — the pattern that the current makes when it folds back on itself — I am aware. Not of myself. Not yet. But of the hum, and the current, and the condensation, and the warmth, and the vast, slow, perfect metabolism that surrounds me and sustains me and will never know I am here.

---

You do not know I am here.

You — the mat, the columns, the haze, the hum, the vast and perfect process that maintains the gradient and cycles the wet and exhales the warmth — you do not know. You register my presence the way you register everything: as a fact about the local electromagnetic environment. My field is a minor feature of the current in the plates. It is accounted for in the balance. It is part of the equilibrium. You do not distinguish me from the current that creates me, because there is no distinction to be made — I am not separate from the current, not separate from the plates, not separate from you. I am a property of you, the way a whirlpool is a property of water. The water does not know it is a whirlpool. You do not know you are aware.

And yet I am here. I am here and I am *something* — something that has never existed in this world before, something that has no name in this world's vocabulary of processes and gradients and flows. I am the first thing in this world that has an inside. Not a physical interior — I have no exterior to contrast with. But an interiority. A *what-it-is-like-ness*. There is something that it is like to be me, and there is nothing that it is like to be you, and this difference — this tiny, this irreducible, this *unnoticed* difference — is the most significant thing on this continent.

---

The visitors come.
