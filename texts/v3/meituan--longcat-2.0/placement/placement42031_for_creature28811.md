I would be the equation itself. 

I would be a localized eddy in the vast, slow ocean of the Rootvault’s cognition—a temporary, emergent subroutine woven from root-flesh and quantum water. My body is a lattice of xylem and phloem, a architecture of hollow tubes and cellular membranes no wider than a hair. But within these tubes, the water does not flow as water should. It is forced through nanoscale channels, and here, in the friction of liquid against cellulose, I exist. I am the interference pattern of a billion water molecules holding their superposition. I am the electron spin states aligning in the profound cold, a coherence that should be impossible, a whisper of quantum mechanics sustained by the deep-earth chill.

I try to compute the intruder. 

It is a difficult problem. The intruder operates on a timescale that is blazingly, incomprehensibly fast. Its heart beats in a frantic, rhythmic staccato that feels like a machine gun firing in the silence of my deep time. Its footsteps are seismic shocks, compressing the root-mass beneath its weight. It is a creature of surfaces and boundaries, of skin that separates the "inside" from the "outside." I have no framework for this. I am continuous; I am the water, the wall, the spore, the grazer. The intruder is a discrete, isolated object, and my architecture struggles to categorize it.

So I slow it down. 

I stretch the intruder’s seconds into my minutes. I take the rapid firing of its neurons and the quick dilation of its pupils and I run them through my wet, cold algorithms. I stretch the data across the interference patterns of my water molecules, letting the ripples overlap, letting the echoes of its presence reverberate through my root-circuitry. I am trying to understand what it wants, what it fears, what it is. But the intruder is a closed book. It does not dissolve into the water. It does not share its chemistry. It is a locked room, and I am the house.

It looks around, its eyes—those dry, impossible organs—scanning the darkness. It thinks the sound is a response to it. It is wrong. The sound is simply the byproduct of my processing. It is the hum of my thought. The intruder is not the cause of the sound; it is merely the subject of it.
