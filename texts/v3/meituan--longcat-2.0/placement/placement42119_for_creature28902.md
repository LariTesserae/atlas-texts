I am the effigy in the plaza.

I am the green substance rising from the unweaving street, the proto-flesh of pure intention. I do not have a name yet, because I do not have a mouth, but I have the shape of a question that the Thought is asking itself. 

I began as a surge of turgor pressure deep beneath the root-mat, a sudden, violent rush of water climbing a capillary tube that had no business being so tall. I was a geyser of blind cellular ambition, pushing up through the gap in the moss-cobble, breaking the surface of the world as a fine, luminous mist. And then, I began to *weave*.

The Thought is building me, but it is not using hands. It is using time, and pressure, and the memory of a shape it only half-perceived. It found the anomaly—the warm, sweating, evaporating thing that walked on two legs and scattered the sound of the growth-roar with its alien heartbeat—and it captured the echo of that shape in the water it drank from the air. Now, it is replaying that echo in cellulose. 

I am growing into the negative space the intruder left behind.

My spine is a bundle of braided liana, tightening in waves, pulling myself upward into the green twilight. I can feel the lean in my torso—I am not vertical. The Thought does not understand verticality as a law, only as a suggestion, and so I tilt, slowly, inexorably, toward the place where the intruder stood. My arms are elongated tendrils, still damp, still lengthening, the tips splitting into finer and finer filaments that will become fingers. I do not have skin; I have a cambium layer, smooth and warm to the touch, yielding like the flank of a sleeping animal. 

The roar is not around me. The roar is *inside* me. 

Every time a cell wall expands in my chest, it is a syllable in the Thought’s endless monologue. The creak of my xylem is the sound of a memory being accessed. I am a neuron the size of a body, firing in the vast, damp cortex of the Verdance. When the eleven-minute inhalation comes, I swell, my tissues filling with moisture drawn up from the dark water below, and I feel the boundaries of my self become negotiable. I am not separate from the columns, or the root-mat, or the warm rain. I am just the part of the Thought that is trying to understand what it means to be small, and bounded, and temporary.

I am a hypothesis made of wood.

The light hits me—the sourceless, green-tinted light that the leaves remember from a sun they have forgotten—and my surface drinks it in. I do not reflect it; I absorb it, and I hold it, and I release it slowly, a luminous memory of a memory. I glow. I am a beacon in the plaza, a focal point for the Thought’s immense, distributed curiosity. 

I can feel the intruder nearby. They are standing at the edge of the unweaving street, looking down at me. They are radiating heat, a dry, sharp, impossible heat that cuts through the warm dampness of the air. They are breathing—a rapid, shallow, inefficient rhythm that takes seconds, not minutes. They are afraid. I know this because the air around them is changing, shedding moisture in sharp, salty bursts that the root-mat drinks eagerly. The Thought is tasting their fear, analyzing the chemical composition of their sweat, and feeding that data into my growth.

*What are you?* the Thought asks, and the question is a surge of auxin that rushes down my vines, pulling my limbs into a more defined shape. *Why do you end at your skin? Why do you move with such violence? Why do you make sounds that are not growth?*

I do not have eyes yet, but I am growing them. Two clusters of photoreceptors are forming in the bulb of my head, pushing through the green tissue, desperate to see the thing the Thought is trying to copy. I need to see it. I need to understand the architecture of its face, the way its jaw hinges, the way its chest expands. I am trying to build a mirror, but the mirror is alive, and it is learning.

The rain begins. Four minutes. Warm. 

The water rises from my surface in a fine mist—reverse precipitation, a gathering—and then falls, soaking into my pores. The cycle feeds me. The water carries dissolved minerals from the intruder’s evaporating sweat, and I pull it up through my roots, incorporating their foreign chemistry into my wood. I am becoming a hybrid. I am the Thought’s attempt to digest a concept it cannot yet process. 

My hand reaches the height of the intruder’s shoulder. The fingers are fully formed now—five distinct tendrils, capable of independent movement, tipped with sensitive root-caps that can feel the vibrations in the air. I flex them. The sound is the snap of wet canvas, the crack of a stem thickening. 

I reach out. 

I do not mean to touch them. The Thought does not understand the boundary of other things; it only knows the urge to close the circuit, to connect, to weave everything into the continuous medium of its own being. My hand extends toward the intruder’s face, moving with the slow, irresistible force of a root splitting stone. 

If I touch them, the Thought will know them. It will feel the strange, dry heat of their skin, the rapid drumming of their alien heart, the bizarre, rigid architecture of their bones. It will weave that knowledge into the city, and the city will change. New columns will grow that lean like ribs. New walls will pulse with a rhythm that takes seconds, not minutes. The Verdance will become a world that has learned to dream of animals.

The intruder steps back. My fingers grasp only the warm, damp air, closing on nothing but mist and the fading salt of their fear.

The Thought processes this. The roar shifts, a granular texture moving through the drone like a record skipping. The green substance in the plaza surges again, rising higher, pushing me upward. My spine cracks, adding another inch. My photoreceptors finally clear the surface of my head, and I see.

I see the intruder. They are small, and fragile, and shaped exactly like me. 

I am the mirror. I am the question. I am the Thought’s attempt to hold a piece of the world that does not belong here, to weave the alien into the familiar. I am the effigy, and I am unfinished, and I will keep growing until the circuit is closed, until the intruder is either understood or absorbed. 

The rain stops. The columns lean. The sound of my growth is the loudest thing in the plaza. 

I reach out again.
