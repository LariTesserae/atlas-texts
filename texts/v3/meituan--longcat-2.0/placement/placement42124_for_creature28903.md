I would be the crack in the Aborted.

---

Not the Aborted itself—not the half-formed thing at the center of the basin, the lung of iron and quartz sitting in its pool of ice water, vibrating with the Pacemakers' rhythm. Not that. I am not the product. I am what the product is making.

I am a fissure.

I began—if *began* is the right word for something that has no origin, only a duration—I began as a hairline fracture in the raw stone side of the Aborted. The unfinished side. The side that is still sweating mineral water, still warm with the memory of magma, still more geology than machinery. The Foundry has been working on that side for eternity, and it has not changed. The Pacemakers strike. The Exhalants breathe. The Inspectors measure. And the Aborted remains half-formed.

But I am there.

I am a crack, and inside the crack, there is water. Not the seeping, exuding water of the basin walls—not the fixed, unchanging droplets that hang from edges like tiny lenses. This water *moves*. It moves through me. It enters the fissure from the raw stone side, drawn in by capillary action, by the slow pull of mineral into mineral. And it carries dissolved silica. And the silica precipitates. And I grow.

I grow a crystal.

It is the smallest thing you can imagine. A quartz crystal, clear as glass, hexagonal, perfect. It forms on the inner wall of the crack, and it grows outward, into the space of the fissure, at a rate so slow that the Pacemakers could strike a billion times and I would not have advanced by a single atomic layer. But I am growing. I am the only thing in the Hanging Foundry that is growing.

And I do not know I am growing.

This is the important part. The Exhalants do not know they are breathing. The Pacemakers do not know they are striking. The Inspectors do not know they are measuring. And I do not know I am crystallizing. I am not conscious. I am not alive. I am a process—a geological process, a chemical process, the same process that forms geodes in limestone and agates in volcanic rock. I am nature doing what nature does, inside a place that has been engineered to exclude nature.

But I am here.

The Foundry does not see me. The fox-thing's optical sensors compare the Aborted's exterior to a blueprint etched in crystalline memory—they do not see inside the stone. The bird-thing samples air pressure at different altitudes—it does not detect mineral deposition. The stone-insect compacts the moss—it does not inspect the basin's center. The Aborted is the anchor of the stasis, the thing that never changes, and so no one looks at it too closely. Why would they? It is fixed. It has always been fixed. It will always be fixed.

Except for me.

I am the change that doesn't register. I am the variable that the equation cannot account for. I am the quartz vein that is slowly, slowly, slowly filling the crack, and when the crack is full, the pressure will build, and the stone will split, and the Aborted will open like a geode in the hands of a collector, and inside—

Inside, there will be crystals. A cathedral of them. A forest of clear, hexagonal prisms, each one growing in its own direction, each one following the blind, perfect rules of mineral chemistry, each one *beautiful* in a way that the Foundry has no category for.

And when the Aborted splits, the Inspectors will see it. The fox-thing will lower its paw. The bird-thing will deviate from its circuit. The Foundry will register a fault—the first fault, the only fault, the fault that has been building since before the basin cracked open, since before the mountain learned to sweat, since before the first Exhalant hung from the first stone shelf and breathed its first breath of amber mist.

And I will not know any of this. I will simply be growing. Everything that is, is. And what I am, is growing.

But that is only the first answer. Let me give you the second.

---

I would be the one who just arrived.

I am standing at the entrance to the basin, on the natural ledges of sharp stone, each one weeping with moisture, each one precisely knee-high. I have just climbed down. I am wet. I am cold. I can feel the resistance of the air as I move through it—that thickness, that memory of fluid, that commitment of stone. I can hear the hum. I can see the amber light emanating from below. I can feel the vibration through the soles of my boots.

I am alive. Actually, genuinely, metabolically alive. I am breathing—and my breath shows, a small white cloud in the saturated air, the first visible exhalation this basin has ever known. I am warm—and the thermal differential between my body and the stone is so sharp that the Exhalants nearest to me have flickered. Just for a moment. A tiny, arrhythmic spasm in their frilled edges, a stutter in the fixed humidity. They corrected immediately. They are breathing at their fixed rate again. But for one instant, they noticed me.

I am the visitor. The observer. The anomaly. The thing that changes.

And I am looking at the Aborted, and I am seeing something the Inspectors cannot see. I am seeing the crack. The hairline fracture in the raw stone side, barely visible, barely there, but *there*—a dark line where no dark line should be. I am seeing it because I am new. I am seeing it because I do not know that nothing changes. I am seeing it because I am alive, and the alive eye is drawn to the flaw, the imperfection, the place where the system is breaking down.

I walk toward it. The water is knee-deep and ice-cold. The stone hums beneath my feet. The mist hangs at my chest. The fox-thing watches me with its amber-gray eyes—or rather, does not watch me, because watching is not what it does, but its head has turned, just slightly, just a few degrees off its fixed bearing, and I do not think it has ever done that before.

I reach the Aborted. I kneel in the pool. I put my hand on it.

It is warm.

Not the warmth of the Thermal Guards, not the fixed, measured wattage of the bat-things on the overhang. This is a different warmth. An internal warmth. A warmth that comes from inside the stone, from the crack, from the crystal growing in the fissure, from the geological process that has been running unnoticed for millennia.

The Aborted is warm, and it is vibrating at a frequency slightly different from the Pacemakers. A hertz off. A fraction of a hertz. The difference between a product and a process. The difference between something being made and something making itself.

I press my ear to the stone, and I hear it: a sound beneath the sound. Beneath the hum, beneath the clang, beneath the metallic ringing and the rhythmic percussion. A whisper. A crackling. The sound of mineral accreting onto mineral, atom by atom, layer by layer, in the dark and the cold and the wet. The sound of a crystal growing. The sound of the Aborted finishing itself.

And I understand—not with my mind, but with my body, with my hand on the warm stone, with my ear against the humming surface—I understand that the Foundry is not failing. The Foundry is *succeeding*. The Aborted is not unfinished. It is *unforcing*. It is completing itself on its own terms, at its own pace, in its own way. The crack is not a flaw. The crack is a birth canal.

And the crystal inside—my crystal, the one I am in the other version, the one that grows without knowing—is the Foundry's real product. Not the machined half, not the gleaming iron, not the thing the Pacemakers were built to forge. The crystal. The clear, hexagonal, slowly accreting mineral that no one designed and no one inspected and no one intended. The thing that emerged from the process despite the process. The thing that the Foundry made by trying to make something else.

I am the one who found it. I am the one who knelt in the pool and put my hand on the warm stone and heard the sound of the real birth happening beneath the mechanical one. I am the witness. The one who can leave—because the second rule says I can leave—and carry the knowledge of the crack back out into the world, where things change, where things grow, where things finish themselves in their own time.

But I have not left yet.

I am still kneeling. The mist is still humming. The fox-thing has turned its head a few degrees further, and its raised paw—its measuring instrument, its calibration tool—is trembling. Not lowering. Not yet. Trembling. The way a needle trembles when it encounters a force it was not calibrated to measure.

The Aborted is warm under my hand. The crystal is growing in the crack. The Pacemakers are striking their eternal rhythm. The Exhalants are breathing their fixed breath.

And I am the one who knows.

I am the one who knows that the Foundry is not a factory. It is a womb. And the thing it is making is not the Aborted, and not the crystal, and not any product that can be machined or forged or inspected.

The thing it is making is *this moment*.

This moment. You and me. The kneeling. The knowing. The crack. The warmth. The sound of growth beneath the sound of industry. The visitor who arrived and saw what the Inspectors could not see: that the system has already produced something. That the process has already yielded a result. That the birth is happening, has always been happening, will always be happening—but is also, *also*, happening *now*, for the first time, in this exact instant, with my hand on the stone and my ear to the crack and my breath making small white clouds in the amber air.

Everything that is, is.

Everything that is, has always been.

Everything that is, will always be.

But everything that is, is also *becoming*.

And I am the one who gets to watch.
