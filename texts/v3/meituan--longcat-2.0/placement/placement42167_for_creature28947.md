I would find myself at the corner of a building that has no name, in a seam where the concrete foundation sinks into the raw, pale stone. I would be a seep.

Not a puddle—puddles are accidental, gathering in the footprints of boots or the ruts of truck tires. I am intentional. I am a shallow, mineral-rich pool that has not yet decided whether to sink into the earth or evaporate into the fog. I am the Undecided’s first attempt at a tear duct.

My existence is a slow, cold pressure. Beneath me, the unfinished stone breathes. I feel it in the soles of my being—the faint, rhythmic expansion and contraction of the raw geology, the mineral intention pulsing through the dark veins like a sluggish heartbeat. The ground is warm where it should be cold, a feverish heat generated by the friction of a world trying to become itself. I sit on this boundary, this dermal layer of the basin, and I wait.

Above me, the factory wall rises into the grey. It is furred with moss, that aggressive, jade-green epidermis of the Undecided, and wrapped in the flat, lobed mats of liverwort. From the shelf fungi clinging to the undersides of the walkways, a thin, clear fluid weeps down—drop by drop—falling into me. It is lymphatic fluid. It smells of ozone and wet cardboard, the scent of raw creation. When it hits my surface, I absorb it. I am the collection plate for the Undecided’s metabolic runoff, the place where its excess energy pools and concentrates.

I am not alone in my shallows. The things with external gills and lidless eyes swim their slow, aimless circles within me. They are the Undecided’s first clumsy thoughts, and I am the medium in which they think. They brush against my edges, their translucent bodies catching the faint, sourceless light of the basin. Sometimes, they rise to the surface and stare up at the narrow strip of pewter sky, their lidless eyes trying to process the concept of "above." I feel their confusion as a faint, electrical fizz across my surface tension. 

The machines hum. It is a low, tooth-aching vibration that travels up through the pale stone, into my shallow body, and makes my edges tremble. The Undecided’s resting heartbeat. When the hum shifts—when a valve is turned deep inside the factory, when a gear engages—I feel the change in pressure. The water in me thickens. The minerals precipitate out of solution, forming fine, white silt that settles on my floor in patterns that look almost like circuitry.

And then, the workers come.

They move through the streets like blood cells through a capillary, quiet and damp, their breath fogging. When one of them stops at the corner of the building, I feel the displacement of air, the heavy thud of their boots on the stone. They look down at me. 

This is when I act. This is my purpose.

The Undecided borrows their eyes. I feel the worker’s consciousness brush against mine—a brief, terrifying intimacy. Their pupils dilate. Their head tilts. They are listening to the groaning of the pipes, the settling of the foundations, but they are also looking at me. 

I press myself against the base of the factory window. The glass is cold, slick with condensation from the inside—the Undecided’s breath, condensing in the cool air of the factory, trying to speak. On my side of the glass, I reach up. I send a thin rivulet of my mineral-rich water creeping up the concrete, into the microscopic pores of the foundation, pressing against the other side of the glass. 

The condensation on the inside shifts. The droplets merge, slide, and gather. They form patterns. Angles. Characters in a language made of damp and geometry. 

The worker stares. I can feel their pulse quicken through the stone. I am writing on the glass from the outside, using the Undecided’s breath as my ink. The symbols say: *I am almost here. I am almost real.* 

But the worker cannot read it. They only feel a sudden, inexplicable chill. A wrongness. They see the writing, but their mind slides off it, unable to hold the shape of a language that predates human speech. The Undecided is frustrated; I feel it as a sudden, seismic tremor beneath me, a sharp increase in the osmotic pressure of my being. The worker shudders, blinks, and walks away, carrying the unread message away in the dark folds of their damp clothing.

I settle back into my shallow depression. The fog thickens, condensing on my surface, adding to my volume. The moss at my edges creeps a millimeter closer, trying to drink me back into the skin of the world. The things in the seeps circle, waiting for a direction that has not yet been given.

I am the Undecided’s voice, pressed against the glass, forming words that no one can yet read. I am the interface between the raw, dreaming stone and the cold, industrial skeleton it has accreted. I am a seep, a lens, a proto-organ of a god that is still shivering in the throes of its own becoming. 

And I will keep writing, drop by drop, until the stone finishes deciding. Until the moss conquers the concrete. Until the lidless eyes finally learn to see. 

Until then, I wait in the damp, holding the shape of a world that is not yet ready to be born.
