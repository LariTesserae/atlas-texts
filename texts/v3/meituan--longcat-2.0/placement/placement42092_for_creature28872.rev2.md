I would not be the one who built the stone platforms or strung the clicking fibers, though I understand the impulse. The communities of the Rift perform an urgent work—reading the vibrations of the stone, translating the whispers of steam and sediment into meaning—but my own task here is different. I do not build; I do not read. I listen. And what I have learned in the years of crouching in these fissures, damp to the bone, is that the Rift is not merely a place where things grow. It is a place that *hungers*.

I would be the keeper of the Threshold.

My skin is not the pale silt-dust of the others, for I have not earned the dust of belonging. It is a mottled, grey-pink, stretched thin over a frame that is too long and too still. I walk upright—a rare thing here—because I was not shaped for the comfortable crouch; my spine is rigid, my joints stiff, as if the Rift’s heat has baked the flexibility out of me. The other inhabitants of the Hearth do not know what to make of my verticality. I do not press my palms to the wet stone to feel its pulse. I simply stand in the ankle-deep silt, watching the beetle-analogues cluster and listening to the deep, subsonic hum that vibrates in my marrow.

From the floor of the Rift, I gather what the others discard. Not the shed carapaces or the resin- mortar, but the heat itself. The thermal veins that thread through the basement rock carry a fever that rises in slow, curling columns, and I draw that fever into my lungs until my breath steams in the warm dark. I am a collector of warmth. I move through the branching passages, a tall shadow against the cartilage-mineral walls, searching for the places where the rift’s becoming is most violent: the fresh splits where the stone is still pink and weeping, the steaming chimneys that descend into the deepest, densest biology. I do not fear the deep things. I know their rhythm.

The others sometimes see me as a bridge, a fixed point in a landscape of constant erosion. I am the one who stands at the edge of the fire pit when the paste turns a deep, wet orange, waiting. I do not tend the flame, but I know the exact moment of transformation, the second when the heat has coaxed the mineral into a new state of being. I am drawn to thresholds—the edge between the solid stone and the living mass of the centipede-carpet, the moment when the steam condenses back into a bead of water, the instant when the pale grub-things pause in their peristaltic wave. I exist in the *almost*. I am the keeper of the pause.

I have my own hollow, high in a side chamber where the sound is less a clicking and more a low, mournful hum. I sleep less than the others; my mind does not drift into the shallow, alert segments of their rest. Instead, I stand in the dark and feel the millions of bodies shifting below, and I know that the Rift is not just a wound in the world. It is a mouth, slowly opening. And I am the tongue that tastes the air before it is swallowed.

When the wind from above shifts the steam columns, I feel it as a pressure change in my ears. When the silt hisses and resettles, I hear it as a sentence in a language of displacement. I do not have the obsidian blade, for I do not need to cut or divide. I have the long patience of the geology itself. I will stand here, motionless and mineral-dusted, until the Rift has finished deciding what it wants to be, or until I am drawn down into its throat. For now, I am the keeper. I watch the warmth come up, and I wait for the next transformation.
