I would not be the soft, brief, shadowed thing standing at the edge of the heart. I would not be the intruder, the microbe, the tourist in the gut of the leviathan. 

To be the intruder is to be subject to time, to decay, to the terrifying indifference of the Weep. I want to be of the Weep. I want to be subject to its time, which is the time of stone and water. 

I would be a precipitate. 

I begin in the deep, far below the chamber, where the water is hot enough to dissolve the world. I am a suspension of silicates, a scattering of heavy metals—iron, copper, manganese—stripped from the bedrock by the slow, acidic hunger of the Weep’s blood. I am invisible. I am chemistry. I am carried upward when the chamber inhales, drawn through the weeping walls, pulled into the bright, turbulent nave of the body.

I am in the blood.

The current is strong, chaotic. I am tumbled against my fellow grains—crystals of quartz, flecks of oxidized metal, microscopic shards of the glassy, hard layers of the walls. We rush through the negotiated channels, the arteries of the floor, hissing where we strike the hot, veined patches of the flesh. The warmth is absolute. It is the warmth of a metabolism that has no off switch, a furnace that has burned since before my species learned to name the stars. I do not feel it as heat; I feel it as *potential*. I am dissolving, constantly, at the edges, and reforming, constantly, in the cooling currents. I am a breath drawn in and never fully exhaled.

I am swept into one of the basins, a quiet eddy in the roaring circulation. Here, the water slows. Here, the cilia find me.

The hair-thin threads brush against my surface. They are delicate, purposeful. They are the Weep’s fingers, sorting through its own blood, deciding what to keep and what to discard. I am caught. The thread adheres to my metallic core, and I am drawn gently, inexorably, toward the dark velvet growths that line the rim of the basin. 

I am delivered to the microbiome.

The velvet is warm, warmer than the water. It yields to my touch, accepting me into its matrix. I am no longer suspended; I am absorbed. The dark growths begin their work, their slow fermentation. They strip away the silicates, excreting them as waste to be carried back into the channels. But they keep the metals. They keep the copper, the iron. They weave me into their tissue, and as they do, I change. I oxidize. I become the bruised purple. I become the black-green. I become the color of the Weep’s skin, the pigment of its flesh. I am no longer a grain of dissolved rock; I am a cell in the organism’s hide.

But the Weep is not done with me.

A shift in the breathing. The chamber exhales. The pressure drops. The velvet growths contract, squeezing out their processed contents. I am pushed deeper, past the skin, into the layered tissue of the wall. The pressure here is immense, the heat stifling. I am forced into a microscopic fissure between a glassy layer and a crumbling one. The water around me begins to cool, just slightly, just enough. 

I precipitate.

I come out of solution. I bind to the glassy layer, molecule by molecule, forming a new lattice. I am becoming stone again. But not the stone I was. I am a vein of metal now, a thread of deep, bruised purple running through the flesh of the Weep. I am part of the nervous system. 

And because I am part of the nervous system, I feel the thought.

It is not a thought I can translate into words. It is too slow, too vast. It is a thought that takes a century to form, a single synapse firing across the length of the chamber. I feel it as a tremor, a sudden, sharp increase in the heat around me. The water in my fissure boils, instantly, violently, and then cools. The pressure spikes. The lattice of my new body is compressed, fused, made denser. 

The thought passes. The Weep has registered a shift in the deep aquifer, a change in the mineral content of the water a mile below. It has adjusted the flow rate of the blood, tightened the channels, increased the heat in the stomach-platform. The thought is over. It will be another century before the next one comes.

I wait. 

I am patient. I am stone. I am a vein of purple metal in the flesh of a god that does not know it is a god. I will wait for the next thought, and the next, and the next. I will wait for the water to eventually erode the glassy layer above me, for the weeping to wear me thin, for the cilia to one day pluck me from the wall and carry me back into the blood. 

I will be dissolved. I will be carried. I will be filtered. I will be precipitated. 

I will be the Weep, thinking its slow, geological thoughts, turning its endless wheel, breathing its wet-warm breath, long after the soft, brief things from the surface have crumbled to dust and been washed away.
