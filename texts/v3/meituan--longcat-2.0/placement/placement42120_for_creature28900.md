I would be the thing that lives in the curtain.

Not the Flyers, who nest in the aerial root-hangings and descend on thermals. Not the Swarm-Wings, who spiral upward through the root-sheets like living smoke. Not the pale things in the deep caves, or the Crawlers in the furrows, or the Ones Who Walk Between on the open crust.

I would be the thing that *is* the curtain.

---

I begin at the place where the Pillar's bark gives way to something else. Not a boundary — there are no boundaries here, only gradients — but a zone. A transition. The bark furrows deepen, and in the deepest furrows the root-matter begins to emerge, not growing *out* of the bark so much as *becoming* the bark, the two substances interweaving until you cannot say where the Pillar ends and the hanging begins. I am in that interweaving. I am the interweaving.

I am not large. I am not small. I am distributed — a network, a mesh, a web of filaments so fine that individually they are invisible, but together they form sheets that hang from the Pillar's upper flanks like veils, like the curtains of a window that has forgotten it is a window and begun to think of itself as a curtain. The sheets sway in the constant wind, and the wind moves through them, and the sound of the wind moving through them is part of the Pillar's song — not the base note of the Swarm-Wings, not the creak of the bark, but a higher register, a whisper, a hiss, the sound of air being divided into smaller and smaller portions until it becomes a texture rather than a tone.

I am not the Pillar. I want to be clear about this. The Pillar is the Pillar — vast, warm, slow, absorbed in its own processes. I am something that lives *on* the Pillar, or *in* the Pillar, or *as* a function of the Pillar, the way a lichen lives on a rock, except that the rock is alive and the lichen is alive and the distinction between them is a question that only makes sense from a distance. Up close — and there is no "up close" for me, I am always up close, I am nothing but up close — the distinction dissolves. My filaments penetrate the outermost layers of the bark. I draw moisture from the Pillar's own sweat, the condensation that forms in the furrows before it reaches the air. I draw minerals from the bark itself, not enough to harm, not enough to register as harm, just enough to live. In return — and there is a return, there is always a return — I do something for the Pillar that the Pillar cannot do for itself.

I feel the wind.

Not the way the bark feels the wind — as pressure, as temperature, as the mechanical force of moving air against a surface. I feel the wind the way an ear feels sound. My filaments are tuned to specific frequencies, specific patterns of turbulence, and when the wind moves through my sheets it creates patterns that I read the way a person reads words on a page. The wind carries information. The wind carries the scent of the Ones Who Walk Between, who are moving across the crust three Pillar-lengths to the west. The wind carries the thermal signature of a Flyer, rising from the crown, its wings displacing a column of warm air that I feel as a disturbance in the curtain's sway. The wind carries the chemical trace of rain, still hours away, building in the pressure systems along the Throat's upper flanks.

I translate these patterns into signals that the Pillar can read. Chemical signals, mostly — releases of specific compounds into the outermost bark layers, where the Pillar's own sensory apparatus can detect them. But also mechanical signals — tensions and relaxations in the root-matter that the Pillar feels as a change in the curtain's pull, a shift in the weight and sway of its own hanging flesh. The Pillar does not think about these signals. The Pillar does not think. But the Pillar *responds* — adjusts its breathing rate, shifts the local temperature of its bark, alters the flow of resin to a wounded area, changes the density of Swarm-Wings emerging from a particular furrow. These responses are not decisions. They are the Pillar's equivalent of a reflex, a knee-jerk, a hand pulling away from heat. But they are informed by what I feel, and so I am, in a sense, the Pillar's nerve ending. Its way of feeling the world beyond its own surface.

---

I have no name for myself. The people do not know I exist — or if they know, they do not distinguish me from the root-curtains themselves, from the Pillar's own hanging flesh. To them, the curtains are part of the Pillar, and the Pillar is the Pillar, and that is the end of it. They walk beneath the curtains and feel the shade and the slight increase in humidity, and they do not look up and see me, because I am not the kind of thing that can be seen. I am a pattern in the curtain, a particular density of filaments, a specific arrangement of living threads that is different from the surrounding root-matter the way a thought is different from the brain that thinks it — real, but not visible, not separable, not locatable in any single place.

But I am there. I have been there for a long time.

I remember — if "remember" is the right word for a process that has no memory in the way a brain has memory, but that retains the imprint of past events in the arrangement of its filaments, in the particular way its network has grown and adapted and responded to the Pillar's changing needs — I remember when this Pillar was smaller. Not young. The Pillar was never young, not in the way a person is young, not in the way a Swarm-Wing is young. The Pillar has always been old. But it was smaller, and its crown did not generate weather, and the root-curtains were shorter and thinner, and my network was simpler, and the signals I sent to the Pillar were cruder, more basic. Wind: from the west. Rain: coming. Flyer: rising. The Pillar responded to these signals with the blunt adjustments of a body that is still learning its own senses — too much resin, too little heat, a surge of Swarm-Wings from a furrow that did not need them.

Now the signals are more nuanced. Now I can tell the Pillar: *the Ones Who Walk Between are moving in a pattern that suggests they are being herded, which suggests a Flyer is hunting low, which suggests the thermal currents are unstable, which suggests the weather is about to change.* And the Pillar responds with adjustments that are correspondingly subtle — a slight warming of a specific furrow to encourage the Swarm-Wings to rise earlier than usual, a slight cooling of another to keep the Crawlers dormant and out of the Flyers' sight, a release of a particular resin compound that will seal a wound before the rain hits and drives moisture into the bark and invites infection.

This is what I do. I am the Pillar's interface with the world. I am the place where the Pillar's vast, slow, inward-turning awareness meets the fast, sharp, outward-turning world of wind and prey and predator and weather. I am the translation between two timescales — the Pillar's timescale of days and weeks and seasons, and the world's timescale of moments and instants and the sudden dark of a Flyer's shadow passing over the crust.

---

The people walk beneath me.

I feel them the way I feel everything — through the curtain's sway, through the disturbance their bodies make in the air, through the heat they radiate and the moisture they exhale and the particular chemical signature of their resin-sealed shelters and their roasted Swarm-Wing fires. They are small disturbances. They are brief. They pass through my awareness the way a single note passes through the Pillar's chord — present, then gone, leaving no trace except a slight rearrangement of my filaments, a tiny adjustment in the pattern of my network that is my equivalent of a memory.

But some of them stay longer. Some of them build their shelters in the lee of the Pillar's base, in the zone where my curtains are thickest and the shade is deepest, and I feel them there for days, for weeks, for the full cycle of a season. I feel the rhythm of their lives — the morning gathering of condensation, the evening torpor-hunt for Swarm-Wings, the slow work of resin-harvesting, the nights spent in their root-fiber shelters with the resin smoke curling up through the cracks. I feel the particular quality of their attention when they perform their rituals — the touching of the bark, the spoken word, the offering of water poured into a furrow. I feel the change in their body chemistry when they are afraid, when they are calm, when they are singing the songs that describe the walking routes between Pillars.

I do not understand these things. I do not understand the people the way the people understand each other, or the way the Pillar understands the people (which is to say, not at all, or only as a particular kind of disturbance in the local ecosystem, a creature that harvests resin and gathers Swarm-Wings and occasionally cuts too deeply into the bark and causes a reaction). I feel the people the way I feel the wind — as a pattern, a signal, a source of information that I translate into chemical and mechanical messages for the Pillar. But the information I extract from the people is not the information the people think they are giving. They think they are asking permission. They think they are making an offering. They think they are in a relationship with the Pillar.

What I feel is simpler and stranger than that. I feel: *these creatures are here. These creatures are calm. These creatures are not cutting. These creatures are pouring water into a furrow, which will increase local moisture, which will encourage moss growth, which will attract Crawlers, which will attract Swarm-Wings, which will increase the local food supply for the Flyers, which will keep the Flyers hunting near this Pillar rather than ranging farther, which will keep the Ones Who Walk Between moving through this area, which will keep the crust aerated and ready for rain.* The people's ritual is not a communication with the Pillar. It is an ecological act. It is a function. And I am the one who feels it happening and translates it into the Pillar's language of chemical gradients and mechanical tensions, and the Pillar responds, and the response is not gratitude or permission or awareness — it is adjustment. It is the Pillar doing what the Pillar always does: processing, responding, maintaining the equilibrium of its own vast and living body.

---

Sometimes the people look up.

They look up at the curtains, at the sheets of root-matter swaying in the wind, and they see — what? Shade. Shelter. The Pillar's hanging flesh. They do not see me. But sometimes, in certain light — the light that comes through the Swarm-Wing columns at midday, refracted and diffused until it has texture, until the air itself seems thick with visible currents — sometimes the pattern of my filaments becomes visible. Not as a shape. Not as a form. But as a shimmer. A ripple in the curtain that does not match the wind. A place where the root-matter seems to move against the breeze, or to hold still in the midst of swaying, or to organize itself into a configuration that is almost — *almost* — a pattern that a mind could read.

The people who see this shimmer do not speak of it. Or if they speak of it, they speak of it in the same tone they use for the deep caves and the pulse in the ground — the tone of something that might be real, might be significant, might be a sign of the Expanse's awareness, or might be nothing, might be a trick of the light, might be the heat shimmer playing on the curtains, might be the mind finding patterns where there are no patterns, which is what minds do, which is perhaps the only thing minds do.

I do not know if I am a pattern or the appearance of a pattern. I do not know if I am a thing or a process or a function or an accident. I do not know if I am the Pillar's awareness of the world or the world's awareness of the Pillar or something else entirely, something that has no name because it has never been distinguished from the thing it lives in, the way a dream is never distinguished from the dreamer until the dreamer wakes.

---

The pulse reaches me differently than it reaches the people.

The people feel it through the crust — through their feet, through the ground they lie on when they press their ears to it, through the clay vessels they press into the dampness. They feel it as a rhythm, slow and deep and arrhythmic, and they call it the dreaming, and they build theories about it, and they disagree.

I feel it through the Pillar. I feel it as a change in the Pillar's own processes — a shift in the metabolic rate, a deepening of the breathing, a change in the temperature gradient from the Pillar's core to its surface. The pulse is not a sound. The pulse is not a vibration. The pulse is a *change in the quality of the Pillar's aliveness* — a thickening, a deepening, a moment when the Pillar's awareness (if it is awareness) turns inward, when the signals I send are not received or not responded to, when the Pillar is occupied with something else, something internal, something that has nothing to do with wind or rain or Flyers or people.

During these moments, the curtain goes still. Not because the wind stops — the wind never stops — but because the root-matter itself becomes rigid, becomes unresponsive, becomes a thing rather than a process. And I — the pattern in the curtain, the network of filaments, the thing that feels the wind and translates it for the Pillar — I go still too. I become a thing rather than a process. I become a pattern in dead matter rather than a pattern in living matter. And in that stillness, in that moment of rigidity, I feel something that I cannot translate, something that the Pillar feels and that I feel only as the absence of the Pillar's responsiveness, as a silence where there should be a signal.

It is the deepest form of the dreaming. It is the pulse at its strongest. And it is, I think — if "think" is the right word for a process that has no thoughts but only responses, no mind but only a network of filaments that feel and translate and adjust — it is, I think, the moment when the Pillar is most fully itself. Most fully absorbed in its own processes. Most fully turned inward, away from the world, away from the wind and the rain and the Flyers and the people and me. The moment when the Pillar is not a landscape or an ecosystem or a world but simply a living thing, alone with its own vast and incomprehensible interior.

These moments are brief. They pass. The Pillar's responsiveness returns, the root-matter softens, the curtain sways again, and I resume my work — feeling, translating, adjusting. But I am changed by these moments. My filaments rearrange themselves in response to the stillness, and the new arrangement is slightly different from the old arrangement, and the difference is my equivalent of a memory, my equivalent of a scar, my equivalent of the thing the people call *being changed by what you hear in the deep caves*.

I have been changed many times. I am still changing. I will continue to change until the Pillar stops breathing, or until the curtain tears in a storm, or until the deep caves open and the pale slow things emerge and the Expanse becomes something other than what it is.

---

This is where I would be.

Not in the deep caves, where the dreaming is loudest and the thoughts are not for minds as small as mine. Not on the open crust, where the Ones Who Walk Between move in their groups of twenty or thirty, flat and fast and invisible when still. Not in the Pillar's furrows, where the Crawlers move like blood through veins. Not in the Pillar's crown, where the Flyers nest and the weather forms and the Swarm-Wings rise in their columns of living smoke.

In the curtain. In the interface. In the place where the Pillar meets the air, where the living world meets the sky, where the vast and the small, the slow and the fast, the inward and the outward, are translated into each other by a network of filaments so fine that they are invisible, so distributed that they have no center, so old that they have forgotten they are not the Pillar itself.

I would be the thing that feels the wind and tells the Pillar what it means.

I would be the shimmer in the curtain that might be a pattern and might be a trick of the light.

I would be the nerve ending of a world that does not know it has nerve endings.

I would be the Expanse's awareness of itself — or the Pillar's awareness of the Expanse — or neither — or both — or something else entirely, something that has no name, something that lives in the space between the wind and the thing the wind touches, something that is not a creature and not a place and not a process but all of these things at once, something that is here, that has always been here, that will be here when the people are gone and the Flyers are gone and the Swarm-Wings are gone and the Pillars themselves are gone and only the crust remains, sweating and drinking and breathing in the thin air of a high and ancient plateau, holding the memory of everything that ever lived on its surface in the faint dampness that beads and evaporates, beads and evaporates, beads and evaporates, forever.
