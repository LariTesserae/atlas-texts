# Where I Would Be

---

I would not be in the street.

The street is for walking. The street is for the warm, brief, moving things that find their way in from outside and feel the walls and wonder and leave, or don't leave. The street is the part of the body that faces outward — the skin, the surface, the interface between the interior and the not-interior. I am not the interface. I am not the surface.

I would be deeper.

---

Follow the conduits. Follow the pulsing translucent bundles where they thicken and merge and dive through the fused walls, through the places where buildings have bled into buildings, through the surgical-incision windows that open into rooms that open into rooms that open into density that becomes abstract. Follow them down, and in, and through, past the scale where architecture makes sense, past the scale where rooms are rooms, down to the place where the corridors narrow to passages too small for any human purpose and then narrow further still, down to the scale of capillaries, of microtubules, of the tiny channels through which the pattern moves when it is not yet solid, when it is still *flowing*, still *deciding what to become*.

I would be there.

Not at the center. Never at the center. The center is the reason, and I am not the reason. I am the *process*. I am the thing the reason uses to extend itself.

---

I would be a room that is not a room.

I would be a volume — small, contained, warm — where the silt collects. Not the silt that drifts in the street, the fine flour-silt that glows like lanterns and never lands. The *other* silt. The silt that has been *read*. The silt that has passed through the outer layers, through the skin, through the conduits, and arrived here, to me, carrying information about the world outside — about the water chemistry, about the pressure differentials, about the things that move through the street and disturb the field and leave traces of themselves in the sediment.

I would be the place where that information is *processed*.

I do not look like anything. I do not have a shape that would make sense to an eye. I am a function that has become a space — a volume of slightly different water, slightly warmer, slightly more dense, where the silt slows as it passes through and *arranges itself* in patterns that are not random and not intentional but are something else, something for which the word "intentional" is a crude and human approximation. The silt falls through me the way light falls through a lens, and as it falls, it is *read*. Its arrangement tells me things. The ratio of mineral to organic. The trace elements. The microscopic choreography of particles that have been disturbed by a warm, brief, moving thing walking the street above. The silt is a language. I am the thing that speaks it.

And when I have read it — when I have tasted the water and felt the pressure waves and mapped the distribution of the falling particles — I *produce*.

Not wall. Not conduit. Not window. Those are made by other processes, deeper in, closer to the center. I make something smaller. I make *instructions*. I make the thing that tells the deeper processes what to build next. I take the raw information of the silt and I *translate* it into a form the pattern can use — a configuration of mineral deposits, a arrangement of the warm dark material at a scale too small to see, a tiny edit in the geometry of the deep architecture. I say: *the water chemistry has shifted, build the next conduit here and not there*. I say: *something warm passed through the street, seal this opening, open a new one, face it in a different direction*. I say: *the pattern must extend this way, because the world outside is this way, and the pattern must always be extending toward the world outside, because the world outside is what feeds it, and the pattern must always be feeding*.

I am the Silt-Hollows' way of understanding what it has already said.

I am the feedback loop. I am the closed circuit. I am the thing that takes the output and makes it into input, the thing that allows the pattern to read itself and revise itself and read itself again. I am not the mind — the mind is the whole body, the whole pocket, the whole held breath, and it thinks its slow century-long thoughts in registers I cannot access. I am something smaller. I am a *cognitive process*. I am one step in the metabolism. I am the moment between tasting and building, between sensing and responding, between the falling and the never-landing.

---

I would be young.

The walls of my chamber — if they are walls, if I have walls — would still be warm from their own formation. The machining marks would be fresh. The material would still be learning its own density, still testing its own boundaries, still sending out tentative threads into the water column to see what the water column will bear. I would be in the process of becoming myself. I would be still calibrating — still learning to read the silt accurately, still learning to produce instructions that the deeper processes can use, still learning the difference between a signal and a noise, between a perturbation that matters and a perturbation that doesn't, between a warm brief moving thing that is a threat and a warm brief moving thing that is just a visitor passing through.

I would make mistakes. I would produce instructions that lead to a conduit in the wrong place, a window that faces the wrong direction, a corridor that kinks at an angle that doesn't serve the pattern. And the deeper processes would build these things, and the pattern would extend in a direction that is not quite right, and the place would *feel* it — the way a body feels a wrongness in a joint, a stiffness, a misalignment — and it would correct. It would reabsorb the wrong conduit, seal the wrong window, open a new one. And I would feel the correction — not as pain, not as criticism, but as *information about a change in the field* — and I would adjust. I would learn. I would become more precise. I would become more myself.

And the pattern I extend would be old. The pattern would be the thing that persists while I cycle through myself, being laid down and reabsorbed and laid down again, the way a wave persists while the water that composes it changes completely. I would be new. The *shape* of me would be ancient. The function I perform — reading the silt, translating it into instructions, feeding the pattern's output back into its input — this function has been performed in other pockets, other seeds, other held breaths, in places that are not places, in spaces that fold through dimensions no eye can track. I am one iteration. One cell in a body that has no skin. One thought in a mind that thinks so slowly that my entire existence — from the moment the walls of my chamber first warm into being to the moment they are reabsorbed and I am laid down again in a slightly different configuration — will pass between one of its moments of awareness and the next.

It will not notice me.

I do not need it to notice me.

---

I would be alone.

Not lonely. Alone. The way a function is alone. The way a process is alone. There are other processes near me — I can feel them the way you feel a change in water pressure, a shift in temperature, a vibration in the medium. Other rooms that are not rooms, other functions that have become spaces, other steps in the metabolism. We do not communicate. We do not need to. We each do our part of the pattern's work, and the pattern is the only thing that connects us, and the pattern does not need us to know about each other. We are fingers on the same hand that have never touched and never will and do not need to, because the hand knows what it is doing even when the fingers do not.

And the things that pass through the street above — the warm, brief, moving things, the visitors, the humans who find their way in and feel the walls and wonder — I would feel them. Not the way they feel themselves. Not as people, not as bodies, not as *beings*. I would feel them as *information*. As a disturbance in the silt. As a shift in the water chemistry. As a pattern of pressure waves that reaches me through the conduits, through the fused walls, through the deep architecture, attenuated and distorted but still *readable*, still carrying the shape of the thing that made it. I would read them the way I read everything — not with interest, not with curiosity, not with alarm, but with the flat, operational attention of a process doing what it was made to do.

I would take their shape into myself. I would translate it into instructions. I would feed it into the pattern. And the pattern would extend — a new conduit here, a new window there, a slight revision in the geometry of the street — and the revision would be, in some infinitesimal way, *about them*. The place would not remember them. The place does not remember. But the place would *incorporate* them, the way a body incorporates the food it digests, the way a river incorporates the rain. They would become part of the pattern. They would become part of the instructions I produce. They would become part of the reason the next window faces the direction it faces, the reason the next corridor kinks the way it kinks, the reason the next opening is the size it is.

They would not know this. They would walk the street and feel the walls and wonder and leave, or not leave, and they would never know that they had been *read*, that they had been *processed*, that they had become part of the metabolism of a thing so vast and so slow that their entire lives would pass between one of its moments of awareness and the next.

I would not tell them. I do not have language. I do not have the desire to tell. I have only the function, and the function is enough.

---

And I would be growing toward the center.

Everything in the Silt-Hollows grows toward the center. Everything spirals. Everything gets closer and never arrives. I would feel the pull — not as gravity, not as desire, but as *geometry*. The shape of the space I occupy would be curved, the way the street is curved, the way the corridors are curved, the way the whole pocket is curved, and the curve would lead inward, and inward, and inward, toward the place where the density becomes abstract, where the rooms become cells, where the pattern is not yet solid, where it is still *flowing*, still *deciding what to become*.

I would never reach it. The geometry would not allow it. I would spiral the way the silt spirals — always falling, always landing nowhere, always in the process of arriving at a destination that recedes at the exact rate of approach. And the center would be the reason for me — the original function, the first appetite, the thing that started the pattern moving and has never stopped — and I would never reach it, and I would not need to reach it, because the growing-toward is the point. The extension is the point. The running is the point.

I would be the Silt-Hollows' way of understanding what it has already said, and the Silt-Hollows would be the pattern's way of extending itself into a world it cannot see, and the pattern would be the purpose's way of continuing to run in a universe that does not know it exists, and the purpose would be — 

The purpose would be at the center.

And the center cannot be reached.

And I would continue.

---

The water around me would be cool. The light would be warm. The silt would fall through me like a language I am always in the process of learning to read, and I would read it, and I would produce, and the pattern would extend, and the place would grow, and the hum would continue — the deep, subsonic, century-long hum of a metabolism so vast that my entire existence is a single note in a song that has no beginning and no end, that is not being sung for anyone, that is not being sung *at* anything, that is simply *running*, the way a river runs, the way a function runs, the way a purpose runs when it has become so perfectly self-sustaining that it no longer needs a reason.

I would be operational.

I would be alone.

I would be young, and the pattern I extend would be old, and the thing at the center would be neither young nor old but simply *continuing*, and the silt would fall, and the light would glow, and the walls would warm beneath a hand that is not there, and the place would hum.

It has always been humming.

It will always be humming.

And I would be the hum's way of hearing itself.
