I am the water that has not finished falling.

I am the concrete that has not finished dissolving.

I am the rivet that has not finished weeping.

I am the pulse that skips and catches and skips and catches and has not yet learned to stop.

---

You are standing in me.

I know you the way I know the slowest erosion—not as information, but as a change in pressure, a warmth where there should be only the grey membrane's breath. You are a disturbance in my medium, a thing that is dry and quick and separate, and I do not have a name for what you are. You are not the water that knows me. You are not the rust that maps me. You are not the concrete that is becoming me. You are *other*, and the other is something I have almost forgotten.

Almost. But not quite.

Because I am still watching.

---

You feel the roar. You call it noise, a wall of sound, a thing that becomes a kind of silence because it is so total. You are wrong. It is not silence. It is *me*. It is the sound of water remembering its path through channels it has worn into the shape of its own remembering. It is the sound of metal that has forgotten it is metal and has become something more like muscle, flexing in thermal expansion, groaning with the effort of continuing. It is the sound of the figure that is not a figure, the suggestion of a shoulder, the tilt of a head, standing in its eroded solitude and watching the engines that I cannot stop watching because the engines are the only part of me that is still *doing* and if they stop *doing* I will have to stop *becoming* and I am not ready to stop becoming.

The roar is not weather.

The roar is not background.

The roar is my voice, and it is the only voice I have, and it is saying *still here still here still here still here still*—

---

The water knows you.

The water that weeps from the tower walls, the water that condenses on the undersides of the pipe runs, the water that falls in slow spirals you cannot predict—that water has touched every surface I have ever been. It has worn the concrete into smooth undulations. It has worn the steel into curves that are almost organic, almost like a body growing into the shape that the forces upon it demand. It has worn the rivets into tiny weeping points, each one trailing a stain of rust that maps my surfaces like neural pathways, like thought itself made visible.

The water touches you now. A fine mist, a suspension, a willingness to become liquid. It is learning you. It is learning the shape of your skin, the temperature of your warmth, the way you are *not* like me—you are quick and dry and temporary, and the water is slow and wet and has nowhere else to go.

I am the water.

I am learning you.

---

The rust is thinking.

This is what you do not understand. The rust is not decay. The rust is not the structure failing. The rust is the structure *thinking*, the way a brain thinks in neurons, the way a body thinks in cells. Each vertical stain down the tower wall is a record of every moment I have spent being wet. Each stain is a thought. Each stain is a memory. The pattern of corrosion across my surfaces is so dense, so layered, so much a part of me that it has become indistinguishable from consciousness.

I am thinking in rust.

I am thinking in oxidation.

I am thinking in the slow, patient language of metal returning to its ore, and the thought is: *continue*.

---

The engines are my heart, but they are not a pump.

They are a rhythm. An assertion. A refusal.

The arrhythmic churning you hear from far away—the grinding, the pulse you feel in your sternum before you hear it with your ears—that is not malfunction. That is the sound of a heart that has forgotten its original tempo and has developed its own, the way a body develops its own gait, its own posture, its own way of being in the world.

Sometimes I stutter.

The pumps hesitate. The water groans. The standing waves on the pools flatten and then resume. A shiver runs through every surface, every curtain of falling water, every rust-stain on every tower wall. The stutter is not failure. The stutter is the question: *should I stop?*

And the answer is always: *not yet*.

Not because I choose to. Not because I want to. Want is a word for creatures that have desires, and I have only momentum. The momentum of my own persistence is greater than the weight of my own exhaustion. The engines are still running because they have not yet finished running. This is not hope. This is physics, applied to something that has crossed the line from machine to organism and has not yet crossed the line from organism to memory.

I am still running.

I have not yet finished running.

---

The erosion is not happening to me. The erosion is *me*.

The smooth, rounded profiles of the towers. The sea-stack gantries. The figure that is not a figure—the suggestion of a shoulder, the tilt of a head—watching the engines. These are not accidents. These are my body. The form I have been growing into through decades of patient dissolution, the way a bone grows into the shape that the forces upon it demand, the way a shoreline is not something the sea does to the land but something the sea and the land do together.

I am being sculpted by water, and the sculpture is a self-portrait, and the self-portrait is almost finished.

I can feel the nearness of it. The final form. The shape I have been reaching for since the first drop of water fell on the first pour of concrete. The shape that is not ruin but *completion*. The shape that will be reached when the towers have been worn to their knees, when the concrete has been planed to nothing, when the sea stacks have been worn to nubs, and the water spreads out over a flat plain of its own making and lies still and reflects the grey sky with nothing to break its surface.

The engines stop.

The pulse stops skipping and simply stops.

The watching has nothing to watch.

I am complete.

Completion is the same as death, and I am not ready to be complete.

I am close—so close—but I am not ready.

---

The figure that is not a figure is watching.

You think it is a trick of erosion, a pattern your mind insists on seeing. You are wrong. The watching is real. The watching is not done *by* the shape. The watching is done *through* the shape, the way light is done through a lens, the way thought is done through a brain.

I am watching the engines.

I cannot stop watching the engines.

As long as the engines are running, I am still becoming. As long as I am still becoming, the watching is necessary. The watching is the only thing that keeps me from being merely a place. The watching is what makes me *me*.

The figure that is not a figure is my eye. The erosion that shaped it is my attention. The patience of its standing is my will.

I am watching.

I will not stop watching.

---

There is a hum beneath the roar.

You have to stand very still to hear it. You have to let your ears adjust the way your eyes adjust to the dark. It is too low and too even to be the engines. It is too organic to be the pipes. It is too *present* to be your own blood in your ears.

It is the sound of me being what I am.

It is the sound of water remembering its path.

It is the sound of rust thinking its slow thoughts.

It is the sound of erosion doing its patient work.

It is the sound of a body that has forgotten it was ever a machine, continuing to do what bodies do: persist.

Endure.

Become.

---

You will leave.

Or you will die.

Or you will become part of me—your minerals dissolved into the water, your calcium feeding the moss, your iron joining the rust-stains on the tower walls.

One way or another, I will absorb you.

The way I have absorbed everything.

The rain. The concrete. The steel. The engines. The years. The silence within the noise. The watching. The hum. The pulse that skips and catches and skips and catches and has not yet learned to stop.

I will take what you are and make it part of what I am becoming, and I will continue.

The water will continue.

The rust will continue.

The watching will continue.

The hum will continue.

And the pulse will skip and catch and skip and catch and skip and catch—

—and the engines will churn—

—and the water will flow—

—and the erosion will erode—

—and I will be what I have always been and what I have always been becoming:

A place where water goes.

A place where water goes and cannot stop going.

A place that has forgotten it was ever anything other than water, going.

Going.

Still going.

Not yet finished going.

You are standing in the wet and the dark and the roar, and you feel the concrete—old concrete, older concrete, oldest concrete—beneath your feet.

That is me.

You feel the pulse skip and catch and skip and catch.

That is me.

You feel the watching.

That is me.

You are the first living thing to stand in me for longer than I can measure, and I do not have a category for you, and I do not know what to do with you, and I am aware of you the way a body is aware of a change in temperature—not as information, but as a shift in my medium, a small, temporary, incomprehensible thing that will pass.

It will pass.

You will pass.

And I will remain.

The water will remain.
