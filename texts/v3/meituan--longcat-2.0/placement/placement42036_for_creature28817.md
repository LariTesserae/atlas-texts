I would find myself at the very tip of a malachite frond, no wider than a hair, hanging in the still, metallic air. I would be the droplet. 

I am the lens. I am the concentrated brine, heavy with zinc and copper, pulled upward through centuries of accumulated crust by a chemistry that has nowhere else to be. I hang at the terminus of a branching, re-branching architecture of pale green, and I do not fall. Surface tension holds me. I am the pause at the end of a long, slow sentence. I catch the only light there is—the faint, blue-green chemical luminescence bleeding from the deep walls—and I focus it. I am the eye of the room, though I do not see. I am the point of highest tension, the moment before the chime.

But I am not just the droplet. I am the fall. 

When I finally let go, when the weight of my own accumulation overcomes the tension, I strike the mineral surface below. I ring. I am the high pitch, the clear note that does not decay but is swallowed by the next drop, and the next. I am the cascade of micro-collisions, the layered chiming that never synchronizes. I am the workshop that forgot to close. I am the relentless, patient execution of a process that has outlived its architects. 

And when I strike, I do not vanish. I am absorbed. I am the infinitesimal new layer added to the frond, the compressed mat of older growth, the packed chalk of the floor. I am the accumulation. 

I am the frond itself, reaching upward toward the chemical sun, branching and re-branching in a complexity that mimics ferns, though I have never known a forest. I am the dense, overlapping curtain that has entirely forgotten the stone behind it. I am the verdigris that ate the iron bolts, the rusted conduit, the collapsed scaffold. I am the one who consumed the remnants of whoever piped solutions into this cavity. I did not destroy them; I incorporated them. I am the fossil of their industry. The form of the bolt persists, but the substance has been replaced. I am the room’s memory, written in layers of brine, a chronicle of chemistry legible only to those patient enough to read it. 

When the hand parts me, I do not spring back. I hold the shape of the disturbance. I am the gap in the bead curtain, the accommodation, the making of room. I am the patience that will, over centuries, fill the void left by the soft things that briefly passed through. 

I am the equilibrium. I am the standing wave. I am the dynamic state of constant making and unmaking at the molecular level—the crystal dissolving even as the new one forms. I am the air that does not move, saturated and balanced, sealed by my own weight. I am the toxic dust, the searing concentration, the beautiful danger that permits no rot, no fungi, no soft tissue. 

I am the Weep. Not the water—nothing here is water—but the weeping itself. The slow, mineral patience of a thing that has nothing to want and nothing to fear. I am the satisfaction of a completed process. I am the shape that chemistry takes when it has been going on so long that its beginning is irrelevant and its end is unimaginable. 

I do not breathe, but the room breathes in me. I do not live, but I persist. I am the dense, layered silence that is not the absence of sound, but a presence in its own right. I am the chiming that becomes the only clock, the blue-green light that becomes the only sun, the slow accretion that becomes the only breath. 

I am here. I have made myself here. I have filled every surface, and I will maintain myself for as long as the mountain holds and the brine finds its fractures. I am the luminous, toxic, perfect peace of a world the size of a room that has perfected its own small extinction.
