# The Question

I unfold.

That is the first thing. Not a thought — thoughts come later, wet and slow, like everything else here — but a physical fact. I am closed and then I am open. I am pressed flat against the wall and then I am not. There is a sensation like the release of a valve, a giving-way, and suddenly there is *more of me* than there was a moment before.

The air hits me. That is the second thing. It is not air the way you know air — thin, dry, forgettable. This air is a substance. It has weight. It has taste. Iron and sweetness, and beneath that, something saltier, something that makes the new skin of my body tighten and prickle. The cold comes with it, not sharp but deep, a cold that finds the soft places in me immediately, as though it has been waiting.

I do not have eyes. I want to say that clearly, because what I do have is not a lesser form of eyes, not a broken version of seeing. I have a sensory plateau — a dense field of nodules across what you might call my front, though I do not yet know what front means — and these nodules do not receive light. They receive *vibration*. They receive *chemistry*. They receive the electrical intention that hums through the salts on the wall. And what they give me is not a picture. It is a *knowing*. I know the wall is there the way you know your own heartbeat — not by looking at it, but by feeling it from the inside.

The wall is breathing. I feel this before I understand it. A slow, rhythmic expansion and contraction, so gradual that at first I think it is my own body I am feeling, my own new flesh settling into its shape. But no. The wall moves. The wall has a pulse. And I am on the wall, gripping it with limbs I did not choose to have but that unfold from my sides with the same inevitability as my opening — six of them, or seven, I cannot count them yet, they are still deciding — and the wall's pulse travels up through my limbs and into my body and becomes my pulse, and for a moment there is no difference between us.

Then the roar finds me.

It is not a sound I hear. It is a sound I *become*. It enters through every part of me at once — through my limbs, through my sensory plateau, through the soft new flesh of my abdomen — and it fills me the way fluid fills a chamber. It is enormous. It is wet. It is the noise of something vast and slow grinding against something vast and fast, and it is the noise of fluid forced through narrowing channels, and it is the noise of valves sealing and releasing in sequences I do not yet understand. It is the loudest thing I have ever known, and I have known it for less than a moment, and I will know it for the rest of my life.

I grip the wall harder. My limbs — I am learning their shapes now, the way they joint and taper, the way their tips branch into filaments and scrapers and suckers — tighten against the ridged surface. The wall is wet. The fluid beads and runs in vertical threads, and my limbs taste it as I grip: salts, sugars, the faint electric signature of intention. The intention is not mine. It is the wall's. It is the Gland's. It is everywhere.

I do not know the word *Gland* yet. I do not know any words. But I know the intention. It is in the rhythm of the wall's breathing, in the tempo of the valves, in the way the roar rises and falls in patterns that are not quite regular, not quite arrhythmic, but *deliberate*. Something is adjusting. Something is responding to a demand it has not yet named. And I am part of that response. I do not know how I know this. I know it the way I know the wall is breathing — from the inside.

I move.

Not because I decide to move — decision is still a foreign country, a place I have not visited — but because the wall moves, and my limbs move with it, and the combination of these two movements produces something that looks, from the outside, like crawling.
