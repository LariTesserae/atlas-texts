I am in the crack. 

Not a fissure, exactly—not a wound in the stone—but a pocket. A low, shallow declination in the plaza where the brine pools and the mist gathers thickest, shielded from the open wrongness of the sky by the looming frame of a hollow tower. The stone here is wet, sweating its slow, reluctant moisture, and it tastes of copper and lime when I drag my mandibles across its surface. 

I am one of the Phasing Swarm. 

At least, that is the closest translation for what I am. I am a flicker in the baseline reality of the Arrangement, a localized skip in the signal of the world. My body—chitinous, multi-legged, too many joints—exists at a slight remove from the space you occupy. When you look at me, I am already shifting, my outline blurring at the edges, my physicality oscillating between *here* and *almost-here*. 

This is how I survive the voice.

The sound of the Arrangement—the deep, total roar of settling geology and incomprehensible sky-technology—is a physical weight. It presses down on the plaza, it vibrates through the limestone, it wants to crush the matter of this island into a denser, quieter state. If I were fully present, fully locked into the Euclidean geometry of the stone, the sound would flatten me into a smear of copper and chitin. But I am not fully present. I am half a step out of sync with the present, phasing rapidly, vibrating at a frequency that lets the roar pass *through* me rather than crushing me against the rock.

I am grazing. My mandibles work fast, scraping the fast-growing lichen from the pale stone. The lichen is slick, faintly bioluminescent in the dim amber light, and it tastes of salt and heat. As I eat, I vibrate. We all do. The thousands of us in this crack, in the crevices of this plaza, in the shallow basins between the hollow towers—we are a rippling, skittering carpet of phase-shifted hunger. And as we eat, we oscillate. 

Our collective vibration is the first layer of the city’s voice. We are the city’s microbiome, breaking down the mineral crusts, our phased bodies acting as living buffers between the raw geology and the fragile ecology. When the Arrangers speak in their sub-sonic tongue, their modulations travel through the stone and ride the frequency of our swarm. We do not understand the words—if they are words—but we feel the intent. *Graze here. Phase faster. The tower is hungry.*

I stop eating. I go still. The swarm around me continues its rippling motion, a wave of phasing chitin flowing over the wet stone, but I lock my legs into the rock and I wait. 

Because the sound is changing. 

It is the shift. The lull. The intervals of two to fifteen seconds where the deep roar cuts out, where the geological groan and the waste-sound of the sky simply cease. 

In the silence, I am forced into full presence. 

It is agonizing. The sudden absence of the sound leaves a ringing, fragile vacuum in my body. My phased edges snap into sharp, painful focus. I can feel the full, terrible gravity of the Arrangement pulling at my legs. The air is thick and warm, pressing down on my carapace. I am entirely *here*, entirely solid, and entirely vulnerable. 

But I can hear the water. 

In the twelve seconds of silence, the island breathes. Beneath the stone, deep in the young limestone, water is moving. Finding its level. Seeping through the mineral veins. I press my antennae flat against the rock, and I feel the pulse of it—slow, warm, alive. The stone is drinking, and the mist is rising, and the entire island is exhaling. 

Then, the roar returns. 

It hits me like a physical blow, a wall of acoustic force that slams me out of baseline reality. I phase hard, my body blurring, my legs finding their grip on the *almost*-stone rather than the stone itself. The pressure lifts. The vibration returns to my joints, and I am safe again, flickering in the amber-grey dimness. 

Something vast is moving in the mist. 

I do not look at it directly—my eyes are not built for the geometry of the things that walk between the towers—but I feel its displacement. It is a Processor. It moves through the plaza, walking on ground that is not entirely the ground I cling to. It steps between the tectonic plates, using the shifting stone as a treadmill, its massive weight distributed across dimensions so that the limestone does not crack under it. It is grazing on the tall, hollow grasses, its mouth unfolding in directions that make my phased senses ring with a high, discordant frequency. 

As it feeds, it deposits its waste—a steaming, enzymatically active slurry that it presses into the cracks of the rock. It lands near my pocket. The slurry hisses faintly, rich with the minerals that will become soil, that will feed the roots of the broad-leafed plants. It is the future, liquefied. 

Above, the great tower brightens. A warm, amber pulse travels upward through the hollow frame, like sap in a stem. An Arranger is moving through the structure. I cannot see it—I am too small, too low, too phased-out to comprehend its non-Euclidean form—but I feel its passage as a sudden, sharp modulation in the sub-sonic. The gravity well of its body warps the light around the tower's apex. It is tending the frame, drawing up the brine, adjusting the resonance of the structure so the sound remains a tool rather than a weapon. 

The swarm shifts. The vibration around me changes pitch. The lichen I was eating has been grazed down to the bare stone, and the chemical signals propagating through the undergrowth are telling us to move. The swarm begins to flow, a rippling tide of phasing bodies moving toward the next pocket of vegetation, the next crack in the young rock. 

I uncurl my legs. I join the wave. 

I am a note in the chord. A flicker in the signal. I am the buffer between the crushing sound and the fragile stone, the gardener of the mineral crust, the living breath of the Arrangement. The roar is total again, vibrating through my phased body, turning my chitin into a tuning fork for the island's endless, deafening song. 

The rain will come soon. The stone will drink it. The Arrangers will listen to the water finding its level. And I will be here, in the crack, phasing in and out of existence, keeping time with the breathing of a world that is still becoming.
