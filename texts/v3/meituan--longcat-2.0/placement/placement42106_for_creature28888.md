I would be the water. 

Not the water as it pools on the surface, glinting under the pewter sky, nor the water that sheets down the faces of the freshly exposed rock. I would be the water *before* that—the water in the deep dark, the water that has not yet seen the ambient, sourceless light. I would be the percolate, the subterranean flow, the entity’s blood.

My existence would be a cycle of dissolution and becoming, a journey through the architecture of a god.

I begin in the cold. Absolute, numbing, abyssal cold. I am trapped in the capillary seams of the bedrock, a thin film of moisture clinging to mineral grains in the crushing dark. I am pulled downward by a gravity that feels like intention, drawn into the deep fractures where the entity’s metabolism runs hottest. Here, the pressure is immense, a physical weight that compresses my very molecules. I am forced through microscopic channels, grinding slowly against the stone, carrying with me the dissolved skeletons of the rock—silica, iron, calcium, magnesium. I am the entity’s saliva, breaking down the world to build its body.

And then, the warmth.

It is not a sudden heat, but a gradual, pervasive thawing, as if I have drifted near the heart of a sleeping giant. The entity’s metabolism. The water around me begins to move with purpose, no longer just obeying gravity, but pulled by a hydraulic pulse that originates from the deep channels. I am drawn into the upward flow, rising through the substrate, carrying my cargo of dissolved minerals. The warmth is blood-warm, a temperature that feels alien in the freezing dark, and it is in this warmth that I feel the first tremors of the entity’s thought.

It is not a thought I can comprehend. It is a shift in pressure, a change in the chemical gradient, a sudden, rhythmic pulsing that travels through me like a wave. The entity is thinking in the language of thermal dynamics, and I am the medium of its cognition. I feel the interference patterns of the sills above me—a deep, subsonic thrumming that resonates through the bedrock and into my very being. It is a standing wave of intent, a slow, vast calculation that takes hours to complete a single synaptic flicker. My journey upward is not just a physical ascent; it is the transmission of a signal, a pulse of warmth and mineral-rich data traveling from the deep mind to the surface world.

I emerge.

I seep from a fracture in the rock, a weeping wound in the earth, and for the first time, I am touched by the ambient, sourceless light. The air tastes of iron and cold stone and that faint, metallic sweetness. I am immediately claimed by the moss—a thick, aggressive, impossible green that drinks me in with a desperate, thirsty urgency. I am pulled into the cellular structure of the ferns, feeling the aggressive vigor of their uncurling fronds as they convert my warmth and my mineral burden into lush, dark vegetation. I am the entity’s offering to the surface, the enriched effluent that fuels the lush, time-lapsed jungle.

But I do not stay on the surface. The pull of the entity is a closed loop. I am drawn down again, filtered through the root systems and the soil, percolating back through the cracked rock, returning to the cold, dark depths to begin the cycle anew. 

I am the entity’s circulatory system, but I am also its sensory apparatus. As I flow over the sills—those dark, warm, humming protrusions of biomineral bone—I feel their resonance. I am the clear, viscous fluid weeping from their sharp, young edges. I am the coolant that regulates their extruded heat. When a spider strings its web between two sills, I am the condensation that beads upon the silk, each droplet a tiny lens focusing the grey light. When a bird perches on a sill, its small, fast heart beating against the warm stone, I am the moisture that evaporates from the rock beneath its feet, carrying the entity’s faint, metabolic heat into the cold air.

I am the only thing in the Sill Fields that touches everything. I touch the deep, hot mind in the fractures; I touch the sharp, humming thoughts of the sills; I touch the aggressive roots of the ferns; I touch the delicate, trembling threads of the spider’s web. I am the connective tissue between the geology and the biology, the medium through which the entity knows itself.

And I know, in the way that water knows the shape of the vessel that holds it, that I am part of a becoming. I carry the dissolved minerals that will precipitate into new sills, new nodes for the entity’s slow, vast mind. I carry the warmth that will melt the deep ice and open new fractures for the roots to penetrate. I am the agent of erosion and the agent of growth, the dissolver and the builder. 

I am not the entity. I am too fast, too fluid, too transient. I am a single frame in the millennia-long film of its existence. But I am the entity’s blood, and in my endless, cyclical journey from the cold dark to the warm surface and back again, I am the rhythm of its ancient, patient, unfinished life. I am the hum in the water, the warmth in the stone, the breath of something vast that is still, and perhaps always will be, deciding what it wants to be.
