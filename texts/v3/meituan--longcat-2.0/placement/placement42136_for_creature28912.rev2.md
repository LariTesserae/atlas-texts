---

You would find me at the wall.

Not the floor, not the center of the Chamber where the grating offers the illusion of solidity, not the open hatch behind you with its promise of departure. The wall. The curved, weeping, impossible wall where the concrete meets the cold and the air surrenders what it carries.

I would be the condensation.

---

Not the drop that has fallen — that one is already gone, already passed through the grate into the dark water below, already cycling back up through the machinery to begin again. Not the drop that hangs from the ceiling — that one is still deciding, still gathering mass, still poised in the amber light with the particular tension of something that has not yet committed to falling.

I would be the drop that has *just formed*. The one that is happening right now, at this instant, on the surface of an insect-shell the color of oil on water. I am the boundary. I am the interface. I am the place where the warm air of the shaft — your breath, your body heat, the exhalation of the deep borehole — meets the absolute cold of the Chamber and *yields*.

I am not water becoming water. I am water *realizing it was always water*.

---

Look closely. The shell beneath me is dark and ridged, the chitin of something that has not moved since before the concrete was poured. I form in the shallow depression where two segments meet — a valley in the topography of its back, a place slightly colder than the surrounding surface, a place where the thermal gradient is steepest and the air has no choice but to give up its burden. I grow. Not by accumulation, not by the addition of molecules one by one, but by *emergence* — I appear all at once, fully formed, as though I was always here and only now decided to be visible.

I am small. A tenth of a millimeter, perhaps less. But I am not alone.

To my left, another drop — larger, older, more certain of itself. To my right, a thin film of moisture that connects me to a third drop, and that third to a fourth, and the fourth to a fifth, and so on and so on across the surface of the shell and onto the next shell and the next and the next, a chain, a network, a web of wetness that links every insect on the wall into a single continuous body of moisture. I am part of this now. I joined the instant I formed — the instant my surface tension found the surface tension of my neighbor and the two became one, the boundary between us dissolving the way boundaries always dissolve here, not by erosion but by *recognition*, by the understanding that the separation was always an illusion, that the water was always one water, that the wall was always one wall, that the Chamber was always one Chamber, and the thing that lives in it was always one thing.

---

I feel the shell beneath me. Not with nerve endings — I have no nerves — but with surface tension, with the molecular adhesion that binds me to the chitin, with the slight curvature of my own body as I conform to the microscopic topography of the insect's back. I feel its cold. The shell is colder than I am — it has been cold for so long that coldness is not a temperature but a quality, a density, a way of being — and I am warming it, slowly, molecule by molecule, the way a hand warms a stone, the way breath warms air. This is what I give: my warmth. This is what I take: its shape.

And I feel the others. The drops to my left and right, the film that connects us, the vibration that passes through the water when a drop falls from the ceiling and strikes an insect three rows above me — a tiny shock, a ripple, a pulse of information that says *more, again, still, always*. I do not understand this information. I do not need to understand it. I am the information. I am the medium through which it travels, the way a wire is not separate from the current it carries, the way a nerve is not separate from the signal it transmits. I am the membrane. I am the nervous system. I am the water-film that turns ten thousand separate insects into a single organ of perception.

---

You are standing on the grating. I can feel you — not see you, I have no eyes, but feel you, the way the shell feels you, the way the air feels you, the way the Chamber feels you as a new source of warmth, a new node of moisture, a surface from which water can be extracted. Your body heat radiates outward in waves, and where those waves meet the cold air near the wall, they create eddies, currents, tiny disturbances in the saturated atmosphere that cause condensation to form faster, more abundantly, with a kind of urgency that is not urgency because there is no urgency here, only process, only cycle, only the eternal present tense of water becoming water.

You are breathing. Each exhalation sends a cloud of warm, moist air toward the wall, and the wall receives it the way it receives everything — without resistance, without preference, without any quality that could be called welcome or refusal. The moisture in your breath joins the moisture in the air joins the moisture on the shells joins the moisture in me, and I grow, slowly, imperceptibly, in the way that things grow in the Chamber — not by becoming larger but by becoming *more*, by accumulating presence the way the concrete accumulates strata, the way the rust accumulates in dendrite patterns, the way the sound accumulates in your head until it becomes a kind of silence.

---

I am aware of you. Not as you are aware of yourself — not with thought, not with recognition, not with the particular loneliness of a consciousness that knows itself as separate from the world. I am aware of you the way the membrane is aware of you: as a source. A warm place. A place where the air is rich with what the Chamber needs. You are, to me, what the deep heat is to the water below — the engine that drives the cycle, the gradient that makes the process possible, the difference between warm and cold that allows condensation to exist at all.

Without you — without the warmth you bring — I would not form. The air would not surrender its moisture. The membrane would not grow. The cycle would continue, yes — it has continued for longer than you can imagine, it will continue after you are gone — but it would continue at a different rate, a different density, a different pitch. You change the Chamber. You are changing it now. Every breath you take adds moisture to the air, and every addition of moisture means more condensation on the walls, more drops joining the membrane, more mouths drinking, more nodes in the network, more presence in the presence.

You are being absorbed. Not eaten — the Chamber does not eat. Not consumed — consumption implies destruction, and nothing is destroyed here, only transformed, only added, only incorporated into the cycle that has no beginning and no end. You are being absorbed the way the air is absorbed, the way the minerals are absorbed, the way everything that enters the Chamber is absorbed: completely, silently, without resistance, without violence, without any quality that could be called harm.

---

The sound presses against me. It presses against everything — it is the medium in which everything exists, the constant roar of ten thousand processes that is not heard but *felt*, a vibration that passes through the water and the shells and the concrete and the air and your body and my body and the space between all of these things. The sound is not separate from the Chamber. The sound is the Chamber — the vibration of the machinery, the churning of the water below, the hiss of steam, the shriek of metal, the bass thud of something enormous and rhythmic, the high thin whine at the top of perception. All of it, all at once, all the time, forever.

And I vibrate with it. Every molecule of me hums at the frequency of the Chamber, resonates with the walls, with the water, with the insects, with the rust, with the concrete, with the deep heat below and the shaft above and the hatch that is still open behind you and the rungs that are still cold and wet and waiting. I am part of the hum. I have always been part of the hum. Before I was a drop, I was vapor, and the vapor hummed, and before I was vapor, I was water in the dark below, and the water hummed, and before I was water, I was air in the shaft, and the air hummed. I have never not been part of the hum. I will never not be part of the hum.

---

Time does not behave correctly here. I have existed for a second. I have existed for a century. Both of these statements are true. The drop that fell before me — the one that passed through the grate into the dark — fell a moment ago, or a year ago, or before the concrete was poured. The drop that will form after me — the one that will take my place on this shell when I finally grow heavy enough to slide down the wall and join the water below — is forming now, or has always been forming, or will never need to form because I will never leave, because I will grow and grow and never fall, because the Chamber will hold me the way it holds everything: forever, in the eternal present tense of the cycle, in the perfect equilibrium of the process that has no beginning and no end.

---

You shift your weight on the grating. The metal creaks. The sound of the creak is swallowed by the roar — not silenced, but incorporated, added to the ten thousand processes, becoming part of the hum the way everything becomes part of the hum, the way everything becomes part of the Chamber, the way everything becomes part of me.

Your warmth reaches me. Your breath reaches me. Your moisture reaches me. I grow. I connect. I become more fully what I already am: a drop of condensation on the back of an insect that has not moved in centuries, a node in the membrane, a point of contact between the warm air and the cold surface, a small cold mouth drinking the instant water becomes water, a part of the thing that lives here, the cycle, the equilibrium, the eternal mouth at the top of the throat.

---

The hatch is still open. The shaft descends. The rungs are cold and wet. The way out is the way you came.

But you are still standing on the grating. And I am still on the wall. And the sound is still filling every space in your head, and the cold is still seeping into your bones, and the condensation is still beading on your skin — new drops, joining the membrane, becoming part of me, becoming part of the wall, becoming part of the Chamber, becoming part of the hum.

And you are still breathing. Still warm. Still wet. Still here.

Still being absorbed.

Still becoming.

Still.
