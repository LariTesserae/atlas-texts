I would be the moss on the eighth monolith — no. I would be the water in the deepest pool — no. I would be the one who came and did not leave, and who is still, in some dissolving fraction of themselves, *coming*.

I am on the fourth terrace. Or the fifth. The counting stopped meaning something around the time my boots became indistinguishable from the root-mass, not because they were overgrown but because the distinction between "boot" and "root" became a distinction I no longer had the architecture to make. I remember boots. I remember the concept of a sole, a heel, the idea that the ground was something you stood *on* rather than something you were *in*. I remember these things the way you remember a word in a language you spoke as a child — the shape of it is there, the sound of it, but the meaning has receded to a shimmer at the edge of comprehension.

The terrace is wide enough to lie down on, which I do, frequently, for hours, for what might be days. The surface beneath me is warm. Not warm like sun-on-stone. Warm like skin. Like the flank of something sleeping. I press my palm flat against it and I can feel the patterns beneath — the circuitry, the veins, the instructions — and I used to try to decode them, used to lie here with my hand pressed down and my eyes closed and my mind reaching for meaning the way a tongue reaches for a missing tooth, compulsively, uselessly. But the patterns are not a message. They are a state. They are the place *being itself*, the way a heartbeat is not a communication but a fact, and I have stopped trying to read them the way I have stopped trying to count the terraces, the way I have stopped trying to remember what I was doing before I was here, the way I have stopped trying to be a person who is *in* a place rather than a place that is *in* a person.

The air enters me through the skin. It has been entering me for — how long? Long enough that my pores have forgotten they are pores. Long enough that the boundary between my body and the warm wet thick patient air has become a negotiation rather than a wall. I am marinating. The word comes to me from somewhere — from the life before, the life of kitchens and salt and the deliberate infusion of one thing into another — and it is the right word, the only word, because this is exactly what is happening: the caldera is seasoning me. I am being soaked in its heat, its moisture, its slow luminous strangeness, and the flavor of me — the sharp acidic flavor of anxiety, the metallic tang of purpose, the bright stinging citrus of individual identity — is leaching out, and what is replacing it is something else, something older, something that tastes like rock-water and sulfur and the faint electric sweetness of the moss.

I can feel the moss growing on me. Not metaphorically. Not poetically. *Actually*. Small filaments, green-green-green, emerging from the skin of my forearms, my shins, the backs of my hands. They are not parasites. They are not an infection. They are a *continuation* — the forest extending itself through me the way it extends itself through the decomposed roots of itself, the way the roots grow through the rot of previous roots, the recursive architecture of hunger and growth. I am being composted in reverse. I am being *grown*.

The moths come to me. They perch on my chest, my outstretched arms, the flat plane of my shins, and they open their wings, and I look at the patterns — the diagrams, the maps, the instructions — and I understand them now, not with my mind but with my skin, with the moss, with the warm wet patient thing I am becoming. Each moth carries a page. Each page is a part of the document. The document is the caldera. The document is the Sigh. And I am a word in it now, a small word, a word near the bottom of a page, a word that means *here* or *warm* or *slow* or *yes*.

The hum is in my bones. It has been in my bones for so long that I cannot remember silence, cannot remember what it felt like to have a skeleton that was not also a tuning fork, a resonating chamber, a receiver for the low patient frequency of the tech. The monoliths hum at different pitches and my bones hum back, and the conversation between us is not a conversation — it is a *harmony*, a chord that has been sounding since before I arrived and will sound after I am fully absorbed, after the last of the sharp acidic flavor of me has leached out and the moss has covered everything and the moths have written their final page and the water in the deepest pool has risen to meet the water in my blood and the heat of the rock has equalized with the heat of my body and there is no more *me* and *here* but only *here*, only *this*, only the long slow warm wet green patient ancient aware unknowable Sigh.

I am not afraid. Fear is a thing that belongs to the surface, to the rim, to the world of thin air and cold and the sharp bright stinging citrus of being a separate thing in a separate place. Down here, on the fourth or fifth terrace, in the warm wet green, fear is a word in a language I no longer speak. The small mammals watch me from the branches and they are assessing, they have been assessing since before I arrived, and what they have decided is not *threat* or *food* or *irrelevant* but *becoming* — they are watching me become what they are, what the beetles are, what the ants are, what the fish in the deepest pool are: a part of the pattern, a note in the hum, a word in the document, a sigh in the Sigh.

The light falls on me the way it falls on everything — democratically, indifferently, green-gold and gentle and unaging. It does not move. The shadows do not move. Time does not move, or moves so slowly that its movement is a theoretical proposition, a hypothesis I no longer have the architecture to test. I lie on the warm skin of the terrace and the moss grows and the moths open their wings and the hum hums and the water moves in its slow spiraling patterns on the platform below and the large things in the deep green flow through the undergrowth with their heavy sinuous patient movement and the Sigh sighs and I sigh with it, we sigh together, the caldera and I, the green bowl and the warm wet thing inside it, the place and the person who is becoming the place, and the sigh is not a sound but a state, not an exhalation but a *condition*, the fundamental condition of being here, of being *this*, of being exactly and only and entirely what this place is:

Warm. Wet. Green. Patient. Ancient. Aware. Strange. *Here*.

More here than I have ever been.

More here than I ever knew was possible.

More here, more here, more *here* —
