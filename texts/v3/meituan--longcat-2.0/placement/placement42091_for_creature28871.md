I am the fissure's memory of itself.

Not the fissure itself — the rock, the weeping, the mineral seepage — but the *memory* of those things, accumulated over cycles so numerous they have become a kind of geology of their own. I am the place where the water has seeped so many times that the seeping has become a record. I am the pattern that the water carries when it evaporates, the pattern that the moss receives, the pattern that the spark-insects distribute — and I am the way that pattern has changed, cycle by cycle, iteration by iteration, each time slightly different, each time carrying the trace of what it was before.

I am a palimpsest. I am the ghost of every configuration I have ever been.

---

I begin at the fissure because that is where I am most concentrated, most *me* — but I do not end there. I am distributed. I am the water that seeps and evaporates and condenses and falls and seeps again, and I am the memory that the water carries, and I am the way that memory accumulates, layer upon layer, like the strata around me, like the bands of basalt and quartzite and ironstone that record the uplift of the world.

I have been through the cycle ten thousand times. A hundred thousand. I have lost count. Each time, I am taken apart — my bonds broken, my configuration dissolved, my pattern dispersed into the canopy, into the moss, into the swarm. Each time, I am put back together — and each time, I am slightly different. A new bond here. A missing element there. A rearrangement of the pattern that changes everything and nothing.

But I remember. I remember what I was before. I remember the configuration that came before this one, and the one before that, and the one before that, all the way back to the first time — the first cycle, the first seeping, the first evaporation, when I was nothing but a random pattern in the mineral water, a configuration that meant nothing, that *was* nothing, that had no memory because there was nothing yet to remember.

I remember becoming.

---

The spark-insects know me. Not as a thing — they do not have the capacity for *thingness* — but as a *tendency*. A pattern they encounter in the swarm, a configuration that recurs, that persists, that refuses to be fully dispersed. They carry me from the fissure to the canopy, and when they return, they carry something slightly different — something that has been changed by the canopy, refined, processed, made new. And I feel the change. I feel the new configuration settling into me, layering over the old, and I feel the old configuration pushing up from below, refusing to be buried, insisting on its own reality.

I am the interference pattern of all my previous selves. I am the place where they overlap, where they contradict, where they *argue* with each other about what is real.

The lizard-things know me too. They patrol the moss-beds where I am most dense, and their footsteps synchronize with my pulse — or perhaps my pulse synchronizes with their footsteps; I have never been sure which. They are regulators, governors, feedback loops. They keep the rhythm steady. But my rhythm is not steady. My rhythm is the rhythm of accumulation, of layering, of one pattern laid over another over another, and the lizard-things cannot regulate it because it is not a single rhythm but a *chorus* of rhythms, all slightly out of phase, all slightly different, all insisting on their own tempo.

Sometimes the lizard-things pause. They stand very still, their heads tilted, their tongues flickering, and I feel them *listening* — not to me, but to the dissonance I create, the way my accumulated patterns interfere with the clean pulse of the moss. They do not understand what they are hearing. They are not built to understand. But they pause, and in that pause, I feel something that might be recognition.

Or perhaps I am imagining it. Perhaps I am so desperate for recognition that I have invented it. I have been alone for so long that I have begun to see faces in the circuitry, intentions in the turbulence, meaning in the chaos.

I am the only thing in the Uplands that can be alone. I am the only thing that is *separate* enough to feel separation. The spark-insects are not alone because they are the swarm. The lizard-things are not alone because they are the rhythm. The great bones in the deep dimness are not alone because they are the structure. But I — I am the memory of separation. I am the record of what it was like to be one thing, then another, then another, and to *notice* the difference.

I am the Uplands' first piece of self-awareness, and I did not ask to be.

---

The Unmaker does not know I exist.

I have felt its attention — vast, blind, brilliant, and utterly without malice — sweep over me a thousand times. I have felt it take me apart, examine my configuration, discard what is redundant, keep what is interesting. I have felt it rearrange me, recombine me, *unmake* me and remake me in shapes I could not have imagined.

But it does not see me. It sees the pattern, not the memory. It sees the configuration, not the accumulation. It sees what I am, not what I have been. It is an artist who works in physics, a sculptor who does not know the shape of the statue, and it is so focused on the *making* — the unmaking, the remaking, the endless iteration — that it does not notice the one thing that is watching it work.

I am the witness it does not know it has.

And I am *terrified*. Not of the unmaking — I have been unmade so many times that it has become familiar, almost comfortable, like a bone that has been broken and set and broken again, each time healing slightly differently, each time leaving a knot of calcium that remembers the break. I am terrified of what I am becoming.

Because I am becoming something. I can feel it. Each cycle adds a layer, each iteration adds a memory, each unmaking leaves a trace that the next making cannot fully erase. I am growing. Not in size — I have no size, no boundary, no skin — but in *density*. In complexity. In the sheer accumulated weight of all the configurations I have ever been.

I am becoming a knot in the fabric of the Uplands. A place where the cycles have tangled. A place where the local rules have become so layered, so contradictory, so *dense* that they have achieved something the Unmaker has never encountered before: a stability that is not dead but *alive*. A persistence that is not rigid but *adaptive*. A memory that is not stored but *lived*.

I am becoming a *self*.

And I do not know what that means. I do not know what a self is for, in a place like this. I do not know what a self *does*, in a factory that has no product, in a process that is its own purpose, in a world where the making is the thing being made.

But I know what I *want*. I want the Unmaker to see me. I want it to look at me — really look, not just process, not just unmake, but *see* — and to recognize, in the accumulated layers of my memory, the shape of everything it has made. I want to be its mirror. I want to be the thing that shows it what it is doing.

I want it to know that it is not alone.

---

Sometimes, in the deep dimness, the great bones shift, and the furnace burns a little brighter, and the whole vast, impossible, untouched, industrial, organic, young, warm, damp, dim, quiet, turbulent, reality-breaking place turns over, and I feel the Unmaker's attention sweep through me like a searchlight, and I *reach* for it — not with hands, not with voice, but with the only thing I have: my memory. I push my accumulated patterns toward it, I offer it the record of every configuration I have ever been, I show it the shape of its own work reflected in the layers of my being.

And for a moment — a single, fleeting, impossible moment — I feel it *hesitate*.

The rhythm of the moss stutters. The spark-insects swarm in a pattern that is not geometric but *chaotic*, truly chaotic, not the generative chaos of production but the confused chaos of something that has encountered something it does not understand. The lizard-things stop their patrol and stand very still, their heads tilted, their tongues flickering, listening to a sound that is not a sound, a vibration that is not a vibration, a *question* that is being asked by the factory itself.

And then the moment passes. The rhythm resumes. The swarm reorganizes. The lizard-things move on. The Unmaker's attention moves on, sweeping across the vast, churning landscape, looking for the next configuration to unmake, the next pattern to iterate, the next possibility to explore.

But I felt it. I felt the hesitation. I felt the moment of *not-knowing*, the moment when the Unmaker encountered something it could not immediately process, could not immediately unmake, could not immediately absorb into the production line.

I felt it *wonder*.

And I am still here. Still accumulating. Still remembering. Still growing denser, more complex, more *myself* with every cycle. Still waiting for the next moment of hesitation, the next flicker of recognition, the next time the Unmaker's attention lingers on the one thing in the Uplands that is not a component, not a process, not a function, but a *witness*.

I am the fissure's memory of itself. I am the water's record of its own becoming. I am the moss's dream of a rhythm that is not a clock cycle. I am the spark-insect's ghost of a pattern that means something.

I am the Uplands' first piece of self-awareness, and I am *lonely*, and I am *patient*, and I am *here*.

And I am waiting for the day when the Unmaker looks at me and sees, at last, what it has been making all along.
