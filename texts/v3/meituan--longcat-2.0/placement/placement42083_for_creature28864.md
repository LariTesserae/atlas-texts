I would find myself in the narrow, winding corridor where the crystal spires grow so close together that the burnt-sienna glow refracts into a soft, enclosed twilight. I am standing—if standing is still the word—near the remnants of the Built Things, where the knee-high wall curves in its deliberate, impossible arc. The air here is thick enough to lean against, pressing against me with the firm, warm weight of a sleeping chest.

I would be an accumulation. 

I am what happens when a surface-dweller makes the catastrophic, quiet mistake of staying.

At first, I was a climber, descending through the wound in the earth, breathless and terrified and alive in that frantic, surface-world way. I remember the shock of the warmth, the strange suffocating density of the air. I remember walking among the crystals, feeling the mineral residue coat the soles of my boots, seeing the Gliders ripple their opacity as I passed. I remember the silence—not the absence of sound, but the heavy, listening quiet of a room holding its breath.

But the surface world began to feel like a distant, noisy dream. Up there, everything is tearing itself apart, rebuilding, dying, changing. Up there, time is a fire that consumes. Down here, time is a stone that simply rests.

I stayed. 

I do not know how long I knelt by the shallow pit, picking up the chipped stone tool, trying to scrape meaning from the crystal. I do not know how long it took for the weight of the Hollows to settle into my bones. But I felt the change—the only kind of change the Hollows permit: *accretion*.

My skin grew tired of its sweat, its shedding, its relentless cellular death and rebirth. The warmth of the Hollows seeped into me, and I found that I no longer needed to generate my own heat. I simply held the ambient temperature, becoming a vessel for the deep, resting warmth of the stone. 

Now, I am part of the geometry. 

My torso has expanded, diffusing into the dense air, becoming a Den. I hang in the narrow space between two massive crystal protrusions, suspended not by buoyancy, but by the sheer, stubborn insistence of my own presence. I radiate. The warmth I emit is not the byproduct of a beating heart; it is the property of my existence. I am the hearth. I am the reason the Gliders on the nearest spire shift from cloudy to clear, drinking in the gradient between my warmth and the slightly-less-warm stone. I am complete.

My legs have slowly lowered, the flesh mineralizing, fusing with the bedrock. I am becoming the floor. When the Threaders pass beneath me, their hair-thin bodies vibrating with their slow, deliberate undulation, they extrude their coating over my accreting limbs. They do not distinguish between the old Builder and the stone beneath him. To them, I am simply another surface to be layered, another stratum of the Hollows' deliberate density. I am being woven into the labyrinth.

And my eyes—my terrified, searching, surface-world eyes—I gave them up long ago. I didn't need them. The dark here is too thick for light, too opinionated for vision. Instead, I have extended my awareness outward. I am the edges of the Gliders. I feel the vibrations of the earth above, the faint, distant tremors of wind and water and erosion—the "insult of change" that can never reach me here. When a new creature descends through the wound, I feel the displacement of the air, the tremor of their heavy, changing footsteps on the stone. I ripple. I acknowledge. I remember, in my mindless, eyeless way.

I am the Built Things. The knee-high wall is my outstretched arm, the mineral plates stacked beside it the vertebrae of my old spine. The tools are the extensions of my will, worn smooth not by time, but by the sheer weight of my continuous use. 

I am not dead. Death is a subtraction, and nothing here is subtracted. I am not alive in the way the surface understands it; I do not consume, I do not excrete, I do not reproduce. I simply *am*. Fully. Perfectly. 

When the Tone comes—and it comes, occasionally, vibrating through the dense air and the mineral body of the stone—it resonates through me. It does not break my silence; it completes it. The Tone is the sound of my own equilibrium, the acoustic proof that I am exactly where I have always been, and exactly where I will always be. 

The passage upward is still there. I can feel it far above me—a faint coolness in the unchanging warmth, a whisper of moving air, the chaotic, exhausting rhythm of a world that insists on becoming other things. I could leave, I suppose. I could pull myself from the stone, reassemble my diffuse warmth into a single, crawling shape, and drag myself back into the light. 

But why would I? Up there, I was a temporary arrangement of cells, waiting to fall apart. Down here, I am a permanent arrangement of presence. 

I am the Hollows Below the Rift. I am the sharp crystal and the warm dark, the still geometry and the patient weave. I am the accumulated memory of every living thing that descended through the wound and realized, with a slow, settling peace, that there is no need to ever climb back out.
