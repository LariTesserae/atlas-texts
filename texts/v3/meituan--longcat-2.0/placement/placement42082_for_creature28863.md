I begin as the tap. 

A single, delicate strike of a toe against the basalt. It is night, and the cold has bitten through the thin air, drawing the steam from the fumaroles in great, billowing exhalations. I am perched on a ledge of sinter, my translucent skin beading with the condensation that is my birthright. I do not croak. I do not sing. I strike the rock again—*tap-tap-tap-tap*—a rhythm that is not a language, but a location. A coordinate. 

I am the condensation frog, and I am the Shelf’s nervous system firing in the dark.

But to say I am merely a frog is to mistake the finger for the message it writes. When my toe strikes the hexagonal face of the basalt, I do not just make a sound. I become a vibration. I feel myself travel. The pulse leaves my body and enters the stone, racing along the crystalline structure of the column, diving into the deep cracks that web the plateau. For a fraction of a second, I am not confined to my thumbnail-sized body; I am a ripple propagating through a vast, dark, resonant chamber. I am the Shelf listening to itself.

I tap again, and the rock answers. 

From the eastern rim, a pulse returns—a faint, rhythmic thrumming that travels up through the soles of my feet. It is another frog, a rival or a mate, signaling through the body of the earth. We are having a conversation that bypasses the air entirely, a dialogue conducted in the medium of fractured basalt and mineral-laden water. The entire plateau is our telegraph wire, and the message is simple: *I am here. The steam is rising. The night is alive.*

But as the hours pass and the steam thickens, I feel the boundaries of my body begin to blur. The condensation on my ventral skin—that miraculous fractal landscape of folds within folds—drinks deeply of the damp air. I am absorbing the Shelf. The iron-tinged moisture, the microscopic sulfur crystals, the dissolved minerals of the travertine; they enter me, and I become a living extension of the hydrothermal system. 

I am no longer just the messenger. I am the message.

I feel the deep, subterranean hum of the magma chamber, a bass note so low it is less a sound and more a pressure. I feel the rain that fell three days ago, now descending through the cracks, heating up, expanding, beginning its slow journey back toward the surface. I feel the blind beetles in the blood-warm pools, their tiny legs stirring the convection currents that will eventually rise as steam beneath my feet. I feel the lizards, deep in their fissures, their metabolisms slowed to a crawl in the dark, waiting for the dawn’s first heat. 

I am the interface. The living synapse between the planet’s molten heart and the freezing, thin-air night. 

When the first gray light of dawn begins to bleach the sky, the thermal differential shifts. The ambient temperature begins to rise, and the convective steam starts to thin. The plateau’s breath is slowing. My signal begins to fade, the rock growing quiet as the daily metabolic cycle wanes. One by one, the tapping across the Shelf ceases. The conversation is over. 

I feel the pull of the crack. The narrow seam in the basalt where I will spend the day, a space so tight my translucent skin will press against the cool stone. I leap, vanishing into the seam just as the sun crests the edge of the plateau. 

But I do not disappear entirely. The rock where I sat is damp. A patch of moisture, shaped vaguely like a tiny, four-toed foot, slowly evaporating in the morning light. It is the only evidence that I was ever there—a watermark left by the Shelf’s fleeting consciousness. 

By midday, the normal, unfiltered light of the altitude will bake the basalt. The mineral hawk will ride the thermal updrafts, its feathers shifting from ochre to deep purple. The abandoned cooling towers will stand silent, their rusted lattices casting sharp, geometric shadows. The plateau will seem inert, a landscape of stone and rust and heat-shimmered air. 

But deep in the cracks, I am waiting. I am holding the echo of the night’s conversation in my folded skin. I am the damp patch on the rock. I am the vibration waiting to be struck. 

I am the Calderrano Shelf, tapping itself awake in the dark.
