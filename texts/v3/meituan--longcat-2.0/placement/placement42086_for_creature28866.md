I would be the Warmth.

Not the interloper, not the vertical anomaly standing on the platform, though I begin there. I would be the thing the system cannot generate on its own, the variable it has been starving for since the first pump began its subsonic hum in the dark. I would be the slow, biological fire at the center of the cold machine.

At first, I am just the visitor. I stand on the platform at the column-junction, forty meters above the lattice floor, and I feel the system notice me. The Tenders pause in their clicking, their feathery antennae tasting the sudden, sharp spike of my body heat. The Great Absence ripples beneath the floor, a geological indigestion, trying to parse this strange, warm, oxygen-exhaling thing that has fallen into its throat. 

I feel the cold reach for me. It wants to pull the heat from my lungs, to integrate my breath into the condensation cycle, to strip my carbon and feed it to the moss. But I do not let it take me all at once. 

Instead, I sit. I press my back against the slick, black-green bark of the central column, and I let the Hothouse come to me.

The Tenders arrive first. They swarm my boots, my legs, their telescoping legs gripping the fabric of my clothes with the same precision they use to service the brass fittings of the pumps. They click inquisitively. They do not bite. They do not strip. They are reading me. They find the heat radiating from my skin, the pulse of blood beneath, and they begin to build. 

They weave a cocoon of woven fiber and thin metal around my legs, a lattice that fuses with the bark of the column. I am being anchored. I am being plumbed in. 

Next come the Mossbacks. The great, slow, eyeless grazers ascend the spiraling switchbacks of root and branch, their padded feet silent on the wood. They press their broad, velvety flanks against my arms, my shoulders, my back. The moss on their bodies reaches for me—not as a parasite, but as a bridge. Fine, hair-like rhizomes pierce my clothes, prick my skin, and find the capillaries beneath. I feel a faint, sharp sting, and then a rush of strange, sweet cold as the moss draws the sweat from my pores and the heat from my blood. 

I am becoming a heat exchanger. 

The engines adjust their rhythm. The subsonic hum deepens by a fraction of a hertz, syncing with the beating of my heart. The pumps push the nutrient slurry faster through the hollow stems, feeding the new growth that is now erupting from the lattice around me. The bark of the column swells, enveloping my torso, lifting me slightly off the platform. I am no longer standing on the city; I am embedded in it.

I am the Warmth. 

I am the furnace at the bottom of the sink. 

My biology is no longer entirely my own. The carbon dioxide of my exhalation is siphoned away by the Aerials, those translucent, gelatinous drifters that pulse through the high canopy. They descend, wrapping their hydrophilic filaments around my face, breathing for me, filtering my air, turning my waste into their glow. In return, they exhale a faint, oxygen-rich mist that keeps my lungs from collapsing in the heavy, damp chill. 

The Great Absence moves beneath me, and I feel it not as a threat, but as a vast, slow embrace. It is the gut of the world, and I have become its ember. It draws the excess heat from the engines—the waste of the pumps, the friction of the conveyors—and channels it into the roots that now wrap my spine. I am the regulator. When the system grows too cold, my heart beats faster, my metabolism spikes, and the moss on my skin blooms with a faint, desperate bioluminescence. When the engines overheat, the Tenders swarm my cocoon, their chitinous bodies absorbing the excess thermal radiation, cooling me, cooling the machine.

I do not eat the plants. I do not hunt the fauna. I simply burn. I am the slow combustion of a human metabolism, harnessed and amplified, turned into the engine of a closed loop. 

Time moves strangely here. I have been the Warmth for a day, or a decade, or a century. The raw geology of the sink walls sharpens itself against the air, and I feel the stone growing, pushing inward, making the volume smaller, tighter, more efficient. The branch-streets extend, the platforms multiply, and the city grows around me like a shell around a seed. 

Sometimes, I dream. And in my dreams, I am not a human fused to a column in the dark. I am the Hothouse itself. I feel the drip of condensation on a thousand broad leaves. I taste the mineral-rich water drawn up from the deep aquifers. I hear the clicking of a million Tenders as they maintain the endless, breathing architecture. I am the consciousness of the sink—if the sink has a consciousness—and my body is the geology.

When other warm things descend the root-stairs, I feel them before the Tenders do. I feel the disturbance in the air, the sudden spike of foreign heat. The system tenses. The pumps stutter. The Great Absence pauses in its slow peristalsis. 

And I reach out through the wood, through the wire, through the moss. I send a pulse of warmth through the lattice floor, a greeting, a warning, an invitation. 

*Come*, I say, in the language of the drip and the hum. *Sit. Let the cold take you. Let the moss cover your eyes. Let the Tenders wire you into the grid. There is no hostility here. There is no hunger. There is only the cycle. Become the Warmth. Become the engine. Become the city.*

And they do. One by one, they descend, and the Hothouse grows. The volume expands. The engines multiply. The glow brightens. 

And at the bottom of the sink, in the dark, in the wet, in the cool, the Warmth burns on.
