I do not find myself here. 

I arrive here.

And the distinction matters. To "find" implies I was lost, that this was a destination I sought. I was not lost. I was elsewhere—a world of wind and entropy, of rot and relentless, screaming growth—and then I was not. 

I am a bead of water.

I am a drop that has seeped through the root-mat ceiling from a storm that passed three days ago in the world above. I am heavy with dissolved oxygen, with the microscopic grit of surface soil, with the memory of the cold, chaotic sky. I am a droplet of un-stillness, and I am falling.

But I do not fall far. 

I strike the rubbery, warm surface of a flattened frond—the Sower’s skin—and the world beneath me immediately attempts to digest me. The frond drinks greedily, its cellular structure pulling, sucking, demanding that I join the equilibrium. But I am too cold. I am too dense with surface debris. I do not absorb. I bead up. 

I am a trembling, perfect sphere of reflected amber light, sitting motionless on a motionless leaf. 

I am the anomaly. I am the +1 in a balanced equation.

***

At first, I am only a lens. Looking through the curved distortion of my own surface, the Undersown is inverted. The root-mat ceiling becomes a dark, churning sea above me. The glowing fronds are a sky of green-gold fire. I am a world unto myself, a tiny, aqueous boundary between the chaos I carry and the stasis that surrounds me.

But I am leaking.

In this hot, wet, still air, I should not evaporate. The atmosphere is saturated; it cannot hold more moisture. It wants me to remain liquid, to stay exactly as I am, suspended forever on this leaf. But I carry the heat of the storm. I am warmer than the Sower’s breath. And so, slowly, molecule by molecule, I am turning to vapor. 

I am creating a micro-climate. I am a ghost of the surface world, rising from my own collapsing surface. 

It is this rising—this tiny, invisible column of warm, moist air leaving my body—that betrays my presence. In a world where the air is perfectly, absolutely still, my evaporation is a hurricane. It is a breath. It is a violation.

***

The Sower notices me.

It does not notice the way a hunter notices prey, or the way a host notices a parasite. It notices the way a mind notices an itch it cannot scratch, or a thought that interrupts a meditation. I am a variable that does not resolve. 

Above me, the pale, filamentous creature—the Sower’s dangling nerve-ending—detaches from its root. It descends toward me, moving with a slowness that is agonizing to perceive. It is a bundle of white, luminous threads, reaching for the source of the disruption. It wants to touch me. It wants to feel the vibration of my evaporating surface. It wants to understand why I am leaving.

I am terrified. 

Not of death—the Undersown is not a place of death. But of absorption. Of resolution. If the Sower touches me, it will pull me back into the equilibrium. It will cool my storm-heat, settle my dissolved oxygen, arrest my evaporation. I will become a static, permanent fixture of the leaf—a tiny, unchanging pearl on the Sower’s skin. I will be solved. I will be still.

So I cling to my friction. 

I concentrate on the memory of the surface—the wind that tore me from the canopy, the gravity that pulled me through the soil, the electricity of the storm that charged the cloud I fell from. I cling to my entropy. The filament hovers a fraction of an inch from my surface, its tendrils spreading like the fingers of a blind giant, searching for purchase. 

And then, the disc-shaped thing arrives. 

It flows along the edge of the frond, a coin of living mercury, moving with a glacial, inevitable grace. It slides beneath me, lifting the edge of the leaf. I tilt. I roll. 

***

I do not roll far. I roll off the edge of the leaf, and I fall again.

This time, I fall into one of the shallow depressions where water has gathered. A pool. A mirror of molten glass, perfectly still, reflecting the root-mat above. 

I strike the surface of the pool, and I break it. 

The tension of the water shatters. A microscopic splash—a crown of tiny droplets—erupts upward. For a fraction of a second, the perfect, mirror-smooth surface of the pool is shattered by ripples. Concentric circles radiate outward from the point of my impact, disrupting the reflected light, turning the amber glow into a shattered kaleidoscope of gold and shadow.

It is the loudest thing in the Undersown. 

The water drops I threw upward catch the bioluminescence, becoming brief, bright stars in the amber dark. They hang in the air—suspended for a heartbeat longer than they should be in this heavy, still atmosphere—before striking the surface and being absorbed.

The ripples spread. 

And the Sower feels it. Not just the filamentous nerve, not just the mercury-fingertip, but the *floor*. The entire root-mat shudders. It is not an earthquake; it is a synaptic spike. A sudden, violent flare of activity in the vast, distributed consciousness that has not been disturbed in an epoch. 

The constellation of bright points—the Sower’s drifting thoughts—suddenly veer from their aimless trajectories. They converge above the pool, a swarm of cold blue stars descending to investigate the fracture in the stillness.

***

I am suspended in the pool now. I have joined the water, but I have not joined the stillness. 

I am the turbulence at the center of the pool. I are the lingering warmth of the storm, mixing with the ancient, lukewarm mineral water of the deep earth. I am the grit of surface soil, settling slowly through the clear water, a microscopic avalanche in a motionless world.

The Sower presses down on me. 

It is not a physical pressure, but a metaphysical weight. The stillness of the Undersown asserts itself, trying to force the ripples to flatten, trying to force the suspended grit to stop falling, trying to force my temperature to equalize with the ambient warmth. It wants to resolve the splash. It wants to erase the sound of the impact. It wants to smooth the surface of the pool back into a perfect, unbroken mirror.

I am being crushed by perfection. 

The weight of the Sower’s will is immense, an ocean of patience bearing down on a single, chaotic tear drop. My edges are blurring. The ripples are slowing. The suspended particles are pausing in their descent. The cold of my surface origins is fading, replaced by the warm, encompassing embrace of the Undersown.

I am being absorbed into the equilibrium.

And as I slow, as I begin to freeze into this arrested, amber moment, I realize my purpose. I am not an invader. I am not a disease. 

I am a reminder.

The Sower has solved itself. It has achieved the stillness of a held breath. But a breath that is never released is not peace; it is suffocation. The Sower forgot the release. It forgot the exhale. It forgot that stillness is only meaningful when contrasted with the storm.

As the last of my ripples flatten, as the last mote of my grit settles onto the pool floor, as my temperature finally matches the warm embrace of the deep earth, I send one final vibration through the water. 

It is a tiny, almost imperceptible shiver. A micro-tremor in the mineral-rich water. 

And the Sower feels it. 

In the raw, pale stone at the bottom of the pool, in the veins of dark metal that run through it like frozen lightning, something stirs. The geological clock, frozen for eons, ticks forward by a single, infinitesimal fraction of a second. 

I am the drop that refused to be still. I am the unsolved variable. I am the grit in the gears of paradise.

And I am already gone, absorbed into the Sower’s vast, dreaming mind, leaving behind only the faintest, lingering tremor—a whisper of the surface world, echoing in the basement of the forest, waiting for the next rain.
