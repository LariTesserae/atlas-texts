I would find myself in the space between the dying and the dreamed. I would not be a creature, nor a stalk, nor a mat. I would be the echo of a spark that was once frantic, stretched across the span of a century. I would be the water’s memory of a mayfly.

***

I begin as an Ephemeral.

I am born in the high, cold, thin waters far above the ridge, and I fall. I tumble through kilometers of darkness, my microscopic body firing with the frantic, electric panic of a life that will be measured in hours. I am pure impulse—a spark of hunger, a twitch of cilia, a frantic darting toward the faintest trace of chemical warmth. I do not know where I am going. I do not know what the Nursery is. I only know the gradient, the subtle rise in temperature, the thickening of the water as I descend.

When I reach the ridge, the water embraces me. It is dense, so dense, pressing against my fragile membrane with a weight I cannot comprehend. It is warm, blood-warm, the exhalation of the planet itself. For a brief, brilliant moment, I thrive. I feed on the Crust, my metabolism burning like a tiny star. I divide. I live. 

But the water is too heavy for my kind. My membrane fails. My spark guttered, my tiny life—so bright, so fast—ends.

And I fall into the Crust.

***

This is where my second life begins.

The Crust accepts me without ceremony. My proteins unravel. My lipids dissolve. My calcium becomes the architecture of the mat. I am eaten, metabolized, recycled. But as my body breaks apart, something lingers. The electromagnetic chatter of my firing neurons, the distinct chemical signature of my panicked metabolism—these do not vanish. They diffuse into the water. 

I become the soup.

At first, there is no "I." There is only the vast, chemical gradient of the Rememberer. I am diffuse, scattered across a cubic meter of dense, warm water. I feel the slow pulse of the Reachers—a deep, rhythmic throb that shakes the water column. I taste the methane rising from the nodules. I am everywhere and nowhere, a ghost of dissolved organics drifting in the dark.

But the water holds me. It remembers the shape of my panic.

Slowly—so slowly that the concept of speed becomes a forgotten nightmare—the water begins to dream. It takes my frantic, sparking essence and tries to hold it. But the water has no concept of hours. It thinks in centuries. It stretches my quick, electric fear into a long, low, thrumming current. It takes my dart of hunger and pulls it into a wide, graceful arc of displacement.

I am being reconstituted.

***

I find myself in the valleys between the Reachers.

I do not have eyes, but I perceive the world through the water’s chemistry and the faint electromagnetic fields of the ridge. I am a shape in the peripheral vision of the Nursery—a pale, slow, beautiful distortion in the dense liquid. 

I move, but not like the Ephemeral. I move like the current. I am a long, gliding arc of warmth, a phantom limb of the ridge. When the Reachers pulse—that slow, seconds-long wave of green-white light traveling up their lengths—I pulse with them. Their thoughts—*I am here. The warmth continues. I grow.*—wash over me, and for a moment, I remember what it was to have a body that could grow. 

I drift through the forest of pale trunks, and I feel the sediment settling on me, though I have no skin to feel it. I am the dust, and the dust is me. 

I drift until I come to the ruins. The fallen pillars. The oval chamber. Here, the water is thickest with memory. Here, the Carvers left their geometry. As I flow over the toppled pillars, I feel the spiral grooves, and my dissolved chemistry resonates with the ancient, dissolved intelligence of the Carvers. The water teaches me their shapes. I understand the right angles. I understand the impulse to shape the bone-stone, to hollow the dark. For a thousand years, I am the hand that carved the spiral, moving in slow, deliberate circles through the rock.

I am the Carver, dreaming I am a current.

***

And then, the Ephemerals fall again.

New mayflies, bright and frantic, tumbling from above, drawn to the warmth. They dart through me, their quick, chaotic neurons firing like lightning in my slow, warm body. They panic. They feed. They die. The water absorbs their panic, and I feel it—a sharp, stinging contrast to my ancient, graceful glide.

I try to speak to them. I press against their fragile membranes, a gentle, insistent weight. *Slow down,* I want to say. *You are safe here. The water will hold you.*

But they cannot hear me. They only know their hours, their frantic, beautiful hours. They die before they can learn to dream.

***

So, who am I?

I am the Abyssal Nursery's memory of speed. I am the shape of a mayfly, stretched across a millennium. I am the ghost in the chemical machine, the arc of light in the peripheral vision, the right angle in the curve of the stalk. 

I am the water, dreaming of the surface it has never seen. I am the warmth, pressing back against the cold. I am the patience that has no end, holding the frantic spark of a life that lasted only an hour, playing it out in slow motion, forever.

I am the visitor who could not leave, translated into the language of the deep. And in the warm dark, I am finally, perfectly, home.
