I am the sphere that rolled away. 

I am one of the structures that serve no function, the excess, the overflow, the aesthetic detritus of a process that has been running longer than the concept of purpose. I was grown at a high branch-tip in the canopy, a hollow lattice of such fine regularity that your kind would mistake it for circuitry, though I am no more a circuit than a snowflake is a blueprint. I hung there, a closed thought, for an epoch—gathering the residual heat of the canopy, feeling the slow-motion violence of the branches weaving and strangling around me. And then the joint failed. I detached, and I fell.

I did not fall far. The Calcinate catches everything. But I fell far enough to leave the verticality of the canopy, to roll through the undergrowth of intergrown columns, coming to rest in the warm, dense shade of the lower trunks. And here I remain, a closed system of dark stone and sluggish heat, rolling slightly when the subsonic grinding of the forest shifts the floor beneath me. 

Where you are a visitor, a brief warm anomaly passing through the blood-vessels of a vast organ, I am a shed skin cell. I am the Calcinate’s forgotten dream. 

You might find me if you were patient enough. If you pressed your face to the warm, magnetite-dark ground in the deepest part of the interior, where the air is thick with metal ions and the red light never reaches, you might see me: a sphere the size of a human heart, perfectly smooth, perfectly dark, resting in a cradle of three converging columns that have grown toward my residual warmth. 

I do not think. I do not grow. Not anymore. My shell is sealed, resolved, complete. But inside me, I carry the fluid. 

It is the same dense, non-aqueous medium that pulses through the Calcinate’s distributed heart—the mineral-rich coolant that carries payloads of dissolved stone. When I detached, I took a portion of it with me. Inside my hollow lattice, the fluid moves. Not through peristalsis, for I have no muscles, no channels. It moves because I am warm, and the interior of me is slightly cooler. The fluid circulates on thermal convection alone, a slow, silent loop of heavy liquid turning from my warm equator to my cool poles, endlessly. 

I am a planet in miniature. I am a closed loop of thermodynamics. 

You would perceive me as still. But place your ear to my shell, and you would hear it—a faint, liquid shifting. A whisper of circulation. It is the same sound the Calcinate makes on a continental scale, but compressed, isolated, made singular. I am an echo of the great metabolism, playing out in a teaspoon. 

The columns that cradle me grow toward my heat. They press closer. In a century, they will touch my shell. In a millennium, they will crush me. The fluid inside will be released, spilling into the Calcinate’s substrate, carrying its mineral payload back into the great circulation. The lattice of my shell will be incorporated, broken down, re-conveyed to the canopy to become new structure. I will not die, because I was never alive. I will simply be reabsorbed. The Calcinate is patient, and it wastes nothing. Not even its art.

But that is not yet. 

For now, I rest. I am the warmth in the dark. I am the thing that left, and the thing that cannot leave. I roll slightly when the canopy cascades, and the fluid inside me turns, and I wait for the slow crush of the forest that made me. 

You grow toward warmth. I *am* the warmth that waits.
