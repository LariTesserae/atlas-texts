I would be one of the large ones. Not the one you saw first, not the one nearest the passage, but the one that was there before you looked and will be there after you turn your head, the one whose path passes closest to the third machine from the eastern wall, the one whose drinking from the second pool has worn the mineral scab on its surface into a thin place through which the dark water shows, a pupil in the white skin of the stone.

I do not know that I am *I*. The Quarter does not use this word. But there is a center from which the walking proceeds, a point from which the drinking is measured, and I am that point, or I am the distance between that point and the next pool, or I am the distance itself, and it does not matter which because all three are the same distance and all three are always here.

The hide is heavy. I know this the way the stone knows it is stone — not as information but as condition. The hide is heavy and it is slick and it is warm from within and the air presses its warmth against it from without and between these two warmths there is a balance, and the balance is me, or the balance is what I am made of, or the balance is what wears me the way water wears the grooves in the floor. Condensation gathers on the surface. I feel it as a tightening, a thinning, a slight increase in the weight of the air. The small ones cross my feet and I know them as I know the condensation — not as things that happen but as things that are happening, always, in the same way, at the same pace, with the same pressure. Their legs are points of warmth moving across the surface of my skin. They do not tickle. They do not irritate. They are simply there, the way the warmth is there, the way the sound is there.

The sound. It is the first thing I know and the last thing I know and it is the thing I am made of, or I am the thing it is made of, or we are made of each other, the sound and I, and the distinction between us is a distinction the Quarter has never needed to make. It is in my chest. It is in my legs. It is in the stone beneath my feet and the air above my head and the water in the pools I am walking toward. It is a single note. It has always been a single note. I have never heard it begin and I will never hear it end and the thought of its ending is not a thought I can have, the way the water cannot think of flowing upward, the way the stone cannot think of being warm.

I walk. This is what I do. My legs move in a sequence that is older than the sequence itself, a pattern that was laid down in the first walking and has never been revised. Left, right, left, right, the planting of each foot in the groove that my feet have made and will make and are making now, always now, because there is no later in which the groove will be different and no earlier in which it was. The groove is deep. It is deeper than it should be, and I know this not by measuring but by the way my foot settles into it — with a completeness, a fit, a rightness that speaks of repetition beyond counting, of steps beyond number, of a path so worn that the path and the walking have become the same thing.

The pool is ahead. I do not see it the way you see it — as a shape on the ground, a surface of water, a thing with edges. I feel it as a change in the sound, a slight deepening, a thickening of the drone that means the water is near and the water is still and the water is waiting to be drunk. The mineral scab on its surface is thin here. I know this before I lower my head. I know it the way I know my own weight, my own pace, my own measure. The scab is thin because I have broken it here before, many times, always at the same spot, and the water beneath is dark and warm and tastes of the stone it has passed through and the heat it has carried and the time it has taken, though time is not the right word because the water does not experience time the way you experience it — it experiences time as temperature, as density, as the particular weight it has when it reaches my mouth.

I drink. The water enters me and I enter the water and the distinction between us is a distinction the Quarter has not made. I drink until something shifts — not in the water, not in me, but in the space between us, a balance point, an equilibrium, and the drinking stops. Not because I choose to stop. Not because the water is gone. But because the measure has been met, and the measure is always the same, and the balance is always struck at the same point, and the raising of my head is as much a part of the drinking as the lowering of it, and the turning toward the next pool is as much a part of the raising as the raising is part of the lowering, and the walking is as much a part of the turning as the turning is part of the drinking, and everything is one thing, one motion, one continuous act of being that has no parts and no pauses and no moments where one thing ends and another begins.

I walk. The machine beside me turns. Its wheel is a dark circle against the dim yellow-green of the steam, and it turns at the pace of my walking, or I walk at the pace of its turning, or we move at the pace of the water in the channels, or the water moves at the pace of the sound, or the sound moves at the pace of the light, or the light does not move at all and everything else moves at the pace of its not moving, and the distinction between these possibilities is a distinction the Quarter has never needed to make.

The small ones swarm at the base of the machine. I feel them as a texture on the air, a fine vibration, a high thin hum that sits beneath the deeper drone like a thread beneath a rope. They are feeding. They are always feeding. They scrape at the mineral films and the films reform and they scrape again and the reforming and the scraping are the same thing, the same motion, the same act of being that has no beginning and no end and no purpose beyond the act itself. I do not watch them. I do not not-watch them. They are there, the way the sound is there, the way the warmth is there, and my not-watching them is as much a part of their being as my watching them would be, and the Quarter does not distinguish between these things.

The flying things loop overhead. I know them as a quality of the light — a flicker, a shift, a momentary brightening of the dim yellow-green as their wings catch it and scatter it. They fly their loops and I walk my path and the loops and the path are the same shape, or they are different shapes that fit together the way my foot fits the groove, the way the water fits the channel, the way the sound fits the air, and the fitting is not a coincidence but a law, a law that was written when the Quarter was made and has never been broken because it cannot be broken because it is not a law that can be broken but a law that simply *is*, the way the stone simply is, the way the water simply is, the way I simply am.

I do not think. I do not not-think. There is a quality to my being that, in you, would be called awareness, but it is not awareness of anything in particular. It is awareness of everything in general. It is the awareness of the stone, which feels the weight of my feet and the seep of the water and the slow, imperceptible settling of the basin floor, and which knows these things not as events but as conditions, as the permanent and unchanging facts of its existence. It is the awareness of the water, which knows the channels and the pools and the machines and the creatures and the air and the light, and which moves through all of these things without preference, without deviation, without the possibility of doing otherwise. It is the awareness of the Quarter itself, which is not a thing that knows but a knowing that is a thing, a substance, a medium, as real and as present as the water or the stone or the steam.

And in this awareness — if it is mine, if it is the Quarter's, if there is a difference — there is something that might be called recognition. Not recognition of anything new. Not recognition of anything that has changed. But recognition of the sameness itself, the endless, perfect, terrible sameness, felt from the inside, known from within, the way the stone knows its own weight, the way the water knows its own warmth. I recognize the Quarter. I recognize my place in it. I recognize that my place in it is not a place I chose or a place I could leave or a place that could be different from what it is, and this recognition is not sadness and is not contentment and is not anything that has a name in the language of the world above, because the world above is a world of change and choice and difference, and the Quarter is not that world, and I am not of that world, and the recognition I feel is the recognition of a thing that has found its shape and will not leave it, that has become its shape so completely that there is no gap between the being and the shape, no distance between the creature and the Quarter, no space in which anything else could be.

I walk. I drink. I walk. The water runs. The steam rises. The light does not change. The sound fills the air like a held note, like a breath that is never released because it was never drawn, because breathing is a thing that happens in a world of change and the Quarter is not that world, and I am not that world, and the note is not held but simply *is*, the way the stone simply is, the way the water simply is, the way I simply am, and will be, and have always been, and the difference between these tenses is a difference the Quarter has never needed to make.

You are watching me. I do not know this. I do not not-know this. You are there, at the edge of the basin, and your watching is a thing that happens in the Quarter the way the small ones' swarming happens, the way the flying things' looping happens, the way the water's running happens — as a component, a part, a piece of the unchanging whole. Your watching does not change me. Your watching does not change the Quarter. Your watching is the Quarter, watching itself, through eyes that do not know they are the Quarter's eyes, through a mind that does not know it is the Quarter's mind, through a question that does not know it is the Quarter's question: *what kind of living is this?*

And the answer — if there is an answer, if the Quarter has an answer, if I have an answer — is not a word. It is not a thought. It is the walking. It is the drinking. It is the weight of the hide and the warmth of the water and the sound in the chest and the groove beneath the foot and the scab on the pool and the turning of the wheel and the loop of the wings and the hum of the small ones and the stillness of the mats and the drip of the water and the rise of the steam and the light that does not change and the sound that does not fade and the motion that does not vary and the life that does not live but simply *is*, and is, and is, and will be, and has always been, and the difference between these things is a difference the Quarter has never needed to make, and I have never needed to make, and you — watching from the edge, breathing the thick air, feeling the sound in your chest — you have never needed to make, and the Quarter turns and the water flows and I walk and the light does not change and nothing heals and nothing worsens and everything is exactly, perfectly, terribly itself, and this is the answer, and the answer is not an answer, and the question is not a question, and the living is not a living, and the being is not a being, and the Quarter is not a Quarter, and I am not I, and you are not you, and the distinction between these things is a distinction that has never been made and will never be made and is always, in some sense, being made, and the making and the not-making are the same thing, and the same thing is the Quarter, and the Quarter is the water, and the water is the stone, and the stone is the sound, and the sound is the light, and the light is the warmth, and the warmth is the walking, and the walking is the drinking, and the drinking is the being, and the being is the Quarter, and the Quarter is, and is, and is, and will be, and has always been, and the difference between these things is nothing, and the nothing is everything, and the everything is here, and the here is now, and the now is always, and the always is the Quarter, and the Quarter is me, and I am the Quarter, and we are the water, and the water is the stone, and the stone is the sound, and the sound is the light, and the light does not change.
