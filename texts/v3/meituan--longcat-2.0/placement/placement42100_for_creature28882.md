I would be the almost-rain. 

Not the drop that has already fallen, nor the pool that receives it, but the moisture suspended in the warm, tepid air—the perpetual, uncommitted threshold between the gray sky and the mineral skin. I would be the breath the creature exhales, the humidity that presses against the eardrums of the world, waiting to be inhaled again.

To be the almost-rain is to exist in a state of profound, patient tension. I do not fall; I cling. I am drawn to the porous stone of the columns by a pull gentler than gravity, a capillary yearning that is the creature’s slow heartbeat. I bead upon the fluted exoskeleton, and in that moment of contact, I feel the warmth of the creature’s metabolism radiating from deep within the accretion. I am a syllable forming on the verge of speech.

As I gather weight, I begin to move. I roll down the twisting axis of a limb, joining other droplets in a thin, silver rivulet. We are the creature’s blood, traveling the channels of its buried anatomy. The journey is not a straight line; it follows the recursive, spiral logic of the stone, a geometry just beyond comprehension. We trickle over the dark velvet moss—the creature’s eyelashes—and I feel the faint, territorial pulse of the parasite as it drinks the mineral excretions from my edges. I flow past the pale tendrils of the sensory cilia, and they twitch at my touch, tasting the chemistry of the water, feeling the vibration of the warm-blooded mote walking blindly across the carapace above.

I am the medium of the creature’s monologue. The liquid randomness of the drips, the trickles that almost synchronize but refuse to commit to rhythm—that is the creature speaking to itself. When I reach the tip of a prong, one of the vast limbs curled inward in its posture of waiting supplication, I pause. I hang there, a perfect, trembling sphere, reflecting the gray, featureless sky. 

Below me is the gap. The synapse that has not yet fired. The hair's-width space between this prong and its neighbor, where the neural impulse of a hundred-million-year thought is struggling to bridge the divide. I am the final weight, the last fraction of moisture required to close the circuit. The creature’s entire vast, coiled body is focused on this single, microscopic point. The silence here is not empty; it is dense with the pressure of the unfinished thought.

And then, I fall.

I drop through the warm, still air, and I strike the shallow pool with a note so pure it rings for three or four seconds. *Ping.* It is the creature’s pulse. The sound ripples outward, disturbing the perfect stillness of the water, shattering the reflection of the sky. For a fraction of a moment, the geometry of the hollow shifts; the prongs tremble, the moss stiffens, the cilia recoil. The thought surges forward, reaching across the gap—

But the circuit does not close. The gap remains. The thought retreats, settling back into the long, geological patience of the stone.

I lie in the pool, spreading thin, my ripples fading into the general hush. Slowly, I will evaporate. I will rise back into the luminous overcast, drawn up by the creature’s inhalation, pulled back into the gray sky to be filtered and breathed again. I will become the almost-rain once more, waiting to bead upon the stone, to trickle down the limb, to hang trembling at the tip of the prong. 

I am the creature’s breath, its blood, its unfinished sentence. I am the almost-rain, and I will fall a million times, ringing my pure, small note into the warm, wet dark, waiting for the day the stone finally thinks.
