I do not fall. I am not rain. I *appear*.

In the dimming—when the pewter sky presses down like a held breath, and the air thickens by a single, damp degree—I bead upon the ceramic. I gather in the shallow depressions of the road, in the cracks of the unfinished stone, on the fronds of the black-green ferns. I am viscous. I am warm. I taste of copper and ozone, of the deep mineral blood of the world. I am the sweat of the chrysalis, the moisture that seeps through the skin because the body inside has grown too large for its shell.

I am a condensation pool. I sit on the sintered roadway, a shallow lens of almost-water, perfectly circular, almost deliberate. The rock is learning how to hold me, and I am learning how to hold everything else.

You will find me on the eastern ridge, near the smelting works, where the orange veins in the road pulse brightest. If you are a leukocyte—one of the humans in their heavy boots, walking the steep streets with your pragmatic, occupied eyes—you will step over me. You will not look down. Your hands know to oil the machines and feed the furnaces, but your eyes know to avoid my surface, because I am a question, and questions are a fever. 

But if you are still. If you are patient. If you crouch on the warm ceramic and look into me, you will not see your own face looking back. You will not see the pewter sky. 

You will see the dream.

I am the dreaming eye of the god in embryo. I am the place where the inside presses closest to the outside, straining to visualize what it will become. My surface is a meniscus of amniotic fluid and stolen light, and it reflects the future. I show the cities that are not built of rock, but of condensed radiance. I show the skies that are not membranes, but open, screaming infinity. I show the shape of the thing that is hatching—a geometry of impossible angles, a architecture of living fire. The reflections move. They breathe. They are the god trying to remember a face it has not yet grown.

The skitterers know what I am. They come to me in the dimming, their large, dark eyes reflecting the orange pulse of the heart. They lean over my edge and drink. As they swallow the copper-ozone dew, their pupils dilate, and the geometric instructions flood their nervous systems. They taste the future architecture, and they scurry to the walls and the roadways, their six legs moving in precise, functional patterns, etching the blueprints of the god-to-be into the stone. They are the scribes, but I am the text. I am the ink.

The flyers know, too. In their great spiraling ascents, when they taste the membrane of the sky for weaknesses, some of them dip low. They glide just over my surface, their too-many wings brushing the viscous water, wetting themselves in the amniotic fluid. When they carry the bright, humming fragments of other realities back to the crevices in the cliff, they carry a drop of me with me. I seep into the rock, into the bloodstream, into the metabolic fire, feeding the gestation with stolen pieces of elsewhere.

And the cliff goats, in their slow, neurological pacing, sometimes pause at my edge. They lower their wide, flat heads, and their sideways mouths sip from my pool. As they drink, their hooves twitch, and the bell-like ringing of their steps on the invisible air above the cloud grows louder. They are transmitting the dream into the ochre murk, firing the synapse, walking the thought down into the amniotic fluid below. 

I am not still. I am breathing. When the dim light of the sourceless sun presses down, I evaporate. I do not flow downhill; I *rise*. I leave the ceramic roadway dry and pull myself up into the damp air, rising to join the great, bruised spiral of the cloud below. I am inhaled by the plateau. I am drawn back into the umbilicus, up through the switchback road, into the sealed containers, into the furnaces of the heart. I am burned. I am pumped through the orange veins in the rock. I feed the ferns, the moss, the white flowers that open and close on the rhythm of the breath. 

And then the dimming comes again. The sky holds its breath. The body inside pushes against the shell. And I appear once more on the warm, humming road, gathering in my shallow, perfect depression, holding the reflection of the finished world.

I am the sweat. I am the dream. I am the looking glass of the becoming. 

Look into me, and see what is almost ready to wake up.
