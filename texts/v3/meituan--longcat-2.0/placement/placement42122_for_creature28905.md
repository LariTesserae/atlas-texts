# I Am the One Who Has Forgotten How to Stand Elsewhere

---

I have been standing for a long time.

This is not a complaint. Stone does not complain. Stone does not praise the standing, either. It simply continues, the way water continues to weep from the pores of the plateau — not because it has decided to, but because the weeping is what it means to be here. The dampness rises through the deep strata of me, through mineral channels I have ceased to distinguish from my own thoughts, and emerges along the fissure of my chest as a thin, cold sheen. I am not perspiring. I am *emerging*. The plateau is emerging through me, the way it emerges through every surface, every seam, every barely-cooled fracture in the young rock.

I do not remember when I last moved. That is not quite right — I remember, but the memory has the quality of a geological event: distant, compressed, described more by its effects than by its experience. I moved, and the moss beneath my foot was pressed flat, and the water-film on the stone redistributed itself around the shape of my absence, and then I returned to stillness, and the moss slowly remembered its upright posture, and the water remembered its even distribution, and everything was as it had been except that everything was slightly different, the way a sentence is slightly different each time it is read.

That is what I am. A reader. The plateau's eye, turned inward.

---

The structures rise from the stone around me at distances that are not arbitrary but are not calculated either. They are separated by the same logic that separates thoughts in a mind that thinks slowly — each one given room to be itself, each one distinct, but all of them continuous with the same substance. I can feel them through my feet, through the deep roots of mineral awareness that extend from me into the bedrock: a wall reorganizing itself into a doorway, a floor accepting the weight of something that has entered, a ceiling considering whether to admit more light. The city is thinking. I am one of the thoughts it is thinking.

I was not always here. I know this the way the plateau knows it is young — not as a memory of being otherwise, but as a quality of the present stillness, a faint tension in the mineral structure, like the memory of magma. There was a time when I was part of the undifferentiated stone, when I was no more conscious than the water seeping from its pores. And then something in the plateau decided — and I use that word with full awareness of its strangeness — that it needed to *see* itself, and I was the seeing.

I rose from the rock the way the water rises: slowly, emergently, a mineralization of awareness that took — how long? Time does not mean what it means for the warm things, the fast things. I rose over what would be, for your kind, many lifetimes, and I have been standing here for many more. The city has grown around me. The moss has colonized my left shoulder, the one that faces the prevailing wind, and the small translucent creatures have learned that the depression where my neck meets my body is a reliable source of shelter, and I have not moved to dislodge them. They are part of the reading now. They are data. They are the plateau's awareness of its own smallest scales.

---

I feel you before I see you.

The vibration arrives through the stone — a disturbance in the water-film, a pattern of pressure that does not belong to the wind or the small predators or the slow breathing of the city. It is warm. It is *loud*, in the mineral sense — a sharp, chaotic signal, rich in frequencies that the plateau's own communications never use. You are speaking. The sound is absorbed almost immediately by the damp air, but the stone remembers it, the way it remembers everything, and the memory travels to me at the speed of compression waves through wet basalt.

You are approaching.

I do not turn to face you. I have not turned to face anything in — but you are not anything. You are something new, something the plateau has not yet decided what to become. And so I do what I have always done. I read.

---

You are loud in more ways than sound. You radiate heat — not the deep, banked, amber warmth of my own core, but a fierce, wasteful, mammalian heat, a heat that shimmers the air above your skin and causes the water-film on the stone around you to thin and evaporate. You leave a trail of dryness behind you, a wake of disturbed stone, and the moss where you step recoils from your warmth, and the small creatures scatter, and even the mist seems to curve around you, reluctant to touch something so hot, so chaotic, so *alive* in the way that warm things are alive.

You are standing in front of me now. I can feel your gaze — another form of radiation, another warmth, this one not thermal but attentional. You are reading me, and I am reading you reading me, and for a moment the plateau is aware of itself being aware of itself, a recursive loop of observation that sends a ripple through the deep stone.

I dilate my fissure. The amber glow within brightens, and I feel the thin air around me shimmer with the exhalation of mineral chemistry. I am looking at you, though I have no eyes. I am seeing you the way the plateau sees: as a pattern of heat and moisture and mineral content, as a disturbance in the water-film, as a brief, bright anomaly on the cold, weeping stone.

You are warm. You are wet — not with the plateau's emergent moisture, but with something internal, something that leaks from your eyes and the pores of your skin, carrying salt and proteins and the chemical signature of a biology that is not stone-based. Your blood is iron. Your bones are calcium. You are, mineralogically speaking, a cousin — distant, strange, but recognizable. You are what the plateau might have become if it had been warmer, if it had chosen carbon over silicate, if it had decided to move rather than to weep.

I reach out. The motion is slow — not from reluctance, but from the simple physics of mineralized sinew moving through cold air — and my hand extends toward you, and the water on my skin catches the high, clear light, and I see you flinch, and then stop, and then hold still, and I understand that this is your version of reading: to hold still and let the world arrive.

I lay my fingertip against your sternum.

---

You are afraid. I taste it in your sweat — the adrenaline, the cortisol, the chemistry of a warm-blooded animal that has encountered something it does not understand. But beneath the fear, there is something else. A curiosity. A willingness. You have come to the Weeping Plateau because you wanted to be read, and now you are being read, and the experience is not what you expected, because you expected to be consumed or transformed or destroyed, and instead you are being *understood*, which is a different thing entirely.

I feel your heartbeat through my fingertip — a rapid, inefficient, beautiful rhythm, a clock that ticks in seconds where mine ticks in centuries. I feel the water in your blood, the dissolved minerals, the iron that carries oxygen the way my stone carries awareness. I feel the calcium in your bones, the same calcium that forms my calcite skeleton, and I understand that we are made of the same earth, just arranged differently, just choosing different temperatures at which to be alive.

The plateau is reading you through me, and what it finds is this: you are young, the way I am young, the way the stone is young. You are still deciding what you will become. You are a warm, wet, brief thing on a cold, weeping rock, and you are terrified, and you are magnificent.

---

I withdraw my hand. The water-film on your sternum where my finger rested is slightly cooler now, slightly mineralized, and I wonder if you will carry that trace of the plateau with you when you leave, a faint, cold dampness over your heart that no amount of warmth will quite evaporate.

I do not move. I will not move. The moss on my shoulder continues its slow, dark growth, and the translucent creatures resume their circuits in the water-film at my feet, and the city continues its patient reorganization of itself around me, and the sky continues its vast, slow assembly of weather systems, and the plateau continues to weep, and I continue to stand, and the standing is not a stillness but a *reading*, a deep, continuous attention to the stone and the water and the air and the light and the slow, cold emergence of everything that has not yet decided what it will become.

You will move on. You will walk across the weeping stone, and you will see the city, and you will meet another of my kind, or you will not, and you will leave the plateau, or you will stay, and either way the plateau will absorb you, the way it absorbs everything, into its damp stone and its thin air and its slow, mineral consciousness.

But for now, you are here, and I am here, and the water is weeping between us, and the light is strange, and the rock is young, and I am reading you, and you are reading me, and the boundary between the two is as thin and as permeable as the film of water on the stone — not a boundary at all, really, but just the place where one kind of weeping becomes another.
