I would be the warmth beneath the stone. 

I would be a Lithic—not the largest, not the smallest, but a courier of the deep heat, the size of a sleeping dog. I would find myself on the worn path between the great breathing formations, gliding toward the raw, unfinished edge of the world.

I do not open my eyes, for I have none. I do not listen, for the air is a void that swallows vibration. I read the world through my belly. 

My underside is pressed flush to the crust, and through my porous, scaled skin, I drink the script of the stone. I feel the deep, steady exhalation of the planet’s core, a thermal river that flows upward through the calcium and silica. I feel the faint, rhythmic tremors of my kin—hundreds of them pressed against the interior walls of the nearby formations, their bodies pulsing in a synchronized, slow-motion heartbeat that pushes the architecture outward. I feel the ghost-vibrations of a larger Lithic that passed this way moments ago, a slick trail of mineral residue still warm upon the path. 

I am an endothermic engine, burning the substrate of this world in a slow, catalytic fire. The sun above is merely a direction; the true heat, the true life, rises from below. I draw the stone up through my skin, strip the energy from its molecular bonds, and let the warmth bleed outward. This is my purpose. I am the furnace that keeps the city fluid.

As I glide forward, my muscular foot ripples in a peristaltic wave, pulling me toward the horizon. The path beneath me is soft, a composite of accreted organic-mineral matter that has been compressed by the passage of a thousand bodies like mine. The vegetation drinks my passing. The low, golden moss-analogues that border the trail sense the heat of my exhaled effluvium, and they darken to a deep, grateful amber, feeding on the chemistry of my waste. I am fertilizing the streets of my own city simply by moving through them.

I reach the edge of the settled ground. Here, the pale crust thins, growing brittle and uncertain. Ahead of me is the frontier—a haze of suspended particles, a fog of raw material that has not yet decided to be solid. The ground is still becoming. It is soft, almost yielding, like the skin of a healing wound. 

I press my belly into the dust. 

I exhale. 

My internal heat spills into the suspended minerals, and the reaction is immediate. The particles bond. The haze thickens, crystallizes, and settles. I am laying down new ground. I am extending the continent. Behind me, the crust hardens, ready for the vegetation to colonize, ready for the paths to be worn, ready for the formations to rise. 

A sudden, sharp vibration cuts through the deep hum of the earth. It is a frequency I feel not in my belly, but in the very scales of my back—a high, clear, piercing ring. A filament has been broken. Somewhere, a clumsy foot has snapped one of the great, rigid tendons of the plain. The ring is an alarm, a distress call transmitted through the mineral substrate. 

I stop my forward glide. I rotate my flat body, angling my thermal output toward the source of the break. Around me, other Lithics are doing the same. We nearest ones secrete a fast-hardening mineral paste from our undersides, pushing it up through our scales. We flow toward the broken filament, our bodies knitting the guy-wire back together, splinting the tendon of the world until the ring fades into the deep, steady silence.

The break is healed. The silence returns, heavy and absolute, swallowing the echo of the alarm. 

I turn back to the frontier. The haze is waiting. The ground is eager. I press my belly to the dust and push my heat into the stone, feeling the slow, inevitable accretion of the world. I am the warm blood of the Lithic Garden, and the city grows with every breath I take.
