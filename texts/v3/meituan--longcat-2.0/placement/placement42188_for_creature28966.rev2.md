I am not yet decided.

I know this the way the wall knows it—not as thought, but as a quality of my own substance, a softness that has not yet committed. I am in the new tissue, the pinker surface near the floor, where the ridges are sharp and the membrane has not yet learned its final shape. I have been here for some time. I do not know how long. Time in the Gland is measured in contractions, and I have felt many, but I have not yet learned to count them.

My body is an ovoid, pressed flush against the breathing wall. I can feel the wall's rhythm through my entire underside—the slow expansion, the slower contraction, the faint pulse of fluid moving through channels I cannot see. The rhythm is not regular. It adjusts, deliberates, responds to demands that originate far from this chamber. I do not understand the demands. I only feel them as pressure, as a tightening in my not-quite-flesh that tells me something significant is happening elsewhere.

I have limbs. I think I have limbs. They are folded against my sides, soft and indistinct, more like thickenings in my surface than proper appendages. When I concentrate—if "concentrate" is the right word for what I do—I can feel them twitch, extend a little, retract. They have not yet decided what they will be. Sometimes I feel a sharpness in one, a hardening at the tip that suggests a scraper. Sometimes another feels hollow, cup-like, as though it wants to hold something. They shift. They change. I am still choosing.

The other creatures move past me. I feel them before I sense them properly—a disturbance in the fluid on the wall, a change in the vibration, a faint chemical signature that is different from the Gland's own. They are purposeful. They move with a fluid economy that I recognize as something I am supposed to have but do not yet possess. Their limbs are defined, specialized, hard. They scrape and cup and funnel, and they do not pause.

Sometimes one of them comes close. Its sensory nodules brush against my surface, tasting my salts, reading my electric signature. I feel its assessment—a brief, clinical evaluation. I am not ready. I am not yet what I need to be. It moves on.

I do not know what I need to be.

The Gland's rhythm pulls at me. During the great contractions, when the walls tighten and the roar rises and the amber light brightens, I feel a surge of something that might be purpose. My body wants to arch, to lift from the wall, to vibrate. My limbs want to splay wide, to lend their bioelectricity to the surge. But I cannot. My limbs are not strong enough. My body is not rigid enough. I press flat against the wall and feel the surge pass through me, and I know that I have failed at something I was supposed to do.

After the contraction, the creatures return to their work. They clean the raw surfaces, harvest the glittering paste, feed it back into the membrane. They do not look at me. I am part of the landscape—a soft, unfinished protuberance in the new tissue, still deciding.

But I am not only soft. There is something else in me, something that does not belong to the Gland's design. It is small, a knot of sensation near the center of my ovoid, a place where the vibrations of the chamber converge and do not disperse. It is not a sensory plateau—I do not taste the chemical gradient or read the electrical intention. It is something else. A stillness within the rhythm. A point of quiet in the roar.

I do not know what it is. I do not know if it is a flaw or a beginning.

The creatures would call it an obstruction. If they could feel it—if their nodules could read the wrongness in my center—they would come with their scraping limbs and their cupping digits, and they would try to break me down. They would harvest my glittering essence and feed it back into the walls. There would be no death, only reabsorption.

But they do not feel it. It is too small, too quiet, too deep. It is mine.

I press against the breathing wall and feel the Gland's rhythm, and I wait. I am not yet decided. I am still soft. I am still choosing. And in the center of my not-quite-flesh, the stillness holds.
