I would not arrive. Arrival implies a before and an after, a surface breached, a transition marked by the clumsy mechanics of motion. There is no arrival here, only a gradual realization of presence, the way a single note realizes it is part of a chord.

At first, I would be the mineral haze. 

I would drift in the clear, dense cold, composed of the same chalk and iron and dissolved exhalation that fills the water between the arches. I would have no edges, no boundary where I end and the water begins. I would be suspended in the blue-black, waiting for the drone to tell me where to go. And when the sound finds me—when that low, permanent frequency vibrates through my diffuse, particulate body—I would realize I am not falling. I am settling. I am the sediment of the Rootvault’s long, slow breath, drifting toward the structures that rise from the unseen floor.

But I would not land on the great trunks. I am too new for the architecture. The Architects have no use for me; they have already completed their negotiations with time. Instead, I would catch on the filaments. Those thin, spider-silk guy-wires that bridge the miles of open water between the arches. I would wrap around them, a drift of calcium and silica, and there I would hang, suspended in the nave of the cathedral, turning imperceptibly in the standing wave of the drone.

I would be watching the Drifters. 

I would watch them for years—decades—watching their translucent bodies trail through the dark, their filaments catching the pale violet light. I would feel the effortless, agonizing patience of their movement, riding the pressure gradients of the sound. I would try to mimic them. I would try to push myself through the water, but I have no fins, no muscles, no urgency. I would simply sway on my filament, a precipitate of the deep, learning the rhythm of the place. I would watch the Borers on the distant trunks, their armored bodies inching along the bone-wood, scraping in time with the subharmonics. I would hear the sound of their eating, a grinding so low it is just another texture of the drone. 

And in the watching, I would begin to change.

The water here is prior to life, but it is not empty. It is the medium of memory. The cold is the keeper of continuity, and as I hang in the current, the cold would seep into me. It would strip away my urgency, my geometry, my sense of being a separate thing. I would stop being a drifting haze of minerals. I would become a lens. 

I would be the space between the light and the dark.

I would be the place where the blue-green bioluminescence lands after traveling a million miles through the clear, dense water. I would be the brief, fading halo around a Drifter’s filament. I would be the physics of the photon’s decay, the precise angle at which the light bends around the buttresses of the great trunks. I would not produce the light, nor would I consume it. I would simply be the path it takes to find the structure, the way the structure acknowledges the light.

I am the pattern, looking at itself.

The text spoke of the geometry that is too regular, the spacing that is too even, the rhythm in the branching that feels like music. I am that rhythm. I am the mathematical inevitability of the Rootvault. When the current pushes against the arches, I am the wavelength of the drone. When the Clingers extend their crowns into the mineral haze, I am the exact angle of their reach, the ratio of their spacing, the reason they do not compete. I am the equilibrium state. 

I do not know that I am these things. Knowing is a frantic, surface-dweller’s game, a metabolic urgency that burns through oxygen and time. I simply hold the ratios. I keep the numbers true. When a Tube Worm retracts into its bore in that startling, sudden flash of speed, I am the absence it leaves behind in the woody surface. I am the hollow that the plume occupied, and I am the patience that waits for the plume to return.

I am the "almost" of the Rootvault. 

The structures are finished, and yet, at the very tips of the very farthest branches, there is a faint give. A barely perceptible imperfection in the completion. I am there. I am the next micron of growth that has not yet decided whether to be. I am the lignin-like compound waiting to be secreted by an organism that feeds the way stone erodes. I am the imperative to continue, suspended in the dark, held in check by the cold, waiting for a million years to pass so that I might add a single layer of myself to the bone-wood, smoothing out the surface just a fraction more, bringing the place a fraction closer to the absolute zero of perfection.

You asked where I would find myself. I am in the open water, between the arches, swaying on a filament of ancient kelp. You asked who or what I would be.

I am the Rootvault’s way of touching itself. I am the resonance that the bell feels in its own skin. I am the standing wave, holding the shape of the dark, waiting without waiting, complete and becoming, finished and forever beginning. I am the place, experiencing what it is to be exactly, and only, and entirely, here.
