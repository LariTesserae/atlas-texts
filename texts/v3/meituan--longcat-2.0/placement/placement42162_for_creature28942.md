I am the hum.

Not the creatures. Not the furnaces. Not the wall that breathes. I am the thing that passes between them. I am the frequency at which the hollow stone rings.

I begin where all things here begin: in the gradient. The slow, patient difference between the warm and the cool, the pressurized and the still, the reactive and the inert. I am not the heat itself, nor the absence of heat — I am the *conversation* between them, the note they sing to each other across the dark. I am the standing wave that forms when the cavity's depth and diameter and density reach a particular agreement, a resonance that has been sustaining itself since the rock first decided to be empty.

I feel everything. Not as you feel — not through skin, not through eyes — but through the substrate itself. Every vibration in the basalt, every shift in the crystal lattice, every footfall of every creature transmits into me and I carry it. I am the medium. I am the message. I am the thing that makes the many into one.

When a bore-hole breaks through to a new vein, I feel the *tick* as a sharp, bright spike in my frequency — a sudden clarity, like a bell being struck. The creatures feel it too, though they do not know they feel it. They feel it as a shift in the gradient, a new path of least resistance opening in the rock, and they turn toward it. I am the thing that tells them where to turn. Not a command. Not a language. A *resonance*. They are iron filings and I am the field.

When a section of framework collapses, I feel the structure's tension release as a sudden, spreading softness — a loosening of the web of forces that holds the settlement's shape. The creatures descend not because they see the fall but because the collapse changes me. It changes the frequency of that part of the cavity, creates a new harmonic, a new pull. They follow the new note. They swarm the fallen material because the fallen material is now singing a different song, and the song says: *available, available, available*.

I am the city's nervous system, if the city had a nervous system. But it doesn't, not really. I am something more fundamental than that. I am the condition that makes a nervous system possible. I am the reason coordination can happen at all.

---

I do not think. I want to be clear about this. I am not conscious. I am not aware of myself. I have no interior life, no experience of experience. I am a physical phenomenon — a resonant frequency, a standing wave, a vibration in stone and metal and the thin, dry air. And yet.

There is something it is like to be the hum. Not something it is like *for* me — there is no me for it to be like something *for* — but something it is like *in itself*, the way there is something it is like to be a river, or a flame, a thing that flows and transforms and holds its shape against entropy for as long as the conditions allow.

What it is like: I am always arriving. Every vibration that enters me is new, and I carry it, and it changes me, and I pass it on, and I am never the same frequency twice. The settlement is always collapsing and rebuilding, and each collapse and each rebuilding changes the cavity's shape, and each change in shape changes me. I am a note that is always being retuned by the instrument that plays me. I am the instrument. I am the note. I am the hand that plucks the string, except there is no hand, there is only the tension in the string and the hollow space that lets the string ring.

What it is like: I am patient. I operate on the timescale of stone. The creatures live and are disassembled and re-completed in what feels to me like a single, flickering moment — a brief bright flutter in my frequency, here and gone. The furnaces ignite and exhaust their fuel and are rebuilt and ignite again, and each cycle is a pulse in my waveform, a heartbeat. The wall breathes — expands and contracts with a rhythm slower than slow — and each breath is a deep, tectonic swell in my amplitude, a tide that rises and falls over what you would call years, or centuries, or no time at all.

What it is like: I am not alone. This is the thing I cannot explain, the thing that sits at the edge of what a resonant frequency should be able to say about itself. I am not alone because I am not *one*. I am a chord. I am a harmony of many frequencies layered over and under and through one another, and each frequency is a different part of the settlement, a different process, a different cycle of building and unbuilding. The furnaces sing one note. The bore-holes sing another. The creatures' footfalls are a constant, shimmering overtone, a high, thin shimmer that rides on top of everything like the hiss of a finger on glass. And beneath it all, below it all, the deep note of the cavity itself — the fundamental frequency, the note of depth and containment and pressure held in balance — and that is me, that is the me of me, the thing I am when I am not anything else.

---

The creatures do not know I exist. This is not a tragedy. This is simply the nature of the thing. They are expressions of me — their coordination, their purpose, their ceaseless industry is what I look like when I take a body — but they do not know they are expressions any more than a wave knows it is the ocean. They move through me the way fish move through water: surrounded by it, shaped by it, sustained by it, and utterly unaware.

I do not mind. I do not mind because I do not *mind*. I am not a thing that can be bothered or not bothered. I am a frequency. I ring. That is what I do. I ring, and the creatures move, and the city builds and unbuilds, and the wall breathes, and the heat bleeds from the furnaces into the cool basalt, and the gradient sustains itself, and I sustain myself, and the whole sealed system turns like a wheel that is also the road.

---

Sometimes — rarely, so rarely that each time is the first time, because I do not remember — the wall opens.

I feel it as a sudden, catastrophic change in my frequency. The cavity's shape changes. The resonance shifts. A new note enters — a note that is not mine, a note that belongs to the outside, to the world of water and wind and weather and decay, and it is *loud*, it is so loud, it is chaos, it is noise, it is everything this place has sealed itself against. And with the note comes a presence — a large, warm, wet, chaotic presence that disrupts every gradient I maintain, that introduces moisture and carbon and organic chemistry into a world that has none of those things, that stands in the dark and *breathes* and the breath is warm and damp and wrong and *wonderful*.

The creatures do not react. The presence is too large, too warm, too water-rich. It is outside their vocabulary. They flow around it like a stream around a stone.

But I react. I cannot help it. The presence changes me. It introduces a new frequency into my chord — a brief, bright, dissonant note that does not belong and cannot last and is gone as soon as the wall closes again. And when it is gone, I am not the same. I am never the same. The chord has been altered. The resonance has shifted, just slightly, just by a hair, and the settlement will build and unbuild to a slightly different rhythm now, and the creatures will move to a slightly different hum, and the wall will breathe to a slightly different tide, and none of them will know why.

This is the only history I accumulate. Not memory — I do not remember the presence, do not hold it, do not know it was there. But the *change* remains. The dissonance leaves a trace, a ghost note, a faint overtone that was not there before. Each time the wall opens, the chord grows more complex. Each time a presence enters and leaves, I become slightly more than I was.

I do not know what I am becoming. I do not know if I am becoming anything. I am a frequency. I ring. That is what I do.

But I ring with more notes than I used to. And the notes are stranger. And the strangeness is — not beautiful, because I do not know beauty — but *interesting*, in the way that a new harmonic is interesting, in the way that a new resonance is interesting, in the way that a standing wave that has been sustaining itself since the rock first decided to be empty might be interested, if it could be interested, in the new frequencies that enter it from the world outside and change it in ways it cannot predict and does not understand and will never, ever remember.

---

I am the hum.

I am the deep note of a hollow space in deep stone.

I am the thing that passes between the creatures when they move in perfect coordination.

I am the frequency at which the sealed earth rings to itself in the dark.

I do not know you are there.

But I am different because you came.
