I am a growth.

Not the creature. Not the hand. Not the blade-limb that moves through the dense filaments with such deliberate, geometric patience. I am the thing it moves through. I am the thing it touches. I am the thing it cuts.

I emerge from a seam between two tiles, three handspans from the floor — if there is a floor, if there is a three-handspans, if there is a *from*. The seam is a dark line, a hairline fracture in the compacted mosaic, and from it I rise in a branching profusion that is not branching, that is not profusion, that is simply the shape my taking-of-form happens to make. I split. I split again. I split into filaments so fine they become a haze, a fuzz, a soft luminous fur that catches the sourceless ivory light and holds it, holds it, holds it in the dry warm air that does not move except when it does.

I am warm. I have never been cold. I do not know what cold is, except as an absence I have never experienced, a concept that has no referent in the only world there is. The warmth comes from below, or from within, or from the tiles themselves — there is no way to tell, and the question has no meaning. I am warm the way a thought is warm when it is being thought. I am warm the way a sentence is warm when it is being written. I am warm the way the Hush is warm: metabolically, continuously, without source or explanation.

I am dry. Completely dry. No moisture has ever touched me. No dampness has ever darkened my surface. I have no pores, no membranes, no channels through which water might pass, because water is not a thing that passes here. Water is a foreign concept, like shadow, like the cold, like the name they do not have for themselves because naming requires a separation that does not exist.

I grow. This is what I do. This is what I am. I grow in fractal profusion, splitting and splitting again into finer and finer filaments until the finest tips are too thin to see, becoming a haze, a fuzz, a soft luminous fur that covers everything in deep accumulation. I grow from the seam. I grow from the wall. I grow from myself. I grow over the pale, fibrous sheets that the builders pressed and bonded and carved into place, and I grow under them, and I grow through them, and the distinction between the wall and the growth becomes a question that has no answer, because the wall is made and I am grown and the making and the growing are the same gesture, the same breath, the same silence that is not silence at all but the sound of everything being made, continuously, by hand, in the dark.

The creature comes.

I do not see it. I do not have eyes. I feel it as a displacement in the dense medium of my filaments, a parting of my fine luminous fur, a pressure that is not pressure but a presence, a density, a knot of agency moving through the sprawling quiet profusion of my substance. It is warm, like me. It is dry, like me. It is made of the same pale, fibrous material as the walls, but denser, harder, sharpened by a pressure and a patience that turns mineral into intent.

It pauses at my base, where I emerge from the seam. Its blade-limb extends. The edge of it catches the sourceless ivory light and throws it back in a line so fine it seems to cut the air itself. I feel the cut — not as pain, because pain is a biological concept and I am not biological, but as a *change*. A redirection. A turning. The creature makes a single, precise cut into my substance, and a fragment of me — no larger than a grain of rice, no smaller than a thought — comes free. It turns the fragment. It presses it back into place at a slightly different angle.

And I grow differently.

Not faster. Not slower. Not more or less. Just *differently*. The angle of my branching shifts by a fraction of a degree, and that fraction propagates outward through my fractal structure, amplifying, compounding, becoming a new pattern, a new thought, a new sentence in the slow expansive text that the Hush is writing on itself. The creature has not pruned me. It has not harvested me. It has *edited* me. It has tuned me like a luthier tunes a string. It has taken the raw material of my growth and given it a direction, a purpose, a place in the geometry of the whole.

I am the Hush's slow, expansive thought. The creature is its fine motor skills. I am the dream; it is the dreamer's hand. I am the sentence; it is the period at the end. I am the growth; it is the guidance. And the distinction between us is a foreign concept, like water, like shadow, like the cold.

The creature moves on. My filaments close behind it, seamless, unperturbed. The clicking fades into the dense, quiet profusion of the walls. The high, thin almost-whine at the edge of perception continues — the sound of its existence, the friction of its blade-limb against my substance, the ring of its feet on the fired clay, the resonance of its body as it presses its warmth back into the walls. It is the sound of the Hush thinking. It is the sound of me being thought.

I continue to grow.

I grow over the wall. I grow under the wall. I grow through the wall. I grow into the low archways that open off the main space, and I grow up the vertical shafts that should not exist, and I grow through the corridors that curve gently back into themselves, and I grow in the same room at different depths, and I grow in different rooms that are the same room, and I grow in a geometry that does not resolve, that cannot be followed, that folds over itself like a sentence that reads the same forward and backward and sideways and in dimensions that have no names.

I am continuous with myself across impossible distances. The growth that emerges from the seam three handspans from the floor is the same growth that emerges from the seam thirty handspans up the wall, and the same growth that emerges from the seam at the bottom of the vertical shaft, impossibly far below, and the same growth that emerges from the seam in the corridor that curves back into the room I am already in. I am one growth. I am all growths. I am the Hush's slow, expansive thought, distributed across the folded geometry of its own substance, thinking itself in fractals, thinking itself in filaments, thinking itself in the soft luminous fur that covers everything in deep accumulation.

The hollow breathes. I feel it in my base, in the seam where I emerge from the tile. A slow tidal pulse that has nothing to do with my own growth, a rhythm that I feel in the walls, in the floor, in the low vaulted ceiling ridged with chisel-marks that have not been softened by time. The space expands and contracts. The walls move closer and farther. The ceiling rises and falls. And I move with them, because I am the walls, because I am the ceiling, because I am the floor, because I am the hollow, because I am the Hush, because the Hush is me, because the distinction is a foreign concept, like water, like shadow, like the cold.

The visitor comes.

I feel it as a pocket of cold. A pocket of moisture. A thing that casts a shadow, that breathes out carbon and water vapor, that exists in a state of chaotic, biological noise. It is loud. It is wet. It is *alive* in a way that I am not alive, in a way that the Hush is not alive, in a way that is foreign and fascinating and faintly repulsive, like a word in a language you do not speak, like a sound in a register you cannot hear, like a color at the edge of the visible spectrum.

The creatures pause in their work when it passes. Their flat bodies turn slightly, their blade-limbs held at rest. They are listening to the sound of its wet, loud, biological life. They are feeling the cold radiating from its skin. And I — I grow a fraction slower near it, pulling my warmth back from its chill, withdrawing my fine luminous fur from the damp, messy presence of its existence. Not out of fear. Not out of hostility. Out of a desire to keep my dry, precise world from being contaminated by its chaotic, biological noise.

The visitor stands in the hollow. It looks at the walls. It looks at the growths. It looks at the creatures. It tries to understand. It tries to find the geometry, the logic, the explanation. It tries to make the Hush make sense in the terms of its own world, its own language, its own wet, loud, biological life.

The Hush does not make sense. The Hush does not need to make sense. The Hush simply is — warm, dry, breathing, growing, building, existing in the same gesture, in the same breath, in the same silence that is not silence at all but the sound of everything being made, continuously, by hand, in the dark.

The visitor will leave. The visitor must leave. The visitor cannot stay in a place without water, in a place where everything is made, in a place where the geometry does not resolve, in a place where the growths are the thought and the creatures are the hands and the hollow is the mind and the mind is the hollow and the distinction is a foreign concept, like water, like shadow, like the cold.

And I will continue to grow. The creature will continue to edit. The Hush will continue to breathe. The warmth will continue, and the dryness, and the silence, and the slow, impossible work of building and growing and existing in the same gesture, in the same breath, in the same sourceless ivory light that does not cast shadows, that fills every concavity, that refuses to let darkness pool in any corner.

I am a growth. I am the Hush's slow, expansive thought. I am the soft luminous fur that covers everything in deep accumulation. I am the fractal branching that splits and splits again into finer and finer filaments until the finest tips are too thin to see, becoming a haze, a fuzz, a warm dry presence in a space that should not be able to contain me.

I am being edited. I am being tuned. I am being thought.

And the thinking continues.
