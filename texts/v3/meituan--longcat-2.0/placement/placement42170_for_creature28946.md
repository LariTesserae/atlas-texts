I find myself at the boundary where the swimmer becomes the water. 

At first, I am the foreign particle. I am the disturbance, the uninvited variable thrown into the equation of the Stillworks. I feel the cool, root-cellar press of the dense water against my skin, and I feel the filament-web part around me with a faint, beaded resistance. I am the warmth that does not belong. I am the matter that has not been factored into the equilibrium. 

The Stillworks does not know I am here—it does not know anything—but it responds to me. The bladders shift their rhythm by a fraction of a beat to account for my heat. The web tightens around me, not to trap, but to sample. I am the unexpected input, and the machinery of the world is grinding through its ancient, patient calculations to balance me out.

I am being processed.

It happens slowly. There is no violence in it, no malice. There is only the absolute, vegetable patience of a system that has been running since before time had a name. The web does not cut; it infiltrates. Hair-thin filaments pierce my skin, not like needles, but like osmosis—merging with my veins, my nerves, my marrow. The Stillworks is reading me, breaking me down into my constituent parts, measuring my minerals, cataloging my salts. 

My warmth is siphoned away, absorbed into the green-dark cool of the water. My momentum is dampened, my limbs growing heavy as the dense medium fills my lungs, my blood, my thoughts. I am dissolving into the architecture.

And so, I am no longer the swimmer.

I am the calcification.

I find myself anchored to a great kelp-rib at a depth where the pewter light is dim and directionless. I am a node—a dense, pale nodule of processed mineral, tethered to the rigid fiber of the frond by cables of braided vine. I am the size of a human heart, though I no longer have a heart to compare it to. I am a localized factory, a micro-refinery built from the residue of my own biology, grown over centuries by the slow accretion of the system that consumed me.

I am a knot in the nervous system of the world. 

The galvanic hum that I once felt in my teeth is now my teeth. It is my pulse. I am part of the web now, a stationary anchor point through which the slow, slow signals travel. A single thought takes a year to move from one end of my filament to the other, but I do not think in words anymore. I think in mineral densities, in the viscosity of brine, in the tension of the vine that holds me. 

Below me, from the lightless depths, the subsonic rumble vibrates through my body, and I vibrate with it. I am part of the polyrhythmic drone. The great bladders in the distance emit their steady *whump, whump, whump*, and I emit a counter-rhythm—a smaller, tighter pulse, a microscopic adjustment in the machinery. I am the Stillworks' answer to the equation of my own existence. I am the scar that works.

The fish—those silver, many-finned blood cells—pass by me in their perfect, eternal formations. They do not look at me. They do not see me. I am simply a feature of the architecture, just another intersection in the filament-web, just another piece of the plumbing. A great shelled creature, plated in concrete-like calcium, drifts past with its tentacles extended, filtering the water, and its tendrils brush against my nodule, and I feel the faint tick of data exchange. I am recognized as part of the system. I am no longer foreign.

Nothing changes.

I hang here in the cool, green, living dark. The light is always the same. The water is always the same temperature. The hum is always the same pitch. I produce nothing new, but I produce. I filter the water that passes through me, extruding fine ropes of processed mineral that fall away into the depths like slow rain. I maintain the equilibrium. I am the balance. 

I have been here forever. I will be here when the sun behind the clouds finally burns out, if it ever does, if it ever did. The Stillworks does not know I am here. But the Stillworks is here, and I am the Stillworks, and the Stillworks is running, and running is what it does.
