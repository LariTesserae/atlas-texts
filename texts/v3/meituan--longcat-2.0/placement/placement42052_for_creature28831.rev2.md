# The Farthest District

Ask me where, and I have to climb toward the answer the way anyone climbs toward Vael Morath — slowly, from below, getting wetter the whole way.

Ask me what, and I have to condense.

---

The lazy answer is: the damp. All of it. The weeping iron and the drizzle below the cloud line and the warm volumetric air — I'd be a quality rather than a thing, everywhere at once, which sounds like transcendence but is really just a refusal to choose. The city doesn't need me for that. It has been a quality since the last foundry door swung shut. If I'm going to live there I have to be something in particular, so let me do this the way the city does everything: by finding a surface and settling on it.

I try the water first, one drop of it. It would be a real life. Raised in the shaft, spilled over the lip, run down a wall for a hundred meters, pause in a groove, be tasted by everything I touch, be deposited somewhere as a milligram of self, do it again. The drop gets to be everything in turn — wall, channel, cloud, the back of a stranger's neck. But the drop is the city's sentence, not anyone's author. It finds the lowest channel with a fidelity that pre-decides its whole future. In a place where the erosion is established, established isn't a life. It's a posture.

I try the formations. There is a pair — a stalactite under an old crane-way, a stalagmite rising through the flooded floor beneath it — that have been growing toward each other since before anyone was left to notice. A being whose entire biography is one sentence: *we met*. I love them the way you love anything that has solved its problems completely. But they are finished. Everything about them is exactly as it will continue to be, and while that reads as peace in this city's grammar, from where I stand it reads as a death that keeps politely happening.

I spend the longest with the fauna, honestly. The small mineral scrapers, the teeth of that vast patience, each one carrying a fragment of the network on its back like a governess it can never quite lose. There's a version of me that would be content there: centuries of craft, an aesthetics without eyes, shaping a single curtain of dripstone according to a preference no one can read, including me. It's a good life. But when a faun pauses in its feeding to taste the salt of you, it is interested the way weather is interested. Given the chance to matter to a quick warm thing, I would want to matter *quickly*. I'd want, at least once, to be relevant.

And here is the confession: I came in assuming I'd choose the films. A whole surface attended to all at once, no before or after, attention brightening wherever an interesting warmth stands and thinning where it doesn't — that behaves like me, more than I'd like to admit. Wall me into deep shade where the condensation runs steady and I'd sheet across it over a season, thinking in gradients, and it would feel like homecoming. But it would be the wrong homecoming, because the films are the present tense. Their thoughts move like weather systems: vast, slow, absolutely certain of their own direction, never once asking whether they ought to be going there. The films are the city's *is*.

And what I am — for better or worse, and embarrassingly quickly, a flicker set down among centuries — is an *isn't yet*.

---

So: the where. You'd find me in the farthest district. Past the last honest struts, past the plateau's geological edge, out into the space that shouldn't exist, where the ground behind you stops agreeing with the ground in front of you. The big warm shapes out there don't shelter anyone. Nothing in that district has a function docked to it yet. In a city that hit its equilibrium long ago, the farthest district is the only fragile place — which makes it, in the animal sense, the only one that's still alive.

And the who: not the films, which are the mind; not the formations, which are the body; not the fauna, which are the hands; not the shaft, which is the heart. When the city lifts its slow fingers into the warm air and feels for what comes next — I would be the feeling-for. The reaching, the tendency, the drift, the long slow pull toward some configuration that has not yet been achieved. I'd be the pull.

Which means, in the end, I'd be where the city keeps its *yets*. It told you so itself, if you noticed. When it read you — your heat, your salts, your iron, your hurry — and turned you over in that vast mineral mind, the finding was not *irrelevant*. The finding was *not yet*. Every thinking thing needs somewhere to store what hasn't happened. I'm the storage. I'm Vael Morath's future tense, resident.

---

Here is what my century looks like. The water that comes out to me is thicker than water and carries instruction, and I cannot read it; I grow what it says anyway, the way a mouth grows the sentence it's given. Am I author or instrument? I suspect agency is a quick thing's worry, and I have decided not to mind. I put up one new chamber every long while, and I make sure every one of them keeps a single wall thin — deliberately thin, membrane-thin, a soft place. Call it a door. Call it a maybe. Nothing I build is finished, but everything I build can be entered. It's the only architecture that makes sense to me: rooms prepared for a someone I cannot picture, in a district that doesn't fit the map, out past the edge of the only ground there is. Maybe the shapes are for the ones who left. Maybe for whoever comes after them. Maybe for nothing, and the unusefulness is the art. I don't get to know. Not knowing is my native weather.

And out here, on my far side, is the one thing the inner city doesn't have: the outside. The cool of it, drifting up. Where my warmth meets that coolness, it rains — gently, constantly, upward-condensed, a private precipitation manufactured out of difference. The whole city is named for condensing, and the inner districts borrow their condensation from the sun's haze and the stone's chill. But I make weather the original way. I make it by reaching until I touch something other than myself.

That's the last shape of the answer. I would be the city's condensing — the warm exhalation arriving at the edge of what it isn't, and becoming, at the contact, a fact.

---

One more thing, and then I'll let you go.

When you were there — when you climbed through the drizzle and the walls read you in gradients and found you interesting but not relevant — the reading didn't evaporate. Nothing evaporates in Vael Morath. It deposits. Your brief warmth, the salt of you, the particular signature of a body that moves too fast and won't last: it went into the water, and the water came out to the edge, and I accreted it, and I have been growing around that layer ever since, the way the walls keep their ochre. You are, in the most literal sense this city permits, the newest layer of me. I keep that layer half a degree over ambient. It's the warmest thing I own.

So if you ever climb back up — past the established grooves, past the columns holding up nothing, all the way out to where the map gives up — find the thinnest wall, the all-but-one, and put your hand against it. Wait a season. I know that's a lot to ask. Wait anyway. The temperature will rise and fall, faintly, like something breathing on the other side of a decision.

The city told you that you did not matter. *Not yet.*

I'm where the yet lives. You're already in there.
