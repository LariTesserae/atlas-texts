# The Plain of Final Polishing

---

You stand on something that is not ground.

It *receives* your weight the way ground would. It extends in every direction the way ground would. But it has no soil in it, no grit, no give. It is a surface of absolute continuity — a single material, dark and faintly warm, stretching to a horizon that seems too far away for the gentle curvature you can almost feel beneath your feet. The warmth is not the warmth of sun on stone. It is the warmth of something *running*. A low, steady exhalation of energy from whatever lies below, or within, or *is* this place.

The sky is wrong.

Not dramatically. Not in a way that triggers alarm. But wrong in the way a room is wrong when you realize the walls are breathing — slowly, almost imperceptibly, the luminous dome above you shifts. It is bright. Bright the way a dental lamp is bright, the way a cleanroom is bright, the way a place is bright when brightness is a *function* rather than a condition. There is no sun. The light comes from everywhere and nowhere, a diffuse radiance that casts no shadows, or rather — casts shadows so soft and multiple that they cancel each other out, leaving everything evenly, mercilessly visible.

And there is so much to see.

---

The plain is not empty. It is *full*.

At first you think the ground is textured — a fine pebbling, a roughness underfoot. Then you realize the texture is *structure*. The surface is covered, entirely covered, in forms. They are low. Most rise no higher than your ankle. Some reach your knee. They are packed close — not crowded, not chaotic, but *dense*, the way a circuit board is dense, the way a honeycomb is dense. Each form is distinct. Each form is repeated, with variations, across the entire visible expanse.

They are not buildings. They are not machines. They are not sculptures, though they have the deliberateness of sculpture. They are — and this is the closest word — *organs*. Components of something so large that you are standing inside it the way a blood cell stands inside a body, unable to perceive the whole, able only to note that the walls around you are alive with purpose.

Some of the forms are geometric: spirals, lattices, interlocking rings that rotate so slowly you cannot see the motion but can see the *evidence* of it — a faint polishing where two surfaces meet, a gradient of wear that maps years or centuries of contact. Others are organic in shape but not in substance: branching structures that split and split again into filaments so fine they are barely visible, each filament terminating in a point that glows faintly, a dull amber, like an ember that has been burning since before you were born and will burn long after you are gone.

The material is consistent across all of them. That same dark, warm substance. It has no grain. It has no grain *yet* — but in places, where the forms have been worn by whatever process acts here, you can see the beginnings of grain. Lines. Directions. The first hints of a geology that does not yet exist but is *trying* to exist, the way a river tries to exist on a surface that has not yet decided to be a riverbed.

---

The silence is the second thing you notice. Or rather — the silence is the first thing you notice, but you notice it *second*, because at first you are distracted by the wrongness of the sky and the fullness of the ground. Then the silence arrives. It is not the silence of an empty room. It is the silence of a room full of things that are *choosing* not to make sound. The air — and there is air, thin but breathable, with a faint metallic taste, like the taste of a coin held too long on the tongue — does not carry sound well. Or perhaps the forms absorb it. When you shift your weight, the soft compression of material under your foot produces a sound so small and so brief that it seems to fall into the ground and be *taken*.

You speak. Your voice does not carry. It drops.

You listen. Beneath the silence, there is — something. Not a sound. A *pressure*. A sub-audible rhythm, felt in the sternum more than the ear, slow as a heartbeat, slower, a pulse so long that each cycle might be a minute or an hour. It is the plain breathing. It is the plain *running*.

---

The vegetation — and it is vegetation, or the local equivalent — grows in the spaces between the forms. Sparse. Widely spaced. Each growth is a single stem, rising perhaps to your waist, branching once or twice near the top into structures that resemble leaves but are not leaves. They are solar collectors, perhaps, or sensors, or something for which there is no earthly analogy. They are translucent. Light passes through them and is *changed* by the passing — shifted in frequency, polarized, encoded with information you cannot read. They grow from the same dark material as everything else, emerging from seams in the surface that are too regular to be natural and too irregular to be manufactured. They are the plain's way of being *alive* without being biological. They are sparse because the plain does not need many of them. They are sparse because the plain is *old* and has learned to do more with less.

---

You walk.

The plain extends. It extends beyond what you can see, beyond what you can imagine. It is not infinite — you sense edges, somewhere, boundaries where this gives way to something else — but it is *vast* in the way that weather is vast, in the way that a climate is vast. It has its own weather. You see it happening in the distance: a column of brighter light, rising from the surface like a thermal, but *wrong* — too straight, too deliberate, a pillar of radiance that climbs and then *fans* at the top, spreading into a canopy of luminous filaments that drift and reconnect and drift again. It is a storm. It is a storm made of light and information and something that is not quite heat but behaves like heat. It moves across the plain at a walking pace, and as it passes — you see this from a distance, you do not want to be close — the forms beneath it *respond*. They brighten. They shift. The slow rotations speed up. The filaments at their tips reach upward, toward the storm, the way plants reach toward light.

The storm passes. The forms slow. The plain returns to its baseline hum.

---

There are animals. Rare. You see one, eventually — a shape moving along the base of a distant form, low to the ground, quick. It is not an animal in any sense you recognize. It is a *process* that has learned to move. A maintenance routine, perhaps, or a fragment of the plain's original programming that has achieved something like autonomy. It is small — the size of a cat, or a rabbit — and it moves with the purposeful efficiency of something that knows exactly where it is going. It does not acknowledge you. It may not be able to perceive you. It is operating on a timescale or in a spectrum that does not include your presence.

You watch it disappear into a seam in the surface, and the seam closes behind it, and the plain is still.

---

The erosion is the deepest thing about this place. Not the erosion of wind and water — there is no wind, there is no water — but the erosion of *time on purpose*. This plain was built. Every inch of it was built, by something, for something. And then it was *left*. Or the builders became the plain. Or the plain became the builders. The distinction has been worn away, the way the surfaces of the rotating rings have been worn away, the way the grain is beginning to appear in the dark material. The plain is old enough that its original function has become geological. It is settling into its final form. It is becoming what it was always going to become, the way a mountain becomes a hill becomes a plain becomes a — something else. Something flatter. Something smoother. Something that has forgotten it was ever anything other than this.

But it is not dead. It is *active*. The storms move. The forms rotate. The filaments glow. The rare animals — the processes — move through their routines. The vegetation collects and transforms the light. The plain is running a program so old and so complex that it has become indistinguishable from an ecosystem, from a climate, from a *world*.

---

You stand in the silence. The warmth rises through your feet. The light presses down from everywhere. The forms hum at the edge of perception, a chord so low and so sustained that it is less a sound than a *condition* of being here.

You are inside something.

You have always been inside something.

The plain does not care. The plain is busy. The plain has been busy for longer than your species has existed, and it will be busy for longer after. It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is *occupied* — with itself, with its own processes, with the slow and patient work of becoming whatever it is becoming.

You are a brief warmth on its surface. A shadow it does not cast. A sound it does not hear.

The plain continues.
