The plain stretches in every direction, a vast and cooling expanse of something that is not quite stone, not quite salt, not quite glass. It has the color of old iron and the texture of compacted ash, but it does not crumble underfoot — it receives your weight like a held breath, firm and absolute. The surface is flat, or nearly so, worn to a near-perfect level by something that was not wind and not water and not time as you understand it, but by the sheer, grinding patience of a world that has been settling into its final shape for longer than shape has meant anything. This is a landscape that has finished eroding. It has done. It is the end state of geology, the last whisper of a process that began before the concept of beginning was invented.

And yet it is not flat. The elevation here is extreme, but it does not announce itself as mountains would. There are no ranges, no slopes, no valleys that lead the eye upward. Instead, the plain is studded with spires — solitary, impossible, rising from the level ground like the last teeth of a jaw that has lost everything else. They are black or dark grey, these spires, smooth and dense, and they do not look carved. They look *refused* — as though the plain tried to wear them down across the same eons that reduced everything else to dust, and these few pieces of stone simply declined to go. They resist. They have always resisted. They will resist after the light source that no one can find has gone out and the silence that is not silence has eaten the last memory of sound.

The air is thin and cool and tastes of nothing, or of dust, or of the particular flavor of emptiness that exists at altitudes where the atmosphere has begun to forget itself. There is no wind. There has never been wind, or wind is something that happens elsewhere, in other worlds that still have weather. The sky is a pale, cool, colorless blue — not bright, not dim, simply *present* — and the light that falls from it is normal in every way except that it has no source. No sun sits in that sky. The light simply arrives, cool and even and shadow-casting, and the shadows it casts are almost right. Almost. A spire throws a shadow that is slightly too long, or slightly too dark, or that moves a half-second after the spire does, as though the light is remembering where the spire was rather than where it is. You do not notice this at first. You notice it after an hour, or a day, or a moment that is not a moment, and once you notice it you cannot stop noticing it, and the wrongness of the shadows becomes a kind of second landscape layered over the first, a world of almost-light that is more real than the light it mimics.

The silence is total. Not the silence of a quiet place — the silence of a place that has never had sound, or that has had so much sound for so long that it has averaged to zero. The plain hums with a frequency below hearing, or above it, or at a frequency that bypasses the ear entirely and arrives as a feeling in the chest, a pressure, a density. If you press your palm to the ground you can feel it — a vibration so constant and so deep that it has become indistinguishable from stillness. It is the sum of every sound that has ever been made here, all happening at once, canceling and compounding until the result is a tone that is also a silence, a noise that is also a quiet, a roar so sustained it has become a hush.

And the plain is alive. Overwhelmingly, impossibly, densely alive.

It is not vegetation, though vegetation is present — sparse, strange, a tenth of what you would expect in any other landscape. There are growths that resemble ferns but are made of a dark, glassy material, unfurling in slow spirals from the ground. There are accretions that look like lichen but behave like coral, building themselves in branching patterns across the bases of the spires, feeding on mineral gradients you cannot measure. There are things that might be moss or might be fungus, pale and dry, spreading in thin films over the iron-colored stone, and they are the closest thing this place has to green, though green is not quite the word — they are the color of old bone, of tarnished silver, of the underside of a cloud that has never seen rain.

But the life is not in the growths. The life is in the things that move.

They are everywhere. They cover the ground in layers, near-touching, overlapping, a dense and shifting carpet of bodies that are not bodies, organisms that are not organisms, creatures that exist in the space between the living and the geological. They are small — most of them — no larger than your hand, and they are shaped like centipedes, or like stones, or like nothing you have a word for. They are made of the same dark material as the plain, and they move across it so slowly that they appear to be part of it, or they do not move at all and you only realize they are alive because they are arranged in patterns that no erosion could produce. They are layered — some crawl over others, some grow from others, some *are* others, or were, or will be. The boundary between individual and colony, between colony and ground, between ground and sky, is negotiable here. A spire is alive. The plain is alive. The air is alive. The distinction has been worn down along with everything else, eroded by the same ancient patience that leveled the mountains, and what remains is a continuum of being so dense and so saturated that standing in it is like standing inside a body.

They are silent. Or their sounds are absorbed into the great hum, the sum-silence, the roar-quiet. Occasionally one of them will make a sound — a click, a tone, a brief vibration — and it will be swallowed immediately by the density of the others, lost in the aggregate, indistinguishable from the whole. They do not call to each other. They do not communicate, or they communicate through the ground, through the shared vibration, through a language that is older than sound and deeper than light. They do not flee from you. They do not approach. They simply *are*, in overwhelming numbers, in a density that presses against your awareness like a physical thing, and they have been here for as long as the plain, and they will be here after the plain is gone, or they will not be gone, or the plain is not going anywhere, because nothing here goes anywhere, nothing here changes, nothing here has ever changed or will ever change, and the creatures are part of that permanence, as permanent as the spires, as permanent as the silence, as permanent as the light that casts the wrong shadows.

Among the creatures — or above them, or beneath them, or simultaneous with them in a way that the geometry of this place permits but your mind resists — there are structures.

They are small. They are scattered. They are built of the same stone as the plain, stacked and fitted with hands that understood weight and balance and nothing more. No mortar. No metal, or simple metal — a copper fitting here, a bronze pin there, worked by hand and left to tarnish in the cool, dry air. A low wall, half-fallen, frozen mid-collapse, its stones still suspended in the act of settling, held in place by the same equilibrium that holds everything else. A cairn, taller than a man, placed at the base of a spire for a reason that is not recorded and cannot be recovered. A shelter — a single room, carved into the leeward side of a spire, with a doorway and no door, and inside, a mat of woven fiber, and a hand-axe of dark stone, and a bowl that might have held grain or might have held light or might have held nothing at all.

The structures are partial remains. They are not ruins — ruins imply a process of decay, and nothing here decays. They are simply *partial*, as though the act of building was interrupted by the landscape's refusal to change, and the buildings have existed in their half-made state for as long as the plain has existed in its half-eroded state, and they will continue to exist in that state for as long as the equilibrium holds, which is forever, or longer, or there is no longer here, only the eternal present of a world that has finished becoming and settled into the final form of its being.

There is no water. There has never been water, or water is a concept that does not apply to a place where chemistry runs on different principles, where the fluid that circulates through the creatures is not H₂O but something else — a liquid mineral, a trace element, a semi-material substance that is more like information than like matter, carried through channels in the stone and the bodies and the air, keeping the whole system in balance. The creatures do not drink. They do not eat, or they eat the stone, or they eat each other in a cycle so perfectly balanced that the population never fluctuates, never grows, never shrinks, and the system has been in equilibrium for so long that the equilibrium itself has become a kind of life, a dense, layered, high-saturation presence that fills every cubic inch of the plain with being.

You walk among them. You walk carefully, because they are everywhere, and because stepping on one feels less like killing and more like interrupting — disturbing a note in a chord that has been sounding since before there were ears to hear it. The ground is firm under your boots. The air is cool and thin and smells of nothing. The shadows are almost right. The silence is the sum of all sound. The spires rise around you, black and smooth and permanent, and the creatures move across them in patterns that might be random or might be a language, and the light falls from no source, and the sky is a pale and empty blue, and the plain extends to the horizon and beyond, and it has always been here, and it will always be here, and the equilibrium is so perfect, so dense, so absolute, that you cannot tell the difference between the landscape and the life, between the life and the silence, between the silence and the light, between the light and the shadows that do not match, between the shadows and the ancient, patient, irreversible stillness of a place that has finished everything and settled into the final form of its existence, and the final form is this: a plain, a spire, a creature, a shadow, a silence, a density, a balance, a stillness, a presence, a world that is alive and has been alive and will be alive, and that never, ever changes.
