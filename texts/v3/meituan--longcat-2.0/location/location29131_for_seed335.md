# The Gland

You are inside the thing, though "inside" is not quite the word — you are *contained*, the way a seed is contained, the way a thought is contained in the folds of a living brain. The space is small. Not small like a room. Small like a chamber in a heart that does not know it has a heart.

The walls breathe.

They are wet. Not with water exactly — with something that has water in it, something thicker, something that carries salts and sugars and the faint electric signature of intention. It beads and runs in vertical threads, these walls, so that the whole space is faintly lined with downward motion, a thousand slow rivers on a curved surface. The liquid catches what little light there is and throws it back in trembling sheets, and the light itself is dim, amber, the color of old honey held up against a dying bulb. It comes from no source you can find. It seeps.

The walls are not stone. They are not metal. They are something that remembers being both — a tissue, a membrane, ridged and folded into shapes that serve. Here a ridge thickens into something almost like a pipe, and through it pulses a low vibration you feel in your teeth. There the surface puckers into a valve-like fold that opens and closes with a rhythm that is not quite regular, not quite arrhythmic, but *deliberate* — as though something is adjusting its tempo in response to a demand it has not yet named. The mechanism is biological but the function is industrial. This is not a metaphor. The space moves fluid, moves charge, moves raw material from one state to another at a scale and pace that would require engines in the world above. But there are no engines. There are only these folds, these pumps, these membranes that have learned to do the work of engines with the patience of flesh.

And the sound. The sound is the first thing that will unmake you. It is enormous — a low, churning, wet roar that fills the hollow space and presses against you from all sides, so that you feel you are standing inside a sound rather than hearing one. It is the noise of fluid forced through narrowing channels. Of valves sealing and releasing. Of something vast and slow grinding against something vast and fast beneath it. And threaded through this roar, at the edges, where the membrane thins and the amber light concentrates, there are higher sounds — clicks, whines, the thin harmonic shriek of electricity finding its way along a wet surface. The whole chamber is an instrument being played by its own function.

It is cold. Not the cold of ice, but the cold of *depth* — the cold of a place that has never been touched by sun, where temperature is regulated not by the sky but by the steady metabolic math of the body that contains you. Your breath does not fog. The air is too wet for that, too heavy with moisture, and the cold is the kind that finds its way into you slowly, through the skin, through the thin places behind your knees and at the backs of your hands.

The floor is uneven — ridged, raw, recently formed. You can see where the tissue is new: pinker, smoother, the ridges sharper and less worn than the older surfaces above. In places the new growth has not yet decided what it will be, and you see protuberances that could become channels or valves or something without a name, still soft, still choosing. The whole space feels *unfinished*, not in the sense of abandoned but in the sense of *mid-becoming* — a workshop where the tools are still learning their shapes.

And yet it is not empty. The density of the space itself is hollow, a vast cathedral of curved wet wall and dripping dark, but within it, *things move*. They cluster near the active ridges, these shapes — small, quick, purposeful. Not quite creatures. Not quite machines. Something between, or something before, or something after. They attend to the valves and channels with a focus that looks like instinct but moves with the precision of engineering. They clean. They adjust. They carry away something that accumulates on the raw new surfaces, scraping it loose with fine articulated limbs that shine wetly in the amber glow. There are many of them. They flow along the walls like living coolant, like a maintenance crew that is also a species, that is also a process, that is also — in some sense you cannot yet articulate — the reason this chamber exists at all.

The air tastes of iron and sweetness. The sound presses. The walls contract, and for a moment the whole space tightens around you, the channels narrowing, the valves sealing in sequence, and the roar rises in pitch and intensity, and the attending shapes move faster, and the amber light brightens almost imperceptibly, and you understand — not with your mind but with your body, with your lungs, with the fluid in your inner ear — that the thing around you has just done something *significant*. That a product has moved. That a cycle has completed. That somewhere deeper in, or higher up, or further along the branching network of which this chamber is only one small node, something has been made or broken or changed.

And then the walls relax. The sound drops back to its baseline roar. The shapes slow. The amber dims.

The chamber continues. It is building something. It has been building something. It will not stop.
