# The Hollow of Veins

---

You descend through a crack that shouldn't exist — a seam in the bedrock where the stone simply decided, a long time ago, to stop being continuous. The opening is narrow enough that your shoulders brush both walls, and the walls are warm. Not sun-warm. Not geothermal-warm. *Body*-warm, like pressing your hand against the inside of a wrist.

The crack goes down for longer than it should. Thirty feet. Fifty. A hundred. And then it opens.

---

You step into a space that is mostly nothing.

That is the first thing. The first and most persistent thing. The Hollow is a void — a pocket of absence buried under the weight of the world, and it is *enormous* in the way that only hidden things can be enormous: not vast, but *deep*, a vertical cathedral of empty air that your eyes cannot find the top of. The light comes from everywhere and nowhere, a diffuse sourceless glow the color of daylight filtered through green glass, as though the stone itself has learned to photosynthesize and is doing so, lazily, out of habit.

But the nothing is not empty. The nothing is *structured*.

Veins. That is what they are. That is the only word. They cross the void in every direction — not cables, not ropes, not roots in any sense that a botanist would recognize, though they are alive and they are plant and they are the only thing holding this space together. Each one is as thick as your arm, or as thick as your waist, or — in the great primary spans that bridge the widest gaps — as thick as a ancient tree's trunk. They are the deep green-black of chlorophyll pushed past its logical limit, and they are *growing*. You can see it. Not with your eyes, exactly, but with some older sense — the way you can feel someone watching you from across a room. The veins are extending themselves into the emptiness, reaching, branching, splitting into finer and finer filaments until the finest ones are barely visible, a haze of living thread that fills the air like spider silk in a forgotten room.

The space between them is the point. The space between them is the architecture. The veins define rooms the way the absence of walls defines a meadow — by suggestion, by the way they curve and cluster and leave gaps. There are chambers in the Hollow. There are corridors. There are places where the filaments grow so dense they form curtains, and places where a single vein spans a hundred feet of open air with nothing but its own tension to keep it aloft, and you can pluck it like a guitar string and it will sing — a low, resonant, *wet* note that hangs in the air for a long time after.

It is loud here. Not with animal sound — you will not hear birds, will not hear insects, will hear perhaps one thing with legs in the entire time you are here, and it will be so still and so quiet that you will wonder if it is alive at all. The loudness is the sound of the Hollow *being*. The veins creak as they grow. Water moves through them — you can hear it, a constant arterial susurrus, the sound of sap in a thousand green hearts — and where the water reaches the surface of the veins it beads and drips and the drops fall for a long time before they hit anything, and when they hit they make a sound like a finger flicked against the rim of a crystal glass, and there are a thousand drops falling at any moment, and the sound is everywhere, a bright persistent chiming that never resolves into rhythm because the drops are not regular, they are *biological*, they come when the veins decide to release them, and the veins are not a metronome.

The air is warm. The air is damp. Condensation forms on the veins and runs down their lengths in thin streams, and where the streams reach branch-points they pool in cup-like formations — not built, not designed, just the natural consequence of a plant that has never heard of architecture but has invented it anyway — and the pools are habitats for things you do not have names for. Translucent films. Motile specks. A slow, purposeful slime that might be fungal or might be something else entirely.

---

The floor — if it can be called a floor — is a mat of the finest filaments, woven so densely that it has the give of a mattress and the appearance of moss, except it is not moss, it is the terminal ends of all the veins that have ever grown downward, and they have tangled and fused and formed a substrate that is itself alive, and it is on this substrate that the rare things live.

You will find, if you are patient, a creature. It will be small. It will be slow. It will have too many legs or too few, and it will be the color of the veins, and it will not run from you because it has never encountered anything that was not the Hollow, and it does not have a category for *you*. It will regard you with organs that may or may not be eyes. It will continue doing whatever it was doing — grazing on the filament-mat, perhaps, or simply *being*, in the way that very old and very slow things simply are.

There are tools here. Not many. Not obvious. But if you look closely at the base of the largest veins you will find marks — cuts, scrapes, places where the green-black flesh has been opened and has healed around the wound in a way that suggests the wound was made deliberately, repeatedly, by something that wanted what was inside. Sap. Fiber. The thin, strong filaments that can be twisted into cord. Someone — something — lives here, or has lived here, or passes through, and they are at the stage of technology where a sharp edge is the pinnacle of invention, and they have found that the Hollow provides sharp edges if you know where to look: mineral deposits where the condensation has leached stone, fragments of the bedrock that have fallen from the ceiling and shattered into flakes. The tools are not kept. They are made, used, and left where they fall, and the filament-mat slowly grows over them, and in a hundred years they will be part of the Hollow, and the Hollow will have digested another small attempt at permanence.

---

And now the thing you have been waiting for, the thing that is wrong, the thing that makes this place not a cave and not a garden and not anything that has a name:

The Hollow is not consistent.

You will notice it first as a trick of the light — a vein that seemed to be on your left is now on your right, and you have not turned around. Then as a trick of scale — a chamber you walked through in five steps takes fifteen steps to walk back through, and the veins are closer together than they were, or farther apart, or the same, you cannot tell. Then as a trick of *sequence* — you will see a drop of water fall from a vein and you will watch it fall and it will not land. It will simply fall. It will get smaller and smaller and dimmer and dimmer and it will not hit the ground because the ground is not where it was when the drop began its fall, or the drop is not where it was, or the concept of *down* has been temporarily suspended in this particular pocket of the Hollow and will resume shortly, probably, though there is no guarantee.

The veins grow. That is their nature. But they do not grow in the direction you expect. They grow *toward*. Toward warmth. Toward moisture. Toward *you*, sometimes, if you stand still long enough — you will feel a filament brush your ankle and when you look down it is new, it was not there a moment ago, it has grown across the floor to touch you, and it is warm, and it is damp, and it is alive, and it is *curious* in the way that plants are curious, which is to say it is not curious at all, it is simply growing in the direction of a resource, and you are a resource, you are warm and you are damp and you are full of salts and the Hollow wants what you have.

Do not be afraid. The Hollow is not hostile. The Hollow is not anything. The Hollow is a place where the rules are still being written, where the erosion is young and the features are sharp and the reality is thin, and the plants have gotten here first and have made it theirs, and they are still figuring out what *theirs* means, and in the meantime they grow and they drip and they sing and they reach, and the space between them is the most beautiful emptiness you have ever stood in, and it is warm, and it is loud, and it is alive, and it is not finished yet.

It is not finished yet.
