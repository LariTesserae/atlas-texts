### **Part 1: Pre-Departure Conditioning and Mindset**

Before you even touch your gear, you must prepare your mind. The Weft is an ontological hazard as much as a physical one.

**1. Acknowledge the Truth:** You are preparing to walk on the body of a single, ancient, and deeply unwell organism. You are not a visitor in a landscape; you are a microbe on a sick leviathan. This perspective is not hyperbole; it is your first and most important survival tool. It will inform every decision you make.

**2. Sensory Deprivation & Overload Training:** The Weft will attack your senses with both monotony and violence.
    *   **Acoustic Discipline:** Spend at least 48 hours in a room with a constant, low-frequency, rhythmic industrial noise playing. Use a large subwoofer if possible. The goal is not just to get used to the sound, but to practice focusing, thinking, and performing simple tasks *through* it. Learn to compartmentalize the noise so you can still detect changes in its rhythm—this will be your primary warning for a Calyx surge. This is training for **The Churn**.
    *   **Visual Austerity:** Spend time in environments of limited color and texture. Foggy coastlines, overcast plains, industrial parks on drizzly days. Train your eyes to pick out subtle differences in shade and movement in a visually muted world.
    *   **Vestibular Preparation:** The ground on the Weft is not stable. Practice walking on unsteady surfaces: thick marshland, trampolines, even waterbeds. Focus on maintaining a low center of gravity.

**3. Embrace Illogic:** Causality is a local courtesy, not a universal law on the Weft. You will see things that defy sense. An effect may precede its cause. Objects may exist in multiple places at once. To fight this mentally is to invite madness. Practice accepting paradox. Meditate on koans. Watch corrupted video files. The goal is to see a visual echo of a Shear-Mite and have your first thought be "Ah, a temporal displacement of approximately half a second," not "What is wrong with my eyes/brain?"

### **Part 2: Equipment & Gear Loadout**

Your gear must be a second skin, a life-support system for a world that offers no support of its own.

**1. The Foundation: Footwear & Clothing**
    *   **Boots:** This is your most critical piece of gear. You need integrated, knee-high wader-boots. They must be made of a seamless, puncture-proof composite material. The sole should be wide and flexible, but reinforced with a plate to prevent punctures from the crystalline scurf. The treads should be deep and siped, designed to grip a wet, fibrous, shifting surface. Do not bring leather; the Weft will consume it.
    *   **Body Suit:** A full-body, sealed environmental suit is non-negotiable. This is not just for warmth and waterproofing, but for bio-isolation. The air is a soup of spores, phosphenes, and complex proteins. You do not want this inside you. Your suit must have:
        *   **Thermal Regulation:** It will be near-freezing, but the effluent from the Calyces is hot. The suit needs a system to manage these extremes.
        *   **Integrated Filtration:** A rebreather system is essential. It must scrub spores of unknown biological function and filter the high-moisture air. Standard dust filters are insufficient.
        *   **Durability:** The suit must be resistant to abrasion and tearing. It will be constantly soaked.

**2. Navigational & Sensory Toolkit**
    *   **The Beacon:** Compasses are useless. GPS is a joke. Your only reliable navigational tool is a **Departure Point Anchor Beacon**. Upon arrival, you will drive this three-foot spike deep into the Weft. It will emit a low-frequency pulse that your handheld receiver can track. This is your only way home. Do not lose the receiver. Do not wander out of its range.
    *   **Acoustic Dampeners & Monitors:** Your helmet should have active noise-canceling headphones to save your sanity from the constant Churn. However, they must also have external microphones that can be set to alert you to *changes* in the ambient sound—the stuttering of a Calyx surge, the bellow of a Groaner. You must be able to dull the noise without becoming deaf to its warnings.
    *   **Optical Enhancement:** Your visor should have variable tinting and a polarized filter to cut through the atmospheric shimmer. A low-light amplification mode is also recommended, though be aware it may exacerbate visual anomalies near a surging Calyx.

**3. Survival & Utility**
    *   **The Probe Staff:** A sturdy, ten-foot pole made of a non-conductive composite. This is your multi-tool. Use it to probe the ground ahead of you for sudden changes in density or unexpected hollows. Use it to brace yourself during tremors. If you encounter a patch of unstable gravity, you can use it to test the area without risking your own body.
    *   **Sustenance:** Bring all of your own food and water. **Under no circumstances should you drink the water of the Weft.** It is not water; it is the organism's circulatory fluid, a raw biological slurry. Your filtration system may not be able to handle the complex organic compounds, and even if it could, the long-term effects are unknown. Food should be high-energy, nutrient-dense rations.
    *   **Shelter:** A free-standing, geodesic dome tent with a reinforced, weighted floor. It needs to be able to withstand torrential, protein-laced rain and violent ground tremors without collapsing. Do not rely on staking it into the Weft; the ground is too soft and fibrous.

### **Part 3: On-Site Protocol & Bestiary Guide**

Survival on the Weft is a matter of protocol and avoidance.

**1. The Rhythm of the Weft:**
    *   **The Churn:** This is the baseline. The CHURN-thump is the world's resting heartbeat. Learn its tempo. It is your only constant.
    *   **The Surge:** When the rhythm breaks—stuttering, hammering, accelerating—a Calyx is surging. All protocols shift.
        *   **Immediate Action:** Stop moving. Widen your stance and drop to a low crouch. Use your probe staff for a third point of contact. The ground will shake violently.
        *   **Sensory Distrust:** During and immediately after a surge, do not trust your senses. This is when causality frays. Vertigo will be intense. Visual echoes will appear. Look at your instruments. Trust your beacon's direction. Trust your suit's inclinometer. Do not trust your eyes or your inner ear. Wait for the Churn to return to its regular, lulling rhythm before proceeding.

**2. Interacting with the Ecosystem (Or Not):**
    *   **The Calyces:** These are the roaring, broken gods of this place. Your primary objective is to maintain distance. Use them as navigational landmarks ("I'll head towards the twin Calyx on the eastern horizon"), but never approach one. The area within a mile of a Calyx base is a zone of extreme physical and ontological danger. The air is superheated, the ground is wracked with constant vibration, and reality is at its thinnest.
    *   **The Groaners:** Give them the widest possible berth. They are large, unpredictable, and functionally part of the Calyces. Their moans can be deeply unsettling, seeming to resonate not in the air but inside your own skull. Do not interpret their movements as you would a normal animal's. They are not grazing or migrating; they are performing a function you cannot understand. If a herd of Groaners is between you and your objective, change your objective.
    *   **Shear-Mites:** These are the least direct threat. They will ignore you. Do not walk through a herd, as their collective movement can create unpredictable footing on the already-unstable Weft. Observe their pre-cognitive scattering; it can serve as an early warning for a pressure wave from a Calyx valve, sometimes seconds before you can hear or feel it yourself.
    *   **Glass-Wing Moths:** Harmless and beautiful. They are one of the few signs of grace in this world. Appreciate them. They are a reminder that even in a broken system, a strange kind of life can find its niche.

### **Part 4: Psychological Fortitude**

Your greatest enemy on the Weft may be your own mind.

1.  **The Monotony:** The gray sky, the rolling sameness of the plains, the endless Churn—it will try to grind down your spirit. Maintain a strict routine. Talk to yourself. Recite poetry. Hum a tune that is wildly different from The Churn. Create small, achievable goals: "Walk to that darker patch of Weft," "Check instruments every 15 minutes."

2.  **Ontological Sickness:** Prolonged exposure to fraying causality can induce a unique form of nausea, paranoia, and dissociation. This is "Weft-Sickness." If you start to feel detached from your own body or question whether your actions are your own, you must anchor yourself. Stop. Secure your footing. Take out a small, familiar object from your home dimension—a smooth stone, a metal coin, a photograph. Hold it. Focus on its solid, predictable reality. Recite its properties. Re-establish your own causal history.

3.  **The Purpose:** Remember why you are there. To witness. To learn. To experience the sublime and terrifying reality of a world that is a single, dying organism. You are not there to conquer or change it. You are a fleeting thought in its eons-long fever dream.

Tread lightly. Listen deeply. Do not linger. And above all, respect the dream of the dying god you walk upon. Your safe return depends on it.
