Here is your comprehensive preparation guide for a visit to the Great Wet Engine.

### **Part 1: Mental and Philosophical Preparation**

Before you pack a single item, you must recalibrate your expectations.

*   **You Are a Microbe:** This world is a single, continental-scale organism. The city, the fauna, the very rock—it is all one metabolic process. You are, in this context, smaller than a bacterium on your own skin. You are utterly irrelevant to its functions. Do not expect acknowledgment, welcome, or even hostility. Expect only the crushing, absolute indifference of a system that has operated without you for eons and will continue long after you are gone. Your goal is not to interact, but to observe without being noticed, like a dust mote in a hurricane.
*   **Abandon Your Senses of Scale and Time:** Your human understanding of a "city" or a "day" is useless. Change happens on a geological timescale. The "noise" is the planet's resting heartbeat. The shadowless, perpetual glare will destroy your circadian rhythm. You are not visiting a place; you are inserting yourself into a single, eternal moment of being.
*   **Embrace the Sublime Horror:** This world is beautiful, but it is the beauty of a churning gut, a magnificent engine, a perfectly balanced apocalypse. You will feel awe and terror in equal measure. Acknowledge this feeling, let it wash over you, but do not let it paralyze you. This is the industrial sublime. Your sanity depends on accepting that you are a temporary ghost in a machine of flesh and stone.

### **Part 2: Pre-Departure Medical Regimen**

Your body is a fragile, water-based ecosystem about to be submerged in a completely alien one.

*   **Immunological Warfare:** Begin a course of powerful, broad-spectrum prophylactic antibiotics and antifungals at least one week prior to departure. The "organic glaze" is a planetary super-organism; a single inhaled spore could colonize your lungs, a single touch could cause a skin infection that your immune system has no framework to combat. You are not just preventing infection; you are preparing for a biological siege.
*   **Pulmonary Fortification:** The air is thick, wet, and full of particulates and ozone. If you have any history of asthma, COPD, or other respiratory ailments, *do not go*. For healthy individuals, engage in respiratory training to increase your vital capacity and CO2 tolerance. You'll be spending most, if not all, of your time on a rebreather, but in case of a suit breach, every second of lung strength counts.
*   **Dermatological Shielding:** The constant, universal dampness is a breeding ground for rashes, fungal growth, and immersion foot (trench foot). Before you even don your gear, apply a total-body barrier cream, the kind used by deep-sea divers—a thick, hydrophobic, anti-microbial layer. Pay special attention to feet, armpits, and all skin folds.

### **Part 3: Essential Gear and Equipment**

Your gear is not for comfort; it is your personal biosphere. Do not skimp.

**Category 1: The Suit - Your Primary Reality**

*   **Sealed Environmental Suit:** Forget Gore-Tex or anything "breathable." You need a fully sealed, non-porous environmental suit with integrated cooling. The ambient temperature is universally warm; without a cooling system circulating fluid through micro-tubules in the suit's lining, you will cook. It must be durable enough to resist abrasion from the porous rock and have reinforced joints.
*   **Helmet & Rebreather System:** Standard SCUBA gear is insufficient. You need a closed-circuit rebreather that scrubs CO2 and recycles your oxygen, with enough surplus O2 for twice your planned stay. The helmet must offer a wide field of view, be proof against condensation (internal and external wipers are a must), and have an integrated comms system and an audio dampening/filtering feature. The constant noise will otherwise drive you mad. This system allows you to control the sound, filtering out the infrasonic thrum or the high-pitched buzzing to focus on what's necessary.
*   **Gloves and Boots:** Your only points of direct contact. Gloves must be tactile enough for equipment operation but thick enough to prevent punctures. Boots need the best traction imaginable. Carbide-studded soles designed for wet, slick rock are essential. Consider a secondary magnetic function for gripping the metal conduits and structures, but test it first—you don't know what magnetic fields are at play.

**Category 2: Navigation and Awareness**

*   **Inertial Navigation Unit (INU):** GPS is unreliable. The thick atmosphere and potential for exotic atmospheric phenomena may block or distort signals. An INU tracks your movement from a fixed starting point, creating a virtual breadcrumb trail. It is your single most important piece of navigational tech.
*   **Multi-Spectrum Visor:** Your helmet's visor should have thermal, ultraviolet, and motion-tracking overlays. Thermal will help you distinguish active machinery and living creatures from the ambient warmth of the rock. It can also help you identify structural weaknesses or heat vents.
*   **Deployable Sensor Beacons:** Bring a dozen small, self-powered beacons. When you establish a base camp or find a point of interest, you deploy one. They will create a network for your INU to reference and serve as a physical trail back. They should have both a visible light strobe and a unique radio signature.

**Category 3: Sustenance and Tools**

*   **Food and Water:** Absolutely nothing in this world is edible for you. The biology is fundamentally alien. All food must be high-calorie nutrient paste, consumed via a port in your helmet. All water must be brought with you and sealed. Bring 200% of what you anticipate needing. Your suit's water reclaimer is a last resort, not a primary source.
*   **The Multi-Tool:** A high-quality tool with a plasma-edged cutter (for sampling or emergency escape from chitinous debris), a grappling hook launcher (for traversing the basins and domes), a diagnostic link for your suit, and a sonic drill.
*   **Geological Hammer & Sampler:** Not for idle rock collecting. This is for testing the stability of the ground before you put your weight on it. The rock is porous and water-saturated; what looks solid may be a thin crust. The sampler allows you to take sterile samples of the organic film or water rivulets without ever exposing your own environment.
*   **High-Lumen, Focused-Beam Light:** The shadowless glare makes everything visible, but it also flattens detail. A powerful, focused beam will allow you to see into crevices, gauge the depth of channels, and look beneath the surface of the murkier, slug-filled basins.

### **Part 4: On-Arrival Protocol & Rules of Engagement**

Your first 24 hours will determine the success of your entire visit.

1.  **Establish a High-Ground Base:** Upon arrival, do not wander. Your first objective is to find a secure, defensible position. Choose a spot on one of the large domes, away from major fauna migration paths (the "canyons" carved by the millipedes) and industrial arteries. You want maximum visibility.
2.  **Calibrate and Observe:** Set up your primary beacon. Sit. Watch. For hours. Learn the rhythms. Which way are the beetles crawling? How often do the dragonfly-scavengers pass? Can you feel a pattern in the thrumming of the engines? You are learning the baseline, the world's normal state. Any deviation from this is your early warning system.
3.  **The Rule of Ridges:** Stay on the high ground as much as possible. The basins are where water pools and the largest, slowest creatures wallow. They are dangerous, and visibility is poor. Travel along the broad, rounded ridges of the domes.
4.  **Respect the Rivers of Chitin:** You will encounter the train-sized millipedes. Do not try to climb them, cross them, or outrun them. They are geological forces. Find a stable vantage point and wait for the river of legs and armor to pass. This could take hours. Be patient. Patience is survival here.
5.  **The City is Not For You:** The carved structures and humming factories are part of the process. You are not. Do not enter them. You risk being caught in machinery, falling into a rendering vat, or being identified as a contaminant and sterilized by whatever automated systems or beings manage this place. The inhabitants, if you ever see them, will not be "people" in any sense you understand. They are the tenders of this world-machine. To them, you are a piece of grit. Avoid them at all costs.
6.  **Leave No Trace, Take No Souvenirs:** You are an observer. Your visit should be a perfect insertion and extraction. Do not leave garbage. Do not interfere with the fauna. If you take samples, do so with sterile equipment and seal them immediately. You do not want a single molecule of this world's biology interacting with your own on the return journey. Decontaminate your entire suit and all gear *before* leaving. A planetary ecosystem hitchhiking on your boot could be the worst kind of plague imaginable.

To be here is to stand in the belly of a god you cannot comprehend. Be humble. Be quiet. Be invisible. And you might just survive to tell the tale.
