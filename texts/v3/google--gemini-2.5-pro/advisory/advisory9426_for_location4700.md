### **Traveler’s Advisory & Preparation Guide: Sector 7G, Designation “The Vessel” (Alt. “The Terrarium,” “The Engine Room”)**

**Foreword: A Note on Temperament**

Before we discuss equipment and protocols, a frank assessment of your own character is required. The Vessel is not an adventure destination. There are no great beasts to conquer, no treasures to plunder, no ancient secrets to uncover in a dramatic denouement. Its primary challenge is not physical, but existential. It is a location of profound, industrial-scale patience, an environment that will test your sanity through sheer, magnificent indifference. If you are a person who requires action, narrative, or external validation, you are advised to choose another destination. This journey is for the observer, the contemplative, the connoisseur of systems. It is for those who can find revelation in the slow, silent turning of a gear that takes a century to complete one revolution. If this sounds like you, proceed.

---

### **Phase 1: Pre-Departure Conditioning**

Your body and mind must be prepared for an environment that is utterly alien to terrestrial and most extraterrestrial life.

**1. Physical Conditioning: The Neutral Buoyancy Protocol**

*   **Zero-G Simulation:** You will spend your entire visit in a state of neutral buoyancy. Begin a regimen of extended sessions in a sensory deprivation float tank, but one modified for active movement. Standard astronaut training in underwater facilities is the closest analogue. You must become comfortable with the complete lack of a dominant gravitational pull, with orienting yourself without an "up" or "down."
*   **Muscular Atrophy Prevention:** Without the stress of gravity, your body will immediately begin to weaken. A daily in-suit regimen of isometric exercises and resistance band training is non-negotiable. This must become as instinctual as breathing.
*   **Thermoregulation Acclimatization:** The solution is constant blood temperature. This is pleasant initially, but it can trick your body’s metabolic regulation. Spend time in warm baths and climate-controlled rooms to accustom your system to a lack of thermal variance. Your suit will be your primary barrier, but your mind must be prepared for the sensation.

**2. Psychological Conditioning: The Anchor & The Void**

*   **Solitude Training:** You must be able to withstand absolute, profound solitude. This is not the silence of an empty room, but the silence of a place utterly devoid of responsive life. We recommend a minimum of one week in a fully isolated, sound-proofed environment with no communication. You must learn to recognize the early signs of sensory-deprivation-induced hallucinations and have a mental toolkit to combat them.
*   **Temporal Disorientation Drills:** Time in the Vessel does not flow; it cycles. The low hum on a twelve-hour cycle will be your only clock. This can severely distort your perception of time. Practice mindfulness and meditation. You must learn to divorce your internal sense of time from external cues. Bring a chronometer, but learn to function without being a slave to it.
*   **Ego Dissolution Practice:** You are a mote of dust in this place. The machine and its garden existed for eons before you and will continue for eons after. It has no need for you, no awareness of you. This can be terrifying or liberating. Meditate on concepts of insignificance and the beauty of observation without participation. If your sense of self is tied to your ability to affect your environment, the Vessel will break you.

### **Phase 2: Essential Gear & Equipment**

Your survival and ability to function depend entirely on your travel suit. Do not skimp.

*   **The Exposure Suit (EX-Suit):** This is a second skin, a micro-habitat.
    *   **Material:** A non-reactive, self-healing polymer composite. It must be completely sealed. The material should be highly resistant to puncture and tearing; the machine’s edges are described as "unnervingly sharp."
    *   **Life Support:** A closed-loop rebreather system is essential. Do not assume the fluid is breathable or that its dissolved gases are compatible with human physiology. The suit must also handle 100% of waste processing and nutrient delivery (via internal paste dispensers).
    *   **Propulsion:** Low-impact, silent propulsion is key. Think gentle, variable-vector water jets or magneto-hydrodynamic drives that create no bubbles and minimal disturbance. Your goal is to drift with the existing circulation, using propulsion only for minor course corrections.
    *   **HUD & Sensory Suite:** Your helmet’s heads-up display is your lifeline. It should display suit integrity, vitals, nutrient levels, and a 3D spatial orientation map. Integrated sensors should constantly analyze the fluid's composition, ambient energy fields, and sonic frequencies, filtering the deep thrumming into something audibly perceptible.

*   **Navigation & Anchoring:**
    *   **Tether System:** A high-tensile strength, auto-retracting tether reel is your most important piece of navigational hardware. Your "entry point" will be your base. You will anchor the tether there. It is your only guaranteed way back. It also serves as a psychological anchor, a silver thread in the green-gold dark.
    *   **Mapping Drones:** Deploy a fleet of three to five palm-sized, passive drones. They should use the same silent propulsion as your suit and do nothing but drift, pinging their location back to your HUD to build a real-time 3D map of the immediate area. This helps combat the claustrophobia/agoraphobia paradox.

*   **The Mental Wellness Kit:**
    *   **Audio/Data Library:** Pre-load your suit’s computer with a vast library of music, audiobooks, and personal logs. Music with a strong, predictable rhythm is especially effective at combating temporal distortion.
    *   **A Familiar Object:** A small, smooth stone from your home world. A digital locket with family photos. Something tactile and personal that you can hold (within your suit) to remind you of a world with floors, skies, and noisy, chaotic life.

### **Phase 3: On-Site Protocols & Etiquette**

You are not a visitor; you are a ghost. Behave as such.

**The Prime Directive: DO. NOT. TOUCH.**

This cannot be overstated. You are in a perfectly balanced, eternally maintained closed system. A single skin cell, a drop of oil, a scratch on a pipe could introduce a flaw with unknowable, cascading consequences.

*   **Observation Only:** Do not attempt to take samples. Your suit’s external sensors are your only tools for analysis. Never attempt to "prune" a vine, open a valve, or harvest a crystalline pod. The temptation will be immense. You must resist it absolutely.
*   **Maintain Your Drift:** Follow the slow, methodical currents. Do not fight them. Use your propulsion system only to avoid collision with the machinery or flora. Sudden, rapid movements are "loud" in this silent place and may register on some unseen monitoring system.
*   **Light & Energy Discipline:** Your suit should run on a minimal-emission power source. Keep external lights off. The ambient light is sufficient, and introducing a new light source is a form of contamination.
*   **Emergency Self-Extrication:** The only exception to the "Do Not Touch" rule is if you become entangled. Your suit should be equipped with a single-use, high-precision laser cutter. Its use should be logged, and you must make every effort to cut only your own equipment if possible. Cutting a vine or a wire is an act of last resort, a desecration that may have unpredictable results.

**Navigating the Experience:**

1.  **Arrival:** Upon entry, you will be instantly submerged and buoyant. The warmth, pressure, and silence will be overwhelming. Do not panic. Float for at least one full cycle of the twelve-hour hum. Let your body and mind acclimatize. Focus on your breathing, even though the rebreather is doing the work.
2.  **Exploration:** Move slowly. Let your tether unspool. Follow a single, massive pipe as a guide. Observe the "stained-glass" membranes; watch the pulsing light in the jade vines. Notice how the growth is not random, but seems to follow a deliberate, engineered pattern. Map the connections between the flora and the machinery.
3.  **Rest:** When you need to rest, find a relatively open area and reduce your suit’s propulsion to zero. Let yourself drift with the tide. Engage your mental wellness kit. Make logs. Describe what you see in exhaustive detail. The act of cataloging is an act of sanity.

**Departure:**

Before initiating your exit, conduct a full scan of your suit. Ensure no biological material is clinging to it. Retract your tether slowly. Your departure must be as clean as your arrival. Leave the Vessel as you found it: perfect, silent, and entirely unaware of your brief, fleeting passage. You will take nothing with you but your data, your memories, and the bone-deep hum of an engine the size of a god's heart, breathing in its sleep.
