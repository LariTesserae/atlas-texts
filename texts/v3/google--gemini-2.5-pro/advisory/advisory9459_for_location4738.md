### **Traveler's Advisory & Preparation Guide: The Caldera-Ward**

**Document Code:** TVL-774-B / **Destination:** The Lenticle, Epsilon Draconis System, Alt-Prime 808
**Classification:** Non-Hostile Sentient Ecosphere. Bio-integration required.

#### **1. Foreword: A Change in Mindset**

Before we discuss equipment and procedure, you must prepare your mind. You are not visiting a city in the human sense; you are entering the circulatory system of a single, ancient, living being that has graciously allowed a culture to flourish within it. Think of it not as architecture, but as anatomy. Think of yourself not as a tourist, but as a temporary, benign microbe. Your goal is not to *see* the Caldera-Ward, but to be *accepted* by it, however briefly. Humility, patience, and near-absolute non-interference are the keys to a safe and meaningful visit. The greatest danger you face here is your own impatience and your instinct to dominate an environment. Leave them behind.

#### **2. Environmental Suit & Life Support: Your Second Skin**

Terrestrial human physiology is wholly incompatible with the Lenticle. Your visit is only possible through extensive technological mediation. Do not cut corners here; your life depends on it.

*   **Full-Body Environmental Suit:** This is non-negotiable. It must be a full-seal, closed-loop system. While the water is "fresh," it is not sterile and its ancient biome is profoundly alien. Ingesting or absorbing it could have catastrophic biological consequences.
*   **Pressure Adaptation:** The Ward is located near the bottom of a "continental lens" of water. The ambient pressure is immense and crushing. Your suit must be rated for deep-sea exploration (at least 200-300 atmospheres as a baseline) with a flawlessly operating auto-regulating pressure system. You will *feel* this pressure—a constant, firm, full-body embrace. Your suit will protect you, but you will still be aware of it.
*   **Oxygen Supply:** A closed-loop rebreather is essential. Do not vent *anything* into the water. No bubbles, no waste, no CO2. Everything you exhale must be scrubbed and recycled within your suit. Venting waste-gas is the equivalent of screaming in a library and poisoning the librarian.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The water is perpetually "cool." Expect temperatures in the range of 4-10° Celsius (39-50° F). Your suit needs an active heating system, but one that is subtle. You should aim for a state of thermal neutrality, not warm comfort. You want to feel the coolness of the place through your suit without suffering from hypothermia.
*   **Buoyancy and Propulsion:** The water's density makes unassisted movement exhausting, a "slow dance." Your suit must have a finely-tuned buoyancy control system and a silent, non-disruptive propulsion unit. Forget propellers or high-energy jets. We recommend a magnetohydrodynamic drive or a biomimetic fin system that moves you with slow, gentle pulses. Your goal is to drift, not to swim. Set your maximum speed to a gentle walking pace and leave it there.

#### **3. Sensory Acclimatization: Calibrating Your Perceptions**

The sensory environment of the Caldera-Ward is overwhelming and will tax an unprepared mind.

*   **Visual:** The light is diffused, polarized, and primarily in the blue-green spectrum. There are no hard shadows. This can cause spatial disorientation and a "lulling" effect. Your visor should have settings to subtly enhance contrast, but avoid full-spectrum "daylight" simulation. To do so would be to blind yourself to the true nature of the light here. Let your eyes adjust. Appreciate the gradients and the shifting "cathedral pillars" of luminescence.
*   **Auditory:** This is perhaps the most critical adjustment. The Ward is *loud*. The sound is a physical presence that will vibrate through your suit and your bones. It is a constant, layered, resonant chorus.
    *   **Initial Shock:** Upon arrival, do nothing. Just float for at least ten minutes and listen. Allow your brain to process the layers: the deep, cellular hum of the city-plant; the high-frequency static of crustacean swarms; the percussive scraping of the Grazers; the distant, pure tones of the chimes.
    *   **Audio Dampening:** Do *not* turn on full noise-cancellation. This will isolate you and create a sense of profound unease. Instead, use your suit's audio baffling system to "turn down" the overall volume to a bearable level while preserving the fidelity of the different layers. You need to hear the city to understand its rhythms. The sound of a Grazer approaching is information. The shift in the chimes is a clock.

#### **4. Etiquette and Interaction: How to Be a Good Guest**

Your guiding principle: **Leave no trace. Take nothing but memories. Disturb nothing.**

*   **The Living Architecture:** Do not touch the alabaster-like pylons or the calcified vine lattices with your bare hands or tools. The oils and microbes on your gloves could be contaminants. Do not push off surfaces; use your thrusters. The woven floors are for the inhabitants; you should float just above them. You are a ghost here. Act like it.
*   **Fauna:**
    *   **The Grazers:** These gentle giants are the city's maintenance system. They are infrastructure. Give them a wide berth. Their paths are ancient and predictable. If one is headed your way, you are the one who moves. Do not interfere with their work. Do not attempt to touch them or "ride" them. Their obsidian beaks are capable of scraping clean the living city—imagine what they could do to your suit. They are placid, but not to be trifled with.
    *   **The Shoals:** The silver fish and jewel-backed crustaceans are part of the water itself. You will pass through their swarms. Move slowly. They will part around you. Sudden movements may startle a shoal into a blinding flash of reflected light, which can be disorienting.
*   **The Inhabitants (The Shapers):** You will likely not see them, and you should make no effort to seek them out. They are, by all accounts, private and integrated with their environment. Their presence is felt in the delicate curtains of glowing anemone cilia. These are doors. They are signs of privacy. To approach a dwelling shimmering with these curtains is the height of rudeness. Your respect for their unseen presence is the single most important test of your worthiness as a visitor. The intricate patterns scored into the walls are their art, their history, their language. Observe them from a respectful distance.
*   **Technology & Tools:** Your only permissible tool is a scanner/recorder. Set it to passive modes only. No active scanning pulses. Absolutely no geological hammers, knives, sample containers, or cutting lasers. If you want a souvenir, take a recording of the sound or a high-resolution image. The only acceptable tool to carry for "craft" purposes would be a polished, inert stylus (perhaps made of obsidian to honour the Grazers) for sketching on a datapad.

#### **5. A Note on "Activities"**

There are no theme parks here. No gift shops. No tour guides. Your activity is *presence*.

*   Find a perch high on a branching walkway and watch the Grazers move through the "streets" below.
*   Drift in the open space at the heart of a pylon nexus and watch the columns of light shift with the passing of the "day."
*   Follow a power-line analogue—a thicker, pulsing vein within the living alabaster—and see where it leads.
*   Close your eyes (metaphorically, by blanking your visor) and simply feel the symphony of the city vibrate through you.
*   Time your visit to coincide with the "tide," the slow current shift that causes the chime-fronds to ring. Witnessing the change of a day is a central experience.

This is a place that challenges the very definition of "city" and "life." It has found a perfect, quiet, and profoundly loud equilibrium. Your role is to witness it, not to participate. You are a guest in a lung. Breathe with it, and be grateful for the moment.
