### **An Acclimatization Guide for the Great Tableland**
_Pub. The Inter-Planar Wayfarers' Guild, 7th Ed._

**Foreword: A Note on Intent**

Traveler, it is crucial that you understand from the outset that the Great Tableland is not a world to be conquered, mapped, or even fully understood in a single human lifetime. It is a world to be *entered*. Your goal is not to leave your mark, but to pass through with as little disturbance as the whisper of a gliding creature. The greatest danger here is not a predator’s tooth, but the subtle, inexorable pull of the wood itself, which may tempt you to stay, to slow down, and to ultimately be absorbed. Prepare not for a journey *across* a landscape, but for a deep immersion *within* an organism.

---

### **Part I: Corporeal Preparation - The Vessel**

Your body is your first and most vulnerable piece of equipment. The Tableland will test it in ways both subtle and profound.

**1. Clothing & Exposure Management:**

*   **The Principle of the Second Skin:** You will be perpetually damp. Not soaked, but constantly touched by moisture. Cotton is a death sentence; it will absorb water, grow heavy, chafe your skin raw, and never, ever dry. Your entire wardrobe must consist of high-performance synthetics or treated wool that retains warmth when wet. Think of your clothing not as layers for warmth, but as a personal environment.
*   **The Outer Shell:** A durable, waterproof, but highly breathable shell is mandatory. It should be slick to shed water and avoid snagging on the ubiquitously rough bark. Its seams must be factory-sealed. Consider something akin to a caver's oversuit or a sailor's foul-weather gear, but tailored for silent movement.
*   **Footwear: The Grip of the Gecko:** Your boots are your connection to the world. The ground is yielding and perpetually slick with organic ooze. Heavy, rigid-soled hiking boots will be disastrous; their deep lugs will fill with muck, and they offer no feel for the ground. You need soft-soled boots with a high-friction, micro-textured tread, something akin to high-end canyoneering or climbing approach shoes. They must be waterproof and extend high up the ankle to be sealed against your trousers. Before you go, practice walking silently on wet, mossy logs.
*   **Sealed Perimeters:** Your cuffs at the wrist and ankle must be sealable via integrated gaiters or elastic closures. The fauna of this world is insidious. A creature that looks like a sliver of bark will not hesitate to explore the warm, dark space inside your sleeve. Full head and neck covering (a balaclava or head gaiter) is also recommended.

**2. Respiratory & Dermal Health:**

*   **Breathe Consciously:** The atmosphere is described as "like the inside of a lung" for a reason. It is thick with spores, pollen, microbes, and complex organic compounds. For the first 48 hours, wear a filtration mask (P100 or equivalent) until your body begins to acclimate. Even then, be wary. Travelers have reported vivid, hallucinatory dreams, and others have developed sudden, severe allergies. Carry a supply of broad-spectrum antihistamines and a powerful corticosteroid inhaler, even if you have no history of asthma.
*   **Skin is a Battlefield:** You will be damp. Every cut, scrape, or blister will be an invitation for fungal or bacterial infection. Your medical kit must be weighted heavily towards dermatological care: antiseptic wipes (iodine-based are best), a potent topical antifungal cream (for athlete's foot, jock itch, and other opportunistic growths), antibacterial ointment, and a large supply of waterproof, breathable wound dressings. A daily ritual of inspecting your entire body for redness, rashes, or new growths is not paranoia; it is essential procedure.

### **Part II: Material Preparation - The Kit**

Choose your gear with the understanding that this world has no concept of fire, metal, or stone. Simplicity and reliability are paramount.

**1. Navigation: The Art of Staying Found**

*   **The Compass is a Liar:** While the Tableland has a Coriolis effect, its bio-geological mass creates strange, localized magnetic anomalies. Your compass will wander unpredictably. GPS is useless beneath the kilometers-thick canopy.
*   **The Breadcrumb Method:** Your primary navigation tool is a spool of high-visibility, non-biodegradable micro-filament line. Tie it off at your insertion point. As you travel, you unspool it. To return, you follow it back. This is your only guaranteed path home. Carry several kilometers of it.
*   **Local Navigation:** Learn to navigate as the creatures do. Orient yourself by the colossal landmarks of the Arch-Boles ("I am three days' walk east of the Great Weeping Bole"). Follow the tannin-rich water channels, noting their direction of flow. Learn the patterns of the bioluminescent fungi; some fields pulse in predictable rhythms that can serve as a "clock," while others mark specific kinds of wood or symbiotic relationships. Your map will not be on paper; it will be a mental tapestry woven from these observations.

**2. Light & Vision:**

*   **Embrace the Gloom:** Your eyes will adapt. The perpetual green-gold twilight is sufficient for movement once you are accustomed to it. A powerful flashlight will destroy your night vision and make you a beacon for things best left unobserved.
*   **The Red Light Rule:** Carry a headlamp with multiple, dim settings and a robust red-light mode. Use red light exclusively for tasks requiring fine detail. It will preserve your eyes' adaptation to the dimness.
*   **Observe, Don't Blind:** Use your light to *glance*, not to *stare*. A quick flash can reveal the texture of a surface or the depth of a pool. A sustained beam is a shout in a library.

**3. Tools & Sustenance:**

*   **No Fire:** This cannot be stressed enough. Attempting to start a fire is not only sacrilege, it is functionally impossible. The wood is too damp, the air too thick. Any flame will be instantly snuffed. This is not a world for campfires.
*   **The Gentle Blade:** Carry a sharp, corrosion-resistant knife and perhaps a small folding saw. These are emergency tools, to be used for freeing yourself from an entangling vine or preparing your own rations. *Never* use them to carve blazes into the living wood. The Tableland will perceive this as an attack, and its immune response, while slow, is comprehensive.
*   **Sustenance is Imported:** Do not eat the local flora or fauna. You cannot possibly know the biochemistry. A fruit that nourishes a bark-grazer may be a deadly neurotoxin to you. All food must be carried in. Nutrient-dense, high-energy rations are your only source of fuel.
*   **Water, Distilled by the World:** Water is everywhere, but it is a soup of tannins, spores, and unknown microorganisms. Your best source is to collect the weeping condensation from the underside of large, non-porous leaves or smooth bark surfaces. Use a silicone sheet or a small cup. Even then, every drop must be filtered through a multi-stage system: first a physical filter to remove particulates, then a chemical purifier or UV sterilizer to handle the microbiology. Never drink from the standing pools or slow-moving channels.

### **Part III: Mental Preparation - The Spirit**

This is the most critical phase of your preparation. The Tableland will challenge your perception of time, life, and self.

**1. The Ritual of Arrival:**

Upon arrival, do not move for one full hour. Sit. Seal your suit. Control your breathing. Your human haste is a discordant noise here. For that hour, your only task is to listen to the *plock* of falling water and the slow groan of the wood. Watch the light shift as a fungal network brightens. Let the world reveal itself to you. You will begin to notice that the bark beside you is breathing. The vine above you is not a plant, but a chain of dormant insects. You must re-calibrate your senses from the frantic pace of your home dimension to the glacial pace of the wood.

**2. The Language of Silence:**

The silence is not an absence of sound; it is the presence of life so total that it needs no announcement. Learn to hear it. The sub-audible hum of the amber "hives" is a form of communication. The resonance of a water drop tells you the size of the cavern it fell into. The creak of a branch is a tectonic event in this world. Your own loud heartbeats and crunching footfalls are a cacophony. Learn to move with the slowness of a root's growth.

**3. The Remnants and the Hum:**

You will find the crystalline structures and the pollen-slabs. Do not treat them as ruins to be explored. Approach them as you would a sleeping demigod. They are artifacts of a biology, not an industry. The low hum they emit is a boundary. Do not cross it. To touch them is to interface with a logic your nervous system is not equipped to handle. Observe from a distance. Record their patterns. Feel the vibrations in the soles of your feet. That is your communion.

**4. A Note on Time and Self:**

You will lose track of days. Your watch is a meaningless counter of a rhythm that does not apply here. The "dawn" may be the four-hour brightening cycle of a mycelial field. "Noon" is a barely perceptible shift in the hue of the canopy-filtered light. This temporal disconnect is the world’s most potent trap. It will lull you. Your ambitions, your memories, your very sense of self will seem loud and pointless. You may feel an overwhelming urge to simply stop, to find a comfortable nook in a Bole's root, and to wait.

This is the absorption. This is how the world takes you.

You must maintain a purpose. Your tether line is your connection not just to your entry point, but to your own timeline. Check it daily. Recite the names of your loved ones. Review your mission objectives. Perform a complex task, like disassembling and cleaning a piece of your gear. These rituals of self are your anchor against the immense, patient gravity of the Great Tableland.

**Departure:**

To leave is to commit an act of violence against the peace the world has offered you. You must re-embrace hurry. You must follow your line back with diligence, even as every fibre of your being screams at you to slow down, to rest, to listen to one more drop of water fall. When you cross back, the noise and speed of your own dimension will feel like a physical assault. Be prepared for a long and difficult period of re-acclimatization. You will have been inside a living, breathing god, and you will not leave unchanged. You will forever carry a splinter of its silence within you.
