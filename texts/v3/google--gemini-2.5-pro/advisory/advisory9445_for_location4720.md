This document is a pre-jump briefing for any traveler intending to visit the dimension catalogued as **The Choral Basin**. Read it three times. If you have any reservations after the third reading, cancel your trip. This is not a place for tourism, exploration in the classical sense, or ego. It is a place for quiet, humble observation, and survival is not guaranteed.

**Subject: Preparation for Visiting The Choral Basin**

**I. Pre-Jump Medical & Psychological Conditioning**

Before we even discuss gear, we must discuss your body and mind. The Basin will assault them in ways you have not yet experienced.

1.  **Auditory Acclimatization:** Standard-issue active noise-cancellation is a child's toy here. The sound is not merely traveling through the air to your eardrums; it is a form of pressure, a physical constant that will vibrate through your entire skeleton. You will need to begin a regimen of deep-bass and high-frequency saturation therapy. Spend at least 48 hours in a sensory chamber that simulates the described frequencies—a ground-shaking 5-20Hz hum, a dense mid-range of textural white noise, and piercing, random high-frequency spikes. The goal is not to get used to it—you won't—but to pre-empt the inevitable shock and vertigo. The primary cause of traveler loss in The Basin is not predation, but panic-induced sensory overload.

2.  **Proprioceptive Enhancement:** Your sight will be useless. You will learn to "see" differently (more on this later), but your primary sense of self and location will come from your body's position in space. Begin balance training now: yoga, slacklining, and extended periods in zero-G or fluid suspension tanks. You must become exquisitely aware of your own footfalls and the distribution of your weight.

3.  **Psychological Fortitude:** You will feel claustrophobia in a space of indeterminate, yet vast, size. You will feel agoraphobia in a space cluttered to the point of solidity. You will feel like an intruder, an impurity, a foreign object. Meditate on this. Practice sensory deprivation to become comfortable with your own thoughts when all external stimuli are alien. Understand this now: The Basin is not hostile. It is simply *occupied*. Its indifference to your existence will be more psychologically taxing than any malice. You are a germ, and the world is a body. Do not provoke the immune system.

**II. Essential Gear & Equipment: The Non-Negotiables**

Standard multiverse kits are inadequate. Your entire loadout must be built around the principles of **Isolation, Insulation, and Invisibility.**

1.  **The Suit: Your Second Skin:**
    *   **Outer Layer:** A non-porous, slick-coated polymer. It must be completely waterproof to shed the constantly dripping oily fluid from the webs. The fluid is corrosive to most organic materials and some plastics over time. The material should also be non-conductive and have a low coefficient of friction to avoid snagging on filaments.
    *   **Mid-Layer: The Haptic Dampener:** This is the most crucial component. It should be a pressure suit with an integrated, semi-rigid exoskeleton filled with a non-Newtonian dampening gel. This is your primary defense against the bone-jarring vibration from the ground. It will not eliminate it, but it will reduce it from a debilitating shudder to a manageable thrum.
    *   **Inner Layer:** Standard bio-monitoring and climate control. The ambient temperature is a constant, deceptive cool. The dampness and vibration will leach heat from you relentlessly. The suit must be able to maintain your core temperature without fail.

2.  **The Helmet: Your Sanctum:**
    *   **Auditory Interface:** Your ears must be completely protected. This means custom-molded, inert-gel earplugs, over which you will wear a sound-insulating helmet. The helmet should not have a standard comms unit. Radio waves will likely not propagate correctly, and speaking is impossible. Instead, it should feature a haptic-feedback wrist-pad for communicating pre-set messages or system diagnostics to a partner, and a direct sub-vocal mic to text display on your HUD if communication is essential.
    *   **Visual Interface:** *ABSOLUTELY NO ARTIFICIAL LIGHT SOURCES.* Do not bring a flashlight. Do not use your suit's external lamp. To introduce a broad-spectrum light source into The Basin is not like lighting a candle in a dark room; it is like screaming a string of obscenities in a library. You will wash out the bioluminescent data-stream, blinding yourself to your only source of information, and you will almost certainly trigger a defensive reaction from the weavers. Your visor should be configured as a low-light, high-contrast data-stream amplifier. It should be programmed to filter the three primary light sources (the low blue-green of the floor, the stark white of the weavers, the pulsing violet of the bells) into distinct, readable channels. Think of it less as a window and more as an information dashboard interpreting the world for you.

3.  **Footwear:** Soft-soled, broad, and with gecko-like micro-tread. You are walking on the backs of living creatures. Hard, heavy boots are an act of aggression. You want to distribute your weight and meld with the yielding surface, not punch through it.

4.  **The Pole:** Your only tool. A 10-foot, collapsible, non-conductive pole. It is not a weapon. It is a sensory extension. You will use it to *gently* feel for filaments in front of you that may be below your line of sight. It is your cane in the darkness. Do not use it to strike or push anything.

**III. On-Site Protocol: How to Be a Ghost**

1.  **Movement:** Slow. Deliberate. Each step is a conversation with the floor. Place your foot down, feel the ground yield, pause as the ripple of blue-green light spreads, and wait for it to be subsumed by the background noise before taking your next step. A frantic pace will send out waves of panic in the substrate, and that is a signal you do not want to send.

2.  **Navigating the Webs:** The filaments are everywhere. Look for the "corridors" where the bells have recently passed, visible on your HUD as channels with fewer white sparks. Move through these. When you encounter a filament, do not touch it with your suit. Use your pole to gently lift it or push it aside. If you brush against one, you will feel a tug. Freeze. The weavers respond to vibrations. If you remain still, your movement may be dismissed as a random tremor from a passing bell. If you thrash, they will interpret it as struggling prey and you will be swarmed. Their legs are sharp enough to puncture your suit.

3.  **Interaction with Fauna:**
    *   **The Floor (The Foundation):** Treat it with the reverence you would give the ground in a cathedral. It is the base of everything. Spilling rations, suit waste, or any other foreign matter onto it is forbidden.
    *   **The Weavers (The Architects):** Give them a wide berth. Their frantic, jerky movements are not random. They are maintaining a world. The white sparks from their legs are their work. If you see a dense cluster of brilliant white sparks, they are on a construction or repair project. Find another route.
    *   **The Bells (The Grazers):** They are beautiful, melodic, and dangerous. Do not move underneath them. Their tendrils can dissolve your suit's outer layer, and the fluid they drip is a resource for the Foundation. If you see a Bell begin to pulse violet erratically and weavers begin to congregate on the surrounding webs, it is about to be culled. Move away. The rain of its internal fluids that follows is a major event and will cause the Foundation below to glow with frenetic energy. You do not want to be in the middle of this feeding response.

4.  **Sustenance:** Your trip must be short. Your suit must have a closed-loop system. The air is breathable but filled with spores, metallic ions, and organic compounds your body has no way of processing. The water is an oily, alien cocktail. Do not, under any circumstances, expose your skin or consume anything. You are a self-contained system. Act like it.

**Conclusion: The Goal is to Leave No Trace**

You are not there to conquer, to claim, or to change. You are there to witness. The Choral Basin is a perfectly balanced, complete system that has existed for eons without you. The fact that it does not immediately annihilate you is a testament not to its weakness, but to its sheer, overwhelming preoccupation with its own existence.

Your mission is to enter, observe, and leave, having disturbed the symphony as little as possible. The ultimate success is for The Basin to have no memory of your ever having been there at all. Be small. Be quiet. Be humble. And get out before the silence within your own helmet becomes the only thing you can hear.
