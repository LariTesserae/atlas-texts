**Multiverse Traveler Advisory M-734-Gamma**
**Designation: The Janitorium**

**Attention Traveler,**

You have filed an itinerary for The Janitorium, a world-system operating in a state of perfect, homeostatic equilibrium. This is not a recreational destination. This is a site for specialists—xenobiologists, materials scientists, systems theorists, and philosophers with a penchant for the profound. Your survival, and more importantly, the integrity of this unique and fragile system, depends on your strict adherence to the following preparatory protocols. Read this document in its entirety. There will be no second chances.

### **I. Environmental Profile & Primary Threats**

The Janitorium is defined by three constants: Heat, Humidity, and Function.

*   **Atmosphere:** The ambient temperature is high, stable, and saturated. Expect 40-45°C (104-113°F) with near 100% humidity. The air is thick with mineral-rich vapor, giving it a tangible, soupy quality. This is not weather; it is the permanent state of the world's breath. The primary threats are hyperthermia, severe skin conditions from perpetual dampness, and potential respiratory complications from the dense, particulate-heavy air.
*   **Terrain:** The ground is a single, unbroken surface of perfectly fitted, off-white ceramic tiles. It is slick with a constant film of moisture. The macro-terrain consists of low, continent-spanning swells, like a petrified ocean. The primary threats are slips and falls, which can be catastrophic in such a remote environment, and spatial disorientation due to the monotonous, featureless landscape.
*   **Infrastructure & Fauna:** The world is a machine tended by its own biological components. All life-forms are integral parts of the system's maintenance cycle. They will not interact with you, but your interaction with them could have devastating consequences. The machinery itself (cubes, pipes, grates) operates on unknown principles and should be considered an unpredictable hazard.

### **II. Mandatory Equipment & Loadout**

Standard field kits are inadequate. Your loadout must be specialized for a high-heat, high-humidity, non-biological environment.

**1. Personal Thermoregulatory Suit (The "Second Skin"):**
Do not even consider textile-based clothing. It will become a sodden, chafing deathtrap within minutes. You require a full-body, non-porous but breathable micro-mesh suit.
*   **Cooling System:** The suit must have an integrated active cooling system. A simple evaporative system will fail in 100% humidity. You need a closed-loop coolant system with heat sinks that can function in high ambient temperatures.
*   **Moisture Management:** The exterior must be hydrophobic to shed the constant weeping moisture. The interior must aggressively wick sweat away from your skin and reclaim it for your water supply.
*   **Durability:** The suit must be resistant to abrasion and puncture. A fall on the sharp-edged tiles could compromise a lesser material.

**2. Footwear: The "Gecko" Boot:**
Your life will depend on your footing.
*   **Soles:** You need boots with active or microscopic traction systems. Soles with deployable micro-suction cups or a "gecko-grip" van der Waals adhesive surface are recommended. Test them extensively on polished, wet ceramic before departure.
*   **Structure:** High-ankled, rigid support is mandatory to prevent sprains or fractures from a slip. They must be perfectly integrated with your Second Skin to form a complete seal.

**3. Life Support & Sustenance:**
There is nothing for you to eat or drink. There is nowhere for you to take shelter.
*   **Rebreather/Air Filtration:** While the atmosphere appears breathable, its long-term effects and specific composition are unknown. A full rebreather is recommended to guarantee oxygen levels and filter unknown particulates. At minimum, a high-efficiency filtration mask must be worn at all times to protect your lungs from the mineral-rich vapor.
*   **Water Reclamation & Purification:** Your suit's moisture-wicking function should feed directly into a high-capacity water purification system. You will be your own primary water source. Bring a reserve of 10 liters as a non-negotiable emergency backup. DO NOT attempt to drink the local water; it is a raw, mineral-heavy industrial run-off.
*   **Nutrition:** All food must be carried in. Nutrient pastes or dense, vacuum-sealed ration bars are ideal. They must be stable in high-heat, high-humidity conditions. Plan for 150% of your expected caloric needs.
*   **Shelter:** A personal habitation unit is essential. It must be able to establish a dry, temperature-controlled micro-environment. Its primary function is to give your body and mind a reprieve from the oppressive environment. It should also have a robust air filtration system to create a "clean room" for equipment maintenance.

**4. Navigation & Safety:**
*   **Inertial Navigation Unit (INU):** Standard GPS may be unreliable. The vast, uniform landscape offers no landmarks. An INU, combined with a manual compass and rigorous distance-tracking, is your only way to avoid getting lost.
*   **Beacon:** Upon arrival, your first action is to deploy a high-power, multi-spectrum beacon at your insertion point. Do not stray beyond its effective signal range unless you are deploying a chain of smaller, secondary beacons.
*   **Cutting Tools:** While destruction of the environment is forbidden, you may need to free yourself if a limb is caught. A plasma cutter or similar high-energy tool is recommended. Note that any incidental damage must be reported in your post-trip debrief.

### **III. Psychological Preparedness**

The greatest threat in The Janitorium may not be to your body, but to your mind. This environment is anathema to the human psyche.

*   **Sensory Monotony:** The world is defined by a handful of repeating sensory inputs: the single low hum, the single visual texture, the single temperature, the single smell of ozone and wet minerals. This can induce a state of sensory deprivation, leading to hallucinations, paranoia, and severe disorientation.
*   **The Hum and The Drip:** The constant, low-frequency hum from the cubes will vibrate through your bones. It will quickly become your baseline for silence. Against this, the slow, thick *plock* of the dripping water will become unnervingly prominent. Travelers report that this sound can become an object of intense, maddening focus. It is recommended to have audiobooks, music, or white noise generators available inside your habitat to break the monotony.
*   **Profound Alienation:** You are a foreign object. Nothing will acknowledge your existence. The rice-sized cleaners will flow around your boots. The armored grazers will continue their silent work even if you are inches away. You are an error, a ghost in the machine. This feeling of total irrelevance can be deeply unsettling. A clear sense of purpose and a rigid daily schedule (e.g., "08:00: Observe Pipe Grazers. 12:00: Return to Habitation. 13:00: Data Log & System Check.") are your best defenses against existential malaise.
*   **Agoraphobia:** The sheer scale of the place, the visible curvature of the world on the clean horizon, can induce a powerful sense of agoraphobia or "kenophobia" (fear of the void). You may feel crushingly small and exposed. Keep your eyes on the middle distance when traversing open plains.

### **IV. Interaction Protocols: The First and Only Rule is OBSERVE**

You are not to interfere. Your mission is to document, not to act.

*   **The Cleaners ("Tileskin"):** You will inevitably step on them. It is unavoidable. Do not let this distress you; they are numberless. However, make an effort to tread lightly. Note the slick, organic residue they leave. Take samples only if your mission requires it, and do so with micro-tools.
*   **The Grazers ("Pipe-slugs"):** These are magnificent subjects for study. Observe their peristaltic motion, the function of their articulated armor, and their clustering behavior around pipe joints. Do not touch them. We do not know if their vitreous flesh is toxic, or if their armored plates have defensive properties. Their sheer mass could also present a crushing hazard.
*   **The Collectors ("Assemblers"):** These are the most complex organisms and must be treated with extreme caution. Maintain a distance of at least 50 meters. Their composite limbs may be capable of swift movement, and their function (recycling) may lead them to perceive your equipment—or you, if injured—as debris to be collected. Their pulsating organic core suggests a much more complex biology than the other creatures. Do not get between them and their objectives.

Your visit to The Janitorium is a privilege. It is a glimpse into a form of order that is utterly alien to the chaotic, evolutionary struggle that defines most life in the multiverse. Be a silent guest. Be a respectful observer. Take your readings, collect your thoughts, and leave it as pristine as you found it. Your sanity may depend on it.
