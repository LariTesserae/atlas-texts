### Traveler's Advisory & Preparation Guide: The Caldera Progenitus

**Designation:** The Caldera Progenitus / The World-Womb / The Forge

**Risk Assessment:** Extreme (Environmental, Mechanical, Biological)

**Primary Appeal:** Witnessing abiogenesis and planet-scale terraforming in real-time. Unparalleled geological and xenobiological survey opportunity. A profound, sublime, and deeply humbling experience.

---

### **1. Pre-Departure Medical & Psychological Screening**

Do not skip this section. The Forge is as much a psychological trial as it is a physical one.

*   **Sensory Acclimatization:** The soundscape is not background noise; it is a physical pressure. The constant, bone-deep hum, layered with high-frequency, chaotic impacts, can induce nausea, vertigo, and severe anxiety. We recommend at least 48 hours of pre-trip exposure to high-decibel, multi-layered audio stimulants. Candidates with sensory processing disorders, tinnitus, or a low tolerance for auditory chaos are strongly advised against this trip.
*   **Psychological Fortitude:** You will be in a place that is perpetually unfinished. The lack of stable reference points, the constantly shifting ground, and the oppressive, bruised twilight can trigger acute agoraphobia, claustrophobia (due to the thick fog), and existential dread. You are a microscopic irrelevance in a process of immense power. A firm grip on your own sense of self is mandatory.
*   **Thermal Tolerance:** The environment is consistently hot and humid. Your body will be under constant thermal stress. Screen for heat intolerance and ensure your physical conditioning is at a peak.

### **2. Essential Equipment & Wardrobe: Your Personal Biosphere**

You are not visiting a world; you are visiting a workshop. Dress accordingly. Standard atmospheric-exposure suits are insufficient. You require a full, sealed **Environmental Work Shell (EWS)**.

**EWS specifications:**

*   **Thermal Regulation:** The primary function of your suit is cooling. The ground can reach temperatures that will melt standard boot soles, and the air is saturated with superheated steam. Your suit must have a high-capacity heat-pump system with redundant power.
*   **Impact Plating:** Prioritize protection for the lower legs and feet. The ground cracks, splits, and extrudes material with explosive force. Shrapnel of cooling protolith is a constant hazard. Your greaves and boots should be reinforced with impact-absorbing composites.
*   **Footwear:** This is paramount. The soles of your boots must be heavily insulated and feature micro-spike/crampon systems made from a high-tensile, heat-resistant carbide. The glassy protolith is slick, and a fall could place you on a surface that is still pliable and searingly hot. Magnetic locking is useless here; the ground is not reliably ferrous.
*   **Sealed System & Air Supply:** The mineral fog is an unknown. We do not know what long-term exposure to atomized embryonic geology does to a human lung, and we do not wish to find out. Your EWS must be 100% sealed with a rebreather and at least a 12-hour reserve oxygen supply. The “air” is not for you.
*   **Condensation Management:** Your suit's exterior will be perpetually wet. Ensure your outermost layer is aggressively hydrophobic to prevent excess weight gain and thermal leeching. The "weeping" water is startlingly pure, but do not be tempted to use it. Its mineral content and thermal energy are wildcards.

**Head-Up Display (HUD) & Audio Suite:**

*   **Visual Spectrum:** Your visor must have multi-spectrum capability.
    *   **Thermal Imaging:** Essential. This will allow you to read the ground, identifying dangerously hot, newly-formed areas and stress lines before they crack. It is also your best tool for spotting the heavily armored predators.
    *   **Low-Light Amplification:** The bruised twilight is constant. You will need amplification to see any detail not provided by thermal signatures.
    *   **Polarized Filtering:** The insectoid grazers have crystalline wings that can catch the light from the central spire in blinding flashes. A polarized filter will save your vision.
*   **Audio Modulation:** Do not simply use earplugs. You need to hear the *right* sounds. Your helmet should be equipped with a tunable audio dampener. Program it to filter out the foundational hum and the constant hiss, while isolating and amplifying the specific frequencies of cracking stone (imminent ground hazard) and the rhythmic *thump* of the six-legged predators (imminent biological hazard).

### **3. On-Site Conduct: How to Not Become Part of the Landscape**

The rules are simple, brutal, and absolute.

*   **Rule #1: The Floor is Lava (Literally).** Never stand still for more than a few seconds. The ground is a living process. That stable-looking patch of dark glass was molten a minute ago and could crack open beneath you without warning. Watch for the glowing orange filaments—they are the seams of this world, and they are about to burst. Move deliberately, testing the ground ahead of you with a shock-absorbing pole or probe if possible.
*   **Rule #2: You Are Not on the Food Chain.** The local fauna is a closed system. The glassy grazers eat the hot rock. The armored walkers eat the grazers. Nothing is evolved to eat you, but that doesn't mean it won't.
    *   **The Grazers:** They will ignore you. Do not swat at them. A swarm of dinner-plate-sized crystalline insects can be disorienting, but they are not hostile. Their mandibles can scrape patterns into obsidian; do not let them touch your suit's visor.
    *   **The Walkers:** These are the apex predators and your single greatest biological threat. Their ceramic carapaces diffuse thermal scans, making them difficult to track. Listen for their footfalls. If you see one, freeze. Do not present as a threat or a target. Do not attempt to engage. Its striking limb moves faster than your eye can track, and it can shatter protolith. Your suit is not protolith, but it's a test you don't want to take. Your best defense is to not be noticed.
*   **Rule #3: Respect the Machinery.** The central spire and the surrounding Conductors are not monuments. They are active, unimaginably powerful pieces of technology.
    *   Maintain a minimum safe distance of 500 meters from the central spire. The moments it phases from solid to light are accompanied by massive energy discharges across multiple spectra.
    *   Do not approach the violet-glowing Conductors. Do not touch the liquid-metal wires snaking from them. Assume they are carrying enough energy to power a continent, and that they are completely unshielded.

### **4. Sustenance**

This is simple: **Bring your own.** All of it.

*   **Food:** Nutrient paste, ration bars. Nothing that requires cooking or preparation.
*   **Water:** Your suit's rebreather and internal climate control should recycle most of your perspiration, but bring emergency reserves. Do NOT, under any circumstances, drink the water weeping from the rocks or pooling on the ground. Its purity is deceptive. It is hot, saturated with unknown minerals, and actively part of the terraforming process. Your biology is not.

### **In Summary: The Experience**

If you have followed this guide, you are prepared for the hazards. Now, prepare for the awe.

Find a relatively stable outcrop—one that has been cool for a while, where the crystalline "vegetation" has had time to grow. Settle in, trust your suit and your senses, and watch.

Watch a world being born. Feel the vibration of physics putting matter into place. See the raw material of a planet churn and cool at your feet. Listen to the chimes of mineral life feeding on pure energy. Witness the brutal, elegant dance of an ecosystem finding its footing on a world that is not yet a day old.

You are a guest in the furnace of creation. You are standing in a place of sublime, raw, and terrifying power. The goal is not to conquer it, or even to fully understand it. The goal is to witness it, and to survive the witnessing.

Act like the privileged, fragile guest you are. And for the love of the multiverse, watch your step.
