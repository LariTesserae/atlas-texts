### **Pre-Departure Briefing: Cinnabar Colonnade (Designation: Resonant Geode A-714)**

Welcome, traveler. You have been cleared for a visit to a location that defies several core principles of what we typically call "a place." It is a world born of sound, existing in a state of perfect, fragile stasis. Your presence—your warmth, your moisture, your very biology—is a fundamental contaminant. Success is measured not by what you achieve within the Colonnade, but by the fact that you leave it at all, having disturbed it as little as possible.

This is a guide for a short-duration, observation-only visit. Any other agenda is unsanctioned and likely suicidal.

---

### **Phase 1: The Approach (The Climb and the Seeking)**

This is a test of endurance and skill before the true trial even begins. Do not underestimate it.

**Physical Preparation:**
*   **Mountaineering Expertise:** You must be an expert climber, comfortable with high-altitude, technical, unaided ascents on sheer granite. There are no ropes, no pitons you can leave behind. This is a free-climb scenario in extreme conditions. Train for this specifically.
*   **Altitude Acclimatization:** Spend at least two weeks in the immediate multiverse cluster, acclimating to a high-altitude, low-oxygen normal. Even so, you will be climbing in the equivalent of the "death zone." Your approach suit should have a supplementary oxygen supply. The air is thin to the point of being a borderline vacuum.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The exterior is knife-cold. Your approach suit must be rated for extreme hypothermic conditions. A standard down suit is insufficient; think expedition-grade, self-heating thermal layering.

**Navigational Gear:**
*   **Fracture Sonar:** The entrance is a "hairline fracture." It is visually indistinguishable from any other crack in the mountain face from more than five feet away. You will need a specialized, short-range geological sonar device calibrated to detect resonant pocket-realities. It will not point you to the entrance, but it will hum with increasing intensity as you get closer, allowing you to narrow your search grid on the rock face.
*   **Anchor Beacon:** Before you enter, you will place a micro-beacon at the exterior of the crack. This is your *only* way of finding your way out. The crack is invisible from the inside. Do not forget this step. Do not use a light-based beacon; the interior light will swallow it. It must be a trans-dimensional signal your suit's HUD can lock onto.

---

### **Phase 2: The Breach (The Transition)**

The moment of entry is a profound sensory shock. You must be prepared for it, or you risk disorientation.

*   **Physical Entry:** The crack is just wide enough for a body. Your gear must be streamlined. No bulky external packs. All equipment must be integrated into your suit. You will have to exhale, compress yourself, and pull your way through. It will feel like being born and buried at once.
*   **Sensory Whiplash Protocol:** Brace yourself. You will fall from a world of screaming wind, biting cold, and near-silence into one of absolute stillness, moderate coolness, and overwhelming, architectural noise. The sudden cessation of wind and the instantaneous onset of the Resonator's hum have been known to cause vertigo and nausea. For the first thirty seconds, do nothing. Remain crouched where you land, let your suit's systems stabilize, and allow your brain to build a new model of reality.

---

### **Phase 3: The Interior (Survival in the Terrarium)**

You are now an alien. Your suit is your world. A breach in your suit is not an inconvenience; it is a death sentence.

**A. Atmospheric & Physiological Isolation: The Dryness**

This is the single most critical environmental factor. The air inside is **completely desiccated**. It is more than dry; it is actively hygroscopic. It will pull the moisture out of any organic material with terrifying efficiency.

*   **Required Equipment: Full-Seal Environmental Suit (Type IV or higher).** This is non-negotiable. No gaps, no exposed skin, no exceptions.
    *   **Respiratory System:** A closed-loop rebreather is essential. Do not attempt to filter the local atmosphere. The lack of H₂O and the high ozone/metallic particulate content will destroy your lungs in minutes. Your suit will be your only source of breathable, humidified air.
    *   **Ocular Protection:** Your helmet's visor is your shield. The air that feels "gritty" on unshielded eyes is actively abrading your cornea and dehydrating your vitreous humor. Do not lift your visor.
    *   **Hydration:** Your suit must have an integrated hydration system, delivering water directly to you. A simple water bottle is useless; the moment you opened it, the water would begin to vaporize.

**B. Auditory Protection & Management: The Sound**

The sound is the second-most critical factor. It is not just loud; it is invasive, operating on multiple frequencies, one of which directly vibrates your skeleton.

*   **Required Equipment: Multi-Layered Auditory Dampening Helmet.**
    *   **Layer 1 (Passive):** Sound-absorbing foam and gel layers to block the high-frequency shrieking and roaring.
    *   **Layer 2 (Active):** An active noise-cancellation system synchronized to the primary frequencies of the Resonator. Your suit's computer will need to analyze the sound and generate a counter-wave. This will reduce the perceptible noise to a manageable, low hum.
    *   **Layer 3 (Inertial):** A skeletal-resonance dampener. This is the most complex component. It consists of small, counter-vibrating nodes in the helmet and collar of your suit that work to nullify the deep, bone-conducting hum. Without this, you will experience debilitating nausea, migraines, toothaches, and potential micro-fractures in your inner ear bones. Your visit must be kept short regardless; long-term exposure can still cause cumulative resonance damage.

**C. Physical Protection & Movement: The Crystal Forest**

The Colonnade is beautiful, but it is a forest of razors and glass. Treat every surface as hostile.

*   **Suit Reinforcement:** Your suit must be armored with a high-tensile, puncture-resistant material. Aramid-weave composites are a minimum. Key areas like shins, forearms, hands, and the soles of your boots should be reinforced with a synthetic diamond or ceramic coating.
*   **Movement Protocol:**
    1.  **Move Slowly.** Every step must be deliberate.
    2.  **Test Your Footing.** The "moss" floor will crunch and can be unpredictably unstable over its deeper mounds. It offers poor traction. Magnetized boots are ineffective on the non-ferrous crystal.
    3.  **Create a Path.** You cannot move without breaking the crystal foliage. Accept this. Clear a path with slow, methodical movements of your arms. Do not swipe or rush. Use your reinforced forearms to push the crystal plates aside. They will shatter. The sound will be a high-pitched tinkle, instantly swallowed by the Resonator's roar.
    4.  **Beware Falling Shards.** When you break a higher "branch," be aware that a cascade of blade-thin crystal will fall. Keep your head down and trust your helmet. The shards are light but incredibly sharp.
    5.  **Look, Do Not Touch.** Do not run your gloved hand along a basalt "trunk" or grasp a crystal "leaf." The microscopic texture can shred even reinforced gloves over time.

### **Psychological Preparation & Rules of Engagement**

*   **Embrace the Alienation:** You are a brief, wet spark in a dry, loud eternity. This feeling can be profoundly unsettling. Meditate on this before entry. Your purpose is to witness, not to belong.
*   **Time Limit:** Your initial visit should not exceed **one hour**. The cumulative stress of the vibration, the disorienting light, and the psychological weight of the place is immense. Set a timer. Obey it.
*   **The Resonator:** You will see the central machine. **Do not approach it.** Its immediate vicinity is a zone of intense, un-dampened sonic energy. The anchor chains vibrate with enough force to tear your suit. The machine is a geological force, not a device to be tinkered with.
*   **Leave No Trace (An Impossibility):** You will inevitably leave traces—broken crystals, footprints in the moss-fuzz. The goal is to minimize this. Do not drop equipment. Do not attempt to leave graffiti. Do not take souvenirs beyond what a micro-drone sample collector can safely extract from an already-broken shard. The ancient wrench fused to the floor is not a souvenir; it is a gravestone. Pay it the same respect.

### **Departure**

*   Follow your suit's HUD back to the beacon you placed outside the crack. It will be your only guide.
*   The exit is as physically demanding as the entry.
*   Be prepared for the return of sensory input: the shock of cold, the sudden violence of the wind, and the deafening, beautiful silence that comes after the roar of the Colonnade is finally cut off. You may find this silence more jarring than the initial noise.

You are visiting a sculpture carved by sound over a million years. You are a microbe on a masterpiece. Go with humility, with overwhelming preparation, and with the understanding that the Cinnabar Colonnade does not care if you live or die. It only sings its one, long, unchanging song. Make sure you are able to walk away to hear silence once more.
