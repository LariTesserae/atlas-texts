Excellent. This is a place of profound and dangerous beauty. Here is how an ordinary human traveler should prepare for a visit to the Arrested World.

### A Traveler's Advisory: Preparation for the Stillness

**Foreword:** You are preparing to visit a location that travelers have colloquially termed "The Stillness," "The Arrested World," or, by the more poetically inclined, "The Land of the Unfallen Tear." Your primary challenges here will not be monstrous beasts or hostile armies, but the environment itself—its deep, pervasive quietude and its fundamental physical stasis. Survival is possible, but sanity and comfort require careful and specific preparation. Read this guide thoroughly. Do not skim. Your well-being depends on understanding the unique rules of this world.

#### I. Psychological Preparation: The Primary Challenge

Before we discuss gear, you must first prepare your mind. The defining characteristic of this world—its absolute lack of environmental dynamism—is a profound psychological poison for the unprepared human mind. Our brains are wired to process change: the shifting of light, the rustle of wind, the flow of water. The absence of these cues can lead to a condition colloquially known as **Stillness Sickness** or **Acoustic Vertigo**.

*   **Symptoms:** Initial feelings of profound peace quickly give way to disorientation, anxiety, and a creeping sense of dread. Your own internal sounds (heartbeat, breathing, digestion) will become unnervingly loud in the foundational silence. The unchanging light and fixed shadows can disrupt your sense of time, leading to insomnia, paranoia, and, in severe cases, hallucinations.
*   **Mitigation:**
    *   **Bring Your Own Noise:** Pack a media player with a robust battery and a solar charger. Fill it with music, audiobooks, and podcasts. Even ambient soundscapes from your home world (rain, wind, city noise) can be a lifeline. Use bone-conduction headphones to remain aware of your surroundings while providing your brain with necessary auditory stimulus.
    *   **Bring Your Own Change:** Pack a deck of cards, a set of dice, or a book of puzzles. Introduce randomness and activity into your personal space. A simple routine of shuffling cards can be a powerful anchor.
    *   **Journaling:** Document your experience rigorously. The act of writing is an act of creation and change. It will help you process the profound strangeness and mark the passage of your own, personal time.
    *   **Embrace Meditation, Carefully:** Do not try to empty your mind. Instead, use mindfulness techniques to focus on your own body's rhythms. Focus on your breath, the beat of your heart. In this place, *you* are the only dynamic system. Ground yourself in that fact.

#### II. Essential Gear & Packing List

Your pack must be tailored to combat two primary environmental factors: **the perpetual, non-precipitating dampness** and **the razor-sharp terrain**.

**Clothing & Personal Wear:**

*   **Outer Layer:** A high-quality, fully waterproof but *highly breathable* hardshell jacket and pants are non-negotiable. The threat is not rain from above, but the constant, clammy dew permeating everything. Non-breathable materials will trap your own body's moisture, leaving you soaked from the inside out.
*   **Mid-Layers:** Merino wool or high-performance synthetic fleece. Wool is a miracle material here, as it insulates even when damp. Bring multiple, lightweight layers.
*   **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking synthetics or merino wool. Absolutely no cotton. Cotton will absorb the ambient moisture, chill you to the bone, and never, ever dry.
*   **Footwear:** This is critical. You need sturdy, high-ankled hiking boots with stiff soles to protect you from the flint-like basalt. The soles must have an aggressive, deep-lugged tread made of a soft, grippy rubber compound. The rock surfaces are perpetually slick with dew. One bad slip can be fatal.
*   **Gloves:** Durable, waterproof work gloves. You will constantly be touching wet, sharp rock. Protecting your hands from cuts and moisture is paramount.
*   **Headwear:** A hooded jacket is superior to a hat. It will keep the un-falling dew from constantly beading on your hair and neck without obstructing your view.
*   **Sleep System:** A synthetic-fill sleeping bag (down will clump and lose all loft in the damp) and a high-quality inflatable sleeping pad with a good R-value for insulation from the cold, damp ground. A waterproof bivy sack is highly recommended as an extra layer of protection for your sleeping bag.

**Sustenance & Health:**

*   **Water:** Do NOT drink the water beaded on surfaces. While it appears pure, its microbiology is unknown and it has been sitting, unmoving, for an unknown length of time. Bring a robust water filter with viral protection and a secondary purification system (UV sterilizer or tablets). Your best bet is to find a large moss cushion and press water from it, then filter/purify.
*   **Food:** Bring high-energy, high-flavor food. Your sense of taste may be heightened in the absence of other stimuli, but monotony is your enemy. Bring varied textures and flavors: salty, sweet, crunchy, chewy. Do not attempt to forage. The local flora is part of a static ecosystem and likely inedible or toxic.
*   **First Aid:** Your kit must be comprehensive. Include a generous supply of antiseptic wipes, antibiotic ointment, and durable bandages for cuts and scrapes. The constant dampness is a breeding ground for infection. Also include blister treatment (moleskin), pain relievers, and any personal medications.

**Tools & Technology:**

*   **Navigation:** A GPS unit is essential. The scale of the landscape is deceptive, and the unchanging light means you cannot navigate by the sun. Mark the location of your arrival portal immediately. Do not rely solely on a magnetic compass, as local geology may interfere with it.
*   **Illumination:** A powerful LED headlamp and a backup flashlight, with extra batteries or a charging solution. While the ambient light is constant, it is not guaranteed to last forever, and you will need to explore dark crevices or the interiors of structures.
*   **Trekking Poles:** Highly recommended. They provide two extra points of contact for stability on the slick, uneven terrain and can be used to probe the depth of moss beds.
*   **Shelter:** A free-standing tent. You cannot rely on staking into the hard basalt flats. A dome or geodesic design will be stable without needing guylines.

#### III. Navigating the Landscape & Its Inhabitants

**The Terrain:**

*   The flat valley floors are the safest areas for travel, but they are vast and exposed. Plan your routes carefully.
*   The mountain slopes are treacherous. The rock is sharp and unstable. Only experienced climbers should attempt any vertical ascents.
*   The great bridges are your friends. They are feats of impossible engineering and offer safe passage over chasms. Do not be unnerved by their height; they are more stable than the mountains they connect to. Respect them.

**The Flora and Fauna:**

*   **Rule #1: Observe, Do Not Disturb.** You are a guest in a perfectly balanced, static diorama. The six-legged grazers are placid; give them a wide berth. The gliding birds follow fixed paths; do not obstruct them. Your interference could have unknown, cascading consequences.
*   The house-sized moss cushions are incredible sound absorbers. If you need to have a private conversation or test a piece of equipment that makes noise, do it near one of these. Be aware that they can be deeper than they look.

**The Builders:**

These silent, gaunt people are the world's only engine of entropy. They are architects of eternity. All your interactions must be guided by one principle: **Profound Respect for Their Work.**

*   **Approach:** Never approach a Builder who is actively working. Do not interrupt the swing of a mallet or the positioning of a stone. Wait at a respectful distance until they pause. Approach slowly, from the front, with your hands visible and empty.
*   **Communication:** Do not expect spoken language. A slight, slow bow of the head is the customary greeting. Their work is their language. To show respect, observe their craft. Notice the precision of their stonework. Run a hand (gently!) over a finished surface. A nod of appreciation is a powerful statement.
*   **Sound:** Do not make unnecessary noise. Their chisel-strikes and shouted commands are the only punctuation in the silence. Your idle chatter is a discordant intrusion. Speak only when necessary, and speak quietly. If you must make a loud noise, move far away from any active construction sites.
*   **Interaction:** They are not hostile, but they are utterly focused. They may ignore you completely. Do not take this as an insult. If one acknowledges you, it will be with a slow, deliberate gaze. They are assessing your purpose. Your purpose should be simple: to witness, to understand, and to leave no mark. Do not ask for aid unless it is a dire emergency. Do not offer help; their methods are their own and perfected over eons.

#### IV. Final Admonition: The Rule of Departure

This world will not reclaim what you leave behind. There is no wind to scatter your trash, no water to wash it away, no soil to bury it. An empty ration packet dropped on the basalt will lie there, glistening with dew, for an eternity. You must adhere to the most stringent principles of "Leave No Trace." Pack out everything you pack in. You are a ghost here. Pass through, witness the silent, magnificent, terrible beauty, and then vanish as completely as a spoken word into the moss. You will be changed by the Stillness, but it must, under no circumstances, be changed by you.
