**Multiverse Traveler's Advisory & Preparation Guide**
**Location Designation: The Bog-Tower of Murkfast (Classification: Biome-Polis, Endosymbiotic)**

**WARNING: Entry to this location is involuntary and unpredictable, typically occurring in liminal spaces adjacent to cold, marshy biomes. There is no known intentional method of entry or exit. Prepare for an indefinite stay.**

### **Introduction: A Change of State**

You are not preparing for a trip. You are preparing for a baptism. The moment you pass through the veil into Murkfast, you cease to be a visitor in a landscape and become a particle within a metabolism. Every piece of advice that follows is based on one unbreakable principle: **You are in the gut of a god, and your survival depends on being good for its digestion.** Do not think of yourself as an explorer. Think of yourself as a harmless bacterium, a piece of undigested matter, a ghost. Your goal is not to conquer or chart, but to pass through with minimal disturbance.

### **Phase 1: Pre-Emptive Carry & Attire**
*For the traveler who frequents environments prone to reality-folds.*

Since you cannot plan your arrival, you must make certain items part of your standard gear whenever you are in a potential transition zone (marshes, fogs, ancient groves).

**Clothing: The Second Skin**

Your clothing is your primary defense against the pervasive, chilling damp.

*   **Footwear:** Your single most critical item. You need boots with the best grip imaginable. The root-mat floor is slick, springy, and treacherous. Look for deep-lugged soles made of a soft, sticky rubber compound. They must be impeccably waterproof. A high-top or mid-calf boot is preferable to prevent the cold, soupy water of the floor-channels from seeping in.
*   **Base Layer:** Merino wool or high-grade synthetic. It must be moisture-wicking and insulating even when damp, because you *will* be damp. The air is cold and saturated. Cotton is a death sentence; it will hold the chill against your skin and invite hypothermia. Pack at least one spare set in a completely waterproof dry bag.
*   **Mid-Layer:** A fleece or puffy jacket (down is risky due to the moisture; synthetic fill is better). This is for stationary periods. You'll likely shed it while moving.
*   **Outer Shell:** Your armor against the world-weep. A durable, fully waterproof, and—crucially—*silent* rain jacket and pants. Avoid noisy nylon. You want to move without a constant *swish-swish-swish* announcing your foreign presence. Dark, earthy colors only. Black, dark brown, deep olive. You must blend into the perpetual twilight.
*   **Gloves:** Two pairs. A thin, dextrous pair of waterproof gloves for tasks, and a thicker, warmer pair of mittens for when you are still. Your hands will be cold.

**Essential Gear: The Survivor's Rosary**

This is the small, sacred kit that lives in your pack.

*   **Water Purification:** **NON-NEGOTIABLE.** The water of Murkfast is a living broth, teeming with unknown microorganisms. It will kill you, drive you mad, or change you into something else. You need a multi-stage approach.
    1.  A physical pump filter with a fine pore size (0.1 microns or less) to remove the particulates and larger organisms.
    2.  A chemical purifier (chlorine dioxide tablets or drops) or a UV sterilizer pen to kill anything the filter misses.
    3.  Bring a durable water bottle or bladder. You will need to process every single drop you drink.
*   **Light Source:** Do NOT bring a high-lumen tactical flashlight. To switch on a 1000-lumen beam in Murkfast would be the equivalent of screaming a string of profanities in a silent monastery. It is an act of aggression. Your light must be modest and respectful. A headlamp with a red-light function is ideal. The red wavelength is less disruptive to nocturnal or low-light ecologies. Use the lowest possible setting you need to see your feet. Your eyes will adjust to the gloom and the bioluminescence. Rely on the Tower's light first.
*   **Cutting Tool:** A sturdy, fixed-blade knife of good steel. It's not a weapon. It is a tool for preparing food (your own), cutting cordage, and, in a dire emergency, defending yourself against the less-sentient fauna. A secondary, smaller multi-tool is also wise.
*   **Navigation:** Your compass is a paperweight. Your GPS is a brick. Navigation is visceral. Bring a spool of biodegradable jute or hemp twine. You can trail it behind you to create a return path. Tying small, distinct knots at intervals can help you map your immediate vicinity. Also consider a small bag of crushed chalk or charcoal to make subtle, non-permanent marks on the pillars. Do not carve them. Do not paint them.
*   **Fire:** Do not attempt to make fire. The humidity is too high, dry tinder is non-existent, and the smoke would be an intolerable pollutant to the Tower and its inhabitants. An open flame is an act of war.
*   **Sustenance:** Pack energy-dense, calorie-rich, and completely odorless food. Vacuum-sealed protein bars, pemmican, hardtack. Anything in a noisy crinkling wrapper should be repackaged into silicone bags. Do not, under any circumstances, eat the local flora or fauna unless gifted to you by an inhabitant. The beetles may be livestock to them, but their biology is alien. You have no idea what toxins or parasites they host.
*   **Shelter:** A small, sil-nylon tarp or bivy sack in a dark color. Not for warmth, but for dryness. Stringing it up will create a space where the constant drip from above does not fall on you, which is a psychological and physical necessity for rest. A hammock is an even better option, getting you off the crawling, saturated floor.

### **Phase 2: The Mental Shift (Acclimatization)**

You will fall, and your world will be gone. The shock will be immense. Ground yourself.

*   **Breathe:** The air is thick, cool, and smells of wet stone, ozone, and mushrooms. Focus on it. It is the first sign that you are alive.
*   **Listen:** Your ears will strain against the silence, and then they will open. Learn the soundscape. The *drip* is your clock. The *hiss* is the weather. The *tear* is construction. The *skittering* is the populace. Do not fear it; learn its rhythms. A change in the skittering is your only early warning system.
*   **See:** Let your eyes adjust. It will take time, maybe an hour. You will start to see the world in shades of glowing blue, green, and profound black. You will learn to read the mycelial "roads." Brighter veins mean more traffic or more recent growth. Fading veins are old, unused paths.
*   **Accept Entomophobia:** If you have a fear of insects, arachnids, or myriapods, you have two choices: enter a state of catatonic terror, or achieve enlightenment. Every surface is alive. The wall is a mosaic of beetles. The ceiling is a carpet of moths. The floor is a tide of isopods. They are not an infestation; they *are* the world. They will crawl over you if you stay still. They are not malicious. They are curious, or indifferent. Let the moth with wings of powdered silver rest on your shoulder. Feel its weight. Watch it breathe. Accept that you are a temporary addition to their terrain. This acceptance is your armor.

### **Phase 3: Interaction & Etiquette**

You are not alone, even when you see no one. The gardeners of Murkfast are reclusive, silent, and entirely integrated.

*   **On First Encounter:** Freeze. Do not reach for anything. Do not make a sound. Lower your gaze in a gesture of non-confrontation. Let them observe you. They have seen others fall into their world before. Your panic is a threat. Your stillness is a question.
*   **Communication:** Do not speak. Your mouth-sounds are jarring and meaningless. Communication is gestural and intentional. If you wish to pass, make a slow, small gesture in the direction you wish to go, and wait. If you are offered food—a roasted beetle grub on a bone skewer, a cup of fermented liquid—it is a profound gesture of acceptance. To refuse is a grave insult. Consider the risk. If you are desperate, accept it with a slow bow of the head. Eat it. Your system may revolt, but you will have honored the pact.
*   **Trade:** Your most valuable possession is novelty that isn't disruptive. A small, polished river stone from your world. A simple, elegant steel knife. A small quantity of salt. These are things they cannot create. They may offer you something in return: a length of gut-cord stronger than any rope, a poultice of glowing moss that has antiseptic properties, or safe passage through their territory. Do not offer them your flashlight or your water filter. Technology that replaces their symbiosis is a poison.
*   **Taboos: The Unforgivable Sins**
    1.  **Wounding the Tower:** Do not chip away the pillars, carve your name, or break off the new growth for fun. This is akin to vivisection. It is an act of unforgivable violence.
    2.  **Polluting the Water:** Do not wash with soap, discard food waste, or relieve yourself in the water channels. Find a waste-heap of discarded husks and rot, and contribute your waste there. Be a part of the decay cycle, not a pollutant.
    3.  **Wanton Slaughter:** Do not kill the fauna out of fear or sport. The beetles on the wall are livestock. The eels in the water are food. The moths are sacred. To kill without purpose is to declare yourself a mindless blight.

### **Exit Strategy: The Hopeful Stumble**

There is no map out. The fold that brought you here may be a permanent tear or a temporary snag. Your best hope is to return to the location of your arrival. It may reopen. Look for dissonance: a patch of air that feels colder, a place where the dripping sound is muffled, a shimmer in the perpetual twilight. These are the "seams" of reality. Approach them with caution.

But do not count on leaving. Your preparations are for survival, not for a round trip. In Murkfast, the only certain destination is the present moment. You fell from the world of sky and sun into the world of root and bone. Survive. Observe. Be quiet. And perhaps, if you are respectful enough, the great, living city will eventually digest you, or BREATHE YOU OUT.
