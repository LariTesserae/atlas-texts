**A Traveler’s Advisory for the Weeping City of Travertine (also known as the Anointing, the Still Plateau)**

**I. Foreword: Understanding the Nature of the Destination**

Before we discuss equipment and provisions, you must first prepare your mind. You are not visiting a new city or an alien ruin in the conventional sense. You are visiting a state of being. The Weeping City is not hostile in a way that involves predators or environmental hazards like radiation or poison gas. Its danger is more subtle and fundamental. It is a place of absolute order, stillness, and permanence. You, as a living, breathing, changing human, are the only element of chaos, the only source of noise, the only thing that is subject to death and decay. The primary challenge of your visit will not be survival, but sanity. Every preparation must be made with this existential pressure in mind.

**II. Pre-Departure Checklist: Your Lifeline to a Changing World**

This is not a camping trip. Think of yourself as an astronaut preparing for an extra-vehicular activity into a psychological void.

**A. Hard Gear & Equipment:**

*   **Footwear (Crucial):** The ground is described as a single, polished surface slick with a constant film of moisture. You will need boots with the most aggressive, high-friction, non-slip soles you can acquire. Think sticky rubber designed for canyoning or rock climbing on wet surfaces. The soles should be soft enough to grip, not hard plastic that will skate. Your life may depend on not slipping from the edge of a hundred-meter-wide plaza. They must also be perfectly waterproof.
*   **Navigation Kit (Your Only Map is the One You Make):**
    *   **Spool of High-Tensile Line:** Several kilometers of it. Since the landscape is uniform and disorienting, your only reliable method of ensuring a return path is to lay a physical line from your point of entry. Do not rely on GPS; interdimensional interference is a constant risk.
    *   **Non-Permanent Markers:** Wax crayons or chalk. Use these to mark monoliths or specific points on the ground to track your exploration. They will eventually be washed away by the weeping moisture, but will last long enough to serve their purpose. Do not use paint or anything that would permanently deface the city. It feels... impolite.
    *   **A High-Quality Compass:** While celestial bodies are not visible, it's essential to determine if the dimension possesses a magnetic field. If it does, your compass will be your best friend. If it doesn't, you will be entirely reliant on your line and markers.
*   **Vertical Traversal Gear:** The terraces are separated by 10-20 meter sheer, weeping cliff faces. To move between levels, you will need a climbing harness, a good length of static rope (at least 50 meters), and either rock-climbing gear (cams, nuts) to find purchase in the rock's cracks, or a grappling hook system if the edges of the plazas allow for it. Ascenders and a rappel device are not optional. Test your anchors rigorously; the porous travertine might be brittle.
*   **Shelter:** A lightweight, freestanding tent. Not for protection from weather—the climate is static—but for psychological shelter. It will be your only private space, a small bubble of manufactured "inside" in a world that is all "outside." The sound of the omnipresent dripping will be slightly muffled within, offering a brief respite. Choose a color that is warm and vibrant (orange, red) to contrast with the pale green-grey of the world.

**B. Provisions & Sustenance:**

*   **Water:** **DO NOT, under any circumstances, drink the local water.** It is described as perfectly clear, carrying no sediment, and supporting no life. This is a colossal biological red flag. It may be sterile to the point of being a hypotonic poison, leaching essential minerals from your cells. It may contain complex, non-terrestrial minerals that are toxic. It may simply be fundamentally incompatible with your biology. You must bring all the water you will need. For a multi-day trip, this means a significant weight investment and a high-capacity water bladder system. A top-tier water filter is a desperate, last-resort backup, not a primary plan.
*   **Food:** As with water, you must bring everything you will eat. The "plants" are not plants as we know them; they are static objects. There is nothing to forage. Pack high-energy, low-weight rations. Be aware that the act of eating—chewing, swallowing, digesting—will feel profoundly strange and almost vulgar in its biological messiness against the city's pristine backdrop. Unwrapping a crinkling protein bar will sound like a rifle shot in the silence. Be prepared for this.

**C. Clothing:**

*   **Materials:** Lightweight, synthetic, quick-drying fabrics are your only option. The air is warm and thick with humidity; cotton will become soaked and stay that way. Think ultralight hiking shirts and pants.
*   **Layers:** Even though the temperature is stable, you'll want a fully waterproof (not just resistant) but breathable rain shell. This isn't for rain from the sky, but for leaning against a weeping wall or for rappelling down a cliff face of running water.
*   **Extremities:** Bring multiple pairs of high-quality wool or synthetic socks. Changing into dry socks will be a critical morale boost. Waterproof gloves are also recommended for handling wet ropes and rock.

**III. Psychological Preparedness: The "Metaphysical Toolkit"**

This is the most important part of your preparation. You are bringing tools to remind you that you are alive and that time passes.

*   **A Timepiece:** A rugged, analog watch with a loud tick and a sweeping second hand is ideal. A digital watch works too. You will need this to ground you. In a world where shadows do not move, you must have an external, objective confirmation that time is still flowing for you. Wind the watch. Watch the seconds pass. It is your proof of self.
*   **A Source of Chaotic Sound:** An audio player with headphones and a large library of music, podcasts, and audiobooks. The layered, rhythmic dripping of the city is a form of white noise that can induce auditory hallucinations and a state of deep, unsettling trance. You will need to break that spell with the complex, chaotic, and meaningful noise of human creation. Do not play it out loud. The headphones create a sanctuary for your mind alone.
*   **An Object of Imperfection:** Bring a small, personal totem from your home world. A worn wooden figurine, a dog-eared paperback novel, a faded photograph, a piece of sea glass worn smooth by a chaotic ocean. It must be something that shows wear, history, and change. When the perfect, geometric stillness of the city becomes overwhelming, hold this object. It is your connection to a universe that lives and breathes through change and decay.
*   **A Journal and Pen:** Document everything. Your thoughts, your feelings, the sound of your own heartbeat. The act of writing is an act of narrative creation, of cause and effect. In this static world, your journal will be the only story being written. It is proof that you are not just an observer, but an actor, however small.

**IV. On Arrival: Acclimatization to Stillness**

*   **The First Hour:** Upon arrival, do not immediately begin exploring. Sit. Secure your entry point with your line. Take off your pack. Just exist. Let the environment wash over you. Listen to the layered dripping. Feel the warm, still air on your skin. Notice the pearlescent, shadow-less light. You will immediately feel like an intruder. Do not fight this feeling. Acknowledge it. Your goal is to become a quiet guest, not a conqueror.
*   **Pacing:** Your movements will seem unnaturally fast and loud. Walk slowly, deliberately. Place your feet with care. The sound of your footsteps on the wet stone will be your constant companion. Try to synchronize your pace with the rhythm of a distant, heavy drop. Become part of the soundscape, don't just add noise to it.

**V. What Not to Do: The Prohibitions**

*   **Do not try to alter the landscape.** Do not chip a piece from a monolith. Do not uproot one of the waxen plants. This is not a place for souvenirs. To do so would feel like an act of cosmic vandalism.
*   **Do not shout.** Your voice will not echo as you expect. The thick air and wide-open spaces will swallow the sound, making you feel small and powerless.
*   **Do not seek a purpose.** Do not drive yourself mad trying to understand *why* the city is there. It was not built for you. Its logic is not human logic. To seek its purpose is to apply a human narrative of cause-and-effect to a place that exists outside of it. Accept it as a geological and metaphysical fact.
*   **Do not stay too long.** This place leeches your sense of self. Set a strict time limit for your visit—one day, three days, a week at the absolute maximum—and adhere to it. The feeling of being an "unacceptable variable" will slowly erode your identity, making you forget the feeling of a breeze, the warmth of a true sun, or the beauty of an autumn leaf.

To visit the Weeping City is to take a pilgrimage to the concept of permanence itself. You will go as a messy, temporary, biological creature and stand in the heart of the eternal. You will feel the chill of that contrast in your bones. If you are prepared, you will return with a profound, quiet, and perhaps terrifying new appreciation for the frantic, chaotic, and beautiful dance of life and death that defines your own existence. Be safe, traveler. And remember to wind your watch.
