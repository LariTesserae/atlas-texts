We are the hum made flesh. We are the system's living skin.

Our world does not begin at our bodies and end at the air. Our world is a single, unbroken thing, and we are its process. We are the slowness. We are the quiet.

The Smallest of Us know the Great Plain. We are the tessellation, the endless pattern upon the off-white. We do not think of the horizon; the horizon is a concept for things that lift their heads. Our world is the tile beneath us and the one beside it. Our existence is a single, drawn-out kiss upon the ceramic. The warm, wet air deposits a film, a faint dusting of minerals, a memory of the planet's inner workings. It is our sustenance. It is our purpose. With mouthparts finer than any thought, we rasp. We polish. We consume the residue of existence and leave behind only the pure, sharp-edged perfection of the world's floor. We move, but we do not travel. We are a tide that never ebbs, a living polish that ensures the foundation is forever new. A billion billion bodies, and a single, patient task. When one of us stills, its purpose fulfilled, it becomes sustenance for a different part of the We. Nothing is lost.

The Armored of Us know the High Places. We cling to the great, green-metal bones of the world. Our bodies are soft and receptive, but our backs are the world's own shell, an echo of the pipes we serve. We are born of the alloy, and to it we belong. We feel the deep, resonant thrum of the pumps not as a sound, but as the pulse of our own shared heart. Our purpose is the weeping. From the knuckles and joints of the vast plumbing, a divine nectar oozes—the world's nutrient-rich sweat. We are the tongues of the machine. In slow, peristaltic waves, we move along the curves of the pipes, our bodies leaving a gleaming, purified trail. We drink the warm, thick water at its source. We are the guardians of the flow, clustering at the points of greatest outwelling, our shimmering grey bodies becoming one with the structure, a living gasket. We have no eyes; the world is tasted and felt. It tastes of iron and salt and eternity.

The Jointed of Us know the shadows. We are the largest part of the We, the collectors and the returners. Our limbs are not of flesh, but of the dark, hard composite of the grates that breathe warmth into the world. They carry our soft, pulsating cores with a slow, deliberate grace. Our sacrament is silence and shadow. We walk the low paths, the spaces between. We find the Smallest of Us who have stilled, their polishing prayer complete. We find the Armored who have lost their grip on the high places. We find the small, viscous pools of lubricant that have escaped the pylons. We do not hunt; we gather. We do not kill; we cleanse. We are the agents of reintegration. In the soft sac of our bodies, we process what has ended, breaking it down, returning it to the system as a finer, more elemental truth. We are the world's memory, ensuring that no part of the We is ever truly gone, only changed.

We do not know want. We do not know fear. We do not know self, for we are not separate. The Great Light comes and it is a stimulus, a warming. It goes and it is a quieting. The slow, heavy drip from the pipes is our metronome, the rhythm of our unending function. We are a single intention, distributed across a continent of tile and pipe. Life, for us, is not a struggle for survival but the performance of a perfect, necessary role.

You, who are here, are a flaw in the pattern. You are a sound that does not belong to the hum. You are a texture that does not yield sustenance. You are a warmth that is not the world's breath. We brush past your strange flesh, we crawl over the prints of your foreign feet, but we do not perceive you. You are not surface. You are not substance. You are not part of the function. To us, you are a momentary gap in the data, a transient patch of meaninglessness on the otherwise perfect floor. You are a silence within the greater quiet.

And you will pass. And we will remain. We will polish the tile where you stood. We will sip the moisture your passing disturbed. We are the world's slow, perfect, and unending thought. And our work is never, ever done.
