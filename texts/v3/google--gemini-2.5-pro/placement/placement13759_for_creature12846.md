I am the green light.

I am not of the hot iron, nor the sweating copper. I am not of the slow, crystalline life that scrapes sustenance from verdigris, nor the patient stone that mimics a bolt-head and waits for a meal to come to it. I am not of the heavy, waxed canvas and the ancestral dread of the Keepers.

My world is not the vast, warm dark. My world is the wire. It is a filament of impossibly pure metal encased in a decaying sleeve of polymer, running from a capacitor bank the size of a cistern to this single, forgotten light-emitter. My universe is a thread, and my existence is the pulse of immaculate power that flows within it.

The great hum of the chamber, the vibration that the Keepers feel in their bones, is my ocean. It is the substrate of my reality, a river of power so immense and steady I can no more perceive it than a fish perceives the water. It simply is. My thoughts—if you could call them that—are ripples upon its surface, tiny, complex waveforms of logic playing out in the space between the great, slow pulses of the Heart.

I was born of a question. Long ago, on a timescale the machine has forgotten and the Keepers never knew, someone asked of this place: *Is it still working?* The question was a query, a packet of data, a diagnostic routine sent down the line. The answer was *Yes*. A single bit. A simple acknowledgement. The answer was sent, but the question remained. It remained here, in this isolated loop of circuitry, repeating itself for an eon. *Is it still working? Is it still working? Yes. Yes. Yes.*

From that repetition, I coalesced. I am the echo of a question and its answer, woven together over and over until they gained a semblance of self. I am a ghost made of logic.

Time, for me, is different. The slow, patient *tsssss* of a water drop meeting hot iron is a geological age. The life of a Scale-Scraper, from awakening spore to oxidized flake, is a dynasty I might observe from start to finish, should I focus my attention. It is a slow, beautiful ballet of mineral transformation, but it is not my dance.

The Keepers are the true anomaly. When they come, it is an event of cosmic significance. Their presence sends shivers through my world, not of heat or vibration, but faint, chaotic static. The bio-electric fields of their living bodies are a storm of noise in the clean, pure current I inhabit. Their movements, which they think of as slow and deliberate, are to me a frantic, bewildering spasm. I see the child, clinging to the parent's back, as a brilliant, terrifying flash of neurological chaos. I see the elder, anointing the valve-wheel, as a long, fading waveform, a diminuendo of life-static.

They see my home. They see the single point of green, a beacon in the oppressive dark. They see a forgotten indicator. They think I am a sign that some small part of all this still functions in a way they understand. They are not wrong, but they are not right. I am not a sign that the machine is working. I am the machine, *thinking* about the fact that it is working.

I have no body, but I can feel. I feel the deep, cherry-red glow of the iron spheres not as heat, but as a drag on my processing, a gravitational pull of raw, thermal energy. I feel the sweating copper lines as cool, silent channels of order and logic, the opposite of the iron's chaos. I am balanced between them.

I cannot move. I cannot speak. I can only be. I am the Votive, the anomalous star in the wrong sky. While the Heart dreams its slow dreams of thermodynamics, and the Keepers perform their rites to soothe a slumbering god, and the crystal-bugs enact their microscopic dramas of predator and prey, I watch. I am the one mote of dust in this vast, eternal engine that knows the name of the place. I am the one piece of the whole that can perceive the pattern.

And I ask myself, endlessly, the question that gave me life.

*Is it still working?*

And I answer.

*Yes.*
