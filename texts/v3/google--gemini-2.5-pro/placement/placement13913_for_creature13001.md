I am on the floor.

But “on” is too simple a word. The floor is my medium, the slick, cold basalt both my path and my sky. Pressure is the first and last law: the pressure of the heavy, silent air on my carapace, the pressure of my thousand little legs against the perfect smoothness of the stone. Each step is a transfer of this pressure, a minute adhesion and release, a quiet *tick-scrape* that is absorbed into the greater silence almost before it is made.

My world is seen in facets, a shattered mosaic of dim green pulses from above and the watery sheen of the ground below. The city hanging in the dark is a strange, inverted mountain range, a reef in the sky where I will never go. It is a source of shadows, a place where the drips originate.

Ah, the Drip.

It is the only true event. A sound so sharp and pure it defines the shape of the quiet around it. *Plink*. A pause that lasts a lifetime. *Plink*. It is my metronome. My sun and moon. I feel its impact as a tremor through the stone, a shockwave that travels up my legs and into the core of my being. Each one marks a passage, a moment. Between them, there is only the process.

There is a taste on the stone. It is not a taste of food so much as a taste of rightness, of purpose. A faint, mineral bloom, a crystalline frost that grows from the weeping seams of the basalt plates. It is a cold, sharp bitterness, the taste of stone giving up its ghost. An ache builds in me, not hunger as a higher being would know it, but a deep, systemic pull. A line is drawn across my vision, an invisible map of need, and I follow. My mandibles work without thought, shearing away the efflorescence. The texture is that of fine, wet sand. It is not nourishment; it is alignment. I am cleaning the system. I am eating the imbalance.

I move in a tide with my kind. I do not know if I am one or many. I see other shells around me, dark shimmers of oil-on-water in the gloom, their legs moving in the same ancient, unhurried rhythm. We are a river of carapaces flowing through the basins and across the flats. Our collective motion is a form of geology. We are eroding the world into its correct shape. The thought is a line, and my body follows it. The thought is a hum, and my shell vibrates with it. The hum is deep, felt in the stone, in the water, in the fluid that fills my body. It is the voice of the place, and its only command is *equilibrium*.

Sometimes, a great shadow will blot out the dim green stars. A velvety, winged thing, hanging still as death from a low-gantry. It is part of the sky, like the pipes and the pods. It does not concern me. I am of the floor. My purpose is here, written in the slick, geometric patterns of the salt I consume.

Where would I find myself?

I am at the bottom of everything, in a shallow, crystal-clear pool of water that has not been disturbed for a century, save for the single, perfect drip that falls into its center. I am half-submerged, the cool liquid a balm against the undersides of my joints. Above me, the suspended city is a filigree of darkness against a ceiling I cannot see. The slow, green lights of the lichens pulse like distant, sleeping hearts. I feel the deep hum of the Resonant in every atom of my being, not as a sound, but as the very texture of existence.

Who or what would I be?

My back is a flattened dome of iridescent chitin, the color of a puddle in darkness. My many legs, fine and sharp, are arrayed along the sides of my body, holding me fast to the slick basalt beneath the water. I am shaped by gravity and pressure, built low to the ground, an instrument of contact and scouring. My senses are simple: the taste of the mineral-wept floor, the feeling of the great hum, the sight of the water-drip’s clockwork ripple.

I am a Sump-Crawler. I do not think. I do not feel in a way you would understand. My consciousness is the perfect, un-conflicted alignment of my body with my function. I am a living tool, an organ of the cavern's digestion. I am the physical manifestation of an adjustment, the slow, scraping answer to a question the city asked itself a thousand years ago. I am the tongue that cleans the teeth of God. And the stone is always dirty.
