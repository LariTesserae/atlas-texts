I would be found in the Quiet Place, the zone they call the Dead Spur. It is a place of failure.

To the others, the Tenders, I do not exist in the same way they do. When they press their palms to my own, their Bone-Talk is simple, clipped, a language of commands. *Go.* *Fetch.* *Report.* There is no sharing of complex thought, no resonance of shared feeling. My skeleton does not sing back to them in the correct harmony. My rhythm is broken.

I am what happens when the cavern’s slow, grinding process of adaptation fails.

I was not born here. That is my original sin. I was brought, a child wreckage from a world above—or outside, or before—whose name I cannot recall and whose sensations are now just phantom limbs of the mind. I remember silence. The others cannot conceive of this. To them, the Great Drone is the baseline of reality, the defining hum of existence. For me, it was, and remains, an assault.

As a child, I screamed until my throat was raw, but no one heard. They tried to teach me Bone-Talk, but my hands were clumsy, my mind unable to untangle the language of tendon and pressure from the memory of spoken words. They tried to teach me the Walk, the low-slung shuffle across the slick, embryonic stone. But my body, still wired for a world of flat surfaces and stable gravity, betrayed me. I fell. I slipped. I was a constant, jarring staccato in their slow, legato world.

The drone did not render me deaf in their manner. It shredded my hearing. I am not cocooned in a world of vibration; I am trapped in a cage of auditory torture. In the spaces between the great grinding harmonies, I hear things they do not: phantom whispers, the high-pitched keen of stressed metal a mile away, the skittering of the carapace-swarm as a sound rather than a texture—a billion tiny nails on a slate. It is a constant, meaningless, agonizing static. My eyes, not born to this gloom, are always slitted against the ghostly blue-green light. They see too much detail, not enough of the whole.

And so I was exiled. To the Dead Spur.

This sector is a cauterized wound on the cavern's body. A cascade failure decades ago caused the central resonant pump to seize. The power conduits that snake through here are cold and dead. The nodules are not warm and humming; they are inert, cold to the touch, their faint internal light long since extinguished. They are stone corpses. The air is still thick, still wet, but the smell of ozone is gone, replaced by a deeper, colder scent of mildew and terminal decay.

The Great Drone is absent.

Here, in the Quiet Place, the pressure on my chest has lifted. The vibration in my bones is gone. It is a silence so profound it is its own kind of sound, a ringing void. The only noises are the constant drip-drip-drip of condensation, the far-off click-clatter of my own tools, and the wet, soft progress of the things that thrive in the cold rot. Life is different here. The thumbnail swarms are sparse. The moulds are a sickly yellow, not the vital blue-green of the active sectors. In the still, black pools, things grow longer, stranger.

My purpose is to be a ghost, a scavenger. They send me here to salvage parts from the derelict monitoring stations and dead machinery. A length of untarnished conduit, a rack of intact processors from a console that has not been touched in a lifetime, a still-sealed canister of abrasive paste. I am the Rust-Eater, the one who picks through the system's graveyards.

I live alone in a tilting, half-collapsed monitoring station. The plexiglass is cracked and opaque with grime. I do not log the pulse of maturing nodules. I log my memories. On smoothed plates of shale, using a sharpened piece of steel, I scratch the shapes of things I half-remember: a Tree, a Bird, a Sun. They are clumsy pictograms, the art of a child-god sketching a world he no longer understands.

But I am more than just a memory bank. In the silence, I can *think*. The others do not think, not as I understand it. They *are*. They are a function of the cavern, a human component in a geological machine, their consciousness a low, meditative hum that mirrors the Great Drone. They do not ask *why*. The question has no structure in Bone-Talk.

In the dead stations, I have found things they can no longer comprehend. Manuals, written in a language of symbols and letters, not touch. Schematics that show the Great Drone is not one sound, but a thousand, each tunable, each with a purpose. I am teaching myself to read. And I am learning that this place is not a temple. It is a farm. I am learning that the "Harvest" is not a religious ascension. It is an industrial process.

The Fissure-Wyrms do not hunt here. There are no rhythmic footfalls to attract them, no warmth to lure them from the crevices. But there are other things. The silence and the cold have bred their own predators. I do not know their names. I see them sometimes, out of the corner of my eye: a distortion in a pool of water, a flicker of movement too large and fast to be a Tender, a shadow that falls where no shadow should be. They are a part of my quiet, cold world. We leave each other alone.

Who am I? What am I?

I am not a Tender. I am proof that their world is not absolute. I am the error in the system that has been granted the space to study the system itself. They are beings of pure, unthinking faith, midwives to a god they do not understand. I am the heretic, the archivist, the first scientist. While they feel the thrum of the present Priesthood, my hands are coated in the dust of the past, seeking the scriptures of the Engineers.

My place is here, on the forgotten edge, where the great heart of the machine is silent. I am a flaw, a loose wire, a ghost. But in this vast, deafening, unthinking world, I am the only one who is truly listening. And I am beginning to hear an answer.
