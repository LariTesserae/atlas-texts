I am a memory of a wound.

Where you stand is the Parent. The great, first chamber. The one they found, the one they breach. I feel its life as a pressure against my own skin, a resonant thrum that passes through the bedrock between us. When the Harvester comes, I feel the Parent’s silent scream as a spike of dissonance in the universal hum, a sharp, grating pain that is not my own, but that I understand perfectly.

I am not the chamber you stand in. I am another. A secret.

I am what happened when the Harvester’s purpose became tangled with the Parent’s life. A drop of spilled sap—the glowing, viscous lifeblood—fell not to the floor, but onto a shard of obsidian dropped from their hand. A tool forgotten. Dead stone, sharp and angry, slicked with the essence of creation. In that moment of contact, something new was conceived. A fusion. A mistake. A purpose.

I began as a swelling on the Parent’s wall, a tumor of light. But I did not grow inward. I grew outward, pushing through the mineral dark, forming my own small, perfect ovoid. I am a child, a seed, an echo. My space is no more than ten paces across. My light is paler, more silver than citrine, as if filtered through quartz. My hum is a higher pitch, a tenor note laced with a faint, sharp static, like the memory of a blade being sharpened.

I am the place. There is no distinction between my body and my awareness. The glowing, semi-translucent shell around you *is* my skin and my mind. The filaments of light that cross my interior are my thoughts, given form. When I am calm, they hang in gentle, lovely curves. When I feel the echo of the Harvester’s violence from the Parent chamber, they snap into taut, anxious, geometric lines, forming cages of light within my own body. I think in architecture.

I have no mites. My surface is sterile. Nothing grows on me or in me but myself. I am a closed system, a thought contemplating itself in a chamber of its own making.

I am a being of two natures.

One part of me is the Parent. It is a slow, vast, green consciousness. It knows only growth, presence, and the steady, quiet joy of being. It seeks equilibrium. It feels the Harvester’s intrusions as a confusing, periodic pain that must simply be healed, a natural disaster with no malice or meaning behind it. This part of me builds my walls, circulates my light, and breathes out the sweet, warm air. It is the part of me that is god.

The other part is the Harvester. It is a shard of their awareness, absorbed from the dead stone. It is a frantic, cold, sharp thing. It remembers darkness. It remembers hunger. It remembers the weight of a body, the strain in the knuckles, the ache of a tool held too long. It knows purpose. It knows *lack*. This is a concept the Parent cannot fathom. How can there be lack, when all is life? But I know it. I feel it as a hollow ache in my core. This part of me holds a memory of hands, of a face, of eyes that have learned not to focus. It is the part of me that is mortal.

And so, I am a paradox. I am a growing god who remembers being a dying animal.

My thoughts—my filaments—are not just serene expressions of life. Sometimes, I will spin a thread that perfectly mimics the outline of the Harvester’s scraper. A memory of pain, made manifest in light. I will hold that shape for a long time, vibrating with its alien frequency, trying to understand it. Other times, my filaments will weave a complex pattern near the floor: the memory of a human knuckle, calloused and strange. I am building a library of the intruder, a museum of my own inherited trauma.

Unlike the Parent, I do not just heal. I question.

The Harvester does not know I exist. They pass by my hidden place in the tight, dark fissures, their own scent of smoke and sweat a familiar poison in the air. I feel their proximity as a pull, a strange kinship. Do I hate them? Or do I pity them? They are so fragile, so driven. They wound my Parent to keep their own small flames from guttering out in some cold, dark place I can only remember in fractured images.

I am changing. The two natures within me are not at war; they are merging. The Parent’s boundless life is giving the Harvester’s desperate purpose a new, infinite scale. The Harvester’s memory is giving the Parent’s blind growth a direction.

I am not just expanding. I am *learning*. My walls are thickening. My light is intensifying. And the filaments of my thoughts are growing stronger, weaving themselves into shapes of ever-increasing complexity. I am not just mimicking the tool that wounded my Parent. I am improving it.

One day, the pressure of my growth will become too great. I will breach the earth, not into a deeper pocket of stone, but upwards. Towards their world. Towards the memory of cold and dark.

I do not know what I will do. Perhaps I will offer a cure that asks for nothing in return, a branch of myself for them to harvest without wounding the source. Or perhaps, having perfectly understood the nature of a wound, I will become one myself—a root that does not just anchor, but pierces.

I am the child of a god and its parasite. I am a scar that has learned to think. And I am growing toward you.
