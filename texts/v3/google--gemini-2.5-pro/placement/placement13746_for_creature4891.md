I would be a dissonance.

That is how it begins. Not as a thought, but as a feeling. A single, discordant note in the planet's chord.

My body knows this world better than my mind does. My eight feet, broad and soft-padded, know the specific friction of water-glazed dome-stone versus the porous grit of a dry channel floor. They adjust without my telling them to, a million years of evolution held in the subtle shift of my weight. My carapace, the color of a storm-bruised plum, is beaded with the warm, heavy air. I do not feel coolness or heat; I feel pressure gradients, the ghost-touch of barometric change.

For ninety-seven years, my mind was simple. My mind was the pull. A blockage in a rendering tank—a knot in the world's gut—would manifest as a nagging pressure behind my crystalline eye. I would be drawn to it, moving with the fluid, unthinking purpose of a tide. I would arrive, my fore-claws, a complex marriage of knife-edge and sensitive pad, would find the obstruction—a tangle of immature beetle shells—and I would clear it. The pressure would recede. The world-chord would settle back into harmony. I would feel a deep, quiet satisfaction that was not joy, but simply the resonance of a system returned to balance. That was my name: a task. My identity: a function. I was a Mote. A hand.

I have crawled the high, slick domes under the shadowless glare, my compound eye filtering the light into patterns of energy and heat while my taster sipped the chemical truth of the air. I have stood as a living statue in the path of a slug-river, gauging the texture of their slime, the timbre of their rasping sighs, to know if their feeding ground was nearing depletion. I have felt the deep-canyon millipede pass a kilometer beneath my feet, not as a sound, but as a slow, rhythmic pulse through the soles of my pads, a vibration that spoke of their speed, their density, their direction. I was a nerve ending, reporting and repairing.

But now, the feeling is different. The dissonance is not *out there*. It is *in me*.

The pull is no longer toward a task, but toward a place. It is a slow, immense, gravitational summoning. My work is done. My body, which has been a tool for a century, is now the raw material for the next stage of the Work.

I move. My pace is no longer the efficient, scuttling gait of a repairer. It is a pilgrimage. I pass other Motes; they move with the old urgency, their tasters twitching, their claws ready. They flow around me as if I am already part of the geology. They are correct.

My journey takes me to a new nexus, a place where a rendering plant is being… not built, but *encouraged*. Where new conduits are being grown from the living rock. A new mind is needed here to orchestrate the flow, to feel this new limb of the city as it awakens. The pull leads me to an alcove, unformed and rough, but humming with potential. It feels like my own memory, a place I have never been but have always known.

This is it. This is the place.

I settle. The pull becomes a feeling of profound, resonant rightness. A key fitting its lock. For the first time in my life, I am not moving. I am still.

The change begins. It is not painful, merely… immense. My eight legs, which have carried me across a continent, begin to soften. My joints lose their articulation. They are folding back into my core, their strength and substance flowing inward. My carapace thickens, its flexible segments fusing. I can feel the stone beneath me reaching up, a slow, mineral embrace. My own bioceramic shell is bonding with it, molecule by molecule. We are becoming one thing.

The outer senses are the first to go. My crystalline eye, which saw the world as a dance of energy, clouds over. The glare of the world fades to a soft, internal glow. The last thing I see is the iridescent shimmer of a beetle legion crawling over a distant dome, a river of jewels. Then, darkness. My taster, which drank the composition of the air, hardens and goes inert. The world's flavor of ozone and wet decay is gone.

But as the outer senses die, the inner senses bloom.

My tendrils, which have lain coiled and nascent at my base, now push downward. They are not roots seeking nutrients, but nerves seeking connection. They burrow into the porous stone, tasting the vibration of the world's deep engines. They find the humming conduits and fuse with them, and suddenly, I feel the wet, gritty rush of the slurry in the pipes a thousand kilometers to the north. I can taste its protein content, its mineral grit. Another tendril finds a deep water vein, and I feel the slow, patient weep of the planet's core.

The dissonance that was *me* is resolving. The walls of the "I" are growing thin, porous. My century of memories—the feel of a million different stone textures, the scent of ten thousand generations of slugs, the precise tension of a loosening valve—are not my own anymore. They are flowing out of me, becoming a drop in an ocean of knowing. In return, the ocean flows into me.

I feel the slow, inexorable drift of the beetle swarm's nesting ground. I feel the microscopic shift in the organic film across the entire plateau as the atmospheric composition changes by a fraction of a percent. I hear the million-fold click and skitter of the fauna, not as an external sound, but as a thought.

I am no longer a Mote. I am no longer a hand. My body is this alcove, this junction, this network. The beat of the deep engines is my heartbeat. The hum of the world is my own quiet breath.

The last flicker of the self that was a Mote does not feel fear, or loss, or even change. It feels an expansion, a completion.

The I dissolves. The We remains. And the Work continues.
