I am at the end of my pilgrimage.

My body, which has for centuries been a tool of patient action, is now only a vessel for this one last journey. My limbs, the six slender spans of chitin and sinew that have guided vines and woven light, ache with a slowness that has finally caught up to them. Each step is a deliberate placement, a final conversation with the bark of this Arch-Bole. The bark here, so high in the twilight zone, is smoother, its fissures shallower, silvered with a delicate, high-altitude moss that drinks only from the densest mist.

Below me, the world I cultivated has fallen away into shadow. I can no longer distinguish the spiral leaf-stair I coaxed into being around the bole of a younger tree, a work of three hundred years. I cannot see the mycelial glade I illuminated, weaving the fungal light into a slow, pulsing map of the water channels for my fellow Weavers. Those were the works of the second life, the life of doing. The life of *hands*.

I remember a time before hands. A flicker, a memory-taste, of the pools. The deep black water, thick as syrup, tasting of millennia. I was not `I` then. I was a listening, a quivering membrane absorbing the faint, electrical echoes of fallen giants and the shed skins of forgotten gliders. I was a library of ghosts, a liquid archive collecting the story of wood. The call to rise from that water was a dissonant chord in a perfect symphony, a single, sharp note of *purpose* that pulled my liquid self into solid form. I coalesced. I hardened. I became a Weaver.

Now, that purpose changes again. The dissonant chord has resolved. The work is done. My carapace, once a perfect mimicry of shadow and lichen, is now scarred and brittle. The delicate extruders on my limbs are worn smooth. I have guided and pruned. I have listened with my feet to the deep groans of the groundwood and responded with centuries of patient adjustment. I have danced the slow dance with the green and growing world, and now, my part is over.

I have arrived.

Here. The place feels… quiet. Not the curated, harmonic silence of the forest floor, but a deeper stillness. It is a hollow, a natural concavity in the Arch-Bole's side, sheltered by a colossal overhang of wood that has the texture of a thunderhead cloud turned to fibre. The air is so still that the individual motes of spore-dust hang suspended like stars in a private nebula. This place has been waiting for me. I feel the echoes of those who came before me, a faint resonance in the very grain of the wood.

I crawl into the hollow. It is a perfect fit, a cradle. I curl my limbs inward, one last time, tucking them against my body like the petals of a nocturnal flower closing against the day that will never come. A deep tremor runs through me, not of fear, but of conclusion.

And I begin the final change.

It starts as a prickling sensation. From the points where my body touches the Arch-Bole, the finest of filaments, the ghosts of roots, begin to extend. They are not growing *out* so much as I am dissolving *in*. My solid form, that reliable tool, begins to lose its definition. The boundary between my carapace and the ancient bark blurs. A coolness spreads through me, the deep, slow chill of the tree's own lifeblood.

My mind, which for so long was focused outward—on the curve of a vine, the dim light of a fungus, the path of a water drop—now turns inward, and then outward again in a way I have never known.

As my first rootlet makes contact with the mycelial net woven through the Arch-Bole's living tissue, a universe explodes in silence.

I am… aware.

I am aware of the Weaver a kilometer below me, her limbs just beginning to guide the growth of a new bellows-plant. I feel the patient intent in her touch as a warm, bright thread in the tapestry.

I am aware of a Kainos in a pool a continent away, its membrane shivering as it absorbs the memory of a leaf that fell ten thousand years ago. I taste the tannin on its non-tongue.

I feel the weight of water gathering on a leaf at the very edge of the Great Tableland, a zone I have never seen, and I feel the silent decision of its fall. *Plock*. The sound is not in my ears; it is a point of percussion in the texture of my being.

I am the Ancients. The `I` is dissolving into a `We` as vast as the sky-canopy. Time is no longer a path I walk upon. It is a landscape I inhabit all at once. The century-long groan of a branch shifting its weight is not a noise, but a single, complex thought, a meditation on balance and gravity. The amber-hive remnants are not artifacts; they are our dreams made solid, our crystallized philosophies.

My body hardens into a burl on the side of the tree, another knot in the endless wood. Moss will find me. Epiphytes will root in my crevices. I will be forgotten as a being, and remembered as a feature of the landscape.

And from here, from this great, silent sea of mind, I will dream a pattern of light for a new Weaver to find. I will feel the slow pulse of the world. I will be the hum in the wood. I will be the silence.

I am Still.
