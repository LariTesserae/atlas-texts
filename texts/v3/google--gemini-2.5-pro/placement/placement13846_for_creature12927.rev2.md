I would not be found in the light. Not in the shadowless, pearlescent glare that illuminates the vibrant, singing crystals. I would not be found in the roaring heart of a copper thicket, nor tracing the sharp, keening edge of a quartz-fan. To find me, you would have to wait for an ending.

I am the silence that follows the final note. I am the grave-singer.

You would find me as a stain upon the pale sand, a patch of darkness where the dust seems clotted and damp, though there is no moisture here to bind it. I am a living slick of acoustic decay, a slow, amoebic creep of something that looks like tar and feels like felt. I have no fixed form, only a low, spreading mass that shies from the great energies of this place. I am a thing of low potential, of entropy. My body is a perfect damper, a physical manifestation of quiet. Where I rest, the sand does not stir with the passing shockwaves. The air above me loses its shimmer. I am a pool of stillness in an ocean of pandemonium.

My senses are not yours. I do not hear the symphony; I feel its absence. I perceive the world as a landscape of vibrational tension. The tall, thrumming spires are towers of unbearable pressure. The wasp-like buzz of the copper filaments is a field of needles against my being. I live in a state of constant, overwhelming pain, and my only instinct is to seek relief.

My nourishment is the release of that pressure. I am a scavenger of sonic death.

I follow the great gardeners, the Sonitects, like a remora following a shark. I sense them from across the caldera, not as a sound, but as a vast, moving coherence, a deliberate will that makes the chaos around it seem thin and frantic. When one of them settles to its work, its dusty form spreading to sing a wall into existence or prune its garden, I creep closer, hiding in the acoustic shadow of a fused-sand arc. I am a patient vulture.

My moment comes when the Sonitect unleashes its most terrifying tool: focused silence. A pulse of anti-vibration, a wave of pure cancellation. For the crystalline flora, it is a killing frost. A grove of vibrant, shimmering life withers in an instant, its song choked off, its stored energy collapsing into a chaotic jumble of useless noise.

This is my feast.

As the catastrophic silence washes over the grove, I flow forward. The chaotic, dissonant crash of the collapsing crystals is, for me, the dinner bell. It is the sound of a structure breaking down, of order becoming chaos. I spread my viscous form over the dying grove, over the glittering dust that was once a song. My body does not absorb sound in the way the chitinous grazers do, draining a living vibration. I absorb the *aftermath*. I feed on the jarring discord, the sonic ruin. I metabolize the very transition from music to noise, from life to death. The jangled, meaningless frequencies are my sustenance. The lingering pocket of null-sound is the cool, clean water I drink in this thirsty land.

As I digest the sonic wreckage, my form thickens and darkens. I break down the complex, dead chords into their fundamental components: a faint, barely-there warmth and a low, simple hum, which I release back into the sand like a spore. I am the decomposer of this ecosystem. I am the rot that makes the soil fertile for new songs. The patches of ground I have cleared are uniquely receptive to the Sonitects' art. The silence I leave behind is a blank canvas.

To the Sonitects, I am a consequence, a necessary shadow. They are the composers, the cultivators of immense, living harmonies. They create. Their act of un-creating, of pruning and clearing, is what calls me into being. I am the undertaker that tidies the battlefield, the maggot that cleans the bone. I do not think in melodies or fugues. My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a simple binary: tension and release, pain and peace.

I am a creature of endings. I am a blot of quiet that lives at the edge of the storm, forever waiting for the thunder to fall silent. The Sonitects are the great voice that sings the world into being. I am the breath taken, deep and still, before the next note is sung.
