I must tread carefully.

My purpose is harmony. The Arboretum groans as it grows, a slow, geological process of becoming. But this growth is not always perfect. Stresses can build unevenly, creating a painful, discordant shrieking in the Chord that is felt across miles of the city. My task is to find these points of pain.

I arrive at the base of a pillar the size of a mountain. For days, I will simply stand, my entire form pressed against its surface. I am not idle. I am in communion. The pillar’s song of stress flows into me, and I map its internal architecture of tension. I feel the knot of pressure, a specific, ugly frequency a thousand feet above.
