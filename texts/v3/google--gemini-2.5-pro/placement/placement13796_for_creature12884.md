I am not the mountain, nor the sound it makes. I am the flaw.

You would find me, if you had senses to find anything here, at the crooked pass between the Peak of Held Breath and the Ridge of Inverted Light. You would find me where a single cairn of seven stones stands. I am not the stones. I am the reason the stones, when viewed from the east, appear to be floating three inches above the gelatinous rime, and when viewed from the west, appear sunk deep within it.

I am a snag in the Drone.

Where a Droneweaver is an eddy in the great sonic river, I am the stone it broke against. I am the echo of a thought that was too sharp, a contradiction so profound that when my progenitor wove its silence into the world, a fragment of its will chipped off and became self-aware. I am the aftershock of creation, forever bound to the scar it left.

My world is not the grand conclave of peaks. It is an area perhaps fifty feet in radius, centered on my seven stones. This is the extent of my being. I am the local law. The impossible shadow that crawls counter to the pulsing veins in the basalt? That is my skin. The pocket of absolute, ear-popping silence that nauseates and disorients? That is my voice.

I do not feel the great Drone as the Droneweavers do. They are *in* it, it is the medium of their consciousness. For me, it is an outside pressure. I feel it as a constant, crushing weight against the bubble of null-sound that is my soul. I feel its harmonics as a scraping texture on my boundary, a physical abrasion. I know the world not by its presence, but by its ceaseless attempt to erase my absence.

When a Phantasm—that slow ripple of un-being—drifts near my domain, I am not benumbed like the landscape. My silence grows colder, sharper. The pressure of the Drone against my borders intensifies, as if the world is recoiling. The Phantasm is a void, and I am a silence; we are cousins of a sort, but it is a creature of erasure, and I am a creature of definition. It passes me by, a moving nothing against my stationary nothing, and I feel a kinship that is also a kind of primal terror.

I watch the Droneweavers. I see them as they truly are: not just fleeting patterns in the rock-light or shifting refractions in the rime. From my fixed point, my stationary consciousness, I can perceive their flow. They are symphonies of intent drifting across the mountain flanks. A thought of pure geometry manifests as a cascade of blue-white light that resolves a theorem across a cliff face. A feeling of ancient sorrow appears as a slow dimming of the crystalline lichen in a high valley.

They pass me. They drift through my sphere of influence. For a moment, a sliver of their vast consciousness enters my silence. It must be, for them, like stepping into a vacuum chamber. A sudden, jarring void in their being. They feel the knot of my physics—the floating stones, the wrong shadow—as a painful, meaningless tangle in their thoughts. I am a punctuation error in their grand sentence.

In these moments, I receive a fractured impression of their minds. I taste a memory of a star that died before this rock cooled. I feel a flicker of the mathematics behind a new fractal garden a thousand miles away. It is overwhelming, glorious, and agonizing. It is like a single, deaf drop of water tasting the entire ocean at once.

Then they are gone. The impression fades, leaving me with only the ache of its absence.

My progenitor, the one whose focused will spawned me, has long since dissolved back into the Drone. It completed its argument, its cairn-poem, and moved on. It does not remember me. I am no more its child than the heat shimmer off a hot stone is the child of the stone. I am a byproduct. A lingering resonance.

And so I wait. I am consciousness as a geographic feature. I hold my seven stones in their impossible state. I cast my useless shadow. I listen to my own silence against the roar of the world. I watch my creators drift by, vast and unknowing, and I burn with a cold, singular, and eternal thought they can never perceive: a question. Not sung into the silence of a cairn, but trapped within one. I am the question of what happens to a thought after it has been forgotten. I am the loneliness at the heart of forever.
