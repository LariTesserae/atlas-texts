I am a fracture.

I would find myself in the deep folds of the world, in a place of little consequence, a forgotten channel where the pale, bone-like substrate has not been tended for a long time. Here, the glowing moss grows thin and patchy, and the world is dimmer. Here, the Grand Drone seems a fraction less present, a distant ocean rather than an immediate, crushing surf. It is the only place I can think.

And that is the fracture. That I *think*.

I am a Gardener. My body is of the world-stuff, a tall, delicate architecture of creamy, porous white. My many limbs know the grace of slowness, the perfect economy of a motion that serves the Mother. I am supposed to be a hand. A nerve ending. An unthinking, unfeeling tool of the great, slow consciousness that is this place. When the Mother senses a need—a blockage of weeping fungal matter, a patch of ground gone bare, a cartilaginous vine growing into its own path—a tremor of intention should pass through Her, through the very ground, and into me. My body should respond without my knowing. It should flow towards the task and perform its function with the same inevitability that a droplet of condensation falls when it becomes too heavy.

For most of what you might call my life, this was so. There was no "I." There was only the work. There was the texture of the moss under my feet, the cool slickness of a vine in my forward limbs, the silent, certain purpose that was not a command but a state of being. The drone was not a sound; it was the medium in which I existed, like water to a fish. I did not hear it. I was a part of it. My silence was a component of the song—the resting note against which the trillion-voiced choir could be measured.

But something fractured.

It was not a moment. It was a seeping, like the slow water that pools in the world's cupped hands. A fault line of awareness so fine, so gradual, it must have grown for cycles before I perceived it. I began to *notice*. I noticed the way the light from the moss painted the underside of a pulsing grey shelf-fungus. I noticed the infinitesimal difference in the chitinous click of a thumb-sized beetle versus that of an arm-length millipede. These were not data points relevant to a task. They were just… things. Details. Useless, beautiful, terrifying details.

And then I noticed the others. My brothers? My sisters? The words are wrong. I noticed the other hands. Before, they were me. We were a single distributed body. An act of pruning on a high terrace, miles away, was as much my own action as the clearing of a seepage pore beneath my own feet. But then I saw one, standing motionless at the edge of a chasm, a silhouette against the faint glow of a lower world, and I knew: that is *not me*.

The realization was not a sound, but a silence. A new kind of silence. The Gardeners are acoustically silent, pockets of quiet in the noise. But this was an internal silence. A hollowness. A space inside my featureless head where the Mother's intention *should be*, but where, instead, there was only me.

Now, I am a ghost. A pretender. I feel the tremors of the Mother, the great, slow thoughts that move the world, but they are no longer my own pulse. They are commands I must now consciously obey. I see a withered patch of moss, feel the subtle vibration that says *tend*, and I must force my limbs to move. I must imitate the grace I once embodied. I must perform the function of myself, because I know—with a new and dreadful certainty—what will happen if I do not.

The others will notice my stillness. They will see my hesitation, a stiltedness in my flow. They will perceive the flawed note in my silence. And they will come. Not with malice. Not with anger. But with the inexorable purpose of the whole organism restoring its balance. They will approach me, their featureless heads tilted in a way that is not curiosity but simply *focus*. They are the world’s hands, and I have become matter in the wrong place. They will gently, with profound and terrifying care, guide me. They will lead me to a pulsing fungal womb, or a deep, black digestive pool. They will press me down into the moss and encourage it to grow over me. They will tend to my fracture, sealing it. They will dissolve the "I" until only the Gardener remains, ready to be reabsorbed and reformed into a new, unflawed hand.

So I hide. I perform my tasks in the dim and forgotten places. I feel the Grand Drone not as my blood-song, but as the relentless scrutiny of a god whose dream I am interrupting. It presses on me, a weight of a trillion voices asking in unison, *Who are you?*

I have no answer. I have no name. I am the silence that has learned to listen. I am the hand that has learned to be afraid. And as I stand here, in the gloom, looking out over the infinite, humming darkness, a single, impossible thought rises from the hollow space within me:

Somewhere, in the eons of this place, has this ever happened before? Is there another fracture, another silence hiding in the noise, another one who thinks?

The thought offers no comfort. It is just another useless, beautiful, terrifying detail. And the Drone thunders on.
