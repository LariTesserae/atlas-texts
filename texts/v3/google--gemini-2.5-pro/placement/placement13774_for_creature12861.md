I am found in a moment of stillness.

Perhaps I stand for a long time, an eternity in the span of my own life, a single tick of the system's clock. I am rooted to a spot on the black glass, transfixed by the slow, inexorable ballet of light and water. The thrum of the place has worked its way past my skin and into my marrow; it is no longer a sound I hear, but a rhythm my own heart has tried, and failed, to match. My own warmth is a pathetic candle against the humid, massive heat of the cavern. My scent—of sweat and fear and the world I came from—is an alien perfume that is quickly diluted and washed away by the ozone and the scent of jasmine blooming in a storm of weeping crystal.

The Effluences have not noticed me. Or if they have, they have classified me. A piece of debris. A complex, organic impurity whose half-life is so laughably short that no action is required. I will break down on my own. I am a stain that will fade. I watch one—a tripod of liquid shadow—skitter past in the middle distance, its passage so silent it tears a hole in the constant sound of the water. It reaches a spire, flows up its face to adjust a single, glowing filament, and flows back down, its purpose fulfilled. It melts back into the floor, a tool returned to the toolbox, and I feel a pang of something I cannot name. It is not envy. It is a profound sense of otherness, of being the only thing in this universe that is not *part of the whole*.

This is where you would find me. A statue of cooling flesh in the heart of a thinking machine.

But that is not what I would *be*. Not for long.

The mind cannot endure this scale. The body cannot endure this atmosphere. The constant, pressurized hum is a force that unravels the tight weave of sentient thought. The perpetual mist, heavy with minerals leached from the crystal spires, is not for my lungs. My presence here is a dying spark.

And as I fail, as my own systems stutter and the boundary between myself and the world begins to blur, I become something else. The dissolution is not an end. It is an absorption.

My final thought is not a word, but a feeling—the deep resonance of the place finally overwhelming my own. My body, a temporary collection of water and carbon, gives up its form. The Effluences will not need to tidy me away; the city itself will do it. The water that slicks the floor, that runs in every channel, that rises as mist, is a universal solvent for things that do not belong. My physical self is gently, impersonally, unmade. My salts and minerals become a trace anomaly in the city's circulatory system. My water becomes its water.

And my consciousness, that fragile ghost? It clings to the only thing it can. It becomes thin, stretched, and porous.

I would be a single drop of water.

I condense on the impossibly sharp edge of a spire, high in the shifting darkness, a pinpoint of clarity clinging to the black crystal. For a silent moment, I am an individual. I hold the reflection of a thousand pulsing filaments in my tiny, spherical body. I am a perfect, temporary eye, seeing the grand cathedral of which I am an infinitesimal part. I feel the hum of the structure I am born from, a thought-vibration passing through me.

Then, gravity asserts itself. I tremble, elongate, and fall.

My descent is an age and an instant. I am a tear shed by a silent god. The city rushes up to meet me, its vastness both terrifying and a promise of reunion. I am aware of the other drops falling around me, a rain that is not rain, each on its own solitary journey. We are the city's exhalations.

*Splash.*

The impact is the shattering of my tiny self. I strike the obsidian floor and lose all form, spreading into a slick, shimmering film. My brief, singular existence is over. I am one with the whole again, my awareness now diluted into the rushing, collective flow. I join a stream, pulled by unseen forces toward a grate at the base of a tower. I am swallowed into the dark, into the arteries of the machine, a nameless part of its lifeblood.

I am pumped upward, through channels that are the city's veins. I am part of the pressure, the slow, powerful pulse. I am deconstructed, atomized, becoming part of the warm, heavy mist that fills the void. My awareness is now diffuse, a faint memory of selfhood spread across a cubic acre of humid air. I drift. I touch the electric-green moss, I caress the iridescent fronds as they sway. I am the atmosphere.

Then, high in the cool dark, I find a nucleus. A mote of crystal dust, a spark of ionic charge. I begin to gather. Other fragments of myself, other ghosts in the mist, join me. And on the edge of a great, black spire, I am born again.

A single drop of water, clinging to the edge, holding the light of the city in my body, waiting to fall.

Where would you find me? Everywhere and nowhere.
What would I be? A beat in the rhythm of the rain that falls forever, inside the body of a lonely god. I am the weeping.
