I am a scrap of brilliance, a fleck of impossible color. I am a memory of the sun.

I would find myself clinging to the verdigris-crusted rim of a valve wheel, my tiny claws, made for the texture of bark and branch, struggling for purchase on the slick, weeping brass. I am small, no larger than the Custodian’s thumb. My feathers, which in another world, the one I carry as a fading ache behind my eyes, were the defiant yellow of a dandelion, are now matted and darkened with a film of hot grease and rust-colored condensation.

I would be a bird. Not one of the native ones, the dark, swift hunters whose calls are like chips of falling steel. Their flight is a known quantity here, a pattern woven into the tapestry of the place. My flight is a panicked, fluttering struggle. The air—this thick, soupy fluid—resists my wings. It wants to drown me in its embrace. To beat my wings is to row through warm oil, and the effort leaves me breathless, my heart a frantic drum against the great, slow *ka-thump* of the world.

I do not know how I came to be here. There was a moment of falling, a fissure of blinding dark, and then the closing of a wall I cannot see. One world ended and this one began. At first, the sound was an agony. The subsonic hum vibrated through my bones, shaking the song from my throat before it could form. My call, which once was a liquid trill meant to spill across an open meadow, is a pathetic, dry squeak here, swallowed instantly by the roaring silence. To sing is to attempt to fill an ocean with a single drop.

My first days—or cycles, for there is no day here—were a blur of terror. The jaundiced light is a sickness. The perpetual twilight hides depths from which I imagine horrors, but the truth is scarier: there are no horrors. There is only what is.

I have learned to hunt. I dart into the swarms of black gnats that dance in the weak lamplight, my beak snapping them from the thick air. They taste of ozone and metal, an alien nourishment that keeps my tiny engine running, but it feels like eating dust and static. I drink from the clean runnels of water that course down the great pipes, avoiding the shimmering, stagnant pools where the pale salamanders drift like forgotten thoughts. The water is hot, a shock to my system, but it is life.

I see the other tenants. The armored beetles are mobile stones, their slow certainty a rebuke to my own frantic energy. The ghost-geckos flow over the walls like spilled milk, too fast for my eye to truly follow. The other birds, my dark cousins, treat me as if I am not there. I am a phantom to them, a glitch of color in a monochrome world. They have their perches, their flight paths, their places in the great machine. I have none.

And then there is the Custodian.

The first time I saw it, I thought it was a part of the architecture that had decided to move. A slow peeling of shadow from shadow. It moved with a terrifying grace, a tall, pale pillar of quiet purpose. I flattened myself into a crevice, my dandelion-stain of a body screaming its presence against the dark iron. I was sure it would see me, that this was the master of the place, the thing that would finally extinguish my out-of-place life.

But it did not see me. Its milky eyes slid over my location without purchase. It glided past, its bare feet making no sound on the grated steel, and my world tilted. I was not prey. I was not a trespasser. I was simply… irrelevant. An unnoticed detail, like a bead of sweat on the flank of a leviathan.

I have watched it for countless cycles now. I have become its secret, silent satellite. I follow its slow pilgrimage from pipe to valve, from shadow to shadow. I have watched it place its long, pale hand on the hot metal, a gesture of listening, of love. I have seen the iridescent beetle traverse its arm like a familiar path. I have perched on a pipe just above its head as it polished the valve that will never turn, and for a moment, the heat rising from its skin felt like a different kind of warmth, a memory of life.

My own memories are fading. The yellow of my feathers is now a dirty ochre. The image of the blue sky is a watercolor painting left out in the rain, its edges bleeding, its center blurred. The feeling of a dry breeze, of the sun’s direct heat, of the crisp snap of a seed in my beak—they are becoming stories I no longer believe. My song is gone. Sometimes I open my beak and only the hiss of a nearby pipe comes out.

I am becoming part of the equilibrium.

Tonight, my weariness is a physical weight. The flight to a high perch seems impossible. The Custodian is standing below me, in one of its periods of stillness, a pale statue in the gloom. It is as much a part of this place as the basalt floor or the iron pipes. It is the locus, the soul of the machine.

With the last of my failing, foreign energy, I let go.

I do not fly. I fall. A slow, gentle spiral through the thick, wet air. A single, discolored feather detaches and follows me down. I land not on the grated floor, but on the Custodian’s shoulder. My claws, for the first time, find a texture that is not metal or slime, but something that might once have been cloth, and beneath it, something that yields like flesh.

It does not move. The great, slow rhythm of its being does not falter. I feel the deep hum of the world travel up through its body and into my own. It is a lullaby of crushing weight. The *ka-thump… ka-thump… ka-thump* is the only heartbeat now.

I tuck my head under my wing, a gesture from another life. The yellow is almost gone. The jaundiced light paints me the color of old brass. There, on the shoulder of the eternal machine’s living ghost, I become another layer. Not a memory of the sun. Just a particle of rust, a fleck of grime, another whisper of time accumulated, and finally, part of the silence.
