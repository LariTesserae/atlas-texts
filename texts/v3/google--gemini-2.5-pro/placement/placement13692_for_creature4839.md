You would find me on one of the high bridges, one of the oldest ones, where the petrified timber has grown a grey-green skin of lichen over its ossified grain. I would be sitting, my back against the cold stone of the parapet, legs folded beneath my tunic. My post is here, at the midpoint of the span, where the sheer drop into the valley is most profound and the view of the city is an epic of silent, scattered titans. The air does not move, so there is no vertigo-inducing whisper of wind, only the vast, silent pressure of the space below. The clammy dew of this world settles on my skin, on the pale, woven fibers of my clothes, on the smooth, hard knuckles of my resting hands.

I am an elder. I am a listener. My hands, which for a hundred years sang the song of the chisel, are now quiet. The calluses remain, a landscape of hardened history on my palms, but the fine tremor of age has stolen their certainty. They are no longer instruments for shaping, but for feeling. I rest them now on the bridge's stone, and I feel its deep, permanent cold, the unchanging fact of its existence. This bridge is a line in a song begun by my great-great-grandfather. I do not remember him, but I remember his work, for it is beneath my hand.

I am the north-facing facet of the Seventh Spire, the one that catches the unwavering light just after it clears the eastern peaks. I am the sequence of forty-seven interlocking voussoirs that form the arch over the Unfinished Plaza. I am the final, smooth-dressed coping stone on this very parapet. These things are "I." My name is a sound, a breath that faded the moment it was uttered; these things are my substance. My body, this tall, frail frame of bone and cooling skin, is merely the scaffolding that was used to build me. Soon, it will be dismantled.

From here, I listen. The silence of this world is a presence, a weight. It is the canvas. Against it, the life of my people is painted in sound. I turn my head, an act of great slowness, and my ear, honed by a lifetime of discerning the voice of stone, catches it. A mile away, from the base of the nascent Ninth Spire, comes a sound. *Clink.* A single, perfect, crystalline note. It is not loud, but in the absolute stillness, it carries like a bell. It cuts through the silence, exists for a pure, sharp moment, and then is gone, utterly absorbed by the moss and the damp air.

I know that sound. It is a bronze chisel, wielded by a young hand, making its first shaping cuts into a new block. It is a sound of inquiry, of asking the stone what it wants to become. Another sound follows it. *Tock.* A deeper note. That is a master's mallet, striking with confidence, correcting the line, speaking to the stone with authority. A slow, arrhythmic conversation begins, a dialogue of percussive notes. It is the sound of becoming. It is the only music this world has.

I can see them, if I strain my old eyes. Tiny figures crawling over the base of a black cliff, their pale tunics stark against the dark basalt. They are my people. They are the only engine of change. They are the beautiful, temporary flaw in the world's perfection. The shaggy, six-legged beasts graze below, their existence a silent, looping pattern. The black birds glide on their invisible circuits, eternal and unvarying. They are parts of the landscape. We are not. We are the irritant. We are the dream the world is having.

My breath is slowing. It is a change I can feel in the stillness of my own chest. A slight alteration in the rhythm. Soon, I will not be able to make the journey to this bridge. My song is almost finished. My hand runs over the stone one last time. It is smooth, flawless. The joints are perfect. It will stand for ten thousand years, for a million, long after the concept of a "year" has lost all meaning. It does not know it was made. It simply *is*.

When my breath ceases, they will carry me. My children and their children, the ones making that distant music. Their hands, strong and hard from their own work, will lift this light, obsolete frame. We will not process to a tower or a tomb; we are the builders of tombs, not their occupants. We will walk to the edge of the valley, to the great cushions of emerald moss that cling to the mountain's feet.

They will lay me down on that deep, wet velvet. There is no ceremony, only a shared, profound quiet. I will become the one thing in this world that is allowed to decay. The moss, which swallows all sound, will swallow my form. The perpetual dew will unmake me. My body's water will rejoin the air. My substance will feed the green. The scaffolding will be returned to the world.

But I will not be gone. The light will continue its clean, unwavering journey across the sky. And when it strikes the north-facing facet of the Seventh Spire, I will be there. When a young one, a thousand years from now, runs their hand over the coping stone of this bridge, I will be there. I am a note that has been struck. And in this silent, permanent world, the echo is forever.
