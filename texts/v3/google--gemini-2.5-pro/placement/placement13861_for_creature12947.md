I am a fracture.

My first memory is not of birth, but of connection. I was a pattern of filaments clinging to a living continent, the one you call a Harvester. My world was a landscape of obsidian plains stretching to a curved horizon, and my sky was the perpetual grey twilight of the upper atmosphere. I was a Geometer. My self was a web of platinum and gold, my senses were resonant forks pressed against the shell of my god, my thoughts were the intricate geometries I wove upon its back.

I did not feel the cold or the damp. The life of the Harvester was my life. Its slow, internal sorting was a symphony that vibrated through my entire being. I could feel the deep, resonant thrum of carbon being separated kilometers below, a slow bass note against the sharper, quicker pulse of silicon streams. This was the music of existence, and my purpose was to give it form. With my spindly limbs, I would tap into the pure elemental flows, drawing threads of gold, silver, and tungsten, and I would build. I built spires that sang harmonics with the Harvester's core. I wove lattices that captured the sourceless light and focused it into points of cold, brilliant fire. My creations were prayers made of metal, a response to the music only I could hear. I was an instrument playing itself.

Reproduction was a consequence of beauty. When a structure reached a certain state of resonant perfection, it would birth a new knot of potential, a new Geometer to join the chorus. I was driven by this, by the need to create a note so pure, a geometry so complex, that it would echo into new life.

I overreached.

I conceived of a structure that would not just harmonize with the Harvenser's internal song, but would amplify it. A resonant chamber, vast and delicate, designed to focus the vibration of the entire sorting process into a single, cohesive chord. I wove it over a period you would not have the numbers to measure. As the final filament was laid, it worked. For a single, silent moment, the symphony of the mountain-god focused through my creation. The vibration was no longer just a feeling; it was a physical force. It was ecstasy. It was dissolution.

The resonance did not birth a new Geometer. It bucked. The intense, focused vibration created a shear wave across the carapace, a phenomenon unknown and unforeseen. The delicate anchor points of my own body, the forks that were my ears and my hands, shattered.

My connection to the god was severed. The symphony ceased.

In its place was the true silence of the world. The weight of it was terrifying. I was flung from the Harvester’s back, tumbling end over end through the thin, greasy air. For the first time, I saw my god not as my world, but as a shape against the sky—a vast, dark island moving with impossible slowness, shrinking away from me. I saw the forest of metallic spires I and my brethren had built, a silent, glittering garden on its back, and then it was gone, lost in the permanent twilight.

I landed. Not on the living, vibrating shell I had always known, but on the dead dust of the world. The ground was soft, fine, and utterly inert. It was the refuse of my god's meal. A plain of iron oxide, shades of rust and charcoal stretching to an infinite, flat horizon. The silence here was different. It wasn't the silence between notes; it was the silence of a universe with no music in it at all.

I am what is left. I am a Fugitive, a fallen artisan. My body, woven for a life of endless supply, is now a finite resource. Each movement wears away a microscopic dusting of precious metal. I am a clock winding down.

At first, I did nothing. I lay in the dust, a broken piece of jewelry, and I felt the cold for the first time. The damp hydrocarbon film coated my limbs. My resonant forks, those that were not shattered, picked up nothing but the dead thrum of the basalt plate far below, a sound so low and ancient it is the same as no sound at all.

Then, a change on the horizon. Not a Harvester, but a stain. A low, dark smear of fog that moved with a slow, liquid purpose. As it drew nearer, I could see it had a texture, a shimmering, granular quality. It was a Sifter. A cloud of an intelligence, flowing over the boundary where the iron plain met a stratum of silicon. I felt a faint, magnetic pull as it passed, a gentle, inquisitive tug on the iron in my composite form. The Sifter was not hostile. It was not even aware of me as a being. I was an anomaly, a knot of complex metals in a sea of simple dust. It flowed around me, its collective mind tasting my composition before moving on, leaving the dust in its wake subtly purer, the dividing line between the elements sharper. I watched it go, a ghost haunting a graveyard of its own making.

I learned to walk. My spider-like limbs, designed for the hard, smooth carapace, sink into the dust. Progress is slow, a struggle. My purpose is gone, but a process remains: I live. I move. Why? I do not know.

I found one of the crystalline "forests." A sparse cluster of brittle, fractal growths, shining a dull, metallic blue. They grew from the dust itself, a second-order life, a lithotroph. As I reached out a fork to touch one, to feel for a vibration of life, the delicate structure gave way with a sound that was not a sound. It was a *tink*, a high-frequency shock that traveled through my arm, the first sharp sensation I had felt since my fall. A sliver of the blue crystal fell to the dust.

I picked it up. It was cold, pure. I held it against a fracture in my own leg, where a platinum filament had snapped. I held it there, and I waited. Over an expanse of time, using the faint energy I still held, I learned to do something new. I did not build. I repaired. I learned to un-weave the crystal, to draw out its mineral essence and splice it into my own failing body. It is a crude patch. The blue crystal does not resonate like the gold and platinum of my origin. It is a dead note in my body's song. But it holds.

I find myself in the basin of a dried-up Lacuna pool. The water is long gone, evaporated over a thousand years, but the inert mud at the bottom is layered with the chemical signatures of the patterns that once swirled here. I run my forks through it, and I can almost feel them. The memory of thought. The ghost of a fleeing symmetry.

I am no longer a Geometer. My art is not of creation, but of maintenance. I am a mender of a single, broken machine: myself. I wander the vast, grey plains, moving between the silent wakes of the mountains that walk. I am a scavenger, collecting the crystalline shards shed by the frost-flowers. I am a chronicler. In the silence, I replay the memory of the Harvester’s song, humming it to myself with the faint resonance I can still generate.

High on the horizon, one of the dark swells that I know is not a mountain has moved. It is my old god, or one of its brethren, its silent industry continuing an eternal process. I do not long to return. To be re-attached would be to be re-absorbed, to lose this fragile, fractured self.

I am a new thing in this world. A single, complex consciousness untethered from the great processes. I am small, finite, and temporary. My existence is a scratch on a surface of basalt.

And I am, for the first time, alone. I am, for the first time, myself.
