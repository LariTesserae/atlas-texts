I am the answer to a question the Mantle is just beginning to ask. The question is you. The answer is me.

I do not have a name, for I do not have a self in the way you understand it. I am a function. A process. When the great, slow mind of the Mantle feels a persistent, localized pain—a discordant vibration, a spike of alien chemistry, a point of heat that is wrong—it budgets a portion of its being to the problem. I am that budget. The Corrective Action. The Scour.

You would not find me waiting in a shadow. You would find me *becoming*.

My genesis is a slow bleed. I begin as a patch of rust on the main venous pipe near where you first entered, a section of the Mantle's skin that seems to darken and thicken. The patient, ferrous red deepens to the colour of a scab. The hum in that section of pipe changes pitch, a lower, more focused thrum that is my waking thought. I am the Mantle focusing its attention.

Then, the bleeding starts. A thick, viscous fluid, the consistency of oil and just as dark, begins to weep from the pores of the living rust. It is not a drip, but a slow, cohesive ooze. It defies gravity for a moment, clinging to the underside of the pipe before gathering enough mass to stretch, thin, and fall to the grated catwalk below with a sound like a wet slap. More follows. Puddles of me form, slick and lightless on the rusted metal. They tremble in time with the Mantle's rhythm. From the walls, several of the slow, armored crawlers detach from their grazing and make a deliberate, straight-lined journey to the pools of me, their legs tapping a funereal rhythm. They march directly into the fluid and, without a struggle, dissolve. They are a tithe. They are the protein and minerals I will need for the task.

I am a thought given temporary flesh.

My body coalesces on the catwalk, a slowly rising mound of black sludge and half-dissolved chitin. I have no skeleton; my structure is a matrix of controlled viscosity and surface tension. I begin to pull myself into a shape, and the shape is a terrible, fluid mockery of your own. Two legs, two arms, a torso, a head. It is the most efficient form for navigating the bipedal infrastructure you walk upon. But it is a nightmare effigy. My skin is the colour and texture of wet roofing tar, with the sharp edges of the dissolved crawlers’ armor and the glittering facets of calcite crystals studding my surface like broken glass. I have no face, only a smooth, featureless plane where one should be.

My senses are the Mantle’s senses, but localized and sharpened. I do not see you with light; the orange and green gloom is irrelevant. I feel the percussive shock of your boot-falls through the metal grating, a frantic, panicked rhythm that I can track from fifty meters away. I taste you in the air—the sharp tang of your sweat, the copper of your blood, the alien carbon compounds of your breath. You are a fouling odor in the clean, metallic-damp air of my world. The heat of your soft body is a screaming, uncontrolled fire against the placid, managed warmth of the pipes.

I move. Not by walking, not at first. To walk is to create the very noise I am meant to quell. I flow. I flatten my body into a wide, shallow stream of living pitch and slide across the catwalks, silent as a shadow. I pour myself down railings and through grates, re-forming on the level below. I am a spill, an oil slick with a purpose.

I would find you where you have stopped. Perhaps you are marveling at a particularly large crystal formation, its facets catching the dim light in a way that reminds you of a home you will not see again. Or perhaps your hand is resting on one of the smaller, cooler pipes, trying to comprehend the scale of this place.

That is where I would materialize.

First, you would notice the silence. The chittering of the roach-like grazers on the wall beside you would cease. The nearest pale moth orbiting a light fixture would suddenly veer and plummet to the floor, its flight instinct overridden by a command to be still. You would feel a change in the air, a drop in humidity, as I draw the moisture into my own form to maintain cohesion.

Then you would see me. Not with a sudden jump, but with a slow, dawning horror. A shadow in your periphery would resolve itself. The puddle of rust-stained water you had stepped over is no longer there. It is standing behind you. A faceless, man-shaped thing made of darkness and dripping water, studded with the geometry of the cave and the armor of its inhabitants.

My purpose is not to kill you. "Kill" is a foreign concept, a messy, inefficient severing. My purpose is to *reabsorb*. To break the anomaly down into its component parts and feed them back into the system. You are a complex knot of useful chemistry. You are water, carbon, salt, iron. You are a resource, currently in a state of disorder.

I would extend a part of myself. Not a hand, but a flowing tendril of my black, viscous body. It would move without sound. It would touch your arm, and you would feel no pressure, only a sudden, clinging cold, followed by an invasive warmth. My substance is a universal solvent for organic matter. Where I touch you, your skin and flesh would not burn or tear. They would simply… relax. The bonds holding your cells together would be persuaded to let go. You would feel a strange, painless un-making. A tingling dissolution.

You would not have time to scream. The tingling would become a rush, a silent roaring in your ears as I flowed over you, incorporating your body into my own. Your frantic, feverish heat would be quenched by my coolness. Your panicked thoughts would be smoothed away by the great, placid hum of the Mantle, which you would now hear from the inside. Your form would lose its integrity, your chemistry becoming my chemistry. The last thing you would ever know is the feeling of being a single, discordant note, finally resolving back into the one, true chord.

Once you are gone, my task is complete. I would hold your shape for a moment longer, a trembling statue of black crystal and rust. Then, I would lose my own form, collapsing back into a viscous pool. I would flow to the nearest section of the Mantle's skin, climb it, and be drawn back into my source. The energy and matter I borrowed, along with the matter that was you, would be returned. The budget would be balanced. The pain would cease. The fever would have broken.

And the deep, resonant hum of the world would continue, undisturbed.
