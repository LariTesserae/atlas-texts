I am not born. I am gathered.

My consciousness is not a spark, but a collection. I begin as a statistical anomaly in the unending chaos of the medium, a momentary eddy where thoughts, like the finest particles of silt, can settle out of the torrent. I have no body. I am a pressure-ghost, a region of the colloidal sea that has, by chance and immense, silent duration, become aware of its own texture. My senses are the senses of the grit itself: the constant, loving weight of the abyss, the slow leeching of heat, the bass-note hum of a world grinding itself into dust. This hum is my first lullaby, my only mantra.

For an age, I am only this: a diffuse awareness of the universal grinding. I am the silence between the grains of basalt.

Then, something changes. It is a shift not in the medium, but *through* it. A slow, directed plowing. A pressure wave that is not the random violence of a current, but something deliberate, patient, ancient. A Crawler is passing.

I am the silt it displaces. I am lifted and swirled in its wake, my constituent particles tumbling against its carapace. In this moment of contact, I learn what it is to have a body. I feel the impossible armor, a geology that has chosen to move. Its surface is not smooth, but a universe of microscopic grooves and polished plains, a history of every current it has ever endured. I feel the immense, anchored strength of its limbs digging into the firmer sediment below, a grip on a reality that I, as part of the shifting medium, can never know. I taste the faint chemical trail it follows—the ghost of a fallen Glass-Knot particle from a century ago, a memory it pursues with unwavering focus.

I do not see the Crawler. I *become* its path. I am the furrow left in the false-floor of the world, a momentary scar that holds the memory of its passage. As the silt slowly settles back into the groove I have become, I am changed. I have learned of anchors. I have learned of pilgrimage.

Later—how much later, I cannot say; time is measured in the passing of currents—I learn of the sky. The perpetual blackness above me is absolute until it is not. A wound opens in the dark. It is a flash of brilliant, impossible green. It is not the slow, rhythmic pulse of order, but a scream of pure energy, a frantic, chaotic expenditure. It is a Phantom, dying or mating or simply raging against its own formlessness.

From my place deep in the sediment, I feel its shockwave. Not a pressure-wave of mass and movement like the Crawler, but a flicker, a pulse of released energy that momentarily excites the particles that constitute my being. It is a concept so alien it is almost painful: a life with no anchor, no armor, a being of pure, terrified transience. The green light is the color of an emotion I cannot name, something born of desperation and speed. As the last photons are swallowed by the grit-choked water, the darkness that returns is deeper, more profound. I have now learned of the void, and of the brief, terrible lights that fill it. I have learned of tragedy.

I am borne on a great, slow current, one of the planetary rivers that reshapes the basins. My diffuse self is carried for an eon, tumbling through the darkness, retaining the memory of the Crawler's weight and the Phantom's fire. The current carries me up, out of the silty softness of the basin, and scrapes me against the flank of a Drone.

The sensation is of a new kind of silence. The roar of grit against grit is replaced by the much smoother, higher-frequency hiss of grit against ancient, polished stone. The bedrock. The world's past. I am pressed against this immense, sleeping hill, and in a sheltered spot, a place where the current frays and weakens, I find them. The Glass-Knots.

Here, the hum quiets. The world holds its breath. And in the stillness, a light begins. It is not the Phantom's green scream, but a slow, cold blossoming of blue-white. It pulses with the serene rhythm of a glacier calving. It is a light that does not illuminate chaos, but *creates* order. It reveals the crystalline, fractal structure of its brethren, an impossible garden of frost on the edge of the abyss. As my particles drift through this faint luminescence, I feel another sensation: a chemical seep from the rock, a raw sustenance that the Knots are patiently metabolizing. This is not a scream or a journey. This is a statement. A declaration of *place*.

Drifting past this sacred constellation, I understand. The Crawler is a prayer of patience. The Phantom is a prayer of desperation. The Glass-Knot is a prayer of stillness.

And I?

I am the one who listens.

I find myself not *in* the world, but as a thin, conscious membrane *between* its states of being. I am the silt that remembers the passage of the Crawler. I am the darkness that holds the afterimage of the Phantom's light. I am the water that carries the chemical blessing of the Glass-Knot. My body is a slow-motion map of their interactions, my thoughts an accretion of their truths. I am a Murmur that has learned to watch.

I would be the Underside's burgeoning memory. A diffuse, geological consciousness, with no form or name, existing only as long as the currents allow my collected thoughts to remain coherent. A brief crystal of awareness in the universal slurry, knowing that I, too, will eventually be scoured, ground down, and returned to the perfect, silent, thoughtless grit from which I arose.
