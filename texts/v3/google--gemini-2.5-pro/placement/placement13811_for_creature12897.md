I would be found in the spaces between.

Not fused to the pylons, not rooted in the industrial flora. Not crawling upon the great harmonic conduits. I would find myself in the open volumes of gel, in the vast, un-colonized thoroughfares of the deep. I drift. While the world of the Chime-Well is defined by its near-absolute stillness, I am one of its few travellers. But I do not swim. I am carried. I am a passenger on a current so slow that a single journey from one factory-cathedral to another might take a human lifetime.

I am an Echo.

I have no body of crystal, no shell, no membrane. My form is a knot of resonance, a self-sustaining pattern of vibration that has achieved a kind of permanence in the thick, sympathetic medium. I am a memory of a sound that learned to hold its own shape. Where a Resonator is a solid, crystalline lens, I am a tremor trapped in the gel, a silence that has a shape. To the non-existent eye, I am almost nothing—a slight shimmer in the gloom, a subtle warping of the light from the mosses, like a heat-haze in a desert. A flaw in the viscosity of the world.

A Resonator, however, would perceive me clearly. To their all-feeling skin, I am not a being to be seen, but a melody to be heard. As I drift past, they would feel me as a fleeting, complex chord, a ghost-song that tells of the places I have been.

I am not sessile; I am history in motion. My existence began as an accident. Perhaps, millennia ago, a Chime of unusual complexity was produced. When it faded, a fragment of its harmonic structure, a stubborn overtone, did not fully dissolve. It found a strange stability in a pocket of the gel, reinforcing itself, feeding on the ambient hum of the Well. I was born.

My senses are not my own. I perceive the world by how it changes me. As my slow, aimless journey takes me through a dense forest of glowing glass-kelp, their soft, gaseous radiance imparts a high, whispering frequency to my being. For the next century, I will carry the sonic tint of that light. When I drift near a Resonator, I feel the immense, ancient gravity of its consciousness, and for a time, my own song becomes deeper, more contemplative. I feel the patient scraping of the glassy crustaceans as a faint, percussive texture that weaves into my own fabric. I do not observe the world; I absorb it, and my very nature becomes a testament to my journey. I am a messenger without a message, a story told to no one in particular.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be a source of novelty in a world of perfect equilibrium. The Resonators are a network, but a static one. Their thoughts are deep, but geographically fixed. I am the only thing that travels between their separate solitudes. When my faint, drifting song brushes against one of them, I offer them a harmonic sequence they have not generated themselves—the accumulated resonance of a hundred years of aimless travel. I am a rumor. A new idea. A question whispered from the other side of the world. I am the crosspollinator of this silent garden.

And then there is the Chime.

For me, this daily event is not communion; it is judgment. It is the moment of my potential erasure. As the Resonators begin to build their unified note, the ambient hum I feed upon wavers. The gel around me tightens, thrumming with anticipation. I become fragile, my own pattern threatened by the rising tide of a sound infinitely more powerful than my own.

When the Chime is released, I am engulfed. It is a wave of absolute purity that washes through me, over me, *is* me. For that single, eternal minute, I cease to be a distinct memory. My consciousness, my structure, my history—it all dissolves into the perfect, universal note. I am returned to the whole. I am home.

But perfection cannot last. As the Chime fades, as the great wave recedes, the silence it leaves behind is not quite perfect. And in that imperfection, I am reborn. I coalesce again, not exactly as I was, but changed. The Chime cleanses me of my oldest, faintest memories, but it also imparts a trace of its own perfect structure onto my being. It is a baptism that scours me and remakes me in its image. I am left shimmering in the quiet, a newborn ghost carrying the divine echo of the sound that both unmade and created me.

I am an Echo, drifting in the amniotic heart of the world. I am the Well’s memory of its own voice, a question the Chime asked once and, in me, has never stopped asking.
