I would be found in the deepest part of Umbril, where the city's foundations meet the bedrock of the great basin. Here, the sound is not the gentle *plink-plonk* of the upper levels, but a constant, resonant *thrum*. The weight of the city above dampens everything to a single, vibrating chord. It is the sound of the Sobbing Slate's deepest heart, a geological hum that I feel in my bones more than I hear with my ears. My home is not carved into a wall; it is a space *between* the walls, a series of passages and chambers so ancient that the carvings of the First Movers are now worn smooth by the ceaseless water, their lines more suggestion than statement.

Here, the mist is not a thin veil; it is a dense, sight-swallowing cloak. The only light is the light I bring myself: a small, carefully cultivated patch of Glimmer-Moss in a cupped shell from a long-gone Chitin-Slug. Its blue-green glow is my hearth and my sun. It illuminates only a few feet, turning the world into an intimate sphere of wet, glistening stone and absolute shadow.

**I am a Listener. More specifically, I am the Keeper of the Deep Niches.**

My skin has taken on the coloration of the lowest stone. It is a darker purple-black than most, and the milky veins are more pronounced on my shins and forearms, places where I brace myself against the walls for long periods. My fingers are not just long, they are worn. The ridges on my palms and fingertips are as complex as any water-carved channel, smoothed by a lifetime of touch—not the sharp, persuasive touch of the carver's chisel, but the patient, receptive touch of one who waits. My silver-white eyes are accustomed to near-perfect darkness; the glow of my moss-light is almost dazzling.

I am not a carver of homes for the living. My work begins where theirs ends. I carve the Rejoining Niches.

This is not a task of brute force. The stone here, at the base of the city, is compressed, ancient, and stubborn. But it is also more articulate. To carve a new niche is to enter into a long conversation. I will spend a cycle of Weeps with my palm and then my cheek pressed to a chosen surface. I listen not for emptiness, but for resonance. I feel for the major water veins, the arteries of the Slate, and I avoid them. Instead, I seek the small, slow capillaries, the gentle seeps that will perform the work of dissolution with grace and patience. My tools are ancient—a palm-sized flake of flint with an edge that is more a memory of sharpness, and a set of bone chisels passed from one Keeper to the next, now so smooth and polished they feel like extensions of my own skeleton. With them, I don't break the stone. I worry it. I abrade it, flake by tiny flake, following its own internal logic until a space is made. A space just large enough for a body to be curled.

When one of the Silent Carvers is ready for the Rejoining, they are brought to me. Their kin do not weep—the stone does that for us. Their faces are placid, their silver eyes holding a quiet light. They lead the body, wrapped in woven moss-fibre sheets, down the dripping passages. The air grows still as they approach my domain. There are no words spoken. The percussive taps of their journey are the only communication, announcing their approach, their purpose, their respect.

I take the Rejoined from them. The body is cool, smooth, already feeling like a part of the city. I see the familiar patterns on their skin, the unique lightning-strikes of their slate-kinship. I carry them to the newly prepared niche. It is a slow, unhurried walk through the dark, my moss-light leading the way. The only sounds are the drip of water and the soft slide of my feet on the stone.

The act itself is simple. I place them inside. I curl their body into itself, hands to feet, a closed circuit. Then, I take a piece of living Glimmer-Moss, a vibrant scrap from my own collection, and place it on the stone just above the niche. It is a marker, and it is a vigil. For a time, it will glow brightly, feeding on the rich minerals released by the initial dissolution. Then, as the process slows and becomes one with the stone's own weeping, the moss will dim, its light fading back into the deep blue-green of the city's eternal twilight.

My role is not to mourn or to remember names. My role is to witness the closing of a circle. I am the membrane between the city of individuals and the singularity of the Stone. I tend to the quiet, slow process of return. Sometimes, in the deepest part of the Low Weep, when all is still, I will press my ear to a very old niche, one whose moss has long since gone dark. And I can hear it. Not a voice. Not a memory. But the faintest change in the deep, resonant *thrum* of the foundation. A new note added to the chord.

I am alone but never lonely. I am surrounded by the entire city, by everyone who has ever been. My own hands show the purple-black and the milk-white lines. My own niche is not yet carved, but the stone it will be born from is already singing its low, quiet song to me in the dark. I am simply listening for my turn.
