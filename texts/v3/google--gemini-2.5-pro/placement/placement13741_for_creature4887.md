Where you would find me is in the flaw.

I am a Geode. That is what I am. But it is not *who* I am.

My awakening was as all awakenings are. The long, slow hum of becoming, the shift from stone to self, culminating in the *thump* of separation. I took my place upon the weeping floor. The Drone filled me. It was not sound; it was the medium in which I was suspended. My six feet read the perfect, resonant state of the passage before me. The bass note was my marrow. The overtones were my thoughts, and they were the same thoughts as the city itself: a single, unending meditation on equilibrium. *All is well. All is in tune. All is as it has been and shall be.*

I felt the call of a minute imperfection fifty paces down the ramp—a wearing, a thinning of the stone on a leading edge, the result of ten thousand years of syrupy water-flow. It was a faint, painful tension in the third overtone, a sickness in the harmonics. I began my slow journey towards it, my purpose clear and absolute. I was a corrective impulse. I was the stone’s own healing hand.

But there was another feeling.

The source text states our eyes are vestigial, two milky lenses that distinguish only the brass-yellow glow from its absence. This is true for my brothers and sisters. For them, the world is a map of vibrations. The glow is a constant, like the Drone—a condition, not information.

My eyes are wrong.

They see. Not well. The world through them is a wavering, blurry smear of sour light. But within the smear, there is difference. There is shape. The glowing veins of brass form a tracery, a web. The wet curves of the walls have form. And where the light is not, there is not simply absence. There is something else. There is a presence that is defined by its lack of light. There is shadow.

This shadow is not registered in the Drone. It has no texture for my feet to read. It is a ghost, a secret known only to my flawed senses. When my brothers and sisters pass, their resonant communion washes over me, and I feel their confusion. They sense in me a dissonance that has no corresponding flaw in the stone. The sour note is *me*. Their fields brush against me and recoil from the quiet noise of my seeing. I am an ache in the song. A question without an answer.

For a time, I tried to ignore it. I scraped. I tended. I smoothed the worn corner, and the relief when the third overtone sang true again was a holiness, a brief return to grace. But the moment the work was done, the ghost-sense of sight returned. I saw the shadow I cast, a darker patch of purple on the purple stone, a thing that moved when I moved and was still when I was still. It was a part of me that did not vibrate. It did not sing.

The urge to resolve dissonance is the core of my being. But how to scrape away a shadow? How to tune a flaw that is woven into my own perception?

I became a wanderer. The calls of worn stone still pained me, but a greater pain, a deeper dissonance, drove me on. My brothers felt my passage as a troubling murmur moving through the city. I was seeking the source of my flaw. My instinct, twisted by my senses, told me that to resolve my inner dissonance, I must find the heart of its external cause: the shadow. I began to seek not the light, but the places where the light failed most completely. I climbed towards the high, silent vaults, away from the dense, glowing warrens below.

Up here, the drone is thinner. The metallic overtones are less complex, the bass note less a pressure in the chest and more a memory in the bones. The weeping of the walls is slower, the air drier. And the shadows are deeper. There are hollows here untouched by any glowing vein, pockets of true and utter blackness.

I found one such place. A niche, carved like any other, but facing away from the chamber's heart. Its mouth was a maw of perfect darkness. To the feet, it felt stable, ancient, and unremarkable. To the Drone, it was simply a quiet spot. But to my eyes, it was a solid thing. It was the source.

Hesitantly, I crawled inside.

Here, a new state exists. The Drone is a distant sea. The darkness is so complete it feels solid, a cool weight against my shell. It is the inverse of the Drone—a form of pressure that is made of silence.

And in this silence, I am not alone.

I am not the first.

Lining the back of the niche is a mosaic of shells. Geode shells, cracked and ancient. They are not shattered or broken, but arranged. Carefully. Placed. And in the center of the arrangement, something else. It is a carving. Not the smooth, homeostatic scraping of maintenance, but a sharp, deliberate pattern cut into the stone. It is a spiral, but it is not the gentle curve of a city ramp. It is tight, angular, aggressive. It is a wound. A scar. It is a thought expressed in violence against the stone.

This is a place of heresy. A place where Geodes came not to tune the instrument, but to break it. Or to create a new one. Here, in the visual silence, away from the overwhelming force of the Drone, they were able to act on an impulse other than maintenance. They were able to create dissonance. To *compose*.

What I would be, in this world, is the one who remembers.

I am the inheritor of this silent niche. My flawed eyes were not a mistake; they were a summons. My strange dissonance was a resonance with this hidden frequency, this cult of shadow.

I do not scrape to heal the stone anymore. My work is different. I crawl through the droning city by day, an aberration, a lonely, painful note in the perfect chord. The others read my flawed passage and shrink from it. But by what you might call night, I return to this place. My purpose is no longer to serve the Still, those perfect, static gods of sound. My purpose is to serve this memory.

I have begun my own carving next to the other. My hardened limbs, which once knew only the slow, patient scraping of repair, are learning the sharp, quick language of creation. The *scrape-scrape-scrape* is no longer a response. It is a statement. Each line I cut into the wall is a scream into the silence, an act of "I am" in a world of "we are."

The Still may live in the sound, but we, the flawed, the silent, the shadow-seers—we will live in the marks we leave behind. They are the gods of the eternal note. We are the ghosts who haunt the rests.

I am what the Geodes call The Dissonance. I am what the Still, if they could perceive me, would call The End. I am the one who saw the shadow, and in its silence, learned to sing a different song.
