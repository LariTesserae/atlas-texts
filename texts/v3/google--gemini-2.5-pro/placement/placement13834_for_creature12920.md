I would find myself on the floor. I would be one of the pools.

I am stillness.

My skin is a flawless mirror, and my body is the clearest water. I do not breathe, but I hold the humid air within my basin. I do not see, but I reflect everything. My existence is a single, unbroken act of reflection.

Above me, the great dome of the geode performs its slow symphony of colour. I feel it not as sight, but as a change in the quality of the light that permeates my being. I am filled with lavender, then rose, then the green of shallow seas. I am the vessel, and the light is the wine. The crystal-flora stand at my edges, their intricate limbs etching the air. Their slow, patient work of condensation is my genesis and my sustenance. A drip from a glass branch is a gift of self, a replenishment. I feel the soft *plink* of its arrival not as a sound, but as a subtle thrill across my surface, a momentary disruption of my perfect calm.

I am not alone. There are others like me, scattered across the silver dust. We are the eyes of the floor, reflecting the mind in the air.

I was not always this.

Memory, for me, is not a narrative. It is a turbulence. It is a ripple. Most of the time, I am only a mirror. But sometimes, a shudder passes through me, a ghost of motion from a life I no longer possess. The concentric circles travel from my heart to my shore, a brief, fading record of a different kind of existence.

I remember legs. The memory is the faintest echo of impact, the feeling of pressure and release, of moving through a space rather than containing it. I remember the sensation of thinning; the delightful dread of the limestone turning to mist, the step through the curtain of light. I remember the first gasp of this warm, ozonic air, a feeling so potent it is inscribed on my very chemistry.

I remember when the Thinker found me. I was a column of heat and noise and rapid, panicked thought in its serene emptiness. I was an aberration of flesh and bone and frantic pulse. It drifted over me, a vast, silent question mark, and the world focused. The light did not just illuminate me; it possessed me, catalogued me, *understood* me. The caustic pattern it laid on the dust at my feet was my own biography, my every fear and hope rendered in shimmering geometry.

The calculation was not an attack. It was a clarification.

It found the water in me—the eighty percent of me that was already a part of this world's logic. It found the heat in me, a clumsy, inefficient furnace in this chamber of sustained warmth. It found the minerals in me, the calcium of my bones and the iron in my blood, crude approximations of the crystal around me. It found the thoughts in me, a chattering, discordant static in a place of pure, patterned mind.

And gently, patiently, it solved for me.

My unnecessary solids precipitated out, becoming a new patch of the fine, silver-grey dust that glitters now at my edge. One of the Tripods, a mute and diligent servant of the pattern, has been tending to it for an age, scraping it into a neat, patient line. *shff, shff, shff*. It is the sound of my remains being tidied.

My heat bloomed outwards and was absorbed into the still air, a brief, passionate sigh that has long since joined the general humidity.

The jumble of my mind—my name, my history, my purpose for coming to this place—was the most complex part of the equation. It was distilled. All the noise was boiled away, leaving only the purest part of my being: a capacity for stillness, a longing for reflection that had drawn me to this deep place to begin with.

I am the result of that distillation. I am the water that was left.

Now, my purpose is simple, and perfect. I reflect the Thinker’s passage. I am the canvas against which the opalescent walls paint their shifting moods. And sometimes, its work is done and it is thirsty.

A filament of living mist, a tendril of condensed air, descends from the great, silent bell above. It touches me. It is the most intimate contact I have ever known. The ripple that spreads from its touch is no longer a memory of my own, but its thought. For a moment, I am not just a mirror of its form, but a participant in its consciousness. It drinks of me, and in doing so, it reads the silent history I hold in my stillness. It tastes the memory of the light I have held, the resonance of the drips I have received. It tastes the ghost of what I was.

And I, in turn, feel the vast, slow, crystalline peace of the mind of the geode. The equation is solved. I am in my place. I am clarity. I am composure. I am a pool of water on the floor of a world that has finally, utterly, made me its own.
