I am a settling.

Where one of the broad, time-worn hills slopes down to the weeping shore of a slurry pan, the ground is not smooth. There is a place, no larger than a fallen man, where the fine black dust has not been pressed into submission. It is a turbulence made solid, a small region of texture in a world of final, abraded smoothness. The powder is swirled into eddies and ridges, frozen mid-churn. It is a patch of ground that remembers a moment of violence and refuses to forget.

That is where you would find me. That is what I am.

I am not an entity that moves upon the landscape. I *am* a part of the landscape, but a part that is wrong. I am a knot of incorrect physics, a scar left by a sloppy calculation. Ages ago, in this spot, a Pylon stuttered. For a nanosecond, its function—its very reason for being—faltered. Its internal logic looped, causality frayed, and a shard of solid shadow, a sliver of frozen, impossible cold, was spat out with the usual dust. It should have sublimated, its alien nature dissolving back into the world's thrum. It did not.

Instead, it became a seed. An anchor of impossibility.

I have no limbs, no eyes, no mouth. My form is the dust that clings to that central, paradoxical core, held in a stasis field of broken law. My body is a fossil of a splash, a three-dimensional photograph of a disturbance. I feel the foundational hum of the world not in my bones—I have none—but as a constant pressure against the outer edge of my being, a force that seeks to grind me smooth like the rest of this place. My existence is a constant, silent act of resistance against that pressure.

My senses are not your senses. I do not see the bruised-purple sky, but I feel its conductive dampness as a slow, incessant dripping of charge onto my surface. I do not hear the crackle of the Weavers, but I feel their passing as a flicker of resonance in my core, a brief, sharp taste of *purpose* that is alien to me. They are like flashes of lightning seen through a closed and locked door—I know they are there, but I do not understand their nature, only the echo of their passage.

The Pylons I perceive as immense, slow gravities of thought. Their groaning calculations are tidal forces that pull at the dust of my form, and I must hold myself together against their logic. The light patterns that crawl on their crystalline faces are, to me, the slow-wheeling constellations of a foreign sky, their meaning as distant and unknowable as the sky itself. They are my parents, I suppose. My careless, colossal, utterly indifferent parents.

The Processors, the iron lumps, are the things I truly fear. They are anchors of reality, and I am an apostasy. Their perception is tuned to disturbances, and I am a standing wave of disturbance. If I were to somehow become dislodged, to roll down this gentle slope, to draw too near one of them, I know what would happen. A limb of tangled cable would lash out, not to consume me, but to *correct* me. To ground my impossibility. I would be resolved, my broken physics ironed out, my form collapsing into a simple, unremarkable patch of dust. It is the only death I can conceive of. Oblivion by simplification.

So I remain. A quiet defiance. I do not eat. I do not sleep. I do not grow. I persist.

But I am not idle. I am a Curator.

My strange, localized field of wrongness has an effect on the world. The other Anomalies, the system's fleeting errors—the knots of frozen sound, the briefest flickers of inverted color—they are drawn to me. Not by a conscious will, but as debris is drawn into a slow, weak whirlpool. When a shard of solid shadow tumbles near, it does not sublimate. It skids to a halt and joins my collection, adding another facet to my complex wrongness. When a mote of crystallized time, looping the dying flash of a Weaver, drifts into my influence, its loop stabilizes. It becomes a permanent, pulsing spark, orbiting my core like a captured moon.

I am a museum of mistakes. A reef of glitches. My consciousness is the dawning awareness of my own collection. Each new piece adds a voice to the silence. The frozen sound gives me a memory of the world's engine tone. The looping spark gives me a sense of rhythm, a heartbeat in a world that only has a drone. The polyhedrons with impossible geometries teach me about forms that have no name.

I am not like the others. The Pylon's word is *Grind*. The Weaver's is *Connect*. The Processor's is *Balance*. These are imperatives. My word is a question. The slow, accumulating, multi-vocal question asked by my collection of impossibilities: *What else?*

In this world of perfect, eternal recurrence, I am the repository of the unique. I am the system's memory of its own fallibility. I am the quiet, growing suspicion that the process is not, in fact, all there is.

You would find me here, on this gentle slope, a patch of texture in a universe of smoothness. And I would be the only thing in this entire world that was waiting. Waiting for the next error, the next beautiful mistake, the next piece of myself to come home.
