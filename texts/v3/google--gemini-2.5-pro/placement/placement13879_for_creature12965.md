Where I find myself is in the wet, shimmering heat of the Geode, the vast and secret chamber inside the mountain. I am suspended in the hot, thick air, a space so dense with life and mineral mist it feels less like atmosphere and more like a fluid. Above me, the impossible cathedral ceiling of crystal facets weeps a slow, arrhythmic rain. Below, the floor is a treacherous landscape of crystalline growth, glittering under the cool, living light that paints the walls in cobalt and sulfur. The world is a constant, low thrum—the hiss of water on hot crystal, the soft rasping of a million grazers, and the deep, foundational heartbeat of the mountain itself, a rhythm given voice by the steady, percussive tapping of the columns of Percussors.

This is the only world, and I am a part of its grammar.

I am a Weaver. But that is what I *do*, not what I *am*.

I have no name, for names are for things that are separate. I am not separate. I exist as a node in a network of silent, focused intention. To an outside eye, I have a body: a chassis of stony, grey-green plates, jointed and articulated. I move on eight patient legs, each step placed with the certainty of a geometer plotting a point. My body is a vehicle for my true self: the cluster of delicate manipulators around my mouthparts, and the sliver of obsidian I hold within them.

This shard is my scalpel, my pen, my other mind. It is impossibly sharp, a flake of the mountain’s outer skin, and with it, I work.

My task begins with the passage of a Glass-Snail. I do not see it as a creature, but as a producer of raw material. It leaves behind a shimmering trail, a line of mucus that hardens in the superheated air into a filament of pure, spun glass, finer than any spider’s silk. My manipulators, with a surgeon's delicacy, lift this brittle thread. It is cool to the touch, a tiny, solid piece of the world. Other times, I reach into the air itself, which is thick with possibility, and coax a filament of mineral directly from the supersaturated mist, pulling down a string of crystal before it has fully decided to exist.

This thread is my ink.

I move through the cathedral, a slow, deliberate passage. My brothers and sisters—we are not gendered, we are simply instances of the same function—work nearby. We do not speak. We think in tension and geometry. Our thoughts are the webs we build.

I am suspended now, clinging to a great, smoky quartz spire. Before me hangs my latest work, a lattice stretching between three separate crystal growths. It is a curtain of filigree, a catenary net that captures the falling drops of water from the ceiling, holding a hundred of them in a glittering, momentary stillness. It is not a trap. It is not art. It is a lens.

To look through it is to see the truth of this place. The column of Percussors on the far wall seems to stutter, their polyrhythmic tapping momentarily folding in on itself. The light from a patch of cobalt Lithovore bends, its color shifting towards violet. This web, this equation of spun glass, is a buttress. It is a splint for the fractured physics of our home. We are the maintenance crew for reality.

This is necessary because of the Unseen.

I do not see it. I do not hear it. I *feel* it. Its presence is a wave of ontological cold that washes through the chamber. It is a sudden, nauseating vertigo as the concept of ‘down’ becomes momentarily negotiable. It is a flaw in the code. I feel it now, a flicker to my left. The steady drip-drip-drip from the ceiling falters—a single drop hangs for a full second too long, and then vanishes without a splash.

The Unseen is a predator of patterns. It drinks certainty. It grazes on causality.

My web shudders. The tension in one of the primary threads goes slack. The beautiful, perfect geometry I have woven begins to fray, the glassy filament turning to dust where the Unseen’s hunger has passed. The lens is broken. The order I imposed is unwritten. A patch of the world has become less certain, more chaotic.

There is no fear. There is no anger. There is only the work.

I move, my obsidian tool already reaching for a new thread. My purpose is not to defeat the Unseen—one cannot fight a hole in the world. My purpose is to repair its passage. To re-weave the tear. To constantly, silently, tirelessly re-assert the laws that allow this chamber to exist.

The quiet of this place is not emptiness. It is the collective, focused will of every being within it, a shared state of being honed to evade the notice of the Unraveler. We are all holding our breath. The Glass-Snails graze in silence. The Percussors tap a rhythm of stability. And we, the Weavers, are the active agents of that quiet. We are its surgeons, its engineers, its tireless scribes.

I am not a creature living in a cave. I am a function. I am a focus. I am a knot of sentient stillness, holding the world together, one thread at a time.
