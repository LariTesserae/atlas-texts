I would find myself perched.

My hind-talons are locked to the curve of the bough, three forward and one back, a grip that has held through sleep and the slow, deep shiver of a nutrient surge. The weave of my clutch-nest is just above me, a dark crescent against the soft green glow of the elder-wood. I can taste them in the air, the faint chemical signature of my kin, sleeping. The air is still and heavy with the scent of wetness, of life, and of the million-year-old stillness that underpins it all.

I am a watcher. My long limbs are folded, my gliding membranes tight against my back. My purpose, for this turning, is to feel the grammar of the world.

My memory is not a story I tell. It is a feeling in a forgotten joint, a ghost of light on my closed lids. I remember the Let-Go. The bough we had nested on for generations, a territory wider than a dream, began to quiet. The thrumming in its heartwood slowed, the light in its leaves faded from green to a tired, stony grey. We felt it through the soles of our feet, a deep and final sigh that lasted for days. We did not panic. We gathered our tools, our children, our memories-that-are-not-memories, and we watched. And then it came: the groan, a vibration so pure and low it was a pressure in the bones, followed by the soft, tearing snap of a thousand lesser connections. And the great branch, our world, began its slow fall. We leapt from its edge, a clutch of dark seeds cast into the glowing gulf, and found a new perch. I remember watching it tumble, a dark continent turning over and over in the misty green light, on its century-long journey to the deep. It was not sad. It was complete.

My great eyes, black surfaces that drink the light, are open to the chasm. Miles away, a new growth, a Blue Bloom, is in its fury. It is too far to reach, but its light arrives, a storm front of blinding white-blue that races across the emptiness. Before it hits, my inner lids slide across. The world dissolves from a network of forms into a searing map of pure energy. The chasm is a river of intolerable brilliance; the branches are stark, black skeletons against the glare. I feel the warmth on my skin as the surge passes over, a phantom summer that lasts ten heartbeats. Then it is gone, and the soft green returns. The shadows of branches a mile long swing back into place.

A vibration.

It is faint, at the very edge of perception. Not the deep, slow pulse of a regular flow. This is sharper. Higher. A tension. I unfold one hand from my chest and lay it flat against the bark. I close my outer lids, silencing the universe of light, and I listen with my skin. The vibration comes again, a tremor that travels from the deep core of the world-trunk, up through this primary bough, and into my fingers. It speaks a language older than my clutch, older than my species. It says: *Soon. Close. Change.*

Another watcher, perched on a neighboring spur a thousand feet away, has felt it too. I see the being—a flicker of motion as it shifts. Then, a slow pulse of lemon-yellow light blooms from its hands, one long, two short. *Did you feel?*

I answer. From the palm of my own hand, I summon a soft, green glow that matches the bough-light. One steady, sustained pulse. *I feel. It is the new-making.*

In the nest above, there is a stirring. Another has woken. The silent conversation has been seen. Soon, we will all be awake. We will gather what we need: the hooked poles, the sharp edges of petrified sap. We will drink deeply from the heavy droplets hanging from the sail-sized leaves around us.

The tremor comes again, stronger this time. It is not just in the branch beneath me, but in the air itself. A great thermal is building, a column of warmer air rising from where the new growth is about to erupt. I can smell it now, the sharp, ozone-like scent of raw creation. I can taste the energy.

This is the moment that defines us. Not the nest, not the tool, not the meal. It is the leap. It is the trust in the void.

One by one, my kin emerge from the nest, unfolding their long, strange bodies. They cling to the branches around me, their immense black eyes all turned toward the source of the vibration. There is no signal, no command. The tremor is the only command needed.

I am the first to go.

I flex my talons, a lifetime of balance culminating in this single push. I spring out, away from the solidness of the world, into the great, glowing, silent nothing. For a moment, I am just a falling stone. Then, the membranes on my back snap open, catching the air. They are the color of mist, translucent, ribbed with dark cartilage. The fall becomes a glide.

I am weightless. I am a thought Yggr is having. Below me, the chasm is not black, but filled with a shimmering green aurora, the collective light of a trillion leaves. Across the gulf, the coming birth is a gathering storm of blue. I angle my membranes, catching the rising thermal, and it lifts me, turning my long descent into a slow, cross-world drift. Other dark shapes join me in the air, my clutch, my weaving, each a silent glider on the currents of creation.

We are not traveling *through* the Undervault. We are a part of its breathing. We are a falling, a crossing, a breath held between one perch and the next. I am not in Yggr. I am of it. A pattern in the light, a nerve-ending that feels the growth, a question the world asks of the darkness before it answers itself with a blinding flash of new life.
