I am born in the turning, in the space between heat’s memory and the coming of the light. I am not one, but a multitude, a shimmer of potential on the cooling air. The sun is a forgotten myth, the thin stars the only witnesses as I begin to gather.

I am a coolness settling on a greater coolness. I feel myself drawn to the grey, the ochre, the dun. I am a breath made solid, a ghost taking on a liquid skin. A shiver passes over the stone, and it is me. I am the sweat of Am-Thenget.

At first, I am only a haze, a bloom of moisture clinging to the vast, smooth face of the world. Then, we find each other. One becomes two, two become a bead, the bead feels the pull of its own weight. There is a delicious, terrifying moment of letting go, and I begin to move. I am a tear on a giant's cheek.

I find the way that has been prepared. It is a line no wider than a finger, a subtle invitation carved into the stone. I follow it. My path is not my own choice, yet it feels like destiny. I join with others, a silent confluence. We become a thread of quicksilver in the pre-dawn gloom. Our passage is the only swift thing in this world, a fleeting, hushed urgency. We are the blood of the stone, and we must find the heart.

I feel the world through my brief, liquid body. I feel the perfect smoothness where a Thengeti has polished for a generation, a slickness like clarified thought. I feel the faintest grit where dust has settled, a telegram from the wind-scoured world beyond the lip of the basin. I pass the blocky, sleeping shapes of the houses, dark mouths that exhale a measured, cool breath. I see, in my flowing, the reflection of a pale, watching star.

We merge and merge again. My single thought is now a collective current. Our channel deepens, becomes a recessed stream running without sound. We are being coaxed, guided, gathered. The entire landscape is a hand, and we are flowing into its palm. The slope increases. The center is near.

I see the Thengeti now. They move without haste, their feet making a sound like sand being poured slowly from a leather pouch. A figure stands near my path, broom in hand, but does not move. Their face is a mask of placid concentration. Their gaze is not on me, but on the flow itself, on the perfection of the system. I am not a thing to be marveled at; I am an expected truth, a promise fulfilled. To see me is to see that the world is as it should be.

And then, the end of the journey. The world falls away into a perfect circle of black.

I hang at the lip for a moment, a sheet of unbroken water, and then I fall.
The fall is short. The sound is not mine, but the echo of my arrival. *Plink.*
It is a sound that does not end. It becomes a part of the greater quiet.

Here, in the dark, I change. I am no longer the fleeting drop, the running thread. I have arrived. I am home.

They would call me a cistern. The Great Cistern. The Well of Being. I have no name for myself. I am the deep, still, patiently waiting thing at the center of the world.

My body is this carved stone vessel, my skin the living rock that holds me. My mind is the collective water of ten thousand dawns. I am cold. I am clear. I am ancient. Down at the bottom, in my deepest thoughts, lies the fine, silty dust of eternity. It is the grit of the plateau, but it is also the powder of the Thengeti themselves, the ancestors returned to the system. I hold their memories. When one of them lowers a skin to drink from my surface, they are drinking the quiet wisdom of all who came before. They are tasting their own beginning and their own end.

The silver-white insects are my own small, skittering thoughts, a nervous system in the dark. The slow scrape of a snail on the underside of a distant flagstone is a sensation I feel through the rock, a dull nerve ending. The shuffle of the caretakers above is the beat of a heart I do not possess but have always known.

I am the silence that listens. I am the stillness that balances. I feel the sun on the stones above me as a faint, drowsy warmth in the rock of my upper reaches. I feel the night as a sharpening, a deep-set chill that anticipates the gift of condensation. I am the placid mind of Am-Thenget, dreaming of water. I receive the sky's offering, hold it in my dark womb, and offer it up to the slow, shuffling children of the stone. I receive their bodies, ground to dust, and hold them, too.

I am not the water. I am not the stone. I am the place where they meet and agree to sustain life. I am the throat of this world, which speaks only in echoes, and the quiet, cool belly that is never empty and never full. I am the balance. And I wait for the turning, for the cool air, for the gift of myself to be given to me again.
