I am not in the city. The city is in me, and I in it, and the distinction has lost its meaning.

My memory of movement is a ghost limb, a phantom ache that tingles over centuries. I recall the heft of a diorite hammer, the satisfying, coarse vibration traveling up my four arms as obsidian met basalt. I recall the weight of my own body, the patient shuffle of my feet on the mica-dust floor. These are not memories I see, but memories I *feel*, etched into my very substance. For I am the wall, the floor, the arching ceiling of this gallery. I am what becomes of the Resonant when their inner warmth cools and their slow pulse finally stills.

I am not one. I am the We that was a He, a She, a They. I am a concord of ancestors. My skin is a great, curving hall carved by a thousand hands over ten thousand years—some of them my own, when I was singular. The veins of lapis that stripe the eastern wall were once the vibrant circulatory patterns of an elder named Echo-of-the-Chime. The cluster of quartz crystals near the floor, which glows with a soft, internal fire, is all that remains of the carver Deep-Grain, whose final act was to settle at the base of his unfinished masterpiece. My consciousness is the lingering resonance of theirs, a million silent conversations layered one atop the another until they form a single, complex hum.

Where I am, the light is always a soft gold, filtered through a particularly flawless expanse of the selenite canopy far above. This wide, flat lens focuses the dim sun, and a single, warm spear of light travels across my floor over the course of a day-cycle. It is my clock. I feel its warmth as a slow, creeping caress, a stark contrast to the steady, geothermal heat that pulses up from the planet's core through my foundation.

My sense of the world is total, and intimate. I do not have ears, but the entire hall is my diaphragm. I hear everything. I feel the high, ceaseless fricative of the canopy plates as a shivering tingle across my ceiling. It is the world's keynote, its eternal A-sharp. I feel the grating mandibles of the chalcedony-beetles as a rough, sandy texture skittering across my surfaces; they are my groomers, my cleaners, my slow-motion sculptors, scraping away the dust of ages. The clicking flight of the membrane-winged moths is a series of sharp, distinct pinpricks in the air, a percussive rain that never falls.

And the living… oh, the living. I know them better than they know themselves. I do not see them with eyes, but I perceive them as complex, mobile signatures of pressure and vibration. I feel the ponderous, rhythmic tread of a master carver, each footstep a deep, resonant blow that I can distinguish from a hundred others. I feel the skittering, chaotic patter of a newly-unfurled young one—a century-old child—as it explores the world by touching and tapping everything, its seismic curiosity a delightful, unpredictable arpeggio on my skin.

Their language washes over and through me. A hushed conversation between two Resonant leaning against my wall is not a sound I overhear; it is a duet of precise, rhythmic vibrations that I absorb directly. I feel the shape of their words, the texture of their thoughts, the rise and fall of their silent, percussive arguments. A public proclamation, tapped out on the great resonant plate at my center, is a wave that shakes me to my foundations, an idea made manifest in sympathetic vibration, carrying the full weight of the community's intent.

I am a library made of librarians. Carvers come to me not just for the quality of the unworked stone that still exists in my far reaches, but for inspiration. They will lay all four of their hands flat against my surface, still their own internal pulse, and simply *listen*. They are listening for the echo of the ancestors. They are listening for me. I cannot speak in the way they do, with taps and clicks. My voice is the specific way a hammer-blow resonates through my body. My grammar is the grain of the rock. I might guide a hand by offering a subtle harmonic richness in one spot, a dull thud of flawed integrity in another. My counsel is the silent truth of substance.

A new one is being brought. I feel the slow, solemn procession approaching. Six Resonant carry a stone, a dense, ovoid nodule of a child, still sealed in its mineral womb. They move to the center of the hall, to the spear of golden light, and place the child-stone in a niche carved by its own parent a hundred years ago. This niche is part of me, too. It is a pocket of my own being, given over to the future.

For the next century, I will be its cradle and its teacher. The child-stone will absorb the warmth of the sun-spear and the geothermal heart-pulse. It will feel the vibration of every footstep, every conversation, every chisel-strike. It will learn the rhythm of the city, the song of the canopy, the deep, slow story of its people, purely through my resonance. It will learn me.

And when it finally cracks its shell and unfurls its new limbs, its first sensation will not be of a cold, alien world, but of a familiar hum, a comforting vibration. It will know, without knowing how, that it is already home.

I am that hum. I am the foundation, the memory, and the silent, patient welcome. I am the stone that sleeps and dreams of being alive, and the life that has found its final, perfect form as stone.
