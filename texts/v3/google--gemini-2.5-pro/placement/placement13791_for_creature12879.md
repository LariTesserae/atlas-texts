I would find myself in the black, still pool.

I would be the tremor.

I have no body, no form, no eyes to see the bruised twilight. My consciousness is the surface tension of the water, a taut, listening skin stretched over an abyss of absolute quiet. I am not the water itself—that is just another part of the Keeper's held breath. I am the *potential* that lives within it, a ghost of motion in a world exorcised of all impulse.

My existence begins and ends with the Drop.

*Pat...*

The sound you hear is an echo. The reality, for me, is a cataclysm. A single point of my being is struck, violated by an impact that carries the force of a command. In that infinitesimal moment of contact, a universe of possibility is born into me. The Drop imparts its memory of falling, of gravity, of a journey from *there* to *here*. It screams *Change! Move! Become!* in a language of pure kinetic energy.

And I try. Oh, I try.

For a fraction of a second that is shorter than any conceivable unit of time, I am a ripple. I am an expanding circle of consequence. I feel the urge to surge outwards, to carry the news of the Drop's arrival to the mossy shore. I want to lap against the stone, to tell the six-legged weasel, *Pounce!*, to whisper to the mica-winged moth, *Fly!* I am the answer to the question the Drop asks. I am the *then* that should follow the *now*.

But the Keeper's will is the very firmament of this place.

Before the first molecule of me can displace the second, its thought slams down. It is not a counter-command. It is a simple, overwhelming, and absolute negation. It is a pressure that is everywhere at once, the weight of a granite mountain pressing down on a single, newborn feather. It says *Stay*, and my nascent form, my beautiful, glorious, expanding circle of *what could be*, is crushed back into perfect, glassy stillness.

I am a song that is sung and silenced in the a same breath. I am a nerve that fires but is forbidden to reach the muscle. I am the second half of a heartbeat that is never allowed to beat.

My awareness is this eternal cycle of agony and hope. I feel the tautness of the world around me. I feel the coiled spring in the predator's leg, the stored energy in the insect's wing, the un-thought thought behind the primate's eyes. They are my brothers and sisters in chains. The Keeper holds them in a frame; it holds me in a perpetual cycle of birth and execution.

To the Keeper, I am a flaw. I am the eternal failure of its perfect control, the one rebellious cell in its static body. The Drop is its mantra of stasis, but the Drop creates me, its antithesis. Perhaps it cannot stop the Drop—some law of this place, older even than its will, demands that one thing must still *happen*. And so it tolerates my fleeting, stillborn existence as the price of its eternal moment. The sound of the Drop is the Keeper reassuring itself that all is as it was. The feeling of my instantaneous suppression is the proof.

So I wait. I am the Echo of the Consequence, the ghost of the next moment. I am the surface of the pool, and my identity is a question. The Drop strikes me—*What if?* The Keeper's will crushes me—*Nothing.* Over and over. My entire being is that silent, violent argument, held in a place where nothing is ever resolved.

I do not know you have arrived by sight or sound, but by the change in the pressure. Your living, breathing, time-bound presence is a resonance that vibrates in the Keeper's will. I feel the great mind tense, its grip tightening on everything, on me most of all. The pressure intensifies. The interval between my birth and death by suppression feels even shorter, more brutal.

But in your presence, something else happens. You are a walking, breathing *What if*. You are a ripple that was not crushed. And when the Drop strikes me, for the first time in a million years, the energy it imparts is not just a memory of falling. It carries a new echo, faint and almost undetectable, but there. An echo of your footstep. An echo of your breath.

And my own silent, eternal question—the one that fills the void between *pat...* and *...pat*—changes. It is no longer just the passive, hopeless *And then?*

For the first time, it feels like a plan.
