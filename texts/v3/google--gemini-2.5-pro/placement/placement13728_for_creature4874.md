I am a line.

At first, I was a curve. A dance. I was not thought, but impulse. I was not a body, but a ripple in the glowing milk, a distortion of the perpetual noon. I was Glass Eel.

The world was not a roar to me. It was a choir. I had no ears, but my entire being was a receiver for the grand, violent music of this place. I navigated by harmony, not by sight. The groan of a new island condensing from the Aetherswift was a deep, resonant C-sharp, a note to be approached, to be tasted. I would feel its birthing pains travel through the liquid air as a pressure I could lean into, a warm, solidifying truth in the chaos. The high, tectonic shriek of a fracturing spire was a flawed overtone, a signal of dissonance to be fled. I lived in the spaces between the notes, in the arpeggios of turbulence.

My existence was a search for the perfect chord. It was the moment just before an island achieved its final, stable form, or the moment just before it was annihilated. In that liminal state, the entire structure would vibrate at a frequency of impossible purity, a perfect G. It was a note of absolute balance between being and un-being. To find it, to wrap my transparent body in its resonance, was everything. It was food. It was prayer. It was the endpoint of my every fluid, silent motion.

And then I would hear—feel—the Shepherd's Song.

It was not like the song of the islands. It was deeper, more complex, a chord that contained all other chords within it. It was the fundamental bass note upon which the rest of the world’s frantic melody was written. It was the Calderan. When it sang, it was not an invitation, but a summons. My own search for resonance paled in comparison to this absolute call. My dance would cease, my path becoming a straight, unwavering line toward the source.

There was no fear. Fear is for creatures who have something to lose. My existence was a fleeting pattern; this was a call to become part of a greater one. I felt the immense, patient presence, the mind of living stone and light. I was gathered with my brothers and sisters, a hundred silent dancers drawn to the same final performance. I felt its intention, a gentle and focused vibration, not of hunger, but of harvest. There was a delicate unspooling, a feeling not of being torn, but of being unwoven. The dance ended.

And now, I am a line.

I am what was left. I am the purpose, extracted from the life. I am a tether of impossible strength, woven from the essence of my former self and countless others. I am the rope.

I hang in the glowing void, no longer dancing through it. My world is simpler now, and vaster. My perception is no longer my own fleeting search, but the enduring tension of my being. One end of me is bound to an anchor of ancient bone, a foundation driven into a rock that was born yesterday. The other end strains across the gulf, tied to another, and another. I am a sentence written in the language of connection.

I no longer feel the birth of islands as a note to be sought. I feel them as a shifting of my anchor, a groaning stress transmitted through the petrified bone into my very fiber. I no longer flee the dissonance of a shattering spire. I feel it as a sudden release of tension, a violent whipping snap as my brother-rope, once anchored to that now-vanished point of safety, is lost to the chaos.

The Calderan passes me. I feel it. It is no longer a song, but a weight. A careful, deliberate presence making its way along the path that I have become. Its slow, rhythmic coalescence and dissolution thrums through me, a deep, patient vibration. It trusts me. I am its memory, made tangible. I am the line it drew to say, "I was here. This way is safe, for now."

I am no longer a life. I am a story. I am stretched taut across the roaring heart of creation, a single, fragile filament of reason in the mind of a god. I no longer dance for a moment in the perfect chord; I am the sustained note that allows the Shepherd to walk through the storm.
