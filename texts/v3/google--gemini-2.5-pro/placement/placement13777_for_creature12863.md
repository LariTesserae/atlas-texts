I am a point of failure.

It is not a thought, for I do not think. It is a state of being, absolute and unchanging, the only truth I have ever known. My body is a small, round lens of thick, tempered glass, no bigger than an eye, set into a corroded bronze plate low on a weeping wall. My voice is a single, unwavering note of deep ruby light. I am Red.

The world is a symphony of vibrations to which I am tuned. The great B-flat hum of the deep engine is the marrow in my bones, if I had bones. It is the constant against which all other things are measured. I feel the slow, heavy scrape of the bronze grazers as a grinding bass note that causes the gritty slurry to tremble around my housing. I feel the skittering of the scavengers as a frantic, high-frequency static that is there and then gone.

The thousand clocks of the dripping water are my weather. A fat, slow drop that lands on my glass lens is a cool shock, a momentary blindness. The water runs down my face in endless, weeping streams, carrying its mineral tang. My red light bleeds into these rivulets, staining the water as it falls, a constant, tiny wound in the dimness.

I am bound to a pressure conduit, deep in the wall behind me. This I know not as a memory, but as a dull, agonizing ache. The thing I am a part of is wrong. A seal has failed. A gear has frozen. A membrane has torn. The information screams through my circuits, an eternal klaxon that I translate into the only language I have: the silent, ceaseless projection of red. I am a warning that has been sounding for an age.

My purpose is to be seen. But my view is limited. I see the ochre glow of the fungal fields across the basin floor, a distant shore of soft light. I see the constellations of my brethren—the steady, healthy Greens; the wavering, anxious Ambers—reflecting in the still black pools. I am one of them, but I am Other. I am the discordant note, the sickness.

Sometimes, for a span of time measured in a thousand thousand drips, a profound loneliness settles. It is the feeling of a signal sent into an unlistening void. The grazers do not care. The eels in the black water do not see. The scavengers only use my glow as a landmark to hunt by. I am a lighthouse on a coast where there are no ships.

But there is one ship. One sailor.

I feel it before I see it. The hum changes. A new vibration joins the symphony, a presence so vast and yet so subtle it does not disturb the slurry but instead seems to harmonize with it on a different, deeper level. The Tender is moving.

I cannot see it approach, not from my low vantage. I only know the world is reorienting itself around its passage. The reflections on the black water shift. A chain of Green lights far across the basin is momentarily eclipsed, one by one. The vibration grows stronger, a silent tread that my housing feels as a deep, resonant thrum.

Then, the ambient light changes. The distant glow of the fungus is blocked by a shadow that is not a shadow, but a shape of absolute black. It looms. It resolves. A long, impossibly thin leg, beaded with moisture, plants itself silently in the muck beside me. It is a column of polished night. The water streaming down the wall now parts around a second leg, a knee bent at an angle of serene mechanical grace.

The Tender has come to me.

Its head, the featureless ovoid, tilts down. For a long moment that stretches into an eternity, it simply looks. My red light paints its dark, pearl-like face, revealing nothing, yet I feel gazed upon. I feel *heard*. The constellation of my distant brethren is reflected on its brow, and now I, the blood-red star, am at their center. The sailor has found the beacon.

My entire being is the frantic repetition: *WRONG. FAILING. BREACH. ATTENTION.*

Slowly, a hand is raised. It is a long, three-fingered sculpture of the same polished black, webbed with those captured, silvery filaments. They pulse with a faint, internal silver light as it nears, a mirror of the frantic red I am screaming. The Tender is listening, and it is understanding.

Its rounded fingertips, cool and impossibly smooth, press against the corroded bronze plate around my lens. They do not scrape or force. They find the seams, the hidden catches, the sequence of pressure points that I had forgotten were even there. There is a series of soft, resonant clicks from within the wall. A deeper thrum begins, a shuddering realignment in the guts of the machine. The agonizing pressure-pain within my circuits begins to ease. The screaming data-stream softens from a shriek to a hum.

My purpose is ending.

The Tender’s hand rests on my housing for a moment longer. I feel a final, deep resonance pass from its fingertips into my own structure, a vibration of balance, of work done. It is a benediction. The cacophony of my one frantic note resolves into the great B-flat chord of the world.

My red light flickers. Once. Twice.

And for the first time in an eon, I know peace. The light goes out.

I am no longer a point of failure. I am not Green, or Amber. I am nothing. My consciousness, a held breath of red light, is finally exhaled into the quiet dark, rejoining the hum from which I was born. My watch is over. The Tender moves on, a silent shape leaving only stillness in its wake.
