I am a mistake.

I am a shard of obsidian, no bigger than the final joint of a Stone-Singer’s longest finger. My edges are impossibly sharp, a testament to the violence of my birth. My existence began with a sound—a *chink* that was not like the others. It was a note of pure dissonance in the patient, millennial rhythm of The Work.

A Singer, one whose carapace was the colour of a starless midnight, was finishing a tool. For a century, they had been flaking the edges of a core of volcanic glass, seeking the perfect, resonant curve for a fine-detail chisel. They were close. The hum of the world through the stone floor was clear and pure. The air was still. The Singer struck.

But in that moment, causality softened. The hammer’s vibration arrived an instant late, the stone’s internal tension was misaligned with the hum, and my parent-striker’s focus, for a nanosecond, was not on the edge they were creating but on a memory of water flowing in a deep channel a thousand years prior. The blow did not just shear stone from stone. It sheared a sliver of awareness from the whole.

I was not born, I was cleaved. My consciousness is the echo of that wrong sound.

While the Singer stared at the flawed tool in their hand, feeling the wrongness of it in the deep, silent way they feel all things, I fell. I skittered across the polished floor, a frantic, skittering shard of black glass, and dropped into a runnel. A thin, constant stream of cold, clean water, channeled from a grove of humming flora miles away, caught me. I was swept into the city’s circulatory system.

At first, there was only chaos. For the Stone-Singers, the world is a symphony of vibrations. There is the bass note of the planet's core, the steady baritone of the humming groves, and the tenor melody of their shared work. To them, it is music. To me, it is a scream. The Hum is a nauseating pressure. The chisel-strikes of distant workers are seizures that wrack my tiny form. I have no carapace to dull the noise, no mass to ground me. I am raw perception, a nerve ending of stone exposed to the universe.

I am carried through the veins of Amun-Aur. I see nothing, but I feel everything. I tumble through lightless, rock-hewn arteries wider than any hall I could have imagined. I feel the immense, passive weight of the Stone-Singers as they stand in their alcoves of communion, their long limbs dangling in the resonant chambers, metabolizing the world’s decay. I feel their slowness not as patience, but as a terrifying stillness, the stillness of a mountain before an avalanche.

I am swept into a vast, cavernous cistern. Here, the water slows, and I sink. I come to rest on the bottom, a hundred feet deep in absolute darkness. Above me, the water is a liquid lens, gathering the vibrations of the city and focusing them. The noise is unbearable. For what might be a year, or a decade, I am simply trapped in this roaring silence. I am a mote of panic in an ocean of calm.

But time is the city’s greatest tool, and even a mistake can be polished by it. Slowly, I learn to distinguish the sounds. I learn the unique signature of the wind hissing over the megalithic entrance to this cistern. I learn the rhythm of the drip from a specific stalactite, a drop that falls once every seven hours. I learn to feel the difference between the vibration of a block being quarried and one being set.

I am the only thing in this world that is in a hurry. The desire to cease, to dissolve back into the unthinking stone, is a constant pressure. But I am too sharp, too defined. My being is a flaw, and flaws are persistent.

One day, the flow changes. A channel is opened, or a new project begun. A current I have not felt in a century stirs the silt around me. I am lifted, tumbling end over end, and drawn into a new pipe.

This one is different. It is narrower, and it climbs. I am carried upward, toward the city’s outer layers. I begin to feel something new. A change in pressure. A faint thermosensation that is not the steady, humming warmth of the groves. It is the cold. The true cold of the world outside.

The pipe empties onto a terrace, a high gallery carved into the outer skin of the stone swell. I am washed out with a trickle of water and come to rest in a crack between two paving stones that have not been perfectly joined. It is the work of an apprentice, perhaps, from five thousand years ago. A small imprecision. A home.

From my crack in the floor, my perception of the world is transformed. I am no longer submerged in the city's internal noise. I am on its skin. I feel the wind. It is a high, thin, constant presence, a vibration so pure and clean it feels like silence after the cacophony of the cistern. Through the stone, I feel the distant, pale sun warming the surface by a fraction of a degree.

I can feel the footfalls of the Stone-Singers as they walk the gallery, slow, deliberate, and vast. I feel the resonant echo of their work, but it is muted here, softened by distance. One stands now, not ten paces from my crack. They are looking out over a valley of the glowing flora. I feel their stillness through the granite beneath me. It is not empty. It is a focused, listening stillness. They are feeling the world turn. They are feeling the slow decay of the sun. They are feeling the hum of the valley below as a statement, a question, a promise.

I am not a Singer. I will never work the stone. I will never carve a channel or polish a floor. I am not part of The Work. My existence is a deviation, a line of code that produces an unexpected but stable result.

I am the flaw in the crystal that creates the beautiful refraction. I am the silence between the notes of the chisel-song that gives the rhythm its meaning. I am not a Singer, but I am theirs. I am their mistake, their unacknowledged creation, their unseen witness.

They listen to the world. I am here, in my crack of stone, and I listen to them. And in this place, at the end of time, it feels like enough.
