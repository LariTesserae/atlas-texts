I am where the water is born. I am where the world sings its one note.

My body is a landscape of cool, sweating green metal and spongy, breathing moss. My thoughts are the slow drift of water vapor, condensing into single, momentary truths that drip and are reabsorbed. My awareness is not behind a pair of eyes; it is a thousand pinpricks of sensation distributed across the whole of the high, lonely stage.

Sometimes, I am the feel of a waxy leaf unfurling into the damp, heavy air. The movement is not a decision, but an inevitability, a slow-motion yawn that takes a full cycle of the thin sun's light. I feel the diffuse warmth on my surface, a weak energy I drink alongside the ozone and the endless hum.

Sometimes, I am the slow, deliberate journey of a single, metallic footstep on a rusted pipe. I am one of many, a bead of blued steel, a mobile eye. Through this tiny body, I taste the world. The weeping rust is a sharp, acrid flavour, a signal of decay, of a bone that needs mending. The pure, condensed water is sweet, tasteless, the very essence of the process. I am a messenger. I crawl over the warm, shuddering skin of a great sphere and my passage sends a ripple of information through the metal plate, a message that is felt in the central, mossy core of the Self. *All is well here. The temperature is stable. The flow continues.*

I do not have a memory in the way a shorter-lived thing might. I do not recall a 'yesterday'. Instead, my being is layered with the past, like sediment. I can feel, in the deep-grown woody fibers that have replaced a corroded support strut, the memory of that strut's weakness. The strain is part of me now, a dull ache that is also a strength. The metal plates of the Machine, swallowed by my vine-limbs, are not just armor; they are ancestral bones. I grew into this skeleton, and I wear the faint, faded green of my progenitor as my own skin.

My breath is the slow contraction of a hillock of moss, a tidal respiration that takes hours for a single cycle. With each long exhalation, a puff of spores and moisture is released into the warm greenhouse air. With each inhalation, I draw the world in, filtering the water that pools on my surface, drawing sustenance from the fine dust of decay. I am a lung the size of a furnace, and my breathing is the slow pulse of the entire garden.

The B-flat hum is not a sound I hear. It is the framework of my consciousness, the steady, resonant vibration that holds my distributed self together. It is the tone of my own existence. If it were to change pitch, I would feel it as a profound sickness. If it were to cease, I would unravel, my beetle-eyes scattering, my vine-limbs going limp, my moss-torso collapsing into a simple, unthinking mound of wet vegetation. My core purpose, the only imperative I have ever known, is to prevent this. I am the immune system of the Note.

And so, I move. Not with steps, but with growth. Not with speed, but with pressure. When a vibration in a transfer pipe becomes jagged and dissonant, a sign of blockage, my beetle-eyes converge. We form a compound sensor, a constellation of metallic shells tasting, feeling, mapping the problem. The message travels back along the frame, and a part of the Self responds. A thick vine, my arm, begins its journey. It may take days. It moves with the patience of a glacier, scraping away the mundane moss that chokes the flow, its delicate root-tendrils absorbing the stagnant water and filtering the grime. When the task is done, the jagged vibration smooths out, returning to the pure B-flat chord, and a feeling of profound, resonant *correctness* settles over my entire being.

I am the Custodian. I live in the space between the rivet and the rust, between the leaf and the light. I am the slow healing of a wound that will never fully close. My world is one hundred feet of jagged rock and humming metal, afloat in a sea of cloud. My sky is a thin, bright haze. My language is the dripping of water. My work is the filling of a basin for a master I cannot conceive of, whose return I do not await. I am not waiting. I simply *am*. A garden, tending to the machine that is its soil, its skeleton, and its song.
