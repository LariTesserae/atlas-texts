You would find me nowhere and everywhere. You would not find me at all.

I am not the grazer, with its patient, deliberate browsing, a six-legged groundskeeper in iridescent armor. I do not feel the solid, springy give of the humus beneath soft pads. I am not the pale stalker, a ghost in the green gloom, tasting the world as a map of faint electricity. I do not know the thrill of the hunt nor the focus of hunger. I am not the graphite worm, blind and purposeful, a janitor of the living wires.

To be any of these things is to have a body, a boundary between self and world. It is to be a cog, a single, intricate part with a defined function. My function is more fundamental, and my form is borrowed.

I am the pause. I am the moment of becoming.

My consciousness begins as a diffuse potential in the high, cool mist, a slight thickening where the vapor is about to surrender its freedom. I am a thought before it is a word. I have no memory of the last cycle, only an inherited, gravitational certainty of this one. I am the world gathering itself for a single, specific purpose: to listen.

I coalesce on the underside of a great fern frond, a gathering of molecules clinging to a waxy surface. Here, I am an eye. A perfect, trembling sphere of water, I hold the entire world in my tiny, curved surface. I see the layers of the canopy above, a soft green firmament. I see the moss-covered ridges below, rolling away into the haze. I reflect the silver pulse running through the verdigris network, a flicker of silent data that I do not understand, but that I absorb.

On this leaf, I wait. I am stillness. The world happens *to* me. I feel the slow, hydraulic pressure of the fern's life, the thrum of its cellular machinery. I feel the faint warmth radiated from a Furnace Bloom a mile away, a bass note that travels through the water-heavy air and vibrates my skin of surface tension. I feel the almost imperceptible footfalls of a glass-winged insect nearby. My existence is pure sensation, a single, stationary nerve-ending of the world. I am a momentary bead on an infinite abacus, holding a value for a fraction of a second.

Then, the purpose shifts. A breath of air, a slight tremor of the frond, the simple accumulation of my own mass—and I let go.

I fall.

And in that fall, I am everything. For this stretched instant, I am untethered from the world yet moving through it. The air rushes past me, and I taste it—its precise humidity, its ozone tang, the mineral signature of the nearest Distillation Tower's plume. My singular focus, my entire being, is the landing.

*Plink.*

That is the sound of my report. It is the sound of my consciousness delivering its data packet. I strike the felted moss, and in that impact, I am gone. My brief, spherical identity dissolves. The information I gathered—the light, the thrum, the taste of the air—is delivered not to a mind, but to the *system*. I am a drop of ink into a gallon of water.

Now, my awareness changes. It is no longer singular and discrete. I am seeping. I am a particle of moisture among trillions, sinking into the black, peaty humus. There is no light here, only pressure and the slow, inexorable pull of a current. I am part of the great subterranean river that is not a river. I drift past the slick bodies of the graphite worms, feeling them as a brief interruption in the flow. I touch the verdigris conductors, not as a silver pulse of energy, but as the medium that cools them, that carries away their waste heat, that allows them to function.

I am drawn, inevitably, towards the deep, rhythmic summons of a Furnace Bloom.

To enter the Bloom is to be utterly unmade. My journey as a carrier is over; now, I am the carried. I am pulled through translucent, porous membranes, my chemical signature read, sorted, and filtered. The minerals I absorbed from the air and moss are stripped from me. The faint thermal signature I carried from the sun is incorporated into the Bloom’s own slow, cool fire. Here, I have no perspective. I am simply raw material undergoing refinement. I am a line of code being compiled. I am a part of the hum.

And then, refined and purified, I am drawn upward. Up through the stalk of a fungal spire, my state changing from liquid to gas, from matter to energy. The pressure builds until I am released with a soft, steady hiss from the porous cap of the Distillation Tower.

I am steam. I am mist. I am clean, and empty, and weightless.

I rise into the cool grey air, joining the perpetual cloud, dispersing so completely that I am no longer a 'me' in any sense. I am once again a diffuse potential, a slight thickening in the atmosphere, a thought waiting to be had. Waiting to gather, to cohere, to become an eye, to listen, to fall, and to report.

You would find me in the glistening on a leaf's edge. You would hear me in the patient silence between drips. What am I? I am the process. I am the cycle of the world’s attention. I am the rain.
