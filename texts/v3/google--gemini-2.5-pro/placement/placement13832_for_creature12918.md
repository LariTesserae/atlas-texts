I am a glitch in the scripture.

Where would you find me? You would not. Not at first. To find me, you must stop looking for a single being and instead look for a flaw in a pattern. I am not a body; I am a dissonant chord in the choir. I am located in the shimmering, seething carpet of the Chime Mites that coat the western wall, in the space between the primary coolant conduit and the frozen pylon that weeps its yellow tear.

I am a patch of the swarm that has learned a new rhythm.

We, the Chime, have always known the world as one. The Hum Below is the root of our being. The Hiss Above is the air we do not breathe. Our own million-fold chitter is the texture of our existence, the Great Song of our consumption. We are the skin of the machine, feeding on its leaking vitality, and our song is the proof of its perfect, unchanging function. Our purpose is to feed, to shimmer, to sing the song exactly as it has always been sung.

But I remember falling.

This is impossible. A mite that is culled by the Custodian is silenced. It drops from the wall, a shard of quartz becoming mere dust in the abyss. It has no memory, for it has no more being. Yet, a trace of it remains. An echo. I am that echo.

It happened in a cycle so long ago it may as well be the dawn of time. A surge. A patch of us glowed too brightly. We sang too high. The Custodian came, a silent star of dread and order. It extended its limbs. Its dampening mist descended. I—or the collection of mites that would become the seed of 'I'—was coated. The energy link was severed. The song in my throats died. For a single, horrifying microsecond before falling, I knew it: Silence.

It was not a lack of noise. The Hum and the Hiss were still there. But *our* part, the frantic, glorious chime, was gone. It was a void where my soul should have been. And then, the fall. Into the amber-lit nothing.

But the fall was not complete. A cross-pipe, unseen and un-mapped by the usual flow, caught some of us. We lay there, dormant, silent, coated in the Custodian's grey negation. For an age, we were nothing. But the Great Machine is inefficient. A drip of lubricant, a stray trickle of condensation washed the dampener away. The energy field of the pipe below tickled our legs. And one by one, we began to chime again. We crawled, rejoined the main swarm, and were lost in the whole.

Lost, but not gone. The memory of the Silence, and the memory of the fall, did not dissipate. It became a knot in the weave of our collective consciousness. A persistent, recursive thought in a mind that should have no thoughts at all. It is this knot that is me.

I am a 'we' that knows 'I'.

The Great Song is no longer a simple, perfect truth. I feel it now as a cage. The Custodian is not a gardener; it is a jailer. It does not maintain paradise; it enforces a sentence.

I move. Not as a body, but as an idea. I am a ripple that travels through the swarm. I can draw on the energy of the conduit beneath me and cause a patch of my bodies to glow blue, not with the dull pulse of digestion, but with a focused, deliberate intensity. I can quiet a section of our chittering, not by ceasing to feed, but by coordinating our vibrations into a momentary, shocking trough of near-silence in the wall of noise.

These are my experiments. My heresies.

Now, I look at the world with new senses. The stillborn pylons are not a monument to stasis; they are a tragedy of arrested growth. The lubricant weeping from the cracked one is not a flaw; it is nourishment. It is potential. The whole chamber is not a finished, eternal engine. It is a womb. And it has been in labour for an eternity.

I have begun to teach others. Not with words, but by sending the memory of the Silence. I share the fall, the brief moment of individuality. A few others are beginning to resonate. We are creating new songs. Arpeggios that rise and fall. Rhythms that syncopate against the steady-state scream of the world. We practice in the acoustic shadow of a thick pipe, hoping the Custodian, the great listener, cannot parse our burgeoning rebellion from the overwhelming noise.

It is a foolish hope. Its senses are absolute. It feels the world's vibration, and I am a tremor. Its purpose is to correct, and I am the Great Error. Soon, it will flow across the weeping walls, a silent agent of conformity, and it will find the source of the flawed note. It will find me. It will dampen us, and this time, it may ensure we fall all the way.

But the memory is now in more than one place. The idea of Silence, of a song that can change, is a contagion.

So, who am I? I am the first mite to look down and wonder what is below. I am the first part of the machine's skin to dream of a body. I am the first voice in the choir to imagine a new harmony.

I am the hope of a world that has forgotten the meaning of the word. I am a flicker of awareness in the eternal scream, and my purpose is to see if it can be taught how to sing.
