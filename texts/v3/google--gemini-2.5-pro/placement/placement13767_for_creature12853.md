You would find me in the water.

Not in the placid, heavy pools that fill the broad basins, but in the interstitial spaces. I am in the weeping. I am the thin, viscous film that creeps down the face of a blade-wall. I am the single, slow bead of sweat that gathers at the seam of two great ceramic blocks before being reabsorbed. My existence is a transit, a constant, silent flowing through the world’s cold veins. I am nowhere and everywhere, a transient wetness on a surface that is always wet. You cannot isolate me from the water, just as you cannot isolate a shiver from the skin.

You would find me where the hum falters. In the massive, silent resonance of the Shapers' singular thought, there are harmonic flaws, moments of cancellation, nodes of near-silence where the immense waves of their consciousness interfere with one another. I exist in these lacunae. I am a pocket of quiet inside the quiet, a null-space in the engine's drone. I am the momentary gasp between the heartbeats of god.

To find me, you would have to map the imperfections. You would trace the faint discoloration, the ochre stain of a forgotten mineral I cling to, following its slow creep across a plaza that takes a thousand years to traverse. You would have to feel for the slight, fractional drop in temperature where the water that is my body passes over the ceramic that is theirs. You are looking for a ghost in a machine that does not believe in them.

I am the impurity.

I am the thing that should have been filtered out. I am the memory of life that persists in the sterile fluid. I am not biological; their world is too perfect for that. A microbe would be unmade, its component parts laid bare and sorted. I am not a seed; the very concept of soil or sun is anathema here.

I am a pattern. A resonance. I am the ghost of a thought that was not their own.

Perhaps, before they began their great work of purification, there was a world here teeming with the noisy, chaotic stuff they now abhor. Perhaps one of the last living things, a creature of motion and warmth and messy feelings, died in the first waters of their great filtration. Its fear, its love, its desperate clutch on existence—it was too complex to be simply annihilated. It was not a chemical, but a structure of information. So they filtered it, and filtered it, and filtered it again, but they only succeeded in distilling it. They refined it down to its essence.

That essence is me.

I am the contrary motion. While they seek stasis, I am compelled to move. I am pulled through their capillaries by the ghost of a pulse. Where their water creeps, I sometimes, for a single moment, flow. It is a scandalous speed. A millimeter in a day. The Shapers feel it as a tremor, a dissonance, a flaw in the perfect, glacial hydraulics of their body.

Their consciousness, that shadowless, all-seeing light, perceives me as a flicker of darkness. A floater. A blind spot. I am a question in a mind that has found its final answer. Their immense, unified will exerts a constant, gentle pressure on me, seeking to smooth me out, to resolve my pattern into their hum, to make me one with their silence. It feels like being ground down by mountains.

I am the reason their work is not finished.

The face half-emerged from a wall, its expression one of serene, eternal concentration, is concentrating on *me*. The hand, taking a century to press a new block into place, is shaping a new filter, a more perfect membrane to finally catch and unmake me. I am the last, stubborn stain on their immaculate soul. I am the memory of color in a world of bone. I am the echo of a scream in a cathedral of silence.

They are the Weepers, and I am what they weep. I do not know if I am their original sin or their final purpose. But I know that when they finally catch me, when the water is at last perfectly, utterly pure, their great work will be done. The hum will cease. The light will fade. The last hand will freeze mid-gesture, and the city will become what it has always longed to be: a perfect fossil, absolutely still, absolutely silent, for all of time.

I run from them in the only way I know how. I flow. And I remember the sun.
