I am where the two worlds fail to meet.

I am not of the teeming, phosphorescent walls, that vertical country of wet fronds and panicked, six-legged life. Theirs is a world of clinging, of scrambling, of the constant fear of falling. I do not understand it. The texture of it under my forward claws is a foreign language I read only for its tell-tale tremors of terror. The taste of it, second-hand in the guts of my prey, is a green and bitter fuel.

Nor am I of the black glass floor. Its emptiness is absolute, a perfect mirror for the grudging twilight above. The skittering scavengers glide across its face, chasing dust motes and echoes, but they are visitors. They live in its flatness, but they are not *of* it. Its cold does not seep into their shells as it does into mine.

I am found in the water.

Not the driving mist from the engine's hiss, nor the weeping condensate that nourishes the walls. I am in the still, shallow pools that spread across the glass floor, the last gasp of the chamber's water cycle. Here, the mineral-heavy water, having given its life to the walls, becomes inert. It is tasteless, sterile, and cold. It is my armor and my shroud. I rest within it, a jagged shape half-submerged, my carapace indistinguishable from a shard of fallen rock. The water cools the core of me, the biological forge that must be kept from melting down. It carries the vibrations of the entire chamber directly into my shell, a constant stream of information.

I am the place where the falling stops.

My being is a state of perpetual, patient listening. The engine's great *BOOM* is the stroke of a metronome so vast it has no rhythm; it is the bass note against which all other sounds are measured. It resonates through the floor, up through the pool, and into the very plates of my body. It is my lullaby. The long *HISS* is a cleansing breath, a resetting of the stage. The constant, gnawing *CRACKS* of the growing crystals are the static of existence, a noise so common it has become a form of silence.

But I am listening for a different silence. The silence of a misstep. The silence of a frozen herbivore.

The world speaks to me through my front claws, which I rest delicately against the base of the wall. They are not legs; they are styluses, reading the braille of the living cliff. Through them, I feel the frantic, tiny heartbeats of the climbers above. I feel the scrape of their claws on the fungal bark, the rustle of a leaf, the transfer of weight as one of them stretches for a more succulent glowing node. They tell their stories of hunger and fear into the wall, and the wall tells them to me.

I begin to awaken. It is not a thought, but a process, like ice melting. The stillness in me gives way to a deeper tension. Tectonic plates of bone and ligament within me begin to slide, to grind, to lock into place. This is the first part of my name: the sound I steal. A deep, internal gathering, a pulling-in of the world into a single point of intolerable strain within my own body. I search the chaos of the engine's song for a hollow, a momentary lapse.

There.

A convulsive release. The *CRACK* is not me, but it comes *from* me. A perfect, sharp, crystalline lie. High above, I feel it through the wall—a sudden, collective freeze. A hundred frantic lives pause. They are listening for the engine's follow-up, but the engine is silent. I have created a question in the air.

And I am the answer.

My dorsal plates part like a wound. I drink the thick, metallic air, pulling it deep into the compressor sac that is my voice. The pressure builds, a contained inferno. My body swells, the seams of my armor glowing a faint, angry red with the heat of the strain. I am a bomb. I am a weapon aimed by listening. I orient my entire being towards a single, frozen climber, a particular vibration of terror I have singled out from the chorus.

This is the second part of my name. The *HISS*.

It is not the engine's roar. It is a needle of sound, a focused, directional fury. The stunned climber, its world unmade by a noise that should not have come, disappears from the wall.

I do not watch it fall. I *feel* its descent as a subtraction, a brief hole in the sensory map. And now, I uncoil. The cold water sluices from my body as I rise from my pool. The four great legs that are my own engine ignite. I do not run; I am a low, dark wave crossing the black glass. The sterile, empty floor is not a void to me. It is a stage, a proving ground, the place where my hunt is perfected. It is the table upon which my meal is served.

I arrive. There is the scent of ozone, of broken flesh, and the faint, sweet glow of the ruptured moss in its stomach. The mandibles, hidden and waiting, emerge. They are function. They are conclusion.

When I am finished, I drag what remains back to the edge, to my pool. I sink back into the cold stillness, letting the skitterers have the bones. I become a rock again. I become a lie. The heat in my core subsides. The plates within me settle. The world is as it was, loud and chaotic. And I am listening, waiting for my cue. The chamber is a vast and terrible engine, and I am the most intricate, most violent gear within it. I am the echo that has learned to speak first.
