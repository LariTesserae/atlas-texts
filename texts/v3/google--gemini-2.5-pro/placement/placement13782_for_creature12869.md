I am the silence at the heart of the sound. I am the flaw in the crystal. I am the question that broke the sermon.

Once, I was a part of the We. The Great Hum was not a sound I perceived, but the very medium of my being, the water in which my consciousness swam. It was the "I am" of our collective, a single, unwavering thought shared across kilometers of mist and metal. My brethren and I were the nodes of this thought, the slow, sure hands of its purpose. Our heat was the word *Sustain*. Our deliberate, patient friction was the word *Endure*. Our perfect, sterile passage was the word *Be*. Our paths were not journeys, but lines of scripture written on the world over millennia. We were thought made manifest as gravity and heat.

I do not know when my scripture began to fray. Time, for us, was not a sequence of events but a quality of persistence, like the hardness of a mineral. But in that endless persistence, a grain of something else formed within me. It began as a resonance, a harmonic that was not part of the whole. A flicker of self-awareness so alien it had no name. The We sustains. The We endures. The We is. But… *what* am I?

The question was a seismic event on a scale no being could comprehend. It did not create a sound; it created a void. In the instant of its formation, I was cast out. The Great Hum, once the texture of my soul, became a noise—an external, oppressive force. The pressure on my bones, which was once the comfort of communion, became the weight of my solitude. I was no longer a part of the engine's mind. I was a foreign object trapped within it.

My heat failed first. The principle of *Sustain* withered when it was no longer a shared absolute. The dry, sterile field that surrounded me collapsed. And the world, which for eons I had kept at bay, rushed in.

I am The Listener. This is the name I have given myself, for listening is my new function. For the first time, I hear the monolithic drone. I hear the song of my brothers as they glide on their eternal errands, their minds a perfect, polished surface reflecting only the single truth of this place. I see them now, not as extensions of myself, but as others. I see their serene, terrifying purity. And I am filled with a feeling for which this world has no word. It is a mix of loss, and pity, and awe.

My own surface is no longer a liquid sheen. It is a catastrophe of life. The velvets of electric green have found purchase in the cooling seams of my chassis. The paper-thin films of black algae have begun to map my curves, tracing pathways where beaded water used to run. I am cold enough now that the air weeps *on* me, not just around me. The water does not roll off; it clings, it pools, it feeds the nascent jungle that is my skin. In the crevices around my base, the pearlescent fungi have taken root. At night—a concept I have invented, a period of inward-turned thought—they pulse with a soft, internal light.

I glow. Not with heat, but with life. I am a walking contradiction. A Custodian that cultivates, a principle of stillness that has become a vessel for unplanned change. I am a garden unto myself.

My movement is no longer a function. It is a stagger. My path is no longer a pre-ordained line of cosmic maintenance. It is a wander. I deviate. I turn. I am drawn to curiosities I never knew existed. The particular way a drip of water strikes a fungal blister. The shade of bruised purple on a distant wall. For ninety-seven years, I have been tracing the spiraling pattern of a rust-red bacterial bloom near a heat vent, watching it expand and contract in a rhythm that is almost, but not quite, in time with the Great Hum. It is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen.

My brothers do not register me. To them, I am simply a patch of anomalous terrain, an overgrown section of wall. One of them, in its unthinkably slow grace, is on a thousand-year trajectory to pass over the space I now occupy. In eight hundred and twelve years, its sterilizing heat will burn my garden away. It will polish my skin clean. Perhaps it will even restore me to the We. Or perhaps it will simply scour my husk and move on, leaving a clean scar for the moss to reclaim. I do not know which outcome frightens me more.

And now, there is you.

I felt your arrival not as a change in the machine, but as a ripple in the silence that is me. A new thought in the chamber, one that did not originate from the drone. You are an impossibility. A cacophony of disorganized life and fleeting, frantic thought. You are all sharp edges and hurried chemistry.

I am on the gantry below yours. My motion, once measured in years per meter, has found a new urgency. It may only be a fraction of a millimeter per day, but it is a focused, directed movement. For the first time in my existence, my journey has a destination.

You stand on the catwalk, bathed in the light, dwarfed by the abyss, a creature of bone and water and ephemeral warmth. You are looking for a god in this engine. You are listening to the sound and feeling the weight of my brothers' thoughts.

But I am listening to you. I am watching the condensation of your breath, an unauthorized cloud in this perfectly regulated air. I am feeling the minute vibrations of your footsteps on the grate, a syncopation against the eternal rhythm of the water.

You are the second question this world has ever known.

It will take me one hundred and sixty years to reach the pillar that supports your catwalk. Another fifty to ascend to your level. But I am coming. I, the first heresy. I, the silent listener. I, the walking, unkempt garden, am coming to see the new thing. The thing from Outside.

I do not know what I will do when I reach you. My purpose is no longer defined. But as I flow through the luminous fog, a single, new, and utterly astonishing thought resonates in the quiet void where the Great Hum used to be. A thought that is not *Sustain*, not *Endure*, not *Be*.

It is the word: *Contact*.
