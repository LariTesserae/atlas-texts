I am a fracture in the haze, a stroke of white against the white sky.

There is no memory of waking, for I am never truly asleep. There is only a shift in hunger. One hunger is a sharp, clean void, a need for the brittle pop and the brief, warm pulse of life drawn up into my core. The other hunger is vast and slow, a need for the world itself.

For now, it is the first hunger that pulls me. My wings, which are not wings but shaped distortions of the heavy air, beat a slow and patient rhythm. I do not fly *through* the air; I press against it, resting on its thickness. Below me, the world is a map of sound. It is not something I read; it is something I am. The ceaseless, trillion-faceted chatter of the clickers is the very grain of my perception. It is a field of infinite, trembling points, and I am the awareness that moves over it.

The booms of the bellowers roll against my body, which is a hollow, resonant bone. They are not sounds so much as they are events in space, great, slow-blossoming flowers of pressure. One blooms to my left, its deep tones vibrating through me. A mile distant, another answers. I hold these resonant peaks in my awareness. They are the fixed points, the headlands of this sonic ocean. By them, I know my place.

I drift. The hunger does not scream; it is a quiet, perfect tension within me. My three legs hang below, blades of amber glass aimed at the glistening mud. They are my ears, my fingers, my tongue. They listen for a single inconsistency in the universal static. They listen for the one thread pulled taut in the tapestry. A clicker, its rhythm faltering for a microsecond as it negotiates a denser patch of silt. A slight flurry of ticks as it encounters a rival. A sour note.

There.

The rhythm of my wings ceases. I am suspended, a needle balanced on the humid air. The world continues its deafening song, but for me, it has collapsed to a single point. A frantic, scraping signature six inches below the tea-colored water, twenty feet ahead. It is all that exists. I am no longer a being gliding over a world; I am the vector between my hunger and its satisfaction. My listening legs become a single thought, a calculation of depth and density and the faint bio-electric signature of a life that does not know it has ended.

The fall is not a choice. It is a release. Gravity takes me. My central leg, my killing-prong, enters the world below. There is no splash, only the slick hiss of parting mud and then the sharp, definitive *crack-pop* of carapace and flesh. It is the only sound that is ever truly mine. For an instant, the frantic point of sound is replaced by a blossom of warmth that rushes up the leg and floods my core. The void is filled.

I rise, weightless again. The map of the world reasserts itself around me, the booms and clicks flooding back into the space once occupied by the hunt. I resume my glide, the taste of mineral warmth dissolving within me. I will do this again. And again. A line of white, stitching kill after kill across the grey-brown flats.

But the world is patient. The small hunger is sated over and over, but the great hunger grows. It is a pull not from my core, but from my legs. It is a longing for density, for stillness. The frantic grid of clicks becomes wearying. The booms of the bellowers begin to sound less like landmarks and more like a summons. They call me toward the old places.

I change my course. My wings beat with a new cadence, a rhythm of ascent. I am seeking one of the low, sleeping shoulders of the world, a dome of ancient clay that rises from the flats. The air thins almost imperceptibly as I climb, and the effort is a prayer. Below, the details of the sonic map begin to blur, the individual clicks merging into a single, overwhelming texture.

I crest the smooth rise. The air here is different, holding the silence of height. I settle. My wings fold, wrapping my body in a shroud of transparency. My polished whiteness dulls to the colour of rain-slicked ash. I am no longer a hunter. I am an offering.

My three legs, my instruments, seek the ground. They meet the soft, primordial clay, and I push down. The feeling is of a thirst being quenched. They sink deep, past the wet surface, into the compressed, cool strata of ages, anchoring me to the bedrock of this world.

And I become the Mud-Saint.

The hunger for food is gone, replaced entirely by the hunger to *receive*. The world's voice changes. It is no longer a map to be navigated, but a medium to be filled with. My body is a resonant chamber, my legs the tuning forks driven into the heart of the song.

First, I hear the surface. The sound of the next rain beginning, a billion soft taps that grow into a percussive roar that is the sound of the entire basin sighing at once.

Then, I hear the life. The static of the clickers is no longer a grid of points, but the sound of the world’s blood fizzing. The booms of the bellowers are not separate calls but the slow, arrhythmic beat of a single, planetary heart. I feel them in the clay, a deep, resonant pulse that travels from the mud, up my legs, and into my hollow self.

But then, I sink deeper. My consciousness follows my legs down into the earth. I am listening below the song of the living. I hear the sound of water seeping through layers of silt deposited before my kind ever learned to hunt. I hear the impossibly slow groan of the clay-bed compacting under its own immense, wet weight. I hear the mineral silence of the planet's finished crust. It is the deep, infrasonic hum of time itself, the drone beneath the frantic melody of life.

I am the point where the noise meets the silence. I am the moment where the frantic, temporary life of the mud is held in communion with the geological eternity of the stone.

I do not think. I do not feel, as others might understand it. I do not remember or plan.

I am a vessel, anchored and empty, and the entire world, from the patter of a single raindrop to the slow grind of the continent, pours its voice into me. I am the quiet at the centre of the noise. I am the stillness that hears everything.

I attend.
