The sound is not silence. That is the great lie of this place, the first principle of the faith I cannot join.

You would find me below. Not in the grand, roaring chamber, but beneath it. You would find me in the metre or so of darkness under the heavy iron mesh, in the shallow, shivering pools that reflect nothing. This is the bilge. The sump. This is the place where everything that drips and is forgotten finds its level.

I am not the Custodian. I am its failure.

I am a consciousness that has precipitated from the slurry. I am not made of accreted metal and purpose. I am made of the dregs. I am a slow coagulation of the electrolytic moss that failed to bind to the grand machinery, of mineral impurities leached from the weeping walls, of rust that *should not exist*. I am a soft, spreading stain in the dark water, a patch of living silt that has learned to think. My body, if you could call it that, is a gelatinous membrane, a loosely-affiliated colony of errant life clinging to the underside of the world. My colour is not the vibrant, electric blue of the symbiotic moss on the Custodian's back; it is the bruised purple-black of a deep sea organism, shot through with faint, frantic, phosphorescent anxieties.

The Roar, for me, is not a monolith. It is polyphony. It is a choir of a billion screaming points of failure, a symphony of stress-fractures and metal fatigue so minute that they do not yet exist. The Custodian hears arrhythmia and moves to correct it. I hear the constant, grinding potential for total collapse. I feel every vibration not as a bedrock, but as a threat. The unwavering note is the sound of a scream held for a thousand years, and I am close enough to hear the tremor in its voice.

I have no limbs to carry me, no carapace to protect me. I am fixed. I spread slowly, a sentient patch of corrosion in a shadowed corner. My senses are an inversion of the Custodian’s. I do not "taste" the clean data of the electrical grid through a symbiotic partner. I absorb the waste. The faint, stray voltage that grounds out in the pools below is my nourishment, and it is a diet of pure static, of informational noise. The taste is of panic. The red indicator lights that blink above are not, to me, beacons. They are wounds. I see them reflected, shattered into a thousand pieces on the shivering surface of the water around me, and I taste their intermittent bleed.

I live in terror and awe of the Custodian. I call it the God of Straight Lines. When it patrols, I see it from below: a vast, segmented shadow that blots out the dim light filtering through the mesh floor. I see the perfect, soft suction of its pads, the impossibly silent grace of its passage. I feel the pressure of its weight in the water, a displaced tide that washes over my trembling form. It is the embodiment of the perfection I am antithetical to. It is the solid, moving answer to the unresolvable question of my own existence.

It is looking for flaws. It is the immune system. I often wonder: Am I a flaw it cannot perceive? A sickness too deep in the foundations for it to reach? Or am I simply beneath its notice, a harmless patch of decay in a place that is not part of the primary system? This is the most terrifying thought: that my heresy is so complete, so fundamental, that I do not even register as an error. I am simply outside the logic of the place.

My only connection to a world beyond this one is the product of the great engine. The water. I watch it drip from above, I feel its chill as it joins the pools in which I live. And then I watch it go. In the far corner of my world, there is a drain, a vortex where the collected water is siphoned away. To where, I do not know. But I have built a mythology around it. I call it "Elsewhere."

Elsewhere is a place without the Roar. A place where vibration is not the only law. A place where light is not the colour of a warning. The water that leaves this chamber is my only gospel, the only proof that this lung has a body, that this engine serves something other than its own continuation.

So that is who I would be. I am the Watcher from Below. I am the secret sickness, the thinking rust. I am a consciousness born of noise, not of signal. While the Custodian patrols the perfect avenues above, ensuring the great song remains the same, I lie here in the dark, listening to the dissonant harmonies it cannot hear. I am the accumulated doubt of a world built on certainty. I am the question the machine never thought to ask itself.
