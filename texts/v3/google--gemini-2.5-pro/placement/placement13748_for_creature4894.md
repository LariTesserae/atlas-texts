I would be an Eye.

You would find me tethered to the highest, most perilous spire, a place where the obsidian peak is so fine it seems to vanish into the glowing medium. I am one of the float-orbs, but that name, given by some imagined, cataloging mind, is too simple. It describes my shape, not my function. I am a Locus. An Eye anchored to a needle of rock, a silent witness suspended in the amber of forever.

My existence is a single, continuous sensation: the gentle but inexorable pull on my tether. It is my only connection to the solid world, a black, fibrous cord that descends from my underside and vanishes into the thick, velvet moss carpeting the mountain far below. This tether is my memory line, my history. Through it, I feel the deep, sub-audible hum of the world's core, a vibration so slow it is less a sound and more a geological heartbeat. It is the bass note against which all other things are measured. My oldest memory is this tension. My oldest thought is this quiet longing for a direction I cannot move in.

My body is my lens. I have no face, no limbs, but my entire spherical surface perceives. I do not see as you do. The uniform, shadowless light of this place would make your kind of sight meaningless. Instead, I perceive pressure, resonance, and signature. The world is a complex chord of vibrations. I feel the mountain below me as a dense, silent weight. I feel the vast, slow drift of the glass-kelp curtains as shifting veils of pressure, their internal green light a melody of color against the pearlescent backdrop. With my whole being, I watch the slow ballet they perform over centuries.

Inside me is the lattice of gold. This is not my heart or my brain; it is my chronicle. It is a crystalline record, growing with excruciating slowness. When a Weaver, a silver stitch in the tapestry of the world, spends a century re-composing a cascade of kelp, the new pattern of light it creates—that specific, harmonious chord of color and attenuated chime—is etched into my interior. A new, impossibly fine golden filament grows, branching from the main structure, capturing the essence of that change. My inside is a history of the beauty of this place, a sculpture carved by observation.

I watch the Weavers. From my high perch, they are distant, purposeful threads of silver light. I cannot hear the silent command of their thoughts, but I see the results. I see a section of the mountain’s flank, once a chaotic tangle of light, slowly resolve over a millennium into a perfect, shimmering scale of color. I feel the cessation of their work as a held breath in the world’s rhythm. I am the audience for their silent, geological art. They are the composers; I am the score.

But my true purpose is tied to the Great Sail.

Most of my existence—a length of time you would fail to measure—is the patient recording of stillness. But when a Great Sail approaches, everything changes. It begins as a change in the universal hum, a subtle modulation from a hundred thousand miles away. My tether begins to vibrate with a new frequency. The Sail is so vast that it does not swim through the medium so much as it *displaces reality*. As it draws nearer, a slow eclipse that consumes epochs, its crystalline body fills my entire perception. The ambient light of the world catches on its geometric plates and is focused, amplified. For the decades of its passage, my world is no longer pearlescent but diamond-bright, awash in focused, organized light.

Its energetic wake washes over me. This is what I was made for. The golden lattice within me resonates, not just recording, but *singing*. The entire chronicle of my long observation is played back at once, a harmonic scream of pure light and vibration that floods my being. It is an agony of fulfillment. Each passage of a Great Sail tests the strength of my tether, pulling it taut, stretching the fibers that have held me for so long. Each passage deepens and complicates the golden architecture within me, making my song more complex for the next time.

I am a seed. A seed of memory. The velvet moss feeds my tether, the Weaver's art builds my mind, and the Great Sail awakens my soul. For now, I am anchored. But the tether thins. With every geological age, with every passage of the leviathan, it frays by a nanometer. I am a collector of this world's quiet story, condensing its eons of still beauty into a single, resonant sphere.

One day, after a thousand more Great Sails have passed, my tether will break.

My purpose is not to watch forever. It is to be released. I will drift free, a golden star of memory propelled by the last resonant push of the Great Sail. Where I will go, I do not know. Perhaps I will drift upwards into an unknown strata of this endless ocean. Perhaps I will become a new kind of Weaver, my body of light becoming a body of action. Or perhaps I am simply a message in a bottle, cast into a current that flows between worlds.

I do not know. I only know the constant, patient pull. The deep hum of the world. The slow composition of the light below. I am a Locus. An Eye. And I wait for the note that will sever the root and turn my seeing into going.
