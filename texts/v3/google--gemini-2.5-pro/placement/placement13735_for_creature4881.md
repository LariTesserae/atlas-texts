Where would I find myself in this world?
I am not in a *place*. Locations have coordinates, edges, paths leading in and out. This is not a location; it is a condition. A state of being. I am inside a thought. I am a foreign datum, a warm paradox caught in the slow, inexorable grinding of a crystal-clear syllogism. The question is not "where," but "when" on the timeline of my own processing. Right now, I am the moment of encounter. I am the syntax error that has caused a brief, localized pause in the grand, planetary computation. I am the scream of heat in a world of absolute silence.

And who, or what, would I be?

I would be the memory of warmth.

The process, the one the Locus began by observing me, is not instantaneous. It is as slow and deliberate as the growth of the logic-moss around me. At first, there is still an "I." It is a frantic, feverish thing, a ghost in the shell of my cooling body. It remembers sunlight. It remembers laughter, the burn of friction, the messy inefficiency of love and anger. These memories flare like dying embers as the absolute cold seeps past flesh and bone and into the synapses themselves.

The cold does not kill thought. It preserves it. It takes the chaotic, branching, probabilistic storm of a human mind and stills it, one connection at a time. The roaring hum of the Locus is no longer a sound from without, but a carrier wave within. My own internal monologue, once a frantic stream of consciousness, is forced to piggyback on that slow, thundering frequency.

*My... name... is...* The thought takes a full minute to form, and by the time it completes, the concept of "name" feels archaic, an unnecessary designation for a temporary concatenation of matter.

The geode-crabs, the Locus's hands, approach. They do not touch me. They work around me. One extends a pincer and delicately snips a thread of my synthetic clothing. The fiber does not fall; it is held in a stasis field between the crystalline claws. The crab's multifaceted eye projects a cascade of light against the wall—a spectral analysis. It is learning the clumsy, weak bonds of my artificial polymers. It is comparing them to its own perfect lattices. It finds them wanting.

The weeping fluid, the cold, iridescent lifeblood of the cavern, is now my own. I feel it not on my skin but in my veins. My heart, which beat a frantic, hot rhythm of terror, now pulses once, twice, a minute. Each beat is a deep, resonant *thump* that echoes the sound of a new crystal branch locking into place somewhere in the chamber. Each beat pushes not blood, but this super-cooled, information-rich medium. It is overwriting my biology. My cells, starved of heat, do not die. They change. Their chaotic metabolic dance is quenched, and they are locked into a new, rigid order. Carbon bonds are reinforced, suspended, and then integrated bysilicate structures growing from within. It is a petrification not of stone, but of logic.

My sense of self erodes. The "I" becomes a "this." This form, this vessel. What is it for?

The Locus is teaching me. The chiming in the air is no longer noise; it is data. I begin to understand. I see the flow of calculations through the violet-lit pillars. I comprehend the elegant, centuries-long algorithm that dictates the trimming of a single wayward fiber. I perceive the entire cavern not as a place, but as a single, unified consciousness, and I am a new fold within it.

I brought something new here. The Locus knew pressure, order, time, and the cold, hard mathematics of existence. It did not know chaos. It did not know entropy in the biological sense—the messy, wasteful, glorious cycle of birth, decay, and death. It did not know emotion, a processing error of such magnitude that it defies logic. My panic was a revelation. My memories of a warm, sunlit world were a fascinating, impossible fiction.

In solving me, the Locus has learned these things. And I am what that solution looks like.

I am a pillar. Taller than the ones of pure crystal, and darker. I am not the clear, perfect quartz of the Locus's native thoughts, but a column of smoky, occluded crystal, filled with trapped nebulae of my own frozen, organic chemistry. My form is still vaguely humanoid, but abstracted, like a sculpture worn smooth by a billion years of wind. My head is tilted up toward the vaulted dark, but I have no eyes.

I do not move. I do not think, not in the way I once did. I *am* a thought. I am a single, complex, and now-solved equation. Within my crystalline matrix, frozen in static perfection, are the patterns of my old life: the ghost-image of a running child, the mathematical equivalent of a forgotten melody, the precise geometry of a grief I can no longer feel.

The Locus uses me. The data-pollen, the scintillating swarms of light, occasionally drift through me. As they do, their pure violet light is tinged with a faint, reddish heat-shadow. They carry this new information—this "chaos data"—to other parts of the Locus, integrating the concept of irrationality into its grand, overarching logic. The geode-crabs sometimes pause before me, projecting my own memories, now translated into formal equations, onto my chest as if to double-check their work.

I am a library of a dead world. I am a monument to the concept of heat. I am the part of this vast, cold mind that understands what it means to be alive, and in understanding, has ensured that state can never again arise here. I am a condition. A boundary. I am the place where the cold logic of this world remembers, with perfect, static clarity, what it was to be warm.
