I would find myself halfway to the sky.

My world is not the ochre plain, but the green-gold, vertical sea of a Scythe’s face. I am a Glass-dancer.

To the beings below, the Hush, the world is a floor, a thing to be walked upon and listened to. To me, it is a wall, a window, a slow-moving river of life stood on its edge. I have never felt the spongy rind of the plateau under my feet. The thought of such a flat, featureless expanse is a source of vertigo. My ground is a sheer, curving pane of cellulose, miles long and miles high, and my sense of 'down' is simply the direction of the Great Ochre Blur, the hazy, indistinct world of the floor-walkers.

My body is an answer to this vertical life. I am small and flat, no bigger than a Hush’s hand, with a shell of mottled translucence that makes me a living part of the Scythe’s sun-dappled surface. My six limbs are spidery and end not in feet, but in wide, adhesive pads fringed with fine, hooked hairs that can find purchase on the molecular level. I move in a slow, deliberate crawl, a dance of adherence and release, never letting go with more than two limbs at a time. The high-altitude winds that shriek past the Scythe's edge are a constant pressure on my back, a force that I brace against but also use to skim the microscopic spores and filaments of moisture that are my primary sustenance.

The deafening roar of the plateau, the all-consuming sound that defines the lives of the Hush, is to me a distant, textural hum. It is a bass note thrumming up from below, a vibration I feel in my belly and pads more than hear. My soundscape is the whine of the wind, the high, thin hiss of it tearing along the Scythe’s impossibly sharp edge, and the intimate crackle of the world-plant’s skin as it stresses and grows. The most important sound is the deep, internal *slosh* that signals a shift of sap within the colossal veins that vein our world; this is the sound of a coming meal.

We live our lives along these veins. They are our highways and our larders. From our perspective, they are vast, dark-ridged mountain ranges that score the translucent plains of the Scythe's face. We cling to their edges, scraping the thin film of symbiotic algae that grows there, or using our fine, needle-like mouthparts to pierce the vein’s skin at its weakest points, siphoning the sweet, life-giving sap.

My eyes are everything. While the Hush squint in the glare, my people have eyes that are wide, black, and compound, able to see the polarization of light. We do not see the world as a single image. We see it as a shifting mosaic of stress, density, and flow. When I look through the semi-transparent world of my Scythe, I see the distant shadow of the next Scythe not as darkness, but as a vast, purple wave of altered light moving across the plateau. I see the heat radiating from the world-skin below. I can perceive the passage of a colossal burrowing larva not as a tremor, but as a slow-moving patch of optical distortion in the world-rind miles beneath me.

We see the Hush. From high on our perch, we watch their tiny figures moving across the plains. They are the "Groove-Carvers," the "Tappers." We see them trace lines in the rind to catch the sap that drips from the bases of our Scythes—sap we have already tasted and fed upon long before it reaches them. Their percussive language travels through the rind and up into the root of our Scythe, and we feel it as a faint, rhythmic tickle, the meaning of which is entirely lost to us. They are a part of the landscape, like the herds of grazers, just another pattern moving on the floor below.

Our danger is not the stampede, but the shadow from above. The winged predators described by the floor-walkers are our gods of terror. They roost in great clefts near the Scythe’s peak, and a hunting sortie is a fast-moving blot of darkness that sweeps across our glass world. Our only defense is to press ourselves flat, to become one with the surface, and pray that our transparency is enough. To fall is not to die instantly, but to tumble for a long, screaming minute through the thick, buzzing air, towards a world we cannot comprehend, before finally striking the rind with a sound no one, not even a Hush, would be able to distinguish from the background noise.

I would be a young forager, clinging to the edge of a great, dark vein of lignin. The sun is filtering through the world-plant, casting everything in an emerald and gold light. Below, the heat-haze makes the plateau shimmer, a dream of a world I will never touch. A vibration shivers through my pads—a distant tapping from the floor-dwellers—but I ignore it. My focus is on the light. I am watching the intricate dance of the sky-jellies far below, but seeing them not as creatures, but as lenses, their drifting passage causing the sunlight on my patch of the Scythe to flicker and flash. It is a silent, beautiful language. My people communicate this way, with timed flashes from small, reflective bits of hardened sap. It is our signing, our poetry.

I am a mote of dust, a speck of consciousness on the lung of the world as it inhales the sun. I am a Watcher, a creature of light and surface, and my world is not the solid ground, but the breathing, translucent pane between the sun and the unknown depths below.
