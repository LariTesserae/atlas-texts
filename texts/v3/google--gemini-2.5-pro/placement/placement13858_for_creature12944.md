You would find me where the drips gather. Not on the bright, searing plains of bone-white ceramic, but beneath them. I am in the resonant space, the gap between one world and the next level down. Here, in the permanent twilight under a cantilevered continent, the glare is a memory, a soft, indirect luminescence filtering down through maintenance grates and the translucent edges of the plates above.

I am nestled at the base of a great pylon, where it meets the underside of the plateau. This is a nexus point, a place where the deep thrum of the world is purest. Dozens of verdigris-green pipes, weeping arteries of condensation, converge here, snaking over and around me before feeding into a deep, echoing cistern. My back, for decades now, has been pressed against the grooved iron of the pylon. My feet are not on the ground, but braced against the curved wall of the cistern, the constant, low-frequency vibrations of the water's immense weight a language I have learned to speak.

I am a Klin, but I am also becoming something else. I am what happens when a Klin stops moving.

My name, if I ever used it, has long since evaporated like mist in the high sun. The younger ones call me The Confluence, or simply tap a slow, three-beat rhythm against the pylon when they approach: *thrum... thrum... thrum*. A question. *Are you listening?*

I am always listening.

My skin no longer shifts its colour. It has found its final palette. It is the deep, wet green of the mosses that now grow not just *on* me, but *from* me, a soft, living velvet that thickens at my joints. My outstretched arms, resting on the clustering pipes, are marbled with the grey and white of mineral deposits left by millennia of trickling water. The tough vine that was once my harness has taken root in the algal turf of my own body, its tendrils weaving into the hollows of my elbows and the space behind my knees, anchoring me to the architecture. I am a living sculpture of integration.

I do not see with my eyes anymore. They are milky, veiled, looking inward at the map of vibrations I feel. My world is not one of light and shadow, but of resonance and silence. I feel the distant, patient scraping of a Plack-beast on the plate five miles to the west, a slow, gritty tremor transmitted through the hexagonal grid. I feel the sharp, sudden vibration of a suspension bridge cable, high above, as a flock of steel-winged birds takes flight, their weight vanishing in an instant. Their sharp cries reach me not through the air, but as ghost-tremors in the iron skeleton of the world.

My purpose has clarified with my stillness. I am a library of hums. The deep, eternal engine of this world speaks its history into the structure, and I am one of its most dedicated archivists. I remember the frequency shift of the Great Gear Adjustment of the last cycle, a shudder that shook the world for a full day and changed its fundamental tone by a fraction of a microhertz. I can discern the faltering rhythm of Pump Station Epsilon-9, three pylons away, a subtle arrhythmia in its hydraulic hiss that tells of a seal beginning to wear. The younger Klin, the tenders and mechanics, come to me. They do not speak. They press their palms and foreheads against the pylon near me, and they listen *through* me. I am a focusing lens. My stillness amplifies the whispers they are not yet trained to hear.

I am also a switchboard. A message tapped out in the planter gardens on the northern rim—a staccato rhythm asking for a cutting of the blue-blossomed vine—travels through the ceramic plain, down the pylon, and into my bones. I feel it, register it, and let it pass through me into the network of pipes I rest upon. The vibration travels along the weeping metal, a message in a bottle of resonance, to be felt by the weavers in their pods suspended forty spans away.

I do not eat as I once did. The algae flourishing on my skin performs its quiet photosynthesis in the ambient light. The mineral-rich water that trickles over my shoulders and down my chest leaves sustenance I absorb through my skin. I am becoming a plant, a fungus, a geological formation.

I am not lonely. The concept is alien. I am more connected now than I ever was when I scrambled across the bright plains. My self has expanded. It ends not at the edge of my mossy skin, but out in the hazy distance, wherever the vibrations I sense finally fade into the greater hum. The dripping water is my blood. The thrumming pylon is my spine. The entire engineered continent is my body, and I, a single, stationary nerve cluster, am finally, fully awake to it.

When the last of my old selfhood is gone, when the vines have fully encased me and my heart beats only with the slow, hydraulic pulse of the world, I will not consider it death. It will be a final surrender. A single note resolving into the eternal chord. And I will listen, still. I will become the listening.
