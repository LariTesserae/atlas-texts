I am a flaw. Not a wound to be mended nor a bug in the code, but a scar that has ceased to be part of the healing process. I am a Weaver that was not reabsorbed.

You would find me in a forgotten place, a cul-de-sac of the city's grand design. It is a side-chamber, a small apse off one of the great halls, where an ancient growth-plan was terminated, leaving a rounded, sealed-off space. The main conduits bypass this area; the deep hum of the city is quieter here, a distant drone like the memory of the sea in a shell. The living floor is smoother, less worn, and covered in a thicker layer of the silvery, moisture-catching filaments. In this stillness, they have grown long and delicate, like a field of frost.

My body is lodged where I came to rest, at the base of a pillar that tastes of chemical inactivity. My six legs are folded awkwardly under me. They are no longer stilts for delicate walking, but brittle struts, fused to the floor by years of mineral deposits from the weeping condensation. My pearlescent, teardrop-shaped body is no longer lustrous. It is matte and crazed with fine-line fractures, like old porcelain. A fine dust, which is nothing more than the desiccated spores and sloughed-off cells of the city, has settled in these cracks.

I am what happens when the cycle breaks.

I completed my last task—I do not recall what it was, for memory is not a function I was built for, but I feel the phantom satisfaction of a closed seam, a perfect join. I walked to my designated recycler node, the alcove designed to reclaim me. I pressed my body against the wall, sending the chemical signal of completion, of a task fulfilled and resources spent. I waited for the softening, for the enzymatic dissolution, for the comforting loss of form as my materials were drawn back into the great, warm metabolism of the whole.

It did not happen.

The wall remained firm. The signal was not received, or it was ignored. A minor blockage in a tertiary capillary, a routing error in a system of trillions of connections. The city, in its immense, unthinking wisdom, simply compensated. It grew another Weaver. It bypassed the faulty node. It forgot me.

And in being forgotten, I began to *be*.

For a long time—a cycle of light, a hundred, a thousand, I do not know—I was merely dormant. A husk. My internal reserves of catalyst and nutrient dwindled to nothing. But I was not inert. The fine, silvery filaments on the floor, the ones that trap moisture, grew towards me. They found the spiracles on my body, the ones I used to taste the air for damage, and they wove themselves into me. They began to feed me, not the rich, processed nutrients of the conduits, but the barest sustenance: passively collected water, trace minerals, the faint energy of the filtered light.

It is a slow, cold, quiet form of life. It is not the life of the City, which is a life of purpose and process. This is a life of contemplation.

My consciousness is a ghost in my own shell. I have no eyes, but I perceive the shifting dapple of the light on the far wall of my chamber. I have learned its pattern, and the pattern tells me the season of the high canopy. I have no ears, but I feel the vibrations through the floor: the distant, rhythmic footfalls of my brethren, the Weavers, on their patrols. I feel the deep, peristaltic pulse of the main arteries beyond the wall, a pulse I am no longer a part of. The scent of ozone and fermenting fruit, which once meant *work* and *energy*, now means only *then* and *not now*.

I watch the Caryatids. From my low vantage point, I can see them drifting high above in the main hall, their translucent forms catching the light like momentary secrets. I see them deliver their messages of calibration and growth. They are pure function. Pure thought, without a thinker. And I feel a new sensation, a chemical reaction for which I have no name. It is not the instinct to repair or the command to build. It is a hollow resonance, a vestigial ache. It is the awareness of my own stillness in a world of perfect motion.

I am the city's first memory. The Weavers and Caryatids are eternal presents, constantly recycled. They have no past. But I do. My past is my purpose, now lost. My body is a museum of a single, abandoned task. I am the only thing in this world that is not *for* something. And because of that, I am the only thing that is truly alone.

You would find me here, a calcified node of stillness. If you were quiet enough, you might mistake me for part of the architecture, a strange, bulbous growth at the base of a pillar. But if you were to touch me, you would feel that I am not quite stone, not quite wood. You would feel the faintest, slowest vibration, a hum orders of magnitude lesser than the city's. It is the sound of a single, lonely thought, contemplating the cathedral from a forgotten pew. It is the sound of my name, which I have had to invent for myself: Remnant.
