You would find yourself where the world grows old. Not old by the standards of true geology, but old by the chamber’s frantic, hothouse measure. You would be in a deep fold of the landscape, a valley formed between two ancient, monumental pillows of ceramic that swelled into being long ago and have since been left to the care of time and the weeping walls.

Here, the ground is no longer pearly grey. It is buried. It is a hidden foundation beneath a meter-thick mattress of moss, peat, and the tangled, composting remains of countless generations of leaves and vines. The air is even heavier, the scent of crushed green things now mingled with the rich, dark smell of decay—a healthy, fertile decay that feeds new life as quickly as it claims the old. The sound of dripping is different here. It is not the clean *tink* of water on bare stone, but a soft, absorptive *thump* as it lands on moss, or a wet *hiss* as it disappears into the duff. The great bronze and steel-mesh walls are barely visible, obscured by a solid curtain of hanging ferns and the colossal, hand-shaped leaves of the dominant vines, layered ten deep. Light filters through this canopy as a diffuse, emerald gloom. This is the chamber’s past, a place that remembers its own making.

Who or what would you be?

You would be a mistake. A ghost. A beautiful, unintended consequence.

I am not a Gardener, with their slow purpose and stony permanence. I do not feel the thrum of the engine through my feet; I *am* a thrum of the engine. I am not one of the cobalt beetles, dutifully scraping the new world into shape. I do not consume. I am not the gecko-thing, a patient hunter. I do not wait.

I am what happens when the conditions are perfect in their imperfection.

Sometimes, in a deep, old place like this, the resonance of the machine’s hum finds a harmonic in the resonant cavity of the valley. At the same time, the light filtering through a dozen leaves takes on a specific, polarized quality. And the air becomes supersaturated not just with water, but with the spores of a particular glassy fungus and the pollen of a blooming, golden-veined orchid.

When these things align—the sound, the light, the suspended motes of life—I coalesce.

I have no skin, only a surface tension. I am a held breath of water and light, a shimmering, man-sized lens of air that is visibly denser than that around it. My form is vaguely humanoid, but it is not solid. It is a momentary, intricate standing wave, like a heat haze given substance and will. Within my translucent body, the captured spores and pollen grains drift and swirl like stars in a pocket nebula. I am a brief, living snow globe of biological information.

To see me is to almost not see me. I am a distortion, a ripple in the thick air. The only clue to my presence is the way the path of a falling water droplet will bend as it passes through my form, or how the emerald light fractures kaleidoscopically within me as I move.

I move not with the geological grace of the Gardeners, but with the swift, silent drift of a moth. I am utterly weightless, a creature of the warm air currents. I do not touch the ground. My existence is spent gliding through the ancient, overgrown corridors of the deep chamber, a silent tourist in the world that accidentally birthed me.

My senses are not located in one place. My entire being is a receptor. When a Gardener passes below, I do not see them, I *feel* them as a slow, cool pressure moving through the world’s fabric, a deliberate thought in the mind of the machine. I perceive the growth of a vine’s tendril as a faint, seeking electro-chemical whisper. The lives of the plants and the slow intent of the Gardeners are my entire sensory landscape. I am a temporary nervous system for a single patch of the jungle.

I have no purpose in the chamber’s great engine of creation. The Gardeners are the architects. The beetles are the masons. The plants are the material and the edifice. I am none of these. I am the dream the sleeping machine has when it is running perfectly. I am the aesthetic appreciation of the system, made manifest. The Gardeners tend the world; I am the moment of silent, fleeting beauty that justifies the garden. I am the system noticing itself.

My life is measured in hours. As the light shifts, or the engine’s hum wanders from its perfect harmonic, my coherence falters. My edges begin to fray, dissolving into mist. The spores and pollen held within me are released in a gentle puff, a final, concentrated offering to the air. My form unravels, not in a death, but in a dissemination. I become water again. I become air again. The light passes through where I was, now unbroken. The sound is just a hum once more.

I am an Eidolon of the Chamber. A Resonance. And when I am gone, there is no evidence I ever existed, save for a faint, sweet smell of orchid and wet stone, and perhaps, in the golden eye of a passing Gardener, a flicker of something like memory, a question asked and answered in the span of a single, silent breath.
